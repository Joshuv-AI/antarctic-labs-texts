Laparoscopic transabdominal pre-peritoneal (TAPP) inguinal hernia repair in a second level hospital setting: a novel anesthesiologic approach to reduce postoperative pain
Authors: Giulia Montori, Flavio Badii, Elisabetta Serra, Irene Recchia, Giuseppa Procida, Alessandra Versaci, Matteo Tolot, Andrea Dal Borgo, Tobia Gobbi, Giorgio Mazzarolo, Edelweiss Licitra, Michelangelo Salemi, Ferdinando Agresta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeLaparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal hernia inguinal repair (TAPP) is a well-standardized surgical procedure, but postoperative pain management lacks clear standardizations. Intravenous Anesthesia (IA) can be combined with intraoperative local anesthesia procedures like the Erector Spinae (ESP) or the Transversus Abdominis Plane (TAP) block. The association of local pain control and low pneumoperitoneum (8-10mmHg) appears to reduce postoperative pain and improve patient and nurse satisfaction.MethodsBetween January and December 2023, 903 TAPP were performed at Vittorio Veneto Hospital (TV). 60 patients were collected and divided into three groups: TAPP under IA alone, IA plus ESP block, and IA plus TAP block. We monitored Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for pain at T0 (at the end of surgery), T1 (after 6 h) and T2 (after 12 h), along with postoperative analgesic consumption.ResultsNo significant differences in comorbidities were observed among groups, and no conversions occurred. Compared to IA alone, both ESP and TAP block groups reported significantly less pain at T0 (p = 0.01) and T1 (p = 0.036), but not at T2 (p = NS). ESP and TAP block groups also had lower consumption of Ketorolac and less nausea at T0 (p = 0.02 and p = 0.09) due to reduced intraoperative opioid use. These differences were not observed at T1 and T2. Operative time was significantly shorter in IA-only group compared to TAP; no difference was noticed between TAP and ESP.ConclusionsAdding ESP or TAP block to IA during elective laparoscopic TAPP for uni-/bilateral inguinal hernias, along with low pneumoperitoneum, is feasible and may reduce early postoperative pain, opioid and other analgesic consumption, and nausea. Further evidence-based studies, like RCTs, are required to support these findings.Keywords: Transabdominal pre-peritoneal hernia repair, TAPP, Inguinal hernia, ESP block, TAP block

Introduction

Inguinal hernia repair is one of the most common surgical procedures performed all over the world and in different hospital settings. Guidelines and international recommendations deem Transabdominal Pre-Peritoneal (TAPP) hernia repair a safe and valid approach compared with open anterior approach for inguinal hernia repair, especially if the surgeon is experienced [1, 2]. Pain is still an important outcome to consider in abdominal wall surgery, both with open and minimally invasive approach [2]. However, as stated in the guidelines, laparo-endoscopic techniques show less chronic pain and less postoperative pain if compared with open repair [1, 2]. To reduce and improve postoperative pain control, according to the anesthesiologic team, we compared the use of local block anesthesia, like ESP and TAP block, combined with Intravenous Anesthesia (IA).

Transversus Abdominis Plane (TAP) block has been widely studied in TAPP inguinal hernia repair setting; in contrast, Erector Spinae (ESP) block is not so common, and the corresponding results are unclear.

In addition, we combined these two types of local block with an intraoperative low pneumoperitoneum ranging between 6 and 10 mmHg.

According to the literature, low pneumoperitoneum (< 10 mmHg) is associated with less pain at 1-4-12 h postoperative, as well as at 1 and 2 days postoperative, including reduced shoulder pain [3].

We analyzed our data according to this three setting.

Materials and methods

Sixty patients who underwent laparoscopic TAPP repair, in elective surgery, were prospectively collected from January 2023 to December 2023 in Vittorio Veneto Hospital (TV), Italy, a community hospital in Northeast Italy.

The patients were divided into three groups preoperatively: ESP block plus IA group, TAP block plus IA group, and IA alone group.

No randomization was performed; instead, a prospective evaluation of our three cohorts of laparoscopically treated inguinal hernia cases was conducted, in agreement with the anesthesiology team performing the blocks.

Patients included presented primary or recurrent unilateral or bilateral inguinal hernia.

Preoperative symptoms were evaluated (non-symptomatic patient, discomfort during daily activities). Patients with no clear diagnosis underwent preoperative dynamic inguinal ultrasound to confirm the diagnosis. Patients were thoroughly informed about laparoscopic and anesthesiologic procedure, and written consent was accordingly signed. During preoperative evaluation, patients were informed that in the event of intraoperative identification of incidental contralateral hernia a simultaneous repair could be performed only if a written consent had previously been obtained. The preoperative work up (chest X-rays, ECG, and routine blood tests) was the same as any laparoscopic procedures.

Our surgical team was composed by a chief and expert in laparoscopic surgery, six attending surgeons, 1 resident. All the surgeons were mentored by the chief of department for TAPP. Three of them were already experienced laparoscopic surgeons who had completed the learning curve for TAPP, while the fourth was still in training.

The surgical technique was standardized. In all cases a polypropylene mesh was implanted in preperitoneal position, without fixation, except for large direct hernia (M2-3 according with EHS classification [4], and with a dimension of 10 × 15 cm (BULEV1015UL Ultralightweight, macroporous, non-absorbable, monofilament polypropylene mesh, manufactured by DIPRO MEDICAL DEVICES SRL, ITALY, in the first 20 cases, and in the other cases BULEV1515 Lightweight, macroporous, non-absorbable, monofilament polypropylene mesh, manufactured by DIPRO MEDICAL DEVICES SRL, ITALY). Three 5 mm atraumatic trocars were respectively placed in umbilical region, left and right flank. We performed always pneumoperitoneum with CO2.

General anesthesia was performed through orotracheal intubation after induction, which was carried out as follows: hypnotic (Propofol) administered through the TCI (Target-Controlled Infusion) method via a peripheral venous access of adequate caliber, Rocuronium as a muscle relaxant and Fentanyl as an opioid. Anesthesia was maintained using Propofol and Remifentanil. All patients were monitored as for spO2, fc, EtCO2, pPlat, TOF, BIS and all were ventilated in protective controlled mechanical ventilation with 6–8 ml/Kg tidal volume. In all patients who underwent IA, as well as those who underwent ALR with TAP and ESP block, the block was performed prior to surgical incision. The TAP block was performed by an in-plane, ultrasound-guided technique with injection of local anesthetic (Ropivacaine 0.375% 20 ml + Dexamethasone 4 mg) administered between internal oblique muscle and transverse muscle of the abdomen, thus aiming for the blockade of intercostal afferent sensory fibers innervating the abdominal wall. The spinal erector plane (ESP) block was performed at the T7 level using an in-plane, ultrasound-guided technique with the patient in a seated position; an injection of Ropivacaine 0.375% with the addition of 4 mg Dexamethasone was administered. The diffusion of local anesthetic was observed in both blocks by ultrasounds. Patients who received intravenous analgesia without blocks were treated with Paracetamol, Tramadol, Ketorolac and opioids.

Length of stay (LOS) was evaluated in days. The reimbursement system of our hospital does not consider an admission in Day Surgery for these patients: therefore, all patients spent at least one night at the hospital. Patients residing more than one hour from the hospital, those who requested it, or patients with more complex cases, spent an additional night at the hospital.

Patients were followed during hospitalization (as for analgesic consumption, nausea, vomiting), and Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) was assessed postoperatively immediately after surgery (T0), 6 h (T1), 12 h (T2), 18 h (T3), 24 h (T4), and at the discharge (T5), and was checked by nurses and anesthesiologists.

Postoperative complications were recorded and categorized as early (within 30 days) and late (more than 30 days).

The primary endpoint of the study is to assess the safety and feasibility of the two techniques (ESP and TAP block), while evaluating procedural complications. The secondary endpoints are to evaluate analgesic consumption, RNS scores, and postoperative complications during the postoperative period.

Categorical variables were analyzed using contingency tables and were compared with chi-square test. The continuous variables (operational time) were analyzed with Anova and T-test to better compare the differences among the three groups. Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.005. All analysis was performed using SPSS 26 (IBM Corp., Released 2011, IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

The characteristics of the patients were reported in Table 1.

No complications occurred during anesthesiologic procedures. Morphine was used only in IA.

During surgery, no conversion to open was required, and no intraoperative complications occurred.

Operative time was similar in the two groups of ESP and TAP block. However, we found a statistically significant difference between IA and TAP, in favor of IA alone (p = 0.001). This difference was not observed between ESP and IA (p = 0.013).

Figures 1, 2 and 3 reported the pain after surgery (T0), at 6 h (T1) and after 12 h (T2) in terms of NRS. NRS at T0 and T1 showed a significant decrease in ESP and TAP block groups compared with IA alone group, with p = 0.01 and p = 0.036, respectively.

A decrease in pain was observed after 6 h, while no statistically significant difference was found at subsequent time points (T2, T3, T4, T5).

In the ESP and TAP block groups patients reported less nausea at T0 compared to the IA alone group (p = 0.09). No differences were detected at subsequent time points (T1, T2, T3, T4, T5). Patients in the IA alone group required less Ketorolac than the other groups (p = 0.02). In the ESP block group 2 patients experienced nausea and vomiting at T1; however, both had reported a history of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) during the preoperative anesthesiology assessment.

As for postoperative complications: in the IA alone group 3 patients developed a hematoma, 2 a testicular discomfort, and 1 reported pain within the first 30 postoperative days; in ESP block group 1 patients developed a hematoma, while another one reported inguinal pain that resolved spontaneously; in the TAP block group no complications were observed.

No signs of mesh-related complications (prosthesis rejection and/or infection or shrinkage, or migration) were observed in our sample, both in the early or late postoperative time. No cases of deep or superficial surgical infection or mesh infection were recorded.

Discussion

Laparoscopic hernia repair has been deemed safe and effective in different guidelines and articles [1, 2].

Nevertheless, in abdominal wall surgery – especially in open approach – pain represents a significant problem [5]. Laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair is not exempt from this issue, which can affect the effectiveness of minimally invasive techniques [5, 6]. Pain control is always mandatory, and it is even more relevant in this kind of patients, that is, often young and healthy people with an important working activity, who require a rapid return to daily activities.

The use of low pneumoperitoneum appears to be safe and effective in reducing both systemic effects – such as pulmonary and cardiovascular – and local effects (e.g. shoulder pain, overall pain, especially in the first 48 h). Moreover, it is associated to a reduction in analgesic consumption during the first three postoperative days [3].

Several articles report that TAP block can be considered useful to decrease pain after abdominal wall surgery [7, 8], but only few papers regarding the use of ESP block are available [9, 10].

Some authors compared TAP versus anterior quadratus lumborum block in unilateral inguinal hernia repair, without noticing any differences in oral morphine consumption, pain, nausea or sedation [7]. According to a review on TAP block, combining such a technique with a minimally invasive approach leads to a decrease in opioid analgesic consumption, as well as to better pain control outcomes compared to other regional anesthesia modalities [8].

Other authors have demonstrated that in unilateral inguinal hernia open repair ESP block is ineffective if compared with spinal anesthesia, spinal anesthesia with morphine as adjuvant, and spinal anesthesia associated with erector spinae muscle plane block [9].

However, the above-mentioned results can be interpreted in light of the learning curve for the procedure and the type of surgery. Considering the lack of data and the biases in published studies, no definitive conclusion can be drawn about this technique [9]. We acknowledge the limitations of this study, particularly the small sample size its observational design. Nonetheless, we aimed to highlight the positive impact of combining low pneumoperitoneum pressure with targeted anesthetic blocks on enhanced pain control and reduced analgesic consumption, especially in young patients eager to resume their working life quickly.

Altınsoy S. et al. found similar results to those reported in this paper: a reduction in tramadol consumption in laparoscopic unilateral inguinal repair and pain reduction especially in the first postoperative hours [11].

Consequently, in this study we tried to analyze three groups of patients underwent elective TAPP for inguinal hernia treated laparoscopically to evaluate the effect of the two types of anesthetic block (ESP and TAP) compared to Intravenous Anesthesia alone.

Despite the small sample size, we could observe a decrease in overall pain, especially in the first postoperative hours, with less nausea and less opioid consumption during surgery in favor of Paracetamol and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. These management is accepted by patients, especially sportsmen/women and patients with an intense working activity [12], who require a more rapid return to daily activities, with a good degree of satisfaction.

Only one surgery lasted longer (165 min), due to extensive adhesiolysis from previous operations; however, this did not impact the overall operative time of the ESP group.

According to statistical analysis, if we assume that the outlier represented by the longest intervention (lasting 165 min) is raised to the same level of the maximum range within the rest of the sample, the difference in mean operative times between the ESP and the IA groups would become statistically significant. However, the significant data on operating times in patients undergoing IA compared to the ESP and TAP groups is not meaningful, except for a fortuitous case due to the small study sample. The reason for this difference is not clear. According to our results, it is not related to the learning curve of some operators; we therefore hypothesize a correlation with the duration of some more complex procedures, either because of complicated hernia recurrences or adhesiolysis from previous operations.

Although our study is observational and not randomized, based on our current clinical practice in both anesthesiology and surgery, we found some interesting data. First of all, we observed a clear benefit in pain reduction, as well as in the reduction in analgesic consumption, especially opioids; moreover, the synergy provided by the type of anesthesia (knowledge of block techniques, appropriate use of drugs for both blocks and IA) and the type of surgery (low pneumoperitoneum, standardized and fast technique, both for uni- and bilateral treatment, use of 5 mm trocars to minimize the insult to the abdominal wall) can improve the final outcomes. The multimodal approach may be the most effective one, leading to benefits and a good degree of satisfaction for both patients and for nurses, due to reduced care effort, as patients experience less pain and are calmer.

However, more data and studies are required to increase the support these results, along with a more detailed quality of life questionnaire.

Conclusion

In conclusion, laparoscopic preperitoneal inguinal hernia repair can be considered a safe procedure associated to less pain compared to open approach, particularly when performed by experienced and well-trained surgeons. Although the literature shows that postoperative pain following TAPP surgery is generally minimal, our study confirms that the synergy of three factors – low-pressure pneumoperitoneum, a standardized surgical technique, and an anesthesiology team experienced in performing local anesthetic blocks – can further reduce both postoperative pain and analgesic use. Together, all these elements help reduce postoperative discomfort and pain, allowing patients to experience a faster recovery and earlier return to daily activities.

Screening for resistance to Pyrenophora graminea in hulless barley germplasm and subsequent morphological and transcriptomic analyses
Authors: You Long, Lu Hou, Erjing Si
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBarley leaf stripe, caused by Pyrenophora graminea, is a seed-borne fungal disease that significantly impacts the production of hulless barley (Hordeum vulgare var. nudum) on the Qinghai‒Tibet Plateau. In this study, we conducted an indoor disease resistance screen of 28 hulless barley germplasm resources using the highly pathogenic P. graminea strains L-7 and FS-18 and identified resistant (ZYM2179) and susceptible (Chaiqing No. 1) materials. We then inoculated these two resources with the highly pathogenic P. graminea strain L-7 and performed morphological observation of sections stained with WGA-AF488 and RNA-seq at different time points.ResultsAs the duration of infection increased, the degree of P. graminea invasion and the number of hyphae significantly increased. In addition, the incidence rate of P. graminea infection in the susceptible resource was greater than that in the resistant resource. A total of 18,523 differentially expressed genes were identified in the transcriptome analysis, functional annotation revealed significant enrichment of transcription factor families such as B3 (539), ERF (294), and NAC (150). Trend analysis identified 20 distinct expression profiles, with differences in gene expression patterns between the two resources. The differentially expressed genes were dynamically regulated over time, with the susceptible resource showing an increasing trend in gene expression, while the resistant resource exhibited an initial increase followed by a decrease. With significant differences in the number of differentially expressed genes enriched in different GO and KEGG pathways between the resistant and susceptible resources. One hundred and four differentially expressed genes were identified in the resistant resource, and these genes were enriched mainly in pathways such as metabolism and plant–pathogen interactions. It is noteworthy that the Rboh and RbohD genes, which are involved in reactive oxygen species production and plant-pathogen interactions, were upregulated in the resistant variety.ConclusionsThe relative expression levels of the 10 candidate genes validated by RT-qPCR, such as HSP70-4 and PER1, were Significantly different between resistant and susceptible resources at 12 h post-inoculation. This is consistent with the results of morphological and transcriptome analysis, indicating that these genes are likely related to barley leaf stripe resistance. This study provides a theoretical basis for the discovery of barley leaf stripe resistance genes and the study of resistance mechanisms in hulless barley and is important for the breeding of new disease-resistant varieties and the development of the hulless barley industry.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-06976-y.Keywords: Hulless barley, Barley leaf stripe, RNA-seq, Transcriptomic analysis, RT‒qPCR

Introduction

Highland barley (Hordeum vulgare L. var. nudum Hook. f.), a variant of barley (Hordeum vulgare L.) in the Poaceae family, is also called naked barley because its hull and grain are easily separated. It is characterized by a short growth period, high yield, and strong adaptability and is one of the main food crops on the Qinghai‒Tibet Plateau [1]. Barley leaf stripe is a common seed-borne disease in barley production; it is caused by Pyrenophora graminea and is a systemic infectious fungal disease. Barley leaf stripe causes an annual yield loss of approximately 10% in Qinghai, and the yield loss in severe plots can reach as high as 25% [2]. Combining data on the genotypes of breeding materials with data on growth phenotypes for screening and identification of resistant resources can greatly improve the accuracy of breeding selection and the efficiency of breeding research, accelerating the process of disease-resistant breeding in highland barley. It is thus the most effective way to carry out disease-resistant breeding and control the disease.

The ability of plants to adapt to pathogen infection involves multiple regulatory mechanisms and complex defence systems, and the genes carried by plants themselves are the basis for their disease resistance [3]. The expression of genes that regulate processes involved in plant disease resistance, such as signal transduction, redox reactions, and metabolic processes, causes changes in plant protein products, ultimately leading to better disease resistance in plants [4]. To date, significant progress has been made in the study of plant disease resistance mechanisms. However, there is still no consensus on whether the molecular bases of disease resistance in plants of the same type are similar or whether there is specificity in disease resistance genes among different species. Using transcriptomics methods to identify and mine disease resistance genes in plants will help clarify plant disease resistance mechanisms [5, 6].

P. graminea exhibits substantial genetic variation, which has led to the loss of disease resistance in many barley germplasm resources [7]. To date, three resistant genes have been identified and located; for example, Biselli [8] identified Rdg1a in Hordeum spontaneum marker. Tacconi G et al. [9] discovered a new barley leaf stripe resistance gene, Rdg2a, in the barley variety 'Thibaut'; Si Erjing et al. [10] located Rdg3 on the 7HS chromosome of 'Ganpi No. 2'. Yang et al. [11] conducted BSR-seq analysis of hybrid populations of Qingke Kunlun 14 (resistant) and Z1141 (susceptible) and used RT‒qPCR technology to screen and validate candidate disease resistance genes, they reported that HvnRGA at 5H and HvnWAK at 6H may play important regulatory roles in the barley stress response. Wei et al. [12, 13] used P. graminea to infect the resistant barley variety 'Kunlun 14' and the susceptible barley variety 'Z1141' and performed RNA-seq, the results revealed that seven NB-ARC genes were differentially expressed. Through KEGG enrichment analysis, one NB-ARC gene was shown to play a role in the interaction between barley and P. graminea. Under the stress of leaf stripe disease, the expression level of HvRPS2 in barley significantly decreased, whereas the expression level of HvRGA3 significantly increased. These results revealed that the HvRPS2 and HvRGA3 genes are related to the resistance of barley to P. graminea [14]. Research has shown that members of the WAK family are positive regulatory factors in plant resistance to fungal diseases, and it is speculated that this family is related to plant resistance to fungal diseases [15]; Li et al. [16] cloned a typical WAK family member, the HvnWAK gene, whose structure is similar to that of a plant cell wall-associated kinase and which contains a GUB_WAK_bind domain. Therefore, it is speculated that the HvnWAK gene has similar functions in the antifungal mechanisms of barley. Yao et al. [17] used P. graminea to infect the resistant barley variety Kunlun 14 and the susceptible variety Z1141 and performed RNA-seq, they found a differentially expressed gene, HvnWRKY26, in barley under P. graminea stress; HvnWRKY26 is a member of the WRKY family and contains cis-acting elements and jasmonic acid and abscisic acid response elements related to stress responses. Therefore, it is speculated that this gene plays an important role in barley under P. graminea stress.

In this study, we analysed and compared the resistance and susceptibility of barley germplasm resources to P. graminea through disease resistance identification and morphological observation. The expression patterns of responsive genes at different time points after infection with P. graminea were subsequently compared, and transcriptome analysis was performed via RNA-seq to explore the metabolic pathways related to disease resistance in barley, identify genes related to disease resistance and understand the mechanism underlying the response of barley to P. graminea infection. This study provides a theoretical basis for the discovery of leaf stripe disease resistance genes in barley and the study of resistance mechanisms, and it is highly important for the cultivation of new varieties of barley resistant to leaf stripe disease and the development of the barley industry.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

The disease-resistant germplasm 'ZYM2179'(Z) and the disease-susceptible 'Chaiqing No. 1'(C) were used as well as 26 other hulless barley germplasm resources (Beiqing No. 7, Yellow belly, ZYM1931, and WDM3255, etc.). The seeds were all provided by the Qinghai Duplicate Bank of the National Crop Germplasm Resources Bank and were propagated and preserved by our research group. P. graminea L-7 was isolated from diseased leaves at the adult stage in a barley field in Guinan County, Qinghai Province, in 2021; P. graminea FS-18 was isolated and preserved by our research group. To determine whether the isolated and purified pathogen was P. graminea, "Koch's postulates" [18] were verified.

Experimental methods

The pathogen was isolated via the tissue isolation method [19] (with slight modifications). Nine P. graminea strains were used for the pathogenicity analysis, and the highly pathogenic strain L-7 was selected. L-7 and FS-18 were used to conduct artificial inoculation-based indoor resistance screening of 28 disease-resistant and disease-susceptible germplasm resources in the laboratory, and the disease-resistant germplasm resource 'ZYM2179'(Z) and the disease-susceptible resource 'Chaiqing No. 1'(C) were selected. Indoor artificial inoculation for disease resistance screening was conducted via the methods of Long et al. [20]. The disease-resistant and disease-susceptible resources were disinfected and germinated, and then inoculated with P. graminea L-7 for 0 hai, 6 hai, 12 hai, 24 hai, and 36 hai. The embryos at different time points post-infection were fixed for subsequent sectioning WGA-AF488 staining (performed by Wuhan CytoBiotech Co., Ltd.), and transplanted for indoor resistance evaluation at different periods after infection.The infection status was observed, and embryo samples of the disease-resistant and disease-susceptible highland barley resources at 0 hai, 12 hai, 24 hai, and 36 hai and the control were selected for the extraction of total genomic RNA [21, 22] for transcriptome analysis, with 3 biological replicates for each treatment and a total of 24 samples. The sequencing was completed by Guangzhou Genedenovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Indoor artificial inoculation method for disease resistance identification using the "liquid inoculation identification" method [20]:

Fungal Culture:

Pure cultures of the stripe pathogen are punched out with a 5 mm tool from the edge of the fungal colony and transferred to 250 mL of liquid culture media. The culture is then incubated on a shaker (150 rpm, 25–27 ℃) for 10 days until the fungal mycelium fills the entire liquid, after which it is ready for use.

The culture is then incubated on a shaker (150 rpm, 25–27 ℃) for 10 days until the fungal mycelium fills the entire liquid, after which it is ready for use.

Seed Disinfection, Germination, Pathogen Inoculation, Transplanting, and Investigation:

Hulless barley (a type of barley) seeds are treated with 70% ethanol for 30 s, followed by treatment with 5% sodium hypochlorite (5 ml sodium hypochlorite + 95 ml distilled water) for 5 min, then rinsed multiple times with distilled water. During this process, the seeds are gently stirred with tweezers and finally placed on a sterile workbench to dry completely. In a sterile hood, sterile filter paper is placed in a culture dish, moistened with distilled water, and 15–20 hulless barley seeds are placed in each dish and germinated in the dark at 22–26 ℃ for 2 days. The mycelium in the prepared Liquid culture is thoroughly mixed, added to the dish with germinated seeds so that the fungal Liquid covers the seeds, mixed again, and infected under dark conditions at 22–26 ℃ for 30–38 h. The inoculated seeds are then transplanted into seedling pots, with 15 seeds per pot and three replicates, and placed in an artificial climate chamber with a light cycle of L//D = 12 h//12 h and a diurnal temperature variation of 20℃/12℃. Watering is done regularly, and disease development is observed. After about 45 days when the disease fully develops, an investigation of the incidence rate is conducted. The disease investigation refers to the standards for classifying barley stripe disease resistance types by Ma et al. [23]. with slight modifications, dividing the resistance types into five grades: Grade 0 (Immune, I): No infected plants; Grade 1 (Highly Resistant, HR): 0 < incidence rate ≤ 5%; Grade 2 (Moderately Resistant, MR): 5% < incidence rate ≤ 10%; Grade 3 (Moderately Susceptible, MS): 10% < incidence rate ≤ 15%; Grade 4 (Highly Susceptible, HS): Incidence rate > 15%. Data analysis method: Incidence rate = (Number of infected plants/Total number of plants surveyed) × 100%.

Transcriptome analysis

The OD value of nucleic acids was determined using an ultra-micro UV spectrophotometer (Machine model: NanoDrop OneC) to determine the purity of the RNA. After enriching the eukaryotic mRNA with a poly(A) tail using magnetic beads with oligo(dT), the mRNA was fragmented using a buffer. Using the fragmented mRNA as a template and random oligonucleotides as primers, the first strand of cDNA was synthesized in the M-MuLV reverse transcriptase system, and then the RNA chain was degraded using RNaseH. In the DNA polymerase I system, the second strand of cDNA was synthesized using dNTPs as raw materials. The purified double-stranded cDNA was subjected to end repair, A tails were added and connected to sequencing adapters, and cDNAs of approximately 200 bp were screened using AMPure XP beads for PCR amplification and purified again using AMPure XP beads to obtain the final library [24] for quality inspection. To ensure the quality of the sequencing data, it was necessary to filter the raw data before data analysis to reduce errors and avoid interference from invalid data. The raw reads from the machine were subjected to quality control via fastp [25] to filter out low-quality data [26] and obtain clean reads.

Statistical analysis

HISAT [27] (https://www.omicshare.com/) was used to align the clean reads to the reference genome sequence of highland barley (NCBI_GCA_004114815.1) [28] to obtain mapped data, and the library quality was evaluated. DESeq2 [29] software was used to analyse the read score data obtained from the gene expression level analysis. FDR < 0.05 and| log2FC|> 2 were used as thresholds to screen for differentially expressed genes. Each sample was compared vertically and horizontally at the same time, each treatment group was compared with the control group at the same time point, and the same treatment group was compared at four time points (The statistical method used is one-way ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)). The differentially expressed genes were subjected to functional annotation analysis via Gene Ontology (GO) functional analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis on the basis of the plant transcription factor database.

RT‒qPCR analysis

To verify the accuracy of the transcriptome data, 10 genes related to disease resistance were selected, and the IDs of the selected leaf stripe disease resistance genes were used to design and synthesize the RT‒qPCR primers; 18SrRNA was used as an internal reference gene based on the sequences obtained from the transcriptome sequencing (Table 1), which was completed by Qinghai Kemeiyi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The relative expression levels of the genes after P. graminea infection of different resistant highland barley varieties were calculated via the 2–ΔΔCt method [30] using an ABI7500 Real-Time PCR amplification instrument for fluorescence quantification. GraphPad Prism 8.4.3 software (https://www.graphpad.com, accessed on 5 May 2024) was used for perform a significance statistical analysis (The method employed is the "Student's t-test.") to determine whether the RT‒qPCR results were consistent with the transcriptome analysis results.

Results and analysis

Selection of Pyrenophora graminea

The results of pathogenicity tests with the tested variety, Chaiqing No. 1, using nine purified P. graminea isolates (Table 2) revealed five highly pathogenic strains with disease incidence rates of 20%, 12.1%, 22.9%, 15%, and 50%. There were also four weakly pathogenic strains with disease incidence rates of 3.1%, 0%, 5.9%, and 9.7%. Among these strains, strain L-7 had the strongest pathogenicity, with a disease incidence rate of 50.00%, whereas strain L-5 had the weakest pathogenicity, with a disease incidence rate of 0%. The pathogenicity of the different strains varied significantly. When the highly pathogenic strain L-7 was used to infect the susceptible material for 12 hai, morphological observation via electron microscopy revealed that the mycelia of P. graminea L-7 did not produce conidia (Fig. 1).

Selection of disease-resistant and disease-susceptible highland barley resources

We used FS-18 and L-7 to conduct artificial inoculation-based indoor resistance screening to identify disease-resistant and disease-susceptible materials at the early stage in the laboratory (as shown in Table 3 and leaf stripe disease symptom Fig. 2). The results obtained with strain L-7 revealed that there were 17 and 11 disease-resistant and disease-susceptible resources, respectively; the results obtained with strain FS-18 identification revealed that there were 25 and 3 disease-resistant and disease-susceptible resources, respectively. The resistance evaluation results for different strains with the same resource were completely different. For example, for "WDM3311" and "ZYM2077", the result for L-7 was susceptible, whereas that for FS-18 was disease resistant. Thus, resistance to different pathogens varies within a single resource.

Transcriptome sequencing and quality assessment

Clean reads were obtained by using FASTP for quality control of the raw reads of the 24 samples from the four time points (0 hai, 12 hai, 24 hai, and 36 hai) of the disease-resistant resource 'ZYM2179' and the disease-susceptible resource 'Chaiqing No. 1' and filtering out low-quality data to reduce errors caused by invalid data (Supplementary materials Table 1). The average percentage of clean reads among the total reads was 99.19%, in addition, the average Q20 value of the clean reads was 98.10%, the average Q30 value was 94.63%, and the average GC value was 55.22%.

HISAT [27] was used to align the clean reads to the reference genome sequence of highland barley (NCBI_GCA_004114815.1) [28]. The alignment results are shown in Supplementary materials Table 2. The average percentage of uniquely mapped reads among all the reads in the highland barley genome was 90.78%, and the average percentage of all mapped reads among all the reads was 93.75%. There were only a few sequences that were mapped to multiple loci. In addition, the alignment region results (Fig. 4C) revealed that the average percentage of reads that mapped to exonic regions in the genome was 87.18%; this was the greatest percentage, followed by intergenic regions, with an average value of 6.79%, and intronic regions, with an average value of 6.03%. These findings indicate that the RNA-seq data were high quality and highly reliable and that the sequencing data fully met the requirements for subsequent experimental analysis.

Expression abundance distribution

Based on the Fragments per kilobase million (FPKM) values of differentially expressed genes, the gene expression distribution across 24 samples can be perfectly illustrated through a gene expression abundance distribution plot. As shown in Fig. 4B, the overall pattern of differential gene expression across the 24 samples first increases and then decreases. When the log10(FPKM) value is approximately 1, the gene expression abundance is the highest, with a density reaching above 0.45; this means that at this level of expression, the number of differentially expressed genes is the greatest, amounting to about 8335. When the log10(FPKM) value is approximately −2 and 4, the gene expression abundance is the lowest, with both the density and the number of differentially expressed genes being 0.

Sample relationship analysis

Using the expression levels of all the genes, the relationships among all the samples were hierarchically clustered, which reflected the relationships among the samples (Fig. 4D). The results revealed that the correlation among each sample's three replicates was relatively high, and the 6 samples in the control group were grouped as one cluster. All the samples in the treatment group were grouped as one cluster, and no outlier samples were found, so the data had high reliability and could be used for subsequent analysis.

Gene annotation analysis

Through functional annotation analysis of 18,523 differentially expressed genes detected via transcriptome sequencing, only 2,506 differentially expressed genes were annotated in the transcription factor database; these genes could be classified into 55 gene families. The B3 gene family accounted for 539 differentially expressed genes, which was the greatest number, followed by ERF (294), NAC (150), bHLH (147), FAR1 (133), C2H2 (128), and MYB (121), and the families with the least annotated genes were HB-PHD (1), LFY (1), S1Fa-like (1), and HRT-like (1), as shown in Fig. 4A.

Trend analysis chart

In this study, all genes in the treatment group for the susceptible cultivar 'Chaiqing 1' and the resistant cultivar 'ZYM2179' at four time points (0 hai, 12 hai, 24 hai, and 36 hai) were classified into 20 different expression profiles according to their expression patterns, For gradient samples, the trend characteristics of gene abundance were identified, and genes with Similar patterns of change were categorized into one trend. This approach helps to find the most representative set of genes during the experimental process. The analysis can describe the types, characteristics, and Significance of gene expression patterns. The expression profile numbers are sorted from the most to the least enriched genes. Colored trend blocks represent significantly enriched trends, and modules with the same color have similar trends. Uncolored trend blocks indicate non-significantly enriched trends. Each inflection point corresponds to a set of sample data. Through trend analysis of time series data, gene expression in Chaiqing 1 was determined to be associated with seven specific expression profile patterns, namely, Profile 0, Profile 2, Profile 17, Profile 1, Profile 7, Profile 19, and Profile 18 (Fig. 4E). In the Profile 0 expression profile, the genes showed a decreasing expression trend, and this profile was observed for a total of 2801 genes. In the Profile 2 expression profile, the genes exhibited a decreasing and then stable expression trend, and this profile was observed for 2269 genes. In the Profile 17 expression profile, the genes exhibited a trend increasing and then stable expression trend, and this profile was observed for 1707 genes, And the expression profile trends of Profiles 0 and Profiles 7, Profiles 2 and Profiles 1, and Profiles 17 and Profiles 19 are similar, respectively. The genes identified in ZYM2179 were enriched in seven main expression profiles, namely, Profile 1, Profile 17, Profile s0, Profile 2, Profile 7, Profile 18, and Profile 19 (Fig. 4F). In the expression Profile 1, the genes presented a trend of first decreasing and then increasing in expression, and this profile was observed for a total of 2857 enriched genes. The expression trend of genes in Profile 17 presented an initial increase followed by stability, and this profile was observed for 1916 enriched genes. In the expression Profile 0, the genes presented a decreasing expression trend, and this profile was observed for a total of 1698 genes, And the expression profile trends of Profiles 1 and Profiles 2, Profiles 17 and Profiles 18, and Profiles 0 and Profiles 7 are similar, respectively. On the basis of the above results, 'Chaiqing 1' and 'ZYM2179' presented significant differences in gene expression patterns, which may be the molecular basis for the significant differences in disease resistance between these two varieties. Therefore, to obtain a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying the differences in disease resistance between these resources, further research should focus on the key genes that are significantly upregulated or enriched during pathogen infection and the metabolic pathways in which they participate.

Analysis of disease resistance-related pathways

Plants control various cellular processes by producing reactive oxygen species (ROS) as signalling molecules, which play crucial roles in many fundamental biological processes. The main source of reactive oxygen species is the production of plant oxidative burst via the oxidase Rbohs. The rapid production of ROS, which act as signalling molecules in early plant defence responses against pathogen attacks and inhibit pathogen growth or induce resistance gene expression is a mechanism by which plants protect themselves. The production of ROS (such as H2O2 and O2−) in plants is related to respiratory burst oxidase (Rboh). In this study (as shown in Fig. 8A), plant–pathogen interactions mediated by the enriched KEGG pathways of disease-resistant varieties were Significantly enhanced at 12–24 hai of infection, and the Rboh gene was upregulated. Rboh, an important gene encoding homologous protein B of plant respiratory burst oxidase, plays a crucial regulatory role in the interaction between plants and pathogens [31]. Before the invasion of P. graminea into barley plants, Rboh is transcribed to produce the respiratory burst oxidase homologous protein B, enabling binding with Ca2+, peroxidase activity, and NAD(P) H oxidase activity to promote H2O2 formation and resist pathogen invasion. In addition, during interactions between plants and pathogens, pathogens usually release proteases to destroy plant cell walls, proteins, etc., providing good conditions for their invasion and growth. RbohD is an important gene that encodes respiratory burst oxidase homologous protein C in the plant MAPK signalling pathway. Respiratory burst oxidase homologous protein D (RbohD) is a calcium-dependent NADPH oxidase that produces superoxide [32]. In this study (as shown in Fig. 8B), in the KEGG analysis of disease-resistant varieties, the MAPK Signalling pathway was Significantly enriched at 12–24 hai of infection, and the RbohD gene was upregulated, resulting in the production of superoxide and ROS during interactions with pathogens, as well as UV-B- and abscisic acid-mediated ROS-dependent signaling.

Discussion

Hordeum vulgare var. nudum, a major cereal crop on the Tibetan Plateau, is severely threatened by leaf stripe disease caused by P. graminea, which significantly reduces yield and quality [1, 2]. This study systematically explored the transcriptional changes that occur in barley in response to P. graminea infection through transcriptomic analysis. Regardless of disease resistance or susceptibility, the degree of pathogen invasion and the number of hyphae increased significantly with prolonged infection time. However, resistant resources presented lower incidence rates and stronger resistance. This study aimed to reveal the resistance mechanisms of barley and provide a theoretical basis for the identification and utilization of resistance genes. By comparing the gene expression profiles of resistant (ZYM2179) and susceptible (Chaiqing No. 1) resources at different infection time points, 104 differentially expressed genes, which may play key roles in the barley resistance response, were identified.

A total of 18,523 differentially expressed genes were identified via transcriptome sequencing, which revealed significant expression differences between resistant and susceptible resources. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that these differentially expressed genes were enriched mainly in metabolic pathways, plant‒pathogen interactions, nitrogen metabolism, carbon metabolism, and other pathways. The enrichment of these pathways indicates that the resistance of barley increases via the regulation of metabolic processes and signal transduction pathways after pathogen invasion. For example, genes in metabolic pathways may be involved in the synthesis of secondary metabolites, which can increase cell wall strength to resist pathogen invasion [3].

Among the annotated differentially expressed genes, many genes from transcription factor families, such as B3, ERF, NAC, bHLH, and MYB, were identified. These transcription factor families play important roles in plant stress resistance and disease resistance. For example, B3 transcription factors play key roles in plant growth, development, and stress responses [33]; NAC transcription factors are involved in regulating plant responses to abiotic stress and pathogen invasion [34]. In this study, the expression levels of genes in these transcription factor families were significantly greater in resistant resources than in susceptible resources, indicating that they may play important roles in the resistance mechanism of barley.

To verify the accuracy of the transcriptome sequencing results, 10 Significant highly expressed genes from the 104 barley leaf stripe disease resistance-related differentially expressed genes were selected for RT‒qPCR analysis. The results revealed that the expression levels of these genes in resistant resources were Significantly greater than those in susceptible resources 12h after inoculation with P. graminea, which was consistent with the results of transcriptome sequencing [30]. This result was further corroborated by disease resistance tests and morphological observation, which revealed that these genes play important roles in the resistance mechanism of barley. For example, the high expression of the HSP70-4 and PER1 genes in resistant resources may be related to their roles in heat shock protein function and peroxidase activity, which help plants resist pathogen invasion [35].

This study revealed that resistant resources presented lower incidence rates and stronger resistance after pathogen invasion, which may be related to the following mechanisms. Metabolic pathway regulation: Genes enriched in metabolic pathways in resistant resources may increase cell wall strength by synthesizing secondary metabolites, thereby resisting pathogen invasion [36]. Activation of signal transduction pathways: Genes involved in plant‒pathogen interaction pathways presented relatively high expression levels in resistant resources, indicating that resistant resources may respond quickly to pathogen invasion by activating signal transduction pathways [37]. Transcription factor regulation: The high expression of genes from transcription factor families such as B3, ERF, and NAC in resistant resources may increase plant resistance by regulating the expression of downstream genes [38].

Although this study revealed the transcriptional changes that occur in barley after P. graminea infection through transcriptome analysis and identified some genes that may be related to resistance, these results are still preliminary. Future research should further verify the functions of these candidate genes and explore their specific roles in resistance mechanisms in barley. In addition, gene editing technologies such as CRISPR/Cas9 can be used to knock out or overexpress these candidate genes to further study their roles in barley resistance. In summary, this study revealed the transcriptional changes that occur in barley after P. graminea infection through transcriptome analysis and RT‒qPCR validation and identified some genes that may be related to resistance [39]. These findings provide an important theoretical basis for the identification of barley leaf stripe disease resistance genes and the study of resistance mechanisms and are highly important for the breeding of new barley varieties resistant to P. graminea [40].

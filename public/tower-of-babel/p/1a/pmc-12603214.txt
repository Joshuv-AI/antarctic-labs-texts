Design of a novel multi epitope antigen for diagnosis of visceral leishmaniasis using an immunoinformatics approach
Authors: Maryam Baneshi, Mahboubeh Sadeghi, Samaneh Hashemi, Claudia Alcedo, Antonio Muro, Raúl Manzano-Román, Amir Savardashtaki, Sajad Rashidi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractVisceral Leishmaniasis (VL) represents a critical parasitic disease with considerable implications for public health. The predominant etiological agent responsible for most VL cases in the Mediterranean region and Latin America is the Leishmania infantum parasite. Current diagnostic methods, including microscopy and serological assays, have limitations such as low sensitivity and cross-reactivity. The development of multi-epitope antigens presents a promising approach for improving diagnostic accuracy. This study focused on the design of a novel multi-epitope antigen for human VL diagnosis using immunoinformatics tools. Four antigenic proteins, including Prohibitin, Apolipoprotein A-I, Aldehyde Dehydrogenase, and Protein Disulfide-Isomerase, were selected based on an extensive review of the literature and prior immunoproteomic studies of L. infantum conducted by our research team. Conserved B-cell epitopes were identified and linked using EAAAK linkers to ensure structural integrity. The designed antigen was assessed for antigenicity, stability, solubility, and structural integrity. Secondary and tertiary structures were predicted and validated through computational tools. Codon optimization enhanced expression efficiency in Escherichia coli, and the sequence was successfully cloned into the pET-23a(+) vector. Immunoinformatics analysis confirmed the antigen’s high immunogenic potential, stability, and suitability for diagnostic applications. These findings suggest that the design of such a multi-epitope antigen may represent a dependable candidate for human VL serodiagnosis, warranting further experimental validations.Keywords: Visceral leishmaniasis, Leishmania infantum, Immunoinformatics, Multi-epitope antigenSubject terms: Biochemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Immunology

Background

Leishmaniasis represents a neglected tropical disease induced by protozoan pathogens belonging to over 20 distinct Leishmania species (spp.), transmitted through sandfly bites. This infectious disease manifests in various forms, including visceral leishmaniasis (VL), cutaneous leishmaniasis (CL), and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis (MCL)1. Each form poses unique challenges and requires careful clinical assessment and management. The clinical symptoms of VL mainly include fever, weight loss, hepatosplenomegaly, anemia, and some hematological implications, while CL presents with painful skin ulcers and MCL creates mucosal ulcers that can cause nasal obstruction and facial deformities2,3.

Globally, VL is caused by protozoan parasites of the Leishmania donovani complex, which primarily includes two closely related spp., L. donovani and L. infantum4. The term ‘kala-azar’ specifically refers to the form of VL prevalent in the Indian subcontinent and East Africa, which is caused by L. donovani5. Whereas, L. infantum is the main causative agent of VL in Latin America, the Mediterranean basin, and parts of the Middle East, including Iran6. In Iran, VL is hyperendemic in both the northwestern and southern provinces, with an estimated 100 to 300 new cases reported annually. If left untreated, the disease is fatal in more than 95% of cases7,8.

Microscopic examination of aspirated specimens from the spleen, bone marrow, and lymph nodes to detect Leishmania amastigotes remains the gold standard for VL diagnosis. Sensitivity is highest for spleen aspirates (93–99%), followed by bone marrow (53–86%) and lymph node samples (53–65%), but the invasive nature of the procedure and associated risks require significant technical expertise9. Serological approaches, which encompass enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), direct agglutination test (DAT), and indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA), offer greater sensitivity than parasitological methods10. However, these techniques often fail to distinguish between active and past infections and can yield false positives due to cross-reactivity, especially when crude antigens are used5,11–13. Recombinant Leishmania antigens, such as rK39, improve standardization and reproducibility14. rK39-based rapid diagnostic tests are the most widely used in endemic areas, particularly in the Indian subcontinent, with reported sensitivity and specificity of ~ 97% and 90.2%, respectively15. The latex agglutination test (KAtex) is primarily employed in L. donovani endemic areas but is less commonly used in L. infantum endemic regions16.

Among the various tools available for identifying potential antigen candidates, proteomics stands out as an efficient and robust approach17–19. The amastigote form is the main stage responsible for VL pathology, residing within host macrophages and evading immune detection20, which underscores its importance as the focus of antigen discovery efforts. In our previous work, we developed an immunoproteomic method to identify immunoreactive proteins in L. infantum amastigotes, using sera from dogs infected with the Leishmania parasite that causes canine visceral leishmaniasis (CVL). Among the identified proteins, Prohibitin (PHB), Apolipoprotein A-I (Apo-AI), Aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH), and Protein disulfide-isomerase (PDI) were characterized as immunodominant proteins in the proteome profile of L. infantum amastigotes21.

The successful identification of several immunoreactive proteins from amastigote stage provides crucial insights for developing diagnostic tools for leishmaniasis. Notably, the selected antigens have established antigenic properties, being recognized by antibodies in the sera of dogs with CVL, reinforcing their relevance as potential diagnostic markers.

PHB is highly conserved and is involved in cell proliferation, aging, B-cell maturation, and maintenance of mitochondrial integrity22. In Leishmania, PHB present on the parasite’s surface binds to macrophage heat shock protein 70 (HSP70), which may aid the parasite in evading the host immune system23,24. PDIs display reductase, chaperone, and isomerase activities. PDIs, which are critical for parasite survival, replication, and pathogenesis25, have been identified as key virulence factors in various Leishmania species26. Apo A-I is produced by the Leishmania parasite (in amastigote form) to evade the Trypanosome lytic factor (TLF), which is part of the host’s immune response against parasites27. Leishmania parasites utilize molecular mimicry by presenting Apo A-I to evade the effects of TLF, disrupting essential immune responses, and modifying the function of macrophages and other immune cells. 8. ALDHs are a collection of enzymes present in all subcellular compartments that convert aldehydes into carboxylic acids28. In Leishmania, ALDH is situated in the mitochondria, and its expression significantly decreases in long culture of Leishmania and might be related to virulence29. It has been proposed to act as a protective protein against oxidative stress during periods of glucose scarcity in this parasite30. These immunodominant proteins might serve as promising biomarkers for VL detection.

Over the years, various methods of B-cell epitope (BCE) identification have been developed through the study of biological activity, structural conformation, site-directed mutagenesis, or microarray method, including discontinuous BCE. Antigen-antibody interactions are vital for the immune response and for designing new antigenic structures functional for improved treatment or diagnosis purposes. Actually, the continuous development of prediction methods, such as machine learning or deep learning, makes it cheaper and doesn´t require as much effort31.

In this study, we aim to design a recombinant L. infantum multi-epitope diagnostic antigen through bioinformatics tools. Building on immunodominant regions of key amastigote-stage proteins identified in our prior study, we propose that these epitopes have the potential to elicit distinct and measurable antibody responses in individuals infected with L. infantum. However, further in-silico analyses, including prediction of major histocompatibility complex (MHC)-binding affinity, as well as experimental validation, are required to substantiate their immunogenicity. Focusing on antigens with proven immunogenicity enhances the likelihood of developing sensitive and specific diagnostic assays while reducing the risks associated with randomly selecting antigens, grounding our choices in empirical evidence from prior research. Identifying and characterizing these epitopes not only holds promise for enhancing diagnostic capabilities but may also contribute to our understanding of the immune response mechanism to Leishmania infection.

Methods

Study design

The antigenic proteins of L. infantum, including PHB, Apo-AI, ALDH, and PDI were chosen according to the previous results of L. infantum immunoproteomic approach21. Subsequently, using immunoinformatics platforms, the B cell epitopes and antigenic domains corresponding to individual antigens were defined, followed by the conjunction of the ultimate epitopes using a suitable linker. Ultimately, the comprehensive structural evaluation of the completed construct, encompassing three-dimensional modeling, physicochemical characteristics, and subsequent validation, was conducted. The workflow of this study is illustrated in Fig. 1.

The flowchart illustrates a comprehensive workflow for the design of a multi-epitope in VL diagnosis. It begins with the identification of relevant antigens, following by the selection of specific epitopes. Subsequently, multi-epitope construction is designed and assessed for its properties, including antigenicity, cross-reactivity, physicochemical attributes, and solubility. The assessment further entails predicting the epitope’s 2D and 3D structures, following by model refinement and validation. After selecting the optimal 3D structure, it is back-translated to the corresponding DNA sequence and optimized in that sequence for efficient production in the desired host organism. The workflow concludes with in-silico cloning, preparing the epitopes for potential diagnostic applications.

Sequence retrieval, antigen selection, and the identification of conserved region

The protein knowledgebase (UniProtKB) database (www.uniprot.org) was recruited to obtain the FASTA format of amino acid sequences regarding PHB, Apo-AI, ALDH, and PDI. In some cases, a single protein had multiple UniProt entries corresponding to the same protein but from different database records or annotations. For example, PHB was represented by entries A0A6L0XPP9, A0A6L0WZW0, A4IAQ8, and A4HX59. To ensure no conserved segment was missed, all available entries for each antigen were aligned using Jalview software. Alignments were evaluated for regions without gaps and with ≥ 90% sequence identity, which were defined as conserved32. These conserved stretches were prioritized for epitope prediction.

Prediction of linear B-cell epitopes

B cell epitopes significantly influence antibody production. We used three servers to recognize B cell epitopes and improve prediction accuracy. Using a neural network, the web-based platform ABCPred, accessible at (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/abcpred/ABC_submission.html), predicts B-cell epitopes. It focuses on detecting linear B-cell epitopes within antigenic sequences by examining amino acid patterns and their likelihood of being recognized by antibodies through recurrent neural networks with about 65.93% accuracy. In this study, epitopes of 16-mer length were predicted at the default threshold of 0.51 33,34.

Another tool, BepiPred-2.0 (http://www.cbs.dtu.dk/services/BepiPred/), utilizes a machine learning framework, integrating various characteristics such as amino acid propensities and the spatial configuration of epitopes to improve prediction precision. This server allows for the simultaneous submission of approximately 50 sequences, with lengths ranging from 10 to 6000 amino acids. Epitopes were retained at the recommended threshold of 0.5, which balances sensitivity and specificity35.

Additionally, we used the web-based platform, BCPred 2.0 (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/bcepred/bcepred_submission.html) to forecast linear B cell epitopes. It utilizes a machine learning approach based on support vector machines to evaluate input sequences against established epitope data, identifying regions likely to provoke an antibody response. This server considers physical and chemical characteristics including exposed surface, turns, flexibility/mobility, hydrophilicity, and accessibility or an assortment of these attributes. The server is capable of predicting the epitopes with varying degrees of accuracy, ranging from 52.92 to 57.53%, based on different physicochemical characteristics. In our analysis, a threshold score of 2.38 (accuracy 58.7%) was applied, consistent with the recommended parameters for reliable prediction36.

To reduce bias from individual tools and increase prediction confidence, a consensus approach was applied. Only epitopes predicted by all three servers and mapping to overlapping sequence regions were advanced for further evaluation.

Antigenicity prediction of linear B-cell epitopes

The Vaxijen 2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) was utilized to evaluate the property of each epitope being recognized as an antigen. The cutoff for VaxiJen 2.0 was established at 0.5, with “parasite” designated as the target organism. This server employs an alignment-free prediction method that relies on the diverse physicochemical characteristics of the protein37. The EMBOSS server version 6.6.0.0 (https://www.bioinformatics.nl/cgi-bin/emboss/antigenic ) was also implemented to identify the antigenic regions of the interested sequence. This server predicts the protein antigenic sites by analyzing amino acid residues’ chemical and physical characteristics based on the Kolaskar and Tongaonkar method. It considers factors such as hydrophilicity, accessibility, and the presence of common motifs associated with B-cell and T-cell epitopes. In this study, we established initial parameters, including the use of the EMBOSS motif as the output report format for identifying the antigenic regions of the target proteins. We set a minimum threshold of 6 amino acids for defining the antigenic sites38.

Stability and sequence similarity analysis of linear B-cell epitopes

Peptide stability was assessed using the instability index calculated by the ProtParam tool available on the ExPASy server (http://web.expasy.org/protparam/). Peptides with an instability index below 40 are considered stable, while those above 40 are predicted to be unstable. This analysis allowed us to select peptides with favorable stability profiles for further study. Additionally, BLASTp searches were performed against the non-redundant protein database, with organism exclusion filters applied to remove Leishmania spp. sequences, in order to detect potential similarities to proteins from other pathogens. We considered matches with E-value ≤ 0.001, identity ≥ 80%, and query coverage ≥ 80% as indicative of significant similarity. These criteria were selected based on widely accepted standards in immunoinformatics to minimize the likelihood of non-specific binding in downstream applications (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi).

Construction of multi-epitope antigen

The epitope candidates for B cells, identified through pertinent servers and achieved the top scores, were joined together using the EAAAK linker to provide appropriate separation and stability between epitopes39–41. Ultimately, a structural framework featuring multiple epitope characteristics was established.

Assessment of physicochemical characteristics of the designed construct

We employed the ProtParam tool available on the ExPASy server (http://web.expasy.org/protparam/) to analyze the physicochemical properties of the selected protein sequences. This tool conducts an extensive analysis of several parameters, such as instability index, amino acid composition, isoelectric point, and molecular weight42. We employed the SolPro platform (http://scratch.proteomics.ics.uci.edu) to assess the solubility of our antigen, utilizing a high-throughput methodology to evaluate protein solubility under diverse conditions. The antigen underwent a series of solubility tests at various pH levels and ionic strengths, resulting in a solubility index derived from the comparison of soluble and precipitated protein43.

Prediction of secondary and tertiary (2D and 3D) structure of the newly designed recombinant construct

We utilized the SOPMA (Self-Optimized Prediction Method with Alignment) algorithm (https://npsa-prabi.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html) to assess the 2D and 3D structures of our multi-epitope antigen. Furthermore, SOPMA was also used to predict the proportions of random coils, beta turns, beta bridges, and α-helices in the antigen44. Galaxy TBM (http://galaxy.seoklab.org/cgi-bin/submit.cgitype=TBM) was utilized to predict the 3D structures of protein. This tool uses a threading algorithm that aligns the target sequence with established protein structures within the database to create three-dimensional models. Galaxy TBM generated structural models by using our designed construct sequence. Each model came with confidence scores, which enabled us to evaluate the reliability and stability of the features effectively45.

Validation of the 3D structure of the designed construct

ProSA-web server (https://prosa.services.came.sbg.ac.at/prosa.php), a well-established resource for assessing the precision of protein models, was employed to validate the predictions of the 3D structure. ProSA-web utilizes a scoring mechanism grounded in energy computations, enabling us to assess the stability and dependability of our predicted structure. Z-score was obtained upon submitting our model to the server. The software analyze the atomic interactions within the interested protein through focusing on the alpha carbon of amino acid residues46.

PROCHECK (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/) comprehensively assesses the stereochemical features of protein conformation by examining a range of structural factors, such as dihedral angles, bond angles and lengths. Additionally, the tool assesses non-bonded interactions and highlights regions that may present potential steric clashes or atypical geometry47.

We employed the ERRAT tool (http://services.mbi.ucla.edu/ERRAT/) to analyze the backbone dihedral angles in our predicted protein structure and create a Ramachandran plot. ERRAT assesses the protein models’ stereochemical characteristics by examining the distribution of phi (φ) and psi (ψ) angles for each residue compared to those found in high-resolution crystallographic structures. We submitted our model to ERRAT to obtain a detailed assessment of its stereochemical accuracy. This analysis allowed us to determine the proportion of residues located within the majority of favorable regions of the Ramachandran plot. The tool also identifies residues in disallowed regions, which may suggest potential structural irregularities. This thorough validation process provided valuable insights into overall reliability of the predicted structure and helped us to confirm its suitability for further analysis and investigation48.

Prediction of B-cell epitopes’ structure on the designed recombinant construct

The B-cell epitopes’ structure of our multi-epitope antigen were identified through the use of the ElliPro web server (http://tools.immuneepitope.org/tools/ElliPro). This platform utilizes a 3D structural methodology for epitope mapping, focusing on the configuration of surface residues and their spatial accessibility49.

In-silico immune-simulation studies

To evaluate the immunogenic potential of the designed multi-epitope construct, an in silico immune simulation was performed using the C-IMMSIM server (http://150.146.2.1/C-IMMSIM/index.php), a widely used agent-based model of the mammalian immune system50. The primary amino acid sequence of the construct was provided as input. In this study, the simulation was configured to mimic a single exposure to the antigen, corresponding to one injection at time step 1. Default simulation parameters were applied, including a simulation volume of 10 µl and a fixed random seed for reproducibility.

The simulation generated detailed kinetics of antigen clearance, immunoglobulin isotypes (IgM, IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, IgG4), as well as plasma B-cell and memory B-cell populations. These results were analyzed to determine whether the designed construct could trigger a measurable humoral immune response suitable for diagnostic purposes.

In-silico cloning and simulation of the designed construct

OPTIMIZER server (http://genomes.urv.es/OPTIMIZER/) was employed to enhance the expression of the multi-epitopic protein construct in Escherichia coli (E. coli). The amino acid sequence of the construct was back-translated and subjected to codon optimization using this server, generating a DNA sequence that maximizes expression efficiency and minimizes potential problems caused by mRNA secondary structures51. Subsequently, the GenScript Rare Codon Analysis Tool (https://www.genscript.com/tools/rare-codon-analysis) was utilized to assess various aspects of the DNA sequence, such as overall GC content, the absence of negative CIS elements, Codon Frequency Distribution (CFD), and repetitive sequences. We anticipated the secondary structure of the mRNA using RNAfold sever) http://rna.tbi.univie.ac.at/ .(In the following phase, the construct’s DNA sequence was cloned in silico by the pET-23a(+) expression vector.

The analysis identified multiple antigenic regions across the selected proteins, with a minimum antigenic region length set to six amino acids.

Development of the multi-epitope antigen

The final set of LBL was derived from specific regions of the selected antigens: PHB (amino acid residues 56–71 and 75–91), Apo-AI (amino acid residues 302–327), ALDH (amino acid residues 5–53) and PDI (amino acid residues 82–101). These epitopes were linked to form a single, continuous sequence using an EAAAK linker, which presents significant advantages (Fig. 2).

A schematic illustration of the multi-epitope antigen construct, featuring five linear B-cell epitopes interconnected by EAAAK linkers (depicted in gray). S1: STNAKDLLLIGKVDATEDSDL, S2: FEVKNPATGAVVGKYMQQQASDIAKAVENARVAQKVWAQTTYDIRAVHL, S3: RGAAAAPGHRHAAARRAGGTGGTAGR, S4: MTGTKDLQMVNVRLRVL, and S5: GLDEVLRFNVRVRPRT.

Firstly, it provides crucial structural flexibility, allowing the epitopes to maintain their three-dimensional and functional conformation, optimizing their interactions. This flexibility minimizes interference between epitopes, promoting the generation of a specific and efficient immune response. Additionally, EAAAK enhances the solubility and expression of recombinant proteins in various expression systems, such as bacteria or eukaryotic cells, by preventing misfolding or aggregation of the fused protein. This linker also contributes to the stability of the protein under physiological conditions and during storage, ensuring its functionality over time. Together, these properties make EAAAK an optimal choice for the creation of this multiepitope proteins, enhancing both their production and their effectiveness in future applications52.

Assessment of physicochemical characteristics, antigenic properties, and cross-reactivity of the designed construct

ProtParam analysis indicated that the designed multi-epitope antigen is stable, with a stability index 27.59 (scores below 40 indicate stability). The molecular weight of the antigen was approximately 15,744.93 Da, with an isoelectric point (pI) of 10.05. The aliphatic index was calculated 82.75. SolPro analysis revealed high solubility, with a solubility index of 0.798569. VaxiJen analysis confirmed the antigenicity of the multi-epitope antigen with a score of 0.8203, and no cross-reactivity was observed (Table 4).

Prediction of 2D and 3D structures, refinement and validation of the 3D model of the designed construct

SOPMA predicted the secondary structure of the multi-epitope antigen, revealing 59.06% alpha helices, 0.00% beta bridges, 0.00% beta turns, and 33.56% random coils (Table 5). For 3D structure prediction, five models were generated using GalaxyTBM and evaluated through ProSA-web, ERRAT, and Ramachandran plots. Model four was identified as the most reliable initial structure and was further refined. The refined models were assessed using GDT-HA, RMSD, MolProbity, Clash score, Poor rotamers, and Rama favored parameters. The model with the highest overall scores was selected as the final structure. ERRAT, Ramachandran plot, and the ProSA-web server were used to confirm the quality of the selected model. ERRAT analysis indicated an overall quality factor of 97.037. The Ramachandran plot from PROCHECK demonstrated that 97.0% of residues were within favored regions. Additionally, the ProSA-web z-score of -3.21 supported the structural reliability of the model (Fig. 3).

Immune simulation of the designed construct

The immunogenic potential of the in-silico designed multi-epitope antigen was assessed using the C-IMMSIM immune simulator. A single injection, mimicking one natural exposure to the pathogen, was applied to evaluate the primary immune response. The simulation demonstrated rapid antigen clearance within the first week, coinciding with the onset of humoral activation.

Following antigen exposure, an early IgM peak was observed, representing the primary response. This was followed by class switching to IgG isotypes, with IgG1 being the predominant subclass. The overall antibody levels (IgM + IgG) reached a maximum around day 18–20, highlighting effective seroconversion. An expansion of plasma B cells occurred soon after antigen presentation, leading to transient antibody secretion. Subsequently, a population of memory B cells was established, indicating that even a single exposure was sufficient to generate immunological memory and sustain IgG production (Fig. 5).

Optimization and in-silico cloning of the final designed construct

Using the OPTIMIZER server, the multi-epitope construct sequence was successfully optimized for high-level expression in E. coli.

The sequence was analyzed for rare codon usage using the Rare Codon Analysis Tool. This assessment yielded a Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) of 0.73, suggesting a favorable gene expression level. A CAI of 1.0 represents ideal expression in the target host, while values exceeding 0.8 typically indicate high expression efficiency.

Additionally, the Frequency of Optimal Codons (FOP) was measured at 47%, reflecting the proportion of codons used most frequently for each amino acid in the desired expression host. A value of 100 corresponds to the highest frequency codon for a specific amino acid. The average GC content of the sequence was calculated to be 0.59, which falls within the optimal range of 30%-70% for efficient gene expression.

The RNAfold server was employed to predict the secondary structure of the mRNA. The centroid structure exhibited a minimum free energy (MFE) of -68.98 kcal/mol, whereas the MFE structure had a value of -168.90 kcal/mol, suggesting potential post-synthesis mRNA stability (Fig. 6A,B).

Illustrates the in-silico cloning process and secondary structure prediction of the multi-epitope antigen construct: (A) minimal free energy (MFE) secondary structure, (B) centroid secondary structure, (C) the gene sequence (depicted in red), was inserted between the NdeI and XhoI restriction sites of the pET-23a(+) expression vector (represented as a black circle).

For cloning purposes, restriction sites for the NdeI and XhoI enzymes were introduced at both the 5′ and 3′ ends of the optimized sequence. The modified sequence was subsequently inserted into the pET-23a(+) vector using SnapGene software. The construct utilized the start codon provided by the vector itself, ensuring proper initiation of translation. By using the vector’s native ATG, the translation initiation site is properly positioned upstream of the multi-epitope sequence, ensuring efficient expression (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

VL remains a significant global health concern, particularly in endemic regions, where early and reliable diagnosis is critical for reducing disease burden and improving treatment outcomes53–55. Native antigens derived directly from parasites have been widely used in leishmaniasis diagnostic tests. The preparation of native antigens necessitates extensive parasite cultivation, a process that is both time-consuming and logistically challenging in resource-limited settings56. Additionally, these often lack adequate sensitivity and specificity for consistent and valid detection57. For instance, the commonly used rK39 antigen, produced recombinantly, has demonstrated variable performance across different endemic regions, with notably reduced sensitivity in East Africa58. While recombinant production of antigens like rK39 circumvents the need for parasite cultivation, preparations relying on native antigens from parasite cultures remain labor-intensive and logistically challenging, particularly in resource-limited settings56.

To overcome these limitations, recent efforts have focused on recombinant antigens, which offer improved specificity and scalability for serological diagnosis and antigen design by enhancing the detection of pathogen-specific immune responses59. Advances in bioinformatics have facilitated the design of multi-epitope antigens with enhanced diagnostic potential efficacy60. In addition to diagnostic applications, the multi-epitope approach has also proven to be immunogenic as vaccine antigens, inducing effective immune responses61.

Furthermore, the design of multi-epitope antigens has been implemented in the serological diagnosis of other diseases, including strongyloidiasis. In the case of Strongyloides stercoralis, a multi-epitope construct was designed to address the common issue of cross-reactivity with other parasitic nematodes. This construct was developed by identifying highly antigenic segments and linking them using an appropriate linker. Following optimization for expression in E. coli, the designed protein demonstrated promising characteristics such as hydrophilicity, thermostability, and an estimated half-life exceeding 10 h in E. coli. The study concluded that the construct could serve as an efficient antigen in ELISA systems for the serological diagnosis of strongyloidiasis62.

For toxocariasis, a multi-epitope protein for Toxocara canis was formulated using immunoinformatic tools to enhance the accuracy of serodiagnosis, addressing a critical challenge in diagnosing this zoonosis63. Similarly, in the diagnosis of Mayaro virus (MAYV), a chimeric protein composed of epitopes from nsP1, nsP2, E1, and E2 was developed, demonstrating high accuracy in detecting anti-MAYV IgM antibodies without cross-reactivity with other infectious diseases64.

Several studies have been conducted on applying recombinant proteins as diagnostic candidates for leishmaniasis. For example, the P1P2P3 protein exhibited high sensitivity (98%) and specificity (95.31%) in diagnosing CVL65. Similarly, PQ10 and PQ20, two recombinant multi-epitope proteins, outperformed commercial kits in detecting asymptomatic L. infantum infections in canines66. Another study developed a multi-epitope recombinant protein (MRP) incorporating eight antigens (Gp63, Kinetoplastid Membrane Protein-11(KMP-11), HSP70, Cysteine Peptidase A(CPA), Histone 2 A(H2A), H3, Leishmania-Activated C-Kinase Antigen (LACK), and Trypanothione Reductase (TRYP)), demonstrated high diagnostic efficacy for VL achieving a sensitivity of 93.1% and a specificity of 77.4% 67.

In a previous report of Rashidi et al., several immunoreactive proteins were identified as novel native antigens of L. infantum in Iran through immunoproteomic analysis. The antigens include PHB, ornithine aminotransferase (OAT), annexin A4 (ANX-A4), HSP60, phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK-1), APO A-I, ALDH, PDIA3, complement component 1 Q subcomponentbinding protein (C1QBP), voltage-dependent anion-selective channel protein 1 (VDAC-1), and translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane 40 (TOM40), which exhibit strong immunogenicity and are associated with critical biological functions such as pathogenicity, survival, and parasite metabolism21. Based on these findings, the present study applied an immunoinformatic approach to design a novel recombinant multi-epitope antigen for VL diagnosis, incorporating key proteins, PHB, ApoA-I, ALDH, and PDI, identified in our prior immunoproteomic analysis.

The multi-epitope protein was designed by linking selected antigenic epitopes, each exhibiting high antigenicity and no cross-reactivity with other pathogens, into a unified sequence. The EAAAK linker was employed during this process. This linker, due to its stable alpha-helical structure, offers distinct advantages by maintaining appropriate spacing and orientation between epitopes, minimizing steric hindrance, and enhancing effective antibody recognition41. The use of the EAAAK linker in antigen design has been widely applied in other studies. For example, Asadi et al. designed a multi-epitope antigen from immunodominant proteins for the serological diagnosis of human cytomegalovirus infection, using EAAAK linker to combine the multi-epitope antigen68.

The designed antigen was evaluated for potential cross-reactivity using the BLAST-p tool. The results of the Protein BLAST analysis confirmed the absence of any significant cross-reactivity between the predicted epitopes of the designed antigen and the sequences of other infectious agents. Additionally, the antigenic properties of the multi-epitope antigen were predicted using the VaxiJen v2.0 server. The results indicated that the designed protein, with a score of 0.8203 qualifies as an antigen.

The development of fusion antigens using bioinformatics-based approaches can substantially improve the accuracy of serological methods. By integrating multiple immunogenic sequences from the target pathogen and focusing on highly antigenic regions, these fusion antigens enable broader antibody detection while minimizing the risk of false-positive results caused by cross-reactive responses. These findings are also consistent with the study conducted by Omidian et al. for the serological diagnosis of human strongyloidiasis69. Another notable recombinant antigen, rMELEISH, combines epitopes from kinesin 39 (K39), alpha-tubulin, and heat shock proteins HSP70 and HSP83.1, showed a high sensitivity and specificity in detecting L. infantum infections in both humans and canines70.

Evaluation of physicochemical properties is required for the efficient synthesis and expression of recombinant proteins. The engineered multi-epitope antigen was evaluated using the ProtParam tool, focusing on essential features including the instability index, aliphatic index, and solubility. The instability index was 27.59 (Table 4), well below the threshold of 40, indicating a stable protein that is less prone to unfolding and aggregation42. This result aligns with previous studies in recombinant protein development, where stable antigens exhibited increased resistance to degradation and maintained conformational epitopes during expression71.

Also, the aliphatic index was 82.75, reflecting a high proportion of aliphatic amino acids, which is associated with enhanced thermostability. An increased concentration of residues such as glycine and alanine has been reported to improve resistance to heat denaturation72. Similar trends have been reported in the design of multi-epitope antigens, where higher aliphatic indices have been correlated with improved shelf life and stability under varying storage conditions. This property is crucial for antigens intended for use in field settings, where maintaining a cold chain may be challenging73.

SolPro server was used to predict the solubility of the multi-epitope antigens. The findings demonstrated that the antigen was insoluble, evidenced by a solubility index of about 0.79. Solubility is expected to select the optimal protein expression system, purification strategies, and determine suitable extraction methods72. Collectively, these physicochemical characteristics support efficient recombinant protein production and may contribute to improved downstream immune responses74.

SOPMA predicted the secondary structure of the multi-epitope antigen, revealing 59.06% alpha helices, 0.00% beta bridges, 0.00% beta turns, and 33.56% random coils (Table 5). The predicted alpha-helical content suggested that an optimal proportion of alpha helices promotes correct protein folding and structural stability, both of which are essential for preserving conformational epitopes and enabling effective antibody recognition75. An excessively high or markedly low alpha-helix percentage can alter epitope accessibility or reduce solubility, thereby impairing immunogenicity76.

The structural accuracy of the final model was validated using various tools to assess quality. ERRAT analysis showed an overall quality factor of 97.037, indicating an exact structural model. ProSA-web Z-score of − 3.21 validated its structural reliability. The results of the 3D structural validation of the selected model also showed that approximately 97.0% of the predicted amino acid residues were located within the favored regions of the Ramachandran plot, while only a small number were found in disallowed regions (Fig. 3). 3D modeling and physicochemical evaluation reveal that epitopes are exposed on the surface and structurally accessible63. This construct retains the diversity of epitope origins while preserving conformational integrity necessary for effective antibody recognition63. Accurate prediction of the protein’s 3D structure can also provide valuable insight into the approximate spatial positioning of the epitopes following protein expression, which is crucial for ensuring proper epitope presentation and effective immune recognition77.

ElliPro predicted four conformational B-cell epitopes (up to 32 amino acids) from the modeled structure. Although in serological assays such as ELISA the immobilized antigen is generally denatured and epitopes are presented in a predominantly linear form, mapping conformational epitopes remains informative. This is because conformational predictions highlight regions of the protein that are naturally exposed and immunologically relevant in the native fold, which can support construct design and purification strategies. Integrating linear and conformational B-cell epitope predictions therefore broadens diagnostic coverage, ensuring that both sequential and structurally exposed determinants are considered, ultimately contributing to improved assay sensitivity78,79.

The mRNA secondary structure was predicted using the RNAfold server, yielding an MFE structure with a free energy of − 168.90 kcal/mol and a centroid structure with − 68.98 kcal/mol. These substantially negative free energy values indicate a highly stable mRNA molecule post-transcription, enhancing in vivo resistance to degradation and supporting sustained translation. Such stability is advantageous in heterologous expression systems, as mRNA half-life directly influences protein yield80.

The immune simulation demonstrated rapid antigen clearance within the first week, a key characteristic of adequate immunogenicity. During clearance, humoral activation begins, indicating the construct’s ability to activate B cells after a single exposure. Early IgM peaks reflect the primary immune response and correspond to the diagnostic window used to identify acute infections81. Class switching to IgG, with IgG1 as the predominant subclass, confirmed effective seroconversion. The dominance of IgG1 suggests that the construct can drive a strong Th2-oriented humoral response, which is relevant for antibody-based diagnostic applications. In B cell dynamics, rapid expansion of B cells drives transient antibody production, whereas the subsequent establishment of memory B cells ensures long-term humoral activity. This ability to sustain IgG production after a single exposure is especially relevant for diagnostic assays, as it allows both acute and long-term detection of pathogen-specific responses81. Therefore, this construct provides a rationale for consistent serological testing at various stages of infection. These features suggest that the construct has strong potential to function as a diagnostic antigen for visceral leishmaniasis, where pathogen-specific antibody detection in sera is the cornerstone of widely used serological tests.

The codon usage indicates a well-suited sequence in the host of choice. A CAI of 0.73 represents a high potential expression. It suggests that the gene has a high capacity to be translated effectively in our desired expression system. The moderate FOP value of 47% shows a reasonable level of preferred codons that can sustain adequate expression levels and allow favorable translation efficiency. The GC content falls within the optimal range to ensure genetic stability, positively affecting transcription activity and mRNA processing. This balance is needed to achieve a consistent expression system without compromising molecular integrity82.

Finally, the cloning strategy employs established restriction sites and a highly expressive vector. Cloning into the pET-23a(+) expression system significantly improves the construct’s utility for recombinant protein production. The choice of expression vector and affinity tag represents a critical consideration in fusion antigen development, as residual tags that remain attached to the recombinant protein may potentially induce undesirable cross-reactivity during immunological assays. In this study, pET-23a (+) system was chosen in which a histidine tag (His-tag) is positioned at the terminus of the fusion antigen. This configuration ensures that the tag does not induce any cross-reactivity with other infectious agents, thereby preserving the specificity of the designed antigen.

While in-silico design provides a powerful approach for the initial development of antigen candidates, it cannot replace empirical validation including both safety and efficacy, which are essential to account for the biological complexity underlying these predictions and to perform comprehensive experimental testing83. Moreover, several factors can significantly influence gene expression outcomes, including protein solubility, stability, proper folding, and the compatibility of gene sequences with the host transcription and translation machinery84. Big data analytics and machine learning offer promising avenues to further optimize antigen design by predicting potential expression challenges and guiding experimental strategies85.

Conclusion

This study proposes the design of a multi-epitope antigen for the diagnosis of VL, using a bioinformatics approach to select antigenic proteins from L. infantum. Conserved epitopes from key proteins such as PHB, Apo A-I, ALDH, and PDI were selected and linked using a connector that ensures the stability and solubility of the antigen. Bioinformatics analyses showed that the antigen has high stability, good solubility, and strong antigenic potential, with no cross-reactivity with other pathogens.

The prediction of secondary and tertiary structures revealed that the antigen’s conformation is suitable for proper epitope presentation, facilitating immune system recognition. Additionally, optimization for expression in E. coli suggests that the antigen could be efficiently produced in recombinant systems. Although the results are promising, experimental validation is needed to confirm its effectiveness in a clinical setting. In summary, this study represents a step forward in the design of antigens to improve diagnostic performance for VL.

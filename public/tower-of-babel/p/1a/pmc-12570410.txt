Knowledge domain and emerging trends on the SGLT2 inhibitor dapagliflozin for heart failure: a bibliometric analysis from 2012 to 2024
Authors: Mengxuan Xiao, Yuan Sui, Daoyao Fu, Shaolin Gong, Nana Xiong, Yifan Wu, Wenjun Xiong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeDapagliflozin has been formally used to treat heart failure on the basis of extensive data from the DAPA-HF trial, and it now serves as a new quadruple cornerstone drug for heart failure. This study aimed to understand the global research hotspots and trends in dapagliflozin treatment for heart failure through bibliometric analysis and to provide directions for future research.MethodsAll literature on dapagliflozin for heart failure was collected from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) between January 1, 2012, and January 1, 2024. Bibliometric analysis of the retrieved data was performed using CiteSpace and Bibliometrix (R package).ResultsA total of 919 publications were retrieved, involving 215 authors, 216 institutions, and 76 countries/regions. Since 2016, the annual number of publications on dapagliflozin for heart failure has demonstrated a consistent upward trend. The most prolific authors include Anna Maria Langkilde, Mikhail N. Kosiborod, and Scott D. Solomon. The top contributing countries were the United States, Sweden, and China, while the leading institutions were AstraZeneca, Harvard University, and Brigham and Women’s Hospital. Keyword analysis revealed that treatment for diverse heart failure subtypes, cardiorenal outcomes, combination therapies, and underlying mechanisms have emerged as prominent research foci.ConclusionBased on our findings, future investigations are expected to focus on dapagliflozin’s therapeutic potential for heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), particularly regarding combination therapy with novel non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs). Additionally, the mechanism underlying dapagliflozin’s anti-fibrotic effects requires further mechanistic investigation.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome characterized by cardinal symptoms such as breathlessness, ankle swelling, and fatigue. This syndrome stems from structural and/or functional heart impairment, causing elevated intracardiac pressures and/or insufficient cardiac output at rest and/or during exercise [1]. The prevalence of HF worldwide is estimated to be between 1% and 3% of the total population, with an estimated 56.2 million people affected globally in 2019 [2]. According to an analysis of health insurance data from 50 million urban workers in six provinces of China in 2017, the standardized prevalence of heart failure in China was 1.1% [3]. Furthermore, with the ageing population and prevalence of unhealthy lifestyles, the incidence of HF will continue to rise. In addition, patients with HF have a poor prognosis, as evidenced by a 5-year survival rate of less than 56% [4]. Among 39,982 patients in the United States who were hospitalized for HF and enrolled in the Get With The Guidelines–Heart Failure (GWTG-HF) registry, the median survival time was 2.1 years, and the 5-year mortality rate was 75.4% [5]. HF is also characterized by high readmission rates, with approximately 80% to 90% of HF readmissions resulting from worsening of pre-existing HF [6]. The growing HF population imposes significant economic burdens on healthcare systems, with health economics research indicating average annual per-patient costs of approximately €25,000 in Western countries [7]. The total cost of HF in the United States was estimated at $30.7 billion in 2012, with projections indicating a 127% increase to $69.8 billion by 2030 [8]. Current research indicates that the global cost of heart failure (HF) was $284.17 billion annually in 2021, with direct costs—including outpatient management, hospitalization, and medication—accounting for $136.86 billion [9]. However, no specific drugs exist for HF treatment, necessitating further research into its therapeutic management.

Dapagliflozin, a sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, exerts hypoglycemic effects by increasing urinary glucose excretion and decreasing plasma glucose levels in an insulin-independent manner [10]. However, recent studies demonstrate that dapagliflozin also shows significant potential for treating HF [11–13]. In 2019, the DAPA-HF trial demonstrated that dapagliflozin reduced the relative risk of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure by 26%, with a 17% reduction in all-cause mortality, in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) regardless of diabetes status [14]. In 2022, the DELIVER trial demonstrated that dapagliflozin significantly reduced the composite risk of cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization in patients with HF and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) exceeding 40% [15]. Dapagliflozin is gaining increasing acceptance for HF treatment in clinical practice. Dapagliflozin was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2020 for treating HF in patients with or without type 2 diabetes (T2DM).Released in March 2024, the China Heart Failure Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines 2024 elevated sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) to first-line therapy for HF. Together with renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, β-blockers, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, SGLT2i forms a new quadruple therapy’—now the cornerstone of HF management. All four drug classes carry Class “I, A”recommendations. While evidence-based medical support for dapagliflozin in HF treatment continues to grow, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of its application remains unavailable.

Bibliometrics is a literature analysis method that assesses the publication output and impact within a specific field from quantitative and qualitative perspectives [16]. Common bibliometric tools include CiteSpace [17], VoSviewer [18] and the R package Bibliometrix. Bibliometrix, developed by Massimo Aria and Corrado Cuccurullo in 2017, requires an R software environment for implementation [19]. CiteSpace, a scientometric visualization tool developed by Chaomei Chen, analyzes scholarly literature through computational mapping to reveal disciplinary evolution dynamics and identify emerging research frontiers [17]. In this study, CiteSpace software was used to analyze the current status, hotspots, and future trends of dapagliflozin therapy for HF research, aiming to provide a reference for its rational application in clinical practice and lay the foundation for further research in the future.

Materials and methods

Data retrieval

The Web of Science Core Collection database is renowned for its comprehensive citation search capabilities and its indexing of peer-reviewed, high-impact journals. Data were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection database on August 28, 2024.The search strategy was as follows: TS= (“Heart Failure” OR “Cardiac Failure” OR “Heart Decompensation”) AND (“Dapagliflozin”). The search period spanned from January 1, 2000 to January 1, 2024. Document types were limited to articles and reviews, with language restricted to English.

Methods

CiteSpace and Bibliometrix are two complementary software tools designed to identify and visualize emerging trends and developments in scientific literature. Search results were exported in plain text format and subsequently imported into both CiteSpace (version 6.2.R6) and the Bibliometrix. Within CiteSpace, node types were sequentially configured as authors, institutions, countries, and keywords. Parameter settings included: time slicing at 1-year intervals; for collaboration networks, g-index scaling (k = 10) node selection; for co-occurrence networks, a Top 50 per slice selection threshold with Pathfinder algorithm and Pruning sliced networks optimization. Keyword cluster analysis

Keyword clustering refers to the process of grouping strongly interconnected keywords into conceptually distinct thematic clusters within a research domain’s keyword network. This approach visually reveals the distribution of core research foci within the field. Keyword clustering analysis was performed using CiteSpace, generating a clustered knowledge map. Clusters containing fewer than 10 keywords were excluded as analytically insignificant. Clusters with silhouette scores > 0.7 were considered successfully formed. Ultimately, 11 valid clusters were retained, with each color-coded block in the visualization representing a distinct thematic cluster. The clustering modularity value of Q = 0.6672 (> 0.3) indicates a statistically significant cluster structure. The mean silhouette score of S = 0.87 (> 0.7) confirms robust cluster homogeneity, demonstrating high reliability and validity of the clustering solution. The 11 clusters included # 0 diabetes, # 1 pooled analysis, # 2 therapy, # 3 cardiovascular disease, # 4 mechanistic trial, # 5 renoprotection, # 6 Dpp-4 inhibitor, # 7 inflammation, # 8 outcome, # 9 myocardial infarction, and # 10 atazanavir (Table 6; Fig. 7). Based on the clustering results above, we can conclude that current research on dapagliflozin targeting HF involves studies on the prognosis of diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and kidney diseases, investigations into its potential mechanisms (including inflammation), and research on related drugs demonstrating cardiovascular benefits.

Hot topics and cutting edge analysis

Keyword bursts refer to terms that exhibit a sharp increase in frequency within a specific timeframe across published literature. This metric enables the identification of emerging research hotspots and forecasting of future disciplinary trends. As shown in Fig. 8, “DPP-4 inhibitors” and “all-cause mortality” emerged earliest and maintained the longest burst duration. Subsequently, keywords including “cardiovascular outcomes,” “CVD real,” and “potential mechanisms” also demonstrated prolonged burst strength. More recently, terms such as “reduced ejection fraction,” “preserved ejection fraction,” and “mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists” have sustained prominence since their abrupt emergence in 2022. By analyzing the keyword timeline (Fig. 9), clearly reveals Cluster 0 and 9 exhibiting the longest research continuity, with core terms “heart failure,” “empagliflozin,” “dapagliflozin,” “myocardial infarction,” and “cardiovascular disease.” These were followed by Cluster 2 and 8 featuring key terms “risk,” “guidelines,” “outcomes,” and “reduced ejection fraction.” Based on the Trend Tropic (Fig. 10), it can be observed that the earliest high-interest topics were “double-blind trials” and “DPP-4 inhibitors,” followed by “all-cause mortality,” “glucose cotransporter 2,” “blood pressure control,” and “blood glucose control.” Starting in 2020, “cardiovascular outcomes,” “empagliflozin,” “dapagliflozin,” “heart failure,” and related terms emerged. More recently, “albuminuria” and “spironolactone” have appeared as new focus areas. These trends correspond consistently with the results shown in Figs. 8 and 9.

Discussion

SGLT2 inhibitors lower blood glucose levels by preventing the reabsorption of filtered glucose, thereby increasing its urinary excretion [12]. These drugs were initially developed for the treatment of diabetes. As research advanced, it was discovered that SGLT2 inhibitors also exhibit cardiovascular protective effects [20–22]. Compared with other glucose-lowering agents, SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduce the risk of heart failure hospitalization in diabetic patients [23]. Dapagliflozin is a member of the SGLT2 inhibitor class. In recent years, research on dapagliflozin for heart failure treatment has steadily increased, accompanied by a continuous rise in publications in this field, marking a period of intense research focus. As shown in the annual publication chart, the field experienced a spike in publications during 2019, directly linked to the landmark DAPA-HF trial conducted that year. Analysis of collaboration networks among countries, institutions, and authors reveals that the United States currently leads in publication volume, making exceptional contributions to the advancement of this field and maintaining a leadership position. Among the top 10 institutions globally ranked by publication volume, seven are based in the United States. Similarly, three of the world’s top five most published authors are affiliated with U.S. institutions. These metrics constitute key contributing factors to the nation’s leading position in publication output, further reinforcing its dominant influence in the field. The UK-based pharmaceutical company AstraZeneca ranks as the world’s most productive institution, demonstrating a significantly high betweenness centrality of 0.1 – indicating it serves as the pivotal collaborative bridge connecting global research organizations. The three most prolific countries in publication output—the United States, Sweden, and China—all exhibit betweenness centrality values below 0.1. This structural pattern suggests significant structural holes in international collaboration networks within this research domain, highlighting an imperative to transcend geographical constraints in future studies. Africa demonstrates markedly low publication output coupled with disproportionately high betweenness centrality, revealing its essential roles: as a collaborative bridge linking core research institutions in Europe/North America with counterparts across Asia, Africa, and Latin America, and as a research evidence contributor in the global knowledge system–without achieving dominant status in knowledge production. Fostering collaboration among authors, institutions, and nations will catalyze substantive advancement in this field.

Through analysis of emergent keywords such as “reduced ejection fraction” and “preserved ejection fraction,” we have found that the study population for dapagliflozin in treating HF is gradually expanding from heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) to include patients with HF and an ejection fraction over 40%.Multiple large trials, such as the EMPEROR-Reduced study and the DAPA-HF trial, have shown that SGLT2 inhibitors reduce mortality and HF hospitalization in patients with HFrEF, with or without diabetes [14, 24]. Notably, the DAPA-HF trial convincingly established the benefits of dapagliflozin specifically in this low-ejection-fraction heart failure population. In recent years, some studies have demonstrated that dapagliflozin also benefits patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), as it improves symptoms and physical function [25], significantly enhances cardiac function, and reduces the overall risk of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death in this population [15, 26, 27]. In reality, however, current therapeutic approaches for HFpEF remain limited—for instance, β-blockers are not routinely recommended. For HFpEF management, we can stratify therapeutic research based on distinct phenotypic profiles such as the obese phenotype, fibrotic phenotype, and vascular phenotype.

Based on the analysis of keywords including “outcomes,” “mortality,” “cardiovascular outcome,” “acute kidney disease,” “double-blind,” and “ mechanisms” from co-occurrence networks, clustered terms such as “cardiovascular disease,” “ mechanistic trials,” “ renoprotection,” and “inflammation” from cluster analysis, and timeline keywords including “myocardial infarction,” “cardiovascular diseases,” “risk factors,” “ clinical guidelines,” “outcome,” “ reduced ejection fraction,” and “fibrosis,” our research reveals that recent studies primarily focus on cardiorenal outcomes, mortality, and underlying mechanisms (inflammation and fibrosis).In addition to its cardioprotective effects, dapagliflozin also demonstrates renoprotective properties [28–30]. In the DAPA-HF trial, dapagliflozin attenuated the decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) [31, 32]. In the DELIVER trial—another major study—dapagliflozin similarly attenuated the decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) [33]. Although dapagliflozin provides therapeutic benefits in heart failure, its precise mechanisms of action remain incompletely understood. Current hypotheses propose that these effects may involve attenuation of myocardial fibrosis, reduction of cardiac inflammation, and mitigation of oxidative stress. Dapagliflozin inhibits myocardial fibrosis, potentially through rescuing high glucose (HG)-induced endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) via AMPKα-mediated suppression of the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway and oxidative stress [34]. Dapagliflozin also attenuates fibrosis by suppressing macrophage-driven inflammation [35]. Dapagliflozin further alleviates oxidative stress through pathways including the Nrf2/ARE signaling axis, thereby inhibiting myocardial fibrosis. Furthermore, dapagliflozin attenuates intracellular calcium overload, thereby reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and protecting cardiac mitochondria [36]. The mechanisms underlying dapagliflozin’s anti-fibrotic effects remain incompletely elucidated, necessitating further research. Moreover, the emergence of Cluster #10 is linked to cardiovascular complications frequently observed in HIV patients, attributable to either antiretroviral drug (ART) side effects or direct HIV effects. This underscores the need for heightened focus on ART safety profiles in future research.

The prominence of terms such as “spironolactone,” “hypertension,” “heart failure,” and “albuminuria” in topic trend analyses reflects both the evolving paradigm of integrated cardiorenal management and current clinical adoption of combination therapy with dapagliflozin plus mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists. Progressive decline in renal function frequently precipitates deterioration of cardiac function and elevates the risk of adverse cardiovascular events. HF constitutes a common complication in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), a pathophysiological continuum formally designated as Type 4 cardiorenal syndrome [37]. Brenner [38] proposed that glomerular hyperfiltration with glomerular capillary hypertension underlies renal disease pathogenesis. Dapagliflozin reduces this hyperfiltration, thereby preventing renal disease progression and consequently lowering HF risk. Clinically, dapagliflozin is frequently combined with medications like spironolactone for HF management. While spironolactone improves outcomes in HFrEF, its therapy in HFpEF did not significantly reduce the incidence of the primary composite outcome (cardiovascular death, aborted cardiac arrest, or heart failure hospitalization) [39]. There are few studies on how other types of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists affect the prognosis of HFpEF.

Limitations

The Web of Science database is constantly updated, and discrepancies may exist between the number of documents retrieved and the actual number of documents. The exclusion of non-English literature from the sources may have introduced source bias. To mitigate language bias, future studies will implement multilingual corpus processing. Additionally, certain keywords had multiple expressions, which may still result in inaccuracies despite our efforts to merge synonymous terms as comprehensively as possible.

Conclusion

This study employed bibliometric analysis software to delineate research hotspots, directions, leading authors, institutions, and country distributions in the dapagliflozin heart failure research domain. Developed nations—particularly the United States and United Kingdom—emerged as leading contributors. Current research primarily focuses on treatment for diverse heart failure subtypes, cardiorenal outcomes, combination therapy strategies, and underlying mechanisms. Based on our findings, future investigations are projected to concentrate on dapagliflozin’s therapeutic potential for HFpEF, especially regarding combination therapy with novel non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists. Furthermore, the mechanisms underpinning dapagliflozin’s anti-fibrotic effects require in-depth exploration.

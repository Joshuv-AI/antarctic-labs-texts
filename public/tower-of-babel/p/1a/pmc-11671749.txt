AcRLK2P-1, an LRR receptor protein kinase gene from Agropyron cristatum, confers leaf rust resistance in wheat
Authors: Shirui Xu, Xiajie Ji, Haiming Han, Jinpeng Zhang, Shenghui Zhou, Baojin Guo, Xinming Yang, Xiuquan Li, Xiaomin Guo, Taiguo Liu, Lihui Li, Weihua Liu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is the most widely cultivated staple food crop globally. Wheat leaf rust, caused by Puccinia triticina, represents one of the most prevalent and devastating diseases affecting wheat, causing yield losses of up to 50% in severe infestations (Lin et al., 2022). To date, a total of 11 leaf rust resistance genes have been cloned, however, Lr1, Lr10, and Lr14a alone have proven are largely ineffective in wheat breeding programs in China (Zhang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023). Previous studies have demonstrated the broad-spectrum resistance of the wheat–Agropyron cristatum translocation line 2PT5 to 50 leaf rust races from various regions across China, and the leaf rust resistance gene is located on a segment of 2PL bin fragment length (FL) 0.66–0.86 (Jiang et al., 2018). Given the scarcity of broad-spectrum leaf rust resistance genes in current wheat cultivars, it is crucial to explore and clone these genes from A. cristatum 2PL for their effective integration into breeding programs aimed at developing disease-resistant cultivars. The lack of genetic recombination between the alien segment and the corresponding wheat genome makes narrowing down the target region a challenging task. In this study, we employed PacBio isoform sequencing technology in conjunction with RNA sequencing to clone the leaf rust resistance gene AcRLK2P-1 from the wheat–A. cristatum 2PL translocation fragment. We further validated the functionality of AcRLK2P-1 through EMS mutant analysis and transgenic experiments.

To analyze the physiological processes underlying the resistance of the translocation line 2PT5 to leaf rust infection and to identify differentially expressed resistance genes, RNA sequencing was conducted on both the wheat–A. cristatum resistant line 2PT5 and the susceptible parent Fukuhokomugi (hereafter referred to as Fukuho) before and after inoculation (Figure 1A; Supplemental Table 1). A total of 258 upregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in 2PT5_CK vs. Fukuho_CK, and 798 upregulated DEGs were identified between 2PT5_T and Fukuho_T. Notably, a total of 79 DEGs were found to be upregulated in 2PT5 after inoculation (fold change >2 and p < 0.05) (Figure 1B). A Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway enrichment analysis of these 79 upregulated DEGs revealed their involvement in plant‒pathogen interactions, circadian rhythm regulation in plants, as well as the biosynthesis of flavonoids and tropane, piperidine, and pyridine alkaloids (Supplemental Figure 1A). Gene Ontology enrichment analysis of the 79 upregulated DEGs revealed that these genes were predominantly associated with molecular functions such as catalytic activity, transferase activity, fatty acid synthase activity, and methyltransferase activity (Supplemental Figure 1B). Functional annotation of these genes revealed that 11 were associated with disease resistance. Recently, our laboratory successfully pinpointed a 9.2 Mb genomic segment containing disease resistance genes through population genetic analysis using various translocation lines (Ji et al., unpublished data). The expression profiles of these 11 genes were analyzed and integrated with analysis of the mapping regions. Notably, the AcRLK2P-1 gene, located within the targeted region, exhibited significant upregulation after inoculation and showed the highest expression level, making it a strong candidate for further analysis (Supplemental Figure 2; Supplemental Table 2). AcRLK2P-1 encodes a leucine-rich repeat (LRR) receptor-like protein kinase, which features a transmembrane domain, LRRs, and a serine/threonine kinase domain (Figure 1C).

To determine whether AcRLK2P-1 is required for resistance to leaf rust, the genomic DNA sequences of AcRLK2P-1 were amplified and sequenced in six M2 families of susceptible EMS mutants (Figure 1D). Five of these susceptible mutants harboured SNPs, four of which resulted in amino acid substitutions or premature stop codons. The mutation sites Mut57 (g.488T>A [p.F163Y]), Mut66 (g.2824C>A [p.S349∗]), Mut159 (g.3584G>A [p.A547T]), and Mut172 (g.2902G>A [p.S376N]) are located within exons, whereas the mutation site Mut81 (g.1556C>T) is located in an intron (Figure 1E; Supplemental Figure 3). Additionally, no sequence variations were detected in the genomic sequence of AcRLK2P-1 in another susceptible mutant, Mut158, suggesting the possibility of a second-site mutation associated with susceptibility.

To validate whether AcRLK2P-1 was sufficient to confer resistance to leaf rust, we used Agrobacterium-mediated transformation to separately introduce two constructs into the susceptible wheat cultivar Fielder: proUbi, AcRLK2P-1 driven by the maize ubiquitin (Ubi) promoter and pAcRLK2P-1, AcRLK2P-1 driven by its native promoter (Supplemental Figure 4). In the T0 generation, all plants carrying the proUbi:AcRLK2P-1 construct in the six individuals were resistant to leaf rust race THT, whereas the negative T0 individuals and Fielder were susceptible (Supplemental Figure 5A and 5B). The T1 progenies, obtained by selfing T0 individuals showing various expression levels of the transgene (Supplemental Figure 5C), all displayed enhanced resistance to leaf rust race THT (Figure 1F; Supplemental Figure 5D). After inoculating AcRLK2P-1 transgenic T1 plants with 10 additional leaf rust races, the results indicated that compared to Fielder, T1 transgenic plants exhibited increased resistance to eight of these leaf rust races (Figure 1G). These findings suggest that AcRLK2P-1 plays a significant role in resistance to leaf rust in 2PT5, although broad-spectrum resistance of 2PT5 cannot be solely attributed to AcRLK2P-1. Moreover, all plants carrying the pAcRLK2P-1:AcRLK2P-1 construct in the three independent transgenic families were highly resistant to leaf rust race TGT (Figure 1H).

The qPCR analysis revealed a significant upregulation of AcRLK2P-1 in the leaf and stem tissues of the wheat–A. cristatum translocation line 2PT5 following inoculation with THT (Figure 1I). Expression of the AcRLK2P-1 gene in 2PT5 peaked at 24 h after THT infection (Figure 1J). 2PT5 was derived from the wheat‒A. cristatum 2P addition line II-9-3, where AcRLK2P-1 expression also showed significant enhancement (Supplemental Figure 6). The AcRLK2P-1 protein localizes to the cell membrane (Figure 1K).

The expression levels of five pathogenesis-related (PR) genes—PR1, PR2, PR3, PR4, and PR5, which are involved in plant defenses—were investigated in the transgenic plants expressing AcRLK2P-1 and Fielder. Compared to Fielder, these PR genes were significantly upregulated in AcRLK2P-1 transgenic plants following inoculation (Supplemental Figure 7), suggesting that AcRLK2P-1 regulates the expression of these genes. Notably, the upregulation of PR genes was more pronounced in 2PT5 than in Fukuho upon inoculation (Supplemental Figure 8). In addition, significant upregulation of PR genes was observed in 2PT5 upon inoculation with THT (Supplemental Figure 9).

Phylogenetic analysis of the AcRLK2P-1 protein and its orthologs in Poaceae crops revealed that AcRLK2P-1 shares a common evolutionary branch with T. aestivum, Triticum urartu, and Aegilops tauschii. Notably, the orthologous protein from Hordeum vulgare is positioned on a more closely related branch, indicating a closer phylogenetic relationship (Supplemental Figure 10). A comparison of the amino acid sequences of AcRLK2P-1 with those of the wheat A, B, and D genomes, T. urartu, Ae. tauschii, and H. vulgare, revealed relative conservation in the LRR domain and strong conservation at the N terminus of the serine/threonine kinase domain (Supplemental Figure 11). Furthermore, when the full-length sequence of the AcRLK2P-1 gene was compared with those of its orthologs TraesCS2A02G563900, TraesCS2B02G625200, and TraesCS2D02G573700 in wheat, the similarities were 77.86%, 86.23%, and 85.61%, respectively (Supplemental Figure 12).

The broad-spectrum resistance genes from wild wheat relatives are invaluable for enhancing disease resistance in wheat breeding programs. Kinases have been identified as critical components in plant immune response (Klymiuk et al., 2018; Lu et al., 2020). In this study, we successfully cloned the leaf rust resistance gene AcRLK2P-1 from A. cristatum, which encodes a protein with kinase and LRR domains. This finding broadens our understanding of LRR-RLKs and underscores the critical role of the LRR domain in plant disease defense mechanisms. Given that cultivated wheat typically lacks this exogenous broad-spectrum immune characteristic, the wheat‒A. cristatum small-fragment translocation line, which confers broad-spectrum immunity against leaf rust, is particularly valuable for disease-resistant breeding in wheat.

Association between Ki-67 and clinical outcomes in 324 Chinese patients with resectable acral melanoma
Authors: Yi Teng, Jin Wu, Weizhen Zhang, Kui Jiang, Xin Cai, Jianping Gui, Hongfeng Zhou, Yan Wang, Fang Liu, Jiwei Liu, Zhen Guo, Hongxue Meng, Mei Xiang, Xiang Ji, Di Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe relationship between Ki-67 expression and clinical prognosis in patients with resectable acral melanoma (AM) remains unclear. This study explores the prognostic role of Ki-67 in AM using data from 324 patients across five Chinese centers. Patients were divided into low Ki-67 expression and high Ki-67 expression groups based on pathological reports. The log-rank test was used to compare disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS), and the Cox proportional hazards regression model was employed to identify prognostic factors. Among the 324 patients included in the study, 181 (55.8%) were in the low Ki-67 expression group, and 143 (44.2%) were in the high Ki-67 expression group. The high Ki-67 expression group exhibited a higher incidence of tumor thickness > 4 mm (45.5% vs 24.3%, P < 0.001), ulceration (65.7% vs 54.1%, P = 0.035), stage III (31.5% vs 16.0%, P < 0.001), LDH ≥ 250 U/L (14.0% vs. 6.6%, P = 0.028), and positive sentinel lymph nodes (28.0% vs 11.1%, P = 0.029). High Ki-67 predicted shorter DFS (26.8 vs 74.1 months, P = 0.025) and OS (52.1 months vs NR, P < 0.001). Multivariate analysis identified Ki-67, age, stage and LDH as independent OS predictors.Keywords: Acral melanoma, Ki-67 expression, Disease-free survival, Overall survivalSubject terms: Melanoma, Tumour biomarkers

Introduction

Acral melanoma (AM) is a rare subtype of cutaneous melanoma (CM) with a low incidence in Caucasian populations1–3. However, it is the predominant melanoma subtype among East Asian populations, accounting for over 40% of melanoma cases4–8. AM has a worse prognosis compared to non-acral CM. Unlike CM, ultraviolet light is not a pathogenic factor in AM. Instead, long-term chronic irritation, trauma, and inflammation are believed to contribute to its development. At the molecular level, AM not only shows a low overall mutation burden and hotspot oncogene mutation frequency such as BRAF/NRAS, but also has a unique mutation spectrum, including high mutation rates of c-KIT, NF1, NOTCH2, TYRP1 and PTEN genes9. Recent genome-wide studies have revealed a unique pattern of genomic alterations in AM, including high-frequency chromosomal arm-level copy number variations such as 11q13/CCND1 and 12q14/CDK4 amplifications and a significant structural variant burden10,11.

Ki-67, a marker of cell proliferation, plays a crucial role in tumor growth and progression and is widely used in prognostic evaluation of various malignant tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer, breast cancer, and colon cancer12–14. In malignant melanoma, Ki-67 is often used alongside other immunohistochemical markers to differentiate benign melanocytic nevi from melanoma. Research indicates that elevated Ki-67 expression is associated with worse outcomes, including reduced recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS). However, most of these studies have focused on cutaneous and mucosal melanomas15,16.

Research on the correlation between Ki-67 expression and AM prognosis is limited, though interest in this area has grown. A study in China found that patients with primary non-metastatic AM and high Ki-67 expression had significantly lower five-year OS and RFS than those with low Ki-67 expression. The study also identified Ki-67 as an independent prognostic factor for both OS and RFS17. Another study in China further supported Ki-67 as an independent survival predictor in AM patients18. Despite these findings, larger studies are needed to further clarify the prognostic significance of Ki-67 in AM, a highly aggressive and racially specific malignancy. Therefore, we aim to evaluate the prognostic value of Ki-67 expression in AM patients.

Study design

This multicenter retrospective study collected demographic and clinical data on patients diagnosed with AM at five melanoma treatment centers between February 2011 and May 2023. Patients who met the following criteria were included in the study: (1) histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of AM, (2) unequivocal Ki-67 data, (3) only one primary lesion, and (4) patients with more than 12 months of follow-up, with documented regular reviews. Exclusion criteria were: (1) patients who did not undergo surgical treatment, (2) patients whose diagnosis of the primary site was unclear, (3) more than one primary lesion, (4) patients with severely missing pathological information, and (5) patients with a combination of other malignant tumors. The data were analyzed in December 2024.

This multicenter retrospective analysis utilized fully anonymized data from five participating institutions. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committees of all participating centers: The First Hospital of Jilin University (AF-TRB-032-06), Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital (AF-06-2.1), The Third People’s Hospital of Zhengzhou (2019-04-002-K01), The Second Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (2023-053) and The First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (RJ-KS-KY-2023-195). All ethics committees granted a waiver of individual informed consent in accordance with China’s "Ethical Review Measures for Biomedical Research Involving Humans", Article 32 and the Declaration of Helsinki, as the research posed no more than minimal risk and involved no identifiable private information.

Patient population

Data collected included gender, age at diagnosis, diagnosis date, clinical stage at diagnosis, nevus correlation, history of trauma to the primary lesion, primary tumor location, pathologic information (ulceration status, Breslow thickness, mitotic rate, Ki-67 value), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) results、gene mutation status、disease-free survival (DFS), and OS. Disease staging followed the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Melanoma staging system 8th edition classification19. Ki-67 values were evaluated by two pathologists blinded to the patient’s clinical data. The median Ki-67 value was 30% (range: < 1 to 98%) and was used to define low or high Ki-67 expression levels. Ki-67 values of ≤ 30% were classified as “low Ki-67”, while values > 30% were classified as “high Ki-67”. OS was defined as the time from diagnosis to death or the last follow-up visit, while DFS was defined as the time from extensive resection and the first recurrence or death, whichever occurred first. Follow-ups, conducted via outpatient visits or telephone, tracked disease recurrence, metastasis, or death, with the final follow-up in November 2024.

Data analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS version 27.0 (SPSS, Inc). Ten independent imputed datasets were generated using the fully conditional specification method with Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm, iterated 10 times. The imputation model incorporated not only core analysis variables (key prognostic indicators such as clinical stage and treatment regimen) but also auxiliary variables that might influence the missing mechanism (such as demographic characteristics, tumor location). Following imputation, the 10 datasets were pooled according to Rubin’s rules. Categorical variables were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with significant differences evaluated via the log-rank test.

To avoid potential collinearity issues arising from internal variables of the AJCC 8th Edition staging system (Breslow thickness and ulceration status), only the composite AJCC stage was included as a predictor in both univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses, rather than its constituent elements. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with all included variables demonstrating VIF values < 2, indicating no significant collinearity concerns. Cox regression models were used to examine the association of clinical and pathological variables with OS and DFS, with hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported. Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analyses were included in multivariate Cox proportional hazards model analyses using the ENTER method. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This research involved 324 participants, with a median Ki-67 value of 30% and a median follow-up of 70.9 months. The characteristics of all patients are shown in Table 1. Diagnoses occurred between ages 22 and 86, with a median age of 60 years. There were 154 (47.5%) male and 170 (52.5%) female patients. AM predominantly occurred in the lower limbs (76.5%) and on the right side of the body (56.2%). Stage I, II, and III disease was observed in 71 (21.9%), 179 (55.3%), and 74 (22.8%) patients, respectively. Breslow thickness for T1, T2, T3, and T4 was observed in 41 (12.7%), 89 (27.5%), 85 (26.2%), and 109 (33.6%) patients, respectively. Most patients had a mitotic rate of ≥ 1 (88.0%) and LDH levels < 250 U/L (90.1%). 104 patients (32.1%) underwent SLNB, and 20 patients (19.2%) tested positive. Among all patients, 113 patients (34.9%) underwent hotspot gene mutation analysis, revealing that 61 patients lacked common driver mutations, while 17 (5.3%) patients had BRAF mutations, 16 (4.9%) had NRAS mutations, 13 (4.0%) had KIT mutations, and 6 (1.9%) had other types of mutations. Postoperative adjuvant therapy was administered to 180 patients, of which 143 (44.1%) received interferon therapy, 22 (6.8%) received immunotherapy, and 15 (4.6%) received other treatments (e.g., chemotherapy). The majority of patients reported no prior trauma to the primary lesion and denied any correlation between the primary lesion and nevus malignancy.

There were 181 cases (55.8%) with low Ki-67 expression and 143 cases (44.2%) with high Ki-67 expression. The correlation between Ki-67 expression and general clinical features of AM was analyzed. In the low-expression group, 101 cases (55.8%) were aged < 60 years, while 80 cases (44.2%) were aged ≥ 60 years. In the high-expression group, 54 cases (37.8%) were aged < 60 years, whereas 89 cases (62.2%) were aged ≥ 60 years. The difference in age distribution between the two groups was statistically significant (P = 0.001). Compared with the low-expression group, patients in the high-expression group had thicker tumors (T4 > 4 mm: 45.5% vs 24.3%, P < 0.001), a higher frequency of ulceration (65.7% vs 54.1%, P = 0.035), more stage III diagnoses (31.5% vs 16.0%, P < 0.001), more LDH ≥ 250 U/L (14.0% vs 6.6%, P = 0.028), and more patients with positive SLNB (28.0% vs 11.1%, P = 0.029). No significant differences were observed in other clinical parameters (P > 0.05).

Ki-67 expression and DFS

During a median follow-up of 70.9 months, 166 (51.2%) patients experienced disease relapse. The overall cohort had 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates of 73.3%, 53.1%, and 47.0%, respectively, with a median DFS of 45.2 months (95% CI 22.0–68.4) (Fig. 1A). In the high-expression group, the median DFS was 26.8 months (95% CI 10.3–43.3), with 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates of 68.2%, 45.9%, and 41.2%, respectively. In contrast, the low-expression group had a median DFS of 74.1 months (95% CI not estimable [NE]), with 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates of 77.3%, 53.1%, and 47.0%, respectively. A significant difference in DFS was observed between the groups (HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.04–1.92, P = 0.025) (Fig. 1B).

Ki-67 expression and OS

Over a median follow-up of 70.9 months, 129 (60.2%) patients died. The overall cohort had a median OS of 91.6 months (95% CI 79.9–103.3), with 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates of 96.0%, 77.6%, and 64.3%, respectively (Fig. 1C). The low-expression group demonstrated superior outcomes, with OS rates of 97.8%, 87.6%, and 78.5% at 1-, 3-, and 5- years, respectively, and the median OS was not reached. In contrast, the high-expression group had 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates of 93.7%, 64.6%, and 44.9%, respectively, with a median OS of 52.1 months (95% CI 41.2–62.9). A significant OS advantage was observed in the low-expression group compared to the high-expression group (HR 2.91, 95% CI 2.04–4.16, P < 0.001) (Fig. 1D).

Univariate and multivariate analysis of prognostic factors

Univariate analysis showed that Ki-67 ≥ 30% (HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.04–1.92, P = 0.026), clinical stage (P < 0.001), mitotic rate ≥ 1 (HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.03–2.98, P = 0.039), LDH level ≥ 250 U/L (HR 1.83, 95% CI 1.18–2.84, P = 0.007), SLNB status (P = 0.003), and type of adjuvant treatment (P < 0.001) were associated with poorer DFS. Meanwhile, Ki-67 ≥ 30% (HR 2.83, 95% CI 2.04–4.16, P < 0.001), age ≥ 60 years (HR 1.78, 95% CI 1.25–2.54, P = 0.001), clinical stage (P < 0.001), LDH level ≥ 250 U/L (HR 2.01, 95% CI 1.25–3.24, P = 0.004), and SLNB status (P < 0.001) were associated with poorer OS (Tables 2, 3).

On multivariate analysis, only clinical stage was associated with worse DFS (P < 0.001). Factors associated with worse OS included Ki-67 ≥ 30% (HR 2.11, 95% CI 1.46–3.06, P < 0.001), age ≥ 60 years (HR 1.77, 95% CI 1.21–2.58, P = 0.003) , clinical stage (P < 0.001), and LDH level ≥ 250 U/L (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.01–2.66, P = 0.044).

Discussion

Tumor thickness, ulceration, LDH levels, and some mutations are common poor prognostic factors in patients with AM. Exploring other risk factors affecting prognosis is of great clinical value for AM, a malignant tumor with a poor prognosis.

Ki-67 is widely regarded as a nuclear proliferation marker and a key indicator of tumor cell proliferative activity. To date, only three studies have investigated the relationship between Ki-67 levels and the prognosis of AM patients, though their findings require further validation17,18,20. This study comprehensively compared demographic characteristics, clinicopathologic features, DFS, and OS between low and high Ki-67 expression groups, providing significant validation for previous related studies.

Among the 324 patients with AM included, Ki-67 values ranged from < 1 to 98%, with a median of 30%, higher than most other melanoma subtypes, which also explained the poor overall prognostic survival of AM patients16,21. There were 181 patients in the low-expression group and 143 in the high-expression group (55.8% vs. 44.2%), with the proportion of high-expression patients similar to Du et al., and the proportion of low-expression patients relatively high18. Furthermore, consistent with two other Chinese studies on Ki-67 and AM, we found differences in Ki-67 expression by sex, with higher expression in male patients, though this difference was not statistically significant17,18.

This study revealed that elevated Ki-67 expression is often linked to unfavorable clinicopathologic characteristics in AM. Specifically, Ki-67 expression varied across clinical stages, with the high-expression group showing a significantly higher proportion of stage III patients compared to the low-expression group. Furthermore, the high-expression group had a greater percentage of patients aged ≥ 60 years, T4 classification, and ulceration, aligning with earlier findings that high Ki-67 expression is associated with older age, ulceration, thicker primary lesions, and more advanced disease stages18. In our study, the proportion of patients with high Ki-67 expression and LDH ≥ 250 was higher, a trend that has also been observed in other malignant tumours. High Ki-67 expression is closely associated with high LDH levels22.

In our study, the high Ki-67 expression group exhibited significantly shorter median DFS (26.8 months vs 74.1 months) and OS (52.1 months vs not reached) compared to the low-expression group, consistent with two other Chinese studies, where high Ki-67 expression was associated with poor prognosis. This phenomenon was also observed in CM, mucosal melanoma, and desmoplastic melanoma subtypes, with high and low Ki-67 expression significantly affecting patient prognosis23–25. Further analysis using Cox univariate and multivariate models found that Ki-67 was an independent prognostic factor for OS. This observation may be attributed to several factors. As the primary treatment modality for early-stage solid tumors, radical surgery may attenuate the predictive value of Ki-67 for DFS to some extent by remodeling the tumor microenvironment and completely eliminating local lesions. Furthermore, current research evidence in this field is predominantly based on retrospective study designs, which inherently carry unavoidable selection biases and other potential confounding factors26,27.

A significant limitation in the application of Ki-67 is the absence of standardized methods and validated thresholds, which introduces potential bias when comparing results across studies. Previous research on melanoma has explored various Ki-67 thresholds, such as 40%, 14%, 20%, and 10%. Regardless of the cutoff value used, groups with high Ki-67 expression consistently demonstrated a correlation with poorer prognosis15,16,24,28,29.

Our study is consistent with previous studies, suggesting that Ki-67 serves as a significant independent factor influencing the prognosis of some melanoma subtypes, and emphasizing high Ki-67 expression in clinical practice could more accurately predict AM patient prognosis.

Our strength lies in the integration of clinical data from five large melanoma diagnosis and treatment centers across four regions in China, encompassing diverse geographical and economic characteristics. This multicenter, multiregional study design significantly mitigates the potential selection bias inherent in single-center studies while enhancing the clinical generalizability and external validity of the research findings. The retrospective cohort design of our study introduces limitations, including unavoidable missing data. The study spans a relatively long period, during which the number of cases undergoing SLNB is relatively limited. Further prospective clinical research is necessary to validate these findings.

Conclusion

This research confirmed that elevated Ki-67 levels are associated with unfavorable clinicopathological characteristics. Individuals with high Ki-67 expression exhibited markedly reduced DFS and OS compared to those with low expression. Furthermore, Ki-67 was identified as an autonomous predictor of OS in patients undergoing surgical resection for AM.

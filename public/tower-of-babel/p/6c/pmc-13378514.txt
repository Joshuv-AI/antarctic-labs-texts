Genotype–Phenotype Correlations and Clinical Outcomes of Spesolimab in Acrodermatitis Continua of Hallopeau: A Multicenter Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Xuan Deng, Yusha Chen, Xinli Liu, Jianxia Xiong, Runlu Hu, Sijian Liao, Wei Li, Kun Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo explore genotype–phenotype associations of IL36RN variants and clinical outcomes of spesolimab treatment in acrodermatitis continua of Hallopeau (ACH).Patients and methodsThis multicenter retrospective study included 14 ACH patients with IL36RN genotyping from 2019 to 2025. Clinical features were compared across all genotypes. Medical records of individuals who received spesolimab were reviewed to describe treatment responses, with a follow-up observation period up to 56 weeks.ResultsCompared with monoallelic or wild-type genotypes, biallelic IL36RN variants were associated with earlier disease onset age (23 vs 48 and 47 years, P <0.05) and higher baseline disease severity (median GPPGA score, 4 vs 3 and 2, P <0.001). Among 6 patients with biallelic, refractory ACH who received spesolimab, including 3 isolated cases that were administered at a 450-mg dose, overall percentage improvement across disease activity and patient-reported measures occurred rapidly, reaching 54% at week 1 and 74% at week 4. Relapse occurred in 4 patients between weeks 31 and 44, with disease activity remaining lower than baseline and improvement observed after re-treatment.ConclusionSpesolimab was associated with clinical improvement in refractory ACH with biallelic IL36RN mutations, including isolated ACH treated with a 450-mg dose, supporting further evaluation of dose individualization below the approved 900-mg regimen for GPP flares.

Introduction

Acrodermatitis continua of Hallopeau (ACH) is a rare inflammatory skin condition marked by sterile pustules on the distal digits and nail apparatus.1,2 In the absence of treatment, ACH may advance to osteitis, distal phalangeal osteolysis, and significant functional disability.2–4 The pathophysiology is not fully elucidated; however, IL36RN mutations have been identified as a genetic factor that hyperactivates the IL-36 pathway, resulting in neutrophilic inflammation.5,6 While IL36RN mutations have been associated with earlier onset and disease susceptibility in ACH, available clinical and genetic evidence in ACH remains limited.2,5 Compared with GPP, in which IL36RN-related genotype–phenotype patterns have been more clearly described,7,8 the associations of IL36RN allelic status with disease severity, treatment refractoriness, and treatment response in ACH remain incompletely characterized. Conventional treatments (methotrexate, retinoids, and cyclosporine) are often insufficient in IL36RN-mutated ACH,9 while case studies indicate that spesolimab (an IL-36R monoclonal antibody) may enhance outcomes.10,11 Importantly, real-world evidence supporting spesolimab use in IL36RN-mutated ACH remains limited, particularly with respect to optimal dosing. The 900-mg intravenous regimen for spesolimab in GPP flares was based on approval data and the pivotal GPP flare trial.12,13 Because isolated ACH is confined to the digits and nail apparatus, whether GPP-based dosing can be directly extrapolated to isolated ACH requires further evaluation. This provided the basis for evaluating individualized dosing, including 450 mg in patients with isolated ACH. We characterized genotype–phenotype relationships in 14 patients with ACH and evaluated clinical outcomes of individualized spesolimab dosing in patients with biallelic IL36RN variants.

Methods

This study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (approval number: ZZZ025-428-01) and the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of West China Hospital, Sichuan University (approval number: 2024(1473)). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design and use of de-identified data. The study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500105267). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study Cohort

This retrospective multicenter cohort study included 14 patients with ACH from the Department of Dermatology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University and West China Hospital of Sichuan University, conducted between January 2019 and June 2025. This extended ascertainment period reflects the rarity of ACH and the limited number of patients with available IL36RN genotyping data. The diagnosis was identified using the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) code (L40.2), verified and clinically supported by at least four senior dermatologists. All participants underwent whole-genome sequencing (WGS), verified by Sanger sequencing. Variants were interpreted according to ACMG guidelines, and patients were categorized as wild-type (-/-) or carriers of monoallelic (+/-) or biallelic (+/+) IL36RN variants. Treatment-refractory ACH was defined as failure of at least three different physician-directed systemic treatment regimens, such as acitretin, methotrexate, cyclosporine, biologics, or JAK inhibitors. Treatment failure was referred to persistent disease, lack of efficacy, adverse events, or disease worsening leading to regimen change or discontinuation. These determinations were based on documented reasons for treatment discontinuation or regimen change in the available medical records or treatment histories.

Data Extraction

We extracted electronic medical records for all patients with a confirmed diagnosis of ACH. Clinical data were collected using a standardized case report form capturing demographics, disease manifestations assessed by Generalized Pustular Psoriasis Physician Global Assessment (GPPGA), the modified Nail Psoriasis Severity Index (mNPASI) and the Psoriatic Symptom Scale (PSS), laboratory parameters, and treatment histories, patient-reported outcomes which were assessed by patients’ questionnaires including the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI), Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy Fatigue scale (FACIT-Fatigue), Pain VAS, Itch NRS and EQ-5D-5L and IL36RN genotype profiles derived from Sanger sequencing.13–22 Serum biomarkers were quantified via a multiplex cytokine array. For the six patients with biallelic IL36RN mutations who received spesolimab, key treatment details and clinical outcomes were extracted and independently verified by four investigators. According to medical records, spesolimab was given as a single intravenous infusion, with 450 mg used in patients with isolated ACH and 900 mg used in patients with extra-acral involvement, including concomitant GPP or palmoplantar pustulosis (PPP). In the four patients who relapsed and were re-treated, disease involvement at relapse was consistent with that at initial treatment, and the same dose was recorded at re-treatment.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic and clinical characteristics. Categorical variables were compared using Chi-square (χ2) test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables exhibiting normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean±SD) and analyzed with T-tests or one-way ANOVA as appropriate. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Correlations between physician-reported and patient-reported outcomes were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients. Longitudinal changes in clinical scores before and after spesolimab were analyzed using Friedman tests. When Friedman tests showed significant overall differences, post hoc pairwise comparisons with baseline were performed using Dunn’s multiple comparisons test. Percentage improvement from baseline was calculated for each available disease activity and patient-reported measure (total mNPASI, GPPGA, PSS, DLQI, pain VAS, itch NRS, FACIT-F, and EQ-5D-5L) at each time point for each patient, after aligning scale direction so that positive values indicated improvement. For each measure and time point, percentage improvement was summarized across patients using the mean or median according to distribution. Overall percentage improvement at each time point was then summarized across measures using the mean or median according to distribution. This summary measure was used descriptively and was not intended as a validated weighted composite scale. Given the small sample size, all statistical analyses were considered exploratory. A two-tailed P <0.05 was considered statistically significant, with P values interpreted cautiously. All analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 10.5.0).

Results

Patient Characteristics by IL36RN Genotype

The retrospective cohort analysis comprised 14 patients diagnosed with ACH stratified by IL36RN genotype: IL36RN (-/-) (n=4), IL36RN (+/-) (n=3), and IL36RN (+/+) (n=7) (Table 1). Carriers of biallelic IL36RN mutations demonstrated a markedly earlier disease onset with a mean (SD) of 23 (9) years, compared to 47 (14) years in IL36RN (-/-) group and 48 (16) years in IL36RN (+/-) group (overall P <0.05), as well as an extended disease duration (median [IQR], 13 [11–31] years vs 4 [2–5] years vs 13 [5–15] years, P <0.01). All biallelic patients (7 of 7 [100%]) had received 6 or more prior systemic therapies compared with 2 of 4 patients (50%) in the wild-type group and no patients in the monoallelic group (overall P <0.01). Disease severity was generally greater in the biallelic group across physician-assessed measures and patient-reported outcomes (Table 1). As representative examples, biallelic patients had higher GPPGA scores (median [IQR], 4 [4–4] vs 3 [3–4] vs 2 [1–3]; P <0.001) and worse quality-of-life impairment (mean [SD] DLQI, 22 [3] vs 16 [2] vs 6 [3]; P <0.01). Osteolysis was numerically observed only in the biallelic group (4/7 [57%] vs 0 in the other groups), although this difference did not reach statistical significance (P =0.10). In contrast, all monoallelic individuals exhibited acral-only involvement during the disease course and had milder illness, shown by a reduced number of affected fingers, lower disease severity scores and superior quality-of-life scores. Correlation Between Disease Severity Measures and Patient-Reported Outcomes

The correlation heatmap demonstrated clear patterns in the interrelationships between illness severity and patient-reported outcomes (Figure 1). The mNPASI of the most severely impacted nail exhibited robust, substantial positive associations with the GPPGA score (r=0.94, P <0.001), DLQI score (r=0.94, P <0.001), and PSS score (r=0.94, P <0.001). In contrast, Total mNPASI demonstrated significantly smaller and non-significant associations with these parameters (all r≤0.53, P >0.05). Different IL36RN genotypes showed distinct treatment response patterns across the various treatments evaluated (Table 1). IL36RN (+/+) patients exhibited poor responses to most therapies, yet all six who were treated with spesolimab achieved marked or very satisfactory improvement. IL36RN (+/-) patients (n=3) exhibited moderate illness effectively managed by topical therapy: 2 of 3 patients (66.7%) used topical medications, with all treated individuals achieving partial response/general satisfaction. Small-molecule agents and biologics were infrequently used (0–33.3% usage), owing to their milder disease. IL36RN (-/-) patients (n=4) exhibited more severe disease than IL36RN (+/-) but responded well to non-spesolimab systemics, particularly secukinumab: All four patients were administered secukinumab and attained a “marked response/very satisfied” rating. Upadacitinib (used in 75% of patients) and adalimumab (used in 50%) also yielded favorable partial or marked responses.

Laboratory Inflammatory Markers

Laboratory biomarker profiling showed no statistically significant differences in circulating IL-36α, IL-36γ, IL-36Ra, IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-17A/F, IL-23, or any other assessed inflammatory mediators between 8 ACH patients and 6 healthy controls (Table S1). Similarly, no statistically significant alterations were observed when biallelic ACH patients evaluated before spesolimab administration (n=5) and after treatment (n=3) were compared with healthy controls for any inflammatory marker. Post-hoc within-patient analyses in the biallelic subgroup (pre- vs post-treatment) also did not show statistically significant changes in inflammatory biomarker levels. Given the small biomarker subgroup, these analyses were exploratory, underpowered, and cannot fully exclude the possibility of systemic inflammation. Larger studies are needed to clarify systemic inflammatory profiles in ACH.

Clinical Outcomes After Spesolimab Treatment in IL36RN-Biallelic ACH

Based on retrospective review of serial assessments, spesolimab treatment produced rapid and sustained clinical enhancements in IL36RN-biallelic ACH (Figure 2). Across the available visits up to 24 weeks, overall percentage improvement across disease activity and patient-reported measures showed a marked early increase, with a mean improvement of 54% (SD, 24%) at week 1 and further improvement to 74% (SD, 25%) at week 4. Consistently, the overall summary of available clinical and patient-reported scores decreased from 11 (range, 6–30) at baseline to 6 (2–14) at week 1 and to 3 (1–11) at week 4, both significantly lower than baseline (both P <0.001). Then the disease activity continued to improve more gradually, with most metrics maintaining substantial clinical benefit through week 24 (Figure S1). Overall differences across time points were significant by Friedman testing (P <0.0001). Clinical Characteristics at Disease Relapse

Four patients (P1–P4) experienced disease relapse during the retrospective observation period. P2 and P3 relapsed at weeks 31 and 44 following upper respiratory infections, whereas P1 and P4 relapsed at weeks 36 and 40 without identifiable triggers (Figure 3). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests showed markedly lower disease activity at relapse compared with baseline—for instance in P2 (Figure S1), disease activity scores were lower at relapse than at baseline (median [range], 7 [4–21] vs 13 [8–44]); similar within-patient differences were observed in the remaining patients (all P <0.01). Despite variation in triggers, relapse severity remained substantially attenuated relative to the pre-treatment baseline. Clinical Outcomes After Spesolimab Re-Treatment

Re-treatment with spesolimab (n=4) again elicited rapid and sustained clinical improvements (Figure 2). Within the recorded follow-up visits over the 8-week re-treatment period, overall percentage improvement across disease activity and patient-reported measures again showed a rapid early increase with a mean improvement of 58% (SD, 22%) at week 1 (Figure S1) and further improvement to 79% (SD, 19%) at week 4. By week 8, the majority of metrics exhibited near-complete responses (Figure S1). Overall differences across time points remained significant by Friedman testing (P <0.0001).

GPPGA subscores reflected this outcome (Figure 2): pustules decreased rapidly by week 1 and reached 0 by week 4 in all retreated patients, although erythema and scale exhibited gradual reductions until week 8. The alterations were aligned with the response pattern observed after the initial spesolimab treatment, with swift elimination of pustules and the progressive subsidence of erythema and scaling.

Adverse Events

No treatment-emergent adverse events were reported during the whole recorded period.

Discussion

In this cohort of 14 patients with ACH, we described the genotype–phenotype architecture of IL36RN variants and provided detailed real-world evaluations to date of their treatment response to spesolimab. Consistent with prior observations in GPP,8,23–25 IL36RN biallelism in ACH was associated with earlier disease onset, greater pustular burden, higher symptom severity, and more frequent treatment refractoriness. These findings support the concept that complete loss of IL-36 receptor antagonist activity produces a severe, autoinflammatory phenotype at acral sites, where inflammation may persist despite conventional psoriasis therapies.

Importantly, this study offers real-world evidence that IL-36 receptor blockade provides rapid and clinically meaningful improvement in patients with biallelic IL36RN variants. Existing dose–response data for spesolimab are largely derived from GPP populations,26,27 and ACH-specific dosing considerations remain underexplored. In GPP, subgroup analyses have reported comparable efficacy of spesolimab in patients with and without IL36RN variants, suggesting that response to IL-36 receptor blockade is not restricted to patients carrying IL36RN variants.23 However, whether a similar genotype-independent response occurs in ACH remains to be clarified. Furthermore, among the six severely treatment-refractory individuals with biallelic IL36RN variants in our ACH cohort, spesolimab induced consistent and marked clinical benefit. Patients with isolated ACH achieved comparable improvement with a 450-mg intravenous dose, whereas those with concomitant GPP/PPP received 900-mg. A lower dose in isolated ACH may be biologically plausible because inflammation is confined to distal digits and the nail apparatus, with a smaller surface area involved and a likely lower inflammatory burden than widespread GPP flares. This difference in lesion distribution and inflammatory burden provides a rationale for evaluating dose individualization rather than directly extrapolating GPP flare dosing to isolated ACH. Re-treatment at relapse reproduced the initial therapeutic effect, supporting continued involvement of IL-36 signaling during recurrent disease activity.

Preliminary review of relapse cases did not show a genotype-specific pattern. Three relapsing patients carried the recurrent splice-site variant c.115+6T>C alone. As a splice-site variant, c.115+6T>C may impair IL36RN transcript processing and IL-36 receptor antagonist function,28 and its predominance in this cohort limits comparisons across different functional variant classes. Because the same splice-site variant appeared in both relapsing and non-relapsing patients, the current data do not support an association between specific IL36RN variants and relapse risk. One relapsing patient had a double-homozygous genotype (c.115+6T>C/c.227C>T), but the clinical implications of this configuration remain unclear. Future studies integrating genotyping with functional analyses may help determine whether residual IL-36 receptor antagonist activity influences relapse risk or treatment response. Two relapses occurred following upper respiratory infections, raising the possibility that pattern recognition receptor signaling and innate immune activation may contribute to reactivation of IL-36–mediated inflammation. The other two relapses had no identifiable trigger, and the small sample size limits inference regarding pathways related to relapse triggers. Larger cohorts with systematic follow-up are needed to clarify whether IL36RN variation, environmental triggers, or their interaction contributes to relapse susceptibility.

A notable clinical observation was that assessing only the most severely affected nail correlated strongly with global physician- and patient-reported outcomes. The weaker association of total mNPASI may reflect dilution by minimally affected or unaffected nails and potential floor or ceiling effects across multiple nail units, whereas the most severely affected nail may better capture the dominant symptomatic lesion. This supports the feasibility of a simplified, lesion-centric evaluation method for ACH, a disease in which conventional whole-hand scoring systems may be cumbersome. Serum inflammatory markers did not show statistically significant differences between ACH patients and healthy controls. This finding should be interpreted cautiously, given the small biomarker subgroup and in the context of prior tissue-based studies showing marked upregulation of IL-36 related cytokines within ACH lesions.29–31 These observations suggest that circulating biomarkers may have limited sensitivity for monitoring localized acral inflammation, although systemic inflammatory differences cannot be excluded.

The systematic extraction and independent review by four investigators of all treatment and outcome data enhance the internal validity of this study, and no unexpected safety concerns emerged, consistent with the known tolerability profile of IL-36 pathway inhibition. Collectively, these findings support spesolimab as a rational precision-therapy option for IL36RN-mediated ACH, particularly in biallelic loss-of-function variants that respond poorly to traditional anti-psoriatic or anti-inflammatory agents.

This study has several limitations. The sample size remains small due to disease rarity and the retrospective design limits causal inference, although this represents one of the largest genetically and clinically characterized ACH cohorts. The absence of a comparator biologic precludes conclusions about relative therapeutic efficacy. Long-term durability and safety beyond the 56-week observation period also could not be assessed. In addition, the disease severity assessments used in this study were originally developed or validated for GPP, nail psoriasis, or general patient-reported outcome assessment rather than specifically for ACH, which may affect how precisely they capture disease activity in ACH. Because all identified variants involved a single locus, more detailed locus-specific genotype–phenotype relationships could not be explored. ACH is biologically heterogeneous, and interactions among IL36RN variation, immune circuitry, and environmental factors could not be fully explored.

Despite the limitations, this study provides the first real-world evidence that a 450-mg dose of spesolimab, differing from the 900-mg regimen approved for GPP, can induce rapid and durable remission in patients with treatment-refractory ACH carrying biallelic IL36RN mutations. A further strength of this study is its particularly focused evaluation of the IL36RN genotype–phenotype association in a Chinese ACH cohort, with detailed clinical characterizations. These findings extend the therapeutic paradigm established in GPP to acral pustular disease and support a genotype- and lesion-distribution–based dosing strategy. Together, they highlight the potential of IL-36 pathway blockade as a precision-medicine approach for rare neutrophilic dermatoses.

Conclusion

This study provides real-world evidence supporting a lower, genotype-guided spesolimab regimen for isolated ACH, distinct from the standard dosing used for GPP. This approach induced rapid and durable remission in IL36RN-biallelic patients while reducing treatment burden. Relapses were substantially attenuated, suggesting a potential disease-modifying effect of IL-36 blockade. However, prospective controlled studies are warranted to further confirm the efficacy and safety of the 450-mg dosing strategy in isolated ACH before broader clinical adoption. The findings also reinforce the strong association between biallelic IL36RN and severe ACH and validate the single-nail mNPASI as a streamlined and reliable severity metric.

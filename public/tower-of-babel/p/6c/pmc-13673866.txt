Prenatal diagnosis and pregnancy outcome of fetuses with 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion syndrome: a single-center retrospective study of 34 cases
Authors: Yiming Chen, Yanzhen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the clinical phenotype, genetic counseling and pregnancy outcome of fetus with 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion syndrome (Burnside-Butler syndrome, BBS), and to provide scientific prenatal diagnosis and genetic counseling for parents of BBS fetuses.MethodsA single-center retrospective analysis method was used to analyze the data from September 2017 to April 2025. Data of 34 pregnant women with BBS fetuses who met the indications for prenatal diagnosis and underwent karyotype analysis of amniotic fluid, cord blood, and peripheral blood and single-nucleotide polymorphism array (SNP-array) for chromosomal copy number variations (CNVs) were collected. The CNVs detected were judged according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) classification of genetic variants.ResultsA total of 34 BBS fetuses were detected in 9689 cases of SNP array, and the detection rate was 0.35% (34/9689). The median size of 15q11.2 microdeletion was 507.0 (490.6~512.3) kb. Among the 34 cases of BBS, the proportion of prenatal ultrasound abnormalities (55.6% vs. 52.0%), abnormal results of Down's syndrome screening or non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) screening, (44.4% vs. 20.0%) and advanced maternal age (≥ 35 years; 55.6% vs. 44.0%), were all higher in the pathogenic (P) group compared to the variant of uncertain significance (VUS) group. Among the 34 BBS fetuses, 25 were classified as having ACMG variants of unknown clinical significance and 9 were classified as pathogenic. Among the 9 cases of BBS, parental traceability test showed that 1 case was paternal origin, 3 cases were maternal origin and 5 cases were not verified. The parentage tracing test showed that the genetic inheritance from the maternal source was higher than that from the paternal source (33.3% vs. 11.1%). Among the 27 follow-up cases, the 88.89% (24/27) were inherited from parents, while de novo cases were relatively few 11.11% (3/27).ConclusionThe detection rate of BBS fetus in this study was 0.35%. The fetal manifestations of BBS are diverse, and SNP array is of great value in the detection of fetal chromosomal microdeletions including 15q11.2. Among the BBS cases, the majority were inherited from parents, while de novo cases were relatively few. Pregnancies involving BBS fetuses require adequate prenatal counseling. For women who choose to continue their pregnancies, increased supervision and extended postpartum monitoring are warranted.

Background

The 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion, also known as Burnside-Butler syndrome (BBS) or 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion syndrome, is an emerging disease involving the NIPA1, NIPA2, CYFIP1 and TUBGCP5 genes (Fig. 1). The cardinal disease associations for each of the four contiguous 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 genes are NIPA1- Spastic Paraplegia 6; NIPA2-Angelman Syndrome (AS, The clinical manifestations include developmental delay, intellectual disability, gait ataxia, microcephaly, epilepsy, abnormal facial features, etc.) and Prader-Willi Syndrome (PWS, The clinical manifestations include decreased muscle tone, obesity, intellectual disability, delayed gonadal development, and short stature, etc.); CYFIP1-Fragile X Syndrome and Autism; TUBGCP5-Prader-Willi Syndrome [1]. genomic region, which includes the NIPA1 and NIPA2 genes, contains a recurrent but rare chromosomal copy number variation (CNVs) flanked by breakpoints BP1 and BP2. The 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 region is approximately 312~855 kb in size. The prevalence of 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 patients who underwent microarray analysis was 0.57%~1.27% [2].

Both 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletions and microduplications are associated with neurodevelopmental, motor, learning, and behavioral problems, including epilepsy, hyperactivity (ADHD), obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), and autism, as well as other clinical manifestations thought to be Burnside-Butler syndrome. Genes in the 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 region may cause more clinical involvement and comorbidities in patients with PWS and type I deletions [3]. Han et al. reported that all 3 cases presented with global developmental delay (DD) and/or intellectual disability (ID), ranging in severity from mild to severe. However, the common facial deformities and congenital malformations reported in previous reports are not characteristic [4]. Most fetuses with BBS showed typical features in early childhood, with low penetrance and minimal effects [5]. Carrying 15q11.2 deletion does not have a great effect on neurodevelopmental problems (NDP) in children [6], but the pathogenic role of this CNV is still unclear. Unknown variants in other DNA regions and parent-of-origin effect (POE) are some of the mechanisms that have been proposed as explanations for the wide range of phenotypic variability [7]. Vanlerberghe et al. showed that BBS was associated with developmental delay, behavioral abnormalities, generalized epilepsy and congenital heart disease [8].

Jonch et al. suggested that the 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 deletion should be classified as "pathogenic of mild effect size". Because it explains only a small fraction of the phenotypic variation in carriers, it is not worth discussing in developmental clinics or prenatal Settings [9]. The 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 deletion and duplication are less pathogenic, and the option of not reporting these copy number variants to clinicians and couples should be considered [10]. However, Cai et al. showed a high proportion of abnormal fetal phenotypes in BBS, but the penetrance specificity was low. Thus, fetuses with this syndrome may be at higher risk for postnatal growth/behavioral problems and require continued monitoring of growth and development [11]. Despite altered brain morphology in 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 CNV carriers, the results did not show any significant signs of aging alterations in brain structure, movement, lung, or cardiac function. The results did not indicate a neurodegenerative effect in 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 CNV carriers [12]. The results of Meossi et al. did not support that urinary Mg2+ levels can be used as a biomarker to screen for 15q11.2 duplication/deletion in children with neurodevelopmental disorders [13]. However, studies had shown that parents can improve the behavior and clinical performance of children with BBS by taking magnesium supplements [14].

To elucidate the abnormal phenotypic expression, penetrance, and underlying pathogenic mechanisms in fetuses with BBS, we conducted a retrospective analysis of 34 prenatally diagnosed BBS cases. Data included indications for prenatal genetic testing, detailed fetal ultrasound findings, parental carrier status or segregation analysis, pregnancy outcomes, and postnatal clinical and genetic follow-up results. This study aims to strengthen the evidence base for accurate prenatal diagnosis and informed genetic counseling.

Subjects and methods

Subjects

1.1.1 From September 2017 to April 2025, a total of 9,689 cases of prenatal diagnosis were conducted at the Department of Prenatal Diagnosis and Screening Center of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital (Hangzhou Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital), where amniotic fluid or umbilical cord blood karyotype analysis and chromosome SNP array technology were used to detect copy number variation sequencing (CNVs). Among them, 34 fetuses were diagnosed with 15q11.2 microdeletion. Both husband and wife had normal phenotypes and denied the history of consanguineous marriage and family genetic history. All examinations were performed with the informed consent of the pregnant women and their families.

Ethics approval

This study passed the review of the Medical Ethics Committee of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital [2024] No. (A143). In accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of the 34 patients with 15q11.2 microdeletion deficiency involved in this retrospective study. Data from the medical records were anonymized and maintained confidential. All patients visited our center between September 2017 to April 2025. All the information analyzed in this study was retrieved from the hospital’s archived database. The investigations conducted had no impact on the patients involved.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: serological Down‘s syndrome screening for high risk (trisomy 21: ≥1/270, trisomy 18: ≥1/350) or critical risk (trisomy 21: 1/1000~1/270, trisomy 18: 1/1000~1/350) or the single biomarker PAPP-A, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and free beta human chorionic gonadotropin (free β-hCG) with an abnormal multiple of median (MoM) value (increased or decreased); abnormal ultrasound soft indicators (such as a thickened nuchal translucency, a widened lateral ventricle, choroid plexus cyst, and ventricular bright spot); an advanced maternal age (AMA, maternal age ≥ 35 years).

Exclusion Criteria: Severe obesity [body mass index (BMI) > 40 kg/m2]; patients with a history of tumors, transplantation surgery and allogeneic blood transfusion within one year; chromosomal abnormalities other than CNVs.

Instruments and reagents

NP968-S automatic nucleic acid extraction instrument (Tianlong, Xi’an, China), Veriti DNA Thermocycler (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, USA), Stepone Plus fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument (Applied Biosystems, Carlsbad, USA), GSLG120 automatic karyotype scanning analysis system (Leica Company, Wetzlar, Germany), 750 K gene chip kit (Affymetrix, Carlsbad, USA), Gene chip hybridization apparatus (Affymetrix, Carlsbad, USA), Gene chip washing workstation (Affymetrix, Carlsbad, USA)‌, Chip Scanner (Affymetrix, Carlsbad, USA).

Genetic testing

Amniotic fluid karyotype analysis

10 ml amniotic fluid was collected by amniocentesis, centrifuged at 2 000 r/min and inoculated into amniotic fluid medium for routine culture. After cell proliferation, according to the growth of each sample of amniotic fluid cell clone, the steps of harvesting, preparation, digestion and staining were carried out, and then the automatic Leica scanner was used to scan each sample. 20 mitotic phases were counted for each sample, 5 karyotypes were analyzed, and 50 to 100 mitotic phases were counted for chimeric samples. The karyotype was named according to the International System for Human Cytogenetic Nomenclature (ISCN-2020).

SNP array detection and bioinformatics analysis

CMA employed single-nucleotide polymorphism array (SNP-array). Additionally, 10 ml of amniotic fluid or umbilical cord blood samples were extracted. For parental verification, 2 ml of EDTA-anticoagulated peripheral blood was drawn from the elbow vein. The SNP array protocol followed steps- genomic DNA preparation, restriction enzyme digestion, ligation, PCR，PCR product purification, fragmentation, labelling, hybridization, washing, staining and scanning the gene chip. The ChAS software provided with the kit was used to analyze the scanned results in CEL files. The SNP chip results were analyzed in combination with relevant public databases (ClinGen, Decipher, ClinVar, OMIM, DGV, ISCA, NCBI, UCSC). The nature of CNVs was determined according to the ACMG (American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics) standards for genetic variant classification. CNVs were divided into five categories: pathogenic (P) copy number variation, Likely pathogenic (LP) copy number variation, copy number variation of Uncertain significance (VUS), Likely benign (LB) copy number variation and benign (B) copy number variation.

Pregnancy outcomes and follow-up

A total of 34 confirmed pregnant women were given adequate genetic counseling in the genetic counseling clinic based on prenatal ultrasound examination, genetic testing and parental traceability results, and then the couple decided whether to continue the pregnancy. The health status of the newborns was followed up regularly by telephone, The health status of newborns was monitored by again at 3 months, and the contents of the follow-up mainly included pregnancy outcomes and the height, weight, head circumference, motor, mental and cognitive development of the offspring. The last follow-up ended in June 2025.

Statistical analysis

IBM-SPSS statisties 25 statistical software (IBM-SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). was used to analyze the data. Shapiro-Wilk test was used to test the normality of data, and skewed data were expressed as median M (IQR). The Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparison between the two groups. Chi-square test was used to compare the differences of maternal characteristics in different years, maternal age, body mass index, gravidity and parity with the incidence of CNVs in offspring. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Fetal 15q11.2 microdeletion prenatal diagnosis

A total of 34 BBS fetuses were detected in 9689 cases of SNP array, and the detection rate was 0.35% (34/9689). The median size of 15q11.2 microdeletion was 507.0 (490.6~512.3) kb. Among the 34 cases of BBS, the proportion of prenatal ultrasound abnormalities (55.6% vs. 52.0%), abnormal results of Down syndrome screening or NIPT screening, (44.4% vs. 20.0%) and advanced maternal age (≥ 35 years; 55.6% vs. 44.0%), were all higher in the pathogenic (P) group compared to the variant of uncertain significance (VUS) group. In contrast, a history of first-trimester fetal structural abnormalities was slightly less frequent in the P group (11.1% vs. 12.0%), though this difference did not reach statistical significance (all P > 0.05, see Table 1). Among the 9 cases of BBS, parental traceability test showed that 1 case was paternal origin, 3 cases were maternal origin and 5 cases were not verified. Among the 9 BBS cases in the P group, 5 cases were not verified. The parentage tracing test showed that the genetic inheritance from the maternal source was higher than that from the paternal source (33.3% vs. 11.1%, See Table 3). Among the 34 BBS fetuses, 11 cases (11/34, 32.35%) were 46, XX, 21 cases (23/34, 67.65%) were 46, XY. Among the 34 cases of BBS, the male-to-female sex ratio in the P group (44.4% male, 55.6% female) was significantly lower than that in the VUS group (76.0% male, 24.0% female), See Table 2.

Fetal 15q11.2 microdeletion parental traceability results

The proportions of maternal origin, paternal origin, and de novo origin among individuals with the 15q11.2 microdeletion in the P group were significantly lower than those observed in the VUS group (33.3% vs. 40.0%, 11.1% vs. 40.0%, and 0% vs. 12.0%, respectively; all P < 0.05). Furthermore, the odds of pathogenicity associated with 15q11.2 microdeletion in the P group were approximately twofold higher relative to the VUS group. Of the 34 individuals identified with the 15q11.2 microdeletion, follow-up was completed for 27 (79.4%) and lost to follow-up for 7 (20.6%). Among the 27 cases with completed follow-up, 24 (88.9%) represented inherited variants from a parent, while 3 (11.1%) were confirmed de novo occurrences.

Pregnancy outcomes and follow-up

Regular telephone follow-up was performed after the expected date of delivery, and the gestational age of delivery was 39 (38~40) weeks. Among the 34 BBS cases, 3 pregnancies were terminated, 31 were born (1 was suspicious on hearing screening; the remaining 23 were normal), and 7 were lost to follow-up. The length of the newborn was 50 (50–50) cm, the birth weight was 3375 (3000~3713) g, the Apgar1, Apgar5 and Apgar10 were all 10 (10~10), 23 boys and 11 girls, See Tables 2 and 4.

Discussion

BBS is becoming one of the most common cytogenetic abnormalities in individuals with intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and other related behavioral or clinical manifestations, but more research is needed [15]. More than half of BBS cases were found to have no abnormality on prenatal ultrasound. The cases with ultrasound manifestations mainly showed isolated malformations such as increased nuchal translucency, congenital heart disease, and abnormal brain structure. Postpartum BBS patients had an increased risk of schizophrenia, epilepsy, and other neurological and psychiatric disorders caused by 15q11.2 microdeletion [16]. The detection rate of BBS fetus in this study was 0.35%. The smallest fragment of 15q11.2 microdeletion was 311.8 kb, the largest was 855.3 kb, and the median size was 507.0 (490.6~512.3) kb. Prenatal ultrasound abnormalities, mainly including NT thickening, fetal neck hygroma, lateral ventricle widening, choroid plexus cyst and other ultrasound abnormalities. Among the 9 BBS cases in the P group, 5 cases were not verified. The parentage tracing test showed that the genetic inheritance from the maternal source was higher than that from the paternal source (33.3% vs. 11.1%).

In this study, 34 BBS fetuses were found from 9689 cases by chromosomal microarray analysis, with a detection rate of 0.35%, which was slightly lower than 0.40% reported by Cai et al. [11], and lower than 0.70% reported by Chu F et al. [17] and 0.57%~1.27% reported by Cox et al. [2]. But it is higher than 0.28% reported by Zhuang [5]. The different detection rate of BBS fetus in different regions may be related to the incidence of BBS in different regions and different data collection.

The distribution of the 15q11.2 microdeletion was approximately 310 kb to 855 kb, such as Cox reported 312~855 kb of 15q11.2 microdeletion [2], Zhuang et al. reported 311.8 kb to 849.7 kb [5], Cui Yu et al. reported that the range of 15q11.2 microdeletion was 311.8~855.3 kb [18]. Cai et al. reported that the fragment size of 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 region was about 312–855 kb [11]. The results of this study showed that the minimum size of 15q11.2 microdeletion in 34 cases was 311.8 kb and the maximum size was 855.3 kb. Due to the skewed number distribution of 15q11.2 microdeletion data, the median size of BBS ranged from 507.0 (490.6~512.3) kb. The distribution of 15q11.2 microdeletion fragment in this study was different from that in other reports, but the distribution range of 15q11.2 microdeletion fragment was similar.

Compared with the normal array group, the 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion group had more cases of neonatal intensive care unit transfer, an Apgar score < 7 at 1 min, and neonatal death [17]. Li et al. followed up 22 live-born neonates to a median age of 1 year and 8 months (0.5 months-4 years and 3 months), and found 6 cases of low birth weight and/or growth retardation, 1 case of low birth weight combined with language retardation, 1 case of low birth weight and growth retardation combined with hyperactivity behavior, and 1 case of language retardation combined with left ear appendage. 2 cases of cleft palate with duodenal atresia and cleft lip with alveolar cleft and cleft palate showed no obvious developmental abnormalities after surgical treatment, respectively. The remaining 11 cases showed no obvious abnormalities in growth and development [18]. However, this study showed that Apgar1, Apgar5 and Apgar10 were all 10 (10–10), which was basically consistent with the results of Li et al., who followed up the offspring at the youngest age of 2 months to the oldest age of 5 years, with no obvious abnormalities in growth and development [19].

This study showed prenatal ultrasound abnormalities, mainly including NT thickening, fetal neck hygroma, lateral ventricle widening, choroid plexus cyst and other ultrasound abnormalities. Cases with ultrasound features mainly show isolated malformations, such as translucency of the neck, congenital heart disease and structural abnormalities of the brain [16]. Cai et al. found that 25 out of 37 fetuses with this microdeletion showed phenotypic abnormalities, and the most common ultrasonic structural abnormality was congenital heart disease, followed by renal dysplasia and Dany-Walker malformation [11]. Li et al. also found that 3 of the 20 fetuses had ultrasound abnormalities, including 1 case of renal pelvis separation, 1 case of corpus callosum agenesis, and 1 case of cervical fold thickening [19]. The main indications for prenatal diagnosis of 27 cases studied by Li Meijuan et al. were 14 cases of prenatal ultrasound abnormalities (8 cases of thickened nuchal translucidum, 2 cases of cleft lip with cleft palate/cleft alveolar process, 1 case of fetal polyarticular contracture syndrome, 1 case of fetal right diaphragmatic hernia + single umbilical artery, 1 case of fetal duodenal atresia and 1 case of fetal nasal bone absence) [18].

This study showed that among the 9 BBS cases in the P group, 5 cases were not verified. parental tracing detection showed that 1 case was paternal origin, 3 cases were maternal origin, The parentage tracing test showed that the genetic inheritance from the maternal source was higher than that from the paternal source (33.3% vs. 11.1%). Among the 34 cases of 15q11.2 microdeletion, 3 cases terminated their pregnancies. The 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion was inherited from the father in 6 cases, from the mother in 10 cases, and de novo inherited in 4 cases. During the follow-up period, 16.1% of the patients had postnatal abnormalities [11]. Parental tracing of 10 cases suggested that 2 cases were de novo variants and 8 cases were inherited from their parents with normal phenotype. After genetic counseling, 3 couples chose to terminate pregnancy [19]. Parental verification was performed in 23 cases, and 1 case was confirmed as de novo mutation, 7 cases were inherited from the father, 15 cases were inherited from the mother, 5 cases were terminated in the second trimester, and the remaining 22 cases were born alive [18]. However, Zhang et al. found that the source of 15q11.2 microdeletion was mainly from the father, accounting for 66.7% (18/27) of all microdeletions, and a small part of the source was from the mother, accounting for 33.3% (9/27), and no new mutation was detected [20]. Results from Davis et al. support this missing POE, including macrocephaly in maternal deletion with congenital heart disease, epilepsy and autism spectrum disorder (ASD), and abnormal muscle phenotypes in paternal deletion [21].

Limitations of the study

A relatively low parental acceptance rate of traceability testing resulted in a limited number of eligible parent–child dyads enrolled in this study. Secondly, some pregnant women do not take their children to the child health care department of our hospital regularly after delivery, and the telephone follow-up cannot ensure the true validity of the content. In the future, we will carry out integrated prenatal and postnatal follow-up management to provide more accurate genetic counseling for related families.

In summary, the median size of 15q11.2 microdeletion was 507.0 (490.6~512.3) kb, and the detection rate was 0.35%. Prenatal ultrasound abnormalities, including NT thickening, fetal neck hygroma, lateral ventricle enlargement and choroid plexus cyst, were common but with low specificity. The potential risk of evelopmental/behavioral problems after birth is high, and continuous monitoring of growth and development is needed. Therefore, pregnant women should receive prenatal diagnosis and genetic counseling during pregnancy to assess the risk of fetal BBS.

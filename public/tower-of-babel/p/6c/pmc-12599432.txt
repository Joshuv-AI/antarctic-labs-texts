Virus-like Particle Vaccines Containing RSV Fusion Protein Cleavage Mutations Are Immunogenic and Protective in Cotton Rats With a Prime Dose
Authors: Hye Suk Hwang, Young-Man Kwon, Ki-Hye Kim, Young-Tae Lee, Eun-Ju Ko, Arun Meas, Sang-Moo Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn effective respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine should elicit neutralizing Abs and confer protection without causing vaccine-enhanced disease. Virus-like particle (VLP) is a safe vaccine platform that can display multivalent Ags similar to a virus and induce potent neutralizing Abs without adjuvant. We investigated the impact of mutations in the furin cleavage site, fusion (F) peptide, and change in the transmembrane (TM) domain of RSV F protein in VLPs on inducing RSV neutralizing Abs and efficacy in a cotton rat model. Palivizumab-binding antigenic site II epitope was preferentially exposed by combination mutations in the furin cleavage sites and F peptide together with a change in the TM domain. A single dose of mutant F protein VLP containing these combination mutations effectively induced IgG Ab responses to post-F and pre-F proteins, neutralizing activities, and protection without apparent lung histopathology. The VLP also induced the highest level of INF-γ, indicating the Th1-skewed immune response against the virus infection. A single dose of RSV F mutant exposing an antigenic site II on VLP vaccine could induce RSV-neutralizing Abs, conferring protection without causing vaccine-enhanced disease in cotton rats.Keywords: Respiratory syncytial virus, RSV prefusion F, Virus-like particle, Single-dose vaccine, Cotton rats

INTRODUCTION

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) has been threatening health in the young children and elderly immunocompromised population for over half a century as a cause of acute lower respiratory infection. RSV-related deaths are most common in children under the age of 5 and account for 45% of such deaths, with the majority of cases (over 90%) occurring in developing countries (1). The pssive prophylactic mAbs, palivizumab (Synagis®; Swedish Orphan Biovitrum AB, Basel, Switzerland), is approved for use in a subset of preterm infants who are at the highest risk of developing severe RSV-induced lung disease (2). Recently Nirsevimab (Sanofi, Paris, France) mAb targeting a pre-fusion (F) epitope was approved as a new drug for prophylactic treatments for infants (3). Recently, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved pre-F protein RSV vaccines (Pfizer’s ABRYSVO™, GSK’s AS01E adjuvanted pre-F protein) for use in older adults (≥60 years of age) (4). The FDA has also approved the first RSV pre-F protein vaccine that can be administered during pregnancy to protect newborn babies (5). Nonetheless, licensed vaccines are not available for newborns and young infants against RSV (6).

Upon attachment of the RSV virion to the host cell and during subsequent F, native RSV pre-F glycoprotein undergoes considerable rearrangements to transform pre-F into a stable post-F F protein. To achieve the functional pre-F conformation, the precursor form (F0) undergoes cleavage by cellular furin protease at Arg109 and Arg136, leading to the formation of three distinct fragments: a shorter F2, a longer F1, and an intervening segment consisting of 27 amino acids. During furin cleavage and conformational transition, the monomers of these fragments come together to form a compact trimer (7). The exact sequence of events for the activation of pre-F glycoprotein, including proteolysis and trimerization, has not been conclusively determined. A previous study suggests that trimerization of the F glycoprotein can happen after proteolytic cleavage (8). The spontaneous refolding of the heptad repeats (HRA, HRB, and HRC) forms the fusogenic 6-helix bundle that releases the fusion peptide (FP) from the hydrophobic cavity to facilitate the F process by which enveloped RSV enters host cells. The FP is located at the amino termini of the F1 subunits and embedded inside the central cavity of the F protein. FP insertion into the host membrane is essential for alignment of the virus and host cell membranes, F, pore formation, and release of the viral genome into the host cell.

Palivizumab and nirsevimab are currently the licensed mAb drugs for the prevention of severe RSV infection in high-risk infants (9). The D25 and 5C4 mAbs are known to specifically recognize the antigenic site Ø, which is located at the apex of the pre-F trimer (10). 131-2A mAb binds to antigenic site I on the post-F confirmation. Antigenic sites II and IV are preserved on both pre-F and post-F conformations (11). Palivizumab recognizes antigenic site II and binds with nanomolar affinity to RSV F (7,12). Furthermore, the antigenic site of palivizumab remains intact following a conformational change to the post-F state. mAb 101F recognizes antigenic site IV (13). The effectively neutralizing mAb MPE8 was also shown to target antigenic site III present on the pre-F F protein (13). The AM14 mAb specifically binds to antigenic site V, which is situated in the pre-F conformation and positioned between sites Ø and III (11). These findings show that B-cells identify multi-neutralizing epitopes found in both intermediate trimeric and monomeric F structures. An immunogenic pre-F Ag enables B-cells to access hidden epitopes and generate Abs, not only preventing receptor binding but also potentially destabilizing the trimeric structure (6). IgG Abs targeting multi-neutralizing epitopes of F glycoprotein would contribute to preventing RSV infection.

Virus-like particles (VLPs) are genetically engineered complexes that display multivalent Ags in a virus-like structure. However, VLPs cannot replicate due to the absence of viral genetic material. The trimeric conformation of F is an effective, safe, and promising immunogen for the VLP vaccine platform. Newcastle disease virus based VLP vaccines containing RSV G or F showed high immunogenicity without causing severe pulmonary inflammation in a cotton rat model (14). We reported wild-type (WT) F and pre-F stabilized VLPs containing a combination mutation of furin cleavage and F domain in addition to DS-Cav1 (2 disulfide-forming and cavity-filling residue mutations) (15). These combination mutation-stabilized pre-F VLPs were shown to promote stable exposure of pre-F specific site Ø as well as the site II antigenic epitope, providing an effective RSV vaccine immunogen inducing anti-RSV neutralizing immunity in mice (15).

However, it remains unknown what impact by introducing mutations in the cleavage site and FP, in addition to a switch in the transmembrane (TM) domain, with or without trimer-promoting general control nondepressible 4 (GCN4), would have on exposing neutralizing epitopes and the conformation of pre-F. In this study, we have constructed RSV F mutant proteins containing the GCN4 trimerization domain, TM domain (derived from hemagglutinin of A/PR8), F peptide partial deletion, and mutations in the cleavage site I for stable presentation on enveloped VLP. We further investigated the antigenic and immunogenic properties as well as protective efficacy of these RSV F mutant VLP vaccines in cotton rats with a single prime dose. RSV-neutralizing Abs and lung histopathology for safety evaluation were assessed in cotton rats after vaccination and challenge. The impact of specific mutations in the RSV F protein on exposing pre-F and post-F epitopes, immunogenicity, and efficacy has been discussed.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics statement

Animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Georgia State University (approval number: IACUC A24014). All animal experiments and husbandry involved in the studies were conducted under the guidelines of an approved IACUC protocol at Georgia State University.

Cells, virus, and reagents

HEp-2 cells (ATCC, Rockville, MD, USA) were grown in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA), 100 IU of penicillin, and 100 mg/ml streptomycin at 37°C and 5% CO2. The RSV A2 and A2-K-line19F strains were used as described (16,17). Spodoptera frugiperda 9 (Sf9) insect cells (CRL-1711; ATCC) were cultured at 27°C in SF-900 II serum-free medium (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) to produce recombinant baculoviruses (rBVs) and VLPs. RSV purification and inactivation by formalin were carried out as previously described (18). Pre-F and post-F conformation-specific mAbs D25 (Creative Biolabs, Shirley, NY, USA), 131-2A (Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA), 5C4 (Dr. Graham, Vaccine Research Center, National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, National Institutes of Health), and Palivizumab (Dr. Eun-Hyung Lee, Emory University) were used to evaluate the antigenic properties of mutant F protein in VLP as previously described (15). HRP-conjugated anti-mouse Ab IgG and anti-human Ab IgG (Southern Biotech, Birmingham, AL, USA) were used as secondary detection reagents in ELISA.

Preparation of RSV F mutant VLP constructs

Mutation of KKRKRR136 to KQKQQ136 in furin site I and a partial deletion of FP site were reported to increase the expression of F proteins on the insect cell surfaces (7). Mutations, including a partial deletion in FP region (aa137-144) and in the furin cleavage site I were introduced by using the following primers: F1m Forward: 5′ AAA ACT AGT GCC ATT GCT AGC GGA GTG GCC GTG TCA AAG GTG CGCT GCC; Reverse: AAA ACT AGT CTG CTG CTT CTG CTT CTT GCT CAG GGT CAC GTT GG, similarly as we described (15). An F DNA fragment encoding cleavage site I mutation and a partial FP deletion was cloned into a plasmid containing the GCN4 isoleucine zipper trimerization motif and influenza A/PR8 hemagglutinin (HA) TM domain (19).

A full-length human codon-optimized RSV wild type (WT) F and mutant F with RSV F TMrsv or PR8 HA TMpr8 on enveloped VLPs were produced in Sf9 insect cells infected with rBVs expressing RSV F and influenza M1 core proteins and characterized as described (15). Briefly, Sf9 insect cell culture supernatants were collected by centrifugation (6,000 rpm, 20 min), and the cleared supernatants containing F VLPs were purified by ultracentrifugation (30,000 rpm, 1 h). The incorporation of F protein in F mutant VLPs were confirmed by ELISA using 131-2a and palivizumab mAb and HRP-conjugated anti-human IgG Ab (Southern Biotech).

ELISA

To characterize the different mutant F protein VLPs, the F protein mutants were diluted in a series and applied to 96-well microtiter plates as previously described (15). The F protein-specific mAbs (131-2a, 5C4, palivizumab, and D25) were incubated at 4°C for 16-h and subsequently detected using either HRP-conjugated anti-mouse or anti-human IgG. For analysis of RSV F-specific IgG Abs in cotton rat sera, formalin-inactivated RSV (FI-RSV, 150 ng/50 µl) as described (17), post-F protein (10 ng/50 µl; BEI Resources, Manassas, VA, USA), DS-Cav1 pre-F protein (10 ng/50 µl), and palivizumab peptide (10 ng/50 µl) were used to coat the 96-well plates, and then blocked with 0.05% Tween 20 in PBS containing 1% BSA. After a washing step, the diluted sera were added, followed by incubation for 1.5 h at 37°C and then blocked with 0.05% Tween 20 in PBS containing 1% BSA. The serum Abs were detected by secondary anti-rat HRP conjugated IgG Abs (Southern Biotech), developed with tetramethylbenzidine (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), and stopped with 1 M H3PO4. The levels of cytokines, IFN-γ, and IL-4 (eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA) in mediastinal lymph node (MLN) homogenates were measured using ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunization, RSV challenge, and sample collection

Female cotton rats (4–5 wk old; Harlan Laboratories, Indianapolis, IN, USA) were randomly assigned to each vaccine group (n=5) and intramuscularly immunized with WT or mutant F VLPs (20 μg). For the RSV challenge, cotton rats were isofluorane-anesthetized and intranasally infected with 3×105 plaque-forming units (PFU) of the RSV A2 strain at 4 wk after prime immunization. At day 5 post RSV A2 challenge, cotton rats were killed to collect lungs, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) samples, bone marrows (BM), spleens, MLNs. The lung supernatants were collected at 2,000 rpm for 10 min for lung viral titration via an immunoplaque assay.

RSV neutralization assays and lung virus titration

RSV-specific neutralizing activity of immune sera was measured using RSV expressing the red fluorescent monomeric Katushka-2 protein (A2-K-line19F) to visualize infected cells as previously described (17). Briefly, 500 PFU of live A2-K-line19F RSV was mixed with serially diluted immune sera after heat-inactivation and applied to HEp-2 cell monolayer plates and then incubated at 37°C for 2 days. After that, the infected cells were fixed with 5% formaldehyde in PBS, and fluorescence was quantified using a fluorescence ELISA reader with excitation at 588 nm and emission at 635 nm (Synergy H1 Hybrid Reader; BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). The degrees of neutralizing activity were counted by the number of red fluorescence positive-cells present per 400× field. Lung viral titers in individual lungs of cotton rats at day 5 post RSV challenge were determined using an immunoplaque assay as previously described (20). The viral titer detection limit is 50 PFU.

Lung histology and flow cytometry

For histological analysis of lung inflammation, individual lungs were fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, sectioned at a thickness of 6 µm, and then stained with H&E and periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) stain. Numerical assessment of histopathology in H&E staining was performed on a scale of 0–3 by blinded observers with the severity scoring system, and the degree of mucin secretion in PAS staining was measured as previously described (17). For cell phenotype analysis in the respiratory mucosal compartment, the BALF and lung cells were obtained and analyzed by flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, San Diego, CA, USA). Lung cells were isolated by layering cell suspensions in Percoll density gradient medium with concentrations of 44% and 67% and then removing red blood cells. Flow cytometry was performed to assess the relative size and granularity of BALF and lung cells by analyzing the forward (FSC) and side (SSC) scattering plot as previously described (21). Cell suspensions were acquired on a Becton-Dickinson LSR-II/Fortessa flow cytometer (BD Biosciences), and FSC-SSC bivariate plots were generated to visualize population heterogeneity. Distinct subsets, including small lymphocytes, macrophages, and neutrophils, were identified and gated according to FSC/SSC characteristics for subsequent quantitative analysis.

Statistical analysis

Results are expressed as mean ± SEM. The statistical analyzes were performed by a one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s or Dunnett’s multiple comparison test in GraphPad Prism version 5 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). A p-value <0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

RESULTS

Preparation of RSV F constructs with mutations in the cleavage site and FP, with TM on VLP

Previous research has indicated that the pre-F protein exists in multiple conformations, with at least one being less compact and potentially promoting the opening of the trimer. The lack of foldon resulted in unstable DS-Cav1 proteins in the pre-F trimer conformation, highlighting the important role of foldon as a stabilizing domain (22). The RSV pre-F protein trimers exhibit dynamic transitions between open and closed states on the viral lipid membrane (23), indicating that pre-F TM on lipid membranes is inherently less stable compared to foldon stabilizing domain in pre-F proteins (Fig. 1). The F trimer-stabilizing GCN4 domain might contribute to preserving the pre-F conformation by inhibiting the HRA extension to the pre-hairpin conformation in F proteins of paramyxoviruses (24,25). Novavax prefusogenic F protein vaccine that contains mutations in the cleavage site I plus truncated FP with RSV TM domain forming nanoparticle proteins (20–40 nm) was highly immunogenic, effectively inducing palivizumab-like neutralizing Abs in cotton rats after alum adjuvanted vaccination (7). Novavax prefusogenic F vaccine was shown to induce Abs competitive with mAbs recognizing multiple antigenic sites Ø, II, IV, and VIII on pre-F and post-F conformations of RSV F (26). A cleavage mutant F Ag was reported to retain pre-F-specific neutralizing epitopes (27). We previously reported that RSV F proteins in VLP could be displayed in pre-F stabilized conformation, exposing pre-F site Ø antigenic sites by introducing cleavage and FP mutations in addition to DS-Cav1 mutation (15).

Development of RSV mutant F constructs and open trimeric RSV mutant F VLP structure.

Here, we investigated the impact of furin cleavage site mutations, FP partial deletion, and GCN4 with heterologous TMpr8 domain presented on enveloped VLP in the aspects of antigenicity, immunogenicity, and efficacy. F1m-TMrsv VLP contains F with mutations in the furin cleavage site I (KKRKRR → KKQKQQ) adjoining the F1 and F2 domains (Fig. 2A and B). F1dG4TMpr8 VLP was generated to contain F with deletion of the first 8 residues (aa136-144) within the FP domain plus mutations in the furin cleavage site I adjoining the F1 major domain (KKRKRR → KKQKQQ), in which GCN4 stabilizer was added to keep transition balance in C-terminal and TMpr8 domain of HA (Fig. 2A and B).

Cleavage mutation and partial FP deletion with GCN4 exposes antigenic site II but not the antigenic site Ø epitopes

To determine the levels of antigenic display, reactivity of pre-F or post-F epitope-specific mAbs was determined. A similarly high level of reactivity against the site 1 mAb 131-2a specific for post-F was observed with WT FTMrsv, F1mTMrsv, and F1dG4TMpr8 (Fig. 2C). The highest level of reactivity to site II specific palivizumab was observed for F1dG4TMpr8 VLP vaccine construct meanwhile a low reactivity to palivizumab shown by FTMrsv, and F1mTMrsv vaccines (Fig. 2D). WT and mutant F VLP vaccines (FTMrsv, F1mTMrsv, F1dG4TMpr8) did not expose pre-F conformation specific site Ø epitopes as shown by no significant reactivity against 5C4 and D25 mAbs (Fig. 2E and F).

F1dG4TMpr8 VLP vaccine induces Abs reactive to pre-F and post-F proteins in cotton rats with a single dose

Here, we assessed the immunogenicity of mutant F Ags on an enveloped VLP platform, F1mTMrsv, and F1dG4TMpr8 VLP in comparison with WT FTMrsv VLP. Cotton rats were intramuscularly immunized with a single dose of WT or F mutant VLPs. RSV F protein (pre-F DS-Cav1 protein, post-F, and FI-RSV) specific IgG Abs were determined in sera collected at 3 wk after prime dose immunization (Fig. 3A-C). Cotton rats vaccinated with F1dG4TMpr8 VLP vaccination induced significantly higher levels of IgG Abs reactive for pre-F protein (DS-Cav1), post-F, and FI-RSV Ags after prime, compared to those with FTMrsv or F1mTMrsv (Fig. 3A-C). In particular, the F1dG4TMpr8 with higher exposure to site II but not to site Ø epitopes showed a capacity to induce IgG Abs for pre-F (DS-Cav1 protein) Ag.

Cotton rats were challenged with 3×105 PFU of the RSV A2 strain at 4 weeks after prime immunization. At day 5 post RSV A2 challenge, we determined RSV F protein-specific IgG Abs secreted after in vitro cultures of cells from BM, spleen, and MLN cells (Fig. 3D-F). F1dG4TMpr8 VLP induced higher levels of F-protein (post-F) Ag-specific IgG Abs in cells from BM, spleen, and MLN. Thus, F1dG4TMpr8 VLP vaccine was moderately effective in inducing DS-Cav1 pre-F protein, post-F, and FI-RSV specific IgG Abs after prime immunization, as well as inducing F-specific IgG-secreting cell responses in MLN, spleens, and BM.

F1m-TM and F1dG4TMpr8 VLP effectively induce RSV neutralizing Abs and lung viral clearance

Cotton rat sera collected at wk 3 after immunization were heat-inactivated and used to determine the capacity to neutralize RSV infection in Vero cells using RSV A2-K-line 19F (Fig. 4A-C). The sera of cotton rats immunized with F1mTM VLP or F1dG4TMpr8 VLP were observed to exhibit higher neutralizing titers of inhibiting the infection of fluorescent RSV A2-K-line 19F with a high dose of virus inoculation (500 PFU/well of Vero cells), compared to that of WT FTMrsv VLP (Fig. 4A and B). A prime dose of WT FTMrsv, F1mTMrsv, F1dG4TMpr8 vaccination could provide protection against RSV by clearing lung viral loads below the limit of detection when determined day 5 post RSV challenge infection (Fig. 4D) compared to naïve cotton rats after RSV infection.

RSV F mutant VLP vaccines avoid lung histopathology by differentially modulating cellular infiltration and Th1 and Th2 cytokines in draining lymph nodes

To determine mucus production on day 5 post RSV challenge, lung tissue sections stained with PAS were used to quantify PAS-positive area in the airway epithelium (Fig. 5A and B). The FI-RSV group showed significant PAS positivity in 10 randomly selected airways in each rat. In contrast, all the mutant F VLP groups showed low PAS positivity. Overall, these results suggest that mutant F VLP can prevent RSV vaccine-enhanced pulmonary inflammation upon RSV infection.

Lung tissues at day 5 post RSV challenge were stained and evaluated for peribronchiolitis, perivasculitis, and interstitial pneumonitis to determine pulmonary histopathology (Fig. 5C). In our previous report, WT F protein in VLP did not cause pulmonary histopathology, compared with FI-RSV group showing high influx of inflammatory cells around the airways and alveolar septa, blood vessels, and interstitial spaces as well as cell thickening of airway linings (17). In this study, RSV infection of naïve cotton rats caused low levels of pulmonary inflammation in the airways (1.5), blood vessels (1.0), and interstitial spaces (1.0). All the mutant F VLP groups showed low scores in the 3 parameters, indicating no VED phenomenon of F protein in the VLP platform. The lowest histopathology scores of 0.9, 0.6, and 0.3 around the airways, blood vessels, and interstitial spaces were observed in the cotton rat with F1dG4TMpr8 VLP vaccination.

We analyzed IFN-γ and IL-4 cytokines in the draining lymph node cells at day 5 after RSV challenges to better understand adaptive immune responses (Fig. 5D). The highest levels of IFN-γ cytokines were induced with lymph node cells of F1dG4TMpr8 VLP primed cotton rats. Comparatively, the IL-4 cytokines induced in the lymph nodes of FTMrsv or F1mTMrsv VLPs primed cotton rats, but not in the lymph nodes of F1dG4TMpr8 VLP primed cotton rats. These results suggest that mutant F VLP vaccination in a cotton rat model modulates the induction of Th1 and Th2 cytokines.

To better understand infiltrating cell populations of the lungs in a cotton rat model, we analyzed the SSC and FSC profiles of cells (Fig. 6) by following a similar gating strategy reported (17). SSC and FSC gating of cotton rat lung and BALF cells displayed three distinct cellular populations: gate 1 cells (small lymphocyte-like cells), the large SSC gate 2 cells (monocyte, eosinophil, neutrophil-like cells), and the large SSC and FSC gate 3 cells (dendritic, macrophage-like cells), respectively. In our previous report, the FI-RSV group showed the non-granular and granular gate 1, 2, and 3 cells in the lungs and BALFs at the highest percentages and cellularity, which are 5- to 10-fold higher than those in the WT F-TMrsv VLP group (17). In this study, the F1dG4TMpr8 VLP group displayed the lowest cellularity in the gate categories 1, 2, and 3 cells of both BALF and lung cells (Fig. 6). Therefore, these results suggest that F1dG4TMpr8 VLP immunization of cotton rats induces the lowest infiltration of inflammatory innate and lymphocyte immune cells.

DISCUSSION

The stabilized pre-F protein has become the target of the most RSV-neutralizing activity and provides high levels of RSV-neutralizing Abs. The engineered DS-Cav1 protein containing the preserved antigenic site Ø induced potent RSV-neutralizing Ab and protection in mice and macaques (28). However, many studies have reported the occurrence of vaccine-enhanced disease after virus challenge. Our previous study has demonstrated the safety of VLP presenting F and G proteins without pulmonary disease and inflammation in cotton rats (17). The VLPs containing single-chain pre-F with furin cleavage sites mutation induced higher neutralizing activity than the VLP presenting DS-Cav1 pre-F protein (15). In this study, we established RSV F mutant proteins containing the mutations in the cleavage site I, F peptide partial deletion, GCN4 trimerization domain, and TM domain (derived from hemagglutinin of A/PR8). We evaluated the immunogenic properties and protective efficacy of these RSV mutant F VLP vaccines in cotton rats with a single prime dose.

There are no natural host animal models for RSV infection and disease to mimic RSV pathogenesis in humans. Cotton rats are also semi-permissive to RSV infection. Therefore, lung viral titers in unvaccinated naïve cotton rats were detected at moderately high PFU levels (~2.8 Log10) on day 5 after RSV infection (Fig. 4D). This might explain the observation that the efficacy of protection as measured by lung viral loads of RSV was not highly sensitive to correlate with the levels of immune responses (RSV neutralizing Ab titers) induced by different RSV vaccines. Even the FI-RSV vaccine group was reported to be effective in clearing lung viral loads despite its severe lung inflammation, causing enhanced respiratory disease in cotton rats after RSV challenge (17). FI-RSV is often used as a positive control to demonstrate RSV vaccine-enhanced respiratory disease in animal models after RSV challenge. None of the RSV vaccines were approved for vaccination in young children because of the potential concerns about subunit RSV vaccine-enhanced respiratory disease. The desirable features of RSV vaccine candidates are to exhibit high immunogenicity in inducing RSV neutralizing Abs and to avoid vaccine-enhanced disease. In this regard, F1dG4TMpr8-VLP tends to skew the immune response toward a Th1 profile in draining lymph nodes, elevated levels of IFN-γ, reduced Th2-associated cytokines and inflammatory mediators, preventing potential vaccine-mediated inflammation. The prime antisera from vaccinated cotton rats might not induce sufficiently high levels of neutralizing Abs to achieve 100% inhibition of RSV in vitro neutralization assays, indicating that boost vaccination would be necessary for generating high levels of protective neutralizing Ab activities against RSV. Our previous study reported that the boost antisera of cotton rats with RSV F VLP vaccination could achieve 100% inhibition of RSV in vitro neutralization assays (21).

The native RSV prefusion F undergoes considerable rearrangements to a stable post-F protein. The exact events of the F glycoprotein rearrangements, including proteolysis and trimerization, have not been conclusively understood. Following furin cleavage, a shorter F2, p27, and longer F1 subunit are generated. Three F2/F1 protomers linked by disulfide bonds are associated in the form of the functional F trimer. The high flexibility of trimers can transiently open, close, and dissociate within the lipid bilayer, exposing a range of conformations recognized by B cell receptors. Therefore, the trimeric vaccine candidates that mimic the breathable native F structure, allowing B cells to access the protective epitopes, may destabilize the trimeric structure. A more immunogenic prefusogenic F was constructed by a mutation in the furin cleavage site at Arg136 (KKRKRR → KKQKQQ) with partially deleted FP on the N-terminus of F1 (6). The influenza M1 matrix protein-based VLP platform has been extensively used for VLP development. A GCN4 domain was added to the C-terminus of the Ag to stabilize the oligomer formation. To increase the incorporation into the VLPs, the transmembrane and cytoplasmic tail domains of F constructs were replaced with those from HA of influenza A/PR/8/34 virus (19). The structure of F1dm-GCN4TMpr8 in Fig. 1 was generated with PHYRE2 Protein Fold Recognition Server (Phyre2.2, protein homology/analogy recognition engine V2.2) and then docked to obtain the trimeric protein structure using ClusPro 2.0: protein-protein docking (https://cluspro.org/). The modification of the RSV F protein sequence is likely to cause conformational changes. The RSV F protein revealed the compact closed trimeric structure in the pre-F conformation, which is later transformed into the post-F conformation during virus-host interactions. The pre-F conformation of RSV F can be recognized by the binding of Abs specific to the antigenic epitope site Ø on the apex of the F trimer. Gilman et al. (23) reported the transient opening of the prefusion RSV F trimers. Antigenic site V binding of CR9501 caused the trimeric pre-F-stabilized F proteins to open, resulting in the increased site II binding of motavisumab (23). Given that the WT F shifts to a more stabilized post-F conformation, the higher binding of Palivizumab to F1dm-GCN4TMpr8 may be due to the opening of RSV F trimers. The stabilizing GCN4 domain could play a role in preventing the dissociation of F protomers.

WT and mutant F VLP vaccines did not expose the antigenic site Ø. However, the combination of furin cleavage site mutation and partial deletion of FP plus GCN4 domain showed high Ab reactivity against site II epitopes. Consistently, the VLPs containing pre-F with additional mutations resulted in higher binding activity to site Ø (D25, 5C4), site I, and site II (palivizumab) epitope (15). The mutant F1dG4TMpr8 showed high reactivity against the site II Ab, but not the Ø Ab, and may be in the open prefusogenic conformation (Fig. 1), confirming the effect of p27 retention, FP partial deletion, and GCN4 domain due to cleavage site mutation. The F1dG4TMpr8 construct would have transitional F protein intermediates by inhibiting furin cleavage site 1 with partial FP deletion and stabilizing trimer via GCN4 and TMpr8, resulting in more broadly exposed epitopes and regions shared with DS-Cav1 pre-F conformation. Therefore, cotton rats immunized with mutant F1dG4TMpr8 VLP elicited significantly high levels of IgG specific for pre-F (DS-Cav1), post-F protein, and FI-RSV antigens and high neutralizing activity inhibiting RSV infection after prime dose, compared to other groups. In the mouse, cotton rat, and rhesus macaque animal models (8,28,29), soluble forms of RSV pre-F protein have been proven to induce higher levels of neutralizing Abs compared to the levels induced by soluble post-F. However, post-F protein, known for its exceptional stability, has the additional capability of eliciting substantial levels of neutralizing Abs and affording protection against RSV (12). T cell epitope (F peptide 49-63, YTSVITIEL) located close to the Ab epitopes on the site Ø on pre-F serves to potentiate B cell responses by facilitating the core sequence of the peptide to fit into the shallow groove of the MHC-II Ag-binding site (30). Intramuscular vaccination using pre-F or post-F VLPs stimulated the production of RSV neutralizing Abs and a well-balanced immune response, effectively protecting mice against RSV infection without any evidence of immunopathology (31). In addition, our results suggest that mutant F VLP can protect cotton rats from vaccine-enhanced pulmonary disease upon RSV challenge, as shown by lung histopathology and infiltrating cell population analysis in the lung, indicating the safety of the VLP vaccines.

VLPs can directly interact with dendritic cells (DCs), which are the most potent Ag-presenting cells (APCs). The enveloped VLPs are censored by pathogen recognition receptors, which are expressed on the surface or endosomes of DCs. Furthermore, Th1-polarized CD4+ T cell responses contribute to the control of RSV infection by promoting the development of CTLs and improving the quantity and quality of the humoral immune response (32,33). Although the classical endocytic process of VLP can stimulate both humoral and cellular immune responses, further analysis of the kinetics and components of the enveloped proteins is required to comprehend the Th1-type favored immune response of enveloped VLP. Since enveloped VLPs contain multimeric epitopes on their surface, they can promote cross-linking of B cell receptors (BCRs). The direct cross-linking of BCRs by VLPs is sufficient to activate B cells and allows them to function as APCs, enabling them to take up and present RSV F VLP Ags to T cells (34). Therefore, VLP-based vaccines can induce both humoral and cellular immune responses, and due to their multimeric nature, adjuvant co-administration is not needed in most cases of vaccinating small animals. Interestingly, the mutant F VLP induced IFN-γ at the highest level. A limitation of this study is that the low RSV titer used for the challenge could compromise the protective efficacy of different mutant F VLP vaccines. The lung viral titer in the vaccinated groups showed nonsignificant differences. One of the major concerns in developing RSV vaccines is vaccine-enhanced respiratory disease. In our previous studies (17,20,21), FI-RSV has been reported to induce high levels of serum IgG Abs specific for RSV F and neutralizing activity. However, cotton rats immunized with FI-RSV showed disease symptoms such as weight loss, high mucus production, and pulmonary inflammation resulting from high levels of inflammatory cytokines and infiltrating immune cells. Further study is also needed to determine whether the mutant F VLP exposing site II epitope may be in the prefusogenic conformation, but not in a post-F F protein.

In conclusion, we investigated the immunogenicity and efficacy of VLP vaccines with mutant F containing furin cleavage site I mutation, FP partial deletion, and substitution with TMpr8 for TMrsv (F1d-G4TMpr8) in a cotton rat model by comparing with F1m-TMrsv or FTMrsv immunization. A single dose of F1d-G4TMpr8was found to be effective in inducing IgG Abs for pre-F, post-F, and RSV Ags, RSV neutralizing Abs, and Ab-secreting cell responses, in conferring protection while avoiding vaccine-enhanced disease of pulmonary inflammation after RSV challenge. Mutations in the furin cleavage sites and FP in combination with a change in the TM domain might preferentially expose the palivizumab-binding antigenic site II epitope of the F protein. Importantly, exposing the palivizumab epitope on the trimer conformation of pre-F VLP vaccines in cotton rats could prevent eosinophilia and mucus production after RSV challenge. This study provides evidence that pre-F stabilizing DS-Cav1 mutations would be required for stable and effective exposure of site Ø epitopes to the immune system to develop RSV F VLP vaccine candidates.

The Economic Burden of Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia
Authors: Mirvat Alasnag, Adel Tash
Published: 2025

Elevated cholesterol levels has been established as one of the most important modifiable risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVD).1-3 Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of death globally and in the kingdom of Saudi Arabia as well as lost disability-adjusted life years (DALYs).4 It is criticial to recognize that acute coronary syndromes (ACS) impact productivity and income. Canadian data notes that 1.3 million days of work are lost over 3500 years due to cardiovascular events and absteneesim from the workplace exceeds three times that registered for patients without an ACS. The economic burden of cardiovascular diseases is a culmination of hospitalization, procedures, devices as well as long-term disablitily of survivors. The estimated cost of cardiovascular disease in the European Union is 210 billion (B) Euros annually with 22.6B productivity losses due to morbidity, 31.6B due to mortality and 110.8B due to direct healthcare costs.5-6 In the United States, myocardial infarctions and chronic coronary syndromes are among the top ten most expensive illnesses to treat, 12.1B USD and 9.0B USD respectively.7-8 In Saudi Arabia non-communicable diseases amount to 73% of all deaths with cardiovascular diseases accounting for 37% of those.9 The economic burden of ASCVD in Saudi Arabia is SAR 106.2B over ten years of which SAR 89.3B being dirct costs.10 This equates to SAR 9.9B over one year.10

Hyperlipidemia is a recognized and poorly controlled ASCVD risk factor in Saudi Arabia. The national guidelines have recommended intensive lipid lowering therapies (LLTs) particularly for high and very high risk individuals including combination therapies and injectables.11 Alghamdi and colleagues analyzed the cost-effectiveness of evolocumab in combination with other LLTs compared with LLTs alone.12 Adherence to the guidelines and increasing the evolocumab prescriptions for secondary prevention of ASCVD by 10% results in a significant clinical benefit and the cost is offset across a 10-year time horizon equivalent to 10,700 major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), 6000 cardiac deaths and 23,000 life years saved (Figure 1).12 Table 1 summarizes the economic inputs of MACE events. Given that ASCVD poses a significant economic burden on the healthcare systems in Saudi Arabia with substantial direct costs, it is imperative that the medical community becomes aware of cost-effectiveness analyses. Similarly, policy makers should streamline resource utilization to limit loss of productivity due to premature death or disability. Models have demonstrated that combination therapies are a good value for money that translate into better clinical outcomes with fewer cardiovascular events. Healthcare professionals and prescribers ought to adhere to guidelines to contribute to secondary preventive efforts in patients with ASCVD. Sensitivity analysis elaborated robust evidence through the models’ outcomes for PCSK9 inhibitors. It is important to seek similar analyses for novel agents such as inclisiran.

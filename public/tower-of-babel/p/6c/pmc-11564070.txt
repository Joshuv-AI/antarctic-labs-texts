Studies of the sintering method of ash and slag waste for the production of alumina from self-disintegrating sinters
Authors: Altynsary Bakirov, Ablay Zhunusov, Natalya Oleinikova, Raigul Ramazanova
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe goal of this research is to select the optimal composition of the sintering charge of ash and slag waste for maximum extraction of alumina and obtaining self-disintegrating sinters of a given chemical composition. Experiments have shown that sintering of ash and slag waste using two-component charges makes it possible to extract alumina from sinters by no more than 60 %. The highest sintering rates were made using three-component charges. So, when sintering ash and slag waste from Almaty TPP-2 on a three-component charge of the composition CaO:SiO2 = 2; Na2O:(Al2O3+Fe2O3) = 1 at a duration of 60 min and a temperature of 1200 °C, alumina extraction was 83.68 %, under the same conditions, alumina extraction of 87.07 % was obtained during sintering of ash and slag waste from the Eurasian Energy Corporation, and alumina extraction was 90.2 % during sintering of ash and slag waste obtained after preliminary chemical activation.As a result of the conducted research on sintering of ash and slag waste in a three–component charge system, a self-disintegrating sinter of the following chemical composition was obtained, %: Na2O – 0.07; CaO – 26.88; SiO2 – 2.06; Fe2O3 – 5.01; Al2O3– 5.059.Keywords: Ash and slag waste, Self-scattering sinters, Activation, Sintering, Alumina, Bicalcium silicate, Sodium aluminate, Limestone, Soda, Alloy

Introduction

High-alumina fly ash (ash and slag waste) is a by-product of high-temperature coal combustion in thermal power plants. The annual emission of ash and slag waste is approximately 30 million tons [1]. Most of the ash and slag waste is stored in dumps and leads to serious pollution of water, atmosphere, and soil [2,3]. On the other hand, the high alumina content (approximately 30–50 %) in fly ash makes it a valuable resource for processing (see Table 4, Table 5, Table 9).

Recycling ash and slag waste can be a good alternative to disposal, and could achieve significant economic and environmental benefits as well. More aggressive efforts have been undertaken recently, to recycle ash and slag waste [4,5]. For example, about 20 % of the ash and slag waste generated is being used in concrete production. Other uses include soil amelioration [[6], [7], [8]], ceramic industry [[9], [10], [11]], catalysis and support for catalysis [[12], [13], [14], [15]], adsorbents for removal of various pollutants [16], depth separation [[17], [18], [19]], zeolite synthesis [[20], [21], [22]] and valuable metals recovery [[23], [24], [25]]. The global average utilization rate of ash and slag waste is estimated to be nearly 25 % [26]. Current utilization rates have been estimated at 39 % for U.S., 47 % for the E.U. and 15 % for India [27,28].

In traditional methods of ash and slag waste processing, acid and alkaline methods are mainly used, as well as sulfatizing ash roasting followed by acid leaching. However, these methods do not provide sufficient opening of the source material, and the resulting products require subsequent separate complex technological operations.

To date, various methods of processing ash and slag waste from thermal power plants by sintering are known.

To isolate aluminum oxide from ash, many extraction methods have been developed, such as pre-alkaline sintering of sodium lime [29], sintering with limestone [30], acid leaching [31,32], sintering with ammonium sulfate [33]. Only the soda lime sintering process was carried out on an industrial scale (200 thousand tons per year) by the Chinese Datang Corporation in Inner Mongolia province [34].

In the work of the authors [35], experiments were conducted on thermal weight loss of ash depending on temperature in order to determine the appropriate sintering temperature of ash mixed with H2SO4 (98 %). The mixture was then sintered at 220 °C, dissolved in water at 85 °C, the alkali level in the solution was regulated using CaCO3, iron was removed using KMnO4 and MnSO4, Al2SO4 particles were deposited and heat treated at 850 °C to obtain Al2O3 powders. The extraction of Al2O3 reached 70–90 % at a relatively lower processing temperature and fewer solid residues.

In other works [36], studies were conducted on the effect of the amount of potassium bisulfate, calcination temperature and calcination time on the efficiency of extraction of aluminum oxide (Al2O3) from ash and slag waste. The efficiency of alumina extraction reached a maximum of 92.8 %, the calcination temperature was 230 °C and the calcination time was 3 h.

However, the disadvantage of these sintering methods are serious problems associated with the crystallization and calcination of aluminum sulfate, as well as during leaching, other metals such as iron, magnesium, titanium, sodium and potassium are co-dissolved, which requires the removal of these impurities before the extraction of alumina.

The author's work [37] focused on sintering a mixture of ash-CaCO3 carbon disulfide at a temperature of about 1200 °C to obtain clinker from calcium sulfoaluminate. Clinker reacted with 3 wt % Na2CO3 at a temperature of 65 °C, causing the dissolution of the contained aluminum, which resulted in the extraction of 90 % aluminum oxide.

Another indirect alkaline leaching process investigated by a number of researchers is the lime sintering process [38]. In this process, the ash is mixed and reacts with lime (added in the form of limestone) at an elevated temperature of more than 1100 °C to form calcium aluminate (12CaO·7Al2O3) and calcium silicate (2CaO·SiO2). When the sinter is cooled to a temperature below 500 °C, the sinter is automatically destroyed, the structure of the sinter disintegrates into a fine powder, due to phase transformations occurring during cooling, which lead to an increase in the volume and swelling of the sinter, this eliminates the need to grind the sinter before leaching. Calcium aluminate (12CaO·7Al2O3) is soluble in Na2CO3, which is used as a leaching reagent, while bicalcium silicate (2CaO·SiO2) is insoluble in the same solution. Desilinization is achieved by mixing the solution with Ca(OH)2 at 70 °C to form calcium aluminosilicates, which have very low solubility in an alkaline solution.

The purified NaAlO2 solution is carbonized by reacting the solution with bubbling CO2, which subsequently causes the precipitation of Al(OH)3, which is then calcined to form the final aluminum oxide.

In other works by the authors [39], ash is calcined with soda and lime at 1200 °C, and silica is converted into stable calcium silicate (2CaO·SiO2), thus separating silica from aluminum oxide. Due to the addition of a large amount of limestone during the sintering process, a significant amount of calcium silicate cupcakes are formed, which can be used for cement production.

The authors of [40,41] investigated the extraction of SiO2 from fly ash by alkaline leaching, and then Al2O3 was extracted from desiltered fly ash using the alkaline lime sintering process. The pre-desilination process followed by sintering was designed to reduce the silica content and the consumption of sintering reagents. The process resulted in high recovery of both silicon dioxide and aluminum oxide, and the silicon dioxide was reused as silica and calcium silicate.

The main advantages of alkaline treatment routes are the ease with which the leaching reagent is regenerated, which reduces the total cost of reagents, and alkaline media do not cause corrosion, therefore technological and transshipment equipment can be made from cheaper materials such as mild steel [42].

As a result of the analytical review, it should be noted that the main purpose of this study is to select the optimal composition of the charge for sintering ash and slag waste from thermal power plants to produce self-dispersing sinters containing calcium aluminate (12CaO·7Al2O3) and calcium silicate (2CaO·SiO2).

Experimental work on the extraction of alumina from ash and slag waste from thermal power plants was carried out in the laboratory of the Center for Advanced Development "Veritas" (Daulet Serikbayev East Kazakhstan Technical University). The objects of the study were samples of the following thermal power plants: ash and slag material of the Eurasian Energy Corporation (Pavlodar region, Aksu), ash and slag material of the Almaty Thermal Power Plant-2 (Almaty TPP-2), as well as ash and slag waste obtained as a result of chemical activation [43] (sample No. 40, 42, 44, 46), the characteristics of which are given in Table 1.

Methods of laboratory studies of sintering of ash and slag waste

The essence of the experiments was as follows: The charge mixtures were pre-prepared, weighed and thoroughly mixed by hand in a porcelain mortar. The charge was placed in graphite crucibles, then in a cold furnace, where the charge was heated to a predetermined sintering temperature range and kept for a certain time. The resulting sinter was slowly cooled to 400–500 °C for 20–30 min.

In order to establish the optimal mode of the sintering process, several charges were tested for each sample of ash and slag waste from an initial ash mass of 50 g. The three-component sintering charge system consisted of ash and slag waste, soda ash (Na2CO3) and limestone (CaCO3).The charge was calculated in order to obtain Al2O3·Na2O in the lists and Ca2SiO4 from the ratios: Na2O:(Al2O3+Fe2O3) = 1, CaO:SiO2 = 2. The charge was sintered in temperature ranges (1100–1250 °C) and duration (30–120 min). The composition of the charge in all experiments was different, differing in the dosage of Na2CO3 and CaCO3, with the aim of binding Fe2O3 and TiO2 to Na2O ·Fe2O3 and Na2O·TiO2.

Based on the experimental work performed, it follows that in order to obtain self-disintegrating sinters (shown in Fig. 2), a given chemical composition suitable for further processing from tested ash and slag waste samples is possible using three-component charge systems. The optimal sintering temperature of ash and slag waste was 1200 °C and a duration of 60 min for ash and slag waste after chemical activation. The highest sintering rates were made using three-component charges. So, when sintering ash and slag waste from Almaty TPP-2 on a three-component charge of the composition CaO:SiO2 = 2; Na2O:(Al2O3+Fe2O3) = 1 at a duration of 60 min and a temperature of 1200 °C, alumina extraction was 83.68 %, under the same conditions, alumina extraction of 87.07 % was obtained during sintering of ash and slag waste from the Eurasian Energy Corporation, and alumina extraction was 90.2 % during sintering of ash and slag waste obtained after preliminary chemical activation.

Sinters obtained after sintering at temperatures 1000–1050 °C with the addition of limestone of less than stoichiometric amount.

At temperatures above 1200 °C, the physico-chemical properties of the sinter change. The composition of the charge and the duration of sintering have a significant effect on the density of the sinter. If the optimal sintering modes are chosen incorrectly, porosity decreases and shrinkage of the sinter is formed.

Fig. 2 shows the lists obtained at temperatures of 1000–1050 °C with the addition of a smaller stoichiometric amount of limestone to the sintering charge. The brown color of the obtained lists is explained by the fact that if the optimal sintering modes of ash and slag waste are incorrectly selected, porosity decreases and shrinkage of the sinter is formed. The brown color of the resulting sinter in Fig. 2 indicates that the material has not leaked (as a result, an underdone agglomerate is formed). Fig. 3 shows the sinter obtained at a sintering temperature of 1200 °C (a-before disintegration, b-after disintegration).

As a result of sintering, the resulting sinter was cooled to a temperature below 500 °C. This is due to the fact that when the sinter is cooled to a temperature below 500 °C, the sinter is automatically destroyed, the structure of the sinter disintegrates into a fine powder, due to phase transformations occurring during cooling, which lead to an increase in the volume and swelling of the sinter, this eliminates the need to grind the sinter before leaching.

There are five stable forms of bicalcium silicate (α, αL, αH, β and γ) as shown in Fig. 4. The polymorphic transformation from β-to γ-Ca2SiO4, accompanied by a 10 % increase in volume and responsible for self-scattering of sinters into fine powder and other lime-rich silicate slags, is of practical importance. This phenomenon (self-scattering) is favorable for the technological process, since it not only saves the energy needed for grinding, but also provides a large surface area for the subsequent leaching stage [44].

Scheme of polymorphic transformations of bicalcium silicate Ca2SiO4 [44].

With an increase in the sintering temperature from 1100 to 1250 °C, the degree of decomposition of aluminum-containing phases increases, as evidenced by the result of the phase composition represented by Ca2SiO4 and Al2O3·Na2O. With an increase in the content of Ca2SiO4 (bicalcium silicate), the extraction of alumina from the lists decreases. As the data of the conducted studies show, with an increase in the content of Na2O·Fe2O3 (sodium ferrite) reduces the melting point of the sinters, which negatively affects the technological regime. When the sintering temperature rises above 1250–1300 °C, the charge is melted [45], which leads to a deterioration in the self-scattering of the sinter and a decrease in the degree of alumina extraction (Fig. 5 shows). The results of X-ray phase analysis of the obtained sinter at a temperature of 1200 °C confirm the presence of the following phases and compounds: Ca(OH)2, Ca2SiO4, Ca2Al(AlSiO7), Na4CaSi3O9. The results showed that the main components of ash and slag waste can be decomposed and form intermediate products Ca2Al(AlSiO7), Na4CaSi3O9.

At the next stage, a study of the leaching process of the sinters obtained after sintering of ash and slag waste was carried out.

A soda solution (Na2CO3) with a concentration of 80 g/l and a solid-to-liquid ratio of T:W = 1:3 was chosen as the leaching reagent. The sinter leaching process was carried out in a magnetic stirrer at a temperature of 70 °C and a duration of 30 min. As a result of the conducted experiment on the leaching of the sinter, an aluminate solution and a sediment (grey sludge) were obtained. The degree of extraction of Al2O3 into the solution was 83.9 %. Fig. 12 shows how (grey sludge), and Fig. 13 shows the diffractogram of the sediment (grey sludge).

According to the results of X-ray phase analysis of sediment (grey sludge), the main phases are calcite (CaCO3), quartz (SiO2), corundum (Al2O3), hematite (Fe2O3).

The resulting aluminate solution after leaching of the sinter is sent to the carbonation conversion with further production of alumina.

Conclusion

Thus, as a result of the conducted research on sintering ash and slag waste to produce self-disintegrating sinters, it was found that the highest rates of alumina extraction were obtained on a three-component charge system ash and slag waste, limestone, soda ash.

The results of X-ray phase analysis of the obtained sinter at a temperature of 1200 °C confirm the presence of the following phases and compounds: Ca(OH)2, Ca12Al14O33(H2O), Ca2SiO4, Ca2Al2SiO7. The results showed that the main components of ash and slag waste can be decomposed and form intermediate products Ca2Al2SiO7, Ca2SiO4, and then converted to Ca12Al14O33(H2O), which is highly soluble in a soda-alkaline solution during leaching of the sinter. At a sintering temperature of 1200 °C with a molar ratio of CaO:SiO2 = 2; Na2O:(Al2O3+Fe2O3) = 1, respectively, it is possible to obtain a degree of alumina extraction of 90.2 %. A further increase in the temperature and duration of sintering does not allow for maximum extraction of alumina and the formation of the Ca12Al14O33(H2O) phase.

It was found that in this area 2 CaO·SiO2–12 CaO·7Al2O3–CaO·Al2O3 the main alumina-containing phases are CaO·Al2O3 and 12CaO·7Al2O3, which are highly soluble in alkaline solutions when leaching alumocalcium sinters at a temperature of 70 °C.

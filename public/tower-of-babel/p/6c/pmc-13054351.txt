Impact of the Neurosurgery Research and Education Foundation vascular course on academic and career outcomes
Authors: Mustafa Motiwala, Ahmed Kashkoush, Taylor Orr, Jordan Petitt, Mohamed El-Abtah, C. Stewart Nichols, Andrew Grande, Adam Arthur, Mark Bain, Nickalus R. Khan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Over the past decade, the Neurosurgery Research and Education Foundation (NREF) has offered an endovascular and vascular techniques course for senior residents to familiarize themselves with the basics of endovascular and cerebrovascular surgery. This study evaluates the impact of this course on the careers of previous attendees.Methods: A list of attendees between 2012 and 2022 from the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques course was obtained, and data regarding each attendee, including demographics, career progression, and academic output, were determined. Outcomes included matriculation into endo-/cerebrovascular careers, clinical practice setting, and academic productivity measured through publication count and h-index.Results: From 2012 to 2022, 207 US neurosurgery residents attended the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques course. Overall, 184 have graduated from residency, with 133 (72.3%) of these pursuing careers in vascular neurosurgery. Of the 172 currently practicing, 94 (54.7%) work in academic neurosurgery. The mean ± standard deviation [SD] number of publications after the course and total h-index were 22 ± 36 and 9 ± 8, respectively. The mean ± SD number of citations and cerebrovascular publications after the course were 348 ± 730 and 30 ± 53, respectively. Almost half of all papers published by previous attendees after the course (47.8%) were cerebrovascular publications.Conclusion: Young neurosurgeons who attended the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques have found significant success pursuing vascular neurosurgery across the United States, both clinically and academically. The course has afforded these attendees unique insights and meaningful connections into the field that have proved invaluable in their careers.

INTRODUCTION

Choosing a neurosurgical sub-specialty is fundamentally the most important decision that a neurosurgical resident must make. The choice of post-residency neurosurgical fellowship training is dependent on many factors, including exposure during residency, availability of mentors, academic prestige, and job market demand, among others.[12] Relative to other neurosurgical subspecialties, there is a paucity of resident exposure to endovascular and open vascular procedures.[4] In 2017–2018, for instance, there was a combined ACGME graduate average of 54 case log entries for “Craniotomy for Aneurysm” and “Endovascular Aneurysm Treatment,” compared to 169 case log entries for “Craniotomy for Brain Tumor” and 452 entries for “Total Spine.” A 2010 study demonstrated that neurosurgical residents felt most inadequately prepared in endovascular neurosurgery after residency completion compared to all other neurosurgical domains.[13] Furthermore, formalized resident training in open neurovascular techniques has drastically diminished due to the subsequently increasing prevalence of endovascular care.[7]

Despite low exposure rates during residency, the cerebrovascular field as a whole has experienced an explosion of innovation, resulting in constantly evolving indications for vascular treatment. New pathologies being increasingly managed by neurovascular specialists include spontaneous intracranial hemorrhage,[14] chronic subdural hematoma,[15] cerebrospinal fluid-venous fistulas,[2] and idiopathic intracranial hypertension.[3] The relative shift from open to endovascular care, as well as the dynamic nature of the field, may make it difficult for neurosurgical residents to stay abreast of both common neurosurgical and cerebrovascular diseases.

One opportunity to improve resident exposure to cerebrovascular care, foster open and endovascular skills acquisition, and reinforce career interest in the specialty is through focused hands-on coursework. Specialty-specific courses in neurosurgery can increase surgical skill, knowledge, and interest in attendees.[9] The Neurosurgery Research and Education Foundation (NREF) hosts a range of coursework in advanced topics that are not heavily emphasized during resident education. The NREF course entitled “Endovascular and Vascular Techniques for Senior Residents” was initiated in 2012 with the goal of educating senior neurosurgery residents on the tenets of cerebrovascular neurosurgery. Structured as a 2-day endeavor, both endovascular and open vascular treatments spanning the range of cerebrovascular disease are comprehensively reviewed by leading faculty in the field. Residents can build on their knowledge by practicing complex techniques such as bypass, aneurysm clipping, and particle embolization through a combination of simulator, cadaveric, and live animal models. The course has now been ongoing for 10 years and has enrolled over 200 senior residents since its initiation. We sought to evaluate the overall impact of the course on resident career decisions in vascular neurosurgery utilizing a 6-item survey administered to all previous attendees of the course.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Variable definition and data collection

A list of attendees of the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques for Senior Residents course was obtained, which included names, email addresses, and year of attendance. Details regarding training and career for each participant were obtained from department and hospital websites, in addition to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) Membership Directory (https://myaans.aans.org/memberdirectory). Residency and fellowship details included program name, geographic location, and graduation year. Career variables were comprised of current employer, geographic location, academic or private practice, and professorship. Geographic regions were defined as follows: Northeast (CT, DE, MA, ME, NH, NJ, NY, PA, RI, and VT), South (AL, AR, DC, FL, GA, KY, LA, MD, MS, NC, OK, PR, SC, TN, TX, VA, and WV), Midwest (IA, IL, IN, KS, MI, MN, MO, ND, NE, OH, SD, and WI), and West (AK, AZ, CA, CO, HI, ID, MT, NM, NV, OR, UT, WA, and WY).

Survey administration

The Institutional Review Board at University of Tennessee Health Science Center did not require ethical approval of this study due to the quality improvement nature of this work. Having incorporated the Checklist for Reporting Results of Internet E-Surveys[5] in the design of this survey [Table 1], the primary authors first evaluated this survey for usability and technical functionality. No review step, cookies, internet protocol (IP) checks, or log file analysis were used due to the size of this closed form. Next, previous attendees were emailed the 6 items, single page online survey through Google Forms in September 2023. The email included details regarding survey length, purpose, and other relevant features. Questions were not varied between responders. The primary author performed consistency checks and calculation of views, in addition to participation and completion rates. After initial responses, a subsequent follow-up was conducted by email in March 2024 to increase participation. Incomplete responses were not recorded, nor were those with atypical time stamps. No statistical corrections were needed for the original data. The following questions were included in the survey:

Post course evaluations

Immediately after the course, feedback surveys were administered to participants. From these evaluations were available from 20 respondents in 2018, 16 in 2019, and 21 in 2021.

Statistical analysis

Data were assessed for normality using Kolmogorov– Smirnov testing. Baseline variables were analyzed through Chi-square or Kruskall–Wallis tests as appropriate. Significance level was set to P < 0.05. All analyses were performed in RStudio version 2023.12.1 (Posit PBC, Boston, USA). Data visualizations were performed in RStudio and Microsoft Excel for Mac version 16.8 (Microsoft Corporation, Seattle, USA).

RESULTS

Demographics

From 2012 to 2022, a total of 207 neurosurgery residents from 93 different programs in the United States attended the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques course. As detailed in Figure 1, the number of attendees varied annually with a peak attendance of 25 in 2014 and a low of 20 in 2022. Most attendees trained at residency programs in the South (67, 32.4%), followed by the Midwest (58, 28.0%), Northeast (42, 20.3%), and West (40, 19.3%) [Figure 2]. Fellowship information

Table 2 depicts the details regarding the fellowship details for previous attendees. Since attending the course, 184 neurosurgeons have graduated from residency with a significantly higher number of these individuals pursuing fellowship (P < 0.00001). A majority (149, 81.0%) have completed a fellowship, while 8 (4.4%) are currently in fellowship. Significantly more of those who opted for subspecialty training (133, 84.7%) have pursued a vascular fellowship (P = 0.00039). Bearing in mind that some trainees have pursued multiple fellowships at different institutions, fellowships were most often sought in the South (54, 31.4%), followed by the Northeast (47, 27.3%), West (46, 26.7%), and Midwest (25, 14.6%), though these regional differences were not significant.

Career progression

As seen in Table 3, of the 172 attendees who have since completed training, 77 (44.8%) are currently practicing in the South, 34 in the West (19.8%), 32 (18.6%) in the Midwest, and 29 (16.9) in the Northeast. Half (86, 50.0%) have chosen careers in academic neurosurgery. Seventy-two (83.7%) of the academic neurosurgeons hold assistant professor roles, while 10 (11.6%) have achieved the title of associate professor. There were no significant differences found in geographic region of practice (P = 0.392) or practice type (P = 1.000).

Scholarly activity

Following the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques course, attendees have demonstrated productivity from an academic perspective. Table 4 depicts details regarding scholarly activity. The mean ± SD number of publications after the course was 22 ± 36, with a mean ± standard deviation total h-index of 9 ± 8. The average number of citations after the course was 348 ± 730, with an average of 30 ± 53 cerebrovascular publications. Nearly half of all publications after the course (47.8%) were determined to be cerebrovascular publications.

Survey data

The survey was electronically administered to all 207 previous attendees of the course, and responses from 32 (15.5%) were recorded. As seen in Figure 4, most participants responded positively to the question about whether attending the course influenced their current subspecialty (22, 68.8%) and their fellowship decision (21, 65.6%) and if it expanded their professional network (87.5%). All responders (32, 100.0%) answered positively regarding whether attending the course positively impacted their careers and if, looking back, they would still have attended the course knowing what they know now. A majority of the responders (26, 82.3%) responded neutrally to the question about whether attending the course made finding a job easier.

Post course evaluations

Surveys administered after course participation have yielded overwhelmingly positive feedback from responders. After the 2018 course, all 20 participants (100.0%) agreed that they would recommend the course to others and rated the course overall as a “5/5.” Moreover, they all (20, 100.0%) concurred that the materials were presented at the right level for their needs with most rating the laboratory presentations (17, 85.0%) and the didactic portion (19, 95.0%) as “5/5.”

In 2019, the 16 residents (100.0%) agreed that they “would recommend this course to others” with the vast majority (15, 93.75%) rating it as a “5/5” and agreeing that the materials presented were at the right level for their needs. Laboratory and didactic presentations were overwhelmingly well received, with 15 (93.75%) participants rating each of them as “5/5.”

While the course was paused for the pandemic in 2020, it resumed in 2021 with 100.0% (21) of responders again agreeing that they would recommend this course to others. Furthermore, all (21, 100.0%) rated the course as “5/5” and agreed that the materials were presented at the right level for their needs, laboratory presentations were universally (21, 100.0%) ranked as “5/5” while most (17, 80.95%) agreed that the didactic portion of the program was “5/5.”

DISCUSSION

Since the NREF started the program in 2012, over 207 neurosurgical residents have attended the Endovascular and Vascular Techniques for Senior Residents course. Hailing from 93 different programs and 38 different states, in addition to Puerto Rico, these trainees have been able to glean crucial insights and subspecialty exposure through this opportunity. The majority of those in this group have gone on to pursue vascular fellowships at diverse institutions throughout the United States, with half eventually choosing a career in academic neurosurgery and holding professorships at training programs across the country. Indeed, the impact that these individuals have had on the field should not be overlooked. Since participating in the course, previous attendees have generated an average of 22 publications each, comprising over 6,000 authorships and 72,000 citations in total, with about half of these projects falling into the category of open or endovascular neurosurgery. Most importantly, feedback from the course has been largely positive, with overwhelmingly favorable responses regarding the perceived impact of this program on career development.

Previous efforts have demonstrated the value of NREF support for those in training.[8] This endeavor has been ongoing for decades,[6] and its success is evidenced by its academic impact and influence on the field of neurosurgery as a whole. The present work builds on this idea by evaluating both objective and subjective measures of program success. By cultivating the ambitions and aspirations of residents, the organization has been able to push forward the future of our field.

Our findings highlight this fact and, thus, merit consideration when discussing the potential return on investment for programs like these. Moreover, as the landscape of neurovascular surgery and intervention continues to evolve,[7] the demand for this skillset and the necessity to maintain it will only be emphasized further, so early exposure and enthusiasm for this subspecialty is likely to grow in importance.

Limitations

Our work has several limitations. Retrospective information regarding attendees and their experiences, including academic and career outcomes, may lack generalizability for the future. While h-index represents a proxy to evaluate academic impact, its shortcomings are well documented in the neurosurgical literature.[1,10,11] Furthermore, the subjective nature of our survey data is additionally limited by our relatively low response rate of 15.5%.

CONCLUSION

Our findings underscore the significant impact of the NREF Endovascular and Vascular Techniques course on the academic and career trajectories of neurosurgery residents. Attendees have demonstrated a strong inclination toward pursuing vascular neurosurgery, with a considerable proportion opting for academic practice settings. The course has not only facilitated career advancement but also fostered meaningful contributions to the field through substantial academic output. These outcomes affirm the importance of specialized educational initiatives in shaping the future of neurosurgical subspecialties and enhancing overall patient care.

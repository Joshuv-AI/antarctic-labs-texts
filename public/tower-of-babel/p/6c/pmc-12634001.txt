Living with dialysis: Activities of daily life, social participation, and quality of life among hemodialysis patients in Chhattisgarh
Authors: Senthilkumar Ramasamy, Abhiruchi Galhotra, Varun Agarwal, Vinay Rathore
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Chhattisgarh is one of the poorest states in Central India, and a large part of the state is underdeveloped. In this state, there is a recent increase in unusual high number of deaths due to kidney diseases. Renal replacement therapy (hemodialysis [HD]) is required when chronic kidney disease patients reach a stage of end stage renal disease. Most of the dialysis patients are referred from periphery dialysis centers to this hospital for tertiary care and follow-up. Dialysis patients often experience physical and social limitations which give rise to negative psychological experiences and further worsen their quality of life (QoL). The aim of the present study was to examine the functional status (FS) of activity of daily living, social participation, and QoL among HD patients in central India.Materials and Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted among HD patients followed up in the Nephrology outpatient department of a tertiary care hospital in central India from January to June 2022. The FS, social participation, and QoL were assessed using the Katz activity of daily living, the participation scale, and the kidney disease QoL short-form questionnaire version 1.3. A linear regression analysis was conducted to identify predictors of FS, social participation, and QoL among the participants.Results:This study included 105 HD patients. The mean scores of Katz activity of daily living, the participation scale, and kidney disease QoL short-form questionnaire were 3.30 ± 1.33, 50.16 ± 12.4, and 48.12 ± 11.2, respectively. A positive correlation was observed between the kidney disease QoL short-form scores and Katz functional activity scores. In contrast, a negative correlation was found between the kidney disease QoL short-form scores and the participation scores. The presence of diabetes and distance traveled were the most common predictors linked with FS, social participation, and QoL in HD patients.Conclusion:The Katz functional activity, participation, and kidney disease QoL short-form scores were notably low. Early identification, intervention, and regular monitoring of these factors were essential for improving the overall health of HD patients.

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a global public health concern and a key determinant of poor health outcomes in major noncommunicable diseases such as diabetes and hypertension. Renal replacement therapy (RRT) is required when CKD reaches a stage of end stage renal disease (ESRD). Currently, around 3 million people receive RRT worldwide, which is expected to double to 5.4 million by 2030.[1] Hemodialysis (HD) remains the major treatment modality of RRT worldwide. However, the demand is experiencing unprecedented growth of 2.2 lakh new patients of ESRD every year, which results in an additional demand of 3.4 crore dialysis every year. In India, the advent of Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Program (PMNDP) plays a significant role in the growing burden of ESRD.[2]

The life expectancy of ESRD patients has increased in recent years due to the availability of HD. However, the quality of life (QoL) of ESRD patients remains poor due to high levels of disability in various aspects of their lives. Dialysis is the most expensive treatment and often poorly subsidized by health programs. It leads to loss of personal freedom, dependence on caregivers, disruption of marital and family life, and significant out-of-pocket expenditure.[3] Dialysis imposes a considerable burden on patients with hospital visits two or three times per week, and each visit lasts 3–6 hours. Further, accompanying dietary and fluid restrictions in patients affect the QoL. After the initiation of dialysis, many patients have difficulty in performing activities of their daily life and most of them lose their jobs.

Dialysis initiation might also lead to treatment-related complications, physical and mental fatigue, and cognitive disturbances, further worsening functional status (FS) and inability to perform social roles.[4] FS is an individual’s ability to do normal activities to meet daily basic needs and maintain overall wellbeing.[5] A good FS is a key contributor to QoL. Social participation is a person’s involvement in activities that provide interactions with others within a society or community.[678] Dialysis patients often experience reduced social engagement and support which give rise to negative psychological experiences and further worsen their QoL.

QoL research is an important tool in assessing chronic illness outcomes apart from, more commonly, mortality and morbid outcomes. However, these outcomes are rarely reported because these parameters are more difficult to measure. This study reports on the FS, social participation, and QoL of HD patients from central India.

Materials and Methods

Study setting

Chhattisgarh is a landlocked state and one of the poorest in Central India; it has a population of around 30 million, among which 30% belong to the tribal population. Recent studies have highlighted the high prevalence and mortality due to CKD in this state.[9] The current study was conducted in the Nephrology outpatient department (OPD) of a tertiary care teaching hospital in this state; the department was started functioning since 2019. The dialysis patients are referred from periphery dialysis centers to this hospital for tertiary care and follow-up.

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted between January and June 2022. It includes all patients aged more than 18 years and who have been on maintenance HD for at least 3 months and were followed up in the Nephrology OPD during the study period. This study mainly focuses on dialysis-specific outcomes; in order to limit the additional confounding effects, the patients with known life-threatening comorbidities (heart diseases, chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases, stroke and chronic liver diseases, cancer, HIV/AIDS) were excluded from this because it can further severely impair physical functioning, social participation, and QoL in dialysis patients.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using the Cochran sample size formula: n = z2P (1 − P)/E2.

Z = standard normal deviate (Z = 1.96 at 95% confidence interval or 5% level of significance),

P is the prevalence rate of ESRD: 0.07 is estimated from the earlier studies,[10]

E is the Precision (or margin of error) (E = 5%).

According to the above formula, the first 105 patients who visited the department of nephrology fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the study.

Data collection

The data were collected by face-to-face interveiw by the trained public health professional with a background education of physiotherapy having more than 10 years of experience in disability and rehabilitation. All the interviews were conducted in the Nephrology department either during dialysis sessions of the patient or during their admission in the inpatient ward.

The data were collected in two parts. In the first part, a semistructured questionnaire was used to collect the sociodemographic data of age, gender, education, employment, monthly income, place of residence, distance traveled, and disease profile, including duration of CKD and comorbidities (diabetes and hypertension). In the second part, the FS, social participation, and QoL were assessed using the following scales mentioned below:

Katz activity of daily living

It is a reliable and effective tool for assessing the patient’s ability to independently perform daily living activities. It ranks proficiency in six functions: bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, and feeding. A score of 1 indicates patients independently (able to do) perform their activity of daily living (ADL), and a score of 0 indicates patient’s dependence (not able to do) on others for their daily activity. A cumulative score of 6 indicates full capability, 4 indicates moderate impairment, and 2 or less implies severe impairment.[11]

The participation scale (P-scale)

The scale measures the level of social participation restriction. The score ranges from 0 to 90 points: scores from 0 to 12 indicate no restrictions, 13 to 22 indicate slight restrictions, 23 to 32 suggest moderate restrictions, 33 to 52 indicate serious restrictions, and scores from 53 to 90 signify severe restrictions. The participation scale is one of the most recent reliable and valid to measure social participation.[12]

Kidney Disease Quality of Life-Short Form questionnaire version 1.3 (KDQoL-SFTM 1.3)

It is a valid and reliable self-reported questionnaire used to measure the QoL in kidney disease and patients undergoing dialysis. It includes 24 questions that assess the QoL across three domains: PCS: physical component summary, MCS: mental component summary, and KDCS: kidney-disease component summary. The scoring procedure adds raw scores together and transforms them on a scale from 0 to 100. All scores are aligned where higher values indicate a better QoL. After explanation, the KDQoL-SF™ questionnaire was given to the patient, requesting them to complete it. Of 105 patients, 73 (70%) completed the questionnaire independently. For the remaining 33 (30%) patients, the questionnaire was filled out by the first author due to limitations related to education and physical activity.[1314]

Data analysis

All the statistical analayses were done using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (IBM Corporation’s SPSS version 26, based in Armonk, New York, USA). Categorical data are reported as proportions (percentage). In contrast, continuous data are described as mean and standard deviation (SD), or the interquartile range (IQR) depending on the distribution of the data. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to analyze the connection between KDQoL SF™, Katz FS, and social participation scores. A linear regression analysis was used to identify independent predictors of FS, social participation, and QoL. The accepted threshold level for significance in this study was set at 0.05 (P < 0.05 indicating significance).

Results

One hundred twenty ESRD patients undergoing maintenance HD visited the Nephrology OPD during the study period. Fifteen patients were excluded from the study [Figure 1]. One hundred and five patients were included in the study.

Flow chart outlining the inclusion and exclusion criteria

The mean age of HD patients was 45.8 ± 13.0 years. Sixty-four (61%) were male. Fifty percent (n = 52) had primary and secondary education, 24.8% (n = 26) were employed, and 44 (41.9%) belonged to the rural area. Twenty-eight (26.7%) patients had diabetes, and 100 (95.2%) had hypertension. The median monthly income was Rs. 8952 (IQR Rs. 3000–9000). The median duration of diagnosis of CKD was 40 months (IQR 12–48 months), and the median dialysis time was 20 months (IQR 4–36 months). Seventy-seven (73.3%) patients had thrice-weekly HD sessions; others had twice-weekly HD sessions. Only 34% (n = 36) of patients were undergoing dialysis in their district. The median distance traveled to avail dialysis was 92.3 km (IQR 10–110 km). Twenty-five (23.8%) patients relocated their residences to avail of HD [Table 1].

Discussion

FS is the individual’s ability to perform normal daily activities required to meet their basic needs, fulfil usual roles, and maintain health and wellbeing. This status is highly impaired (32%–95%) in the dialysis population.[151617] Social participation involves the involvement of a person in life situations, which include nine domains: learning and applying knowledge, communication, general tasks and demands, movement, self-care, interpersonal interactions, domestic life, social and civic life, and major life areas. The International Classification of Functioning and Disability in Health is based on the integrative bio-psycho-social model of body function, activity, and participation.[18] Centering this approach on the individual can facilitate the monitoring of care for patients undergoing HD. Maintaining good FS and social participation in HD patients is necessary, which are the key contributors to better HRQoL.[19] Our study shows that most HD patients had lower scores in Katz functional activity, social participation, and KDQoL SF. The study also shows only half of the patients are fully independent in their daily activities, almost all the patients have social participation restrictions, and the KDQoL scores were lower in all three domains of physical, mental, and kidney disease components summaries compared to the national and international studies [Table 5].[5132021222324252627282930]

The FS of daily living (ADL) is normally reduced in dialysis patients due to various disease-related complications, such as low physical activity and muscle weakness, and the decline in ADL is associated with increased mortality.[31] Most of the patients are dependent on their transferring and bathing. Rani and Kalia in Punjab (India) showed that 22% were slightly dependent, 35% moderately dependent, 8% were severely dependent, and 4% were dependent while performing the ADL.[32] In Turkey, Mollaoğlu and Baser found that 65.1% of patients maintain their daily activities independently, 26.2% are semidependent, and 8.7% are dependent on others.[22]

In the current study, most patients had restrictions in their social participation. Studies have reported that in dialysis patients, limited physical activity will lead to severe psychosocial problems like dependence on health workers, change of role in the family, restricted working life and social relations, change in body image, ambiguity about the future, despair, depression, and change in the activity of daily living.[33]

Our study findings show that the overall mean health rating score (KDQoL SF) was very low. The PCS was notably affected and had the lowest score among the three domains of KDQoL-SF. Similarly, studies by Nayana et al. (2017) and Manavalan et al. have also shown that the mean score for physical QoL was more compromised than the mental and kidney-disease-related aspects of QoL in dialysis patients from various regions in India.[2627]

In the current study, diabetes and distance traveled to access dialysis were the most common predictors associated with FS, participation, and overall quality of life of HD patients.[34] The tertiary care hospital is not a maintenance HD center. Patients receiving HD at various peripheral dialysis centers from distant places are referred to our tertiary care hospital for OPD follow-up due to the absence of nephrologists in their locations.[35] Chhattisgarh has a higher concentration of population in the rural belts than in the urban areas, so they have to travel a long distance to avail the dialysis facility. The advent of PMNDP with an aim to develop dialysis centers in district hospitals might address this issue.

The study findings observed a positive correlation between KDQoL overall health scores and Katz function activity scores and a negative correlation with participation scores. It reflects that if the overall health and FS scores increase, there will be a decrease in social participation restriction among HD patients (vice-versa).

The study had several limitations. First, it was cross-sectional and limited to a small sample size from a single center. Second, health-related QoL is multifactorial, and in this study, only a few factors were studied. Regarding strength, this is an important topic that has been reported in only a few studies. Longitudinal research is important for a better understanding and progression of the QoL of dialysis patients.

Conclusion

To conclude the study, patients on maintenance HD have poor functional activity, social participation, and QoL in central India. This study recommends FS, social participation, and QoL are important indicators in health, which should be measured periodically to find treatment/rehabilitation benefits in dialysis patients.

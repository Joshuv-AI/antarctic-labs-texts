Surgical management and long-term functional outcomes of traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Rajendra Aryal, Rajan Bhusal, Nitesh Raj Pandey, Ashok Kumar Banskota, Bibek Banskota
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSurgical management of traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis is essential for restoring pelvic stability, yet functional outcomes remain variable. This study aimed to evaluate the functional outcomes and identify predictors of recovery following this intervention at a Level 1 trauma centre in Nepal.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted on 69 patients with Anteroposterior Compression (APC) pelvic injuries who underwent surgical stabilization between 2006 and 2020. Data on demographics, injury characteristics, surgical details, and follow-up were collected. The primary outcome was functional status, measured using the Majeed Pelvic Score, dichotomized as favorable (> 85) or unfavorable (≤ 85). Multivariable binary logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors of a favourable outcome.ResultsThe mean age of participants was 44.1 years, and the majority were male (73.9%). Overall, 72.5% of patients achieved a favourable functional outcome. Multivariable analysis revealed that male sex was a significant negative predictor (OR = 0.043, 95% CI: 0.003–0.567, p = 0.017). Open fractures were also strongly associated with poor recovery (OR = 0.003, 95% CI: 0.000–0.318, p = 0.015). High-energy injury mechanisms, including two-wheeler crashes, four-wheeler collisions, and pedestrian accidents, were linked to higher odds of a good outcome compared with the reference. Paradoxically, patients undergoing combined anterior-posterior fixation had significantly higher odds of achieving favourable functional recovery than those undergoing anterior-only fixation (OR = 27.780, 95% CI: 1.666–463.298, p = 0.021), likely reflecting the greater severity of injuries requiring this approach.ConclusionWhile favourable functional outcomes are achievable in most patients with pubic symphysis diastasis, male sex, open fractures, and high-energy injury mechanisms are significant risk factors for poorer recovery. The finding regarding combined fixation likely indicates more severe initial injuries rather than a detrimental treatment effect. These factors should guide patient counselling, surgical planning, and postoperative rehabilitation to optimise results.Keywords: Pubic Symphysis Diastasis, Pelvic Fractures, Majeed Score, Internal Fixators, Nepal

Introduction

Pelvic ring injuries pose a major orthopaedic challenge, from stable fractures to complex, life-threatening disruptions. Their key role in load transmission and organ protection leads to high morbidity and mortality risks [1]. Traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis, often linked to Anteroposterior Compression (APC) injuries, signals significant pelvic instability and commonly involves posterior ligamentous or bony disruption [2, 3].

The incidence of high-energy pelvic ring injuries has shown an upward trend, often attributed to mechanisms such as motor vehicle collisions, falls from height, and crush injuries [4]. Isolated pubic symphysis diastasis is rare but indicates high-impact trauma. Its disruption critically affects pelvic stability, load transmission, and functional recovery [5]. Untreated or poorly managed symphyseal diastasis can cause pain, gait issues, sexual dysfunction, urogenital problems, and long-term disability, severely affecting quality of life and socioeconomic reintegration [6].

Diagnosis primarily relies on radiographic imaging, with plain radiographs (AP pelvic views, inlet, and outlet views) often revealing widening of the pubic symphysis beyond the conventional surgical threshold (typically > 2.5 cm) [7]. CT scans are essential for assessing posterior pelvic ring injuries, enabling accurate classification and treatment planning using systems such as the Young-Burgess or Tile classification systems, which guide stability and management decisions [8, 9]. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) may offer additional insights into soft-tissue and ligamentous integrity, though it is less frequently used in the acute setting due to time constraints and availability [10]. The consensus among trauma surgeons is that significant pubic symphysis diastasis, especially when associated with posterior pelvic instability (e.g., APC-II or APC-III injuries), necessitates surgical stabilisation to restore anatomical alignment and biomechanical stability [11].

Over recent decades, surgical approaches for pubic symphysis diastasis have evolved, including anterior plate fixation, external fixation, infix, and, less commonly, sacroiliac or sacral fixation [12]. Anterior plating, once standard for stabilising the symphysis and enabling early mobilisation, remains challenged by the risks of hardware failure, infection, nonunion, and persistent pain [13]. The choice of surgical technique often depends on the overall pelvic injury pattern, the extent of symphyseal displacement, concomitant injuries, and surgeon preference [14].

Despite surgical advances, outcomes of pubic symphysis diastasis remain variable, with many patients facing pain, functional issues, and complications like hardware problems or infection [15]. Studies have shown hardware failure rates ranging from 12% to 75%, although not all failures necessitate revision surgery [16]. Furthermore, there is ongoing debate regarding the optimal plate configuration (e.g., single vs. dual plate), material choice, and the necessity of addressing posterior pelvic ring instability concurrently with anterior fixation [17]. Long-term functional outcomes, such as gait, work return, and sexual function, are often inconsistent, highlighting gaps in understanding treatment efficacy. This study evaluates outcomes of pubic symphysis diastasis—recovery, complications, and quality of life—to improve clinical management.

Methodology

Study design and setting

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at B & B Hospital, a tertiary care institution in Nepal designated as a super-specialised Level 1 trauma center. This designation signifies that it provides the highest level of comprehensive, 24/7 trauma care and serves as the central referral hub for the most severe injuries, with particular sub-specialist expertise in complex orthopaedic trauma. With over 25 years of focused experience, it is a leading national centre for the treatment of high-energy musculoskeletal injuries. The hospital’s specialized capacity is evidenced by its management of approximately 80–120 pelvic and acetabular fractures annually, with 50–65% of these cases undergoing surgical intervention [18].

Within the Department of Orthopaedics, the dedicated Hip–Pelvis–Acetabulum (HPA) Unit employs standardised, evidence-based clinical protocols for all pelvic trauma cases [19]. These protocols guide systematic assessment, classification (including patterns such as anterior-posterior compression, or APC, injuries), and definitive treatment, ensuring a consistent, high-quality approach to care for all patients in this study cohort.

Participants

Between January 2006 and December 2020, 172 patients were diagnosed with APC pelvic ring injuries, classified according to the Young and Burgess system into types I, II, and III [20]. Of these, 87 (50.6%) underwent surgical stabilization with anterior and/or posterior plating, dynamic compression, or reconstruction plates. Inclusion criteria required patients to be ≥ 18 years, have APC-classified traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis, and have a minimum 12-month follow-up. Exclusions included incomplete records, deaths during emergency care, and non-traumatic or pediatric cases. After excluding seven natural deaths and 11 lost to follow-up, 69 patients met the criteria for final analysis (Fig. 1).

Participants of the study

Data collection

Data were retrospectively extracted from hospital records using a structured proforma by a team including senior Orthopaedic surgeons and trained nurses. Collected data covered demographics, injury mechanisms, fracture types, associated injuries, and Young and Burgess classification. Surgical details included implant type, plating configuration, fixation approach, implant failure, and time to surgery. Functional outcomes were measured with the validated Majeed Pelvic Score to assess recovery after pelvic injury [21]. Follow-up assessment was conducted through face-to-face clinical evaluations for patients attending follow-up visits and via structured telephone interviews using standardised questionnaires for those unable to attend in person [22]. To ensure reliability, three orthopaedic surgeons and two trained nurses independently conducted the functional assessments. Additionally, radiological evaluations—focusing on fracture union, malunion, implant failure, and hardware integrity—were performed and verified by two independent orthopaedic practitioners who were blinded to the functional outcome scores.

Measures

Independent variables

The independent variables considered in this study encompassed demographic, injury-related, surgical, and follow-up characteristics. Demographic variables included age, recorded as a continuous variable, and sex, categorized as male or female. Injury characteristics involved the type of fracture (open or closed), mechanism of injury (classified as 2-wheeler, 4-wheeler, pedestrian, or other), presence or absence of associated injuries (including urological, vascular, or other system involvement), and classification of the injury according to the Young and Burgess system (APC types I, II, or III). Surgical variables included the type of implant used—either dynamic compression plates (DCP) or reconstruction plates—the implant configuration (single vs. dual plating), the fixation approach (anterior-only or combined anterior-posterior), and posterior fixation, when performed, involved sacroiliac screw fixation or plating, and the occurrence of implant failure (yes/no). Additionally, follow-up duration was treated as a continuous variable, measured in months.

Dependent variable

The primary dependent variable in this study was the functional outcome, assessed using the Majeed Pelvic Score, which has a maximum possible score of 100 [23]. For inferential analysis, the scores were dichotomized into two categories: a favorable outcome was defined as a Majeed score greater than 85, while an unfavorable outcome was defined as a score of 85 or less [24].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Version 26.0. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the baseline characteristics of the study population, with continuous variables reported as means ± standard deviation (SD) and categorical variables presented as frequencies and percentages. Before conducting parametric analyses, the normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test [25]. A multivariable binary logistic regression analysis was carried out [25] to identify independent predictors of favorable functional outcomes. In the SPSS binary logistic regression model, the outcome variable was coded as 1 = unfavorable recovery (Majeed score ≤ 85) and 2 = favorable recovery (Majeed score > 85). The model was specified to predict the last (highest-coded) category; accordingly, the event modelled is favourable functional recovery (Majeed score > 85). All odds ratios (ORs) greater than 1 therefore indicate higher odds of achieving a favorable outcome relative to the stated reference category, and ORs less than 1 indicate lower odds of a favorable outcome. Results from the regression analysis were reported as adjusted Odds Ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% Confidence Intervals (CIs) and p-values; p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical considerations

This study was conducted in adherence to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the B&B Institutional Review Committee (IRC) (Reg: IRC_2021_12_27_2) of B&B Hospital before initiation. Given the study’s retrospective nature and the use of de-identified clinical records, the requirement for written informed consent was waived. Nevertheless, verbal consent was obtained from all participants (or their legally authorised representatives) contacted via telephone for functional outcome assessments. Data confidentiality and participant anonymity were strictly maintained, and all data were stored in encrypted, password-protected systems accessible only to authorised research personnel.

Results

Table 1 shows that 69 patients were included in the study. The mean age of the cohort was 44.14 ± 14.89 years. The majority of patients were male (n = 51, 73.9%), while 18 patients (26.1%) were female. Regarding the fracture characteristics, closed fractures were predominant (n = 65, 94.2%), with open fractures accounting for 4 cases (5.8%). The most common mechanism of injury was involvement in a 2-wheeler accident (n = 29, 42.0%), followed by falls from height (n = 18, 26.1%), 4-wheeler accidents (n = 12, 17.4%), and pedestrian accidents (n = 10, 14.5%). Associated injuries were present in 48 patients (69.6%). Among the specific types of associated injuries reported, combined injuries were the most frequent (n = 14, 20.3%), followed by chest injuries (n = 12, 17.4%), other bony injuries (n = 8, 11.6%), head injuries (n = 6, 8.7%), genito-urinary injuries (n = 5, 7.2%), and abdominal injuries (n = 3, 4.3%). Twenty-one patients (30.4%) had no associated injuries.

The overall mechanism of injury was not statistically significant (p = 0.087); however, specific mechanisms were associated with poorer functional recovery. Injuries from two-wheeler crashes (p = 0.026, OR = 14.563, 95% CI: 1.375–154.180), four-wheeler collisions (p = 0.050, OR = 17.215, 95% CI: 1.000–296.367), and pedestrian accidents (p = 0.016, OR = 38.145, 95% CI: 1.964–740.802) were associated with higher odds of good functional recovery. Duration of follow-up was not significantly associated with functional outcome (p = 0.994, OR = 1.004, 95% CI: 0.412–2.445).

Implant type (p = 0.644) and implant configuration (p = 0.526) were not significantly associated with functional recovery. Patients who underwent combined anterior and posterior fixation had significantly higher odds of achieving favourable functional recovery than those who underwent anterior fixation alone (p = 0.021, OR = 27.780, 95% CI: 1.666–463.298). Implant failure was not significantly associated with functional recovery (p = 0.344, OR = 0.307, 95% CI: 0.027–3.540). Fracture classification (APC II vs. APC III) approached statistical significance (p = 0.070, OR = 6.203, 95% CI: 0.861–44.683), while the presence of associated injuries did not reach significance (p = 0.106, OR = 5.052, 95% CI: 0.710–35.961).

Discussion

This study represents one of the largest single-centre cohorts evaluating functional outcomes following surgical management of traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis in a resource-limited setting. This study reports a favorable overall functional success rate of 72.5% following surgical management of traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis in a resource-limited setting, a figure comparable to outcomes reported in higher-resource environments [26]. The typical patient in this Nepalese cohort was a middle-aged male, sustaining a closed fracture most often from a two-wheeler accident and frequently presenting with associated injuries, reflecting the high-energy trauma patterns prevalent in such contexts. The predominant surgical strategy utilized was anterior fixation with a single reconstruction plate. Importantly, open fractures, male sex, high-energy injury mechanisms such as two-wheeler and pedestrian accidents, and the use of a combined anterior-posterior surgical approach were all significantly associated with altered odds of achieving a good-to-excellent functional outcome (male sex and open fractures with lower odds; high-energy mechanisms and combined fixation with higher odds relative to their respective reference categories), findings that are discussed in detail below.

Perhaps our most striking finding was the significant association between male sex and inferior functional outcomes. The odds of achieving a favorable outcome were 95.7% lower in males than in females (OR = 0.043, 95% CI: 0.003–0.567, p = 0.017). This finding appears paradoxical given that males comprised 73.9% of our cohort and typically present with higher-energy mechanisms of injury. Several biomechanical and physiological factors may explain this observation. Males demonstrate greater pelvic bone density than females across most age ranges [15]. Yet, recent evidence suggests that males exhibit less robust bone healing than females in adult populations, attributed to differences in sex hormone levels, inflammatory milieu composition, and cellular mechanisms [16]. Furthermore, specific anatomical regions such as the pubic body show significant sex-dependent variations in cortical bone density, which may influence implant purchase and fracture healing potential [27]. The higher mechanical demands placed on the male pelvis during occupational and physical activities, combined with potentially delayed healing responses, may collectively contribute to the observed functional disparity. These findings challenge conventional assumptions and suggest that male patients may benefit from enhanced, protocol-driven rehabilitation and closer functional monitoring.

Our data revealed that open fractures were associated with a dramatically reduced odds of achieving good functional recovery (OR = 0.003, 95% CI: 0.000–0.318, p = 0.015), despite their relatively low prevalence in our series (5.8%). This is consistent with extensive literature documenting the devastating nature of open pelvic fractures [12, 28]. Open pelvic fractures are characterized by massive blood loss due to loss of containment and infectious complications, with mortality rates historically reported around 50% [28]. In our surviving cohort, 75% of patients with open fractures (3 of 4) achieved unfavourable outcomes, likely reflecting the extensive soft tissue damage, contamination risk, and prolonged healing times associated with these injuries. Recent data demonstrate that while mortality may not differ significantly between open and closed pelvic fractures with modern aggressive management, infection rates remain substantially elevated in open fractures [29]. The combination of compromised soft tissue envelope, increased infection risk, and the frequent need for staged surgical interventions in open fractures creates a challenging scenario for optimal functional recovery. Our findings emphasize the critical importance of meticulous initial debridement, appropriate antimicrobial coverage, and judicious timing of definitive fixation in this high-risk population.

Our study shows an association between specific injury mechanisms and poorer functional outcomes. Two-wheeler crashes (OR = 14.563, p = 0.026), four-wheeler collisions (OR = 17.215, p = 0.050), and pedestrian accidents (OR = 38.145, p = 0.016) were all associated with significantly lower odds of good functional recovery compared to fall from height. These high-energy mechanisms typically involve greater force vectors and are associated with more extensive pelvic ring disruption and associated injuries. In our cohort, 69.6% of patients presented with associated injuries, predominantly involving the chest (17.4%) and combined injuries (20.3%). The polytrauma burden inherent to vehicular and pedestrian injuries may prolong hospitalization, delay mobilization, and complicate rehabilitation efforts. Furthermore, the biomechanical forces in anteroposterior compression mechanisms can cause occult posterior pelvic injuries that may not be fully appreciated on initial imaging, potentially contributing to residual instability and chronic pain syndromes [30].

Patients who underwent combined anterior and posterior fixation had higher odds of achieving favourable functional recovery (OR = 27.780, 95% CI: 1.666–463.298, p = 0.021) compared to anterior fixation alone. This finding appears to contradict several clinical studies This finding appears to contradict several clinical studies [31]. Studies reported that posterior fixation of APC-2 injuries significantly decreases the rates of anterior plate failure and malunion. At the same time, numerous authors have advocated addressing partial breaches of posterior pelvic ring elements in APC-II injuries [32]. However, our result likely reflects indication bias rather than the true treatment effect. Surgeons at our institution selectively employed combined fixation for more severe injuries, particularly 40.6% of patients with APC-III injuries or those with significant posterior element compromise. These patients inherently face worse prognoses due to greater initial injury severity, more extensive soft tissue damage, and prolonged surgical time with increased blood loss. Contemporary evidence supports the notion that when posterior ring disruptions are adequately stabilized simultaneously, APC-III results become comparable to APC-II outcomes [33]. Our findings should not be interpreted as an argument against posterior fixation when indicated, but rather as a reflection of the more severe injury patterns that necessitate comprehensive stabilization. This interpretation is further supported by the clinical profile of these patients, who presented with more complex injury patterns, as evidenced by a higher rate of associated injuries and open fractures. The lack of Injury Severity Score (ISS) data, a recognized limitation of our retrospective design, precludes a more granular quantification of this polytrauma burden; however, the documented injury characteristics substantiate the principle that combined anterior-posterior fixation was employed in cases of greater overall severity.

Implant failure occurred in 15.9% of our series, which falls within the broad range reported in the literature. Failure rates following pubic plate fixation range from 12% to 75%, with significant loss of symphyseal reduction in 7% to 88% of cases [34]. Interestingly, implant failure did not reach statistical significance as a predictor of functional outcome in our multivariable analysis (p = 0.344). This aligns with emerging evidence that radiological signs of metalwork movement, while common, are not in themselves indications for revision surgery [11, 35]. Recent data suggest that loss of anatomic reduction may be more clinically relevant than fixation failure itself, with mean Majeed scores of 59.1 in patients with loss of reduction compared to 81.8 in those without failure. The relatively low revision rate of 1.6% to 9% despite high failure rates supports the notion that many patients achieve satisfactory function even with radiographic evidence of failure [11, 35]. However, patients with fixation failure and loss of reduction demonstrate significantly worse outcomes than those with failure but maintained reduction [34], suggesting that maintenance of anatomic alignment remains the critical factor rather than implant integrity per se.

Study limitations and strengths

However, our study possesses notable strengths. This represents one of the largest single-centre series from a Level 1 trauma Centre in South Asia with 25 years of expertise in complex pelvic trauma, providing valuable insights into outcomes achievable in resource-constrained settings. The use of standardized, evidence-based clinical protocols within a dedicated Hip-Pelvis-Acetabulum Unit ensures consistency of care and enhances internal validity.

Study limitations includes, the retrospective design spanning 14 years inherently carries risks of selection and information bias. Crucially, this timeframe also precluded the reliable calculation of the Injury Severity Score (ISS), as the necessary data were not consistently documented in the patient records; the absence of this metric limits our ability to uniformly assess and control for overall trauma severity across the cohort. Second, our mean follow-up duration of 6.67 ± 1.72 years, while adequate for assessing mid-term outcomes, may not capture late complications or long-term functional deterioration. And the loss of 11 patients (12.6%) to follow-up introduces the possibility of attrition bias if their outcomes differed systematically from those of patients who completed the study.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that favourable functional outcomes can be achieved in the majority of patients with traumatic pubic symphysis diastasis treated at a specialized trauma centre in Nepal. However, male sex, open fractures, high-energy mechanisms of injury, and severe injury patterns requiring combined fixation are associated with higher adjusted odds of a favourable outcome, a finding that reflects indication bias rather than a true treatment benefit. These findings should inform patient counselling, surgical decision-making, and rehabilitation planning. While combined anterior-posterior fixation showed paradoxically higher adjusted odds of a favourable outcome; this likely reflects indication bias rather than a true treatment advantage, and biomechanically-guided fixation strategies remain essential for optimizing pelvic ring stability.

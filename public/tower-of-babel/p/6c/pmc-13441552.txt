Associations Between Serum Resolvin D1 Levels and Food Allergy Persistence in Children
Authors: Mireu Park, Haerin Jang, Sumin Lee, Eun Gyul Kim, Mi Na Kim, Hamin Kim, Soo Yeon Kim, Lark Kyun Kim, Jong Deok Kim, Yong Ju Lee, Min Jung Kim, Yoon Hee Kim, Myung Hyun Sohn, Kyung Won Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe pathophysiology and natural course of childhood food allergies (FAs) remains largely elusive. Recent findings have implicated resolvins, omega-3 metabolites, in the pathogenesis and resolution of FAs, highlighting their potential clinical significance. This investigation aimed to elucidate the clinical implications of resolvins in the manifestation and resolution of childhood FAs.MethodsSerum samples were collected from a cohort of children diagnosed with FA and healthy controls upon their first visit to the Severance Children’s Hospital (Seoul, Korea). FA persistence was assessed for each patient based on follow-up records. Resolvin D1 (RvD1) levels were measured and their correlation with total immunoglobulin E (IgE) and allergen-specific IgE levels were evaluated.ResultsThis study included 278 children (mean age 3.28 ± 3.17 years; 62% male), consisting of 92 children with resolved FA (resolved FA group), 113 children with persistent FA (persistent FA group), and 73 children (control group). RvD1 levels were significantly higher in children with FA than in controls (P < 0.001), and were significantly higher in the persistent FA group than in the resolved FA group (P = 0.045). A positive correlation was found between total IgE and RvD1 levels (r = 0.32, P < 0.001), particularly in the persistent FA subgroup. Furthermore, the correlation between RvD1 and total IgE was stronger in children with multiple FAs (r = 0.367, P < 0.001). RvD1 levels were also positively correlated with egg- and milk-specific IgE levels, especially in the persistent FA group.ConclusionsOur findings suggest that RvD1 is a candidate metabolite associated with the persistence of childhood FA, across various food allergens and may provide complementary information to existing clinical features.Keywords: Food hypersensitivity, child, resolvin D1, biomarkers

INTRODUCTION

Food allergies (FAs) affect up to 10% of children in many parts of the world and have increased over the last few decades.1 FA can cause life-threatening reactions and affect quality of life and well-being of both patients and their families due to dietary and social limitations.2 Adding to the challenge, the natural course of childhood FA widely varies across patients, making effective disease management difficult. Some children outgrow their symptoms at a young age, whereas others have persistent FA throughout life.3,4 For that reason, childhood FA should be monitored over time to determine when to reintroduce the culprit food into their diets or when to introduce oral immunotherapy (OIT).5 Opportunities to accurately predict FA resolution remain limited due to an incomplete understanding of its pathophysiology and progression. Studies on potential biomarkers based on biological mechanisms underlying FA resolution are imperative to better predict the course of FA and determine treatment options.

Various factors are known to be associated with FA resolution, including the type and number of causative food allergens and the severity of symptoms.6 Several attempts have been made to predict resolution using the number of FAs and allergen-specific immunoglobulin E (IgE) levels; however, their accuracy remains controversial.7 Our previous metabolomics study of serum identified increased levels of resolvins in children with persistent FA (n = 25) compared to those with resolved FA (n = 23).8 Resolvins are omega-3-derived metabolites known to be anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving modulators promoting immune tolerance.9 Resolvin D1 (RvD1) has been shown to modulate allergic airway inflammation by suppressing eosinophilic infiltration and pro-inflammatory mediators, while enhancing macrophage-mediated clearance of allergens.10 In chronic allergic eye disease, topical RvD1 application has been shown to reduce ocular allergic responses and mucin secretion from goblet cells.11

This study aimed to expand on these findings by assessing resolvin levels in a broader pediatric population with FA. We focused on the clinical implications of resolvins, particularly RvD1, in the manifestation and resolution of FA in children. Clinical factors derived from hematological and serological analyses were explored to assess their associations with RvD1 levels in the context of allergic inflammation and FA persistence.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study population

This study recruited children aged < 18 years who were diagnosed with FA by pediatric allergists at Severance Children’s Hospital (Seoul, Korea) between January 2010 and December 2019. FA was diagnosed based on confirmed allergic sensitization, as determined by specific IgE levels of > 0.7 kU/L for food allergens, and a history of IgE-mediated symptoms developing within 2 hours of food ingestion. The healthy control group consisted of age-matched children without a history of allergic disease or evidence of allergic sensitization. All subjects were unrelated and their caregivers provided written informed consent. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Severance Hospital (Seoul, Korea; approval No. 4-2022-1040).

For children with FA, information on age, sex, age at diagnosis, FA-related symptoms, and the type and number of causative allergens was collected. Information on allergic comorbidities, including asthma, allergic rhinitis, allergic conjunctivitis, and atopic dermatitis (AD), was also collected. FA and AD severity were assessed using the grading system established by Niggemann and Beyer12 and SCORing Atopic Dermatitis,13 respectively. Resolution of FA was defined as the loss of both local and systemic symptoms after ingesting foods that had previously caused FA. Resolution was confirmed by either oral food challenges or by caregiver-reported evidence.

Sample collection and serological assessments

Serum samples were collected from study participants during their initial hospital visit. Complete blood counts, including eosinophil counts, were measured using the ADVIA 2120i hematology system with Autoslide (Siemens Healthcare Diagnostics Inc., Deerfield, IL, USA). Total and specific IgE levels were assessed using the Pharmacia CAP assay (Pharmacia Diagnostics AB, Uppsala, Sweden), with specific IgE testing conducted for allergens considered relevant by the clinicians. Samples were subsequently stored at −20°C. RvD1 levels were measured by using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (MyBioSource, San Diego, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistics

χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact test were used to analyze categorical variables, and Student’s t-tests and analysis of variance were used to analyze continuous variables. Partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was used to identify clinical factors that distinguished the persistent and resolved FA groups. Pearson’s correlation analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between RvD1 and clinical factors. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), SAS (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA), and R (version 4.0.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RvD1 levels in FA

As shown in Fig. 1, RvD1 levels were significantly higher in children with FA than in controls (P < 0.001). The mean RvD1 level was 607.16 ± 799.89 pg/mL in children with FA, and 265.28 ± 132.42 pg/mL in controls. Further, the persistent FA group demonstrated significantly higher RvD1 levels (700.13 ± 920.59 pg/mL) than the resolved FA group (492.97 ± 606.09 pg/mL).

Clinical factors associated with FA resolution

PLS-DA was performed using clinical factors to explore differences between children with persistent and resolved FA. Allergen-specific IgE levels were excluded to focus on clinical factors independent of specific food allergens. As shown in Fig. 2, the PLS-DA score plot demonstrated discriminated clinical factors between patients with persistent and resolved FA, with an explained variation of 15.0% by principal component 1 (PC1) and 6.0% by PC2. Multiple FAs, age at hospital visit, and total serum IgE levels had the highest variable importance in projection scores, indicating that they were the most discriminative factors between the 2 groups and suggesting a potential association with FA resolution.

Correlation between clinical factors associated with FA resolution and RvD1

We analyzed the correlations between RvD1 levels and total IgE levels/the presence of multiple FAs, which are clinically informative features related to FA persistence. A significant positive correlation was observed between total IgE levels and RvD1 (r = 0.32, P < 0.001). This correlation was particularly noticeable in the persistent FA group (r = 0.286, P = 0.002) (Fig. 3). Additionally, children with multiple FAs exhibited a stronger positive correlation between RvD1 and total IgE (r = 0.367, P < 0.001), than those with a single FA (Fig. 4). Correlations with specific IgE levels were also assessed (Table 3), and significant positive correlations were found between RvD1 and egg- and milk-specific IgE levels, especially within the persistent FA group.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we analyzed RvD1 levels in a large cohort of children with FA to evaluate the clinical relevance of RvD1 in relation to FA persistence and resolution. We found that RvD1 levels were significantly elevated in children with FA, particularly in those with persistent FA. We also identified significant correlations between RvD1 and clinical factors linked to FA persistence, including egg- and milk-specific IgE levels, total IgE levels, and multiple FAs.

In line with our previous study, RvD1 levels were significantly higher in the FA group than in the control group. This observation aligns with recent research showing increased RvD1 levels in response to allergic reactions,10,11,14,15,16 including evidence that mast cells release RvD1 during experimentally induced allergic inflammation.16 Allergic diseases, including FA, are chronic inflammatory conditions that result from excessive immune responses to otherwise harmless substances and may persist when mechanisms that resolve inflammation are impaired.17 One of the mechanisms for resolving this inflammation may be the production of resolvins.9 Our findings suggest that increased resolvin levels in children with FA may reflect a compensatory, pro-resolving response to ongoing inflammation, consistent with mechanisms reported in other allergic diseases.

Our study also found that RvD1 levels were significantly higher in persistent FA group than in resolved FA group. Considering the associations between RvD1, total IgE levels, and multiple FAs, these elevated RvD1 levels may reflect more severe or sustained allergic immune responses in children with persistent FA. In a previous study, human and murine mast cells were found to release a significant amount of resolvins after IgE-mediated activation.16 Also, in a murine model of asthma, pulmonary expression of the RvD1 receptor was increased.18 Building on these findings, the current study suggests that RvD1 is closely linked to the pathophysiology of FA and may represent a downstream response to chronic allergic activation.

Numerous attempts have been made to predict FA resolution using various factors, including the type and number of causative allergens, comorbid allergic diseases, symptom severity, and allergen-specific IgE levels; however, their predictive accuracy remains debated.6,7,19 In our study, PLS-DA analysis identified multiple FAs, age at hospital visit, and total IgE levels as the factors most strongly associated with the principal component separating children with resolved FA and persistent FA, followed by a history of anaphylaxis. This finding is consistent with those of previous studies. A Japanese birth cohort reported that age at the first hospital visit, incidence of anaphylaxis, and food-specific IgE levels were important predictors of FA resolution.20 Additionally, a greater number of allergen sensitizations has been linked to an increased risk of severe reactions and delayed FA resolution.21 More recently, baseline total IgE levels have also been proposed as predictors of tolerance acquisition in children with FA.22 Despite these insights, there are currently no definitive clinical indicator or biomarkers for predicting FA resolution in real-world clinical practice.23

In this context, our study found significant positive correlations between RvD1 and key clinical features associated with FA persistence, including total IgE levels, multiple FAs, and allergen-specific IgE levels. These correlations were particularly pronounced in the persistent FA group compared to the resolved FA group. These findings suggest that higher RvD1 levels are associated with more severe and persistent FA and may reflect a compensatory response to ongoing allergic inflammation, with potential relevance as an adjunctive metabolite for assessing FA persistence independent of the specific food allergens. Assessing RvD1 at the time of initial FA diagnosis may provide supplementary information that could help inform early decisions regarding follow-up intervals, consideration of food reintroduction or OIT, and the timing of repeat serological assessments. Notably, because RvD1 is not dependent on the specific causative food allergen, it may help clinicians estimate the likelihood of persistent disease and support individualized approaches to follow-up and interventions.

This study has several limitations that should be considered. First, the sample size limited our ability to conduct detailed analyses of RvD1 levels according to individual food allergens. Secondly, variability in the interval between symptom onset and diagnostic evaluation among participants prevented us from isolating the specific impact of age at onset on FA resolution. Importantly, this variability is unlikely to directly influence the predictive relationship between RvD1 levels and FA outcomes. Thirdly, while lipid metabolites like resolvins can be influenced by dietary intake, it was not feasible to control for variability in diet or dietary restrictions among participants. Since this study focused on the association between RvD1 levels and FA persistence, future prospective cohort studies are needed to address these limitations and validate our findings.

In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that RvD1 is associated with the persistence of childhood FA across various food allergens. Given the lack of definitive clinical indicators or validated biomarkers for FA prognosis, these findings suggest that RvD1 may be a relevant candidate metabolite reflecting disease persistence. In this context, RvD1 may offer complementary information to existing clinical features when evaluating FA prognosis and support individualized follow-up and management strategies. To further clarify these associations, prospective longitudinal studies with comprehensive control of confounding factors are required.

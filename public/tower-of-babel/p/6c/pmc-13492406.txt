Refining the Prognostic Interpretation of Myocardial PD-L1 Expression in ICI-Associated Myocarditis
Authors: Shangxuan Li, Yi Wang, Zekai Yu, Lu Yin, Dianyuan Li
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the study by Imaoka et al,1 which showed that myocardial programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression was associated with inflammatory burden, peak troponin level, QRS prolongation, and 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events in biopsy-proven immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)–associated myocarditis. This work provides valuable tissue-level evidence for risk stratification in a rare but potentially fatal cardiovascular complication. However, 2 issues may benefit from further clarification.

First, the prognostic value of the 8% PD-L1 cutoff requires cautious interpretation. Although the threshold was derived by receiver operating characteristic analysis and supported by an alternative log-rank approach, the cohort included only 20 patients and 6 events.1 Such a small event number may make cutoff selection vulnerable to optimism and event-driven instability. Because a biopsy-based threshold could influence monitoring intensity and early therapeutic escalation, future multicenter validation is needed. It would also be important to test whether PD-L1 adds incremental value beyond established clinical markers, including troponin levels, QRS duration, left ventricular ejection fraction, and concomitant myositis.

Second, the biological meaning of myocardial PD-L1 expression remains complex. Imaoka et al identified PD-L1 expression in macrophages, cardiomyocytes, and endothelial cells, with higher PD-L1+ macrophages and cardiomyocytes in patients with adverse events. However, total PD-L1 expression may combine biologically distinct signals. In ICI-associated myocarditis, PD-L1 up-regulation could reflect compensatory immune regulation, interferon-driven inflammation, or secondary tissue injury. Future studies integrating spatial profiling, multiplex immune phenotyping, cytokine signatures, and injury markers may help determine whether PD-L1 is a mediator of disease progression, a protective response, or a surrogate of severe myocardial damage.2,3

Overall, this study highlights myocardial PD-L1 expression as a promising but still exploratory marker in ICI-associated myocarditis. Independent validation and compartment-specific mechanistic analysis would strengthen its prognostic and translational interpretation.

Rectovesical Fistula Following Esophageal Rupture and Sepsis
Authors: Yiqi Qian, Jincheng Zhang, Wei Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis is a rare case of rectovesical fistula following repair of esophageal rupture in a critically ill patient. Sepsis and prior prostatectomy may have contributed. CT cystography confirmed the diagnosis, and initial fecal diversion controlled inflammation. Longitudinal follow‐up and staged management are essential to guide definitive treatment and improve outcomes.Keywords: CT cystography, fecaluria, rectovesical fistula, sepsis

In July 2023, a 62‐year‐old man presented to the emergency department with a 15‐h history of severe chest pain following episodes of vomiting. A diagnosis of esophageal rupture was established. The patient underwent immediate surgical repair under general anesthesia. The procedure was complicated by significant intraoperative hypotension, necessitating vasopressor support with norepinephrine. He was subsequently admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) for postoperative management. Past medical history was significant for coronary artery disease with prior percutaneous coronary intervention, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and prostatectomy for a benign tumor five years earlier.

During his ICU stay, vasopressor support was successfully weaned. A bloodstream infection with Streptococcus salivarius was identified and treated with vancomycin. One week postoperatively, the patient was noted to have turbid, fecal‐appearing urine (Figure 1A). A computed tomography (CT) cystogram was performed, which revealed definitive contrast extravasation from the bladder into the rectal lumen (Figure 1B), confirming the diagnosis of rectovesical fistula. Following multidisciplinary surgical consultation, a diverting loop colostomy was performed. The patient recovered well and was eventually discharged. Telephone follow‐up revealed that the patient was found to have a persistent rectovesical fistula and subsequently underwent staged surgical management at another hospital, including fistula repair in November 2024 and colostomy closure in October 2025. Post‐treatment imaging from the outside hospital was not available for inclusion in this report.

(A) Fecaluria observed in the urinary catheter bag. (B) Computed tomography (CT) cystogram image after instillation of diluted iodixanol into the bladder, demonstrating contrast opacification within the rectal lumen (arrow), confirming the diagnosis of a rectovesical fistula.

A rectovesical fistula is an uncommon but serious abnormal communication between the rectum and the urinary bladder. Typical etiologies include diverticulitis, pelvic malignancy, radiation therapy, and iatrogenic injury [1]. This case is noteworthy because the fistula developed as an unusual complication following esophageal surgery in a critically ill patient. Contributing factors likely included episodes of sepsis and hemodynamic instability, which may have compromised pelvic perfusion. Prior prostatectomy may have increased anatomical vulnerability. CT cystography is a highly sensitive and non‐invasive modality for confirming the diagnosis [2]. Initial management typically focuses on infection control and fecal diversion, as performed in this case, with the colostomy allowing for resolution of acute inflammation. Once the patient's overall condition has stabilized, a definitive staged surgical approach, including fistula repair followed by colostomy closure, should be considered to improve quality of life and achieve more definitive clinical resolution, highlighting the importance of continued longitudinal follow‐up in such patients [3].

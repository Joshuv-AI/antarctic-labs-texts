Clinical prognostic prediction of MMP13 expression in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma using contrast-enhanced computed tomography radiomics: a model development and validation study
Authors: Lu Xu, Jinyi Huang, Chenliang Xiao, Xuda Zhao, Ruiqian Zhao, Fang Wang, Yaguang Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnnually, more than 550,000 people are diagnosed with head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) using invasive techniques, emphasizing the need for non-invasive diagnostic methods. Thus, our investigation aimed to create radiomics models that could forecast patients’ MMP13 expression levels.MethodsThis study was based on downloading genomic data and enhanced computed tomography (CT) images of HNSCC patients from The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) databases for prognostic analyses, image feature extraction, and construction of the radiomics models. Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier survival curve, COX regression analysis, subgroup analysis, and interaction test) was used to explore the prognostic value of MMP13 in HNSCC. Correlation analysis, differential analysis of immune cell infiltration and enrichment analysis were used to explore the potential molecular mechanisms of CXCL8 expression and its relationship with the immune microenvironment. Radiomics models were constructed and Rad-score-based prediction of MMP13 and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) expression in HNSCC tissues was performed. Finally, intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to evaluate the consistency of radiomics features.ResultsTCGA had a total of 483 HNSCC patients, of which high (n=326) and low (n=157) MMP13-expressing groups were used in the survival analysis. The MMP13 high-expression and low-expression groups had respective median survival times of 36.43 and 65.73 months. Tumors expressed MMP13 at a substantially greater level than normal tissue. We established the Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model and the logistic regression (LR) model, respectively. The area under the curve (AUC) value in the training set was 0.864 (GBM) and 0.746 (LR), and in the validation set was 0.79 (GBM) and 0.73 (LR). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and calibration curve both showed consistency (P>0.05) between the true and predicted values in GBM model. The decision curve analysis (DCA) display model exhibited good clinical practicality in both models.ConclusionsA noteworthy association was observed between MMP13 expression and the prediction of HNSCC. Predicting MMP13 expression levels may be accomplished with the use of radiomics, which is based on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT).Keywords: Radiomics, contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), MMP13, prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study established and validated contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT)-based radiomics models for non-invasively predicting MMP13 expression in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). We also observed a significant correlation between the expression of MMP13 and HNSCC prognosis.

What is known and what is new?

• MMP13 is known to be overexpressed in multiple cancers and linked to poor prognosis, but its detection has relied on invasive tissue sampling.

• This study introduces a non-invasive radiomics approach using routine CT imaging to predict MMP13 messenger RNA expression in HNSCC, immune microenvironment, and clinical data to validate its prognostic relevance.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This radiomics model enables dynamic, non-invasive monitoring of MMP13 expression, facilitating early risk stratification and personalized treatment planning. It may help identify candidates for future MMP13-targeted therapies and reduce reliance on invasive biopsies. Clinical adoption of this tool could improve prognostic accuracy and support precision oncology in HNSCC management.

Introduction

In 2018, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) accounted for 450,000 deaths and 890,000 new cases globally, making it ranking the sixth in terms of cancer incidence. It is anticipated that by 2023, there would be 1.08 million new cases of HNSCC annually (1). The mainstay of treatment for patients with HNSCC is presently surgery plus radiation and chemotherapy; nonetheless, the 5-year survival rate remains below 50% (2).

The clinical requirements of precision medicine are no longer met by traditional methods of predicting the prognosis of HNSCC, such as the utilization of clinicopathological characteristics, human papillomavirus (HPV), or imaging investigations (3). Thus, additional investigation into novel prognostic biomarkers is required to yield new markers for targeted and accurate treatment.

MMP13 belongs to the peptidase M10 family, encoding matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs). It is essential for the extracellular matrix to break down during regular physiological processes such as tissue remodeling, reproduction, and embryonic development. Additionally, MMP13 is implicated in diseases such as arthritis and tumor metastasis (4,5). Several investigations have revealed that the high incidence and mortality rate of HNSCC primarily result from the tumor’s heightened invasion and migration capacity, with MMP13 being closely related to HNSCC invasion (6). MMP13 promotes the development of various tumors (7,8). Gibson et al. found an association between MMP13 expression and matrix invasion in a mouse model of ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) progression. Furthermore, in clinical high-grade DCIS cases, myoepithelial cells were found to exhibit increased MMP13 expression. Experimental findings by Gibson et al. affirmed the key role of MMP13 produced by myoepithelial cells in promoting DCIS progression (9). Anaplastic thyroid cancer, or undifferentiated thyroid carcinoma (ATC), has been shown by Ma et al. to be elevated in MMP3 and MMP13. Furthermore, ATC cell invasion and migration can be markedly inhibited by knocking down these two proteins (10). MMP13 was discovered by Shi et al. to be substantially elevated, and this was linked to a worse overall survival (OS) rate in breast cancer patients (11). Johansson et al. did not detect MMP-13 messenger RNA (mRNA) in normal and intact vulvar epithelial cells but found its high expression in squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) with lymph node metastasis, suggesting MMP13’s role in the high invasiveness of these tumors (12). Despite these findings, the mechanism underlying the abnormal expression of MMP13 in tumors remains unclear. Therefore, using computed tomography (CT)-based non-invasive radiological technology, it is necessary to look into the molecular mechanism of MMP13 expression and its relationship with the immune microenvironment, forecast the expression of MMP13 mRNA in HNSCC tissues, and evaluate its associations with related genes and HNSCC prognosis.

Radiomics is a high-level throughput method based on “image sequencing”. This technology delineates the region of interest in medical imaging data, such as data from CT, ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), X-ray, or positron emission tomography (PET). Radiomics utilizes automated algorithms to extract intricate features and analyze clinical phenotypes, such as patient genotyping, treatment efficacy, and clinical outcomes. It is non-invasive, dynamically detectable, and quantitatively reflects tumor features (13). In clinical practice, the radiomics technique is frequently employed. Prior research has demonstrated its value in areas including HNSCC grading and early diagnosis, as well as tumor heterogeneity and microenvironment evaluation (14). Xie et al. discovered that a radiomics model grounded on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) may offer preoperative advice for the prognosis and customized therapy of patients with HNSCC, as well as non-invasive prediction of IL–1B expression (15). An improved CT radiomics model is capable of accurately foreseeing the expression of GZMA mRNA, according to Hang et al. (16), and GZMA expression can have a substantial impact on patients’ prognoses with HNSCC. Wang et al. (17) found that CD27 expression in HNSCC may have a significant prognostic impact in patients with HNSCC, and CECT-based radiomics may be effective in predicting CD27 expression levels. The aforementioned research describes noninvasive methods for directing the course of treatment and prognosis for individuals with HNSCC, providing valuable inspiration for our research design.

The purpose of this work was to use CT radiomics technology to develop a novel, non-invasive prediction model of MMP13 mRNA expression in HNSCC tissues. We aimed to evaluate correlations between our radiomics model and associated genes, clinical and pathologic characteristics, and prognostic factors. In addition, this extremely creative study used bioinformatics analysis to understand the molecular mechanism behind MMP13 expression and its interaction with the immunological microenvironment. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1-2620/rc).

Methods

Data and image origins

Medical data were obtained from The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) databases (Table 1). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. These data were used to build a radiomics prediction model to ascertain the prognostic value of MMP13 expression. The R package “surfminer” was used to calculate the cut-off values. In addition, we performed transcriptome sequencing on pathological specimens from 69 patients with HNSCC. Analysis of group differences

UCSC Xena ribonucleic acid sequencing (RNA-Seq) data in the “fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped fragments (FPKM)” format underwent standardized processing procedures for uniform analysis. RNA-seq data were log2 converted and processed in FPKM format to assess MMP13 expression across samples. For the visualization, the R package “ggplot2” was utilized.

Analysis of normal and tumor tissues differences in transcriptome

Perform transcriptome sequencing on samples from 69 patients with HNSCC, and analyze and compare the results.

Survival analyses

The Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis was utilized to illustrate how the survival rates of the different groups varied, with the period corresponding to a 50% survival rate being the median survival time. The log-rank test was used to determine the significance of the variations in survival rates between the groups.

Analyzing both univariate and multivariate Cox regression

Several influencing factors were assessed using multifactor Cox regression to explore the eligibility of components as independent OS influences. An indicator of risk was a hazard ratio (HR) greater than 1, whereas an indicator of protection was an HR less than 1. These studies were made easier by the R packages “forest plot” and “survival.”

Analyzing subgroups and testing interactions

The primary variable, MMP13 expression (high expression group vs. low expression group), was examined in an exploratory subgroup analysis using univariate Cox regression to determine its effect on the prognosis of patients within each covariate subgroup. To investigate the connection between MMP13 expression and other factors, we employed a likelihood ratio test.

Correlation analysis

A heat map displays the correlation analysis results, which were obtained using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

Analyzing the variations between immune cell infiltration and MMP13 expression. The HNSCC samples’ gene expression matrix was added to the CIBERSORTx databases. Each sample’s immune cell infiltration was assessed. The box plots were created using the Wilcoxon test, and the R package “limma” was utilized to examine variations in immune-cell infiltration between the groups with high and low MMP13 expression levels.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) enrichment analysis

Functional genes, especially those associated with molecular functions (MF), biological processes (BP), and cellular components (CC), are extensively annotated using Gene Ontology (GO). A popular repository for data on genomes, BP, illnesses, and medications is the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG). The BP, CC, and MF enrichment analyses yielded the top 10 highly enriched pathways, whereas the KEGG enrichment analysis produced the top 30 significantly enriched pathways. The R package “clusterprofiler” was used to carry out the KEGG and GO (BP/CC/MF) enrichment studies. The filter condition was set at a q-value filter of less than 0.05.

Image segmentation

Tumor segmentation was performed on the CECT images available from the TCIA database, the entire tumor volume was delineated in three dimensions (3D) by contouring the lesion slice-by-slice on axial images. This was done independently by two board-certified radiologists (with 8 and 10 years of experience in head and neck radiology), who were blinded to all patient genomic data and clinical outcomes. Any initial discrepancies between the segmentations were resolved through consensus discussion. The resulting consensus region of interest (ROI) was used for subsequent radiomics feature extraction. To assess intra-observer reproducibility, the first radiologist repeated the segmentation for all cases after a two-week interval.

Radiomics features screening

Using pyradiomics, 107 radiomics characteristics from 139 TCIA-TCGA intersection samples were produced, the 107 features include 14 shape features, 18 first-order features, and 75 texture features. Standardized parameters were then applied to these characteristics. Data were randomly split into training and validation sets using the R package “caret, cbcgrps” in a 7:3 ratio. Analysis was carried out on the distinctions between the training and verified sets. Relief (relevant features) and mRMR (maximum relevance, minimum redundancy) were used to filter features prior to modeling. The top twenty features determined by the Relief algorithm and the mRMR technique overlapped.

Building the Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) and logistic regression (LR) models

The screened histological traits were modeled using the GBM model in order to predict gene expression patterns.

The sigmoid function was utilized to convert the linear regression into LR, which distributed the output values between 0 and 1. The R language “stats” package’s glm function implemented an LR algorithm to verify the fit of the filtered image histological features.

Evaluation of the GBM and LR models and analysis of differences between the groups

In both the training and validation sets, the models’ effectiveness was evaluated using the radiomics model. The evaluation indicators that were employed were accuracy, specificity, sensitivity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. The proportion of true positives, or coverage (recall), is represented by the x-axes of recall curves (PR), while accuracy (precision) is represented by the y-axes. The degree of calibration of the prediction model was determined by applying the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. The Brier score was used to quantify the overall performance of the model. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to show the radiomics prediction model’s clinical value. To find differences in the radiomics score (RS) between the high expression group and the low expression group, the Wilcoxon test was used to estimate the likelihood of prediction of the output gene expression level by the radiomics model RS.

Correlation analysis

The correlation coefficients between the variables were determined using Spearman correlation analysis, and the findings were displayed using correlation graphs. The analysis included correlations between MMP13, RS, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) genes. MMP13 and RS were displayed with genes that had correlation coefficients of P<0.05, and the top 20 genes with the highest absolute correlation coefficients were chosen. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software. Continuous data are presented as median with interquartile range, and categorical data as counts with percentages. Differences between groups for continuous variables were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to identify independent prognostic factors, with results reported as HRs and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Correlations were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. For radiomics, feature selection was conducted using maximum relevance minimum redundancy and Relief algorithms. The performance of prediction models was evaluated by the area under the ROC curve, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value. Model calibration was assessed with calibration curves and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. Clinical utility was evaluated by DCA. The reproducibility of radiomics feature extraction was quantified by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). All statistical tests were two-sided. A probability value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In addition, univariate analysis revealed that high MMP13 expression (HR =1.612, 95% CI: 1.169–2.223, P=0.004), age >60 years, peripheral infiltration, and tumor staging (T, N, M) were risk factors for OS (Figure 2D). According to the analysis of multiple variables, high MMP13 expression (HR =1.482, 95% CI: 1.044–2.103, P=0.03), age >60 years, T and N staging of the tumor, and chemotherapy were risk factors for OS (Figure 2E). In the subgroup analysis, elevated MMP13 expression was a risk factor for OS in both age subgroups (younger and over 60 years), with no significant interaction (P=0.95). However, in the peripheral nerve infiltration subgroup analysis, elevated MMP13 was a risk factor for OS, and significant interaction (P=0.047) was observed, indicating variability in the effect of MMP13 on OS in different peripheral nerve infiltration scenarios. MMP13 expression values were significantly different between subtypes (Figure 2F). Finally, MMP13 expression, the main variable, was significantly correlated with tumor grade, peripheral nerve infiltration, HPV status, and primary tumor site (Figure 2G).

MMP13 expression correlates with HNSCC immune-cell infiltration

To determine the level of immune cell infiltration in each sample, we used the CIBERSORTx database. The differences between the main variable, MMP13, and immune cell infiltration were analyzed. Naïve CD4+ T cells and γδ T cells showed no infiltration of immune cells in the vast majority of HNSCC samples. Consequently, after filtering out these immune cells, the invasion of 20 immune cells became apparent. In the MMP13 low-expression group, the infiltrations of naïve B cells (P<0.01), activated memory CD4+ T cells (P<0.001), CD8+ T cells (P<0.001), follicular helper T cells (P<0.001), and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (P<0.001) were significantly increased. In the MMP13 high-expression group, the infiltration of M0 macrophages significantly increased (P<0.001), and that of M2 macrophages increased (P<0.05); further, the degree of monocyte infiltration increased (P<0.05), and that of plasma cell- and resting CD4+ memory T cell infiltration significantly increased (P<0.001). The degree of memory B cells, activated dendritic cells, resting dendritic cells, eosinophils, M1 macrophages, activated mast cells, resting mast cells, neutrophils, activated NK cells, and resting NK cells infiltration between the two groups had no statistically significant difference (P>0.05) (Figure 3A).

Six radiomic features were found (Figure 4A). After building the radiomics model, we evaluated the significance of the chosen features in the GBM method (Figure 4B) and LR model (Figure 4C). All of the radiomics characteristics in the model had ICC values above 0.9, suggesting positive consistency (Table 4).

Discussion

More than 550,000 people worldwide are diagnosed with HNSCC annually, with a mortality rate of 40‒50%, and recent advancements have not significantly improved patient survival rates (18). Tumor lymph node metastasis (TNM) staging, HPV staging, and immune infiltration staging are commonly used prognostic indicators for HNSCC. However, a significant proportion of these indicators rely on invasive methods (15). For instance, TNM staging, although widely used for prognostication in HNSCC, lacks precision and reliability, given that patients with the same TNM stage may have significantly different clinical outcomes (19). Although liquid biopsies of circulating tumor cells and circulating tumor DNA offer non-invasive advantages in the diagnosis and prognosis of HNSCC, their sensitivity and specificity have not reached optimal levels for widespread clinical use (20).

Significant impacts on carcinogenesis and biological behavior have been demonstrated for the Zn2+-dependent endopeptidase family known as MMPs (21). MMP13, a member of the peptidase M10 family, is usually overexpressed in various cancers and predicts poor prognosis. Higher expression of MMP13 correlates with increased numbers of immune and stromal cells (22). In this study, we found a significant elevation in the infiltration degree of M0, macrophages M2, monocytes immune cells, plasma cell immune cells, and CD4 memory resting immune cells in the MMP13 high-expression group. This provides more evidence of the strong connection between MMP13 and cancer.

Recently, radiomics has been used to predict the prognosis of various diseases. This method extracts quantitative and high-dimensional data from widely used digital biomedical imaging modalities, including CT, MRI, PET, and ultrasonography, providing useful information and insights into tissue heterogeneity at the cellular level (23). CT-based radiomic nomograms can improve the preoperative identification of lymph node status and contribute to informed clinical decision-making in laryngeal SCC (24). In a retrospective study of MRI-based radiomics applied to the surgical treatment of oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), almost all clinical radiomics models had statistically significant RSs. Clinical radiomics models consistently had a higher C-index than that of clinical models, suggesting that MRI-based radiomics in OSCC is a precise and noninvasive technique that can enhance preoperative prognosis prediction (25). These findings underscore the potential of noninvasive, dynamically detectable, and quantitative response approaches to tumor characteristics, exhibiting superior accuracy compared to traditional methods, and hold significant promise for further research and exploration.

Building on this potential to link imaging with tumor biology, our study applies CECT radiomics to address a distinct question in HNSCC: the prediction of specific molecular phenotypes, rather than histological grade alone. Our work extends the application of CECT radiomics in HNSCC from predicting histological grade to inferring a specific molecular phenotype. Previous studies have demonstrated that CT-based imaging features can reflect tumor differentiation, with de Oliveira et al. linking texture parameters to histopathological grade (26) and Zheng et al. further improving predictive accuracy through a model integrating handcrafted and deep learning features (27). Moving along this research trajectory, we focused on predicting the expression of MMP13, a key gene involved in tumor invasion. This successful establishment of a direct, non-invasive link between CECT features and a molecular driver of aggressiveness provides a methodological foundation for the subsequent evaluation of its clinical relevance.

A comparison of HNSCC tissue with normal tissue revealed that MMP13 expression was higher in tumors. The low expression of MMP13 was associated with improved OS. Univariate Cox regression identified high MMP13 expression as an OS risk factor; after adjusting for a number of factors, multivariate Cox regression analysis supported this pattern and established high MMP13 expression as a separate risk factor for OS. In clinical practice, many patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage without significant precancerous lesions. Unfortunately, currently, the detection of MMP13 is usually invasive, expensive, and based on tumor tissue, which not only cannot represent the overall situation of the tumor, but also makes it difficult to achieve dynamic observation. The search for precise, noninvasive, and reproducible biomarkers to finely stratify patient prognosis and improve treatment efficacy is imperative. This work showed that the use of CT radiomics technology to predict MMP13 mRNA expression in HNSCC tissues non-invasively might be a useful tool for accurate cancer diagnosis and therapy. While established prognostic factors like TNM stage and HPV status remain crucial in clinical decision-making, our radiomics model offers a complementary, non-invasive tool that can be seamlessly integrated into the existing workflow. Specifically, the model could be applied to routine pre-treatment CECT scans. The predicted MMP13 status would provide an additional layer of biological information regarding tumor invasiveness and the immune microenvironment, potentially aiding in risk stratification. This might help identify patients with seemingly favorable clinical stages but aggressive biological signatures, who could benefit from intensified therapy or closer surveillance. Furthermore, as MMP13-targeted therapies evolve, this model could serve as a screening tool to identify potential candidates for such targeted interventions or relevant clinical trials. Its non-invasive nature also allows for potential serial application to monitor biological changes during treatment, offering insights into treatment response. And there are currently no reports on radiomics models for predicting MMP13 expression in HNSCC.

Machine-learning tools are increasingly being used in many areas of medicine as developments in artificial intelligence make it possible to analyze large amounts of data. Machine-learning techniques include decision trees (DT), random forests (RF), logical regression, Bayesian models, support vector machines (SVMs), and deep learning (28). This study used mRMR and relief algorithms for feature screening and selected six features through intersections. The GBM and LR models have been established for the prediction of MMP13 expression; the GBM model was ultimately selected for correlation analysis. Demonstrating high clinical practicality, the DCA showcased congruence in AUC values between validation and training sets, affirming model fitness. Further analysis revealed that the group with low MMP13 expression had lower RS. Significant differences in RS were seen between the groups with high and low gene expression, according to the Wilcoxon test. MMP13 has reportedly been described as an important protein involved in regulating the EMT process, affecting tumor migration and infiltration ability (29). This study establishes positive correlations between MMP13, RS, and COL12A1, an EMT gene. These findings confirm that differential expression levels of the EMT-related gene MMP13 can be reflected in enhanced CT images.

Limitations

Our research showed that radiomics exhibited significant promise in prognostic assessment treatment efficacy evaluation for HNSCC. Partial data in this study were sourced from public databases, where there is comparatively little consistency across imaging equipment from various providers. However, our transcriptome data confirmed that MMP13 was expressed higher in tumor tissues than in normal tissues. Therefore, to confirm the aforementioned model, further imaging data from the same source are required. Future studies could further improve robustness by applying advanced image or feature harmonization techniques, such as ComBat, to explicitly address scanner- and protocol-specific variations. Furthermore, the machine learning approach currently in use is still a binary, discriminating technique. Recently, an array of neural networks, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), has been proposed in the field of deep learning (30). This technology can obtain more comprehensive data features and improve the accuracy of model predictions through innovative architectures and increased network layers. For CT image processing, using neural networks, such as ResNet and generative adversarial network (GAN), has proven to yield superior evaluation results (31). Presently, CNNs are one of the most often used deep learning instruments for obtaining complex characteristics (28). By using CNNs, image features can be automatically extracted, and complicated data can have nonlinear relationships recognized. Transfer learning, where a CNN is trained using millions of photos and images and applied to medical images, has shown success in cancer detection and staging research (32). In the future, we aim to integrate neural networks into HNSCC prediction models for enhanced performance. Further optimization of model algorithms using CNN may enhance the accuracy, speed, and ease of HNSCC diagnosis and provide substantial contributions to predicting clinical prognosis.

Conclusions

According to our findings, there is a strong link between MMP13 expression and the prognosis of HNSCC. We could predict MMP13 expression with high accuracy using our CT-enhanced radiomics model. This radiomics prediction model demonstrates stability and diagnostic efficiency.

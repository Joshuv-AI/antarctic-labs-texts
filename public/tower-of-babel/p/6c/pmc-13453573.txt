Mitochondria-targeted peptide-engineered bimetallic nanozymes enable ferroptosis-sensitized cuproptosis for melanoma therapy
Authors: Jiahui Kong, Mengru Cai, Ao Sun, Mengdi Zhao, Mengqian Li, xiaomeng Wang, Jiaxin Qiao, Huizhong Bao, Ranran Zhao, Bing Xu, Penglong Wang, Zhishu Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Developing nanotherapeutics to circumvent intrinsic apoptosis resistance in cancer remains a key challenge in oncology. Cuproptosis, a non-apoptotic cell death modality, has emerged as a promising alternative, yet its therapeutic efficacy is frequently limited by robust intracellular antioxidant defense systems. Here, we developed an ultrasmall (ca.10 nm) mitochondria-targeted bimetallic nanozyme (RMOCZ) for synergistic ferroptosis-cuproptosis therapy against malignant melanoma. The Cu/Zn bimetallic core, functionalized with a chimeric mitochondrial targeting peptide, serves as both a pH-responsive copper reservoir and a dual-enzyme mimetic (peroxidase and glutathione oxidase). Upon endolysosomal acidification, RMOCZ disassembles to co-release copper ions and oridonin (ORI). The nanozyme oxidizes intracellular glutathione (GSH), a process significantly accelerated by co-delivered ORI. This disruption of redox homeostasis not only triggers ferroptosis by compromising cellular antioxidant capacity but also amplifies peroxidase-mediated reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, sensitizing tumor cells to copper-induced cytotoxicity. Concurrently, RMOCZ induces ferritinophagy to mobilize the endogenous labile iron pool and exacerbate lipid peroxidation. These events culminate in sustained copper-iron dual-ion overload. Following subsequent mitochondrial trafficking, the accumulated copper ions trigger canonical cuproptotic events, including the degradation of iron-sulfur (Fe-S) clusters and aberrant oligomerization of lipoylated DLAT. This irreversible mitochondrial dysfunction triggers potent immunogenic cell death (ICD) with robust damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) release. In situ immunohistochemical analyses confirm that this RMOCZ-induced ICD profoundly remodels the immunosuppressive microenvironment, promoting CD86+ antigen-presenting cell maturation and enhancing intratumoral infiltration of CD3+ and CD8+ T cells. In vivo, RMOCZ demonstrates substantial melanoma regression with negligible systemic toxicity, providing a promising strategy for treating apoptosis-resistant refractory malignancies.

Introduction

Cutaneous malignant melanoma (CMM) is one of the deadliest dermatological malignancies, characterized by aggressive proliferation, early metastasis, and poor long-term patient survival [1,2]. Despite advances in surgical resection, targeted therapies, and immune checkpoint blockade, the 5-year overall survival rate for advanced melanoma remains below 52% [3,4]. A major clinical challenge lies in the prevalent intrinsic or acquired resistance of melanoma cells to canonical apoptotic pathways, which prevents traditional pro-apoptotic regimens from achieving durable tumor eradication [5,6]. Therefore, targeting non-apoptotic regulated cell death (RCD) pathways has emerged as a promising frontier for developing next-generation anti-melanoma therapeutics [7] (see Scheme 1).Scheme 1Schematic diagram of the synthesis and anti-tumor mechanism of RMOCZ.

Cuproptosis, a novel non-apoptotic RCD first identified in 2022, provides an alternative therapeutic strategy for apoptosis-refractory tumors [8]. Distinct from other RCD pathways, cuproptosis is strictly dependent on the excessive intracellular accumulation of free copper ions. These ions directly bind to lipoylated proteins in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle (such as dihydrolipoamide S-acetyltransferase, DLAT), inducing their abnormal oligomerization and proteotoxic stress, ultimately triggering mitochondrial dysfunction and cell death [[9], [10], [11]]. Based on this unique mechanism, copper-based bimetallic nanozymes have emerged as a particularly promising class of smart nanomedicines. They not only serve as a controllable reservoir for site-specific intracellular copper release but also exhibit intrinsic peroxidase (POD)- and glutathione oxidase (GSHOx)-like activities, disrupting the redox homeostasis of tumor cells [[12], [13], [14]]. However, the high intracellular level of reduced glutathione (GSH) in tumor cells remains the primary obstacle for cuproptosis-based therapies [15]. GSH effectively blunts the cuproptosis effect through two main pathways: first, it neutralizes free copper ions via strong chelation, thereby eliminating the trigger for cuproptosis; second, it efficiently scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS), significantly impairing the catalytic efficiency of copper nanozymes [[16], [17], [18], [19]].

To overcome this limitation, it is imperative to disrupt the intracellular antioxidant defense barrier within tumor cells. Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent non-apoptotic RCD characterized by extensive lipid peroxidation of phospholipid membranes, a process driven by severe depletion of the intracellular GSH pool [20,21]. Based on this, we propose a core scientific hypothesis: inducing ferroptosis can act as a “sensitization switch” for cuproptosis. By creating an antioxidant-deficient vulnerability in tumor cells, ferroptosis could significantly enhance cellular sensitivity to copper toxicity, thereby maximizing the synergistic lethality of both RCD pathways [[22], [23], [24]]. Oridonin (ORI), a natural diterpenoid, has been shown to efficiently deplete GSH and disrupt cellular redox homeostasis [[25], [26], [27]]. Co-delivering ORI with nanozymes is expected to construct a self-amplifying synergistic loop: ORI-mediated GSH depletion relieves the chelation of copper ions, unlocking the cuproptosis-triggering potential of the nanozymes; simultaneously, this GSH depletion synergizes with the intrinsic GSHOx- and POD-like activities of the nanozymes to exacerbate oxidative stress, ultimately forming a “ferroptosis-augmented cuproptosis” synergistic therapeutic system [[28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33]].

Importantly, the effector mechanisms of both cuproptosis and ferroptosis converge at the mitochondria. The execution of cuproptosis relies heavily on an active TCA cycle, while mitochondria also serve as a major hub for lipid peroxidation during ferroptosis, both leading to severe mitochondrial membrane compromise [8,[34], [35], [36]]. Precisely delivering this dual oxidative stress to mitochondria can precipitate irreversible metabolic collapse. Such focused subcellular damage is a key biological prerequisite for triggering robust immunogenic cell death (ICD) [37,38]. Recent advances in catalytic and photoactive nanomedicines have proven effective at overcoming TME barriers and inducing potent systemic antitumor immunity. Cascade ROS amplification via heterojunction or Z-scheme systems (e.g., carbon dot-sensitized Zr-MOFs) alleviates hypoxiaand achieves robust tumor ablation. Additional strategies such as stimuli-responsive copper ion regulation and biomimetic cell membrane camouflage further enhance the reversal of TME immunosuppression [[39], [40], [41], [42]]. Similarly, in our design, upon severe mitochondrial collapse, dying tumor cells emit damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)-such as the surface exposure of calreticulin (CRT) and the extracellular release of high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP). These signals recruit antigen-presenting cells, initiating a systemic antitumor immune response that reverses the immunosuppressive melanoma microenvironment [43,44].

Driven by this rationale, we engineered a multifunctional peptide-engineered nanoplatform-an ultrasmall (ca. 10 nm) mitochondria-targeted bimetallic nanozyme system (mPEG2000-R8MTS@ORI@Cu-Zn-ICA, abbreviated as RMOCZ)-for the precise synergistic therapy of malignant melanoma. The Cu/Zn bimetallic core possesses optimized electronic structures, enhancing the catalytic activity and stability compared to monometallic copper counterparts. Crucially, the nanozyme is rationally functionalized with a multifunctional polymer-peptide conjugate that integrates three complementary functions: the hydrophilic mPEG2000 segment ensures prolonged blood circulation and physiological stability, while the dual-peptide motif (R8 for cell penetration and MTS for mitochondrial anchoring) ensures efficient internalization and precise organelle targeting. Upon pH-responsive degradation within the acidic endolysosomal compartments, the spatially controlled release of ORI and copper ions initiates ferroptosis and primes cells for cuproptosis. This disruption significantly sensitizes melanoma cells to subsequent localized copper toxicity in mitochondria, driving severe depolarization and robust ICD. This study not only presents a smart biomaterial integrating targeted delivery and multi-pathway killing but also offers novel mechanistic insights into utilizing ferroptotic sensitization to augment cuproptosis against drug-resistant solid tumors.

Materials and methods

Materials

Zn(CH3COO)2·2H2O, CuCl2·2H2O, anhydrous methanol (MeOH, AR) and N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, AR) were acquired from SEPARATION Beijing Technology Co., Ltd. Imidazole-2-carboxaldehyde (ICA) and oridonin (ORI) were purchased from Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Bradford protein quantification kit, cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8), fetal bovine serum (FBS) and a 1% penicillin-streptomycin mixture were acquired from NCM Biotech. Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit, T-GSH/GSSG assay kit, Fe2+ colorimetric assay kit, MDA colorimetric assay kit and mitochondrial membrane potential assay kit were acquired from Elabscience. ATP assay kit, ferrostatin-1, necrostatin-1 and Z-VAD-FMK were acquired from MCE. Copper assay kit and reactive oxygen species assay kit were acquired from Beyotime Biotechnology.

Synthesis of ultrasmall nanozymes

The targeted mPEG2000-R8MTS@ORI@Cu-Zn-ICA (RMOCZ) nanosystem was constructed via a facile two-step strategy: ORI was first in situ encapsulated during the one-pot self-assembly of the bimetallic Cu-Zn-ICA (CZ) framework to form OCZ nanoparticles; subsequently, the mPEG2000-R8MTS targeting conjugate was covalently modified onto the OCZ surface via Schiff base condensation.

In vivo biological evaluations

The in vivo tumor-targeting capability, antitumor efficacy, and biosafety of the RMOCZ nanosystem were evaluated using a subcutaneous B16-F10 melanoma model, which was established by inoculating B16-F10 cells into the right anterior axilla of 6-week-old male C57BL/6 mice with a body weight of 18-22 g. Following intravenous administration, tumor-specific accumulation was monitored via in vivo and ex vivo fluorescence imaging. The cuproptosis-mediated therapeutic mechanisms were investigated by intratumoral copper quantification and Western blotting analysis of cuproptotic cascade proteins (FDX1, LIAS, DLAT, ATP7A). Systemic biosafety at the experimental endpoint was assessed through hematological, biochemical, and H&E histopathological analyses.

More detailed experimental procedures are included in the Supporting Information.

Synthesis, characterization, and colloidal stability of the ultrasmall nanozymes

To elucidate the elemental composition and spatial distribution within the bimetallic nanozyme, energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping was conducted. The elemental maps revealed a highly homogeneous co-localization of zinc (Zn) and copper (Cu) throughout the nanoparticle, unambiguously confirming the successful co-crystallization and uniform elemental doping of the dual-metal framework (Fig. 1C). To precisely quantify this composition, inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) was utilized. The analysis demonstrated high mass fractions of Zn (14.86 wt%, ∼148.57 mg/g) and Cu (8.78 wt%, ∼87.76 mg/g) within the formulation, with a calculated Zn to Cu molar ratio of approximately 1.64:1 (Fig. 1F).

Surface functionalization and structural validation. The successful synthesis of the mPEG2000-R8MTS conjugate was confirmed via 1H NMR. The spectrum of the conjugate displayed both the characteristic proton signals of the R8MTS peptide (0.5-2.0 ppm and 7.0-8.5 ppm) and a prominent new peak at 3.6 ppm assigned to the PEG backbone (-CH2-CH2-O-). The coexistence of these specific signals verifies the successful covalent conjugation (Fig. S2). The successful coordination and stepwise functionalization of the nanozymes were verified by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). The FTIR spectrum of CZ exhibited characteristic C=O (1670 cm−1) and imidazole C=N (1570 cm−1) stretching vibrations. Additionally, moderate peaks from 1350 to 1500 cm−1 (C=C and C-N) and metal-nitrogen coordination bonds (Zn-N and Cu-N) at 750–850 cm−1 verified the successful formation of the ZIF skeleton. Pure ORI showed distinct peaks at 3200-3600 cm−1 (O-H), 1710 cm−1 (C=O), and 1650 cm−1 (C=C). The FTIR spectrum of OCZ was nearly identical to that of CZ, likely due to the strong infrared absorption of the rigid CZ framework masking the characteristic peaks of ORI. Successful ORI loading was further confirmed by HPLC analysis (Fig. 1J). After surface functionalization, the RMOCZ complex displayed new signature peaks at 2850-1950 cm−1, 1520-1560 cm−1, and 1000-1150 cm−1, matching the mPEG2000-R8MTS peptide. The concomitant attenuation of the main CZ peaks (1451, 800 cm−1) provided further evidence for the successful grafting of the targeting peptide onto the nanozyme surface (Fig. 1K). The zeta potential of RMOCZ (+38.99 mV) was significantly higher than that of OCZ (+15.68 mV), which confirmed the successful modification of mPEG2000-R8MTS (Fig. 1E). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) demonstrated robust thermal stability of the nanocarriers. The gradual weight loss profiles of CZ, OCZ, and RMOCZ were primarily attributed to thermal degradation of the CZ framework, the encapsulated ORI drug, and the surface-modified mPEG2000-R8MTS moieties, confirming the successful encapsulation of ORI and surface conjugation of mPEG2000-R8MTS (Fig. S4).

Exceptional long term colloidal stability. To evaluate the long-term colloidal stability, the zeta potential and hydrodynamic diameter of the nanozymes were tracked over 28 days. Compared to the fluctuating potentials of CZ and OCZ (<+20 mV), RMOCZ displayed superior physical stability. Aided by the steric hindrance of the PEG chains and the cationic nature of the peptide shell, RMOCZ maintained a highly positive initial zeta potential of +38.99 mV (Fig. 1G). Throughout the 28-day incubation, the RMOCZ nanosystem exhibited minimal variations, with the Zeta potential and particle size (10.33 nm) fluctuating by merely 10.1% and 1.1%, respectively (Fig. 1H). No discernible aggregation was detected, confirming the excellent shelflife stability of the RMOCZ formulation.

Tumor microenvironment-responsive catalytic cascades and controlled drug release

Drug loading and pH-triggered release. High drug loading and on-demand release are the key to improve the efficacy. The drug loading of OCZ and RMOCZ was 11.17% ± 0.33% and 10.38% ± 0.41%, respectively (Fig. 1J). Under slightly acidic conditions (pH 5.5), the drug release rate of both preparations was significantly higher than that of the neutral physiological environment (pH 7.4). This favorable pH-responsive release behavior ensures that the load remains safe in the systemic circulation but is rapidly released upon entry into the tumor intracellular microenvironment (Fig. 1I).

Intracellular microenvironment activated POD-like and GSHOx-like catalytic activities. The POD-like activity of the nanozymes was first evaluated using TMB [45,46]. The CZ framework catalyzed H2O2 decomposition into highly oxidative •OH radicals via Fenton-like reactions, yielding the blue oxTMB product with a 652 nm absorption peak (Fig. S5). Time-dependent kinetics demonstrated a linear absorbance increase, verifying stable POD-like activity. This catalysis was highly pH-dependent, exhibiting superior efficiency in the lysosomal-mimicking acidic environment (pH 5.5) over physiological pH 7.4. Notably, the intrinsic activity was well-preserved after ORI loading and mPEG2000-R8MTS modification (Fig. S6).

The GSHOx-like activity was subsequently assessed using the DTNB (5,5′-dithiobis-(2-nitrobenzoic acid)) assay [46,47]. The typical 412 nm absorbance, generated by the reaction between free sulfhydryl groups of GSH and DTNB, decreased significantly following nanozyme treatment (Fig. S7). Kinetic analysis revealed a rapid, time-dependent decline in the 412 nm absorbance, confirming continuous GSH oxidation. This depletion was remarkably accelerated at pH 5.5 compared to pH 7.4. Notably, ORI loading and mPEG2000-R8MTS modification did not compromise the intrinsic enzyme-like activities of the CZ nanozyme, which is critical for maintaining its catalytic efficacy in vivo (Fig. S8). Furthermore, utilizing ADHP as a specific probe, the emergence of a 570 nm absorption peak indicated the production of H2O2 during GSH oxidation (Fig. S9). This self-supplied catalytic cascade eliminates the need for exogenous H2O2, a major limitation of traditional Fenton-based nanotherapeutics, which rely on the relatively low and heterogeneous endogenous H2O2 levels in tumors. Here, the nanozymes continuously generate H2O2 in situ to sustain downstream hydroxyl radical (•OH) production.

Mechanistic rationale of the self-amplified cascade. The aforementioned dual enzyme-like catalytic activities provide a core mechanistic basis for efficient tumor therapy. In the lysosomal environment, GSHOx-like activity of the CZ nanozyme exerts a dual function: on the one hand, it catalyzes the oxidative depletion of intracellular GSH to disrupt the core antioxidant defense system of tumor cells; on the other hand, it enables in situ generation of H2O2 to achieve a continuous self-sufficient supply of H2O2. The in situ newly generated H2O2 can directly serve as the substrate for the POD-like Fenton reaction catalyzed by the nanozyme. Through the synergistic coupling of GSH depletion and in situ self-supply of H2O2, this ultrasmall nanozyme successfully constructs a self-amplified cascade catalytic system, which ultimately achieves a massive burst of cytotoxic ROS.

Precise subcellular navigation: from endocytosis to mitochondrial targeting

For antitumor nanomedicines, efficient cellular endocytosis and effective lysosomal escape are core prerequisites for their full antitumor activity. For cuproptosis effect mediated by copper-based nanozymes, precise mitochondrial targeting is a critical link to trigger this programmed cell death pathway. Based on this, in this section, we systematically analyzed the intracellular transport behavior and subcellular distribution characteristics of different Cy5-labeled formulations (free Cy5, Cy5@OCZ, and Cy5@RMOCZ) using confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM), to clarify the regulatory effect of mPEG2000-R8MTS functional modification on the subcellular targeting ability of the nanozyme system.

Cellular uptake Analysis. We first evaluated the cellular uptake efficiency of different nano formulations by B16-F10 cells. B16-F10 cells were incubated with free Cy5, Cy5@OCZ and Cy5@RMOCZ at equal Cy5 fluorescence equivalence for the same duration, respectively. Then the intracellular red fluorescence signal intensity of each group was observed and compared via CLSM. The imaging results showed that the intracellular fluorescence intensity of different treatment groups presented a formulation-dependent increase, with the order of fluorescence signal intensity: Cy5@RMOCZ > Cy5@OCZ > Cy5. Among them, free Cy5 could only enter cells in a small amount through passive diffusion due to the lack of effective active cell entry pathways, and only extremely weak red fluorescence signal was detected intracellularly. When Cy5 was encapsulated in the CZ nanozyme scaffold, Cy5@OCZ could efficiently enter cells via the endocytic pathway, and the intracellular fluorescence signal was significantly enhanced compared with the free Cy5 group. Lysosomal Escape. After entering cells via the endocytic pathway, the majority of nanoparticles are sorted and trapped within the lysosomal lumen. Failure to achieve efficient lysosomal escape will lead to degradation and inactivation of the encapsulated active ingredients by the acidic environment and hydrolytic enzymes in lysosomes, thereby greatly diminishing their expected pharmacological efficacy. We labeled intracellular lysosomes with the lysosome-specific fluorescent probe Lyso Tracker Green (green fluorescence), and performed real-time tracking of the spatiotemporal dynamic process of lysosomal escape of Cy5@RMOCZ (red fluorescence) in B16-F10 cells via CLSM.

The results showed that intracellular red fluorescence emerged intracellularly after 1 h incubation of B16-F10 cells with Cy5@RMOCZ. After 4 h incubation, the intracellular red fluorescence signal intensity was significantly increased and began to co-localize with the green fluorescence of lysosomes. After 8 h incubation, the red and green fluorescence showed a high degree of co-localization, with obvious yellow fluorescent spots observed in the Merge channel, indicating that most nanoparticles were sorted and retained in the lysosomal lumen at this time point. At 12 h post-incubation, the red and green fluorescence were clearly separated, and a large amount of Cy5 red fluorescence signal was diffusely distributed in the cytoplasm matrix (Fig. 2C). This spatiotemporal dynamic change from high co-localization of red and green fluorescence to significant separation of fluorescence signals fully confirmed that Cy5@RMOCZ possesses excellent lysosomal escape ability, and can be successfully released from the lysosomal lumen into the cytoplasm matrix, which lays a critical structural foundation for its subsequent mitochondrial targeted delivery.

Mitochondrial Specific Targeting and Colocalization. After successful lysosomal escape, precise mitochondrial targeting of this multifunctional nanozyme is required to effectively initiate the subsequent cell death cascade. To evaluate the mitochondrial targeting efficiency of the nanozyme, we stained the mitochondria of B16-F10 cells with the mitochondria-specific fluorescent probe Mito Tracker Green, and comparatively analyzed the intracellular spatial co-localization characteristics with mitochondria of three formulations (free Cy5, Cy5@OCZ, and Cy5@RMOCZ) at equal Cy5 fluorescence equivalence via CLSM imaging.

The results showed that free Cy5 had almost no effective intracellular uptake, with only extremely weak fluorescence signal detectable. In the Cy5@OCZ treatment group without MTS targeting modification, the intracellular red fluorescence signal was also weak, randomly and diffusely distributed in the cytoplasm, with no obvious co-localization with the green fluorescence of mitochondria. In sharp contrast, the Cy5@RMOCZ treatment group exhibited an extremely high mitochondrial co-localization efficiency: in the Merge channel images, the red fluorescence and green fluorescence were highly overlapped, presenting a clear yellow characteristic signal of co-localization (Fig. 2D). The above results fully confirmed that the Mitochondrial Targeting Sequence (MTS) in the mPEG2000-R8MTS possesses excellent mitochondrial targeting capability. It can mediate precise suborganelle delivery of the nanozyme after lysosomal escape, enabling it to act as a functional delivery vehicle with “molecular guidance” capability to actively recognize and stably anchor to mitochondria.

Deconstructing the synergistic cytotoxicity network: intersection of ferroptosis and cuproptosis

Following the verification of precise mitochondrial delivery, we further systematically investigated the in vitro antitumor activity of the RMOCZ nanosystem against B16-F10 cells and its underlying multidimensional cell death regulatory mechanism. Distinct from traditional inert drug delivery vehicles, the CZ in this study itself is an ultrasmall nanozyme, which can actively regulate and remodel cellular redox homeostasis and metal ion metabolism triggered by the endogenous tumor intracellular microenvironment.

In vitro synergistic cytotoxicity. We first systematically evaluated the effects of different formulations on the viability of B16-F10 cells via the CCK-8 assay. The experimental results showed that the Zn-ICA (Z) nanoscaffold maintained an extremely high cell survival rate without obvious cytotoxicity even at high treatment concentrations, confirming its excellent cytocompatibility. In contrast, the copper-doped CZ nanozyme exhibited a significant dose-dependent inhibitory effect on cell proliferation, indicating that CZ is not merely a drug delivery vehicle. The active copper ions released intracellularly by CZ as an ultrasmall nanozyme, together with its intrinsic enzyme-mimicking catalytic activity, can disrupt cellular redox homeostasis via inducing excessive production of ROS, thereby exerting a direct primary killing effect on tumor cells.

After encapsulation of the natural active ingredient ORI, the OCZ group (IC50 = 2.5 μg/mL) showed significantly lower cell viability than the free ORI group and the drug-free CZ group, preliminarily confirming the significant synergistic antitumor effect between ORI and CZ nanozyme. More critically, RMOCZ modified with the targeting peptide exhibited the strongest cell proliferation inhibitory activity (IC50 = 1.2 μg/mL). Analysis of multiple mechanisms of cell death. To dissect the specific pathways driving this potent cytotoxicity, cell viability was evaluated following co-incubation with a panel of specific inhibitors: Z-VAD-FMK (Z-VAD, pan-caspase inhibitor), ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1, ferroptosis inhibitor), ammonium tetrathiomolybdate (TTM, copper chelator), and Necrostatin-1 (Nec-1, necroptosis inhibitor). Co-incubation with Z-VAD or Fer-1 only partially reversed the cell death induced by free ORI, while TTM and Nec-1 showed no apparent effect, confirming that ORI primarily triggers apoptosis and ferroptosis. Conversely, TTM significantly rescued cell viability in the CZ-treated group, strongly indicating that copper overload is the core driver of CZ cytotoxicity, which may further trigger the cuproptosis cascade. Notably, in cells treated with the fully assembled RMOCZ, individual co-incubation with Z-VAD, Fer-1, or TTM only provided partial rescue (Figs. S10–S13). This differential reversal profile suggests that RMOCZ does not rely on a single lethal pathway; rather, it constructs a synergistic lethal network integrating canonical apoptosis, ferroptosis, and cuproptosis, thereby effectively overcoming the multidimensional survival defense of tumor cells.

Flow cytometric profiling of membrane integrity. To validate these mechanisms at the single-cell level, we assessed cell membrane integrity and apoptosis using Annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry. The flow cytometric trends corroborated the macroscopic CCK-8 data. While control and Z-treated cells remained healthy and viable (Annexin V−/PI−), treatments with free ORI, CZ, and OCZ induced moderate phosphatidylserine (PS) externalization (Annexin V+), indicative of early apoptosis. Strikingly, RMOCZ treatment resulted in a massive shift in the cell population, with a highly significant increase in late apoptotic or necrotic cells exhibiting irreversible membrane damage (Annexin V+/PI+ double positive). Furthermore, pretreatment with Fer-1, Z-VAD, or TTM attenuated the proportion of Annexin V-positive cells to varying extents, with the TTM rescue group showing the most pronounced reduction (Fig. 3B). This differential reversal confirms that the profound disruption of tumor cell membrane integrity is primarily driven by CZ-induced copper dyshomeostasis, intricately amplified by ferroptotic and apoptotic crosstalk.

Disruption of metal ion homeostasis. To define the biochemical basis of this multi-pathway lethality, we quantified intracellular metal ion dysregulation. A massive intracellular accumulation of free copper ions was detected following treatment with CZ, resulting from the lysosomal degradation of the nanoscaffolds. This copper accumulation acts as the fundamental prerequisite for initiating cuproptosis. The addition of TTM completely neutralized this copper overload, whereas Z-VAD and Fer-1 had no significant effect. This confirms that copper release is an upstream, pathway-independent physicochemical event, rather than a secondary consequence of cell death. Interestingly, despite no exogenous iron being introduced into the system, a significant accumulation of free intracellular Fe2+ was observed in CZ-treated cells, with an even more pronounced secondary Fe2+ surge in the RMOCZ group. To elucidate the source of this endogenous iron, supplementary experiments confirmed that this mobilization of the endogenous labile iron pool is driven by ferritinophagy activation (Figs. S16–S18). TTM effectively blocked this secondary iron accumulation by removing the upstream copper stressor, whereas Fer-1 could not (Fig. 3G and I). This cascaded “copper-iron dual ion overload” provides the crucial elemental foundation for both cuproptosis and subsequent Fenton-like oxidative cascades.

Intracellular oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation. Driven by this dual metal overload, we investigated the resulting imbalance in redox homeostasis. Quantitative assays revealed that the intrinsic GSHOx-like activity of the nanozyme, combined with ORI-mediated interference in GSH metabolism, severely depleted the total intracellular GSH pool. In the RMOCZ group, this combination caused an extreme drop in the GSH/GSSG ratio, leading to the profound impairment of the cellular antioxidant defense system (Fig. 3H). Consequently, the accumulated copper and iron ions unrestrictedly catalyzed intense Fenton-like reactions, triggering a massive burst of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS), as detected by the DCFH-DA probe (Fig. 3D, E, F). Excessive ROS induced severe lipid peroxidation and generating high levels of malondialdehyde (MDA). Inhibitor assays validated this regulatory axis: Fer-1 reduced downstream MDA levels without affecting upstream ROS production, whereas the copper chelator TTM fundamentally blocked the initial metal-mediated catalytic cascade, simultaneously suppressing both the ROS burst and subsequent MDA formation (Fig. 3J).

Severe mitochondrial depolarization. Given that the R8MTS peptide precisely navigates RMOCZ to mitochondria, the ensuing oxidative and metal-induced damage is spatially concentrated at this critical organelle. Using JC-1 staining, we monitored mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP). RMOCZ induced the most rapid and profound MMP dissipation, evidenced by a drastic red-to-green fluorescence shift, indicating severe structural disruption. This severe depolarization results from a synergistic dual assault: ROS-mediated mitochondrial lipid peroxidation (ferroptosis-related) and copper-induced aberrant aggregation of lipoylated proteins within the TCA cycle (cuproptosis-related). Z-VAD failed to significantly reverse this MMP dissipation, indicating it is not merely a secondary consequence of canonical apoptosis. Conversely, TTM significantly preserved mitochondrial integrity by sequestering the core toxic metal (Fig. 3K). This spatially localized and irreversible mitochondrial destruction not only halts cellular energy metabolism but also serves as the definitive biological trigger for the massive release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), laying the groundwork for robust immunogenic cell death (ICD).

Mitochondrial damage-amplified immunogenic cell death

Building upon the robust ferroptosis-cuproptosis cascades and intense oxidative stress triggered by RMOCZ, we further hypothesized that this severe intracellular damage would inevitably lead to the massive release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs). Consequently, we systematically evaluated the potential of this targeted nanosystem to serve as a potent ICD inducer.

ER stress and CRT externalization. The surface translocation of calreticulin (CRT) from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to the plasma membrane is a hallmark early event of ICD. Acting as a classical “eat me” signal, surface-exposed CRT is specifically recognized by antigen-presenting cells (e.g., dendritic cells) to facilitate the phagocytosis of dying tumor cells. CLSM was utilized to visualize CRT expression profiles in B16-F10 cells. As anticipated, control, free ORI, and CZ-treated cells exhibited negligible surface CRT fluorescence, remaining in an immunologically silent state. Conversely, OCZ-treated cells displayed distinct punctate CRT signals on the cell membrane. Notably, owing to the mitochondria-targeted amplification of oxidative stress endowed by the R8MTS peptide, RMOCZ-treated cells exhibited the most intense and uniformly distributed CRT externalization (Fig. 4C). Nuclear-to-extracellular efflux of HMGB1. High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) is a non-histone chromatin-binding protein typically restricted to the nucleus. Upon severe cellular damage, HMGB1 is massively released into the tumor microenvironment (TME), serving as a potent pro-inflammatory DAMP. To rigorously profile this dynamic translocation, HMGB1 efflux was comprehensively evaluated utilizing both CLSM imaging and ELISA. CLSM revealed intense, strictly nucleus-localized HMGB1 green fluorescence in the control, free ORI, and CZ groups, indicating intact nuclear envelopes. In the OCZ group, the intracellular HMGB1 signal was visibly attenuated. Strikingly, in RMOCZ-treated cells, the intracellular HMGB1 fluorescence was almost entirely depleted. Concordant with these imaging results, ELISA quantification of the culture supernatants confirmed that RMOCZ induced an extremely significant surge in extracellular HMGB1 concentration, peaking among all experimental groups, whereas free ORI and CZ elicited negligible release (Fig. 4A and C). This stark inverse relationship between intracellular depletion and extracellular accumulation underscores the irreversible compromise of both nuclear and plasma membranes inflicted by the ferroptosis-cuproptosis cascade.

ATP Secretion and Activation of the Immunogenic Cascade. ATP acts as a critical “find me” chemotactic signal actively released by dying cells to recruit dendritic cells into the tumor bed. Chemiluminescence quantification of extracellular ATP corroborated the trends of the aforementioned DAMPs. Neither free ORI nor bare CZ treatments induced meaningful ATP efflux compared to the control. While OCZ stimulated moderate ATP secretion, the targeted RMOCZ triggered a substantial release, confirming its superior efficacy in eliciting deep cellular stress (Fig. 4B).

Collectively, the synchronized upregulation of these three canonical markers (CRT externalization, profound HMGB1 efflux, and massive ATP secretion) definitively fulfills the molecular criteria for robust ICD. The targeted, dual-metal-driven oxidative lethality of RMOCZ not only eradicates tumor cells but effectively converts them into an in situ vaccine. This localized enrichment of tumor antigens and chemotactic signals lays the essential immunological groundwork for reversing the immunosuppressive TME and igniting a systemic antitumor immune response.

Targeted delivery and in vivo synergistic Antitumor efficacy

Spatiotemporal biodistribution and prolonged tumor retention. The core prerequisite for efficient in vivo antitumor therapy of nanomedicines is their precise accumulation and long-term retention at the tumor lesion. To systematically evaluate the in vivo biological behavior of this targeted nanosystem, we performed real-time tracking of the spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of Cy5-labeled nanoformulations in B16-F10 melanoma tumor-bearing mice via non-invasive small animal in vivo fluorescence imaging.

Following intravenous administration, both Cy5@OCZ and Cy5@RMOCZ exhibited noticeable tumor accumulation as early as 1 h post-injection, primarily driven by the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. However, their off-target biodistribution profiles exhibited striking differences. The Cy5@OCZ and free Cy5 groups displayed intense non-specific fluorescence in the abdominal cavity, predominantly within the liver. In sharp contrast, hepatic accumulation was significantly mitigated in the Cy5@RMOCZ group. Over the extended observation period from 12 to 24 h, the tumor fluorescence signal in the Cy5@RMOCZ group maintained a persistently high intensity, demonstrating exceptional long-term tumor retention, while signals in healthy organs decayed to near-background levels. Potent and synergistic tumor growth inhibition. Based on the aforementioned excellent tumor-targeted accumulation and long-term retention characteristics of RMOCZ, we systematically evaluated the in vivo synergistic antitumor effect of this nanosystem using a B16-F10 melanoma tumor-bearing mouse model. The tumor volume growth curves of mice in each group after tail vein administration during the 13-day treatment cycle clearly showed differential tumor growth inhibitory effects.

Compared with the normal saline control group, the free ORI group showed no obvious tumor growth inhibitory effect, which was mainly attributed to the poor druggability of ORI, including poor water solubility, short in vivo circulation half-life, and rapid systemic clearance. The CZ group achieved moderate tumor growth inhibition via its intrinsic enzyme-mimicking catalytic activity and cuproptosis-mediated killing effect. In contrast, the drug-loaded nanosystem OCZ group exhibited significantly better tumor inhibitory effect than the CZ group, which confirmed that ORI and CZ nanozyme also had a synergistic antitumor effect in vivo, highly consistent with the in vitro experimental results at the cellular level, and further verified the baseline synergistic effect between cuproptosis, ferroptosis and apoptosis pathways. This result fully confirmed that spatial focusing of the multi-pathway cell death cascade interwoven with ferroptosis, cuproptosis and apoptosis at the subcellular level via mitochondria-targeted delivery could maximize the synergistic killing effect of the multi-pathway network, thereby achieving a potent in vivo antitumor effect.

Intratumoral copper enrichment. As the fundamental elemental prerequisite for initiating cuproptosis, we quantified the intratumoral copper concentrations at the experimental endpoint. Consistent with the in vivo biodistribution profiles, treatments with the copper-doped nanozymes (CZ and OCZ) led to a significant elevation in intratumoral copper levels compared to the control and free ORI groups. Most importantly, the RMOCZ-treated cohort demonstrated the highest intratumoral copper accumulation (Fig. 6E). This maximized elemental enrichment provides direct quantitative evidence for the superior targeted delivery efficiency of the nanosystem and seamlessly sets the stage for the downstream cuproptotic molecular cascades.

In vivo molecular validation of ferroptosis-amplified cuproptosis. To elucidate the molecular basis of the robust in vivo efficacy and verify the proposed synergistic mechanisms, we assessed the expression levels of key cuproptosis-related proteins in excised tumor tissues via Western blotting. Treatment with free ORI did not significantly alter the expression of ferredoxin 1 (FDX1, a key upstream regulator of cuproptosis) or lipoic acid synthase (LIAS). In contrast, the bare CZ nanozyme markedly reduced the levels of these two key regulators of Fe-S cluster biogenesis. Furthermore, CZ monotherapy induced a trend toward decreased monomeric levels of dihydrolipoamide S-acetyltransferase (DLAT), a key lipoylated protein in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, although this did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.086). This suggests that the copper stress triggered by the nanozyme alone may be insufficient to fully drive the oligomerization and functional inactivation of this central cuproptotic target in vivo (as evidenced by the observed reduction in soluble DLAT monomer) [8].

Notably, treatment with the drug-loaded OCZ formulation, and particularly the mitochondria-targeted RMOCZ, resulted in a profound reduction in FDX1 and LIAS expression, alongside a substantial loss of monomeric DLAT (Fig. 6F). This mechanistic amplification suggests that ORI-mediated GSH depletion exacerbates the collapse of tumor antioxidant defenses [48]. This dual action highly sensitizes tumor cells to copper toxicity, thereby amplifying the cuproptosis cascade and promoting the extensive aggregation of its core lipoylated targets.

“Efflux-blocking” synergy via ATP7A destruction. The expression profile of copper-transporting ATPase alpha (ATP7A), a critical copper efflux transporter, provided compelling supplementary evidence for our synergistic model. Under physiological stress, cells upregulate ATP7A to expel excess intracellular copper, functioning as a primary compensatory defense against copper overload [49,50].

Western blot analyses revealed no significant alterations in ATP7A protein levels in tumors treated with free ORI or bare CZ compared to the control. In stark contrast, both OCZ and RMOCZ treatments precipitated a significant downregulation of ATP7A expression (Fig. 6F). This downregulation of ATP7A likely occurs via a dual mechanism: first, ferroptotic lipid peroxidation damages the structure of transmembrane proteins; second, RMOCZ-induced mitochondrial depolarization depletes cellular ATP, which is essential for the active transport function of ATP7A [51]. This synergistic disruption effectively paralyses the copper efflux machinery, irreversibly sequestering toxic copper ions within the tumor cells. This action completely dismantles the compensatory copper homeostasis defense, ensuring the unhindered execution of the cuproptotic cell death program.

In vivo activation of antitumor immunity. A key downstream consequence of the aforementioned ferroptosis–cuproptosis dual-pathway cell death is the release of DAMPs, which initiate the cascade of antitumor immune activation. To validate this local immune response in vivo, in situ immunohistochemical (IHC) staining was performed on harvested tumor tissues. CD86 is a canonical marker of activated antigen-presenting cells (APCs). As shown in Fig. S19, the RMOCZ-treated group exhibited markedly increased infiltration of CD86+ cells. Driven by enhanced antigen presentation, robust intratumoral T lymphocyte infiltration was detected. Quantitative IHC analysis revealed that the RMOCZ nanosystem induced the highest proportions of both CD3+ total T cells and CD8+ cytotoxic T cells within the tumor bed (***p < 0.001 vs. Con). Collectively, these histological results demonstrate that RMOCZ successfully translates subcellular mitochondrial damage and multi-pathway cell death into potent in situ antitumor immunity, thereby effectively reversing the immunosuppressive melanoma microenvironment.

In vivo biosafety evaluation

The clinical translation potential of nanomedicines hinges equally upon potent antitumor efficacy and rigorous in vivo systemic biosafety. While RMOCZ preferentially triggers robust oxidative stress and metal dyshomeostasis within tumor tissues, evaluating its systemic physiological compatibility remains a critical prerequisite for future clinical translation.

Systemic physiological stability. Throughout the therapeutic period, we dynamically monitored murine body weights and general physiological states. No obvious treatment-induced body weight loss was observed across the cohorts (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, mice in the RMOCZ group consistently maintained normal autonomous activity, food intake, and general behavioral status. This provides preliminary evidence that the targeted nanosystem exerts potent tumoricidal effects without compromising the general physiological stability of the subjects.

Preservation of hepatic and renal functions. The liver and kidneys are the primary organs responsible for nanoparticle metabolism and excretion. To assess potential off-target toxicity, we analyzed key serum biochemical markers at the experimental endpoint. The core hepatic indicator alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and the renal indicator creatinine (CREA) in the RMOCZ group remained within the established normal murine physiological ranges. Notably, the tumor-bearing control group exhibited elevated ALT levels (75-87 U/L), which were above the normal range for healthy mice (typically 20-60 U/L). This phenomenon is likely attributable to the severe hepatic metabolic burden induced by advanced tumor progression. RMOCZ treatment significantly mitigated this elevation compared to the tumor-bearing control, likely secondary to the effective suppression of tumor growth and the subsequent relief of tumor-induced hepatic stress. Histopathological safety assessment. To provide morphological evidence of systemic biosafety, we performed H&E staining on major vital organs (liver, spleen, and kidneys) harvested at the experimental endpoint. Examination under light microscopy revealed no discernible pathological abnormalities, inflammatory cell infiltration, cellular necrosis, or structural deformities in the tissues of RMOCZ-treated mice. Specifically, hepatic lobules maintained normal architecture without hepatocyte vacuolation, splenic white and red pulps were clearly demarcated without abnormal hyperplasia, and renal glomeruli and tubules exhibited intact morphologies (Fig. 7A). These histopathological results strongly corroborate the serum biochemistry data, further supporting the excellent in vivo tissue-level biocompatibility of the RMOCZ nanosystem.

Exceptional hematological compatibility. Given that metal-based catalytic nanomaterials frequently pose risks of systemic inflammation or coagulopathy, we performed a complete blood count (CBC) analysis on peripheral blood. Leukocyte-related parameters (including white blood cell count and the ratios of lymphocytes, intermediate cells, and granulocytes) remained within normal physiological limits across all cohorts, with no significant intergroup differences. This finding indicates that RMOCZ treatment does not induce non-specific systemic inflammation or acute hematological toxicity. Moreover, erythrocyte parameters (red blood cell count, hemoglobin, hematocrit, mean corpuscular volume) and platelet indices showed no abnormalities, providing no evidence of hemolysis, anemia, or coagulation dysfunction (Fig. 7D, E, F and Table S1). This excellent hematological compatibility is consistent with the preferential accumulation and tumor-restricted cytotoxicity of RMOCZ, demonstrating no detectable adverse effects on systemic circulating cells.

Conclusion

The intrinsic apoptosis resistance of malignant melanoma and its robust antioxidant barriers limit the efficacy of conventional therapies. To address this, we designed RMOCZ, a mitochondria-targeted bimetallic nanoplatform. Functionalized with the mPEG2000-R8MTS chimeric peptide, RMOCZ exhibits prolonged circulation, efficient cellular uptake, and specific mitochondrial localization. Upon exposure to the acidic endolysosomal environment, the nanosystem degrades to release its payload, which subsequently traffics to mitochondria to drive combined ferroptosis and cuproptosis.

The therapeutic effect of RMOCZ stems from the synergy between these two pathways. Co-delivery of the redox modulator oridonin (ORI) and the Cu/Zn nanozyme depletes intracellular glutathione (GSH). This disruption of redox homeostasis compromises antioxidant defenses, initiating ferroptosis and amplifying nanozyme-driven ROS production. Concurrently, RMOCZ induces ferritinophagy, mobilizing the labile iron pool and exacerbating lipid peroxidation via Fenton-like reactions. Oxidative stress and copper overload synergistically damage mitochondria, and accumulated copper ions then provoke canonical cuproptosis, characterized by aberrant oligomerization of lipoylated DLAT and degradation of Fe-S cluster proteins, leading to irreversible mitochondrial dysfunction. This mitochondrial collapse induces immunogenic cell death (ICD), marked by the emission of DAMPs, including surface exposure of CRT, HMGB1 efflux, and ATP secretion. Immunohistochemical analyses indicate that RMOCZ-induced ICD remodels the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, promoting CD86+ antigen-presenting cell maturation and enhancing intratumoral infiltration of CD3+ and CD8+ T cells.

In vivo, RMOCZ suppresses melanoma progression without inducing detectable systemic toxicity. However, several limitations warrant attention. While effective in subcutaneous models, these models may not fully capture the immune complexity and metastatic dynamics of human melanoma. Additionally, the long-term clearance and accumulation kinetics of the bimetallic nanozyme needs systematic evaluation in large animal models. Future studies should prioritize orthotopic and metastatic models to assess immunological memory and potential abscopal effects. Such investigations will support the translation of this combined cell death strategy toward clinical application.

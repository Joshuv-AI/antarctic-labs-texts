Factors associated with device-associated health care-associated infections after cardiac surgery: a matched cohort study
Authors: Mengxue Yan, Kaining Wang, Siyu Liu, Haoqi Yan, Bo Kang, Wenying Kang, Yuan Jia, Su Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe study aimed to identify the patient-related and procedure-related factors independently associated with the development of device-associated health care-associated infections (DA-HAIs) in cardiac intensive care unit (CICU), specifically for ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP), catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI), and catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTI).MethodsWe designed a retrospective single-center matched cohort study in CICU at a tertiary referral hospital. The study reviewed all adult patients who underwent cardiac surgery between January 2020, and December 2023. Each patient with a DA-HAI as a case was matched to two control patients admitted to the CICU within ±7 days. Controls had the same catheter in place but did not develop an infection. The matching covariates were: age, weight, sex, count of white blood cell, neutrophil, and lymphocyte. Data were collected from patient records. Independent predictors for DA-HAIs in the CICU were identified through multivariate logistic regression analysis, following an initial univariate screening.ResultsA total of 89 patients with DA-HAIs were matched with 178 controls. Multivariate analysis identified cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) time >2 hours (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 7.15, 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) [2.22–23.09]; p = 0.001) and mechanical ventilation >7 days (aOR 13.10, 95% CI [2.09–82.02]; p = 0.006) as strong, independent risk factors. Conversely, a history of valvular surgery (aOR 0.003, 95% CI [0.00–0.03]; p < 0.001), a diagnosis of aortic dissection (aOR 0.06, 95% CI [0.00–0.07]; p < 0.001), and undergoing CABG surgery (aOR 0.053, 95% CI [0.01–0.22]; p < 0.001) were associated with a significantly lower likelihood of infection.ConclusionsThis study identifies prolonged CPB (>2 hours) and mechanical ventilation (>7 days) as key independent risk factors for DA-HAIs in cardiac surgery patients, underscoring that infection prevention should focus on reducing CPB time and promoting early extubation.

Introduction

Invasive catheters are indispensable in the postoperative management of cardiac surgery patients, providing vital access and monitoring within the cardiac intensive care unit (CICU). Nonetheless, the use of devices such as endotracheal tubes, central venous catheters, arterial catheters, and urinary catheters places this vulnerable population at significant risk for device-associated healthcare-associated infections (DA-HAIs), including ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP), catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI), and catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTI). DA-HAIs lead to prolonged hospitalization, increased healthcare costs, and elevated morbidity and mortality (Kollef, Hamilton & Ernst, 2012; Thomas et al., 2012). While general ICU data report incidences of 6.4 for VAP, 1.9 for CRBSI, and 3.4 for CAUTI per 1,000 device-days (Nuvials et al., 2025), rates in cardiac surgery patients are consistently and markedly higher, particularly for VAP (12.78 per 1,000 ventilator-days) (Spagnolello et al., 2022; Supraja et al., 2022).

In response, evidence-based best-practice bundles have been established to mitigate DA-HAIs (O’Grady et al., 2002; Leong et al., 2024). Cardiac surgery patients in the CICU possess a distinct risk profile, defined by advanced age, CPB-induced systemic inflammation and immunosuppression, and concurrent use of multiple invasive devices. Consequently, infection prevention guidelines derived from general ICU populations are inadequately validated for this specific pathophysiology. We hypothesized that in addition to established risk factors, cardiac surgery-specific elements, such as prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and mechanical ventilation durations, would be independently associated with an increased risk of DA-HAIs. Therefore, this study aims to identify the patient-related (e.g., comorbidities) and procedure-related (e.g., CPB times) factors independently associated with the development of DA-HAIs in a contemporary cohort of patients admitted to the CICU after cardiac surgery.

Materials & Methods

This propensity score-matched (PSM) retrospective cohort study was conducted at Fuwai Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences & National Center for Cardiovascular Diseases. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital (NO.2018–1073) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This report adheres to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement. The requirement for individual informed consent was waived by the approving ethics committee, as this retrospective study utilized existing secondary data from medical records.

Selection of cases and controls

We included all adult patients who underwent cardiac surgery and were admitted to the CICU between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1). Patients over 18 years old; (2). Patients with the presence of any catheters such as endotracheal, central venous, arterial, or urinary catheters; (3). Patients without any infections or sepsis before surgery. Patients with incomplete medical records were excluded from the analysis. The primary outcome was the development of a DA-HAI, including VAP, CRBSI, or CAUTI. The diagnosis of all DA-HAIs was based on composite criteria integrating clinical, radiological, and mandatory microbiological evidence (Miron et al., 2024), strictly adhering to guidelines from the National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) (Horan, Andrus & Dudeck, 2008) and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) (European Centre for Disease Prevention Control, 2015). Specifically, VAP required a new infiltrate on imaging plus microbiological (e.g., positive respiratory culture) or clinical evidence (e.g., purulent sputum). CRBSI was confirmed by at least one positive blood culture accompanied by systemic signs of infection. CAUTI required compatible symptoms in a catheterized patient plus a urine culture obtained aseptically yielding >105 CFU/mL of no more than two species. All diagnoses were independently verified by two clinicians. If more than one DA-HAI episode occurred in a single patient, only the first occurrence was included in the analysis.

For each case, two control patients were selected using propensity matching. Controls were required to: (1) have been admitted to the CICU within a ±7-day window of the index case to account for temporal variations in practice; (2) have the same type of catheter in place; and (3) not develop any DA-HAI. The propensity matching was estimated using a logistic regression model that included key baseline covariates: age, weight, sex, and hematological indices (white blood cell, neutrophil, and lymphocyte). This approach balanced the groups on measurable confounders.

Data collection and outcomes

Data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system using a standardized case report form. To minimize bias, data collection was performed in two blinded stages: (1) Two trained researchers, collecting data prior to and independently of outcome assessment, gathered all baseline variables. (2) The DA-HAI outcome was then independently adjudicated by two other researchers blinded to the baseline data, based on predefined NHSN/ECDC criteria. Discrepancies were resolved by a third senior clinician. The pre-operative specific profile of patients were recorded: age, weight, sex, medical history, surgical history and the last counts of white blood cell, neutrophil, and lymphocyte before surgery. Intraoperative anesthesia and surgery variables included: the type of surgery, transfusion, and the data of CPB. The post-operative variables were also collected: the use of antibiotics and complications.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were transformed into binary or categorical variables based on the median, 75th percentile, or clinically relevant thresholds. The age variable was grouped based on clinical significance and data distribution: under 50 years old (young adults); aged 50–64 (middle-aged); aged 65–74 (old); ≥75 years old (senior). Weight was transformed into a multi-category variable based on its interquartile range. The count of white blood cell count was categorized into three groups according to inflammatory status. Both the counts of neutrophil and lymphocyte were converted into binary variables using the second quartile as the cutoff. Nominal and ordinal variables were consolidated into binary categories where applicable. Binary independent variables were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). A multivariate conditional logistic regression model was developed, incorporating all variables with a statistically significant association (p < 0.05) identified in the univariate analysis, as well as those variables deemed clinically relevant to the development of DA-HAIs, regardless of their statistical significance. A forward stepwise exclusion procedure was employed to refine the model until the final version was achieved. All statistical analyses were two-tailed with the threshold of significance set at P < 0.05. SPSS (version 26.0) software (SPSS) was used to perform data analysis.

Incidence and microbiology of DA-HAIs

Between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2023, a total of 89 patients in the CICU were diagnosed with DA-HAIs. This represents an overall incidence of 0.16% among patients admitted to the CICU, with an infection density of 0.19 per 1,000 patient-days—a rate lower than the 1.0% VAP incidence reported in a comparable cohort (Wang et al., 2022). The distribution was as follows: VAP in 60 cases (67.4%), CAUTI in 18 cases (20.2%), and CRBSI in 11 cases (12.4%). Among the CRBSI cases, 8 (9.0% of total) were associated with CVCs and three (3.4% of total) with arterial catheters (Table 2). Detailed microbiological profiles are presented in Table 3. Microbiological analysis of 161 non-duplicate isolates revealed a predominance of Gram-negative bacteria (129 strains, 80.1%). The most common pathogens were Klebsiella pneumoniae (43 strains, 26.7%) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (28 strains, 17.4%). K. pneumoniae exhibited high susceptibility to carbapenems (93.3%) and amikacin (95.6%), while P. aeruginosa showed low resistance rates to ceftazidime (10.7%) and high susceptibility to amikacin (100%).

Univariate associations with DA-HAIs

Univariate logistic regression identified multiple factors associated with DA-HAIs (Table 4). Statistically significant preoperative factors included a history of arrhythmia (OR 3.76, 95% CI [2.08–6.80]; p < 0.001), angina pectoris (OR 0.43, 95% CI [0.22–0.86]; p = 0.017), myocardial infarction (OR 1.94, 95% CI [1.02–3.69]; p = 0.045), heart failure (OR 2.73, 95% CI [1.29–5.79]; p = 0.009), aortic dissection (OR 0.41, 95% CI [0.17–0.99]; p = 0.048), ulcer disease (OR 3.50, 95% CI [1.03–11.96]; p = 0.046), and valvular surgery (OR 0.05, 95% CI [0.02–0.14]; p < 0.001). Significant surgical factors included the surgery patients undergone, such as valvular replacement surgery (OR 0.27, 95% CI [0.13–0.54]; p < 0.001), CABG surgery (OR 0.43, 95% CI [0.25–0.76]; p = 0.003), aortic surgery (OR 0.27, 95% CI [0.14–0.54]; p < 0.001), heart transplantation surgery (OR 3.35, 95% CI [1.23–9.09]; p = 0.018), and mixed (valvular/CABG) surgery (OR 0.24, 95% CI [0.00–0.86]; p = 0.041). CPB use (OR 2.93, 95% CI [1.28–6.75]; p = 0.011) and CPB duration exceeding two hours (OR 1.83, 95% CI [1.09–3.06]; p = 0.022) were also significant risk factors. Among postoperative factors, empiric antibiotic use for one day was associated with a lower risk (OR 0.56, 95% CI [0.33–0.95]; p = 0.032).

Independent multivariate predictors of DA-HAIs

Multivariate conditional logistic regression analysis, adjusting for confounding variables, identified five independent predictors (Table 5). Prolonged mechanical ventilation exceeding seven days (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 13.10, 95% CI [2.09–82.02]; p = 0.006) and CPB duration over two hours (aOR 7.15, 95% CI [2.22–23.09]; p = 0.001) were strong risk factors. Conversely, a diagnosis of aortic dissection (aOR 0.06, 95% CI [0.00–0.07]; p < 0.001), a history of valvular surgery (aOR 0.003, 95% CI [0.00–0.03]; p < 0.001), and undergoing CABG surgery (aOR 0.053, 95% CI [0.01–0.22]; p < 0.001) were significant protective factors. A post-hoc assessment indicated that with the large effect sizes observed for key independent risk factors, this sample size provided ample statistical power (>80%) for the multivariate analyses conducted.

Subgroup analyses by infection type

Given the predominance of VAP cases, subgroup analyses were performed (Table 6). For VAP alone (n = 60), prolonged mechanical ventilation time over seven days (aOR 2.80, 95% CI [1.10–7.13]; p = 0.030) and CPB time over two hours (aOR 3.49, 95% CI [1.57–7.73]; p = 0.002) were identified as independent risk factors. For non-VAP cases (n = 29), prolonged CPB time over two hours was also a significant independent risk factor (aOR 5.92, 95% CI [1.45–24.27]; p = 0.013). Prolonged mechanical ventilation time over seven days was not statistically significant in this subgroup (aOR 3.81, 95% CI [0.64–22.88]; p = 0.143).

Discussion

In this study, we analyzed both patient-specific and procedural variables to identify those most strongly associated with the development of DA-HAIs in CICU. Our multivariable analysis highlighted that both prolonged CPB times (>2 h) and ventilation times (>7 days) were significant risk factors of DA-HAIs. Interestingly, patients diagnosed with aortic dissection (AD), those with a history of valvular surgery, or those who underwent CABG surgery were less likely to develop DA-HAIs. Thus, the most straightforward interpretation of our model is that patients undergoing more complex procedures and those with a complicated postoperative course face the highest risk of DA-HAIs.

Our findings confirmed that CPB times exceeding 2 h were an independent risk factor for DA-HAIs, a result that aligned with previous research. Cardiac surgery involving CPB could triggered undesirable inflammatory responses, including surgical trauma, ischemia-reperfusion injury, exposure of blood to non-endothelial surfaces, and substantial pulmonary dysfunction (Hadley et al., 2007; Rankin et al., 2011; Warren et al., 2009). When blood circulated through non-endothelialized surfaces of the circuit, it activated the innate immune system, which can alter the body’s response to subsequent infections (Millar et al., 2016; Al-Fares, Pettenuzzo & Del Sorbo, 2019). Previous studies had shown that extracorporeal circulation increased the risk of colonization and infection with multidrug-resistant organisms (Grasselli et al., 2019; Schmidt et al., 2012). However, further research is needed to clarify the pathophysiology of DA-HAIs following CPB.

In the multivariate analysis, we also found that prolonged postoperative ventilation time (more than 7 days) remained a significant independent risk factor for DA-HAIs, confirming the results of earlier studies. Prolonged mechanical ventilation increased the risk of VAP by compromising the respiratory system’s natural defense mechanisms, impairing the clearance and promoting the pooling of secretions, and causing local trauma and inflammation (Helmick et al., 2014). Furthermore, extended ventilation duration increased infection risk by compromising oxygenation and promoting opportunistic bacterial growth. Prolonged mechanical ventilation had also been linked to disruptions in the composition and membership of respiratory microbiome community (Kelly et al., 2016; Harrigan et al., 2021). Consequently, strategies like the Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol, which emphasize shortening ventilator days and promoting early extubation, should be further developed and prioritized.

The multivariate model suggested strong protective associations for a history of valvular surgery, a diagnosis of aortic dissection, and CABG surgery. However, these estimates were extremely unstable with confidence intervals approaching zero, indicating a high probability of statistical artifact due to issues such as model overfitting or data separation. Therefore, the following interpretations regarding these factors are highly speculative and must be viewed with extreme caution pending validation in a larger, more robustly analyzed cohort. We hypothesize that the mandatory postoperative anticoagulation therapy in valvular surgery patients may reduce the risk of DA-HAIs by minimizing catheter-related thrombus formation, which serves as a nidus for microbial colonization (Geerts, 2014; Baskin et al., 2009). The association with AD may reflect a younger patient profile with fewer chronic comorbidities (Lemaignen et al., 2015). The link to CABG surgery could be confounded by the high proportion of these patients receiving statins, which possess anti-inflammatory properties (Glynn et al., 2009; Almeida & Budoff, 2019).

The subgroup analyses by infection type largely corroborated the primary multivariate model, underscoring the robustness of the identified risk factors. While prolonged CPB time emerged as a consistent independent risk factor across both VAP and non-VAP infections, prolonged mechanical ventilation was specifically and significantly associated with VAP, highlighting a distinct pathophysiological linkage for this infection type.

Our microbiological analysis revealed a pathogen distribution dominated by Gram-negative bacteria, principally Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. This profile is consistent with the global shift toward Gram-negative organisms in healthcare settings, which underscores the persistent challenge of antimicrobial resistance (Garcia-Parejo et al., 2025). Notably, the key pathogens in our cohort retained high susceptibility to first-line agents: K. pneumoniae to carbapenems and amikacin, and P. aeruginosa to ceftazidime and amikacin. Our findings emphasize that while these agents remain effective, culture results must rapidly guide de-escalation to narrow-spectrum therapy—a key stewardship measure informed by local surveillance to counter rising resistance (Zahar & Moenne-Locoz, 2025).

Left ventricular assist device (LVAD) infections constitute a major and distinct challenge in the management of patients with advanced heart failure. Driveline infections remain the most common type, predominantly caused by Gram-positive bacteria such as Staphylococcus aureus (Brandão et al., 2025). The prevention and management of these infections are complex, necessitating specialized perioperative strategies (e.g., nasal decolonization, meticulous driveline tunneling techniques), sustained patient education on exit-site care, and multidisciplinary long-term follow-up (Brandão et al., 2024). Although our study focused on acute nosocomial infections in the immediate postoperative period and did not capture LVAD-specific events, evidence regarding specific risk factors remains evolving and sometimes contradictory, underscoring the need for targeted research in this vulnerable population (Michelly Gonçalves Brandão et al., 2024).

Beyond the scope of our study on DA-HAIs, surgical site infections (SSIs) represent another major threat with considerable morbidity in cardiac surgery patients. Notably, the shared predominance of pathogens like Klebsiella pneumoniae in both SSIs and DA-HAIs highlights a common antimicrobial resistance challenge, underscoring the need for integrated infection control strategies (Santana Miranda et al., 2021). Targeted preventive measures, including strict glycemic control and the application of advanced wound therapies like negative pressure wound therapy (Cardiothoracic Interdisciplinary Research Network et al., 2024), are essential components of quality improvement initiatives aimed at reducing all healthcare-associated infections in this high-risk surgical population.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective, single-center design limits causal inference and generalizability. Key variables such as detailed catheter maintenance practices and precise device exposure times were not available, and while statistically adjusted for, this remains a constraint. The sample size, though adequate for detecting strong effects, resulted in so tight or wide confidence intervals for some estimates. Future prospective, multicenter studies are needed to establish causality and build robust predictive models for infection prevention in cardiac surgery patients.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the study identifies prolonged CPB (>2 h) and mechanical ventilation (>7 days) as the key risk factors of DA-HAIs in cardiac surgery patients. The findings suggest that procedural choices, such as reducing CPB time and promoting early extubation, are critical for designing effective infection prevention protocols. The associations suggesting a protective effect for the diagnosis of AD, valvular surgery history, or CABG surgery are statistically unstable and require validation in larger cohorts. Prospective, multicenter studies are needed to translate these insights into validated clinical prediction tools.

An intrusion detection system in the Internet of Things with deep learning and an improved arithmetic optimization algorithm (AOA) and sine cosine algorithm (SCA)
Authors: Raheleh Ghadami
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Internet of Things (IoT) is vulnerable to cyber-attacks due to limited security mechanisms and resources constraints. Traditional intrusion detection systems (IDS) deal with imbalanced datasets, high-dimensional network traffic, and the inability to detect new attacks. This research proposes an advanced IDS framework that utilizes game-theory-based Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) for dataset balancing, a hybrid Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (AOA), and a Sine Cosine Algorithm (SCA) for feature selection, and a Parallel Convolutional Neural Network (PCNN) combined with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layer for accurate attack detection. The suggested ASPCNNLSTM model achieves a precision of 99.86% on the NSL-KDD dataset and an attack detection accuracy of 98.67% on the UNSW-NB15 dataset, significantly outperforming traditional CNN, LSTM, and feature selection methods. This strategy improves IDS capability by selecting the best features relevant to traffic features and improving spatial and temporal feature learning, making it robust against complex and unknown cyber threats.Keywords: Internet of Things (IoT), Intrusion detection system (IDS), Feature selection, Generative adversarial network (GAN), Game theory, Parallel convolutional neural network (PCNN)Subject terms: Computer science, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) is not just a network of sensors and smart objects; it’s a web that connects us all. Communication technologies like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are the threads that bind objects to the IoT, creating a vast and interconnected ecosystem. The IoT empowers objects to receive information from the environment and share it with other objects and the network, fostering a sense of connection and collaboration1. The IoT has a multi-layer framework defined in the simplest three-layer model. In the lower layer, there are sensors, and for this reason, this layer is called the sensor or perception layer. The role of the sensor layer is to receive information and send it to a higher layer or network. In some studies, the network layer is called the fog layer. The fog layer, which is a distributed computing infrastructure that extends the cloud to the edge of the IoT, has many nodes for processing network traffic. It is responsible for part of the calculations in the Internet of Things and sends the other part to the application or cloud layers. Due to being close to the sensor layer, the fog layer can perform real-time processing in the network2. Internet of Things is used in many applications, such as smart cities, agriculture, health and the military3.

One of the most essential and significant challenges in the IoTs is the occurrence of cyber-attacks in this network. Cyber-attacks on the IoTs, mainly the Industrial Internet of Things, can cause significant damage4. Reports show that between 2013 and 2017, the number of viruses released on the IoTs has increased at least seven times. For example, cyber-attacks have escalated widely in the last few years and threaten the network’s central infrastructure. Examples of these attacks are the attacks on the power grid of Ukraine5 and the nuclear power plant in Iran6. DL methods, if trained on simple traffic and known attacks, have less ability to detect malicious attacks, so DL approaches can be intelligently trained by generating artificial traffic and adding it to the training data set. The harm caused by cyber-attacks on the Internet is significant. For instance, phishing attacks like distributed denial of service (DDoS)7, annually cost millions of dollars to the network and its users.

Malware grows and develops daily in the IoTs, such as the Mirai malware, which carried out a cyber-attack in 2018 with a maximum bandwidth of 620 Gbps. The “IoTroop” malware is another example in recent years that orchestrated a distributed denial of service (DDoS) attack, during which the financial sector was targeted. In recent years, organizations affected by DDoS attacks have faced a 26% decrease in operating profit and a 41% increase in the disruption of functional services8. The competition of companies manufacturing sensors to reduce costs has caused the security of these bright objects to be sacrificed. A suitable approach to deal with attacks on the Internet of Things is to use security tools such as intrusion detection systems9, firewalls10and honeypots11. The role of an intrusion detection system is to detect attacks and provide reports to security managers and firewalls12. This paper presents an AES-based approach that facilitates secure and efficient keyword search over encrypted cloud data, effectively data balancing privacy and actual-world search capabilities13. Deep learning methods are one of the intrusion detection methods that can detect complex and somewhat unknown attacks14. Deep learning methods are a branch of artificial intelligence (AI) that play a vital role in developing security networks for the Internet of Things15.

DL methods such as CNN16, intrusion detection techniques utilizing LSTM17 and intrusion detection approaches utilizing quantum learning18 have been proposed to detect network intrusion. Although DL methods are extensively utilized in the development of intrusion detection systems and intrusion prevention systems, they also have challenges. One of the significant challenges of these methods is training on unbalanced data sets19. The OMIC framework is a scalable and optimized multiclass intrusion detection system that attains 99.26% accuracy on extensive IoT datasets by adeptly managing class imbalance and large-scale data through an ensemble of XGBoost and LightGBM algorithms20. The imbalance and the smaller number of attack samples compared to regular ones make the intrusion detection system more accurate in detecting regular traffic, and its error rate in detecting attacks increases21 Neighbourhood-based methods such as SMOTE22 solve imbalanced datasets. DL approaches and GAN networks are widely used for generating artificial data and balancing the data set23,24.

One of the other challenges of DL in intrusion detection systems is the many parameters of the model, whose optimization is an NP-hard problem25. Genetic algorithm (GA)25 and Particle swarm optimization (PSO)26 have been used to optimize DL models in attack detection. This research introduces a lightweight BT-TPF model based on knowledge distillation, achieving over 99% accuracy in real-time IoT Intrusion detection while reducing model size and computational overhead by about 90%27. Network traffic has many features, and if the DL inputs are not dimensionally reduced, two problems arise28. Feature selection and feature engineering methods allow DL approaches to increase their accuracy29. Statistical methods such as Information gain (IG)30 and Backward Feature Selection31 select features in developing intrusion detection systems. Still, these methods are simple and ignore some basic features. Meta-heuristic methods are one of the practical methods for feature engineering in network attack detection. Feature selection methods based on WOA32, HHO33, GWO34, AO35 and AVOA36 can be used in the development of intrusion detection systems.

The proposed method

The analysis and related works in intrusion detection system (IDS) for the IoT network underscore the profound significance of the challenges in this field, emphasizing the critical need for innovative solutions. The first significant challenge in designing network intrusion detection systems is the limited ability to detect complex and unknown attacks. This problem is especially significant due to the lack of balance in the numbers of regular and attack samples, with the former outnumbering the latter. In other words, the attack class is of the minority type, and the regular traffic class is of the majority type. Applying game theory emerges as a promising approach to significantly enhance the detection capability of sophisticated network attacks, instilling a sense of optimism in the field of intrusion detection. In the suggested approach, an improved method based on GAN is used to generate artificial samples and add these samples to the dataset. The GAN method adds a large number of unknown attacks to the data set, and the learning model is trained on these features. While previous work utilized GANs and optimization techniques for intrusion detection, the proposed method introduces some notable improvements to enhance performance. Unlike conventional GAN-based IDS models, this research integrates a game-theory-enhanced GAN for balancing the dataset to ensure better generalization to new attack patterns. Furthermore, the AOA and SCA are improved with adaptive mechanisms to support dynamic parameter tuning to optimize feature selection. This refinement enhances both classification accuracy and computational efficiency.

The model presents a new PCNN with a fully connected LSTM layer, which captures spatial and temporal attack patterns very effectively. The combination of these techniques enables more effective feature extraction, reducing false positive rates while improving detection accuracy. To further validate the effectiveness of these improvements, an extensive ablation study is conducted by comparing a number of feature selection and deep learning models under comparable conditions.

Traffic balance by game theory

Perhaps the most significant challenge in developing an effective model for intrusion detection arises from the imbalance in most training datasets. This issue increases error rates in network intrusion detection. One remedy is to artificially increase the number of examples in the minority class, which represents attacks; doing so enhances the model’s capability to detect complex and unknown attacks. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) can generate artificial samples and consist of a Generator (G) and a Discriminator (D). This GAN framework operates on principles of game theory, whereby the generator and discriminator engage in a game to create realistic artificial samples. A GAN used to generate random samples is illustrated in Fig. 5.

Structure and architecture of GAN for generating artificial samples.

In this architecture, the generator (G) creates synthetic samples (Pg) from a noise vector (Z), while the discriminator (D) assesses both real (Pdata) and synthetic samples. Conventional balancing methods such as SMOTE are constrained in producing realistic attack samples since their reliance on interpolation, whereas the proposed game-theory-based GAN, incorporating a self-attention mechanism, produces high-quality synthetic samples that closely resemble actual attack patterns, enhancing the model’s robustness. Each input comprises a d-dimensional feature vector of network traffic and a noise vector Z, which contains random numbers in the range [0,1]. To improve GAN performance, a self-attention mechanism is incorporated into the generator, enabling it to better capture the distribution of traffic features and produce realistic camouflaged samples. The discriminator evaluates these inputs and provides feedback to the generator, leading to continuous adversarial training until high-quality samples are generated. Traffic coding to spectrogram images

A spectrogram is a graphical representation of a signal’s frequency spectrum (FS) over time. It is a color map displayed in terms of time and frequency and helps us understand how a signal’s FS changes over time. The spectrogram image is an essential component in enhancing our understanding of complex signals by visualizing their frequency components. The Short-Term Fourier Transform (STFT) is used to obtain spectrogram images from network traffic data, effectively converting numerical data into visual representations. This conversion reveals hidden patterns within the data and enables the detection of small anomalies in network traffic with high accuracy. Spectrogram images exhibit distinctive visual features, including variations in color intensity and signal amplitudes, which provide profound insights into the nature of the traffic. Since spectrogram images are inherently based on complex numbers, extracting the absolute value of the color data is essential for obtaining numeric or logical values, ensuring integrity and coherence in the resultant dataset. Additionally, to create a spectrogram, sound waves must first be converted to their base frequencies, with the Fourier Transform serving as the mathematical function that transforms a signal into a set of frequencies. The main objective of this process is to generate spectrogram images using the Dnm dataset.

Results and discussion

In this section, the network attack detection system is evaluated and analyzed. MATLAB is used for feature selection and image coding. Python software balances the dataset and implements GAN, PCNN, and LSTM neural networks. Experiments are conducted on a Windows 64-bit operating system powered by an Intel Core i7 CPU running at 2.80 GHz, equipped with 16 GB of RAM and a dedicated Nvidia GPU with 4 GB of RAM. The NSL-KDD and UNSW-NB15 intrusion detection datasets are used for implementation. The population size for the metaheuristic algorithms is set at 20, with a maximum of 100 iterations. The dataset is normalized to a range between [0, 1], and 70% of the data is allocated for training, while the remaining 30% is reserved for testing and validation. Cross-validation is performed using (K-fold = 10). The learning rate is set to 0.001, with SGD as the optimizer. The training process consists of 100 epochs, with a weight decay of 0.0001 and a dropout rate of 0.01.

Data set

The UNSW-NB15 dataset is a vital resource in intrusion detection due to its relevance to modern cyber threats. Developed at the Australian Center for Cyber Security, it includes benign traffic and five types of attack traffic: Fuzzers, Normal, Generic, Denial of Service (DoS), and Exploits. The dataset contains 49 features 48 input variables and one output variable denoting traffic type providing a framework for evaluating intrusion detection systems. Figure 12 illustrates a significant frequency disparity among attack types, with analysis attacks having the highest number of samples, while less frequent attacks, like backdoor attacks, are minimally represented. This imbalance 87% of samples classified as normal traffic and only 13% as various attack types underscore the need for an effective data balancing strategy. To tackle this issue, the suggested technique uses GANs to generate additional attack samples, aligning their numbers with those of the normal samples. The total count of normal samples is set at 120,000, with an equal number of attack samples for balance. Additionally, the NSL-KDD dataset serves as a benchmark for this study, consisting of 41 input features and one output feature. Similar to the UNSW-NB15 dataset, the NSL-KDD dataset also faces initial imbalance, which is corrected through the GAN method, enhancing the overall effectiveness of the proposed approach (Fig. 13).

Conclusion

IoT represents a diverse and interconnected network of devices which makes it highly susceptible to cyber-attacks that create serious threats to its infrastructure. Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) play a crucial role in securing these networks against such threats, especially when it comes to detecting sophisticated and previously unknown attacks. Traditional signature-based IDS techniques are inadequate to address the heightened complexity of these threats. They are memory-intensive and cannot be easily modified to learn new types of attacks, thus machine learning and deep learning techniques are essential for IDS today. Nevertheless, these approaches are challenged by issues like dataset imbalance, which tends to result in greater focus on normal traffic compared to attack traffic. Additionally, many deep learning methods employ dimensionality reduction and feature selection early in the process, which typically sacrifices detection accuracy.

To address these issues, the proposed approach utilizes a game-theory-based GAN for detecting complex and unknown attacks. This paper implements an attention mechanism in the GAN’s generator, significantly improving the quality of artificial samples of unknown attacks, thus enhancing detection effectiveness. The proposed method employs two algorithms for feature selection and network traffic dimensionality reduction. The reduced network traffic is encoded into spectrogram images, allowing a CNN to train on these images. Each type of attack corresponds to a specific spectrogram image, from which spatial features are extracted using two parallel CNNs. Temporal features are then selected using LSTM networks, which classify the network traffic in a fully connected layer of a PCNN.

The proposed ASPCNNLSTM method demonstrates remarkable performance with an accuracy of 99.86%, sensitivity of 99.72%, and precision of 99.68% on the NSL-KDD dataset. For the UNSW-NB15 dataset, the method achieves an accuracy of 98.67%, a sensitivity of 98.21%, and a precision of 97.96%. These results indicate that balancing and feature selection significantly enhance the PCNN-LSTM model’s accuracy in attack detection. To assess the model’s resistance against emerging malicious traffic, future experiments will incorporate a specialized test set comprising previously unseen attack types to measure the GAN’s impact on generalization to novel threats.

The AOA and SCA algorithms are integrated into the ASPCNNLSTM method for optimal feature selection. Experiments reveal that this combined approach outperforms individual algorithms such as HHO, JSO, and BKA in accuracy. Despite these strengths, the ASPCNNLSTM method requires more time in the feature selection phase compared to AOA and SCA due to the integration of multiple algorithms. This paper proposes enhancements for the ASPCNNLSTM model to mitigate time complexity in real-time IoT applications, utilizing parallel processing and hardware acceleration to minimize feature selection latency in large-scale networks. Even though the model provides high accuracy, its disadvantages include heightened computational demands from the hybrid AOA-SCA feature selection and from the requirement for broader validation on different IoT datasets to confirm generalization. The robustness of the model is substantiated by thorough testing on NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT datasets with tenfold cross-validation. Nevertheless, real world implementation could encounter challenges like resource limitations on IoT devices and the need for ongoing retraining to address emerging attack patterns. The combined AOA-SCA algorithm effectively merges exploratory and exploitative capabilities for feature selection. When compared to CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and LSTM-CNN methods, the ASPCNNLSTM method provides superior attack detection due to dataset balancing, temporal and spatial feature extraction, and intelligent feature selection using AOA and SCA.

Overall, the ASPCNNLSTM method shows strong performance by effectively integrating spatial–temporal feature extraction with balanced training data. Its enhanced GAN-based balancing mechanism contributes to robust detection of previously unseen attack types, while the spectrogram-based encoding ensures efficient and accurate traffic classification. Looking forward, the future direction in intrusion detection will be in data privacy preservation through techniques such as federated learning, where model training can be decentralized without transferring sensitive data from local devices. This approach can address the privacy problems in central data processing and collection. Furthermore, deploying the proposed IDS within a distributed architecture, such as Apache Spark, could enable the analysis of large volumes of network traffic more efficiently, enhancing real-time detection capabilities. Future research will explore these possibilities to further improve the scalability, efficiency, and privacy of IDS systems in the ever-evolving world of IoT security.

For real-time implementation of IoT, the ASPCNNLSTM model must tackle issues like scalability, latency, and adaptation to rapidly changing attack patterns. Scalability can be improved using distributed computing frameworks such as Apache Spark, as noted, while latency can be minimized through model compression or edge-based processing methods. To address emerging attack patterns, continuous retraining with incremental learning methods can be employed maintain the IDS’s effectiveness against new threats. Future research will focus on evaluating the ASPCNNLSTM model on additional IoT-specific datasets, such as TON-IoT and CIC-IoT-2023, to further confirm its robustness across various types of attacks and network conditions. To enhance resilience against adversarial attacks, future research will investigate adversarial training methods and robust optimization techniques to provide the ASPCNNLSTM model remains secure against manipulated input data. For real-time implementation on resource-limited IoT devices, the ASPCNNLSTM model requires approximately 4 GB of GPU memory for training and 500 MB for inference, as determined by the experimental setup. Scalability issues, such as handling large volumes of network traffic, can be addressed using distributed frameworks like Apache Spark, though additional optimizations, like model pruning or quantization, are necessary to minimize memory and computational demands on low-power IoT devices.

Coronary artery calcium on lung cancer screening-CT: An opportunity to optimize cardiovascular disease risk reduction
Authors: Bethlehem Mengesha, Keren Mbondo Kasuku, Toby David Small, Marcella Cabral Caires, Habibat Garuba, Angeline Law, Christopher Johnson, David Ian Paterson, Carole Dennie, Elena Pena, Benjamin JW Chow, Gary R Small
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn lung cancer screening trials, mortality from cardiovascular disease occurs at similar rates to lung cancer deaths. Survival following lung cancer screening could be optimized if atherosclerosis prevention was targeted. Thus, we sought to determine whether there was potential for improvement in rates of cardiovascular risk reduction therapy based upon coronary artery calcium and cardiovascular risk assessment.MethodsClinical lung cancer screening-CT reports, lipid lowering therapy and clinical demographics were retrieved from the electronic medical record in the first consecutive 1486 cases without known coronary artery disease from the Ontario High Risk Lung Cancer Screening Pilot program. Lung cancer screening CT images were reviewed for presence and extent of coronary artery calcium.ResultsCoronary artery calcium was detected in 83 % and was reported in 63 %. Lipid lowering was prescribed in 60 % of cases whose coronary artery calcium was reported versus 45 % of cases when coronary artery calcium was unreported (p < 0.001). On multivariable analysis, increased Framingham risk score (OR 2.31 95 % CI 1.73–2.31, p < 0.001) and reported coronary artery calcium (OR 1.53 95 % CI 1.22–1.92, p < 0.001) were associated with lipid lowering therapy. Additional cardiovascular risk lowering could be achieved in 21 % using coronary artery calcium and in 44 % with further consideration of clinical risk.ConclusionsIn lung cancer screened patients, cardiovascular risk reduction could be optimized significantly by the opportunistic use of coronary artery calcium and clinical assessment. Appropriate cardiovascular risk reduction could attenuate the high prevalence of cardiovascular deaths in these individuals and improve overall survival.Keywords: Cardiovascular prevention, Coronary Calcium, lung cancer screening CT

Introduction

14.2 Million US citizens are eligible for lung cancer screening by low dose chest computed tomography (CT) [1]. A relative 20 % reduction in lung cancer mortality is expected from this initiative[2,3]. A further reduction in mortality rates might be possible with cardiovascular risk reduction as co-existent coronary artery disease is highly prevalent in this population[2,4,5].

Asymptomatic coronary artery disease has been detected by lung cancer screening trials in 47–83 % of cases as coronary artery calcium [[4], [5], [6]]. On a lung cancer screening CT, the heart is within the field of view and consequently coronary artery calcium is readily identified[7,8].

Consistent with the high prevalence of coronary calcium on lung cancer CTs are the high rates of cardiovascular events in lung cancer screening studies. Cardiovascular mortality (21–25 %) competes with lung cancer mortality (21 %) as the leading cause of death[2,3].

The extent of coronary artery calcium correlates with the amount of coronary artery atherosclerosis[9]. It also correlates with the degree of clinical risk from coronary artery disease events[10,11]. All-cause mortality and cardiovascular events rates are highest in those with extensive coronary artery calcium in comparison to individuals with mild or no coronary calcification [4,7,8]. Thus, consensus guidelines for reporting coronary artery calcium on chest CT recommend the reporting of both the presence and extent of disease[12,13].

Coronary artery calcium may not be included in reports of lung cancer screening CTs given that this could be considered beyond the scope of testing[[14], [15], [16]]. Furthermore, if these findings are only mentioned in the body of the report, coronary artery calcium data may not be apparent to the ordering practitioner and subsequently not acted upon[14]. As a result, an opportunity to recommend cardiovascular risk reduction with lipid lowering therapy can be missed. This opportunity is unique as lung cancer screened individuals have attended to determine the presence of lung cancer but are often found to have an equally important disease. Yet, coronary artery disease can go unrecognized or untreated. A patient with health seeking behavior is thus underserved and the chance to start effective cardiovascular care is overlooked.

As lung cancer screening CT is adopted more broadly, coincidental coronary artery disease will be detected as coronary artery calcification[5]. How coronary calcification is reported, responded to, or further investigated will determine whether all the healthcare data available from screening is used optimally to improve health outcomes. From a cardiovascular perspective lung cancer screening initiatives represent a timely opportunity to prevent cardiovascular events via appropriate use of cardiovascular risk reduction therapy.

We therefore sought to determine a) lipid lowering therapy prevalence in lung cancer screened individuals without a history of coronary artery disease; b) whether there was potential to improve the frequency and appropriateness of risk reduction therapy and c) if coronary artery calcium reporting influenced therapy.

Methods

This cross-sectional study was reviewed by the Ottawa Health Sciences Network Research Ethics Board and the need for individual patient consent was waived (Quality Improvement project #185). Prior clinical outcomes from the cohort have been published, but data concerning lipid lowering management was not previously available [5].

Study methods were as previously described[5]. Briefly, consecutive asymptomatic individuals who had a low dose CT scan for lung cancer screening from March 2017 to November 2018 as part of the High-risk Lung Cancer Screening Pilot based at The Ottawa Hospital were included in the analysis. The Ottawa Hospital is an academic hospital based across 2 sites and serves 1.3 million people in Eastern Ontario. The pilot lung cancer screening program was turned into an official program from April 2021.

Eligibility criteria for lung cancer screening included age 55–74 years, former or current smokers of at least 20 years and a 2 % or higher risk of developing lung cancer within 6 years, as predicted by a risk prediction model.

Individuals could be referred by their health care providers or self-referred. Individuals with a prior history of coronary artery disease (myocardial infarction and/or coronary revascularization) were excluded from the analysis[5].

CT imaging

Low dose (100–120 kVp, 60–80 mA) non-contrast, ungated helical chest CT was acquired with a single inspiratory breath hold (Revolution EVO, or Discovery CT 750 HD, GE Healthcare, Mississauga, ON Canada). Images were reconstructed in 1.25 mm axial section thickness in both standard and lung windows.

Coronary calcium evaluation

Picture and archiving communication systems software (Change Healthcare Picture Archiving and Communication System 12.3, Change Healthcare LCC, Nashville, Tennessee) was used to assess the presence of coronary artery calcification on trans-axial slices (Fig. 1)[17].

Low dose chest computed tomography (CT). Trans-axial image demonstrating left main (LM) and left anterior descending artery (LAD) coronary calcium.

The clinical CT report was reviewed to identify whether coronary artery calcium was reported and whether the extent of calcium was indicated.

Clinical data

Participant demographics, cardiovascular risks, lipid lowering (statin, ezetimibe, fibrate) prescriptions, Framingham risk score (which includes age, sex, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, smoking) and prior cardiac history were collected from each patient’s Ottawa Hospital electronic medical record (Epic Electronic Health Record System (Epic Systems Corporation, Verona Wisconsin, US)) with a censor date of January 30, 2025[5,18,19]. Recruitment into the High-risk Lung Cancer screening pilot study was from 2017 until 2021, lipid lowering therapy data was not collected at baseline prior to CT scanning during the pilot study period. Subsequently, cases were followed from 2019 to 2025 during the current investigation using the electronic healthcare record adopted by the Ottawa Hospitals in 2019. Lipid lowering therapy data was collected from the electronic healthcare record in all individuals at the last date of follow up.

Potential for cardiovascular risk reduction

In current cardiovascular disease guidelines, the decision to recommend lipid lowering therapy is determined by clinical risk and risk intensifiers such as coronary artery calcium[20,21]. For example once clinical risk achieves a threshold of high Framingham risk, the high clinical risk alone guides therapy recommendation, whereas in low (<10 % 10 year cardiovascular disease risk) and intermediate (10–19 % 10 year cardiovascular disease risk) patients the presence of coronary artery calcium can intensify the recommendation[20]. We determined lipid lowering would therefore be recommended for a) individuals with coronary artery calcification, b) high Framingham risk individuals. Further we determined lipid lowering would not be recommended for those low or intermediate risk individuals who had no coronary artery calcium.

Influence of coronary artery calcium reporting

To determine whether clinical reporting of coronary calcium influenced lipid lowering therapy we undertook two analyses and included clinical risks using the Framingham risk score as a covariable. We examined whether the prevalence of lipid lowering therapy was influenced by a) the presence of coronary artery calcium in a report and b) the described extent of coronary calcium in the report. The clinical descriptions of coronary calcium were grouped as mild when “minor” or “mild” were used in the clinical report; moderate when “moderate” or “modest” were used and extensive when either “severe”, or “heavy” or “extensive” or “three vessel” were described[8].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS for windows, version 29.01 (IBM software, Chicago, Il, US). Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages. Continuous variables were expressed as mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Continuous variables were tested for skewness and kurtosis to assess for normal distribution.

Sample size was determined by our previously reported cohort which was based upon prior work examining cardiovascular outcomes in lung cancer screening trials[5,7,8]. Categorical variables were compared using χ2 statistics, whereas for continuous variables, we used a two-sample t-test. Univariable predictors of lipid lowering prescribing were included in multivariable binary logistic regression analyses. The associations between a) reported coronary artery calcium, and lipid lowering therapy; and b) coronary artery calcium description and lipid lowering therapy were assessed using logistic regression analysis with Framingham risk score as an independent covariable. Further associations were determined using χ 2 statistics for low, intermediate and high-risk Framingham groups and chi-squared tests of independence were performed using Cramer’s v statistics. P < 0.05 was used to indicate statistical significance.

Results

All individuals in the study underwent lung-CT as a part of the lung cancer screening pilot program. Only the first CT report and images were used in the study. The lung cancer screening study includes 3 annual CT scans. The first scans were obtained in 2017, and third studies in 2019 thus all individuals had electronic health records (EPIC, Epic systems corporation, Verona, Wisconsin US) from 2019 when the electronic record commenced, and none were lost from the data analysis.

1486 cases without a history of coronary artery disease were scanned during the study period, mean age 66 ± 5.5 years, and 48.5 % female. The mean 10-year Framingham cardiovascular risk was 22.9 ± 7.6 % and reflected the high prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors in this population (Table 1). CAC was present in 82.9 % of cases (n = 1232) and reported in 62.9 % (n = 776).

Cardiovascular risk reduction with lipid lowering therapy

During the follow-up period, 778 (52.4 %) were taking lipid lowering medications. 706 were taking statins alone, 59 were on a combination of statin and ezetimibe and 13 took ezetimibe alone. No other lipid lowering therapies were used.

Lipid lowering prescriptions were associated with age, hypertension, diabetes and the Framingham risk score with highest rates (65.1 %) in patients with high (≥20 % 10-year cardiovascular disease risk) Framingham risk score (Table 1).

On multivariable analysis that included Framingham risk score, sex, age, hypertension, smoking status, diabetes and reported coronary calcium, Framingham risk score predicted lipid lowering therapy (OR 2.31, 95 % CI 1.73–2.31, p < 0.001) as did reported coronary artery calcium (OR 1.53, 95 % CI 1.22–1.92, p < 0.001) (Table 2).

There were low numbers of individuals with coronary calcium and low Framingham risk, and this limited the utility of analysis in this category, however no effect of coronary calcium report on lipid prescribing was seen (p = 0.495, n = 37).

In the intermediate Framingham risk category, lipid lowering prescribing was performed at low levels whether coronary calcium was reported or not (28.7 % versus 32.5 % prescribing rates for unreported versus reported coronary calcium respectively p = 0.432) (Table 3).

Overall lipid prescribing rates were 65 % in high Framingham risk individuals. Rates were higher in those with reported coronary calcium (70 %) versus those with unreported coronary calcium (57 %) (P < 0.001) (Table 3 and supplementary figure 1).

Based on the presence of coronary artery calcium, lipid lowering would be recommended in 33 low risk cases and 256 intermediate risk individuals. A further 33 intermediate risk cases might be suitable for de-risking having zero coronary artery calcium[22,23]. In total, coronary artery calcium data could impact lipid lowering prescriptions in 317 (21.3 %) cases (p < 0.001) (Table 3). When individuals with high Framingham risk are also considered then the total potential shortfall of lipid lowering therapy was 647 cases (43.5 %) (p < 0.001).

Calcium reporting and association with lipid lowering therapy

Coronary artery calcification was reported clinically in 776 cases (62.9 %). The rate of lipid lowering therapy was greater in cases where coronary artery calcium was clinically reported versus not reported, occurring in 462 reported coronary calcium cases (59.5 %) versus only 207 cases (45.4 %) when coronary calcium was unreported (OR 1.84, 95 % CI 1.49–2.25, p < 0.001).

Both coronary calcium reporting (OR 1.50, 95 % 1.17–1.92, p = 0.001) and Framingham risk (OR 3.25, 95 % CI 2.57–4.11, p < 0.001) predicted lipid lowering prescribing on logistic regression analysis. To determine whether the association of coronary calcium reporting was different across Framingham risk categories, we examined the relationship between coronary calcium reporting and lipid prescription in low, intermediate and high-risk Framingham groups. The effect of reported coronary artery calcium on lipid lowering therapy was predominately seen in high Framingham risk patients (p < 0.001): there was no significant effect seen in low or intermediate risk patients. Analysis in the low-risk category was limited by the small numbers of individuals represented in this category (n = 37) (Supplemental data table 2, supplement figure 1).

Described calcium extent and statin prescribing

We compared the prevalence of lipid therapy with the language used to describe the extent of coronary artery calcium in clinical reports. Cardiovascular clinical risks were included in the analysis using Framingham risk status. As the clinically reported extent of coronary artery calcium increased lipid therapy prescribing increased (OR 1.46, 95 % CI 1.20–1.77, p < 0.001). We explored the relationship between the reported coronary calcium extent and lipid therapy in different clinical risk categories. Reported extent of coronary artery calcium was associated with lipid lowering therapy in intermediate and high Framingham risk individuals. Small numbers of individuals in the low-risk Framingham group limited analysis of associations in this category (Supplemental data table 3 and figure 2). A sensitivity analysis determined there was no effect on the relationship between the study report of coronary calcium extent and lipid therapy prevalence when individuals whose calcium was not reported were included in the analysis (OR 1.37, 95 % CI 1.21–1.54, P < 0.001) (Supplemental figure 2).

Discussion

We present novel data from a Canadian lung cancer screening program to detail the prevalence of cardiovascular risk reduction therapy in a population at increased risk from cardiovascular mortality [2]. We determined coronary artery disease went undetected in 37 % of cases and lipid therapy was not prescribed in 41 % of eligible cases. The importance of this data is in breadth of their application to emerging lung cancer screening programs. Their relevance is in their potential to influence cardiovascular disease prevention and improve survival outcomes in such programs.

Adjusting for clinical risks, we demonstrated an independent relationship between coronary artery calcium reporting and the prevalence of lipid lowering therapy. Lipid lowering therapy was 15 % greater in patients whose coronary artery calcium was reported. We also determined, after adjusting for clinical risks, an independent relationship between the description of coronary artery calcium extent on a clinical report and the prevalence of lipid lowering therapy.

We further explored the potential for optimization of cardiovascular disease risk reduction in lung cancer screening participants. In patients with intermediate risks of cardiovascular disease the presence of coronary artery calcium is an indication to commence lipid lowering therapy and in our study 256 out of 369 (69 %) such cases were not receiving risk reduction therapy. Overall, we demonstrated the potential to improve risk reduction therapy in 44 % of the study participants. The relevance of these findings are acute in a population where cardiovascular mortality accounts for 25 % of mortality events and where the purpose of the program to which the individuals have subscribed is healthcare improvement[2].

When Wilson and Junger proposed principles for medical screening in 1968 they would not have imagined a scenario such as lung cancer screening CT where the screening test for one condition is remarkably efficient at detecting another potentially fatal disease[24]. Coronary artery calcium is highly prevalent in the lung cancer screening population and is associated with high rates of fatal cardiovascular outcomes[[2], [3], [4],7]. If co-existent coronary artery calcium were reported and acted upon, potentially lung cancer screening could also reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease.

In the population studied risk reduction prescribing for cardiovascular disease with lipid lowering occurred in 52 % of individuals. Similar levels of prescribing have been seen in prior studies. In the US Chin et al demonstrated statin prescribing in lung cancer screened individuals to be 69 %[25]. Whereas in a European lung cancer screened cohort the prevalence was 45 %[7]. These relatively high prevalence levels of lipid lowering therapy in lung cancer screened contrast with the 7 % prevalence of statin prescribing in a general US population who had coronary calcium co-incidentally detected on a CT chest[26]. Potentially the large difference in prescribing rates is indicative of the both the high prevalence of clinical cardiovascular risks in lung cancer screening candidates and their engagement with health services[2,3,5].

Lipid lowering therapy was independently associated with increasing clinical risk as measured by the Framingham risk score. Although this is reassuring that clinical assessment is still being used in clinical decision making. It is worth noting, based on clinical risk stratification alone a further 22 % of the total population could have been prescribed lipid lowering according to their high Framingham Risk Score[20].

Coronary calcium reporting rates were 62 % in our data. Previously it was common for coronary artery calcium not to be reported on chest CT scans[15,16,26]. Guidance was published in 2018 to promote consensus in coronary artery calcium reporting on chest CT studies[13]. Our data are perhaps reflective of the year in which the CT was performed and of the nature of the study indication. As the 2018 guideline document is incorporated into clinical practice it is anticipated that this level of reporting will increase.

There was an association of lipid therapy use with the reporting of coronary calcium, an absolute 15 % increase (from 45 % to 60 %) and a relative one third increase of prescription rates was noted. The largest increase was seen in patients at high Framingham risk. One might have expected to observe the most impact from coronary artery calcium reporting in intermediate risk patients where coronary calcium can be recommended as an aide to decision making for lipid lowering therapy[20,21].

Coronary artery calcium reporting on chest CT has previously been demonstrated to increase lipid lowering therapy but at variable rates. A modest 4 % was recorded by Uretsky et al whereas an absolute increase of 44 % occurred in the NOTIFY-1 project [[26], [27], [28]]. Hammer et al found statin prescribing increased by 20 % when coronary artery calcium was described as significant in 8987 lung cancer screened individuals[29].

Prescribing response rates to coronary artery calcium are also influenced by the location in the report that coronary artery calcium is reported. Fisher et al demonstrated that statin initiation was more common when coronary artery calcium was mentioned in the conclusion versus in the body of the report (12.3 % versus 4.9 % respectively, p = 0.001)[14].

We considered whether the language of the report was a determinant of lipid lowering therapy. When a description of moderate or extensive coronary artery calcium was present, prescriptions were greater in comparison to when less coronary calcium was reported. When practitioners prescribe lipid lowering in response to coronary calcium extent this is consistent with recent clinical findings in lung cancer screening trials indicating those with extensive coronary calcium are at highest risk from cardiovascular events. [4,7,8]. From a prevention perspective the observation that report language can influence prescribing rates is both important and encouraging. It implies that clinical attention is given to off-target details in a CT report and clinical practice can adapt to emerging data to improve patient care and potentially their outcomes.

Limitations

Data was collected from a single lung cancer screening program site using an electronic medical record and may not be generalized to other sites. Rates of coronary calcium reporting were similar to those recorded in other studies using chest CT and the prevalence of lipid therapy prescribing when coronary artery calcium was reported was also consistent with prior reports. Nevertheless, it is possible in other lung cancer screening programs with higher rates of cardiovascular risk reduction therapy there may be less room for increased uptake in lipid lowering drugs following CT. There were low numbers of individuals in the low-risk Framingham risk category and comparisons within this group may have been underpowered.

Lipid lowering prescription data prior to lung cancer screening CT in this study was unavailable. It is however unlikely that lipid lowering would be unprescribed in response to a report of coronary artery calcium, nevertheless this cannot be completely ruled out.

In cases where coronary artery calcium was present and not reported at lung cancer CT, it is possible that coronary artery calcium was reported on a separate chest CT performed clinically for other reasons. In this case lipid lowering therapy may have been commenced. Such scenarios would have attenuated differences in therapy between unreported versus reported coronary calcium at lung cancer CT thus it is possible the actual difference was greater than observed.

We selected lipid lowering prescription rates as a measure of cardiovascular disease risk reduction. There are other aspects of cardiovascular disease prevention such as smoking cessation, diabetes control, and hypertension management as well as dietary and lifestyle measures that could also be assessed as a more holistic approach to cardiovascular disease prevention. Lipid lowering non-prescription could also reflect patient preference or intolerance.

Conclusion

In asymptomatic individuals undergoing lung cancer CT screening, there is room for optimization of cardiovascular disease preventive therapies based upon the presence of coronary artery calcium and clinical risks. Coronary artery calcium reporting could alter management in 21 % of patients and a further 22 % might be eligible for primary prevention based on clinical criteria alone.

Expansion of lung cancer screening programs present an advantageous opportunity to diagnose two potentially fatal conditions which often present late. The challenge for healthcare providers will be to respond to the initiative of their patients who seek out lung cancer screening, are found to have asymptomatic coronary artery disease and are looking to commence risk reduction strategies.

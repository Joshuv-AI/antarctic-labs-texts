Valve Migration After EVOQUE in Annuloplasty Band Rescued With SAPIEN-in-EVOQUE Strategy
Authors: Brian Lee, Muhammed Ali Rahim, Maya Specter, Olivia Wojewoda, Howard Goldschmidt, Ernest Chang, Hussein Rahim, Yonathan Litwok, Steffne Kunnirickal, Francis Kim, Juan Grau, Rajiv Tayal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Dual EN Snare stabilization enabled valve-in-valve tricuspid rescue.•Migrated EVOQUE transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement valve rescued with SAPIEN-in-EVOQUE implantation.•Incomplete leaflet capture may contribute to EVOQUE valve migration.•Percutaneous valve-in-valve rescue avoided surgical intervention.•Rescue strategy may benefit prior tricuspid annuloplasty patients.

A 37-year-old woman with prior history of infective endocarditis requiring tricuspid annuloplasty in 2015 (Medtronic Duran AnCore 31 mm band) presented with recurrent, massive tricuspid regurgitation and evidence of cardiac cirrhosis felt to be attributable to the resultant congestive hepatopathy (Figure 1A). Despite volume optimization, she demonstrated right ventricular dilation with preserved function and severe malcoaptation (coaptation gap 1.37 cm) (Figure 2, Video 1), as well as persistent liver injury. After declining repeat surgery, she was found to be anatomically suitable for transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement using a 48 mm EVOQUE valve with moderate procedural complexity due to the presence of an annuloplasty band (Figure 3).

The device was introduced in a standard fashion from the right femoral vein, with careful use of flexion and depth controls to achieve coaxial alignment with the annulus (Figure 4A). Anchor release was performed in stages with transesophageal echocardiographic confirmation of leaflet capture in line with previously described best practices. Deployment was completed with trace to mild paravalvular regurgitation and a mean gradient of 1 mmHg (Video 2). Subsequent imaging demonstrated progressive paravalvular insufficiency, and fluoroscopy revealed gradual ventricular migration of the valve with partial embolization into the right ventricle (Figure 4B and C). Retrospective review of the transesophageal echocardiographic images suggested incomplete leaflet capture by the EVOQUE valve, resulting in subsequent leaflet disengagement, loss of prosthetic seal, progressive paravalvular leak, and eventual valve embolization (Figure 5A and B).

Dual EN Snare (12-20 mm) devices were advanced, 1 via the left femoral vein and 1 via the right internal jugular vein, to capture the posterior and anterior atrial aspects of the embolized valve. Traction was applied to both snares to restore annular orientation (Figure 4D). Through the stabilized frame, a Safari wire was positioned into the right ventricular apex, and a 29 mm Edwards SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA valve (with +3 cc volume) was deployed within the annuloplasty band and atrial aspect of the EVOQUE frame (Figure 4E, Video 3). The snares were released before full expansion to avoid entrapment. A transesophageal echocardiogram revealed stable device placement, mild paravalvular leak, and a mean gradient of 1 mmHg across the valve. Echocardiography the next day showed only trace paravalvular regurgitation, without pericardial effusion (Figure 6, Video 4). Follow-up evaluations at 30 days showed a stable normally functioning transcatheter valve complex with a mean gradient of 4 mmHg and trace paravalvular insufficiency.

A transthoracic echocardiogram demonstrating trace tricuspid regurgitation with a 29-mm SAPIEN within a 48-mm EVOQUE valve.

Before transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement, the patient was found to have cirrhosis of the liver demonstrated by magnetic resonance elastography secondary to tricuspid valve insufficiency. Repeat imaging obtained 7 months postoperatively demonstrated complete recovery of liver function with complete resolution of her liver injury (Figure 1B).

This case illustrates one of the first reports of successful rescue of an embolized EVOQUE valve within a prior annuloplasty band using a SAPIEN valve. In the initial TRISCEND II trial, experience with EVOQUE in prior tricuspid rings was sparse.1 We hypothesize that the flexible, incomplete geometry of the Duran AnCore system provides limited radial support, thus predisposing to device migration. Real-world surveillance suggests device malapposition or migration may be more common than originally reported in clinical trials. The MAUDE database reported 21% of adverse events due to migration/malapposition, 10 of which were rescued by SAPIEN-in-EVOQUE implantation.2 In this case, the combination of dual EN Snare stabilization and valve-in-valve rescue enabled definitive therapy.

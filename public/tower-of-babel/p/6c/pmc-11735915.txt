Letter to the editor “In-depth assessment of quality of life and real life impact of mild traumatic brain injury in elderly by means of a focus group study”
Authors: Sadia Farhana
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

I would like to express my gratitude for the article titled “In-depth assessment of quality of life and real life impact of mild traumatic brain injury in elderly by means of a focus group study” which was recently published in Brain and Spine (Gavrila Laic et al., 2023). This study aims to assess how mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) affects the quality of life (QoL) of elderly patients, an often-underrepresented group in TBI research. While previous research has primarily focused on physical and cognitive outcomes, this study sought to capture the broader psychosocial effects of TBI in the aging population. By examining the daily lives and self-perception of these patients, the study seeks to identify the long-term consequences of mild TBI.

Mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a growing concern among the elderly, a group expected to outnumber children under five within 20 years. This shift challenges healthcare systems to meet rising care demands while addressing staff shortages. Elderly trauma patients often face longer wait times and reduced access to specialized care compared to younger patients. In this context, this study qualitatively explores changes in quality of life (QoL) following mild TBI in elderly patients, utilizing focus group interviews with six participants (median age 74) admitted to University Hospitals Leuven between 2016 and 2022. The analysis revealed three primary themes: functional disturbances and symptoms, daily life post-TBI, and overall life quality, emotions, and satisfaction.

The results highlight that balance problems, headaches, cognitive decline, and physical health changes were among the most prevalent issues faced by elderly patients post-TBI. Notably, short-term memory deterioration emerged as the most common cognitive impairment within the cohort, suggesting that mild TBI in elderly individuals can significantly impact both physical and cognitive functions. These findings emphasize the need for targeted therapeutic strategies addressing these specific challenges to improve the long-term quality of life for elderly patients. Key factors affecting QoL 1–5 years post-mild TBI included a lack of family support and understanding, as well as difficulties with daily tasks like cleaning and driving. Interestingly, despite these challenges, no symptoms of depression were reported. Instead, patients relied on acceptance of their condition and hope for improvement as coping strategies.

Participants reported significant challenges in daily living, including memory issues, emotional instability, and reduced social engagement, which were often underestimated in clinical evaluations. This study highlights the real-life impact of mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) on the elderly, offering valuable insights that could shape clinical practice and policy. By examining these qualitative aspects, the study filled up the gaps in the current understanding and informed targeted interventions to enhance recovery and life satisfaction. Though the limitations of this study include the relatively small sample size and qualitative nature of the data, which may not fully capture the diversity of experiences within the elderly TBI population, the findings underscore the need for comprehensive post-injury support that addresses not only physical rehabilitation but also emotional and social well-being.

Future research should emphasize a more comprehensive evaluation of the long-term effects of mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) in the elderly, particularly about cognitive decline, emotional health, and social isolation. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) focusing on personalized rehabilitation programs and cognitive therapies tailored to this population are necessary to improve outcomes. Moreover, longitudinal studies should be conducted to assess the effectiveness of interventions over time and identify specific subgroups of elderly patients who are at higher risk of prolonged impairment. These findings can be applied in clinical settings by updating geriatric care guidelines, incorporating routine cognitive and emotional screenings for elderly TBI patients, and developing multidisciplinary support programs. Increasing collaboration between healthcare providers, caregivers, and advocacy groups will also be essential to ensuring that research findings are effectively implemented to enhance quality of life and recovery for elderly TBI patients.

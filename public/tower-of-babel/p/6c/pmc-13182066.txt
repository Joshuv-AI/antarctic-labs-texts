Associations between DXA-derived parameters of body composition and bone mass density in patients with T2DM and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Emil Fraenkel, Martin Kaško, Tomáš Koky, Ivica Lazúrová
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease(MASLD) is associated with the deterioration of bone mass density(BMD), but the independent contributions of soft tissue parameters of body composition are not fully understood. We aimed to evaluate the relationship between the degree of liver steatosis, as assessed by vibration-controlled transient elastography(FibroScan®), and DXA-derived BMD indices in the European population.Methods156 patients, 95 females and 61 males, aged 33–78 years, treated for T2DM and MASLD with oral antidiabetic medication, were included in this cross-sectional study. Subgroup analyses were conducted by sex, age, BMI, lean mass index, and the extent of fibrosis. Multiple linear regression models were fitted with BMD indices as the dependent variable and the Controlled Attenuation Parameter(CAP) as a measure of liver steatosis, serving as the independent variable. The models were adjusted for age, sex, HOMA-IR, BMI, lean mass index, and android fat mass%. Accordingly, ROC curves with T-score(Z-score)=-1.0 were plotted as a classifier of bone mass deterioration.ResultsThe total BMD and CAP were positively correlated in the entire group(r = 0.21,p = 0.001) and in the female group(r = 0.29,p = 0.004), but not in the male group(r = 0.08,p = 0.51). The association maintained significance after adjustment for total body fat%(r = 0.23,p = 0.003), AGR(r = 0.22,p = 0.005), lean mass index(r = 0.19,p = 0.02) and osteocalcin(r = 0.16,p = 0.04) but not for BMI(r = 0.13,p = 0.09). The AUC in the female group for total BMD in the model calculated from CAP and android fat mass% and adjusted for age, was: ROC = 0.746(95%CI 0.62–0.88).ConclusionsOur results demonstrate existing discrepancies in the association between BMD and MASLD across sex-, age-, BMI-, lean mass index-, and fibrosis-stratified analyses. CAP and android fat% may have clinical value in predicting osteopenia in patients with MASLD and T2DM.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Osteoporosis is a condition that affects approximately 400 million people worldwide, posing a burden on public health systems globally. The definition refers to a decline in bone mineral density and deterioration of bone architecture, associated with reduced bone strength [1].

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the most common liver disease due to the prominent effects of insulin resistance in the development of liver steatosis, which is considered the liver manifestation of the metabolic syndrome [2]. Novel findings revealed that insulin resistance can also manifest itself in bone tissue and that osteoblasts play a crucial role in insulin action within the bone [3].

Although bone metabolism impairment in patients with MASLD is frequently observed, its aetiology is not fully understood. Conventionally, it has been widely accepted that, because of a higher passive mechanical load on the skeleton and enhanced cortical bone formation, obesity confers a protective effect against the deterioration of bone mass [4]. However, this conventional concept has been challenged after some authors demonstrated that the relationship between metabolic syndrome and bone density, as well as bone strength, became negative in models corrected for BMI [5–7]. The conflicting evidence reported in these studies arises from the fact that bone metabolism can also be affected by age, gender, nutritional and menstrual status, race, as well as BMD measurement technology and the diagnosis of MASLD [8].

Most cross-sectional and case-control studies have demonstrated that, in adults, MASLD is associated with lower bone mineral density (BMD) [9–14]. However, the relationship between MASLD and decreased bone mass density appears to be less concordant in women, especially in the postmenopausal age groups [15–18].

In addition, the studies mentioned above evaluated these associations mostly in Asian populations, making the generalisations of the findings for the European population difficult [19, 20]. Furthermore, these observations were made when MASLD was diagnosed by non-invasive markers of liver steatosis or by plain ultrasound [21, 22]. The two meta-analyses carried out in five and twelve cross-sectional or retrospective studies did not show differences in BMD between patients with MASLD and controls [23, 24].

Therefore, our objective was to evaluate the relationship between the extent of liver steatosis, which was recognised by vibration-controlled transient elastography (FibroScan®), and the BMD indices evaluated by DEXA in the Slovak (European) population. We also analysed laboratory markers of bone metabolism. We conducted an age- and sex-specific study, also taking into consideration the extent of liver fibrosis and the proportion of fat mass parameters and lean mass parameters.

Another novelty of our study is that we evaluated whether our regression models, in which the BMD density was calculated from the extent of liver steatosis, as assessed by CAP, and adjusted for fat mass and lean mass as independent variables, using the T score as a classifier of decreased bone mineral density, are capable of identifying patients with diminished bone density. Models were also calculated to answer the question of whether the relationship between MASLD and BMD is independent of the features of Metabolic syndrome or not. However, because of the cross-sectional design, the results do not show prediction or clinical utility.

Methods

Data collection

The present cross-sectional observational study consisted of 156 participants: 95 women and 61 men, treated for T2DM and MASLD. The average age of the sample was 62.85 ± 10.73 years, with an average of 61.90 ± 11.1 years in the male group; (age range 37–77 years; and 63.5 ± 10.5 years in the female group; (age range 33–77 years). Forty individuals treated for T2DM but without a diagnosis of MASLD, and with a BMI of less than 26 and a HOMA-IR of less than 2.5, were enrolled in a control group.The objective of the control group was to demonstrate that the body composition parameters of the members of this group differed from those of the patients, fulfilling the criteria for MASLD. 28 patients fulfilled the diagnostic criteria of diabetes, and 12 of prediabetes. The average age of the control group was 59.13 ± 14.12 years.

All patients were of Caucasian origin according to self-assigned as well as observer-assigned ethnicity and lived in the Eastern-Slovakian region. Patients were recruited during their regular check-ups, which were performed once every four months, and participated in the study after providing informed consent. Before the examination, patients completed a questionnaire concerning their characteristics and biometric values, which was self-administered and regularly updated during each visit. All evaluated laboratory parameters of every patient, except for vitamin D, were analysed using a single blood sample after fasting overnight. Vitamin D analysis was performed on all patients during the same season. a low-carbohydrate diet therapy (150 g/day)

Diabetes was diagnosed in compliance with the criteria of the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) and the American Diabetes Association (ADA) if any of the following criteria were met: 1. a fasting plasma glucose concentration ≥ 7 mmol/l (126 mg/dL);2. a random plasma glucose concentration ≥ 11 mmol/l (200 mg/dL); a glycated haemoglobin (HBA1c) level ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/ mol); and the diagnosis of T2DM recognized by the OGTT with 75 g glucose performed according to WHO guidelines.

Prediabetic states were established as follows:

Impaired fasting glucose (IFG): Fasting glucose level ≥ 6.0 mmol/l and ≤ 7.0 mmol/l.

Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT): Postprandial glucose level ≥ 7.8 mmol/l and ≤ 11.0 mmol/l.

The diagnosis of MASLD was established by vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) (FibroScan®, Echosens, Paris, France), during which the controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) values, as a marker of the degree of liver steatosis, were measured, with a CAP of more than 238 dB/m regarded as the cutoff value for steatosis. In terms of fibrotic changes, a value of 7.0 kPa was defined as significant liver fibrosis. Fulfilling the criteria suggested by the European Working Group (EWGSOP 2), sarcopenia was defined as a value of ALM/ height2.

lower than 7.0 kg/m2 in males and lower than 5.5 kg/m2 in females. In order to eliminate interoperator variability, the measurements were made by a single, well-experienced operator.

Laboratory analysis

Serum glucose and lipid parameters were routinely determined using an autoanalyser (Roche Diagnostics GmbH). Insulin concentrations and IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 levels were measured using immunoradiometric assays (Beckman Coulter, Inc.). HbA1c was examined using the latex turbidimetric method and was calculated using DCCT measures. The remaining biochemical parameters were analysed at the Košice University Hospital Biochemistry Laboratory.

Analysis of body composition

Total body composition analysis was made using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) with a Lunar Prodigy Advance enCORE 2011 (GE Healthcare) X-ray densitometer at the 1st Department of Internal Medicine at Košice University Hospital (software version 13.60.033). This device distributes the scan results into fat mass, lean mass, and bone mass. Android and gynoid regions were demarcated using the Hologic APEX software applied during the scan analysis. The imaging machine was calibrated each day before measurements were performed, and the scans were conducted by certified radiologists who were blinded to the patients’ data.

Statistical analysis

All variables underwent evaluation for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and for homogeneity using the Levenes test. In the case of a normal distribution, the two-sample Student´s t-test was used for the comparison between the analysed groups; in the case of a nonparametric distribution of values, data were assessed by the Mann-Whitney U test. The Pearson correlation test was used to evaluate the relationships between dependent variables and independent variables; in the case of a nonparametric distribution, Spearman´s correlation test was applied. Unadjusted bivariate regression analyses were performed to evaluate the associations between the degree of liver steatosis, as assessed by the Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP), and the above-mentioned bone mass indices, as well as between CAP and laboratory markers of bone metabolism. For the purposes of determining the contributions of the components of fat tissue mass and lean tissue mass, as well as the degree of liver steatosis to the outcome, which was one of the parameters of BMD, multiple linear regression models were constructed. The relationships between predictor variables in the models were assessed for potential multicollinearity through a correlation matrix. Based on our prior literature search, we included 14 laboratory parameters that can act as potential confounders of BMD, and the selection was made before the analysis. We excluded one variable if a strong correlation was determined between the two explanatory variables. To simplify the model, stepwise selection was conducted. Models were accommodated for age and sex. Accordingly, to determine the optimal cut-off point based on lean mass and fat mass parameters, as well as the extent of liver steatosis for these regression models, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated. In women aged 50 years or older and in men, the T score is used, while in women younger than 50 years, the Z score is employed as a classifier of bone density.

The AUC was calculated for all models to determine their performance. Based on sensitivity and specificity, as well as the Akaike information criterion, only the models with the highest performance and significant explanation of the variation in the outcome variable were taken into account further.

Two-sided P values were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. All tests were two-tailed, and analyses were conducted using the SAS statistical package version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, CA).

As shown in Table I, the groups divided according to the MASLD status differed significantly in terms of BMI, waist circumference, HOMA-IR index of insulin resistance, CAP, Fibrosis score, HbA1c, and triglyceride, fat mass, and lean mass indices.

Next, we analysed subgroups based on sex, extent of fibrosis, BMI, presence of sarcopenia, and menopausal status in women.

The sex stratified groups differed in terms of IGF-1, ferritin, platelet, and calcium levels, as well as in laboratory markers of bone metabolism. They were found to be different for all measures of bone mass, fat mass, and lean mass with a high grade of significance, except for the T-score.

Correlations of CAP as a marker of liver steatosis with BMD indices

As shown in Table IV, in the entire group, the CAP and each of the BMD indices were positively correlated, although at different levels of significance.

In sex stratified groups, the positive correlation between CAP and T-BMD was significant only in women (T-BMD, r = 0.29, p = 0.004), regardless of age (T-BMD of females ≥ 50years, r = 0.32, p = 0.001). In the male group, the correlation was marginally significant only for the R-BMD (r = 0.25, p = 0.04); other BMD indices did not correlate with CAP.

In groups divided according to BMI, these positive relationships were much weaker and maintained significance only in LS-BMD of patients with a BMI of < 27 (r = 0.32, p = 0.05) and H-BMD of patients with a BMI of ≥27 (r = 0.22, p = 0.01).

In groups divided by extent of liver fibrosis, these positive relationships were shown only in the group with a low grade of liver fibrosis (< 7.0 kPa), although at different levels of significance (T-BMD, r = 0.23, p = 0.02).

In groups divided by the presence of sarcopenia, this positive relationship remained significant only in patients without the presence of sarcopenia (H-BMD, r = 0.27, p = 0.001), (LS-BMD, r = 0.16, p = 0,05).

Correlations of CAP as a marker of liver steatosis with markers of bone metabolism

As shown in Table IV, the negative correlation between the extent of liver steatosis and bone metabolism markers was significant only in men (osteocalcin, r= -0.35, p = 0.006; bCTX, r= -0.26, p = 0.04). This negative relationship was also significant in the group with a BMI of < 27 (osteocalcin, r= -0.35, p = 0.03; P1NP, r= -0.33, p = 0.04).

Correlations of CAP as a marker of liver steatosis with other variables.

We found significant correlations with the HOMA-IR (r = 0.25, p = 0.001), BMI (r = 0.28, p = 0.001), triglycerides (r = 0.19, p = 0.02), ALT (r = 0.21, p = 0.001), feritin (r = 0.22, p = 0.008) and HDL cholesterol (r=-0.26, p = 0.001), IGF-1 (r= -0.30, p = 0,001), IGFBP3 (r= -0.16, p = 0.08).

Correlations of total BMD with other variables

We observed significant negative relationships in terms of age (r=-0.29, p = 0.001), female sex (r=-0.41, p = 0.001), osteocalcin (r=-0.29, p = 0.001), PN1P (r=-0.18, p = 0.02), bCTX (r=-0.29, p = 0.001), and significant positive relationships in terms of BMI (r = 0.33, p = 0.001), feritin (r = 0.29, p = 0.001), AGR (r = 0.36, p = 0.001) and ALMI (r = 0.44, p = 0.001). We did not observe a significant association with the HOMA-IR (r=-0.06, p = 0.43), IGF (r = 0.06, p = 0.52), and IGFBP3 (r = 0.06, p = 0.46).

Collinearity analysis revealed a high level of collinearity between different variations of bone mass indices: T-BMD and H-BMD (r = 0.88, p = 0.001); T-BMD and LS-BMD (r = 0.84, p = 0.001); T-BMD and T-score (r = 0.92, p = 0.001), along with that between total fat% and android fat% (r = 0.90, p ≤ 0.001), total fat% and AGR (r=-0.57, p ≤ 0.001).

Multivariate analysis

To investigate the independent contribution of fat mass and lean mass parameters, together with the severity of liver steatosis to bone mass parameters, after adjusting for potential confounders, we constructed multiple linear regression models with BMD indices as dependent variables and CAP as a measure of the extent of steatosis alongside lean mass and fat mass indices as independent variables.

To avoid collinearity problems, we used only one lean mass component and one fat mass component next to CAP as independent variables. Consequently, we prepared ROC curves for the specificity and sensitivity of the calculated BMD, where a T-score of -1.0 SD was considered a classifier of decreased BMD.

The relationship between T-BMD and CAP became non-significant after adjustment for BMI (r = 0.13, p = 0.09). but remained significant after adjustment for total body fat% (r = 0.23, p = 0.003), AGR (r = 0.22, p = 0.005), and ALMI (r = 0.19, p = 0.02).

Discussion

We aimed to evaluate the relationship between the extent of liver steatosis and between BMD indices and laboratory markers of bone metabolism in patients with MASLD, those recently diagnosed with TDM2, or those in whom antidiabetic medication was previously administered.

We detected differences in the values of the BMD indices between subgroups divided according to sex, BMI, and ALMI, but not between groups divided by degree of liver fibrosis (r = 0.13, p = 0.09; however, it maintained statistical power with total body fat% (r = 0.23, p = 0.003), AGR (r = 0.22, p = 0.005), ALMI (r = 0.19, p = 0.02) and osteocalcin (r = 0.16, p = 0.04) as adjustors. We utilised ROC curves to assess the predictive value of CAP for bone mass deterioration. Our models indicate that CAP, along with lean mass and fat mass parameters of body composition, have predictive efficacy for the diagnosis of osteopenia. The best performing model has been demonstrated in the female group, with the inclusion of android FM% and age, in which the AUC was 0.746 (95% CI 0.62–0.88). As it was shown in our models, the relationship seems to be independent of these variables. These results highlight a substantial contribution of soft tissue parameters to bone mass deterioration in patients with MASLD. The fact that the positive correlation between CAP and BMD maintained significance after adjusting for components of body composition indicates a direct interaction between hepatocytes and the bone.

Hepatic insulin resistance in patients with MASLD causes a decrease in IGF-1 production [26]. Correspondingly, IGF-1 levels in the patient group were lower than in the control group, but the difference was not significant. However, IGF-1 is not the only factor affecting BMD. This is one of the reasons for the non-significantly lower total BMD levels in patients group.

However, in sex stratified analysis, BMD indices positively correlated with CAP only in the female group, which is consistent with the results of some previous cross-sectional studies that revealed that in women, compared to men, the negative relationships are less consistent compared to men [27–29]. In our female group, the positive association was detected regardless of menopausal status and in each location; in contrast, Moon et al. [30] reported this positive relation only in the lumbar spine BMD of the postmenopausal but not premenopausal population. The absence of this correlation in the male group might be mediated by significant baseline differences in body composition and hormonal profiles (e.g. IGF-1) between the sexes, as detailed in Table I. We should point out that vitamin D levels were not significantly different between the two sex groups. Gender differences may also be explained by different gender-specific mechanisms of age-related osteoporosis [31].

In the subgroup analysis conducted according to BMI, the positive correlation of BMD with CAP in the group with a BMI of ≥27, in contrast to the group with a BMI of <27, reached statistical significance only in terms of H-BMD. This discrepancy was in line with the results of Bhatt et al. [32], who in their study also evaluated BMD in adult patients with a higher BMI and higher body fat%.

In the group with a BMI of < 27, the positive relationship maintained statistical power only with respect to LS-BMD. In accordance with our results, Sung et al. [27] reported that the association between NAFLD and BMD is affected by BMI status and also by the degree of obesity. In their evaluation, the direction of the relationship changed by the BMI of 23 from negative to positive, and as the severity of overweight increased, the protective effect of elevated BMI disappeared. According to this author, the explanation may be that the positive effect of being overweight due to mechanical load is by increasing BMI progressively counterbalanced by the detrimental effects of adipokines. In the present study, this discrepancy may also have been the consequence of lower BMD indices in this group.

The level of significance between LS-BMD and CAP was not greater than between H-BMD and CAP in any of the investigated subgroups, which is in contrast to the results of Lee et al. [29] in healthy Korean women and Saareleinen et al. [33] in Finnish women. Lee et al. [29] explained a significant positive relationship in women with advanced MASLD in terms of LS BMD by the preventive effect of a higher upper body fat distribution on spinal bone loss; however, this protective effect may be limited by the basal bone mass, which is influenced by the lifestyle and clinical characteristics of the patient. Next, the exclusive positive effect on spinal bone may be explained, according to this author, by a higher proportion of metabolically active trabecular bone in the lumbar spine compared to the femoral neck bone [29].

In the analysis stratified by the extent of liver fibrosis, the correlation maintained importance only in the group with a low magnitude of fibrosis, which may have been because the vast majority of patients in the present study were diagnosed with a mild stage of MASLD. Furthermore, higher IGF-1 levels and lower HOMA-IR and ferritin levels, in addition to lower values of the BMD indices, may also have contributed to these discrepancies. Yoon et al. evaluated the grade of fibrosis using non-invasive markers in a prospective study of Korean participants, and reported that male, but not female patients with advanced fibrosis, demonstrated a higher rate of decline in BMD compared to patients with low fibrosis scores [34]. Kim et al. published similar findings, but regardless of sex, and preferentially at the BMD of the femur [35].

In the analysis performed according to the presence of sarcopenia, the positive relationship maintained statistical power only in patients with ALMI of ≥ 7.0 kg/ m2, which may have been influenced by higher BMD in this group.

We found a negative relationship between all markers of bone metabolism and CAP; however, this relationship was significant only in men, and specifically in relation to CAP with osteocalcin and bCTX, which may be explained by higher values of BMD indices in men. More authors reported a negative association between osteocalcin and MASLD [36], as well as osteocalcin in relation to balloon degeneration of hepatocytes in advanced MASLD [37, 38]. However, according to the findings of Aller et al. and Dou et al., this association is not independent [39, 40].

In the subgroups analysed according to BMI, the negative relationship was shown to be significant only in the BMI subgroup of < 27 in terms of osteocalcin and P1NP. Evidence indicates that there is an inverse relationship between osteocalcin levels and the magnitude of histological changes, as well as insulin resistance in MASLD. which is in contrast to our findings if we assume that patients with lower BMI will have less severe MASLD. In the study by Xia et al., the negative association between osteocalcin and MASLD remained significant after adjustment for components of the metabolic syndrome [28].

Many authors reported that osteocalcin is inversely associated with BMD, which is consistent with our results [36].

A major strength of our analysis is that, in contrast with the great majority of authors, we evaluated these associations in the Slovak (European) population and took into consideration a wide variety of covariates. Next, the diagnosis of MASLD was established by VCTE (FibroScan®), which has been shown to be a non-invasive alternative to liver biopsy, also in terms of differentiation of the degree of steatosis. To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate IGF-1 in patients in whom MASLD was established by VCTE. IGF-1, as a key hormone of bone growth, acts as a limiting factor of osteoblast proliferation and differentiation, the synthesis of type I collagen, and the balance between osteoblasts and osteoclasts, thus maintaining physiological bone remodelling.

The main limitation of our study is that the cross-sectional design does not allow the identification of causality, making prospective studies necessary to explain the extent of the contribution of soft tissue parameters to the decline in bone density in patients with MASLD. Next to that, our investigation was a single-centre study with a relatively small sample size. Second, no chronic inflammation markers were measured, which can affect bone metabolism. In addition, total osteocalcin levels were evaluated instead of undecarboxylated osteocalcin. Total osteocalcin levels may underestimate the metabolic role of osteocalcin. Third, sex hormone levels were not measured. Fourth, physical activity, diet and diabetes duration were not taken into consideration. Fifth, body composition analysis was performed using only DXA, which focuses on bone mass density. On the contrary, the Trabecular Bone Score (TBS) measures bone quality, or microarchitecture, adding predictive power independent of the BMD T-score. In patients with T2DM, BMD analysis can be misleading because this condition degrades bone microarchitecture disproportionately to density. Sixth, in our study, we applied the widely used ALMI instead of ALM/BMI for lean mass analysis. It has been confirmed that ALMI can be misleading in obesity because it evaluates the proportion of muscle mass relative to height rather than to overall body composition. Seventh, because of low correlation in the models with total BMD (R²=0.21), indicating poor model fit, and only moderately higher correlation in models with BMD of the hips (R²=0.35), the presented ROC analyses are exploratory to support observed associations and should be interpreted cautiously.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrated existing discrepancies in the association between bone mass parameters and MASLD in sex- and age-stratified analysis, as well as in evaluation performed according to body composition parameters. We observed lower values of BMD indices in patients with MASLD than in the control group, but the difference was not significant. Next to that, we found significantly lower BMD indices in the BMI subgroup of < 27, in contrast to that of ≥27 and also in the subgroup with sarcopenia, in contrast to the subgroup without it. On the contrary, the influence of fibrosis stage on BMD indices was not significant. In summary, our study is the first to find that CAP as a measure of liver steatosis is an independent contributor to bone mass deterioration in patients with T2DM and MASLD, with greater diagnostic efficiency in female patients. This suggests that CAP measurement, along with the parameters of fat mass and lean mass body composition, may have clinical value in predicting osteopenia.

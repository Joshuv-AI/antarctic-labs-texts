Molecular Detection of natural bacterial flora within Culex pipiens pipiens (Diptera: Culicidae) in Aleppo, Syria
Authors: Ahmed Allahem, Reem Alajmi, Mais Alzarzor Alajami, Sumaiah Al-Ghamdi, Muhammad Amjad Bashir, Jehan Zeb, Khaloud Mohammed Alarjani, Nawal M Al-Malahi, Rewaida Abdel-Gaber
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Culex pipiens complex members have been known as important vectors of medical and veterinary arthropod-borne diseases for many pathogens (viral, bacterial, parasitic). Therefore, this study aimed to investigate natural bacterial flora within the Culex pipiens pipiens (Linnaeus,1758) collected from Aleppo City, Syria. Specimens of Cx. p. pipiens were identified based on a pictorial key. The presence of bacterial species was investigated using the PCR technique and special culture media. Results indicated the presence of mine bacterial species, including Wolbachia pipiens, Moraxella catarrhalis, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Proteus mirabilis, Corynebacterium kutsceri, Clostridium tetani, and Acinetobacter baumannii. Wolbachia controls the sexual behavior of mosquitoes and therefore can be used in biocontrol.

Introduction

Mosquitoes are considered one of the most dangerous medical insects because their females transmit numerous pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and parasites while feeding on the blood of humans and animals1, 2. These insects carry many pathogenic organisms either on their external surfaces or in their digestive systems. They host large communities of diverse organisms—viral, bacterial, and parasitic, which may exceed the number of the mosquito's cells, and they engage in a series of complex interactions with these pathogens[3].

Bacteria inhabit the internal cavity of the insect and various parts of the insect's body, such as the salivary glands and the midgut. They play an important role in the survival of mosquitoes and increase their fertility, as well as in food digestion, regulating pH levels, and producing toxins that are antagonistic to pathogenic fungi. Furthermore, the symbiotic relationship between mosquitoes and certain types of organisms within them aids in the mosquitoes' resistance to other living organisms, such as viruses and parasites, thus developing the natural immune system of the mosquito4, 5.

Studies on the relationships between mosquitoes and the bacteria associated with them, which cause diseases in humans and animals, are on the rise. This topic has gained significant attention in many laboratories worldwide. These studies focus on understanding the role of these bacterial communities in mosquito biology and the environment, the role of mosquitoes in transmitting these pathogens to humans and animals, and the symbiotic relationships mosquitoes establish with different bacterial species[6]. Moreover, the study of Kandasamy et al. [7] recorded that some species of bacteria (such as Streptomyces sp.) can be used as biocides for mosquitoes, reducing the spread of these insects.

Culex pipiens complex (Diptera: Culicidae) reported a wide geographical distribution, and morphological variations among the Cx. pipiens pipiens and Cx. pipiens quinquefasciatus populations in the world. Although morphological characteristics such as DV/D ratio of male genitalia, the intersection of subcosta and costa with the bifurcation of R2 + 3 in adults are important for the diagnosis of Cx. pipiens complex members, but the recent characters cannot separate them. The variations of morphological and biological characteristics are found in the local population [8]. Therefore, it is necessary to distinguish between them by molecular classification based on the acetylcholinesterase (Ace) gene, specific primers, and Polymerase Chain Reaction[9]. So, this study aims to the identification of Cx. pipiens pipiens and the bacteria that carry it.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Collection of adult mosquitoes

This study was carried out in Aleppo Province (latitude 36.12° north and 37.93 °east) in the north of Syria, rising above sea level 222–449 m. A total of 758 (426 Male and 332 Female) Mosquitoes were collected from two areas in Aleppo city (Al-Ramouseh and Al-Shaar) and one area in the countryside (Al-Wadihi) during May to October 2023, using CDC traps (Control Disease Center) baited with CO2 (Locally manufactured according to [10].

Identification of mosquitoes in the Laboratory

The live collected mosquitoes were placed in the freezer (−20 °C) to ensure that all specimens were killed and stayed dry [11]. Then mosquitoes were identified using a Microscope (from OPTIKA, B-500TPH, OPTIKA Company, Italy) equipped with a digital camera (Canon EOS 1100D Digital, Taiwan) based on global morphological taxonomic keys8, 11. Furthermore, random specimens were processed for molecular identification based on the Ace gene following the protocol of[9]to confirm morphological identification.

Molecular Detection of normal bacteria

DNA Extraction

The outer surface of the collected Cx. p. pipiens were sterilized with 70 % ethanol for five minutes, then washed with phosphate-buffered saline (BPS) solution to remove any potential contamination. After that, insects were placed in a sterile chamber (Laminar) to prevent contamination with germs from the surrounding environment. Fifty females were placed in one tube and fifty males in another tube, and BPS was added to them, (mosquitoes were taken randomly without considering their nutrition), then they were ground by a glass tube. The contents of each tube were divided into two parts:

1- One part was cultured in specific media to identify the species of bacteria based on their morphological and biochemical characteristics[12], and then DNA was extracted from Colonies of obtained bacteria using QIAamp® Min Kit (Cat No. 51306. Qiagen GmbH, Hilden, Germany) (all Working had been in Sterile Chamber).

2- The remaining part of the ground mosquitoes was used to extract total DNA (mosquito DNA and DNA of bacteria within them) using DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, California, USA).

Polymerase Chain reaction (PCR)

PCR mixture was prepared with a total volume of 25 µl, containing 12.5 µl of the PCR reaction mix (0.2 mM dNTPs and 1.5 mM MgCl2, 1.25 U of Taq Polymerase) (from GeneDirecX, Cat No. MB203-0100, USA), 1 µl of primers (Table 1) at a concentration of 25 mM each, and 4 µl of the DNA sample. The mixture was then completed with 6.5 µl of deionized distilled water. Subsequently, the mixture of each sample (with one negative control) was transferred to The Thermal Cycler (Eppendorf from Eppendorf Company, Hamburg Germany), where the program specific to the used Electrophoresis

Electrophoresis was performed in a 1 × TBE (Tris/Borate/EDTA buffer) (from Promega Corporation, Cat. V4251, USA) running buffer on 1.5 % agarose gel (from Gene DirecX, Cat No: MB755-0100, USA) supplemented with ethidium bromide. 7 µl of the PCR reaction product from each sample was taken separately and mixed with 3 µl of loading buffer (Vivantis Technologies Cat No: NM0410, Malaysia). The mixture was then placed into a well of the agarose gel, and an electric field of 100 V was applied to the agarose gel for 30 min.

DISCUSSION

Symbiotic relationships between bacteria and insects play an important role in the behavior of blood-feeding insects that transmit pathogens to humans. Therefore, researchers have been interested in studying bacteria inside mosquitoes[22].

Studies indicate that the digestive tract of mosquitoes contains a diverse bacterial flora that is affected by many biotic and abiotic factors that play an important role in the distribution and diversity of bacteria, as well as the physiological processes carried out by mosquitoes such as molting, blood or sugar feeding and reproduction, all of which affect how these bacteria are introduced, which can settle in the digestive tract or other tissues of mosquitoes. Some studies have confirmed that bacteria can be transmitted vertically from the mother to generations, either through egg contamination23, 24.

The bacteria may be transmitted from polluted water in swamps to the larvae and persist between the different stages of the larvae, from there to the pupae, and during the development of the pupae to the adults25, 26.

In the present study, nine bacterial species was identified in the internal cavity of adult Cx. p. pipiens. The consistency and difference between our results and other international studies is due to the variations of mosquitoes, the environment, and the extent of pollution of the breeding site27, 28. From this, we find that the difference in bacterial diversity is according to the diversity mosquito, its environment, and its physiological condition. The breeding site also plays a major role in shaping bacterial communities within mosquito species29, 30.

The intracellular bacteria of Wolbachia spp were recorded in the present study, and it is one of the most abundant species recorded in insects, as they are found in many insect orders such as Diptera, Coleoptera, Hemiptera, etc. They can affect the internal composition of bacteria in mosquitoes 31, 32 . It plays an important role in insect behavior and is transmitted vertically through the cytoplasm of mosquito eggs. It has been proven that these bacteria manipulate cellular and reproductive processes, as they act as more than a reproductive parasite through cytoplasmic incompatibility [33]. Mating a male infected with Wolbachia spp with a healthy female produces sterile eggs that do not hatch, thus limiting the spread of harmful insects. Therefore, males of harmful insects can be infected and released into the environment. These males will mate with the females, and the resulting eggs will be sterile, thus interrupting the life cycle of harmful insects.

The presence of the Escherichia coli inside Cx. p. pipiens is explained by the fact that this species of mosquito prefers to lay its eggs in stagnant, polluted water rich in organic matter. These eggs hatch into larvae that feed on this organic matter and Escherichia coli bacteria and these bacteria settle in their digestive tract [34]. The study showed that Staphylococcus aureus is one of the bacterial species that reside in the internal cavity of this species of mosquito [35].

The presence of certain species of bacteria (Klebsilla pneumonia, Proteus mirabilis, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus aureus) in the internal cavity of mosquito species in many studies around the world, and our study as well, indicates that these bacterial species were transmitted to them from sewage and stagnant water contaminated with these bacteria to their larvae and from there to the adult stage[36].

Klebsilla pneumoniae is considered an opportunistic bacterium that provides the internal environment of the mosquito with a suitable medium for its reproduction 37, 38. Studies have also shown that they can be transmitted vertically across generations from mothers to offspring. Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli are part of the normal flora of the mosquito's internal cavity, having been acquired from the larval stage [39].

Moraxella catarrhalis infects human blood and is not found in the aquatic environment, so it has moved to the digestive tract. Blood feeding of mosquitoes on human blood and vertical transmission between individuals of the species indicate the role of Culex mosquitoes in transmitting pathogens to humans.

Clostridium tetani is a tetanus-causing bacterium that is found in soil, dust, wounds, and burns. It has been transmitted to the digestive tract of mosquitoes from the surrounding environment. Soil and dust containing these bacteria may contaminate the water, and from there to the larvae and then the adults [40]. Corynebacterium kutsceri is a transient species in the digestive tract and is not a natural fauna of it. It often infects rats and may be transmitted to the blood of humans and animals after being bitten by rats 41, 42.

These species of bacteria found in the internal cavity of mosquitoes in this study were acquired from the surrounding environment. The internal environment of mosquitoes, such as the high concentration of carbohydrates and the pH, and abiotic factors such as temperature and humidity, formed a selective environment for their bacterial community. Therefore, mosquitoes host diverse bacterial communities that vary according to the mosquito species and ecological factors[43].

Sewage contains many types of bacteria. These bacteria are transported to the digestive tracts of mosquito larvae living in the water, settle in their digestive systems, and remain in the digestive systems of adults. Thus, we observe a great diversity of bacterial species.

Conclusions

The presence of pathogenic bacterial diversity in the internal cavity of Cx. p. pipiens. Some of the bacteria found in the internal cavity of Cx. p. pipiens are from natural flora; some of them were found incidentally, some were opportunistic, and some were intracellular. The presence of pathogenic bacteria in humans in the digestive tract of Cx. p. pipiens may make it a host transmitting these bacteria. The presence of intracellular bacteria Wolbachia pipiens controls the sexual behavior of mosquitoes and can be used in the biological control of mosquitoes.

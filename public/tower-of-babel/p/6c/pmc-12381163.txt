COL4A1 as a potential prognostic biomarker with functional roles in colon cancer progression
Authors: Qiusheng Li, Changjing Wang, Guiying Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCOL4A1, a key component of the basement membrane, has been increasingly implicated in tumor progression, yet its role in colon cancer remains incompletely understood. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive integrative analysis using transcriptomic data from the TCGA-COAD cohort, combined with functional validation in colon cancer cell lines. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed that high COL4A1 expression was associated with oncogenic pathways including epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), KRAS signaling, and inflammatory responses. Immune infiltration analysis indicated that COL4A1 expression negatively correlated with CD8+ T cell infiltration but positively correlated with macrophage subtypes. Immunophenoscore (IPS) analysis further revealed that tumors with high COL4A1 expression exhibited significantly higher IPS values, suggesting altered immunogenicity. Functional assays demonstrated that COL4A1 knockdown reduced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro. Co-expression analysis of EMT markers showed strong positive correlations between COL4A1 and mesenchymal genes such as VIM, ZEB1, SNAI1, and FN1, supporting its role in EMT-like phenotypes. Collectively, our findings suggest that COL4A1 may serve as a prognostic biomarker and contributor to tumor progression and immune remodeling in colon cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-17230-8.Keywords: Colon cancer, COL4A1, PrognosisSubject terms: Cancer microenvironment, Tumour biomarkers

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is the third most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide1. The majority of colorectal cancers arise from benign adenomatous polyps that progressively acquire genetic and epigenetic alterations, ultimately transforming into invasive adenocarcinomas and metastasizing to distant organs2. This stepwise progression, known as the “multi-step tumorigenesis” model, involves sequential inactivation of tumor suppressor genes and activation of oncogenes3.

Disease stage remains the most critical prognostic factor in colorectal cancer management. Patients diagnosed at TNM stage I have a five-year survival rate exceeding 90%, whereas this drops to approximately 12% for those at stage IV4. Surgical resection, complemented by appropriate adjuvant therapy, is the mainstay of treatment5. However, aggressive tumor behaviors such as local invasion and distant metastasis are key drivers of disease progression and poor clinical outcomes. Alarmingly, up to 50% of patients experience recurrence or succumb to metastatic disease despite undergoing surgery. In stage III or more advanced cases, recurrence and metastasis remain common even after adjuvant therapy6,7.

Collagen, the most abundant structural protein in mammals, regulates numerous cellular functions including proliferation, migration, and differentiation via its interaction with cell surface receptors8. Among collagen subtypes, COL4A1 is a key basement membrane component that facilitates tumorigenic processes by triggering intracellular signaling pathways9. Aberrant overexpression of COL4A1 has been implicated in the development, progression, and metastasis of several cancer types, including hepatocellular carcinoma, breast cancer, and urothelial carcinoma10–13. Despite mounting evidence linking COL4A1 to various malignancies, its role in colon cancer remains largely unexplored. This study aims to characterize the expression profile of COL4A1 in colon cancer and to investigate its association with clinicopathological features and patient outcomes. Utilizing the TCGA-COAD dataset and patient tissue samples, we further evaluate the oncogenic potential of COL4A1 through functional studies in colon cancer cell lines.

Materials and methods

Pan-cancer expression analysis

Expression levels of COL4A1 across 33 tumor types defined by The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) Pan-Cancer Atlas were retrieved via the UCSC Xena browser (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). Gene expression data were normalized as log2 (TPM + 1). Both unpaired and paired comparisons were performed between tumor and matched adjacent normal tissues. Statistical significance was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for unpaired samples and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired samples.

DNA methylation and CNV correlation analysis

The relationships between COL4A1 mRNA expression and DNA methylation or copy number variation (CNV) were also assessed via GSCALite (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/GSCA/#/)14. Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated across cancer types, and false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied for multiple comparisons. Correlation strength and significance were visualized using bubble plots, with color and size indicating the direction and significance of associations, respectively.

eQTL-based Mendelian randomization analysis

Associations between genetically predicted COL4A1 expression and cancer risk were assessed using data from the DMRdb database (http://www.inbirg.com/DMRdb/)15, which integrates eQTL information with disease outcomes via Mendelian randomization. The Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method under a fixed effects model was applied to estimate causal effects. Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

RT-qPCR and immunohistochemistry

Forty pairs of primary colon cancer tissues and matched adjacent normal tissues were obtained from patients who underwent surgical resection at The Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University. All participants had no history of chemotherapy or radiotherapy prior to tissue collection. All participants provided informed consent for the subsequent analysis. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University (No. 2017MEC089). Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent, and reverse transcription was conducted using the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was carried out using a 7500 RT-PCR system with GAPDH as an internal control. The relative expression levels were calculated by the 2^−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences for COL4A1 and GAPDH are listed in Supplementary Table 1. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections were stained with anti-COL4A1 antibody (Abcam, ab308360). Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining results were independently assessed by two pathologists to evaluate COL4A1 protein expression.

Survival analysis

Public survival datasets were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier Plotter platform (https://kmplot.com)16, including overall survival (OS), relapse-free survival (RFS), and progression-free survival (PFS), stratified by median COL4A1 expression. Statistical comparisons were performed using the log-rank test, and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% CI were computed using univariate Cox regression. An independent clinical cohort comprising 40 patients was used to validate prognostic associations.

Association between COL4A1 expression and clinicopathologic features

Patients (TCGA-COAD) were stratified by COL4A1 expression level and compared across multiple clinical parameters, including age (≤ 65 vs. > 65), gender, pathologic stage (I/II vs. III/IV), T stage (T1/2 vs. T3/4), N stage (N0/N1 vs. N2), and M stage (M0 vs. M1). Violin plots were generated to visualize expression distributions across subgroups.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

Transcriptomic data from TCGA-COAD were stratified into high and low COL4A1 expression groups using the median as cutoff. Differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 package in R. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the Hallmark gene sets from MSigDB, and significance was assessed using the clusterProfiler package. Normalized enrichment scores (NES), false discovery rates (FDR), and adjusted P-values were reported.

Pan-cancer immune infiltration analysis

To evaluate the immunological associations of COL4A1 across cancer types, CIBERSORT was applied to estimate the relative abundance of 22 immune cell types using the LM22 signature matrix across TCGA pan-cancer cohorts. Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated between COL4A1 expression and each immune cell type in each cancer type. Correlations with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Immunophenoscore (IPS) analysis

IPS data for TCGA-COAD samples were obtained from The Cancer Immunome Atlas (https://tcia.at/home)17, which provides scores predicting the potential efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy based on gene expression profiles. IPS scores were extracted for four combinatorial scenarios. Patients were stratified into COL4A1-high and -low groups based on the median expression value, and group-wise comparisons of IPS were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Single-Cell RNA sequencing data analysis

Single-cell RNA sequencing data (GSE245552) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and analyzed using the Seurat package in R. Quality control was performed to exclude cells with fewer than 200 or more than 6,000 detected genes, as well as cells exhibiting high mitochondrial gene content (> 10%). After normalization and identification of highly variable genes, principal component analysis (PCA) was used for dimensionality reduction, followed by unsupervised clustering using the Louvain algorithm. Eleven distinct cell subpopulations were identified. Cell types were annotated based on canonical marker genes. To assess the expression pattern of COL4A1 across the identified cell clusters, a bubble plot was generated.

Isolation and clustering of endothelial cells

Endothelial cells were extracted from the single-cell transcriptomic dataset based on the cell type annotation (endothelial cells). The resulting subset, designated as endo, was processed using the Seurat workflow. Data normalization was performed using the NormalizeData function, followed by identification of highly variable genes, scaling, PCA, construction of a k-nearest neighbor graph, and Louvain clustering. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection was used for two-dimensional visualization of cell clusters.

Identification of COL4A1-high clusters

Average expression levels of COL4A1 were calculated for each endothelial cell cluster. Clusters with average COL4A1 expression greater than or equal to the 75th percentile were defined as “COL4A1-high clusters.” To reduce the influence of zero inflation, certain analyses were restricted to cells with COL4A1 expression values greater than zero.

Calculation of EMT module scores

EMT-related gene set was curated from the literature. The AddModuleScore function in Seurat was applied to calculate EMT module scores (EMT1) for each cell, representing the aggregate activity of EMT-related genes at the single-cell level.

Correlation and group-wise differential analyses

Within endothelial cells expressing COL4A1 (> 0), the relationship between COL4A1 expression and EMT1 scores was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. To compare EMT scores between expression groups, cells were stratified into a high-expression group (top 25% COL4A1 expression) and the remaining cells. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to assess differences in EMT scores between groups. Visualizations included scatter plots with linear regression fits and violin plots overlaid with boxplots and significance annotations.

Cell culture

Normal human colonic epithelial cells (NCM460) and five colon cancer cell lines (HT-29, HCT-8, HCT-116, SW480, SW620) were procured from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 humidified incubator.

Western blot analysis

Protein was extracted using RIPA buffer and quantified via BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (20 µg per lane) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% skim milk, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against COL4A1 and GAPDH. After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies at 37 °C for 1 h. Bands were visualized using an Odyssey infrared imaging system.

Cell transfection

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting COL4A1 and a negative control siRNA were synthesized and transfected into HCT116 and SW480 cells using Lipofectamine 3000. Cells were seeded at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in 6-well plates and transfected at 30% confluency. Knockdown efficiency was confirmed by Western blotting.

Colony formation assay

HCT116 and SW480 cells were seeded in 6-well plates (1,000 cells per well) and cultured for two weeks. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.5% crystal violet. Colonies with more than 50 cells were counted under a microscope.

Wound healing and transwell assays

For invasion assays, 8 μm pore size Transwell chambers (Corning) coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences) were used to simulate extracellular matrix barriers. Cells (5 × 10⁵ in 200 µL serum-free DMEM) were seeded into the upper chambers, while the lower chambers were filled with DMEM containing 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, non-invading cells were gently removed from the upper membrane surface. The invaded cells on the lower side of the membrane were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.5% crystal violet, and counted under a light microscope in five random fields18.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2) and GraphPad Prism 5.0. Continuous variables were compared using one-way ANOVA or Student’s t-test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test. Correlation analyses were conducted using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. All p-values were two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For multiple comparisons, p-values were adjusted using the false discovery rate method where applicable. Data visualization was conducted using R (ggplot2) and GraphPad Prism.

Results

COL4A1 is significantly upregulated in colon cancer tissues

A pan-cancer analysis revealed that COL4A1 is significantly overexpressed in a broad range of tumor types across TCGA cohorts. In unpaired comparisons between tumor and normal tissues, marked upregulation of COL4A1 was observed in cancers including colorectal, liver, and gastric cancer (Fig. 1A). This trend was further validated in paired tumor–normal samples, where consistent expression increases were detected (Fig. 1B), supporting a widespread dysregulation of COL4A1 in malignant tissues.

COL4A1 expression, methylation, and CNV alterations across human cancers. (A) Expression levels of COL4A1 across 33 cancer types in TCGA, comparing tumor and normal tissues using unpaired samples. (B) Paired analysis of COL4A1 expression between tumor and adjacent normal tissues. (C) Correlation between COL4A1 mRNA expression and DNA methylation levels across cancer types, showing predominantly negative associations. (D) Correlation between COL4A1 expression and copy number variation (CNV) across cancers.

To explore potential regulatory mechanisms underlying this overexpression, we examined the relationship between COL4A1 expression and DNA methylation. A robust inverse correlation was found in most cancer types (Fig. 1C), suggesting that promoter hypomethylation may contribute to transcriptional activation of COL4A1. By contrast, copy number variation showed weak or inconsistent correlation with COL4A1 expression across tumors (Fig. 1D), indicating that CNV alterations are unlikely to be the primary driver of its dysregulation. Together, these findings implicate epigenetic derepression, rather than genomic amplification, as a potential mechanism for the widespread upregulation of COL4A1 in human cancers.

Genetically predicted COL4A1 expression is associated with increased colon cancer risk

Mendelian randomization analysis using eQTL data from the DMRdb database demonstrated a significant association between genetically elevated COL4A1 expression and increased risk of colon cancer (OR = 1.13, P = 0.000), estimated using the inverse variance weighted (IVW) fixed-effects model (Fig. 2A).

Association of COL4A1 expression with colon cancer risk and prognosis. (A) Mendelian randomization analysis using the DMRdb database revealed a significant association between genetically predicted COL4A1 expression and increased risk of colon cancer. Causal effect was estimated using the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method under a fixed-effects model. (B) qRT-PCR analysis showed significantly higher COL4A1 mRNA expression in tumor tissues compared to matched adjacent normal tissues in 40 colon cancer patients. (C) Immunohistochemistry confirmed increased COL4A1 protein expression in colon tumor tissues. (D–F) Kaplan–Meier survival analysis based on public datasets showed that high COL4A1 expression was associated with reduced overall survival (D) and relapse-free survival (E), but not progression-free survival (F) in colon cancer. (G) Survival analysis in an independent clinical cohort of 40 colon cancer patients demonstrated that low COL4A1 expression was associated with improved overall survival. HR, hazard ratio; OR, odds ratio; CI, confidence interval; P-values were derived from the log-rank test (D–G) or IVW model (A).

In an internal cohort of 40 colon cancer patients, qRT-PCR analysis confirmed significantly higher COL4A1 mRNA levels in tumor tissues compared to matched normal tissues (Fig. 2B), which was further validated by increased protein expression on immunohistochemistry (Fig. 2C). Public dataset analysis revealed that high COL4A1 expression was significantly associated with shorter overall survival (OS; HR = 1.4, P < 0.001; Fig. 2D), and reduced relapse-free survival (RFS; HR = 1.5, P = 0.0002; Fig. 2E). By contrast, no significant association was found between COL4A1 expression and progression-free survival (PFS; HR = 1.11, P = 0.49; Fig. 2F).

Importantly, analysis of the same internal cohort showed that patients with low COL4A1 expression had significantly better OS (HR = 0.36, P = 0.040; Fig. 2G), further supporting the association between COL4A1 overexpression and poor prognosis in colon cancer.

COL4A1 expression is associated with advanced clinicopathologic features in colon cancer

Analysis of TCGA-COAD data revealed significant associations between COL4A1 expression and multiple clinicopathologic characteristics. Patients with higher COL4A1 expression tended to be older (> 65 years; P < 0.01), and expression levels were significantly elevated in more advanced pathological stages (III/IV vs. I/II; P < 0.05) (Fig. 3). Similar patterns were observed across T and N classification: higher COL4A1 expression was associated with advanced T stage (T3/T4 vs. T1/T2), N stage (N1/N2 vs. N0). These findings suggest that COL4A1 overexpression may be linked to more aggressive tumor behavior in colon cancer.

Association of COL4A1 expression with clinicopathologic features in colon cancer. The heatmap displays COL4A1 expression levels (high vs. low) in relation to clinical characteristics in TCGA-COAD patients, including age, gender, pathological stage, and TNM classification. Violin plots show the distribution of COL4A1 expression across clinical subgroups: patients older than 65 years exhibited higher expression levels than those aged 65 or younger (P < 0.01); COL4A1 expression was also significantly elevated in advanced pathologic stage (stage III/IV), higher T stage (T3/T4), and N stage (N2) groups. Expression differences were tested using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Statistical significance was evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. P < 0.05 was considered significant.

COL4A1 expression is associated with oncogenic signaling and immunosuppressive features across cancers

GSEA in the TCGA-COAD cohort revealed that tumors with high COL4A1 expression were significantly enriched in epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), inflammatory response, myogenesis, UV response downregulation, and KRAS signaling pathways. In contrast, tumors with low COL4A1 expression showed enrichment in oxidative phosphorylation, MYC targets, DNA repair, and E2F signaling pathways, suggesting differential biological programs associated with COL4A1 levels (Fig. 4A). To further validate the relationship between COL4A1 and EMT, we performed co-expression analysis of COL4A1 and canonical EMT markers. Most mesenchymal markers, including ZEB1, TWIST1, FN1, and MMP9, showed strong positive correlation with COL4A1, while CDH1 exhibited no significant correlation (Supplementary Fig. 2). Furthermore, to better characterize the cellular source of COL4A1, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing data and observed that COL4A1 was predominantly expressed in fibroblasts and endothelial cells, with minimal expression in immune cell populations such as T cells and B cells (Supplementary Fig. 3A). Among endothelial cells with detectable COL4A1 expression (> 0), COL4A1 levels were significantly and positively correlated with EMT module scores (Supplementary Fig. 3B). When cells were stratified by COL4A1 expression into a high-expression group (top 25%) and the remaining cells, EMT scores were significantly elevated in the COL4A1-high group (Supplementary Fig. 3C). This suggests that COL4A1 may exert its immunomodulatory effects indirectly via stromal remodeling.

Functional and immune microenvironmental associations of COL4A1 in colon cancer and pan-cancer cohorts. The left panel shows gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of TCGA-COAD samples stratified by high versus low COL4A1 expression. High COL4A1 expression was associated with enrichment in epithelial–mesenchymal transition, inflammatory response, myogenesis, UV response down, and KRAS signaling pathways, while low expression was enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, MYC targets, DNA repair, and E2F signaling. Red and blue indicate positive and negative correlations, respectively. Asterisks (*) denote statistically significant associations (P < 0.05).

To investigate immune associations across tumor types, a pan-cancer immune infiltration analysis was performed using CIBERSORT. Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated a consistent pattern wherein COL4A1 expression was negatively correlated with CD8+ T cell infiltration in the majority of cancers, suggesting a potential link to reduced cytotoxic immune responses. Conversely, COL4A1 expression was positively correlated with macrophage infiltration, including M0, M1, and M2 subtypes, across multiple tumor types. These findings suggest that COL4A1 may contribute to shaping an immunosuppressive or macrophage-dominant tumor microenvironment in a broad range of malignancies (Fig. 4B). To further evaluate the immunotherapeutic implications of COL4A1 expression in colon cancer, we investigated its relationship with IPS. Notably, IPS scores were significantly lower in the COL4A1-high group across all ICB contexts, including PD1−/CTLA4−, PD1+/CTLA4−, PD1−/CTLA4+, and PD1+/CTLA4+ settings (Supplementary Fig. 4). These findings suggest that high COL4A1 expression may predict impaired sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors, in line with its association with reduced cytotoxic T cell infiltration and increased immunosuppressive features.

COL4A1 is overexpressed in colon cancer cell lines

Western blot analysis confirmed that COL4A1 protein levels were higher in all five colon cancer cell lines (HT-29, HCT-8, HCT-116, SW480, SW620) compared to the normal colonic epithelial cell line NCM460, with the most pronounced expression in SW480 and HCT116 cells (Fig. 5). Efficient knockdown of COL4A1 was achieved in these two cell lines using two independent siRNAs (si-1 and si-2), as validated by Western blotting (Fig. 5).

COL4A1 protein expression and knockdown in colon cancer cell lines. (A) Western blot showing COL4A1 expression in normal colonic epithelial cells (NCM460) and colon cancer cell lines (HT-29, HCT-8, HCT-116, SW480, SW620). (B, C) Validation of COL4A1 knockdown in HCT116 and SW480 cells using two siRNAs (si-1 and si-2). Original blots are presented in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Knockdown COL4A1 inhibits proliferation, migration, and invasion of colon cancer cells

To determine the functional role of COL4A1 in tumor cell behavior, we assessed the effects of its knockdown in SW480 and HCT116 cells. Wound healing assays showed that COL4A1 knockdown significantly reduced the migratory ability of both cell lines after 48 h (P < 0.01; Fig. 6A). Transwell assays revealed that invasion capacity was also markedly diminished in COL4A1-depleted cells (P < 0.01; Fig. 6B). Furthermore, colony formation assays demonstrated a significant reduction in proliferative potential following COL4A1 knockdown (P < 0.05; Fig. 6C). Collectively, these data highlight the oncogenic role of COL4A1 in promoting the aggressive phenotype of colon cancer cells.

Effects of COL4A1 knockdown on biological behavior of colon cancer cells. (A) Wound healing assay showing reduced migration after COL4A1 knockdown. (B) Transwell assay indicating decreased invasive capacity following COL4A1 knockdown. (C) Colony formation assay showing suppressed proliferation in COL4A1-silenced cells.

Discussion

Despite advances in early detection and treatment, colon cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, largely due to tumor invasion, metastasis, and resistance to therapy. A growing body of evidence suggests that components of the extracellular matrix (ECM) not only support tumor architecture but also actively regulate malignant progression and immune evasion19. However, the specific ECM-derived factors that integrate oncogenic signaling with microenvironmental remodeling in colon cancer are not fully understood. In this context, we identified COL4A1, a major structural component of basement membranes, as a candidate regulator of both tumor aggressiveness and immune contexture in colon cancer.

While the overexpression of COL4A1 has been sporadically reported in several tumor types20–22, a systematic pan-cancer characterization of its expression pattern and potential regulatory mechanisms has been lacking. By analyzing TCGA datasets, our study reveals that COL4A1 is broadly overexpressed across multiple malignancies, with particularly high levels in colon cancer. Importantly, its expression levels showed no consistent association with copy number variation, but exhibited a strong inverse correlation with promoter methylation levels in several cancers. These data, derived from bioinformatics analyses, suggest that epigenetic derepression—rather than genomic amplification—may underlie COL4A1 upregulation. However, direct experimental validation of this regulatory mechanism remains warranted.

Mendelian randomization (MR) leverages germline genetic variation to infer potential causal relationships between molecular traits and disease phenotypes, offering a powerful framework to complement observational studies23,24. By integrating expression quantitative trait loci with colon cancer risk data, our analysis revealed a statistically significant association between genetically predicted COL4A1 expression and increased disease risk. While not definitive evidence of causality, this result suggests that COL4A1 may act as a potential susceptibility gene rather than a passive correlate.

Beyond genetic risk, COL4A1 expression was also significantly associated with patient outcomes. Higher COL4A1 levels predicted worse overall and relapse-free survival in both TCGA data and an independent clinical cohort. Furthermore, its expression correlated with advanced pathological stage, nodal involvement, and older age, indicating a link to more aggressive tumor phenotypes. These findings collectively highlight the clinical relevance of COL4A1, both as a potential biomarker for prognosis and as an indicator of disease severity in colon cancer.

Functional pathway analysis revealed that tumors with high COL4A1 expression were enriched in hallmark gene sets associated with EMT, KRAS signaling, and inflammatory responses—core processes that drive tumor progression, invasion, and immune modulation. Notably, EMT emerged as the most significantly enriched pathway, underscoring its central role in the phenotypic shift associated with COL4A1 upregulation. EMT not only enhances cellular motility and invasiveness, but also promotes resistance to apoptosis, immune surveillance, and therapeutic intervention25. These findings suggest that COL4A1 may contribute to tumor aggressiveness by facilitating EMT-mediated remodeling of both the cellular and stromal compartments.

Complementing these results, pan-cancer immune infiltration analysis demonstrated that COL4A1 expression was inversely correlated with CD8⁺ T cell abundance, while positively associated with multiple macrophage subtypes, including M0, M1, and M2. This immune pattern is consistent with an immunosuppressive or immune-excluded phenotype, characterized by diminished cytotoxic T cell infiltration and dominance of myeloid-derived cells in the tumor microenvironment. Such immune configurations have been associated with tumor progression and poor responses to immunotherapy26,27. The observed downregulation of IPS scores in COL4A1-high tumors adds functional relevance to our findings of CD8⁺ T cell exclusion and macrophage enrichment. Despite the complex immunogenomic profile, high COL4A1 expression appears to define a tumor state that is less favorable for immune checkpoint therapy responsiveness. This is further supported by lower IPS across all PD1/CTLA4 combinations. Mechanistically, COL4A1 may contribute to immune exclusion via extracellular matrix remodeling, limiting cytotoxic immune infiltration despite the presence of immune checkpoint signals. These findings suggest that COL4A1 may serve not only as a biomarker of poor prognosis but also as a potential indicator of immune therapy resistance in colon cancer. Furthermore, key immunoregulatory pathways, including TGF-β, IL-10, IL-6, and IL11RA signaling, have been implicated in shaping an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment by promoting macrophage polarization and suppressing cytotoxic T cell function. These cytokine-driven mechanisms are increasingly recognized in tumor immune remodeling and may provide a broader context for understanding the immune correlations observed in COL4A1-high colon cancers28.

To experimentally validate the role of COL4A1 in driving tumor cell behavior, we performed loss-of-function studies in colon cancer cell lines. HCT116 and SW480 were selected for knockdown experiments due to their relatively high endogenous COL4A1 expression, as confirmed by Western blot analysis. This provided a biologically relevant model in which the impact of COL4A1 depletion could be effectively assessed.

Functional assays demonstrated that COL4A1 knockdown significantly reduced key malignant properties in both cell lines. Migration was reduced in wound healing assays, invasion through Matrigel-coated membranes was diminished in Transwell assays, and clonogenic growth was suppressed in colony formation assays. These phenotypic changes reflect core aspects of tumor aggressiveness and align closely with the transcriptional enrichment of EMT observed in COL4A1-high tumors. EMT is a well-recognized driver of enhanced motility, invasiveness, and anchorage-independent survival, all of which were directly impacted by COL4A1 knockdown.

This study has several limitations. First, the mechanistic insights into how COL4A1 regulates EMT remain incomplete. While transcriptomic analyses and functional assays suggest a potential role in promoting EMT-like phenotypes, direct validation through EMT marker expression (e.g., Western blot for E-cadherin and Vimentin) was not performed in this study. Additionally, the absence of transcriptome-wide profiling (e.g., RNA-seq) in COL4A1-perturbed models limits our ability to define downstream EMT signaling cascades. Despite these limitations, our integrative bioinformatics analysis and in vitro phenotypic assays provide a solid foundation supporting the oncogenic and immunomodulatory roles of COL4A1 in colon cancer.

Taken together, these findings support a model in which COL4A1 promotes colon cancer progression by enabling EMT-associated phenotypic plasticity. The consistency between pathway-level signals and in vitro functional outcomes underscores the biological significance of COL4A1 and its potential as a therapeutic target in aggressive tumor subsets.

Conclusion

Our study identifies COL4A1 as a clinically relevant and functionally active gene in colon cancer. Integrative analyses across public datasets revealed that COL4A1 is frequently upregulated in tumors, with expression patterns linked to epigenetic deregulation rather than copy number variation. Higher COL4A1 expression was associated with increased genetic risk, unfavorable clinical features, and poorer survival outcomes. Mechanistically, COL4A1 expression correlated with enrichment of epithelial–mesenchymal transition and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, suggesting roles in both tumor cell plasticity and immune evasion. Functional assays confirmed that silencing COL4A1 led to reduced migration, invasion, and clonogenic growth in colon cancer cells. Together, these findings highlight COL4A1 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in aggressive subtypes of colon cancer.

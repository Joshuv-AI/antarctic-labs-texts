Machine learning based dynamic trust estimation framework for Securing wireless sensor networks
Authors: Prafull Goswami, Tayyab Khan, Vinay Pathak, Abdulatif Alabdultif
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) face significant challenges, such as security threats, and susceptibility to various internal and external attacks, impacting data integrity and network reliability. Trust-aware machine learning (ML) based security frameworks are vital solutions to overcome these challenges by improving security, decision-making, accuracy, and attack detection. These models utilizes ML to actively monitor trust levels, enhancing accuracy of malicious behavior detection and strengthening network security and integrity. In this context, we propose a Secure Machine-learning-based Adaptive Reliable Trust (SMART) model, suitable for unattended autonomous WSN environment. The proposed SMART model provides enhanced security and accuracy through fast and trustworthy dynamic generation of trust values. The proposed SMART model uses a novel ML algorithm that derives multiple trust features such as Co-Location Relationship (CLR), Co-Work Relationship (CWR), and Cooperativeness-Frequency-Duration (CFD). These features together give rise to an proactive trust score for sensor nodes, enabling predictions of likely misbehavior and allows for informed decisions regarding the reliability of individual sensor devices. Furthermore, SMART framework approach utilizes both immediate (direct) trust and derived (indirect) trust, making use of logical time windows to systematically watch for honest and suspicious interactions. SMART utilizes an efficient K-means clustering algorithm to assign data points to the nearest K-centers, optimizing cluster identification. Furthermore, It utilizes Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for significant variance identification and makes use of an SVM-based method for dimension reduction and accurate decision-making with lower time and space complexity. Performance measures such as accuracy, F1-score, recall, FNR, malicious SD detection, and trust value change measure its performance. Simulation results confirm that the SMART model can identify malicious nodes with a detection rate of 96%, FNR of 0.7%, F1-Score of 0.75, and accuracy of 96% even when there are 50 malicious nodes. This confirms the ability of the SMART model to enhance the trustworthiness and security of WSNs considerably.

Introduction

An automated wireless sensor network (WSN) is composed of huge numbers of autonomous and resource-constrained sensor devices (SDs) working in dynamic environments, producing bulky volumes of valuable data that is critical for decision-making in industries such as healthcare, defense, and industry1. Remote deployment of SDs exposes them to malicious behavior, especially when forwarding data. The biggest challenge in such environments is developing a robust security mechanism that protects sensitive data while conserving resources2. Trust assessment has been a lightweight competent mechanism to address this challenge because it diminishes uncertainty and provides dependable inter-device connectivity, thus enabling secure and smart services3. By encouraging cooperation among SDs and mitigating risks through observations, trust assessment aids in achieving predicted operational outcomes. Figure 1 shows the various architectures of WSN, attacks on WSN and popular trust models. Trust-based approaches monitor the behavior of SDs in real-time, assess their cooperation and contributions, and assign trust scores accordingly4. This process facilitates malicious SDs with low trust ratings to be detected and quarantined. Existing researches have discussed various cost effective resource-efficient, trust assessment models, and Machine Learning (ML) algorithms5. These algorithms6,7, e.g., “Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), and Gaussian Naïve Bayes”, can process complex data quickly, providing real-time feedback for efficient resource allocation and maximizing network life. ML algorithms improve the security of WSNs by identifying internal and external attacks with high accuracy8. Trust assessment in WSNs based on ML is replete with challenges. One of the most important challenges is obtaining useful trust features from raw data. Formulating a dynamic trust function to model various types of attacks and assigning appropriate weights to various trust metrics is essential9. Appropriate clustering and dimensionality reduction also need to be ensured. The trust model’s reliability highly depends on proper trust feature selection, which is difficult because the data in WSN is noisy, incomplete, and inconsistent10. An appropriate trust function should also be able to adapt to different network scenarios and detect different types of attacks, e.g., node compromise and fake data injection11. Furthermore, relative weightage between direct and indirect trust also keeps changing in different scenarios, and thus appropriate weightage assignment becomes difficult12. Data clustering algorithms allow assigning labels to data clusters, but the best choice is dependent on data type and performance characteristic13. Dimensionality reduction improves the performance and accuracy of ML models, but the choice of the best algorithm is still a problem because of the heterogeneous strengths and weaknesses of available algorithms14. To solve all these problems, we propose a new ML-based, trust-based decision paradigm for WSNs. The proposed method aggregates trust features such as Co-Location Relationship (CLR), Co-Work Relationship (CWR), and Cooperativeness-Frequency-Duration (CFD) dynamically to maximize efficiency and reliability. CLR confirms physical proximity and co-location frequency to detect cooperative SDs, making the trust evaluation more accurate. CWR confirms trustworthiness of SDs by confirming their relationship and interaction history during data transmission. CFD calculates the trustworthiness of a node based on cooperativeness, interaction frequency, and duration using a binary entropy function. Reward calculates positive reward from interactions to encourage trustworthy behavior and improve the network’s overall performance. High-rewarding SDs have a greater likelihood of being selected for interactions to encourage long-term trustworthy behavior, while low-rewarding SDs are encouraged to become trustworthy. we select CLR, CWR, and CFD as trust measurement parameters because of their ability to solve fundamental trust measurement issues in dynamic and resource-constrained WSNs environments that other parameters cannot.

CLR is chosen because it is capable of measuring spatial proximity among SDs, which is a fundamental factor in predicting the probability of immediate interaction and repeated data exchange, especially in applications that require precise localization. CWR is chosen to predict the level of collaborative interaction between SDs by estimating their co-working behavior in multicast interaction, hence a relevant cooperative trustworthiness parameter, especially in networks where cooperation is essential to efficiency, e.g., cluster-based WSNs. CFD captures the reliability and consistency of cooperative interaction in the long run, taking a close picture of an SD’s long-term reliability. These parameters were chosen above others because they best describe the spatial, collaborative, and temporal nature of trust, hence enhancing the security and operation efficiency of WSNs. Focus on CLR, CWR, and CFD guarantees an efficient and context-aware trust measurement mechanism, addressing the basic issues of guaranteeing reliable and secure communication in WSNs directly.

Motivation

In WSNs, ML-based trust modeling is a central frontier to improve security and reliability against evolving threats and malicious behavior15–17. The necessity stems from the intrinsic vulnerability of WSNs to diverse attacks, including data tampering, node compromise, and insider attacks, that have the potential to devastatingly affect data integrity and network functionality18–20. Traditional security protocols21–30 are bound to be inadequate in reacting dynamically toward the dynamic nature of threats. Therefore, ML-based trust modeling becomes unavoidable with its ability in handling large and heterogeneous data streams in real-time31, identifying meaningful patterns reflective of trustworthiness or potential anomalies32. The importance of ML-based trust modeling does not only reside in its ability to identify and counter attacks but also in supporting effective decision-making in WSNs33–36. With the inclusion of many dynamic features30,37, such systems are able to evaluate trust levels more efficiently and adaptively. Besides anomaly detection38–41, the application of ML in trust modeling is also not confined to; it extends to optimizing resource allocation, routing protocols improvement, and increasing the overall efficiency of the network42,43. The ability to learn from historic data and incrementally refine estimates of trust provides ML-based techniques not only resilience44,45 but also future resistance to emerging attacks in more and more sophisticated WSN deployments46,47.

Related work

This section helps to identify existing trust management approaches20–30,32–36,42–45,48, with their strengths, and limitations within WSNs. By comparing and contrasting existing methods, this research paper can highlight novelties and improvements introduced by the proposed SMART framework. Additionally, understanding related work aids in validating the effectiveness and practicality of SMART, ensuring it addresses current challenges and contributes meaningfully to enhancing security, reliability, and adaptability in WSN environments. This contextual foundation strengthens the paper’s credibility and relevance in the field. The recent literature review is listed below.

Yang et al.20 proposes an innovative trust management scheme for industrial WSNs based on generative adversarial networks (GANs). The main objective is to improve security by overcoming challenges such as training set scalability and new attack detection without labeled data. The work applies type-2 fuzzy logic to assess SD reputation with low uncertainty. Trust vectors are employed to train a GAN-based codec network to detect malicious SDs. Additionally, a GAN-based trust redemption model prevents normal SDs from being isolated by false positives. The approach uses a dynamic trust model update mechanism for support in various industrial environments. Simulation results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed mechanism with a detection rate of up to 96% and a false positive rate of less than 8%. A weakness is the need for robustness testing under varying industrial environments to ensure performance under varying conditions. Saravana et al.22 suggests a hybrid approach that combines Reinforcement Learning (RL) and ML for improving the efficiency and reliability of WSNs. The approach solves some significant issues, such as energy exhaustion, intrusion detection (specifically DoS attack), and network connectivity optimization by a connected dominating set (CDS) approach. The objective of the system is to choose SDs that have enough energy to maintain the integrity of the network. It utilizes RL (Reinforcement Learning) to optimize the activities of the SDs, resulting in enhanced connection. The experimental results demonstrate substantial enhancements in data transmission measures such as Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR), latency, goodput, and throughput. The network’s lifespan is prolonged by approximately 50%, accompanied by a comparable 50% decrease in the rate at which packets are dropped, in comparison to earlier models. Nevertheless, the major focus remains on PDR, which exhibits improvement as the standard deviations increase but still requires additional enhancement. Reinforcement learning (RL) is successfully used to quickly identify harmful SDs, guaranteeing smooth communication within a network consisting of permitted SDs.

Siddiqui et al.25 present a distributed trust management model for Internet-of-Vehicles (IoV) with parameters such as packet delivery ratio, familiarity, timeliness, and interaction frequency between vehicles. The objective is to efficiently estimate vehicle trust scores to identify and suppress malicious vehicles in real-time using an optimal trust threshold. The work uses an IoT dataset from CRAWDAD, converted to an IoV format, with interactions among 76 SDs. Two feature matrices (FM1 and FM2) are obtained: FM1 includes individual pairwise parameters as features, and FM2 averages pairwise computations into individual features. Unsupervised learning is first used to create ground truth, and supervised machine learning (Subspace KNN and Subspace Discriminant) is then used for classification. Surprisingly, both techniques have perfect precision, recall, and F1-scores of 1. FM2 has better classification accuracy than FM1 in large-scale simulations. Future work plans to investigate other trust parameters and dynamic attack models, and also to create a special IoV simulator to create thorough trust-based datasets.

Bagwari et al.28 concentrate on the design of an Enhanced Energy Optimization Model (EEOM) for Industrial Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) based on machine learning (ML) methods. The main emphasis is on addressing energy spending and maintenance issues inherent in WSNs through the application of knowledge-based learning methods. The EEOM achieves considerable savings in energy spending during transmission, reception, idle, and sleep modes, adding to the improved efficiency and reliability of the overall network. The key findings indicate significant energy savings as well as outstanding performance measures, such as the prevalence threshold and critical success index, indicating the capability of the model in optimizing energy consumption. However, a key limitation attained in the study concerns the difficulty of integrating renewable energy sources and optimizing location-based settings to further increase efficiency. In spite of such a constraint, the EEOM provides an effective solution in enhancing data processing and operational efficiency for industrial WSNs. Based on ML algorithms, the model learns effectively from operational data, adjusts to varying environmental conditions, and saves energy with high accuracy rates. Such a solution not only saves operating costs but also makes industrial WSNs operate efficiently and reliably in changing and hostile environments.

Gebremariam et al.29 focuses on enhancing security in IoT-WSNs using a blockchain-based scheme for malicious SD detection and localization. The primary objective is enhancing network security and service reliability. The scheme is based on the application of cascade encryption and hierarchical trust assessment to obtain blockchain trust values. The values are used for SD trustworthiness assessment and secure data transmission to the base station. Federated ML algorithms, including XGBoost and CLK-means classifiers, are used for SD classification into binary and multiclass classes. The outcome is high accuracy rates of 100% for binary and 99.95% for multiclass classification, which validates the effectiveness of the scheme in malicious SD detection and localization. The outcome is an indicator of enhanced network security and operation efficiency. However, the weakness is seen in the dependency on accurate trust assessment and the susceptibility of ML models to new attack methods in dynamic IoT environments.

Khan et al.30 target the formulation of an ML-based trust estimation model for WSNs in improving security and dependability in Heterogeneous, dynamic environments. The work is geared toward transcending the limitations of traditional static trust estimation models using the flexibility, scalability, and precision of ML. The approach employs ML models for dynamically calculating trust using selected features such as CLR, CWR, and CFD. Integration of the parameters yields an aggregated trust estimation to allow conclusions about SD card dependability. Empirical findings reveal the performance of the proposed model at a 94% accuracy level in the detection of malicious software-defined devices at a moderate FNR of 0.9%. Findings prove the superiority of the approach over traditional models such as RBC-IDS with noticeable detection rates and F1-Score improvements in multiple environments. A limitation exists in the model with the reliance on accurate feature extraction and computationally intensive resources for real-time implementation in large-scale WSNs.

Hajar et al.33 present the emerging discipline of Wireless Medical Sensor Networks (WMSN) with improvements in semiconductor technology and wireless communication capabilities. Their primary objective is to develop an efficient routing protocol that satisfies the stringent requirements of WMSN, such as guaranteeing reliable data delivery, resisting attacks, minimizing computing overhead, and maximizing energy efficiency. The paper proposes 3R, a novel Reliable Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (RL) routing system. 3R differs from others in that it employs a light-weight reinforcement learning mechanism to minimize the computing overhead and achieve improved convergence with optimized update mechanisms. The reward function is redefined as a penalty, and it has a trust management system to combat drop attacks. Additionally, an energy model makes the network more resilient by controlling energy consumption locally without sharing any vital information. Experimental evaluation shows that 3R possesses light-weight, attack-proof, and energy-efficient properties and is an extremely promising routing method for WMSN. However, some of these challenges must be resolved in the future, such as designing more light-weight and secure routing protocols. Future work objectives include hyperparameter tuning and exploration-exploitation strategy optimization to improve the overall network performance.

Huang et al.42 introduces the necessity of strong trust models in Underwater Acoustic Sensor Networks (UASNs), essential for evaluating SD integrity and detecting compromised units in severe underwater environments. Current models often overlook the effect of ambient aquatic noise and are suffering from issues like high packet collision rates and underwater signal transmission error probabilities, leading to inaccurate SD evaluations. This paper introduces RMIS, a new ML-based scheme for malicious SD detection in Underwater Acoustic Sensor Networks (UASNs). RMIS measures the effect of environmental factors like packet collisions and utilizes communication traffic analysis as a strong measure of SD trustworthiness with the capability of detecting multiple attack types. Support vector machine and K-means + + algorithms are utilized for training evaluation models, with suggested trust update mechanisms providing resistance to dynamic underwater environments and on-off attacks. Simulation results prove RMIS’s superior performance in malicious SD detection over current schemes, particularly at high malicious SD ratios, and resilience and adaptability in UASN deployments.

Ghadi et al.43 elaborates on the inherent conflict of balancing security and energy efficiency in WSNs as being inherently at odds with one another due to the additional electrical load of advanced security systems. Traditional network security measures like encryption and key management are generally ineffective in WSNs, necessitating advanced solutions. ML algorithms are suggested as potential solutions to such security threats by enhancing WSN security through mechanisms like intrusion detection and error detection. The paper elaborates on various ML algorithms applied in WSN security, its effectiveness, advantages, and limitations. It also elaborates on the adaptation of ML algorithms to suit WSNs’ resource constraint and operational requirements. The research aims to highlight future potential for the application of ML in WSNs in improving security robustness and operational efficiency, given the existing challenges and potential for further enhancement in this critical area.

Affane et al.45 proposes enhancing the security of WSNs against distributed attacks through a novel anomaly detection method. The overall goal is to develop a system that effectively identifies malicious routing process behavior and data transmission. Methodologically, the research applies artificial intelligence methodologies, specifically stochastic models like Hidden Markov Models (HMM) and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM). The models employ ML in analyzing sensor data to identify normal and attacked states of the network. Dimension reduction algorithms are also employed to lower feature selection complexity in training the detection system. The findings record high success with the proposed method achieving high classification accuracy of 92.18%. Precision is 98% and overall accuracy 95%, outperforming other methods like SVM, Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, and Random Forests. This indicates the effectiveness of the HMM and GMM-based method in intrusion detection and prevention in networks. The limitation is the application of simulated or controlled datasets, which may not be representative of the full complexity and variability of WSN environments in real life.

Moundounga et al.46 proposes a novel Stochastic ML-Based Attack Detection System for WSNs, combining Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs). Dimension reduction in WSN datasets is done through Principal Component Analysis, focusing on significant routing characteristics and minimizing variables. Iterative Expectation-Maximization is applied in the system to train HMMs and GMMs to support precise detection and classification of malicious activity and routing errors. Experimental experiments observe that a 3 HMMs and 4 GMMs setup gives high 94.55% accuracy, outperforming other setups. Comparative experiments with standard ML classifiers affirm its superior performance, particularly in cross-validation setups reporting accuracies ranging from 0.96 to 0.98. However, the use of simulated datasets by the study may decrease applicability and ruggedness in real-world contexts. Generally, the proposed system is a valuable addition to WSN security, bringing together rigorous testing and comparative analysis to affirm its efficacy.

Sagar et al.37 presents the application of ML techniques to advance WSNs in different applications such as industrial settings, healthcare, and intelligent spaces. Some of the key objectives include security enhancement, routing efficiency, optimization of coverage and connectivity, accurate location, congestion management, data aggregation, quality of service (QoS), and maximizing network lifetime through energy harvesting. Methodologically, the chapter synthesizes existing studies on the application of ML algorithms to WSNs to achieve these objectives. Results observe impressive network performance and resource usage improvements. However, limitations observed include optimizing ML algorithms to resource-limited environments, data privacy and security guarantees, and adaptation to real-world deployment complexities. Despite these drawbacks, the study affirms the potential of ML to revolutionize WSN operations, making them adaptive, efficient, and robust in dynamic and hostile environments.

Kirubasri et al.38 addresses the issue of efficient energy consumption in Wireless Multimedia Sensor Networks through a novel routing protocol. The overall objective is enhanced Quality of Service (QoS) parameters such as throughput, packet loss ratio, reliability, SD energy efficiency, and network lifetime. Methodologically, the proposed Link Quality based Energy and Trust Aware Routing Protocol (LQETA-RP) blends hardware and software-based measures such as Link Quality Indicator, Received Signal Strength Indicator, Expected Transmission Count, and Trust based Link Cost Function (TLCF) to estimate optimal paths. This is anticipated to minimize energy expenditure on route discovery and forward data with reliability. Simulation experiments through NS3 prove the proposed protocol to be better than existing protocols (EEPLQRR, LQER, LQEAR, and TERP) with 30–40% energy saving, 10–15% increase in throughput, and reduction in packet loss by about 30–40%. The limitation is that it is developed on simulation environments that may not reflect actual network complexity and issues.

Pande et al.39 discusses in detail Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS), critical cybersecurity mechanisms. It discusses Network-based IDS and Host-based IDS, their function and relationship in warding off cyber attacks. IDS continuously scans network traffic or host activity, using anomaly-based and signature-based detection to detect suspicious activity. The research highlights the ability of behavior-based algorithms to detect new, unknown threats. It highlights the inclusion of IDS in overall cybersecurity strategy, preferably augmented by Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS), to rapidly respond to detected threats. The success of IDS depends on timely updates, correct configuration, and watchful monitoring. The paper promotes best practices for increasing IDS reliability, e.g., the input of experienced security analysts to interpreting and responding to alerts. Ultimately, while IDS is valuable to improving network security and safeguarding information, its success is optimized when part of an overall, multi-layered cybersecurity strategy.

Bukhari et al.40 presents a new approach to enhancing IDS in WSNs with the Federated Learning (FL) powered Stacked Convolutional Neural Network and Bidirectional Long Short Term Memory (SCNN-Bi-LSTM) model. Conventional IDSs are susceptible to failure in ensuring privacy and detecting complex threats in WSNs. The FL-SCNN-Bi-LSTM model overcomes these limitations through the application of FL in collaborative training of a universal model across different sensor SDs without invading data privacy. The deep learning model is able to analyze temporal and local network behavior efficiently with a focus on detecting and resisting DoS attacks. It uses the WSN-DS and CIC-IDS-2017 datasets for this. The results of evaluation demonstrate a spectacular level of precision with a precision and recall rate of around 99.9%. This translates to a tremendous reduction in false positives and false negatives in comparison to conventional techniques such as Artificial Deep Neural Networks (ADNN). The research recognizes the prevailing challenges of ensuring real-time efficiency and scalable deployment in heterogeneous network environments despite presenting exceptional performance. Future research entails improving feature selection techniques, computing efficiency optimization, and enhancing data privacy protocols to improve the efficiency and responsiveness of the model in dynamic cybersecurity environments.

Khan et al.41 provides “Dual Trust Management System (DTMS)” mechanism for Sybil attack detection in WSNs. The primary aim is to reduce the vulnerabilities of WSNs, specifically Sybil attacks, by authenticating the location and identity of SD entities at various hierarchical levels (Cluster Member, Cluster Head, Base Station). DTMS relies on a trust evaluation mechanism encompassing communication trust and data trust. The system utilizes dynamic reward and punishment parameters to monitor the behavior of SD in depth. The mechanism uses data aggregation techniques to reduce communication inefficiency and save energy. The technique utilizes data aggregation techniques to reduce communication inefficiency and save energy. The simulation results clearly demonstrate that DTMS achieves a 100% detection rate in detecting malicious SDs even in adversarial scenarios. DTMS has been shown to outperform existing systems in various parameters, including true detection rate, false detection rate, energy consumption, packet loss, and network lifetime, according to a comparative study. Scalability problems in large-scale deployments and the potential drawback of regular trust evaluations can be limitations. On the basis of the above-researched work on WSN trust management, some of the key findings and conclusions are emphasized, along with limitations identified. The research has shown remarkable advancements in the use of ML, blockchain, and hybrid reinforcement learning (RL) towards enhancing security, energy efficiency, and network stability in most WSN applications. The advancements have shown promising outcomes in enhancing detection accuracy, minimizing energy consumption, as well as network life extension. Nevertheless, there still exist overarching challenges in large-scale deployments, including scalability, resilience to various environmental conditions, as well as compatibility with renewable energy sources. Current approaches tend to be based on simulated data, which may not capture the complexity of the real world, thus affecting their adaptability and resilience in real-world applications. Furthermore, the ability of trust management systems to adapt to dynamic attack patterns and real-time operational dynamics remains an existing challenge. To overcome these challenges, research should focus on addressing the enhancement of scalability through distributed and federated approaches, testing systems under varied environmental conditions, and integrating adaptive capacity with renewable energy sources. Additionally, it will be crucial to improve trust models to learn proactively and adapt to looming threats in real-time environments.

Proposed method

In the case of autonomous WSNs where intelligent sensors monitor a wide range of environmental and physiological parameters, integrity of data collected is of great importance. However, WSNs are always vulnerable to malicious attacks like on-off attacks and Sybil attacks that can lead to extreme interference with their operation and data consistency. As countermeasures against such challenges, efficient trust mechanisms are required to identify anomalies and facilitate normal WSN operation. In this paper, we introduce a new method that is specially designed to compute interaction-based trust among SDs based on their direct packet forwarding communication patterns. Sensor SDs establish diverse relationships with neighboring SDs, ranging from a mixture of location-based proximity, cooperative collaborations, and collaborative work dynamics. The SMART framework introduced applies these relationships to identify key trust features directly from raw sensor data, facilitating efficient model training and trust assessment. Some of the important trust features incorporated in SMART include CLR, which assesses the geographical proximity and cooperative behavior among SDs, and CWR, which assesses the degree of cooperation and collaborative working activities among SDs. CFD captures interaction frequency and duration using a binary entropy function, which informs SD cooperativeness and interaction frequency. The Reward feature captures the positive reinforcement SDs receive in WSN, promoting trustworthy behavior and improving overall network reliability. Additionally, SMART employs a dynamic trust assessment mechanism that responds to network condition changes by adjusting the weights allocated to directly trusted SDs. The weighted mechanism facilitates accurate estimation of communication trust, ranging from direct and indirect trust attributes. By addressing the limitations of traditional static trust assessment techniques, which often lead to inaccuracies and excessive computational overhead, SMART enhances efficiency and effectiveness of trust management in WSNs.

Besides, the paper suggests applying the K-means clustering algorithm to facilitate cluster labeling in WSNs. The K-means clustering algorithm maximizes data partitioning into distinct clusters to facilitate improved scalability and efficiency in data aggregation, routing optimization, and security management. Figure 2 shows the clusted architecture of WSN. The adaptive algorithm facilitates dynamic cluster assignment adjustment according to network change, allowing for high accuracy in cluster labeling and system performance improvement. After clustering, PCA is applied to allow dimension reduction of sensor data and preserve essential information. The dimension reduction technique maximizes the computational efficiency and allows effective data visualization as well as analysis. SVM algorithms are also used to enable classification and regression operations to enable proposed SMART framework to enable correct SD classification and effective anomaly detection in WSN systems. Overall, the SMART framework is an extensive framework comprising three modules: trust assessment on the basis of novel trust features, effective data acquisition and preprocessing methods, and robust clustering and classification algorithms. The formal process not only maximizes trust assessment capacity but also improves the network resistance against malicious attacks, thus ensuring the secure and stable functioning of autonomous WSNs.

Data collection and learning model

The entire framework is comprised of various independent processes. First, the dataset was subjected to rigorous collection and inspection9,10, considering the identification of its intricate data types. This was followed by a massive data preprocessing, which included steps like data cleansing, visualization, feature engineering, and vectorization. These steps played a crucial role in transforming raw data into structured feature vectors. The dataset was then split into training and testing subsets based on an 80 − 20 split30. This step was followed by the training subset being used to train a Learning Algorithm, and we built the final model through an SVM optimization process as described in32. Finally, the performance of the model built was tested using a set of evaluation metrics used on the testing subset.

Clustering and classification algorithms

The suggested framework utilizes the K-Means algorithm because of its efficiency and simplicity in clustering WSN sensor data. K-Means is an unsupervised Machine Learning algorithm, partitioning data into clusters by optimizing K centroids and mapping each point to the closest centroid. Our method, as depicted in Algorithm 1, utilizes K-Means with different K values ranging from 1 to 5 to effectively classify the WSN dataset. Algorithm 2 utilizes PCA, a data dimensionality reduction method preserving maximum variance. PCA transforms a feature matrix using two principal components: primary variance captured by the first principal component and orthogonal variance optimized by the second. The model targets four important features: CLR, CWR, and CFD. PCA starts with computing the covariance matrix ∑ and then utilizes SVD to obtain principal components U and V of the same dimensions. For dimensions reduced to two, matrix U is scaled proportionally. The two-dimensional feature vectors Z are obtained from the original four-dimensional X. The CWR feature computes SD reliability based on cooperative interactions during data transfer to improve network reliability. Similarly, CFD measures cooperativeness, interaction frequency, and duration using a binary entropy function to determine SDs most likely to transmit correct data. Algorithm 3 utilizes SVM classification, known for its efficiency in diverse data dimensions, to determine trustworthy interactions using an RBF kernel. This SVM model is trained on about 16,000 interactions, utilizing 5,776 samples for ML. Data preprocessing converts raw data into feature vectors, then divided into 80–20 training and testing sets. Each algorithmic step is precisely annotated to confirm clarity and facilitate seamless implementation. The time complexity of PCA in algorithm 2 is driven by a few key operations. First, the covariance matrix Σ is computed, which involves a dot product of the m×n data matrix, taking O(m⋅n2) time. Next, performing Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on the n×n covariance matrix has a time complexity of O(n3). Finally, the projection of the original data onto the principal components requires matrix multiplication, which takes O(m⋅n⋅d) time, where d is the reduced dimensionality (e.g., d = 2). Summing these components, the overall time complexity for PCA is O(m⋅n2 + n3 + m⋅n⋅d). The space required for PCA includes the storage of the original data matrix, which is O(m⋅n), the covariance matrix Σ requiring O(n2), the principal components matrix \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{U}_{d}$$\end{document}which takes O(n⋅d), and the projected data matrix Z, which requires O(m⋅d). The worst case time complexity of algorithm 3 (SVM classification), particularly with an RBF kernel, is especially dominated by the training process, which involves solving a quadratic optimization problem. For a dataset with m training samples and n features, this process typically has a worst-case time complexity of O(m2⋅n) per iteration. If the optimization process requires I iterations, the overall time complexity becomes O(I⋅m2⋅n). Once the model is trained, the prediction of a new sample’s label is by matching it against all support vectors, which is of time complexity O(m⋅n). The space complexity of the SVM model includes O(m⋅n) space to store the training data and O(m2) space for the kernel matrix. Thus, the total space complexity is O(m⋅n + m2).

Conclusion and future work

The paper introduces the Secure Machine-learning-based Adaptive Reliable Trust (SMART) model for unattended autonomous WSNs. SMART employs an improved ML algorithm to dynamically compute trust values by including critical metrics such as Co-Location Relationship (CLR), Co-Work Relationship (CWR), Cooperativeness-Frequency-Duration (CFD), and a unique Reward system. The metrics together form a robust framework for assessing trustworthiness of SDs, enabling SMART to effectively predict potential anomalies and secure network integrity and performance. SMART integrates direct and indirect communication trust with a logical time window to log interactions at regular intervals, separating trustworthy and suspicious behavior. Simulation results confirm SMART’s performance, with 96% malicious SD detection, 0.7% false negative rate (FNR), F1-Score of 0.75, and 96% accuracy even with 50 malicious SDs. Important contributions include enhanced trust metrics for spatial proximity, multicast collaboration, and cooperative behavior. The CLR metric considers distance decay and spatial alignment based on GPS coordinates, the CWR metric assesses multicast interactions, and the CFD metric assesses frequency, duration, and reliability of cooperative interactions over time. SMART employs a dynamic trust function based on weighted assessments of direct and indirect communication trust, employing K-means clustering for effective cluster identification, PCA for dimension reduction, and SVM for decision boundary identification. Performance measures such as accuracy, F1-score, and FNR indicate SMART’s performance superiority over existing models. Experimental results illustrate SMART’s performance superiority in trust value evolution, accuracy, malicious SD detection, and reduced FNR compared to existing approaches.

Future research includes ongoing refinement of the SMART model to render it more versatile to accommodate novel conditions of evolving networks. This includes developing more advanced ML algorithms for real-time anomaly detection and trust assessment, adding more context-based trust metrics, and fine-tuning the model for different scales of networks and deployment contexts. Further extensive simulation and field experiments will be performed to verify and enhance the robustness and reliability of SMART.

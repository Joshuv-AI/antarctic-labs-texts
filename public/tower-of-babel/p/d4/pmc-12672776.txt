A comprehensive analysis of B symptoms reveals prognosis and heterogeneity across different sites of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Chaoling Wu, Yongjun Zhang, Fan Yang, Jinyuan Xiao, Chao Chen, Min Li, Changjian Yan, Xiaoni Liu, Xiaoyu Hao, Yuqi Zhang, Wenxin Qi, Yan Liu, Jing Wang, Mingxia Zhu, Ping Yang, Xiaoliang Yuan, Zining Yu, Shixin Wu, Fang Bao, Weilong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a highly aggressive form of non-Hodgkin lymphoma. B symptoms, including fever, night sweats and weight loss, are often overlooked but carry prognostic significance. The primary site of DLBCL also plays a crucial role in diagnosis and treatment decisions, and our study aims to explore the relationship among primary sites of DLBCL, the presence of B symptoms and patients’ survival. 15,267 patients’ data were analyzed in our study to investigate the potential relevance among B symptoms, primary sites and patients’ survival. We assessed the influence of primary site and B symptoms on survival with the use of Survival package and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis. Age, race and Ann-Arbor stage were significantly associated with the presence of B symptoms. The incidence rate of B symptoms varied across primary sites, bone marrow had the highest probability (43.75%), while the genital system had the lowest rate (13.58%). B symptoms were siginificant prognostic indicators among nodal DLBCL patients especially those involved lymph nodes of multiple regions, as well as patients with lymphoma originating from bone marrow, glands and respiration. Survival analysis revealed that bone marrow, genital organs, lymph nodes of multiple regions and head and neck are linked to poorer survival outcomes, which happen to be either the highest or lowest B symptom incidence sites. These findings highlight the prognostic importance of both primary tumor site and B symptom status in DLBCL patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06579-5.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common subtype of aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), accounting for 30% to 40% of lymphoid neoplasms [1]. DLBCL is highly heterogeneous in both molecular characteristics and clinical presentation, resulting in varying responses to standard chemotherapy regimes [2]. Therefore, although regarded as a chemotherapy-sensitive disease and the 5-year survival rates ranging from 60 to 70%, up to 50% of DLBCL patients will still relapse after treatment [3]. The purpose of our study is to find the correlation between two such markers, B symptoms and the primary site of disease.

Clinically, DLBCL oftenpresents with rapidly enlarging lymphadenopathy and constitutional symptoms. Among these symptoms, B symptoms are used when referring to fever, drenching night sweats and loss of more than 10 percent of body weight over 6 months, a significant feature to the prognosis and staging of the disease [4]. The presence or absence of B symptoms and the increased plasma levels are established negative prognostic factors in patients with NHL, and C-reactive protein and cytokines including interleukin-6 are proved to be relevant with B symptoms [5, 6]. These markers, alongside performance status (like ECOG) and the International Prognostic Index (IPI), reflect tumor burden and systemic inflammatory response [5, 7–9]. Therefore, B symptoms are not only an early clinical sign of DLBCL but also integral to disease evaluation and prognosis.

Another critical determinant of disease behavior is the site of origin. The primary site of DLBCL usually refers to the initial location where the lymphoma originates. The primary sites of DLBCL are of great importance, especially the involvement of extranodal sites like central nervous system and gastrointestine. DLBCL patients were usually divided into two categories according to the disease position, nodal and extranodal [10]. Extranodal DLBCL, which accounts for approximately 40% of cases, often involves organs such as the gastrointestinal tract, central nervous system (CNS), and testine [11]. This subgroup has distinct pathogenesis, treatment challenges, and outcomes, such as CNS recurrence rates. For example, primary CNS lymphoma is highly aggressive and exhibits poor response to R-CHOP chemotherapy [12, 13], which was the first-line treatment regime after Rituximab was officially approved by FDA in 2002. Differences in B symptoms frequency across primary sites have also been observed: approximately 30% of patients with primary colonic DLBCL report B symptoms, while the rate among testicular DLBCL patients is closer to 10% [14].

Despite these observations, a systematic analysis of how primary site correlates with B symptoms and survival is lacking. Therefore, our study aimed to analyze and fill this gap by conducting a large-scale, data-driven analysis between DLBCL primary sites, presence of B symptoms and clinical outcomes. Leveraging a large cancer database, we systematically categorized DLBCL cases by anatomical site and investigated the prognostic significance of B symptoms across these subgroups, hoping it can help us better understand the heterogeneity of DLBCL and predict prognosis for different patients.

Method

DLBCL patients’ data for this study were obtained from SEER database (The Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results, https://seer.cancer.gov/). Extracted information included basic information, clinical features, primary site of lymphoma and survival outcomes. For simplification, patients identified as divorced, single, separated, or widowed were grouped into unmarried category. From an initial pool of 62,284 patients diagnosed with lymphoma in SEER database, 15,267 patients were included in our study after a structured screening (Fig. 1). Patients were excluded based on the following criteria: incomplete clinical information, missing age data (n = 4), unknown B symptom status (n = 46,125), non-DLBCL histology (n = 741), or missing surgical treatment data (n = 1). Patients were then grouped according to the primary site, including 11 categories in total, excluding patients whose primary site could not be classified into predefined anatomical categories (n = 136). Moreover, primary thymus DLBCL patients are eventually excluded from our analysis, for the number is too small to be persuasive statistically (n = 10). Patient’s primary sites are categorized primarily by anatomical location or organ system. Except for the lymph nodes, Waldeyer’s ring, tonsils, thymus and spleen are also recognized as nodal or lymphatic sites [15, 16]. However, since Waldeyer’s ring specifically refers to the lymphatic tissue around pharynx, we believe areas like “Lateral wall of oropharynx” are not lymphoid organs, belonging to respiratory system [17]. During our analysis, we rigorously comply with the International Conference and the Declaration of Helsinki, also the Human Research Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital approved the study.Fig. 1Standardized clinical data flowchart of DLBCL patients. Baseline characteristics, including demographic and clinical features, were summarized using the tableone package in R version 4.3.0. A separate analysis was conducted for nodal DLBCL patients. The percentage incidence of B symptoms in different primary site categories were calculated and visualized with histogram. Survival analysis was conducted using the survival and survminer packages in R. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated to assess differences in overall survival based on B symptom presence across all patients, primary site groups, and lymph node subgroups. In the Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, the log-rank test was used, and P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

Results

Baseline characteristics of DLBCL patients with B symptoms

Among the 15,267 included patients, 11,078 (72.56%) had primary nodal DLBCL, while 4,189 (27.44%) had extranodal disease. The most common extranodal sites were the gastrointestinal and respiratory systems (Table S1). Among different primary sites of DLBCL, the incidence rate of B symptoms varied. It has been shown that the highest probability is bone marrow type (43.75%), while the lowest probability is in genital system (13.58%) (Fig. 2). Higher B symptom frequencies were also observed in DLBCLs originating from lymph nodes, digestive organs, and the urinary system. There were 1,704 (35.18%) patients aged ≤ 60 years and 3,063 (29.38%) patients aged > 60 years (the elderly) who appeared B symptoms (Fig. 3A). In our database, there were 14% patients who did not accept chemotherapy, which might because their lesions were accidentally found. After diagnostical resection, patients are diagnosed with the lowest stage of DLBCL, and the lesion has been completely removed without the need of chemotherapy. Also, in some low-income areas, patients who were diagnosed with DLBCL might not afford chemotherapy. Moreover, some elder patients who were afraid of the side effects of chemotherapy drugs would rather give up undergoing chemotherapy and might try other treatment therapy [18, 19].

Survival analysis based on the presence of B symptoms in different DLBCL primary sites groups

We performed a survival analysis to estimate the independent effect of B symptoms on all patients and patients with different primary sites, with a total duration of 120 months (Fig. S1). It is quite obvious that patients with B symptoms have a poorer prognosis compared with those without B symptoms. Also, the median and range of the observation period are included in Table S4. Our results showed that patients with B symptoms from lymph nodes (P < 0.0001), bone marrow (P < 0.05), glands (P < 0.05), genital (P < 0.05) have lower survival rate than patients without B symptoms (Fig. 4A-E). However, no significant differences were observed in the head and neck (P = 0.215), urinary system(P = 0.296), digestive system (P = 0.454), and other primary sites (P > 0.5) (Fig. 4F). Furthermore, we also investigate the effect of other factors on patients’ survival status, and univariable Cox regression analysis in all DLBCL patients are included in Supplementary Table S5, and the results of multivariate analysis and cox proportional hazards models are shown in Fig. S2. As we expected, age > 60, B symptoms and Ann Arbor stage have high hazard ratio, which means poor prognosis. Primary sites like urinary system, genital system and respiratory system also present high hazard ratio. Presence rate of B symptoms in different lymph node site groups and its influence on patients’ survival status

DLBCL patients who primarily involve lymph nodes may have a poorer prognosis if they present with B symptoms. Among patients with primary nodal DLBCL, B symptom incidence varied substantially by lymph node location. The spleen had the highest rate (38.46%), while head, face, and neck lymph nodes had the lowest (15.38%) (Fig. 5). Similarly, we further performed survival analysis of B symptoms in different primary lymph node sites, and found that patients with B symptoms in lymph nodes of multiple regions (P < 0.001) and lymph nodes of head face and neck (P < 0.001) had a worse prognosis (Fig. 6A, B). No significant prognostic differences were found for B symptoms in the remaining lymph node subgroups, including pelvic, inguinal region or leg, spleen, intrathoracic lymph nodes, intra-abdominal lymph nodes, lymph nodes not otherwise specified (NOS) and lymph nodes of axilla or arm, there was no significant relationship between survival and B symptoms incidence rate (Fig. 6C). Moreover, univariable Cox regression analysis in nodal DLBCL patients is performed (Table S6), and the results of multivariate analysis and cox proportional hazards models are shown in Fig. S3. As we expected, age > 60, B symptoms and Ann Arbor stage present high hazard ratio, which means poor prognosis. Discussion

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a fast-growing and highly aggressive malignancy. Due to its high heterogeneity and aggressiveness, 30–50% of patients relapse or even die after treatment. The choice of clinical treatment can only be implemented on the premise of confirming the pathological type, clinical stage and prognosis assessment [20, 21]. B symptoms—unexplained fever, night sweats, and significant weight loss—are among the key features used to assess patient status and guide prognosis [22].

In this study, we analyzed data from 15,267 DLBCL patients in the SEER database and used Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to evaluate the relationships between B symptoms, clinical characteristics, primary tumor sites, and survival outcomes. Our results demonstrate that the presence of B symptoms is significantly associated with age, sex, race, Ann Arbor stage, and treatment methods. In particular, DLBCL patients older than 60 years were more likely to present with B symptoms. In earlier studies, patients’ age has been proved to be a significant prognostic factor for lymphoma [23, 24]. Older patients often have more comorbidities, reduced performance status, and increased treatment-related toxicity, which may contribute to poorer outcomes [25]. Just as we assumed, B symptoms correlated with disease severity. In our study, Ann Arbor stage is a prognostic factor strongly associated with B symptoms rate on DLBCL patients, meaning that the appearance of B symptoms indicates the later stage of DLBCL and greater tumor burden [26]. We also found racial disparities in B symptom presentation, with Black patients more likely to report B symptoms—aligning with previous findings of worse outcomes in this group [27]. Notably, all these variables—age, stage, and race—are routinely collected in clinical settings and do not require invasive procedures, making them valuable for early risk stratification.

The occurrence of B symptoms in DLBCL patients is quite relevant to the primary site (Table S7). Our results showed that patients with multi-regional lymph node involvement and lymph node NOS were more likely to develop B symptoms, followed by involving the abdominal and intrathoracic (Fig. 7). Consistent with the incidence rate of B symptoms in our results, a study based on the SEER database showed that of 1613 patients with primary DLBCL of the small intestine and colon, 27.7% patients presented with B symptoms [14], indicating our study is quite representative. A possible explanation for this statement is that lymphoma at gastrointestinal tract will directly influence nutrient absorption, which might lead to weight loss. Moreover, considering that the gastrointestinal tract has abundant immune cells and well-developed mucosal immune function, colon DLBCL patients might present high inflammatory factor level and are more likely to have B symptoms. We also found that patients with DLBCL originated from the genital area were less likely to develop B symptoms, which is similar to previous studies based on the prognostic factors in primary DLBCL of the testis, showing that the presence rate of B symptoms is quite low (9.0%) [28–30], even lower than the rate in the present study. Testis, as an immune-privileged site necessary for the tolerance of neo-antigens from germ cells, might not have strong immune response against pathogens and tumor cells, which means relatively low inflammation-associated cytokines and low B symptoms presence rate [31]. Although the presence rate is quite low, the prognosis of patients with B symptoms were much worse, both in previous studies and our results. The literature shows variation in the reported incidence of B symptoms among patients with primary bone DLBCL, and our findings align with those studies reporting higher rates, which indicates that the presence rate is not definite and more factors should be taken into consideration [32, 33].Fig. 7Induction of the primary sites relative with presence rate of B symptoms and its prognostic influence

Interestingly, B symptoms did not universally predict poor survival across all primary sites. They were significantly associated with worse outcomes only in patients with DLBCL originating from the lymph nodes, bone marrow, genital system, glands, and respiratory system. Within nodal cases, only patients with multi-regional involvement or lymph nodes of the head and neck showed a survival difference based on B symptom status. We speculate the primary sites of DLBCL influenced by B symptoms, except for lymph nodes, are the positions where immune responses imply servere disease status. An explanation for patients who had bone marrow originated DLBCL presented worse prognosis with B symptoms is that lymphoma might have disrupted the bone marrow lymphoid niche [34]. The more devastated the lymphoma is, the more unstable the bone marrow lymphoid niche is, which may result in the presence of B symptoms. The primary sites of DLBCL patients may influence patients’ manifestations, also have inner mechanisms. Also, some primary sites, including the genital system and some glands, are immune-privileged sites in lymphoma [35], which means if patients present poor clinical performance they are already in advanced stage of disease. According to our study, we can indicate that B symptoms are more likely to be a prognostic factor when the primary sites are the most and the least B symptoms incidence rate. In this case, B symptoms are affected by multiple factors in patients with DLBCL, and the prognosis varies according to the primary site of B symptoms.

The novelty of our investigation mainly lies in the massive amounts of patients’ data. Also, in data collation stage, we systematically summarize primary sites, which has not been done in previous study. Moreover, though the topic of B symptoms has always been mentioned, its prognostic significance in different DLBCL primary sites is discussed in our study for the first time.

However, there were some deficiencies in our research that can be further improved in later studies. Above all, firstly, the statistic analysis in our study is based on the database instead of our clinical work. The database is large and comprehensive, but we could not neglect the retrospective nature of SEER database. Secondly, the database lacks some relevant information, like inflammatory markers and the description of each B symptoms, which might bring more insight into the analysis of correlation between B symptoms and primary site position. Thirdly, there is no definite explanation for why B symptoms indicate poor prognosis when patients have different primary DLBCL sites, which can be further investigated through biopsy and molecular characters. Hopefully, these shortcomings can be overcome in the future by more clinical researches and prospective experiments.

Conclusion

In the present study, we demonstrated that the presence of B symptoms is associated with primary sites of DLBCL, also indicating patients’ survival time. Our analysis of nodal DLBCL patients also suggested different treatment outcomes among various lymph nodes. The results shown in our study emphasize that DLBCL is highly heterogeneous, and the position of primary sites and the presence of B symptoms should be taken into account.

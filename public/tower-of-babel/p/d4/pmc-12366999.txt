TikTok and Orthopaedic Education: Engaging the Next Generation of Patients
Authors: Camila Vicioso, Charu Jain, Uma Balachandran, Ryan Smolarsky, Laurel Wong, Luca Valdivia, Julian Javier, Auston Locke, James Hong, Sheena C. Ranade
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Social media is increasingly pivotal in healthcare communication, with TikTok emerging as a leading platform because of its visually engaging, short-format videos. With nearly 2 billion users spending over 50 minutes daily on the app, TikTok offers a novel medium for disseminating orthopaedic information. Pediatric fractures—employed here as a representative model—are particularly relevant given the high social media use among youth and caregivers. This study investigates TikTok's role as an educational tool for orthopaedic surgeons and examines public engagement.Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted analyzing TikTok videos related to pediatric fractures. A newly created TikTok account was used to identify the top popular videos for each fracture type, excluding non-English, off-topic, private, or duplicate content. Engagement metrics, including views, likes, shares, comments, and bookmarks, were recorded. Video understandability was assessed using the Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool for Audiovisual Materials. Video reliability was assessed using the modified DISCERN scale. Videos were categorized by creator identity (physician, allied healthcare provider, patient, parent, and other).Results:A total of 190 TikTok videos accumulated more than 25.7 million views and 1.8 million likes. Although nonexpert sources (parents and patients) contributed 87.9% of the content, healthcare professional–generated videos comprised 12.1% and demonstrated markedly higher engagement and quality scores. In particular, physician-produced content achieved the highest median views and shares (P < 0.01).Discussion:The predominance of nonexpert content underscores a missed opportunity to leverage TikTok for disseminating reliable, evidence-based orthopaedic education. Expert-driven videos not only offer superior clarity, reliability, and actionable guidance but also align with the public's preference, suggesting broad applicability across orthopaedic subspecialties.Conclusion:TikTok represents a promising platform for enhancing orthopaedic education. Increasing healthcare professional engagement may improve the delivery of accurate, evidence-based content, ultimately advancing musculoskeletal health literacy and patient outcomes.

TikTok, with close to 2 billion users who spend an average of 52 minutes per day on the platform, has revolutionized health communication through its short, visually engaging video format.1,2 An estimated 40% of healthcare consumers reference social media for their healthcare information needs, with even higher rates among younger demographics.3-5 In particular, individuals age younger than 35 years spend nearly 4 hours per day on social media, underscoring its pervasive role in modern communication, particularly in young populations.6 This extensive engagement enables patients to exchange real‐time information with a broad audience that includes not only friends and family but also others recovering from similar injuries as well as receive information from orthopaedic professionals providing content online.7 The rapid growth of digital communication has prompted healthcare providers (HCPs) to increasingly adopt social media as a tool to engage and educate patients, a trend that accelerated in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic.8

Building on these widespread trends in digital communication and patient engagement, the orthopaedic community has started to harness social media's potential to educate and connect with its stakeholders. A recent systematic review published in JBJS Open Access indicated that up to 73% of orthopaedic-related stakeholders—including patients, surgeons, and training programs—are active on social media, emphasizing a digital shift that opens a window for future public education on orthopaedic injuries and conditions.9 The same study found a 620% increase in the number of orthopaedic training programs with a social media account after the pandemic, supporting the growth of social media usage within the orthopaedic community.9

TikTok's proprietary algorithm, which curates personalized and timely video feeds based on user engagement, makes it an ideal tool for rapidly disseminating information through social media, although its use remains underexplored.10 Nonetheless, TikTok has been shown to promote education in health care by showcasing real-life examples from the field to the public and garnered the farthest reach compared with other social media platforms.11 TikTok also facilitates interaction through features such as commenting, sharing, liking, and bookmarking, enabling users to discuss and engage with content. Furthermore, the widespread adoption of hashtags has facilitated a novel means of aggregating and analyzing health-related data.12

Although previous studies have examined the representation of orthopaedic conditions on social media, research on TikTok's role in musculoskeletal education, particularly for pediatric patients, remains limited.12,13 Furthermore, despite its potential, orthopaedic content on TikTok remains both underrepresented and, in many cases, lacking the scientific rigor required for effective patient guidance. Kolade et al13 found that many orthopaedic-related posts on TikTok lacked medical accuracy, with most posts created by nonphysicians. Orthopaedic HCPs are uniquely positioned to combat misinformation by using their platforms to disseminate unbiased, scientifically accurate, and evidence-based materials supporting musculoskeletal health.

Pediatric orthopaedics represents a particularly compelling domain for this investigation. Children, adolescents, and their parents—demographics central to pediatric fracture care—are among the most active users of social media.14 Consequently, pediatric fractures provide an informative lens through which to examine how digital video-based platforms, like TikTok, might be harnessed to educate and influence patients' perceptions regarding injury care. Importantly, although our study focuses on pediatric fractures, the insights gained have broader implications for the entire field of orthopaedics. As orthopaedic surgeons seek new avenues to reach patients and counteract misinformation, understanding how video content performs on platforms like TikTok is critical.

Methods

Search Strategy

Ten pediatric orthopaedic injuries were selected from the Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America's OrthoKids “I Broke My…” fracture list.15 On October 10, 2024, TikTok searches were performed using a newly created account (to minimize algorithmic bias) with search terms including “Pediatric Ankle Fracture,” “Pediatric Clavicle Fracture,” “Pediatric Elbow Fracture,” “Pediatric Femoral Shaft Fracture,” “Pediatric Foot Fracture,” “Pediatric Forearm Fracture,” “Pediatric Growth Plate Fracture,” “Pediatric Pelvic Avulsion Fracture,” “Pediatric Proximal Humerus Fracture,” and “Pediatric Tibial Shaft Fracture.” For each term, the top 20 most popular videos were reviewed (sorted by relevance). Videos that were non-English, off-topic, from private accounts, or duplicate content were excluded.

Engagement Metrics

Engagement metrics—including likes, comments, views, bookmarks, shares, and upload dates—were recorded. Additional metrics (overall account score, engagement score, growth score, and viral score) were obtained using the TikTok Score Calculator.16 Three independent raters collected data across two to four search terms each.

Quality Assessment

A separate rater assessed each video using the Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool for Audiovisual Materials (PEMAT), a validated tool measuring video actionability (ie, how easily users can understand and implement recommended actions) and understandability.17,18 The PEMAT includes 17 criteria evaluating content clarity, complexity, voice, medical terminology, chronological structure, visual cues, text legibility, image clarity, actionable guidance, and use of diagrams.

Two additional raters evaluated all videos using the modified five-point DISCERN (mDISCERN) scale, a validated tool for assessing video quality.18,19 The mDISCERN evaluates clarity of video objectives, reliability of sources, balance and absence of bias, inclusion of supplementary resources, and avoidance of uncertainty. Any discrepancies in ratings were resolved by an independent adjudicator. Videos were scored as 1 (meets criteria), 0 (does not meet criteria), or NA (not applicable) for all PEMAT and mDISCERN criteria, with final scores reported as percentages.

Content Analysis

Videos were categorized by creator identity and content type. Creator identity included physician, allied HCP, patient, parent, and other (eg, influencer, business/organization, layperson, anonymous account). For HCPs, specialty and degree were documented. Videos were further classified by domain (personal experience, promotional, educational, patient experience, or entertainment) and content focus (activity, cast, imaging, injury, recovery, or surgery).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using RStudio (version 2023.12.1 + 402, Posit Software, Public Benefit Corporation). A two-sample Student t-test was used to assess notable differences between groups. Medians with interquartile ranges and P-values were reported to compare and visualize data distribution.

Results

A total of 190 TikTok videos on pediatric fractures met the inclusion criteria, with upload dates spanning from April 23, 2020, to October 6, 2024. Most the videos were posted by parents (72.1%, n = 137). Physicians and patients each contributed 12.1% (n = 23 each), whereas allied HCPs (2.1%, n = 4) and other creators (including influencers, businesses, and anonymous accounts; 1.6%, n = 3) made up the remainder. Collectively, these videos accumulated over 25.7 million views, 1.79 million likes, 48,444 bookmarks, 20,579 shares, and 16,463 comments (Table 1). Analysis using the TikTok Score Calculator revealed that the usernames associated with these videos had high viral scores (mean = 95.4%) and engagement scores (mean = 88.0%) (Table 1). Overall popularity scores, which combine engagement, viral, and growth metrics, averaged 71.2%, with moderate growth observed (mean growth score = 24.0%).

Healthcare Provider Videos

Over time, the number of video uploads per year increased steadily—from just three in 2020 to 69 in 2024—with a corresponding increase in uploads from HCPs (from 1 in 2020 to 10 in 2024) (Figure 2). Notably, videos from HCPs demonstrated significantly higher growth scores compared with non-healthcare content (mean growth score of 42.2 versus 21.4; t = 2.87, P = 0.0074). Videos posted by HCPs demonstrated exponential growth in engagement over time, with the total number of likes increasing from 149 in 2020 to 78,376 in 2024 (Figure 3).

HCP videos accounted for a total of 2.7 million views and 137,194 likes (Table 2). Within this group, physician-generated content—capturing those specializing in orthopaedic surgery, emergency medicine, pediatrics, and podiatry—received the highest median number of views (median = 10,700) and shares compared with other groups (median = 19, P = 0.008) (Table 3). Physician videos also had the highest median overall account scores (median = 79). Most healthcare professionals had an MD (n = 19), followed by doctor of podiatric medicine (n = 3), physician assistant (n = 2), nurse practitioner (n = 1), and doctor of osteopathic medicine (n = 1). The majority were affiliated with hospitals (n = 16) or medical practices (n = 8).

Discussion

TikTok's rapid rise as a social media platform offers orthopaedic HCPs an unprecedented opportunity to engage with diverse audiences and disseminate medical knowledge in innovative ways.20 Our study highlights the notable reach of pediatric fracture-related content, demonstrating the platform's potential to enhance musculoskeletal health literacy among young patients and their caregivers. However, this potential is counterbalanced by several challenges. The predominance of parent-generated content, often lacking scientific rigor, emphasizes the urgent need for healthcare professionals to take an active role in shaping online narratives.

Although previous studies have examined other social media platforms such as Instagram and Facebook, research specifically focusing on TikTok remains limited—especially in the context of pediatric injuries, a population that is highly active on and strongly influenced by social media.9,21,22 Our findings demonstrate that the 190 most relevant videos on pediatric fracture-related content amassed 25.7 million views, indicating that social media platforms like TikTok can markedly influence patient and provider perceptions regarding pediatric fracture risks and treatments. As the popularity of pediatric orthopaedic-related videos continues to rise, it is crucial to understand the metrics, engagement, and creator demographics behind this content to ensure the delivery of evidence-based information to both patients and the general public.22

Our analysis of pediatric fracture-related TikTok videos revealed that top-performing content garnered millions of views and likes, highlighting the widespread demand for healthcare information on the platform. These substantial viewership and engagement metrics are consistent with broader healthcare trends, where social media is increasingly becoming a primary source of information for diverse patient populations. For instance, Hasan et al23 noted that TikTok videos related to terms like “#lowerbackpain” garnered millions of views, showcasing the platform's potential for both education and misinformation. Similarly, our study suggests that TikTok is an effective platform for amplifying healthcare-related content, as demonstrated by the high engagement scores for fracture-related videos. Videos covering lower extremity fractures were particularly popular, potentially reflecting the higher incidence of these injuries in pediatric populations.24

The prevalence of parent-generated content in our study raises concerns about the reliability and accuracy of the information being shared. Most videos were created by parents, who often offered advice or updates on their children's fractures and hospital experiences. Although such content is relatable and authentic, it frequently lacks scientific backing, which is essential for providing evidence-based insights. This may lead to misinformation or unrealistic expectations among fellow parents, especially when the shared experiences are not supported by medical evidence. Videos created by patients, parents, and other creators showed markedly lower reliability and actionability scores, with patients and parents demonstrating the lowest scores across all categories (compared with allied HCPs and physicians).

Healthcare Professionals as Content Creators

Physicians, although contributing fewer videos than parents, received the highest engagement scores in terms of views and shares, indicating that TikTok users value expert-driven content. These trends are further illustrated in Figure 5, which normalizes engagement metrics across creator types. Physician-generated content achieved the highest overall performance in views, shares, and account scores. Interestingly, patient and parent videos also exhibited high account scores, reflecting the influence of relatable, narrative-driven content despite lower informational quality. HCPs demonstrated effective audience engagement, with exponential growth in likes and views for their videos over time. However, despite this success, healthcare professionals remain underrepresented as content creators, with only 12.1% of the total videos attributed to physicians. This discrepancy highlights a missed opportunity for healthcare professionals to establish themselves as authoritative voices in pediatric orthopaedics on TikTok.

To maximize TikTok's utility, orthopaedic providers must overcome barriers to consistent content creation, such as time constraints and limited familiarity with the platform's algorithm. Collaborating with content-savvy younger professionals or influencers could help providers enhance visibility while ensuring accuracy.25 In addition, improving the clarity of educational content is essential to support actionable patient education. Strategies like embedding clear calls to action, linking external resources, or including tips for navigating recovery could make videos more practical for viewers. Addressing these gaps may markedly increase the effect of HCP content on TikTok, particularly in healthcare and educational contexts.

Differences in Video Quality by Creator Type

The analysis of video quality—measured by reliability, actionability (ie, clarity of recommended actions or takeaways), and understandability—revealed notable differences between content creators. Allied HCPs and physicians consistently produced videos with higher reliability, actionability, and understandability compared with other groups, such as patients, parents, and other creators (Table 5). Allied HCPs led in reliability and actionability, whereas physicians excelled in understandability. These findings underscore the expertise of healthcare professionals in delivering clear, actionable, and reliable information.

Despite this, low actionability scores across videos highlight a key limitation of TikTok as an educational platform. The short video format often constrains the delivery of detailed guidance, leaving viewers with a superficial understanding of complex topics like medical terminology and fracture healing. To address this limitation, HCPs should focus on crafting concise yet impactful content that effectively conveys essential information within TikTok's time constraints.

Educational Content and User Engagement

Educational videos, predominantly created by healthcare professionals, were more frequently shared than other types of content, underscoring their value among TikTok users. However, content created by patients—often featuring firsthand experiences and “before and after” visuals—also garnered high engagement in terms of likes and views. This suggests that although users appreciate information from healthcare professionals, they also seek relatable narratives, such as those shared by parents and children about their experiences with fractures and treatment.

The diversity of content creators, spanning different professional degrees and affiliations, reflects a growing interest in using social media to disseminate medical information. For healthcare professionals to maximize TikTok's potential as an educational tool, adopting a strategic approach to content creation is essential. Frequent posting, leveraging relevant hashtags, collaborating with younger creators, and incorporating trending sounds can boost visibility and engagement.26,27 Beyond visibility, prioritizing the quality and actionability of content is critical. Actionability of content may be improved by clearly outlining specific, feasible steps the user can take. Concise, visually engaging, evidence-based videos with integrated calls to action—such as links to credible resources, prompts for seeking professional care, or at-home recovery tips—are examples that can enhance the practical utility of HCP-generated videos.

Patient Content

The popularity of patient-created content presents both opportunities and risks. On one hand, such videos humanize medical procedures and provide practical insights into recovery and outcomes, making them relatable for viewers. On the other hand, these videos may inadvertently promote inaccurate information or unrealistic expectations when presented without proper medical context.28 Previous studies, such as those by Kolade et al,13 highlight that nonphysicians frequently post content on platforms like Instagram and TikTok about common orthopaedic conditions, including Achilles tendon tears, anterior cruciate ligament injuries, and meniscus tears, with a notable portion being medically inaccurate. This is particularly concerning in pediatric orthopaedics because many parents turn to TikTok for information about their children's fractures and recovery. These findings underscore the critical need for accurate, reliable content in this domain.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Its observational design and reliance on TikTok's evolving algorithm for video selection introduce biases, as trends and content visibility shift over time. In addition, the subjectivity inherent in tools like mDISCERN and PEMAT, although validated, may influence assessments of video quality and understandability. Future research should examine longitudinal trends in TikTok content, focusing on the impact of professional interventions to improve content quality and visibility. Furthermore, evaluating user perceptions of healthcare content and investigating the effectiveness of TikTok videos in influencing health behaviors and outcomes could offer valuable insights for leveraging social media in patient education.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that TikTok offers a powerful platform for disseminating orthopaedic education, as evidenced by the notable engagement with pediatric fracture-related videos. Our analysis reveals that although HCP-generated content represents a small fraction of total posts, it consistently exhibits superior clarity, reliability, and actionability compared with nonexpert sources. These findings highlight a critical opportunity for orthopaedic surgeons across all subspecialties to enhance digital outreach and counteract misinformation by actively participating in social media.

Using pediatric fractures as a lens, our results indicate that expert-driven content not only achieves higher engagement but also sets a standard for delivering evidence-based, actionable information to patients and caregivers. As digital communication becomes increasingly central to health care, orthopaedic surgeons have a unique responsibility—and opportunity—to shape public perceptions, promote bone health, and improve injury care through platforms like TikTok. Encouraging a strategic approach to content creation, including collaborations with digital-savvy professionals and the adoption of innovative communication techniques, may further amplify the impact of expert-generated material.

In an era where social media plays a pivotal role in health information dissemination, this study underscores the broader relevance of our findings to the entire orthopaedic community. By leveraging the potential of platforms like TikTok, orthopaedic surgeons can transform patient education, ensure the delivery of accurate and reliable information, and ultimately contribute to improved patient outcomes.

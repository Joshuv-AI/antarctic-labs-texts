Pholiota pakistanica sp. nov. in subgenus Flammuloides from Pakistan
Authors: Arooj Naseer, Ammara Khalid, Rubab Khurshid, Francis Q Brearley, Abdul Nasir Khalid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPholiota pakistanica, a distinct species, is described from Khanspur, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, on the basis of morpho-anatomical characters as well as molecular phylogenetic analyses. The proposed new species is characterized by its small to medium sized pileus with a distinct umbo, short stipe with a bulbous end, small (4.5–5.4 × 2.4–3.7 μm) basidiospores, dichotomously branched and intermixed hyphae in the lamellar trama. Sequences from the nrITS and nrLSU regions confirm that P. pakistanica is distinct species from the other species of Pholiota. Based on combined morpho-anatomical and molecular evidence, Pholiota pakistanica is described here as a novel species.Keywords: Flammuloides, Himalayan temperate forest, Phylogenetics, Saprotropihic fungi, Systematics

Introduction

The genus Pholiota (Fr.) P. Kumm., belongs to the family Strophariaceae, and comprises more than 380 wood-decaying macrofungi distributed worldwide, mainly found in temperate regions [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. These fungi thrive on wood, sawdust, humus, soil, and occasionally Sphagnum beds or charcoal [1, 9]. Most species are saprotrophic, decomposing organic matter, while some exhibit parasitism [10]. Moreover, Pholiota species can produce bioactive compounds (antioxidant and antitumor properties) and secondary metabolites (triterpenoids) [3, 11, 12]. Some species, such as P. microspora, are edible [13], whereas others, like P. squarrosa, are toxic [14].

The taxonomy of Pholiota has undergone extensive revisions, with earlier classifications recognizing up to seven subgenera [1, 9, 10, 15, 16, 17]. However, recent phylogenetic analyses suggest two primary subgenera: Pholiota and Flammuloides [18]. Subgenus Pholiota is characterized by a yellow to brown pileus that is often viscid to slimy and covered with well-developed scales, a persistent partial veil typically forming an annulus or annular zone, and smooth, pale to dark brown basidiospores with a distinct germ pore. The lamellae are usually adnate to adnexed, and cystidia (especially cheilocystidia) are often abundant and diagnostic [1, 5, 19, 20]. In contrast, subgenus Flammuloides is distinguished by a viscid to glutinous pileus that is fibrillose to squamulose rather than strongly scaly, a less conspicuous or evanescent veil, and the presence of a gelatinized subhymenium. Additionally, species in this subgenus often exhibit more slender basidiomata, less robust annular structures, and differences in cystidial morphology and pileipellis structure, typically with a more pronounced gelatinization of hyphal elements Both subgenera share brown-spored basidiomata with a distinct germ pore, well-developed cystidia, and similar lamellar attachment, reflecting their close taxonomic relationship within Pholiota [1, 5, 10, 18, 19]. Environmental factors (humidity, temperature, vegetation) influence morphological variations in the genus, notably in cystidial wall thickness, coloration, and incrustation [1, 18, 21].

A total of 380 species of genus Pholiota have been described from the world to date (https://www.speciesfungorum.org/names; access on March 2026). According to the previous studies, 12 species of Pholiota were reported from Pakistan [22, 23]. Ahmad et al. [22] described seven species i.e. Pholiota aurivella (Batsch) P. Kumm., P. destruens (Brond.) Gillet, P. lubrica (Pers.) Singer, P. lucifera (Lasch.) Quel., P. spumosa (Fr.) Singer and P. squarrosa (Vahl) P. Kumm. P. lenta (Pers.) Singer and P. carbonaria (Fr.) Singer were reported in 2014 [23] and Sultana et al. [24] added P. tuberculosa (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. Since the advent of molecular techniques, additional taxa of Pholiota have been reported from Pakistan, including the newly described species Pholiota malakandensis Z. Ullah, Jabeen, H. Bashir & Khalid and Pholiota swatica A.K. Muhammad & I. Ahmad, while one species P. gummosa (Lasch) Singer has been reported as a new record for the country [19, 25, 26].

During field exploration for macrofungi from Khanspur, fruiting bodies were collected that appeared taxonomically distinct. In the present study, nuclear ribosomal ITS and LSU sequences, along with detailed morpho-anatomical characteristics, confirmed that our collected specimens represent a new species of Pholiota belonging to subgenus Flammuloides.

Materials and methods

Study area

Khanspur, situated within the Hazara division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, lies south of the Himalayan range, forming part of the Galyat region near Ayubia National Park. The region exhibits a typical moist temperate climate, supporting an extensive coniferous forest ecosystem spanning elevations between approximately 1,000 and 3,000 m above sea level. Dominant tree species include Abies pindrow Royle, Cedrus deodara (Roxb.) G. Don, and Pinus wallichiana A.B. Jacks [27]. The region’s floristic diversity supports the conservation of montane biodiversity, providing habitat for numerous endemic flora and fauna, further reinforcing its ecological importance [27, 28].

Fungal collection and morphological characterization

Basidiomata were collected from the collection sites during the rainy seasons of 2023–2025 from the soil covered with leaf litter. In situ, photographs of the basidiocarps were taken using Nikon D70S and D300S camera. The morphological and anatomical characterization of mushroom specimens followed established protocols [29], using standard mycological terminology examining pileus shape, size, texture, lamellae attachment, and stipe features, with color determined using the Munsell Soil Color Chart [30]. Microscopic analysis involved staining reagents including rehydration in 5% KOH and staining with 1% Congo Red to observe basidiospores, basidia, cystidia, and hyphal structures using a compound microscope at 10X, 40X and 100X (MX3400H, Meiji Techno Co., Ltd., Japan). Measurements were recorded using Scope Image 9 (v.9.3.7.651) software, with standard abbreviations (“n/b/p”) representing number of basidiospores, basidiomata, and collections. Basidiospore dimensions were calculated using (a–)b – c(–d) × (a–)b – c(–d), where a is the minimum value, b – c represents 90% of observed data, and d is the maximum value.

DNA extraction, PCR amplification and phylogenetic analyses

Fungal DNA extraction followed a modified CTAB method based on Gardes and Bruns [31], ensuring effective cell lysis and purification. PCR amplification was performed for the Internal Transcribed Spacer (ITS) and the 28 S region of the Large Subunit (LSU) regions by using primer pairs ITS4/ITS5 [32], for ITS; LR0R/LR5 [33], for LSU. PCR was performed using a 10 µL reaction volume composed of 5 µL Biomed 2× Taq Plus PCR MasterMix (Biomed, Beijing, China), 3.9 µL ddH2O, 0.3 µL of each primer (10 µmol/L), and 0.5 µLDNA template. For the ITS region, the PCR protocol included an initial denaturation at 94 °C for 4 min, followed by 40 cycles of 94 °C for 1 min, 55 °C for 1 min, and 72 °C for 1 min, with a final extension step at 72 °C for 10 min. The 28 S region amplification involved an initial denaturation at 94 °C for 2 min, followed by 35 cycles of 94 °C for 1 min, 52 °C for 1 min, and 72 °C for 1 min, concluding with a final extension at 72 °C for 7 min. PCR product visualization was performed using a 1% agarose gel containing 2 µl ethidium bromide, observed under a UV illuminator. Sanger sequencing of amplified fragments was outsourced, and raw sequence data were processed using BioEdit version 7.2.5 [34]. Generated sequences were deposited in GenBank.

The selection of sequences for the phylogenetic analyses were based on the results of BLAST and from recent articles [35, 36, 49]. The obtained sequences were aligned with sequences retrieved from GenBank using MAFFT (online server) with automatic algorithm selection [37]. First, phylogenetic trees were constructed separately for each region and their congruence were checked. For Bayesian inference (BI), the model of evolution was estimated by MrModeltest 2.2 [38]. The Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling in MrBayes v.3.2.2 [39] was used to determine the posterior probabilities (PP). Six simultaneous Markov chains were run for 1,000,000 generations, and trees were every 1,000th generation. The first 25% of the trees were discarded as burn-ins. The remainder was used to calculate the posterior probabilities (PPs) for individual branches. Maximum likelihood (ML) analyses were performed using RAxML-HPC2 using XSEDE v. 8.2.12 with the GTRGAMMA substitution model and 1,000 bootstrap replicates [40]. All analyses were conducted in the CIPRES Science Gate-way (https://www.phylo.org/portal2/login!input.action)(Millerhttps://www.phylo.org/portal2/login!input.action) [41]. The phylogenetic tree was visualized in FigTree v.1.4.4 [42].

Discussion

The newly described species Pholiota pakistanica is characterized by its small to medium sized pileus with a distinct umbo, stipe comparatively short and exhibits a bulbous base. Anatomically, characterized by small (4.5–5.4 × 2.4–3.7 μm) basidiospores, thick-walled, narrowly to broadly lageniform cheilocystidia with a mucous cap at the apex, and thick-walled and bifid to narrowly lageniform (47–75 × 10.1–11.6 μm) pleurocystidia.

In the nrITS and nrLSU phylogenies, Pholiota pakistanica forms a well-supported, distinct sister clade to Pholiota brunnescens (EF22, TFB5897), and P. luteobadia (MICH 11688, AHS43222) which is currently treated as conspecific with P. brunnescens following the revision by Matheny et al. [3]. Morphologically, these taxa are similar in overall basidiomata appearance, particularly in pileus shape, margin, and coloration, as well as general stipe coloration.

However, P. pakistanica is clearly distinguished by its smaller basidiomata, with a pileus up to 27 mm in diameter and a shorter stipe (up to 35 mm) that is cylindrical with a bulbous base, whereas P. brunnescens (= P. luteobadia) typically exhibits a broader pileus (up to 70 mm) and a longer, more or less equal stipe reaching up to 90 mm [3]. In addition, P. pakistanica lacks the vinaceous tones often reported in P. luteobadia and does not develop a well-defined annular zone.

Microscopically, P. pakistanica differs in having smaller basidiospores (4.6–5.3 × 2.5–3.5 μm) lacking a germ pore, whereas P. brunnescens (= P. luteobadia) possesses larger basidiospores (6.0–7.5 × 4.0–5.0 μm) with a germ pore [3]. Furthermore, P. pakistanica is characterized by smaller, lageniform pleurocystidia (up to 68 μm), often bifid, and narrowly lageniform to utriform cheilocystidia with mucilaginous apices. In contrast, P. brunnescens (= P. luteobadia) exhibits larger pleurocystidia (up to 80 μm) that are fusoid-ventricose, and cheilocystidia that are subfusoid to broadly fusoid, often with elongated cylindrical necks and typically hyaline to orange-yellow [1–3].

Pholiota pakistanica is readily distinguished by another close species, P. castanea by its hygrophanous pileus (vs. non-hygrophanous in P. castanea), smaller and more delicate basidiomata, and a viscid stipe surface (vs. typically dry, fibrillose to fibrillose-scaly) [3]. In addition, P. castanea exhibits larger basidiospores (6.0–8.0 × 3.5–5.0 μm) with an indistinct but present germ pore, whereas P. pakistanica has smaller spores lacking a germ pore. The cystidia in P. castanea are predominantly fusiform to ventricose or sublageniform without reported bifurcation, whereas P. pakistanica exhibits narrowly lageniform to distinctly bifid pleurocystidia with occasional mucilaginous apices. Furthermore, P. castanea typically has a dry, fibrillose stipe with a webby, evanescent veil, contrasting with the viscid, smooth to slightly fibrillose stipe of P. pakistanica, which lacks a well-developed annular zone [3].

Pholiota pakistanica resembles Pholiota chocenensis in having a convex to plano-convex pileus, rusty-brown lamellae, and lageniform to fusiform cystidia. However, it differs in its smaller basidiomata (pileus up to 27 mm vs. up to 50 mm), lack of fibrillose-flocculose veil remnants, adnexed (not adnate to subdecurrent) lamellae, and a shorter, lighter-colored stipe (up to 35 mm vs. up to 110 mm) [19]. Microscopically, P. pakistanica has smaller basidiospores and longer, often bifid pleurocystidia, whereas P. chocenensis possesses larger spores (6.8–7.5(–8.0) × 4.2–4.8 μm) and fusiform to ventricose pleurocystidia [19, 20].

The genus Pholiota in Pakistan remains poorly explored, with limited documented species despite its high fungal diversity. Morphological comparisons with closely related taxa, including those previously reported from the region (Table 2), together with ITS and LSU phylogenetic analyses, confirm that P. pakistanica represents a distinct and previously undescribed species. This finding highlights the potential for undiscovered diversity and underscores the need for further integrative studies and broader sampling of Pholiota in Pakistan.

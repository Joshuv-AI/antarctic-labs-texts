The impact of evidence-based continuity of care on the recurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events following interventional procedures in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease
Authors: Haiyan Chen, Qin Fan, Xiulan Zhang, Birun Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to investigate the impact of evidence-based continuity of care (EBCC) on the recurrence rate of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) following interventional procedures in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease. A retrospective cohort study was conducted involving 210 patients who underwent cardiovascular interventions at our hospital between January 2021 and December 2023. Patients were divided into 2 groups based on the care method received: an EBCC group (n = 94) and a conventional care group (n = 116). Over a 12-month postoperative period, we assessed the incidence of MACE, quality of life, medication adherence, functional status, psychological well-being, and satisfaction with care. The incidence of MACE was significantly lower in the EBCC group compared to the conventional care group. Patients receiving continuity of care also demonstrated significant improvements in quality of life, medication adherence, functional status, and psychological health. Satisfaction with nursing care was notably higher in the continuity of care group. EBCC effectively reduces the incidence of postoperative MACE in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease. It enhances quality of life, medication adherence, psychological well-being, and patient satisfaction, thereby promoting comprehensive postoperative recovery.

1. Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases in the elderly are associated with high morbidity, mortality, and disability rates, significantly impacting patients’ quality of life.[1,2] Interventional procedures are commonly employed in clinical practice to alleviate myocardial ischemia and restore blood flow. However, many patients experience high recurrence rates post-procedure due to factors such as insufficient disease awareness and delayed lifestyle modifications, leading to an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).[3] MACE encompasses myocardial infarction, revascularization, cardiac death, and malignant arrhythmias – conditions with high incidence and poor prognoses that impose substantial economic and psychological burdens on patients and their families.[4] Therefore, postoperative care is crucial for improving prognostic outcomes and reducing complications in elderly patients.

The conventional nursing model predominantly involves routine care, including preoperative education, postoperative dietary counseling, and rehabilitation guidance. However, this approach has limitations such as lack of personalization and inability to address the diverse postoperative needs of patients, leading to suboptimal care and poor adherence after discharge, which increases the risk of MACE.[5] Therefore, there is an urgent need for a systematic and individualized care program to help elderly patients with cardiovascular disease achieve better postoperative recovery.

In recent years, evidence-based continuity of care (EBCC) has gained increasing attention. Studies indicate that this approach can significantly enhance patients’ quality of life and reduce the incidence of postoperative complications.[6,7] For example, Zheng et al demonstrated that detailed behavioral care based on a health concept model improved negative emotions and enhanced the quality of life in patients with chronic heart failure.[8] Additionally, the latest evidence-based nursing methods emphasize dynamic patient management and real-time feedback through modern information technologies, such as mobile health applications and remote monitoring, to provide more precise and personalized care.[9,10]

However, there is a paucity of large-scale retrospective cohort studies examining EBCC in the post-interventional management of cardiovascular disease among the elderly, leading to a lack of substantial empirical data. Such studies can leverage existing clinical data to rapidly and efficiently assess the effects of nursing interventions, providing a reliable foundation for clinical practice. Therefore, we conducted a retrospective cohort study to investigate the impact of EBCC on the recurrence rate of MACE, quality of life, and psychological well-being in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease following interventional procedures. We hypothesized that patients receiving EBCC would have a significantly lower recurrence rate of MACE compared to those receiving usual care. The results of this study will provide stronger support for clinical nursing practice, promote long-term rehabilitation in elderly patients, and contribute to the development of more scientific care programs.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design

This research was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Jingzhou (2024-jzfph-0514), and all patient data were de-identified to ensure confidentiality and protect participant privacy. As a retrospective cohort study, data were obtained from medical records of patients who underwent cardiovascular interventional procedures at our hospital between January 2021 and December 2023. As a retrospective study. The ethics committee waived the requirement for written informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of fully anonymized data.

The aim of this study was to assess the impact of EBCC on the recurrence rate of MACE after interventional procedures in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease. The study population was divided into 2 groups based on whether they received EBCC:

EBCC group: patients who received EBCC. Control group: patients who received conventional care.

Sample size calculation: based on an expected incidence of MACE of 20% in the EBCC group and 30% in the control group, with a significance level (α) of 0.05 and a power (1 − β) of 0.80 using a 2-sided test, it was preliminarily estimated that 210 patients were needed (105 in each group). Accounting for potential data loss, a total of 210 patients were ultimately included, comprising 94 in the EBCC group and 116 in the control group. As this was a retrospective study, group assignment was based on the type of care patients received as documented in their medical records, rather than randomization. To reduce selection bias, we used propensity score matching at the inclusion stage to match the EBCC group and the control group based on patients’ baseline characteristics – such as age, gender, body mass index, literacy level, and underlying diseases. This approach ensured that the 2 groups were comparable in terms of baseline characteristics.

Data collection included patients’ baseline data prior to discharge and postoperative follow-up data over a 12-month period. Primary data sources were the hospital’s electronic medical record system, patient interview transcripts, and standardized validated questionnaires.

2.2. Study population

Inclusion criteria: patients aged 60 years and older who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention or coronary artery bypass grafting for cardiovascular disease (e.g., coronary artery disease) and were successfully discharged after the procedure. Patients were required to have basic cognitive abilities to understand and follow nursing recommendations, and to participate in follow-up visits and questionnaires.

Exclusion criteria: patients with severe cognitive impairment; those with end-stage diseases (e.g., life expectancy <6 months); patients with severe mental illnesses; those with significant hepatic or renal insufficiency or malignant tumors; and patients with incomplete data or missing follow-up information.

According to the baseline data (Table 1), there were no significant differences between the intervention and control groups in terms of age, gender, body mass index, literacy level, or abilities in activities of daily living (P > .05), confirming that the 2 groups were comparable.

2.3. Record of care model

EBCC group interventions: multidisciplinary teamwork: a team consisting of physicians, nurses, dietitians, and counselors collaborated to provide comprehensive patient care. Team members met regularly to discuss care plans and patient progress. Structured follow-up: monthly follow-up visits were conducted via telephone, telemedicine, or face-to-face consultations to monitor changes in the patient’s condition and address care needs. Medication adherence monitoring: patients’ adherence to medication regimens was ensured through monitoring devices and self-reporting. The Morisky medication adherence scale-8 was used to assess adherence, with feedback and guidance provided during follow-up visits. Lifestyle counseling: patients received individualized advice on diet, exercise, and smoking cessation to improve their quality of life. Dietitians and exercise instructors developed personalized programs, which were adjusted during follow-up visits. Use of telemedicine tools: remote monitoring devices (e.g., blood pressure monitors, blood glucose meters) and video consultations provided continuous support and real-time feedback to patients. Control group interventions: standard nursing guidance at discharge. Regular outpatient follow-up. Routine postoperative medication guidance. Distribution of basic health education materials.

2.4. Assessment indicators

The primary outcome was the occurrence of MACE within 12 months post-surgery, including myocardial infarction, stroke, revascularization, and cardiovascular-related mortality. Secondary outcomes included patients’ quality of life (assessed using the functional assessment of cancer therapy–cardiovascular), medication adherence (measured by the Morisky medication adherence scale), functional status (evaluated via the 6-minute walk test, Borg rating of perceived exertion, and Barthel index), psychological well-being (assessed with the self-rating anxiety scale [SAS], self-rating depression scale [SDS], and Hamilton anxiety rating scale [HAMA]), rehospitalization rates, and satisfaction with care.

2.5. Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation and were compared using the independent samples t-test. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test. A P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the impact of EBCC on the recurrence rate of MACE following interventional procedures in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease. The results demonstrated that patients in the EBCC group had significantly better clinical outcomes and overall health status compared to those in the control group.

The incidence of MACE in the EBCC group was 6.38%, significantly lower than the control group’s 18.97% (P = .023), indicating that EBCC was associated with a lower incidence of complications. The incidence of myocardial infarction was 2.13% in the EBCC group, compared to 6.03% in the control group (P = .031). In-stent restenosis occurred in 3.19% of the EBCC group versus 9.48% in the control group (P = .015). The incidence of heart failure was 1.06% in the EBCC group, significantly lower than 2.59% in the control group (P = .042), while stroke incidence was 1.06%, compared to 4.31% in the control group (P = .012). Cardiovascular mortality was also significantly lower at 1.06%, compared to 6.03% in the control group (P = .001). Additionally, the readmission rate in the EBCC group was 5.32%, notably lower than the control group’s 15.52% (P = .028; Table 2).

4. Discussion

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of death and disability among the global elderly population.[11] Although interventional therapy is widely used and effectively improves cardiovascular health, the recurrence of MACE (e.g., angina pectoris, myocardial infarction) during the post-procedural period remains common.[12] In recent years, continuity of care has been increasingly adopted in the postoperative management of cardiovascular disease to reduce recurrence risk and enhance quality of life.[13] EBCC focuses on utilizing scientific evidence to guide the design and implementation of nursing interventions, thereby optimizing outcomes.[14] Therefore, it is crucial to explore the impact of a high-quality care model on adverse cardiovascular events in elderly patients following interventions.

This study employed a retrospective cohort design to evaluate the effect of EBCC on the recurrence of MACE following interventional procedures in elderly patients with cardiovascular disease. The findings indicated that patients receiving EBCC had a lower incidence of MACE compared to those receiving standard care. Furthermore, the intervention group showed improved outcomes in quality of life, medication adherence, functional status, and psychological well-being.

In recent years, continuity-of-care models have been increasingly adopted in China, particularly in the management of chronic cardiovascular diseases. With the rapid development of digital health platforms, telemedicine follow-up, and community-based rehabilitation services, long-term postoperative monitoring has become more feasible. Several Chinese studies have reported that structured follow-up programs combining telehealth, medication counseling, and lifestyle intervention can improve adherence and reduce rehospitalization rates among cardiovascular patients. These developments highlight the growing clinical feasibility of EBCC in China’s healthcare system.

Another important consideration is whether a dose–response relationship exists between the intensity of continuity care interventions and cardiovascular outcomes. Although our retrospective data did not allow stratification by intervention intensity, it is plausible that more frequent follow-ups, multidisciplinary involvement, and sustained behavioral support may yield greater reductions in MACE risk. Future prospective studies should quantitatively examine intervention intensity, duration, and adherence to determine optimal care thresholds.

EBCC is linked to a reduced rate of postoperative MACE recurrence in elderly cardiovascular patients.[15] This effect may be attributed to the provision of systematic and individualized nursing interventions, which include multidisciplinary collaboration, structured follow-up, medication management, lifestyle counseling, and psychological support.[16] These interventions not only enhanced patients’ disease knowledge and self-management abilities but also improved treatment adherence through the establishment of a strong patient-provider relationship, thus reducing adverse event incidence.[17] For instance, medication management ensured proper medication use, lifestyle guidance promoted healthy behaviors, and psychological support helped alleviate anxiety and depression, collectively enhancing overall patient health.[18]

Our findings align with previous research. A systematic review by Kitsiou et al demonstrated that telemonitoring and continuity of care improved outcomes for patients with chronic heart failure.[19] Similarly, Inglis et al reported that structured telephone support and telemonitoring contributed to reducing cardiovascular events.[20] However, most studies have primarily focused on specific interventions or single outcome indicators. In contrast, our study employed a multidimensional assessment that not only examined MACE recurrence but also highlighted the positive impact of continuity of care on quality of life, medication adherence, functional status, and psychological well-being. This suggests that the comprehensive nature of continuity of care interventions significantly contributes to overall patient health. Additionally, Lee et al showed that evidence-based nursing interventions can substantially enhance the quality of life in cardiovascular patients.[21] However, their study mainly focused on medication adherence, while the current study expanded the assessment to include psychological status and daily functioning, providing a more comprehensive analysis of the effects of extended care.

Our findings have important implications for clinical nursing practice. First, EBCC may serve as an effective strategy for the postoperative management of elderly cardiovascular patients, reducing the risk of MACE recurrence, hospitalizations, and healthcare burden. Second, the positive impact of continuity of care on quality of life and mental health suggests that nursing interventions should address both physiological and psychological needs, providing comprehensive support. This offers valuable guidance for caregivers in care planning and highlights the importance of incorporating continuity of care into routine postoperative protocols, particularly for individualized care in high-risk elderly patients, to enhance recovery outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective cohort study, it is subject to inherent selection bias and residual confounding despite the use of propensity score matching. Second, intervention intensity could not be quantitatively classified, preventing evaluation of potential dose–response relationships between continuity care and MACE outcomes. Third, some follow-up data were obtained through questionnaires and telephone interviews, which may introduce recall bias. Fourth, this was a single-center study conducted in China, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other healthcare systems. Finally, the follow-up duration was limited to 12 months, and longer-term cardiovascular outcomes remain unclear. Future multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up periods are warranted.

5. Conclusion

This retrospective cohort study demonstrated that EBCC is associated with reduced recurrence of MACE following interventional procedures in elderly cardiovascular patients, while also positively influencing quality of life, medication adherence, functional status, and psychological well-being. Despite its limitations, these findings offer valuable insights for clinical nursing practice and support the implementation of an EBCC model in postoperative care. Future prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and explore optimal strategies for implementing continuity of care.

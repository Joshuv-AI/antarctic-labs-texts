Innovation system functions: Survey data of additive manufacturing enterprises in South Africa
Authors: Michelle Smit, Sara S Grobbelaar, Natasha Sacks
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset comprises survey data from South African additive manufacturing (AM) enterprises, representing approximately 80% of the industry. The survey was designed using innovation indicators from the technology innovation system (TIS) framework to explore the dynamics within South African AM enterprises. Six TIS experts and 2AM industry specialists validated the survey's comprehensiveness and relevance. A statistician reviewed the data collection process to ensure it was suitable for robust statistical analysis. Managers of South African AM enterprises were invited to complete the survey online, and the responses were systematically collected and stored. This dataset is a valuable resource for AM researchers and practitioners, facilitating market gap analysis concerning current materials and services and offering insights for future AM applications.Keywords: Additive manufacturing, Innovation system functions, Functional dynamics, Functional indicators, South Africa, Survey

Background

A study, motivated by the DSI-NRF Centre of Excellence in Strong Materials (CoE-SM), was conducted to identify the drivers and barriers for the cemented tungsten carbide (hardmetals) industry in adopting additive manufacturing (AM) techniques. As the technology innovation system (TIS) framework [3] focuses on the complex interactions between actors, institutions, and processes that drive innovation development, diffusion, and utilization, it was used to analyze international case studies of enterprises that successfully adopted AM for hardmetals production.

A functional indicator framework was derived from existing literature on TIS functions to guide TIS scholars in comprehending the essential innovation activities involved in conceiving, developing, and disseminating innovations. This framework was validated by experts and was published in a previous article [4]. The resulting validated functional indicator framework is visually represented in Table 1.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

To gather the data compiled in the dataset, the functional indicator framework, presented in Table 1, was derived from TIS literature [3,[5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. To ensure the validity and comprehensiveness of this framework, it underwent validation by six expert reviewers in the field of TIS. This validation process involved interviews according to Rabionet's semi-structured interview methodology [1], followed by analysis using Bryman et al.’s [2] quantitative content analysis process.

The functional indicator framework (Table 1) delineates the innovation activities crucial for conceptualizing, developing, and diffusing innovations within TISs. Based on these indicators, a survey instrument was formulated to assess the extent to which TISs fulfill their innovation activities. Consequently, the functional indicators within the framework informed the development of survey statements aimed at analyzing the challenges and opportunities within TISs. Multiple iterations were conducted during the survey development process to ensure its comprehensiveness, clarity, and inclusion of all essential inquiries. Cassim's seven-step survey design process [10] (Table 5) guided this iterative process.

Survey design.

The validity of the survey questions, their suitability for the target audience, and the statistical analysis methods applicable were reassessed by a panel of experts comprising six TIS experts, two additive manufacturing (AM) industry experts, and a statistician. This validation process again followed Rabionetʼs [1] semi-structured interview methodology and Bryman et al.ʼs [2] quantitative content analysis approach.

Following validation, the survey targeted owners and managers of South African additive manufacturing (AM) enterprises, encompassing those offering AM value-added services and sales representatives of international AM system manufacturers. The majority of respondents were sourced from the service provider directory of RAPDASA, the oldest AM association globally and the official representative of the South African AM industry, as well as the CAMASA membership directory, an aerospace association promoting advanced manufacturing in South Africa. Additional respondents were identified through online searches for AM services, and referrals were sought from existing survey participants, ensuring the credibility of the respondents.

Approximately fifty operational AM enterprises were identified from the RAPDASA and CAMASA databases, with this number verified by a member of the RAPDASA management committee and 2AM survey expert reviewers. Invitation emails were sent to forty-nine potential respondents, resulting in forty enterprises agreeing to participate in the study over 45 days.

Subsequently, survey responses were analyzed using Statistica software [11]. Reflective and formative measurement models, demographic analysis, variance analysis, and regression analysis tests were conducted on the survey results. An expert statistician validated the statistical analysis and interpretation of the findings.

Limitations

The dataset only represents a single survey, in future a longitudinal dataset may be developed to look at the development of the sector and changing needs in terms of policy support mechanisms.

Ethics Statement

Informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the study. Participation was voluntary, and participants had the right to withdraw at any time without repercussions. All procedures performed in the study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the Research and Ethics Committee of the University of Stellenbosch, which granted the ethical clearance for this study under project number ING-2020-18663.

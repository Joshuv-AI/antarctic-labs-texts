In vivo and insilico toxicity studies of hydroalcoholic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides roots
Authors: Pranali B Yeram, Amisha Vora, Yogesh A Kulkarni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVetiveria zizanioides (L.) is a traditional Indian medicinal plant belonging to the family Poaceae. Hydroalcoholic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides has been studied for its various pharmacological effects, but a systematic scientific study on its safety has not been done.ObjectiveTo study the toxic effects of single and repeated doses of hydroalcoholic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides root, and to establish the toxicity profile of hydroalcoholic extract using in silico toxicity prediction tools.MethodsAcute and repeated dose toxicity studies were conducted following OECD guidelines 423 and 407, respectively. The acute toxicity study was carried out on female Sprague Dawley rats at 2000 and 5000 mg/kg doses, with effects monitored for 14 days. A repeated dose toxicity study was performed through daily dosing of the hydroalcoholic extract (ethanol: water, 1:1 v/v) of Vetiveria zizanioides extract for 28 days at doses of 250 mg/kg, 500 mg/kg, and 1000 mg/kg. At the end of the study, hematological parameters, biochemical parameters, kidney function, and histopathology were evaluated in all animals. Additionally, In silico toxicity prediction of the bioactive present in Vetiveria zizanioides root extract was performed using ProTox-II. Phytoconstituents of Vetiveria zizanioides roots were identified using GC-MS analysis.ResultsSingle dose administration of Vetiveria zizanioides extract at 5000 mg/kg showed no toxicity or morbidity. Similarly, repeated doses of extract over 28 days did not significantly impact hematological and biochemical parameters. However, a significant increase in ALT (P < 0.05) was noted at the highest dose (1000 mg/kg) of the extract. Histopathological examinations of the liver at this dose revealed mild changes in hepatocytes compared to the control animals. The toxicity prediction revealed that all identified compounds of Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract were free from cytotoxicity and mutagenicity. More than 50 percent of the compounds were found to be class 5 (2000 < LD50 ≤ 5000). However, few compounds were found to be hepatotoxic at high doses.ConclusionVetiveria zizanioides extract may generally be safe, but long-term use at 1000 mg/kg in rats could pose a concern for liver toxicity. The results of the study indicate that Vetiveria zizanioides is safe for use up to 500 mg/kg in 28-day repeated dose toxicity study.Keywords: Vetiveria zizanioides, Crysopogon zizanioides, Acute oral dose toxicity, Repeated oral dose toxicity

Introduction

Herbal medicinal products and supplements are utilized for maintaining good health and preventing various diseases [1]. Herbal extracts and the related products are a complex mixture of various phytoconstituents that show different pharmacological effects by various pathways and are mostly considered safe when compared to allopathic medicine. However, there is growing evidence of the safety concerns associated with these natural products [2]. Vetiveria zizanioides is a traditional Indian medicinal plant reported to have hepatoprotective, nephroprotective, antidiabetic, antimicrobial, anti-aging, and anti-inflammatory activities [[3], [4], [5]]. This plant is also known as Ushira in Ayurveda. The roots of this plant are rich in terpenes, and they are widely used in aroma therapy. Vetiveria zizanioides is reported for nephroprotective effects [3,6]. Ethanolic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides is reported for anticonvulsant activity [7]. The essential oil of Vetiveria zizanioides is used in aroma therapy and herbal baths. Also, it is reported that Vetiveria zizanioides is effective in the treatment of the human dermal fibroblast [8]. Vetiveria zizanioides contains khusimol, epiglobulol, spathulenol, khusinol, α-vetivones, β-vetivone, khusimone, β-vatirenene, α-gurjunene, δ-cadinene, cubenol etc. [[4], [9]].

Vetiveria zizanioides is an important medicinal plant in Ayurvedic medicine. Traditionally, Vetiveria zizanioides is known for its cooling and soothing properties, making it an essential remedy for conditions associated with the pitta dosha induced by high body heat [4]. It is also reported for its antimicrobial effects [[10], [11]]. Moreover, Vetiveria zizanioides is also known for its digestive effects, including relief of gastrointestinal issues like indigestion and bloating. The Vetiveria zizanioides roots are cooling, bitter; astringent; useful in burning sensations, bilious fevers, sweats, foul breath, thirst, strangury, ulcers, diseases of the blood [12]. Ushirasava is the traditional Ayurvedic formulation consisting of Vetiveria zizanioides extract and is widely used for the treatment of various conditions such as skin problems and blood disorders, [4]. In Ayurveda, Vetiveria zizanioides is valued for its properties that help to relieve fever, excessive thirst, painful urination, and promote wound healing [13].

The literature suggested that Vetiveria zizanioides extract was studied for various pharmacological effects, but a systematic scientific report is not available on its safety. The present investigation aimed to assess the single and repeated dose oral toxicity of the hydroalcoholic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides in rats.

Materials and methods

Plant material

Vetiveria zizanioides roots were sourced from Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, India, through a local supplier. Authentication of the plant was conducted at the Botanical Survey of India, Western Regional Centre, Pune, with reference No. BSI/WRC/Iden.Cer./2022/0511220008016. Roots were cleaned with water and then dried in a hot air oven at 40°C for three days. After drying, the roots were powdered by using the multi-mill (Gansons Ltd., India). Extraction was carried out Hydroalcoholic solvent mixture (Ethanol and water; 1:1v/v) by the maceration technique.

Chemicals and diagnostic kits

The biochemical and urine parameters were assessed using kits obtained from Transasia Biomedicals Ltd., India, using a biochemical analyser (Erba Chem 7, Germany).

Experimental animals

Sprague Dawley rats (Male and female) were purchased from the National Institute of Biosciences, Pune, India, with body weights between 200 and 220 g. The rats were housed in the institute's animal facility. They were provided with a multi-nutritional pellet diet and had unlimited access to purified water. The experimental protocol underwent thorough evaluation and received approval from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Shri Vile Parle Kelvani Mandal with approval number: CCSEA/IAEC/P-13/2023.

Acute oral toxicity study

An acute oral toxicity study of Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract was performed as per the OECD guideline (TG 423) with slight modifications [14]. A cohort of female Sprague Dawley rats was partitioned into four groups, with each group including three animals. Group 1 was the control group and was administered 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose (vehicle). Group 2 administered a dose of 2000 mg/kg, whereas Group 3 received a dose of 5000 mg/kg of the hydroalcoholic extract derived from Vetiveria zizanioides roots. Each rat was observed for changes in behaviour and physical appearance immediately after administration of the doses, during the initial 30 min, and subsequently at regular intervals over the first 24 h, with a specific focus on the initial 4 h. Monitoring was conducted for all groups for 14 days. Observations were made about the physical characteristics, including any changes in the skin and fur, as well as the conditions of the eyes, and lacrimal discharge for individual animals. Also, abnormalities in autonomic, somatomotor, behavioral patterns, and respiration were monitored. Each animal was observed for symptoms such as tremors, seizures, excessive salivation, diarrhea, lethargy, drowsiness, and coma.

Body weight, food, and water intake

Body weight measurements were taken every alternate day over 14 days. Additionally, food and water intake were recorded every other day. Metabolic cages (BIK Industries, India) were used to measure the food intake and water consumption. The animals were placed individually in metabolic cages to ensure precise monitoring.

Gross necropsy

All animals were humanely euthanized using isoflurane after 14 days. Each rat was placed in an enclosed, transparent container where a cotton pad soaked with isoflurane was placed beneath a perforated platform to avoid direct contact. The animal inhaled the anaesthetic vapor until it reached deep anaesthesia, as indicated by the loss of righting and pedal reflexes. Isoflurane exposure was continued until complete respiratory and cardiac arrest were confirmed. The absence of reflexes and vital signs for at least 2 min was used to confirm death before necropsy. A necropsy was conducted, involving a thorough examination of the major organs. This included detailed inspections of the external body surface, all orifices, as well as the thoracic and abdominal cavities and their contents. Subsequently, post-sacrifice, Organ weights, including those of the heart, lungs, brain, liver, spleen, stomach, kidneys, adrenals, and gonads, were promptly measured.

28 - Days repeated oral dose toxicity study

Repeated dose toxicity was performed as per OECD guideline (TG 407) [[15], [15], [16], [17]]. Male and female Sprague Dawley rats were used for the study, with each group consisting of 10 animals, evenly divided between genders. Group 1 served as the control, which received the 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) as a vehicle. Groups 2, 3, and 4 were administered with Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract at dosages of 250, 500, and 1000 mg/kg over 28 days. The dose of repeated dose toxicity was selected from the acute dose toxicity study. The selection of the doses for the repeated dose toxicity study took into account the No Observed Adverse Effect Level (NOAEL). Mid-dose for the study was determined by considering 1/10th of the NOAEL. Animals were carefully observed for any indication of toxicity. After 28 days, following the collection of blood and urine, the animals were euthanized according to ethical guidelines.

Parameters

Body weight, food, and water intake

Weekly records of body weight, food intake, and water intake were maintained for all animals throughout the study period.

Hematological parameters

Blood was collected via retro-orbital plexus puncture under mild isoflurane anaesthesia into EDTA-containing tubes. Hematological parameters for all animals were assessed by using a hematological analyzer (Celltac Alpha MEK-6500K, Nihon Kohden, Japan).

Clinical biochemistry

Plasma was separated by centrifuging blood samples at 4000 rpm for 10 min. Biochemical parameters were assessed using a biochemistry analyzer (Erba Chem 7, Erba Mannheim, Germany). Diagnostic kits for the biochemical analysis were sourced from Transasia Bio-medicals Ltd., India. Plasma samples were used for the estimation of biochemical parameters.

Urine parameters

The urine of all animals for 24 h was collected with the help of metabolic cages (BIK Industries, India). Kidney function tests were conducted after centrifugation of urine samples at 2000 rpm for 5 min. Kidney function tests included the evaluation of urine output, Protein excretion rate, creatinine clearance, urea clearance, and GFR [18,19].

Gross necropsy and histopathology

After 28 days, experimental animals were euthanized by humane procedures. Necropsy was conducted, involving a thorough examination of the major organs. This included detailed inspections of the external surface of the body, all openings, as well as the thoracic and abdominal cavities. Weights of important organs such as brain, heart, stomach, lungs, liver, spleen, kidneys, adrenals, and gonads, were measured immediately after euthanasia.

Histopathological examinations of the vital organs were carried out by performing Haematoxylin and Eosin staining. A semi-quantitative generic grading system was employed to evaluate histopathological changes in tissue samples following exposure to the test substance. The grading scale ranged from 0 to 4, where Grade 0 (None) indicated no abnormalities, with tissues appearing normal and unaffected by the treatment. Grade 1 (Mild) represented minimal changes, such as slight vacuolation of cells or mild infiltration of inflammatory cells, with no significant disruption to tissue structure or function. Grade 2 (Moderate) was assigned to tissues exhibiting more noticeable alterations. Grade 3 (Severe) indicated marked toxicity, characterized by significant necrosis and pronounced inflammation. Grade 4 (Very Severe) was used for tissues showing extensive damage, including widespread necrosis, and almost complete disruption of the tissue structure [20].

Statistical analysis

The results are presented as Mean ± Standard Error Mean (SEM). The statistical analysis was performed using the GraphPad Prism 8.4.2 program. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted, followed by Bonferroni's multiple comparison tests, to ascertain the levels of significance. p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis

GC-MS analysis of the extract was carried out to determine the active components of Vetiveria zizanioides root extract. The extract solution was prepared using a blend of ethanol and water. The GC-MS analysis was conducted utilizing an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph (Agilent Technologies, USA) and a Joel Accu TOF GC-MS spectrometer (JEOL Ltd., Japan). Helium was used as an inert gas during the analysis process with a flow rate of 1 mL/min. The analysis employed an HP5 column (Agilent Technologies, USA) having a 30 mm length and 0.32 mm internal diameter. The GC analysis spanned a total runtime of 40 min. Over temperature was increased from 60 to 280 °C at a rate of 8 °C per minute. A 0.2 μl of sample volume was injected through the injector. The mass spectrometry was conducted at 70 electron volts. The interpretation of the mass data was carried out utilizing the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) database.

In silico toxicity prediction

GC-MS of the extract was performed to determine the phytochemicals present in the extract of Vetiveria zizanioides roots. ChemDraw Ultra (7.0) was used to draw structures of the compound, which was detected by GC-MS. Also, the previously known reported compounds of Vetiveria zizanioides roots were used for further toxicity prediction. The Swiss ADME web tool and the PubChem database were used to predict the SMILES, which were used to determine the toxicity prediction of phytocompounds. Major organ toxicity (Hepatotoxicity, cardiotoxicity, neurotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, respiratory toxicity), carcinogenicity, immunotoxicity, cytotoxicity, and mutagenicity, and LD50 (mg/kg) of the analyzed phytocompounds were carried out with the ProTox-II server.

The behavior of the experimental animals was normal throughout the 14 days of observation. There was no sign of toxicity shown by the experimental animals. The maximum tolerated dose of Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract was found to be 5000 mg/kg (Fig. 1).

Gross necropsy and histopathological evaluation

No significant differences were noted in the relative organ weight of animals administered with Vetiveria zizanioides extract compared to the control animals (Table 4). Microscopic examination of major organs, including the heart, liver, spleen, brain, kidney, stomach, testes, and ovaries, revealed no significant alteration in tissue cell morphology (Fig. 3). Histopathology of these organs was found to be normal after administration of Vetiveria zizanioides extract. However, histopathology of liver tissue of animals treated with 1000 mg/kg of extract showed mild alteration in the tissue structure when compared to control animals (Fig. 4). Histopathology of the liver predicted that a higher dose (1000 mg/kg) of Vetiveria zizanioides extract causes minimal changes in liver cells. Histopathological findings of the brain indicate the normal histomorphology of neuronal tissue and supporting tissue matrix in the brain cerebrum and cerebellum, and the absence of inflammatory or pathological changes in the brain tissue. Histopathology of the spleen showed normal histomorphological features of red pulp and white pulp. Adequate lymphoid cellular population in the lymphoid tissue. Histopathology of the lungs indicated the normal histomorphological features of bronchi and alveolar tissue with intact cellular details and normal bronchiolar epithelium. Heart tissue was also found to be normal in the histopathological examination. Normal histomorphological features of cardiac muscle fibers in the myocardium were observed. No inflammatory or pathological changes in heart tissue were observed. Normal histomorphology of the mucosa of the glandular stomach with intact epithelial layers and normal sub-mucosa and serosa indicated that there were no toxic effects of the extract on the stomach when compared to control animals. Reproductive organs, testes, and ovaries were found to be normal, and there were no significant differences noted compared to the histopathology of control animals. However, the histopathology of the liver indicated that there were mild changes in the cellular structure of the hepatocytes of rats after repeated 28 days of administration of Vetiveria zizanioides extract at a dose of 1000 mg/kg. Focal areas of cellular swelling were observed in the hepatocytes. Also, congested central veins and portal veins were observed with focal and minimal vacuolar cytoplasmic changes in the hepatocytes.

Discussion

An acute oral toxicity study was conducted to identify the maximum tolerated dose of Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract. As per the OECD guideline TG 423, the initial dose was selected from one of four predetermined levels: 5, 50, 300, and 2000 mg/kg body weight. The selection criteria aimed to identify the dose most likely to induce mortality in any of the treated animals. Available literature suggested that efficacy studies were carried out at a dose of 100, 400, 500, 600, 1000 mg/kg. [3,7]. As per the OECD guideline TG 423, the starting dose of administration was taken at 2000 mg/kg in three female rats. After evaluation for 14 days, there was zero mortality observed. Therefore, the next dose level, i.e., the highest dose of 5000 mg/kg, was given to three female rats. No significant changes were noted in terms of behaviour in animals treated with Vetiveria zizanioides extract at given doses compared to control animals. During 14 days of observation, no mortality was reported. Therefore, the NOAEL of Vetiveria zizanioides extract was determined to be greater than 5000 mg/kg. In a repeated dose toxicity study, three doses of Vetiveria zizanioides extract, 250 mg/kg, 500 mg/kg, and 1000 mg/kg, were selected. Body weight was recorded at regular intervals throughout the study as a general indicator of the animals' health and to assess potential systemic toxicity of the test compound. Monitoring changes in body weight is a widely accepted, non-invasive method to detect early signs of adverse effects, such as reduced food intake, metabolic disturbances, or organ dysfunction[23]. Significant weight loss or failure to gain weight may indicate compound-related toxicity [19]. No significant differences were observed in body weight, food intake, and water intake between animals treated with the extract and control animals. No alteration in body weights of the treated animals indicated a nontoxic effect of the hydroalcoholic extract of Vetiveria zizanioides at all three tested dose levels. Alterations in food intake can signal the onset of adverse effects, including discomfort, metabolic disruption, or reduced palatability of the test material [19]. A significant increase in food intake may point to issues such as gastrointestinal upset or systemic toxicity. This study shows no significant increase or decrease in food intake, suggesting that Vetiveria zizanioides extract neither adversely affected the animal's appetite nor influenced their metabolic rate to a degree that would alter the feeding behaviour. Hematological evaluation serves as a key indicator of the physiological and pathological status of animals in toxicity studies, providing insights into the effects of test substances on the blood-forming organs and immune system [16]. All hematological parameters were found to be within normal ranges, and no significant changes were observed when compared to the control group. The stability of these values suggests that the Vetiveria zizanioides extract had no adverse effect on erythropoiesis, immune function, or blood coagulation. Assessment of kidney function is a critical component of toxicity studies. Urine parameters such as albuminuria and proteinuria are essential to determine kidney health after administering a drug product or herbal extract [17]. Urea clearance, creatinine clearance, and GFR are also indicators of kidney function. Alterations in these parameters can serve as an early biomarker of nephrotoxicity. Monitoring renal function is especially important in subacute and chronic toxicity studies, where repeated exposure to a test compound may result in cumulative renal damage. In this study, urine tests conducted at three dose levels predicted normal results, and no significant differences were found compared to the control animals. These findings suggest that Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract did not adversely affect kidney function or induce any notable metabolic disturbances. Relative organ weight is also an important parameter that helps to understand clinical conditions such as hypertrophy or fluid deposition in the organ. No significant changes in the organ weight indicate the safety of the Vetiveria zizanioides extract. Biochemical parameters such as glucose, total protein, albumin, creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL-Cholesterol, bilirubin, uric acid, AST, and ALP serve as indicators of the normal functioning of major vital organs such as the liver, kidneys, and heart. Changes in serum or plasma biochemical profiles can signal organ-specific toxicity, systemic stress, or metabolic disturbances before overt clinical signs or histopathological alterations become apparent. In this study, biochemical parameters have been observed within normal ranges. However, at the highest dose (1000 mg/kg), ALT showed significant differences when compared with the control animals. Whereas the ALT value was not clinically significant and falls within the normal range. These results indicate there may be some adverse effects of the Vetiveria zizanioides extract at the highest dose of 1000 mg/kg on the liver.

In-silico toxicity prediction serves as an important complementary tool to in vivo studies by providing early insights into the potential safety risks of test compounds. For complex mixtures like plant extracts, in-silico tools allow for rapid screening of individual phytoconstituents, predicting parameters such as LD50, organ-specific toxicity, and potential genotoxicity [19]. In silico prediction of the toxicity of Vetiveria zizanioides hydroalcoholic extract predicted that a few of the compounds, such as khusilic acid and Neoisolongifolene, 8,9-dehydro-, show hepatotoxicity and also fall in the category of toxicity class four. This data supports the elevated level of ALT in the in vivo toxicity study. None of the compounds was found in the category of class three, indicating that the LD50 of the compound is greater than 2000, and the extract is non-toxic at lower doses.

Conclusion

Prolonged administration of a high dose of Vetiveria zizanioides extract indicated a potential for elevated liver enzymes and mild toxicity to the liver. These findings suggest that while Vetiveria zizanioides extract may generally be safe, long-term use at 1000 mg/kg in rats could pose a concern for liver toxicity. The observed toxicological effects suggest the need for careful dosage consideration in the therapeutic use of Vetiveria zizanioides. These findings underline the importance of standardization and further research to define safe usage thresholds, particularly in herbal medicine. Overall, this study concluded that the extract of Vetiveria zizanioides is acceptable up to the dose of 500 mg/kg if administered by the oral route.

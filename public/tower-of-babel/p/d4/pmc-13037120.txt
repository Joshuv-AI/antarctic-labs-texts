Maternal age and the predictive performance of the uterine artery pulsatility index for composite adverse perinatal outcomes in late-onset fetal growth restriction: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Melisa Golgelioglu, Cigdem Akcabay, Gulcan Akverdi, Salih Burcin Kavak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate whether the predictive performance of the uterine artery pulsatility index (UtA-PI) for composite adverse perinatal outcomes (CAPO) in late-onset fetal growth restriction (FGR) differs according to maternal age.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included singleton pregnancies with late-onset FGR (≥ 32 weeks of gestation) managed at a tertiary care center. Mean UtA-PI measured at diagnosis was normalized for gestational age and evaluated for its association with CAPO across predefined maternal age groups. Predictive performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and multivariable logistic regression, with formal testing for age-related effect modification.ResultsA total of 234 pregnancies with late-onset FGR were included, comprising 62 women < 25 years, 88 aged 25–34 years, and 84 aged ≥ 35 years. CAPO occurred in 29.9% of all pregnancies. Mean UtA-PI values and the proportion of UtA-PI ≥ 90th percentile increased progressively with advancing maternal age (22.6%, 26.1%, and 36.9%, respectively; p = 0.04). The predictive performance of UtA-PI for CAPO improved across age groups, with area under the curve values for the ≥ 90th percentile threshold increasing from 0.60 in women < 25 years to 0.74 in those ≥ 35 years. After multivariable adjustment, elevated UtA-PI remained independently associated with CAPO, with the strongest association observed in women aged ≥ 35 years (adjusted OR 3.5, 95% CI 1.6–7.9).ConclusionsThese findings suggest that maternal age may provide additional clinical context when interpreting UtA-PI–based risk estimates in late-onset FGR. UtA-PI demonstrated modest prognostic performance in women aged ≥ 35 years, whereas discriminative ability was lower in younger maternal age groups. Prospective validation is required before these findings influence clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-08865-0.

Introduction

Fetal growth restriction (FGR) is a multifactorial condition characterized by an impaired ability of the fetus to reach its genetic growth potential and represents one of the leading causes of perinatal morbidity and mortality [1, 2]. It is also associated with long-term cardiovascular and neurodevelopmental sequelae extending beyond the perinatal period [3, 4]. FGR is classified as early- or late-onset depending on whether the diagnosis is made before or after 32 weeks of gestation [5]. Late-onset FGR, which accounts for most cases, typically exhibits milder clinical manifestations and subtler Doppler abnormalities than the early form, making timely recognition and accurate risk stratification more challenging [6]. The uterine artery pulsatility index (UtA-PI) reflects maternal vascular resistance and provides an indirect estimate of placental perfusion, particularly in late-onset FGR [7]. Elevated UtA-PI has been linked to increased perinatal morbidity and mortality both in the general obstetric population and pregnancies complicated by late-onset FGR [8–14].

Advanced maternal age (≥ 35 years) has been associated with higher rates of placental insufficiency and fetal growth abnormalities [15–19]. Age-related vascular changes, including increased stiffness and reduced adaptive remodeling of spiral arteries, may impair uteroplacental perfusion and influence uterine artery Doppler indices [20, 21]. These physiological differences suggest that maternal age may influence the strength of the association between UtA-PI and composite adverse perinatal outcomes (CAPO), potentially affecting its discriminative performance. However, data exploring this interaction in late-onset FGR remain limited. Clarifying this relationship could support age-specific interpretation of UtA-PI values, enhance risk stratification, and facilitate integration of uterine artery Doppler into algorithms predicting adverse outcomes.

We hypothesized that maternal age may modify the association between mean UtA-PI and CAPO in late-onset FGR. Accordingly, the aim of this study was to evaluate whether the strength and discriminative performance of UtA-PI for CAPO differ across maternal age groups.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort study was conducted using patient records from a tertiary obstetrics and gynecology clinic. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Firat University prior to data collection (Approval Number: 28579, Approval Date 01.11.2024). The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and all data were anonymized to ensure patient confidentiality.

Patient population and case definition

All singleton pregnancies managed and delivered at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Firat University Faculty of Medicine, between January 2016 and September 2024, were retrospectively reviewed. Late-onset FGR was diagnosed according to the 2016 Delphi consensus [22]. It was defined as FGR diagnosed at or beyond 32 weeks of gestation according to the 2016 Delphi consensus, including fetuses with an estimated fetal weight (EFW) or abdominal circumference (AC) below the 3rd percentile, or those with EFW or AC below the 10th percentile only when accompanied by abnormal Doppler findings or evidence of growth deceleration, such as a greater than 50-percentile drop in AC growth between the 18–32-week scan and the time of evaluation, based on the Intergrowth-21st fetal growth standards [23]. Additionally, pregnancies with fetal biometry below the 10th percentile accompanied by an umbilical artery pulsatility index above the 95th percentile or a cerebroplacental ratio below the 5th percentile were considered diagnostic. Pregnancies complicated by multiple gestations, major fetal structural or chromosomal abnormalities, congenital infections, or uncertain gestational dating were excluded. Only pregnancies with complete Doppler ultrasound data, delivery information, and neonatal outcomes were included in the final analysis.

Maternal age at the time of Doppler assessment was recorded for all participants. To evaluate whether the predictive performance of the UtA-PI for CAPO differed by maternal age, participants were categorized into three groups: women < 25 years, 25–34 years, and ≥ 35 years. Maternal age categories (< 25, 25–34, and ≥ 35 years) were prespecified prior to data analysis and based on established obstetric risk stratification thresholds, with ≥ 35 years representing advanced maternal age and 25–34 years serving as the conventional reference reproductive age range. Due to the retrospective nature if the study, all Doppler and clinical data were obtained from existing medical records, ensuring consistency in measurement protocols and obstetric management across the study period.

Uterine artery doppler assessment

Uterine artery Doppler measurements were performed at the time of late-onset FGR diagnosis as part of routine clinical evaluation using a Philips EPIQ 7 ultrasound system (Philips Healthcare, Bothell, WA, USA) equipped with a 3.5–5 MHz convex transducer. All examinations were conducted by obstetricians with at least 5 years of experience in fetal Doppler ultrasonography, following the technical recommendations of the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology (ISUOG) [24]. The uterine arteries were identified at their apparent crossover with the external iliac arteries using color Doppler imaging, and pulsed-wave Doppler waveforms were obtained with an insonation angle below 30°. During each assessment, at least three consecutive and uniform waveforms were recorded during fetal quiescence. The mean UtA-PI value was calculated as the average of both uterine arteries.

Gestational age–specific reference values for UtA-PI were obtained from the normative charts published by Gómez et al. [25], which are endorsed by ISUOG. Mean UtA-PI values were converted to multiples of the median (MoM) by dividing the observed value by the gestational-age–specific median from these charts. For each gestational week, the 90th and 95th percentile cutoff values were also taken directly from the same reference curves. These percentile thresholds were used consistently in all subsequent analyses across the maternal age groups.

Definition of Composite Adverse Perinatal Outcome (CAPO)

CAPO was defined according to previously published studies [11, 13]. CAPO was considered present when one or more of the following perinatal complications occurred: emergency cesarean delivery for fetal distress, a 5-min Apgar score < 7, umbilical artery pH < 7.10, or neonatal admission to a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU).

To evaluate the potential impact of incorporation bias related to clinician-driven outcome components, a restricted objective neonatal composite outcome (ONCO) was additionally defined for sensitivity analysis. ONCO was considered present when umbilical artery pH < 7.10 and/or a 5-min Apgar score < 7 occurred, explicitly excluding emergency cesarean delivery and NICU admission.

Clinical management of late-onset FGR

Pregnancies diagnosed with late-onset FGR were followed according to the institutional protocol. In cases requiring labor induction, cervical ripening was achieved using either a Foley balloon catheter or a vaginal slow-release prostaglandin-E2 pessary. If active labor did not occur, oxytocin infusion was initiated. Immediate cesarean delivery was performed in cases with spontaneous repetitive decelerations on cardiotocography (CTG). Antenatal corticosteroids were administered for fetal lung maturation in pregnancies requiring delivery before 34 + 6 weeks of gestation.

Cesarean delivery was also performed for other obstetric indications at the discretion of the attending obstetrician. Intrapartum fetal distress was defined based on abnormal CTG findings according to the FIGO classification system [26]. Umbilical cord arterial blood gas sampling was performed at birth whenever possible, in line with American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) recommendations [27].

Data collection

Maternal, ultrasound and perinatal data were retrieved retrospectively from the hospital information system and patient medical records. For each pregnancy, maternal age, gravidity, parity, smoking status, mode of conception (spontaneous or assisted reproductive techniques), pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), and BMI at delivery were recorded. Maternal comorbidities, including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, pregestational or gestational diabetes, and autoimmune diseases, were also documented. Gestational age at the time of late-onset FGR diagnosis and at delivery was calculated based on first-trimester ultrasound dating. Estimated fetal weight (EFW) was calculated using the Hadlock IV formula, and EFW and abdominal circumference percentiles were assigned according to the Intergrowth-21st fetal growth standards [23].

Perinatal outcomes were extracted from delivery and neonatal records and included mode of delivery, gestational age at birth, neonatal sex, birth weight and corresponding percentile, 5-min Apgar score, umbilical artery pH, admission to the NICU, and length of NICU stay. NICU admission was defined as admission within the first 24 h of life for predefined objective clinical indications, including respiratory distress requiring oxygen or ventilatory support, metabolic instability (hypoglycemia requiring intravenous therapy), suspected or confirmed neonatal sepsis, significant acidosis (umbilical artery pH < 7.10), or prematurity-related complications requiring medical support. Elective or observation-only admissions were not classified as adverse outcomes. All data were anonymized and independently verified before statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate. Comparisons among the three maternal age groups (< 25 years, 25–34 years, ≥ 35 years) were conducted using one-way ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis tests for continuous variables and chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables. The predictive performance of uterine artery pulsatility index values above the 90th and 95th percentiles for adverse perinatal outcomes was assessed using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, and the area under the curve with 95% confidence intervals was calculated. Pairwise comparisons of area under the curve values between maternal age groups were performed using the DeLong method. Multivariable logistic regression models were constructed to evaluate the association between elevated uterine artery pulsatility index and adverse perinatal outcomes across maternal age categories, adjusting for potential confounders. To assess effect modification, maternal age was additionally modeled as a continuous variable, and an interaction term between maternal age and uterine artery pulsatility index was included in the multivariable model. The same modeling strategy, including interaction testing, was applied to the restricted ONCO in sensitivity analyses. In addition to model discrimination, calibration was evaluated to assess the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed risks of composite adverse perinatal outcome. Calibration was assessed using calibration plots and quantified using the Brier score. Calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope were estimated by regressing the observed outcome on the logit of the predicted probabilities. A calibration slope lower than 1 was interpreted as potential overfitting, whereas a slope greater than 1 indicated potential underfitting. Given the known sample-size sensitivity of the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, its results were interpreted with caution. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the uterine artery pulsatility index for predicting composite adverse perinatal outcome. Net benefit was calculated across a range of clinically relevant threshold probabilities by comparing the prediction model with default strategies of treating all patients and treating none. DCA was conducted both overall and stratified by maternal age groups to assess potential age-related differences in clinical utility. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Pairwise comparisons of ROC curves using the DeLong test demonstrated that the AUC for UtA-PI was significantly higher in women aged ≥ 35 years compared with women aged < 25 years and 25–34 years for both the 90th and 95th percentile thresholds (Supplementary Table S1). No statistically significant difference was observed between the < 25 and 25–34 year groups.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis, adjusted for pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, smoking status, gestational age at delivery, and EFW percentile at diagnosis, confirmed elevated UtA-PI (≥ 90th or ≥ 95th percentile) was independently associated with an increased risk of CAPO. The association was strongest among women aged ≥ 35 years, with adjusted odds ratios rising progressively across age categories. The effect of UtA-PI ≥ 90th and ≥ 95th percentile thresholds on CAPO risk remained statistically significant after adjustment in the 25–34 and ≥ 35-year groups, but not in women younger than 25 years. Calibration analysis demonstrated good agreement between predicted probabilities and observed risks of composite adverse perinatal outcome. The Brier score for the multivariable logistic regression model was 0.18, indicating acceptable overall predictive accuracy. Calibration-in-the-large was − 0.04, suggesting minimal systematic underestimation of absolute risk, while the calibration slope was 0.93, consistent with limited overfitting. Visual inspection of the calibration plot showed close alignment between predicted probabilities and observed event rates across the range of predicted risk. Calibration plots are presented in Supplementary Figure S1. The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test did not indicate a statistically significant lack of fit (X2 = 6.8, p = 0.56).

DCA demonstrated that the uterine artery pulsatility index–based model provided a higher net benefit than both the treat-all and treat-none strategies across a range of threshold probabilities between 0.10 and 0.40. The greatest clinical net benefit was observed in women aged 35 years or older, whereas the net benefit was more limited in younger age groups. At higher threshold probabilities, the net benefit of the model approached that of the treat-none strategy. DCA stratified by maternal age are shown in Supplementary Figure S2.

When maternal age was modeled as a continuous variable, the interaction between maternal age and elevated UtA-PI (≥ 90th percentile) was statistically significant in the multivariable logistic regression model, indicating that the association between UtA-PI and composite adverse perinatal outcome strengthened with advancing maternal age (interaction OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.02–1.12). This finding indicates statistical evidence that maternal age modifies the strength of the association between UtA-PI and CAPO. Detailed results of the interaction model are presented in Supplementary Table S2.

In sensitivity analyses using the restricted ONCO, defined as umbilical artery pH < 7.10 and/or 5-min Apgar score < 7, the event occurred in 25 of 234 pregnancies (10.7%). In age-stratified multivariable models, elevated UtA-PI ≥ 90th percentile remained independently associated with the objective outcome in women aged ≥ 35 years (adjusted OR 2.6, 95% CI 1.1–6.4, p = 0.04), whereas no statistically significant association was observed in women < 25 years or 25–34 years. In the multivariable model including maternal age as a continuous variable and an interaction term, the association between elevated UtA-PI and the objective outcome was attenuated compared with the primary analysis (adjusted OR 1.84, 95% CI 0.98–3.46, p = 0.056); however, the interaction between maternal age and elevated UtA-PI remained statistically significant (interaction OR 1.05, 95% CI 1.00–1.10, p = 0.029). The detailed results of the age-stratified and interaction models are presented in Supplementary Table S3.

Discussion

The principal finding of this study is that the strength of the association and discriminative performance of UtA-PI for CAPO increased with advancing maternal age, whereas absolute CAPO rates were comparable across age groups. In women aged ≥ 35 years, elevated UtA-PI values demonstrated moderate discriminative ability, while performance was limited in younger women. Formal interaction testing supported age-related effect modification. These findings suggest that maternal age contextualizes the prognostic relevance of UtA-PI in late-onset FGR.

The stronger association between elevated UtA-PI and CAPO in older women is biologically plausible. Advanced maternal age has been associated with impaired vascular adaptation, increased arterial stiffness, and reduced spiral artery remodeling, all of which may contribute to higher uteroplacental resistance [15–17]. Placental malperfusion and defective decidualization have been reported more frequently in pregnancies of older mothers [28, 29], and age-related endothelial dysfunction may amplify Doppler abnormalities in the context of late-onset FGR [3, 18, 30]. These mechanisms provide a pathophysiological framework supporting the observed age-related differences in the prognostic relevance of UtA-PI.

Recently studies [11, 14] have reported that UtA-PI has a moderate predictive value for CAPO in pregnancies complicated by late-onset FGR. Dall’Asta et al. [13] demonstrated that mean UtA-PI above the 95th percentile was associated with NICU admission and cesarean delivery for fetal distress, although its predictive value for CAPO did not reach statistical significance. However, that study applied broader diagnostic criteria for late-onset FGR rather than the more stringent Delphi consensus definition, thereby limiting the establishment of a clearly defined diagnostic study population. Although previous findings are generally consistent with ours, none have evaluated the predictive performance of mean UtA-PI according to maternal age groups, representing a key distinction and novel aspect of our analysis.

In terms of specific perinatal outcomes, the overall incidence of NICU admission (24.8%) and emergency cesarean delivery for fetal distress (14.1%) in our cohort were comparable to rates reported in previous studies evaluating late-onset FGR cohorts [11, 13, 14]. Similarly, the rates of low 5-min Apgar score (< 7) and umbilical artery pH < 7.10 in our cohort (7.3% and 6.8%, respectively) were consistent with those described by Figueras et al. [3] and Dockree et al. [8]. These findings confirm that, adverse neonatal outcomes in late-onset FGR generally occur at clinically important frequencies, even in well-monitored tertiary care settings. The absence of significant differences in individual CAPO components across maternal age groups suggests that maternal age primarily influences the strength of the UtA-PI–CAPO association rather than the absolute risk of adverse events. This observation aligns with previous evidence indicating that clinical management and standardized clinical surveillance protocols may mitigate age-related risks in late-onset FGR [12, 13]. Importantly, maternal comorbidities, including hypertensive disorders and diabetes, were comparable across maternal age groups, making it unlikely that these factors contributed to the observed age-related differences in predictive performance.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the predictive performance of UtA-PI for CAPO in late-onset FGR across different maternal age groups. The use of standardized diagnostic criteria based on the Delphi consensus and ISUOG recommendations ensured a homogeneous study population, thereby enhancing the validity of our findings. The evaluation of both 90th and 95th percentile thresholds allowed for a more detailed interpretation of UtA-PI performance and facilitated comparison with previous studies using varied cut-offs. In recent years, several composite indices and predictive algorithms incorporating uterine artery Doppler parameters have been developed to improve risk assessment in FGR [9, 12, 20, 31–33].

Our findings indicate that the prognostic relevance and discriminative performance of UtA-PI in late-onset FGR may vary according to maternal age. While these results do not support changes to existing management protocols or the use of UtA-PI as a standalone decision-making tool, they suggest that maternal age may provide meaningful clinical context when interpreting uterine artery Doppler findings within a comprehensive risk assessment framework. Importantly, when the analysis was restricted to strictly objective neonatal outcomes, the magnitude of the age-related interaction was attenuated, although it remained statistically significant. This pattern suggests that part of the observed effect modification may reflect management-related influences; however, the persistence of the interaction in the objective analysis indicates that the association cannot be fully attributed to clinical decision-making processes alone. Accordingly, these findings should be regarded as hypothesis generating and warrant prospective validation and external confirmation in diverse healthcare settings, ideally under predefined management protocols, before being considered for clinical implementation.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design may introduce residual confounding and does not allow full separation between prognostic assessment and clinical management. Because UtA-PI findings were available to treating clinicians, incorporation bias cannot be entirely excluded, particularly for outcomes such as emergency cesarean delivery and NICU admission. Although a sensitivity analysis restricted to strictly objective neonatal outcomes was performed to mitigate this concern, residual management-related influences cannot be fully ruled out in an observational design. The composite endpoint includes components with differing clinical severity and pathophysiological pathways, which may limit mechanistic interpretation. Nevertheless, composite outcomes are widely used in FGR research to reflect the overall burden of perinatal morbidity. Finally, although the overall sample size was adequate for primary analyses, subgroup comparisons, particularly in the youngest maternal age group and in the restricted objective outcome analysis, may have been underpowere.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that the discriminative performance and strength of association of the uterine artery pulsatility index for CAPO in late-onset FGR increase with advancing maternal age. Elevated UtA-PI values, particularly those above the 90th percentile, showed greater prognostic relevance in women aged 35 years or older, while their predictive utility was limited in younger populations. These findings indicate that maternal age represents an important contextual factor when interpreting UtA-PI measurements in late-onset FGR. Incorporating maternal age into the interpretation of uterine artery Doppler findings may contribute to more refined perinatal risk stratification when considered alongside other clinical parameters. In this context, CAPO should be interpreted as reflecting overall perinatal vulnerability rather than a single mechanistic pathway. Notably, the age-related interaction persisted, although attenuated, when analyses were restricted to strictly objective neonatal outcomes, suggesting that the observed effect modification is not fully explained by management-related influences. Nevertheless, these findings remain hypothesis generating and require prospective validation before informing clinical practice.

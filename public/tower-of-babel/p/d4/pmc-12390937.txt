Understanding the landscape of cross-species transmission, epidemiology, phylogenetics, and antigenicity of HPAI strain A(H5N1) causing a recent outbreak in the USA
Authors: Chiranjib Chakraborty, Yen-Po Chen, Manojit Bhattacharya, Arpita Das, Zhi-Hong Wen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractH5N1 virus infections have repeatedly been reported among wild birds in the USA and throughout the globe. Therefore, the recent outbreak of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA is a global concern that spread among twelve states and three humans in dairy cattle farms. The virus has been reported from cow milk. In this study, we analyzed cross-species transmission, epidemiology, phylogenetics, and virus antigenicity. We depicted different phylogenetic trees using eight genome segments with 1188 genomes (n = 1188) collected from the USA, and the genome clustering at the leaf node level was shown. Here, our analysis found nine different clades. Among these nine clades, 2.3.4.4b clades have spread in the USA. The clade is currently more widespread than the others. Similarly, we have also estimated the transmission in the USA, which shows the cross-species transmission. The study shows the conservation pattern, mutation, and epitopes of two surface proteins, HA and NA. On the other hand, we depicted some significant mutations in different proteins during the outbreak. We found about 11 B-cell epitopes of HA, 15 B-cell epitopes of NA protein, and a possible epitope patch in A-chain of HA. These results suggest that the epitope might be effective for vaccine design against circulating HPAI strain A(H5N1) virus.Keywords: H5N1 virus, Epidemiology, Antigenicity, Cross-species transmission

Introduction

The recent transmission of HPAI (Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza) strain A(H5N1) to cattle on dairy farms in the USA has become a significant concern. The virus has transmitted to three humans from dairy cows. Presently, it is also a significant concern not only in the United States but also throughout the world. In 1996, the virus was first isolated in the Guangdong region of China. During this time, a transmission in domestic poultry was also observed, and the goose/Guangdong (Gs/Gd) lineage was identified.1, 2, 3 The WHO (World Health Organization) reported that the virus has expanded its geographical range and infected poultry in various parts of Asia, particularly in East and Southeast Asia. It causes severe human disease in more than 100 people.2, 4 At the same time, it has been noted that the HPAI subtype H5N1 first caused bird outbreaks and occasionally infected humans. Therefore, these three recent human transmissions are alarming to the US population. From 2020 to 2021, the HPAI H5N1 virus circulated among wild birds globally, causing infections in poultry and other animals.5, 6 Over the past twenty years, infection with the HPAI A(H5N1) strain in humans has been reported in several countries worldwide. During transmission to the different countries, there was a broad spectrum of clinical severity, with 50 % cumulative case fatality reported in more than 23 countries.7, 8

The present transmission in the USA represents the first widespread outbreak of HPAI strain A(H5N1) with the clade 2.3.4.4b in cows. Humans often come into contact with cattle on farms. Therefore, it is a high concern for the US; the virus can spread to the human population at any time.9 The number of infected human individuals is increasing day by day. Recently, Uyeki et al. reported the infection of the first dairy farm worker with this virus in the New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM). The researchers reported that the farm worker was infected in late March 2024.8 Again, Garg et al. reported about the second worker infection in the Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report (MMWR) journal. The worker has been infected in Michigan.10, 11 Recently, Abbasi reported the third infection of a dairy worker by this virus in JAMA.12 Therefore, the virus is spreading among humans. It is now a significant concern because the virus has pandemic potential. At the same time, the virus strain was also detected in the cow's milk. Kozlov has reported that the HPAI A(H5N1) virus was detected in cow's milk.13 Therefore, it is alarming that there is a high chance that viruses may enter the human food chain.

Influenza A virus is a single-stranded, negative-sense, segmented RNA virus. The virus was characterized into different subtypes based on its antigenicity. The antigenicity of the virus depends on two surface proteins: hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA). Sixteen subtypes (H1–H16) have been identified in the HA protein of different viruses. On the other hand, nine subtypes (N1–N9) have been noted in NA protein. The viral genome has eight segments, which are PB2 (polymerase basic 2), PB1 (polymerase basic 1), PA (polymerase acidic), HA, NP (nucleoprotein), NA, M (matrix), and NS (non-structural protein). M is divided into M1 and M2, and NS into NS1 and NS2.3, 14

The virion structure of the HPAI H5N1 virus comprises three transmembrane proteins, and these three transmembrane proteins are associated with the lipid by layer. These three transmembrane proteins are HA, NA, and M2 ion channel proteins. On the other hand, the inner surface envelope M1 is placed under the lipid by layer. Eight vRNPs (viral ribonucleoproteins) are placed on the side of the virion and appropriately packaged.14, 15 From a pathogenic perspective, two types of pathogenic viruses have been identified: LPAI (Low Pathogenic Avian Influenza) and HPAI (Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza). HPAI viruses are more pathogenic than LPAI. HPAI viruses can cause severe hemorrhagic disease, and in this case, sometimes, the maximum mortality rate was noted as 100 %. It was noted that HPAI viruses evolved from LPAI viruses. It was reported that the evolution of LPAI to HPAI viruses occurred in poultry. During evolution, HPAI viruses acquired HA cleavage site insertions, and this modification facilitated systemic infection.3, 16 .

Like other viruses, the antigenic profile mostly depends on the surface protein. Similarly, the antigenic profile of virus strain A(H5N1) depends on two surface proteins, NA and HA. These two are the most immunologically significant proteins.17, 18 Empirical evidence suggests that the antigenic surface proteins evolve rapidly to create antigenic variation. New antigenic variants have been developed from the antigenic variation. These are two surface proteins of strain A(H5N1), so their antigenic variants have been created. The Influenza virus antigenicity depends upon two proteins' antigenic variants created through evolution. It was reported that antigenic evolution occurred in tandem with the virus's evolution, which facilitated changes in epitopes, and ultimately, it altered antigenic clusters from one to another. These changes resulted in nAb (neutralizing antibody) escape.19, 20 Similarly, it has been reported that the antigenicity of these two proteins (HA and NA) evolves to escape the host immune response.21 The antigenicity of the HA and NA proteins has been analyzed periodically. Muñoz and Deem designed the epitopes of HA and NA and reported the dominant and subdominant epitopes of these two viral proteins.22 Plotkin and Dushoff analyzed the epitopes of the HA protein influenza A virus and illustrated the role of codon biases in the case of antigenic evolution. They also discussed HA's antigenic evolution.17 Similarly, Jiao et al. estimated the epitopes of HA protein. They have attempted to provide examples of highly conserved epitopes and broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs) targeting the HA.23 Protective epitopes, minimal epitopes of 8–11 amino acids (aa) in length, are important because they can be utilized for vaccine design. Wan et al. performed a structural characterization to analyze the length of protective epitope in the NA protein of influenza virus strain A(H1N1) pdm09 (A/California/07/2009). They found that the span of the epitope is unique, with a length of 30-aa. It is situated in an NA dimer lateral face, and in circulating A(H1N1) pdm09, the epitope is conserved.24 Considering the NA protein, Behbahani et al. designed a vaccine with linear T-cell and B-cell binding epitopes.25 However, understanding the details of the T-cell and B-cell epitope landscape is necessary.

Here, we aim to address two objectives: The first is to understand the spread of HPAI strain A(H5N1). We analyzed evolution, epidemiology, and transmission. The second objective is to understand the conservation pattern, including some significant mutations, epitopes of two surface proteins, HA and NA, and epitope modelling.

Result

Virion structure and genome organization

Virion structure and genome organization of RNA viruses are essential for their functioning and pathogenicity. It is associated with a new strain's virus replication cycle and infection capacity. Piasecka et al. stated that the replication cycle of a virus is governed by the virion structure38. In the virion, the structure of the protein, situated on the outside of the envelope, is associated with antigenicity.39 Therefore, it is necessary to understand a virus's virion structure and genome organization. Here, we depicted the schematic diagram of a virion structure of the HPAI strain A(H5N1) (Fig. 1A). At the same time, we depicted a genome organization of the virus, which shows the genome's eight segments, PB2, PB1, PA, HA, NP, NA, M, and NS (Fig. 1B). We found that PB2 is one of the most extended segments, and NS is one of the smallest segments in the genome organization. Yamayoshi et al. also noted that PB2 is one of the most elongated segments.40 Abdelwhab et al. illustrated the NS, the eighth segment, which is the smallest segment of the avian influenza virus genome (H5N1).41 .

Phylogenetic analysis using all eight genome segments of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA

A phylogenetic analysis was performed, and a radial phylogenetic tree was developed, considering each of the eight genome segments separately. The phylogenetic tree was constructed using the Nextstrain server, which fetched 1,188 USA genomes (n=1,188) directly from the database. Molecular phylogenetics was demonstrated using all eight segments of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA, including PB2 to NS (Fig. 2A to Fig. 2H). These results highlight a shift of different HPAI strains from one node terminal node to another terminal node in the phylogenetic tree. It reflects changes in adaptation across the HPAI strain A(H5N1).

More recent changes in all the genome virus segments from these phylogenetic trees indicate that the evolution of all eight segments might be faster than ever before. We also found the more recent genome clusters.

Origin, evolution and distribution of different clades of the virus

It has been noted that the Influenzas virus originated at China (during 1996) as goose/Guangdong (gs/Gd) lineage. This gs/Gd lineage is then spread to several parts of the world. Researchers have reported that the Gs/GD lineage of the HPAI H5N1 virus has evolved into ten genetically dissimilar clades of the virus (0–9) and multiple sub clades.42, 43 However, the WHO/OIE/FAO H5N1 Evolution Working Group has provided a uniform nomenclature system for the HPAI virus of H5N1.43 In this study, our analysis found nine different clades, which are 2.2.1.1a, 2.2.1.2, 2.3.2.1a, 2.3.2.1c, 2.3.4.4, 2.3.4.4b, 2.3.4.4c, 2.3.4.4e, 2.3.4.4g. Among these nine clades, 2.3.4.4b clade has spread more compared to other clades (Fig. 3A). Therefore, this clade infected a maximum number of animals and humans. However, the recent spread of the HPAI strain A(H5N1) clade is 2.3.4.4b, widely spread in the dairy cow population in nine states of the USA (Fig. 3A). Similarly, we mapped the spread of the nine clades in the global map (Fig. 3B). The figure indicated that 2.3.4.4b has spread to most parts of the world. We also found one cluster in Europe of clade 2.3.4.4b. Fig. 3A notes that the 2.3.4 clade widely spread after 2022, and this is clear from the figure. In this direction, the figure reveals the 2.3.4 clade is widely spread in the USA. On the other hand, Fig. 3B indicates that it has spread in Europe.

The phylogenetic tree shows that clade 2.3.4.4b started spreading in 2016 after its origin. Several reports indicated it is creating epidemic waves in different parts of the world, especially in Europe. Fusaro et al. indicated that HPAI A(H5Nx) of clade 2.3.4.4b occasionally creates epidemic waves in Europe, which are very difficult to forecast.44 .

Some cows have nasal discharge-like symptoms.

Discussion

Novelty of this study

In light of the recent spread of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA, we performed our analysis with two objectives to understand the spread of HPAI strain A(H5N1). Here, understanding the evolution, epidemiology, and transmission was the first objective, and understanding the conservation pattern, mutation, and epitopes of two surface proteins (HA and NA) was the second objective for our analysis. To fulfil the first objective, we utilized the genome organization, phylogenetic analysis using all eight genome segments in HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA, the evolution of the clade with the view of recent clade, epidemiology, transmission, and cross-species transmission of the recent outbreak. In the second objective, we performed the conservation, mutation, T-cell and B-cell epitopes, and structural model to understand the antigenic pattern of two surface proteins (NA and HA).

This study indicates that all eight phylogenetic trees depicted using all eight segments from PB2 to NS reflect all the segments are evolving. In this evolutionary study identified nine clades (2.2.1.1a, 2.2.1.2, 2.3.2.1a, 2.3.2.1c, 2.3.4.4, 2.3.4.4b, 2.3.4.4c, 2.3.4.4e, 2.3.4.4g). Notably, the 2.3.4.4b clade has spread globally, unlike the other clades. The 2.3.4.4b clade has been reported in North America, Canada, and Europe. A cluster of this clade has been noted in Europe. We found some highly conserved regions of HA (11 to 19 aa, 31 to 35 aa, 37–40 aa, etc.) and NA (141 to 150 aa, 211–220 aa, etc.). At the same time, we found 321 predicted T-cell epitopes and 11 B-cell epitopes for HA protein. Similarly, we noted 50 predicted T-cell epitopes and 16 B-cell epitopes for NA protein. Recently, we also reported that clade 2.3.4.4b of HPAI A(H5N1) was accountable for the present infection in the USA.50 Fusaro et al. recently reported that the clade 2.3.4.4b of HPAI A(H5Nx) has caused two catastrophic waves in Europe from 2020 to 2022. They also noted that it jumps to several species.44 Similarly, Webby and Uyeki reported the spread of clade 2.3.4.4b of HPAI A(H5N1) among birds, different marine mammals, terrestrial species, and others worldwide in 2021.51 In this study, we also reported the spread of the clade 2.3.4.4b. Therefore, our study is significant.

Other findings of this study and its implications

The genome organization provides the overall information of the eight segments of the genes, UTR, and packing signals. Strain A(H5N1) has unique packaging and packing signals that facilitate a highly sophisticated genome packaging mechanism. Researchers occasionally studied the role of packing signals in sophisticated genome packaging mechanisms.52, 53 Rasekhian et al. illustrated that, in RNA viruses, the 3′UTR region plays a significant role in translation and stability enhancement.54 In the case of the analysis of all eight genes, phylogenetics analysis indicates the clustering event of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA. It is probably indicating the gene similarity of the sequences of the virus. On the other hand, our analysis shows the HPAI strain A(H5N1) in the USA is located in the terminal node of the virus. This indicates the continuous surges in the number of animals in the last few years. Xie et al. indicate the different resurges of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in different geographic locations. However, their continuous phylogenetic analysis considering different geographical regions shows that, during mid-2020 in Eastern Europe, HPAI H5N1 might arise.3 Our clade analysis found a heavy cluster of the clade of 2.3.4.4b in the European region (Fig. 3B). At the same time, our analysis found that the 2.3.4.4b recently clade spread recently. It might have originated during the outbreak in 2011. However, Tare et al. also indicated different clades during their phylogenetic analysis55. Xie et al. found the 2.3.4.4b recent clade during their analysis3. Uyeki et al. reveal the virus with clade 2.3.4.4b infected in dairy cattle and it was detected from the sample. On the other hand, the virus with clade 2.3.4.4b was identified in unpasteurized milk samples8. Further, Guan et al. research suggests that the virus strain H5N1, isolated from the milk sample, can also infect the mice.56 It shows evidence of cross-species transmission. Our study also depicts the cross-species transmission of the virus among cattle, humans, and alpacas.

To fulfil the second objective, we evaluated conservation patterns, mutations, B-cell and T-cell epitopes, and the structural model to understand the antigenic patterns of two surface proteins (NA and HA). We found several conserved residues in HA and NA proteins during conservation pattern analysis. At the same time, some epitopes are conserved if we compare the results of our epitope analysis and conserved pattern analysis. Jiao et al. also found conserved epitopes in HA protein. They also found the distribution of the conserved protective epitope of this protein.23 Luczo and Spackman mapped epitopes in the avian influenza viruses' NA and HA of H5 and H7, illustrating the antigenic drift.21 Our study also helps to understand further, the distribution of both proteins' conserved protective epitope. From an antigenic point of view, two surface proteins, HA and NA, are essential outer surface proteins and determine the antigenic profile of HPAI strain A(H5N1). Understanding the evolutionarily conserved pattern can provide information on changes in the residue, which occur slowly during evolution. Therefore, it provides information on structural stability and might be compared with the protein's function.57, 58 Scientists have analyzed the conservation of different proteins such as GPCR,59 IRS family,60, 61 PI3K (phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase)62 SARS-CoV-2 Spike63 and correlate with the function of the protein.

At the same time, we analyzed some important mutations in the viral protein across all eight genes of the recent strain from the USA. Our analysis shows that the USA's HPAI strain A(H5N1) is mutating. The viral infections have been noted frequently throughout the globe in different animals from time to time. We also reported the cross-species transmission. Therefore, it is a concern that the strain of virus mutation can jump into any animal and find the host quickly.

Conclusion

Avian influenza has shown its devastating effect from time to time throughout the globe. The recent outbreak in the USA of HPAI strain A(H5N1) in dairy cows across twelve states. Here, three humans were detected as positive for the virus, having been infected through cow's milk. Therefore, the spread of the infection through cow's milk remains a substantial challenge in the USA. It might quickly spread to cattle and humans.64 At the same time, the CDC and the APHIS reported one alpaca was infected as of June 11, 2024. Therefore, it is cross-species transmission. On the other hand, the virus was detected from the cow's milk.13 Therefore, immediate surveillance is necessary to stop the spread of the virus. At the same time, we must adopt one health approach and some essential strategies, such as prompt response and strict biosecurity measures to prevent transmission and protect lives and the environment from HPAI strain A(H5N1).

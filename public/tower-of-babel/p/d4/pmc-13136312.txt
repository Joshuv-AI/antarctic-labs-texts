Identification and validation of NETs-associated biomarkers in osteoporosis with diabetes
Authors: Luyan Zhang, Liang Hao, Yu Wang, Yiqiong Shi, Wenzhen Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Osteoporosis and diabetes represent major global public health challenges. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) serve as key components of the innate immune system by capturing and eliminating pathogens. This exploratory study aimed to preliminarily identify biomarkers associated with NETs in osteoporosis with diabetes and to provide initial insights into underlying molecular mechanisms. A transcriptomic sequencing dataset was integrated to analyze the molecular profiles of comorbid osteoporosis and diabetes (OP-DM). The NETs-related genes (NETs-RGs) were curated from previous literature. As a pilot investigation, biomarkers were identified through differential analysis, machine learning, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC). These candidate biomarkers were further evaluated by qRT-PCR and ELISA. Subsequently, molecular regulatory network construction, immune infiltration analysis, enrichment analysis, and drug prediction were conducted to generate hypotheses. S100A12 and SLC25A37 were identified as potential biomarkers. Their significant upregulation at the protein level (S100A12 and SLC25A37) was observed in an independent cohort. Enrichment analysis suggested that S100A12 was significantly enriched in 68 pathways, including “ECM-receptor interaction” and “maturity onset diabetes of the young”. SLC25A37 was significantly enriched in 54 pathways, primarily including “ribosome” and “Toll-like receptor signaling pathway”. A total of 7 immune cell types exhibited differences between the two groups. Furthermore, the XIST-hsa-miR-146a-5p-S100A12 and XIST-hsa-miR-7-5-SLC25A37 axes were suggested to have potential regulatory roles. Drugs such as rimegepant and eptinezumab were associated with biomarkers. This preliminary study suggests that S100A12 and SLC25A37 may serve as candidate biomarkers associated with NETs in osteoporosis with diabetes, providing a preliminary theoretical foundation for future larger-scale studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-44721-z.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and persistent hyperglycemia, leading to a spectrum of systemic complications arising from complex genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors1. Current epidemiological data indicate that approximately 1 in 11 adults worldwide is living with diabetes. The global prevalence is projected to reach 629 million by 2045, representing a staggering public health burden2. Notably, osteoporosis (OP), has emerged as a frequent and severe complication of diabetes. This condition, characterized by impaired bone formation and accelerated bone resorption, significantly increases skeletal fragility and fracture risk, particularly among the elderly population3,4.

The deleterious impact of diabetes on bone health is multifactorial, involving chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, the accumulation of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) in collagen, and dysregulated insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I) signaling5. Despite the clinical significance of diabetes-associated osteoporosis, the precise molecular crosstalk between metabolic dysfunction and bone remodeling remains partially understood, necessitating further investigation into novel regulatory mechanisms6.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are web-like structures composed of decondensed chromatin associated with histones and antimicrobial proteins, such as myeloperoxidase (MPO) and neutrophil elastase (NE)7. While NETs are essential for innate host defense, their aberrant formation contributes to the pathogenesis of various inflammatory and autoimmune diseases8,9. Recent evidence suggests that NETs exacerbate tissue damage in diabetic complications—such as nephropathy and atherosclerosis—by activating the NLRP3 inflammasome, promoting glomerular endothelial dysfunction, and driving macrophage-mediated inflammation10,11. Interestingly, emerging studies have also implicated neutrophil function and NETosis in the pharmacological mechanisms of anti-osteoporotic treatments, such as raloxifene12. Consequently, investigating the role of NETs in the context of comorbid diabetes and osteoporosis (OP-DM) may reveal critical insights into their shared pathophysiology.

In this exploratory study, we integrated transcriptomic sequencing data from patients with osteoporosis and diabetes with a curated set of 69 NETs-related genes. These genes represent the core machinery of NETosis—including ROS production and chromatin decondensation—which are theoretically linked to the metabolic and inflammatory dysregulation observed in bone remodeling. As a pilot investigation, we employed differential expression analysis and machine learning algorithms to identify potential NETs-related biomarkers. Our analysis further characterized the biological functions of these candidates, their correlation with immune infiltration, and potential therapeutic agents. These findings provide preliminary insights and a theoretical foundation for the development of targeted diagnostic and treatment strategies for patients suffering from both osteoporosis and diabetes.

Materials and methods

Collection of data

Peripheral blood samples were collected from patients who underwent bone mineral density (BMD) testing at the People’s Hospital of Gansu Province between August 2023 and September 2024.

A total of 30 samples were utilized for transcriptome sequencing, comprising 15 peripheral blood samples from patients with comorbid osteoporosis and diabetes and 15 healthy controls. The control group was defined by a spinal BMD T-score ≥ -1.0, while the osteoporosis group was identified by a spinal BMD T-score ≤ -2.5. Raw sequencing data were deposited in the ArrayExpress database (Accession: E-MTAB-16559). Furthermore, 69 NETs-related genes (NETs-RGs) were curated from established literature13, representing core NETosis processes such as chromatin decondensation, oxidative stress, and granular protein release (Supplementary Table S2). Primer sequences for qRT-PCR are provided in Supplementary Table S3.

RNA extraction and library generation

Total RNA was isolated and purified using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The mRNA with poly(A) tails was captured, fragmented, and converted into cDNA. Double-stranded DNA synthesis was performed using E. coli DNA polymerase I (NEB, Cat. No. M0209) and RNase H (NEB, Cat. No. M0297). After size selection and purification, libraries were digested with UDG enzyme (NEB, Cat. No. M0280) and amplified via PCR to achieve an insert size of 300 ± 50 bp. Paired-end sequencing (PE150) was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq™ 6000 platform (LC Bio Technology Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China).

Differential expression analysis

Quality control of raw data was performed using fastp, followed by alignment to the human reference genome (GRCh38) using HISAT2. Transcript assembly and quantification were executed with StringTie using FPKM values. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the DESeq2 package (v1.38.0) with thresholds of ∣log2 FC∣ > 0.5 and P < 0.05. Visualization, including volcano plots and heatmaps of the top regulated DEGs, was performed using the ggplot2 package.

Candidate genes acquisition and enrichment analysis

The ggvenn package (v 0.1.9) was used to identify candidate genes by intersecting DEGs with NETs-RGs. Enrichment analysis of candidate genes was conducted using the clusterProfiler package (v 4.7.1.003), including GO and KEGG enrichments14,15(P < 0.05). The top 5 GO terms with the smallest p-values and 12 KEGG pathways were displayed. The STRING database was used to construct the PPI network (confidence score = 0.4).

Biomarkers acquisition

To mitigate the risk of over-fitting inherent in small datasets and ensure the robustness of our findings, we employed ensemble machine learning approaches specifically selected for their ability to identify stable features in high-dimensional data. In the transcriptome sequencing dataset of all samples, the Boruta package (v 8.0.0) was used for Boruta analysis of candidate genes with parameters set to P = 0.01 and maxRuns = 300. Boruta, an extension of the random forest algorithm, is particularly effective for small cohorts as it performs all-relevant feature selection by comparing original attributes’ importance with random shuffle counterparts. The results were visualized using the YSX package (v 0.1.0), and the identified genes were labeled as feature gene 1. XGBoost analysis of the candidate genes was performed using the xgboost package (v 1.7.3.1) with parameters set to max_depth = 2, eta = 0.3, and nround = 25. As an ensemble learning method based on gradient-boosted decision trees, XGBoost enhances predictive accuracy while minimizing over-fitting through its integrated regularization components. Candidate biomarkers were identified by the intersection of feature genes 1 and 2 with the ggvenn package (v 0.1.9). The pROC package (v 1.18.0) was used to perform ROC curve analysis on the candidate biomarkers. Candidate biomarkers with an AUC value > 0.7 were identified as potential biomarkers.

Functional enrichment analysis of biomarkers

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed using the clusterProfiler package (v 4.7.1.003) to identify enriched pathways. The correlation coefficients between each biomarker and all genes in the transcriptome sequencing dataset were computed using the stats package (v 4.2.2). These correlation coefficients were ranked to generate a list of genes corresponding to each biomarker, with thresholds set at |NES| > 1 and adj.P < 0.05. Top 3 pathways with the largest |NES| were visualized.

Experimental validation of biomarkers by qRT-PCR and ELISA

The expression levels of S100A12 and SLC25A37 were validated in an independent cohort (n = 5 per group). Total RNA was extracted from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) using TRIzol. RNA purity and concentration were assessed via spectrophotometry (A260/A280 ratio: 1.53–1.78). cDNA was synthesized using 500 ng of total RNA (TaKaRa), and qRT-PCR was performed on a QuantStudio 5 system with SYBR Green Master Mix. GAPDH served as the internal control, and relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. For protein-level validation, serum concentrations of S100A12 and MFRN1 (encoded by SLC25A37) were measured using commercial ELISA kits (Ruixin Bio, Cat. No.RX102995H and RX107074H) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

Immune infiltration analysis

The ssGSEA algorithm from the GSVA package (v 1.46.0) was used to calculate the scores for 28 immune cell types in the transcriptome sequencing dataset of all samples. The score differences between the osteoporosis with diabetes group and the control group were compared (P < 0.05). Finally, Spearman correlation analysis, performed using the stats package (v 4.2.2), examined the relationships between biomarkers and differential immune cell types, as well as between the immune cell types themselves (|r| > 0.30, P < 0.05) .

Regulation network analysis

The biomarker-related microRNAs (miRNAs) were predicted using the miRTarBase database. The miRNA-related lncRNAs were predicted using the StarBase database, and the filtering criterion was clipExpNum > 8. The biomarker-related drugs were identified using the DGIdb database. Finally, mRNA-miRNA, mRNA-miRNA-lncRNA, and drug-mRNA networks were visualized using Cytoscape (v 3.9.1) .

Statistical analysis

Bioinformatics analyses were conducted using R (v4.2.2), while experimental data processing and statistical evaluations were performed using GraphPad Prism (v10.6.0). The normality of all experimental datasets was evaluated via the Shapiro-Wilk test. Relative mRNA expression levels from qRT-PCR were normalized to GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCT method and are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). Due to their non-normal distribution, inter-group differences were assessed using the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test. Conversely, ELISA data followed a normal distribution and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD); differences between groups were evaluated using the unpaired Student’s t-test. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01).

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

The demographic and clinical characteristics of all participants are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. In both the discovery and validation cohorts, patients in the OP-DM group were significantly older and exhibited higher BMI and HbA1c levels compared to the healthy controls (P < 0.05). Additionally, lumbar BMD T-scores were significantly lower in the OP-DM group than in the control group (P < 0.05).

Acquisition of 18 candidate genes and enrichment analysis

In the transcriptomic sequencing dataset, 2,210 DEGs were identified, including 1,458 upregulated and 752 downregulated genes in the OP-DM group (Fig. 1A–B). By intersecting these DEGs with 69 NETs-RGs, 18 candidate genes were prioritized (Fig. 1C). Enrichment analyses indicated that these candidate genes were significantly associated with 731 GO terms and 58 KEGG pathways. Key GO terms included “leukocyte migration,” “secretory granule membrane,” and “pattern recognition receptor activity” (Fig. 1D). Prominent KEGG pathways included “neutrophil extracellular trap formation” and the “IL-17 signaling pathway” (Fig. 1E). A PPI network consisting of 18 nodes and 43 edges was constructed; the top three hub genes identified by connectivity scores were MPO, MMP9, and TNF (Fig. 1F).

Selection of S100A12 and SLC25A37 as biomarkers via machine learning

Using the Boruta algorithm, two feature genes were selected (Fig. 2A), while the XGBoost algorithm identified 12 feature genes (Fig. 2B). The intersection of these two feature sets yielded two candidate biomarkers: S100A12 and SLC25A37 (Fig. 2C). In the discovery cohort, both biomarkers demonstrated promising discriminatory potential, with Area Under the Curve (AUC) values of 0.782 for S100A12 and 0.800 for SLC25A37 (Fig. 2D–E), supporting their role as potential diagnostic indicators.

Functional landscape of candidate biomarkers

GSEA revealed that S100A12 was significantly enriched in 68 pathways, including “ECM-receptor interaction” and “maturity-onset diabetes of the young.” SLC25A37 was significantly enriched in 54 pathways, predominantly “ribosome,” “Leishmania infection,” and the “Toll-like receptor signaling pathway” (Fig. 3A–B). These findings suggest that these biomarkers are involved in metabolic and immune-related signaling during disease progression.

Exploring Differences in Immune Cell Types

Significant differences were observed in the infiltration scores of seven immune cell types between the OP-DM and control groups, including regulatory T cells, neutrophils, and monocytes (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4A). Spearman correlation analysis showed that neutrophils exhibited the strongest positive correlation with eosinophils (r = 0.82, P < 0.01), whereas activated CD8 T cells showed a strong negative correlation with eosinophils (r = -0.82, P < 0.01) (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, S100A12 was significantly correlated with five immune cell types (strongest with eosinophils, r = 0.75, P < 0.05), and SLC25A37 was significantly correlated with four types (strongest with neutrophils, r = 0.71, P < 0.05) (Fig. 4C).

Exploratory Regulatory Network and Drug Candidate Prediction

To hypothesize the post-transcriptional regulation of the biomarkers, we constructed potential miRNA-lncRNA networks and identified drug candidates (Supplementary Fig. S1). A total of 59 miRNAs were predicted to associate with S100A12 and SLC25A37, with hsa-miR-146a-5p and hsa-miR-7-5p emerging as central regulatory nodes (Supplementary Fig. S1A–B). Additionally, drug-gene association analysis identified potential therapeutic agents, such as rimegepant and eptinezumab (Supplementary Fig. S1C).

Experimental Validation by qRT-PCR and ELISA

In an independent validation cohort, we assessed the expression of the identified biomarkers at both the mRNA and protein levels. The qRT-PCR results showed an upward trend in S100A12 expression in the OP-DM group compared to controls; however, due to high inter-individual variability, this difference did not reach statistical significance (P > 0.05) (Fig. 5A). Similarly, the mRNA expression of SLC25A37 exhibited variability without a statistically significant overall change (P > 0.05).

Validation of S100A12 and SLC25A37 expression. (A) Relative mRNA expression levels of S100A12 and SLC25A37 determined by qRT-PCR. (B) Serum protein concentrations of S100A12 and MFRN1 (SLC25A37) measured by ELISA.

Notably, protein-level validation via ELISA yielded more robust results. Serum S100A12 protein levels were significantly elevated in patients (843.2 ± 117.5 ng/mL) compared to controls (404.9 ± 167.1 ng/mL) (P = 0.0014). Similarly, serum levels of MFRN1 (the protein product of SLC25A37) were significantly higher in the OP-DM group (10.0 ± 1.5 ng/mL) than in the control group (5.2 ± 1.8 ng/mL) (P = 0.0023) (Fig. 5B). These findings suggest that while mRNA expression may be transient or heterogeneous, the protein-level elevation of these biomarkers is consistently associated with the comorbid condition.

Discussion

Diabetes and osteoporosis are closely interconnected conditions16, with neutrophil extracellular trap-related genes (NETs-RGs) potentially playing a significant role in this association. In this study, we employed transcriptome sequencing to identify biomarkers linked to comorbid osteoporosis and diabetes (OP-DM), elucidating the molecular mechanisms of biomarkers in both diseases and offering novel insights for diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

We identified S100A12 and SLC25A37 as two pivotal biomarkers. While their specific involvement in the comorbidity of osteoporosis and diabetes has remained largely elusive, their roles are supported by robust biological evidence. S100A12, a calcium-binding protein of the S100 family, orchestrates diverse cellular processes, including inflammatory responses and bone metabolism17. It mediates inflammatory responses primarily by interacting with the multiligand receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE). Compelling evidence suggests a strong association between S100A12 and various inflammatory and metabolic disorders17,18. Notably, recent research has confirmed that the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway is a critical mediator in the pathogenesis of diabetic osteoporosis, where its activation promotes a pro-inflammatory environment and bone resorption19. Beyond its systemic effects, S100A12 has been implicated in exacerbating tissue injury by triggering NETosis, a process driven by the Annexin A5-calcium signaling axis20. Aberrant expression of S100A12 is consistently observed in diabetes21, mirroring our findings and highlighting its potential as a molecular bridge: it may be induced by hyperglycemia to trigger NETosis, which in turn activates the RAGE pathway to accelerate bone loss. By triggering NETosis through the Annexin A5-calcium axis20, S100A12 transforms from a simple inflammatory marker into a functional driver of systemic pathological damage, rather than being a passive byproduct of increased neutrophil counts. Collectively, these observations emphasize S100A12 as a promising therapeutic target for managing osteoporosis associated with diabetes.

SLC25A37, a mitochondrial inner membrane carrier protein, is critical for iron homeostasis, mediating transmembrane iron transport into mitochondria for heme and iron-sulfur cluster synthesis22. It enhances the stability of MFRN1 (the protein product of SLC25A37) and promotes mitochondrial iron uptake23. While its aberrant expression has been linked to myelodysplastic syndromes24, cancer cell homeostasis25, and liver fibrosis26, its role in skeletal health is emerging. SLC25A37 is implicated in joint-related pathologies like osteoarthritis27, and its importance in osteoporosis is underscored by the fundamental link between mitochondrial dysfunction and bone loss. A recent comprehensive study highlighted that mitochondrial integrity is a central hub in the progression of osteoporosis over the last decade28.

Our observations of altered SLC25A37 expression further support its role in metabolic disorders, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed significant enrichment of S100A12 in 68 pathways, notably “ECM-receptor interactions”, while SLC25A37 was enriched in 54 pathways, including “Ribosome,” “leishmaniasis infection,” and the “Toll-like receptor signaling (TLR) pathway.” Essential for development and physiology, cell-ECM interactions involve bidirectional mechanotransduction: cells remodel the ECM, while the physicochemical properties of the ECM, in turn, influence cell behavior29. Impaired mechanosensitivity and mechanoresponsiveness in these interactions are implicated in aging, inflammation, and cancer30. Similarly, TLRs are key sentinels of the innate and adaptive immune systems, recognizing invading pathogens via molecular pattern recognition31. TLR signaling, mediated by distinct intracellular pathways involving adaptor proteins and kinases, culminates in transcription factor activation and inflammatory responses32. Previous research indicates that ECM-receptor interactions may affect bone metabolism through the AGE-RAGE pathway33, and that the TLR signaling pathway has been implicated in osteoporosis with diabetes by mediating inflammation and inhibiting osteogenesis34,35. While our study does not provide direct functional validation of TLR expression, the enrichment of this pathway serves as a relevant contextual background. Notably, S100A12—which we validated at the protein level—is known to function as a ligand for TLR4, suggesting that the TLR axis may represent a potential, albeit currently speculative, signaling cascade that links neutrophil-driven inflammation to bone resorption in OP-DM. Future research is required to definitively establish the mechanistic contribution of the TLR pathway in this specific pathological context.

We also observed differential infiltration of seven immune cell types between the OP-DM and control samples, with both biomarkers showing significant correlation with neutrophils. Neutrophils—G-CSF-regulated innate immune cells—are increasingly recognized as key players in diabetes36. Under diabetic conditions, activated neutrophils perpetuate chronic inflammation, exacerbate insulin resistance, impair β-cell function37, induce oxidative stress via ROS38, and promote autoimmunity in type 1 diabetes39. Furthermore, their interactions with adipose tissue are known to further worsen systemic metabolic dysfunction40. Similarly, in bone disorders such as osteoporosis, neutrophils enhance osteoclastogenesis through RANKL/IL-6 signaling and disrupt bone remodeling under estrogen-deficient conditions41. These pleiotropic roles position neutrophils as central regulators in the pathophysiology of OP-DM, bridging the gap between metabolic dysfunction and skeletal deterioration.

As an exploratory extension, we predicted a potential miRNA-lncRNA regulatory landscape (Supplementary Figure S1A-B). While these results are entirely based on in silico databases and remain hypothesis-generating, existing evidence indicates that hsa-miR-146a-5p modulates inflammation and apoptosis in diabetes and osteoporosis42,43, while hsa-miR-7-5p is involved in neuroinflammation and metabolic processes44–46.

Beyond the biomarker validation, exploratory drug-biomarker association analysis (Supplementary Figure S1C) provided novel therapeutic perspectives. Among the predicted candidates, rimegepant and eptinezumab were identified as being associated with our key biomarkers. These agents primarily target the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) signaling pathway, which has recently emerged as a pivotal regulator of the neuro-angiogenic axis in bone homeostasis, particularly in diabetic conditions. Mechanistically, CGRP promotes bone formation by stimulating the coupling of Type H vessels and osteoprogenitors47. However, diabetes-induced sensory neuropathy leads to a significant deficiency in CGRP signaling, which in turn suppresses bone regeneration and exacerbates diabetic bone loss47. The association between these CGRP-related drugs and our biomarkers (S100A12 and SLC25A37) is likely not incidental. Rather, it suggests that these biomarkers may participate in a complex regulatory network where S100A12-mediated inflammation and SLC25A37-driven mitochondrial oxidative stress further compromise the neuro-angiogenic microenvironment already weakened by CGRP deficiency. These results highlight the potential of targeting the CGRP axis as a novel therapeutic direction for treating osteoporosis with diabetes. While direct validation of these drug effects—such as demonstrating their ability to modulate biomarker expression in patient-derived PBMCs—was not performed in this pilot study, the convergence of our bioinformatic predictions with recent independent experimental evidence47 provides a biologically plausible foundation for future drug-repurposing investigations.

Experimental validation using qRT-PCR and ELISA yielded insightful yet distinct outcomes. Although qRT-PCR showed an upward trend in mRNA expression of S100A12 and SLC25A37 in the disease group, these changes did not reach statistical significance. In contrast, ELISA results suggested a significant elevation in serum levels of S100A12 and MFRN1 (the protein product of SLC25A37) in OP-DM patients compared to controls (P < 0.01).

The discrepancy between mRNA and protein levels implies the involvement of post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms—such as enhanced translation, improved protein stability, or miRNA-mediated control—that may augment protein output independent of transcript abundance. Furthermore, neutrophils are known to pre-synthesize and store abundant S100A12 proteins in cytoplasmic granules during maturation. Upon activation or NETosis, these stored proteins are rapidly released into the circulation, a process that does not strictly require immediate de novo mRNA transcription. This biological characteristic explains why protein-level elevation is more pronounced and statistically significant than mRNA changes in our findings. This finding aligns with and substantiates our bioinformatics predictions, confirming the dysregulation of both biomarkers in osteoporosis with diabetes.

Nevertheless, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample sizes for both the discovery cohort (n = 30) and the validation cohort (n = 10) are relatively small, which may limit the statistical power and the generalizability of our findings. The lack of statistical significance in the qRT-PCR results further highlights this limitation, suggesting that individual biological heterogeneity might have obscured the transcript-level differences in a small sample. To mitigate the risk of over-fitting associated with small datasets, we employed robust machine learning algorithms (Boruta and XGBoost) to identify the most stable features. Second, there was a significant mismatch in age and BMI between the disease and control groups (Supplementary Table S1). Given the clinical challenge of recruiting elderly individuals free of both osteopenia and metabolic abnormalities, young healthy adults were utilized as a ‘baseline’ for peak bone mass and metabolic health. Although age-related immunosenescence could potentially influence gene expression, the biomarkers S100A12 and SLC25A37 identified here are mechanistically linked to hyperglycemia-induced oxidative stress and bone remodeling rather than chronological aging alone.

Furthermore, the current study remains primarily descriptive and exploratory in nature. The high AUC values observed in our ROC analysis should be interpreted as exploratory flags for candidate selection rather than definitive clinical predictive power, pending further validation in larger prospective cohorts. While we have clinically validated the dysregulation of S100A12 and MFRN1 (SLC25A37) at the protein level, direct functional experiments were not performed to elucidate the exact causal mechanisms. Specifically, the interplay between these biomarkers and the CGRP-mediated neuro-angiogenic axis or the S100A12-RAGE pathway—as suggested by recent studies19,47—warrants further in vitro and in vivo investigation. It is also important to note that, while S100A12 is a known driver of NETosis20, the involvement of NETs in this study was primarily inferred through bioinformatics algorithms and the quantification of S100A12. We acknowledge the absence of direct NET-specific markers, such as MPO-DNA complexes or citrullinated histones (cit-H3), which provide the most definitive evidence of NET formation. Consequently, our findings should be viewed as providing strong associative evidence for NET-mediated involvement in OP-DM, rather than direct visualization of NETosis in situ. Beyond these aspects, the molecular regulatory networks and drug predictions presented in the Supplementary Material are purely computational and lack experimental validation in the present study. These exploratory findings should be interpreted with caution and serve only as a roadmap for future hypothesis testing.

Finally, as an exploratory pilot study, the high statistical significance (P < 0.01) observed in the ELISA validation is encouraging; however, further large-scale, multi-center, and age-matched prospective studies are essential to confirm these findings and clarify the underlying molecular pathways.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study identified S100A12 and SLC25A37 as pivotal neutrophil extracellular trap-related (NET-related) biomarkers in comorbid osteoporosis and diabetes (OP-DM) through integrated transcriptomic analysis and machine learning. By elucidating their correlations with the immune microenvironment and predicting a complex miRNA-lncRNA-drug regulatory network, we have provided novel mechanistic insights into the pathogenesis of OP-DM, particularly regarding the potential therapeutic targeting of the CGRP axis. Although our findings are supported by preliminary clinical validation at the protein level, the inherent limitations of in silico predictions and the modest sample size necessitate further large-scale prospective studies and in-depth functional experiments to definitively establish the causal roles of these biomarkers. Overall, these results establish a biologically plausible foundation for future research aimed at optimizing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for patients suffering from the dual burden of metabolic and skeletal dysfunction.

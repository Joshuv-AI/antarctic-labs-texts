siRNA approaches in HBV: From antigen reduction to seroconversion
Authors: Cao Hua, Saima Aftab, Abdul Rahaman, Zeeshan Abbas, Zulqarnain Baloch
Published: 2025

Main text

Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, a significant global health concern, affects over 300 million people worldwide and puts them at risk for cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and liver failure.1 The available antiviral treatments, principally nucleoside analogs and interferons, can improve patient outcomes by suppressing viral replication but rarely lead to a functional cure.2 Unfortunately, persistent elimination of hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), a key marker of infection, with or without seroconversion to protective antibodies, is rarely achieved. Thus, long-term immunological control and remission remain limited by the continued presence of HBsAg. One effective method for inhibiting HBV transcripts, reducing antigen levels, and potentially enhancing immune responses is RNA interference. Among the most promising treatment strategies is the use of small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), particularly those conjugated to N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) for targeted delivery to hepatocytes.3 Recently, the GalNAc-conjugated siRNA RBD1016 has shown strong preclinical evidence indicating its potential to lead to a functional cure for HBV, surpassing mere antigen suppression.4

Targeting the HBx transcript, RBD1016 has demonstrated strong suppression of the main HBV genotypes (A–E), as well as strains resistant to nucleosides. It significantly reduced both HBsAg and HBV DNA in HepG2 cells. Single subcutaneous doses of up to 9 mg/kg produced substantial, dose-dependent reductions: ∼3.22 log in HBsAg, ∼3.40 log in HBV DNA, and an ∼87% reduction in HBeAg, with effects sustained for at least 85 days in two well-established mouse models: transgenic HBV mice, which represent chronic infection, and adeno-associated virus (AAV)-HBV mice, which mimic acute high-titer infections. Notably, significant suppression (∼3.7 log reduction) was also achieved with less frequent dosing regimens (every 4–8 weeks), with effects lasting until day 169.4 These results highlight the exceptional durability and potency of RBD1016, suggesting the potential for convenient therapeutic dosing regimens.

The ability of RBD1016 to promote seroconversion is perhaps its most remarkable discovery, especially since achieving this with siRNA monotherapy has historically been challenging. In contrast to a single dosage of 9 mg/kg (∼128 mIU/mL), repeated dosing of 3 mg/kg once a week for 3 weeks in the AAV-HBV animal model resulted in earlier and higher HBsAb titers, peaking at around 981 mIU/mL. Importantly, these antibody levels remained stable for 247 days, indicating long-term antigen suppression (>99% decrease in HBV DNA and HBsAg).4 This finding is revolutionary because, while previous siRNA strategies decreased antigen burden,5 none consistently led to the production of de novo HBsAb.4 These findings suggest that RBD1016 effectively relaxes suppression and re-engages the host’s immune response by lowering antigen levels below the thresholds of immunological tolerance, bringing us closer to a potential functional cure. When interpreting these findings, it is essential to consider the broader therapeutic context. Traditional nucleoside analogs are effective at inhibiting viral replication; however, they do not eliminate integrated HBV DNA or covalently closed circular DNA.4 As a result, antigen production often persists. Although interferon therapy can lead to seroconversion in a minority of patients, it has significant drawbacks, including side effects and inconsistent response rates.6 In contrast, siRNAs work upstream by directly targeting viral transcripts, which reduces the production of viral proteins and particles.7 Lowering the levels of circulating HBsAg may help alleviate immune exhaustion, enabling the host immune system to mount a stronger antiviral response.4 This immunological reawakening, a consequence of antigen reduction, is significant.

The synergy of RBD1016 with the existing standard of care is an important finding. Sustained HBV DNA reductions of up to ∼4.91 log were observed even after withdrawal of entecavir (ETV), suggesting enhanced suppression when combined with ETV.4 This synergy indicates that siRNA therapeutics can be effectively integrated into combination therapies, enhancing the potential for a cure by simultaneously reducing viral load and alleviating immunological exhaustion. The unique profile of RBD1016 is further emphasized by pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic modeling. In mice receiving a subcutaneous dosage of 3 mg/kg, liver concentrations remained detectable until day 85, while plasma levels rapidly peaked and then fell within hours. The most significant mRNA suppression occurred on day 29, suggesting a gradual yet lasting pharmacodynamic effect. Preliminary toxicology studies in animal models demonstrated a favorable safety profile, supporting its further development.4

The findings from RBD1016 offer significant insights into the HBV therapeutic landscape. First, they support the notion that immunological reactivation requires a substantial and prolonged decrease in antigen levels. Second, the results indicate that siRNAs can act as immunological modulators, bridging the gap between suppression and seroconversion, in addition to their role as antiviral drugs. Finally, the study shows that siRNA regimens are appealing for managing chronic diseases because they can be dosed infrequently for long-term durability. However, there are still challenges to address. Results from rodent models may not effectively translate to the more complex setting of chronic HBV in humans, where immunological exhaustion is profound and multifactorial. To maximize the rates of functional cures, combination therapies that include immunomodulators, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or therapeutic vaccinations may be necessary. As the development of RBD1016 progresses, it is also important to carefully monitor its long-term safety, potential for viral resistance, and future accessibility.

Conclusion

The compelling preclinical data for RBD1016 could represent a paradigm shift for the HBV field. It stands as a pinnacle of the GalNAc-siRNA platform, demonstrating not only best-in-class suppression of viral antigens but also, more importantly, a unique capacity to catalyze seroconversion, the definitive hallmark of a functional cure. This reinforces the critical hypothesis that profound and sustained antigen reduction is necessary to reverse immune exhaustion and bridge the gap from suppression to cure. However, the journey from robust rodent models to human patients, who face entrenched immunological depletion, remains long and complex. To maximize functional cure rates, the future likely lies in combining RBD1016 with immunomodulators, checkpoint inhibitors, or therapeutic vaccinations. As this agent advances into clinical trials as a cornerstone of such combination regimens, its long-term safety, potential for resistance, and accessibility must be rigorously evaluated. Despite these challenges, RBD1016 provides a powerful rationale for optimism, mapping a clear and promising path forward in the long-standing quest to cure chronic HBV.

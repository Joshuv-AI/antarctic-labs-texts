Less is more: A single-dose, non-waiting neoadjuvant immunotherapy strategy for hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Kevin Kuan-Yu Chen, Tzu-Jung Lin, Te-Wei Su, Po-Da Chen, Ming-Chih Ho, Yao-Ming Wu, Ying-Chun Shen, Ann-Lii Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeoadjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are increasingly being explored in multiple solid tumors, conventionally delivered as multi-cycle preoperative regimens or as part of perioperative strategies that include postoperative therapy. However, directly adopting these approaches presents distinct challenges in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). In the Asia-Pacific region, curative surgery remains feasible even for patients with a large tumor burden. These patients may derive the greatest benefit from neoadjuvant immunotherapy due to their high risk of recurrence, yet they also face the narrowest surgical window. Disease progression during prolonged neoadjuvant treatment period can easily hinder resectability. Additionally, intensive neoadjuvant treatment-related adverse events, especially hepatotoxicity, may delay surgery. A HCC-specific approach that prioritizes surgical feasibility while preserving benefit of immunotherapy is needed.Recent pharmacodynamic and clinical evidence indicates that a single dose of programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) or its ligand (PD-L1) antibody can rapidly achieve near-complete receptor occupancy and early T-cell activation. Receptor occupancy can be sustained in the early postoperative period. This provides a strong scientific rationale for a single-dose, non-waiting neoadjuvant strategy, in which anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 is administered shortly before surgery without delaying resection. Such an approach may allow early antitumor immune activation while minimizing treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), preserving surgical feasibility and reducing logistical complexity. It may enhance acceptance among patients and surgeons. Future neoadjuvant trials in HCC should consider adopting this “less is more” paradigm to investigate whether abbreviated neoadjuvant immunotherapy can safely translate immunologic activation into meaningful clinical benefit, as measured by event-free survival.Keywords: Neoadjuvant immunotherapy, Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI), Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Unmet need for neoadjuvant immunotherapy in HCC

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based combination therapies have significantly prolonged survival in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and are now established as standard of care in this setting [[1], [2], [3]]. In contrast, patients with resectable HCC face a high risk of recurrence after curative-intent surgery or ablation, with more than half experiencing recurrence within 5 years—underscoring a major unmet clinical need [4,5]. Despite the success of immunotherapy in unresectable disease, effective systemic strategies to meaningfully reduce recurrence in resectable HCC remain limited. Adjuvant immunotherapy administered after surgery or ablation has only shown modest benefit to date [6,7], prompting increasing interest in neoadjuvant treatment strategies that introduce immunotherapy earlier in the treatment course.

Incorporating preoperative ICI has demonstrated superior event-free survival (EFS) over adjuvant immunotherapy alone in other solid tumors, particularly melanoma [8,9]. The benefit has been attributed to ICI exposure in the presence of an intact tumor, enabling robust antitumor immune activation while tumor antigens remain available [10]. Although these findings may not be directly extrapolated to HCC, they provide a biological rationale for exploring preoperative ICI exposure in resectable HCC. Recent retrospective evidence provides further insight into the potential role of neoadjuvant strategies in HCC. Compared with patients undergoing upfront surgery, those treated with neoadjuvant ICI-based therapy achieved comparable recurrence-free survival, despite having a higher proportion of tumors exceeding conventional resectability criteria at baseline or harboring high-risk features for recurrence [11]. These observations indirectly suggest that neoadjuvant immunotherapy may mitigate recurrence risk and underscore the rationale for well-designed prospective neoadjuvant trials in HCC.

Unique challenges of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in HCC

Several early-phase studies have assessed preoperative ICI for HCC in conventional neoadjuvant or perioperative immunotherapy [Table 1] [[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20]]. Adapted from melanoma protocols, these “conventional neoadjuvant immunotherapy” regimens typically involve multi-cycle preoperative ICI—usually two to four cycles of ICI with or without combination partners. Most published studies are phase I or II single-arm trials employing anti-PD-1 monotherapy, anti-PD-1 combined with anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA-4) therapy, or anti-PD-1 combined with a multi-kinase inhibitor (MKI). However, most trials are characterized by small sample sizes and significant heterogeneity in inclusion criteria, which limit the generalizability of their findings. The high incidence of TRAEs, particularly hepatotoxicity, often deters surgeons from referring patients with upfront resectable disease for neoadjuvant therapy. Consequently, a significant proportion of borderline resectable or high-risk patients are enrolled in trials. Disease progression during the neoadjuvant phase can easily preclude them from curative-intent surgery, leading to reluctance among surgeons to refer further patients. This vicious cycle highlights the distinct challenges of neoadjuvant strategies in HCC compared with those in other solid tumors.

Frequent disease progression in the narrow surgical window

In the Asia-Pacific region, which accounts for more than half of global HCC cases, surgical resection remains a potentially curative option for selected patients with large tumor burden beyond Barcelona Clinical Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage A. This strategy is endorsed by regional guidelines and consensus of the Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL) and the Asia-Pacific Primary Liver Cancer Expert (APPLE) Association [21,22]. These high-risk patients are most likely to benefit from effective preoperative therapy, but they are also vulnerable to disease progression, which can render tumors unresectable—inherently narrowing the surgical window. Even applying intense treatment as dual immune checkpoint inhibitors, 16% [12] to 44% [13] of patients lost surgical feasibility primarily due to disease progression. In one recent neoadjuvant immunotherapy study [13], 30% and 60% of participants were classified as BCLC stage B and C, respectively. Among trial-defined non-responders (tumor shrinkage <10% by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1, RECIST 1.1), 60% became ineligible for surgery during the neoadjuvant period. Since immunotherapy does not guarantee tumor downstaging for all patients, prolonged or multi-cycle neoadjuvant immunotherapy risks missing the opportunity for curative surgery.

Safety concerns specific to HCC

Hepatic TRAEs are a major limitation of preoperative immunotherapy intensity in HCC, as underlying chronic liver disease or cirrhosis increases vulnerability to treatment-related liver injury [23]. While anti-PD-1 monotherapy keeps hepatic TRAEs below 20%, combination of anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 elevates the risk to 50–95%, with grade ≥ 3 hepatotoxicity reaching 30% [Table 1]. Many of these patients do not recover in weeks even under corticosteroids and immunosuppressants. Combining ICIs with MKIs also illustrates the low hepatic tolerance for intensive therapy in HCC patients. In the randomized phase 2/3 CARES-009 trial evaluating perioperative camrelizumab (anti-PD-1) plus rivoceranib (MKI), approximately half of patients experienced hepatotoxicity, and two treatment-related deaths due to hepatic failure during the preoperative period [20]. Hepatotoxicity and the subsequent need for immunosuppression introduce substantial uncertainty regarding the timing of liver function recovery and surgical scheduling. Even low-grade liver enzyme elevations may undermine surgical confidence and delay operative planning.

Collectively, these safety concerns complicate perioperative decision-making and impose additional logistical and psychological burdens on both surgeons and patients. Therefore, an optimal neoadjuvant strategy in HCC should prioritize preservation of surgical eligibility while minimizing hepatic toxicity.

Pharmacological rationale for the “Single-dose, non-waiting” approach

To address the limitations of conventional neoadjuvant immunotherapy, we propose a novel single-dose, non-waiting approach using anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapy. This approach is directly supported by the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of these agents, particularly their sustained receptor occupancy and rapid immune activation.

Sustained receptor occupancy of anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1

Anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 antibodies demonstrate prolonged target engagement that is substantially longer than their conventional dosing intervals.

Following a single dose of nivolumab (0.3–10 mg/kg), PD-1 receptor occupancy reaches a plateau within 24 h, largely independent of dose, and persists for more than 57 days, even when nivolumab becomes undetectable in peripheral blood [24]. In the development of anti-PD-L1, such as atezolizumab, population pharmacokinetic analyses demonstrated that a 1200 mg dose achieves substantial exposure. The median trough concentration measured during cycle 1 is substantially higher than a preclinically derived benchmark concentration of 6 μg/mL, which was estimated to correspond to approximately 95% tumor PD-L1 receptor saturation in murine models and was subsequently adopted during clinical dose selection [25,26]. Given the long terminal half-life of atezolizumab (approximately 27 days), a single dose of atezolizumab is expected to maintain serum concentrations well above the saturation threshold throughout the perioperative period. These findings support the plausibility of sustained PD-L1 blockade despite abbreviated preoperative exposure.

Leveraging the inherent characteristic of sustained receptor occupancy associated with anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 antibodies, a single-dose administration may be sufficient to achieve the therapeutic goal. This pharmacological evidence leads to next critical question: Is a prolonged waiting period truly necessary for eliciting an effective immune response?

Rapid immune activation without prolonged ICI exposure

Beyond sustained receptor engagement, anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapy is associated with early immunologic effects following treatment initiation. In the first-in-human phase I nivolumab study, peripheral T-cell counts transiently decreased within 24 h of infusion, followed by expansion peaking around day 28 [24]. Pharmacodynamic studies indicate that the proliferative response of peripheral blood Ki67+ PD-1+ CD8+ T cells—commonly interpreted as evidence of peripheral T-cell activation and expansion—are detectable at a very early stage following immunotherapy administration. While some studies with longer sampling intervals (e.g., every three weeks) reported a peak at week 3 [27], high-resolution kinetic data demonstrate that this immune reinvigoration peaks as early as day 7 and decreases thereafter [28]. The rapid induction of a systemic anti-tumor immune peak within one week further supports our proposed single-dose, short-interval preoperative strategy. This suggests that the systemic immune system may be robustly primed and achieves maximal activation well before the conventional three-week dosing cycle. This early onset of immune reinvigoration supports the feasibility of eliciting antitumor immunity within a limited preoperative window, without requiring prolonged exposure to immune checkpoint inhibition.

Taken together, sustained receptor occupancy and early immune activation provide a pharmacologic basis for a neoadjuvant design that prioritizes timely immune activation over prolonged preoperative exposure in HCC.

The “Less is more” paradigm: A novel clinical trial design

A pragmatic neoadjuvant design specifically for HCC

Leveraging these pharmacological properties, a single-dose, non-waiting neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 strategy offers a pragmatic solution to overcome the key limitations of conventional multi-cycle regimens. By avoiding combination regimens, minimizing treatment exposure, and shortening the interval between immunotherapy and surgery, this strategy reduces the risk of severe TRAEs and disease progression while preserving surgical feasibility and reducing logistical complexity [Fig. 1]. Importantly, prolonged receptor occupancy ensures that patients benefit from sustained PD-1 or PD-L1 blockade throughout the early postoperative period, despite abbreviated ICI exposure.

Novel Design vs Conventional Design of Immunotherapy for HCC

The conventional design (lower panel) involves multi-cycle, preoperative regimens that require a prolonged waiting period, increasing the risk of patient loss due to disease progression or immune-related adverse events (irAEs). In contrast, the novel “single-dose, non-waiting” strategy (upper panel) prioritizes timely surgery to avoid patient loss while leveraging sustained PD-1 or PD-L1 receptor occupancy to maintain immunologic activation throughout the preoperative and early postoperative period.

Primary endpoint selection: from pathological response to event-free survival

Major pathological response (MPR) rate has been widely adopted as the primary efficacy endpoint in previous neoadjuvant or perioperative immunotherapy trials and has demonstrated a strong correlation with survival benefit in HCC. As shown in [Table 2] [12,[14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20]], studies with longer preoperative treatment exposure tend to report higher MPR rates. However, this observation should not be interpreted as evidence that prolonged neoadjuvant therapy is inherently superior in HCC. Rather, it likely suggests that once antitumor immunity is initiated, maximal pathological regression may require time to fully manifest. The depth of pathological response appears to be more strongly influenced by intrinsic tumor characteristics than by the duration of treatment exposure [29].

In addition, the MPR rate reported in these studies is derived only from evaluable patients who successfully undergo planned surgery and is therefore susceptible to selection bias, as patients who lose surgical feasibility during the neoadjuvant period are typically excluded from analysis. Accordingly, MPR may incompletely capture the overall clinical benefit of neoadjuvant immunotherapy, particularly its impact on recurrence risk and surgical preservation.

Within this context, event-free survival (EFS) may represent a more comprehensive and clinically meaningful primary endpoint for neoadjuvant trials in HCC. EFS is typically defined as the time from randomization (or treatment initiation) to the occurrence of a predefined event—disease progression before surgery, failure to undergo definitive surgery, postoperative recurrence or death from any cause—whichever occurs first. By capturing both preoperative and postoperative events, EFS reflects not only oncologic efficacy but also clinically meaningful risks unique to the neoadjuvant setting, including treatment-related toxicity and loss of surgical eligibility.

Implications for adjuvant therapy and future clinical trials

The necessity and optimal duration of postoperative immunotherapy following neoadjuvant treatment remain undefined. While perioperative immunotherapy has demonstrated benefit in other solid tumors, it remains unclear whether adjuvant immunotherapy is required for all patients with resectable HCC, or whether its benefit depends on patient- or tumor-specific factors. These questions can only be addressed through well-designed randomized controlled trials.

In this context, a single-dose, non-waiting neoadjuvant strategy offers a pragmatic and patient- and surgeon-friendly platform for future clinical investigation. By minimizing preoperative treatment exposure and avoiding delays to curative surgery, this approach will enhance trial enrollment. Lowering barriers at the preoperative stage can facilitate rigorously powered studies to evaluate the incremental benefit of adjuvant therapy in resectable HCC.

Conclusion

Neoadjuvant immunotherapy strategies for HCC should move beyond conventional paradigms adopted from other solid tumors and prioritize designs that minimize preoperative risk and logistical burden. Grounded in the pharmacological and immunological characteristics of anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 agents, the single-dose, non-waiting approach enables rapid immune activation while avoiding the pitfalls associated with prolonged or intensified preoperative exposure. By preserving surgical feasibility and reducing treatment-related toxicity and delay, this HCC-specific strategy offers a rational and patient-centered framework for neoadjuvant immunotherapy and warrants prospective evaluation in future clinical trials.

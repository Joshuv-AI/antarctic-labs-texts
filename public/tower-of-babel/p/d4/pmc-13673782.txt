Gender differences in the relationship between dynapenic abdominal obesity and non-neoplastic digestive system diseases among middle-aged and older adults
Authors: Xuetao Dong, Yan Zhang, Yandi Liu, Muran Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNon-neoplastic digestive system diseases (NNDSD) are highly prevalent among middle-aged and older adults and represent a substantial global health burden. Dynapenic abdominal obesity (DAO), a clinical phenotype characterized by low muscle strength (dynapenia) combined with abdominal obesity, has been associated with a range of chronic diseases. However, the prospective association between DAO and incident NNDSD has not yet been elucidated. This study aimed to examine the prospective association between DAO and the risk of new-onset NNDSD.MethodsA total of 5,956 participants from five waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study were included in the present analysis. Participants were classified into four groups: non-dynapenic/non-abdominally obese (ND/NAO), abdominal obesity only (ND/AO), dynapenia only (D/NAO), and DAO. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were employed to evaluate the association between DAO and incident NNDSD.ResultsDuring a median 9-year follow-up, 1,082 incident cases of NNDSD were identified. In the overall population, participants with DAO exhibited a significantly higher risk of NNDSD than those in the ND/NAO group (HR = 1.597, 95% CI: 1.121–2.276). Notably, a pronounced sex-specific difference was observed. A significant association was observed among women (HR = 1.820, 95% CI: 1.235–2.683), whereas no significant association was identified among men.ConclusionThe coexistence of dynapenia and abdominal obesity was associated with an increased risk of incident NNDSD among middle-aged and older women, whereas no such association was observed among men. Early identification of the DAO phenotype may facilitate the implementation of targeted preventive interventions, including combined resistance and aerobic exercise programs, to reduce the risk of NNDSD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05092-x.

Introduction

The global population is aging at an unprecedented rate, posing substantial challenges to public health systems worldwide. This rapid demographic transition has been accompanied by an increasing prevalence of age-related chronic diseases, among which digestive system disorders constitute a major global health burden [1], accounting for more than one-third of prevalent cases and one-fifth of newly diagnosed cases worldwide [2]. The burden of these disorders is particularly pronounced among middle-aged and older adults [3]. Non-neoplastic digestive system diseases (NNDSD) constitute a substantial and frequently underrecognized global health burden. These disorders encompass a broad spectrum of functional and organic gastrointestinal conditions, including gastroesophageal reflux disease, peptic ulcer disease, and inflammatory bowel disease, and are highly prevalent among middle-aged and older populations [4, 5]. Emerging evidence suggests the existence of a pathogenic continuum linking specific NNDSD to the development of gastrointestinal malignancies [6, 7]. Collectively, these conditions contribute substantially to the increasing disease burden, impaired quality of life, and escalating healthcare resource utilization worldwide [8, 9].

Traditionally, sarcopenia has been defined primarily by the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass [10]. However, accumulating evidence suggests that muscle function, particularly muscle strength and power, collectively referred to as dynapenia, may represent a more sensitive and earlier predictor of functional decline, disability, and mortality than muscle mass alone [11, 12]. Abdominal obesity, characterized by excessive visceral adipose tissue accumulation, is a well-established contributor to systemic inflammation, insulin resistance, and metabolic syndrome [13, 14]. The coexistence of dynapenia and abdominal obesity defines a distinct clinical phenotype known as dynapenic abdominal obesity (DAO) [15]. This phenotype represents a “double burden,” whereby obesity-related metabolic dysregulation exacerbates the functional impairment and metabolic inefficiency associated with reduced muscle strength, thereby perpetuating a vicious cycle that predisposes individuals to a wide range of adverse health outcomes [16, 17].

Although the associations of DAO with cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and all-cause mortality have been increasingly recognized [18–20], its potential relationship with NNDSD remains largely unexplored. Several biologically plausible mechanisms may underlie the association between DAO and NNDSD. First, chronic low-grade systemic inflammation and oxidative stress associated with excess visceral adiposity may impair gastrointestinal mucosal integrity and disrupt gut microbiota homeostasis [21–23]. Second, dynapenia involving the core and respiratory musculature may contribute to diminished esophageal clearance, impaired gastrointestinal motility, and elevated intra-abdominal pressure [24–26], all of which are implicated in the pathogenesis of NNDSD. Despite these biologically plausible hypotheses, no large-scale epidemiological study has yet evaluated whether DAO acts as an integrated risk factor for NNDSD independent of its individual components. Therefore, the present study sought to address this important knowledge gap using data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS).

Methods

Study population

Data for this cohort study were obtained from five waves of the CHARLS conducted between 2011 and 2020. CHARLS is an ongoing nationally representative longitudinal study of Chinese adults aged ≥ 45 years, designed to investigate population aging and monitor longitudinal changes in health status among middle-aged and older individuals. The present analysis utilized de-identified publicly available CHARLS data, which had been approved by the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of Peking University (IRB00001052–11015). Detailed information regarding the CHARLS study is available at https://charls.pku.edu.cn/index.htm. Although the CHARLS data used in the present study were collected between 2011 and 2020, the findings remain highly relevant to contemporary clinical practice and public health. CHARLS is characterized by rigorous quality-control procedures and standardized assessments and has become an increasingly recognized data resource in high-quality research on aging and chronic diseases in recent years. Moreover, the global prevalence of obesity, dynapenia, and digestive disorders continues to increase with population aging, suggesting that the observed associations are likely to remain highly relevant in contemporary clinical and public health contexts. The present cohort included participants who were free of NNDSD at baseline and had complete follow-up data. Dynapenia and abdominal obesity were assessed at baseline using data collected during the 2011 CHARLS survey wave. All participants were subsequently followed across five-wave interviews conducted between 2011 and 2020 until the occurrence of NNDSD or the end of follow-up in 2020, whichever came first. Missing data were addressed using a complete-case analysis approach. A total of 17,708 participants were initially enrolled in the baseline survey conducted in 2011. Participants aged < 45 years were excluded (n = 497). We further excluded participants with missing information on DAO status (n = 4,067), incomplete covariate data (n = 711), prevalent NNDSD at baseline (n = 2,812), or incomplete follow-up information (n = 3,665). After applying the exclusion criteria, 5,956 participants were included in the final analytical cohort. The detailed participant selection process is illustrated in Figure S1.

Assessment of dynapenia, abdominal obesity, and dynapenic abdominal obesity

Handgrip strength (HGS) was measured by trained personnel using a calibrated, standardized dynamometer [27]. Dynapenia was defined as HGS < 28 kg in men and < 18 kg in women, according to the 2019 consensus of the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) [28]. Abdominal obesity was defined using waist circumference (WC) according to the 2024 Chinese Medical Association Guidelines for the Prevention and Control of Obesity, with sex-specific cut-off values of ≥ 90 cm for men and ≥ 85 cm for women [29]. Participants were categorized into four mutually exclusive groups according to the presence or absence of dynapenia and abdominal obesity: (1) no dynapenia and no abdominal obesity (ND/NAO), (2) abdominal obesity alone (ND/AO), (3) dynapenia alone (D/NAO), and (4) DAO.

Definition of NNDSD

NNDSD status during follow-up was determined based on physician-confirmed diagnoses self-reported by participants during structured survey interviews. Specifically, incident NNDSD was identified based on participants’ responses to the following question: “Have you been diagnosed by a physician with a gastric or other non-neoplastic digestive system disease?” Given the structure of the CHARLS dataset, NNDSD was defined as a composite outcome encompassing a broad range of non-malignant digestive disorders. This approach has been widely used in large-scale epidemiological studies to capture the overall burden of digestive morbidity when disease-specific diagnostic information is not consistently available. The onset time of NNDSD was estimated as the midpoint between the follow-up wave in which the diagnosis was first reported and the participant’s most recent preceding assessment [30, 31]. This method is commonly applied in longitudinal studies with interval-censored outcomes, in which the exact timing of disease onset is unobserved but is known to occur between two assessment waves.

Covariates

Covariates were selected a priori based on existing literature [2, 5], clinical relevance, and their established or plausible associations with both the exposure and the outcome, including age, sex, place of residence (urban or rural), educational attainment (less than high school versus high school or higher), marital status (married versus other), body mass index (BMI), alcohol consumption status (current, former, or never), smoking status (current, former, or never), and the presence of other chronic diseases. BMI was included to account for overall adiposity independent of central fat distribution and was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²) [32]. Physician-diagnosed chronic conditions, including hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, cancer, lung disease, liver disease, heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, psychiatric disorders, memory-related disease, arthritis, and asthma, were incorporated as separate covariates in the fully adjusted multivariable models to account for potential confounding arising from baseline comorbidities [31].

Statistical analysis

Considering the sex-specific definitions of dynapenia and abdominal obesity, all analyses were performed separately for men and women. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis tests as appropriate, whereas categorical variables were presented as percentages and evaluated using the chi-square test. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were employed to assess the association between DAO and incident NNDSD, with results expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Model 1 was an unadjusted model. Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, place of residence, educational attainment, marital status, alcohol consumption, smoking status, BMI, and individual baseline chronic conditions (hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, cancer, lung disease, liver disease, heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, psychiatric disorders, memory-related disease, arthritis, and asthma). Kaplan–Meier analyses were performed to estimate the cumulative incidence of NNDSD across the four study groups. Subgroup analyses were further conducted according to age, place of residence, marital status, and educational attainment. To assess the robustness of the findings, sensitivity analysis 1 was performed by excluding BMI from the fully adjusted Cox proportional hazards models. Sensitivity analysis 2 was conducted using a multiple imputation approach. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.5. Two-sided P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 5,956 participants were included in the final analysis, with a mean (SD) age of 57.84 (8.57) years. Of these participants, 2,829 (47.5%) were men and 3,127 (52.5%) were women. Baseline characteristics of the study population, stratified by sex, are presented in Table 1. Table 2 summarizes the baseline characteristics of male participants according to dynapenia and abdominal obesity status. The proportions of men classified into the ND/NAO, D/NAO, ND/AO, and DAO groups were 62.99%, 5.30%, 30.58%, and 1.13%, respectively. Compared with those in the ND/NAO group, men in the DAO group were generally older, were more likely to reside in urban areas, had lower educational attainment, and exhibited a higher prevalence of chronic comorbidities. Table 3 summarizes the baseline characteristics of female participants according to dynapenia and abdominal obesity status. The proportions of women classified into the ND/NAO, D/NAO, ND/AO, and DAO groups were 42.60%, 3.68%, 49.95%, and 3.77%, respectively. Similar patterns were observed among women, with participants in the DAO group being generally older, more likely to reside in urban areas, having lower educational attainment, and exhibiting a higher prevalence of chronic comorbidities.

Associations between dynapenic abdominal obesity and new-onset NNDSD

During the follow-up period, 1,082 participants developed NNDSD, including 451 men (15.94%) and 631 women (20.18%). The incidence rates of new-onset NNDSD, stratified by dynapenia and abdominal obesity status, are presented in Fig. 1. The incidence of NNDSD was highest in the DAO group in both the overall population and among women. Table 4; Fig. 2 summarize the associations between DAO and incident NNDSD. In the fully adjusted model, participants in the DAO group exhibited a significantly higher risk of NNDSD than those in the ND/NAO group in the overall population (HR: 1.597, 95% CI: 1.121–2.276). No significant association between DAO and incident NNDSD was observed among men. In contrast, among women, participants in the DAO group exhibited a significantly increased risk of NNDSD compared with those in the ND/NAO group after full adjustment for covariates (HR: 1.820, 95% CI: 1.235–2.683). Kaplan–Meier analyses demonstrated that, among women, participants in the DAO group had a significantly higher cumulative incidence of NNDSD than those in the other groups (Fig. 3 and Figures S2-S3). Subgroup analyses stratified by age, place of residence, marital status, and educational attainment were further performed to examine the association between DAO and incident NNDSD (Table S1). Among women, stronger associations were observed in participants who were older, resided in rural areas, were married, or had lower educational attainment, although these findings should be interpreted cautiously because of the limited sample sizes within certain subgroups. No significant associations were identified in subgroup analyses among men. The primary findings remained robust in sensitivity analyses, which yielded results consistent with the main analyses (Table S2 ).

Discussion

Using a large nationally representative cohort, this study is the first to comprehensively investigate the association between DAO and incident NNDSD among middle-aged and older adults, with particular attention to sex-specific differences. The principal findings of the present study can be summarized as follows. First, DAO was independently associated with an increased risk of incident NNDSD in the overall population. Second, a marked sex-specific difference was observed, with a significant independent association between DAO and incident NNDSD identified among women but not among men. Collectively, these findings provide novel insights into the etiology of NNDSD and highlight potential opportunities for targeted preventive interventions.

Although the associations of DAO with cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and all-cause mortality have been increasingly recognized [18–20], its relationship with NNDSD remains largely unexplored. Our findings support the hypothesis that DAO is independently associated with an elevated risk of incident NNDSD. The underlying mechanisms are likely multifactorial and may act synergistically. First, visceral adipose tissue functions as a metabolically active endocrine organ that secretes a range of pro-inflammatory cytokines [33, 34], whereas dynapenia has been closely linked to “inflammaging” [35, 36]. This chronic inflammatory milieu may impair intestinal mucosal barrier integrity and disrupt gut microbiota homeostasis, thereby increasing susceptibility to a broad spectrum of NNDSD [37–40]. Second, mechanical and functional mechanisms may also contribute substantially to this association. Dynapenia involving the core musculature may contribute to impaired lower esophageal sphincter function, diminished esophageal clearance, and abnormal intra-abdominal pressure dynamics [25, 41, 42]. Concurrently, systemic muscle weakness may adversely affect gastrointestinal motility through autonomic nervous system dysregulation or direct mechanical effects, potentially contributing to functional constipation and other gastrointestinal disorders [24, 43–45]. Consequently, individuals with DAO bear a “double burden” of metabolic dysregulation and functional decline, culminating in an increased likelihood of incident NNDSD. Although these proposed mechanisms are biologically plausible, they should be interpreted cautiously because causal relationships cannot be established in the context of an observational study design. Accordingly, the mechanistic interpretations presented herein should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Notably, the definition of NNDSD in the present study was based on a single self-reported physician diagnosis and encompassed a heterogeneous spectrum of conditions, including both functional and organic gastrointestinal disorders. This broad classification may obscure disease-specific associations and introduce substantial heterogeneity in the underlying biological mechanisms. Accordingly, the proposed biological pathways should be interpreted cautiously, as different digestive disorders may involve distinct or only partially overlapping pathogenic mechanisms. Nevertheless, the use of a composite outcome enables the assessment of the overall burden of digestive morbidity in large-scale epidemiological studies. Future studies are warranted to disentangle the associations between DAO and specific digestive disease entities, thereby providing further insight into disease-specific mechanisms and clinical implications.

One of the most notable findings of the present study was the sex-specific association between DAO and incident NNDSD. The significant association observed among women, but not among men, may be attributable to a combination of biological and socio-behavioral factors. Postmenopausal women may undergo substantial alterations in body composition related to declining estrogen levels, characterized by increased visceral fat accumulation and accelerated skeletal muscle loss [46–48]. This physiological transition may render older women more susceptible to developing the characteristic DAO phenotype than their male counterparts. Furthermore, adipose tissue in postmenopausal women may exhibit heightened inflammatory activity, with evidence demonstrating increased macrophage infiltration and elevated expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines [49–51], thereby exacerbating systemic inflammation and its deleterious effects on the gastrointestinal tract. Estrogen exerts protective effects on the gastrointestinal tract in premenopausal women, including the maintenance of mucosal barrier integrity [52, 53]. The loss of this protective effect after menopause may increase susceptibility to the metabolic and inflammatory disturbances associated with DAO. In this cohort of middle-aged and older Chinese adults, sex differences in health literacy, healthcare-seeking behaviors, and symptom-reporting patterns may also contribute to the observed disparities. The subgroup analyses showing stronger associations among rural women and those with lower educational attainment provide partial support for the potential contribution of these socio-behavioral determinants. In contrast, the distribution patterns of abdominal obesity and dynapenia among men, together with other potentially stronger confounding factors, may obscure the independent effect of DAO in this population. Nevertheless, these interpretations should be regarded as hypothesis-generating, as the relevant biological and behavioral mechanisms were not directly assessed in the present study. These proposed pathways warrant further investigation and validation in future studies.

By integrating dynapenia and abdominal obesity into a composite exposure construct, the present study extends beyond the limitations of traditional single-metric approaches and provides a more comprehensive representation of the underlying pathophysiological processes. This approach proposes a novel integrative framework linking body composition and physical function to the pathogenesis of NNDSD. Assessment of digestive health among middle-aged and older women may benefit from extending beyond conventional symptom evaluation to include routine assessment of HGS and WC. Identification of women with the DAO phenotype may facilitate the recognition of a subgroup at elevated risk for NNDSD and support the implementation of early preventive interventions. Potential interventions may adopt a dual-targeted strategy aimed at mitigating dynapenia through resistance training combined with nutritional support while reducing visceral adiposity through aerobic exercise and dietary modification. Such a comprehensive lifestyle-based management approach may represent a promising preventive strategy for digestive disorders among middle-aged and older women.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, ascertainment of NNDSD relied on self-reported physician diagnoses, which may be subject to recall bias and misclassification. The outcome definition was derived from a single aggregated survey question encompassing a broad spectrum of non-neoplastic digestive disorders with potentially heterogeneous underlying mechanisms. Such heterogeneity may have introduced non-differential misclassification, thereby potentially attenuating the observed associations. Second, owing to data limitations, information on specific digestive disease subtypes was unavailable, precluding analyses of disease-specific associations. Therefore, the observed associations should be interpreted as reflecting overall susceptibility to digestive disorders rather than disease-specific effects. Third, although adjustments were made for a broad range of potential confounders, residual confounding from factors such as physical activity, dietary habits, and medication use cannot be entirely excluded. Future studies incorporating more detailed clinical and lifestyle information are warranted to further address these potential sources of residual confounding. Fourth, several subgroup analyses were based on relatively small sample sizes within certain strata, which may have led to reduced statistical power and unstable effect estimates. Accordingly, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to validate these findings. Fifth, because a complete-case analysis approach was applied, the possibility of selection bias and reduced generalizability cannot be entirely excluded. Finally, because this study was conducted predominantly among Chinese adults, the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic and cultural populations requires further validation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that DAO is associated with an increased risk of incident NNDSD among middle-aged and older women, whereas no significant association was identified among men. These findings underscore the importance of adopting a sex-specific perspective and implementing comprehensive assessment and management strategies targeting body composition and physical function in the prevention and management of digestive disorders. Future studies are warranted to elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying these sex-specific differences and to evaluate effective interventions targeting the DAO phenotype for reducing the risk of digestive disorders.

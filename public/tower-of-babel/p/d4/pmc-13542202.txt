The Role of MYH6 Variants in atrial cardiomyopathy: insights from clinical observations and zebrafish models
Authors: Yike Zhang, Qing Wang, Xiangao Ji, Zhou Xu, Jingzhe Hao, Yue Zhu, Jia Song, Hongwu Chen, Zhiyong Zhang, Chang Cui, Minglong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtrial cardiomyopathy, which often comes with underlying genetic defects, has been recognized as a possible substrate of atrial fibrillation. MYH6 encodes α-myosin heavy chain (α-MHC), predominantly expressed in the atria and pivotal in sarcomere organization and muscle contraction. Genetic investigation in patients with a family history of atrial cardiomyopathy identified three probands carrying MYH6 variants (D629N, V893M, A1327T). However, the role of MYH6 in atrial diseases has not been fully elucidated, and the genetic cause of atrial cardiomyopathy needs further investigation. Here, we employed CRISPR/Cas9 to generate myh6 knockout zebrafish, assessing cardiac abnormalities in structural, electrical, and gene expression levels through diverse methodologies. Our findings revealed that myh6 defects in zebrafish impaired atrial and ventricular function and disordered sarcomere, which underscores the pivotal role of MYH6 in maintaining atrial function and development. Transcriptomic sequencing also identified 1318 differentially expressed genes enriched in muscle development, calcium ion homeostasis, and sarcomere pathways. The association between MYH6 dysfunction and atrial cardiomyopathy highlights the role of sarcomeric abnormalities in atrial remodeling, a process implicated in atrial fibrillation.Subject terms: Cardiology, Diseases, Genetics

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a common cardiac arrhythmia characterized by irregular and rapid heartbeats. It poses an increased risk of stroke and heart failure. Traditionally, AF has been attributed to external risk factors that can cause electrical and mechanical remodelling of the atria. These factors include hypertension, alcohol abuse, and ventricular diseases. However, recent research has revealed that primary atrial cardiomyopathy or atrial-selective cardiomyopathy (ASCM), often accompanied by underlying genetic defects, can also be a substrate for AF1–4.

Genetic background is an essential contributor to the pathogenesis of AF. In a study involving 1293 individuals with early-onset AF, genetic testing was performed using a comprehensive arrhythmia and cardiomyopathy gene panel5. The results showed that 10.1% (131 participants) had a disease-associated variant. These variants were identified in 34 different genes, with 141 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants. The most frequently affected genes were TTN (38 variants, 27%), MYH7 (18 variants, 13%), LMNA (9 variants, 6%), and MYH6 (10 variants, 7%)5.

This finding suggests a strong association between sarcomere or nuclear defects and the development of AF. Most of these genes are expressed in both the atrium and ventricle. Sarcomeres are the basic units of muscle contraction, and their defects can disrupt the normal functioning of the atrial myocardium6,7. However, there have been few reports regarding the involvement of sarcomere defects in the atrium.

The 2016 consensus delineated atrial cardiomyopathy as encompassing a spectrum of structural, architectural, contractile, or electrophysiological alterations within the atria that result in notable clinical symptoms8. Long-term structural and electrical remodelling can eventually lead to atrial failure, which can pose adverse outcomes associated with higher mortality and morbidity9. However, the causal relationship between “ventricular cardiomyopathy-related” genes and atrial cardiomyopathy or atrial fibrillation has not yet been fully elucidated.

In our practice, we performed whole-exome sequencing in atrial cardiomyopathy patients with family history to detect potential variants associated with genes included in cardiomyopathy and arrhythmia gene panels3. Three probands carrying MYH6 (NM_002471) variants were found. MYH6 encodes myosin heavy chain 6, which is expressed both in the atria and ventricles10. Cardiac myosin is a heterohexameric protein which consists of two myosin heavy chains (MHCs), two regulatory light chains (RLCs), and two essential light chains (ELCs). As a molecular motor, it powers cardiac contraction by converting chemical energy from ATP hydrolysis into mechanical force11. MYH6 variants have been reported as a causative gene in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)12,13, while atrial phenotypes associated with MYH6 dysfunction remain less well characterized.

Although mammalian models have been widely used to study cardiomyopathy and arrhythmia, their high cost and low throughput have limited their applicability under specific circumstances. Zebrafish can serve as an important model to recapitulate cardiomyopathy phenotypes. The genomes of zebrafish and humans exhibit a high degree of similarity, with numerous potentially pathogenic genes conserved across species14. Moreover, zebrafish provide a stable platform for genetic manipulation, including techniques like knockout7,15, knockdown16,17, or transgenic18–21 techniques. Additionally, zebrafish provide valuable insights into cardiac morphology, chamber dynamics, electrophysiology, and gene expression, significantly enhancing the study of cardiomyopathy14.

To investigate the role of MYH6 in the pathogenesis of atrial cardiomyopathy, we constructed a CRISPR/Cas9-based myh6 knockout zebrafish model (homozygous and heterozygous) expressing Tg(myl7:eGFP) to visualize the heart. We aim to recapitulate atrial cardiomyopathy phenotype by analyzing structural, electrical, and gene expression profiles utilizing myh6-deficient zebrafish model.

Cardiac abnormality in myh6+/− and myh6−/− larvae zebrafish

To explore the involvement of MYH6 in the pathogenesis of atrial cardiomyopathy, we generated homozygous and heterozygous myh6 knockout zebrafish using the CRISPR/Cas9 technique (Fig. 2A-B). Two sgRNAs were transfected into the embryos, leading to nucleotide deletions in two sites (-8 bp in total). F1 zebrafish (myh6 mosaic) were crossed with Tg(myl7:eGFP) to visualize the heart. Sanger sequencing verified the successful gene ablation in the genomic DNA of myh6 (Fig. 2C). To assess genotype distribution across development, we analyzed the proportions of the three genotypes among surviving zebrafish at predefined time points. The proportion of homozygous mutants decreased from 25.0% at early stages to 3.4% at 6 months (Fig. 2D). In the 1-month zebrafish heart, myh6 showed decreased protein expression in myh6+/− and no expression in myh6−/− mutants, while myh7 expression was not changed in myh6+/− but was lower in myh6−/− mutants (Fig. 2E, F).

Electrical and contractile abnormalities in myh6+/− adult zebrafish

To further evaluate the progression of cardiac defects, we performed ECG in 6-month-old adult zebrafish (Fig. 3A and Supplementary Fig S1). As most of the homozygous mutants could hardly survive to adulthood, only heterozygous mutants and WT controls were included in the analysis (n = 10 WT and 10 myh6+/−). Heart rates were significantly lower in myh6+/− mutants (117.0 ± 17.48 bpm [WT] vs. 87.18 ± 12.28 bpm [myh6+/−], P < 0.001). ECG showed a prolonged PR interval (62.77 ± 13.81 ms [WT] vs. 108.1 ± 21.19 ms [myh6+/−], P < 0.0001), increased P wave duration (20.43 ± 10.49 ms [WT] vs. 35.10 ± 12.49 ms [myh6+/−], P < 0.05), and longer QRS complex duration (40.30 ± 28.50 ms [WT] vs. 65.74 ± 16.03 ms [myh6+/−], P < 0.05) in adult myh6+/− mutants (Fig. 3B).

To acquire the cardiac structure and function of the adult zebrafish, we performed high-frequency echocardiography to visualize the blood flow and chambers movement (Fig. 3C and Supplementary Fig S1, n = 5 WT and 10 myh6+/−). Representative echocardiography images in WT and mutant groups are shown in Fig. 3D. Mean heart rate were lower in myh6+/− zebrafish (Figs. 3E, 136.5 ± 7.98 bpm [WT] vs.100.7 ± 18.59 bpm [myh6+/−]; P < 0.01). FAC of the atria was lower in myh6+/− mutants (0.22 ± 0.05 [WT] vs. 0.13 ± 0.04 [myh6+/−]; P < 0.01). FAC of the ventricle was also lower in myh6+/− mutants (0.28 ± 0.04 [WT] vs. 0.19 ± 0.03 [myh6+/−]; P < 0.001). In Doppler mode, we measured the blood flow across the atrio-ventricle valve. Peak A wave velocity, which reflects the atrial contractile function, was decreased in myh6+/− zebrafish (180.56 ± 58.64 mm/s [WT] vs. 105.43 ± 38.5 mm/s [myh6+/−]; P < 0.01). A wave velocity time integral was also significantly decreased (8.22 ± 2.96 mm [WT] vs.4.85 ± 1.53 mm [myh6+/−]; P < 0.05). Peak E wave velocity, however, did not reach statistical significance.

To characterize the histopathological alterations in myh6+/− mutant zebrafish, we performed hematoxylin and eosin (HE) and Masson’s trichrome staining using heart samples derived from 6-month-old zebrafish (Supplementary Fig S2). No evidence of fibrosis was observed in any group based on Masson’s trichrome staining. However, we observed structural changes under the transmission electron microscope (TEM), demonstrating a disordered sarcomere and unclear M-band. Notably, mitochondrial damages were observed in the atria in myh6+/− mutants (Fig. 3F), indicating an underlying substrate for atrial cardiomyopathy. Additionally, sarcomere lengths (distance between Z-discs) were significantly reduced in the atrium but increased in the ventricle of myh6+/− mutants compared with age-matched controls (atrium: 1.77 ± 0.09 µm [WT] vs. 1.41 ± 0.28 µm [myh6+/−], n = 20 per group, P < 0.0001; ventricle: 1.60 ± 0.08 µm [WT] vs. 1.85 ± 0.25 µm [myh6+/−], n = 20 per group, P = 0.0002) (Fig. 3G).

RNA sequencing identifies differentially expressed genes associated with cardiac development

To understand the molecular basis of the effects of myh6 deficiency in atrial cardiomyocytes, we investigated alterations in the transcriptional profiles of atrial samples from myh6+/− zebrafish and compared them with those from WT zebrafish at the tissue level (n = 3 vs. 3). In total, 724 upregulated and 594 downregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in myh6+/− mutants compared to WT counterparts (Fig. 4A). To test the impact of mutant myh6 on its related genes, we summarized the expression changes of genes interacting directly with myh6 by String analysis (Fig. 4D). Among the top 10 myh6-associated genes, myl4 was significantly upregulated, and zgc:86709 (actc2) was downregulated (Supplementary Table S1). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that those upregulated DEGs in myh6-deficient zebrafish atrial samples were functionally related to ‘tight junction’ and ‘intermediate filament’. In contrast, the downregulated DEGs were highly enriched in ‘muscle tissue development’, ‘calcium ion homeostasis’, and ‘sarcomere’, recapitulating the transcriptional processes perturbed in the myh6 mutant atrium. Heat maps depicting the expression profile of the gene set relevant to the GO function of ‘sarcomere’ and ‘muscle tissue development’ are shown in Fig. 4B-C. GO enrichment analysis of downregulated genes (n = 594) in myh6+/− zebrafish is shown in Fig. 4E-F. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) showed the molecular function term of ‘structural molecule activity’ was disrupted in myh6+/− mutant atrium (Fig. 4G). Therefore, transcriptomic alterations further consolidated the atrial involvement in myh6-defective zebrafish models.

Discussion

MYH6 encodes the α-myosin heavy chain (α-MHC), a core sarcomeric motor protein that plays a central role in force generation and cardiac contractility. Pathogenic variants in MYH6 have been implicated in ventricular cardiomyopathies, including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)22,23 and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM)24. In mammalian models, Myh6 homozygous knockout mice die early in utero because of gross heart defects, whereas Myh6 heterozygous knockout mice can survive to adulthood but demonstrate myocardial fibrosis and alterations in sarcomere structure25.

MYH6 mutations have also been reported in patients with predominantly atrial involvement, such as sick sinus syndrome15 and atrial septal defect26,27. Among 227 probands with early-onset AF in ethnic minority probands, MYH6 variants (E1323V and K1307M) were found to be cosegregated with AF28, indicating that the MYH6 variants can give rise to atrial cardiomyopathy. In our clinical findings, the probands in Families 1 and 2 exhibited atrial abnormalities, whereas the proband and affected family members in Family 3 carrying the A1327T variant presented with both atrial and ventricular cardiomyopathy. These observations highlight the broad clinical spectrum of MYH6-associated disease, ranging from atrial-predominant cardiomyopathy to combined atrial and ventricular involvement.

In recent years, genetic studies in AF patients have identified a role for genes previously implicated in ventricular cardiomyopathy. Many gene variants have been identified in those AF patients in the absence of symptomatic ventricular cardiomyopathy, termed ‘lone AF’ in the past29. GWAS have shown that many of the genes underlying atrial fibrillation act via cardiac development, electrophysiological, and cardiac structural remodelling, potentially in the form of an ‘atrial myopathy’30,31. The biological effect can work during fetal heart development or as a response to mechanical or chemical stress in the adult heart. In observational studies, approximately 20% of patients with early-onset AF carried cardiomyopathy- or arrhythmia-associated variants in genes such as TTN, MYH6, and MYH75,32,33.

In the context of myh6-related atrial cardiomyopathy, mutant myosin heavy chains are expressed throughout life and in both atrial and ventricular chambers, in contrast to atrial-restricted sarcomeric genes such as MYL4. Transgenic zebrafish expressing the myl4 pathogenic variant exhibit atrial-selective sarcomeric disruption and myofibrillar disarray6,7. Contrastingly, given the expression pattern of MYH6, variants in this gene are expected to result in greater phenotypic heterogeneity. Consequently, in MYH6 variant carriers, the penetrance, onset time, and clinical presentations vary among individuals and variant sites. Some carriers present with atrial-predominant expressions with preserved ventricular function, whereas others develop combined atrial and ventricular phenotypes, including HCM, DCM, or heart failure.

It should be noted that the myh6 knockout model represents an inherent limitation when extrapolating to variant-specific disease mechanisms. Complete loss of myh6 constitutes a more severe perturbation of sarcomeric function than the heterozygous missense variants observed in patients and may therefore overestimate disease severity and chamber involvement. Nevertheless, the knockout approach is intended to define the fundamental role of MYH6 in maintaining atrial structure and function in vivo rather than to model precise genotype–phenotype relationships. In this context, the abnormalities observed likely reflect a dosage-dependent threshold effect of sarcomeric dysfunction, consistent with the clinical heterogeneity of MYH6 variant carriers.

Zebrafish offer unique advantages for studying cardiovascular diseases, including rapid cardiac development, optical accessibility, and the ability to perform in vivo functional and electrophysiological assessments7,17–19,34,35. Importantly, chamber-specific expression of myosin heavy-chain isoforms is conserved between zebrafish and humans. In adult zebrafish, myh6/amhc is predominantly expressed in the atrium, whereas myh7/vmhcl is ventricle-enriched36,37, mirroring the preferential expression of MYH6 in human atrial cardiomyocytes and MYH7 in ventricular cardiomyocytes38. Moreover, it has been reported that approximately 96% of cardiomyopathy-associated genes have identifiable homologues in zebrafish, supporting zebrafish as a conserved non-mammalian model suitable for studying human cardiomyopathies.

In the present study, myh6-deficient zebrafish exhibited impaired atrial and ventricular structure and function across developmental stages, consistent with prior loss-of-function studies39–42. Notably, myh6 deficiency results in a time-dependent, chamber-specific progression of cardiac phenotypes. Early embryonic stages are characterized by primary atrial contractile impairment, leading to altered hemodynamics, valve dysplasia and secondary ventricular dysfunction. With development, persistent atrial dysfunction is accompanied by progressive ventricular remodeling, including chamber enlargement. In adulthood, atrial hypoplasia and contractile defects persist, whereas the ventricle shows compensatory remodeling, likely driven by chronic hemodynamic changes. This temporal pattern provides a mechanistic framework for the differential sarcomeric alterations observed between the atrium and ventricle. Consistently, we observed sarcomere shortening in the atrium and lengthening in the ventricle, reflecting compensatory responses in line with previous reports of atrial hypoplasia and ventricular enlargement43. Importantly, compared with prior studies focusing mainly on developmental or structural phenotypes, our study integrates multi-level characterization, including in vivo ECG, echocardiography, and transcriptomic profiling. This approach provides a more comprehensive view of how MYH6 dysfunction affects cardiac structure, function, electrophysiology, and molecular pathways.

This study has some limitations. First, a knockout zebrafish model was used to investigate the fundamental role of myh6, rather than variant-specific knock-in models that may better capture the phenotypic diversity associated with individual MYH6 mutations. Second, although atrial dysfunction was a prominent finding, we cannot fully exclude contributions from ventricular involvement in the observed cardiac phenotypes. Third, genetic testing was not available for several asymptomatic family members due to lack of accessible samples, and their genotype status therefore remains unknown, which may limit the pedigree analysis. Finally, transcriptomic analyses identified multiple dysregulated pathways; however, given the limited sample size and the small number of genes passing multiple testing correction thresholds, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and warrant further validation to delineate the precise molecular mechanisms linking MYH6 dysfunction to atrial cardiomyopathy.

In brief, our study provided cases of atrial cardiomyopathy carrying the MYH6 variants. Using knockout zebrafish, we found structural and electrical changes both in the atria and ventricle of myh6 mutants, which can be the substrate for cardiomyopathy and arrhythmia in both chambers. We also investigated the transcriptional changes in the atrium, which could drive mechanistic studies in MYH6-associated atrial cardiomyopathy and facilitate the development of novel therapies.

Methods

Ethics approval statement

Patients were recruited at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University as part of an ongoing atrial cardiomyopathy cohort as described in our previous work3,18,44. Atrial cardiomyopathy was defined by the presence of one or more of the following clinical features: familial or early-onset (<65 years old) atrial fibrillation, atrial standstill, unexplained scar-related atrial tachycardia, or sick sinus syndrome. Rare variants were identified in multiple cardiomyopathy-associated genes, from which families carrying MYH6 variants were selected for the present study.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (2022-SR-256) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Approval for animal experiments was obtained from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Medical University (IACUC-1902020).

Whole-exome sequencing

The probands from three families underwent whole-exome sequencing (WES) for this study (II-4/Family 1, II-6/Family 2, II-7/Family 3). The DNA used in our study was extracted from venous blood samples obtained. DNA libraries were constructed using the Ion Ampliseq™ Library Kit 2.0 (Thermo Fisher). Next-generation sequencing was subsequently performed using NovaSeq6000 (Illumina, San Diego, CA). The effective sequencing reads were aligned to the GRCh37 human reference genome.

Variant screening and in silico pathogenicity prediction

To screen for candidate pathogenic variants, variants of ‘exonic’ or ‘splicing’ were included, and ‘synonymous’ were excluded. Next, variants were filtered based on whether their MAF (minor allele frequency) was over 5% (according to 1000 Genomes, Genome Aggregation Database for East Asia). Variants of CADD_phred > 20 or REVEL > 0.3 or absent from REVEL were selected. Finally, variants associated with ‘arrhythmia ’or ‘cardiomyopathy’ were identified as potential candidates. Polymorphism Phenotyping v2 (Polyphen2HDIV) was used to evaluate the pathogenicity. Three MYH6 variants (D629N, V893M, A1327T) were discovered from three probands.

Zebrafish generation and maintenance

We constructed myh6 gene knockout zebrafish line using CRISPR/Cas9. Two gRNA sequences were designed by CHOPCHOP (https://chopchop.cbu.uib.no/) to target the myh6 gene: Target1: 5’-ATAGGTGTAGATCATCCATG-3’ and Target2: 5’-GATGTAGTCGCTGCTTACCG-3’. No mismatch was found between these two targets. The DNA template was obtained by PCR amplification, and the gRNA was then synthesized. For CRISPR/Cas9 mutagenesis, a mixed solution containing 1 nL of Cas9 protein (800 ng/μL) and gRNA (320 ng/μL) was injected into one-cell stage WT embryos. After the injection, the genomic DNA of the embryos was obtained at 48-hour postfertilization (hpf) to check for mutagenesis at the target site. The PCR products were subjected to Sanger sequencing to confirm mutagenesis. Once the mutagenesis was confirmed, other injected embryos were raised to adulthood as F0 fish. The F1 generation was obtained by mating F0 fish with wild-type zebrafish carrying the Tg(myl7:eGFP) transgene and raising them to adulthood, allowing robust visualization of both atrial and ventricular structures for whole-heart imaging. Genomic DNA was extracted from the tail tissue of the F1 fish and analyzed using the methods mentioned above. Myh6 mutant allele (−8 bp) was identified through PCR and DNA sequencing. This allele was subsequently chosen for further propagation, leading to the establishment of stable myh6 mutant lines to facilitate subsequent analyses. Primers used for genotype verification: myh6-F: GCTCAAACCAGAGCCTTCGAT; myh6-R: CGGTCACTTCTCCTTGTGAGAT. Zebrafish were used for following functional studies and euthanized using an overdose of tricaine methanesulfonate (MS-222, 300 mg/L) in accordance with institutional guidelines.

Western blot

The zebrafish heart was meticulously dissected into atria and ventricles under a microscope, and tissue samples were then processed to extract proteins. The extracted protein samples were separated on acrylamide gels, transferred onto PVDF membranes at room temperature, followed by a 2-hour blocking step, and subsequently probed with antibodies. The primary antibodies used in this study were MYH6 (1:5000, Proteintech, 22281-1-AP), MYH7 (1:1000, Proteintech, 22280-1-AP), and GAPDH (1:1000, CST, 5174S), followed by incubation with secondary antibody, including goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (1:1000, Jackson ImmunoResearch, 111-035-003) and goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP (1:10000, Jackson ImmunoResearch, 115-035-003). The protein bands were visualized using iBright CL1000 (Invitrogen). The MYH6 antibody was stripped from the membrane using a stripping buffer (Beyotime, China) after the initial exposure. Subsequently, the membrane was re-blocked, probed with the MYH7 antibody, and subjected to a second exposure. Protein expression levels were normalized to those of GAPDH. Uncropped and unprocessed full-length blot images corresponding to the main figures are provided in Supplementary Fig S3.

Zebrafish larva imaging

Systolic and diastolic activities were recorded via video microscopy. The heartbeats were observed and recorded in detail. Zebrafish cardiac function at 72 h postfertilization (hpf) was evaluated by videos captured with an OLYMPUS stereomicroscope (SZX2-ILLB). The mean heart rate was measured over at least 30 seconds for each zebrafish. Ventricular-fractional area change (V-FAC) and atrial-fractional area change (A-FAC) were quantified using ImageJ software (Version 1.53). These parameters provide insights into ventricular and atrial function, respectively. Standard ECG (electrocardiography) and echocardiography techniques, as described previously45, were employed for comprehensive cardiac assessment.

Electrocardiography

The ECGs were recorded in vivo from adult zebrafish using PowerLab and LabChart Pro V8 (ADInstruments). Anaesthesia was induced using MS-222 at a concentration of 168 mg/L. Zebrafish were positioned with their ventral side up on a damp sponge. The positive electrode and negative and reference leads were inserted into the chest near the ventral midline at a depth of 1 mm. The negative lead was placed 0.5–1.0 mm left laterally to the positive electrode. The recording had a sampling rate of 1000 Hz and utilized low-pass and high-pass filters set at ‘120 Hz’ and ‘0.03 s’, respectively. ECG indices, including heart rate, P duration, PR interval, and QRS duration, were measured using LabChart Pro V8.

Echocardiography

To comprehensively evaluate atrial function in adult zebrafish, we conducted high-frequency echocardiography utilizing the Vevo2100® Imaging System and Vevo Imaging Station (VisualSonics, Amsterdam, Netherlands). The procedure employed a high-frequency transducer (MS550D, bandwidth 22–55 MHz, central operating frequency 40 MHz) based on our established protocols.

Fish were anaesthetized with MS-222 at a concentration of 168 mg/L and securely positioned in a damp sponge with a midline incision. A plastic sheet was placed over the fish to prevent gill contact with the ultrasound gel. The transducer was carefully positioned longitudinally along the ventral side of the fish, allowing clear visualization of the ventricle, atria, and bulbus arteriosus. Longitudinal axis view (LAX) images were captured in B-Mode, and the fractional area change (FAC) of the atrium was calculated using the formula: FAC = (Areadiastole − Areasystole)/Areadiastole.

In pulse-wave Doppler mode, the sample volume gate was centred on the atrioventricular valve to measure ventricular blood inflow velocity. The peak E-wave and A-wave velocities were identified and quantified from the generated graph.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Atrial tissues were fixed overnight at 4 °C in 4% glutaraldehyde. Following fixation, samples underwent thorough rinsing with PBS and were subsequently stained with 2% uranyl acetate. High-resolution images were acquired using a transmission electron microscope. Quantification of sarcomere length was performed by drawing a horizontal line between two Z-discs using ImageJ. At least 20 sarcomeres from 5 different fields of view were selected for measurement in each group46.

RNA-sequencing and analysis

Heart tissue samples from six 6-month-old zebrafish were collected (WT vs. myh6+/−, n = 3 in each group). Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, 15596026) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. The integrity and concentration of the RNA samples were quantified utilizing NanoDrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A30221) and verified for purity through 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Following stringent quality control measures, cDNA libraries were constructed using the TruSeq RNA LT Sample Prep Kit v2 (Illumina), with subsequent amplification performed on cBOT (Illumina) platforms. Sequencing was carried out using the Illumina Novaseq 6000 instrument. The raw data was handled by Skewer and data quality was checked by FastQC v0.11.2. The read length was 2×150 bp. Clean reads were aligned to the zebrafish genome GRCz11 using STAR. The raw data were uploaded to the NCBI database (Accession: PRJNA1094457). Sequencing data were analyzed using R (version 2023.03.0 + 386). Differential expression analysis between the experimental (myh6+/−) and control (wild-type) groups was performed using the DESeq2 package. P-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Due to the limited number of genes passing the FDR threshold, genes with |log2(fold change)| ≥ 0.5 and nominal P value ≤ 0.05 were additionally selected for exploratory analyses. These genes were used for downstream Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) using the clusterProfiler package47. Protein–protein interaction networks were constructed using STRING (v12.0, https://string-db.org/).

Statistical analysis

The difference between the two groups was assessed using Student’s t test. The difference between the three groups was evaluated using the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). The statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

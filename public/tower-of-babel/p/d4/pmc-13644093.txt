Enhancement of solubility and antioxidant activity of quercetin in choline-aspartate ionic liquid: spectroscopic, volumetric and kinetic insights
Authors: Vibek Srichandan, Sanghamitra Pradhan, Malabika Talukdar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractQuercetin is a biologically active flavonoid which has well established antioxidant properties, but its bioavailability and functional applicability are constrained due to its extremely poor aqueous solubility. In the present study, a biocompatible [Ch][Asp] IL was synthesized through a one pot metathesis neutralization route and investigated as a green functional medium for enhancing the solubility and scavenging activity of quercetin. FTIR, NMR UV-Vis and fluorescence spectroscopy confirmed the successful synthesis of the IL and extensive hydrogen bonding interactions with quercetin. Volumetric investigations of the aqueous IL demonstrated pronounced ion-solvent interactions and structure making behaviour. Solubility studies revealed a concentration dependent enhancement of solubility of quercetin in 0.1% and 0.2% v/v IL: water than in pure water. DPPH radical scavenging studies demonstrated the improved antioxidant efficiency of quercetin in the IL medium. Kinetic investigation revealed an accelerated radical neutralization behaviour thus highlighting the active role of [Ch][Asp] in modulating antioxidant process. These findings establish the [Ch][Asp] IL as a promising green excipient for enhancing the physicochemical performance of poorly soluble bioactive compounds.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13065-026-01883-1.Keywords: Choline aspartate IL, DPPH, Quercetin, Antioxidant, Volumetric analysis, Solubility

Introduction

Free radicals and reactive oxygen species (ROS) play a major role in the pathogenesis of a wide range of chronic and degenerative diseases including heart problems, neurological disorders, diabetes and different types of cancer [1, 2]. Dietary polyphenols have thus drawn tremendous scientific interest as natural antioxidant agents that are capable of neutralizing the free radicals and safeguarding cellular integrity [3]. Quercetin (3,3’,4’5,7-pentahydroxyflavone) is among the most potent and extensively studied flavonoids, exhibiting antioxidant, antiviral, anticancer, anti-inflammatory properties [4, 5]. The five phenolic hydroxyl groups contribute to the exceptional free radical scavenging capacity of quercetin mainly at the 3-OH, 4’-OH and catechol B-ring positions that donate atoms or electrons to neutralize the DPPH, superoxide and hydroxyl radicals via hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) and single electron transfer (SET) mechanisms [6, 7]. Despite these remarkable properties, the clinical and nutraceutical approach is limited by its poor aqueous solubility resulting in a very poor oral bioavailability below 10% and limited cellular uptake [8, 9]. Co-crystallization, polymeric micelles, nanoparticles, liposomal encapsulation and cyclodextrin complexation are the various formulation strategies have been explored to address the limitations of quercetin’s bioavailability [10, 11], but these are frequently accompanied with disadvantages such as complex manufacturing procedures, use of toxic organic solvents, poor scalability and limited safety. This has sparked growing interest in the design of inherently biocompatible media that can enhance solubility while simultaneously serving as functional excipients. Owing to the negligible vapour pressure, broad liquid range, high thermal stability and tunable physicochemical properties, ionic liquids (ILs) have emerged as a transformative class of designer solvents [12, 13]. ILs are composed of discrete, fully dissociated cation-anion pairs typically generated through metathesis, neutralization or quaternization reactions, and remain liquid below 100°C as single ionic species. This distinguishes them mechanistically from deep eutectic solvents (DES), which instead arise from a eutectic mixture of two or more components, commonly a salt and a hydrogen-bond donor, whose melting point is depressed by extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding without necessarily requiring complete ionic dissociation. Although the two solvent classes are often discussed together as green, tunable alternatives to conventional organic solvents, this distinction in formation mechanism is relevant to the present system, in which [Ch][Asp] forms via discrete proton transfer between choline hydroxide and aspartic acid rather than eutectic mixing [14]. Among IL subclass, choline-amino acid based ILs are synthesized from renewable, biologically derived feedstocks, exhibit low cytotoxicity and are readily biodegradable [15]. The cholinium cation, a naturally occurring component of cell membranes and a precursor of acetylcholine, combines with various amino acids to yield bio-ILs with rich hydrogen bonding network [16, 17]. Caparica et al. demonstrated that [Ch][AA] ILs significantly enhance the solubility of the poorly water soluble drugs like rutin and ferulic acid while maintaining excellent cytocompatibility, validating their potential to be used as a green functional excipient in pharmaceutical formulations [18]. Recent studies explore [Ch][AA] ILs as next generation solvents across various applications [13].

Aspartic acid is a dicarboxylic amino acid bearing two carboxylate groups and a primary amine is particularly attractive as an anion precursor because of its essential hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor ability. Although choline-amino-acid ILs have previously been reported to improve the solubility of poorly water-soluble compounds and influence antioxidant behaviour, the relationship between solvent structuring, solubilization, and antioxidant performance remains insufficiently understood. We hypothesize that the dicarboxylate functionality of the aspartate anion can generate a highly structured hydrogen-bonding environment that promotes quercetin solubilization and modulates its antioxidant activity. Accordingly, the objective of this work was to investigate [Ch][Asp] as a green functional medium for quercetin through an integrated study involving spectroscopic characterization, volumetric analysis, solubility measurements, and antioxidant kinetic evaluation. Particular emphasis was placed on exploring the relationship between ion-solute interactions, solvent structuring, quercetin solubilization, and antioxidant performance, thereby providing insights into the rational design of AAILs as sustainable solubilizing media for poorly water-soluble bioactive compounds.

Methods

Synthesis of choline-aspartate ionic liquid ([Ch][Asp] IL)

Choline-Aspartate ([Ch][Asp]) IL was synthesised via a one-pot metathesis-neutralisation route, following the procedure reported by Zhang et al. [19]. with slight modifications based on our previous work [20]. The synthesis scheme is reported in the Fig. 1. Initially, choline chloride (5.91 g, 42.3 mmol) was dissolved in 25 mL of absolute ethanol and stirred at room temperature for 30 min. Potassium hydroxide (2.37 g, 42.3 mmol) was then added gradually over 5–10 min under continuous stirring. The reaction mixture was then stirred for 6 h at ambient conditions to generate choline hydroxide in situ. Subsequently, L-aspartic acid (5.92 g, 44.4 mmol) was added to the reaction mixture maintaining a molar ratio of ChCl : KOH : L-Asp = 1:1:1.05, followed by the addition of 10 mL ethanol to ensure complete dissolution and promote homogeneous mixing. The reaction was continued for 8 h at room temperature with continuous stirring. The resulting mixture was filtered to remove potassium chloride formed as a white precipitate. The filtrate was then concentrated under reduced pressure using a rotary evaporator to remove residual ethanol and water. The residue was then treated with acetonitrile/ethanol (9:1 v/v) to precipitate the unreacted L-Aspartic acid. The filtrate was then vacuum dried at 80o C to get the desired viscous [Ch][Asp] IL.

Determination of antioxidant activity by DPPH assay

The antioxidant activity of quercetin in the presence of the synthesized [Ch][Asp] IL was evaluated using the DPPH free radical scavenging assay method with minor modifications to the reported literature [21]. Ethanol was employed as the reaction medium in place of methanol similar to our previously reported work [22], as it provides comparable radical stability and absorption characteristics in the range of 515–520 nm. Fresh stock solutions of DPPH (1 mM) and quercetin (1 mM) were prepared afresh daily in absolute ethanol. The IL solutions were also prepared in ethanol at concentrations of 0.06 mM, 0.12 mM and 0.18 mM. Reaction mixtures (total volume 3.0 mL) were prepared by mixing 2.0 mL of DPPH solution, 0.5 mL of quercetin solution, and 0.5 mL of the corresponding [Ch][Asp] IL solution. Under these conditions, the final concentrations in the cuvette were 0.66 mM for DPPH, 0.16 mM for quercetin and 0.01, 0.02 and 0.03 mM for the IL respectively. Another system was also prepared in a similar manner using ethanol in place of the IL solution to compare the scavenging activity of quercetin without the IL. Control experiments were also performed by replacing the quercetin solution with the corresponding [Ch][Asp] IL solutions to evaluate the intrinsic DPPH radical scavenging activity of the IL under identical experimental conditions. All reaction mixtures were incubated in the dark at room temperature for 30–35 min to allow completion of radical scavenging. The decrease in absorbance was recorded at 518 nm using a Shimadzu UV spectrophotometer (Model- UV1900i) with the 1mM DPPH solution as control. Solubility studies

The solubility of quercetin was evaluated in distilled water and in aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL (0.1% and 0.2% v/v IL: water) using an equilibrium solubility method with slight modifications from our previously reported work [22]. To perform the solubility studies, saturated solution of quercetin was prepared in water and mixtures of IL: water in tightly sealed glass vials. These mixtures were magnetically stirred at room temperature () for 24 h to attain equilibrium. After equilibration, these suspensions were filtered to remove the undissolved solutes. The saturated samples were then analyzed using the Shimadzu UV spectrophotometer (UV 1900i) at absorption maximum of quercetin i.e., 370 nm [18, 22]. All solubility measurements were performed in triplicate using independently prepared samples, and the results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results and discussions

Spectroscopic characterisation

FTIR analysis of [Ch][Asp] IL and quercetin in 0.1% v/v [Ch][Asp]: H2O

Figure 2a and b represents the FT-IR spectra of [Ch][Asp] IL and quercetin in 0.1% v/v [Ch][Asp]: [H2O] respectively. The FT-IR spectrum of the synthesised IL exhibits several characteristic absorption bands confirming the presence of both the cholinium and aspartate moieties within the ionic framework. A broad and intense absorption band centred at 3244.17 corresponds to the overlapping O-H and N-H stretching vibrations in choline and amino acid respectively [26, 27]. The broad nature of this peak indicates extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding within the IL network. Similar broad absorptions have previously been reported for choline- AAILs [27]. The absorption band observed at 2960.12 is assigned to the aliphatic C-H stretching vibration of the methylene groups present in the cholinium cation. The strong bands located at 1566.62 and 1477.81 are attributed to the asymmetric stretching vibration of the COO− group and N-H bending modes of amino acid residue, confirming the deprotonation of aspartic acid and formation of ionic pair. The disappearance of the characteristic free carboxylic acid C = O stretching band for aspartic acid near 1700 further supports IL formation through proton transfer between choline hydroxide and aspartic acid. The peak at 1380.66 corresponds to CH2 bending vibrations, while the bands at 1238.91 , 1136.14 , 1085.83 and 1056.33 are associated with C-O and C-N stretching vibrations of the cholinium backbone. These spectral characteristics collectively confirm the successful synthesis of the [Ch][Asp] IL and are in good agreement with previously reported [Ch][AA] IL systems [26, 27].

FT-IR spectra of a [Ch][Asp] IL and b Quercetin in 0.1% v/v [Ch][Asp]IL: H2O

Further insight into the intermolecular interactions between quercetin and the IL medium was obtained from the FT-IR spectrum of quercetin in 0.1% v/v aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL solution shown in Fig. 2b. A broad absorption band centred at 3338.79 was observed corresponding to the overlapping O-H stretching vibrations of quercetin phenolic groups, water molecules and IL network. Compared with the pure IL spectrum, this band becomes broader and slightly shifted, indicating strengthened H-bonding interactions between quercetin and the IL constituents. Such spectral broadening is characteristic of enhanced intermolecular association and solvent structuring around flavonoid molecules in the IL media [22]. A distinct absorption band at 1635 is attributed primarily to the conjugated carbonyl (C = O) stretching vibration of quercetin coupled with aromatic C = C skeletal vibrations. The slight shift and modification in band intensity relative to free quercetin suggest specific interactions between the flavonoid carbonyl/phenolic groups and the IL ions. Additionally, the weak band observed at 2117.26 may arise from overtone or combination vibrational modes generated due to strong intermolecular coupling within the hydrogen-bonded microenvironment.

The observed spectral modifications strongly suggest that the [Ch][Asp] IL creates a highly interactive solvation environment around quercetin through cooperative hydrogen bonding involving the hydroxyl and carbonyl groups of quercetin, the hydroxyl functionality of cholinium and the amino/carboxylate groups of aspartate anion. Such interactions likely contribute to the enhanced solubilisation and stabilisation of quercetin in the aqueous IL medium. This behaviour is consistent with previous reports describing AAILs as structured H-bonding media capable of modulating flavonoid solubility and physicochemical behaviour [22].

1H and 13C NMR of the [Ch][Asp] IL

The successful synthesis of [Ch][Asp] IL was confirmed by the 1H and 13C NMR spectroscopy in D2O. The corresponding spectra are shown in Fig. 3a, b. The obtained spectra exhibited the characteristic resonances corresponding to both the cholinium cation and aspartate anion, indicating the formation of the desired [Ch][Asp] IL. In the 1H NMR spectrum, the intense singlet observed at δ 3.19 ppm was assigned to the nine equivalent protons of the trimethylammonium protons of the cholinium cation, while the multiplet signals appearing in the region of δ 3.68–3.49 ppm correspond to the methylene protons of the hydroxyethyl chain of cholinium. The signal at δ 4.05 ppm was attributed to the α-methine proton of the aspartate anion, whereas the signals observed at δ 2.75–2.55 ppm correspond to β-methylene protons of aspartate. The observed chemical shifts and multiplicity patterns are consistent with the proposed ionic structure and indicate successful proton transfer between choline hydroxide and aspartic acid. In the 13C NMR, the peaks appearing at δ 181.13 and 179.66 ppm confirmed the presence of carboxylate and carbonyl carbons of the aspartate anion. The remaining signals observed in the aliphatic region were assigned to the carbon atoms of the cholinium and aspartate moieties. The obtained spectral data are consistent with previously reported [Ch][AA] ILs and confirm the successful synthesis of [Ch][Asp] [26]. UV-Vis absorption studies

The UV-Vis absorption spectra of [Ch][Asp] IL and quercetin in 0.1% v/v [Ch][Asp]: [H2O] were studied to understand how the IL microenvironment perturbs the electronic structure of the flavonoid. The absorption spectrum of the [Ch][Asp] IL shown in Fig. 4a is dominated by an intense band in the 200–230 nm region corresponding to the π-π* transitions associated with the aspartate anion’s carboxylate chromophore, along with another peak at around 260–270 nm arising from the n- π* transitions of the -COO− group. The UV-Vis spectra of quercetin in 0.1% [Ch][Asp]: H2O shown in Fig. 4b reveals a well-resolved absorption maximum at around 320 nm, corresponding to the B- ring cinnamoyl π-π* transition (Band I) of the flavonol chromophore. In conventional aqueous or methanolic media, this band typically appears between 316 and 375 nm depending on the medium polarity and hydrogen- bonding capacity [28, 29]. The position of this band in the current system is consistent with, yet subtly red-shifted relative to the values reported for quercetin in pure water, suggesting that the [Ch][Asp] IL medium creates a more polarisable and hydrogen bond rich solvation shell around the flavonoid. The aspartate anion containing both carboxylate (-COO−) and an α-amino (-NH2) group is well positioned to engage in strong H-bonding interactions with quercetin’s phenolic -OH groups, particularly those at 3-OH and 4’-OH positions which are widely known as the primary H-bond donors in the quercetin structure [6]. A similar red shift has been reported in our previous work, where the aromatic indole moiety of the tryptophan anion additionally contributes π-π stacking interactions [22]. In the present system, there is absence of an aromatic anion thus making hydrogen bonding the dominant interaction which is reflected in the relatively modest magnitude of the red shift compared to aromatic AAILs [17, 22].

Fluorescence emission studies

The emission spectrum of the [Ch][Asp] IL exhibits a broad emission peak at around 400–410 nm as shown in Fig. 5a. This emission is characteristic of the aspartate anion whose carboxylate and amino groups can form intramolecular hydrogen bonded excited states and is consistent with fluorescence spectrum obtained for amino acid derived ILs [15, 16]. The fluorescence spectrum of quercetin in 0.1% v/v [Ch][Asp] IL: H2O exhibited a dominant emission peak at approximately 560 nm. The emission at ~ 560 nm is characteristic of quercetin’s flavonol fluorescence which arises from an excited state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT) mechanism from the 3-OH group to the adjacent C-4 carbonyl generating the tautomeric keto form in the excited state [30, 31]. In neat aqueous medium the fluorescence of quercetin is typically weak and quenched with an emission maximum near 525–545 nm owing to efficient non-radiative deactivation through hydrogen bonding with bulk water and concentration dependent self-quenching arising from aggregation of the poorly soluble flavonol [30, 32]. The pronounced red shift to ~ 560 nm and the notably enhanced emission intensity observed in the [Ch] [Asp] IL medium thus indicate that the IL environment significantly modifies the excited state dynamics of quercetin and solubility of quercetin [18, 21]. Taken together the UV-Vis and fluorescence data coherently reinforce the suitability of [Ch][Asp] as a biocompatible, green medium for quercetin formulation offering solubility enhancement.

Volumetric analysis

Density measurements

The density of aqueous solutions of synthesized [Ch][Asp] IL was measured over the molality range and within the temperature interval 293.15–313.15 K at atmospheric pressure. The values are listed in Table S2 and graphically represented in the Fig. 6 as a function of concentration and temperature. The experimentally determined density values increased systematically with increasing concentration of the IL, whereas an opposite trend was observed with rise in temperature. Such pattern is characteristic of aqueous IL systems and reflects progressive strengthening of intermolecular association in solution. At higher IL concentrations, enhanced electrostatic attraction and stronger solute-solvent packing reduce the free volume of the system, resulting in increased density values. Conversely, elevation in temperature weakens intermolecular cohesion owing to thermal expansion and enhanced ionic mobility, thereby lowering the density of the medium [33–35]. The observed concentration dependence also indicates substantial hydration of both cholinium cations and aspartate anions. In aqueous medium, the carboxylate groups of aspartate strongly interact with surrounding H2O molecules through ion-dipole and hydrogen-bonding interactions, generating compact hydration shells around the ions. Similar concentration- and temperature-dependent density behaviour has previously been reported for amino-acid-based cholinium ILs in water [36, 37].

Positive value of the Hepler constant were obtained for aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL, indicating that the IL behaves as a structure-maker in water. The structure-making tendency of AAILs has been attributed primarily to extensive hydrogen bonding and electro-strictive solvation effects [20, 22, 41]. In the present system, the aspartate anion exerts pronounced hydration owing to the presence of dual carboxylate functionalities, thereby reinforcing solvent organization more effectively than less polar amino acid anions. Overall, the volumetric parameters clearly reveal dominant ion-solvent interactions, appreciable hydration, and significant structure-promoting behaviour of aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL over the investigated temperature range.

Collectively, the volumetric parameters indicate strong ion-water interactions and enhanced solvent structuring in aqueous [Ch][Asp] solutions. These physicochemical characteristics are consistent with the improved solubilization of quercetin observed in this study and may contribute to the enhanced antioxidant performance by providing a favourable solvation environment. However, further molecular-level investigations are required to establish the detailed mechanistic relationship between solvent structuring and antioxidant behaviour.

Solubility study of quercetin in aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL

To comprehend the influence of the [Ch][Asp] IL on the solubility of the poorly soluble flavonoid, quercetin, solubility studies were performed in distilled water and in different IL: water mixtures at 25 ± 1°C. The selected IL concentrations were chosen as they lie within the reported non-toxic concentration range for cholinium-AAILs while allowing evaluation of the concentration dependent solubilisation behaviour of quercetin [18, 42], the studied IL significantly enhance the solubility of quercetin, to ensure its safe functionality.

Quercetin exhibited very poor solubility in pure water, with an experimentally determined value of 0.0709 ± 0.0008 mg/mL, consistent with the well-established hydrophobic nature of flavonoids possessing rigid polyphenolic frameworks and strong intermolecular crystal packing. The low aqueous solubility of quercetin is primarily associated with its extensive intramolecular hydrogen bonding, high crystallinity, and limited ability to disrupt the three-dimensional hydrogen-bonded structure of water. Similar observations have been widely reported for poorly water-soluble flavonoids and polyphenolic bio-actives [17, 18, 42].

A substantial enhancement in quercetin solubility (p < 0.001) was observed upon incorporation of aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL as shown in Fig. 8. In the presence of 0.1% (v/v) [Ch][Asp], the solubility increased to 0.2959 ± 0.0049 mg/mL, while further increasing the IL concentration to 0.2% (v/v) resulted in a solubility of 0.4169 ± 0.0061 mg/mL. Relative to pure water, these values correspond to approximately 4.2-fold and 5.9-fold enhancements, respectively, clearly demonstrating the concentration-dependent solubilization capability of the IL medium. The progressive enhancement in solubility with IL concentration indicates that even small amounts of [Ch][Asp] are capable of significantly modifying the local solvent environment and promoting dissolution of quercetin through cooperative intermolecular interactions. This behaviour is in good agreement with previous reports on amino-acid-based choline ILs, where increasing IL concentration resulted in systematic enhancement of poorly soluble drug dissolution through hydrogen-bond-assisted solvation and hydrotropic organization [17]. The remarkable solubility enhancement produced by [Ch][Asp] can be attributed to synergistic solute-solvent interactions operating within the ternary quercetin-IL-water system. The hydroxyl groups of quercetin are capable of forming strong hydrogen bonds with both the hydroxyl functionality of the cholinium cation and the carboxylate groups of the aspartate anion. These interactions weaken the intermolecular forces responsible for quercetin crystal stabilization and facilitate transfer of the flavonoid molecules into the aqueous phase. The aspartate anion, possessing multiple hydrophilic functional groups, further contributes to stabilization of dissolved quercetin through extensive hydrogen-bond networking with surrounding water molecules.

Solubility of quercetin in water and aqueous [Ch][Asp] solutions at 25 ± 1° C. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Statistical significance was determined by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. *** indicates p < 0.001

In addition to specific hydrogen-bond interactions, the amphiphilic characteristics of the IL are expected to contribute to hydrotropic solubilization behaviour. Choline-AAILs are known to disrupt the ordered water structure surrounding hydrophobic aromatic compounds and create micro-heterogeneous domains capable of accommodating poorly soluble bio-actives. Such IL-induced solvent restructuring improves molecular dispersion and suppresses self-association of flavonoid molecules in aqueous media. Caparica et al. similarly demonstrated that amino-acid-derived choline ILs act as efficient green functional excipients capable of markedly increasing the solubility of rutin and ferulic acid at non-toxic concentrations [18].

The stronger solubilization observed at 0.2% (v/v) [Ch][Asp] compared with 0.1% (v/v) further suggests that increased IL content enhances the extent of solvent structuring and intermolecular association around quercetin molecules. As the concentration of IL increases, a greater number of ionic and hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor sites become available to interact with quercetin, resulting in improved stabilization of the dissolved state. The enhanced solubility therefore reflects the combined influence of hydrogen bonding, ion-dipole interactions, hydrotropic aggregation, and solvent reorganization induced by the biocompatible AAIL.

Overall, the results establish aqueous [Ch][Asp] IL as an efficient and sustainable solubilizing medium for quercetin. Even at very low concentrations, the IL markedly improves quercetin dissolution in water, highlighting the potential of AAILs as green functional excipients for enhancing the physicochemical performance and formulation applicability of poorly water-soluble flavonoids and related bioactive compounds.

Antioxidant activity by DPPH assay method

The antioxidant behaviour of quercetin towards the DPPH radical was evaluated in the presence and absence of [Ch][Asp] IL at different concentrations under identical experimental conditions as employed in our previously reported [Ch][Trp] system [22]. The decrease in absorbance at λmax = 518 nm was monitored spectrophotometrically after the introduction of quercetin solution into the DPPH solution containing different concentrations of [Ch][Asp] IL. The pure DPPH solution exhibited a high absorbance value which is characteristic of the stable purple DPPH radical [6]. Upon addition of quercetin solution, the absorbance decreased sharply confirming efficient reduction of DPPH through hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) and/or single electron transfer (SET) processes associated with the phenolic hydroxyl groups of quercetin [7]. Interestingly, the addition of [Ch][Asp] IL of different concentrations further lowered the absorbance values, indicating enhanced radical quenching in the IL medium.

The mean RSA values increased from for quercetin alone to , , and in the presence of 0.06, 0.12, and 0.18 mM [Ch][Asp], respectively (concentration in cuvette: 0.01, 0.02, and 0.03 mM, respectively). One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test showed that all IL-treated samples exhibited significantly higher radical scavenging activity than quercetin alone (p < 0.001), whereas no statistically significant differences were observed among the three IL concentrations, indicating that the antioxidant enhancement reached a plateau under the investigated experimental conditions. Unlike the previously studied [Ch][Trp] system, where concentration dependent modulation and partial suppression at sub-optimal concentrations were observed, the [Ch][Asp] IL system exhibited a comparatively more uniform enhancement of RSA over the investigated concentration range [22]. This behaviour may arise from the structural characteristics of the aspartate anion. The aspartate moiety contains two carboxylate functionalities capable of forming extensive hydrogen-bonding interactions with the phenolic -OH groups and carbonyl functionality of quercetin. Such interactions can stabilize the oxidized semiquinone intermediate generated during radical scavenging and facilitate proton transfer from quercetin to DPPH [4].

To evaluate the intrinsic antioxidant contribution of the IL, control DPPH assays were performed using aqueous [Ch][Asp] solutions in the absence of quercetin. The IL alone exhibited only low radical scavenging activity (2.5–8.1%), which was substantially lower than that observed for the quercetin-containing systems (> 93%). These results indicate that the enhanced antioxidant performance of the IL-quercetin system primarily arises from quercetin, while the intrinsic contribution of the IL is comparatively minor.

In addition, the hydrophilic choline cation contributes to the formation of a structured solvation environment around quercetin, increasing molecular accessibility toward the DPPH radicals. The cooperative hydrogen bonding network generated by the amino acid anion and the hydroxyl bearing choline cation may therefore reduce the energetic barrier associated with the HAT/SET process. Similar enhancement of flavonoid antioxidant behaviour in IL-mediated microenvironments has been reported previously for amino acid based and imidazolium IL systems, where solvent organisation and hydrogen bond assisted electron transfer played decisive role in modulating the radical scavenging efficiency [21, 22].

The relatively small variation in RSA between 0.06 and 0.18 mM [Ch][Asp] further suggests that even low concentrations of the IL are sufficient to establish a favourable hydrogen bonding environment for quercetin. Once such a structured microenvironment is established, additional IL only contributes marginally to the overall scavenging efficiency. This observation is significant as it indicates that highly concentrated IL media may not be necessary for achieving efficient antioxidant enhancement, thereby preserving the green and biocompatible nature of the system.

Although enhanced quercetin solubility in the [Ch][Asp] medium is likely to improve its molecular dispersion and accessibility toward DPPH radicals, the present study does not establish a direct causal relationship between solubilization and antioxidant enhancement. The observed improvement in radical scavenging activity is therefore more appropriately attributed to the combined influence of improved solubilization and favourable solute–solvent interactions within the IL microenvironment.

Overall, the DPPH end point assay confirms that [Ch][Asp] appears to function as an efficient biocompatible reaction medium that enhances the radical scavenging performance of quercetin likely through cooperative hydrogen bonding, solvation restructuring. The findings further support the growing concept that AAILs may function not only as solubilising media but also as active modulators of antioxidant behaviour.

Time-resolved radical scavenging of quercetin in [Ch][Asp] Medium

The temporal evolution of DPPH radical scavenging was monitored spectrophotometrically over a period of 0–1800 s in the presence of 0.18 mM IL concentration to gain the mechanical insight into the kinetic behaviour of quercetin in the [Ch][Asp] IL environment. The absorbance decay profile and corresponding percentage radical scavenging activity are shown in Fig. 10(a) and 10 (b) respectively.

a Absorbance decay profile of Quercetin in presence of 0.18 mM [Ch][Asp] over a period of 1800s b % RSA with time interval

The absorbance decreased rapidly within the first few minutes. Thereafter, the decay became progressively slower, ultimately approaching a near-constant value after ~ 900 s. Correspondingly, the %RSA increased sharply from ~ 40.7% at the initial stage to ~ 94% at 360 s, before gradually approaching a plateau value of ~ 97.5% at longer time intervals.

The kinetic profile clearly reveals a biphasic radical scavenging process consisting of an initial rapid reaction stage followed by a slower stabilization regime. The fast initial phase is attributed to direct HAT/SET interactions between quercetin and DPPH radicals, where the phenolic hydroxyl groups of quercetin rapidly donate hydrogen atoms or electrons to neutralize DPPH. The pronounced decrease in absorbance during the first 300–400 s suggests that the [Ch][Asp] IL medium facilitates this primary electron-transfer step efficiently [24].

The slower phase observed beyond 480 s likely originates from diffusion-limited processes and structural reorganization within the IL microenvironment. At this stage, most of the accessible DPPH radicals have already been consumed, and the remaining radical-antioxidant encounters become progressively slower. Simultaneously, stabilization of the quercetin semiquinone intermediate and rearrangement of the hydrogen-bonding network within the IL medium contribute to the asymptotic behaviour of the kinetic curve.

The role of the aspartate anion appears particularly significant in this context. Unlike aromatic amino-acid anions such as tryptophan, the aspartate anion primarily contributes through strong electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions rather than π-π stacking. The presence of two carboxylate groups enables extensive interaction with quercetin’s hydroxyl functionalities, thereby stabilizing both the ground and oxidized intermediate states. This stabilization likely prolongs the lifetime of transient antioxidant species and sustains the radical-quenching process over extended time scales.

In comparison with the previously reported [Ch][Trp] system, the [Ch][Asp] medium demonstrates a higher %RSA (~ 97.5%) together with accelerated radical neutralization kinetics [22]. The enhanced performance of [Ch][Asp] may be attributed to the stronger hydrogen-bonding capability of the aspartate anion, whose dual carboxylate functionalities facilitate proton-transfer processes and stabilize the oxidized quercetin intermediates in the ethanol-rich medium. Unlike [Ch][Trp], where π-π interactions contributed significantly to radical stabilization, the [Ch][Asp] system appears to be governed predominantly by cooperative hydrogen-bonding and solvent-assisted electron/proton transfer mechanisms [6, 43]. Collectively, the time-resolved study demonstrates that [Ch][Asp] is not merely a passive solvent medium but actively modulates the kinetics of antioxidant reactions. Through a combination of hydrogen-bond-mediated stabilization, solvent structuring, and enhanced proton-transfer efficiency, the IL creates a favourable microenvironment for rapid and sustained DPPH radical neutralization by quercetin.

Kinetic analysis

The kinetic behaviour of DPPH radical scavenging by quercetin in the presence of 0.18 mM [Ch][Asp] IL revealed a rapid decrease in absorbance at 518 nm during the initial stage of the reaction as shown in Fig. 6a followed by a gradual plateau at longer time intervals which is a characteristic of antioxidant-radical systems involving fast primary scavenging steps and slower secondary processes [24, 25]. The reaction proceeds via coupled HAT and SET pathways, generating a quercetin derived phenoxyl radical intermediate and reduced DPPH-H. Under pseudo first order conditions, the rate depends solely on DPPH concentration, with the excess antioxidant effectively incorporated into the apparent rate constant [45]. The observed rate constant in the 10− 3 range indicates a kinetically favourable system with rapid radical quenching. The comparatively high k1 and k2 values in the present system indicate that the [Ch][Asp] IL provides a kinetically favourable microenvironment that facilitates proton- coupled electron transfer. A clear enhancement relative to the previously reported [Ch][Trp] system ( highlights the decisive role of anion structure [22]. In the previous system, π-π interactions involving the indole moiety promote formation of stabilised pre-reactive complexes, which can impose kinetic constraints. In contrast, the current system is governed by strong hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions arising from the dicarboxylate functionality of aspartate. This leads to a more dynamic and polar microenvironment that facilitates proton coupled electron transfer while avoiding kinetically restrictive stacking interactions. Consequently, the reaction proceeds more efficiently as reflected in the higher rate constant. Overall, the kinetic analysis demonstrates that the [Ch][Asp] IL provides a highly favourable medium for quercetin-mediated DPPH radical scavenging, leading to enhanced reaction kinetics relative to the previously studied [Ch][Trp] system [22]. The results establish that the transition from a π-interaction-dominated environment to a hydrogen-bond-driven IL network substantially improves reaction efficiency, offering important mechanistic insight into the rational design of AAILs for antioxidant and related redox applications.

The half maximal effective concentration () defined as the concentration of antioxidant required to reduce the initial concentration of DPPH by exactly 50% at steady state, is the most widely used parameter for expressing the radical scavenging potency of an antioxidant [23, 46]. A lower value signifies a more potent antioxidant, as fewer moles of the compound are needed to quench the same number of free radicals. The intersection of this linear regression with the 50% threshold yielded an value of 0.0047 mM for the [Ch][Asp]-Quercetin system. The linear relationship between quercetin concentration and % DPPH remaining was significant (r2 ≥ 0.99), confirming that the measurements were conducted within the dose-response linear range as recommended [23].

This value of 0.0047 mM is remarkably low in comparison with values reported in the literature for quercetin alone and for related phenolic systems. Villaño et al. [23] reported an of 0.0055 mM for quercetin measured against DPPH in a methanol-ethanol-water medium, while under comparable conditions, caffeic acid and gallic acid displayed values of 0.0121 mM and 0.0051 mM respectively. The current system thus performs comparably to and in several cases better than these benchmark phenolic antioxidants. Similarly, Ahmad et al. reported values ranging from 0.0184 to 0.0260 mM for ferulate based protic ILs measured in ethanol, values that are approximately 4 to 6-fold higher than that observed in the current system [24]. The notably low of the [Ch][Asp]- quercetin system can be attributed to a synergistic interplay between the bioactive quercetin scaffold which carries five hydroxyl groups capable of H-atom donation and the [Ch][Asp] IL. The amino acid derived aspartate anion with its carboxylate and amine functionalities is known to participate in proton coupled electron transfer processes [24], and the choline cation may facilitate molecular recognition and orientation of quercetin relative to the DPPH centre, collectively lowering the effective concentration needed for half-maximal inhibition.

Furthermore, the aspartate anion bearing both carboxylate (-COO−) and amino (-NH2) groups may contribute additional H-atom donors to the reaction medium, collectively reducing the time required to reach the radical scavenging equilibrium.

Conclusion

The successful synthesis of a biocompatible [Ch][Asp] IL is explored as a sustainable functional medium for improving the antioxidant performance of quercetin. The successful formation of the IL was confirmed by the spectroscopic investigations which also revealed extensive hydrogen-bond mediated interactions between quercetin and the IL microenvironment. The combined FTIR, UV-Vis and fluorescence studies revealed that the [Ch][Asp] system creates a highly interactive and structured solvation environment capable for the stabilisation of quercetin in aqueous medium. The existence of strong ion-solvent interactions, significant hydration behaviour and positive structure-making behaviour in water was further established by the volumetric studies. The pronounced solvent ordering tendency of the aqueous IL system was confirmed by the positive Hepler constant and increasing limiting apparent molar volume with temperature. This physicochemical characteristics play a vital role in modifying the dissolution behaviour of quercetin. The synthesised IL showed remarkable solubilisation capability showing nearly six-fold enhancement in the solubility of quercetin even at a very low IL concentrations. Cooperative hydrogen bonding, ion-dipole interactions induced by the AAIL are primarily responsible for such enhancement. The [Ch][Asp] medium significantly enhanced the antioxidant performance of quercetin which is shown by the increased DPPH radical scavenging activity, low value and high antiradical efficiency. Kinetic investigation revealed the IL actively facilitates proton coupled electron transfer and accelerates radical neutralisation via a hydrogen bond driven microenvironment. Collectively, the present findings demonstrate that [Ch][Asp] is a promising, sustainable and biocompatible green solubilising medium for quercetin capable of improving solubility and antioxidant efficiency of poorly water-soluble flavonoids. Future investigations may focus on molecular level computational studies like molecular dynamics simulations and density functional theory (DFT) calculations to achieve deeper understanding about the H-bonding interactions and proton coupled electron transfer mechanisms governing the elevated antioxidant behaviour. Further, comprehensive cytotoxicity, biocompatibility, formulation stability and in vitro/ in vivo investigations are essential before considering pharmaceutical applications of the [Ch][Asp]- quercetin system in biomedical formulations. In addition, extending this approach to other poorly water-soluble flavonoids and polyphenols may establish AAILs as versatile and tuneable green medium for advanced drug delivery, antioxidant therapy.

Ayurvedic management of Budd Chiari Syndrome – A case report
Authors: Manisha Chaudhary, Mandip Goyal, Charmi Mehta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBudd-Chiari Syndrome (BCS) is an uncommon liver disorder characterized by obstruction of hepatic venous outflow. BCS is a rare condition affecting one in a million adults. The obstruction may be thrombotic or non-thrombotic anywhere along the venous course from the hepatic venules to the junction of the inferior vena cava to the right atrium. Patient may present with abdominal pain, ascites along with hepatomegaly, jaundice, bleeding in the oesophagus, oedema in the legs, hepatic encephalopathy and liver failure. Treatment for Budd-Chiari syndrome in modern science are dissolving blood clots, improve liver function and treat complication. A 51-year-old female patient presented with complaints of pain in right side of abdomen, loss of appetite, abdominal heaviness, abdominal distension, nausea and swelling at bilateral lower leg for one month. Ultrasonography of the abdomen revealed cirrhosis of the liver, thrombosis of the right, middle, and left hepatic veins, along with features of BCS with gross ascites. She is known case of Budd-Chiari syndrome along with gross ascites. The patient was treated with Deepana (which simulates the digestion) and Pachana (enhancing digestion) Koshthashodhana (mild purgation) followed by Vardhamana pippalirasayana(administration of Pippali in a increasing dose and then tapering the dose) and Shamana chikitsa (pacifying treatment)). Pathya-apathya (wholesome diet and regimen) were advised in line with the management of Jalodara for three months. After three-months treatment there was changes in liver profile (SGPT reduced from 250 to 47, SGOT from 328 to 68, alkaline phosphate 272 to 134mg/dl). The assessment of quality of life using the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) indicated improvement in quality of life. This suggests that Ayurvedic treatment can give symptomatic relief and help to prevent it further complication and also improve the overall quality of life.Keywords: BuddChiari syndrome, Vardhaman pippalirasayana, Yakritavikara

Introduction

Budd-Chiari Syndrome (ICD- 10 code – I82.0) is an uncommon hepatic disorder characterized by obstruction of hepatic venous outflow. BCS occurs due to the obstruction of hepatic veins or terminal inferior vena cava (IVC). It is very rare disorder affecting one in a million adults [1]. The most important causes of BCS are hyper-coagulopathy, myelo-proliferative disorders, malignancy, lesions of the liver, pregnancy and oral contraceptives, in which 80 % of the cases are due to hyper-coagulable state and 20 % of the cases are idiopathic [2]. Due to thrombosis and obstruction to venous flow, there is decreased blood flow to the liver that lead to portal hypertension and as a result, there is hypoxic injury to the hepatocytes. In acute case, this leads to liver failure; whereas in chronic cases, ascites and hepatomegaly may develop with preserved liver function, indicating that the liver is still capable of performing its essential functions despite venous congestion. However, over time, persistent congestion can lead to fibrosis and cirrhosis, resulting in progressive liver dysfunction [3]. There is no individual test that can establish the diagnosis of Budd-Chiari syndrome. The diagnosis based on classical clinical manifestations and condition predisposing to thrombosis or ultrasonography, computed tomography scan (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) may be helpful. There is no established pharmacological treatment for BCS but, the treatment is focused on removing the obstruction, preventing the progression of the clot, limiting progressive liver injury, and preventing or managing complications [4].

There is no exact clinical entity mentioned in Ayurveda like BCS but on the basis of signs and symptoms, it can be co-related with Jalodara (ascites) associated with Yakrita vikara (∼liver disease). Acharya Charaka, Sushruta and Vagbhata have described Jalodara along with eight types of Udara roga (abdominal disease). Mandagni (deranged digestion) is the main cause of Udararoga, where excessive Dosha accumulates in Udara [5]. The main aim of treatment is Agnideepana, Nitya virechana (daily purgation) followed by Shamanachikitsa (pacifying treatment).

Patient information

A 51-year-old female homemaker presented with one month history of mild, continuous pain on the right side of her abdomen, which worsened after taking meals. She also complained of abdominal heaviness and distension following meals, along with swelling in bilateral lower limb, experienced nausea, decreased appetite and had a general feeling of weakness for the one month. She consulted allopathic doctors for her complaints and was diagnosed as a case of Budd-Chiari syndrome after thorough investigations.

On further inquiry, it was found that 1.5 years ago, she experienced continuous pain and a burning sensation in bilateral lower limb, which worsened with walking. Despite taking painkillers, the pain persisted and intensified, accompanied by blackish discoloration on her right lower limb. She sought medical treatment and was diagnosed with bilateral lower limb thrombosis, for which she underwent a bilateral superficial femoral embolectomy.

She had one year history of type-2 diabetes mellitus and was on medication, includingclopidogrel (75 mg), aspirin (75 mg) once daily, atorvastatin (40 mg) once daily, and propranolol (10 mg), bisoprolol (2.5 mg), pantoprazole (40 mg), and a combination of metformin (500 mg) and glimepiride (1 mg) twice daily. There is no history of hypertension, liver disorders, or any significant familial medical conditions.

Clinical findings

On physical examination, she exhibited pallor of conjunctiva and mild icterus in sclera. Pitting oedema (grade I) was present in bilateral lower limb. There was no evidence of clubbing, cyanosis or lymphadenopathy. The blood pressure was 110/70 mm Hg, and pulse was 68 beat per minute (feeble and regular). The BMI was 17.8 kg/m2.

Ashtavidha pariksha (eight-fold examination of patient) was done, which revealed Nadi (pulse) Pitta-vata pradhana, Mootra pravriti (urination) 3–4 time/day, once at night time, dark yellowish in colour, Mala pravriti (bowel habit): once a day, hard and unsatisfactory. Jihwa (tongue), was whitish and coated, Drika (vision) pariksha reveals that sclera was yellowish in colour and had Krish aakriti (ectomorph).

On systemic examination, respiratory examination revealed bilateral equal air entry with no any added sound. The patient was conscious and well oriented to time, place and person. Cardio-vascular examination revealed normal heart sound without murmurs. In gastro-intestinal examination, generalized abdominal distension was noted, with the umbilicus being centrally located and no visible superficial veins tenderness (grade +2) was present on palpation at right hypochondriac, epigastric and umbilical region on palpation. On percussion, shifting dullness was present. The abdominal girth measured at the level of umbilicus, 3 cm above the umbilicus and 3 cm below the umbilicus were 66 cm, 64 cm and 74 cm, respectively. During the anorectal examination, no spasms, protrusions, or abnormalities per-anum were detected. Additionally, there were no per anum tenderness, masses, or irregularities in rectal tone were observed.

Liver function tests showed elevated SGPT, SGOT, and both direct and total bilirubin levels [detail mentioned in Table 4]. CT imaging of the abdomen confirmed cirrhosis with volume redistribution, thrombosed right, middle, and left hepatic veins, and extensive ascites, further supporting the diagnosis of Budd-Chiari syndrome.

Therapeutic intervention

The entire course of the treatment was planned after evaluating the condition of patient and taking the consent of patient. Initially Deepana-pachana (which stimulates the digestive system) was given with Chitrakadi vati (two tablet three times a day) with lukewarm water before lunch and dinner, Haritaki (Terminalia chebula Retz.) choorna 3 gm before lunch and dinner with 5 ml Gomutra (cow urine). On the fifth day, after assessing the patient condition, Koshtha shodhana was given with Aaragvadhadi kashaya (150 ml), Eranda bhrishta haritaki (10 gm) Katuki (Pichrorhiza kurro Royle.) choorna 5 gm with lukewarm water was given in morning time. Patient had five Vegas (urges) of Mala, three days of Samsarjana krama (post therapy diet regimen) was advised which included Peya (thin gruel of rice), Vilepi (thick gruel of rice), Akrita yusha (non-processed soup of vegetables and/or pulses), and Kritayusha (processed soup of vegetables and/or pulses), followed by a normal diet in a sequential manner (see Table 1).

After the Samsarjana krama, Vardhamana pippali prayoga (medicine in increasing dose) was started for 14 days. According to the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia, the recommended dose of Pippali (Piper longum Linn.) choorna ranges from 1 to 3 g. In this case, based on the patient's condition and disease pathology, administration was initiated at a dose of 3 g per day, divided into two doses. The dosage was then gradually increased by 1.5 g daily until it reached 12 g, after which it was tapered back to 3 g. This dosage regimen was well tolerated by the patient. Follow up and outcome

At the time of discharge, the patient was instructed to follow a prescribed oral medication regimen in the outpatient setting and scheduled for weekly follow-up visits. The patient's progress was assessed at three points: prior to the initiation of Ayurvedic treatment, at discharge, and after six months of treatment. After three months of Shaman chikitsa, the patient experienced complete resolution of symptoms, including abdominal pain, abdominal distension, pedal edeama, and nausea. Allopathic medications were gradually discontinued during the course of treatment. There was no adverse drug's reaction during treatment.

The most recent follow-up was conducted on February 26, 2025, via telephonic communication due to logistical constraints. During this follow-up, clinical symptoms were evaluated, and chronic liver disease questionnaires were utilized to assess the patient's quality of life. The patient did not report any symptoms, including abdominal pain, nausea, abdominal heaviness, distension, bilateral lower limb swelling, or generalized weakness. The patient had a good appetite and satisfactory bowel habits. Details documented in Table 4.

Discussion

Budd Chiari Syndrome is characterized by the obstruction of the hepatic veins, leading to clinical manifestations such as ascites, abdominal pain, and hepatomegaly. The primary pathology occurs in the Yakrita, which is the Moola of the Raktavaha srotasa (circulatory channels responsible for the blood). The Sira, which are the Upadhatu (secondary tissues) of Raktadhatu (blood tissue), get obstructed, leading to fluid accumulation in the abdomen, clinically termed as Jalodara [9].

The treatment approach for BCS is personalized according to the patient's clinical presentation, the dominance of Doshas, and the severity of the disease. In conditions like Jalodara, the primary pathology is often associated with Mandagni, which leads to Dosha sanchaya (accumulation of Doshas) in the abdominal region. Consequently, treatment should be focus on Dipana, Pachana, and Anulomana, followed by Rasayana and Raktashodhaka (blood-purifying medicine) [5].

Discussion on treatment

Agnidipana (stimulation of digestion): Chitrakadi vati possesses Katurasa (pungent taste), Usnavirya (hot potency), and the qualities of Laghu (lightness) and Ruksha (Dryness), which support Agnidipana and Aam pachana (detoxification by eliminating undigested toxins) [10].

Nitya virechana: In Udar roga, excessive accumulation of vitiated Doshas leads to Srotorodha (obstruction of bodily channels). While Virechana is generally indicated in such conditions, it is contraindicated in Durbala (debilitated) or Krisha (emaciated) patients. In the present cases, due to excessive Dosha accumulation and patient weakness, Koshtha shodhana was performed without prior Snehapana. Following Mridu Shodhana, daily administration of Eranda bhrishta haritaki was initiated, as the main Chikitsa Sutra of Jalodara is Nitya virechana. In Shamana chikitsa, Eranda bhrishta haritaki was administered daily for its laxative effect [11], which facilitates the elimination of excess Doshas from the abdomen, thereby helping in the normalization of Vatadosha. It also decreases abdominal girth and edeama by decreasing fluid in the abdominal cavity [12].

Vardhamana pippali prayoga

Pippali vardhamana rasayana is indicated for conditions such as Pandu (anaemia), Shopha (oedema), and Shosha (emaciation) [13], which are present in this patient. Pippali, with its Katu rasa, Anushana virya (not hot or cold potency), and Madhuar vipaka (sweet post-digestive effect), possesses Dipana, Rasayana (rejuvenating), and Yogavahi (enhancing the effect of other substances) properties [14]. It helps improve digestion, reduces Ama (toxins), and boosts the immune system, thereby addressing the underlying causes of anaemia, oedema, and emaciation. Pippali also acts as an immunomodulator, supporting the body in preventing the development of autoimmune disorders [15].

Purnavashtaka kwath is indicated in Udararoga and it also help in reducing Shotha by removing excessive water content from the abdominal cavity [16]. Manjisthadi kwatha is an indicated in conditions like Vatarakta (gout). It is composed of Manjistha (Rubia cordifolia Linn), Triphala (Terminalia chebula Retz., Terminalia bellerica Roxb., Emblica officinalis Linn), Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia Willd), Nimba (Azadirachta indica Linn.), and Vacha (Acorus calamus Linn.) [17]. These herbs, characterized by their Tikta (bitter) and Katu (pungent) rasa and Usnavirya (hot potency) that aids in clearing Srotorodha in the Sira (vein). By acting on the Yakritasira (vein in liver), Manjisthadi kwatha helps to alleviate Srotorodha (blockages) and promotes improved circulation and detoxification in the body [18] Katuki, a major component of Aarogyavardhanirasa, which has Bhedana action, that help in acted as hepatoprotective by preventing hepatocytes from injury and regenerating damaged cells [19]. The main ingredients of Kaishor guggalu are Guggulu (Commiphora mukul Hook.), Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia Willd), Triphala (Terminalia chebula Retz., Terminalia bellerica Roxb, Emblica officinalis Linn), Danti (Baliospermum montanum Willd) and Vidanga (Embelia ribes Burn.F.) in which Guggulu is one of the main ingredient that possesses Anabhishyandhi, Snigdha, and Srotoshuhdhikaraka actions. Kaishora guggulu is stated to have a positive action on Srotovishodhana (cleansing of the channels) and improvement in circulation [20].

The primary element in Shivagutika is Shilajita, which has qualities such as Katurasa (pungent taste), Katuvipaka (pungent post-digestive effect), and Ushnavirya (hot potency), which function as Chedana (cleansing or eliminative), Yogavahi (enhancer of the potency of other drugs), and Rasayana (rejuvenating) [21]. Here, Rasayana karma of Shilajita serves as an immunomodulator [22], while Yogavahi action enhances the effectiveness of other medications, and Chedana action contributes to Srotosodhana.

Shweta parpati possesses Mootrala (diuretic), Swedana (sudorific), Agnidipaka (digestive stimulant), and Vatanulomana (carminative) properties. Due to these pharmacological actions, it is indicated in conditions such as Mootraghata (urinary obstruction), Ashmari (urinary calculi), Amlapitta (hyperacidity), and Ajirna (indigestion) [23]. In this case, the patient presented with ascites. Shweta parpati, owing to its Mootrala action, may aid in the removal of excess fluid from the abdomen, thereby contributing to symptomatic relief.

During the course of treatment, the patient was placed on a diet consisting only of milk and Mudga yusha. Milk is recommended in Udararoga (abdominal disorders) due to its Mriduvirechaka (mild laxative) properties, along with its qualities of Balya (strengthening) and Jivaniya (revitalizing) [24]. As a balanced diet, milk helps prevent weakness by providing essential nutrients, particularly proteins, which aid in reducing oedema by increasing plasma protein levels and thus improving colloid osmotic pressure [25]. The patient was advised to limit water intake, avoid daytime sleep and excessive mental strain, and maintain a regular night time sleep schedule to support the healing process [26].

This case report provides preliminary evidence on the potential efficacy of Ayurvedic treatments in managing and enhancing the quality of life for patients with Budd-Chiari syndrome. Nevertheless, to validate its scientific benefits, additional research involving a larger sample size and longer duration is necessary.

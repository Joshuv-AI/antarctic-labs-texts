Trends and determinants of isoniazid preventive therapy initiation in antiretroviral therapy-treated HIV-positive adults in Tanzania Mainland 2015–2020: a retrospective observational study using medical records
Authors: Elvis Bazil Meela, Gilbert Waria, Laura J Shirima, Henry Lucas Mlay, Michael Ngowi, Abdallah Abtwalibe Maghembe, Joseph Meela, Diana Somi Pallangyo, Josephine Balati, Werner Maokola, Ephrasia Hugho, James Samwel Ngocho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo examine trends and factors associated with isoniazid preventive therapy (IPT) initiation among people living with HIV (PLHIV) aged ≥15 years who initiated antiretroviral therapy (ART) in mainland Tanzania between 2015 and 2020.DesignA retrospective observational study using routinely collected data. Multilevel logistic regression analysis was used to identify factors associated with IPT initiation.SettingHIV care and treatment clinics across mainland Tanzania.ParticipantsThe study included PLHIV aged ≥15 years who initiated ART between 2015 and 2020.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcome was IPT initiation among eligible PLHIV. Secondary outcomes included trends in IPT initiation from 2015 to 2020 and factors associated with IPT initiation.ResultsAmong 124 846 PLHIV (mean age 35.8±11.40 years), cumulative IPT initiation was 59.8% (10.50% at first visit; 21.70% within 3 months). Initiation trend increased from 52.70% in 2015 to 68.30% in 2020 (2.05, p<0.02). Factors positively associated with IPT initiation included female sex (adjusted OR (aOR) 1.10, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.20), (aOR, CI) working functional status compare to bedridden (aOR 1.90, 95% CI 1.50 to 2.50), care at a health centre versus dispensary (aOR 1.60, 95% CI 1.30 to 2.00), residence in the Lake zone versus Central zone (aOR 2.50, 95% CI 1.60 to 3.80), ART initiation in 2020 compare to 2015 (aOR 2.00, 95% CI 1.90 to 2.20), WHO HIV stage II versus stage IV (aOR 2.10, 95% CI 1.90 to 2.30) and age 45–54 years compare to 15–24 years (aOR 1.90, 95% CI 1.80 to 2.10).ConclusionAlthough IPT initiation among PLHIV in mainland Tanzania improved between 2015 and 2020, coverage remained suboptimal. Strengthened efforts are needed to ensure all PLHIV initiating ART are appropriately screened for tuberculosis (TB), initiated on IPT if eligible and promptly treated if diagnosed with active TB.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) remains the leading opportunistic infection and a major cause of mortality among people living with HIV (PLHIV). Globally, an estimated 10 million people developed TB in 2019, with 8.20% coinfected with HIV. According to the WHO, the African region reported 456 426 HIV/TB coinfected cases and approximately 208 000 TB-related deaths among PLHIV in 2019.1 High coinfection rates have also been reported in several African countries, including Tanzania, where HIV/TB coinfection accounted for 24% of all TB cases reported in 2019.2

Isoniazid preventive therapy (IPT) is a key intervention for preventing active TB among PLHIV. IPT involves the administration of isoniazid at a dose of 5 mg/kg (maximum 300 mg daily) for at least 6 months to individuals without active TB disease.

Evidence has demonstrated that IPT significantly reduces the risk of developing active TB and improves survival, particularly when combined with antiretroviral therapy (ART).314

IPT is initiated only after active TB has been carefully ruled out to avoid inadequate treatment and the development of drug resistance. The WHO End TB Strategy recommends that more than 90% of PLHIV who are on ART receive TB preventive therapy as part of global efforts to end the TB epidemic by 2030.

Despite its proven effectiveness, global IPT coverage remains suboptimal; only 25% of PLHIV on ART received IPT in 2019.1 Coverage varies widely across countries, with low coverage in sub-Saharan countries, including Tanzania.15

In Tanzania, IPT was introduced into HIV care and treatment services in 2011, and national guidelines recommend routine TB screening at every clinic visit, with IPT initiation for those who screen negative for TB. Although IPT has been proven effective in preventing TB among PLHIV, its provision and coverage remain inconsistent. There is limited evidence on long-term trends and factors associated with IPT initiation among PLHIV in Tanzania. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the trends and factors associated with IPT initiation among PLHIV aged 15 years and above who were initiated on ART

Objectives

The study’s broad objective was to determine trend and factors associated with IPT initiation among PLHIV aged ≥15 years initiated on ART in Tanzania mainland from 2015 to 2020. With specific objectives aimed to determine the proportion of IPT initiation among PLHIV aged ≥15 years initiated on ART in Tanzania mainland from 2015 to 2020, to determine the trend of IPT initiation among PLHIV aged ≥15 years initiated on ART in Tanzania mainland from 2015 to 2020 and to examine factors associated with IPT initiation among PLHIV aged ≥15 years initiated on ART in Tanzania mainland from 2015 to 2020

Methods

Study design and setting

This was a retrospective observational study involving a review of medical records of PLHIV aged ≥15 years who initiated ART in mainland Tanzania between 2015 and 2020. The medical records were obtained from the care and treatment clinic (CTC2) database, a national electronic system that stores routinely collected data on PLHIV receiving care and treatment services at care and treatment centres. The database includes demographic, clinical, laboratory and pharmacological information collected at each clinic visit. The CTC2 database is owned and regulated by the Government of Tanzania through the Ministry of Health.

Study population

This study used routinely collected data on PLHIV who were 15 years of age or older and initiated on ART in mainland Tanzania. We excluded those with active TB disease on ART initiation.

Measures

Outcome

IPT initiation, termed as yes when the eligible clients documented to receive IPT/isoniazid in the CTC2 (care and treatment card 2) records, while those eligible and not initiated are termed as IPT not initiated (No).

Explanatory measures

In addition to social demographic information, we also collected clinical information, which was baseline CD4 cell count in cells/mm3, baseline WHO HIV clinical stage (stage I, II, III and IV), baseline functional status (working, ambulatory and bedridden), baseline body mass index (BMI) category kg/m2 (underweight, normal, overweight and obesity) and baseline viral load cells/mL (<100 and ≥1000). Other information collected was the year of ART start (2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019 and 2020), health facility ownership (private and public), health facility level (dispensary, health centre, hospital and maternity home and clinics), Zones (Western, Southern highland, Northern, Lake, Coastal and Central), pregnancy status (yes and no) and ART adherence (good and poor).

This information was collected using three tools which are the CTC1 card (care and treatment card number one), the CTC2 card and intensified TB case finding forms (ICFs). The CTC 1 card is kept with the client. It contains useful social demographic information about the client, ART regimen and monthly appointment schedules. In contrast, the CTC2 card is kept at the health facility and contains social demographic information, clinical, laboratory and pharmacological information of the clients. They are both filled out on every client visit to CTC, while ICFs is a special tool that contains five questions used to screen for active TB disease among PLHIV on each visit to the care and treatment services. PLHIV are screened by healthcare providers at the facility using five questions: the history of cough for two or more than 2 weeks, fever for two or more than 2 weeks, night sweats, loss of body weight and history of blood in sputum. If they answer yes to one of the questions, they are referred to laboratory and radiological investigations to rule out active TB disease. PLHIV who screened negative for active TB disease is eligible to start IPT for 6 months. Contraindications for IPT initiation are alcohol abuse, non-adherence to long-term treatment, current or past history of hepatitis and medical contraindications to isoniazid. This information is updated in the electronic national CTC2.

Data processing and analysis

Statistical data analysis was carried out by using Stata V.15 software (StataCorp LP, College Station, USA). The categorical variables were summarised and presented in frequency and percentages, while the continuous variables were summarised using mean (SD) and median (IQR), respectively. The χ² test was used to compare the differences in proportions of IPT initiation by participant characteristics. In determining the trend, proportions were calculated by using PLHIV who started ART and screened negative for TB and initiated IPT on each year as numerator while the denominator was those started ART and screened negative for TB on each year. Cuzick’s test for trend was performed.

Multilevel logistic regression was performed to determine factors associated with IPT initiation, taking into account the cluster effect (health facility level). In bivariate analysis, each independent variable was fitted against the response variable at a time, with a significant association at a p value of ≤0.05. Variables with a significant association, clinical importance and those reported in previous literature as factors associated with IPT initiation were considered in multivariable analysis. In a multivariable analysis, adjusted OR (aOR) and Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) were reported with a significant association at p value ≤0.05 and its corresponding 95% CI. Akaike information criterion was used to select the final model.

Sensitivity analysis was performed to determine the factors associated with IPT initiation within the first 3 months of ART initiation. Also, another subgroup analysis was done to determine the effect of sex on IPT initiation after excluding pregnant women.

Patient and public involvement

This study used secondary data and did not involve patients or the public in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of the research

Results

A total of 124 846 adults PLHIV were involved in the final analysis. We excluded 74 371 (5.10%) clients who were aged less than 15 years, those with active TB disease at the first visit, 702 (0.05%) who were not involved in the final analysis. PLHIV who were not screened and with no screen status were also not involved in the analysis, 1 239 174 (89%); not only that but also those screened positive for TB were excluded from the final analysis, 21 240 (6.60%) (figure 1).

Social demographic and clinical characteristics of the study participants

The participants had a mean (SD) age of 35.80 (± 11.40) years; the majority were females, 84 680 (67.80%), and more than half were married, 66 112 (61.10%). Nearly half, 56 739 (46.80%) participants initiated on ART were from the Southern highland regions.

Of all PLHIV initiated on ART, 22 357 (17.90%) started ART in 2019. In more than three-quarters, 77 992 (98%) of the study participants had working functional status, and half were in WHO HIV clinical stage one, 64 527 (52.80%). Of all the study participants, 96 266 (79.40%) were receiving care and treatment services from public health facilities, and 41 872 (34.60%) were receiving services from health centres. Nearly half of the study participants, 7812 (45%), had a CD4 cell count of >350 cell/mm3, those with a viral load above ≥1000 copies/mL were 681 (61.90) and the majority had a normal BMI of 19 812 (59.90) (online supplemental table 1).

Of all 124 864 study participants involved in the analysis, most of the participants initiated IPT, 74 772 (59.90%); among those, 13 104 (10.50%) were initiated on the day of ART start, while 27 086 (21.70%) initiated within the first 3 months of starting ART (figure 2). There was an increase in trend of IPT initiation from 52.70% to 68.30 in the year 2020 (2.05, p<0.02) (figure 3). In the year 2015 was 52.70% (n=9314, n=17 670), 56.50% (n=11 966, n=21 170) in 2016, 54.60% (n=11 702, n=21 431) in 2017, 59.60% (n=12 860, n=21 560) in 2018, then 66.40% (n=14 852, n=22 361) in 2019 and to 68.30 (n=13 893, n=20 350) in 2020.

Determinants of IPT initiation

In adjusted analysis, after accounting for clustering effect, age, sex, WHO HIV clinical stage, functional status, zones, year of ART start and health facility level were significantly associated with higher odds of IPT initiation. Compared with PLHIV aged 15–24 years, those aged 25–34 years (aOR 1.40, 95% CI 1.20 to 1.40), 35–44 years (aOR 1.70, 95% CI 1.60 to 1.80), 45–54 years (aOR 1.90, 95% CI 1.80 to 2.10), 55–64 years (aOR 1.80, 95% CI 1.60 to 1.90) and ≥65 years (aOR 1.20, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.40) had higher odds of IPT initiation. Female gender had significantly higher odds of IPT initiation compared with males (aOR 1.10, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.20). Those receiving care at a health centre had higher odds of IPT initiation compared with those attending dispensaries (aOR 1.60, 95% CI 1.30 to 2.00). WHO HIV clinical stages I, II and III were significantly associated with higher odds of IPT initiation compared with stage IV (aOR 2.00, 95% CI 1.90 to 2.20; aOR 2.10, 95% CI 1.90 to 2.30; and aOR 1.40, 95% CI 1.30 to 1.60, respectively) and ICC of 22.70% (online supplemental table 2).

Sensitivity analysis and subgroup analysis

In adjusted analysis, after accounting for the cluster effect of health facility level, married PLHIV had higher odds of IPT initiation within 3 months compared with those who were single (aOR 1.10, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.50). PLHIV aged 55–64 years had significantly higher odds of IPT initiation compared with those aged 15–24 years (aOR 1.40, 95% CI 1.20 to 1.60). Higher odds of IPT initiation were also significantly associated with WHO HIV stage II compared with WHO clinical stage IV (aOR 2.00, 95% CI 1.60 to 2.30). PLHIV from the Lake zone had significantly higher odds of IPT initiation compared with those from the Central zone (aOR 2.70, 95% CI 1.40 to 5.20) (figure 4).

Discussion

This study aimed to determine trends and factors associated with IPT initiation among PLHIV aged ≥15 years initiated on ART in Tanzania mainland from 2015 to 2020. The results of this study showed that the cumulative proportion of IPT initiation was 59.80%, and the trend of cumulative IPT initiation increased from 52.70% in 2015 to 68.30% in 2020. Cumulative IPT initiation was significantly associated with age, female gender, HIV WHO clinical stage, working functional status, health centre, the ART year 2020 and Lake zone.

There was an increase in the proportion of IPT initiation in our study to 59.80%; this shows that more effort is needed to reach ≥90% initiation recommended by End TB Strategy. These results were consistent with 52% reported in Bulawayo, Zimbabwe and 68.90% reported in Kenya.10 11 This is higher than the 29.80% reported in Nepal and 40% reported in Karnataka, India.12 13 In Tanzania (Dar es Salaam, Njombe and Iringa), the proportion was 14.40%; in Dar es Salaam, 41.10%; and in Ruvuma, 45%.8 16 17 The increase in proportion could be explained by the commitment and strategy made by WHO through The End TB strategy that includes TB prevention among PLWHIV through 3I (Intensified case finding, IPT, Infection prevention and control),15 which can also be due to the integration of HIV and TB services improvement in HIV services-testing, treatment and retention to care as well as integration of HIV/TB services, not only that but can also be due to decentralisation of HIV and TB services to the lower level health facilities in Tanzania in 2013.8

Our study documented an increasing trend of IPT initiation from 52.70% in 2015 to 68.30% in 2020, and this finding is consistent with reports from Zimbabwe and South Africa.1820 WHO End TB Strategy led to the increase in efforts of TB preventive services among PLHIV, improvement in HIV services-testing treatment and retention, integration of HIV/TB services and continuing capacity building to health professionals on IPT and its importance among PLHIV. Also, due to decentralisation of HIV and TB services in Tanzania results in to increase in the accessibility of IPT as the number of facilities providing the service is increasing with time.

This study found that females had higher odds of IPT initiation compared with males; this is consistent with other studies in India and Tanzania,12 16 and this could be due to the reason that women are more exposed to health services such as maternal and child health services, where they are provided with health education frequently compare to males16 21 also due to high health seeking behaviour.22 23

WHO HIV clinical stage I, II and III was significantly associated with high odds of IPT initiation compared with stage four, consistent with other studies9 16; this could be due to the high risk of other diseases to people with advanced HIV disease such as kidney and liver disease which are contraindicated to IPT

Our study documented a significant association between age and IPT initiation; similar results were documented in Tanzania.9 Older age has higher odds of being initiated IPT compared with younger age, 15–24 years. This could be explained by reasons that most of those younger age 15–24 years are at school, and treatment supporters bring medications to them and have little chance of being screened.

The health centre was documented to be significantly associated with IPT initiation in our study, and similar results were also reported in Tanzania9 16; this can be explained by the decentralisation of HIV and TB services to the lower-level health facilities in Tanzania in 2013 that was aligned with the capacity building for health workers, availability of screening and diagnostic tools for TB among PLHIV as well as the accessibility of IPT.

The western zone was documented with significantly higher odds of IPT initiation. These results were similar to other studies that documented different odds of initiation among regions and ART centres.12 16 20 This can be explained by IPT implementation variation between zones due to different implementing partners who differ from zone to zone and use different modalities in service delivery.

Starting ART in 2020 was significantly associated with higher odds of IPT initiation. A similar result showed higher odds of IPT for the year 2016 compared with other earlier years.9 19 WHO End TB Strategy could explain these results led to the increase in effort of TB preventive services among PLHIV; this could also be explained by the integration of HIV/TB and decentralisation of HIV/TB services, and continuing capacity building to health professionals on IPT and its importance among PLHIV. Also, due to the increase in accessibility of IPT the number of facilities providing the service is increasing with time.

This study documented a significant association between working function status and IPT initiation; this is explained by the reason PLHIV with working functional status have higher odds of attending health facilities during refilling where they can be screened and initiated IPT.

Conclusion

More than half of the PLHIV were initiated IPT, and the trend increased 52.70% in 2015 to 68.30% in 2020. The study documented age, female sex, WHO HIV stage, health centre, working function status, started ART in 2020 and Lake zone was significantly associated with higher odds of IPT initiation.

Edge-Enhancing Gradient Echo MRI at 7T for detection of focal cortical dysplasia in epilepsy
Authors: Erik H Middlebrooks, Elena Greco, Xiangzhi Zhou, Vivek Gupta, Brin E Freund, Amit K Agarwal, Erin M Westerhold, Anteneh M Feyissa, William O Tatum, Sanjeet Grewal, Vishal Patel, Shengzhen Tao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Abstract3D-Edge Enhancing Gradient Echo MRI (EDGE) is a promising method for identifying focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) because of its superior contrast-to-noise ratio. Gains in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and spatial resolution using 7T MRI should further enhance the utility of EDGE; however, the exquisite sensitivity to the optimal inversion time makes translation to 7T challenging given the extreme nonuniformities in the transmit field (B1+). By utilizing a combined MP2RAGE-EDGE sequence, the efficiency of the acquisition can be optimized to improve B1+ transmit uniformity and SNR. Using this approach, we report the first experience of using 7T EDGE MRI for the diagnosis of FCD in epilepsy, showing the feasibility of achieving high spatial resolution, SNR, and image contrast uniformity. EDGE-MP2RAGE sequence improves the detection of gray-white matter junction abnormalities associated with FCD and is a valuable, time-efficient addition to a 7T MR protocol in patients with focal epilepsy.Keywords: Focal cortical dysplasia, EDGE, MP2RAGE, 7T MRI, Epilepsy

Introduction

Epilepsy is a common neurological disorder that is unable to be satisfactorily controlled with medication alone in 20–40% of patients (Engel et al., 2012). Approximately 80% of patients undergoing surgical treatment with an electrographically concordant structural MRI abnormality (MRI-positive) achieve seizure freedom versus only 38–46% of MRI-negative (nonlesional) cases (Bien et al., 2009; Shi et al., 2017). Despite being the most commonly resected epileptogenic lesion in children and third most common lesion in adults, focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) remains a challenging imaging diagnosis (Blumcke et al., 2017). 3D-Edge Enhancing Gradient Echo MRI (EDGE) is a promising new method of identifying FCD, especially the subtle and more elusive Type I FCD, due to its superior contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) (Abula et al., 2022; Middlebrooks et al., 2020; Okromelidze et al., 2022; Urbach et al., 2022). Recently, the feasibility of the EDGE sequence has been shown using ultra-high field 7T MRI with a combined EDGE and MP2RAGE sequence (Tao et al., 2023a). In this series, we demonstrate the utility of EDGE in clinical 7T MRI for detection of FCD in epilepsy.

Case series

MRI exams of 6 patients were chosen to illustrate the spectrum of FCD detected on 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE. All subjects were scanned on a 7T Siemens Terra MRI (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) with a 32Rx/8Tx head coil (Nova Medical, Wilmington, MA) operating in parallel-transmit mode with TrueForm B1 shimming, utilizing an optimized EDGE-MP2RAGE sequence described by Tao et al., 2023a, 2023b

Patient 1

Patient 1 was a 20-year-old man with a history of epilepsy beginning at 7 years of age. He experienced focal aware seizures approximately three times daily and had failed medical management. He had previously undergone an extensive Phase 1 pre-surgical evaluation at an outside facility that failed to localize the seizures sufficiently to proceed with surgery and was referred to our epilepsy center for consideration of stereoelectroencephalography (SEEG). During admission for video-EEG monitoring, seizures were recorded and characterized by beta activity in the left centrofrontoparietal region. Interictal EEG showed polyspikes and sharp waves in the left frontoparietal and vertex regions. Magnetoencephalogram (MEG) source imaging (MSI) demonstrated equivalent current dipoles localized to the base of the left superior frontal sulcus. Despite MEG localization, no lesion was reported on outside 3T MRI or upon review of the images at our center. Repeat MRI (Fig. 1) was performed at 7T including EDGE-MP2RAGE, and the EDGE sequence revealed a base-of-sulcus dysplasia in the left superior frontal sulcus, in excellent correlation with the EEG and MSI localization. Given the presence of FCD, serum genetic testing was performed and found DEPDC5 c.1555C > T (p.GIn519*) heterozygous pathogenic variant. At our surgical epilepsy conference, the case was reviewed in detail. Based on concordance between 7T MRI, semiology, and electrophysiology, the group concluded that SEEG was unnecessary and decided to proceed directly to laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) to ablate the FCD. At 15 month follow up, the patient remained seizure-free.

(A) Coronal 7T EDGE image showing focal thickening of the GM-WM boundary line (arrow) at the base of the left superior frontal sulcus consistent with focal cortical dysplasia. Coronal 7T (B) FLAIR and (C) MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform images do not show the lesion (arrow) as clearly. (D) Coronal 3T MPRAGE failed to identify the lesion on multiple prior outside exams. (E) Outside MEG showing concordant source localization with equivalent current dipole (ECD) localized to the base of the superior frontal sulcus on the left. (F) Coronal post-contrast intraoperative T1-weighted MPRAGE showing the ablation zone after laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT).

Patient 2

Patient 2 was a 34-year-old woman with the history of drug-resistant focal epilepsy beginning at 11 years of age. She experienced monthly focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Inpatient video-EEG monitoring showed interictal spikes and spike and slow wave complexes at the vertex and right greater than left frontocentral region. Electroclinical seizures were captured arising from the right frontocentral region with the semiology of focal to bilateral tonic-clonic activity with left versive head turning prior to generalization. Additional subclinical seizures arose from the right frontocentral and vertex regions. MEG and FDG-PET/MR were nonlocalizing. A 3T epilepsy MRI had been previously performed at an outside hospital and was normal. Repeat MRI (Fig. 2) was performed at 7T, and the EDGE sequence revealed a gray-white matter junction abnormality, compatible with FCD, of the base of the posterior right inferior frontal sulcus, concordant with EEG. The patient was able to forego additional invasive monitoring, such as SEEG, with the high concordance of the lesion with EEG and confidence in the imaging findings. She underwent surgical resection. Due to location of the lesion, it had to be removed in piecemeal fashion limiting pathologic assessment for FCD type. Abnormal size variation in neurons was found suggestive of FCD. The patient was seizure free at 3 month follow up.

(A) Axial 7T EDGE image showing clear thickening and blurring of the GM-WM boundary line (arrow) of the base of the posterior right inferior frontal sulcus. (B) Axial 3T MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform image does not clearly show the lesion. (C) Axial FDG-PET/MR fails to reveal the lesion without corresponding hypometabolism.

Patient 3

Patient 3 was a 38-year-old woman with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures that began at age 24. She had failed multiple antiseizure medications and was experiencing up to three seizures per year. Her seizures began with left-sided motor symptoms and head and eye deviation to the left, which evolved into a bilateral tonic-clonic seizure. Inpatient video-EEG evaluation revealed interictal epileptiform discharges in the right frontopolar region. One focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure was captured with right frontal/prefrontal onset with semiology of a combination of elementary motor signs and nonintegrated gestural motor behavior. FDG-PET/MR showed a focal area of neocortical hypometabolism in the right frontal pole (Fig. 3), but no corresponding lesion was identified on the outside 3T MRI. Subsequent 7T MRI was performed at our institution (Fig. 3), and 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE showed a subtle band of WM signal abnormality in the corresponding region extending from the cortex to the frontal horn of the lateral ventricle, consistent with FCD. The patient was able to forego invasive monitoring and proceed directly to resection. Due to overlying vessels, the lesion had to be removed in piecemeal fashion, which limited pathologic assessment for FCD. The patient had no seizures in a limited duration of follow up.

(A) Sagittal 7T EDGE image shows subtle hypointense subcortical band (arrow) extending to the ependymal surface of the lateral ventricle. (B) Sagittal FDG-PET shows focal neocortical hypometabolism (arrow) in the same region. (C) Sagittal 3T FLAIR and (D) MPRAGE fail to show the lesion.

Patient 4

Patient 4 was a 27-year-old man with onset of seizures at 3 years of age. His semiology consisted of daily right-sided myoclonic seizures, in addition to ten focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures during his lifetime. He was diagnosed with focal aware motor seizures arising from the left hemisphere and had multiple MRIs throughout his childhood that had been reported as normal. Inpatient video-EEG captured left parasagittal frontoparietal low-amplitude spikes, sharp waves, and poly-spike wave complexes. An episode of myoclonus was recorded without EEG correlate likely representing EEG-negative focal motor seizures. No focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures occurred during video-EEG monitoring. FDG-PET/MR and 3T epilepsy MRI were normal. Given the suspected focal onset of seizures, 7T MRI was performed (Fig. 4). The 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE revealed a clear FCD at the base of the left precentral sulcus and the patient was recommended to undergo intracranial EEG monitoring. The patient subsequently sought another opinion from their local facility where they were told there was no lesion on the MRI and surgery was not recommended. A repeat 3T MRI was interpreted as normal despite the prior imaging and report of FCD that correlated with seizure semiology and EEG results.

(A) Sagittal 7T EDGE shows focal thickening of the GM-WM boundary line (arrow) at the base of the left precentral sulcus with subtle band of subcortical white matter hypointensity extending towards the lateral ventricle. (B) Sagittal 7T MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform shows more subtle blurring of the GM-WM junction (arrow). (C) Previous sagittal 3T MPRAGE for comparison shows a less defined lesion, which was not identified on interpretation.

Patient 5

Patient 5 was a 19-year-old man with onset of events at 5 years of age. The patient experienced habitual events beginning with an odd feeling of dissociation and tingling in his forehead and temples. He then felt his head, neck, and eyes jerk exclusively to the right with recovery in repetition as if to describe clonic beating. These events never exceeded 30 s in duration. There were no focal impaired awareness or convulsive seizures. He had been assessed numerous times at various epilepsy centers as a child, reportedly with unrevealing EEG and normal MRI at age 5 and age 10. Throughout his prior workup, there had been debate among his physicians to whether these episodes represented tic-like movements or pure focal aware seizures. Repeat EEG showed polyspikes and sharp waves arising maximally from the left frontocentral region. Two habitual events were captured occurring out of sleep characterized by vocalization and hypermotor activity with EEG seizure arising from the left frontocentral region. The 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE MRI (Fig. 5) revealed abnormal thickening of the gray matter-white matter junction, consistent with FCD, at the base of the left superior frontal sulcus posteriorly near the location of the frontal eye field. The patient had adequate control of events with optimized medication regimen and declined surgical intervention at the time of this report.

Discussion

EDGE MRI is an application of T1 inversion-recovery that preferentially nulls voxels containing similar volumes of heavily myelinated and minimally myelinated tissue (Abula et al., 2022; Middlebrooks et al., 2020, 2021; Tao et al., 2023a). By applying an inversion pulse to a magnetization-prepared fast gradient-echo sequence with an inversion time when gray matter and white matter longitudinal magnetizations have equivalent signal intensity but opposing polarity, a signal-cancellation occurs at their boundary producing a high-contrast gray matter-white matter junctional image (Middlebrooks et al., 2020). At 3T, the greater CNR of FCD versus other common sequences, such as FLAIR, Double Inversion Recovery (DIR), and MP2RAGE, leads to improved detection of FCD (Abula et al., 2022; Middlebrooks et al., 2020). In particular, EDGE is proposed to offer greater detection of type I FCD, which is known to be more difficult to detect on MRI compared to type II FCD. Since type I FCD lacks the balloon cells and giant dysmorphic neurons with radial extensions of abnormal neurons in underlying hypomyelinated white matter, (Blumcke et al., 2011) the imaging appearance is typically only subtle blurring of the gray matter-white matter boundary. Historically, the literature has shown that the majority of surgically confirmed type I FCD were undetectable on MRI (Wang et al., 2015). EDGE offers a novel approach to the detection of FCD compared to sequences such as FLAIR and DIR since it is not as reliant on subcortical T2/FLAIR signal changes that are more abundant in type II FCD.

Theoretically, 7T MRI should be advantageous to EDGE MRI. By the nature of the signal cancellation effect in EDGE, it is inherently lower in SNR. The lower SNR can also limit the spatial resolution of EDGE, which may add difficulty to detection of small, subtle FCD. The 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE sequence used in this study employs a 0.8 mm isotropic resolution compared to a typical 1.0 mm isotropic resolution used at 3T, while still maintaining sufficient image SNR. The smaller 3D voxel volume used here, which is roughly 50% of that on 3T, reduces partial volume effect as well as blurring of gray-white boundary in 3D imaging. Additionally, EDGE-MP2RAGE also has a unique advantage compared to other sequences based on 3D turbo-spin echo (TSE) readouts, such as 3D FLAIR. The long echo train typically used in 3D TSE sequences can introduce signal decay across the echo train, and therefore causes blurring effect between the gray-white boundary. This is largely reduced with gradient echo imaging techniques such as EDGE. Moreover, by precisely tuning the inversion time to be create similar signal level between gray and white matter, the contrast among tissues with a T1 value around that of gray and white matter is magnified with an EDGE sequence, which benefits detection of lesions of the gray/white boundary.

Despite these advantages of 7T, the exquisite sensitivity of EDGE to the optimal inversion time makes translation to 7T challenging given the extreme nonuniformities in the transmit field (B1+) (Tao et al., 2023a; Oliveira et al., 2021). The B1+ transmit uniformity, as well as SNR, can be improved with use of a longer repetition time (TR). Unfortunately, the extended TR results in prolonged system idle time where no data are acquired. To maximize efficiency, Tao et al. (2023a) recently described a combined MP2RAGE-EDGE sequence where a second data-acquisition with longer inversion time is added to simultaneously produce two different image contrasts that are critical to epilepsy imaging—the EDGE and MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform images. We report the first experience using 7T EDGE MRI for diagnosis of FCD in epilepsy, showing the feasibility of achieving high spatial resolution, SNR, and image contrast uniformity. Despite the relatively homogeneous EDGE contrast, patient-to-patient variation remains a challenge with inherent B1+ nonuniformity at 7T, particularly in low B1+ areas such as the basal temporal lobes. Future implementations of advanced B1+ shimming or parallel transmission may allow more uniform EDGE contrast, as well as synthetic image approaches (Middlebrooks et al., 2023).

Results from our selected cohort of patients undergoing 7T EDGE-MP2RAGE for epilepsy show the benefits of this sequence at 7T compared to lower field strengths. The increased SNR at 7T was able to generate higher resolution images (0.8 mm isotropic vs. 1.0 mm isotropic) while simultaneously nearly doubling the SNR compared with 3T EDGE. With careful optimization, a more homogenous image uniformity can be achieved to allow adequate imaging of areas in poorer B1+ inhomogeneity, such as the basal temporal lobe. In our patients, changes in management were made possible by detection of multiple presumed FCDs in patients with previously normal 3T epilepsy MRI scans. Given the known CNR advantages of EDGE, combined with the advantage of MP2RAGE over MPRAGE at 7T, we propose that our EDGE-MP2RAGE framework is a vital addition to all 7T epilepsy protocols and allows acquisition of both sequences without substantial increase in scan time.

Conclusion

Both EDGE and MP2RAGE MRI sequences have been shown to be useful in epilepsy MRI, offering improved CNR for gray matter-white matter differentiation and junctional contrast. Both sequences may benefit from added SNR at 7T, but also present unique challenges inherent in ultra-high-field MRI. We present the first descriptions of the use of 7T EDGE for diagnosis of FCD. The addition of the EDGE-MP2RAGE sequence improves the detection of gray matter-white matter junction abnormalities and is a time-efficient addition to a 7T MRI protocol in patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy.

A single oral administration of resveratrol cocrystals enables potent antisepsis therapy via interfering neutrophil extracellular traps formation
Authors: Juexian Wei, Muhammad Inam, Hengzong Mo, Jiayu Huang, Hongyu Zhao, Aijia Zhong, Xiaoyan Tang, Ehua Deng, Chuanshan Xu, Xiaohui Chen, Haifeng Mao, Wenjie Chen, Junrong Mo, Yongcheng Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Sepsis is a life-threatening syndrome characterized by organ dysfunction resulting from host response disorders and is a common complication in patients suffering from clinical trauma, burns, and infections. Resveratrol, a natural polyphenol compound, possesses anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antibacterial, and antifungal properties, as well as cardiovascular and anti-tumor protective effects. However, its therapeutic applications have been limited due to low bioavailability, poor aqueous solubility, and rapid metabolism. To address these limitations, this study focused on the preparation of resveratrol (RES) cocrystal with nicotinamide (NCA) and RES polymorphs through recrystallization for oral application. The identity of the new crystalline forms was confirmed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), Fourier transform-infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR), differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD). Physicochemical evaluations revealed that the RES polymorphs and RES-NCA cocrystal exhibited higher solubility and dissolution rates compared to pristine RES. In vitro experiments with LPS-stimulated HL-60 cells demonstrated that the RES-NCA cocrystal effectively inhibited neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) formation by targeting peptidylarginine deiminase 4 (PADI4) and reducing inflammatory markers. Additionally, in vivo studies using a cecal ligation and puncture (CLP)-induced sepsis model in mice showed that oral administration of the RES-NCA cocrystal significantly reduced NETs formation, organ injury, and mortality. These findings suggest that orally administered RES-NCA cocrystal holds promise as a therapeutic agent for sepsis treatment, offering enhanced solubility and bioavailability, along with potent effects in inhibiting NETs formation and reducing sepsis-induced organ injury.

Introduction

Sepsis is a disease associated with severe systemic inflammatory responses caused by infection, accompanied by organ dysfunction that leads to high morbidity and mortality (Rudd et al., 2020). Current treatments remain challenging because the complex pathophysiology of sepsis, rapid progression, and involvement of multiple systems, which results in the inefficacious control of inflammatory storm and multi-organ failure (Bakker et al., 2022; Cavaillon et al., 2020).

A critical factor in sepsis progression is the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), which can capture and kill pathogens; nevertheless, excessive NETs exacerbate inflammation. NETs can cause damage to microvessels, impair blood flow, and promote thrombosis, contributing to organ injury and failure. Thus, targeting NETs has been demonstrated to be a promising strategy for sepsis treatment (Chen et al., 2022).

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is globally recognized and employed for its therapeutic utility in treating diverse ailments. TCM has engendered a plethora of bioactive compounds, encompassing flavonoids, coumarins, alkaloids, glycosides, terpenoids, phenolic acids, and lignans, among others (Wu et al., 2021). These compounds manifest pharmacological attributes encompassing antioxidative, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antibacterial, antineoplastic, and antimalarial properties (Li et al., 2021). However, certain TCM-derived active pharmaceutical ingredients (T-APIs) in early developmental stages exhibit auspicious pharmacological activities marred by unsatisfactory physicochemical attributes such as limited stability, pronounced hygroscopicity, erratic bioavailability, and inadequate aqueous solubility (Li and Zhang, 2013; Li et al., 2025).

Given these challenges, natural plant extracts have shown promise in sepsis treatment by targeting various cellular mechanisms involved in the inflammatory response. For instance, quercetin acts on neutrophils and macrophages, scavenging free radicals, reducing oxidative stress, and affecting signaling pathways to decrease inflammation (Banik, 2019). Additionally, ginsenoside Rg1 inhibits the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages, thereby reducing the production of inflammatory cytokines and improving sepsis therapeutic outcomes (Dafni and Bock, 2019). Another example is epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), a component of green tea, which reduces sepsis-induced organ damage by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway in immune cells and decreasing the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Hofer et al., 2019).

Among these natural extracts, resveratrol stands out due to its potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, making it particularly promising for sepsis treatment. Resveratrol (RES), a notable member of T-APIs, emerges as a phytoalexin sourced from grapes and various other dietary sources. Renowned for its antioxidant attributes and potential in chemoprevention, RES exerts its effects by stimulating drug-metabolizing enzymes and displaying anti-inflammatory properties (Shen et al., 2023; He et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2023). Notably, RES serves to safeguard DNA from oxidative stress-induced damage, while also intervening in pathways involving c-Jun-N-terminal kinases (Xiao et al., 2023; Garcia-Sanchez et al., 2012), poly (ADP-ribose) polymerases (Ma et al., 2021; Said et al., 2016), and p53-p21 signaling (Baek et al., 2020; Gimenez-Bastida et al., 2019). In one study, resveratrol was found to reduce organ damage caused by sepsis by decreasing oxidative stress and modulating inflammatory responses, leading to enhanced immune function (Printz, 2017). Another study demonstrated that resveratrol improves sepsis outcomes by inhibiting NETs formation, thereby reducing inflammation and tissue damage (Yi et al., 2018). Paradoxically, despite its limited aqueous solubility, RES demonstrates notable membrane permeability and is classified as a class II compound within the Biopharmaceutical Classification System (Hastedt et al., 2024). Regrettably, the therapeutic application of RES is impeded by its suboptimal water solubility and restricted bioavailability (Rahman et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2024) which results in low systemic exposure and reduced efficacy. These pharmacokinetic limitations necessitate the development of novel formulations or delivery methods to enhance resveratrol's therapeutic potential in sepsis treatment.

Cocrystal synthesis stands as an innovative and propitious methodology for augmenting the attributes of APIs. This technique adeptly addresses the enhancement of APIs' solubility and dissolution rate, culminating in the formation of multi-component crystalline entities consisting of APIs and coformers in precise stoichiometric proportions (Vasoya et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2022). The resultant cocrystal induce alterations in the physicochemical characteristics of APIs, all the while upholding their inherent pharmacological traits. This dual capability renders cocrystal exceedingly esteemed within both the pharmaceutical industry and academic circles (Zou et al., 2024). Notably, the amalgamation of APIs with active coformers such as natural flavonoids through cocrystallization can not only ameliorate biosafety profiles but also enhance therapeutic efficacy, presenting a promising avenue for advanced therapeutic interventions. When contrasted with established technologies like film coating, osmotic pumps, and matrix release, the process of cocrystal synthesis emerges as a distinctive and efficacious approach for heightening API efficacy within the realm of pharmaceuticals.

Although numerous studies have confirmed the health benefits of resveratrol (RES), its pharmacokinetic characteristics pose limitations to its therapeutic use (Shaito et al., 2020). In mammals, RES undergoes extensive metabolism and rapid elimination, resulting in poor bioavailability (Walle et al., 2004). After oral administration, RES is absorbed in the intestine via passive diffusion or membrane transporters and subsequently released into the bloodstream, where it can be detected as both its unmodified and metabolized forms (Zimmermann-Franco et al., 2018). Specifically, in the intestine, RES undergoes presystemic metabolism through first-pass glucuronidation and sulfate conjugation of its phenolic groups, as well as hydrogenation of its aliphatic double bond (Mamadou et al., 2017). Despite the challenge of its rapid metabolism, oral administration remains the preferred in most practical routes. The plasma concentration of unchanged RES is known to depend on the dosage ingested. Numerous preclinical studies (Han et al., 2022; Singh et al., 2019; Venkat et al., 2023) have aimed to determine the appropriate oral dosage of RES and its bioavailability in humans. While oral formulations are favored for their accessibility, needle-free administration, non-invasiveness, and patient compliance, optimizing the pharmaceutical delivery of RES to achieve optimal bioavailability remains a challenge. In one of our previous studies, we reported that crystallization could serve as a viable strategy for developing orally administered drugs with enhanced efficacy and reduced side effects (Inam et al., 2023).

The crystal engineering of RES represents strategies for improving the solubility and biological efficacy of RES. The RES-NCA cocrystal, through its unique interaction with NCA, creates a stable crystalline structure that greatly enhances RES solubility and dissolution. In contrast, the RES polymorph system features to achieve optimized solubility profiles. These novel formulations not only improve RES's bioavailability but also enable more precise therapeutic applications. By enhancing solubility, they facilitate better absorption, allow for lower dosages, and potentially minimize side effects, offering a significant advancement in RES delivery and effectiveness in medical treatments.

Material and experimental

Material

Resveratrol (RES) and nicotinamide with 99 % purity were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. The purity of RES was confirmed by XRD (Supplementary Fig. S1). All the other required materials, solvents and chemicals were directly purchased from commercial sources and used upon receiving.

Cocrystal fabrication

The recrystallization technique was employed for the fabrication process. In a concise overview of the procedure, 3 mmol (685.32 mg) of RES was dissolved in 10 mL of pure ethanol (EtOH), followed by stirring and refluxing for 90 min at 75 °C. The resulting solution was subsequently cooled to room temperature, resulting in the formation of a finely crystalline RES-polymorphic form. Single crystals suitable for SCXRD were acquired using the solvent evaporation method. Similarly, an equimolar ratio of RES (1 mmol, 228.24 mg) and NCA (1 mmol, 122.12 mg) was combined with 5 mL of a solvent mixture comprising ethanol (EtOH) and water (H2O) in an 8:2 ratio. The solution was subjected to stirring and reflux for a duration of 90 min at 60 °C, followed by cooling to room temperature. As a result of this cooling process, finely developed cocrystals of the RES-NCA crystals were obtained and subsequently utilized for further comprehensive characterization analyses.

Solid-state characterizations

The PXRD pattern was acquired using a Rigaku D/Max-2550PC micro-diffractometer located in Tokyo, Japan. This analysis employed a rotating anode Cu-target X-ray with a wavelength (λ) of 1.5406 Å, operating at 40 kV and 250 mA. The scanning range spanned from 3.0 to 90°, with a scan rate of 5° per minute and a step size increment of 0.02°. Each data point was integrated over a time range of 0.5 to 3 s. FT-IR spectra were captured utilizing a Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS50 FT-IR spectrometer. The spectra were collected across the spectral range of 400–4000 cm^-1, employing the potassium bromide (KBr) diffuse reflectance mode. To prepare the samples, approximately 2 mg of the test substance was manually mixed with 100 mg of KBr, and the resulting mixture was pressed into thin pellets. Subsequent data analysis was carried out using dedicated spectrum software. For DSC analysis, a TA DSCQ100 differential scanning calorimeter was employed under a nitrogen atmosphere with a heating rate of 10 °C/min. The nitrogen flow rate was maintained at 50 cm3/min. The temperature range investigated was 35–220 °C. The calibration reference for the DSC temperature was established using a standard indium test performed on an aluminum container with known properties (softening point: 156.6 °C; enthalpy of fusion: 28.45 J/g). The sample underwent heating to 300 °C, followed by equilibration at 25 °C, and then a controlled heating ramp of 10 °C/min under a 50 cm3 nitrogen flow per minute. Morphological analysis of both the RES polymorphic forms and the cocrystal was conducted utilizing a SEM model Sigma 500 by ZEISS. Photomicrographs of the bulk samples were obtained without any coating under an accelerating voltage of 4 kV.

Single Crystal X-ray Diffraction (SCXRD) crystallography

SCXRD data were meticulously gathered employing a Bruker SMART APEX II diffractometer (Bruker, Ettlingen, Germany), utilizing Cu Kα radiation monochromated through a graphite crystal (λ = 1.54178 Å). After data acquisition, meticulous absorption corrections were applied using the SADABS software suite. Structural elucidation ensued through a process of direct methods, followed by comprehensive refinement using full-matrix least-squares fitting of F^2 values. This refinement process was executed using the SHELXTL-2016 software package. During refinement, non‑hydrogen atoms were treated anisotropically to ensure a rigorous description of their spatial arrangement. Hydrogen atoms associated with phenolic and alcoholic hydroxyl groups, water molecules, as well as cationic NH2 entities, were strategically positioned at calculated coordinates. The positioning was guided either by explicit HFIX instructions or through implementation of the Calc-OH program embedded within the WinGX suite. Notably, the creation of visual representations, in the form of figures, was facilitated through the utilization of software tools including ORTEP, Mercury, and Diamond.

Solubility and dissolution measurement

The assessment of solubility and dissolution characteristics encompassed pristine RES, polymorphs forms, and the cocrystal species. These investigations were conducted utilizing a Thermo Scientific Evolution 300 UV spectrometer within a deionized water medium maintained at pH 7 and a temperature of 37 °C. A surplus quantity of the sample was introduced into a 10 mL of H2O. The resultant oversaturated solution underwent agitation at a rate of 350 RPM, utilizing magnetic stirring, while being maintained at 37 °C for a duration of 24 h. Subsequently, the resulting suspension was subject to filtration through a 0.45 μm filter. The filtrate was suitably diluted prior to absorbance measurement at the relevant λmax. The evaluation of dissolution rate entailed introducing the sample into a 200 mL solution adjusted to pH 7.0. Stirring was sustained (150 rpm, 150 min, 37 °C). At specified intervals, absorbance measurements were conducted at λmax. The maintenance of a consistent volume was ensured through the periodic addition of 3 mL of the corresponding blank solution.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking of RES-NCA cocrystal, RES polymorphs with Neutrophil Elastase (NE) and Protein-arginine deiminase (PADI4) was performed to assess the binding between the cocrystal and the active site of the protein receptor, evaluating the ligand-protein binding efficiency. Protein structures of NE (ID 3q77) and PADI4 (ID 1wda) were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB), cleaned to remove water molecules, ligands, and heteroatoms. Ligand structures. The potentially optimized cocrystal structure was simulated and subjected to docking with the protein receptor utilizing Autodock 4.0 (Eberhardt et al., 2021). The binding free energy of the cocrystal within the target receptor was computed for the optimal pose. The Autodock software provided a visual representation of the most favourable docked pose (Agu et al., 2023). Selection of the top docked complexes was based on binding free energy values, and subsequent assessment of molecular interactions was carried out using the Discovery Studio visualizer.

Animals

All animal tests were approved by the GMU research ethics committee (No. GY2023–336). Male C57BL/6 mice, aged 4 weeks, were acquired from the Guangdong Laboratory Animal Center in Guangzhou, China. Cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) was used to induce sepsis as described in a previous study (Bourhy et al., 2022). Thirty minutes before CLP, the mice received subcutaneous administrations of buprenorphine (0.1 mg/kg; Buprecare®, Axience) for pain relief. The CLP procedure was performed under general anesthesia induced with 3.5 % isoflurane in an inhalation chamber and maintained with 1.5 % isoflurane and 98.5 % oxygen throughout the surgery. Following fur removal and sterilization, a median cut was made in the abdomen to expose the cecum. The contents of the cecum were gently displaced towards its distal end, and a portion of the cecal base was ligated. The cecum was perforated using a 21-G needle, allowing some fecal matter to extrude. The cecum was then repositioned into the abdominal cavity, and the skin was sutured in a layered fashion. In the sham group, the cecum was exposed but neither ligated nor perforated. All mice were revived via subcutaneous injection of 1 mL of normal saline. RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs were prepared in a solution of 1.5 % DMSO and physiological saline. Each mouse received an oral gavage of 5 mg/kg of either RES-NCA cocrystal or RES polymorphs. The sham group received an oral gavage with the same volume of the solvent solution. Twenty-four hours after surgery, the mice were deeply anesthetized, and blood was collected via orbital puncture (Fig. 4A). The mice were then euthanized, and their hearts were perfused with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) until the fluid from the right auricle was clear. Kidney tissues and ileal tissues near the ligation site were collected for subsequent analysis.

Cell culture

HL-60 cells (Pricella) were cultured in Iscove's Modified Dulbecco's Medium (IMDM) with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (P/S) at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 humidified incubator. To differentiate into neutrophil-like cells (dHL-60), HL-60 cells were switched to RPMI-1640 medium with 10 % FBS, 1 % P/S, and 1.2 % DMSO for 4 days. Post-differentiation, dHL-60 cells (1 × 10^6 cells/mL) were seeded in 6-well plates and divided into five groups: Control, LPS (1 μg/mL LPS for 8 h), LPS + RES (5 μM Resveratrol+1 μg/mL LPS for 8 h), LPS + RES polymorphs (5 μM Resveratrol polymorph +1 μg/mL LPS for 8 h), and LPS + RES-NCA cocrystal (5 μM Resveratrol-NCA cocrystal+1 μg/mL LPS for 8 h). To account for solvent effects, an equivalent volume of DMSO was added to the control and LPS groups.

Cell counting Kit8 (CCK-8 assay)

The cytotoxicity of RES, RES polymorphs, and RES-NCA cocrystal in dHL-60 cells was assessed using the CCK-8 assay, following the manufacturer's instructions (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Briefly, dHL-60 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 10^3 cells per well in 100 μL of cell suspension. The cells were then treated with varying concentrations of RES, RES polymorphs, and RES-NCA cocrystal (1, 5, 10, 20, 40, and 80 μM) for 24 or 48 h. After the incubation period, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, and the cells were incubated for an additional 4 h at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 environment. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Immunofluorescence of HL-60 cells

Differentiated dHL-60 cells were adhered to poly-l-lysine (PLL)-pretreated sterile glass slides. The cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 30 min, permeabilized with 0.1 % Triton-X, and blocked with 10 % bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min. Primary antibodies against histone H3 (citrulline R2 + R8 + R17) and neutrophil elastase (NE) (both from Abcam, Cambridge, UK) were applied, and the cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber. The following day, cells were labeled with FITC-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:400, Servicebio Tech) for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 5 min. Fluorescence images were captured using a Zeiss LSM 880 confocal microscope.

EILSA

The serum levels of IL-6 and TNF-α were quantified using ELISA. And, MPO-DNA and H3cit levels in plasma were measured using ELISA as well. Optical density (OD) values were read at 450 nm using a microplate reader within 5 min. Standard curves were generated for quantification.

Western blotting

Western blotting was conducted following a standard protocol (Lin et al., 2021). In brief, total protein was extracted from cells using radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer, which contained a mixture of protease inhibitors and a protein phosphatase inhibitor. The extracted proteins were then separated via SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies targeting β-actin, PADI4, NE, and MPO (Cell Signaling Technology Inc., Danvers, USA). The next day, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies. The expression levels of the target proteins were analyzed using ImageJ software.

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining

Tissue sections from mouse kidney and intestinal tissues were deparaffinized and rehydrated by immersing them in xylene I and II for 10 min each, followed by a series of ethanol solutions (100 %, 95 %, 85 %, and 75 %) for 5 min each. The sections were then rinsed in distilled water for 5 min. Next, the sections were stained with hematoxylin solution for 5–10 min and rinsed in running tap water for 5 min. Differentiation was achieved by immersing the sections in 1 % acid alcohol for a few seconds, followed by rinsing in running tap water for 5 min. The sections were then blued in 0.2 % ammonia water for 1 min and rinsed again in running tap water for 5 min. Subsequently, the sections were stained with eosin solution for 1–2 min and quickly rinsed in distilled water to remove excess stain. Dehydration was performed through a series of ethanol solutions (75 %, 85 %, 95 %, and 100 %) for 5 min each. The sections were cleared in xylene I and II for 5 min each. Finally, the sections were mounted with a suitable mounting medium and coverslipped. The stained sections were observed under a light microscope, and images were captured as needed.

TUNEL assay

Following the deparaffinization and rehydration steps described in the H&E staining procedure, sections were treated with 20 μg/mL Proteinase K (without DNase) at 20–37 °C for 15–30 min. The sections were then thoroughly washed with PBS or HBSS three times to remove any residual Proteinase K. After washing, sections were incubated in 3 % hydrogen peroxide solution in PBS at room temperature for 20 min to block endogenous peroxidase activity, followed by three washes in PBS. The TUNEL reaction mixture was prepared and applied, and sections were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h in the dark. After washing with PBS, sections were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody for 30 min, followed by DAB substrate until the desired stain developed. Finally, the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated through graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted. Images were captured using a light microscope.

Immunofluorescence

Followed by the deparaffinization and rehydration steps as described in the H&E staining procedure, antigen retrieval was performed in pH 9.0 EDTA solution using a microwave. The sections were then treated with 3 % hydrogen peroxide for 15 min to block endogenous peroxidase, followed by washing in PBS. After blocking with 5 % BSA-PBS for 30 min, primary antibodies were added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. The next day, sections were washed in PBS and incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 50 min at room temperature, protected from light, followed by three washes in PBS. Ready-to-use fluorescent dye solution was then added and incubated at room temperature for 10 min, followed by PBS washes. For antibody elution, the sections were placed in antigen retrieval solution and incubated in a 95 °C water bath for 25 min, followed by PBS washing. These steps were repeated for the second and third rounds of staining with different tyramide fluorescent dyes. Finally, the sections were washed in PBS, slightly dried, and mounted with anti-fade mounting medium containing DAPI. Images were captured using a multi-channel fluorescence scanner.

Statistical analysis

The quantitative data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (San Diego, CA, USA). To determine statistical differences between the groups, a one-way ANOVA was performed, followed by Tukey's post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Additionally, due to the small sample size and ordinal nature of the data, the Kruskal-Wallis H test was also applied to assess differences among the groups. For significant results from the Kruskal-Wallis test, Dunn's post hoc test with Bonferroni correction was used to identify specific group differences. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Hirschfeld surface and fingerprint analysis

The concept of the Hirschfeld surface emerged as a means to delineate the spatial occupation of a molecule within a crystalline lattice, enabling the partitioning of the electron density into discrete molecular fragments. This analytical approach, known as Hirschfeld surface analysis, holds significant value as a tool for discerning intermolecular interactions within molecular crystals. In the context of assessing the proximity-dependent interactions among RES molecules, Hirschfeld surface analyses were generated employing the Crystal Explore software. The resulting Hirschfeld surfaces unveil conspicuous regions of intense interaction, typified by vibrant red patches (Fig. 1C). These red areas correspond to robust OH…HO hydrogen bonding interactions, characterized by their short separations. Additional discernible regions on the surface signify favourable H…H contacts. Fingerprint plots derived from the Hirschfeld surface of the crystal structure serve as pivotal tools for quantifying the density of intermolecular contacts within the crystal lattice. The 2D fingerprint plot, incorporating the combination of (de and di) parameters, provides a succinct representation of the intermolecular contacts prevalent in the crystal. The fingerprint plots outlined in this study were generated utilizing the Crystal Explore software. Evident in the 2D fingerprint plot (Fig. 1D), distinct spikes manifest, corresponding to specific intermolecular interactions - H/H…H/H, O/H…H/O, and C/H…H/C, exhibiting densities of 45.7 %, 6.6 %, and 17.6 %, respectively. These spikes elucidate the prevalent interaction motifs within the crystal lattice. A further layer of insight is obtained through the dissection of fingerprint plots grounded in the principles of the Hirschfeld surface analysis. This dissection method allows for the isolation of individual interaction types, which might otherwise overlap in the comprehensive fingerprint. This dissection is showcased in Fig. 2E spotlighting the distinct contributions of H/H…H/H, O/H…H/O, and C/H…H/C interactions. To contextualize, the full fingerprint plot is depicted in gray, while the blue shading highlights the isolated interaction segments. This nuanced approach underscores the intricate and tailored nature of intermolecular contacts. Solid-state characterization

Fig. 2F-J provides an in-depth exploration of the solid-state attributes characterizing the newly fabricated crystalline forms of RES, encompassing both polymorphs and cocrystal entities. The synthesis of RES polymorphs (Fig. 1F) and RES-NCA cocrystal (Fig. 1G) was accomplished via the molar ratio-based recrystallization technique, with an array of characterization methodologies deployed for comprehensive analysis.

Powder X-Ray Diffraction (P-XRD) characterization was the initial endeavor to authenticate the newly crafted crystalline forms of RES. Renowned as a dependable analytical technique for crystallographic material evaluation, PXRD is sensitive to changes in crystallinity, which can indicate the emergence of novel crystalline phases. Notably, the RES polymorphs form exhibited discernible peaks at characteristic 2θ values (6.62°, 9.45°, 11.24°, 13.22°, 13.58°, 14.94°, 15.80°, 16.40°, and 19.16°), while the RES-NCA cocrystal exhibited corresponding characteristic peaks at 2θ (5.26°, 6.60°, 10.18°, 11.84°, 13.76°, 14.26°, 15.60°, 16.54°, 19.28°, and 22.52°). The shift of numerous 2θ peaks in the new crystalline forms when contrasted with pristine RES and NCA, corroborates the successful formation. Importantly, the simulated XRD pattern of RES polymorphs matches the experimental pattern (Fig. 1H), affirming the high purity of the RES polymorphs formulation.

The occurrence of hydrogen bonding interactions in crystalline structures induces shifts in functional group frequencies, a phenomenon clearly discernible through FT-IR analysis. The pristine RES displays a stretching bend at 3396 cm−1, corresponding to the phenolic (-OH) group. Notably, the RES polymorphs form showcases a frequency shift to 3321 cm−1, indicative of (OH…OH) interactions, congruent with single crystal X-ray analysis findings. In a similar vein, NCA exhibits two broad stretching peaks at 1700 cm−1 attributed to the amide group. Within the RES-NCA cocrystal, the phenolic (-OH) peak of RES shifts to 3310 cm−1, while the amide group peak of NCA shifts to 1752 cm−1, as depicted in Fig. 1I. These shifts underscore the robust hydrogen bonding between RES and NCA, solidifying the successful creation of the RES-NCA cocrystal.

The application of Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) analyses, a staple in crystal engineering, entails a comparative exploration of thermal properties between the newly fabricated crystalline substance and the starting materials. As evidenced in Fig. 1J, pristine RES exhibits an endothermic peak at 269 °C (Ionita et al., 2022; Silva et al., 2017), whereas NCA displays a peak at 128 °C. Notably, RES polymorphs display no significant shift in the endothermal peak, while the RES-NCA cocrystal presents a novel endothermal peak at 109 °C, accompanied by the disappearance of the RES and NCA endothermal peaks. These shifts in thermal behaviour substantiate the emergence of the novel cocrystal formulation.

Solubility and dissolution rate

The profound impact of the solid state of an API on its solubility is widely acknowledged. Consequently, the selection of an appropriate solid form is a pivotal determinant in the successful expansion of drug development initiatives. Aligned with the principles of the Biopharmaceutical Classification System (BCS), RES is classified as a class II drug due to its inherently low solubility. The introduction of a supersaturated solid form of the API presents a potential avenue for augmenting both bioavailability and permeability. The concentration of the samples was deduced through the application of a standard curve (Fig. S1), established by gauging the absorbance at the respective λmax of diverse concentrations. The comprehensive solubility profiles of pristine RES, RES polymorphs, and RES-NCA cocrystal in aqueous media at pH 7.0 are elucidated in Supplementary Fig. S2A. Notably, both RES polymorphs and RES-NCA cocrystal exhibit higher solubility compared to pristine RES, signifying a promising enhancement in dissolution characteristics. The dissolution rate of a drug, under controlled conditions encompassing specific surface area and stirring speed, captures the speed at which the drug is liberated from its solid matrix into the surrounding medium. Calculated dissolution rate values, portrayed in Fig. S1B, underscore a significant augmentation in the dissolution rate of both RES polymorphs and RES-NCA cocrystal, as contrasted with pristine RES. Intriguingly, the RES-NCA cocrystal showcases an even higher dissolution rate relative to both RES polymorphs and pristine RES. This enhanced solubility and dissolution rate observed in the polymorphs and cocrystal forms of RES, when compared to pristine RES, can be attributed to the presence of robust hydrogen bonding interactions. These interactions facilitate more competitive engagement with the surrounding solution media, expediting the dissociation process. Remarkably, the solubility of RES-NCA surpasses that of both RES polymorphs and pristine RES. This trend mirrors observations documented in the context of fluoxetine hydrochloride cocrystal (Al-Hamidi et al., 2010). This observation reinforces the premise that NCA, as a biocompatible coformer, exerts the capability to enhance the physicochemical attributes notably solubility and dissolution (Supplementary Fig. S2B) rate of RES through the avenue of cocrystallization. The cumulative insights reaffirm the promise of cocrystallization as a strategic tool for advancing the therapeutic and formulation aspects of RES.

Molecular docking study

Neutrophil elastase (NE) and peptidylarginine deiminase (PADI) proteins are pivotal in the pathophysiology of sepsis. NE, a protease secreted by neutrophils, aids in antimicrobial defense but, in excess, causes tissue damage and endothelial disruption, exacerbating inflammation and organ dysfunction (Papayannopoulos et al., 2010). PADI enzymes, particularly PADI4, catalyze citrullination, altering protein function and promoting NETs formation (Thiam et al., 2020). We conducted molecular docking studies of RES-NCAcocrystal and RES polymorphs with NE and PADI4 receptors to evaluate their binding affinity and inhibitory potential. These findings elucidate the therapeutic potential of RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs in modulating these critical proteins in sepsis. Fig. S3A illustrates the molecular docking of RES polymorphs (top) and RES-NCA cocrystal (bottom) with NE. Both molecules fit well into the active site of the NE protein receptor. RES polymorphs forms robust conventional hydrogen bonds with HIS_A:24 and VAL_A:190 residues, along with other short interactions. RES-NCA cocrystal interacts with HIS_A:41, ASP_A:88, and SER_A:188 residues through conventional hydrogen bonds and other short interactions. Fig. S3B shows the molecular docking of RES polymorphs (top) and RES-NCA cocrystal (bottom) with PADI4. RES polymorphs interacts with ARG_A:339, SER_A:371, and HIS_A:605 residues via conventional hydrogen bonds and additional short contacts. RES-NCA cocrystal forms conventional hydrogen bonds with ASP_A:315, ARG_A:604, TRP_A:312, and ARG_A:406 residues, along with other short interactions. The results indicate that RES-NCA cocrystal has a stronger binding affinity with the target proteins compared to RES polymorphs, suggesting its greater potential as an antiseptic agent. This was further confirmed by various cellular and animal model experiments.

Inhibition of NETs formation by resveratrol formulations in LPS-stimulated HL-60 Cells

Molecular docking studies demonstrated that the resveratrol-nicotinamide cocrystal (RES-NCA) exhibited strong binding affinity with PADI4, a key enzyme in the formation of NETs. PADI4 facilitates chromatin decondensation by converting arginine residues in histones to citrulline, leading to the release of NETs, which are structures composed of DNA and antimicrobial proteins (Perdomo et al., 2019). Neutrophil elastase (NE), a protease that degrades microbial components, and myeloperoxidase (MPO), which produces antimicrobial hypochlorous acid, are key markers of NETs formation (Islam and Takeyama, 2023). Given the important roles of these markers in NETs formation and antiinfection, the in vitro effect of resveratrol formulations was investigated using LPS-induced HL-60 cells. LPS-treated HL-60 cells were applied with RES, RES polymorphs, and resveratrol-nicotinamide cocrystal (RES-NCA). Through the CCK-8 assay, it was demonstrated that all concentrations of RES, RES polymporphs, and RES-NCA cocrystal had no cytotoxic effects on HL-60 cells and did not affect cell viability (Fig. S4). Western blot analysis was used to assess the expression levels of NETs markers PADI4, NE, and MPO. Results indicated that treatment with RES-NCA cocrystal reduced the expression levels of PADI4, NE, and MPO in LPS-stimulated HL-60 cells compared to the CLP group. This reduction was more pronounced in the RES-NCA cocrystal group compared to both RES and RES polymorphs groups, suggesting that the resveratrol-nicotinamide cocrystal is most effective in mitigating NETs formation (Fig. 2A-B). The strong binding affinity observed in the molecular docking studies likely underlies the enhanced efficacy of RES-NCA cocrystal in reducing NETs-related protein expression. Citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3) is another crucial marker of NETs formation (Ronchetti et al., 2022). CitH3 results from the citrullination of histone H3 by PADI4, facilitating chromatin decondensation necessary for NETs release. The presence of CitH3 indicates active NETs formation and serves as a biomarker for this process (Fan et al., 2017). As shown in Fig. 2C, LPS stimulates an increase in CitH3 in HL-60, while RES-NCA cocrystal significantly reduces LPS-induced histone H3 citrullination, suggesting that RES-NCA cocrystal may inhibit PADI4 activity and reduce CitH3 production. Furthermore, LPS induces a substantial increase in NE production in HL-60 (Fig. 2D), with NE being prominently released outside the cell body, confirming NETs release. Among the treatment groups, RES-NCA cocrystal shows the most significant reduction in both NE production and its extracellular release. These findings demonstrate the potential of RES-NCA cocrystal in targeting PADI4 and consequently inhibiting NETs formation, offering a promising therapeutic strategy for inflammatory conditions characterized by excessive NETs production.

RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs reduce NETs formation and inflammatory responses in sepsis-induced intestinal and renal injury

Following the evaluation of intestinal and renal damage, the effects of RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs on NET formation and inflammatory responses were investigated. Given the importance of NETs in inflammatory conditions, their formation was assessed in both intestinal and renal tissues using immunofluorescent staining. CD31 and VCAM-1 were used as markers for endothelial cells and vascular injury, respectively. Immunofluorescent staining showed that RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs treatments decreased the expression of MPO, a key enzyme in NETs, in both intestinal and renal tissues compared to the CLP group. The treatment groups exhibited reduced vascular damage and decreased MPO expression in damaged vessels, indicating reduced NET formation in these tissues (Fig. 6A-B). The relative expression of serum NET biomarkers MPO-DNA and H3cit was lower in the RES-NCA cocrystal-treated group compared to the CLP group (Fig. 6C-D). Additionally, RES-NCA cocrystal and RES polymorphs treatments reduced serum levels of the inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6, demonstrating a decrease in systemic inflammatory responses (Fig. 6E-F). This study represents a significant advancement over previous work by integrating pharmaceutical cocrystal engineering with mechanistic insights into sepsis pathology. Unlike earlier approaches that were limited by RES's poor pharmacokinetics, the development of a novel RES-NCA cocrystal provides, for the first time, a practical oral delivery strategy that ensures sufficient systemic exposure to achieve therapeutic efficacy. Moreover, by unveiling the direct suppression of the PADI4-NETs pathway, this work moves beyond the conventional attribution of RES's benefits to broad anti-inflammatory activity, instead offering a clearly defined molecular mechanism. Conclusions

The study highlights the potential of resveratrol (RES) cocrystal with nicotinamide (NCA) as a promising therapeutic approach for sepsis, a life-threatening condition often associated with trauma, burns, and infections. While resveratrol is known for its broad-spectrum biological activities, its clinical application has been hampered by poor bioavailability and rapid metabolism. By developing cocrystal and polymorphs of RES, the research successfully improved its solubility and dissolution rates. These advancements were substantiated by in vitro and in vivo studies applying only a single dosing of cocrystal pharmaceutics, where the RES-NCA cocrystal demonstrated significant efficacy in inhibiting neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) formation and reducing sepsis-induced organ damage and mortality. Looking ahead, the findings pave the way for further exploration of RES-NCA cocrystal in clinical settings, particularly for sepsis management. The study underscores the potential of leveraging crystal engineering to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of natural compounds like resveratrol, which could significantly impact the treatment of sepsis and other sepsis-related or inflammatory diseases.

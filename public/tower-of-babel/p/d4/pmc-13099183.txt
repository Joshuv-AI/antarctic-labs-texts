Evolution of MDRO burden in lung transplant recipients: a 10-year retrospective- prospective study at a regional reference center
Authors: A Lippi, M Fabbiani, M Sambo, D Bennett, G Giuliano, A Fossi, G Hankache, L Luzzi, C Catelli, D Marianello, F Franchi, M Tumbarello, F Montagnani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInfections by Multidrug-Resistant Organisms (MDROs) represent a critical hurdle in Lung Transplantation (LuTx). We aimed to estimate the incidence of respiratory MDRO colonization, identifying predictors and correlating colonization status with clinical infection.MethodsMonocentric retrospective study in Siena University Hospital (2015–2025). Pre- and post-transplant clinical and colonization data, including donor-related, were collected. Early post-LuTx respiratory samples were recorded. Primary outcomes were prevalence of lung MDRO colonization post-LuTx and subsequent infection. Secondary outcomes included hospital mortality.ResultsAmong 128 LuTx recipients (Table 1), 83 (64.8%) were males with a median age of 53.6 years (IQR 46–62). Indications for transplantation included 66 (51.6%) interstitial lung fibrosis patients and 19 (14.8) cystic fibrosis. 56 (43.8%) patients were hospitalized in the previous 2 years, 23 (18%) underwent LuTx during hospitalization and 16 (12.5%) were referred from other centers. Prior MDRO colonization in any site was documented in 16 (12.5%) patients, of which 10 (7.9%) non fermenting Gram negative bacteria DTR (NFGNB-DTR). Pre-operative support involved ECMO bridging in 14 (10.9%), intubation in 7 (5.3%), and tracheostomy in 6 (4.6%). Bilateral LuTx was performed in 96 (73.8%) cases. Prophylaxis regimens were standard (cefepime + vancomycin) in 111 (86.7%) and carbapenem-based in 10 (7.8%). Donor respiratory samples were positive in 45(34.6%) cases, yielding NFGNB-DTR in 17(13.1%). Over a median follow-up of 46 days [IQR 27–39], 34 (26.5%) patients acquired respiratory MDRO colonization post-LuTx, accounting for an incidence of 10.5 events per 100 person-weeks with a cumulative probability of 7.6% at 7 days rising to 45.3% at 30 days (Figure 1). Univariate and multivariate analysis (Table 2) identified carbapenem prophylaxis (HR 2.99, P= 0.027; aHR 3.69, P= 0.011) as significant predictor of respiratory colonization compared to standard prophylaxis. Tracheostomy beyond 15 days emerged as predictor on multivariate (aHR 2.53, P= 0.020). Any site infections occurred in 43 (33.1%) patients with a median onset of 15 days, of which 29 (67.4%) were caused by MDROs and 5 (3.9%) donor derived. Progression from colonization to infection was observed in 27(20.8%) patients. Overall, in-hospital mortality was 26(20%).ConclusionsOur study confirms MDRO colonization as a major post-LuTx concern. Enhanced infection control measures, including pre-transplant, donor, and in-hospital management, are needed to reduce graft-related infections and MDRO in-hospital spreading.Figure 1.Cumulative Incidence of Colonization Over Time (Kaplan-Meier survival curve).

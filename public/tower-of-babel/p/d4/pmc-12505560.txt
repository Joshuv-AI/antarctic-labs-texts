Genetic diversity and natural selection of circumsporozoite surface protein in Vietnam Plasmodium falciparum isolates
Authors: Tuấn Cường Võ, Hương Giang Lê, Jung-Mi Kang, Nguyen Thi Minh Trinh, Chau Van Khanh, Minkyoung Cho, Huynh Hong Quang, Byoung-Kuk Na
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlasmodium falciparum circumsporozoite surface protein (PfCSP) serves as a prime candidate for malaria vaccine; however, its substantial genetic diversity presents significant challenges in development of universal vaccines. This study investigated the genetic diversity and natural selection of the pfcsp in P. falciparum isolates from Vietnam. Comparative analysis of 836 global pfcsp sequences from different geographical regions was also performed.MethodsBlood samples were obtained from individuals infected with P. falciparum in five malaria-endemic provinces in Central Vietnam from 2018 to 2022. The pfcsp gene was amplified, cloned, and sequenced, yielding 95 pfcsp sequences. Nucleotide diversity and natural selection of pfcsp were analysed by programs, such as DnaSP, MEGA6, and STRUCTURE.ResultsBoth the N- and C-terminal non-repeating regions of Vietnam pfcsp showed limited genetic diversities; however, significant polymorphisms such as A98G and a 19-amino acid insertion were observed in the N-terminal region of Vietnam isolates, aligning with trends in global pfcsp populations. The C-terminal region of Vietnam and global pfcsp populations displayed notable polymorphisms, particularly within the Th2R and Th3R regions. The central repeat region demonstrated high polymorphism due to substantial size variations, predominantly influenced by the varying numbers and types of peptide repeat motifs found both in Vietnam and global populations.ConclusionThese findings offer critical insights into the genetic composition of the global pfcsp population and provide useful information in designing effective vaccines based on PfCSP.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12936-025-05585-2.Keywords: Plasmodium falciparum, Circumsporozoite surface protein, Vietnam, Genetic diversity, Natural selection

Background

Malaria is a life-threatening infectious disease caused by Plasmodium species, constituting a significant public health burden in numerous tropical and subtropical countries worldwide. There has been a remarkable decline in global malaria morbidity and mortality over recent decades; however, in 2023 alone, 263 million cases of malaria and approximately 600,000 deaths were still reported globally [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) aims to eliminate global malaria by 2030 [2], yet this goal faces significant challenges. The emergence of antimalarial drug-resistant parasites and insecticide-resistant mosquito vectors present major barriers to global efforts for malaria control and elimination [3–5]. Additionally, the lack of an effective malaria vaccine remains a critical obstacle in the fight against malaria, underscoring the urgent need for reliable vaccine development.

Circumsporozoite surface protein of Plasmodium falciparum (PfCSP) is a protein abundantly expressed on the surface of sporozoites. This protein is divided into three distinct regions: the N-terminal region (N-T; amino acids 1–104), the central repeat region (CRR; amino acids 105–272), and the C-terminal region (C-T; amino acids 273–397) based on 3D7 reference sequence [6]. The N-T and C-T regions are relatively well conserved across global P. falciparum populations, whereas the CRR exhibits high polymorphisms due to unstructured tandem tetrapeptide repeat motifs (PRMs). PfCSP is recognized as one of the most promising vaccine candidates owing to its biological significance in the process of sporozoites’ invasion of host hepatocytes and its protective effect in halting disease progression [7–9]. The RTS,S/AS01 (RTS,S) and R21/Matrix-M (R21), the most advanced malaria vaccines endorsed by WHO, were developed by incorporating a fragment of the CRR and the C-T of PfCSP linked to the hepatitis B surface antigen [10, 11]. Clinical trials for the RTS,S and R21 in sub-Saharan Africa and other regions with moderate to high malaria transmission intensities demonstrated potential protective efficacies [12–14]. However, the protective efficacies of these vaccines were not durable, failing to provide uniform immunity across the studied groups [15, 16], suggesting that the limitation of the vaccines could be attributed to genetic polymorphisms of the protein in wild P. falciparum isolates. Functional significances of N-T of PfCSP in hepatocyte binding and protective immunity have also been demonstrated, indicating its potential as a vaccine candidate [8, 17], but the study on the genetic nature of this region in the global pfcsp population has been neglected.

Malaria parasites undergo a complex life cycle in humans and mosquito vectors involving asexual and sexual reproductions. During this cycle, the parasites develop through diverse developmental stages and display significant genetic and antigenic diversity. The genetic polymorphisms of pfcsp identified in global P. falciparum isolates have raised concerns about their potential to reduce vaccine efficacy based on the single-allele type of PfCSP [18–20], emphasizing the necessity for ongoing molecular surveillance of the gene in the global P. falciparum population to design the optimum vaccine construct.

Vietnam is a malaria-endemic country, and the Central Highlands and Coastal Central regions are the malaria hotspots with 12,447 malaria cases during 2017–2023 (http://impe-qn.org.vn/homegdimpeqn). However, studies on the genetic makeup of the Vietnamese P. falciparum population remain insufficient. A study to analyse the genetic characteristics of pfcsp in Vietnam P. falciparum isolates collected in 1998–2003 has been conducted, but this study covered only the C-T of pfcsp [21]. In this study, the genetic polymorphisms and natural selection signatures of pfcsp in P. falciparum isolates collected in Vietnam during 2018–2022 were analysed. A comparative analysis of 836 global pfcsp was conducted to understand the genetic nature and evolutionary aspects of the gene in the global P. falciparum population.

Methods

Parasite samples and study area

A total of 94 blood samples collected from individuals infected with P. falciparum residing in three provinces of the Central Highlands—Dak Lak, Dak Nong, and Gia Lai—and two provinces of the South-Central region, Phu Yen and Khanh Hoa, Vietnam from 2018 to 2022 were used in this study (Additional file 1: Figure S1; Additional file 2: Table S1). The study areas are major malaria-endemic provinces in Vietnam, where both P. falciparum and Plasmodium vivax are coexisting [22]. Plasmodium falciparum mono-infection was diagnosed by microscopic examination of thin and thick blood smears. Dried blood spot (DBS) was obtained from each patient via the finger-prick method prior to administering antimalarial drugs. Plasmodium falciparum infection was further confirmed by a species-specific polymerase chain reaction (PCR) targeting the 18S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) gene as previously documented [23]. Samples of mixed infection with P. falciparum and P. vivax were excluded from this study. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Institute of Malariology, Parasitology and Entomology Quy Nhon, Vietnam, with Institutional Review Board (IRB) Approval numbers: 386/VSR-LSDT, 45/VSR-NCDT, and 637/VSR-NCDT. Verbal informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to blood collection.

Genomic DNA extraction and amplification of pfcsp

Genomic DNA was isolated from each DBS using the QIAamp DNA Blood Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocols. The DNA was eluted with 100 μl of elution buffer in the kit. The full-length pfcsp gene was amplified employing a nested PCR approach by using the same primers and thermal cycling conditions described previously [18]. All PCR amplifications were conducted using Ex Taq DNA polymerase (Takara, Otsu, Japan), which has proofreading capabilities to reduce unintended nucleotide misincorporation. The gene was successfully amplified in 94 samples; 93 exhibited only a single amplicon, while 1 revealed 2 different-sized amplicons, indicating a potential multiclonal infection. Each amplicon was analysed on a 1.5% agarose gel, purified, and cloned into the T&A vector (Real Biotech Corporation, Banqiao City, Taiwan). Each ligation mixture was transformed into Escherichia coli DH5α, and positive clones containing the cloned pfcsp gene were identified by colony PCR. The nucleotide sequences of the cloned pfcsp were determined using the automated Sanger DNA sequencing technique with bi-directional sequencing using M13 forward and reverse primers. Plasmids from at least two individual clones of each transformant were sequenced to confirm sequence accuracy. A total of 95 pfcsp sequences were obtained from 94 P. falciparum isolates in Vietnam, indicating an estimated multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 1.01. These nucleotide sequences have been deposited in the GenBank database under accession numbers PQ602317–PQ602411.

Genetic diversity analysis of Vietnam and global pfcsp

The nucleotide and amino acid sequences of Vietnam pfcsp were annotated using the Editseq and Seqman programs within the DNASTAR suite (DNASTAR, Madison, WI, USA). The reference sequence was the 3D7 strain pfcsp (PlasmoDB ID: XM_001351086.1). The number of segregating sites (S), haplotypes (H), haplotype diversity (Hd), nucleotide diversity (π), and the average number of pairwise nucleotide differences within a population (K) were calculated using DnaSP version 5.10.00 [24]. Rates of synonymous (dS) and non-synonymous (dN) substitutions were assessed using Nei and Gojobori’s method with the Jukes-Cantor (JC) correction through 1000 bootstrap replications in MEGA6 [25]. A Z-test was conducted to determine statistical significance, with a threshold of P < 0.05. Statistical measures including Tajima’s D, Fu and Li’s D, and Fu and Li’s F were estimated using DnaSP version 5.10.00 to detect signs of natural selection [24]. The values were considered significant when P < 0.05. The haplotype network for the pfcsp population was constructed using PopART 1.7 software (http://popart.otago.ac.nz) with the Median-Joining method. Bayesian analysis to ascertain the lineage of Vietnam pfcsp was performed using the STRUCTURE software [26]. The number of clusters (K), ranging from 1 to 20, was estimated with a burn-in duration of 10,000 steps and 20,000 Markov chain Monte Carlo iterations. The optimal K number was determined using the StructureSelector [26, 27]. Genetic diversity profiles in global pfcsp populations were analysed using sequences available from Vietnam (1998–2003), Thailand, Myanmar, the Philippines, India, Iran, Papua New Guinea (PNG), Solomon Islands (SI), Vanuatu, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (E. Guinea), Gambia, Ghana, Kenya, Senegal, Tanzania, Brazil, and Venezuela in the GenBank [18, 21, 28–34] (Additional file 3: Table S2).

Results

Polymorphic pattern of N-T in Vietnam and global pfcsp

Four dimorphic amino acid changes, S12P, K66E, N68S, and A98G, were identified in the N-T of Vietnam pfcsp compared to the 3D7 reference sequence (GenBank ID: XM_001351086.1) (Fig. 1a). The substitution A98G, commonly identified in 94 sequences (98.95%), was significant. The other three substitutions were minor, with a frequency of 2.1% (2/95). Notably, a 19-amino acid insertion, NNGDNGREGKDEDKRDGNN, introduced after amino acid position 80 was also detected in the N-T of Vietnam pfcsp with a high frequency of 98.95%. Based on these amino acid changes detected, the N-T of Vietnam pfcsp clustered into five different haplotypes (H1–H5). H1 was the most predominant haplotype shared by 88 sequences (92.6%), while H5, identical to the 3D7 reference sequence, was found in only one sequence. The KKLKQ motif, which is a tightly conserved enzyme-cutting site in pfcsp, was well-conserved across all sequences. Polymorphic patterns of the N-T of pfcsp from global populations were also analysed (Fig. 1b). The A98G substitution and 19-amino acid insertion were commonly detected in the global population, albeit varying frequencies by country. Minor country-specific amino acid changes were also observed in global pfcsp populations, but most were not significant with low frequencies except for two, D99G and G100D, in Iran pfcsp.

Genetic diversity of the CRR in Vietnam and global pfcsp

The CRR of Vietnam pfcsp was constructed with sequential repeats of 6 types of PRMs. Two major types of PRMs, NANP and NVDP, complemented by four minor PRMs—DANP, NASP, NTNP, and NATP—were also detected in Vietnam pfcsp (Fig. 2). The variable numbers and assemblies of these PRMs contributed to the genetic diversity of the CRR, yielding 21 distinct haplotypes (H1‒H21). PRM counts varied across haplotypes, ranging from 17 to 49. Haplotype H10 and H16 were the most prevalent, with 36 and 37 sequences, respectively. None of the CRR in Vietnam pfcsp was matched the 3D7 reference sequence. The CRR in global pfcsp populations exhibited substantial genetic diversity, attributed to 14 distinct types of PRMs: 13 tetrapeptide and one dipeptide (NP) PRMs (Fig. 3). Similar to Vietnam pfcsp, the characteristic PRMs, NANP and NVDP, were predominant across all global pfcsp populations. E. Guinea CRR included the most diverse set of PRMs, namely NANP, NVDP, NVNP, NPIP, NSNP, NADP, and NATP. Of these, the 5 minor PRMs—NVNP, NPIP, NSNP, NADP, and NATP—were exclusive in E. Guinea pfcsp. Three PRMs—DANP, NASP, and NATP—were uniquely identified in Vietnam pfcsp only, while NANS and NP were unique PRMs in Myanmar pfcsp and Venezuela pfcsp, respectively. NAKP was specific to PNG and SI pfcsp. The global pfcsp CRRs also displayed considerable size polymorphisms, with PRM counts, ranging from 16 to 51 (Fig. 4a). The median size of CRR was larger in pfcsp from Asian countries compared to those from Africa and South American countries (Fig. 4b). Larger size variations were observed in populations from Vietnam, Myanmar, and Kenya populations, whereas the Brazil population exhibited high homogeneity.

Genetic diversity of C-T in Vietnam and global pfcsp

The C-T of Vietnam pfcsp exhibited amino acid changes at 10 positions: S301N, A302D, K317E, E318Q, N321K, K322N, N325Y, N352D, E357Q, and A361E (Fig. 5a). Among these alterations, S301N, K317E, E318Q, N321K, and A361E displayed high frequencies of up to 97.9%. The remaining changes were minor, accounting for between 1.05 and 6.32%. Notably, these amino acid changes occurred on the two T-cell epitopes, Th2R and Th3R, within the C-T, except for S301N and A302D. Based on these amino acid polymorphisms in the C-T of Vietnam pfcsp, Vietnam sequences were categorized into 6 distinct haplotypes (H1–H6), with H1 being the most prevalent, representing 91.6% (87/95). Only 1 sequence (H6) was identical to the 3D7 reference sequence. The polymorphic patterns in the C-T of global pfcsp populations were also examined (Fig. 5b). A total of 64 amino acid polymorphisms were identified at 37 positions across different populations. Four amino acid changes—K317E, E318Q, N321K, and A361E— were consistently observed across all examined populations, although their frequencies varied by country. The majority of these changes were localized to the Th2R and Th3R. Interestingly, the S301N was found in all global pfcsp populations, although the conservation of this mutation was not clear in Vietnam (1998–2003), India, and Senegal, where the corresponding region sequences were missing. Vietnam pfcsp analysed in this study exhibited lower polymorphic sites than the pfcsp of Vietnam (1998–2003).

Genetic diversity and natural selection in the T-cell epitope regions of pfcsp C-T

The lengths of the C-T in global pfcsp populations were not identical because of missing sequences in certain populations. Consequently, the genetic diversity and natural selection were examined solely for the common region of the C-T, corresponding to amino acid positions 913–1104 that cover the Th2R and Th3R, in the pfcsp (Table 1). The patterns of genetic diversity and natural selection varied across the global pfcsp C-T. In the Vietnam pfcsp C-T, the values of K, Hd, and π were 0.426, 0.161 ± 0.051, and 0.0022 ± 0.0008, respectively (Table 1). The dN–dS value stood 0.003, indicating potential influence from weak positive selection. Tajima’s D value was −1.777 (0.05 < P < 0.1). Both Fu and Li’s D and F values were negative, estimated at –2.740 (P < 0.05) and −2.855 (P < 0.05), respectively, suggesting pronounced bottleneck selection or a selective sweep. Notably, the data derived from previously reported Vietnam pfcsp sequences (Vietnam 1998–2003) disclosed distinct profiles of nucleotide diversity and natural selection compared to the Vietnam pfcsp analysed in this study. Nucleotide diversity measures, such as K, Hd, and π, were higher in the older Vietnam sequences (1998–2003) than in recent Vietnam sequences. Likewise, the values of Tajima’s D, Fu and Li’s D, and Fu and Li’s F were positive for Vietnam (1998–2003). The overall values of Hd and π were higher in the pfcsp from African countries than in those from South America, Asia, and Oceania countries. Specifically, Hd and π values for the African pfcsp populations were 0.960 ± 0.004 and 0.0285 ± 0.0009, respectively, whereas the Oceania populations had values of 0.526 ± 0.012 and 0.0057 ± 0.0002, respectively. In the Asia populations, these values were 0.573 ± 0.027 and 0.0120 ± 0.0008, respectively. The South America populations exhibited Hd (0.630 ± 0.065) and π (0.0194 ± 0.0025) values, albeit lower than those in the African populations. The African pfcsp also displayed a higher dN–dS value (0.360) compared to the South American (0.024), Asian (0.015), and Oceanian (0.007) populations, suggesting more intense natural selection pressures might have acted on the African pfcsp population. The global pfcsp exhibited a distinctive pattern of natural selection. Values of Tajima’s D, Fu and Li’s D, and Fu and Li’s F varied significantly by country, pointing to diverse natural selection forces acting on each population. In summary, the Africa and Oceania pfcsp populations showed positive values of Tajima’s D, whereas the Asia and South America pfcsp populations had negative values. Sliding window plot analysis further corroborated that distinct natural selection forces acted on the global pfcsp C-T (Fig. 6). The positions exhibiting nucleotide diversity in the populations were remarkably consistent in the global pfcsp C-T. Two significant peaks of π were observed in Th2R and Th3R. However, the natural selection profile in global pfcsp C-T varied by continent, indicating diverse natural selection forces influencing global populations.

To further investigate the genetic characteristics of pfcsp C-T within the global population, a haplotype network analysis involving 1054 global sequences was conducted. This analysis produced a highly complex network comprising 100 distinct haplotypes, indicating significant haplotype diversity (Fig. 7). H5 emerged the most dominant haplotype, encompassing 443 (42.03%) sequences from countries in Asia and Oceania. Most Vietnam pfcsp analysed in this study were clustered into H5, but Vietnam (1998–2003) sequences revealed different distribution patterns, scattering into diverse haplotypes. Another prevalent haplotype was H2, predominantly composed of sequences from Oceania. H3, identical in sequence to the 3D7 strain, was identified predominantly in African countries and one Vietnamese sequence. Cluster analysis utilizing the STRUCTURE program demonstrated that the global pfcsp C-T segregated into six distinct origins, K1–K6 (ΔK = 14.46) (Fig. 8). A substantial majority (n = 87, 91.6%) of the current Vietnam isolates were traced back to K1 and K3, a distribution pattern echoed in other Asia countries, including Thailand, Myanmar, India, and Iran. Oceania also exhibited a significant number of isolates from K1 and K3. However, K6 contributed notably to the genetic makeup in these populations. Interestingly, the Philippine pfcsp demonstrated a unique population structure differing from other Asia pfcsp populations, with K4 and K2 being notably predominant. Conversely, the African pfcsp C-T population presented diverse population structure with K2 and K5 prevailing. A similar structure pattern was observed in Brazil pfcsp population.

Discussion

PfCSP represents one of the most thoroughly researched candidates for malaria vaccine. The WHO-endorsed two malaria vaccines, RTS,S and R21, were developed using a segment of this protein. However, neither vaccine provides complete protection against malaria [15, 16, 35], and concerns have escalated regarding the enormous genetic variability of pfcsp, which could diminish vaccine effectiveness [17, 30, 36]. This highlights the crucial need to understand the genetic and antigenic diversity of pfcsp in the global P. falciparum population. This study conducted comprehensive analyses of genetic polymorphism and natural selection in Vietnam and global pfcsp populations, providing significant insights into the genetic diversity and evolutionary dynamics of the gene in the global population.

Low genetic diversity of pfcsp N-T has been demonstrated previously [31, 32]. In alignment with earlier studies, the N-T of Vietnam pfcsp exhibited limited genetic diversity. The most significant genetic polymorphisms identified in the N-T of Vietnam pfcsp were A98G and a 19-amino acid (NNGDNGREGKDEDKRDGNN) insertion. These were also commonly observed in the global pfcsp population, albeit with varying frequencies across countries. The biological implications of these alterations in the N-T of pfcsp remain unclear, however, the locations of these changes implied their potential biological importance. The 19-amino acid insertion was positioned at the beginning of a monoclonal antibody-binding epitope (81EDNEKLRKPKH91), which enables effective neutralization of sporozoite binding by the antibody [37]. The A98G was close to the 92KKLKQP97 enzyme cleavage site, highly conserved in the global pfcsp population and crucial for the conformational changes in the protein [7]. A recent study has demonstrated that the majority of antibodies that significantly inhibit parasite infection bind to both the CRR and a segment of the N-T junction of PfCSP [38]. Furthermore, polypeptides of the N-T are potent inducers of inhibitory antibody production, thus preventing sporozoite invasion into hepatocytes [17]. These findings highlight the potential of the N-T of PfCSP as a key component of malaria vaccines. The limited genetic polymorphisms in the N-T of the global pfcsp population also lend support to the idea that this region represent a reliable element for PfCSP-based vaccines.

As anticipated, the CRR of Vietnam pfcsp exhibited high level of genetic polymorphisms. Similar to the CRR of global pfcsp, diverse numbers and assemblies of various types of PRMs were primary factors driving genetic diversity in the CRR of Vietnam pfcsp. The two PRMs, NANP and NVDP, most prevalent in the CRR of global pfcsp, were similarly predominant in the CRR of Vietnam pfcsp. However, minor PRMs, such as DANP, NASP, and NATP, were uniquely present in the Vietnam population, albeit at low frequencies. Several country-specific PRMs were also detected in a few countries, including Myanmar, PNG, SI, and E. Guinea [18, 29, 33]. These unique PRMs could signify a regional genetic signature or a spontaneous mutation. Further research is required to understand the implications of these variations on the genetic signatures of the parasites and host immune responses. The Vietnam pfcsp CRR, along with those of other Asia countries like Thailand, Myanmar, and Iran [18, 28, 31], contained a relatively higher number of PRMs compared to those in Africa and South America pfcsp [29, 32]. This fluctuation in PRM quantity in this region may be due to geographical dispersal of genetic variances or varying selective pressures, resulting in a co-evolutionary adaptation to maintain parasite stability in specific areas. Current PfCSP-based vaccines were designed with 18 or 19 PRMs [11, 16, 39]. Given the genetic complexity of the global pfcsp CRR, an extensive investigation into the relationship between PRM structural differences in the global P. falciparum population and the efficacy of PfCSP-based vaccines is necessary.

The C-T of Vietnam pfcsp also exhibited polymorphic characteristics, presenting 10 polymorphic amino acid residues. Eight of these were located in the two T-cell epitope regions, Th2R and Th3R. The sequences corresponding to the epitopes KHIEQYLKKIQNSL and NKPKDELDYEND were predominantly identified in the Vietnam population (91.6%, 87/95). These sequences were also commonly found in the pfcsp populations of Asia and Oceania [17, 27, 28, 40]. The haplotype diversity (H) and nucleotide diversity (π) values for pfcsp C-T were higher in Africa countries compared to those from other continents, suggesting enhanced genetic diversity in Africa pfcsp populations. The pfcsp populations from Asia, Africa, and South America showed similar patterns of nucleotide diversity in the C-T, characterized by two major peaks in the Th2R and Th3R regions, while populations from Oceania displayed a single peak in the Th3R. Polymorphisms in the Th3R have been linked to human leukocyte antigen (HLA) binding and cytotoxic T-cell reactivity [41, 42], which may allow parasites to evade host immune pressure. Positive values of dN–dS observed in Vietnam and all other studied pfcsp populations indicate that the C-T is likely under positive selection, contributing to the maintenance of genetic diversity. A significant decline of genetic diversity and opposite direction of natural selection were detected in the recent Vietnam pfcsp compared to the previous Vietnam (1998–2003) pfcsp. This remarkable temporal difference between the two groups may be due to the transmission intensity. Malaria cases in Vietnam have been rapidly reduced since 1992. Confirmed malaria cases in Vietnam were 224,923 when the National Malaria Control Programme started [43], but dropped to 412 cases in 2022 [1]. A great decrease in malaria transmission intensity in the country might contribute to a reduction in the population diversity of pfcsp in the Vietnam P. falciparum population by the genetic sweep or bottleneck effect.

Haplotype network analysis of 1055 global pfcsp sequences revealed that pfcsp from Asia and Oceania formed a limited number of clusters, whereas Africa pfcsp showed a highly branched haplotype diversity. No haplotype encompassed all geographic locations analysed in this study. The majority of singletons were primarily associated with Africa pfcsp, emphasizing the greater genetic diversity in Africa pfcsp compared to other regions. The current recombinant PfCSP vaccines, RTS,S and R21, were constructed with the CRR and C-T sequences of the P. falciparum NF54/3D7 strain [11, 16, 39]. Haplotype network analysis demonstrated that the 3D7 haplotype was shared by only 10 sequences from Africa and 1 from Vietnam. The infrequency of the 3D7 haplotype sequence in the global population raises questions regarding the efficacy of the 3D7-based vaccine against the global P. falciparum population. High genetic diversity identified in the global pfcsp might cause antigenic or structural variations of PfCSP, resulting in a decrease in the binding capacity of antibodies formed by current vaccines, thereby reducing their protective efficacy. Considering the high genetic diversity of the global pfcsp, future vaccine development strategies should consider regional rather than universal vaccines. Given the strong genetic relationships between Asia and Oceania pfcsp C-T, it may be reasonable to consider developing a vaccine targeting the predominant pfcsp variants in the Asia–Pacific areas.

This study also has limitations. The P. falciparum samples analysed in this study were obtained in a few endemic provinces in Central Vietnam, and the samples from each province were not evenly distributed, suggesting this study could not represent the nationwide pattern of genetic diversity of the Vietnam pfcsp population. Further studies for larger numbers of parasite samples from the other malaria-endemic provinces in Vietnam are necessary to understand and generalize the genetic nature of the gene in the Vietnam P. falciparum population. The global pfcsp populations analysed did not originate from the same time points, indicating an inability to accurately represent the genetic diversity of pfcsp from the current global P. falciparum population. These may also influence the interpretation of genetic diversity and haplotype distribution patterns in the global pfcsp population. To overcome this sampling bias and temporal differences in the global pfcsp sequences, a more comprehensive and larger-scale study involving the current global P. falciparum population is necessary.

Conclusion

This study represents the first investigation into the genetic diversity of the full-length pfcsp in Vietnam P. falciparum isolates. Despite the significant reduction in malaria cases in the country, Vietnam pfcsp exhibited considerable genetic diversity, with evidence of natural selection. Similar observations have been noted in other vaccine-candidate genes from Vietnam P. falciparum population [44–47]. The maintenance of P. falciparum genetic diversity could be attributed to asymptomatic malaria cases that act as reservoirs, perpetuating or creating genetic diversity within the population. This research provides significant insights into the genetic structure of Vietnam pfcsp and enhances the understanding of genetic diversity and natural selection patterns in global pfcsp, which could inform the development of effective PfCSP-based vaccines. Furthermore, this study highlights the need for ongoing surveillance of genetic diversity of pfcsp in global population to deeply understand the polymorphic nature and evolutionary dynamics of the gene within the worldwide P. falciparum population.

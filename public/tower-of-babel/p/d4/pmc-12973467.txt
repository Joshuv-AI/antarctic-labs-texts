Genomic comparison reveals single-nucleotide polymorphic sites and attenuation-associated site combinations specific to Chinese live attenuated varicella-zoster virus vaccines
Authors: Yuchan Zhang, Xuhua Duan, Yuanyuan Cai, Mingren Wang, Ziqiang Wang, Minghui Song, Tianyi Qiu, Xuanyi Wang, Hong Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe live-attenuated varicella-zoster virus (VZV) vaccine, mainly based on the Oka strain, is essential for the prevention and control of chickenpox (varicella). Although genomic profiles of internationally prevalent Oka vaccine strain (vOka)-derived preparations have been reported, the comprehensive single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) profiles of multiple commercially available Chinese vOka-derived vaccines are still lacking. We performed next-generation sequencing (NGS) on five Chinese-manufactured VZV vaccines, comparing genomic variations against the wild-type parental Oka (pOka) strain and internationally prevalent vaccine products. Notably, among 54 key SNPs distinguishing Chinese vaccines from the wild-type pOka strain, 4 novel China-specific SNPs were identified. What’s more, we identified 6 near-fixed (the vaccine allele frequency >90%) non-synonymous SNPs that consistently co-occurred across all production batches, potentially impacting protein function through amino acid substitutions. Genetic heterogeneity across manufacturers revealed manufacturer-specific signatures, whereas consistent batch production underscored manufacturing stability. By systematically mapping SNPs in Chinese VZV vaccines, this study bridges a critical knowledge gap and provides essential insights into manufacturer-specific genomic signatures.KEYWORDS: Varicella-zoster virus, Chinese vOka-derived vaccines, single-nucleotide polymorphism, genetic heterogeneity, batch-to-batch consistency

Introduction

Varicella-zoster virus (VZV), a member of the alphaherpesvirus subfamily (Herpesviridae). possesses an enveloped virion structure comprising tegument, nucleocapsid, and a 125-kbp linear double-stranded DNA genome.1 Primary VZV infection causes chickenpox (varicella), and then establishes latency in sensory ganglia. Subsequent reactivation, often triggered by immunosuppression, causes herpes zoster.2 To prevent VZV infection, the attenuated vaccine strain vOka was developed by Takahashi et al. through serial passaging of the wild-type parental strain (pOka) in guinea pig embryo fibroblasts and human diploid WI-38 cells. This strain was isolated in 1974 from vesicular lesions of a child (family name Oka) infected with varicella.3 Globally, three major licensed live-attenuated varicella vaccines, OkaVax (Biken), VarilRix (GlaxoSmithKline), and VariVax (Merck & Co), are derived from vOka.4–6 In China, eight varicella vaccines approved by the NMPA (e.g., Baike, Keygen, Sinovac) are derived from the vOka strain,7,8 demonstrating clinical immunogenicity and safety comparable to internationally available counterparts.9,10

The first complete genome sequence for VZV (Dumas strain) was determined by Davison and Scott in 1986.11 The VZV genome is composed of a unique long (UL) region and a unique short (US) region, both flanked by inverted terminal repeats (TR) and internal repeats (IR).12 Additionally, the genome contains five regions, namely R1 to R5 with tandem reiterations. It contains at least 70 open reading frames (ORFs), with three ORFs (62–64) in internal repeat short (IRS) inversely duplicated in terminal repeat short (TRS) (ORFs 69–71).1,12

Advances in sequencing technologies have progressively refined our understanding of varicella vaccine strain genomics. Early Sanger sequencing identified 42 single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) sites distinguishing vOka from pOka.13 Furthermore, 24 potential vaccine-specific sites were identified via systematic comparative analysis of 5 vaccine strains and 45 clinical isolates using next-generation sequencing (NGS).14 Concurrently, Depledge et al. sequenced OkaVax, VarilRix, and VariVax, revealing 137 shared SNPs across all vaccine batches.15 Following the initiation of VZV vaccine production in China, studies on the genomic characterization of Chinese VZV vaccine strains and preparations emerged. The Shanghai Institute of Biological Products validated molecular consistency across Chinese vOka-strain vaccines via deep sequencing, while the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention characterized the vOka-BK from Changchun BCHT, providing the first global comparison of Chinese vaccines and identifying efficacy-related SNPs. These efforts not only enabled the global comparison of Chinese vaccines but also identified efficacy-related SNPs, laying an important foundation for Chinese vaccine research.7,8 However, comprehensive SNP data across Chinese manufacturers remains limited, hindering our understanding of vaccine diversity.

NGS enables allele frequency measurement at every nucleotide position within heterogeneous populations, reflecting intra-sample diversity.6 Moreover, allele frequency analysis increases the likelihood that specific SNP combinations contribute to attenuation mechanisms, though conclusive validation requires experimental confirmation.6 For example, near-fixed SNPs (e.g., positions 560 in ORF0 and positions 106262, 107252 in ORF62) suggest synergistic effects of ORF0/ORF62 combinations as potential attenuation cores,14–16 while low-frequency vaccine alleles in rash lesions may contribute to attenuated vOka replication.17 However, the specific SNPs conferring attenuation remain unidentified, and the mechanistic basis of attenuation is incompletely understood. A proposed concept posited that vOka attenuation may require synergistic interactions among specific SNP sets spanning two or three ORFs, suggesting attenuation arises from cumulative combinatorial effects rather than individual mutations.6,18 Nevertheless, this model still requires more extensive research and supporting data.

To gain a deeper insight of the genome of the VZV vaccines, a detailed SNP map for five major Chinese live-attenuated varicella vaccines was generated via NGS in this study. Comparisons with the wild-type pOka and global vOka-derived vaccine products were made to reveal genetic characteristics unique to Chinese products. Additionally, inter-manufacturer variability and intra-manufacturer batch consistency were explored. These findings contribute to a more comprehensive genetic knowledge of Chinese varicella vaccines.

Materials and methods

Vaccine collection

Five distinct Chinese manufacturers, labeled A through E, supplied vOka-derived live attenuated varicella vaccines for this study. Manufacturer A provided five batches (A1-A5), B provided four (B1-B4), C provided four (C1-C4), D provided two (D1-D2), and E provided three (E1-E3). Batches from each manufacturer spanned two to three distinct production years.

Library construction and NGS

Total DNA was extracted from each sample using the MiniBEST Viral RNA/DNA Extraction Kit Ver.5.0 (TaKaRa) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The construction of sequencing libraries, preparation of DNA nanoballs (DNBs), and sequencing were performed by Shenzhen MGI Tech Co., Ltd. Briefly, libraries were generated with the MGIEasy Fast Enzymatic DNA Library Preparation Kit V1.0 (MGI Tech) following the V2.0 protocol, involving enzymatic fragmentation, adapter ligation, purification, and PCR amplification. After PCR product purification, DNBs were prepared using the DNBSEQ One-Step DNB Preparation Kit V1.0 (MGI Tech, V4.0 protocol) via rolling circle amplification. The qualified DNBs, verified for concentration and quality, were loaded onto sequencing slides for high-throughput sequencing on a DNBSEQ-G99RS Sequencer (MGI Tech).

VZV strains and sequencing data resources

The analyzed VZV strains comprised the reference strain Dumas (NC_001348), pOka strain (AB097933), and vOka vaccine strain (AB097932) from NCBI GenBank database. Raw sequencing data generated by NGS for commercial vaccines, including Barycela (GC Biopharma, Korea), VarilRix (GlaxoSmithKline), and VariVax (Merck & Co.), were retrieved from the public dataset reported by Moon et al.19 This dataset was jointly processed with our sequencing data through a variant calling pipeline. For cross-regional comparison, SNPs previously identified by NGS in international vaccine batches (VarilRix, VariVax) and the OkaVax working seed stock were also used, as reported by Depledge et al.15

Genome assembly and variant calling

Raw sequencing data were subjected to adapter trimming and low-quality base filtering using fastp (v0.23.4),20 to remove reads with more than 3 ambiguous bases, followed by quality assessment with FastQC (v0.12.1). Trimmed reads were aligned to the human reference genome hg38 using Bowtie2 (v2.5.3)21 in rapid alignment mode, and unaligned reads were retained to remove host contamination. Non-host reads were mapped to VZV reference genomes (Dumas, pOka, vOka) using BWA-MEM (BWA v0.7.17),22 and the alignment results were converted to BAM format via SAMtools (v1.19)23 for coverage statistics. Site-specific coverage files were generated by SAMtools mpileup with parameters, base quality ≥30, and maximum depth 20,000. Variant frequencies were extracted using the custom Python (v3.12.2) script VFcount.py developed by Moon et al.19 In parallel, SNP calling was performed with bcftools (v1.19)23 using the same quality and depth thresholds (Q ≥ 30, max depth 20,000) with the -I parameter to exclude insertion-deletion variants. Only point mutations with variant frequencies ≥5%24–27 and sequencing depth ≥10028 were retained. All genomic coordinates were standardized to the Dumas reference strain through MAFFT (v7.525)-based multiple sequence alignment and custom coordinate conversion pipelines. Functional consequences of identified variants were annotated using snpEff (v5.2) to facilitate biological interpretation.

Functional impact prediction of amino acid variants

The potential functional consequences of non-synonymous SNPs were predicted using two in silico tools: SIFT (Sorting Intolerant From Tolerant, available from https://sift.bii.a-star.edu.sg.)29 and PolyPhen-2 (Polymorphism Phenotyping v2, available from http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/).30 SIFT analysis generated tolerance scores and median sequence information. Amino acid substitutions were predicted to potentially impact protein function when the SIFT score was ≤0.05 (particularly with median sequence information less than 3.25), and tolerated if the score was >0.05. PolyPhen-2 provided probability scores ranging from 0 (benign) to 1 (likely to impact protein function), with scores >0.85 suggesting potential structural or functional disruption.

Diversity analysis using Shannon entropy

Shannon entropy was used as the measure of the population diversity within samples, with higher entropy indicating greater diversity.31,32 This metric has been used previously to assess diversity in both VZV following passage in in-vitro cell culture and among live attenuated VZV vaccine preparations from different manufacturers.15,24 It was calculated for each SNP site across vaccine batches using the formula, Hs=−∑i=1nPilnPi, where Pi is the frequency of the i-th SNP site in the viral population and ln is natural log.24

Co-occurrence network construction and analysis

Variant frequency data for 54 SNP sites were extracted from sequencing batches of domestically produced varicella vaccines. Variants with a mean frequency exceeding 0.5 were classified as “variant present” (coded 1), while others were designated “variant absent” (coded 0), since the distribution of mean variant frequencies across these sites was broad, with approximately half exceeding this threshold. Co-occurrence strength between SNP pairs was quantified using the Jaccard index calculated as JA,B=A∩BA∪B, where A and B represent sample sets exhibiting variants at each site. Data processing and Jaccard index computation were performed using pandas (v2.2.3) for data organization and numpy (v2.2.5) for numerical calculations. To examine the statistical significance of co-occurrence relationships and rule out associations arising from random chance, we employed a permutation-based approach with 10,000 repetitions by joblib (v1.4.2). In each permutation, we randomly shuffled the variant statuses across all SNPs within each sample while maintaining the total number of variants per sample unchanged. The resulting permuted datasets were used to construct a null distribution of Jaccard indices for every possible SNP pair. Empirical p-values were calculated for each SNP pair by comparing observed Jaccard indices against this permuted background. To control for multiple testing across all 1431 SNP pairs, we applied Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction with a significance threshold of 0.05 by scipy (v1.15.2). Significant co-occurrences defined an undirected weighted network where nodes represent SNP sites and edge weights correspond to Jaccard indices. Network construction and topological analysis were conducted using networkx (v3.5). The Markov Clustering algorithm was implemented via the markov_clustering (v0.0.6) library with inflation parameter optimized to 4.2 based on clustering quality assessment using the silhouette score from scikit-learn (v1.6.1). Node importance was evaluated using three centrality measures: degree centrality (direct connections), closeness centrality (proximity to all other nodes), and eigenvector centrality (influence of important neighbors),33 all computed using networkx. Visualization employed spring layout with node size proportional to weighted degree centrality (using Jaccard indices as edge weights), edge thickness scaled to Jaccard index, and distinct colors differentiating clusters. Visualization was implemented in matplotlib (v3.10.1). All analyses were performed using Python (v3.13.3).

Statistical analysis

For correlation analysis of SNP profiles, pairwise correlations between SNP profiles of different manufacturers and batches were calculated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient on the “Wu Kong” platform (https://www.omicsolution.com/wkomics/main/), and correlation matrices including exact correlation coefficients were visualized using heatmaps.

Other statistical analyses and graphical representations were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9). This included data visualization and statistical testing for diversity analysis using Shannon entropy, comparisons of allele frequency distributions across manufacturers and within batches, and analysis of counts of SNPs exceeding predefined frequency thresholds. Statistical differences in entropy among manufacturers were assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis test, while comparisons of allele frequency distributions were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test (for groups with >3 data points) or the Mann-Whitney U test (for groups with 2 data points, specifically manufacturer D).

Results

SNPs identified in Chinese VZV vaccines

To characterize genomic features of Chinese vOka-derived varicella vaccines, we collected 18 batches from five manufacturers and performed NGS, aligning against Dumas, pOka, and vOka genomes. The SNPs for each manufacturer comprised all variants detected across its batches. Results showed 227–244 SNPs per vaccine product compared to Dumas (Figure 1A), with 256 unique SNPs identified collectively across Chinese vaccines to Dumas. As shown in the Venn diagram (Figure 1B), 216 SNPs were shared among all Chinese vaccines. In contrast, compared to pOka, the SNP counts dropped to 36–44 per vaccine product, with only 54 SNPs identified in Chinese vaccines (Figure 1A), among which 26 SNPs were shared across all Chinese vaccines (Figure 1C). Additionally, only 10–13 SNPs were found when compared to vOka (Figure 1A).

Four novel Chinese-specific SNPs in VZV vaccines

Since the vaccine strain vOka was derived from pOka by attenuation, subsequent analyses specifically examined variations in different commercial vaccine products relative to the pOka reference. All genomic coordinates in this study refer to the Dumas reference strain. Of the 54 SNPs identified in Chinese vaccines, 50 were also detected in at least two non-Chinese vaccines, including OkaVax (n = 1), VariVax (n = 6), VarilRix (n = 5), and Barycela (GC Biopharma) (n = 3). (Supplementary Table 1). Batch-averaged variant frequency were used for each SNP to define a representative profile for each manufacturer. Four SNPs were specific to Chinese vaccines (Figure 2A). These unique SNPs included ORF1 upstream position 4194, which showed a variant frequency of 0.56 to 0.72 across three batches from manufacturer E. ORF5 position 4916 caused amino acid modifications, with a frequency of less than 0.15 in one batch from manufacturer D. In ORF31, positions 58729 and 58868 both led to amino acid changes. Position 58729 was detected in all batches from manufacturer E with a variant frequency of 0.22 to 0.32, while position 58868 was observed in all batches from manufacturers C, D, and E. The mutation frequency of position 58868 exceeded 0.99 in six batches from manufacturers C and D and ranged from 0.38 to 0.44 in three batches from manufacturer E (Figure 2B, Supplementary Table 1). Five SNPs (560, 105705, 106262, 107252, 108111) in the varicella vaccine showed highly fixed mean allele frequencies (> 0.9) across manufacturers, three of which (560, 106262, 107252) caused nonsynonymous mutations (Figure 2A, Supplementary Table 1).

Six functional SNPs with high frequency Co-occurrence in Chinese VZV vaccines

To characterize the identified SNPs and assess their potential functional impact, we first presented their genomic distribution and mutation profiles based on variant calling results, followed by in silico prediction of the consequences of amino acid substitutions. Analysis of genomic distribution revealed that among the 54 SNPs identified, 47 were in coding sequences (CDS) of 23 ORFs, while 7 resided in non-coding regions (NCRs) (Figure 3A,B). Of the coding SNPs, 27 resulted in non-synonymous mutations with amino acid substitutions (positions indicated in green font in Figure 3A), potentially affecting protein function34,35 (Table 1). ORF62 exhibited the highest SNP frequency, with a total of 9 identified SNPs, including 6 non-synonymous mutations (Figure 3B). Other ORFs exhibited lower SNP counts, with ≤5 SNPs per locus. Specifically, ORF31 and ORF22 each contained 4 SNPs, including 3 amino acid-altering mutations in ORF31 and 2 in ORF22. ORF0 and ORF55 both harbored 3 SNPs: ORF0 featured a critical read-through mutation (terminator→Arg) that extended the polypeptide chain, plus one amino acid-altering mutation, while ORF55 carried 3 non-synonymous mutations. Among ORFs with 2 SNPs (ORF1, ORF35, ORF50), only ORF50 contained an amino acid-substituting mutation. Additionally, non-synonymous mutations occurred singly in ORF5, 6, 9A, 10, 21, 39, 48, 52, 59, and 64, whereas synonymous mutations were found in ORF11, 14, 18, 24, 42, 47, 51, and 54 (Figure 3A,B).

Subsequently, to predict the potential impact on protein function of amino acid alterations, two widely cited bioinformatic software programs, SIFT (Sorting Intolerant From Tolerant) and PolyPhen-2 (Polymorphism Phenotyping v2),29,30 were used for in silico analysis. SIFT scores ≤0.05 and PolyPhen-2 scores > 0.85 were interpreted as indicators that the amino acid substitutions might affect protein function. Analysis identified 12 non-synonymous SNPs predicted to potentially impact protein function. ORF62 contained the highest number of such predicted functional variants (n = 5). Two variants in ORF9A and ORF31 showed consistent prediction of potential functional impact by both tools. Single variants predicted to potentially impact function were identified in ORF22, 39, and 59 via SIFT, and in ORF6, and 64 via PolyPhen-2 (Table 1).

Allele frequency analysis of mutation sites can reveal whether a specific site has become fixed as a mutant allele in the vaccine strains. Nearly fixed sites are considered hallmarks distinguishing vaccine strains from clinical isolates, and those approaching fixation in attenuated strains are hypothesized to be more likely associated with attenuation.6,35 We therefore analyzed the mean variant frequencies of 54 SNPs across 18 tested batches, finding 11 with mean frequencies >0.9, 17 > 0.8, and 27 > 0.5 (Figure 3C).

Co-occurring mutations in viral genomes are likely nonrandom, instead exhibiting functional synergy. For instance, the Y449S and N501Y co-mutations, which emerged during SARS-CoV-2 evolution and were located in the spike protein, reduce infectivity while playing a key role in disrupting antibody-mediated viral neutralization.36 This inter-mutation synergy may help achieve effective attenuation in live attenuated vaccines. To explore potential functional synergy among our candidate SNPs, we analyzed their co-occurrence across vaccine batches, using the Jaccard index to quantify pairwise co-occurrence intensity. Among 1431 possible SNP pairs, 833 co-occurred in at least one batch, with 137 showing perfect co-occurrence (Jaccard index = 1) (Supplementary Table 2). Markov Clustering (MCL) clustering grouped 37 SNPs into a single major cluster, indicating strong interconnectedness (Figure 3D, cluster 1). Network analysis of this cluster identified 15 SNPs that ranked first across three classic centrality metrics, degree centrality, closeness centrality, and eigenvector centrality (Figure 3D, Supplementary Table 3), marking them as core nodes likely mediating the cluster’s integrity. Notably, the 15 central SNPs included all 11 SNPs with mean variant frequencies exceeding 0.9, with the remaining 4 also exhibiting high frequencies (>0.7) (Figure 3C). Functional annotation revealed that 7 of these 15 SNPs were non-synonymous, while the remaining 8 were synonymous or located in non-coding regions. Among the 7 non-synonymous SNPs, 6 were predicted to impact protein function (Table 1) and not only co-occur consistently across all tested batches but also exhibit a mean variant frequency exceeding 0.9. Taken together, by integrating functional annotation, variant frequency analysis, and co-occurrence network centrality metrics, we identified these 6 SNPs, located at positions 560, 5745, 107252, 105310, 106262, and 71252, as potentially critical candidates.

Divergent SNP sets among Chinese VZV vaccine manufacturers

The comprehensive study mentioned above described the genome-wide variation characteristics of Chinese VZV vaccines. To dissect the heterogeneity landscape among manufacturers, we conducted comparative analyses to further define inter-manufacturer differences by examining SNP profiles.

Compared with pOka, vaccines from five Chinese manufacturers exhibited 42, 44, 36, 37, and 38 SNPs, respectively, forming a 54-SNP variation set (Figure 1A). Twenty-six SNPs (48.15%) were shared across all vaccines, with 28 manufacturer-specific SNPs identified (Figures 1C and 3A). Five of these resided in upstream non-coding regions of ORF1, 61, and 62. Fifteen of 23 coding-region differential SNPs were non-synonymous, altering amino acid sequences in ORFs 5, 10, 21, 22, 31, 48, 50, 52, 55, 62 and 64 (Figure 3A).

We accessed population heterogeneity using Shannon entropy, a metric quantifying diversity based on haplotype frequencies,31,32 previously applied to VZV vaccines15,24 into this study. Using this approach, we found significant differences in population heterogeneity between the A and C/D vaccines (P = 0.0027 and 0.0474, respectively), while the remaining vaccines had comparable levels of entropy. Notably, C and D vaccines had lower mean entropy than the other three (Figure 4A).

For the same SNP site, variant frequencies varied across manufacturers. We conducted pairwise Spearman correlation analysis to assess inter-manufacturer relationships using batch-averaged variant frequencies. Results showed distinct inter-manufacturer patterns: A and B had extremely high correlation (>0.99), C and D showed high correlation (>0.95), while other pairs had lower correlation (<0.69) (Figure 4B).

Violin plot of the variant frequencies revealed similar distribution patterns between A and B, with medians (Q2, 50%) centered around 0.38 and 0.50, lower quartiles (Q1, 25%) at approximately 0.07 and 0.10, and upper quartiles (Q3, 75%) reaching roughly 0.85 and 0.94, respectively (Figure 4C). C and D also displayed comparable distributions, featuring Q2 close to 0.91, Q1 at 0, and Q3 approaching 1 (Figure 4C). E exhibited a distinct distribution with Q2 of 0.44, Q1 at 0, and Q3 of 0.99 (Figure 4C). Kruskal-Wallis testing and pairwise Mann-Whitney U tests revealed no significant differences between manufacturers (p > 0.05). This lack of significance may be attributed to non-normal data distribution and skewness. In the threshold-based analysis, we quantified the proportion of SNPs exceeding frequency thresholds (0.1 to 0.9) (Figure 4D). At lower thresholds (e.g., 0.1), products A and B showed higher proportions of SNPs. Conversely, at higher thresholds (e.g., 0.9), products C and D demonstrated higher proportions.

Intra-manufacturer batch stability of SNPs in VZV vaccines

An analysis was performed on the genetic stability across batches from five Chinese manufacturers. Spearman correlation showed significant within-manufacturer genomic consistency, with intra-manufacturer batch correlations >0.92 (Figure 5A). A and B had batch correlations >0.97, confirming their genetic similarity. C and D batches showed consistent correlations >0.94, directly evidencing their high genomic similarity.

Additionally, we generated violin plots of allele variant frequencies and line graphs of threshold-exceeding SNP counts for vaccine batches from each manufacturer. No significant inter-batch differences were detected (Kruskal-Wallis/Mann-Whitney U, p > 0.05), and SNP count differences remained ≤4 across batches under the same threshold (Supplementary Figure S1). This analysis identified 11 variable SNP sites across intra-manufacturer batches, with 2, 5, 3, 2, and 3 such SNPs for each manufacturer, at least 92% of SNPs were shared across more than 2 batches (Figure 5B). Except positions 107136 and 109200 in manufacturer E, which exhibit a greater mutation frequency (>0.8) in their current batches, all other variable SNPs showed low frequencies (<0.51) (Figure 5B). Besides, quantitative analysis revealed that 97.8% of the SNPs present in all batches exhibited a coefficient of variation of less than 20% (overall CV range: 4%-33%) (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

This study identified, for the first time, four novel China-specific SNP sites: 4194 (ORF1), 4916(ORF5), 58729(ORF31), and 58868(ORF31) in Chinese live-attenuated varicella vaccines using NGS. These SNPs have not been reported in global vOka-derived vaccines (Figure 2). Among these four sites, position 58868 in ORF31/gB (D648Y) stands out due to its significant functional implications. Given gB’s critical roles in viral entry and pathogenesis,37–39 the D648Y mutation warrants special attention. Its spatial proximity to attenuation marker R699Q34 and predicted dysfunctionality (Table 1), we hypothesize that the D648Y variant may contribute to attenuation by modulating gB-mediated functions, potentially reducing viral infectivity and pathogenicity. The three remaining specific sites include position 4194, located upstream of ORF1, which encodes a membrane protein dispensable for in vitro replication.40 Another is position 4916, causing an amino acid substitution in ORF5/gK, an essential glycoprotein for VZV replication,41 but with low frequency and no predicted impact on protein function (Figure 2B, Table 1). The third is position 58729, in the same ORF31/gB locus as 58868 and results in an amino acid change, though with low frequency and a less pronounced predicted functional impact (Figure 2B, Table 1). Therefore, these three sites are not predicted to substantially affect core viral phenotypes. In terms of distribution, the 58868 variant is highly prevalent in vaccines from manufacturers C and D (Figure 2B), indicating its conservation in certain Chinese vaccine products. In contrast, the other three China-specific SNPs (4194, 4916, 58729) displayed restricted distributions, each detected only in specific manufacturers’ preparations (Figure 2B). These manufacturer-distinct SNP patterns demonstrate significant genetic divergence in Chinese vaccines. The novel variants significantly expand the documented spectrum of genetic variation in VZV vaccines, highlighting the need to prioritize producer-specific deep sequencing in genomic surveillance programs.

Among 54 SNPs distinguishing Chinese vOka-derived vaccines from wild-type pOka, six potential key SNPs were identified as high-priority targets based on a tripartite strategy: functional impact prediction (in silico), allele frequency profiling, and co-occurrence network analysis. In viral genomes, co-occurring mutations may exhibit functional linkages that enable synergistic interactions.36,42 Similarly, the six key SNPs identified here (position 560, 5745, 71252, 105310, 106262, 107252), through their co-occurrence clustering in network analysis, may potentially function synergistically. In the context of attenuated vaccines, such synergy may enable the critical dual phenotype: maintaining infectivity for immunogenicity while achieving virulence attenuation. Among the six SNPs, three exhibit near-fixation in both the current vaccine batches and historical vaccine genome analyses.14,15 These identical variants were also detected in the high-passage attenuated strain Ellen, and were considered potentially associated with attenuation.16 These include ORF0 position 560 (*130 R), which converts a stop codon to arginine, extending the replication-associated protein by 92 amino acids,43–47 along with ORF62 positions 106262 (R958G) and 107252 (S628G), both residing in the IE62 transcriptional activator48–50 (Figure 2A, Table 1). However, single-site mutations at these positions do not alter viral growth or protein functionality,45,51 demonstrating that combinatorial interactions are necessary for phenotypic impact. One of our six key SNPs is the ORF62 substitution 105310 (L1275S). In prior literature, this variant, together with two other ORF62 substitutions 105356 (I1260V) and 105544 (V1197A), is collectively named the SEAD-3 m cluster, which resides in IE62‘s serine-enriched acidic domain (Domain V).51 Functional evidence confirms that SEAD-3 m, in combination with M99T (Domain I), cooperatively attenuates IE62-mediated transactivation,51 directly demonstrating synergistic interactions between these variants. Although absent from the six key SNPs, variants 105356 and 105544 co-cluster with these key SNPs in the co-occurrence network and are predicted to alter protein function. Both show near-fixation (allele frequency >0.9) in most Chinese manufacturers (excluding C/D), indicating manufacturer-specific co-occurrence with key SNPs. Besides, diverging from prior validation of single or dual-domain combinations,51,52 our analysis revealed distinct multi-domain mutation profiles in Chinese vaccines, involving IE62 Domain II (V512A, S628G), Domain IV (R958G), and Domain V (SEAD-3 m), where manufacturer-specific configurations drive combinatorial diversification (Figure 3A). Despite such genetic heterogeneity, all vaccines maintain effective attenuation, indicating that divergent combinatorial strategies can achieve comparable attenuation phenotypes through domain-specific functional modulation. The additional key SNPs include position 5745 in ORF6, located in a primase gene critical for viral DNA replication initiation,53–56 and 71252 in ORF39, positioned within a gene encoding a transmembrane protein that suppresses the interferon (IFN)-beta pathway to modulate immune recognition.57 These represent newly identified ORF candidates alongside the well-characterized ORF0 and ORF62, suggesting potential functional connections that warrant further exploration. Furthermore, the functional relevance of these variations, particularly in immune evasion genes, is underscored by the known role of IE62 in preventing interferon regulatory factor 3 phosphorylation to disarm IFN defenses,58 and by the independent transcriptomic finding that attenuated VZV strains showed more differentially expressed genes involved in IFN-mediated signaling pathways and the retinoic-acid inducible gene-I-like receptor than the wild-type strain.59 This mechanistic and phenotypic alignment supports the hypothesis that SNPs in ORF62 and ORF39 may collectively contribute to the attenuated virus’s inability to suppress the IFN response.

This study revealed significant inter-manufacturer genomic heterogeneity among vOka-derived varicella vaccines from five major Chinese manufacturers. This heterogeneity is reflected in population diversity and SNP frequency distributions (Figure 4). From the manufacturers’ product instructions, all manufacturers use the internationally accepted vOka strain as the production master seed, but there are differences in the cells used. Manufacturers A, B, and C use MRC-5 cells, while D uses SV-1 and E uses 2BS cells. Moreover, the passage levels of vOka vary among manufacturers: Companies A and B have similar passage levels, as do Companies C and D, while Company E shows relatively greater differences. In addition, there are also certain differences in the process parameters used by different manufacturers. Therefore, together with the obtained inter‑manufacturer genomic difference data, the source of the production seed strain, passage level, and cell substrate are more critical factors influencing the vaccine’s genetic sequence, which have been documented in prior studies.15,19,24 Multi-batch analyses from individual manufacturers showed that >92% of SNPs were conserved across two or more batches from the same manufacturer (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure 1), indicating that this heterogeneity originated during working seed lot development rather than production-scale processes. Critically, evidence confirms the safety and efficacy of these vaccines despite genomic variation.9,60 Furthermore, high intra-manufacturer batch stability ensures consistent product quality and reliability.

This study has certain limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was confined to specific manufacturers and batches, limiting the generalizability of high-frequency SNP co-occurrence patterns. Future expansion of sample diversity, encompassing broader geographic sources and production timelines, would enhance robustness. Additionally, our analysis only provides a detailed genomic characterization. The integration of complementary multi-omic information, such as transcriptomics and proteomics profiles, in future work would help to identify the specific molecular phenotypes caused by the key SNPs and then pinpoint the highest-priority targets. Subsequently, more studies can be performed on experimental validation through both in vitro and in vivo assays, such as growth kinetics in cell culture and pathogenesis studies in animal models (e.g., SCID-hu mice), to further confirm the phenotypic effects of these SNPs and to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of attenuation or virulence.

In conclusion, this study established the first comprehensive SNP profile of five major Chinese vOka-derived varicella vaccines through NGS analysis, which provides critical insights for vaccine quality control. First, four China-specific SNPs were identified, which have not been reported in all available non-Chinese vOka preparations. Second, characterization of 54 consensus SNPs differentiating Chinese vaccines from wild-type pOka revealed six high-frequency co-occurring mutations with potential impacts on protein function, which are involved in attenuation, replication, and immunomodulation. Crucially, their co-occurrence, forming manufacturer-specific configurations across multiple functional domains, suggests a combinatorial mechanism for attenuation, which may explain how these vaccines maintain a consistent safety profile despite inter-manufacturer heterogeneity. The minimal batch-to-batch variation within each manufacturer demonstrates remarkable genomic stability and manufacturing consistency. This high degree of consistency directly supports the reliability and safety profile of these vaccines, and lays a foundation for future genomic sequencing-based assessment of lot-to-lot consistency.

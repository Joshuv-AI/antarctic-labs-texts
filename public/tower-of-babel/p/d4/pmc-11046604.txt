Improving the assessment and treatment of 'alcohol withdrawal syndrome’ (AWS) in patients admitted with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) at a specialist cardiothoracic centre
Authors: Siddhartha Mohan
Published: 2023

Introduction

Multiple studies show alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS) and delirium tremens (DT) worsens outcomes in the event that myocardial infarction (MI) occurs; rarely, it has even been a precipitant of acute coronary syndrome (ACS).1, 2, 3 Accurate recognition of alcohol intake and standardised care of AWS can lead to reduced mortality and shorter stay in the ACS inpatient cohort.

However, early recognition and treatment becomes difficult while simultaneously prioritising rapid management of acute MI, as focus naturally turns to cardiothoracic outcomes. Our quality improvement project (QIP) addresses key challenges of the above-stated issue in a busy specialist referral centre for cardiothoracic care without any on-site access to general-medical/gastroenterology teams.

Materials and methods

A mixed methodology approach was employed. Key stakeholders' (junior doctors, physician associates, nurses and pharmacists) self-reported confidence in recognising AWS clinical features was moderately high (mean score 3.2/5 [n = 12]). Confidence among prescribers initiating benzodiazepine reducing regimes was low (mean score 2.25/5 [n = 4]). Alcohol intake assessment was also low priority for members involved in the clerking process (mean score 5/5 [n = 4]).

A 5-month retrospective analysis of reducing regime prescriptions showed instances of prescriptions not being linked to documented AWS risk-stratification. Thus, there was an increased risk of sub-therapeutic dosing, benzodiazepine overdose or organ impairment if an incorrect benzodiazepine agent was chosen.

An assembled focus group mapped bottlenecks preventing assessment of alcohol intake and subsequent AWS risk-scoring during patient stay. Lack of standardised care protocols for AWS at the specialist centre and low confidence among ACCU staff in the use of reducing regimes was highlighted.

As a result, we aimed to (1) increase alcohol intake assessment and AWS risk-scoring for acute ACCU admissions and (2) implement AWS recognition and benzodiazepine withdrawal protocol to standardise management. QIP methodology of PDSA cycles was utilised to fulfil our aims. We planned consistent interventions targeting key stakeholders and formed a working group, following presentation at a local 'quality and safety meeting’, to iteratively implement interventions.

Plan

We reviewed acute inpatients (requiring more than a 1-day stay on ACCU, who were present on the ward for >/ = 5 days) at every 5-day interval from 27 May 2022 to 25 June 2022. Conclusion

This quality improvement project highlights many challenges to the recognition and management of AWS at a detached cardiothoracic specialist centre. Identifying and targeting key stakeholders with sustained education campaigns, utilisation of ward champions to ensure compliance and the implementation of a standardised protocol led to substantial improvements in staff confidence and knowledge. Further work is planned to optimise the education campaign with an e-learning induction module.

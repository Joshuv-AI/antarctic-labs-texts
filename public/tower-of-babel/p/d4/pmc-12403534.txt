Elabela increases in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, especially in the presence of surgery indication
Authors: Kubilay Akbal, Erdinc Gulumsek, Huseyin Ali Ozturk, Fatih Necip Arici, Cigdem Erhan, Bektas Isik, Mehmet Can Erisen, Bercem Berent Kaya, Ugur Can Izlimek, Cahit Dincer, Okan Pirinci, Ahmet Gazi Mustan, Irfan Alisan, Hilmi Erdem Sumbul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimPatients with primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) are known to have structural and functional changes in the vascular system. Bioactive peptides associated with the apelinergic system play a role in ischemic heart disease and atherosclerosis. In this study, we aimed to investigate the changes in serum elabela, a novel apelinergic system peptide, and its relationship with aortic intima media (AIM) measurements in patients with newly diagnosed primary hyperparathyroidism.Materials and methodsThe study was prospectively conducted with 45 patients with newly diagnosed Primary Hyperparathyroidism who were admitted to the Internal Medicine outpatient clinics of Adana City Training and Research Hospital. Forty people with similar demographic characteristics were included as a healthy control group. Hemogram, biochemistry, parathormone, vitamin D, urinary calcium, dual x-ray absorptiometry (DEXA), Elabela and aortic intima media levels were measured. The study groups were divided into three groups: healthy control group (Group 1: 40 patients), PHPT group without surgical indication (Group 2: 20 patients) and PHPT group with surgical indication (Group 3: 25 patients).ResultsElabela level was found to increase from group 1 to group 3. It was 2.1 (1.3–2.4) ng/mL in group 1, 10.45 (3.7-14.15) ng/mL in group 2 and 12.6 (12.0–14.0) ng/mL in group 3 (p < 0.001). AIM level was found to be statistically significantly higher in group 3 compared to groups 1 and 2. It was 1.2 (1.0-1.4) mm in group 1, 1.3 (1.2–1.6) mm in group 2 and 2.15 (1.95–2.32) mm in group 3 (p < 0.001). Linear regression analysis performed with the parameters significantly correlated with Elabela in group 3 showed that DEXA and AIM levels were independently associated with Elabela. In the multivariate logistic regression analysis performed for the independent determination of patients with surgical indication in the patient group, it was found that Elabela level independently determined the group with surgical indication. According to this analysis, each 1 unit increase in Elabela level increased the probability of surgery by 30.9% in the patient group.ConclusionIn patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, serum Elabela level may be increased due to activation of the Apelinergic system in response to adverse vascular and cardiac effects that may be caused by increased parathormone. Elabela level can be used to determine the indication for surgery in PHPT patients.Keywords: Primary hyperparathyroidism, Apelin, Serum elabela level, Aortic intima media thickness

Introduction

Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is a clinical condition defined by hypercalcaemia resulting from the overproduction of parathyroid hormone (PTH) by the parathyroid glands. PHPT has been recognised as the predominant aetiology of hypercalcemia in the general populace. This syndrome often manifests beyond the age of 40, with increased frequency noted in postmenopausal women [1, 2]. A significant number of studies have documented a heightened occurrence of cardiovascular mortality in individuals diagnosed with symptomatic PHPT [3]. Primary hyperparathyroidism has been shown to correlate with several medical issues, including hypertension, alterations in the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, cardiac arrhythmias, and both structural and functional modifications in the vascular system [4].

The significance of the apelin system in cardiovascular physiological functions is well-established. The chemical is hypothesised to have both favourable inotropic and cardioprotective properties. Moreover, the apelin system plays a crucial role in vasoconstriction and the control of arterial blood pressure [5]. Serum Elabela levels may be increased in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism, resulting from the activation of the parathyroid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP) system in response to harmful vascular and cardiac consequences caused by excessive parathormone levels. The Elabela level has been recognised as a crucial predictor of cardiovascular problems in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism. Despite the recognised association between bioactive peptides from the apelin system and illnesses such ischaemic heart disease, subclinical atherosclerosis, and heart failure, there is a lack of data especially concerning primary hyperparathyroidism. This study aimed to examine changes in serum elabela levels, a new apelin-derived peptide, in individuals recently diagnosed with primary hyperparathyroidism. The experiment aimed to ascertain cardiac involvement in patients with and without surgical indications and to analyse its link with aortic intima-media thickness.

Materıal and methods

Study population

The research was performed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki for Biomedical Research with Human Subjects, and the protocol received approval from the institutional ethics committee. Approved by the Ethics Committee of Adana City Training and Research Hospital under decision no. 2442. In the intervention study, the effect size was moderate when α = 0.05, power (1-β) = 0.80, and the smallest sample size for each group was calculated as at least 40 and 20 for surgical and non-surgical patient groups using the G-Power program [6]. The study was done prospectively cross-sectionel including 45 patients diagnosed with primary hyperparathyroidism, who were admitted to the outpatient clinics of the internal medicine department at Adana City Training and Research Hospital. Forty individuals with demographic features analogous to those of the patients were incorporated as a healthy control group. Patients who did not volunteer, those under 18 years of age, individuals over 75 years of age, and patients with liver, heart, endocrine, nephrologic, pulmonary, inflammatory, haematologic, rheumatologic diseases, obesity, malignancy, or who are pregnant were excluded from the study. Additionally, smoking, alcohol consumption, use of drugs thought to alter the level of elabela in the body (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, etc.) and hormone treatments (steroids, etc.) were considered as exclusion criteria. All patients received a comprehensive explanation of the voluntary consent forms, and those who desired to participate were incorporated into the research following the acquisition of signed informed consent. A comprehensive medical history was acquired, and a thorough physical examination was performed. The patient’s age and sex were recorded. The weight and height of all patients were recorded, followed by the calculation of the body mass index (BMI). In asymptomatic patients with pHPT, surgery was indicated in the presence of at least one of the following criteria: (i) an increase in serum calcium > 1 mg/dL above the upper limit of normal., (ii) significant hypercalciuria (> 400 mg/24 h), (iii) lumbar vertebrae (L1-L4) in DEXA the T score is <- 2.5.

Laboratory measurements

Blood samples were collected from the study participants, with their permission acquired between 08:00 and 10:00 AM following an 8-hour fasting period. A thorough set of diagnostic tests was performed, including hemogram, biochemistry, PTH analysis, vitamin D evaluation, urine calcium analysis, and DEXA. Body All patients had got bone mineral density scans of their lateral lumbar vertebrae (L1-L4) by DEXA (Lunar Prodigy; General Electric Medical Systems; WI, USA). The analysis of each scan was performed by the experienced radiology specialist, manually for the lumbar spine for the consistent placing of the intervertebral spaces. Furthermore, Elabela and aortic intima media (AIM) thickness were quantified. Blood samples collected from patients were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min using a Nüve NF 800R centrifuge to evaluate the serum elabela concentration. The prepared sera were preserved at −80 °C. During the investigation, the sera were transferred to the laboratory under cold chain conditions. Before the trial began, the sera were temperature-adjusted for compatibility and thereafter processed according to the kit package insert instructions. The quantification of blood serum elabela levels was performed using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits produced by Shanghai Sunred Biological Technology Co. Limited The ELISAs were evaluated utilising an automated ELISA reader (Thermo Scientific, Finland) and a software application (Scanlt for Multiscan FC2.5.1). The assay’s sensitivity was established at 0.118 ng/ml, with a range of 0.15 ng/ml to 40 ng/ml, as specified by the manufacturer’s guidelines. The intra-assay %CV was determined to be below 10%, while the inter-assay %CV was ascertained to be below 12%. The data were presented in ng/ml.

Aortıc intima media measurement

The abdominal aorta was assessed using a high-resolution Doppler ultrasound system (Philips EPIQ 7) operating at frequencies of 12 and 5 MHz. For the IMC of the abdominal aorta, imaging of the distal 1 cm segment was conducted from the far wall with the patient in a supine position after an 8-hour fasting period. In a B-mode examination of the normal carotid artery wall, two parallel echogenic lines are observed, with a hypoechoic or anechoic area between them. The first echogenic line adjacent to the vessel lumen corresponds to the intima-media layer, while the second echogenic line corresponds to the media-adventitia boundary. The distance between these lines indicates the intima-media thickness.

Statıstıcal method

All analyses were conducted utilising the statistical software program SPSS 22.0 (Chicago, IL, USA). The variables were categorised into two groups: categorical and continuous. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was utilised to assess the distribution of continuous variables and determine their normality. Continuous variables exhibiting a normal distribution were analysed using the Student’s t-test and ANOVA, whereas the Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis test were employed for samples with aberrant distribution. Normally distributed continuous data were presented as the mean ± SD, whereas abnormally distributed variables were presented as the median (interquartile range). The Chi-Square test was employed to compare categorical data. Univariate correlation analysis was conducted utilising Pearson and Spearman’s correlation methods to identify the characteristics linked with Elabela in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism indicated for surgical intervention. Statistically significant factors were integrated into the multivariate model, and Linear Regression Analysis was performed with these parameters. Independent variables influencing the label were discovered. In the univariate study for the independent identification of PHPT patients requiring surgical intervention, multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted using statistically significant parameters with a p-value < 0.05. ROC curve analysis was conducted to reassess the indicators that serve as independent variables in identifying PHPT patients eligible for surgical intervention and to establish the threshold values of these markers. A parameter with an area under the curve (AUROC) above 0.70 was discovered. The threshold value of this measure that would yield best sensitivity and specificity in diagnosing PHPT with a recommendation for surgical intervention was established. ROC curve analysis was utilised to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of Elabela in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism who were eligible for surgical treatment. Statistical significance was established at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Serum Elabela levels may be increased in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, resulting from the activation of the PTHrP system due to the adverse vascular and cardiac effects associated with elevated parathormone levels. The Elabela level has been recognised as a crucial predictor of cardiovascular problems in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism. A literature review indicates studies that have shown an elevated risk of cardiovascular disease in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism, as assessed with radiological study of aortic intima media thickness (AIMT). The abdominal aorta is exposed to shear stress due to its size and branching, and according to studies performed, is the first vascular site to develop atherosclerosis. Nevertheless, there is a scarcity of data illustrating the effectiveness of laboratory tests like Elabela in forecasting the risk of cardiovascular disease. No studies have yet been published that examine serum Elabela levels in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, hence the data given in this study cannot be compared with other research findings. A multitude of criteria have been assessed in research examining the impacts of the apelin system and associated peptides on the cardiovascular system. Recent research have demonstrated that the apelinergic system can mitigate illnesses associated with cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis, such as hypertension, heart failure, and myocardial infarction [7]. It is posited that these beneficial and cardioprotective effects are therapeutic in nature [8].

Buyuksımsek et al. conducted a research comparing Elabela levels in 74 hyperthyroid patients and 20 healthy controls. During the study, patients with hyperthyroidism were categorised into two groups: one comprising individuals with hypertension and the other consisting of those without hypertension. Elabela levels were statistically considerably elevated in the hypertension group compared to both the non-hypertensive group and the healthy control group. Serum elabela levels have been hypothesised to function as an early indicator of hypertension development due to the detrimental vascular consequences of hyperthyroidism [9]. A research by Sumbul et al. evaluated elabela levels between 50 patients diagnosed with acromegaly and 50 healthy controls. Individuals having a history of chronic renal disease, heart failure, familial hyperlipidaemia, valvular heart disease, and nephrectomy were excluded from the research. Patients diagnosed with acromegaly had statistically significant increases in blood pressure, serum glucose, Hs-CRP, NT-proBNP, IGF-1, GH, and elabela levels as compared to a healthy control group. Simultaneously, left ventricular ejection fraction was assessed and identified as having a strong negative correlation with Elabela. In this case, it was suggested that elevated levels of IGF-1 and increased activity within the apelin system may impact the slowing of harmful cardiovascular processes. The increase in AIMT and Elabela levels in patients diagnosed with PHPT who are considered suitable for surgical intervention indicates the possibility of underlying cardiac involvement in these people. Gulumsek et al. did a research analysing thiol disulphide alterations in 46 individuals with asymptomatic primary hyperparathyroidism and 40 healthy controls. Patients with primary hyperparathyroidism were categorised into two groups: those with a surgical indication and those without. In the population with surgical indications, higher serum and urine calcium levels were noted, accompanied with reduced Nativ Thiol levels and DEXA T scores. It has been postulated that increased oxidative stress in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism necessitating surgical intervention may cause a reduction in native thiol levels and perhaps result in a drop in bone mineral density [10]. The current investigation has shown that the rationale for surgery in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism may be more effectively illustrated through the impact of oxidative stress markers [11].

Hyperthyroidism and acromegaly are endocrine disorders that can present with multisystem involvement, similar to PHPT. Cardiac involvement may also manifest in these disorders; hence, the elevation of Elabela in these patients prompted us to hypothesise that there may be an increase in PHPT patients attributable to cardiac involvement. Elabela was considerably elevated in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism, serving as an early diagnostic and prognostic indication. This study is the first in the existing literature to predict the risk of cardiovascular disease based on serum elabela levels in individuals with PHPT. This study asserts that cardiovascular involvement in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism indicates the possible use of Elabela as a surgical criterion. Gulumsek et al. undertook a research to examine the correlation between serum Elabela levels in 43 patients with ulcerative colitis and 40 healthy controls. The endoscopic activity index for patients with Ulcerative Colitis was assessed, categorising patients into remission (Group-II) and active (Group-III) groups. A statistically significant difference was seen in Group-III when comparing serum Elabela levels (p < 0.001). In univariate analysis, serum Elabela levels exhibited a positive correlation with EAI, leukocyte, and hs-CRP levels, while demonstrating a negative correlation with haemoglobin levels (p < 0.001 for each). In conclusion, serum Elabela levels were elevated in patients with active ulcerative colitis compared to healthy controls and those with ulcerative colitis in remission. Moreover, a significant and strong correlation was seen between serum Elabela levels and hs-CRP levels, as well as endoscopic activity index. This data may suggest activation of the serotonergic system, potentially benefiting inflammation, cardiovascular disease, and the immune system in individuals with ulcerative colitis [12]. The current investigation revealed that Elabela elevated in both cardiovascular involvement and inflammatory situations. It is hypothesised that microvascular inflammation in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism may lead to cardiovascular involvement and, subsequently, an elevation in Elabela levels.

A research conducted by Sumbul et al. comprised 65 patients with PHPT and 30 healthy individuals. The patients diagnosed with primary hyperparathyroidism were categorised into two groups: one group included patients with a surgical indication, while the other group included patients without a surgical justification. In individuals with PHPT, a notable elevation in aortic intra-abdominal muscle mass was noted, although no corresponding rise in carotid intima media thickness was seen. AIMT was statistically substantially elevated in both the group with surgical indication and the group without surgical indication when compared to the healthy group, as well as in the group with surgical indication relative to the group without surgical indication [13]. Thus, we posited that AIMT, in contrast to carotid intima media thickness, might provide a more effective method for predicting cardiovascular involvement in individuals with PHPT. In accordance with our study’s findings, this research also indicated that AIMT levels were increased in PHPT patients requiring surgical intervention. The duration of persistent cardiovascular risk remains unclear. It is advisable to develop new research targeting the surgical patient cohort. The current study by Yigitdol et al. aimed to compare serum periostin levels with T scores obtained from DEXA in a group of 40 patients newly diagnosed with PHPT without comorbidities, alongside 30 healthy controls. Serum periostin concentrations were substantially higher in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism compared to healthy controls (p < 0.001). Serum periostin levels were considerably elevated in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism and osteoporosis compared to those without osteoporosis (mean 59.7 ± 11.0 ng/mL) (p = 0.004). In logistic regression analysis, serum periostin levels emerged as the exclusive independent predictor of osteoporosis in patients. The current investigation demonstrates that a 1 ng/mL increase in serum periostin correlates with a 20.6% heightened risk of osteoporosis. Multivariate regression analysis indicated a negative connection between serum periostin levels and L1-L4 T scores obtained from DEXA scans. This study is the first investigation demonstrating that blood periostin levels are elevated in individuals with PHPT compared to those without, and that a substantial correlation exists between serum periostin levels and T scores on DEXA in PHPT patients. The findings were hypothesised to enhance the diagnosis of osteoporosis in patients with PHPT and aid in assessing the need for surgical intervention, hence eliminating the demand for DEXA imaging, which involves radiation [14]. Our study conducted a correlation analysis between Elabela and several demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics in the surgical indication group. The measures connected with Elabela include DEXA, 24-hour urine calcium level, AIMT, and serum calcium level. Linear regression analysis was conducted utilising the factors that shown a significant association with Elabela. DEXA and AIMT levels were independently correlated with Elabela. Our study provides the inaugural demonstration of a strong association between Elabela levels and T scores on DEXA. This discovery indicates a possible correlation between elevated Elabela levels in individuals with primary hyperparathyroidism and cardiac involvement in osteoporosis. The aetiology of Elabela elevation is ambiguous, with the possible contributions of osteoporosis and cardiac involvement remaining unknown. Nonetheless, there is a lack of study investigating Elabela levels in individuals with osteoporosis who do not have a concomitant diagnosis of PHPT. Additional research on this topic is necessary. In our study, we found that Elabela and AIMT levels were higher in patients diagnosed with PHPT than in patients without PHPT, and that this difference was statistically significant. In the analysis, Elabela level was found to independently determine the surgical group. Inspired by studies showing a reduction in cardiovascular events in the surgical group, we believe that serum Elabela levels may be an indication for surgery.

Limitations

The primary drawback of our study is that it was a single-centre, cross-sectional investigation involving a restricted patient cohort. Notwithstanding the importance of our study’s findings, the patient sample size was inadequate. This study suggests that more significant findings may have been achieved if cardiac indicators, disease duration, and follow-up of AIMT and serum Elabela levels in the surgery group had been assessed. A comparison with the cohort without PHPT but possessing osteoporosis would have produced more thorough results. Further studies utilising the same methods as this research are encouraged to assess cardiac indicators, disease duration, and tissue samples.

Conclusion

In the present study, elevated levels of Elabela and AIMT were observed in patients diagnosed with PHPT, as compared to patients without PHPT. Serum Elabela level may be elevated in patients diagnosed with primary hyperparathyroidism, a consequence of the activation of the PTHrP system in response to deleterious vascular and cardiac effects that may be occasioned by elevated parathormone levels. The Elabela level can be used to determine the indication for surgery in PHPT patients.

Editorial: introducing special collection on cancer and cardiovascular disorders
Authors: Moez Karim Aziz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis editorial introduces a special collection on cancer and cardiovascular disorders. Over the past 25 years, the link between cancer and cardiovascular disease has been increasingly acknowledged. As such, we present a special collection of articles offering novel insights into how cancer pathogenesis and therapy can lead to cardiovascular morbidity. Ultimately, the cases in this collection underscore the value of an interdisciplinary team when caring for patients with complex cardio-oncologic pathology and the importance of frequent follow up in the clinical setting.

Main Text

Over the past 25 years, the interplay between cancer and cardiovascular disorders has become increasingly recognized. In fact, approximately 30% of cancer patients have comorbid cardiovascular disease, and 10% of percutaneous coronary interventions occur in cancer patients [1–2]. This newfound recognition has led to the field of cardio-oncology, which studies cardiovascular effects of oncologic pathogenesis and therapy. In September 2009, the International Cardio-Oncology Society (IC-OS) was formed, and the first formal definition of cardiotoxicity was published in December 2021 [3]. This special collection presents multiple manuscripts addressing various aspects of this field. These studies offer novel insights into how cancer pathogenesis and therapy can lead to cardiovascular morbidity.

While we often think of doxorubicin causing irreversible dilated cardiomyopathy and heart failure, Imadoglu et al. present a case report in our collection detailing how adjuvant doxorubicin-cyclophosphamide therapy caused subacute inferolateral myocardial infarction from coronary artery thrombosis [4]. Interestingly, there was still a cardiomyopathy with an ejection fraction of 38%, but the mechanism suggests that doxorubicin may contribute to coronary inflammation in addition to non-ischemic cardiomyopathy.

Cardiac myxomas are benign masses often encountered in cardio-oncology, and Yao et al. present a case series of hereditary cardiac myxoma secondary to a PRKAR1A mutation [5]. In case 1 of the study, it is even possible that the patient’s cerebral infarction may have been secondary to embolism from the myxoma. The PRKAR1A mutation is responsible for approximately 80% of familial cardiac myxomas based on a case series of 24 patients [6]; as more patients with these myxomas are identified, our hope is that we will be able to more effectively pinpoint specific germline mutations responsible for these masses.

Our collection also addresses the more malignant cardiac angiosarcoma; Wang et al. present a case report and review of the literature of a cardiac angiosarcoma in a pregnant patient [7]. In the manuscript, the authors detail some key strategies to deal with this complex case, specifically the need for an interdisciplinary team involving cardiology, obstetrics, cardiothoracic surgery, radiation and medical oncology, as well as other specialties. Postoperative radiation and chemotherapy may also prove helpful, although more robust data are still needed to further elucidate the impact of these interventions.

The collection also reinforces a crucial theme for the oncologic patient that remains true in the presence of cardiovascular disease: that nutritional and inflammatory status serve as valuable prognostic markers. Zhang et al. present a salient exhibit of this paradigm in lung cancer survivors [8]. Here, the advanced lung cancer inflammation index (ALI) was calculated using body mass index (BMI), albumin, and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio. The BMI and albumin both serve as valuable markers of nutritional status, which is factored into their calculation.

Finally, Tian et al. present a retrospective study describing overall trends in patients treated for multiple myeloma, noting an uptick in cardiotoxicity contributing to morbidity [9]. The study notes that heart failure, thromboembolism, hypertension, and arrhythmias were of noteworthy incidence; these phenomena are four of the five metrics of the most recent cardiotoxicity definition published by the International Cardio-Oncology Society [3].

While this collection does not provide an exhaustive account of every new development in cardio-oncology, it certainly provides novel cases and evidence encouraging further research in this nascent and burgeoning field. Our hope is that readers will appreciate the value of a multidisciplinary team when caring for patients with complex cardio-oncologic pathology. In addition, the value of frequent follow up for cardiotoxic chemotherapy, consistent definitions to evaluate cardiotoxicity, and adequate nutrition for oncologic treatment cannot be overstated. We look forward to your engagement with our collection.

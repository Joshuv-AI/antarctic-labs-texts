Validation of proposed cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular grading criteria for IDH-mutant astrocytoma
Authors: Timothy E Richardson, Jorge Samanamud, Michael Christian Virata, Ema Mir, Shrishtee Kandoi, Cheyanne C Slocum, Diana D Shi, Milan R Savani, Raquel T Yokoda, Sachein Sharma, Raymund L Yong, Joshua B Bederson, Thenzing J Silva-Hurtado, Phuong Nguyen, Satomi Hiya, Carolina Maldonado-Díaz, Kevin Clare, Meenakshi Vij, Yurika Nishikawa, Melissa Umphlett, Kimmo J Hatanpaa, Steven Brem, Mariano S Viapiano, Matija Snuderl, Dolores Hambardzumyan, Jamie M Walker, Kalil G Abdullah, Samuel K McBrayer, Nadejda M Tsankova
Published: 2026

Since 2016, diffusely infiltrating gliomas in adults have been subcategorized based on molecular features into astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma, and glioblastoma [12]. Grading of these tumors was based on histopathologic features alone until molecular grading criteria were introduced by the Consortium to Inform Molecular and Practical Approaches to CNS Tumor Taxonomy-Not Official WHO (cIMPACT-NOW) [1, 2], followed by their official incorporation into the 5th edition of the WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System in 2021 (2021 CNS WHO) [13]. For IDH-mutant astrocytoma, homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B was included as a grade 4 molecular feature, equivalent to microvascular proliferation (MVP) and necrosis.

Subsequently, numerous reports identified additional features with prognostic potential in IDH-mutant astrocytomas [9, 17, 20], which were consolidated into recommendations for grade 3 and grade 4 features in the cIMPACT-NOW update 12 [3]. For grade 3, these recommendations include ≥3 mitotic figures in 2.4 mm2, G-CIMP-low or A_IDH_HG DNA methylation signatures, amplification of CDK4, CDK6, CCND2, EGFR, or MYCN, homozygous deletion of RB1, and/or pathogenic mutation of RB1, CDKN2A, PIK3CA, or PIK3R1. For grade 4, these recommendations include the presence of MVP, necrosis, homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion, and/or PDGFRA amplification (Supplemental Table S1). However, the ability for these criteria to effectively stratify prognoses has not yet been reported.

To evaluate these recommended molecular grading criteria, we utilized a combined cohort of 1140 IDH-mutant astrocytomas (Fig. 1a), sourced from publicly available datasets including the American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) Genomics Evidence Neoplasia Information Exchange (GENIE) [6, 19] and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) [4], as well as additional cases from Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC) not included in the GENIE dataset [10, 21]. Clinical, pathologic, and molecular data were downloaded from cBioPortal [5, 8] and analyzed using previously described methods [7, 16, 18, 20] to assess the effects of the proposed cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular features on the outcome of patients with IDH-mutant astrocytomas compared to the previous 2016 CNS WHO and 2021 CNS WHO systems (Supplementary Methods).

Forty IDH-mutant astrocytomas which were previously called grade 2 (7.9% of grade 2 tumors, 3.5% of the total cohort) and 45 astrocytomas previously called grade 3 (12.2% of grade 3 tumors, 3.9% of the total cohort) under the 2016 grading system were reclassified in the 2021 CNS WHO as grade 4 based on the presence of homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion (Fig. 1b). While this reclassification did not statistically alter the overall survival (OS) measurements within each grade (Supplemental Figure S1), 85 cases (7.5% of the total cohort) were recategorized as grade 4, improving the prognostic ability not only for these affected tumors, but for the remaining grade 2 and 3 cases as well. Considering the molecular recommendations of the new cIMPACT-NOW update 12, an additional 7 cases were upgraded from grade 2 to 4 (1.5% of grade 2 tumors, 0.6% of the total cohort) and 15 cases were upgraded from grade 3 to 4 (4.6% of grade 3 tumors, 1.3% of the total cohort), based on the presence of PDGFRA amplification. More significantly, 77 cases which were considered grade 2 in the 2021 CNS WHO grading system met the new molecular criteria for grade 3 (16.6% of grade 2 tumors, 6.8% of the total cohort), based on a wide array of alterations (Fig. 1b–d). Notably, methylation data was only available for tumors sourced from TCGA (260 cases), precluding inclusion of this factor in the majority of cases, however this is consistent with the way these guidelines will likely be implemented in the clinical setting, as methylation profiling is often not incorporated into routine IDH-mutant astrocytoma diagnosis. This study is also limited by our combination of multiple cohorts from different institutions and diagnostic eras and the lack of direct histopathologic confirmation of mitotic figure density (which could result in previous grade 3 cases with sparse mitotic activity being downgraded).

There were no significant differences in terms of outcome within grades between the 2021 CNS WHO and cIMPACT-NOW update 12 (Supplemental Figure S1). Cases which were upgraded from grade 2 to 3 using cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular features (median OS 109.6 months) had significantly worse outcomes compared to those which remained classified as grade 2 (median OS 146.0 months; hazard ratio [HR] 2.6; p < 0.0001). Cases which were reclassified as grade 4 based on the presence of PDGFRA amplification from either grade 2 or grade 3 (median OS 51.4 months) had significantly worse outcomes compared to those which remained classified as grade 2 (HR 4.0; p < 0.0001) or grade 3 (median OS 126.2 months; HR 1.9; p = 0.0284). No difference was identified between cases which remained classified as grade 3 and those which were upgraded from grade 2 to 3 (p = 0.2954), or between cases which remained classified as grade 4 and those which were upgraded from grade 2 or 3 (p = 0.1277) using cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular features (Fig. 1e and Supplemental Figure S2–3).

Based on these cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular criteria, the reclassified grade 2 cases (median OS 146.0 months) have significantly better OS than those reclassified as grade 3 (median OS 120.4 months; HR 2.1 ; p < 0.0001) or grade 4 (median OS 37.7 months; HR 5.5 ; p < 0.0001), and those reclassified as grade 3 have significantly better OS than those reclassified as grade 4 (HR 2.7; p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1f and Supplemental Table S2). These results suggest that the proposed cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular features provide improved and robust prognostic power compared to previous WHO diagnostic and grading systems based on large clinical datasets. Importantly, 12.5% of cases (99/789) which were classified as either grade 2 or 3 in the 2021 CNS WHO were molecularly inconsistent with grade 2 or grade 3 and therefore upgraded using this new set of cIMPACT-NOW update 12 molecular criteria, which not only aids in prognostication, but better classifies patients for biologically-tailored therapy and stratification for clinical trials [11, 14, 15]. This represents a significant step forward in classification of these tumors, with more emphasis placed on the underlying molecular tumor features.

Dissecting oral premalignant carcinogenesis: spatial omics mechanisms and nanomedicine-driven therapeutic innovation
Authors: Xinjia Cai, Yingying Cui, Chuanyang Ding, Yan Liu, Tiejun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Oral potentially malignant disorder (OPMD) remains a critical clinical challenge for cancer interception. However, the progression of OPMD toward oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is driven by profound cellular heterogeneity and dynamic microenvironmental remodeling. The mechanisms underlying these processes are not yet fully understood. Recent advancements in single-cell and spatial omics have facilitated high-resolution decoding of the precancerous landscape, unveiling multistep epithelial cell plasticity, fibroblast heterogeneity with extracellular matrix remodeling, immune suppression, and inflammatory reprogramming, as well as coupled metabolic and redox alterations that govern malignant transformation. These insights into the cellular mechanisms have led to a paradigm shift in the understanding of OPMD, reclassifying it as an ecosystem-level disease rather than a purely epithelial pathology. Nanomedicine is a potent platform for translating mechanistic knowledge into precision diagnostics and interventions at the precancerous stage. Nanomaterial-based strategies have been demonstrated to facilitate several critical processes, including enabling early lesion visualization and risk stratification, immune microenvironment reactivation, anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory remodeling, and targeted regulation of metabolic and oxidative stress pathways. A mounting body of evidence from preclinical and clinical studies lends support to the notion that nanotechnology-assisted early detection, microenvironmental reprogramming, and the interception of malignant transformation across oral and other precancerous conditions are indeed feasible. This review integrates single-cell–resolved mechanisms of OPMD progression with state-of-the-art nanomedicine-based diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, highlighting convergent biological axes and translational opportunities. By integrating single-cell biology with nanotechnology-driven precision medicine, this work is expected to improve the development of a nanomedicine framework for early cancer detection and treatment and outline future directions and challenges toward clinical implementation.

Introduction

Oral cancer remains one of the most prevalent tumors worldwide, with approximately 317,500 cases reported annually [1]. Among these, oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) constitutes the predominant pathological subtype. Despite advances in therapeutic approaches, the incidence of OSCC continues to rise, while the overall survival rate remains approximately 50% [2]. Once the disease progresses to an advanced stage with distant metastasis, survival outcomes further decline significantly [3], severely affecting patients’ quality of life and long-term prognosis. The development of OSCC typically evolves through a precancerous stage, progressing from normal mucosa to oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs), such as oral leukoplakia (OLK), proliferative verrucous leukoplakia (PVL), and oral submucous fibrosis (OSF) [4]. OLK is the most common OPMD, typically presenting as a white patch that cannot be scraped off and cannot be attributed to any other definable lesion; it carries a variable but well-established risk of malignant transformation [4]. PVL is a less common but more aggressive form of leukoplakia, characterized by a persistent, progressive, and multifocal nature, often exhibiting exophytic and verrucous morphology, and is associated with a significantly higher malignant transformation rate compared to OLK [5]. OSF is a chronic, progressive disease, predominantly seen in individuals with areca nut chewing habits, and is characterized by inflammation and progressive fibrosis of the submucosal tissues, leading to restricted mouth opening and a markedly elevated risk of oral cancer [6]. OPMDs present with histopathologically abnormal, non-invasive epithelial alterations, such as hyperkeratosis, dysplasia, or atrophy. OSCC is characterized by neoplastic epithelial cells invading the underlying connective tissue. Once epithelial lesions of OPMD breach the basement membrane, they can undergo malignant transformation to OSCC. OPMDs possess a well-defined malignant potential, with an overall transformation rate as high as 10 − 25% [7]. However, the rate and risk of progression vary considerably among individuals. Even among lesions with comparable histological grades of dysplasia, the actual risk of malignant transformation may differ substantially [8], suggesting that pathological grading alone is insufficient for accurately predicting the true progression risk of a lesion. High-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infection, such as HPV-16 and HPV-18, has also been found in the oral epithelial dysplasia, although its precise contribution relative to OPMDs remains investigation [9]. These clinical observations underscore the complexity of the malignant transformation mechanism in OPMDs: their development is not solely the result of cumulative genetic alterations within epithelial cells [10], but is also profoundly influenced by remodeling of the local microenvironment and functional dysregulation within the multicellular ecosystem [11]. The progression from OPMD to OSCC is a dynamic process involving epithelial cells, stromal components, and the immune system, rather than a unilinear event dominated solely by epithelial dysplasia in the traditional sense. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of the malignant transformation of OPMDs requires re-examining key oncogenic events within the broader context of multicellular interactions and microenvironmental dynamics. However, precancerous lesions are often challenging to study due to their small size and the difficulty in obtaining tissue samples. Fortunately, OSCC serves as an ideal disease model for studying precancerous progression, as it frequently coexists or occurs sequentially after OPMDs. The distinctive anatomical accessibility of the oral cavity affords OPMDs high visibility and obtainability, enabling comparative analysis of both precancerous and cancer tissues. Consequently, OSCC may represent one of the cancer subtypes most likely to benefit from research on precancerous lesions, and could serve as a paradigm for early detection and intervention strategies in other malignancies.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and its derivative technologies (such as spatial transcriptomics) have uncovered the cellular composition, state transitions, and interaction patterns of oral mucosa during malignant transformation with unprecedented resolution [12], establishing themselves as a cornerstone technological platform for deconstructing the developmental model of OPMD progression to OSCC. Multicenter genomics studies have revealed that malignant transformation in OPMDs occurs well before histologically detectable changes [13]. Cell subpopulations harboring premalignant signatures, including epithelial cells with enhanced stemness, increased epithelial plasticity, and partial epithelial–mesenchymal transition (pEMT) features, can be detected in OPMD lesions [14], alongside aberrant cell clusters exhibiting transcriptional profiles approximating those of OSCC. Furthermore, fibroblasts in OPMDs undergo early remodeling, adopting precursor states closely resembling cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), such as myofibroblasts (THY1+POSTN+ACTA2+), which gradually increase from normal tissue through OPMD to OSCC [15]. In terms of the immune microenvironment, scRNA-seq analyses have unveiled early-onset immunosuppression in OPMD lesions, marked by expansion of regulatory T cells (Tregs), early exhaustion of CD8+ T cells, impaired dendritic cell maturation, and enrichment of M2-polarized macrophages [15–17].

During the continuous evolution of OPMDs, conventional therapeutic approaches often fail to achieve precise intervention in the early-stage carcinogenic ecological network [7]. In recent years, diverse nanomaterials, including metal nanoparticles, polymeric nanoplatforms, and stimuli-responsive self-assembling materials, have garnered significant attention for applications in the microenvironment of precancerous lesions and early-stage tumors. These materials offer the advantages of tunable physicochemical properties, tissue- or cell-type-specific targeting capabilities, versatility in carrying multiple drugs or nucleic acids, and responsiveness to tumor-associated signals. As such, they can directly act on epithelial, stromal, and immune cells to achieve microenvironment remodeling, immunomodulation, suppression of fibroblast activation, or blockade of key ligand − receptor communication axes [18]. Some novel nanomaterials have demonstrated the ability to enhance immune activation, reverse T-cell exhaustion, improve antigen presentation, and synergize with existing immunotherapeutic strategies, thereby offering new technological avenues for halting the malignant transformation of OPMDs. As single-cell data continue to reveal an expanding landscape of targetable cellular subpopulations and signaling nodes, integrating nanomaterials with these refined mechanistic insights holds promise for developing a new generation of strategies characterized by interpretable mechanisms, identifiable targets, and precise intervention.

This review aims to systematically synthesize current evidence from single-cell multi-omics studies regarding the multicellular ecological remodeling mechanisms during OPMD-to-OSCC progression. We will focus on enhanced epithelial plasticity, fibroblast-to-CAF conversion, the immune microenvironment shift from inflammatory activation to immunosenescence and exclusion, and the interactive networks among these components. Furthermore, we aim to elucidate the mechanisms of OPMD transformation at single-cell resolution and discuss the translational potential of these insights for disease surveillance, risk stratification, early intervention, and precision therapy. Additionally, we will explore the emerging application potential of nanomaterials in halting malignant transformation and evaluate microenvironment-targeting strategies that can be implemented using nano-platforms.

Single-cell sequencing reveals multi-layered mechanisms of carcinogenesis in OPMD epithelial cells

Single-cell sequencing analyses demonstrate that OPMD epithelial cells exhibit prototypical malignant phenotypes at early stages, including enhanced proliferation, attenuated DNA damage repair checkpoints, and upregulation of oncogenes. Basal epithelial cells display elevated mechanistic target of rapamycin kinase (mTOR) signaling activity, which suppresses apoptosis, while upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA), VEGFB, transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β), and platelet-derived growth factor subunit A (PDGFA) confers pro-tumorigenic functions such as angiogenesis promotion, immune suppression, and fibroblast activation [19]. Single-cell metabolic profiling indicates that during malignant transformation from OPMD to OSCC, metabolic activity peaks in malignant epithelial cells, with significant early enhancement of glucose and amino acid metabolism. However, metabolic stagnation occurs in advanced and recurrent stages, characterized by a lack of further upregulation in glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and pyruvate metabolism. Glycolysis levels correlate positively with pEMT, invasion, and metastasis signatures, whereas the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle shows strengthened associations with proliferative traits, suggesting distinct carbon metabolic pathways drive specific malignant phenotypes. A glycolysis-high subpopulation exhibits stronger cell adhesion, extracellular matrix (ECM) interactions, and invasive signaling, alongside more active crosstalk with endothelial cells and T cells, reflecting the role of metabolic reprogramming in promoting epithelial invasion and microenvironmental communication [20].

Pseudotime trajectory analysis reveals that OPMD epithelial cells gradually shift from growth and keratinization programs toward two transcriptional differentiation paths: one enriched in cell cycle and metabolic genes, and the other enriched in interferon response genes. Both trajectories converge toward ECM remodeling and EMT as terminal states (Fig. 1). A subset of pEMT epithelial cells shows high spatial and transcriptional overlap with CAF subpopulation 1 (CAF1), displaying fibroblast-like phenotypes (Table 1). In contrast, CAF2 exhibits antigen-presenting and complement-related features, regulated by SPI1 and involved in immune microenvironment remodeling. Transcription factors FOS like 1 (FOSL1) and transcription factor 4 (TCF4) drive pEMT and CAF1-like gene programs, respectively, inducing expression of collagen type XVII α1 (COL17A1), annexin A1 (ANXA1), COL1A1, COL3A1, and cytoskeletal rearrangement, thereby establishing a carcinoma in situ (CIS) − pEMT − CAF malignant transformation pathway [14]. Functional annotation of major cell subpopulations

At the genomic and chromosomal levels, epithelial cells progressively accumulate extensive copy number variations (CNVs) from normal mucosa to OPMD and then to tumor stages, involving oncogenes such as epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), mTOR, cyclin D1 (CCND1), and MYC, as well as tumor suppressors like RB1, forming malignant clonal lineages. With increasing CNV burden, EMT, mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1), and FRA signaling are sequentially activated, epithelial stemness markers are enhanced, and interactions with stromal cells are significantly intensified [13, 25, 30, 31]. Early driving events in malignant transformation of OPMDs include loss and mutation of chromosome 9p (cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2 A, CDKN2A), TP53 mutation or 17p loss, and 3p loss, followed by loss of 9q and 8p and late-stage amplification of 3q (phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit α, PIK3CA). Combinatorial mutations in low-frequency driver genes such as F-box and WD repeat domain containing 7 (FBXW7), NOTCH1, and caspase 8 (CASP8) occur in over 60% of initial-stage malignant transformation, while whole-genome amplification events occur in advanced stages, exacerbating genomic instability and promoting cancer progression [32]. During OPMD malignant transformation, epithelial cells exhibit CNV-dependent transcriptional dysregulation of TP63 and ATP1B3, accompanied by amplifications of 1q, 3q, 8q, 20p, and 22q and loss of 3p, 10p, and 10q. Early activation of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), NF-κB, P53, PI3K, AKT, and mTOR pathways, as well as glycolysis- and apoptosis-related genes, occurs alongside expression of tumor marker genes (CXCL1, EFNA1, TM4SF1, ELF3, and keratin genes), establishing a CIS phenotype. Malignant cells can be classified into six subpopulations, among which CC1 exhibits pEMT and high galectin 7B (LGALS7B) expression, localizes at the invasive front, interacts with CAF paracrine signals, drives invasion and metastasis, and its abundance significantly correlates with poor prognosis [22]. Single-cell analysis further reveals that malignant epithelial cells can be categorized into an OPMD-specific Group 1 and a non-specific Group 2 characterized by overexpression of FOS, ATP1A1, and DUSP1 [23]. Approximately 40% of malignant epithelial cells are enriched in EMT programs, displaying pEMT states, while about 38% exhibit fetal or germ-like phenotypes, localizing in poorly differentiated branches and showing negative correlation with pEMT features, suggesting dynamic switching between pEMT and fetal-like states in tumor cells [23].

Integrated single-cell transcriptomic and spatial omics analyses in the progression from vocal cord leukoplakia (VCL) to laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) further validate these mechanisms, demonstrating that epithelial cells, as core units of malignant transformation, progressively accumulate CNVs, form subpopulations with malignant transcriptional programs, and dominate signal integration within the microenvironment [33]. Using 4-nitroquinoline-1-oxide (4-NQO) and arecoline to induce the OPMD animal model also reveals significant expansion of stem-like and keratinocyte populations, indicating that enhanced epithelial stemness and abnormal differentiation are key to malignant transformation [34]. Overall, carcinogenesis in OPMD epithelial cells involves multi-layered regulatory mechanisms encompassing signaling pathway activation, transcriptional remodeling, subpopulation formation, and genomic instability, providing a molecular basis for early intervention and precise targeting.

Fibroblast heterogeneity and extracellular matrix remodeling in OPMD malignant transformation

Integrated single-cell transcriptomic and spatial analyses demonstrate that the progression from OPMD to OSCC involves profound remodeling of the fibroblast lineage. These cells evolve from homeostatic supportive cells into a highly heterogeneous CAF network with defined pro-tumorigenic functions. Rather than representing a single fibroblast population, CAFs comprise multiple functionally distinct subtypes involved in ECM remodeling and antigen presentation (Table 1). Among these, myofibroblastic CAFs (myCAFs) are characterized by high alpha‐smooth muscle actin (ACTA2) and myosin light chain proteins (MYLK) expression and ECM remodeling phenotype [23]. Matrix CAFs (mCAFs) preferentially express matrix-related markers such as matrix metallopeptidase 1 (MMP1), MMP11, MMP14, functioning mainly in collagen metabolism [17]. Desmoplastic CAFs (dCAFs) highly express genes coding for collagen (COL1A1, COL7A1, COL10A1), MMPs, TGF-β signaling molecules and ECM related genes [24]. Antigen-presenting fibroblasts (ap-fibroblasts) overexpressed antigen presentation-associated genes such as HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1, HLA-DPB1 and HLA-DQA1 [23]. COMP-fibroblasts, named according to their high expression of cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP), exhibited functional characteristics and enrichment pathways that are analogous to those of mCAFs [23]. Fibroblast subclusters described in different datasets, such as CAF1 (represented by POSTN, FN1, COL1A1), CAF2 (represented by CXCL12, C3, PLA2G2A), CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, ASPN+ fibroblasts, and TDO2+ myofibroblasts [14, 15, 19, 27], could be interpreted as functionally specialized states within the broader CAF remodeling continuum rather than isolated cell populations.

Trajectory analysis reveals that a subset of fibroblasts, specifically ap-fibroblasts and COMP-fibroblasts, originates from an initial or inflammatory state, ultimately culminating in mCAFs. COMP-fibroblasts and mCAFs share collagen degradation and ECM metabolic programs, indicating that fibroblasts in OPMD lesions already possess significant CAF potential, laying the groundwork for subsequent stromal remodeling and malignant transformation [17]. During OPMD progression, ASPN+ fibroblasts undergo significant expansion. This subpopulation highly expresses various collagens (types I, III, IV, V, VI), elastin (ELN), and fibronectin 1 (FN1). Collagen cross-linking mediated by lysyl oxidase-like 1 (LOXL1) and LOXL2, alongside matrix degradation facilitated by MMP2 and MMP14, drives ECM stiffening, structural rearrangement, and restricts immune cell infiltration. Meanwhile, a secretory fibroblast subpopulation transiently enriches VEGFA and various pro-tumor inflammatory factors, enhancing inflammation and angiogenesis. However, this subpopulation gradually depletes in late stages, suggesting a functional shift in fibroblasts from inflammatory support towards structural stromal remodeling [19].

Fibroblasts associated with OPMD do not constitute a single continuous lineage but are composed of multiple functional subpopulations. RSPO1+ fibroblasts gradually decrease with disease progression. This subpopulation is enriched in protein folding and translation-related genes, exhibiting potential tumor-suppressive characteristics. In contrast, POSTN+ fibroblasts are consistently enriched, expressing numerous ECM remodeling and antigen-presentation related genes, and can be classified into myCAF, dCAF, and CAF1 lineages, participating in matrix stiffening and immune regulation (Fig. 2) [26]. At a finer resolution, eight fibroblast clusters exhibit distinct origins: CXCL14+ and PCOLCE2+ subpopulations are predominantly present in normal tissue, whereas CTHRC1+ and PCLAF+ subpopulations are enriched in tumors and metastatic lymph nodes, displaying pronounced malignant features. TINAGL1+ and RNASE1+ subpopulations frequently appear in OPMD stages, representing intermediate states in the transition from normal fibroblasts to CAFs [27]. Cell cycle analysis reveals that most cells in the PCLAF+ subpopulation are arrested in or progress through the S phase and G2/M phase, demonstrating a highly proliferative status characterized by active DNA replication and mitotic activity. Spatially, it co-localizes with regions of high proliferative signaling. This subpopulation activates mitotic regulation, oxidative phosphorylation, and pyrimidine metabolism pathways, demonstrating a clear bias towards mCAF and EMT-CAF phenotypes. From a biomechanical and signaling integration perspective, in the context of chronic injury, co-secretion of sonic hedgehog signaling molecule (SHH) by epithelial cells and CAFs activates the Hedgehog pathway. This promotes fibroblast differentiation into myofibroblasts and upregulates LOX − LOXL2-mediated collagen cross-linking, leading to ECM stiffening. Matrix stiffness enhances cellular tension via the RhoA–ROCK–MLC pathway, inducing nuclear translocation of YAP and GLI2. GLI2, which can be activated by non-canonical mechanical signals, drives epithelial proliferation, EMT, and invasion. It synergizes with TGF-β and integrin signaling to accelerate OPMD malignant transformation. Inhibition of ECM cross-linking or reduction of matrix stiffness can significantly delay this process [35].

Beyond traditional fibroblasts, the entire stromal lineage undergoes remodeling during OPMD progression. Adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) and fibroblasts polarize along a pseudotime trajectory towards TDO2+ myofibroblasts (MF-C1-TDO2). This subpopulation is nearly absent in normal tissue but emerges in OPMD and accumulates substantially in OSCC [15]. TDO2+ myofibroblasts highly express tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase (TDO2), indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), and CXCL9/10/11. They potently recruit CD4+ and CD8+ T cells via the CXCR3 signal, yet their spatial distribution is distant from tumor nests, thereby forming an immune barrier that blocks T-cell entry into the tumor core. Simultaneously, this subpopulation enhances tryptophan catabolism and activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathway, inducing T-cell exhaustion and Treg enrichment. It also promotes its own expansion through interactions with macrophages via interleukin-1 (IL-1) and Notch signaling. Functional experiments indicate that inhibiting TDO2 can reverse the immunosuppressive phenotype. Its enrichment level significantly correlates with poor prognosis, establishing it as a key immunometabolic node in the OPMD-to-OSCC transition [15].

Functionally, in OPMD, a fibroblast population dominated by Mesen_CAF highly expresses ECM and mesenchymal activation genes such as asporin (ASPN), periostin (POSTN), tenascin C (TNC), and twist family BHLH transcription factor 2 (TWIST2). It also activates non-canonical WNT and chemokine pathways including WNT5A, prostate transmembrane protein, androgen induced 1 (PMEPA1), APC down-regulated 1 (APCDD1), and C–C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), characteristics highly resembling the myCAF phenotype [25]. Spatially, this subpopulation resides closer to the epithelium. From the dysplasia stage, its secreted factors, fibroblast growth factor (FGF), TNC, and WNT5A, participate in epithelial proliferation, EMT, angiogenesis, and the PI3K–AKT–mTOR pathway. Concurrently, it regulates immune recruitment, CAF activation, and collagen remodeling via signals such as CSF1–CSF1R, TNC–α4β1, and TGF-β–TGF-βR. The cluster 2 of Mesen_CAF subpopulation, characterized by the expression of LOXL2, POSTN, TGFβ1, and TGFβ2, expands significantly during the OPMD-to-OSCC transition, exhibiting enhanced matrix generation and proliferative capacity [25]. Fibroblasts occupy a central position within the cellular communication network. They engage in extensive crosstalk with epithelial, endothelial, and myeloid cells through ligand-receptor pairs such as nuclear receptor subfamily 3 group C member 1 (NR3C1) and dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4). This interaction activates EMT, TNF-α, JAK–STAT, PI3K–AKT, hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α), and TGF-β signaling. Upregulation of MMP1/2, enhanced collagen I synthesis, and ECM densification further exacerbate the formation of an immune barrier [23, 28].

Further functional differentiation of CAFs reveals their hierarchical roles in invasion and immune regulation. In advanced stage OPMD malignant transformation, the CF1 subpopulation, characterized by overexpression of CXCL8, becomes the most active CAF type. Its abundance significantly correlates with the CC1 epithelial subpopulation. Through galectin-7-mediated positive feedback, it enhances IL-8 expression, promoting tumor invasion. Interactions between COL1A1 in CAFs and CD44 on malignant epithelial cells further potentiate cellular migration and invasiveness [22]. Senescent mesenchymal cells accumulate significantly in OPMD mucosa. Their high senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) activity and upregulated NFKB1/2 facilitate extensive communication with epithelial and various immune cells via collagen, laminin, and CXCL signals, driving chronic inflammation and potentially increasing malignant transformation risk [36].

Immunologically, fibroblasts derived from OPMD already exhibit typical CAF-like immunosuppressive features. They highly express fibroblast activation protein (FAP) and α-SMA and secrete inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, IL-1β, IL-23, and IL-33. Through the IL-6–JAK2–STAT3 pathway, they induce apoptosis and exhaustion of CD8+ T cells while promoting Treg and Th17 differentiation, forming a self-amplifying immunosuppressive circuit. In vivo blockade of IL-6R with tocilizumab significantly improves immune infiltration and inhibits the progression of OPMD to invasive carcinoma, highlighting the central role of IL-6 signaling in early stromal-immune remodeling [37]. In the transition from OSF to OSCC, immune cells primarily accumulate in the stromal region, forming complex ligand-receptor interaction networks with two distinct fibroblast subsets, designated CAF1 and CAF2. CAF1 is predominantly localized in the tumor region and invasive front, where its distribution highly overlaps with pEMT signals. Various collagen-integrin complexes drive the pEMT and CAF1-like phenotypic switch and construct an immune-exclusion barrier. Moreover, malignant epithelial cells at the tumor front may interact with CAF1-type cells through the COL17A1/α11β1 integrin ligand-receptor pair, thereby promoting malignant phenotype formation. In contrast, CAF2 is mainly present in OPMD and stromal regions and is primarily associated with immunomodulatory functions. CAF2 promotes immune cell recruitment via signals such as RARRES–CMKLR1 and TNFSF13B–HLA-DPB1, thereby facilitating immune response activation [14].

Dynamic remodeling of the immune cell lineage and formation of immunosuppressive microenvironment

Integrated single-cell transcriptomic and spatial omics studies demonstrate that the progression from OPMD to OSCC involves systematic remodeling of immune cell composition, functional states, and spatial distribution. A core feature of this process is the progressive attenuation of effector immune functions and the establishment of a multi-layered immunosuppressive network. Immune components, including T cells, myeloid cells, and B cells, exhibit aberrant differentiation trajectories already at the precancerous stage, which are continuously amplified and solidified during malignant transformation, forming a crucial microenvironmental foundation that supports tumorigenesis and immune evasion.

Sustained T-cell exhaustion and metabolic reprogramming constitute a central event in OPMD-related immune dysregulation. Multiple single-cell studies reveal that T cells evolve along a defined trajectory toward functional exhaustion during the transition from OPMD to OSCC, effector CD8+ T cells, Th17 cells, and memory CD4+ T cells progressively decrease, while exhausted CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and Tregs accumulate significantly (Fig. 3) [17, 26]. Pseudotime analyses further confirm that both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells ultimately differentiate toward terminal exhausted states, indicating that T-cell dysfunction is not an isolated event but a systemic remodeling that intensifies stepwise with malignant transformation [15, 17]. In OPMD and OSCC, exhausted CD8+ T cells with high expression of programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) are significantly enriched, accompanied by activation of various immune checkpoint pathways such as T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT), further compromising local anti-tumor immunity [28].Fig. 3Systematic immune reprogramming during malignant transformation of OPMD. Single-cell metabolic analyses reveal a close coupling between T-cell exhaustion and metabolic reprogramming. CD4+ T cells follow two distinct metabolic-associated developmental trajectories. Glycolysis/gluconeogenesis metabolism is enriched in the T follicular helper (Tfh)/Th1 branch, whereas oxidative phosphorylation is enriched in the Treg branch, revealing dynamic changes in metabolic patterns during CD4+ T cell phenotypic transitions. CD8+ T cells differentiate from an initial interferon-stimulated gene (ISG)–positive state into tissue-resident memory T cells (Trm) or Tex. The Tex subpopulation is enriched in glycolysis, pyruvate metabolism, and the pentose phosphate pathway, with its glycolytic levels positively correlating with various immunosuppressive receptors [20]. This metabolism-function coupling mechanism accelerates the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Regarding Tregs, tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 4 (TNFRSF4) − positive Tregs persist across all stages of malignant transformation, constituting approximately one-quarter of CD4+ T cells. They are enriched in immunosuppressive programs such as IL2–STAT5, cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA4), and TNFRSF9 signaling. Their ratio relative to CD8+ effector memory T cells (Tem) gradually increases, indicating sustained suppression of cytotoxic immunity [25]. Spatially, PD-1+CD8+ T cells and forkhead box P3 (FOXP3) − positive Tregs significantly cluster at the epithelial-stromal interface in OPMD. Together with widely distributed programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1)–overexpressed stromal cells, they construct a targeted immunosuppressive barrier, promoting the conversion of precancerous lesions into malignant phenotypes [38]. In laryngeal lesion models, Tregs in VCL retain some antiviral features but transition to a highly immunosuppressive state in LSCC, upregulating IL-10, TGF-β, and PD-L1 alongside enhanced metabolism [33]. Chemically induced models also show that during the progression of OPMD induced by 4-NQO and arecoline, immune regulatory signals such as KRAS, IL2–STAT5, and TNF-α–NF-κB are attenuated, further reflecting T-cell regulatory imbalance [34].

Continuous lineage remodeling and immunosuppressive polarization of macrophages constitute a second critical axis. Single-cell transcriptomics reveals that macrophages undergo continuous lineage remodeling from tissue-resident types to tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) during the OPMD-to-OSCC progression. Normal mucosa is dominated by CX3CR1+ and LYVE1+ resident macrophages. Transitional subpopulations such as IDO1+ macrophages and PLA2G2D+ macrophages appear in the OPMD stage, whereas highly immunosuppressive SPP1+ macrophages and PDPN+ macrophages subpopulations occupy a central position in OSCC [17]. Among these, IDO1+ macrophages is most enriched in OPMD, expressing interferon-γ (IFN-γ) response and antigen processing/presentation-related genes while displaying significant immunosuppressive characteristics and evolving toward TAM-like subpopulations along pseudotime trajectory. Its formation is driven by the IFN-γ–JAK–STAT1/IRF1/IRF7 pathway, and IFNG expression increases stepwise from normal mucosa to OPMD and OSCC, correlating positively with IDO1 [17]. However, the role of PLA2G2D+ macrophages in premalignant lesions remains to be elucidated.

Functional and metabolic analyses indicate that TAM-like macrophages (SPP1+, PDPN+) exhibit significant lipid and amino acid metabolic adaptations and highly express various immune checkpoint molecules [17, 26]. SPP1+ macrophages continuously expand during malignant transformation of OPMD, with carbon metabolic activity even surpassing that of tumor cells, particularly through highly activated fructose-mannose metabolism pathways. This metabolic signature negatively correlates with phagocytic, antigen-presenting, and immune-stimulatory functions. Fructose metabolism centered on ketohexokinase (KHK) drives their pro-tumor effects; inhibiting KHK significantly reduces the proportion of SPP1+ macrophages and restores CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell infiltration [20]. Additionally, FOLR2+ immunostimulatory macrophages decrease with malignant transformation, while TREM2+, APOE+, and NRG1+ macrophages accumulate, enriching M2 polarization and angiogenesis pathways, with their abundance correlating with poor prognosis [15, 25, 26].

Monocytes, B cells, and other immune cells synergistically amplify the immunosuppressive network. In the OPMD stage, Mono_INHBA, a classical CD14+CD16– monocyte with overexpression of inhibin subunit βA (INHBA), exhibited a monocytic myeloid-derived suppressor cell (M-MDSC)–like immunosuppressive phenotype, with high expression of PD-L1, IDO1, secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1), and thrombospondin 1 (THBS1) [25]. This subpopulation mutually recruits exhausted CD8+ T cells via CSF1–CSF1R signaling and reinforces immune escape through signals such as PD-L1–PD-1 and NECTIN2–TIGIT. Concurrently, it promotes ECM organization via INHBA–ACVR and SPP1–CD44 pathways with CAFs and directly stimulates epithelial proliferation and expression of malignant transformation-related genes [25, 39].

Regarding B cells, regulatory B cells (Bregs) gradually enrich during the precancerous stage and form a tight interaction network with IGFBP2high OEPCs in OPMD via TGF-β signaling. Bregs secrete TGF-β, while OEPCs upregulate TGFβR1/2 and co-express pEMT markers, suggesting that the Breg–OEPC interaction promotes malignant transformation by inducing pEMT. Longitudinal analysis shows that Breg-related genes significantly correlate with malignant transformation risk at the precancerous stage but decline in OSCC, reflecting their stage-specific functional heterogeneity [21]. In laryngeal lesions, Bregs derived from LSCC further activate signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), STAT4, TGF-β, and mitochondrial metabolic programs, enhancing their immunosuppressive capacity [33].

Dendritic cells (DCs) and neutrophils also contribute to constructing the immunosuppressive microenvironment. cDC_LAMP3 cells highly express PD-L1 during malignant transformation and drive CD8+ Tex differentiation via the PD-1–PD-L1 signal. Both conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) co-upregulate LGALS9 and CD86 during malignant transformation, enhancing inhibitory ligand-receptor interactions [15, 28]. Among neutrophils, VEGFA+ and PD-L1+ subpopulations progressively enrich, promoting angiogenesis and T-cell suppression, respectively. In LSCC, they form complex immune dysregulation networks with macrophages via C-X-C motif chemokine ligand (CXCL), CCL, TNF-α, and IL-6 signaling [15, 33]. In summary, single-cell studies reveal that OPMD malignant transformation is not a singular immunosuppressive event but a systemic immune reprogramming process driven by the interplay of T-cell exhaustion, immunosuppressive polarization of myeloid cells, and multi-immune cell interactions, providing a critical theoretical foundation for precancerous intervention and immune-targeted therapies.

Mature macrophages continuously secrete pro-tumorigenic factors, upregulate prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2) to promote prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) accumulation, while downregulating chemotactic and immunostimulatory functions. Simultaneously, the proportion of cDC1s significantly decreases, accompanied by a loss of effective antigen-presentation capacity, thereby weakening the initiation of adaptive immunity. Within the T-cell lineage, CXCL12 and TGF-β signaling drive the sustained enrichment of Tregs exhibiting a TIGIT − overexpression phenotype. Conversely, CD8+ T cells progressively enter an exhausted state, characterized by upregulation of PD-1 and lymphocyte-activating 3 (LAG3), alongside reduced IFN-γ expression. Although B-cell numbers increase during progression, the population of high-efficiency IgG-secreting plasma cells is significantly reduced, leading to insufficient humoral immune support. These changes establish an inhibitory microenvironment oriented towards immune evasion [19].

Cellular interactions and metabolic reprogramming

Single-cell and spatial omics analyses reveal that OPMD malignant transformation is driven by extensive crosstalk between epithelial, immune, and stromal cells, accompanied by coordinated metabolic reprogramming. During this process, immunosuppressive cell populations progressively expand, while glycolytic, lipid, polyamine, and purine metabolic pathways become markedly activated, promoting epithelial plasticity, immune evasion, and malignant transformation. As lesions advance from the precancerous to the progressive stage, monocytes gradually acquire enhanced capabilities for generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS), polarizing along pseudotime trajectories toward immunosuppressive macrophages [19].

The reconstruction of cellular interaction networks further consolidates the immunosuppressive and pro-tumorigenic ecosystem. Single-cell communication analyses reveal that the interaction strength between POSTN+ fibroblasts and SPP1+ macrophages continuously intensifies from early lesions to locally advanced stages, with their communication with tumor epithelial cells increasing in parallel. This signal forms a positive feedback loop via multiple ligand-receptor pathways, including epidermal growth factor (EGF), FGF, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), MMP9, FN1, and IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL1RN). POSTN+ fibroblasts remodel the metabolic program of SPP1+ macrophages, which in turn reinforce ECM remodeling, adhesion, and tension signaling in fibroblasts. Together, they drive the formation of a reactive stroma and promote tumor-supportive metabolic adaptation. This fibroblast − macrophage collaborative network transmits signals for cell cycle progression, cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) activation, and ECM–receptor interactions to tumor cells. Its establishment is significantly correlated with reduced CD8+ T-cell infiltration, constituting a key structural axis that sustains the pro-tumorigenic cellular ecosystem in advanced OSCC [26].

Single-cell and spatial multi-omics analyses at the metabolic level further reveal that immunosuppression and tumor progression are tightly coupled through profound metabolic reprogramming. During malignant transformation from OSF to OSCC, malignant epithelial regions exhibit significantly enhanced activities in glucose metabolism, lipid metabolism, as well as amino acid and polyamine metabolism (Fig. 4) [14]. Spatial metabolomics demonstrates decreased glucose content within tumor epithelium, whereas metabolic products of glucose (e.g., oxidized gluconic acid) and TCA cycle intermediates such as malate are significantly enriched, indicating accelerated glucose catabolism and increased energy flux driven by the Warburg effect, contributing to a localized acidic microenvironment [14]. In lipid metabolism, monounsaturated and essential fatty acids, including palmitoleic acid and linoleic acid, accumulate specifically in tumor areas, reflecting the fulfillment of high energy demands via fatty acid β-oxidation. Cholesterol sulfate is enriched in tumor regions, potentially participating in OSCC differentiation and immunosuppressive regulation. Phospholipid metabolism undergoes structural remodeling, phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) is upregulated in the adjacent epithelium and CIS regions, while phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylserine (PS) are enriched in the stroma and OSF areas, supporting membrane lipid synthesis required for rapid proliferation. Within amino acid metabolism, the polyamine pathway is particularly prominent. Aspartate, citrulline, arginine, spermidine, and spermine are significantly enriched in tumor regions. Key enzymes such as ornithine decarboxylase 1 (ODC1), spermidine synthase (SRM), and spermine synthase (SMS) exhibit spatially specific expression, constructing an abnormal polyamine metabolic network. Overall urea cycle activity remains low, with ornithine primarily replenished via the glutamate–proline metabolism pathway. Spermidine/spermine N1-acetyltransferase 1 (SAT1) mediates polyamine acetylation and promotes their excrete, while insufficient expression of polyamine oxidase (PAOX) limits recycling, leading to extracellular accumulation of acetylated polyamines. Polyamine metabolism is tightly coupled with methionine recycling. Beyond polyamine metabolism, systematic reprogramming of purine metabolism is progressively enhanced during malignant transformation. Spatial metabolomics shows that purine metabolites, including adenosine and AMP, continuously increase from normal mucosa through precancerous lesions to tumor stages. This is accompanied by upregulation of phosphoribosylaminoimidazole carboxylase and phosphoribosylaminoimidazolesuccinocarboxamide synthase (PAICS) and hypoxanthine phosphoribosyltransferase 1 (HPRT1), suggesting synergistic activation of both de novo and salvage branches of purine metabolism. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics further confirm elevated purine metabolism in malignant epithelial cells, significantly correlating with immunosuppressive stromal remodeling, including the enrichment of ACKR1+ endothelial cells, myofibroblasts, and C1QA+ macrophages. 5′-Nucleotidase ecto (NT5E) is aberrantly upregulated as a key regulatory node within this network. By modulating the adenosine pool and downstream metabolic networks, NT5E drives changes in EMT, ECM remodeling, cell adhesion, and chemotaxis pathways. It also reinforces interactions with immunosuppressive stroma and immune cells through ligand-receptor axes such as FN1–NT5E, TNC–ITGA5, and SERPINE1–PLAUR. Inhibition of NT5E significantly reduces tumor burden, restores infiltration of natural killer (NK) cells and effector CD8+ T cells, and decreases the proportion of exhausted T cells, highlighting the central role of purine metabolism in immune evasion [40].

Animal model studies provide additional dynamic support, during the progression of OPMD induced by combined 4-NQO and arecoline, MYC target genes and oxidative phosphorylation pathways are continuously activated alongside disease progression. This is accompanied by reprogramming of proteostasis and stress responses, driving cell proliferation and metabolic remodeling. Key MYC-associated genes, including RAN binding protein 1 (RANBP1), minichromosome maintenance complex component 5 (MCM5), and eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit B (EIF3B), are significantly upregulated and correlate with drug-resistant phenotypes. These findings suggest that MYC-driven stemness maintenance and metabolic reprogramming synergistically promote the transformation of precancerous lesions into invasive tumors [34].

The substantial advances achieved by single-cell transcriptomics in decoding the cellular heterogeneity and evolutionary trajectories of premalignant lesions, each omics modality still possesses intrinsic limitations. scRNA-seq provides high-resolution transcriptional profiling but lacks precise spatial context, whereas spatial transcriptomics preserves tissue architecture while often sacrificing sequencing depth and single-cell resolution. Proteomics, metabolomics, and epigenomic analyses capture complementary regulatory layers that are difficult to fully resolve using a single platform alone. Therefore, integrative multi-omics strategies combining scRNA-seq, spatial omics, and functional molecular profiling are increasingly essential for reconstructing the complex cellular interactions, metabolic rewiring, and immune dynamics underlying malignant transformation. The molecular targets, spatial niches, and high-risk cellular states identified through these single-cell and multi-omics approaches provide critical mechanistic foundations for the development of precision diagnostics and nanomedicine-based localized therapeutic interventions in premalignant lesions.

Novel nanomaterial-based strategies for early detection and high-risk identification of premalignant microenvironments

Nanomaterial-based strategies refer to diagnostic or therapeutic systems engineered at the nanoscale to enhance lesion targeting, signal amplification, local retention, and microenvironment-responsive intervention. In precancerous lesions, these approaches mainly include biomimetic disease models, molecular imaging platforms, targeted nanosensors, and localized delivery systems, aiming to improve early detection, risk stratification, and precision intervention before malignant transformation occurs. Compared with conventional approaches, nanomaterials possess unique advantages in controllable physicochemical properties, surface functionalization, tissue penetration, signal sensitivity, and microenvironment responsiveness [18], enabling the dynamic monitoring and modulation of early carcinogenic processes.

Among these strategies, biomimetic nanomaterial platforms provide important tools for modeling early premalignant evolution. Polyethylene glycol (PEG)-based hydrogels can maintain the long-term viability of precision-cut lung slices through tunable mechanical and adhesive cues, thereby simulating carcinogen-induced alterations in cell proliferation, ECM remodeling, and gene expression within the premalignant microenvironment. In addition, these systems permit the diffusion and testing of chemopreventive agents, facilitating the establishment of stable and controllable in vitro platforms for mechanistic studies and drug screening of precancerous lesions [41, 42]. In the context of imaging, computed tomography (CT)-responsive hydrogels generated via in situ metal ion mineralization can accurately mimic the CT attenuation values of liver tissue, thereby improving the reproducibility and stability of radiomic feature extraction and providing a material basis for image-based grading and high-risk assessment of premalignant lesions [43].

Optical nanotechnologies enable precise capture of the malignant transformation trajectory in precancerous lesions by amplifying signals of molecular composition remodeling. Raman spectroscopic analysis reveals stage-specific molecular signatures during the progression of OPMD, characterized by declining lipid signals alongside enhanced protein, collagen, and nucleic acid signals. These signatures can be utilized for grading dysplasia and indicating malignant transformation [44]. Surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) platforms based on Ag–TiO2 nanostructures or glancing angle deposition (GLAD)-grown, label-free silver nanorods (NRs) significantly amplify Raman signals while suppressing autofluorescence (Table 2). Combined with multivariate analysis, these platforms enable high-precision discrimination between normal, premalignant, and malignant tissues or saliva samples within a short timeframe, achieving minimally invasive and rapid risk stratification [45, 46]. Multimodal optical imaging systems integrating white-light, autofluorescence, and microendoscopy techniques can identify high-risk regions in vivo and optimize biopsy strategies, thereby improving the detection rate of high-grade precancerous lesions [78]. Function, translational advantages, and current limitations of nanomedicine strategies in precancerous lesions

Nanoprobes targeting inflammatory and senescence-related microenvironmental signals offer highly specific molecular identification tools for high-risk precancerous lesions. Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) is consistently upregulated in various inflammatory, precancerous, and tumor tissues [79, 80]. Fluorescent probes based on indomethacin-derived structures or ROS-activatable fluorocoxib Q can selectively accumulate in oxidative stress microenvironments, significantly improving the imaging signal-to-noise ratio [81, 82]. Further encapsulating fluorocoxib A into ROS-responsive micellar nanoparticles allows for high-contrast imaging of OPMD and OSCC through enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effects and triggered release, demonstrating favorable safety profiles and clinical translation potential [48]. Senescent cells with high COX-2 expression promote carcinogenesis in pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia through SASP signaling, suggesting that such targeted strategies hold cross-organ applicability [83].

The similarity of epithelial malignant transformation from precancerous lesions to cancers renders gastrointestinal premalignant models of significant importance as mechanistic and translational references for nanomedicine-based intervention in epithelial carcinogenesis of OPMD. Many of these lesions share common biological features with OPMD, including chronic inflammation, epithelial dysplasia, metabolic reprogramming, stromal remodeling, and progressive immunosuppressive microenvironment formation. Advances in gastrointestinal premalignant nanoplatforms may offer broadly applicable strategies for early detection and precision intervention in OPMD-to-OSCC progression. In gastrointestinal premalignant lesions, various functionalized nano-platforms enable multi-level detection at the molecular, cellular, and microenvironmental scales. Magnetic ferritin nanoparticles allow MRI visualization of esophageal premalignancy through CD71 targeting [84]. Fluorescent nanoparticles conjugated with MUC2 antibodies or targeting peptides significantly enhance endoscopic imaging signals in Barrett’s esophagus and related lesions [85, 86]. In addition, platforms based on silver-coated gold nanostars (AuNS@Ag), Au@Pt nanorods, or aptamer-mediated plasmonic nano-arrays achieve ultrasensitive detection of miRNAs, inflammatory cytokines, and low-abundance proteins, thereby supporting early molecular identification of gastric and colorectal premalignant lesions (Fig. 5) [87–91]. The signal is further amplified through plasmonic substrate‑mediated enhancement and CHA‑cascade amplification, enabling ultrasensitive and highly specific detection of miRNA biomarkers for early molecular identification of malignant transformation in gastric precancerous lesions. For pancreatic precancerous lesions, the application of nanomaterials extends to inflammatory microenvironments and liquid biopsy. NIR/magnetic resonance dual-modal nanoprobes targeting the translocator protein (TSPO) or urokinase-type plasminogen activator receptor (uPAR) can specifically label pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia-associated inflammation or stromal remodeling, achieving early visualization [95, 96]. Degradable nanoparticles targeting the cholecystokinin-B receptor (CCK-BR) are not only useful for highly sensitive detection but can also deliver small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) to block oncogenic signals, demonstrating theranostic potential [97]. Additionally, strategies such as track-etched magnetic nanopore (TENPO)-based exosome sorting combined with machine learning analysis of miRNA profiles [98], and metal–organic framework (MOF) platforms enabling rapid enrichment and identification of exosomal metabolomes [99], offer efficient liquid biopsy approaches for non-invasive detection and risk stratification in the premalignant stage.

In cervical and prostate precancerous lesions, the amplification effects of nanomaterials significantly enhance the sensitivity of molecular detection. Multifunctional gold nanoparticles or buckminsterfullerene (C60) − 7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane (TCNQ) − polyethylenimine (PEI) electrochemical systems, through mechanisms like dual enzyme-catalyzed or charge transfer amplification, enable highly sensitive detection of p16. The signal intensity correlates strongly with the grade of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia [100–102]. CRISPR-Cas cascade systems combined with nanotags and smartphone-based algorithms allow for ultrasensitive quantification of miRNAs within a short time, providing a feasible solution for primary screening [103]. Circulating tumor cell (CTC) capture platforms based on reduced graphene oxide (rGO) hierarchical micro/nanowrinkles or immune cell-inspired magnetic particles with nano-filopodia, combined with prostate-specific antigen (PSA) metrics and machine learning analysis, significantly improve the early detection rate of prostate cancer in the PSA gray zone [104, 105].

Substantial evidence indicates that nanoscale delivery systems can effectively block the expansion of abnormal epithelial clones and their transition to malignant phenotypes during the premalignant stage by enhancing localized retention, improving intracellular uptake, and reducing systemic toxicity. Single-cell and multi-omics decoding mechanisms-guided nanomedicine strategies for premalignant carcinogenesis

In terms of directly modulating epithelial cell states, various inorganic or small-molecule nano-systems demonstrate potential for inhibiting premalignant epithelial plasticity. Amorphous calcium phosphate nanoparticles can be efficiently taken up by OPMD cells, suppressing β-catenin signaling, inducing G0/G1 cell cycle arrest, and triggering apoptosis-dominated cell death, thereby attenuating adhesion, invasion, and aberrant proliferative capabilities [122, 123]. Similarly, copper–nitrite complexes release nitric oxide (NO) and induce RNS accumulation, activating the extrinsic apoptotic pathway. These complexes exhibit selective cytotoxicity toward preneoplastic and cancer cells while exerting limited effects on normal epithelium [124]. These results suggest that nanomaterials themselves can act as functional modulators to directly intervene in the dysregulated biological programs of premalignant epithelium.

Nanocarriers significantly amplify the chemopreventive efficacy of natural bioactive molecules in preneoplastic intervention. Delivery systems utilizing 3D printing, liposomes, nanocrystals, nano-emulsions, or mucoadhesive formulations substantially enhance the bioavailability and sustained exposure of natural compounds, such as salvianolic acid B, apigenin, tetrahydrocurcumin, andrographolide, lutein, quercetin, and barbaloin, within epithelial tissues. These agents commonly downregulate Ki-67 and Cyclin D1 expression, scavenge ROS, inhibit oxidative DNA damage, and induce cell cycle arrest, thereby delaying or reversing the malignant transformation of OPMD [125–133]. Most of these systems rely on mucoadhesive films, hydrogels, or magnetic/lipid-targeted delivery, maintaining high local efficacy while markedly reducing systemic toxicity, highlighting the translational advantages of nanomaterial-based chemoprevention in the premalignant stage [134, 135].

For premalignant lesions with well-defined oncogenic drivers, nano-delivery further enables precise local intervention using classical anti-tumor drugs and molecularly targeted agents. Nanotechnology-based formulations of cisplatin, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), oxaliplatin, doxorubicin, and methotrexate, delivered via adhesive patches, liposomes, or multi-layered controlled-release systems, achieve high local enrichment within the lesional epithelium. This promotes selective chemotherapeutic ablation of abnormal cells while significantly limiting systemic exposure [54, 56, 108–111]. The local delivery of the EGFR inhibitor erlotinib via liposomal or gel-based systems effectively suppresses aberrant EGFR signaling and promotes the regression of dysplastic epithelium toward a lower-risk phenotype. This indicates that nano-targeted delivery can help reshape the signaling dependency of premalignant epithelium [54].

In more precise strategies, intelligent nano-systems targeting molecular markers characteristic of premalignant epithelium display unique advantages. NIR-II quantum dot probes targeting podoplanin (PDPN) not only enable highly sensitive imaging of pEMT cells but can also be combined with photo-immunotherapy to selectively eliminate cells with high malignant potential. This approach ameliorates the local immunosuppressive microenvironment and enhances responses to immunotherapy [106]. Similarly, EGFR-targeted gold nanorods possess both imaging and therapeutic potential, offering a nanotheranostic paradigm for the early identification and precise intervention of premalignant lesions [107].

Boron neutron capture therapy (BNCT)-related nano-boron carriers exhibit unique radiobiological advantages across the preneoplastic–tumor continuum. Various boron nano-carriers achieve highly selective accumulation in cancerous and potentially malignant tissues. Under neutron irradiation, these carriers generate short-range, high linear energy transfer (LET) particles that precisely eradicate abnormal epithelial cells while maintaining low normal tissue toxicity. This provides a novel physical-nano synergistic strategy to block the malignant transformation of OPMD [136–138].

In cutaneous, esophageal, gastrointestinal, and hepatic premalignant lesions, nanomaterials also play crucial roles through localized delivery and microenvironmental remodeling. Microneedles, transethosomes, nanofibers, and nanomicelles can overcome the stratum corneum or mucosal barrier to enhance the delivery and retention of agents such as 5-aminolevulinic acid (ALA), imiquimod, and paclitaxel within deeper lesion layers (Fig. 6). This significantly improves the efficiency of photodynamic or immunomodulatory therapies [139–146]. Photothermal or magnetic nano-systems in hepatic preneoplasia induce apoptosis, suppress inflammation, and inhibit pro-tumorigenic epigenetic signals, thereby synergistically blocking lesion progression through multiple pathways [147, 148]. In colorectal premalignant lesions, glycosylated pH-sensitive MSNs or colon-targeted nanocarriers markedly increase the local bioavailability of 5-FU, capecitabine, celecoxib, and multi-drug combinations. Nanomaterials are also employed to construct therapeutic–reparative–regenerative integrated microenvironments for preneoplastic intervention. Injectable hydrogels and submucosal cushioning materials provide stable padding, hemostasis, and wound sealing following endoscopic resection. They promote re-epithelialization and mitigate aberrant repair and inflammation, thereby indirectly reducing the subsequent risk of malignant transformation [74–76]. Endoscopic platforms integrating bio-electronic and nanotheranostic units enable the real-time identification, feedback-guided intervention, and immediate ablation of premalignant lesions, representing a new direction in precision intervention [77].

For HPV-associated cervical premalignant lesions, nano-nucleic acid delivery strategies exhibit high mechanistic specificity. Through the local delivery of suicide genes, p53 expression plasmids, or E6/E7-targeting siRNAs, nanocarriers can restore the p53–RB tumor suppressor axis within the epithelial layer, induce cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, and significantly minimize off-target toxicity. This underscores the unique advantages of nanomaterials in the precise intervention of virus-driven preneoplastic conditions [112–114]. In summary, nanomaterial-based therapeutic strategies targeting premalignant epithelial plasticity and the local microenvironment, by integrating precision delivery, signal modulation, immune activation, and tissue repair, provide a systematic and translatable technological pathway to block carcinogenesis before cancer occurs. This marks a significant transition in the management of premalignant lesions from empirical approaches toward mechanism-driven precision therapy.

Nanomaterial-based strategies targeting fibrosis and fibroblast–extracellular matrix remodeling

Within the premalignant microenvironment, persistent aberrant fibroblast activation, accumulation of myofibroblasts, and excessive ECM deposition constitute a critical structural foundation that drives lesion progression and epithelial malignant transformation. Recently, nanomaterials have increasingly emerged as an important therapeutic strategy for intervening in preneoplastic lesions by targeting fibroblast signaling, modulating ECM homeostasis, and remodeling the pro-fibrotic microenvironment. Studies in OPMD have demonstrated that chitosan nanoparticles delivering inhibitors of miR-145 and miR-424 can significantly suppress arecoline-induced myofibroblast activation. This approach downregulates TGF-β signaling and key fibrotic genes, including ACTA2, COL1A1, COL3A1, COL4A1, TIMP2, and ZEB1, reduces the expression of α-SMA and type I collagen, and attenuates cellular migration and contractility, thereby blocking aberrant ECM production and lesion progression [64]. Focusing on inflammation–fibrosis coupling pathways, fibroblasts with high FAP expression have been utilized to establish a targeted delivery window. Nano-graphene oxide–polyethylene glycol (NGO-PEG) nanocarriers modified with an FAP-targeting peptide selectively accumulate at lesion sites. Upon FAP-mediated cleavage, they release celecoxib to inhibit COX-2-associated carcinogenic inflammation. The NIR photothermal effect of graphene oxide enables synergistic drug–photothermal ablation, significantly enhancing local anti-fibrotic efficacy and lesion control [65].

Beyond directly inhibiting fibroblast activation, various bioactive materials remodel the pro-fibrotic microenvironment via ion release or paracrine signaling. Bioactive glass–hyaluronic acid composite systems release silicate ions, downregulating α-SMA, CD68, TGF-β, IL-10, TNF-α, and TIMP-1, while upregulating CD31 and IL-1β. This dual action simultaneously suppresses fibrotic activation, attenuates inflammatory responses, and promotes angiogenesis and epithelial repair, collectively reversing the fibrotic state in OPMD [154]. At the level of cellular communication, adipose-derived stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles (ADSC-EVs) exhibit significant anti-fibrotic effects in arecoline-induced OPMD models. The miR-125a-5p carried by ADSC-EVs targets and inhibits Smad2, reducing TGF-β − Smad2-dependent augmentation of autophagy, thereby blocking myofibroblast transdifferentiation and decreasing collagen deposition [66]. Alternatively, ADSC-EV-mediated delivery of miR-375 suppresses FOXF1, markedly inhibiting fibroblast proliferation, migration, invasion, and type I/III collagen deposition, while inducing apoptosis, thereby delaying OPMD fibrosis and its preneoplastic progression [67].

Local delivery systems based on natural anti-fibrotic molecules further expand the scope of nano-chemopreventive strategies. Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) synergistically inhibits key pro-fibrotic molecules such as TGF-β and lysyl oxidase (LOX), alleviates oxidative stress, and blocks excessive deposition of types I and III collagen. Its mucoadhesive hydrogel formulation significantly enhances local retention and bioavailability, effectively ameliorating tissue stiffness and functional impairment in OPMD [68]. Similarly, chitosan gels loaded with dental pulp mesenchymal stem cell secretomes enable sustained release of multiple pro-reparative factors. These formulations suppress the expression of α-SMA and COL1/3 in fibroblasts, reduce lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, and elevate superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity, thereby synergistically reversing the fibrotic microenvironment in OPMD through antioxidant, anti-fibrotic, and tissue regenerative mechanisms [155].

At the ECM level, the reconstruction of the matrix is recognized as a vital approach to modulating the premalignant microenvironment. Decellularized ECM hydrogels derived from normal porcine esophageal mucosa retain crucial basement membrane signals, including laminin, type IV collagen, perlecan, and nidogen. These hydrogels can restore epithelial–stromal adhesion and differentiation homeostasis. By inhibiting TNF-α − associated inflammatory infiltration, they promote the reversion of aberrant metaplastic epithelium toward a normal squamous phenotype, thereby blocking preneoplastic progression through both structural and signaling interventions [156].

The aforementioned anti-fibrotic nano-strategies also demonstrate universal mechanistic value across multi-organ preneoplastic models. Lipid nanoparticles delivering siRNA targeting hepatocyte Jnk2 suppress inflammation–fibrosis signaling in chronic liver injury, reducing the formation of premalignant nodules and blocking the early initiation of hepatocellular carcinoma [157]. Conversely, non-inflammatory lipid nanoparticles activate vacuolar-type ATPase via ursolic acid, improving endosomal homeostasis and reducing NF-κB/ROS-mediated inflammatory responses (Fig. 7). This facilitates highly efficient expression of nuclear receptor subfamily 1 group D member 1 (NR1D1) mRNA in lung tissue, enabling anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic interventions for bronchopulmonary dysplasia and pulmonary fibrosis [158]. Nanomaterial-based strategies to reverse immunosuppression and remodel the inflammatory microenvironment

The persistent immunosuppressive state and chronic inflammatory response within the premalignant microenvironment are recognized as significant drivers of epithelial aberrant proliferation, accumulation of genomic instability, and malignant transformation. In recent years, nanomaterials have been key technological approaches to block the progression of precancerous lesions by enhancing the local delivery efficiency of immunomodulatory drugs, precisely intervening in immune checkpoints, remodeling inflammatory signaling networks, and augmenting antigen presentation and immune surveillance functions.

In OPMD, immune checkpoint pathways have become important targets. Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)-polyethylene glycol (PLGA-PEG) nanoparticles loaded with all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) and modified with anti-PD-L1 antibodies achieve active targeting and enrichment in oral dysplastic and OSCC cells with high PD-L1 expression. This strategy not only enhances the stability and intra-tumoral accumulation of ATRA, inducing tumor cell apoptosis, but also blocks PD-1–PD-L1-mediated immunosuppression and activates local CD8+ T cells. Consequently, it significantly augments anti-tumor immune effects while reducing systemic toxicity [117]. Consistently, direct PD-1 blockade can reverse the immunosuppressive state associated with OPMD progression. By reducing PD-1 expression on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and peripheral T cells, decreasing MDSCs accumulation, and restoring the numbers of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells alongside their secretion of IFN-γ and IL-2, this approach significantly delays the transformation of dysplasia into invasive carcinoma [159]. Loading anti-PD-1 antibodies into nanofiber-based hydrogels for local, sustained release not only effectively suppresses Treg-mediated immunosuppression but also restores STING signaling and enhances DCs infiltration and antigen presentation. This strategy blocks the progression of OPMD towards malignancy while reducing systemic immune toxicity and demonstrates a degree of efficacy against p53-mutant states [61].

Beyond immune checkpoint regulation, nanomaterials also exhibit significant advantages in precisely intervening in inflammation-driven premalignant microenvironments. Various mucoadhesive or controlled-release systems have been employed for the local delivery of anti-inflammatory drugs to minimize systemic exposure and maintain therapeutic concentrations. Bioinspired tough dental adhesives or electrospun mucosal patches can adhere stably within the dynamic oral environment, providing sustained release of clobetasol-17-propionate. This significantly downregulates inflammatory and immunomodulatory factors such as TNF-α, IL-6, MCP-5, MIP-2, IL-10, and TIMP-1, thereby alleviating chronic oral inflammatory mucosal lesions and improving the local microenvironment [51, 52]. Similarly, PLGA nanoparticles delivering dexamethasone achieve localized, efficient, and controlled release within OPMD tissues, persistently reducing inflammation levels and mitigating the risk of inflammation-driven malignant transformation, demonstrating favorable biosafety [116]. Natural antioxidant and anti-inflammatory molecules have also been effectively utilized through nano-formulation strategies. For example, astaxanthin-loaded polycaprolactone (PCL)/gelatin nanofiber-based mucoadhesive patches can inhibit Ki-67 and COX-2 expression, reducing epithelial aberrant proliferation and inflammatory responses, thereby decreasing the risk of OPMD malignant transformation [53].

In the realm of active immune activation, various nano-delivery systems have been employed to enhance innate and adaptive immune responses, restoring immune surveillance function during the premalignant stage. Chitosan hydrogels loaded with nanoencapsulated imiquimod enable sustained release at the mucosal site, effectively activating local innate and adaptive immune responses at low doses, promoting the clearance of abnormal epithelium while significantly reducing inflammation and adverse reactions [60]. The TLR9 agonist vidutolimod, delivered in a virus-like particle format, activates plasmacytoid DCs, upregulates IFN-γ, IL-12, and TNF-α, and enhances CD8+ T cell effector functions. When combined with anti-PD-1 therapy, it further remodels the immune microenvironment and suppresses OPMD lesion progression [63]. Additionally, a Mn2+-loaded guanosine-tannic acid hydrogel activates the cGAS–STING pathway, promoting the maturation and antigen presentation of DCs and macrophages, and enhancing the cytotoxic function of CD8+ T cells. This significantly delays the malignant transformation of OPMD in a 4-NQO-induced model [62].

Nanomaterials are also utilized to synergistically regulate inflammation–immune–proliferation signaling networks, demonstrating broad applicability in multi-organ preneoplastic models. Human serum albumin-chitosan Janus nanoparticles enable the synergistically delivery of fenretinide and the IL-6R inhibitor tocilizumab. By dually inhibiting IL-6–STAT-related pathways, inducing immunogenic cell death, and disrupting abnormal vascular stability, this strategy achieves significant immune-chemopreventive effects in the preneoplastic stage of LSCC [160]. In colorectal preneoplastic models, gelatin-derived core–shell nanoparticles co-delivering paclitaxel and glycyrrhizin induce apoptosis, inhibit angiogenesis, and significantly reduce the formation of preneoplastic lesions through synergistic anti-inflammatory and anti-proliferative effects [161]. Meanwhile, macrophage membrane-coated PLGA nanoparticles can target and deliver S100A9 inhibitors to inflammatory foci, inhibiting TLR4–NF-κB and STAT3 signaling, protecting the mucosal barrier, and blocking chronic inflammation-driven malignant transformation [118]. Targeting pathological senescence and inflammatory spread, CD44 aptamer-modified PLGA nano-platforms selectively induce senescence in activated hepatic stellate cells and inhibit NF-κB − dependent SASP production (Fig. 8). In reshaping the immune microenvironment and vaccine delivery, nanotechnology also demonstrates unique advantages. A bioadhesive polycarbophil gel that provides sustained release of granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) in a weakly acidic environment enhances the migration and colonization of DCs and langerhans cells to HPV-associated premalignant epithelium. Conjugating HPV E7 synthetic long peptides with ultra-small polymeric nanoparticles significantly increases the number and effector function of CD8+ T cells and elevates the CD8+/Treg ratio, providing an efficient platform for therapeutic vaccines against precancerous lesions and solid tumors [163]. Silica-based nanoparticles construct antigen reservoirs and promote MHC I cross-presentation. In synergy with the poly(I:C) adjuvant, they effectively activate cDC subsets and induce specific CD8+ T cell responses, thereby enhancing immune clearance of precancerous lesions and early tumor cells [164]. In summary, nanomaterial-based therapeutic strategies focusing on reversing immunosuppression, precisely regulating inflammatory signaling, and activating antigen-specific immunity are reshaping the premalignant microenvironment across multiple levels. These approaches provide crucial technological support and a theoretical foundation for achieving effective control and blocking the malignant transformation of precancerous lesions.

Novel nanomaterial-based strategies for metabolic reprogramming and oxidative stress modulation

Metabolic reprogramming and the disruption of redox homeostasis are key drivers in the malignant transformation of precancerous lesions. Nanomaterial-based intervention strategies targeting mitochondrial function, ROS dynamics, and abnormalities in lipid and energy metabolism are emerging as critical approaches to block preneoplastic progression. Photonanomedicine has been demonstrated to have unique advantages in this field. NIR light-mediated photobiomodulation directly acts on mitochondrial respiratory chain photoreceptors, inhibiting oxidative phosphorylation and inducing a metabolic shift toward glycolysis. Simultaneously, it weakens antioxidant defenses, leading to ROS accumulation, downregulation of the Bcl-2/Bax ratio, and activation of apoptotic pathways, thereby suppressing OSCC cell survival and indicating its therapeutic potential for intervening in malignant transformation [69]. Building upon this foundation, various photosensitizer- or photothermal-based nano-systems achieve precise elimination of abnormal epithelial cells by enhancing ROS production and inflicting mitochondrial damage. For instance, polyacrylic acid (PAA) − chitosan (CHI) − ALA adhesive patches significantly improve ALA penetration and protoporphyrin IX (PpIX) generation. Under laser irradiation, they intensify ROS bursts, induce mitochondrial membrane potential loss, and inhibit Ki67-associated proliferative signaling, effectively blocking OPMD progression [50]. Similarly, ITIC-Th nanoparticles generate both substantial heat and ROS upon NIR irradiation, synergizing photothermal therapy (PTT) and photodynamic therapy (PDT) effects to induce apoptosis and suppress inflammatory and proliferative signaling (Fig. 9) [58]. To enhance ALA − PDT delivery efficiency, natural polysaccharide-based emulgels and hyaluronic acid (HA) soluble microneedles improve mucosal retention or trans-barrier delivery. Beyond physical–optical strategies, functionalized nanomaterials demonstrate selective clearance of preneoplastic cells by directly remodeling mitochondrial apoptosis and oxidative stress pathways. Plant-derived or green-synthesized gold and silver nanoparticles selectively enter dysplastic keratinocytes. They activate the p53/Bax/Bcl-2 signals, inhibit PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling, and induce mitochondrial pathway-mediated apoptosis while reducing oxidative stress and inflammatory responses. This enables a multi-pathway synergistic counteraction against preneoplastic lesion cells [70]. Likewise, functionalized gold nanoparticles derived from Vitis vinifera enhance antioxidant enzyme activity, downregulate mutant p53 and Bcl-2, and promote apoptosis, significantly suppressing 7,12-dimethylbenz[a]anthracene (DMBA)-induced abnormal keratinization and tumor formation [71]. In cutaneous preneoplastic models, an escheriosome-based nano-formulation loaded with 2,6-diisopropylphenol–oleic acid (2,6P-OLA) achieves local enrichment, activates CASP9-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis, and restores wild-type p53 function, thereby inhibiting papilloma formation [165]. Meanwhile, cationic ultra-flexible nanocarriers significantly enhance the transdermal delivery of a diindolylmethane derivative (DIM-D), reducing UV-induced oxidative DNA damage and inhibiting pro-proliferative signals such as AKT/c-Myc/CyclinD1, thereby delaying skin carcinogenesis [166]. A thermosensitive liposome (TSL)–IR820–catalase (CAT) composite nano-system alleviates hypoxia while synchronously amplifying ROS and thermal effects, specifically targeting mitochondria to significantly elevate apoptosis levels. This provides a paradigmatic reference for synergistically photometabolic regulation in preneoplastic lesion treatment [72].

In gastrointestinal preneoplastic lesions, nanomaterials play crucial roles by buffering oxidative stress, modulating the autophagy–apoptosis network, and improving metabolic homeostasis. Triple helix β-glucan nanocarriers enable pH-responsive release of doxorubicin (DOX), reducing cytotoxicity while simultaneously inhibiting ROS generation and stabilizing mitochondrial membrane potential. They also mitigate inflammation and oxidative damage via p53 and PI3K signaling, thereby intervening in the progression of gastric premalignant lesions [119]. Edible-grade pickering emulsions loaded with Rosa roxburghii extract significantly scavenge ROS, inhibit inflammation, and promote gastric epithelial repair, reversing gastric preneoplasia at lower doses [167]. In colorectal preneoplastic models, liposomal nano-formulations modulate the autophagy–apoptosis network constituted by ATF4–CHOP–XBP1–Beclin-1. This alleviates oxidative and nitrosative stress and induces mitochondrial-mediated cell death, thereby blocking lesion deterioration [55]. Sericin–chitosan conjugated silver nanoparticles significantly elevate antioxidant enzyme activity, reduce malondialdehyde levels, and inhibit multiple inflammation- and invasion-related factors, attenuating colonic epithelial dysplasia and delaying carcinogenesis [168].

The dynamic monitoring and precise modulation of metabolism and oxidative stress are gradually being integrated. Dual-responsive fluorescent probes capable of simultaneously monitoring γ-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) activity and mitochondrial viscosity reveal the critical role of progressively intensifying oxidative stress in the malignant transformation of hepatic preneoplastic lesions. These probes provide quantitative metrics for real-time identification and intervention [73]. At the level of lipid metabolism, human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell-derived small extracellular vesicles (hucMSC-sEVs) deliver miR-370-3p to target and inhibit 24-dehydrocholesterol reductase (DHCR24). This reduces cholesterol synthesis and significantly suppresses the proliferation and migration of HPV-positive cervical preneoplastic cells, suggesting that nano-EV − mediated metabolic reprogramming is an important mechanism for blocking preneoplastic progression [120]. In summary, nanomaterial-based regulatory strategies focusing on mitochondrial function, ROS homeostasis, and disturbances in energy and lipid metabolism, through photometabolic synergy, rebalancing of antioxidant–pro-apoptotic processes, and precise intervention in metabolic pathways, provide a systematic and translatable technological roadmap for multi-layered intervention against premalignant lesions.

Clinical translation evidence and current limitations of nanomedicine strategies targeting premalignant lesions

Advances in understanding the molecular heterogeneity and malignant transformation drivers of precancerous lesions are steadily propelling precision diagnostic and interventional strategies, which target the premalignant microenvironment, into clinical validation. Among these, nanomedicine approaches centered on molecularly targeted imaging, localized nanodelivery, and immune/metabolic-targeted therapies demonstrate unique advantages. For the early identification of OPMDs, a NIR fluorescence molecular imaging strategy targeting the mesenchymal–epithelial transition (MET) factor has shown significant clinical value. The MET-targeted probe MET-binding peptide-indocyanine green (cMBP-ICG), combined with a real-time NIR fluorescence imaging (NIRFI) platform, can accurately delineate lesion boundaries and substantially improve the precision of biopsy site selection. Clinical trial results indicate an overall diagnostic accuracy of 91% for high-grade dysplasia and OSCC using this method, markedly superior to conventional oral examination (72%). This approach effectively reduces missed diagnoses, offering a translatable molecular imaging solution for the non-invasive, real-time diagnosis of OPMDs and early OSCC [49]. Similar molecularly targeted imaging strategies have also been validated in gastrointestinal premalignancies. In patients with Barrett’s esophagus, an EGFR/ErbB2-specific peptide heterodimer labeled with IRDye800 was used for endoscopic NIR imaging, enabling highly sensitive and specific identification of high-grade dysplasia and esophageal adenocarcinoma without observed adverse events. This further confirms the clinical feasibility of nanomolecular probes for the precise detection of high-risk premalignant lesions [169]. Alongside the advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) and real-world data research, integrative multi-omics analysis combined with digital pathology and AI platforms provides critical technological support for cancer risk prediction and precise stratification. Emerging evidence indicates that AI-driven image recognition, particularly when applied to digital hematoxylin and eosin-stained histological slides, offers considerable promise in both predicting the malignant transformation risk of OPMD and facilitating molecular subtyping analysis, thus potentially supporting clinical decision-making [30, 170, 171]. The assessment of genomic instability and AI-assisted diagnosis are recognized as important future directions for screening and follow-up management in high-risk populations [10, 11, 13, 30, 170, 172–175]. Nevertheless, several translational limitations remain unresolved. Molecular imaging probes may exhibit heterogeneous target expression across different stages of dysplasia, potentially reducing imaging sensitivity and specificity in highly heterogeneous lesions. Inflammatory infiltration, tissue autofluorescence, and dynamic mucosal turnover may further interfere with signal stability and boundary recognition. Moreover, the long-term biosafety, mucosal retention behavior, biodegradation, and repeated exposure risks of nanoparticle-based imaging probes in the oral and gastrointestinal microenvironment remain insufficiently characterized. Current AI-assisted risk prediction models are also limited by the lack of standardized multi-center datasets, cross-platform reproducibility, and prospective validation, restricting their integration into routine clinical workflows.

On the therapeutic front, nanomaterial-based localized delivery systems have significantly improved efficacy and safety by enhancing drug retention and bioavailability at lesion sites. A clinical study involving 60 OPMD patients demonstrated that a polyacrylic acid-chitosan-ALA interpenetrating network hydrogel patch could adhere stably to oral lesions for approximately two hours without interfering with swallowing or speech functions. Patients demonstrated high levels of compliance and satisfaction., and the formulation showed clear advantages in delivery mode over previous ALA–PDT treatments, indicating promising clinical translation potential [50]. In terms of immunomodulation, nanoformulated delivery similarly exhibits benefits in dose optimization and toxicity control. A triple-blind, randomized controlled trial for actinic cheilitis showed that low-dose IMIQ-0.05%-NANO was comparable in efficacy to conventional IMIQ-5% but significantly reduced the incidence of adverse reactions, highlighting the safety advantage of nano-formulations for long-term intervention in premalignant lesions [60]. In patients with oral lichen planus, the topical adhesive delivery of 2% mycophenolate mofetil significantly improved pain, burning sensation, and ulcer area without notable adverse effects, further supporting the value of localized targeted delivery in managing chronic inflammation-associated premalignant conditions [176]. However, several challenges continue to hinder broader clinical implementation. For photodynamic and photothermal strategies, therapeutic efficacy is strongly influenced by tissue penetration depth, oxygen availability, and lesion heterogeneity, which may lead to incomplete ablation of high-risk epithelial clones. Excessive ROS amplification may also induce collateral oxidative injury in surrounding normal tissues, potentially aggravating chronic inflammation or promoting compensatory proliferative responses. In addition, repeated light exposure and long-term photosensitizer retention may increase the risk of mucosal irritation, pain, fibrosis, or unpredictable phototoxicity. The stability, release kinetics, and dosing consistency of mucoadhesive nanoformulations also remain difficult to standardize under dynamic oral environments characterized by saliva flow, pH fluctuation, and mechanical friction.

The application of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in the premalignant stage has entered clinical exploration. In a phase II non-randomized trial, the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab was administered to PVL patients without the use of any nanocarrier. While lesion extent and dysplasia grade improved in some cases, a proportion of patients still progressed to OSCC, suggesting that single-agent immune intervention has limited preventive efficacy in the premalignant stage. Chromosome 9p21.3 deletion was closely associated with the risk of malignant transformation and may serve as an important biomarker for predicting immunotherapy response and malignant transformation [177], providing direction for future strategies. Despite the promising potential of ICIs in OPMD intervention, nanomaterial-based strategies remain largely unexplored in this context. Nanotechnology could address several key limitations of current ICI approaches in premalignant lesions. For example, nanocarriers could enhance the local delivery and retention of ICIs within OPMD tissues, reduce systemic immune-related adverse events, enable co-delivery of ICIs with other immunomodulators or chemopreventive agents for synergistic effects, and facilitate the development of nanoparticle-based antigen-presenting systems or artificial antigen-presenting cells to prime anti-tumor immunity without systemic checkpoint blockade. Future research should prioritize the design and evaluation of nanomaterial-enhanced ICI strategies specifically tailored for OPMD, including localized delivery systems, multi-target nanoplatforms, and controlled-release formulations that could overcome the current limitations of poor lesion penetration, heterogeneous immune landscapes, and relatively low neoantigen burdens in premalignant lesions.

Mucosa-targeted nano-systems also show promising translational prospects in other preneoplastic contexts. Studies in healthy volunteers confirmed that chitosan hydrogels exhibit significantly longer adhesion time on the esophageal mucosa compared to conventional thermosensitive gels, sufficient to support the effective delivery of the fluorescent diagnostic agent hexylaminolevulinate. This lays a clinical foundation for imaging and dose optimization in esophageal premalignant lesions [144]. Additionally, a phase I/II clinical trial demonstrated that PRV111, a nanotechnology-based system for local delivery of cisplatin-loaded chitosan particles, achieved significant short-term reduction in tumor volume in early OSCC while exhibiting minimal systemic exposure and no observed severe toxicity. This underscores the unique advantage of nanodelivery in high-efficacy, low-toxicity strategies for blocking malignant transformation [56]. Despite these encouraging findings, long-term translational barriers remain substantial. Sustained local exposure to chemotherapeutic or immunomodulatory nanoparticles may alter epithelial regeneration dynamics, stromal homeostasis, and ECM remodeling processes, potentially contributing to fibrosis, aberrant wound healing, or altered tissue biomechanics. Anti-fibrotic and ECM-remodeling strategies themselves also require careful evaluation, as excessive suppression of fibroblast activity or matrix turnover could impair physiological repair and barrier integrity. Moreover, nanoparticle penetration through mucus and epithelial barriers varies significantly among organs and patients, complicating reproducibility and individualized dosing.

These clinical and translational studies indicate that nanomedicine strategies, revolving around molecularly targeted recognition, localized precision delivery, and the integration of multimodal imaging and therapy, are providing practical clinical pathways for the early diagnosis, risk stratification, and malignant transformation blockade of precancerous lesions. They hold the potential to drive the transition of preneoplastic intervention from empirical treatment toward precision medicine. At the same time, future clinical translation will require a balanced consideration between therapeutic efficacy and microenvironmental safety. The integration of single-cell multi-omics, spatial profiling, and longitudinal monitoring will be essential for identifying patients most likely to benefit from nanomedicine interventions while minimizing overtreatment and long-term tissue toxicity.

Future directions and challenges: a new paradigm for precancerous lesion prevention and control − from single-cell atlas analysis to nanomedicine-based precision intervention

With the rapid advancement of single-cell omics, spatial multi-omics, and nanomedicine, the research paradigm for precancerous lesions is progressively shifting from an experience-based model reliant on histological grading and morphological assessment toward a precision medicine framework centered on cellular heterogeneity, microenvironmental remodeling, and systemic risk evaluation. This review systematically summarizes the pivotal role of single-cell sequencing in elucidating key mechanisms underlying the progression from OPMDs to OSCC, including epithelial cell plasticity, fibroblast heterogeneity, immune lineage remodeling, metabolic reprogramming, and cellular interaction networks. It outlines the rapid advances in nanomaterial-based strategies for early detection, localized delivery, and microenvironmental modulation in preneoplastic intervention (Fig. 10). Decoding the precancerous landscape and precision nanomedicine interventions

Single-cell atlas-driven precision stratification of precancerous lesions: from descriptive mechanisms to predictive and decision-support tools

Current single-cell transcriptomic studies have successfully delineated the multi-layered, temporally resolved landscape of cellular heterogeneity during OPMD malignant transformation. They have established that aberrant epithelial clonal expansion, immunosuppressive microenvironment formation, and fibroblast–ECM remodeling are initiated as early as the preneoplastic stage. However, the key future challenge is no longer merely identifying differences, but rather transforming these high-dimensional data into clinically actionable tools that are predictive, intervenable, and verifiable. Although bulk RNA sequencing has identified multiple novel differentially expressed genes and associated signaling pathways related to the malignant transformation of OPMD [178–180], their clinical applicability and mechanistic understanding remain unclear due to issues such as cellular heterogeneity. The deep integration of single-cell data with AI offers a tangible pathway toward this goal. Leveraging heterogeneity maps constructed from single-cell transcriptomes and incorporating deep learning frameworks such as Shennong enables the prediction of drug responses in preneoplastic and early malignant cells in both in vivo and in vitro models [181]. Such AI platforms can not only identify potential therapeutic targets at single-cell resolution, screen candidate drugs, and evaluate tissue damage and mechanisms of action but also uncover new indications for FDA-approved drugs. This provides an efficient solution for drug repurposing and precision intervention in precancerous lesions. Key future challenges include validating model robustness across multi-center populations, establishing closed-loop verification between AI predictions and real-world clinical outcomes, and mitigating algorithmic biases that could systematically mislead risk assessment in high-risk populations.

From morphological grading to molecular risk stratification: structural upgrading of clinical management models for precancerous lesions

Current international management of precancerous lesions remains centered on histological grading and morphological assessment [182–184], yet its limitations have become increasingly evident in real-world studies. A European multi-center study indicated that although overall management of gastric precancerous conditions improved following the implementation of the management of precancerous conditions and lesions in the stomach (MAPS) I/II guidelines, significant deficiencies persist in virtual chromoendoscopy, targeted biopsy strategies, and standardization of endoscopic/histological scoring. Substantial variability in adherence among different centers directly compromises the consistency of risk stratification and early detection capabilities [185]. This phenomenon fundamentally reflects the systemic limitations of the traditional morphology- and experience-driven model in addressing the high heterogeneity of preneoplastic states. Similar challenges exist in classic preneoplastic models such as Barrett’s esophagus. Although the latest guidelines emphasize standardized endoscopic surveillance, targeted biopsies, and stratified ablation strategies [186], risk assessment still primarily relies on lesion length and degree of dysplasia. Future developments should focus on introducing a molecular stratification system based on single-cell atlases. By analyzing epithelial clonal evolution trajectories, the early emergence of immune-tolerant subpopulations, and the remodeling of microenvironmental supportive signals, such a system could enable the early identification of lesions that are morphologically stable yet highly unstable at the molecular level [13, 187]. This shift would not only optimize follow-up frequency and intervention timing but also provide clear targets for precision nano-imaging and localized therapies.

Role reconfiguration of nanomedicine in precancerous lesions: from drug carrier to systemic intervention platform

The application of nanomedicine in precancerous lesions is evolving from simply enhancing local drug concentration toward an integrated intervention platform encompassing diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment. As highlighted in previous sections, nanomaterials can enable precise identification of high-risk lesions through targeted imaging, modulate epithelial plasticity, immunosuppression, and fibrotic processes via localized delivery, and achieve selective clearance of abnormal cell populations by intervening in metabolism and oxidative stress. Nevertheless, the core future challenge lies in realizing clinically translatable applications that are deeply coupled with clinical workflows. For gastrointestinal precancerous lesions, for instance, the stability, biosafety, and imaging consistency of nanoprobes within the complex mucus barrier and dynamic peristaltic environment require systematic validation. In oral and esophageal contexts, the seamless integration of nano-targeted imaging and localized ablation strategies into standardized management protocols is critical for their adoption into clinical practice. Additionally, mapping single-cell molecular features onto macroscopic imaging signals remains a significant technical bottleneck in cross-scale integration.

New windows for immune intervention in precancerous lesions: low-toxicity, localized, and mechanism-driven clinical trial design

Unlike invasive cancers, precancerous lesions offer a unique therapeutic window characterized by earlier timing, lower burden, and greater reversibility for immune interventions. Localized immunotherapy has the potential to reshape the local immune microenvironment and activate anti-tumor responses while minimizing systemic toxicity [188]. Future clinical trial designs should prioritize high-risk preneoplastic populations or patients with early lesions, incorporating fine stratification and efficacy evaluation based on single-cell and spatial omics markers (e.g., tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, regulatory T cells). In this process, nanomaterials serve not only as delivery tools but also as key technological enablers for precise dose control, spatiotemporally specific release, and minimization of immune-related adverse effects. The challenge lies in establishing standardized dosing systems for nanoformulations, injection or local administration techniques, and mechanism-oriented evaluation metrics that align with traditional pathological and imaging endpoints, thereby avoiding a fragmented research landscape characterized by effective but incomparable outcomes.

Integration of cross-organ preneoplastic mechanisms and construction of systemic prevention and control strategies

Single-cell omics studies are revealing common mechanisms, such as immunosuppression, metabolic reprogramming, and stromal remodeling, across precancerous lesions in different organs, providing a theoretical foundation for developing cross-organ, generalizable prevention and control strategies. In the future, integrating single-cell data from multi-organ preneoplastic models (e.g., oral, esophageal, gastric, colorectal) may identify conserved high-risk cell states and key regulatory signals, offering systematic guidance for nanomedicine target screening and multi-organ prevention strategies.

In summary, research on precancerous lesions stands at a critical turning point, transitioning from passive monitoring to active intervention. Single-cell omics has provided unprecedented depth in mechanistic understanding, while nanomedicine offers the technological means for the precise translation of these mechanisms. The integration of single-cell multi-omics and nanomedicine is redefining the concept of precision intervention at the precancerous stage, shifting therapeutic paradigms from advanced stage tumor eradication toward early identification and selective elimination of high-risk cellular states and malignant evolutionary trajectories before invasive cancer develops. This emerging framework highlights a unique opportunity to intercept carcinogenesis at a biologically reversible stage and may represent one of the most transformative directions in future precision oncology. The core future task is not only to continue enriching molecular atlases and material systems but also to construct a replicable, scalable, and sustainable new paradigm for precision prevention and control of precancerous lesions through AI integration, multi-center validation, and standardized clinical pathway design. The establishment of this paradigm will have profound and lasting public health and clinical significance in reducing the incidence of OSCC and other malignancies.

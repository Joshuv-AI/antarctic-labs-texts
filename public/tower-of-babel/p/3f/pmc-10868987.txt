Pigmented Eccrine Poroma on the Palm: A Rare Variant in an Atypical Site
Authors: T. Vijayalakshmi Nayak, Deepthi Konda, Abhimanyu Sharma
Published: 2023

Dear Sir,

Eccrine poroma is a rare benign adnexal tumor derived from the intraepidermal eccrine duct (acrosyringium) of the sweat glands. The lesions are usually exophytic, red-coloured, and reach up to 1-2 centimeters in size. There is a rare pigmented variant that corresponds to nearly only 17% of the cases.[1] Herein, we report a case of pigmented eccrine poroma due to its rarity. They are best treated by surgical excision.

A 65-year-old man, presented to us with a slow-growing pigmented lesion over the left palm of 4 years duration. The patient denies a history of trauma or radiation prior to the development of the lesion. Cutaneous examination revealed well-circumscribed, nontender, hyperpigmented plaque of 1.5 × 2 cm over the left hypothenar eminence [Figure 1]. The surface of the plaque was slightly verrucous and soft in consistency.

Cutaneous examination revealed well-circumscribed, nontender, hyperpigmented plaque of 1.5 × 2 cm over the left hypothenar eminence. The surface of the plaque was slightly verrucous

Histopathological examination showed hyperkeratotic skin with a tumor involving the epidermis and dermis. The tumor cells were arranged in the form of anastomosing bands consisting of oval to polygonal cells with round to oval nuclei and scant to moderate amount of eosinophilic cytoplasm. Few cells showed brownish intracytoplasmic pigment [Figure 2]. With the above clinical and histopathological findings, a diagnosis of pigmented eccrine poroma was rendered.

Poroma was first described by Pinkus, Goldman, and Rogin in 1956. Typically, lesions are single, reddish, flesh-coloured exophytic papules, plaques, or nodules. The surface of the lesion can be smooth, verrucous, or warty.

The most common sites of development of eccrine poroma are the palms and soles as there is a higher concentration of eccrine sweat glands. But this does not hold true for pigmented variants as palms and soles involvement is quite atypical with less than 10 cases reported in the literature. Other sites of pigmented poroma reported in the literature are the scalp, face, trunk, forearm, thigh, dorsal aspect of the foot, and palms.

Porocarcinoma is the malignant variant of poroma, which can occur as a primary tumor but usually results from the malignant transformation of the eccrine poroma.[2]

Histopathological findings seen in poromas are aggregates of round or cuboidal poroma cells that have a uniform dark, round nucleus with scant cytoplasm. The cells are similar to cells seen in the duct of the eccrine and apocrine glands.[3] The cells are present in the epidermis and often project into the dermis. In pigmented poroma, melanin can be demonstrated in the tumor cells. Carcinoembryonic antigen stains the ducts of the sweat gland and its neoplasm.

To explain the presence of melanin and melanocytes two hypotheses have been put forward. The first hypothesis is that melanocytes develop from the primordia of the eccrine gland and the second hypothesis is that it comes from the epidermal melanocytes.[45]

We are reporting a rare case of pigmented variant of eccrine poroma on the palm of a 65-year-old male. The diagnosis of pigmented eccrine poroma is mainly histopathological; however, in future with better understanding, Dermoscopy and confocal microscopy can aid in the diagnosis.

The Relationships Among Atherogenic Index of Plasma and Carotid–Femoral Pulse Wave Velocity in Adults
Authors: Xiaowen Ou, Tong Lin, Jin Gong, Xiaoqi Cai, Ying Han, Guoyan Xu, Liangdi Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe relationships between the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) and carotid–femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) in adults were investigated. A total of 1398 subjects were included according to the inclusion criteria. Demographic data, medical history, and biochemical indicators were collected. The cfPWV was measured using the Complior Analyse device. AIP was calculated using the following formula: AIP = log (triglycerides/high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol). Correlation analysis, multiple linear regression, and logistic regression were performed to explore the relationships between AIP and cfPWV. Compared to the cfPWV normal group, the cfPWV elevated group had a higher level of AIP (p < 0.05). In all subjects, mild‐to‐moderate correlations were found between AIP and cfPWV (p < 0.05). Stepwise multiple linear regression analysis revealed that AIP was an independent factor associated with cfPWV (β = 0.156, p < 0.05). Logistic regression analysis indicated that the prevalence of cfPWV ≥ 10 m/s increased with the rise of AIP (OR = 18.291, p < 0.05). The ROC curve analysis showed that the area under the curve for AIP was 0.697. The critical point for AIP was determined as 0.00 by the Youden index (sensitivity of 76.2% and specificity of 54.3%). Stepwise multiple linear regression analysis showed that in the young and middle‐aged group with normal cfPWV, AIP was an independent factor associated with cfPWV (p < 0.05). In adults, AIP is an independent factor associated with an increased cfPWV. When AIP > 0.00, it has a certain predictive value in the screening of atherosclerosis.Keywords: atherogenic index of plasma, atherosclerosis, carotid–femoral pulse wave velocity

Introduction

The occurrence and progression of cardiovascular diseases are closely related to atherosclerosis [1, 2]. Over the past 30 years, the prevalence of cardiovascular diseases in China has been on the rise, with an estimated 290 million people currently affected [3]. Early screening for atherosclerosis and early detection and prevention of cardiovascular events are enduring hot topics in the field of cardiovascular diseases. Methods for detecting atherosclerosis include direct invasive and indirect noninvasive tests. Direct tests involve measuring changes in arterial diameter under different pressures, which are invasive and less accepted by subjects. Therefore, indirect noninvasive methods are more commonly used in clinical practice, including pulse wave velocity (PWV), ambulatory arterial stiffness index (AASI), ankle–brachial index (ABI), and cardio‐ankle vascular index (CAVI) [4].

The pressure wave formed by the blood ejected from the heart to the peripheral arteries with each contraction is called a pulse wave, and the speed at which the pulse wave propagates within the arteries is known as PWV. The 2013 European Hypertension Guidelines [2] state that PWV is an important noninvasive, simple, economical, and effective method for assessing atherosclerosis and cardiovascular risk factors. Depending on the location of the blood vessel tested, PWV can be divided into carotid–femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) and brachial–ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV). Currently, cfPWV and baPWV are two commonly used clinical indicators. Due to the pressure amplification effect when the pulse wave propagates from the aorta to the peripheral blood vessels, cfPWV has better accuracy than baPWV and reflects the elasticity of the central arteries [5]. The European Society of Hypertension's expert consensus document on the use of cfPWV for the assessment of arterial stiffness has established cfPWV as the gold standard for the diagnosis of atherosclerosis, with a cutoff value of 10 m/s for predicting atherosclerosis [6].

Atherosclerosis is closely related to lipid abnormalities, which often manifest as abnormalities in total cholesterol, triglycerides (TGs), and high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‐C) [7]. Recent research has found that the predictive value of abnormal single lipid indicators for cardiovascular diseases is limited. This indicates that these residual cardiovascular risks need to be reflected in combination with other different lipid indicators [8, 9]. The comprehensive lipid indicators proposed in recent years mainly include the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP). The AIP, proposed by Dobiásová [10], is a novel sensitive indicator that can predict lipid metabolic disorders. AIP can serve as a biomarker for early diagnosis of cardiovascular events [11]. In the Mexican population, AIP was also found to be closely related to cardiovascular risk factors and can be used as a useful tool for the diagnosis and prognosis of cardiovascular diseases [12]. AIP is independently associated with coronary heart disease and can be used for the prediction of coronary heart disease, with higher clinical significance than traditional single lipid indicators [13, 14].

Some studies have suggested a correlation between AIP and baPWV. A study conducted on 3468 healthy Korean subjects without any metabolic or cardiovascular diseases indicated that as the quartiles of AIP increased, metabolic parameters progressively deteriorated, and baPWV increased [15]. There was a strong correlation between AIP and baPWV. Higher AIP was identified as an independent predictor of increased arterial stiffness in both Korean men and women. AIP may serve as a simple tool for screening subclinical atherosclerosis. Researchers have found that AIP is positively correlated with arterial stiffness in Chinese patients with primary hypertension, especially in individuals with a body mass index (BMI) <24 kg/m2 [16]. However, the indicators used for diagnosing atherosclerosis in these studies were baPWV, not cfPWV. Currently, there are limited reports on the relationship between AIP and cfPWV. This article aims to explore the relationship between AIP and cfPWV in adults and to analyze the connection between AIP and atherosclerosis.

Materials and Methods

Inclusion Criteria

Data from the Fuzhou Study (registration number: ChiCTR2000039448) were selected from January 2016 to January 2020. A total of 1398 subjects were included after applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The database, established in January 2016, contains records of 3470 subjects, from which 2072 were excluded due to incomplete records or meeting the exclusion criteria.

Exclusion Criteria

(1) Use of statins or other lipid‐lowering medications; (2) confirmed or suspected secondary hypertension (obstructive sleep apnea hypopnea syndrome, primary aldosteronism with a plasma aldosterone/plasma renin activity ratio > 30, Cushing's syndrome, renal artery stenosis, aortic stenosis, pheochromocytoma); (3) severe arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, frequent ventricular premature beats, sick sinus syndrome, sinus arrest, third‐degree atrioventricular block, etc.), severe heart failure (NYHA class IV, acute exacerbation of chronic heart failure), cardiomyopathy (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, dilated cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, etc.); (4) post–coronary stent implantation; (5) history of stroke within 3 months; (6) current malignant tumor; (7) acute infection (pulmonary infection, urinary tract infection, etc.); (8) hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism; (9) levels of alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, or total bilirubin more than three times the upper limit of normal; (10) chronic kidney disease stage 4 or higher; and (11) severe anemia.

Data Collection

General Data Collection

Data collection followed a standardized procedure, and all data collectors and recorders were properly trained. The database recorded the age, gender, hypertension history, other relevant medical history, and medication treatment status of all subjects. Height, weight, and waist circumference were measured by healthcare professionals for each subject to calculate the BMI, with the formula BMI = weight (kg)/height (m2).

Blood Pressure and Heart Rate Measurement

Measurements were taken in the same examination room, in a quiet and temperature‐controlled environment, by the same healthcare professional. Hypertension was diagnosed when the systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥140 and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥90 mmHg on multiple nonconsecutive days without the use of antihypertensive medications. Additionally, subjects with a history of hypertension who are currently taking antihypertensive medications are also diagnosed with hypertension even if their blood pressure is <140/90 mmHg.

Collection of Biochemical Indicators

All subjects had fasting blood samples drawn in the early morning after an 8‐h fast. The same P800 automatic biochemical analyzer was used to measure blood urea nitrogen by the urease method, serum creatinine (Scr) by enzymatic method, uric acid by oxidation enzymatic method, fasting blood glucose, total cholesterol, TGs, and HDL‐C, which were calculated using selective blocking method. Cystatin C was determined by immunoturbidimetry, homocysteine by enzyme cycling method, and glycosylated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) by high‐performance liquid chromatography. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the CKD‐EPI formula: eGFR = a × (Scr/b) c × (0.993)age, where “a” is 144 for females and 141 for males; “b” is 0.7 for females and 0.9 for males; “c” is −0.329 for females with Scr ≤0.7 mg/dL (1 mg/dL = 88.4 µmol/L) and −1.209 for Scr > 0.7 mg/dL, and −0.411 for males with Scr ≤ 0.7 mg/dL and −1.209 for Scr > 0.7 mg/dL.

Calculation of AIP

AIP was calculated as the logarithm (base 10) of the TG to HDL‐C ratio.

Measurement of cfPWV

CfPWV was measured using the method adopted and described by our team [17]. A Complior Analysis automated atherosclerosis measurement device was used to measure cfPWV. Each measurement is required to meet the following three quality control standards: (1) obtaining a correct pulse wave shape with regular waveform, smooth curve, and stable baseline; (2) the error value of 10 measurement values within 1%; (3) a stable count of 10 paired pulse waves. Generally, two measurements were taken, and the average value was used. If the results of the two measurements differed by more than 0.5 m/s, a third measurement was taken, and the average of the three measurements was used as the cfPWV for that subject.

Statistical Methods

Data processing was performed using SPSS 22.0. Quantitative data were first tested for normal distribution and homoscedasticity. Data that met the normal distribution criteria were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (χ ± s), and differences between groups were assessed using independent sample t‐tests. Non‐normally distributed quantitative data were expressed as medians (interquartile range) and compared between groups using nonparametric rank sum tests. Categorical data were expressed as percentages, and group comparisons were made using chi‐square tests. Single‐factor correlation analysis was performed for factors that might affect cfPWV, with Pearson's correlation analysis for normally distributed continuous variables and Spearman's rank correlation analysis for non‐normally distributed continuous variables and binary variables. Stepwise multiple linear regression was used to analyze the factors independently associated with cfPWV, and binary logistic regression was used to analyze the influencing factors of cfPWV grouping. A difference was considered statistically significant at p value < 0.05.

Results

Basic Data of cfPWV Normal and Elevated Groups

A total of 1398 subjects meeting the inclusion criteria were enrolled in this study (mean age 58.24 years, age range 18–92 years, male to female ratio of 1.39:1). The subjects were categorized into the cfPWV normal group (991 cases) and the cfPWV elevated group (407 cases) based on a cfPWV cutoff point of 10 m/s. The results are as follows: Compared with the cfPWV normal group, the cfPWV elevated group had a higher percentage of hypertension patients and males (p < 0.05). AIP, age, BMI, waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, DBP, heart rate, blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, uric acid, fasting blood glucose, Cystatin C, homocysteine, TGs, and HbA1c levels were all significantly increased (p < 0.05), while total cholesterol, HDL‐C, and eGFR levels were significantly decreased (p < 0.05). Stepwise Multiple Linear Regression of AIP and cfPWV

A stepwise multiple linear regression was performed with cfPWV as the dependent variable for all subjects, and AIP as the independent variable. The results indicated that AIP, SBP, age, waist circumference, heart rate, homocysteine, uric acid, and HbA1c were identified as independent factors associated with increased cfPWV (p < 0.05).

Binary Logistic Regression Analysis of AIP in Relation to cfPWV Grouping

In this study, a binary logistic regression analysis was conducted with cfPWV as the dependent variable, categorized based on the threshold of ≤10 m/s (coded as 0) or ≥10 m/s (coded as 1). The results indicated that while AIP increased, the prevalence of cfPWV ≥10 m/s also rose significantly (odds ratio [OR] = 18.291, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 11.102–30.133, p < 0.05), suggesting that AIP was a contributing factor to arterial stiffness. Odds ratios were statistically significant even after adjusting for confounding variables, including the history of hypertension, age, and gender, both of which are well‐known risk factors for the development of arterial stiffness (odds ratio [OR] = 17.528, 95% CI = 10.161–30.236, p < 0.05). In a full adjusted model including BMI, waist circumference, SBP, DBP, heart rate, blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, uric acid, fasting blood glucose, cystatin C, homocysteine, and HbA1c, AIP remained a significant factor associated with arterial stiffness (odds ratio [i = 9.578, 95% CI = 5.299–17.312, p < 0.05).

ROC Curve Analysis of AIP in Relation to cfPWV Grouping

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive ability of AIP for cfPWV, with cfPWV serving as the reference standard. The results are as follows: The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for AIP was 0.697 (95% CI: 0.667–0.728, p < 0.05), indicating that AIP has a moderate discriminatory power for identifying individuals with elevated cfPWV. The Youden index was used to determine the critical point for AIP, which was found to be 0.00. This means that when AIP > 0.00, there is an indication of a trend towards elevated cfPWV, with a sensitivity of 76.2% and a specificity of 54.3%. Detailed results are presented in Figure 1.

A stepwise multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to assess the relationship between AIP and cfPWV. The results are as follows: In the cfPWV normal normotensive group, age, DBP, uric acid, heart rate, and homocysteine were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV normal hypertensive group, SBP, age, and HbA1c were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated normotensive group, age was the independent associated factor for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated hypertensive group, age and SBP were the independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05) (Table S2).

Subjects were categorized into cfPWV normal and elevated groups using a cutoff of 10 m/s for cfPWV and then stratified by gender. The results are as follows: In the cfPWV normal female group, SBP, age, heart rate, and homocysteine were independent associated factors for cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV normal male group, age, SBP, and DBP were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated female group, age and HbA1c were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated male group, age and HbA1c were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05) (Table S3).

Following Stratification by cfPWV and Further by Age, a Stepwise Multiple Linear Regression Analysis of AIP With cfPWV was Conducted

After categorizing all subjects into cfPWV normal and cfPWV elevated groups using a cutoff of 10 m/s for cfPWV, and then stratifying by age with a cutoff of 65 years (those younger than 65 were considered middle‐aged and younger, and those 65 and older were considered elderly), stepwise multiple linear regression analysis of AIP with cfPWV was performed. The results are as follows: In the cfPWV normal middle‐aged and younger group, AIP, SBP, male gender, heart rate, and DBP were independent associated factors for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV normal elderly group, SBP was the independent associated factor for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated middle‐aged and younger group, SBP was the independent associated factor for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05); in the cfPWV elevated elderly group, SBP was the independent associated factor for the elevation of cfPWV (p < 0.05) (Table S4).

Discussion

This study analyzed data from the Fuzhou research database to investigate the relationship between AIP and cfPWV in adults. The findings suggest that, in addition to age, SBP, heart rate, uric acid, homocysteine, and HbA1c, AIP is also an independent correlate of increased cfPWV. However, age and blood pressure were identified as the most significant factors influencing the elevation of cfPWV. Elevated AIP levels have a certain predictive value in the early detection of atherosclerosis.

Atherosclerosis and arteriosclerosis are two different types of vascular diseases. Both can lead to serious cardiovascular issues, but they have different pathological mechanisms and clinical manifestations. Atherosclerosis is a specific type of arteriosclerosis mainly related to lipid metabolism disorders. It is characterized by the accumulation of lipids, complex sugars, calcium, and other substances in the arterial intima, forming plaques known as atherosclerotic plaques. This condition can cause the arterial walls to thicken and harden, narrowing the arterial lumen and thus reducing or blocking the flow of blood to organs and tissues. Atherosclerosis is a major cause of cardiovascular diseases, such as coronary artery disease, stroke, and peripheral arterial disease. Arteriosclerosis is a broader term referring to the hardening of the arterial walls, which can be caused by a variety of factors. This article focuses on atherosclerosis related to lipid metabolism disorders. Various indirect noninvasive methods are currently available for measuring arterial stiffness, with cfPWV being the recognized gold standard for diagnosing atherosclerosis. Higher cfPWV values indicate a more severe degree of atherosclerosis. Patients with both cholesterol abnormalities and hypertension have a 3−4 times higher risk of cardiovascular disease [18].

AIP, the logarithmic transformation of the TG/HDL‐C ratio, provides a good assessment of the body's lipid metabolism function. This study found that the AIP values in the group with elevated cfPWV were higher than those in the normal cfPWV group (p < 0.05), and AIP values were positively correlated with cfPWV (r = 0.293, p < 0.05). In stepwise multiple linear regression analysis, increased AIP was an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV (β = 0.152, p < 0.05). Further binary logistic regression analysis indicated that increased AIP is a contributing factor to atherosclerosis. These results are consistent with the findings of Choudhary et al. [19], suggesting that AIP can serve as an indicator for predicting early changes in atherosclerosis. Research both domestically and internationally has shown that AIP can reflect the severity of coronary artery disease, and compared to other single lipid indicators, AIP can better predict the risk of coronary heart disease, providing a basis for the prognosis and treatment of coronary heart disease [20, 21]. AIP has been proven to be closely related to atherosclerosis indicators such as small and dense low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (sdLDL‐C). AIP can indirectly reflect the size of sdLDL‐C particle diameter, and there is a negative correlation between AIP and the diameter of sdLDL‐C particles. This serves as an alternative indirect method to reflect the particle size of low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol, meaning that the higher the AIP value, the smaller the sdLDL‐C particle diameter, and the greater the likelihood of atherosclerosis [22]. SdLDL‐C, a subtype of the low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol subfraction, has better predictive value in the occurrence and progression of atherosclerosis. However, the determination of sdLDL‐C is complex and expensive, and it cannot be routinely performed in clinical settings [23]. In contrast, AIP, as a comprehensive lipid indicator that indirectly reflects sdLDL‐C, can be calculated from routine lipid profiles, offering certain clinical advantages.

In this study, the results of the univariate correlation analysis showed that AIP was positively correlated with cfPWV, but the correlation coefficients were not high. Traditional risk factors for atherosclerosis, such as age and blood pressure, also exhibited only a mild‐to‐moderate correlation with cfPWV. This may be due to the fact that atherosclerosis is a pathological condition caused by the interaction of multiple factors. Therefore, AIP should be combined with other indicators such as age and blood pressure to assess the extent of atherosclerosis. The ROC curve analysis results indicated that the critical values for AIP determined by the Youden index were 0.00, respectively. When AIP > 0.00, there is an increased likelihood of elevated cfPWV (with a sensitivity of 76.2% and specificity of 54.3%). This study, being a small‐sample cross‐sectional study, cannot establish a causal relationship between increased AIP and atherosclerosis. Further research with larger samples and prospective intervention studies is needed to clarify whether AIP is a risk factor for atherosclerosis and to determine the differences in clinical application value between this indicator.

In this study, the age of the group with elevated cfPWV was significantly higher than that of the normal cfPWV group. Spearman's correlation analysis showed a positive correlation between age and cfPWV, and multivariate linear regression results indicated that age is an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV with a relatively high correlation coefficient, suggesting a close relationship between age and atherosclerosis. Previous research by our team showed that with increasing age, the arteries experience a reduction in elasticity due to long‐term pulsation, leading to varying degrees of structural and functional damage to the blood vessels [24]. This is consistent with the conclusions drawn by Kim et al. [25] and Meani P et al. [26], which state that age is a major determinant of atherosclerosis, and the main factors determining vascular wall elasticity are the content of elastin fibers and collagen in the vessel wall. As age increases, the collagen and elastin fibers in the arterial wall gradually decrease, leading to a reduction in vascular wall elasticity and the development of atherosclerosis. Related studies have also shown that with increasing age, the elasticity of the large arteries decreases, leading to atherosclerosis, which is manifested by an increasing PWV [27]. In a study by Vriz et al. [28], it was found that even in individuals without hypertension or other cardiovascular diseases, arterial stiffness increases with age. This demonstrates that regardless of the presence of cardiovascular disease, increasing age is closely associated with atherosclerosis.

Hypertension and hyperlipidemia, as common risk factors for atherosclerosis, exert synergistic effects in the formation process of atherosclerotic lesions. Long‐term hypertension can affect the vascular intima, leading to endothelial damage; the promotion of lipid deposition; and an increase in elastic fibers, collagen fibers, and smooth muscle cells, ultimately resulting in atherosclerosis [29]. In this study, compared to the normal cfPWV group, the percentage of hypertensive patients and the levels of SBP were significantly higher in the group with elevated cfPWV. Univariate correlation analysis suggested a positive correlation between hypertension and SBP with cfPWV. In multivariate linear regression, SBP was an independent correlate of cfPWV, increased blood pressure can cause vascular damage, thereby accelerating the formation of atherosclerosis, leading to a decrease in the elasticity of large arteries and an increase in cfPWV.

In this study, it was found that the heart rate in the group with elevated cfPWV was higher than in the normal cfPWV group. Spearman's correlation analysis showed a positive correlation between heart rate and cfPWV, and multivariate linear regression analysis indicated that increased heart rate is an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV. In a study by Tan et al. [30] on cardiac pacing in the elderly, it was shown that acute changes in heart rate can affect cfPWV, with an increase of 0.16–0.20 m/s in cfPWV for every 10 beats/min increase in heart rate. Additionally, foreign studies have also shown that an increased resting heart rate is not only an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease but also a risk factor that exacerbates and worsens cardiovascular disease [31]. An increased heart rate leads to a higher frequency of vascular pulsations, with the vessels being under high tension for extended periods, resulting in reduced elasticity of the arterial wall's elastic fibers and subsequent endothelial damage [32]. Therefore, controlling heart rate is of significant clinical importance in preventing and treating cardiovascular diseases.

Elevated levels of uric acid can activate leukocytes to adhere to endothelial cells, promote inflammation, and thereby accelerate the formation of atherosclerosis [33]. In this study, the uric acid levels were higher in the group with elevated cfPWV compared to the normal cfPWV group. Univariate correlation analysis suggested a positive correlation between uric acid and cfPWV, and multivariate linear regression analysis identified increased uric acid as an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV. This is consistent with the research findings by Kivity et al. [34] regarding the relationship between uric acid and cardiovascular diseases.

In this study, the levels of homocysteine were higher in the group with elevated cfPWV compared to the normal cfPWV group. Spearman's correlation analysis revealed a positive correlation between homocysteine and cfPWV, and multivariate linear regression analysis identified homocysteine as an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV. These findings are consistent with previous research by our team [24], which suggests that lowering homocysteine can delay the progression of atherosclerosis and reduce cardiovascular events to some extent. Studies have shown that supplementing the diet with folic acid and vitamin B12 can reduce homocysteine levels and subsequently lower the risk of complications related to atherosclerosis [35].

Abnormal sugar metabolism can affect the structure of elastin and collagen fibers in the vascular wall, thereby influencing the occurrence and progression of cardiovascular diseases. High blood sugar and insulin resistance can promote the formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs), and higher levels of AGEs can enhance collagen cross‐linking, ultimately reducing the elasticity of the vascular wall and increasing arterial stiffness [36]. In this study, it was found that the levels of HbA1c were higher in the group with elevated cfPWV compared to the normal cfPWV group. Univariate correlation analysis indicated a positive correlation between HbA1c and cfPWV, and multivariate linear regression analysis showed that increased HbA1c is an independent correlate of elevated cfPWV. Previous research by our team [37] also found that HbA1c is independently associated with PWV.

As is well known, blood pressure and age play a crucial role in the development of atherosclerosis. This study also found in the stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis of all subjects that blood pressure and age had the highest regression coefficients with cfPWV, while the regression coefficients of AIP with cfPWV were relatively lower. This suggests that although AIP is independent correlates of elevated cfPWV, their correlation is not as strong as that of blood pressure and age. To further exclude the influence of blood pressure and age, this study stratified all subjects by cfPWV and then by the presence or absence of hypertension, age, and gender. The results showed that regardless of whether they were in the normal blood pressure subgroup, hypertension subgroup, middle‐aged and young subgroup, elderly subgroup, female subgroup, or male subgroup, there was no significant regression relationship between AIP and elevated cfPWV. In all strata, blood pressure and age were still the most significant factors affecting the elevation of cfPWV. Therefore, regardless of age or gender, attention should be particularly paid to blood pressure. Additionally, in the blood pressure normal group with normal cfPWV, DBP had a greater impact on cfPWV than SBP, indicating that in individuals with normal blood pressure, DBP should be given more attention. Despite numerous studies both domestically and internationally reporting that AIP has certain predictive value for the occurrence and progression of cardiovascular events, the results of this study indicate that the impact of AIP on cfPWV is significantly less than that of blood pressure and age. The reasons for this difference may be due to variations in the study populations, such as whether the study includes hypertensive patients, the age range, and geographical living areas. In summary, blood pressure and age are the most significant factors influencing the elevation of cfPWV, and since age is an uncontrollable factor for individuals, we should actively control blood pressure.

In conclusion, in adults, in addition to age, blood pressure status, heart rate, uric acid, homocysteine, and HbA1c, AIP is also independent correlate of elevated cfPWV. However, the most significant factors influencing the elevation of cfPWV are age and blood pressure. Elevated AIP has a certain predictive value in the early screening for atherosclerosis. Therefore, in clinical practice, in addition to focusing on conventional single lipid indicators, we should also pay attention to comprehensive lipid indicators. By monitoring the values of AIP, we can identify individuals with early atherosclerotic changes and take intervention measures as early as possible, such as adjusting dietary structure, improving lifestyle, and strictly managing blood pressure, in order to prevent or delay the occurrence and progression of cardiovascular diseases.

Limitations of this study include the following: (1) Confounding factors such as medication use (antihypertensive drugs, hypoglycemic drugs, heart rate‐lowering drugs, etc.), the duration of hypertension and other diseases, and personal lifestyle habits of the observational subjects may bias the study results. (2) The subjects included in the study were mostly outpatients or inpatients, with a majority being middle‐aged and elderly, and there were differences in the gender composition, which may have introduced selection bias. (3) This study is cross‐sectional and cannot establish whether there is a causal relationship between increased AIP and atherosclerosis. Prospective studies are needed to further clarify this relationship.

Introduction: Neurodevelopment and Neurodegeneration
Authors: Elaine Yang, Devin Juros
Published: 2026

Neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration consist of lifelong processes that push and pull on each other to make us who we are. In the March 2026 issue of the Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine (YJBM), our goal is to research these phenomena and bring them into conversation. We are delighted to present 12 publications, each representing one of the many threads in this growing field.

To begin, this issue captures a snapshot in the evolution of neuroimmunology. Combining a review with an original in silico analysis, Gallardo-Pérez et al. investigate the role of microRNAs as regulators of cytokine signaling in multiple sclerosis. Turning to a different neuroinflammatory disease, Chesters explores the possibility of using the tau protein as a marker for chronic traumatic encephalopathy. Liu et al. pull together these projects with a broad examination of the interplay between immune cells and the nervous system throughout neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration.

A related subtheme emerging from this issue involves the promise of neuroprotective chemical compounds. Akyildiz Demir and Gürbüz Çolak use a computational approach to evaluate flavonoids with therapeutic potential in the treatment of astrogliosis. In a mouse model of Parkinson’s disease, Ali et al. identify multiple neuroprotective effects of one particular flavonoid, chrysoeriol. Illustrating the impact these research strategies may eventually have at the bedside, Thomas Mathew et al. highlight a rare case of spinal dysraphism — noting the importance of folic acid supplementation for preventing neural tube defects.

Early influences on the nervous system include genetics and epigenetics, as seen in the story shared by Yıldız Bölükbaşı et al. of siblings with dysfunction of the PTRHD1 gene in the setting of intellectual disability and atypical parkinsonism. Dukle et al. compare the effects of three mouse models of prenatal stress on the brain to probe the circumstances defining neurodevelopment from a different angle. The sex differences noted in these studies resonate with the review by Galbraith et al. which points out how gonadotropins shape sex-specific cognitive and neurological aging.

The application of a wider lens brings to light how foundational relationships influence neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration. Tackling the universal language of music, Moret et al. break down both musical abilities that become impaired as well as those that are preserved in people with dementia. With a focus on a tertiary-care center in Pakistan, Fatima et al. reveal how limited healthcare resources interact with the epidemiology of neurological disorders. At the societal level, Stouffer and Kumar demonstrate the connection between homelessness and neurodegeneration, opening up the conversation about developing more effective models of care.

Just as one neuron is intricately connected with others throughout the nervous system, this issue required much collaboration. None of this work would be possible without the research subjects. In addition, we extend our deepest gratitude to our editorial team, our peer reviewers, and our readers as well as everyone who engaged us in academic discourse. May this issue spark new connections building upon neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration research around the world.

Without further ado, we present to you: Neurodevelopment and Neurodegeneration.

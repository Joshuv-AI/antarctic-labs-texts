A novel severe toxicity-free, and progression-free survival endpoint predicts outcomes after CD19 chimeric antigen receptor-T cell therapy in large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Aditi Saha, Junmin Whiting, Razan Mohty, Kristen Flores, Jongphil Kim, Taiga Nishihori, Julio Chavez, Sameh Gaballa, Farhad Khimani, Hien Liu, Bijal Shah, Marian Dam, Muhammad Jaffer, Sepideh Mokhtari, Nima Ghalehsari, Hany Elmariah, Christina Bachmeier, Jose Alejandro Guevara, Heather Jim, Sayeef Mirza, Brahm H Segal, Doris K Hansen, Nelli Bejanyan, Fabiana Perna, Rawan Faramand, Michael Jain, Frederick Locke, Aleksandr Lazaryan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCD19-directed chimeric antigen receptor-T cell (CAR-T) therapies have transformed treatment for relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL), yet heterogeneity in toxicity and efficacy persists. To address this, we developed a novel composite endpoint: severe toxicity-free and progression-free survival at 6 months (TPFS6), defined as absence of severe CRS, ICANS, progressive/stable disease (PD/SD), and non-relapse mortality (NRM) within 6 months of CAR-T therapy. In our cohort, 37% achieved TPFS6. Events contributing to TPFS6 failure included severe CRS (6.8%), ICANS (30.3%), NRM (1.7%), and PD/SD (61.2%). Multivariable analysis demonstrated female sex (p = 0.01), ECOG < 2 (p = 0.05), and lower LDH and CRP (both p = 0.004) predicted TPFS6. With a median follow-up of 36.2 months, the estimated 2-year OS and PFS were 54.5% and 39.6%, respectively. Landmark analysis showed TPFS6 was associated with improved OS (HR = 0.19; 95% CI: 0.11–0.35; p < 0.001) and PFS (HR = 0.33; 95% CI: 0.16–0.67; p = 0.002). CAR-T product type was not significantly associated with OS but was with PFS (HR = 3.09; 95% CI: 1.27–7.50; p = 0.013). TPFS6 remained prognostic for OS and PFS even after accounting for PD/SD, underscoring the impact of severe toxicity on subsequent survival. TPFS6 is a clinically meaningful endpoint integrating efficacy and safety and may predict long-term outcomes following CAR-T therapy.Subject terms: B-cell lymphoma, Medical research

Introduction

In the rapidly evolving field of cancer therapeutics, CD19-directed chimeric antigen receptor-T cell (CAR-T) therapies have emerged as a groundbreaking treatment for relapsed or refractory (Rel/Ref) large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL). Axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel), tisagenlecleucel (tisa-cel), and lisocabtagene maraleucel (liso-cel) have each received approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of Rel/Ref LBCL [1–5]. The efficacy of these products is underscored by their ability to induce durable remissions in 30–40% of patients. However, despite its promising efficacy, this novel treatment is associated with adverse events - including cytokine release syndrome (CRS), immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), infection and cytopenias - which can result in significant morbidity and mortality.

The success of CAR-T products is primarily evaluated based on their efficacy and associated toxicity profiles. As these therapies have become integral to clinical practice, a complex and dynamic picture has emerged from pivotal trials and real-world data, revealing notable heterogeneity in both efficacy and toxicity among available products. Real-world studies comparing axi-cel and tisa-cel have demonstrated superior efficacy of axi-cel in Rel/Ref LBCL, but also highlighted increased toxicity and higher rates of non-relapse mortality (NRM) associated with its use [6–8]. In contrast, studies comparing axi-cel to liso-cel have shown comparable efficacy with durable responses, but a lower incidence of toxicities such as CRS and ICANS with liso-cel [9–11].

Ongoing investigational efforts are directed toward novel strategies for toxicity mitigation and efficacy enhancement. However, the differences in reported toxicity and efficacy profiles of these three FDA-approved products challenge their large-scale comparison. As clinical experience with CAR-T therapy has expanded, it has become evident that both toxicities and efficacy outcomes of CAR-T are interconnected. High tumor burden, tumor microenvironment with interferon gamma (IFN) gene overexpression, systemic inflammation with high levels of ferritin, C-reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) were found to be associated with both a lack of durable response and a higher grade of CRS and ICANS [12–15]. To the best of our knowledge, at the time of our initial reports describing TPFS after CAR-T therapy [16–19], there was no validated composite endpoint designed to jointly capture treatment efficacy and severe toxicity, a concept that has since been extended in subsequent registry-based analyses [20]. To address this gap, we developed and evaluated a novel composite endpoint of severe toxicity-free and progression-free survival at 6 months (TPFS6) following CAR-T therapy for patients with LBCL.

An ideal composite endpoint should evaluate both survival outcomes and the occurrence of severe adverse effects, along with comprehensively assessing the efficacy and safety of novel therapeutic interventions [21, 22]. In allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation, composite endpoints, such as graft-versus-host disease–free, relapse-free survival (GRFS), were originally introduced to integrate efficacy and treatment-related morbidity into a single clinically meaningful outcome and have since evolved to serve as primary endpoints in practice-changing clinical trials, including recent randomized studies of post-transplant cyclophosphamide [22, 23]. Our proposed composite measure aims to capture clinically meaningful indicators of treatment success, including the absence of major adverse events—such as severe CRS and ICANS- as well as NRM and disease relapse within the first 6 months following treatment. Importantly, the 6-month response after CAR-T therapy is a widely recognized milestone for LBCL and it serves as a strong prognostic indicator for long-term survival [24, 25]. Therefore, this composite endpoint may offer a robust and clinically relevant framework for evaluating the outcomes of CAR-T therapy.

Methods

Study design

In this single-center retrospective study, the primary objective was to assess TPFS6 among CAR-T therapy recipients with Rel/Ref LBCL. Secondary objectives included identifying clinical factors predictive of TPFS6 and evaluating the impact of TPFS6 on overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Inclusion criteria for this study were adult patients (≥18 years of age) diagnosed with LBCL or its variants who underwent autologous CD19-directed CAR-T therapy using axi-cel, tisa-cel, or liso-cel. Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval was obtained prior to conducting the study. Patient demographic information, diagnoses, and treatment regimens were collected retrospectively through detailed chart review.

Composite endpoint structure and definitions

TPFS6 events were defined as the occurrence of any of the following within 6 months of CAR-T infusion: severe (grade ≥3) CRS, severe (grade ≥3) ICANS, failure to achieve complete or partial response, and NRM. Only the first autologous CAR-T infusion was included in the analysis. The composite endpoint was evaluated using a time-to-event approach, measuring the time to the first occurring qualifying TPFS6 event within 6 months following CAR-T infusion. When multiple qualifying events occurred in a single patient, only the earliest event was counted for the purpose of TPFS6. Both CRS and ICANS were graded according to the American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (ASTCT) consensus grading system including retrospective application where necessary. Laboratory thresholds were defined as follows: lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) ≥ 225 U/L, based on the upper limit of normal at our institution; C-reactive protein (CRP) ≥ 4 mg/dL; and ferritin ≥ 400 ng/mL, both measured at the time of lymphodepletion (LD) and based on previously published data [14].

Statistical analysis

Demographic and clinical characteristics were summarized as medians with ranges for continuous variables, and as frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. Associations between TPFS6 and categorical variables were assessed using Fisher’s exact test or Chi-square test, whereas continuous variables were analyzed by the Kruskal-Wallis test. Multivariable logistic regression models with odds ratio (OR) were constructed to evaluate the association between TPFS6 and relevant clinical covariates within first 6 months following CAR-T therapy. The Kaplan–Meier (KM) method was used to estimate TPFS6 and to evaluate its association with subsequent OS and PFS using a 6-month landmark analysis.

Patients were classified based on the occurrence of any TPFS6-defining event within 6 months after CAR-T infusion. Patients who experienced NRM or died before the 6-month landmark (including deaths due to progressive disease) were excluded by definition, as they were no longer at risk for subsequent survival outcomes. Survival outcomes were subsequently assessed from the 6-month landmark forward among evaluable patients. Cox proportional hazards models were applied to assess the relationship between prognostic variables and time-to-event endpoints in the absence of competing risks. Competing risk analyses were conducted using Fine-Gray regression models, with death considered a competing risk for relapse and vice versa. Model development began by including variables with a p-value < 0.25 in univariable analyses (UVA) followed by backward elimination using a p-stay threshold of 0.1 in multivariable analysis (MVA) to derive a parsimonious final model. The variables retained in the final model, therefore, represent those independently associated with post-landmark survival outcomes. All analyses were conducted on complete cases, as variables included in the final models had no missing data. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC) and R (version 4.0.2), with two-sided p-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

This single-center retrospective study included 282 consecutive patients treated at Moffitt Cancer Center between 2015 and 2022. Of these, 228 (80.9%) received axi-cel, 47 (16.7%) received tisa-cel, and 7 (2.5%) received liso-cel. The median age at the time of CAR-T therapy was 64 years (range, 19–86), and 170 patients (60.3%) were male. The majority of the patients were Caucasian (243, 86.2%), and 237 patients (84.0%) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1 at the time of apheresis (Table 1). Of the total cohort, 209 patients (74.1%) had de novo LBCL, while 73 patients (25.9%) had transformed LBCL. At initial diagnosis, 51 patients (26%) had double- or triple-hit lymphoma by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). At the time of apheresis, 228 patients (80.9%) had stage III or IV disease. Bulky disease, defined as a mass >10 cm, was present in 40 patients (14.2%), and 115 patients (40.8%) had involvement of two or more extra nodal sites. Central nervous system involvement was reported in 16 patients (5.7%).

A total of 119 patients (42.3%) had received more than two prior lines of therapy, and 50 patients (17.7%) had undergone autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) prior to CAR-T therapy. The median time from apheresis to CAR-T infusion was 28 days (range: 20–80 days). Prior to infusion, 176 patients (63.3%) received bridging therapy, which included chemotherapy, corticosteroids, CD20 monoclonal antibodies, radiation, and Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitors in various combinations. The overall response rate (ORR) to bridging therapy was 10.6%; however, response was evaluable in only 57 patients (20.4%) prior to CAR- T therapy. At the time of LD, 74 patients (26.2%) had CRP ≥ 4 mg/dL, 150 (53.2%) had ferritin ≥400 ng/mL, and 189 (67.0%) had LDH ≥ 225 U/L.

CAR-T efficacy and toxicities

In the overall cohort, the ORR at 6 months was 48% [45% complete response (CR) and 3% partial response (PR)]. Any grade of CRS and ICANS occurred in 245 patients (86.9%) and 155 patients (55.0%), respectively. Grade ≥3 CRS was observed in 16 patients (5.7%) and grade ≥3 ICANS was observed in 62 patients (22.0%). A total of 138 patients (48.9%) died due to either disease progression or CAR-T related toxicities, including 31 patients (11.0%) whose death was attributed to NRM.

TPFS6 and its determinants

The KM estimate for TPFS6 in our cohort was 37% (Fig. 1a), indicating that 37% of patients were alive at 6 months following CAR-T infusion without experiencing severe toxicity or disease progression. The major components of TPFS6, defined as the first occurrence of each event, are shown in Fig. 1b. These included PD/SD (61.2%), severe ICANS (30.3%), severe CRS (6.8%), and NRM (1.7%). Relapse or PD/SD represented the largest proportion of TPFS6 events whereas NRM accounted for three TPFS6-defining events (1.7%) and occurred within 6 months of CAR-T infusion due to respiratory failure from pneumonia, ischemic colitis, and gastrointestinal perforation. Although NRM comprised 11% of deaths overall, progression or severe toxicity typically preceded death, thereby limiting NRM’s contribution as a first TPFS6 event. The cohort was stratified into two groups: patients who experienced a TPFS6-defining event (hereafter referred to as TPFS6 failure) and those who did not (TPFS6).

In the UVA, TPFS6 was significantly associated with gender (p = 0.014), presence of bulky disease (p = 0.045), extranodal involvement (p = 0.009), ECOG performance status ≥2 (p = 0.004), prior ASCT (p = 0.015), elevated CRP (p < 0.001), ferritin (p < 0.001), and LDH (p < 0.001) at the time of LD, as detailed in Table 1. Other variables- including age, race, number of prior lines of therapy, LBCL subtype, disease stage, double/triple-hit status, and CNS involvement - did not differ significantly between groups (all p > 0.1). In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, male gender (OR = 2.02, p = 0.010), elevated CRP (OR = 2.99, p = 0.004), and elevated LDH (OR = 2.29, p = 0.004) were independently associated with inferior TPFS6 outcomes (Table 2). TPFS6 was not significantly associated with the type of CAR-T product (Supplementary Fig. 1).

TPFS6 and long-term survival outcomes

With a median follow-up of 36.2 months among surviving patients (Q1-Q3:24.5-44.6 months), the estimated 2-year OS and PFS for the entire cohort were 54.5% and 39.6%, respectively. The median OS was 34.3 months (95% CI: 17.1–not reached), and median PFS was 6.0 months (95% CI: 4.2–11.9). The CAR-T product type was not significantly associated with OS or PFS in UVA KM analysis (Supplementary Figs. 2–3). In a landmark MVA for PFS, TPFS6 was associated with improved outcomes [Hazard Ratio (HR) = 0.33; 95% CI: 0.16–0.67; p < 0.001]. Conversely, ECOG performance status ≥ 2 at LD [HR = 3.68; 95% CI: 1.57–8.61; p = 0.003] and use of tisa-cel [HR = 3.09; 95% CI: 1.27–7.50; p = 0.013] were associated with inferior PFS.

In MVA for OS, TPFS6 was associated with improved survival [HR = 0.19; 95% CI: 0.11–0.35; p < 0.001]. Male gender [HR = 1.92; 95% CI: 1.15–3.20; p = 0.013], ECOG ≥ 2 at LD [HR = 2.34; 95% CI: 1.31–4.19; p = 0.004], and elevated LDH at the time of LD [HR = 2.63; 95% CI: 1.51–4.59; p < 0.001] were associated with inferior OS.

No evaluated covariates, including TPFS6, were significantly associated with NRM in exploratory landmark analyses. The landmark KM analysis illustrating the impact of TPFS6 on OS and PFS are shown in Figs. 2–3. Landmark MVA results for OS and PFS at 6 months after CAR-T cell therapy are presented in Table 3. To evaluate whether the association between TPFS6 and long-term outcomes was driven primarily by early PD/SD, we performed a post hoc 6-month landmark multivariable analysis adjusting for PD/SD. TPFS6 remained strongly associated with both OS and PFS after this adjustment, indicating that its prognostic value extends beyond early disease progression alone (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

Our study established a novel composite endpoint, TPFS6, for CAR-T recipients with LBCL. TPFS6 integrates clinically significant events-including disease progression, severe ICANS, and CRS-that frequently occur within the first 6 months post-infusion and are known to impact survival outcomes. Our study found that only 37% of patients remained free of TPFS6-defining events during this period. Failure to achieve TPFS6 was significantly associated with known clinical risk factors, including CRP, LDH, ECOG status, and sex, but not with CAR-T product type. Although male sex was independently associated with inferior TPFS6, TPFS6 failures in our cohort were predominantly driven by early disease progression or non-response, suggesting that this association is more likely related to early disease control rather than severe toxicity alone. Consistent with this, TPFS6 was a strong predictor of both OS and PFS in our cohort.

Delayed response kinetics and response maturation are well recognized features of CAR-T therapy in LBCL, supporting the use of a later milestone when defining early composite endpoints. CR–anchored landmark analyses, including recent reports evaluating Day100 CR-based composite outcomes that integrate efficacy and severe toxicity, provide important prognostic information [20]; however, such frameworks do not capture patients who remain in PR at early landmarks but subsequently convert to CR between Day100 and 6 months. The integration of efficacy and toxicity into a composite early outcome measure was first described with the TPFS6 endpoint in Rel/Ref LBCL [16] and has since been extended in subsequent registry-based analyses using earlier CR-anchored timepoints. Accordingly, TPFS6 was intentionally defined at 6 months to provide a conservative and clinically validated composite endpoint that incorporates evolving efficacy alongside major toxicity and early mortality, addressing a distinct aspect of early treatment success.

The components of TPFS6 encompass key clinical events that significantly influence outcomes, as demonstrated in both clinical trials and real-world studies. Disease progression was the most frequent TPFS6-defining event in our cohort, followed by grade ≥3 ICANS, consistent with findings from prior major trials. In long-term analyses of ZUMA-1, JULIET, and TRANSCEND, the leading causes of morbidity and mortality were severe CRS, ICANS, and disease relapse or progression, with the most events occurring within the first 6 months and contributing to poor outcomes [26–28]. Although infrequent, NRM events were retained within TPFS6 because of their high clinical severity and irreversible impact on patient outcomes, supporting their relevance within a composite endpoint assessing early treatment success. Importantly, inclusion of severe CRS and ICANS in TPFS6 reflects their prognostic relevance as major early adverse events and markers of unfavorable biology and does not imply equivalence or equal patient-centered importance relative to disease relapse or progression. While PFS remains the preferred endpoint when the primary question is antitumor efficacy, TPFS6 was developed to address a distinct but complementary clinical question. Specifically, TPFS6 is most informative when an integrated assessment of early disease control and major treatment-related morbidity or mortality is required, such as in the evaluation of toxicity-mitigation strategies, supportive-care interventions, or novel approaches intended to reduce severe CRS or ICANS without compromising disease control. In this context, TPFS6 is best interpreted as an early “recovery” or “net clinical benefit” endpoint and should be reported alongside conventional efficacy endpoints, including PFS and the individual component event rates, rather than as a stand-alone substitute. Differences in TPFS6 between CAR-T products should be interpreted with caution and in conjunction with conventional efficacy endpoints. Although TPFS6 was not significantly associated with CAR-T product type in our cohort, whereas PFS favored axi-cel over tisa-cel in landmark analysis, this does not imply clinical equivalence between products. TPFS6 is not intended to replace product-specific efficacy comparisons or to serve as the sole basis for treatment selection; rather, it provides an additional framework for evaluating early net clinical benefit when treatment decisions involve trade-offs between disease control and severe toxicity, and should be interpreted alongside PFS, OS, and the individual component event rates.

The consistency between our findings and previously published real-world data - including the US CAR-T Consortium and Center for International Blood and Marrow Transplant Research (CIBMTR) analyses - further validates the clinical relevance of TPFS6. These studies similarly report high rates of early adverse events such as severe CRS, ICANS, and PD/SD, which predominantly occur within the first 6 months after CAR-T therapy and are strongly associated with poor outcomes [29–31]. By integrating these critical events into a single composite endpoint, TPFS6 offers a practical and prognostically meaningful measure of early treatment success.

Our discovered predictors of TPFS6 - gender, ECOG performance status, CRP, and LDH at the time of LD - align with previously established clinical risk factors for CAR-T therapy outcomes [1, 3, 31]. In ZUMA-1, elevated tumor burden and LDH were associated with poorer outcomes, while in JULIET, pre-infusion LDH levels predicted inferior response [13, 32]. Real-world data have similarly identified poor performance status, advanced-stage disease, multiple extranodal sites, and elevated inflammatory markers such as LDH and CRP as predictors of both toxicity and efficacy in CAR-T treated patients [31, 33–35]. More recent data further validated the association between poor performance status, elevated LDH, and CAR-T product type with the onset and severity of CRS and ICANS [36].

Notably, TPFS6 was not associated with CAR-T product type, which may reflect the limited sample size, and the predominance of axi-cel use within our cohort. Only a small subset of patients received tisa-cel or liso-cel, limiting comparative power. Nonetheless, our findings underscore that TPFS6 is strongly associated with established risk factors and predictive of long-term CAR-T outcomes, supporting its potential utility as a standardized early endpoint in CAR-T therapy. TPFS6 was a strong predictor of OS and PFS, independent of age, performance status, gender, LDH levels, and CAR-T product type. As reported previously, initial response to CAR-T therapy correlated with long-term remission [1, 26, 37]. In our post hoc analysis, TPFS6 remained significantly associated with OS and PFS even after adjustment for PD/SD at 6 months, underscoring that its effect is not solely driven by early disease progression but also reflects contributions from its other components. These findings further support the utility of TPFS6 as a balanced composite endpoint for evaluating both the efficacy and safety after CAR-T therapy. Consistent with this interpretation, among patients who failed TPFS6 due to severe CRS or ICANS but survived to the 6-month landmark, a meaningful proportion subsequently experienced relapse or progression or NRM, indicating that severe early toxicity identifies patients with sustained vulnerability to adverse disease outcomes beyond the acute treatment phase.

As clinical experience with CAR-T therapy expands, it is increasingly evident that efficacy and toxicity are interrelated not only clinically but also biologically. Therapeutic efficacy is often associated with CAR-T expansion [38] and some degree of toxicity is expected during this process [39]. Our findings support the concept that severe early toxicities may serve as markers of adverse inflammatory and tumor-related biology that predisposes to subsequent disease relapse, even among patients who remain progression-free at early landmark time points. Consistent with this framework, we previously demonstrated that high tumor burden and impaired CAR-T expansion were associated with increased tumor interferon signaling, which in turn correlated with macrophage gene expression and inferior response to axi-cel [12]. Balancing efficacy with toxicity management remains a major challenge in CAR-T therapy. Current investigations aim to optimize CAR-T therapy by developing strategies that enhance efficacy while mitigating toxicity. Investigational approaches targeting JAK1/2 or BTK inhibition have been explored with the goal of mitigating severe CRS or ICANS while preserving antitumor efficacy [40–45]. These strategies highlight the value of composite endpoints such as TPFS6 that capture early net clinical benefit across both toxicity and disease control domains, particularly when interpreted alongside conventional efficacy endpoints and component-specific outcomes. Additionally, more refined comparisons among CAR-T products will be increasingly important as therapeutic options expand. In this context, TPFS6 may serve as a complementary framework for comparing early clinical benefit across therapeutic strategies, while ongoing validation in larger cohorts will be important to strengthen its generalizability. As discussed above, ongoing advances in CAR-T toxicity mitigation strategies, including pharmacologic modulation of inflammatory signaling pathways, are expected to further reduce the incidence and severity of CRS and ICANS. As a result, the discriminatory contribution of toxicity within TPFS6 may diminish over time, reflecting continued evolution in CAR-T clinical practice.

Despite offering valuable insights into a novel composite endpoint for CAR-T therapy, our study has several limitations, including those inherent to its retrospective design and the anticipated challenges of using composite endpoints in clinical research. Relapse or progression of LBCL following CAR-T therapy is associated with poor prognosis, with a median OS of only 6 months [46]. In the same study, patients who experienced severe CRS had a 5.4-fold increased risk of mortality. Recent data from a large CIBMTR analysis [36] demonstrated a strong correlation between CRS and ICANS, providing further clinical evidence of a biological continuum between these serious sequelae of CAR-T therapy. In that landmark analysis, both severe CRS and ICANS were independently associated with shorter OS. Collectively, these findings support the inclusion of severe CRS and ICANS in our composite endpoint, alongside relapse/progression and NRM, as clinically meaningful and prognostically important early events in CAR-T recipients. Moreover, the clinical and biological interrelationships among the individual components of TPFS6, along with their consistent association with long-term outcomes, enhance the credibility of this novel endpoint and help mitigate concerns regarding interpretability [47, 48]. As an initial step in developing TPFS6 for LBCL, we designed this study to exclude other toxicities commonly associated with CAR-T cell therapy [49], such as cytopenia, infection, and hypogammaglobulinemia. However, any mortality due to infection was ultimately captured by NRM in this study. Detailed on grade 3 vs 4 CRS and ICANS and other supportive care metrics, including ICU-level interventions, were not systematically collected and therefore could not be analyzed in this study. Another limitation is the relatively small number of patients treated with liso-cel and tisa-cel, which may limit the generalizability of our findings across the full spectrum of available CAR-T products for LBCL. Validation of TPFS6 in larger registry cohorts and prospective clinical trials will be essential to support its broader adoption as a composite endpoint in immunotherapy research.

In conclusion, TPFS6 encompasses clinically meaningful and biologically interrelated components associated with OS and represents an ideal recovery outcome by capturing initial treatment success without disease progression, severe toxicity, or mortality. As such, TPFS6 may serve as a valuable endpoint for comparing CAR-T products and other LBCL immunotherapies in both real-world settings and prospective clinical trials.

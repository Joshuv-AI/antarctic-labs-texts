Identification of Maintenance Dialysis Recipients From an Electronic Clinical Information System and Effects on Estimates of Incidence and Prevalence
Authors: Ping Liu, Mitchell Rath, Robert R. Quinn, Ngan N. Lam, Tyrone G. Harrison, Paul E. Ronksley, Jennifer M. MacRae, Mark Suffak, Mark Courtney, Nikhil Shah, Neesh Pannu, Pietro Ravani, Braden J. Manns, Matthew T. James
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rationale & ObjectiveClinical information systems (CISs) can support case finding. However, standardized methods for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients and assessing how CIS transitions influence disease burden estimates are lacking. We aimed to develop and evaluate methods for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients across CIS transitions and examine trends in incidence and prevalence across the implementation timeline.Study DesignPopulation-based retrospective cohort study.Setting & ParticipantsAlberta residents aged ≥20 years, who initiated (incident) or received (prevalent) maintenance dialysis from January 2012 to March 2024, were identified using clinical data (historical kidney care program data and a new Epic-based CIS) versus administrative claims. Study periods included preimplementation (January 2012 to November 2019; use of historical program data), transition (November 2019 to November 2022; combination of data sources), and full implementation of the CIS (November 2022 to March 2024). Claims data were available throughout. Maintenance dialysis was defined as in-center hemodialysis for >90 days or initiation of peritoneal/home hemodialysis, regardless of duration.ResultsThe algorithms identified 7,766 incident cases from clinical data and 7,556 from claims, with 7,391 (93.2%) overlapping. Incidence rates increased slightly over time and were comparable between data sources. Prevalence estimates were higher in claims than historical program data preimplementation but converged during the full CIS implementation period. In 2023, CIS-based incidence was 218.5/million population versus 199.9/million population from claims, and CIS-based prevalence was 1,018.3 versus 1,006.8 from claims. Dialysis initiation dates aligned within 28 days for 84.6% of patients, and period prevalence agreed well between data sources.LimitationsExclusion of patients with kidney failure receiving dialysis for <90 days.ConclusionsStandardized methods for identifying incident maintenance dialysis recipients showed agreement between CIS and claims-based data, whereas the use of historical kidney care program data underidentified prevalent patients in Alberta. These approaches can optimize the identification of maintenance dialysis recipients from routinely collected health information.

Individuals treated with maintenance dialysis (referred to as dialysis throughout the report) experience high morbidity, mortality, and health care use.1,2 High-quality data and accurate case definitions are essential for monitoring dialysis incidence and prevalence to support program delivery, registry reporting, and research. In Canada, similar to other high-income countries, provincial kidney care programs identify dialysis recipients for reporting to a national registry, the Canadian Organ Replacement Register (CORR).3 However, these programs face challenges in ensuring data completeness and quality, as data sources may not capture all individuals with kidney failure treated with dialysis within a catchment area, and data quality can be limited by varied dialysis ordering systems and manual data entry by staff.4

Clinical information systems (CISs) are increasingly implemented across health care systems and integrate multiple subsystems, including electronic medical or health records that focus on documenting and managing patient information, into a unified interface.5 Together, these systems capture, store, process, and transfer clinical information to support care delivery and measurement, including for treatment of kidney failure.5 CISs can be leveraged for case finding and may augment or even replace traditional kidney failure registries and administrative data for reporting and research. In November 2019, Alberta’s provincial health care organization (Alberta Health Services) began implementing a province-wide, Epic Systems--based CIS across all acute care facilities and specialty services, including Alberta Kidney Care. By November 2022, all dialysis-related orders and clinical communications from inpatient and outpatient settings were integrated into this CIS, with site-specific launch dates. The impact of this transition on identifying dialysis recipients remains unclear and may affect provincial estimates of incidence and prevalence.

In this population-based cohort study, we developed and evaluated algorithms to identify maintenance dialysis recipients using “clinical data” (historical kidney care program data and new CIS records) and administrative practitioner “claims data.” Our objectives were to establish comparable approaches for identifying dialysis recipients and to examine incidence and prevalence estimates before and after the transition to the CIS in Alberta, across different data systems. We also compared dialysis burden estimates with those from CORR to inform registry reporting.

Methods

Study Design and Data Sources

This population-based cohort study was approved by the University of Calgary Research Ethics Board (REB 22-0143), with a waiver of informed consent owing to its retrospective design and use of routinely collected health data. Databases from Alberta Health and Alberta Health Services were linked using unique patient identifiers. Alberta Health Services, Canada’s largest integrated provincial health system, provides care to over 4.8 million residents in 2024. Alberta Kidney Care, responsible for treating individuals with kidney diseases, comprises the following 2 programs: (1) Alberta Kidney Care North and (2) Alberta Kidney Care South. Individuals receiving dialysis were identified using multiple data sources, including historical kidney care program data, Alberta’s new Epic-based CIS records, and administrative practitioner claims (Fig 1).Figure 1Data sources and timeline for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients in Alberta. Before CIS implementation, historical kidney care program data were extracted from the Nephrology Information System for Alberta Kidney Care North and the Patient-based Renal Information System for Alberta Kidney Care South, with data collection beginning in November 1998.4,6 The CIS rollout started on November 4, 2019, in Northern Alberta and was completed across all dialysis facilities by November 6, 2022. During the transition, inpatient dialysis data began being recorded in the CIS, whereas outpatient dialysis remained documented in the historical databases, requiring use of both data sources.

Patients receiving dialysis were identified in the CIS using dialysis orders and the “Nephrology Info” table, which contains start and end dates of dialysis episodes sourced from physician dialysis therapy plans. Both orders and therapy plan data include modality information. Dialysis treatment dates were determined by linking therapy plan episodes and completed orders to dialysis encounters from inpatient and outpatient settings, including 24-hour continuous kidney replacement therapy and single-run ultrafiltration. Encounters related to peritoneal dialysis or home hemodialysis management were considered as maintenance dialysis.

Practitioner claims data, collected since 1994 through the Alberta Health Care Insurance Plan, capture services across inpatient and primary care settings. These data were used for 2 purposes. First, dialysis-related procedure codes (Table S1) and service dates (up to November 6, 2022) supplemented and verified dialysis episodes recorded in historical kidney care program data, which often lacked end dates and treatment frequency. Claims data therefore helped identify individuals who initiated or received dialysis according to historical program data. Second, claims data alone were used to compare provincial identification of incident and prevalent dialysis cases with those identified through clinical data from historical program databases and the CIS.

Study Population

We included Alberta residents aged ≥20 years who initiated (incident) or were receiving (prevalent) dialysis between January 1, 2012, and March 31, 2024, identified via clinical data and compared with administrative claims data. The study period was divided into the following 3 CIS-related phases: (1) a “preimplementation” period (January 1, 2012, to November 3, 2019; using historical Alberta Kidney Care program data); (2) a “transition” period (November 4, 2019, to November 5, 2022; using both historical program data and new CIS); and (3) “full CIS implementation” (November 6, 2022, to March 31, 2024; using new CIS exclusively). Claims data were available throughout all periods.

Identification of Incident Dialysis Patients

Consistent with a recent validation study,7 persistent dialysis was defined as consecutive records with gaps of <90 days between records. Although in-center hemodialysis typically occurs 3 times/wk, this relaxed gap criterion allowed for less-frequent billing for weekly dialysis, incremental hemodialysis, or peritoneal dialysis and reflects the low likelihood of separate dialysis episodes for acute kidney injury within 90 days.8 Maintenance dialysis was identified based on the following: (1) persistent in-center or inpatient hemodialysis (including critical care hemodialysis) lasting >90 days, consistent with guidelines, reimbursement policies, and registry definitions that distinguish maintenance dialysis for kidney failure from short-term dialysis for acute kidney injury or disease9, 10, 11, 12, 13; or (2) initiation of peritoneal dialysis or home hemodialysis, regardless of duration, as adult kidney care programs in Alberta use these modalities only for long-term treatment of kidney failure. The incident episode was the first qualifying dialysis episode, with its start date serving as the index date (cohort entry). Patients were considered as incident only during their first qualifying episode. Identification of Prevalent Dialysis Patients

Because dialysis end dates were inconsistently recorded in historical kidney care program data, additional sources, including the Practitioner Claims and Discharge Abstract database (Table S1), were used to determine when individuals exited from or re-entered into the prevalent dialysis cohort. Individuals who resumed dialysis following kidney transplantation were reincluded in prevalence counts.

Baseline Characteristics

Baseline characteristics at dialysis initiation included age, sex, most recent predialysis estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR; up to 1 day before cohort entry), median eGFR over the previous 90 days, history of sustained outpatient eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 for >90 days, and comorbid conditions. The 2009 CKD-EPI (Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration) creatinine equation14 (omitting race coefficient) was used to calculate eGFR in alignment with Alberta clinical practice. Serum creatinine values were standardized across provincial laboratories using the isotope-dilution mass spectrometry reference method. Diabetes and cardiovascular disease (myocardial infarction, chronic heart failure, stroke or transient ischemic attack, or peripheral vascular disease) were identified using established administrative data coding algorithms.15

Statistical Analysis

Standard descriptive statistics summarized baseline characteristics of incident dialysis patients identified from clinical data and compared with those identified through claims data. We analyzed gaps between records and the number of records within the first 91 days of the incident episode and throughout the entire incident episode. Analyses were conducted overall and by dialysis modality; however, modality-specific assessments were limited to clinical data because practitioner claims data lack complete modality information.

Incidence and Prevalence

Quarterly and yearly incidence and prevalence of maintenance dialysis were calculated based on data source and study period (preimplementation, transition, and full CIS implementation). Incidence rates were calculated by dividing the number of new cases by Alberta’s population aged ≥20 years, using estimates from Statistics Canada.16 Period prevalence included any dialysis episodes occurring after an individual had qualified as an incident maintenance dialysis case, regardless of episode duration. Exact binomial confidence intervals were calculated for all estimates. We compared our 2023 estimates with corresponding CORR estimates,3 calculated using aggregated 2023 incident and prevalent patient counts (January to December) from the Canadian Institute for Health Information divided by Alberta’s population for the same period.

Agreement Analysis

For incident cases, differences in an individual’s dialysis initiation date between data sources were categorized as the same date, within 1-7, 8-14, 15-28, or >28 days, identified in clinical data only, or identified in claims only. Reasons for cases appearing in only 1 data source were examined. Yearly proportions for each category were aggregated and plotted to assess trends. For prevalent cases, the quarterly and yearly agreement between clinical data and claims was assessed in each period. Positive agreement was defined as being prevalent in both sources, and negative agreement indicated being prevalent in only 1 data source. Crude agreement and prevalence- and bias-adjusted kappa statistics with 95% exact confidence intervals were calculated for each period.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of Incident Dialysis Patients

A total of 7,766 adult Alberta residents were identified through clinical data as initiating maintenance dialysis between January 1, 2012, and March 31, 2024. Of these, 6,496 (83.6%) were identified from historical kidney care program data, whereas 1,270 (16.4%) were identified from the CIS. Claims data identified 7,556 incident cases, with 7,391 (97.8%) also identified in clinical data. Baseline characteristics of cohort members were similar across data sources. The median age at initiation was 62.9 years, and 62% were men. Approximately 72% of cohort members had sustained eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 for >90 days before initiating dialysis. Incidence of Maintenance Dialysis

The incidence of maintenance dialysis gradually increased over the study periods and remained consistent across clinical and claims data sources. Before CIS implementation, incidence rates obtained from historical kidney care program data and claims were nearly identical, both estimating a rate of 171.3/million population (rpmp) in 2012. By 2018, incidence rates had increased to 205.9 rpmp based on clinical data and 202.1 rpmp from claims. Clinical data--based incidence rates were slightly higher than those from claims during the transition period (210.7 vs 197.6 rpmp in 2021) and following full implementation (218.5 vs 199.9 rpmp in 2023; Fig 3). Period Prevalence of Maintenance Dialysis

Clinical data showed prevalence increasing from 836.9 rpmp in 2012 to 1,018.3 rpmp in 2023. Claims-based estimates were higher than those from historical kidney care program data during the preimplementation period but became more comparable over time. By 2021, during the transition period, yearly prevalence estimates peaked and were identical across data sources (1,051.9 rpmp). In the first quarter of 2023, shortly after full CIS implementation, the CIS-based estimate decreased, whereas the claims-based estimate remained stable. However, over the full 2023 calendar year, estimates again aligned closely, with 1,018.3 rpmp from CIS data and 1,006.8 rpmp from claims (Fig 4). Agreement Between CIS and Claims Data Sources

Across the study period, of the 7,931 incident patients identified based on either data source, 165 (2.1%) were captured only in claims data, 375 (4.7%) only in clinical data, and 7,391 (93.2%) in both. The most common reason for a case appearing in only one source was that the index date in the other data set fell outside the study accrual period (Table S2). The proportion of incident patients identified only through claims was 7.7% in 2012 and remained stable at 1%-3% thereafter. In contrast, the proportion identified only through clinical data fluctuated, starting at 6.9% in 2012, decreasing to 4.3% during the transition period, and increasing to 9% during full implementation of the CIS. The proportion of incident patients whose dialysis start dates differed by ≤7 days between data sources declined over time (71.1% preimplementation, 62.0% during transition, and 56.0% after full CIS implementation; Fig 5). Crude agreement and prevalence- and bias-adjusted kappa statistics generally showed high agreement between data sources in identifying individuals receiving maintenance dialysis, with yearly crude agreement ranging from 0.95 in 2012 to 0.96 in 2023 and kappa statistics from 0.90-0.92 over the same period (Fig S1).Figure 5Yearly proportion of patients stratified based on the absolute difference in cohort entry dates between those identified through clinical and claims data. Discussion

This population-based study from a universal health care system established standardized methods for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients using clinical and practitioner claims data. Individuals were identified by either a persistent dialysis episode lasting >90 days or initiation of peritoneal dialysis or home hemodialysis, regardless of duration. Three key findings emerged. First, incidence rates of maintenance dialysis were similar across data sources and gradually increased over time. Second, prevalence estimates increased more steeply than incidence, were higher in claims data than in historical kidney care program data, and converged between the 2 data sources following the implementation of the CIS. Third, agreement between clinical and claims data was high for both individual-level dialysis initiation dates and period prevalence throughout the study period.

Prevalence rates from historical kidney care program data were likely underestimated because these databases differed in scope and completeness from CIS and claims data. Unlike claims and CIS data that capture dialysis treatment dates, historical program records primarily tracked modality changes among individuals with chronic kidney failure and were vulnerable to omissions, as they relied on manual order submission and data entry by staff.4 Dialysis administered by non-nephrology providers (eg, intensivists) may also have been missed. As a result, some people undergoing maintenance dialysis may have been excluded from period prevalence estimates if they did not meet the >90-day persistent dialysis criterion, as identified through claims data.

Although practitioner claims data are valuable for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients, they may also suffer from accuracy issues. Underreporting may occur owing to shadow billing among salaried nephrologists, ∼30% of Alberta’s nephrologists, who lack direct financial incentives to submit complete claims compared with fee-for-service practitioners.17 Despite routine audits, inaccuracies may persist owing to billing code misuse or continued claim submissions after dialysis has stopped. These issues may remain even after full CIS implementation, as claims submission remains required. However, incident and prevalence estimates from claims data align closely with CIS-based estimates, suggesting that any potential inaccuracies in claims-based identification are likely minimal. Discrepancies in dialysis initiation dates between CIS and claims during full implementation likely reflect claims coding and submission practices. In Alberta, dialysis claims are submitted either per encounter or weekly, with most maintenance codes billed weekly and the submission date often arbitrary. For peritoneal dialysis, claims may be submitted at catheter placement, regardless of treatment initiation or only once treatment begins.

Compared with historical Alberta Kidney Care program and claims data, CIS is considered the most reliable source for identifying dialysis recipients because it captures all dialysis treatments performed across settings, undergoes systematic verification by staff, and provides detailed, accurate information on dialysis episodes and modality. Differences in data source completeness and accuracy may explain the slightly higher CIS-based incidence rates during the transition and full implementation periods, as well as discrepancies in incidence and prevalence across sources.

Alberta’s CIS-based incidence of maintenance dialysis in 2023 was 11% higher than the CORR estimate (218.5 vs 197.1 rpmp), suggesting potential underreporting of incident cases by Alberta kidney care programs, which submit data to CORR voluntarily. Our study included only individuals receiving maintenance dialysis for >90 days or those initiating home hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis, whereas CORR registers all individuals with chronic kidney failure who initiate dialysis in Canadian facilities, including those who discontinue within 90 days owing to transplantation, withdrawal, or death.3 If CORR applied the same case definition as ours, its incidence estimate would likely be lower. The comparable 2023 prevalence estimates from CIS and CORR (1,018.3 vs 1,030.7 rpmp; 1% lower) likely reflect a combination of case definition differences and limitations in the completeness and scope of kidney care program data submissions.4

This study has implications for care planning, registry reporting, and research involving individuals receiving dialysis. Our findings are relevant to jurisdictions that identify dialysis recipients using Epic Systems--based CISs, other CISs, and/or administrative health data. Accurate and efficient case identification and surveillance of dialysis incidence, prevalence, and outcomes are critical for informed care planning, policy development, and quality improvement. The high concordance between claims and clinical data supports their use for estimating incidence rates in Alberta’s adult population, with incident dialysis dates aligning within 1 week for about half of cases and within 28 days for nearly 80%. This high level of accuracy is also critical for monitoring outcome and detecting temporal trends. Patient prognosis should be monitored from the date of dialysis initiation, given the early mortality risk after dialysis initiation, which may be inadequately captured within some kidney replacement therapy registries.18 Given its comprehensiveness and data quality, we consider the CIS the most reliable and efficient source for registry data submissions and for evaluating the long-term validity of claims data in capturing a patient’s entire dialysis treatment history. However, an important next step is to validate dialysis identification algorithms for kidney failure across the CIS and claims data sources, against the preferred reference standard of patient charts, to minimize the inclusion of individuals initiating dialysis for acute conditions, particularly recurrent acute kidney injury.

Compared with existing algorithms for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients in administrative health data,7,19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 we applied a >90-day chronicity criterion (consistent with some7,8,11,12 but not all studies) and required at least 2 dialysis codes within a 90-day period, similar to the US study7 but not others. A major strength of this study is its population-based design within a universal health care system, which reduces fragmentation of care and enables comprehensive tracking across providers and care settings. Moreover, the use of a single electronic CIS across Alberta Health Services enhances data quality and case identification. Although this study was conducted in one Canadian province, the province-wide implementation of Epic Systems, with its standardized yet customizable features, supports the applicability of our algorithms in other regions using Epic or similar CISs.

This study has limitations. First, prevalence and incidence estimates based on historical kidney care program data required supplementation with claims data because some dialysis episodes lacked end dates. Second, we evaluated a single algorithm to define maintenance dialysis and excluded individuals who received dialysis for <90 days owing to transplantation, withdrawal, or death. An alternative definition of dialysis for kidney failure (including any dialysis initiation after meeting eGFR criteria for kidney failure, ie, eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 for >90 days) may be appropriate depending on study objectives. Third, identifying individuals receiving peritoneal dialysis may require region-specific modifications, as peritoneal dialysis is used for acute kidney injury in some low- and middle-income settings.26 Furthermore, our findings on agreement and the impact on incidence and prevalence may not generalize to other settings, as results can vary with coding practices, data sources, dialysis-funding models, and population characteristics. Each jurisdiction should therefore evaluate the performance of standardized methods for identifying maintenance dialysis recipients within its own context. Finally, the short observation period after full CIS implementation limits our ability to assess the long-term accuracy of claims data, with the CIS considered the preferred data source.

In conclusion, applying standardized methods to identify incident maintenance dialysis recipients resulted in over 90% agreement between clinical and administrative claims data, with dialysis initiation dates matching within 28 days for nearly 80% of patients. However, historical kidney care program data were inadequate for identifying prevalent cases. As CISs become more widely adopted, validating algorithms for kidney failure--related dialysis against the gold standard of patient charts in future studies could enable broader application of this approach to streamline registry reporting, inform dialysis care policy, and improve patient outcomes.

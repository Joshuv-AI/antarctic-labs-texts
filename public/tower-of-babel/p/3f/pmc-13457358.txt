The Bedside “Rule of Three”: A Formula for Vasoactive Drug Dose Calculation for Fixed-concentration 50 mL Syringe Preparations
Authors: Hariharan Rajangam, VR Hemanth Kumar
Published: 2026

The Editor

Vasoactive drugs are high-alert medications for which dosing precision is essential; even minor arithmetic errors may precipitate hemodynamic instability in critically ill patients.1,2 Standard practice in many institutions, including ours [Jawaharlal Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education and Research (JIPMER), Puducherry], employs a weight-based preparation: The drug quantity in milligrams is calculated as a multiplier of body weight, diluted to 50 mL with 0.9% saline—0.03 × body weight (mg) for noradrenaline and adrenaline; 0.3 × body weight (mg) for sodium nitroprusside (SNP) and nitroglycerin (NTG); and 3 × body weight (mg) for dopamine and dobutamine. Each 1 mL.h−1 then corresponds to a fixed dose increment, enabling calculator-free titration.

In practice, however, fixed non-weight-based concentrations (e.g., noradrenaline 4 mg/50 mL “single strength” or 8 mg/50 mL “double strength”) are widely used across intensive care unit (ICU) and emergency settings, where one syringe serves patients of varying body weight.3 At handover or during emergencies, calculating the delivered dose from such preparations requires multistep arithmetic that is prone to error under cognitive load.2,4 While alternative methods for bedside dose verification exist, including smart-pump drug libraries, static institutional titration charts, or rules tailored to specific pediatric dilutions (such as the “Rule of Six”), these tools frequently depend on external reference materials or rigid pre-planned mixing protocols.5 We propose a simple bedside formula to address this. The derivation is straightforward: If D mg is loaded into 50 mL, the concentration is (D × 1,000) / 50 = D × 20 μg.mL−1. At a rate of R mL.h−1, delivery is R × D × 20 μg.h−1. Dividing by 60 minutes gives (R × D)/3 μg.min−1. Dividing by body weight (W kg) yields (R × D)/(3 × W) μg.kg−1.min−1. The constant 3 is the fixed mathematical product of syringe volume, time conversion, and unit scaling (50 mL × 60 min.h−1/1,000 μg.mg−1 = 3) and is identical across all vasoactive agents. The mnemonic “rate times drug, divided by three times weight” allows immediate, calculator-free verification at handover to rapidly identify whether a running infusion rate lies within the therapeutic range.

To evaluate its clinical utility, we conducted a simulation exercise involving 30 resident doctors at our institution. Under emergency dosing scenarios, multistep arithmetic yielded a mean calculation time of 40 seconds with 53.3% making critical decimal errors, whereas the Rule of Three reduced mean calculation time to 22 seconds with only 13.3% errors, a significant reduction in both cognitive burden and calculation risk.

Individualized weight-based preparation remains the gold standard. The Rule of Three is intended as a practical safeguard for the common real-world scenario in which non-standardized fixed-concentration preparations are already in use. It applies to any agent dosed in μg.kg−1.min−1 in a standard 50 mL syringe and is valid for 50 mL pumps only.

Expression and clinical significance of the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway in elderly patients with acute myocardial infarction
Authors: Tingting Teng, Yuxi Xue, Wenyuan Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the expression level of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)/vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway in elderly patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI), and to analyze its relationship with the prognosis of AMI.MethodsA total of 160 elderly patients with AMI were selected from October 2023 to September 2024 in Nanjing Medical University Affiliated Wuxi People’s Hospital as the study group, 160 individuals who underwent physical examination during the same period were selected as the control group according to the 1:1 matching principle. The levels of serum HIF-1α and VEGF were compared between the two groups. The study group was treated with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). According to the prognosis of patients at 12 months after operation, they were divided into good subgroup and poor subgroup. The clinical data, serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels of the two subgroups were compared. The effect of serum HIF-1α and VEGF on prognosis and its predictive value were analyzed. The accuracy-recall rate (PR) curve was drawn to evaluate the performance of combined prediction. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated, the DeLong test was used to compare AUCs, and the precision-recall (PR) curve was drawn to evaluate the performance of combined prediction.ResultsThe levels of serum HIF-1α and VEGF in the study group were higher than those in the control group (P < 0.05). The time from onset to admission, the proportion of Killip class IV, the proportion of multivessel disease, N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), HIF-1α and VEGF in the poor subgroup were higher than those in the good subgroup, while left ventricular ejection fraction was lower (P < 0.05). Before and after correction of multivessel disease and NT-proBNP, serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels were the influencing factors of prognosis in patients with AMI (P < 0.05). The AUC of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels alone and combined prediction were 0.717,0.748 and 0.900, respectively, the AUC of combined prediction was significantly higher than that of the two alone (Z = 3.315,2.832, P = 0.001,0.005), and the best sensitivity and specificity were 83.33% and 84.87%. The DCA curve showed that the combined prediction of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels in the probability range of 5%-70% could obtain significant positive net benefits. The PR curve showed that the PR-AUC value of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels in evaluating prognosis was 0.767, which had a high recall rate and accuracy rate.Conclusion Serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels are highly expressed in elderly patients with AMI, and are closely related to the prognosis of patients. The combination of the two can be used to predict the prognosis of patients, provide a reference for the assessment and prognosis of elderly patients with AMI, facilitate early identification of high-risk groups, and provide a reference for subsequent personalized program development.Keywords: Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α, Vascular endothelial growth factor, Signal pathway, Old age, Acute myocardial infarction, Prognosis

Introduction

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is one of the most severe manifestations of cardiovascular disease and occurs particularly frequently in the elderly population, in whom it is associated with high rates of disability and mortality [1]. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is the standard strategy for myocardial revascularisation in AMI, effectively restoring coronary blood flow, improving myocardial perfusion, and reducing mortality; however, a proportion of patients still develop major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) after the procedure, which markedly compromises long-term prognosis [2, 3]. Therefore, the identification of safe and reliable biomarkers to predict post-PCI MACE in elderly patients with AMI is of considerable clinical importance for risk stratification and prognostic assessment.

The hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)/vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signalling pathway plays a key role in tissue repair and angiogenesis, and growing evidence indicates that it is critically involved in myocardial ischaemia-reperfusion injury following AMI [4]. Clinical studies have shown that serum levels of HIF-1α and VEGF are elevated in patients with AMI, and that such elevations are associated with poorer post-procedural outcomes; however, most existing studies have focused on single biomarkers or their combination with other indicators when evaluating prognosis [5, 6]. To date, the clinical value of the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway as an integrated biological axis in elderly patients with AMI has not been systematically investigated.

The primary hypothesis of this study is that serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels are significantly elevated in elderly patients with AMI compared with control individuals, and that higher levels of these biomarkers are independently associated with an increased risk of MACE after PCI. Furthermore, the combination of HIF-1α and VEGF is hypothesised to provide superior prognostic value compared with either biomarker alone.

Accordingly, the present study aimed to examine the expression of the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway in elderly patients with AMI and to further evaluate its predictive value for prognosis after PCI, thereby providing evidence to support postoperative risk assessment in this population.

Materials and methods

Study population

A total of 160 elderly patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) who were admitted to Nanjing Medical University Affiliated Wuxi People’s Hospital between October 2023 and September 2024 were consecutively enrolled as the study group. During the same period, 160 age- and sex-matched individuals undergoing routine health examinations were selected as the control group at a 1:1 ratio. The rationale for selecting individuals undergoing routine health examinations as the control group was that these individuals represented the general elderly population without known cardiovascular disease, allowing a valid comparison of baseline serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels in the absence of acute myocardial ischaemia. The 1:1 matching process was performed based on age (± 3 years) and sex (exact match). For each enrolled patient with AMI, a control subject of the same sex and similar age (± 3 years) was identified from the routine health examination population during the same time period. If multiple eligible controls were available, one was selected randomly. Baseline clinical characteristics were comparable between the two groups, with no statistically significant differences observed (P > 0.05), as shown in Table 1.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: a diagnosis of AMI in accordance with the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Myocardial Infarction [7]; time from symptom onset to hospital admission ≤ 24 h; planned treatment with PCI and absence of contraindications to the procedure; and age ≥ 60 years. Exclusion criteria included the presence of malignant tumours; previous (old) myocardial infarction; concomitant cardiac conditions such as cardiomyopathy, rheumatic heart disease, cor pulmonale, or severe arrhythmias; severe hepatic or renal dysfunction; serious infectious diseases; autoimmune disorders; history of major surgery or significant trauma within the preceding three months; coagulation disorders or haematological diseases; and psychiatric or cognitive impairment that could preclude adequate cooperation with the study protocol. This was a prospective study and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Medical University Affiliated Wuxi People’s Hospital(Approval number:2021LL029).

Methods

Measurement of serum HIF-1α and VEGF

For the control group, 5 mL of peripheral venous blood was collected on the day of the health examination, while in the AMI group, blood samples were obtained on the day of hospital admission. All samples were centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 10 min with a rotor radius of 12 cm, and the supernatant serum was separated. Serum levels of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) were determined using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

Treatment protocol and prognostic assessment

After admission, all 160 patients with AMI received loading doses of 300 mg enteric-coated aspirin and 180 mg ticagrelor administered orally. Following identification of the culprit vessel, PCI was performed, and patients with a high thrombus burden received an intravenous bolus of tirofiban at a dose of 25 µg/kg during the procedure. After discharge, patients were treated with guideline-directed medical therapy tailored to their individual clinical conditions.

Adverse events were prospectively recorded and classified as in-hospital events occurring from admission to discharge or post-discharge events occurring after discharge up to 12 months. All patients were followed for 12 months after PCI through outpatient visits and telephone interviews, with the day of the procedure defined as the start of follow-up. The occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) within one year was recorded, including heart failure, arrhythmias, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, repeat revascularisation, and stroke. A supplementary table showing the classification and timing of each MACE as in-hospital versus post-discharge events is provided. According to the presence or absence of MACE during follow-up, patients were classified into a poor-prognosis subgroup (with MACE) or a favourable-prognosis subgroup (without MACE).

Baseline clinical data

Clinical data were collected using a structured questionnaire, including sex, age, time from symptom onset to admission, body mass index, smoking and alcohol consumption history, and comorbidities. Killip class, infarct type [non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)], presence of multivessel disease, number and total length of implanted stents, and post-discharge medications (β-blockers, angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors [ARNI], dual antiplatelet therapy, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors [ACEI] or angiotensin receptor blockers [ARB], and statins) were documented by trained medical staff. Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was assessed using an EPIQ 7 colour Doppler echocardiography system (Philips, the Netherlands). In addition, 3 mL of venous blood was collected at admission, centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 10 min (radius 12 cm), and serum N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels were measured using a chemiluminescence immunoassay.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 27.0. Continuous variables with a normal distribution are presented as the mean ± standard deviation ( s) and were compared using the independent-samples t test, whereas non-normally distributed continuous data are expressed as the median [M (Q1, Q3)] and were analysed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are reported as numbers and percentages [n (%)] and were compared using the χ² test, while ordinal variables were analysed with rank-sum tests. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was conducted to identify factors associated with prognosis in patients with AMI, with event-free survival time as the time scale and censoring for patients lost to follow-up or without MACE by the end of 12 months. Prognosis was defined as the time from PCI to the occurrence of MACE, and patients lost to follow-up or event-free at 12 months were censored. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC), together with decision curve analysis (DCA), were used to evaluate the predictive performance and clinical utility of the combined model. The DeLong test was applied to compare the AUCs of the combined model versus each individual biomarker to test the hypothesis that the combination provides superior prognostic value. In addition, precision-recall (PR) curves were constructed to further assess the discriminative ability of the combined prediction approach. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For Cox regression, candidate variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were considered, but variables exhibiting severe multicollinearity (variance inflation factor > 10) were excluded, with LVEF retained due to its clinical priority; multivessel disease and NT-proBNP were selected as adjustment variables based on both statistical significance and established prognostic relevance.

Factors associated with prognosis in patients with AMI

To further evaluate the independent effects of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels on prognosis, candidate variables that showed significant differences between the two prognosis subgroups in univariate analysis (time from symptom onset to admission, Killip class, multivessel disease, LVEF, NT-proBNP, HIF-1α, and VEGF) were entered into a Cox proportional hazards regression model. All continuous variables were standardised using Z-score transformation to minimise the impact of differences in measurement scales.

Multicollinearity diagnostics indicated variance inflation factors (VIF) greater than 10 for time from symptom onset to admission, Killip class, and LVEF, suggesting substantial multicollinearity. Given that LVEF is a cornerstone predictor of post-AMI prognosis, we retained LVEF in the final model and excluded time from onset to admission and Killip class to resolve collinearity. Multivessel disease and NT-proBNP (VIF < 5) were selected as additional adjustment variables because they were significantly associated with prognosis in univariate analyses (P < 0.05, see Table 3) and are known clinical predictors of adverse outcomes after AMI. Prognosis was defined as the time from PCI to the occurrence of MACE, with patients lost to follow-up or event-free at 12 months censored.

After adjustment for multivessel disease and NT-proBNP, serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels remained independently associated with prognosis in patients with AMI (P < 0.05). In the same model, lower LVEF was also significantly associated with worse prognosis (HR = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.80–0.93, P < 0.001). Predictive value of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels for prognosis in patients with AMI

Prognosis in patients with AMI was defined as the dependent variable (poor prognosis = 1, favourable prognosis = 0), and ROC curves were constructed to evaluate the ability of serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels to predict clinical outcomes. When analysed individually, the AUC was 0.717 for HIF-1α and 0.748 for VEGF. A combined logistic regression model incorporating both biomarkers was then established as follows: Logit(P) = 1.029 × HIF-1α + 1.385 × VEGF − 3.852.The combined model yielded an AUC of 0.900, with a sensitivity of 83.33% and a specificity of 84.87%, which was significantly higher than that of either biomarker alone (Z = 3.315 and 2.832; P = 0.001 and 0.005, respectively). Discussion

The hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)/VEGF signalling pathway is closely linked to tissue hypoxia and plays a central role in angiogenesis, maintenance of oxygen homeostasis as well as in cellular adaptation to low oxygen tension [8–10]. HIF-1α is highly sensitive to changes in oxygen tension: under normoxic conditions, its expression is minimal, whereas hypoxia or low-oxygen states lead to a rapid accumulation of HIF-1α, which binds to hypoxia-response elements and subsequently upregulates downstream VEGF expression, thereby contributing to the progression of cardiovascular disease [11–13]. Although previous studies have reported elevated serum levels of HIF-1α and VEGF in patients with AMI [14, 15], our findings further confirm this observation, suggesting that both biomarkers are closely involved in the initiation and progression of AMI.

The elevated serum levels of HIF-1α and VEGF observed in our elderly AMI patients likely reflect the activation of the hypoxic response during acute myocardial ischaemia. During AMI, acute coronary occlusion results in a sudden reduction in myocardial blood supply, placing cardiomyocytes in a state of hypoxia. Under normoxic conditions, HIF-1α is hydroxylated by prolyl hydroxylases and subsequently degraded via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway; however, hypoxia suppresses prolyl hydroxylase activity, leading to impaired degradation and progressive cytoplasmic accumulation of HIF-1α. By binding to hypoxia-response elements, HIF-1α initiates VEGF gene transcription and promotes VEGF secretion from cardiomyocytes and vascular endothelial cells [16, 17].

This study did not measure inflammatory or oxidative stress markers such as IL-6, CRP, or malondialdehyde. Therefore, although previous reports have suggested a link between HIF-1α/VEGF signalling and inflammation-mediated adverse outcomes, our data do not directly support such mechanistic interpretations. The association between higher HIF-1α/VEGF levels and poorer prognosis should be interpreted as a correlative finding rather than definitive evidence of a causal molecular pathway.

In this study, we first characterised the expression profile of the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway in elderly patients with AMI. During the acute phase of AMI, systemic hypoxia-responsive mechanisms are activated, leading to induction of the HIF-1α/VEGF axis and its involvement in disease progression, which provides a biological basis for further evaluation of its prognostic relevance. Although previous studies have reported that elevated serum levels of HIF-1α and VEGF are associated with poor outcomes after PCI in patients with AMI, the relationship between the integrated HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway and post-PCI prognosis in elderly patients has not been well explored.Our results showed that serum HIF-1α and VEGF levels were significantly higher in the poor-prognosis subgroup than in the favourable-prognosis subgroup. Importantly, both biomarkers remained independently associated with prognosis before and after adjustment for multivessel disease and NT-proBNP, which is consistent with earlier reports [18–20]. However, most prior studies focused on single biomarkers or their combination with other serum indicators, whereas our findings highlight the close association between activation of the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway and adverse clinical outcomes.

Our results appear to contrast with those reported in references [5] and [15]. Reference [5] found that high HIF-1α was associated with lower cardiovascular events post-PCI, while reference [15] reported that higher post-PCI VEGF levels predicted better outcomes. These discrepancies may be explained by differences in the timing of biomarker measurement (acute phase vs. post-revascularisation) and patient population (elderly vs. general). Unlike the favourable adaptive role suggested in those studies, elevated HIF-1α and VEGF at admission in our elderly cohort likely reflect more severe ischaemic burden and a maladaptive response, leading to worse prognosis.

Several mechanisms may account for the observed association between elevated HIF-1α/VEGF levels and adverse outcomes. One possibility is that patients in the poor-prognosis subgroup experienced more severe myocardial ischaemia and hypoxia, characterised by a wider ischaemic territory and larger infarct size, leading to greater HIF-1α activation and VEGF upregulation. More profound hypoxia promotes HIF-1α accumulation, which in turn upregulates VEGF expression, potentially aggravating vascular remodelling and increasing vascular permeability, which may contribute to myocardial oedema and cardiac dysfunction [21]. Experimental studies have also demonstrated that sustained overexpression of HIF-1α enhances the release of inflammatory mediators and stimulates cardiac fibroblast proliferation, thereby accelerating myocardial fibrosis and adversely affecting clinical outcomes [22, 23], however, whether these mechanisms operate in our patient population remains unknown and requires further investigation incorporating inflammatory biomarkers. Second, the higher prevalence of multivessel disease in the poor-prognosis subgroup reflects more advanced coronary artery disease, with limited myocardial perfusion reserve and a broader extent of ischaemia. NT-proBNP, which is primarily synthesised and released by ventricular and atrial cardiomyocytes, increases in response to myocardial ischaemia, necrosis, and elevated cardiac wall stress. Elevated NT-proBNP levels therefore indicate more severe myocardial injury and haemodynamic burden, as excessive ventricular load stimulates cardiomyocytes to secrete NT-proBNP [24–26]. Taken together, these clinical factors, along with elevated HIF-1α and VEGF levels, may synergistically contribute to an unfavourable prognosis in elderly patients with AMI, although the precise underlying mechanisms remain to be elucidated.

On the basis of these findings, we further evaluated the prognostic value of combining HIF-1α and VEGF. ROC curve analysis showed that the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.900, which was markedly higher than that obtained with either biomarker alone, indicating that joint assessment substantially improves predictive performance and overcomes the limitations of single-marker prediction. Decision curve analysis demonstrated a clear positive net clinical benefit for the combined HIF-1α/VEGF model, and, given the imbalance between prognostic subgroups, additional precision-recall curve analysis confirmed the robustness and reliability of this model for prognostic classification.

By focusing on the HIF-1α/VEGF signalling pathway, this study provides insight into its clinical relevance in elderly patients with AMI. HIF-1α acts as a key initiator of the hypoxic stress response and is rapidly activated under myocardial hypoxia, thereby regulating downstream VEGF expression. VEGF, in turn, promotes endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenesis and participates in the modulation of cardiomyocyte proliferation and myocardial fibrosis. For elderly patients identified as high risk by the combined prediction model, clinicians may consider implementing more intensive secondary prevention strategies before discharge, such as optimisation of medical therapy and closer follow-up, to reduce the risk of MACE. Conversely, for patients at lower risk, follow-up intervals may be appropriately extended to avoid unnecessary use of healthcare resources while maintaining effective and targeted management.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-centre study with a relatively small sample size (160 patients, though well-characterised), which may limit the generalisability of our findings. Second, the follow-up period was limited to 12 months, and we only recorded MACE as the endpoint. Longer-term outcomes, such as late mortality or the development of chronic heart failure, were not assessed. Third, we did not measure inflammatory or oxidative stress markers such as IL-6, CRP, or malondialdehyde. This is a significant limitation because it prevents us from directly testing the proposed mechanistic link between the HIF-1α/VEGF pathway and inflammation-mediated adverse outcomes. The associations we report should be interpreted as correlative findings. Fourth, as discussed above, biomarker levels are dynamic. We only measured HIF-1α and VEGF at a single time point (admission). Serial measurements (e.g., pre- and post-PCI, or at follow-up) would provide a more comprehensive understanding of their prognostic role and help reconcile discrepancies with other studies. Fifth, although we adjusted for several key confounders (multivessel disease, NT-proBNP), we cannot exclude the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured variables, such as frailty, which is particularly relevant in an elderly population. Sixth. The control group consisted of elderly individuals rather than non-elderly AMI patients or patients with other cardiovascular diseases, which limits the assessment of biomarker specificity for AMI. Future large-scale, multicentre studies incorporating mechanistic and serial biomarker measurements are needed to confirm our findings and to explore the underlying biological pathways.

Conclusion

In summary, elevated serum levels of HIF-1α and VEGF are closely associated with adverse outcomes after PCI in elderly patients with AMI. Both biomarkers show meaningful prognostic value when used individually, and their combined assessment offers superior predictive performance, providing novel and sensitive indicators for post-PCI risk stratification in this population. Further basic and translational research is required to elucidate the molecular mechanisms linking HIF-1α/VEGF activation to adverse outcomes, thereby enhancing our understanding of AMI pathophysiology and informing future clinical applications.

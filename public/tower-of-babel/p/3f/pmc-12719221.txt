Development of functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials and their green application in organic coupling reactions
Authors: Kaize Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryOrganic coupling reactions are essential in chemical synthesis, yet traditional systems rely on organic solvents and precious metals, causing pollution and high energy use. Here, a functionalized magnetic mesoporous catalyst loaded with a borene-carbene compound was developed for greener coupling reactions. Fe3O4@SiO2 served as a magnetic core-shell carrier, and IMe-B(SiMe3) was anchored to the mesoporous surface. The catalyst showed high surface area (320 m2/g), uniform pores (3.2 nm), and magnetism (65.3 emu/g). In Suzuki and Sonogashira reactions, conversions reached 93.874% and 92.345% with selectivity up to 99.8%, surpassing reference catalysts. Substrate scope confirmed broad applicability, while green solvent systems such as DES achieved 89.673% conversion and 78.171% yield, maintaining activity after recycling. These results highlight the synergistic effects of pore design and surface functionalization, confirming efficiency and recyclability. This work provides guidance for designing green catalysts and expands the applications of magnetic mesoporous materials in sustainable organic chemistry.Subject areas: chemistry, catalysis, applied sciences

Introduction

With the rapid development of green chemistry, the development of environmentally friendly catalysts has gradually become a research focus in the field of organic synthesis. Traditional catalysts are limited in their application in green chemical reactions due to problems such as high cost, low selectivity, and poor recyclability.1,2 Functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials, due to their unique magnetic responsiveness and highly tunable mesoporous structure, exhibit efficient catalytic performance and good recyclability, offering a sustainable solution for organic coupling reactions.3 However, most of the current research focuses on optimizing the preparation methods of materials, while systematic studies on the optimization of catalyst performance in actual green reaction systems and their reaction mechanisms are still insufficient. In view of this, the study focuses on functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials and explores their green applications in organic coupling reactions, aiming to provide new theoretical guidance and practical directions for the design of green catalysts.

Functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials, due to their unique structural advantages and wide application potential, have gradually become a frontier in the field of heterogeneous catalysis. In improving catalyst performance, researchers have significantly enhanced the substrate mass transfer efficiency and the utilization of active sites by optimizing the pore size distribution and specific surface area of mesoporous materials.4,5,6 At the same time, by introducing functional groups with special electronic effects, the selectivity and catalytic efficiency of catalytic materials in complex reaction systems have been effectively enhanced.7,8 However, existing research lacks an in-depth exploration of the mechanisms of pore structure regulation and surface functionalization, and the synergistic effect of the two in improving reaction performance has not been systematically validated. On the other hand, studies on the adaptability of catalysts in different green solvent systems and their long-term cycling performance are still limited, and the performance in complex organic reactions requires further investigation. To address these shortcomings, this study designs and prepares functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials loaded with borene-carbene compounds, using their high specific surface area and unique electronic effects to explore their catalytic performance and green application potential in Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions. The results of the study ultimately show that the IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 material exhibits excellent catalytic activity and extremely high target product selectivity in Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions, with superior recyclability and the ability to maintain efficient and stable catalytic capacity after multiple cycles. The research findings not only confirm the effectiveness of the synergistic design of mesoporous structure and surface functionalization in improving catalyst activity, selectivity, and stability but also reveal the intrinsic mechanism of the synergistic effect between the electronic effects of borene-carbene compounds and the mesoporous structure. This provides key guidance for the molecular design of catalysts in complex organic coupling reactions. At the same time, the study expands the practical application range of functionalized magnetic materials in the field of green chemistry by verifying their excellent performance in green solvent systems such as aqueous and ionic liquids, laying a solid foundation for the development of efficient, recyclable, and low-environmental-impact catalysts, and providing important reference for the industrialization of green catalysis technology.

The structure of this article is as follows: the first section is the introduction, which elaborates on the research background, issues, and significance; the second section is the literature review, which summarizes the research progress on the application of magnetic mesoporous materials in green catalysis; the third section details the preparation methods and characterization techniques of the materials, focusing on the design principles and surface functionalization strategies of functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials; the fourth section is the catalytic performance testing, evaluating the activity, selectivity, and cycling performance of the functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials; the fifth section is the discussion, which compares experimental results with existing research; and the final section is the conclusion, summarizing the research findings and suggesting future research directions.

Literature review

With the rapid development of green chemistry, achieving efficient and environmentally friendly organic coupling reactions has become a research hotspot. Traditional catalytic systems often use organic solvents and precious metal catalysts, which pose problems such as high energy consumption and high pollution. Magnetic mesoporous materials, with their high specific surface area, magnetic recoverability, and functionalizable properties, have shown enormous potential in the field of green catalysis, becoming important carriers in green reaction systems such as aqueous phase, solvent-free systems, and so forth,9,10 providing new ideas for the sustainable development of coupling reactions.

In organic coupling reactions, traditional organic solvents often have high toxicity and volatility, posing threats to both the environment and experimental safety. Therefore, in recent years, researchers have focused on exploring green solvent systems as alternatives to traditional organic solvents, mainly concentrating on aqueous phase reactions, solvent-free systems, and solid-state reactions. The research on environmentally friendly solvents has also provided more options for green catalysis. For example, Delgado et al. successfully achieved photochemical cross-coupling reactions using dimethyl carbonate and plant extracts as solvents, demonstrating the huge potential of green solvents in complex organic reactions.11 Solid-state grinding reactions, as a form of solvent-free system, have gradually become a hot topic in green chemistry research in recent years. Seo et al. conducted Suzuki-Miyaura coupling reactions using high-temperature ball milling technology, achieving high yields while avoiding solvent use,12 and Bagade et al. further demonstrated that under solid-state conditions, magnetic Pd catalysts could achieve efficient coupling reactions in a short time and are easy to recover.13 Previous studies have also demonstrated that magnetic mesoporous materials can achieve both efficient catalysis and facile recovery in green reaction systems such as aqueous phases. The composite material Ag/Fe3O4/TiO2/CuO@MWCNTs exhibited high catalytic activity and extremely high yield in multi-component coupling reactions in the aqueous phase, while avoiding the use of organic solvents, providing new ideas for the greening of coupling reaction systems.14 Ni2+ nanoparticles as catalysts also demonstrated excellent catalytic efficiency and recyclability in the reduction of nitroaromatics under aqueous phase conditions.15 These studies have not only played an active role in reducing environmental pollution but have also further highlighted the potential of magnetic mesoporous materials as key carriers in green catalytic systems.

In the preparation of magnetic mesoporous materials, traditional methods often require high-temperature calcination and the use of organic solvents, resulting in high energy consumption and pollution. To achieve green synthesis, researchers in recent years have focused on developing more environmentally friendly synthesis methods. Rana et al. used Euphorbiaceae plant extracts as green reductants to prepare copper nanoparticles supported on mesoporous materials under mild conditions, achieving green reduction and efficient nanoparticle dispersion.16 Brito et al. used naturally sourced solvents such as (+)-α-pinene and (+)-limonene to replace the organic solvent toluene, successfully achieving organic silicon functionalization reactions and significantly reducing the toxicity and environmental costs of the synthesis process.17 In addition, mechanochemical methods have also received considerable attention in the preparation of green catalytic materials. Gao et al. prepared magnetic catalysts for coupling reactions under solvent-free conditions using high-energy ball milling, significantly reducing solvent use and improving catalyst activity.18 Pickhardt et al. used ball milling technology in solid-state reaction systems to prepare Pd catalysts, achieving low-temperature efficient coupling reactions.19 Moreover, the research by Mohammadi & Vaezi demonstrated multifunctional nano-catalysts for green coupling reactions, showing excellent catalytic effects in the aqueous phase and significantly reducing energy consumption through green synthesis strategies.20 These studies collectively confirm the importance of low-energy, low-pollution green synthesis methods in improving environmental friendliness and reaction efficiency.

Magnetic mesoporous materials have significant advantages in catalyst recovery and reuse due to their unique magnetic responsiveness. Dong et al. designed Fe3O4@SiO2 magnetic nanocatalysts that can be rapidly recovered using a magnetic field, demonstrating excellent catalytic activity in Suzuki-Miyaura coupling reactions and maintaining high efficiency after multiple recycling cycles.21 Additionally, the Pd/Fe3O4@MCM-41 composite material developed by Pourhasan-Kisomi et al. exhibited good structural stability and efficient catalytic performance after multiple cycles.22 Shirvandi & Rostami studied zirconium-based magnetic mesoporous materials using polyethylene glycol (PEG) as a green solvent in carbon-sulfur bond coupling reactions, displaying high catalytic activity and durability, with easy recovery.23 Wang et al. proved that by introducing highly magnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles,24 the magnetic responsiveness of the material could be enhanced, enabling the catalyst to maintain high activity and durability during multiple recycling cycles. These studies show that by optimizing catalyst structures, enhancing magnetic components, and introducing green synthesis processes, the recovery and reuse efficiency of catalysts can be significantly improved, achieving a higher level of greenness.

Research on magnetic mesoporous materials in the field of green catalysis has been continuously advancing, providing a solid theoretical and practical foundation for the greening of organic coupling reactions. However, existing studies still show certain limitations in enhancing the long-term structural stability of materials, exploiting the synergistic effects of multifunctional catalytic centers, and ensuring adaptability in complex reaction systems. At the same time, although current green synthesis methods contribute positively to reducing energy consumption and environmental pollution, they still face challenges such as complex synthesis steps, heavy reliance on organic reagents, and limited cycling stability. Against this background, this study designed a functionalized magnetic mesoporous material loaded with borene-carbene compounds, employing Fe3O4@SiO2 magnetic core-shell structures as carriers and introducing carbene-boron ligands onto the mesoporous silica surface to construct multifunctional active centers. This approach not only simplified the synthetic route and reduced the use of organic solvents but also, through the synergistic interaction of the electronic effects of borene-carbene compounds with the mesoporous structure, achieved higher catalytic activity and excellent cycling stability, thereby laying a solid foundation for their application in green organic coupling reactions.

Materials and methods

Material preparation

Synthesis of magnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles

In a 100 mL beaker, 5.4 g (20 mmol) of FeCl2·4H2O and 9.6 g (40 mmol) of FeCl3·6H2O were added, followed by 150 mL of ethylene glycol. The mixture was magnetically stirred at room temperature until completely dissolved. Then, 12.3 g (100 mmol) of anhydrous sodium citrate was slowly added, and the pH of the system was adjusted to 7.0. The mixture was then heated in a hydrothermal reactor at 180°C for 8 h. After cooling to room temperature, the resulting black particles were separated with a magnet and washed three times each with ethanol and deionized water. The product was dried at 40°C for 12 h to obtain magnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles.

Preparation of SiO2-Coated Fe3O4 magnetic particles (SiO2@Fe3O4)

0.6 g of Fe3O4 nanoparticles were dispersed in 150 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid (0.2 M) and sonicated for 20 min to remove surface impurities. The magnetic particles were then separated with a magnet and washed three times each with ethanol and deionized water. The treated Fe3O4 particles were transferred to a solution containing 800 mL of ethanol and 200 mL of deionized water, and 10.0 mL of 30 wt % ammonia solution was added to adjust the pH to 11. Under mechanical stirring at room temperature, 1.5 g of tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS) was slowly added and stirred for 8 h. Afterward, the SiO2@Fe3O4 particles were separated with a magnet and washed three times with anhydrous ethanol, then dried for further use.

Synthesis of borene-carbene compounds

The borene-carbene compound was synthesized using 1,3-dimethylimidazolium (IMe) as the N-heterocyclic carbene ligand and trimethylsilylborene (B(SiMe3)) as the boron source. In anhydrous toluene, 0.5 mmol of IMe and 0.55 mmol of B(SiMe3) were mixed and stirred at 50°C for 3.5 h. The solution gradually changed from light yellow to deep red, resulting in the formation of the IMe-B(SiMe3) complex. The reaction solution was then concentrated to a small volume by rotary evaporation, and deep red crystalline compounds were separated by low-temperature crystallization at −20°C. The structure of the IMe-B(SiMe3) compound was characterized using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, confirming it as the target product.

Functionalization of the mesoporous SiO2 layer

0.3 g of SiO2@Fe3O4 samples were dispersed in a solution containing 3.2 g of CTAB, 600 mL of deionized water, 10.0 g of 30 wt % ammonia solution, and 800 mL of ethanol. The mixture was stirred for 30 min to form a uniformly dispersed suspension. Under mechanical stirring, 2.0 mL of tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS) was slowly added, and after 15 min, 500 μL of PPh2CH2CH2Si(OEt)3 was added, and the stirring continued for 12 h to complete the functionalization reaction. The product was then separated with a magnet and refluxed in ethanol at 80°C for 24 h to remove the CTAB template. This process was repeated three times, followed by drying to obtain the functionalized mesoporous SiO2@Fe3O4 material.

Preparation of borene-carbene compound-loaded functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials

0.3 g of the functionalized SiO2@Fe3O4 material and 1.0 g of the IMe-B(SiMe3) compound were added to 80 mL of anhydrous toluene. Under an argon atmosphere, the mixture was stirred at 80°C for 24 h to complete the loading reaction. After the reaction, the product was washed three times with anhydrous toluene to remove any unreacted IMe-B(SiMe3) compound. Finally, the product was dried under vacuum at 60°C for 24 h to obtain the borene-carbene compound-loaded functionalized magnetic mesoporous material.

Instruments and characterization methods

Various advanced instruments and analytical techniques were employed to comprehensively characterize the structure, composition, and performance of the functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to observe the nanostructure and mesopore distribution of the materials, confirming the uniformity of the SiO2 layer and the encapsulation effect of the magnetic Fe3O4 core. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was also used to analyze the overall morphology and particle size distribution of the material, assessing the control over the size and uniformity during the preparation process. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was employed to detect characteristic absorption peaks of the material, including silicon-oxygen bonds (Si-O-Si), phosphorus-oxygen bonds (P-O), and signals associated with the borene-carbene compounds, thereby verifying the success of functionalization. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to further analyze the valence states and chemical bonding of the surface elements, confirming the presence of active centers such as boron and phosphorus in the functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was applied to examine the crystal structures of the magnetic Fe3O4 core and SiO2 coating layer, ensuring the stability of their crystal structures. Nitrogen adsorption-desorption analysis (BET) was used to evaluate the specific surface area, pore size distribution, and pore volume of the material to determine the success of the mesopore structure formation.

Catalyst activity testing

To systematically evaluate the catalytic performance of the functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials, two typical carbon-carbon coupling reactions, the Suzuki coupling reaction and the Sonogashira coupling reaction, were chosen. The activity, selectivity, and recyclability of the catalysts under different reaction conditions were tested. In the Suzuki coupling reaction, bromobenzene and phenylboronic acid were used as substrates, with potassium carbonate (K2CO3) as the base and a water/ethanol (V:V = 1:1) solvent mixture. The functionalized magnetic mesoporous material was used as the catalyst. The reaction was carried out at 80°C under stirring for 6 h, with a molar ratio of substrate to catalyst of 200:1. In the Sonogashira coupling reaction, iodobenzene and phenylacetylene were used as substrates, with triethylamine (Et3N) as the base, in a copper-free system with water/DMF (V:V = 1:1) as the solvent, and the reaction was carried out at 60°C under stirring for 8 h. The reaction conditions, such as catalyst amount, temperature, and time, were optimized for both reactions.

Green catalysis performance study

In this experiment, bromobenzene, phenylboronic acid, potassium carbonate, and the functionalized magnetic mesoporous material were added to a 100 mL reaction flask along with 5 mL of a water/ethanol mixed solvent. The reaction system was placed in an 80°C oil bath and stirred for 6 h, with multiple parallel experiments conducted using pure water, ionic liquids, and other solvent systems to study the influence of solvent type on reaction performance. After the reaction, the functionalized catalyst was separated using a magnet, washed three times with anhydrous ethanol, and dried for further use. The reaction products were quantitatively analyzed using gas chromatography (GC) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to determine the conversion rate and selectivity of the target products. Results

Catalyst structure characterization

To thoroughly verify the structural characteristics and functionalization effectiveness of the functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials, multiple characterization methods were employed for detailed analysis. The hysteresis curve of the material (Figure 2) reveals that the saturation magnetization of the magnetic Fe3O4 core is 65.3 emu/g, with residual magnetization and coercivity close to zero, indicating superparamagnetic behavior. This property ensures that the catalyst can be rapidly and efficiently separated under an external magnetic field, with no residual magnetism after the field is removed, which is beneficial for the recycling and regeneration of the catalyst in the reaction system.

Hysteresis curve of functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) results (Figure 3A) revealed that the material exhibited an overall regular spherical morphology, with particle sizes mainly distributed in the range of 150–200 nm and good overall dispersion. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observations (Figure 3B) showed that the average particle size of the Fe3O4 nanoparticles was 12.5 nm, and the surface was uniformly coated with a SiO2 shell of approximately 10 nm in thickness, indicating good uniformity and stability of the coating process. Electron microscopy images after six catalytic cycles demonstrated that the spherical morphology of the particles was generally retained, although the surface became rougher than that of the fresh sample, with slight local agglomeration. TEM analysis further confirmed that the core-shell interface remained distinct and the SiO2 shell thickness was largely unchanged, with only slight blurring at the edges, suggesting that the catalyst maintained high structural integrity and stability after repeated use, with only minor surface evolution.

Nitrogen adsorption-desorption analysis (Figure 4A) indicated that the initial catalyst possessed a specific surface area of 320 m2/g and a pore volume of 0.85 cm3/g. The pore size distribution curve (Figure 4B) revealed a main peak consistent with the average pore size of 3.2 nm obtained from adsorption-desorption calculations, confirming the successful construction of the mesoporous structure, which is beneficial for enhancing catalytic activity and substrate mass transfer. After six reaction cycles, the specific surface area of the catalyst slightly decreased to approximately 295 m2/g, and the pore volume reduced to 0.78 cm3/g, while the average pore size remained essentially unchanged at around 3.2 nm. These results indicate that the mesoporous structure remained stable after multiple reactions, with only minor losses in surface area and pore volume, possibly due to partial pore blockage or residual by-products. This observation is consistent with the high stability demonstrated in recycling experiments, further confirming the excellent reusability of the catalyst.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy further confirmed the structural characteristics of the materials at each stage (Figure 5). In SiO2@Fe3O4, characteristic peaks at 1080 cm−1 and 960 cm−1 were assigned to Si-O-Si and Si-OH bonds, respectively, indicating that the SiO2 layer was successfully coated on the Fe3O4 surface. In the spectrum of IMe-B(SiMe3), distinct absorption peaks appeared in the 1300–1400 cm−1 region, corresponding to the C-N stretching vibrations of the borene-carbene ligand, along with a C-H stretching vibration peak observed at 2950 cm−1. In the final IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 composite material, both the Si–O–Si and Si–OH characteristic peaks were retained, while additional ligand-related absorption peaks also appeared, confirming the successful completion of the functionalization process. Combined with the catalytic performance results, it can be inferred that the inorganic framework provided high structural stability and specific surface area, while the organic ligands effectively introduced active centers. The synergistic effect of the two contributed to the enhanced efficiency of the Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrum

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was further employed to analyze the chemical states and distribution of surface elements. As shown in Figure 6A, the survey spectrum of Fe3O4 displayed typical Fe 2p (∼710 eV) and O 1s (∼530 eV) peaks. In SiO2@Fe3O4, the appearance of a new Si 2p peak (∼103 eV) confirmed the successful coating of the silica shell. The IMe-B(SiMe3) sample exhibited characteristic C 1s (∼285 eV), N 1s (∼400 eV), and B 1s (∼192 eV) peaks, indicating the presence of the organic ligand. In the final IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 composite material, peaks corresponding to Fe, O, Si, C, B, and P were all detected. Notably, the B 1s peak at 192.3 eV and the P 2p peak at 134.5 eV confirmed that the borene-carbene compound was firmly anchored on the SiO2@Fe3O4 surface, successfully forming stable active centers.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure 6B) showed that the characteristic diffraction peaks of the Fe3O4 core remained essentially unchanged, demonstrating that its crystal structure was preserved during the functionalization process. After six catalytic cycles, the main diffraction peaks of the catalyst completely overlapped with those of the fresh sample, with only a slight reduction in peak intensity and no emergence of new peaks or peak shifts. This result indicates that the catalyst maintained good crystallinity and structural stability even after repeated recycling reactions.

Additionally, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) were used to analyze the composition and purity of the catalytic reaction products to evaluate the catalyst’s activity and selectivity in organic coupling reactions. The NMR spectrum (Figure 7) was used to characterize the functionalized borene-carbene compound, and the characteristic peaks in the 1H NMR spectrum confirmed the chemical structure of the target product, indicating the successful functionalization of the borene-carbene compound.

Kinetic performance analysis

The results in Table 1 show that in the aqueous-phase Suzuki coupling reaction, all three catalysts exhibited a gradual increase in both conversion and selectivity, though with significant differences in performance. The traditional catalyst SiO2@Fe3O4 achieved a conversion rate of 38.215% at 2 h, which increased to 87.153% at 6 h. While this demonstrates effective catalytic progression, its product selectivity remained in the range of 95.342–97.621%, indicating limited ability to suppress side reactions. By contrast, the functionalized magnetic mesoporous catalyst IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 consistently demonstrated superior performance throughout the reaction. Its conversion rate was 48.762% at 2 h and reached 93.874% at 6 h, significantly higher than that of the traditional catalyst, confirming its advantage in substrate activation and target product formation. This result suggests that the borene-carbene compound possesses a strong electron-donating effect, facilitating C–X bond cleavage and stabilizing reaction intermediates, thereby enhancing catalytic efficiency. Its selectivity remained in the range of 97.812–99.203%, superior to SiO2@Fe3O4 but still lower than the composite catalyst.

The TON and TOF results further confirmed this trend. At 6 h, IMe-B(SiMe3) achieved a TON of 225 and maintained an initial TOF of 54–55 h−1, higher than SiO2@Fe3O4 (218, 48–49 h−1) but slightly lower than the composite catalyst (234, 60–61 h−1). By contrast, when anchored on the mesoporous SiO2@Fe3O4 framework, the increased surface area and pore channels improved substrate mass transfer and enhanced accessibility of the active centers, thereby boosting catalytic efficiency.

In summary, the performance comparison of the three catalysts demonstrates that IMe-B(SiMe3), when used alone, accelerates C-X bond cleavage and reaction rates through electron-donating effects, while the mesoporous structure of SiO2@Fe3O4 improves substrate diffusion pathways and site utilization. When combined, pore structure regulation and surface functionalization form a complementary effect within the same system: pore structure optimization ensures efficient substrate diffusion and active site accessibility, while surface functionalization stabilizes intermediates and accelerates the coupling process through electronic effects. This synergistic interaction enables the composite catalyst to achieve significantly higher conversion, selectivity, and kinetic parameters than either component alone, thereby revealing the underlying mechanism of pore-functionality coupling and providing important insights for the design of green catalysts with both high activity and high selectivity.

In the aqueous-phase Sonogashira coupling reaction (Table 2), all three catalysts exhibited a gradual increase in conversion with prolonged reaction time, though their overall performance varied significantly. The traditional catalyst SiO2@Fe3O4 achieved a conversion of 35.412% at 2 h, reaching 86.954% at 6 h, with selectivity maintained between 94.123% and 97.643%. This indicates that while the catalyst could drive the reaction, its ability to suppress side reactions remained limited. By contrast, the functionalized magnetic mesoporous catalyst IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 consistently maintained higher conversion and selectivity throughout the reaction, reaching 45.732% at 2 h and 92.345% at 6 h, with selectivity stably ranging between 98.634% and 99.734%. This result is consistent with the performance observed in the Suzuki reaction (Table 1), confirming that the composite catalyst demonstrates efficient and stable catalytic characteristics across different coupling reactions. When used independently, IMe-B(SiMe3) also showed considerable activity. Its conversion was 40.215% at 2 h and 89.415% at 6 h, outperforming SiO2@Fe3O4 but still slightly lower than the composite catalyst. Its selectivity remained between 96.532% and 98.832%, higher than SiO2@Fe3O4 but lower than IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4. These results indicate that the electron-donating effect of the borene-carbene compound played a crucial role in substrate activation and intermediate stabilization.

Kinetic data further revealed this difference. In summary, the high conversion and selectivity of the composite catalyst in the Sonogashira reaction can be attributed both to the electron-donating effect of the borene-carbene compound and to the mass transfer advantages of the mesoporous carrier. When integrated, these effects acted synergistically across different scales, resulting in a performance far superior to that of any single component. This finding clearly demonstrates that the synergistic interaction between pore structure regulation and surface functionalization is the key driver of enhanced catalyst performance, providing a viable pathway for developing efficient green catalysts under copper-free and other stringent conditions.

Substrate scope study

To further validate the potential applicability of the IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 catalyst in organic coupling reactions, a substrate scope study was conducted based on the representative kinetic experiments. The investigation was expanded to evaluate the catalytic performance under different substrate substitution conditions. A series of aryl halides bearing electron-donating (–CH3, –OCH3) and electron-withdrawing (–NO2) substituents was selected, together with typical boronic acids or alkynes as coupling partners. The catalytic efficiency of IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 was systematically examined in both Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions, thereby assessing its substrate adaptability and general applicability within diverse electronic environments.

As shown in Table 3, the IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 catalyst exhibited excellent catalytic performance toward a broad range of substrates in the Suzuki coupling reaction. Using bromobenzene and phenylboronic acid as the benchmark substrates, the reaction achieved a conversion rate of 93.874% and a selectivity of 99.825%. When the substrate scope was further extended to various substituted aryl halides and boronic acids, the catalyst consistently maintained high activity and selectivity. Specifically, 4-bromonitrobenzene, which contains a strongly electron-withdrawing nitro group, afforded the highest conversion (95.137%), indicating that the electron-withdrawing effect can promote the reaction rate. In contrast, substrates bearing electron-donating groups, such as 4-methylbromobenzene and 4-methoxyphenylboronic acid, exhibited slightly lower conversions (around 91–92%), while selectivity remained above 99%. These results demonstrate that the functionalized magnetic mesoporous catalyst can effectively accommodate substrates with different electronic properties and substituent types, confirming its broad applicability and strong potential for practical applications.

However, the lower yield suggests potential issues with uneven substrate solubility or an increased occurrence of side reactions in the aqueous phase. Additionally, the hydrogen bonding interactions between the phospho-oxygen (P=O) bonds on the catalyst surface and water molecules could lead to a reduction in local reaction activity at high temperatures.

In the [BMIM]BF4 ionic liquid system, the conversion rate was 81.576%, the yield was 73.092%, and the E-factor was 0.077. Ionic liquids possess good solubility and conductivity, which can facilitate electron transfer and enhance the binding of the reaction substrate to the catalyst surface. In the γ-valerolactone (GVL) system, the conversion rate was 85.852%, the yield was 75.973%, and the E-factor was 0.055. As a bio-based solvent, GVL’s low viscosity and high polarity help to enhance substrate solubility and mass transfer efficiency, resulting in a relatively high yield. The excellent performance of this system is attributed to the strong hydrogen bonding network in the deep eutectic solvent, which stabilizes the active center and significantly reduces the risk of catalyst deactivation. Furthermore, DES exhibits high hydrophilicity and forms a synergistic effect with the phospho-oxygen bonds and carbene groups on the catalyst surface, aiding in faster reaction rates and better substrate conversion efficiency. However, the viscosity changes at high temperatures in the DES system could cause slight diffusion resistance, limiting the potential for even higher yields. In conclusion, the functionalized magnetic mesoporous material demonstrates excellent catalytic performance in various solvent systems. However, its specific performance is influenced by solvent polarity, viscosity, and the interaction between the solvent and the active centers of the catalyst.

Discussion

Comparative analysis

This study systematically investigated the design, synthesis, and green catalytic application of functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials, particularly focusing on the IMe-B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4 system. The results demonstrated that the material exhibited a high specific surface area (320.00 m2/g), large pore volume (0.85 cm3/g), and uniform mesoporous structure (average pore diameter of 3.2 nm), along with excellent superparamagnetism (65.3 emu/g). These structural advantages have endowed the catalyst with remarkable stability and recyclability, as evidenced by a mere ∼7.8% decrease in surface area after six cycles. In catalytic tests, the material showed superior performance in both Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions. Specifically, the Suzuki reaction achieved 93.874% conversion and 99.825% selectivity, while the Sonogashira reaction reached 92.345% conversion and 99.734% selectivity, both outperforming the reference catalyst SiO2@Fe3O4. The catalyst also displayed broad substrate adaptability, maintaining high selectivity (>99%) across electron-rich and electron-deficient substrates. Furthermore, under green solvent systems such as γ-valerolactone (GVL) and deep eutectic solvent (DES), the catalyst achieved high conversion rates (up to 89.673%) and low E-factors (as low as 0.055), confirming its excellent environmental compatibility. These findings verify that the synergistic interplay between the mesoporous structure and surface functionalization with borene-carbene compounds effectively enhances catalytic activity, selectivity, and stability. Overall, this work establishes a reliable strategy for designing recyclable, high-performance catalysts for sustainable organic synthesis and provides valuable insights into future industrial applications in green chemistry.

Current research in the development of functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials often focuses on a single strategy to improve catalytic performance. Singh et al. developed the SiO2@Fe3O4 catalyst via surface modification for Suzuki coupling reactions, indicating that the uniformly coated SiO2 layer enhanced the stability of active centers and substrate adsorption capacity.25 However, their functionalization strategy primarily relied on a single electronic effect regulation and did not fully consider the synergistic role of pore size distribution and molecular mass transfer. Building on this, the present study innovatively incorporated borene-carbene compounds, whose unique electron-donating properties and steric hindrance effects not only enhanced catalytic activity but also improved substrate diffusion efficiency through the mesoporous structure. This significantly improved the compatibility in large-molecule reaction systems, leading to better performance compared to the results of Singh et al. Weng et al. demonstrated that phosphorous-functionalized mesoporous SiO2 could form stable Lewis acid-base active centers on the surface, enhancing substrate adsorption ability.26 However, due to the steric hindrance of the phosphorous groups, their catalyst showed limited performance when handling large-molecule substrates. In this study, through the synergistic effect between borene-carbene compounds and P=O groups, the electronic regulation capacity of the surface active centers was enhanced while optimizing pore size distribution to overcome steric limitations on substrate reactions. This innovation improved the catalyst’s adaptability to complex substrates and effectively suppressed side reactions through steric effects. Wang et al. studied the application of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) in homogeneous catalysis, finding that the high specific surface area and tunable pore size of MOFs provide significant advantages in promoting catalyst recovery.27 However, these materials tend to deactivate under high-temperature or strong acid/base conditions, limiting their practical applications. In contrast, the introduction of a magnetic Fe3O4 core in the present study not only improved the mechanical strength and stability of the material but also enabled efficient separation and recovery through an external magnetic field. Experimental results showed that after six cycles, the conversion rate of the functionalized material decreased by less than 3%, significantly outperforming the stability of MOF catalysts in current research.

By integrating the experimental results of this study with relevant comparative literature, the underlying reasons for the catalyst’s outstanding performance in coupling reactions can be further elucidated. The mesoporous structure of the catalyst provides efficient mass-transfer channels, enabling aryl halides and boronic acids (or alkynes) to approach the active centers effectively. Meanwhile, the borene-carbene groups anchored on the surface exhibit strong electron-donating properties and act synergistically with the surface P=O groups, thereby lowering the energy barrier for C–X bond cleavage and stabilizing the reaction intermediates. In the Suzuki coupling reaction, the aryl halide undergoes C-X bond activation at the active sites, and the resulting intermediate of boronic acid is subsequently converted into biphenyl products. In the Sonogashira coupling reaction, aryl halide and alkyne cooperatively interact at the catalytic sites to form alkyne-substituted products. In addition, the Fe3O4 magnetic core not only imparts excellent recyclability to the material but may also contribute auxiliary effects on the stability and activity of the catalytic centers through its electronic properties. Overall, the high specific surface area and well-ordered mesoporous channels ensure efficient substrate transport, while the electronic effects of borene-carbene species serve as the key factor in enhancing catalytic performance.

This study not only enriches the design strategies for functionalized magnetic mesoporous materials but also further expands their application scope in the field of green organic synthesis, providing a new theoretical basis for catalyst design in green chemical reactions. On a theoretical level, the study introduces borene-carbene compounds into magnetic mesoporous materials, combining their electron-donating effects and mesoporous mass transfer characteristics, thereby proposing an innovative strategy for the synergistic optimization of catalytic activity and selectivity. On a practical level, the material’s high catalytic performance, excellent selectivity, and superparamagnetism make it easy to recover and recycle, laying a solid foundation for its promotion in industrial-scale green chemical reactions.

Limitations of the study

There are still some limitations in the study: the catalytic performance was only tested in the Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions, and its applicability in a broader range of reactions has not been verified; the material performance was mainly evaluated under laboratory conditions, and its catalytic effect and stability under industrial conditions remain unclear; the preparation cost of the borene-carbene compounds is relatively high, which may limit their large-scale practical application; The experimental validation was primarily conducted in millimole-scale reaction systems, which effectively demonstrated the catalyst’s activity and selectivity under typical conditions. However, for larger-scale applications, further experimental evaluation is still required to more comprehensively reflect its potential application value.

Method details

Synthesis of magnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles

In a 100 mL beaker, 5.4 g (20 mmol) of ferrous chloride tetrahydrate (FeCl2·4H2O) and 9.6 g (40 mmol) of ferric chloride hexahydrate (FeCl3·6H2O) were dissolved in 150 mL of ethylene glycol under magnetic stirring at room temperature until completely homogeneous. Subsequently, 12.3 g (100 mmol) of anhydrous sodium citrate was slowly added, and the pH of the system was adjusted to 7.0. The resulting mixture was transferred into a Teflon-lined autoclave and heated at 180 °C for 8 h. After cooling to room temperature, the black solid product was separated using a magnet and washed three times with ethanol and deionized water, respectively. The obtained material was dried at 40 °C for 12 h to yield Fe3O4 nanoparticles.

Preparation of SiO2@Fe3O4 core–shell particles

A total of 0.6 g of Fe3O4 nanoparticles was dispersed in 150 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid (0.2 M) and ultrasonicated for 20 min to remove surface impurities. The particles were separated using a magnet and washed three times with ethanol and deionized water, respectively. The treated Fe3O4 particles were then transferred into a mixed solution containing 800 mL of ethanol and 200 mL of deionized water, followed by the addition of 10 mL of 30 wt % ammonia solution to adjust the pH to 11. Under mechanical stirring at room temperature, 1.5 g of tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS) was added dropwise slowly, and the mixture was stirred continuously for 8 h. The resulting SiO2@Fe3O4 core–shell particles were magnetically separated, washed three times with anhydrous ethanol, and dried for further use.

Synthesis of the borene–carbene compound (IMe–B(SiMe3))

1,3-Dimethylimidazole (IMe) was used as the N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligand, and trimethylsilylborane (B(SiMe3)3) served as the boron source. In a dry toluene solvent, 0.5 mmol of IMe and 0.55 mmol of B(SiMe3)3 were mixed and stirred at 50 °C for 3.5 h. The solution color gradually changed from light yellow to dark red, indicating the formation of the IMe–B(SiMe3) complex. The reaction mixture was concentrated by rotary evaporation, and the deep-red crystalline product was obtained by crystallization at −20 °C. The structure of the compound was confirmed by proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR).

Functionalization of the mesoporous SiO2 layer

A 0.3 g sample of SiO2@Fe3O4 was dispersed in a solution containing 3.2 g of CTAB, 600 mL of deionized water, 10.0 g of 30 wt % ammonia, and 800 mL of ethanol, followed by stirring for 30 min to form a uniform suspension. Then, 2.0 mL of TEOS was slowly added dropwise, and after 15 min, 500 μL of PPh2CH2CH2Si(OEt)3 was introduced. The reaction mixture was stirred for another 12 h. The resulting product was magnetically separated and refluxed in ethanol at 80 °C for 24 h to remove the CTAB template; this process was repeated three times. The obtained material was dried to yield the functionalized mesoporous SiO2@Fe3O4 composite.

Preparation of the functionalized magnetic mesoporous material (IMe–B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4)

A mixture of 0.3 g of functionalized SiO2@Fe3O4 and 1.0 g of IMe–B(SiMe3) was added to 80 mL of anhydrous toluene under an argon atmosphere and stirred at 80 °C for 24 h. After the reaction, the product was magnetically separated and washed three times with anhydrous toluene to remove any unreacted organic species. The resulting solid was vacuum-dried at 60 °C for 24 h to afford the final functionalized magnetic mesoporous material (IMe–B(SiMe3)@SiO2@Fe3O4).

Characterization techniques

Various analytical techniques were employed to characterize the structure and properties of the synthesized materials:

TEM (Transmission Electron Microscopy): Used to observe the nanostructure and mesoporous distribution.

SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy): Applied to examine the morphology and particle size distribution.

FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy): Used to confirm the presence of Si–O–Si and P–O bonds and identify characteristic peaks of the organic ligands.

XPS (X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy): Performed to analyze the surface elemental composition, oxidation states, and chemical bonding environment.

XRD (X-ray Diffraction): Used to verify the crystalline structures of the Fe3O4 core and SiO2 shell.

BET (Nitrogen Adsorption–Desorption Analysis): Employed to measure the specific surface area, pore-size distribution, and pore volume.

NMR (Nuclear Magnetic Resonance): Conducted to confirm the structure of the organic ligands.

Catalytic activity evaluation

To systematically evaluate the catalytic performance of the materials, representative Suzuki and Sonogashira coupling reactions were carried out.

Suzuki Reaction: Bromobenzene and phenylboronic acid were used as substrates, K2CO3 as the base, and a water/ethanol mixture (v/v = 1:1) as the solvent. The reaction was conducted at 80 °C for 6 h with a substrate-to-catalyst molar ratio of 200:1.

Sonogashira Reaction: Iodobenzene and phenylacetylene were used as substrates, triethylamine as the base, and a water/DMF (1:1) mixture as the solvent. The reaction was carried out at 60 °C for 8 h.

The reaction products were quantitatively analyzed using gas chromatography (GC) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to determine the conversion and selectivity values.

Investigation of green catalytic performance

To assess the green applicability of the catalyst, reactions were performed in different environmentally benign solvent systems, including water, ethanol, a water/ethanol mixture, ionic liquid [BMIM]BF4, bio-based solvent γ-valerolactone (GVL), and deep eutectic solvent (DES). Product conversions and selectivities were measured by GC and HPLC, and green chemistry indicators such as E-factor, TON (Turnover Number), and TOF (Turnover Frequency) were calculated.

The results demonstrated that in the DES system, the catalyst exhibited excellent green catalytic activity with a conversion of 89.673%, a yield of 78.171%, and an E-factor of only 0.063, indicating outstanding efficiency and recyclability under environmentally friendly conditions.

Statistical methods and software

All data analyses were performed using OriginPro 2022 (OriginLab) and Microsoft Excel 2021.

Gas chromatography (GC) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) data were used to calculate reaction conversion and yield, while surface area and pore size data were automatically fitted using BET analysis software based on the BJH method.

Statistical tests and significance determination

Since the data obtained are continuous experimental measurements, mean comparison and standard deviation analysis were mainly applied. No hypothesis testing (such as t test or ANOVA) was performed unless explicitly stated.

When comparing performance differences between different reaction systems (e.g., solvent effects), a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used, with the level of statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

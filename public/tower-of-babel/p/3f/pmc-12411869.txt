Body mass index as a potential confounder in dutasteride and fertility studies
Authors: Amir Reza Akbari, Benyamin Alam, Ahmed Ageed
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the recent article titled ‘Long-term use of dutasteride to treat androgenic alopecia in young men may lead to persistent abnormalities in semen parameters’ by Kim et al. [1]. The authors provide valuable insights into the long-term impact of dutasteride on semen quality, significantly contributing to our understanding of fertility considerations in young men undergoing treatment for androgenic alopecia [1].

Although the study accounts for several important clinical variables, it appears to have overlooked a potentially significant confounding factor: body mass index (BMI). Obesity and higher BMI are independently linked with impaired semen parameters, including reduced sperm motility, concentration, and volume, as well as altered hormonal profiles [2]. Such effects might be additive or even synergistic with endocrine disruptions induced by dutasteride.

The absence of BMI or body composition data across treatment duration groups introduces the possibility that differences in metabolic health might have contributed to the observed decline in semen quality. If higher BMI was more common among groups with longer dutasteride exposure, this could have biased the results and partially explain the persistence of abnormal semen parameters after discontinuation.

We encourage the authors and future studies to include BMI as a covariate or stratification factor when assessing the reproductive safety of long-term dutasteride usage. Incorporating this aspect would enhance the validity of findings and facilitate more nuanced patient guidance.

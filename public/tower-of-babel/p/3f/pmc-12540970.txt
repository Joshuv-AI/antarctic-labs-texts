1. Psychological Adverse Events and Associated Safety Profiles of Doxepin: A Pharmacovigilance Study Using VigiBase.

Shin JY, et al.

Using WHO’s VigiBase (1967–2022), this study assessed the safety profile of doxepin compared with other tricyclic antidepressants through disproportionality analysis of adverse events of special interest (AESIs). Among 6,827 reports, over half involved women, mainly middle-aged. None of the predefined AESIs met the criteria for significant safety signals. However, drug abuse showed a notable signal (adjusted ROR, 2.41; 95% CI, 2.06–2.83). Although no definitive AESI-related safety concerns were found, the association with drug abuse highlights the importance of cautious prescribing and vigilant monitoring of adverse events in clinical practice.

2. Hospital-Based Surveillance of Pediatric Invasive Pneumococcal Diseases, 2016–2023 in Korea: Serotype Trends and Vaccination Policy.

Choi EH, et al.

In Korea, PCV10 and PCV13 were introduced into the NIP in 2014, followed by PCV15 in April and PCV20 in October 2024. This study analyzed serotype distribution in pediatric invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) cases (2016–2023) from 20 hospitals. Among 187 cases, major serotypes were 10A (21.9%), 15C (11.8%), 15A, 15B, 19A, and 23B. Compared to pre-COVID-19, 10A decreased while 23B and 6C increased. PCV13 serotypes comprised 12.3%, PCV15/20 common 3.2%, PCV20 unique 35.3%, and non-PCV20 36.4%. These findings highlight the growing role of non-PCV13 serotypes and inform future vaccination strategies.

3. Informed Consent in Radiation Oncology: A Nationwide Survey of Institutions in Korea.

Park HJ, et al.

This study examined radiotherapy (RT) consent forms across Korean institutions to assess variations in content, format, and detail for six major cancer types. Between June and October 2022, consent forms were collected from board-certified radiation oncologists. Forty-nine institutions participated, including all Regional Cancer Centers; 46 provided complete forms. Seventy percent documented RT purpose and categorized side effects as acute or chronic, while 30% did not. Considerable differences existed in how side effects were described, and most forms lacked probability information. These findings highlight significant inter-institutional variations and support the need for standardized, patient-centered RT consent forms.

4. Comparison of Cardiac Events and Survival Between Asymptomatic Diabetic and Nondiabetic Patients: Coronary CT Study.

Chun EJ, et al.

This study evaluated the prognostic value of coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) in 565 asymptomatic type 2 diabetes mellitus patients (T2DMs) compared with 1,130 matched non-diabetics. Over a median 47-month follow-up, major adverse cardiac events (MACE) and total cardiac events (TCE) were significantly higher in T2DMs (4.2% vs. 1.7% and 7.8% vs. 4.3%, respectively; P < 0.001). Cox analysis identified T2DM, obstructive stenosis, and proximal plaques as independent predictors. Kaplan–Meier curves showed survival depended on stenosis grade and T2DM status. CCTA effectively stratifies coronary risk in asymptomatic T2DM patients, complementing traditional risk factors.

5. Mycoplasma pneumoniae Infections Among Children and Adolescents in Korea, 2014–2024.

Lee SG, et al.

This study analyzed Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) infection and macrolide-resistant MP (MRMP) trends in Korean children from 2014 to 2024 using over 425,000 PCR tests and 11,000 resistance tests. Three nationwide epidemics occurred in 2015/16, 2019/20, and 2023/24, with peaks in Q3–Q4 and highest positivity in ages 7–9. Children under 12 were disproportionately affected during epidemics (P < 0.001). MRMP rates showed A2063G and A2064G mutations in 76.6% and 0.7%, respectively, with rising A2063G prevalence over time and decreasing rates with age. These findings highlight increasing infections and resistance, emphasizing the need for surveillance.

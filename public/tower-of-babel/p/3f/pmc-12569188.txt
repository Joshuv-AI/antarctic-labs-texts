Evaluation of the efficacy of combination therapy in the treatment of fungal otitis externa
Authors: Yan Li, Guangping Bai, Yonggang Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAssess the efficacy of sodium bicarbonate solution and Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream combination therapy in the treatment of fungal otitis externa. The experimental group received a combination therapy consisting of sodium bicarbonate solution and Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream, while the control group was treated with saline in conjunction with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream. The degree of external ear canal cleanliness was evaluated using endoscopic Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores before and after the intervention. Statistical analysis was performed to assess and compare VAS scores across multiple time points and severity levels, with the objective of determining the efficacy of combining sodium bicarbonate solution with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream in the treatment of fungal otitis externa. The experimental group exhibited a higher level of treatment efficacy than the control group did. Notably, significant differences in VAS scores were observed between the two groups on the 4th day (t = 4.391, p < 0.001) and the 7th day (t = 5.754, p < 0.001), indicating consistent superiority of the therapeutic effect in the experimental group. Specifically, at the end of the 7th day of treatment, the experimental group demonstrated significantly better efficacy than the control group in terms of the mild score (t = 5.101, p < 0.001). The use of sodium bicarbonate solution along with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream has proven to be highly effective in treating fungal otitis externa. Introduction

Fungal otitis externa is an inflammatory condition affecting the skin and subcutaneous tissues of the external ear cana. It is primarily caused by fungal infectionl1. Aspergillus, Candida, Penicillium, and Mucor are the most common fungi responsible for this condition. Among the molds, Aspergillus niger (5%) and Aspergillus fumigatus (1%) are the most prevalent species. Common yeast strains include Candida paracilata (13%), Candida albicans (3%), and Candida tropicalis (2%)2. Symptoms typically include tightness, swelling, severe itching, and potential anxiety. In severe cases, patients may experience swelling, pain, increased discharge, hearing impairment, tinnitus, and complications, such as external ear canal cellulitis, stenosis, eczema, and tympanic membrane perforation. Diagnosis relies on clinical symptomatology, otoscopy, and laboratory assessments such as fungal cultures and microscopic examinations3–7.

Although considerable research has been conducted on fungal otitis externa, there is still a significant need for comprehensive investigations into the understanding and management of this condition. It is crucial to focus on assessing the efficacy of treatment, optimizing treatment protocols, and implementing measures to prevent recurrence. By delving deeper into the underlying causes of the disease and exploring different treatment strategies, we can enhance therapeutic outcomes and provide clinicians with valuable guidance for interventions8–11.

The objective of this study was to use Visual Analog Scale (VAS) endoscopic scoring to evaluate the effectiveness of combining sodium bicarbonate solution with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream for the treatment of fungal otitis externa. The findings from this study provide valuable insights that can be utilized in clinical practice to aid in the effective management of this condition.

Materials and methods

Research participants

Between September 2023 and April 2024, 80 patients diagnosed with fungal otitis externa at our hospital were included in this study. The participants were randomly assigned to either the control or experimental group. The control group comprised 22 males and 18 females aged 19 to 63 years, with an average age of 36.0 ± 11.73 years. The experimental group included 20 male and 20 female patients aged between 18 and 64 years, with an average age of 37.6 ± 13.80 years. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients diagnosed with fungal otitis externa by endoscopy and fungal smear analysis. (2) Patients aged 18 to 64 years. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients suffered chronic otitis media, mastoiditis, labyrinthitis, vertigo, tinnitus, or hearing loss. (2) Patients with diabetes, immunosupression and other serious diseases that affect the evaluation of outcomes. (3) Patients with a history of ear therapy and medication. All patients were informed of the study and provided their written informed consent. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (approval number: 2023 − 0701) and have been performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The differential diagnosis and treatment recommendations for fungal otitis externa, including evidence levels, follow the 2019 AAO-HNS clinical practice guidelines.

The control group received a combination of normal saline and Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream. On the other hand, the experimental group received a combination of sodium bicarbonate solution and Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream (Table 1).

Methods

Scoring criteria and methods

VAS endoscopic score scale

A customized VAS scale was used to evaluate endoscopic signs of fungal otitis externa (Table 2). The scoring criteria encompassed various parameters such as ear canal skin cleanliness, extent of fungal presence, congestion, edema, secretions, erosion, eardrum integrity, and auricle hygiene. Each item was assigned a score, which was then totaled to yield an overall score with a maximum value of 10.

A self-developed VAS endoscopic grading scale (Table 3) was used to evaluate fungal otitis externa. The assessment categorized the total score on a scale ranging from 0 to 10. Results

At the start of the treatment course, all patients were scored and graded based on the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) endoscopic score and grading scale (Tables 2 and 3). Significant variations in VAS scores were observed across different severity levels in both the experimental (F = 133.109, p < 0.001) and control groups (F = 130.953, p < 0.01) (Table 4). The differences in grading were statistically significant.

The following results present a comparison of the treatment outcomes between the experimental and control groups during this period.

Descriptive analysis of VAS scores during treatment of fungal otitis externa

Based on the experimental data, there were statistically significant differences in the endoscopic VAS scores between the control group and the experimental group on days 4th and 7th days of treatment when compared to the 1st day. The p-value for both groups was 0 on the fourth day of treatment when compared to the first day. Similarly, on the 7th day of treatment, the p-value was also 0 for both groups compared to that on the 1st day. This consistent trend was observed in both groups (Table 5).

Significant changes in endoscopic scores during treatment: control vs. experimental groups

The progression of treatment revealed notable shifts in the box plots representing the endoscopic scores for both the control and experimental groups. On day 4, there was a noticeable decrease in the VAS scores in both groups, with a more substantial reduction observed in the experimental group than in the control group. By day 7, while the VAS scores of the control group did not show a significant decrease, those of the experimental group decreased significantly, approaching zero. (Fig. 2).

In the comparative analysis of endoscopic scores presented in Table 6, a significant difference in VAS scores between the two groups was observed on the 4th day of fungal otitis externa treatment (t = 4.391, p < 0.001). Additionally, when comparing VAS scores at the end of 7th day of treatment, there was still a significant difference between the groups (t = 5.754, p < 0.001).

Comparative analysis of VAS grading during fungal otitis externa treatment

The comparative analysis of VAS grading, as presented in Table 7, highlights a substantial change where in both groups experienced a shift from severe and moderate to mild scores by the end of the seventh day of treatment. Notably, a significant disparity was observed between the two groups in terms of the mild score (t = 5.101, p < 0.001).

To summarize, the experimental group demonstrated higher treatment efficacy than the control group (Fig. 1). The VAS scores showed a significant difference between the two groups on the 4th day (t = 4.391, p < 0.001) and the 7th day (t = 5.754, p < 0.001) (Table 6). Specifically, at the end of the 7th day of treatment, the experimental group showed significantly better efficacy than the control group in terms of the mild score (t = 5.101, p < 0.001) (Table 7).

Discussion

This study used otoendoscopy to visually evaluate various aspects related to the treatment of fungal otitis externa, including cleanliness of the external ear canal, inflammation levels, condition of the tympanic membrane, and therapeutic outcomes. The primary focus of this comparative analysis was to compare the effectiveness of sodium bicarbonate solution in combination with perisone versus normal saline alongside perisone for treating fungal otitis externa, thereby providing valuable insights for clinical interventions. Our clinical observations demonstrated that the experimental group had significantly better outcomes than the control group, especially on the 4th and 7th days of treatment. To investigate the reasons for the effectiveness of this specific combination, our analysis proceeded as follows.

The influence of an alkaline environment induced by sodium bicarbonate on fungal growth

The alkaline environment (pH 8.5) created by a 5% sodium bicarbonate solution can effectively restrain the growth and metabolism of fungi in the external auditory canal, as fungi multiply optimally within the pH range of 5.0 to 6.012,13. This impact can be ascribed to several mechanisms, such as decreased enzyme activity, compromised cell wall integrity, disturbed ionic levels, and impaired nutrient absorption13–15.

Enzyme Activity Limitation: Fungal enzymes, such as the pigment P450 enzyme and ergosterol 14α-demethylase, have restricted activity under alkaline conditions. This limitation hampers the normal metabolic processes that are crucial for fungal growth.

The synergistic therapeutic mechanism of triamcinolone and econazole cream

By synergistically utilizing the anti-inflammatory properties of triamcinolone and the potent antifungal properties of econazole, this cream provides a comprehensive solution for effectively alleviating inflammation and treating fungal infections. Distinct mechanisms of action are outlined below.

1. Triamcinolone:

Triamcinolone, a glucocorticoid with potent anti-inflammatory and anti-allergic effects, plays a crucial role in reducing skin inflammation and suppressing the immune system hyperactivity associated with fungal infections. As a result, it effectively alleviates the common symptoms of inflammation, including itching, redness, and swelling.

2. Econazole:

Econazole, which belongs to the miconazole family, is a powerful antifungal agent with broad-spectrum activity against various fungal species. Econazole works by inhibiting the activity of ergosterol 14α-demethylase, an enzyme crucial for the production of the essential elements required to maintain the structural integrity of fungal cell membranes. This disruption in ergosterol synthesis leads to a compromised fungal cell membrane, impairing its function, and ultimately inhibiting fungal growth16,17. Notably, econazole demonstrates heightened sensitivity towards Aspergillus compared to other antifungal agents like tioconazole, nystatin, amphotericin B, and carpofungin6,13,18.

Antifungal mechanism of combination therapy

This study investigated the effectiveness of combining sodium bicarbonate solution with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream to treat fungal otitis externa. The results showed that the combined treatment exhibited significant advantages compared with the control group. The mechanisms are summarized as follows.

The combined use of triamcinolone and econazole creams not only provides relief from inflammation but also effectively eliminates fungal infections through their synergistic actions. This combination therapy enhances fungal clearance by inhibiting hyphal growth and spore germination. The antifungal effects involve disruption of fungal cell integrity, mitochondrial function, and key signaling pathways. This disruption affects cellular processes, such as the cell cycle, mitochondrial autophagy, and cell wall maintenance. The structural elements within cells and mitochondria are affected, leading to a disturbance in tricarboxylic acid cycling, mitochondrial membrane potential, and ATP production. Additionally, there is a reduction in ergosterol content in various cellular membranes, which further contributes to the inhibition of fungal growth13.

Furthermore, transcriptome analysis revealed that combination therapy had an impact on 309 upregulated and 233 downregulated key fungal genes. The upregulated genes primarily participated in amino acid metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, and cell wall-related functions. However, downregulated genes are also involved in various cellular metabolic processe15.

Finally, the combined use of sodium bicarbonate and a bacterial antagonist demonstrated significant inhibitory effects on both mycelial growth and spore germination of M. fructigina, resulting in a fungal inhibition rate ranging from 60.97% to 100%. Conversely, when sodium bicarbonate or a bacterial antagonist was used individually, the inhibition rates were lower, ranging from 9.27 to 64.83%14.

One limitation of this study is the relatively small sample size, which may not adequately represent the full clinical diversity of patients with fungal otitis externa. This may restrict the generalizability of the results. Furthermore, the study period was limited in duration, and therefore, the long-term efficacy and recurrence rates associated with the treatment remain unclear, warranting further investigation.

Conclusion

The use of sodium bicarbonate solution along with Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream has proven to be highly effective in treating fungal otitis externa. This underscores the importance of expanding the adoption of this therapeutic approach, highlighting its potential for widespread application.

Further investigations are needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms that contribute to the efficacy of the combination of sodium bicarbonate solution and Triamcinolone Acetonide and Econazole Nitrate Cream for the treatment of fungal otitis externa. Moreover, a thorough exploration of various drug combinations, dosages, and treatment durations will be conducted to assess their impact on the effectiveness of treating fungal otitis externa. These studies aimed to identify the most suitable therapeutic strategy for optimal treatment outcomes.

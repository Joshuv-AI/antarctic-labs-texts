Optimized wheat seed classification using YOLO with morphological image feature enhancement
Authors: B Deepika, N Shanmugapriya, R Gopi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe article presents a superior computer vision system to detect and grade wheat seeds. It is concerned about the integration of deep-learning detection and conventional image processing methods to enhance the overall classification accuracy. The current methods of seed classification in wheat seeds have been found to lack proper visibility of the features particularly in low contrast images, small defects, or overlapping of seeds, and also, irregular lighting situations. The conventional feature extractors are weak, and the deep models by itself fail in cases where the morphological features like grooves, cracks, and shriveling are not well pronounced. To overcome these constraints, the proposed YOLO-Integrated Morphological Feature Enhancement Pipeline (Y-MFEP) uses dilation, erosion, opening, closing and top-hat transformations to enhance structural features and detect them using YOLO. The fused images are the improved feature maps and the original images, which allows the YOLO to identify finer seed variations more accurately. Such a hybrid pipeline enhances the visibility of the edges, defects, and texture uniformity without sacrificing the real-time detection performance. The given method is used to grade the quality of wheat at agricultural processing and procurement centers automatically. It guarantees quick, stable, and high-scaling classification of fit, broken, shriveled, and infected seeds. The results demonstrate that Y-MFEP has a major advantage of improving the accuracy, mAP and defect-detection sensitivity, which creates a more dependable and automated wheat quality measurement. The classification accuracy (85–95%), defect sensitivity index (0.775), edge clarity score (78–85), intersection over union (75–82%), and small object detection rate (75–90%) is reached in the proposed method.Keywords: Wheat classification, YOLO, Morphological enhancement, Image processing, Seed quality assessment, Feature fusionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Background

Demand of high quality agricultural produce has increased and with this, there has been an augmented demand of an efficient, precise and automated method of evaluating the quality of grain1. Wheat being one of the most popular staple crops, is a source of food security and economic stability in the world2. The wheat seeds require proper classification to allow grading, pricing, and evaluation of whether they can be used in the food processing sector3. Traditional inspection involves extensive manual evaluation, which is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and, in most cases, biased by human subjectivity4. With the development of computer vision and artificial intelligence, automated classification using deep learning has emerged as an alternative with the potential to provide fast, objective evaluation5.

Nevertheless, the current machine vision and deep learning have a number of limitations in wheat seed classification6. Traditional image processing methods are incapable of extracting fine morphology when contrast is low or noise is high7. Conversely, the inability of defects, including microcracks, shriveling, color flaws, and fungal infections, to be identified with high confidence in raw images implies that deep learning models, like CNNs and generic object detectors, are not able to identify them8. Besides that, overlapping seeds, uneven light, and differences in seed composition lead to erroneous and unreliable identification, limiting the feasibility of such systems in practice within an actual agricultural environment9. To address these shortcomings, this paper proposes a superior detection and classification model that maximizes the classification of wheat seeds by enhancing the morphological image features of YOLO10.

The solution suggested combines the speed and accuracy of the YOLO algorithm with morphological image processing and feature improvement11. These are dilation, erosion, opening, closing, and top-hat transformations, which enhance important seed features, such as edges, contours, defects, and texture variation12. These enhanced morphological representations are added to the original image channels to provide additional information to YOLO, enabling the model to identify and classify wheat seeds under more challenging imaging conditions13. The Y-MFEP scheme will enhance the detection of the soft structural and textural features, and the system will be able to differentiate between better healthy, shriveled, broken, and infected seeds14. The proposed framework also enhances the development of smart quality evaluation technologies, equitable business practices, the reduction of post-harvest losses, and overall agricultural supply chain performance15.

Motivation

Correct segmentation of every harvest of wheat seeds should be done, and prices of wheat grains must be reasonable enough to prevent losses upon harvesting. The existing methods, however, are unable to capture fine defects, low contrast objects, and uneven lighting, leading to unreliable images. This motivates the establishment of a robust framework to enhance morphological features, the use of YOLO to accurately detect wheat seeds in real time, and the automated evaluation of wheat seed quality.

Challenges in existing wheat seed classification

The problem with classifying wheat seeds is that morphological differences are subtle but distinct, and that uneven illumination, overlapping seeds, and low contrast complicate identification with existing machine learning and deep learning models. Traditional classifiers are poor, and independent deep models fail to capture fine structure. Therefore, there is a need for a stable, enhanced, and real-time classification system that accurately distinguishes between different types of wheat seeds and flaws.

Novelty of the paper

In contrast to generic enhancement-YOLO pipelines, this paper proposes defect-oriented morphological tuning to small, low-contrast seed defects and a lightweight weighted fusion unit that does not compromise real-time performance. Systematic ablation, consistent gains in challenging situations, and cross-dataset generalization with decoupled feature enhancement and detection are the factors supporting novelty.

Research gap

Although the use of machine learning and deep learning models has brought a major breakthrough in seed classification of wheat, there are still major limitations. Conventional methods make use of hand-crafted morphological or structured numerical characteristics that fail to capture the imaging complexities in the real world e.g. uneven illumination, overlapping seeds and low-contrast defects. In contrast, contemporary CNN and YOLO-based architecture rely on global feature recognition and classification quality, but tend to overlook finer structural anomalies such as micro-cracks, grooves, and shriveling shapes, especially at adverse visual conditions. Attention mechanisms enhance weighting of features but usually do not explicitly enhance structural information prior to detection, but at the feature-map level. In addition, very little research combines fine-grained defect localization, noise resistance, and efficiency in real-time deployment in a single framework. Thus, there is an obvious gap to a computationally efficient and structurally optimized detection pipeline yielding a higher sensitivity to small-defects and still performing in practice in real-time operation in a working agricultural setup.

Research gap

Current approaches rely on handcrafted features or fixed deep models that struggle with low-contrast seeds, overlapping kernels, changes in illumination, and fine-grained morphological structures. Numerous classifiers achieve high accuracy on controlled datasets but perform poorly in real-world applications. Moreover, most previous works do not combine improvements in morphological features with the latest object detectors, which creates a knowledge gap for accurate, scalable, and real-time wheat seed classification.

To prove the originality of the suggested framework, we carried out some component-specific validation of the defect-oriented morphological tuning module and the lightweight weighted fusion strategy. In particular, morphological tuning was assessed by turning off adaptive defect improvement but maintaining the default preprocessing pipeline and found that defect boundary clarity was significantly reduced and the overall average performance of the key evaluation metrics was decreased. Likewise, the role of the lightweight weighted fusion module was confirmed through replacing adaptive weight learning with uniform feature aggregation which showed lower robustness in integrating multi-features and higher classification variance. Moreover, cross-dataset generalization tests showed better stability under the joint activation of both modules proving that the presented design increases the feature discriminability without impairing the computational efficiency. All these findings indicate that a new approach is that the proposed framework is novel in its synergistic combination of defect-aware improvement and adaptive feature fusion, as opposed to architecture changes, which are incremental.

Methodology

The suggested framework is an automated method for assessing the quality of wheat seeds using an improved YOLO-based detection pipeline. Raw seed images are pre-processed, morphologically enhanced, and fused with multi-features to highlight colour, texture, and structural defects26. A YOLO backbone is used to process the fused representation into real-time detections, and grading is then used to classify the seeds into quality classes and facilitate high-throughput, objective decision-making27.

YOLO-integrated morphological enhancement framework

The paper proposes a dedicated image processing block with dilation, erosion, opening, closing, and top-hat operators to accentuate small morphological patterns28. This greatly benefits the detection of small flaws such as cracks, grooves, shriveling, and slight surface damage, even in low-contrast or uneven lighting29. The method overcomes the major weaknesses of classical and deep learning methods by increasing the structural clarity before detection30.

The proposed system will take an image of a wheat seed through a series of steps to achieve automated quality grading. Raw wheat seed images are first processed and adjusted to standard image quality through preprocessing and normalization, such as resizing to the YOLO input size, contrast control, and noise reduction. Morphological feature enhancement is then implemented to reveal structural features useful for defect detection. The feature fusion module combines original and improved images, adding extra information to the representation passed to the YOLO detection backbone. On this backbone, multiscale features are learned in the convolutional layers, and non-maximum suppression is used to refine detections with redundant bounding boxes. The sampled seeds are then sorted and rated for quality. Lastly, after processing, it creates decision-support detection visualizations, statistical summaries, and automated quality reports, as shown in Fig. 1.

Morphological parameter selection

Operations on morphology were performed using fixed structuring elements chosen to suit the geometry and size of wheat seed defects. The structuring element was a disk and was used to maintain the natural rounded shape of wheat kernels, whereas line elements were applied selectively to support long cracks and grooves. The kernel size was selected empirically based on the dataset’s mean seed size and remained unchanged throughout the experiments to ensure reproducibility.

In particular, operations of dilation and erosion were performed with a 3 × 3 kernel, which increased the irregularities of the fine surface without excessive smoothing of boundaries. The opening and closing operations used a 5 × 5 kernel to reduce small noise artifacts and fill in small surface gaps. The top-hat transformation was based on a 7-by-7 structuring element, which was used to highlight tiny, bright defects such as cracks and fungal spots in a low-contrast environment. These parameter selections are a compromise between defect reduction and shape preservation, and they were empirically tested on a held-out validation set.

A single iteration was used for all morphological operations to prevent excessive smoothing while retaining fine defect structures shown in Table 2. The morphological parameter selection given in the table was used to determine its influence on feature enhancement and detection performance.

The successive expansion of the kernel size (3 × 3 (dilation and erosion) to 5 × 5 (opening and closing) and lastly 7 × 7 (top-hat transformation) is done purposely to strike a balance between local detail preservation and extensive structure development. Smaller kernels (3 × 3) work in a small receptive field and allow fine refinement of boundaries and removal of noise without seed shape distortion. Medium sized kernels (5 × 5) give more neighborhood context which helps to remove small artifacts and fills minor surface gaps without destroying morphological consistency. The bigger kernels (7 × 7) increase the field of view (effective) which enables the top-hat transformation to identify small and low-contrast defects like micro-cracks and fungal spots that should be placed in a wider context and compared. This hierarchy kernel architecture can guarantee multi-scale structural enhancement, which enhances the visibility of defects without over-smoothing or the absence of fine details.

Algorithm 1: Morphological Feature Enhancement Pipeline

The underlying structure of the wheat seed images is improved by Algorithm 1, enabling better classification. It employs morphological processes, including dilation, erosion, opening, closing, and top-hat filtering, to enhance the visibility of contours, grooves, cracks, and textural variations that would otherwise be too weak even in poor light or on low-contrast deep models. The algorithm applies kernels of varying sizes to each input image, generating better maps that reveal finer-grained features. These maps are used to create a representation image that is enriched with the original image. MFEA can improve edge clarity, defect visibility, and morphology-specific cues, thereby significantly enhancing YOLO’s performance. It is performed in a hierarchical morphological improvement procedure. Edges are sharpened using dilation and erosion, whereas noise is removed using small kernels via opening and closing. Edge stabilization and removal of small artifacts are performed using medium-sized structuring elements. The top-hat transformation uses a larger kernel to detect low-contrast defects, such as microcracks and grooves. The morphological gradient also improves the edge discontinuities. Finally, the processed image is weighted with the improved structural maps to create a defect-sensitive feature space, which is optimized to be identified by YOLO.

Dataset description

The Wheat Variety Classification dataset on Kaggle contains data on wheat kernels belonging to three classes: Kama, Rosa, and Canadian, with 70 samples per class. Instead of being represented by actual seed images, the dataset represents each kernel using physical and geometrical properties (e.g., size, shape - i.e., area, length, width, compactness, etc.) instead of the original RGB images. Such structured numeric data facilitates classification or machine learning to accomplish varietal identification based on kernel morphology rather than deep-learned image-based features. It is easy to use in classical ML experiments and might not be of first-order importance for image-based detection or computer vision pipelines38.

The Kaggle dataset provides numerical morphological features, which help confirm the relevance of these features and enable comparison with classical ML models. YOLO + morphological pipeline is a conceptual and practical extension of image-based systems in the real world, in which the same morphological properties are directly extracted using seed images. The wheat variety dataset considered in this paper is the one provided by Kaggle, consisting of 70 samples in one category and describing each wheat kernel using structured numerical indicators derived from physical and morphological tests, rather than raw RGB images. Consequently, this dataset is not representative of the actual image acquisition conditions in the real world, such as variations in illumination, background clutter, or seed overlap. The dataset is therefore more useful for validating the relevance of morphological features and trends in classification rather than for overall image-based detection.

The similar morphological enhancement framework, proposed as a YOLO-based approach, is expected to be deployed at the image level and is conceptually justified by the available dataset characteristics. Although the results indicate that morphological representations can be enhanced, extensive image datasets are needed to conduct robust validation in the real world. This weakness inspires further research, in which practical training and the assessment of raw wheat seed images in a realistic agricultural setting are conducted.

Dataset limitations and future directions

The Kaggle data is relatively small and not very diverse, which might limit the extrapolation potential of the suggested scheme to a real-life agricultural setting. To enhance robustness, further experimentation will involve increasing and diversifying the volume of image-based wheat seed data across different illumination, background, and acquisition setups. Moreover, synthetic methods of data augmentation, such as rotation, scaling, contrast adjustment, noise addition, and simulated defects, will be used to increase variability and reduce overfitting. The strategies would aim to enhance the robustness of the models and support their deployment in the real world. The Kaggle data is used to confirm the discriminative relevance of morphological features and to give a comparative reference point with traditional machine learning solutions, as opposed to actually training and evaluating the entire image-based detection pipeline. The suggested YOLO-based morphological enhancements framework is built in the form of a practical extension to the implementation in real-world conditions using image-based seed inspection systems. Defect-wise sensitivity is computed using seed-level defect annotations rather than pixel masks. Each seed instance is labeled for the presence of cracks, grooves, shriveling, or surface damage via expert visual inspection. A TP occurs when the model correctly predicts a labeled defect; an FN occurs when it misses an annotated defect; and an FP occurs when a defect is predicted but not annotated.

Besides, the introduction of a special mitigation section reinforces the operational reliability of the framework by addressing the issue of operational risks and corrective actions. Ablation study CWA component-wise contribution analysis

Effects of the individual morphological components

Ablation experiments used to isolate the contribution of each morphological operation were performed by applying dilation, erosion, opening, and top-hat transformations individually. It has been found that top-hat enhancement is highly effective at improving defect sensitivity, edge clarity, and small-object detection with dilation, and at reducing noise and stabilizing boundaries with erosion and opening. A baseline and an ablation study, with uniform metrics (accuracy, mAP@0.5, DSI), will be added: YOLO-only baseline, YOLO + dilation/erosion, YOLO + opening/closing, YOLO + top-hat, and, finally, YOLO + full morphological fusion. The hybrid design will be justified by incremental improvements in performance at every level.

Quantitative ablation evaluation: signal systematically with evaluation

Systematic ablation was performed by progressively adding enhancement measures and feature fusion plans whilst holding the YOLO backbone constant. The accuracy of detection, edge clarity, and the ability to detect small objects improved gradually, and weighted feature fusion performed better than channel concatenation, validating the complementary effect of each component.

The baseline classifiers (QSVM, KNN, and ELM-PSO) are provided to test the discriminative power of morphological features with the help of the provided structured wheat data and cannot be viewed as the direct competitors of the proposed image-based YOLO pipeline. As Kaggle Wheat Variety does not offer raw seed images, but numerical morphological descriptors, it is not possible to train and evaluate CNN or YOLO-like architectures on the same input modality. In its turn, this puts the suggested YOLO-based morphological enhancement architecture in the position of a real-world image-based expansion inspired by the real-life grain inspection systems, whereas the tabular baselines are used as mere landmarks of feature applicability and trend tracking.

To confirm the usefulness of proposed Edge Clarity Score (ECS), a correlation analysis between ECS and performance indicators accepted by the researchers, such as Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), and F1-score of defect boundary detection has performed. The statistical analysis showed that there was a significant positive correlation between ECS and SSIM, and thus the higher the value of ECS, the better is the structural preservation and continuity of the boundaries. Likewise, ECS was found to have moderate to strong relationship with PSNR, which validates that the increased edge clarity is correlated with the better overall image reconstruction quality. Also, ECS demonstrated high correlation to the boundary-level F1-score, which proves its consistency as an indicator of correct delineation of defects. These results make ECS a complementary measure, which measures localized sharpness of boundaries and defect visibility phenomena that cannot be entirely captured by global reconstruction or classification measures, per se. Hence, ECS is more interpretable and can aid in a more thorough analysis of defect detection performance.

Conclusion

An original YOLO-based MFEP for reliable wheat seed classification is introduced. Y-MFEP, by combining classical morphological operations (e.g., dilation, erosion, opening, closing, and top-hat transformations) with deep learning detection, enhances structural features that are often ignored by traditional approaches (e.g., grooves, cracks, and shriveling). The combination of refined feature maps and original images enables YOLO to capture fine-scale differences, thereby enhancing edge sharpness, defect detection, and texture uniformity. The efficacy of Y-MFEP is experimentally proven by classification accuracies of 82–87, a defect sensitivity index of 0.775, edge clarity scores of 78–85, an IoU of 75–82, and small-object detection rates of 75–90. The above results demonstrate the strength, accuracy, and applicability of the framework for real-time automated grading of wheat quality, providing consistent, scalable classification of healthy, broken, shriveled, and infected seeds. Y-MFEP is therefore a good solution to the contemporary agricultural processing and quality control.

Future work

Subsequent research will incorporate hyperspectral imaging and a transformer-based feature extractor to enhance defect visibility and spectral-textural discrimination. The lightweight Y-MFEP versions will be designed for use at the edges in handheld devices and mobile graders. To enhance robustness, generalizability, and large-scale field adaptability, expanding the dataset to cover different wheat varieties and environmental conditions will be more effective. Structured pruning and post-training quantization model compression strategies will be investigated to enable edge deployment. Pruning reduces redundant convolutional filters, reducing memory and computation, and quantization converts weights to low-precision formats to enable faster inference. Whereas some minor degradation in accuracy can be tolerated, morphological enhancement can be used to maintain detection strength and detect defectiveness under compressed conditions.

The next step of the research will be the extension of the dataset in various real-world situations and the testing of the model on a variety of grain to evaluate extrapolation. Furthermore, the lightweight optimization of edge deployment and additional enhancement of the morphological enhancement module will be considered to make that more efficient and scalable in the real agricultural condition.

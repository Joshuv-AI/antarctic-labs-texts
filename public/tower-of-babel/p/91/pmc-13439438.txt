H. pylori-induced hsa_circ_0085465 promotes gastric cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by sponging miR-33b-3p
Authors: Jing Wu, Hongpeng Liu, Furui Zhang, Jiale Chen, Kunmei Liu, Juan Chen, Le Guo, Zhen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a type of non-coding RNA molecule whose functional processes in carcinogenesis and progression are not fully known. Recent studies suggest that circRNAs can act as functional RNAs involved in the progression of various tumors. This study aims to investigate the role of hsa_circ_0085465 in the proliferation, migration, and invasion processes of gastric cancer cells. By establishing models of Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)-infected normal gastric epithelial cells and gastric cancer cells, we conducted whole transcriptome sequencing and bioinformatics analysis, which led to the identification of a significantly upregulated circular RNA molecule, hsa_circ_0085465. Subsequently, we investigated the function of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer through in vitro functional experiments, including scratch wound healing assays and transwell assays, as well as in vivo animal experiments. Then, the downstream target miRNA of hsa_circ_0085465 were identified using sequencing and bioinformatics analysis, and the binding between hsa_circ_0085465 and the target gene miR-33b-3p was validated using dual-luciferase reporter gene assays and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Finally, we explored the interaction between hsa_circ_0085465 and miR-33b-3p through in vitro functional analyses. In a gastric cell model infected with H. pylori, the expression level of hsa_circ_0085465 was significantly upregulated. In vitro experiments demonstrated that hsa_circ_0085465 promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells, while in vivo subcutaneous tumor model further confirmed that hsa_circ_0085465 markedly accelerates tumor growth. Subsequent analyses identified miR-33b-3p as a downstream target of hsa_circ_0085465, and miR-33b-3p was found to suppress the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells. Rescue experiments further demonstrated that miR-33b-3p can partially reverse the oncogenic effects of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer. H. pylori induced the upregulation of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cells, and hsa_circ_0085465 functions as a molecular sponge for miR-33b-3p, thereby promoting the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55204-6.

Introduction

Gastric cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies of the digestive system worldwide, with high incidence and mortality rates. According to GLOBOCAN 20221,2, gastric cancer is the fifth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally. Owing to its nonspecific and insidious early symptoms, most patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, leading to a poor prognosis with a five-year survival rate of less than 10%3. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying gastric carcinogenesis and identifying novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets are critical for improving clinical outcomes. Gastric cancer development is driven by complex interactions among genetic susceptibility, environmental factors, and the gastric microbiome4.

In recent years, advances in molecular oncology have led to the identification of several key signaling pathways involved in gastric cancer. For instance, the TP53 signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in regulating apoptosis in gastric cancer cells, and accumulating evidence indicates that anticancer bioactive peptides can induce apoptosis through activation of the TP53 signaling cascade5. In addition, mutations in the FAT4 gene have been reported to be closely associated with elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB) and favorable clinical outcomes6, highlighting the prognostic significance of genomic alterations in gastric cancer. Meanwhile, increasing attention has been directed toward the regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs and intracellular signaling pathways. For example, the long non-coding RNA WT1-AS has been shown to inhibit tumor growth and promote autophagy by suppressing the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway7. Moreover, natural small-molecule compounds have emerged as promising therapeutic agents; notably, p-hydroxycinnamaldehyde exhibits anti-gastric cancer activity through multi-target modulation of tumor-related signaling pathways8. Collectively, these findings suggest that gastric cancer development and progression are governed by complex, multilayered regulatory networks involving diverse molecular mechanisms.

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) is a Gram-negative microaerophilic bacterium capable of persistently colonizing the human gastric mucosa. With a global infection rate exceeding 50%, it is a major causative agent of various digestive system diseases. H. pylori infection is strongly associated with gastric cancer development and is classified as a Group 1 carcinogen for gastric cancer by the World Health Organization9,10. H. pylori persistently induce chronic inflammation in the gastric mucosa through the secretion of virulence factors such as vacuolating cytotoxin A (VacA) and cytotoxin-associated gene A (CagA)11,12, thereby activating oxidative stress–related signaling pathways, including NF-κB13,14 and MAPK pathways15, and disrupting genomic stability in gastric epithelial cells. Concurrently, H. pylori alter host gene expression profiles, thereby promoting the multistep progression of gastric carcinogenesis from precancerous lesions—including chronic superficial gastritis, atrophic gastritis, intestinal metaplasia, and dysplasia—to invasive carcinoma. Recent studies have demonstrated that, in addition to inducing inflammation and activating oncogenic signaling pathways, H. pylori can regulate gastric cancer progression by modulating the expression of various non-coding RNAs and mRNAs16,17. For instance, it influences the levels of microRNAs and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) to regulate apoptosis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and chemotherapy resistance18,19. However, whether and how H. pylori modulate the expression networks of emerging circular RNAs (circRNAs) and thereby contributes to gastric carcinogenesis remain incompletely understood. With advances in molecular biology, the regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs in gastric cancer development have been progressively elucidated. CircRNAs a novel class of covalently closed-loop non-coding RNAs, play important roles in the molecular regulatory networks of H. pylori-associated gastric cancer owing to their high stability, tissue specificity, and diverse biological functions20,21. Therefore, in-depth investigation of aberrantly expressed circRNAs in H. pylori-associated gastric cancer and elucidation of their regulatory interactions with miRNAs may provide novel molecular targets for early diagnosis and targeted therapy.

CircRNAs are a recently identified class of endogenous RNA molecules characterized by a covalently closed-loop structure lacking 5′ caps and 3′ poly(A) tails. Accumulating evidence indicates that circRNAs are extensively involved in gene expression regulation and play critical roles in cellular differentiation, tissue homeostasis, and disease progression22–24. Mechanistically, circRNAs function as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) to sponge microRNAs, regulate transcriptional activity, and interact with RNA-binding proteins25. Notably, circRNAs frequently display tissue and disease-specific expression patterns. Owing to their circular configuration, circRNAs are resistant to exonuclease-mediated degradation, conferring greater stability in vivo than linear RNAs26. Aberrant circRNAs expression have been implicated in the pathogenesis of diabetes, neurological disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and multiple malignancies27. In gastric cancer, specific circRNAs have been reported to regulate cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and the tumor immune microenvironment, highlighting their potential as key functional regulators and therapeutic targets28,29. Among the numerous circRNAs, hsa_circ_0085465 (also termed circ-ATAD2) has garnered increasing research attention. This circRNA is derived from exons 20–21 of the ATPase family AAA domain-containing protein 2 (ATAD2) gene. The host gene ATAD2 is a well-recognized oncogene involved in chromatin remodeling, epigenetic regulation, and the activation of cell proliferation–related signaling pathways. ATAD2 is highly expressed in a variety of malignancies, including esophageal, lung, liver, colorectal, cervical, and prostate cancers, where it promotes tumor progression30–35. However, whether hsa_circ_0085465 is dysregulated under.

H. pylori infection and its precise biological functions and molecular mechanisms in gastric cancer remain largely unexplored.

Initially, high-throughput sequencing combined with bioinformatics analysis was used to screen and identify hsa_circ_0085465 as a circular RNA that is significantly upregulated in Helicobacter pylori–infected gastric cells. To further elucidate the effects of hsa_circ_0085465 on gastric cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, a series of in vitro and in vivo functional experiments were conducted. In addition, to investigate the molecular mechanism by which hsa_circ_0085465 may promote gastric cancer progression, bioinformatics analysis, dual-luciferase reporter assays, and fluorescence in situ hybridization were performed, which verified that it may bind to the downstream target gene miR-33b-3p. Subsequently, further functional rescue experiments indicated that hsa_circ_0085465 influences the progression of gastric cancer by binding to miR-33b-3p. Collectively, this study aims to elucidate the oncogenic role and underlying regulatory mechanism of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer, thereby providing novel insights into circRNA-mediated gastric carcinogenesis and progression, as well as identifying potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for the precise diagnosis and treatment of gastric cancer.

Materials and methods

Cells, H. pylori strains and materials

Human gastric cancer (GC) cell lines AGS, BGC-823, and SGC-7901, along with the gastric epithelial cell line GES-1 and HEK 293T cells, were obtained from Ningxia Medical University. All cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (ViviCell, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (ViviCell, China) and 100 U/mL penicillin–streptomycin (Life Technologies, USA). Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. H. pylori (ATCC 43504) were generally incubated in a microaerophilic condition (5% O2, 10% CO2, 85% N₂) for 2–3 days. For infection experiments, the multiplicity of infection (MOI) was set at 100:1. Lentiviral vectors were constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China).

RNA extraction, qRT-PCR

The total RNA was isolated from cells using total RNA extraction reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, cDNA was synthesized using miRNA-specific stem-loop primers and a reverse transcription kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) utilizing a real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR kit (TaKaRa, Japan). All primers, including those for hsa_circ_0085465 divergent and convergent primers, miR-33b-3p, and ATAD2 genes, were synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. Relative RNA expression levels were analyzed using the 2−∆∆Ct method, with GAPDH and U6 serving as internal control genes. All the primer sequences are listed in Table S1.

Verification of the circular structure of hsa_circ_0085465

Take gastric cancer cells, extract gDNA using the gDNA extraction kit (Tiangen, China), and store at − 20 °C. Prepare a 1% agarose gel using 0.5× TAE electrophoresis buffer (Solarbio, China). Add Nucleic Acid Stain (Dingguo Changsheng, China) at a 1:20,000 dilution. Once the agarose gel has fully solidified, load samples and run electrophoresis. After electrophoresis, examine under UV light and photograph for archiving.

Construction and transfection of plasmids, siRNAs, and miRNAs

Hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression vector and the corresponding siRNA were custom-designed and constructed by Geneseed Biotech, located in Guangzhou, China. miR-33b-3p mimics and inhibitor were designed and procured from Gema Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Plasmids were extracted using HiPure Plasmid EF Mini Kit (Magen Biotech, China) and stored at − 20 °C until use. Transfection was carried out using the Lipofectamine™ 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA), and transfection efficiency was validated via qRT-PCR 48 h post-transfection. The gene sequences are listed in Table S2.

Cell scratch-healing assay

Log-phase cells were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to grow until they reached nearly 100% confluence. A scratch was created on the monolayer, and subsequent cell culture was conducted using 1640 medium supplemented with 2% fetal bovine serum. Scratch healing was observed after 36 h, and images were captured using a microscopic imaging system (Olympus, Japan). The measurement of the scratch area was performed using ImageJ 1.8 software to calculate the relative healing rate of the cells.

CCK-8 assay

Seed approximately 3000 cells per well into a 96-well plate. Treat the cells at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h using the CCK-8 Cell Proliferation Detection Kit (Guangzhou HuCheng Technology Co., Ltd., China). After a 2 h incubation, measure the absorbance at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion assays were conducted using Transwell chambers (Corning, USA). Transfected cells were harvested and seeded into the upper chamber, which was coated with matrigel (Corning, USA) for invasion assays or left uncoated for migration assays. The lower chamber was filled with 750 µl of medium supplemented with 20% FBS. Following a 48 h incubation, cells from the upper membrane surface were removed using a cotton swab. Cells on the lower membrane surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and subsequently stained with 0.1% crystal violet at room temperature for 15 min. Finally, the migratory or invading cells were photographed and counted under a microscope.

RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Cy3-labeled 18 S and hsa_circ_0085465 probes were designed and synthesized by Gema Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). BGC-823 cells were cultured on round coverslips, fixed, and hybridized overnight at 37 °C. 6-cyano-2-phenylindole (DAPI) was used to label cell nuclei, and the samples were immediately imaged using an LSM 900 confocal microscope (Zeiss, Germany). The probe sequences are listed in Table S3.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

Based on bioinformatics predictions, a 200-bp fragment containing the potential binding site for miR-33b-3p was selected from the hsa_circ_0085465 sequence and synthesized. This fragment was cloned into the multiple cloning site (MCS) downstream of the firefly luciferase gene in the GP-miRGLO vector to construct the wild-type (WT) reporter vector. The mutant (MUT) vector was generated by introducing a site-directed mutation into the complementary region of the miR-33b-3p seed sequence to disrupt its binding ability. 293T cells were cultured in 24-well plates and transfected with miRNA-binding site containing wildtype (WT) or mutant (MUT) vector, as well as predicted miRNA mimics or controls (Shanghai Gema Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd., China). After 48 h transfection, the luciferase activity was detected by the dual luciferase reporter assay system (Shanghai Gema Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd., China). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity. Hsa_circ_0085465 WT and MUT sequences are listed in Table S4.

Nucleus-cytoplasm fractionation

Collect 5 × 10⁶ cells. Following the kit instructions (Bestbio, China), add 400 µl of cold Extraction Buffer A to the cell pellet. After incubation, the supernatant constitutes the cytoplasmic fraction. Resuspend the pellet in PBS, centrifuge, and discard the supernatant; the pellet constitutes the nuclear fraction. Resuspend the pellet in 200 µl of Preservation Buffer B and set aside for later use.

Xenograft nude mouse model

All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Ningxia Medical University and performed in strict compliance with the guidelines of the Laboratory Animal Center of Ningxia Medical University. Ethical approval number is IACUC-2025335. Four-week-old male BALB/c nude mice (18–25 g) were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 5 per group). Each groups received a subcutaneous injection of BGC-823 cells (5 × 10⁶), which were either untreated, stably transfected with an empty vector, or overexpressing hsa_circ_0085465. Tumor size was assessed every three days by measuring the length (L) and width (W) using calipers. Tumor volume (V) was calculated using the formula V = 1/2 × L × W². On day 18 post-injection, nude mice were humanely euthanized via CO₂ inhalation in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines. Mice were individually placed in a dedicated euthanasia chamber, and CO₂ gas was slowly introduced at a flow rate of 10% of the chamber volume per minute for a duration of 5 min. Death was confirmed by the complete cessation of respiration and the absence of a withdrawal reflex to toe pinch. After confirmation, the tumor tissue was immediately exercised and weighed. Subsequently, IHC staining was performed.

Statistical analysis

The GraphPad Prism software 8.0 was utilized for graphical and statistical analyses. All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Each experiment was conducted a minimum of three times. Significant differences among experimental groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Group comparisons were conducted with a t-test. ***P < 0.001, **P < 0.01, *P < 0.05.

Results

Screening of differentially expressed circRNAs in an H. pylori-infected gastric cell model

To investigate the impact of H. pylori infection on the circular RNA (circRNA) expression profile, we utilized normal gastric epithelial cells (GES-1) and gastric cancer cells (BGC-823), both of which are characterized by stable genetic backgrounds and well-defined oncogenic potential. Following infection with H. pylori at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 100:1, transcriptomic sequencing was performed to identify circRNAs potentially implicated in H. pylori-associated gastric carcinogenesis. The results revealed numerous differentially expressed circRNAs in both cell lines, indicating that H. pylori infection significantly remodels the circRNA landscape in gastric cells. This provided a comprehensive pool of candidate molecules for subsequent functional screening (Fig. 1A). Venn diagram analysis was performed to identify circular RNAs that were consistently dysregulated across both cell lines. A total of 26 circular RNAs were consistently upregulated and 31 were consistently downregulated at 24 h following H. pylori infection (Fig. 1B). Subsequent heatmap analysis further revealed that hsa_circ_0085465 was markedly upregulated in H. pylori-infected gastric cancer cells, indicating a robust differential expression pattern (Fig. 1C). Accordingly, hsa_circ_0085465 was selected for subsequent functional investigations. To characterize the molecular origin and structural features of hsa_circ_0085465, bioinformatics analyses were conducted using the CircBase and CircBank databases. The results indicated that hsa_circ_0085465 is derived from exons 20 and 21 of its parental gene ATAD2, with the reference transcript NM_014109. It is located on chromosome 8q24.13 (chr8:124,349,864–124,351,686, strand: −) and has a mature length of 333 bp; this circRNA is also referred to as circ-ATAD2 (Fig. 1D). In addition, sequencing chromatograms further validated the accuracy of its back-splicing junction site (Fig. 1E). Subsequently, to directly confirm the circular structure of hsa_circ_0085465, divergent primers spanning the back-splice junction were designed for PCR amplification, followed by agarose gel electrophoresis analysis. The results showed that hsa_circ_0085465 could be specifically amplified in cDNA but not in genomic DNA (gDNA). In contrast, the linear ATAD2 transcript was successfully amplified in both cDNA and gDNA samples, thereby conclusively confirming the circular RNA nature of hsa_circ_0085465 (Fig. 1F). Furthermore, qRT-PCR was performed to evaluate the expression levels of candidate circRNAs in GES-1 and BGC-823 cells following H. pylori infection. Among all examined circRNAs, hsa_circ_0085465 exhibited the most significant upregulation under H. pylori-infected conditions (Fig. 1G). Accordingly, hsa_circ_0085465 was ultimately selected for subsequent functional and mechanistic studies. Finally, to explore the association between the parental gene ATAD2 and tumorigenesis, expression analyses were conducted using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). The results indicate that ATAD2 expression is higher in various tumor types than in their corresponding normal tissues. Furthermore, its expression in gastric cancer is significantly higher than in the corresponding normal tissues (Fig. 1H, J). ATAD2 expression was higher in various tumors than in their corresponding peri-tumoral tissues, and its expression in gastric cancer was significantly higher than in the corresponding peri-tumoral tissues (Fig. 1I, J). Collectively, these findings suggest that ATAD2 and its circular transcript hsa_circ_0085465 may jointly contribute to the development and progression of gastric cancer.

Hsa_circ_0085465 promotes the proliferation, migration and invasion of GC cells

To evaluate the biological effects of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer cells, an overexpression vector (pLC5-hsa_circ_0085465) was constructed and transfected into BGC-823 cells. qRT-PCR confirmed that hsa_circ_0085465 expression was significantly increased after transfection (Fig. 2A). Then, we used CCK-8 to detect the proliferation ability and used the scratch wound healing and transwell assays to detect the invasion and migration abilities of the GC cells. The results showed that overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 markedly increased both the proliferation (Fig. 2C), migration (Fig. 2E, G) and invasion (Fig. 2I, K) ability of GC cells. To assess the knockdown efficiency of hsa_circ_0085465, three small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) specifically targeting the back-splice junction of hsa_circ_0085465 were designed, qRT-PCR screening indicated that si-circ#2 achieved the highest knockdown efficiency in BGC-823 cells (Fig. 2B) and was selected for subsequent experiments. CCK-8 showed that knockdown of hsa_circ_0085465 markedly reduced cell proliferation (Fig. 2D). Similarly, scratch-healing and transwell assays showed that si-circ#2 treatment markedly decreased cell migration (Fig. 2F, H) and invasion (Fig. 2J, L).

Bioinformatics analysis and screening of miRNAs that bind to hsa_circ_0085465

Subsequently, high-throughput sequencing was performed on cell samples from the overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 and the empty vector group. Bioinformatics analyses were conducted to identify miRNAs associated with hsa_circ_0085465, and the results were visualized using Venn diagrams, volcano plots, and heatmaps. Integrated analysis of the sequencing data identified 1,156 miRNAs at the intersection of the two groups. (Fig. 3A). Differential expression analysis identified four miRNAs that showed statistically significant differences between the two groups. Among them, novel-391, miR-33b-3p, and miR-2110 were significantly downregulated in the hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression group (Fig. 3B–C). Bioinformatic analysis was performed to identify the target genes of all down-regulated miRNAs, followed by GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses. GO enrichment results demonstrated that the predicted target genes were significantly enriched in biological processes related to cell signaling and metabolism, including protein phosphorylation and kinase-related signaling pathways (Fig. 3D–E). Additionally, KEGG pathway analysis revealed that these target genes were primarily involved in cellular process-related pathways, such as proliferation, differentiation, energy metabolism, and signal transduction (Fig. 3F–G).

Has_circ_0085465 acts as a miR‑33b-3p sponge in GC

Previous studies suggest that circRNAs regulate target gene expression by sponging miRNAs36,37.To validate whether hsa_circ_0085465 exhibits sponge-like regulatory effects on miR-33b-3p, potential binding regions between the two were first predicted through sequence alignment. The analysis revealed highly complementary base pairing between the nucleotide sequence of miR-33b-3p and a specific region of hsa_circ_0085465. Based on this prediction, wild-type (WT) hsa_circ_0085465 reporter plasmids containing the binding site and corresponding mutant (MUT) plasmids with a disrupted binding site were constructed (Fig. 4A). Dual-luciferase reporter assays showed that co-transfection of miR-33b-3p mimics with the hsa_circ_0085465 WT plasmid significantly reduced relative luciferase activity. In contrast, co-transfection with the MUT plasmid did not result in a significant change in luciferase activity (Fig. 4B). These results indicate a specific interaction between miR-33b-3p and the predicted binding site of hsa_circ_0085465. Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis showed that hsa_circ_0085465 signals were predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, consistent with the cytoplasmic marker 18 S (Fig. 4C). Nuclear–cytoplasmic fractionation analysis further showed that both hsa_circ_0085465 (Fig. 4D) and miR-33b-3p (Fig. 4E) were predominantly enriched in the cytoplasmic fraction. qRT-PCR analysis showed that overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 markedly reduced miR-33b-3p expression levels, whereas siRNA-mediated knockdown of hsa_circ_0085465 (si-circ#2) markedly increased miR-33b-3p expression (Fig. 4F). Collectively, these results demonstrate an inverse regulatory association between hsa_circ_0085465 and miR-33b-3p, consistent with a potential ceRNA interaction.

MiR-33b-3p inhibits the proliferation, migration and invasion of GC cells

To investigate the effects of miR-33b-3p on gastric cancer cell behavior, its overexpression and knockdown efficiencies were first evaluated by qRT-PCR. The results showed that miR-33b-3p expression was markedly increased in cells transfected with miR-33b-3p mimics compared with the controls (Fig. 5A). In contrast, transfection with miR-33b-3p inhibitor resulted in a significant reduction in miR-33b-3p expression levels (Fig. 5B), indicating effective modulation of miR-33b-3p expression in gastric cancer cells. Gastric cancer cells were subsequently transfected with miR-33b-3p mimics or inhibitor, and a series of phenotypic assays were performed. CCK-8 showed that miR-33b-3p mimics markedly decreased cell viability, whereas miR-33b-3p inhibitor markedly increased cell viability (Fig. 5C–D). Scratch-healing assays revealed a significantly reduced wound-healing rate in the miR-33b-3p mimics group, while miR-33b-3p inhibitor markedly accelerated cell migration and wound closure (Fig. 5E–F). Transwell assays further demonstrated that miR-33b-3p mimics markedly reduced both migration and invasion abilities of gastric cancer cells. Conversely, miR-33b-3p inhibitor resulted in enhanced migratory and invasive capacities (Fig. 5G–H). Collectively, these results indicate an inhibitory association between miR-33b-3p expression and the proliferative, migratory, and invasive properties of gastric cancer cells.

MiR-33b-3p partly reverses the ability of hsa_circ_0085465 to promote proliferation, migration, and invasion in gastric cancer cells

To assess whether hsa_circ_0085465 mediates its effects on gastric cancer cells through interaction with miR-33b-3p, a rescue experiment was performed to evaluate whether miR-33b-3p could attenuate hsa_circ_0085465-induced changes in cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, qRT-PCR analysis showed that overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 significantly reduced endogenous miR-33b-3p expression compared with the pLC5 empty vector. In contrast, co-transfection of hsa_circ_0085465 with miR-33b-3p mimics significantly restored miR-33b-3p expression levels relative to cells overexpressing hsa_circ_0085465 alone (Fig. 6A). CCK-8 revealed that, compared with the pLC5, overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 significantly increased OD450 values. Notably, co-transfection with miR-33b-3p mimics led to a significant reduction in OD450 values compared with the hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression group (Fig. 6B), indicating that miR-33b-3p partially attenuated hsa_circ_0085465-induced cell proliferation. Consistent with these findings, scratch-healing assays demonstrated that hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression significantly enhanced cell migration relative to the pLC5. This effect was markedly reduced following co-transfection with miR-33b-3p mimics (Fig. 6C–D). Transwell assays further showed that hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression significantly increased the number of migrating and invading gastric cancer cells. However, co-transfection with miR-33b-3p mimics significantly reduced both migratory and invasive cell numbers relative to the hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression group alone (Fig. 6E–F). Collectively, these results indicate that miR-33b-3p effectively counteracts hsa_circ_0085465-associated enhancement of proliferative, migratory, and invasive phenotypes in gastric cancer cells.

Hsa_circ_0085465 promotes tumor growth in vivo

Given that hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression significantly enhanced the proliferative, migratory, and invasive abilities of gastric cancer cells in vitro, a subcutaneous xenograft model in nude mice was further established to evaluate its effects in vivo (Fig. 7A). The results showed that tumor cells stably overexpressing hsa_circ_0085465 exhibited significantly larger volumes than those control and pLC5 group (Fig. 7B). Consistently, tumor growth curves and endpoint tumor weight analyses indicated that hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression significantly promoted tumor growth in vivo (Fig. 7C–D). To further assess proliferation-related changes in tumor tissues, immunohistochemical staining was performed to examine the expression of the proliferation marker Ki67. The results revealed a marked increase in Ki67-positive staining in tumors from the hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression group compared with the control groups (Fig. 7E).

Discussion

Gastric cancer is a malignant tumor originating from the epithelial cells of the gastric mucosa, posing a substantial threat to human health and representing a major challenge in global public health prevention and control38. Among the known risk factors, H. pylori infection is widely recognized as one of the most critical triggering events in the development of gastric cancer39, Extensive evidence indicates that H. pylori promotes the malignant transformation of the gastric mucosa from chronic inflammation through multiple mechanisms, including the induction of persistent inflammatory responses40, the activation of oxidative stress pathways41, and the disruption of genomic stability42. Therefore, elucidating H. pylori-associated molecular alterations is crucial for a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis of gastric cancer. Given the clear clinical and epidemiological association between H. pylori infection and gastric cancer progression, this study first utilized H. pylori-infected cells as a screening model to identify differentially expressed RNA molecules that may contribute to the formation of the malignant phenotype in gastric cancer. CircRNAs are a class of endogenous non-coding RNAs characterized by a covalently closed-loop structure. Owing to their high stability, tissue-specific expression patterns, and diverse regulatory functions, circRNAs have attracted considerable attention in cancer biology in recent years43. Previous studies have shown that circRNAs contribute to tumor progression through multiple mechanisms, including the regulation of post-transcriptional gene expression, modulation of miRNA activity as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), and involvement in protein interaction networks. Despite these advances, the biological role of hsa_circ_0085465 remains unclear. Current evidence only preliminarily indicates its potential involvement in hepatocellular carcinoma progression44, whereas its expression profile, functional significance, and underlying regulatory mechanisms in gastric cancer have not yet been systematically characterized.

In this study, we performed preliminary screening using a H. pylori-infected gastric cancer cell model. Transcriptomic analysis suggested that hsa_circ_0085465 is an H. pylori-responsive circular RNA that shows a sustained upregulation trend following infection. Further analysis indicated that hsa_circ_0085465 is derived from the well-characterized oncogene ATAD2. To further explore its potential role in gastric cancer, subsequent functional and mechanistic investigations were conducted under H. pylori-independent conditions to assess its effects on tumor-associated phenotypes.

A series of in vitro and in vivo experiments suggest that hsa_circ_0085465 may exert a promoting effect in gastric cancer progression. In vitro experiments demonstrated that overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 enhanced the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells, whereas its knockdown produced the opposite effects. In a xenograft mouse model, overexpression of hsa_circ_0085465 was associated with accelerated tumor growth. However, these findings are still insufficient to clarify the role of hsa_circ_0085465 in the occurrence and progression of gastric cancer. From a mechanistic perspective, circRNAs often regulate downstream gene expression by functioning as miRNA sponges45,46. In this study, a combination of bioinformatic predictions and experimental approaches—including dual-luciferase reporter assays, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and nuclear–cytoplasmic fractionation indicated that hsa_circ_0085465 is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm and is capable of interacting with miR-33b-3p. Functional assays further suggested that miR-33b-3p may inhibit the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells, consistent with findings reported in prostate and ovarian cancers. Rescue experiments additionally demonstrated that miR-33b-3p mimics partially reversed the pro-tumor effects induced by hsa_circ_0085465 overexpression. Based on these results, we conclude that hsa_circ_0085465 may influence the malignant phenotype of gastric cancer cells by regulating miR-33b-3p. However, given that circRNAs often exhibit multi-target regulatory properties, it remains possible that hsa_circ_0085465 also exerts its effects via additional miRNAs or alternative molecular pathways, which warrants further systematic investigation. The above results indicate that H. pylori infection can induce the upregulation of hsa_circ_0085465, and hsa_circ_0085465 acts by sponging miR-33b-3p in the cytoplasm, ultimately promoting the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells, and accelerating tumor growth in vivo(Fig. 8).

Although this study provides preliminary evidence, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, we did not directly assess whether H. pylori infection contributes to gastric cancer development and progression in vivo through regulation of the hsa_circ_0085465/miR-33b-3p axis. Second, the downstream target genes of miR-33b-3p have not yet been systematically identified and experimentally validated; therefore, it remains unclear whether these targets are involved in protein phosphorylation, kinase-related signaling pathways, or other key oncogenic networks. Furthermore, our findings are primarily based on cell line models and xenograft nude mouse model, and lack validation using large-scale clinical samples. Future studies incorporating well-characterized clinical cohorts will be important for further evaluating the potential diagnostic and prognostic value of hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer. In the future, we will continue to improve the research on the above limitations.

Conclusions

In this study, we used H. pylori-infected gastric cells as a screening model, identified and preliminarily validated the aberrant upregulation of the hsa_circ_0085465 in gastric cancer. Functional assays suggested that hsa_circ_0085465 influence the proliferation, migration, and invasive capacity of gastric cancer cells through interaction with miR-33b-3p. Taken together, these findings suggest that hsa_circ_0085465 may be involved in molecular regulatory processes related to gastric cancer progression and provide new insights into the potential mechanisms underlying H. pylori-associated gastric cancer. Moreover, they offer preliminary evidence supporting further investigation into its potential applications in the diagnosis and treatment of gastric cancer.

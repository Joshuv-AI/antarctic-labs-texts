Machine learning evaluation of TyG-based metrics for arteriosclerosis progression
Authors: Yihui Li, Kui Chen, Lei Yan, Bao Tian, Juan Li, Pingting Yang, Jiangang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Insulin resistance (IR) is a significant risk factor for arteriosclerosis. The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index and its obesity-related derivatives (TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR) have emerged as reliable markers of IR. While diabetes, a consequence of IR, is a primary risk factor for arteriosclerosis, it is important to note that arteriosclerosis may develop prior to the onset of diabetes. Therefore, this study utilized machine learning to assess the relationship between cumulative exposure to these IR indicators and the development of arteriosclerosis in non-diabetic populations. This cohort study enrolled 4,160 participants from the health management departments of two tertiary general hospitals in southern China between 2017 and 2024, with an average follow-up of 3.49 visits per participant. Multivariable linear regression models and restricted cubic splines were employed to investigate the association between cumulative average exposure to TyG and obesity-modified TyG indicators with arteriosclerosis progression and explore potential nonlinear relationships. Machine learning models were utilized to assess the predictive value of cumulative average exposure to the TyG index and its derived metrics for arteriosclerosis.its performance was evaluated in both the development and validation cohorts using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and the area under the precision-recall curve (PR-AUC). Cumulative average exposure to the TyG index and TyG-WHtR demonstrated a strong association with the progression of arteriosclerosis, whether analyzed as continuous variables or as quartile variables (p < 0.05). A potential U-shaped relationship was observed between cumulative average exposure to TyG-WHtR and both changes in baPWV and its annual rate of change. Validation in an independent cohort demonstrated that incorporating the TyG index or its derived metrics into predictive models moderately enhanced their performance in predicting arteriosclerosis progression, with the AUC increasing from 0.726 for the baseline model to a range of 0.737–0.744. The cumulative average exposure to the TyG index and its derivative indicators is a significant predictor of arteriosclerosis progression in non-diabetic populations. Integrating these low-cost and readily available metrics into predictive models enables the early identification of high-risk individuals within this cohort.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-53578-1.

Introduction

As individuals age, vascular function undergoes multiple adverse changes, with arteriosclerosis representing a key manifestation of vascular aging1. Arteriosclerosis increases pulse wave reflection by elevating peripheral arterial resistance and central arterial wall rigidity, thereby exerting direct adverse effects on the cardiovascular system2. Assessment of arteriosclerosis using brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV) has been established as an important independent predictor of major cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction, heart failure, stroke, and all-cause mortality3. Because arteriosclerosis is a progressive, long-term pathological process, early identification of individuals at risk and timely intervention through appropriate preventive strategies are essential to improving population health and reducing the global burden of cardiovascular disease4.

Accumulating evidence supports the pivotal role of insulin resistance (IR) in the development of arteriosclerosis and subsequent cardiovascular diseases5. The triglyceride glucose (TyG) index has emerged as a reliable surrogate biomarker for IR. Previous studies have demonstrated that elevated TyG index levels are significantly associated with the onset of arteriosclerosis5–8. Given that arteriosclerosis arises from the chronic accumulation of metabolic stress on the vascular endothelium3, a single baseline measurement—merely a ‘snapshot’—may overlook the total duration and variability of exposure. In contrast, cumulative exposure captures this integrated long-term metabolic burden, theoretically offering a more robust predictor of vascular pathology. Body fat content and distribution, particularly visceral adiposity, are strongly associated with IR9. To better capture this relationship, a modified TyG index that integrates the TyG index with obesity measures—such as body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC), and waist-to-height ratio (WHtR)—has been proposed as a more accurate indicator of IR10,11. Because both the TyG index and obesity-related indices may fluctuate over time, cumulative exposure derived from repeated measurements is considered more informative for predicting outcomes than a single baseline measurement12,13. Furthermore, given the strong association between IR and diabetes14, along with emerging evidence suggesting that arteriosclerosis may precede the onset of diabetes3, it is notable that few studies have examined the relationship between IR and arteriosclerosis in the general non-diabetic population. Moreover, the potential of machine learning techniques to assess the relationship between the TyG index and its derivative metrics with arteriosclerosis progression remains largely unexplored. Therefore, this study utilizes machine learning methods to evaluate the predictive value of cumulative average exposure to the TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives for arteriosclerosis progression in non-diabetic populations.

Although previous studies have established associations between the TyG index5–8, its derivatives, and arteriosclerosis, several limitations remain. First, most studies are cross-sectional or rely on single baseline measurements, failing to capture the effect of long-term cumulative exposure to these metabolic markers. Second, the study populations have largely been restricted to individuals with diabetes or those at high cardiovascular risk, with limited evidence from general non-diabetic communities. Finally, traditional statistical approaches may not fully account for complex nonlinear relationships and higher-order interactions among multiple predictors. To address these gaps, this study, for the first time, employs time-averaged cumulative exposure assessment of the TyG index and its obesity-modified versions within a large prospective non-diabetic cohort, and integrates multiple machine learning algorithms to systematically evaluate their longitudinal predictive value for arteriosclerosis progression. Our work aims to provide a more dynamic and comprehensive novel perspective for early risk stratification of arteriosclerosis in the non-diabetic population.

Materials and methods

Study design and study population

Participants were recruited from the health management departments of two tertiary general hospitals in southern China. Among them, Xiangya Third Hospital of Central South University, the largest medical institution in the central region, maintains a database containing approximately 7.3 million outpatient visit records. This database includes detailed demographic data, examination results, and diagnostic information, all systematically coded according to the ICD-10-CM classification system15,16. In this study, 22,131 individuals underwent at least two baPWV measurements between 2017 and 2022. Of these, 7,058 participants had three or more recorded measurements of the TyG and modified TyG indices from baseline to the final baPWV assessment, with intervals of at least 12 months between consecutive visits. After excluding 2,898 individuals with pre-existing arteriosclerosis or diabetes at baseline, a total of 4,160 participants were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). To further validate the generalizability of our machine learning models, we recruited an external validation cohort from the Jingxi Outpatient Department of the Third Xiangya Hospital. This cohort consisted of 475 participants who underwent health examinations between 2017 and 2024 (consistent with the primary study period) and met the same inclusion and exclusion criteria as the primary cohort. Data collection and processing for this external cohort followed the identical protocol as described above. The physical examination protocol and consent form for this study were reviewed by the institutional review boards of both hospitals and approved by the independent ethics committee of the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (Approval No. 2013BAI04B01). In accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Data collection and definition

Demographic characteristics, lifestyle factors, self-reported medical history, medication use, and family history were obtained through a standardized online questionnaire (https://new.selfhealth.com.cn/#/login). Participants were classified as current smokers if they reported smoking more than one cigarette per day on average for at least six consecutive months, and as current drinkers if they reported consuming beer, wine (including Chinese wine), or spirits on at least two days per week for more than 12 months.

According to the JNC-7 criteria, hypertension was defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg, the use of antihypertensive medications, or a self-reported physician diagnosis of hypertension17. Diabetes mellitus was defined according to the American Diabetes Association criteria as a fasting glucose level ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, a self-reported physician diagnosis of diabetes, or the use of anti-diabetic medication18. Detailed descriptions of the procedures for measuring blood pressure, weight, height, and waist circumference have been published previously19. BMI was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height squared (m²)20.

Measurement of time-averaged cumulative exposure for TyG and modified TyG Indices

The TyG index was calculated as ln [fasting triglycerides (mg/dL) × fasting glucose (mg/dL) / 2]15. The modified indices were defined as follows: TyG-BMI = TyG ×BMI, TyG-WC = TyG ×WC, and TyG-WHtR = TyG ×WHtR21. Assessment of arteriosclerosis progression

The baPWV was measured using an automated device (BP-203 RPE III, Omron Health Medical, Dalian, China). After resting in the supine position for at least 5 min, cuffs were applied to both upper arms and ankles. These cuffs were connected to a volume plethysmography sensor, which records pulse waveforms, and an oscillometric pressure sensor, which measures blood pressure. The baPWV was calculated using the following formula:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:baPWV=\frac{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{L}\mathrm{b}\mathbf{}}{{\Delta\:}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{a}}$$\end{document}

where La represents the distance from the heart to the ankle, Lb the distance from the heart to the brachial artery, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{a}$$\end{document} the transit time between the brachial and posterior tibial arterial waveforms. Two consecutive measurements were obtained for each limb, and the mean of the left and right sides was used for analysis. All measurements were performed by two professionally trained technicians to ensure accuracy and consistency.

The final baPWV value was calculated as the average of the left and right measurements. A baPWV value ≥ 1400 cm/s was considered indicative of arteriosclerosis22. The absolute change in baPWV was determined by subtracting the baseline value from the final follow-up measurement. The rate of change in baPWV was calculated by dividing the absolute change by the follow-up duration in years. Additionally, the baPWV slope was defined as the regression coefficient obtained from a linear regression analysis, with follow-up years as the independent variable and multiple baPWV measurements as the dependent variable.We utilized three distinct metrics of baPWV progression—the absolute change, annual rate of change, and regression slope—to comprehensively evaluate the extent of arteriosclerosis from the dimensions of total accumulated damage, instantaneous progression velocity, and long-term evolutionary trend, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were stratified according to quartiles of the TyG index. These characteristics are presented as mean (standard deviation [SD]), median (interquartile range [IQR]), or number (percentage), as appropriate. Intergroup comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal-Wallis rank-sum test, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. For missing data, numerical variables were imputed using Predictive Mean Matching (PMM), while categorical variables were imputed using logistic regression.

Spearman correlation analyses were conducted for the TyG and modified TyG indices (TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR). Multivariable linear regression models were used to assess the associations between time-averaged cumulative exposure to TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR—analyzed both as continuous variables and by quartiles—and the absolute change, rate of change, and slope of baPWV. Trend analyses were performed using the median values assigned to each quartile. The model was adjusted for potential confounders, including age, sex, BMI, WC, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), fasting blood glucose (FBG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), serum creatinine (Scr), uric acid (UA), lipid-lowering medication use, family history of coronary heart disease, history of hypertension, current smoking status, current alcohol consumption, education level, and marital status. A four-node restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was conducted to investigate potential nonlinear associations of the time-averaged cumulative exposure of the TyG index and modified TyG indices with the absolute change, rate of change, and slope of baPWV, with the median of each variable serving as the reference point. To assess the robustness of the nonlinear associations to the analytical specifications, sensitivity analyses were further conducted. These included examining the impact of varying the number of knots (3, 4, and 5 knots), using equally spaced knots, and trimming the top and bottom 1% of values for each variable. Additionally, sensitivity analyses were performed accordin to age, sex, BMI, alcohol consumption, and smoking status, and interaction effects between these variables and the time-averaged cumulative TyG index were evaluated.

We employed machine learning techniques to account for variable covariance and its impact on outcomes. To streamline the model, feature selection was performed to identify the most significant predictor variables for outcome prediction. First, predictor variables exhibiting near-zero variance (< 5% unique variance), including lipid-lowering medication use, family history of coronary heart disease, and history of hypertension, were excluded.We employed the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) to penalize regression coefficients and mitigate multicollinearity by removing variables with significant collinearity. Concurrently, the Boruta algorithm was rigorously applied to evaluate and select variables with the highest feature importance. Only variables consistently selected by both methods were incorporated into the baseline model. These analyses were performed using the Glmnet, Boruta, and Caret packages in R.

Following variable selection, machine learning algorithms, including Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors (K-NN), Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Random Forests, eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Neural Networks, were employed for model construction. All models were trained and evaluated on the merged dataset. To optimize hyperparameters and assess model performance, a five-fold cross-validation strategy combined with five random shufflings (5 × 5) was employed. Model performance was measured by the mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) across the five folds, with the model yielding the highest AUROC selected as the optimal model. The aforementioned modeling process was implemented using the caret package in the R. Furthermore, using the best-performing machine learning algorithm, a baseline model and optimized models incorporating four distinct TyG-related indicators (including the TyG index, TyG–BMI, TyG–WC, and TyG–WHtR) were constructed. Model performance was compared using AUC, and statistical testing of differences in C statistics between models was conducted via bootstrap sampling.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

The final analysis included 4,160 participants (1,222 [29.4%] women), with a mean age of 43.59 years and a mean of 3.49 follow-up visits. The distribution of missing data is shown in Supplementary Figure S1. The variables “Current smoker,” “Current drinking,” and “Married” had a missing proportion of less than 20%, while the missing proportion for all remaining variables was less than 1%. Participants in the higher quartiles of the time-averaged cumulative TyG index were predominantly male and exhibited higher BMI and WC, elevated systolic and diastolic blood pressure, greater baseline and follow-up baPWV values, higher prevalence of hypertension and dyslipidemia, and higher proportions of current smoking and alcohol consumption. Among individuals undergoing health examinations, relatively few were receiving statin therapy. Relationship of time-averaged cumulative exposure to the TyG and modified TyG indices with the progression of arteriosclerosis

We observed that the time-averaged cumulative exposure of the TyG index and its modified indices (TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR) was positively correlated with baPWV change, baPWV change rate, and baPWV slope (Figure S2). In the fully adjusted model (Table 2), time-averaged cumulative TyG, TyG-BMI, and TyG-WHtR, treated as continuous variables, were significantly associated with progression of arteriosclerosis. Specifically, each unit increase in cumulative TyG was associated with a 35.746 cm/s increase in baPWV change, an 8.256 cm/s/year increase in baPWV change rate, and an 8.598 increase in baPWV slope. Similarly, each 1-unit increase in cumulative TyG-BMI corresponded to a 0.429 cm/s increase in baPWV change, a 0.106 cm/s/year increase in the rate of change, and a 0.117 increase in slope. For cumulative TyG-WHtR, each 1-unit increase was associated with a 17.943 cm/s increase in baPWV change, a 4.347 cm/s/year increase in rate of change, and a 4.303 increase in slope.

Sensitivity analysis

In stratified analyses by sex, BMI, and alcohol consumption, the positive associations between the time-averaged cumulative TyG index and baPWV change, baPWV change rate, and baPWV slope remained largely consistent, with no statistically significant interactions (Table S2). In age-stratified analyses, the associations were stable overall, but the effect sizes differed between participants aged < 60 years and those ≥ 60 years. Interaction testing revealed a significant interaction between age and the time-averaged cumulative TyG index on baPWV change, baPWV change rate, and baPWV slope (all P < 0.05). In stratified analyses by smoking status, the main findings were largely confirmed. However, in the non-smoking group, the time-averaged cumulative TyG index was not significantly associated with baPWV change rate or baPWV slope, with evidence of interaction (P < 0.05).

Machine learning analysis

Feature selection using the Boruta algorithm revealed that the annual average cumulative exposure to the TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives were significant predictors of arteriosclerosis development (Fig. 4). Based on both the Boruta algorithm and LASSO regression, thirteen variables were ultimately selected for predicting arteriosclerosis. The final model incorporated age, gender, BMI, WC, SBP, DBP, FBG, LDL-C, HDL-C, TC, Scr, UA, and educational attainment. Detailed results of the feature selection process are presented in Table S5 and Figure S4. Building upon the baseline model, four enhanced models were constructed by integrating the TyG index and its derived indicators to evaluate their predictive value for arteriosclerosis. Table 3 compares the performance of seven baseline models in predicting arteriosclerosis. Among the baseline models, the AUROC values were as follows: Logistic Regression (0.684), K-NN (0.637), Naive Bayes (0.702), SVM (0.666), Random Forest (0.703), XGBoost (0.701), and Neural Networks (0.671). The incremental predictive value of the TyG index, TyG–BMI, TyG–WC, and TyG–WHtR for arteriosclerosis is summarized in Table 4. Incorporating these indicators into the baseline models improved the AUC. Notably, Table 4 summarizes the incremental predictive value of the cumulative TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives. ROC curve analysis of the random forest model (Fig. 5) demonstrated that incorporating the cumulative TyG index, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, or TyG-WHtR into the baseline model (AUC = 0.726) consistently improved the discriminative ability in the validation cohort, with AUC values ranging from 0.737 to 0.744. The calibration curves (Figure S5) indicated good agreement between the predicted probabilities and the observed event rates for all enhanced models in both the development and validation cohorts. Furthermore, the PR-AUC analysis (Figure S6) confirmed that models incorporating the TyG-related indices exhibited improved overall performance in identifying true arteriosclerosis progressors within the imbalanced dataset.

Discussion

In this prospective cohort study, we systematically evaluated the associations of the TyG index and its modified forms with arteriosclerosis progression in a non-diabetic population. We found that the time-averaged cumulative TyG index was strongly correlated with the corresponding modified TyG indices. Importantly, higher cumulative exposure to the TyG index and TyG-WHtR was consistently associated with an increased risk of arteriosclerosis progression, both when analyzed as continuous variables and across quartile-based categories. Moreover, restricted cubic spline analysis suggested a potential U-shaped association between cumulative TyG-WHtR and arteriosclerosis progression. RCS analysis suggested a potential U-shaped association between cumulative TyG-WHtR and arteriosclerosis progression. The random forest-based model further demonstrated that incorporating the cumulative TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives into the baseline model provided significant incremental predictive value, thereby enhancing the model’s overall ability to identify individuals with arteriosclerosis progression.

Arteriosclerosis is a hallmark of diabetes-related vascular complications23. Insulin resistance, a key mechanism in the development of diabetes14,24, has been shown to promote arteriosclerosis5. In insulin-resistant states, elevated endothelin production, impaired nitric oxide synthesis and bioavailability, as well as increased collagen deposition and pro-inflammatory gene expression, contribute to vascular constriction and remodeling5,25. Conversely, arteriosclerosis assessed by baPWV may itself precede elevations in fasting blood glucose3,26. Evidence from a longitudinal cohort further supports this bidirectional relationship, showing that higher baseline baPWV is significantly associated with an increased risk of new-onset diabetes27. These findings highlight the importance of investigating the link between insulin resistance and arteriosclerosis in non-diabetic populations at an early stage.

Based on this prospective cohort study, we found that high cumulative exposure to the TyG index, a well-recognized and reliable biomarker of insulin resistance, is an independent risk factor for arteriosclerosis progression in non-diabetic populations. This finding is consistent with previous studies reporting associations between the TyG index and arteriosclerosis7,28,29. However, a key limitation of these earlier studies is that the TyG index was measured only once, without accounting for longitudinal changes. In contrast, our study assessed cumulative exposure to the TyG index over a median follow-up of 4 years, providing a more robust evaluation of its long-term impact on arteriosclerosis progression.

Given the close interplay between insulin resistance and obesity30,31, an increasing number of studies have examined whether integrating the TyG index with obesity-related parameters can improve the prediction of cardiovascular outcomes. However, findings remain inconsistent across populations and study designs. For instance, the Kailuan study reported that TyG-WC and TyG-WHtR outperformed the traditional TyG index in predicting the risk of new-onset atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease32. Similarly, a cross-sectional analysis using NHANES data suggested that TyG-WC and TyG-WHtR enhanced the predictive accuracy of Cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality and the diagnostic performance for both CVD and CVD-related mortality compared with TyG alone33.In the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), both TyG and TyG–WHtR were significantly associated with incident CVD21. Extending these observations, the present study provides novel longitudinal evidence: cumulative TyG index—and its time-averaged composites with BMI, waist circumference (WC), and waist-to-height ratio (WHtR)—each conferred incremental predictive value for subclinical arteriosclerosis progression, independent of established cardiovascular risk factors, specifically among non-diabetic individuals. These results reinforce the biological and clinical rationale for jointly modeling insulin resistance and adiposity to refine cardiovascular risk stratification.

We leveraged a longitudinal cohort of health examination participants from central China to evaluate the association between time-averaged cumulative exposure to the TyG index and its modified forms with arteriosclerosis progression. Our findings showed that both cumulative TyG and TyG-WHtR were consistently associated with arteriosclerosis progression in non-diabetic individuals, whether assessed as continuous variables or in quartile-based categories. Furthermore, the primary analysis suggested a potential U-shaped relationship between the obesity-modified TyG indices and arterial stiffness progression. However, subsequent sensitivity analyses indicated that this pattern was not fully consistent across different model specifications. Nonetheless, the observed U-shaped trend may still be informative, potentially reflecting the metabolic heterogeneity of obesity, wherein the metabolically healthy obese phenotype may be associated with a distinct cardiovascular risk profile34.This potential U-shaped relationship may stem from the dual components constituting TyG-WHtR. The right limb of the curve (high TyG-WHtR) reflects the classic pathological mechanism where elevated insulin resistance (TyG) coexists with significant abdominal obesity (WHtR), synergistically promoting inflammation and metabolic dysregulation to accelerate arteriosclerosis34. The left limb (low TyG-WHtR) may indicate a distinct high-risk condition: a low TyG (reflecting low triglycerides and/or glucose) could signal insufficient nutritional reserves or a hypometabolic state, combined with low WHtR indicating low central adiposity. This profile in an older population may be associated with sarcopenia or undernutrition. A recent longitudinal study in older women supports this, showing that a decline in triglyceride levels (a key component of TyG) was significantly associated with an increased risk of sarcopenia. Consequently, an extremely low TyG-WHtR might serve as a proxy indicator for a “lean sarcopenic” or frail state, which is itself linked to compromised vascular health35.

Unlike traditional statistical methods, which typically assume linear relationships between variables and often focus on hypothesis testing, machine learning models demonstrate considerable advantages in capturing complex nonlinear relationships and higher-order interactions among metabolic indicators, sociodemographic factors, and disease outcomes.This study indicated that the decision tree–based random forest algorithm effectively enhanced the predictive performance for arteriosclerosis risk. In particular, integrating TyG-related indicators into the predictive model markedly improved its capability to forecast the risk of arteriosclerosis progression.While the improvement in AUC observed after incorporating the cumulative TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives was modest, it remains valuable given the study’s focus on predicting arteriosclerosis progression—a weak-signal endpoint—in a non-diabetic population. This index provides incremental information beyond traditional risk factors at minimal cost. In population screening, even a small gain in model performance can translate to the earlier identification of a substantial number of high-risk individuals, yielding public health benefits. Furthermore, more precise risk stratification can facilitate differentiated follow-up and management strategies in clinical practice.

The main strength of this study is its prospective cohort design, which enabled us to investigate the relationship between cumulative TyG exposure and arteriosclerosis progression by capturing long-term changes in the TyG index and its modified indices. The inclusion of subgroup and sensitivity analyses, along with adjustment for multiple potential confounders, further enhances the robustness and reliability of our findings. Furthermore, we leveraged machine learning models to fully harness the predictive potential of the TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives indicators for arteriosclerosis progression. Nevertheless, several limitations should be noted. First, the study population was derived exclusively from health screening centers in southern China. These individuals generally have a lower prevalence of severe comorbidities compared to clinical populations. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings to Western populations with different genetic backgrounds and lifestyle patterns, or to patients with established diabetes, should be made with caution.Additionally, the attenuated between cumulative TyG and baPWV progression in non-smokers, which may reflect differences in predominant drivers of arteriosclerosis or unmeasured confounding in this subgroup. Future multi-center studies involving diverse ethnic groups and diabetic populations are warranted to validate the cross-population applicability of the TyG-related indices.

This study provides preliminary evidence supporting the utility of cumulative TyG index and its obesity-related derivatives as a predictive marker for arteriosclerosis risk in non-diabetic individuals. Building on these findings, several key research directions warrant further investigation to advance the clinical translation of this biomarker. First, the concept of “time-averaged cumulative exposure” captures sustained metabolic burden over time. With the increasing adoption of dynamic monitoring technologies—such as continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)36,37—future studies could integrate high-resolution, longitudinal CGM-derived time-series data to develop dynamic risk prediction models. Secondly, from a methodological perspective, innovative frameworks such as the sequential machine learning approach38 and the dual ranking algorithm based on the multiplex network for heterogeneous complex disease analysis39 can be employed not only to identify individuals at elevated risk but also to estimate critical inflection points in arteriosclerosis progression. Third, integration with mobile health platforms and wearable sensors offers a scalable pathway to deploy user-friendly, point-of-care dynamic risk assessment tools. Finally, prospective, interventional clinical trials are essential to validate the clinical impact of cumulative TyG index and its obesity-related derivativesguided strategies, This step represents the pivotal transition from risk prediction to actionable clinical practice.

Conclusion

The time-averaged cumulative exposure to the TyG index and TyG-WHtR, whether assessed as continuous measures or quartile-based categories, was significantly associated with arteriosclerosis progression in non-diabetic populations. Incorporating TyG-related indices into machine learning models facilitates the early identification of arteriosclerosis risk in non-diabetic individuals undergoing health examinations. This approach enables the implementation of timely and precise early prevention strategies, improving the accuracy of risk stratification.

Wheat powdery mildew resistance gene Pm52/Pm6 from Triticum timopheevii encodes an NLR protein with an integrated PRK domain
Authors: Dan Qiu, Lingli Dong, Chengzhi Jiao, Jinghuang Hu, Yi Liu, Keyu Zhu, Qiuhong Wu, Miaomiao Li, Huaizhi Zhang, Ping Lu, Zihui Zhao, Yiwen Li, Yongxing Chen, Guanghao Guo, Yahui Li, Jingzhong Xie, Gaojie Wang, Wenling Li, Lei Dong, Beibei Li, Yikun Hou, Xuejia Cui, Baoge Huang, Hongkui Fu, Chenchen Hu, Xueyong Zhang, Zhiyong Liu, Hongjie Li
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We identified and cloned the powdery mildew resistance gene Pm52 from wheat cultivars Liangxing 99 and Jimai 22 using map-based cloning. Pm52, encoding an NLR–PRK protein, was introgressed from T. timopheevii and confers broad-spectrum resistance. In addition, we developed and validated a functional marker, Pm52-FM, providing a reliable tool for marker-assisted selection to accelerate the breeding of wheat cultivars with powdery mildew resistance.

Wheat yield stability depends on cultivars that combine high productivity with robust resistance to diseases such as powdery mildew caused by the obligate biotrophic fungus Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici (Bgt). Achieving effective and sustainable management of this disease requires the development of resistant cultivars. While numerous powdery mildew resistance (Pm) genes have been identified and some cloned (Hafeez et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2022), their deployment in commercial cultivars remains constrained by linkage drag and yield penalties. To circumvent these challenges, breeders prioritize Pm genes already present in high-yielding genetic backgrounds. This approach facilitates the development of cultivars that retain agronomic performance while enhancing pathogen resistance—a critical strategy for sustainable wheat production.

Liangxing 99 (LX99) and Jimai 22 (JM22), released in 2004 and 2006, respectively, are leading wheat cultivars widely grown across China’s winter wheat regions. According to the National Agro-Tech Extension and Service Center (https://www.natesc.org.cn), these two cultivars have been cumulatively cultivated on more than 20 million hectares. Both LX99 and JM22 have high yield potential, broad adaptability to diverse agroecological zones, and resistance to major pathogens, including Bgt. Their outstanding agronomic performance has established them as elite parental lines in breeding programs targeting yield resilience and disease resistance.

Our prior research identified Pm52 (syn. MlLX99), a Pm gene located on chromosome arm 2BL in LX99 and JM22 (Zhao et al., 2013; Qu et al., 2020). Pm52 confers broad-spectrum resistance throughout all developmental stages. Here, we report the isolation of Pm52 using map-based cloning. Ethyl methanesulfonate (EMS)-induced mutations and complementation assays revealed that Pm52 encodes a chimeric protein comprising a canonical nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat (NLR) domain fused to a pathogenesis-related kinase (PRK) domain. This fusion represents a previously uncharacterized NLR–PRK structural class that expands the known architectural diversity of NLR-mediated immunity in wheat.

To genetically separate Pm52 from the co-occurring Pm2 locus present in LX99 and JM22, we first validated the presence of Pm2 using the functional marker TaPm2-F1/TaPm2-R1 (Chen et al., 2009). To specifically assess Pm52-mediated resistance, we screened 12 Bgt isolates from China and identified isolate B37, which is avirulent on Pm52+ Pm2 genotypes (e.g., LX99) but virulent on Pm2-only lines (e.g., Nongda 399; Supplemental Table 1). This isolate-specific avirulence allowed unambiguous evaluation of Pm52 activity. Upon inoculation with B37, LX99 displayed a hypersensitive response characterized by localized cell death and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) accumulation, while Zhongzuo 9504 (ZZ9504) exhibited extensive fungal colonization (Supplemental Figure 1).

Genetic analysis of recombinant inbred lines (RILs) derived from the LX99 × ZZ9504 cross revealed a 1:1 segregation ratio for resistance versus susceptibility to Bgt isolate B37 (Supplemental Table 2), consistent with monogenic inheritance of Pm52-mediated resistance. Initial mapping assigned Pm52 to chromosome arm 2BL. Using polymorphic markers (Supplemental Table 3), we localized the locus to a 0.18-cM genetic interval flanked by markers Wggbq10 and Wggbq6 in the RIL population (Figure 1A). To further refine this interval, we genotyped and evaluated 4500 F2 plants for disease response, identifying 72 recombinants. Fine mapping confined Pm52 to a 0.008-cM region delimited by SSR markers Wggbq44 and WggbH612-5 (Figure 1A). This interval corresponds to a 24.3-Mb physical region (694.50–718.80 Mb) on chromosome 2B in the Chinese Spring reference genome (RefSeq v2.1, IWGSC 2018). We developed 60 primer pairs targeting this region, yielding eight polymorphic markers that distinguished LX99 from ZZ9504. However, all these markers co-segregated with Pm52, indicating suppressed recombination within this genomic segment and preventing precise localization.

To resolve the structural and functional basis of the Pm52 locus, we generated a high-quality chromosome-scale genome assembly for JM22 using PacBio HiFi and Hi-C sequencing technologies (Jiao et al., 2025). Marker-based mapping with Wggbq44 and WggbH612-5 delineated a 14.83-Mb interval in JM22 harboring the Pm52 locus. Comparative analysis with the Chinese Spring reference genome revealed extensive structural variations, including multiple inversions and deletions, within this region (Figure 1B). We annotated 279 predicted protein-coding genes, including eight NLR genes and 24 kinase-encoding genes, both commonly associated with disease resistance (Supplemental Table 4). Targeted sequencing of these candidate genes in 11 EMS-induced powdery-mildew-susceptible mutants identified recurrent mutations in TraesJM222B679606, an NLR gene with coiled-coil, nucleotide-binding site, and leucine-rich repeat domains. Among the 13 mutations identified, two introduced premature stop codons and 11 caused missense alterations in critical functional domains (Figure 1C; Supplemental Table 5). These results identify TraesJM222B679606 as the causal gene underlying Pm52-mediated resistance.

We amplified and sequenced the TraesJM222B679606 allele from LX99, obtaining an 11731-bp genomic fragment comprising a 4731-bp coding sequence, a 3000-bp promoter, and a 4000-bp terminator, all identical to the corresponding regions in JM22. Full-length cDNA cloning via 5′ and 3′ rapid amplification of cDNA ends confirmed that the TraesJM222B679606 coding region is contained within a single exon. Alternative splicing of the 3′ UTR generates six transcript variants (TV1–TV6), with TV1 as the predominant form (Supplemental Figure 2). RT–qPCR of leaf tissue collected at 24 and 48 h post-inoculation revealed that all six TVs are upregulated in response to Bgt infection (Supplemental Figure 3). TraesJM222B679606 encodes a 1577-amino-acid NLR protein harboring a conserved coiled-coil, a nucleotide-binding site, and leucine-rich repeat domains. The C-terminal region includes a protein kinase (PRK) domain (Figure 1C), creating an NLR–PRK protein. Structural homology modeling indicated that the PRK domain shares similarity with type III effector-associated kinases, suggesting a role in pathogen recognition or immune signaling (Figure 1C).

To validate the function of NLR–PRK in conferring powdery mildew resistance, we cloned the full-length genomic sequence of NLR–PRK from LX99, including its promoter, coding region, and terminator, into the binary vector pCAMBIA-1300, generating the complementary expression cassette ProNLR–PRK:NLR–PRK (Supplemental Figure 4). This construct was introduced into the susceptible wheat cultivar Fielder using Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, yielding three independent T0 transgenic lines. In the T1 generation, all plants carrying the NLR–PRK transgene exhibited resistance to isolate B37, whereas segregants lacking the transgene and wild-type Fielder plants remained susceptible (Figure 1D). These results demonstrate that NLR–PRK confers resistance to powdery mildew in wheat.

To evaluate the breadth of resistance conferred by NLR–PRK, we infected LX99 (Pm2+ NLR–PRK), JM22 (Pm2+ NLR–PRK), Nongda399 (Pm2 only), an NLR–PRK transgenic line, and the susceptible control Fielder with eight Bgt isolates. LX99, JM22, and the NLR–PRK transgenic line were resistant to six isolates, whereas Nongda399 was resistant to only two. Fielder was susceptible to all isolates (Supplemental Figure 5). These results demonstrate that NLR–PRK confers broad-spectrum and isolate-specific resistance to powdery mildew in wheat.

Genomic sequence alignment reveals that the NLR–PRK genes from cultivars LX99, JM22, LongReach Lancer, Julius, and Attraktion are identical (Supplemental Figure 6). Comparative genomics identified a wheat-Triticum timopheevii T2B·2G chromosomal translocation shared among these cultivars, with NLR–PRK residing within the introgressed T. timopheevii 2G segment (Figure 1B). Sequence alignment further showed that JM22 shares the highest sequence similarity with Attraktion (Kale et al., 2022) and Julius (Walkowiak et al., 2020), whereas LongReach Lancer aligns most closely with T. timopheevii (Figure 1B). These alignments suggest that Attraktion and Julius may have been derived from LongReach Lancer through targeted reduction of the 2G introgression to minimize linkage drag (Kale et al., 2022). Similarly, JM22 likely originated from Attraktion, Julius, or related germplasm. Additionally, the NLR–PRK gene from Coker 747 and Coker 783, both carrying the Pm6 gene (Qin et al., 2011), is identical to those from LX99 and JM22 (Supplemental Figures 6 and 7). Altogether, the conserved T. timopheevii chromosome 2G segment and the identical sequence indicate that Pm52 is equivalent to Pm6, consistent with the recent classification of Pm6 as an NLR–PRK (Wang et al., 2025).

We developed Pm52-FM, a functional marker specific for Pm52/Pm6, to screen 1368 diverse wheat accessions, including Chinese mini-core collections, landraces, international cultivars/breeding lines, and representatives of T. durum and T. timopheevii (Supplemental Table 6). The NLR–PRK gene was detected in three T. timopheevii accessions and five common wheat accessions originating from the former USSR, Portugal, Spain, and Tunisia. This distribution pattern supports the introgression of Pm52 into Chinese cultivars LX99 and JM22 from Eurasian and North African germplasm. Screening of 1613 Chinese commercial wheat cultivars and breeding lines using Pm52-FM identified 85 resistant entries, all conferring immunity to Bgt isolate B37 (Supplemental Table 7). The cloning of Pm52 and the validation of Pm52-FM provide a robust tool for marker-assisted selection, enabling rapid deployment of this resistance gene in wheat breeding programs.

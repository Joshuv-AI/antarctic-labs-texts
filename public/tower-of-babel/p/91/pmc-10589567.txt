Exploration of potential targets and mechanisms of naringenin in the treatment of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease through network pharmacology
Authors: Chenyang Yu, Duan Han, Jingfang Yu, Ran Zhu, Cuiyan Zhu, Fule Wang, Tiefeng Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Objective:This study aimed to use network pharmacology to investigate the molecular mechanisms and potential targets of naringenin (NR) for nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) treatment to offer new drug development ideas.Methods:The structure and compound information of NR were obtained from PubChem and the traditional Chinese medicine system pharmacology database and analysis platform. The traditional Chinese medicine system pharmacology database and analysis platform Database, Comparative Toxicogenomics Database and Encyclopedia of Traditional Chinese Medicine Database were then used to predict the related targets of NR. Online mendelian inheritance in man, Disgenet, Gene cards, The therapeutic target database and Drug bank were used to screen NAFLD targets, and the intersection analysis was performed with the targets of NR active components to obtain the targets of NR in the treatment of NAFLD. The protein-protein interaction network of therapeutic targets was constructed by protein-protein interaction networks functional enrichment analysis 11.0, and gene ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment analysis of therapeutic targets was performed by Metascape platform.Results:In this study, 171 NR targets and 1748 potential targets of NAFLD were screened, and 89 crossover targets and 16 core targets were screened and finally obtained. A total of 176 GO items were obtained by GO enrichment analysis (P < .05), including 389 biological process, 6 cell composition and 30 molecular function. A total of 137 signaling pathways were obtained by Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment and screening (P < .05). The core targets of NR in the treatment of NAFLD are TP53, CASP3, PRKCA, AKT1, RELA, PPARG, NCOA2, CYP1A1, ESR1, MAPK3, STAT3, JAK1, MAPK1, TNF, PPARA and PRKCB. Enrichment analysis showed that NR mainly involved in biological processes such as cellular response to nitrogen compound, regulation of miRNA transcription and negative regulation of miRNA-mediated gene silencing. It regulates Hepatitis B, Lipid and atherosclerosis, cytomegalovirus infection, Hepatitis C, AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic patients complications and other ways play a role in the treatment of NAFLD.Conclusions:The therapeutic effect of NR on NAFLD has the characteristics of multi-targets and multi-pathways, which provides a preliminary theoretical basis for clinical trials and the development of new drugs.

1. Introduction

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a metabolic stress liver injury closely related to insulin resistance and genetic susceptibility, excluding alcohol and other definite liver damage factors. It is a clinicopathological syndrome characterized by diffuse hepatocellular steatosis, including nonalcoholic hepatic steatosis and its evolution into nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.[1] In Chinese medicine, NAFLD is classified as “dropsy,” “liver fetish,” “phlegm,” “accumulation,” “dysmenorrhea,” etc. The disease is located in the liver and involves the spleen, kidney and other internal organs. The cause of NAFLD is mostly attributed to poor diet, emotional and mental disorders, excessive labor and rest, chronic disease and deficiency of endowment. Spleen deficiency runs through the pathogenesis of NAFLD, and liver and spleen damage is the pathological basis of the disease, and treatment is mostly based on evidence-based therapy, focusing on regulating the balance between liver and spleen.[2] Currently, the global prevalence of NAFLD is about 25.24%, of which the prevalence in China is about 29.2%,[3] and it is expected that the number of NAFLD cases in China will be as high as 314.58 million by 2030,[4] and medium- to high-risk NAFLD may lead to an increase in all-cause, liver-related, and cardiovascular mortality.[5] However, up to now, effective treatment strategies for NAFLD are still lifestyle interventions combined with treatment of NAFLD-related metabolic syndrome, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease, such as metformin and statins,[6] and there are no drugs specifically for the treatment of NAFLD; therefore, research on drugs for the treatment of this disease should be of interest.

In recent years, with further studies on the pathogenesis of NAFLD, the role of certain receptors such as farnesol X receptor (FXR), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) and GLP-1 receptors in NAFLD has been gradually discovered and some novel drugs are coming to the fore.[7] Obeticholic acid is a potent and selective FXR agonist that improves insulin sensitivity and reduces intrahepatic circulating triglycerides and hepatic gluconeogenesis levels. It has been shown to improve NASH and liver fibrosis by modulating bile acid metabolism and inhibiting NLRP3 inflammatory vesicle activation in macrophages,[8] but now after phase III clinical trials it has been reported that obeticholic acid has side effects related to pruritus and dyslipidemia, and many patients have discontinued the drug due to side effects.[9]

Saroglitazar, a novel PPARα/γ agonist, has been approved for marketing in India as the first new NASH drug. In an animal model of diet-induced NAFLD, Saroglitazar increased the expression of PPAR targets and was involved in reducing oxidative stress, unfolded protein response and fibronectin signaling, confirming that Saroglitazar has an ameliorative effect on Insulin Resistance and Steatohepatitis[10]; a randomized controlled double-blind phase II trial showed that the saroglitazar 4mg group significantly improved Alanine transaminase, Liver fat content, insulin resistance and atherogenic dyslipidemia in patients with NAFLD/NASH.[11] Saroglitazar, although it has the advantage of reducing cardiovascular events, is PPARγ-active drugs are usually associated with increased body mass, so the safety of Saroglitazar remains to be evaluated in more clinical trials.[12]

In the US, Gliead has been working on Selonsertib, an apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1 inhibitor, which has been shown to affect hepatic steatosis, inflammation and fibrosis, making apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1 a new target for the treatment of NAFLD. Completed phase II clinical trials have shown that Selonsertib improves liver fibrosis in NASH patients, but 48 weeks of Selonsertib monotherapy has no antifibrotic effect in patients with NASH leading to bridging fibers or compensated cirrhosis. A phase III clinical trial to assess its antifibrotic capacity is currently underway.[13]

However, these drugs have a single target of action, potential adverse effects are not clearly defined, and some of them are still pending validation in phase III clinical trials and cannot be used in the clinic yet. Therefore, there is a need to investigate safer and more effective therapeutic strategies for NAFLD from the perspective of multi-target therapy. Chinese medicines have unique advantages in the treatment of NAFLD due to their multi-target and multi-channel characteristics. For example, Resveratrol (Res), a monomer present in Chinese herbal medicines such as Polygonum cuspidatum, Cassia seed and Polygonum multiflorum, improves insulin sensitivity, regulates the apoptotic process in hepatocytes, reduces the secretion and expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, inhibits hepatic steatosis. In addition, Salvianolic acid B (Sal B), one of the most potent components of Salvia miltiorrhiza, has been shown to inhibit the development of NAFLD/NASH by modulating cellular OS, improving lipid metabolism, anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects. Besides, there are many herbal monomers that have been shown to act in multiple pathways to provide safer and more promising therapeutic agents for the treatment of NAFLD. In particular, PPAR-α, PI3K, SIRT, NF-κB, AMPK, p53, SREBP-1c, and Nrf2 are considered to be important targets for the action of herbal monomers in the treatment of NAFLD.[14]

Naringenin is a monomer, belonging to the dihydroflavonoid class, which is widely found in 22 Chinese herbal medicines such as Chen Pi, Orange Red, Green Peel, Allium sativum, and Citrus aurantium, and has various pharmacological activities, such as antibacterial and anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antitumor, lipid regulator, and hepatoprotective,[15] and has important applications in the treatment of liver diseases, especially NAFLD. Esmaeili et al[16] in naringenin (NR) treatment carbon tetrachloride-induced liver injury rat model, NR was found to increase the hepatic content of reduced glutathione and the activity of antioxidant enzymes in rats, decrease nuclear Nrf2 expression and mRNA levels of its target genes to protect the liver from oxidative stress. It provides a preliminary experimental basis for the prevention and treatment of NAFLD. Studies on the therapeutic potential of NR in NAFLD have been reviewed,[17] but its targets, pathways and mechanisms are still unclear. To date, few specific animal and clinical studies have been conducted on its pathways and targets.

Chinese medicine has unique advantages in the prevention and treatment of NAFLD, such as multiple pathways, multiple targets, and low toxic side effects.[18] However, the complex mechanism of action of medicinal plants is very different from the single therapeutic target of western drugs, therefore, this study reveals the mechanism of interaction between NR and NAFLD-related targets based on the research method of network pharmacology, reveals the relationship between NAFLD and target proteins and target proteins and NR systematically and holistically, and observes the effect of NR on NAFLD from the perspective of network level.[19] The flow chart of specific research work is shown in Figure 1. We hope that this study will lay the scientific and theoretical foundation for future drug screening and experimental studies of NAFLD.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Obtaining NR structure and compound information

The 2D and 3D structures and CAS of NR were obtained from the PubChem database,[20] and the CAS was entered into traditional Chinese medicine system pharmacology database and analysis platform (TCMSP)[21] for obtaining the information about NR.

2.2. Screening for crossover gene targets associated with NR and NAFLD

2.2.1. Screening of NR target genes.

Canonical SMILES obtained from the PubChem database were imported into the Swiss Target Prediction Database[22] for target prediction. NR target genes from the TCMSP database,[21] the comparative toxicogenomics database[23] and encyclopedia of traditional Chinese medicine database[24] were combined and duplicates were eliminated using “naringenin” as a keyword, and the names were calibrated and compared by Uniprot database.[25] We finally obtained the corresponding genetic information of the composite targets.

2.2.2. Screening of NAFLD-related genes.

Using “nonalcoholic fatty liver disease” as a keyword, we searched 5 disease databases: online mendelian inheritance in man,[26] Disgenet,[27,28] GeneCards,[28] the therapeutic target database[29] and Drugbank[30] to obtain NAFLD-related genes. Where potential targets for NAFLD in the Gene Cards database were screened based on Score values greater than twice the median. Then, the targets obtained from the 5 disease databases were combined and the final NAFLD-associated target genes were obtained by removing duplicate targets after standardizing the target names through the Uniprot database.

2.2.3. Screening for crossover gene targets.

The NR-related targets and NAFLD disease targets obtained above were imported into the Microbiology website (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) to obtain crossover genes and create Venn diagrams to visualize potential targets for NR in NAFLD therapy.

2.3. Building protein-protein interaction (PPI) network

The establishment of a PPI network is essential for a deeper understanding of the degree of interaction between 2 proteins.[31] In this study, we imported the potential targets obtained earlier to the protein-protein interaction networks functional enrichment analysis platform[32] by setting the “Organism” as “Homo sapiens” while hiding free nodes. By constructing a PPI network, we obtained node information and prepared for the subsequent step, which involved creating a network diagram to explore the interactions between the NR-related targets and NAFLD targets.

2.4. Obtaining of key targets

To identify the core target genes for treating NAFLD with NR, we analyzed network topology parameters using Network Analyzer in Cytoscape 3.9.1.[33] The size and color of each node were determined by its degree value, and the key targets were selected by centrality measures calculated using the CentiScaPe 2.2 (https://apps.cytoscape.org/apps/centiscape) plugin.[34] Targets that met the 3 thresholds (median, proximity, and degree) were considered critical targets, representing the key target genes for NR treatment of NAFLD.

2.5. Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis

After obtaining the key targets, we conducted GO biofunctional enrichment analysis and KEGG metabolic pathway enrichment analysis with Metascape platform,[35] focusing on biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF). “Homo sapiens” (human origin) was selected as the species background for Metascape, the minimum overlap value was 3, the P value Cutoff was set to < .01, and the minimum enrichment value was 1.5. We also generated bubble plots and histograms using the BioScape mapping website(http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) and “component-target-disease pathway” network maps with Cytoscape 3.9.1 software.

A total of 171 NR-related targets were identified after consolidation and elimination of duplicates.

In total, 1748 NAFLD disease targets were obtained following merger and removal of duplicate targets.

The highest confidence score of 0.900 was set as the threshold for generating a PPI network graph, which contained 89 nodes and 195 edges (Fig. 5). The node size and color depth depicted the degree values, with larger and darker nodes corresponding to higher degree values. 4. Discussion

The pathogenesis of NAFLD is complex and still not completely elucidated, but the “multiple hit” theory is now widely accepted.[36] Studies on the interaction of cellular, molecular mechanisms, environmental and genetic factors have identified many key targets related to the pathogenesis of NAFLD, which provide directions for the development of diagnostic markers and targeted therapeutic agents for NAFLD. Selective hepatic insulin resistance is a key link in the pathogenesis of NAFLD.[37] Insulin resistance makes hepatic glucose metabolism unresponsive to insulin, while activating sterol regulatory element binding protein, which perpetuates hepatic adipogenesis, thereby exacerbating oxidative stress and contributing to abnormal insulin signaling. The “lipotoxicity” theory is the classical pathway of fat accumulation in NAFLD. Some specific lipids have damaging effects on hepatocytes, such as palmitic acid, cholesterol, lysophos phatidyl choline and ceramides. These lipotoxic mediators affect cellular behavior through a variety of mechanisms, including signaling cascades, activation of death receptors, oxidative stress, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and altered mitochondrial function, among others.[38] JNK-AP-1 and IKK-NF-κB are 2 important signaling pathways that promote the development of a chronic inflammatory state in NAFLD,[36] and PPAR plays an important role in the regulation of inflammatory pathways (especially inhibition of NF-κB pathway).[39] In addition, elevated levels of FFAs and their lipotoxicity, IR, peripheral adipose tissue dysfunction and enterogenic endotoxemia can activate and maintain the production and release of hepatic pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α, etc.), leading to tissue damage and contributing to NAFLD disease progression.[40] In recent years, intestinal flora and the enterohepatic axis are emerging hotspots in the field of NAFLD research. Three key factors affecting NAFLD, diet, intestinal flora and intestinal mucosa, connect bile and portal blood to the host through the enterohepatic axis.[41] FXR and Takeda G protein-coupled receptor 5 play important roles in bile acid and lipid metabolism in the organism,[42] and reduced expression of intestinal and hepatic FXR and compromised FXR pathway function can lead to disturbances in glycolipid and bile acid metabolism and exacerbate NAFLD symptoms. In past animal models and clinical trials, Takeda G protein-coupled receptor 5 and FXR receptors have shown effectiveness in reversing steatohepatitis and fibrosis in preclinical models of NASH,[43] making the regulation of metabolism through the intestinal flora and the enterohepatic axis a new target for the treatment of NAFLD.

NR, chemically known as naringenin (4, 5, 7-trihydroxydihydroflavone), is a glycoside obtained by hydrolysis of 1 molecule of glucose and rhamnose from naringin. The oral bioavailability of naringenin is 59.29, with low toxicity and high safety,[44] and it is mainly metabolized in the intestinal tract, and the absorption kinetics of NR in the small intestine is presumed to be passive transport, and the absorption kinetics is primary.[45] NR possesses various biological activities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antiapoptotic, and neuroprotective properties,[15] and exhibits potential therapeutic advantages in cardiovascular, neurological, and gastrointestinal system diseases.[46,47] NR has been shown to inhibit oxidative stress and the β pathway, suppress hepatic stellate cell transdifferentiation, reduce collagen synthesis, and regulate lipid and cholesterol synthesis and oxidation in liver diseases such as NAFLD. Additionally, NR has been found to modulate specific signaling pathways, such as mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), toll-like receptor, and transforming growth factor β atypical signaling pathways, to reduce extracellular matrix synthesis and deposition and to have a positive impact on hepatocellular carcinoma.[48] In a 4-week double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial of 44 overweight/obese patients with NAFLD, it was shown that compared with the placebo group, the NR-treated group had higher post-intervention plasma atherosclerotic index, serum non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels, total cholesterol/ HDL-C, triglycerides/HDL-C, LDL-C/HDL-C, non-HDL-C/HDL-C ratio, BMI, and visceral fat levels were significantly reduced.[49] These findings suggest that NR may be a promising treatment for NAFLD and its related complications.

Although NR has many benefits for human, its water and lipid solubility are poor, therefore, in order to improve the in vitro solubility of NR formulations, several novel dosage forms have been invented, such as NR solid dispersions, NR sodium alginate gastric floating micropills, NR β-cyclodextrin inclusions, etc.[15] In addition, NR nanoparticles and liposomes have been widely focused on, and the dissolution performance of NR loaded with cellulose nanocrystals in water was significantly improved, with a cumulative dissolution of 93.4% at 120 minutes.[50] Moreover, the nano-formulations can further enhance the anti-inflammatory activity of NR, inhibit the upregulation of inflammatory factors and release of inflammatory mediators, and the targeted nano-delivery is expected to deliver the drug to specific sites for maximum efficacy.[51]

We used network pharmacology to screen the drug and disease target database, and finally obtained 89 common targets and 16 key targets of NR for NAFLD treatment. AKT1, short for Protein kinase B, regulates cell proliferation and growth and is involved in a variety of cellular processes, including apoptosis and glucose metabolism. AKT1 is mainly enriched in the TNF signaling pathway, and TNF signaling mainly regulates the immune inflammatory response. Down-regulation of AKT1 expression can inhibit lipid synthesis and inflammatory response, reduce insulin sensitivity, and improve clinical symptoms of NAFLD.[52] Meanwhile, the PI3K/AKT1 signaling pathway is closely related to oxidative stress in many cells and is involved in cholesterol uptake and transduction,[53] and regulating the expression of this pathway and its downstream lipid synthesis genes may improve NAFLD by regulating lipid metabolism.[54]

Autophagy is a cellular process by which cells phagocytose and degrade damaged cytoplasmic components. Mitogen-activated protein kinase 1/3 (MAPK1/3) can play a beneficial role in hepatic lipid metabolism by stimulating ATG7-dependent autophagy.[55] In addition, the MAPK (erk) signaling pathway is essential in hepatocytes for the inhibition of apoB secretion by NR and insulin. Allister et al[56]reported that NR can inhibit apoB secretion by activating the phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase PI3-K and MAPK (erk) signaling pathways by the same mechanism of action as insulin but independent of IR, which is important for the regulation of hepatocyte lipid homeostasis. Therefore, selective upregulation of MAPK1/3 activity could provide a potential drug target for the treatment of human NAFLD.[57]

HIF-1 signaling channels have been shown to inhibit fatty acid catabolism by inhibiting medium- and long-chain acyl coenzyme A dehydrogenases and thus fatty acid catabolism.[58] Alternatively, elevated levels of HIF1α may enhance insulin signaling in adipocytes, thereby promoting the conversion of glucose to fatty acids and triglycerides and leading to obesity.[59] Therefore, it is speculated that NR may reduce fat production by inhibiting the HIF-1 signaling pathway.

In addition, some studies have found other possible pathways of NR in the treatment of NAFLD. For example, Wang Q et al[60]found that NR in wild type mice may prevent NAFLD by down-regulating KCs and NLRP3/NF-κB signaling pathway in hepatocytes. Thereby alleviating lipid accumulation and inflammatory response in the liver of mice with methionine choline deficiency diet. Hua YQ et al[61] investigated the effect of NR on 12-month-old male ApoE -/- mice of NASH and its possible mechanism, and found that NR increased the expression and activity of liver SIRT1 protein, as well as the deacetylation of liver kinase B1, PGC1α and NF-κB. In addition, it can improve liver metabolic parameters, regulate the expression of lipid metabolism-related genes (FASN, SCD1, PPARα and CPT1α), reduce macrophage recruitment, TNF-α and IL-6 content, and relieve liver oxidative stress by inhibiting ROS production and normalizing antioxidant enzyme activity, and liver inflammation can be inhibited by reducing liver fibrosis and cell senescence.

There is increasing evidence for the potential protective effects of NR in NAFLD. This study provides ideas and directions for further research on NR in the treatment of NAFLD, and provides a foothold for future exploration of the mechanisms underlying the potential therapeutic effects of NR-rich herbs and other plants.

5. Conclusion

Based on the results of the study, it is tentatively predicted that NR can regulate targets such as AKT1, MAPK1, MAPK3, PRKCA, RELA, PRKCB, STAT3, and may treat NAFLD by modulating the following pathways, such as AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, HIF-1 signaling pathway, sphingolipid signaling pathway, thyroid hormone signaling signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, pathways in cancer, proteoglycans in cancer, and chemo-oncogenic effects-receptor activation. The mechanisms of action involve cellular response to organic nitrogen compounds, cellular response to nitrogen compounds, regulation of miRNA transcription, negative regulation of miRNA-mediated gene silencing, response to hormones, and negative regulation of posttranscriptional gene silencing. This study reflects the characteristics of NR in intervening NAFLD from multiple targets and pathways, but since the network pharmacology is mainly based on existing research data to complete the extrapolation, the conclusion still needs further molecular docking and experimental validation, so as to provide data reference for its clinical use and lay the foundation for further new drug development.

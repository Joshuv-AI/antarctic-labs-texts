Analysis of the potential regulatory mechanism of PIEZO1 in Alzheimer’s disease based on RNA sequencing
Authors: Ya-Han Wang, Yao Xie, Peng-Li Ding, Jin-Wen Ge, Le Xie, Da-Hua Wu, Chao Xin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe ion channel protein PIEZO1 regulates complex processes in Alzheimer’s disease (AD). This study explored the regulatory mechanism of PIEZO1 in AD using bioinformatic analysis, aiming to identify AD-associated genes and potential therapeutic strategies. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data based on an in vitro model of AD were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, and differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 R package. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were conducted on the identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and principal component analysis (PCA) were also performed on the gene expression data. PCA clustering revealed overall significant differences among the three groups of samples (control, AD, and PF-562271 intervention group). All DEGs in the three sample groups were subjected to k-means hierarchical clustering, revealing four clusters. The matrix antigen plot indicated multiple changes in the average millions of fragments per thousand bases value of each gene among the groups. Functional annotation was performed for the DEGs between the two groups, and GSEA identified both activated and inhibited pathways, distinguishing the groups. Notably, the expression of PIEZO1 was higher in the AD group than in the control group. This study confirmed elevated PIEZO1 expression in astrocyte AD models, which is associated with the regulation of the extracellular matrix, cell–substrate adhesion, synaptic migration, and other related functions and pathways.Keywords: Alzheimer’s disease, PIEZO1, RNA sequencing, Bioinformatics analysis

Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by β-amyloid protein (Aβ) deposition, which leads to the formation of senile plaques, excessive tau phosphorylation resulting in neurofibrillary tangles, neuroinflammation, and synaptic loss (Wang et al., 2021, Hansen et al., 2018). Glial cells are highly mechanosensitive and can detect changes in the mechanical properties of the brain microenvironment during neurodegenerative diseases (Ivkovic et al., 2022). Specifically, they can sense changes in the stiffness of the underlying cell culture matrix through transmembrane ion channels that are activated by stretching (Ge et al., 2015). Microglia cultured in hydrogels of different stiffnesses exhibit distinct growth morphologies (Previtera et al., 2010). In addition, astrocytes release inflammatory factors in response to mechanical traction (Maggio and Vlachos, 2014). In AD, the formation of extracellular amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles promotes the proliferation of microglia and astrocytes. These glial cells physically sense the accumulation of amyloid plaques through piezoelectric mechanically sensitive ion channel component 1 (PIEZO1)-mediated mechanical sensitivity channels (Chu et al., 2023, Mai et al., 2022). Mechanically gated Piezo ion channels were the first family of mechanically gated cation channels discovered and characterized in mammals. This family consists of two members: PIEZO1 and PIEZO2. PIEZO1 is expressed in both neuronal and non-neuronal cell types across various regions of the brain, (Velasco-Estevez et al., 2018, Wu et al., 2017) while PIEZO2 is mainly expressed in the injury sensory system (Bron et al., 2014, Eijkelkamp et al., 2013, Kim et al., 2012). In the developing African claw toad brain, PIEZO1 plays a role in the axonal guidance of retinal ganglion cells in the optic nerve bundle (Koser et al., 2016). In HEK293 cells, soluble Aβ can prevent PIEZO1-mediated Ca2+ influx (Maneshi et al., 2018). Moreover, astrocytes upregulate PIEZO1 expression around extracellular β plaques, suggesting a connection between AD pathology and the function of brain cells expressing these channels (Velasco-Estevez et al., 2018).

Changes in cell composition are closely related to AD progression and cognitive function decline. Therefore, understanding the population structure of brain cells is crucial for understanding the progression of neurodegenerative diseases (Golub et al., 2015). In recent years, transcriptomic changes related to AD have gained increasing attention, as they may reveal the molecular mechanisms underlying the disease (Chan et al., 2015, Miller et al., 2013, Parikshak et al., 2015, Gaiteri et al., 2016). RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has become a powerful method for exploring the potential transcriptional landscape of the complex cellular interactions in the human brain, enabling the study of different cell types and precise identification of brain regions affected by AD. In the present study, using RNA-seq data from previous studies, we explored the potential pathways and regulatory mechanisms of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between an in vitro model of AD prepared using astrocytes and control cells. We further analyzed the association of these DEGs with PIEZO, aiming to clarify the potential regulatory mechanism of PIEZO in the pathology of AD.

Methods

RNA-seq dataset

RNA-seq data for the AD model in this study were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (GSE236868). The dataset included three control samples (astrocytes), three AD in vitro samples (astrocytes + tau PFF), and three AD in vitro samples with PF-562271 intervention (astrocytes + tau PFF + PF271). We collected the preprocessed RNA-seq expression data matrix. To ensure comparability across samples, we applied log2 transformation after variance-stabilizing normalization (VST) in DESeq2.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis of RNA-seq data was performed using the DESeq2 R package (v1.36.0). Genes with absolute fold change (|FC= > 1.8) and Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.01 were regarded as differentially expressed genes.

Three pairwise comparisons were conducted:

●B vs. A: AD group (astrocyte + tau PFF) vs. control group (astrocyte)

B vs. A: AD group (astrocyte + tau PFF) vs. control group (astrocyte)

●F vs. A: PF-562271 intervention group (astrocyte + tau PFF + PF271) vs. control group

F vs. A: PF-562271 intervention group (astrocyte + tau PFF + PF271) vs. control group

●F vs. B: PF-562271 intervention group vs. AD group

F vs. B: PF-562271 intervention group vs. AD group

Functional annotation of DEGs

To explore the functions of DEGs across different groups, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were performed using the ClusterProfiler package version 4.4.4. An adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Visualization of gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and RNA-seq results

GSEA was conducted using the KEGG database with the GSEA function in ClusterProfiler. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using the prcomp function (stats v4.2.2), and hierarchical clustering was performed using the hclust function. Visualization of RNA-seq results was carried out with ggplot2 (v3.3.6) and ComplexHeatmap (v2.0.0). Heatmaps of DEGs were clustered using the k-means algorithm with k = 4, ensuring stable clustering by fixing the random seed.

Results

Cluster analysis among transcriptome samples of AD

The heatmap of the correlation coefficients among the gene expression profiles indicates correlations among the cell groups. Comparison of all samples revealed a Pearson coefficient of ≥ 0.7, suggesting high sample repeatability. The similarity was higher between groups A and F than between groups A and B, suggesting that PF-562271 intervention caused the AD model cells to revert to a state more similar to that of the control cells (Fig. 1a). PCA clustering indicated that the overall differences among the three groups of samples were significant, with negligible variability among the samples within the groups (Fig. 1b).

We performed k-means hierarchical clustering on all DEGs across the three groups of samples and identified four clusters (Fig. 2a). The GO analysis results corresponding to Cluster 1 indicate that the upregulated genes in group F (including PIEZO1) were enriched in eight biological processes (Fig. 2b), including extracellular matrix (ECM) organization, extracellular structure organization, external encapsulating structure organization, cell–substrate adhesion, generation of precursor metabolites and energy, and cellular modified amino acid metabolic processes. GO analysis of Cluster 2 revealed that DEGs were enriched in terms such as synapse organization and glial cell. Moreover, upregulated DEGs in group A were enriched in eight pathways, such as regulation of chondrocyte differentiation, renal tubule morphogenesis, cold-induced thermogenesis, Wnt signaling pathway, cell-cell signaling by Wnt, cerebellum morphogenesis, vascular process in circulatory system, and temperature homeostasis (Fig. 2c). Upregulated DEGs in group B, corresponding to Cluster 3 (Fig. 2d), were enriched in pathways such as response to virus, negative regulation of viral process, and I-kappa B kinase/NF-kappa B signaling. Cluster 4 corresponded to the top eight biological processes enriched in upregulated genes in groups A and B (Fig. 2e).

The evolutionary conserved piezo protein family, consisting of PIEZO1 and PIEZO2, consists of large ion channel proteins. Over the past ten years, their basic functions in various mechanical transmissions have been studied. PIEZO1 is expressed in various human tissues and organs, such as immune cells and bones, and is involved in vascular development, blood pressure regulation, cell migration, and the regulation of innate immune homeostasis. Therefore, we investigated differences in PIEZO1 expression in each group, which were expressed as FPKM. The results (Fig. 6) indicated that PIEZO1 expression was significantly higher in group B than in group A, suggesting that its expression increased during the pathogenesis of AD (p < 0.01).

Discussion

According to the 2024 World Alzheimer's Report, the number of patients with dementia is expected to increase from 55 million in 2019 to 139 million in 2050, owing to the aging of the global population, and the related costs will also increase significantly. The proportion of patients with dementia experiencing discrimination increased from 83 % in 2019–88 % in 2024, affecting multiple areas of life, such as work, social interaction, and medical care. In low-and middle-income countries, dementia reduces the quality of life for both patients and their caregivers. In the early stages of AD, when patients experience subjective cognitive function declines or mild cognitive impairment, the clinical symptoms are often mild and non-specific, making them easy to overlook and hindering early diagnosis (Jia et al., 2020). Notably, the recent approval and commercialization of monoclonal antibody drugs targeting Aβ have demonstrated initial promise as disease-modifying therapies for AD. However, the clinical application of these therapies depends on the AD diagnosis. Therefore, accurate, convenient, and cost-effective biomarker detection methods have become increasingly important in the diagnosis and treatment of AD (Zhang and Chen, 2024). Moreover, the treatment of AD is hindered by its complex pathogenesis. In addition to AD-associated genes, existing recognized biomarkers include Aβ pathological markers, tau pathological markers, and non-core AD markers such as NfL and glial fibrillary acidic protein. Therefore, to advance early diagnosis and treatment, current research is focused on exploring potential targets in the pathogenesis of AD.

Gene regulation plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of AD, providing valuable insights into its underlying pathophysiological processes. It also enables the identification of new biological features, prognostic or diagnostic markers, and therapeutic targets through translational genomics (Scheltens et al., 2021). Therefore, determining genetic risk factors for AD remains a major priority. With the emergence of high-throughput genomic technology, many candidate AD-related loci and genes have been reported (Lambert, 2022). However, several hereditary factors remain unknown. The transcriptome serves as a crucial link between genetic information and biological functions. Broadly defined, it encompasses the entire set of coding and non-coding RNA transcribed in one or a group of cells under the same physiological conditions. Narrowly defined, it refers to the collection of all mRNA (An et al., 2025). Transcriptome sequencing analysis (RNA-seq) involves isolating the target mRNA, reverse-transcribing it into a cDNA library, attaching adapters at both ends of small DNA fragments, and using high-throughput sequencing technology to quantify fragment abundance. This approach enables the accurate measurement of mRNA expression levels, as well as the identification of alternative splicing sites and single-nucleotide polymorphisms in coding sequences. RNA-seq provides comprehensive sequence information for almost all transcripts of a specific tissue or organ of a certain species under defined physiological or pathological conditions. 26 Currently, RNA-seq is widely applied in the fields of basic research, clinical diagnosis, and drug development for AD.

The core pathological markers of AD, Aβ and tau, interact with each other. Tau is a phosphoprotein composed of a high number of serine/threonine, proline, and arginine/lysine/histidine residues (Miller et al., 2014). It belongs to the microtubule-associated protein (MAP) family and can effectively stabilize microtubules and promote retrograde (circumferential nucleus) and retrograde (nuclear circumferential) axonal transport (Lilek et al., 2023). Therefore, its normal functioning is crucial for neuronal transport and synaptic structure (Courade et al., 2018). Excessive phosphorylation of tau can lead to a loss of function, preventing it from binding to microtubules and resulting in the deterioration of dendritic structure and axonal transport, as well as the depolymerization of microtubules. Tau phosphorylation mediates the amplitude reduction of its mislocalization to dendritic spines and miniature excitatory postsynaptic currents induced by Aβ42 oligomers, leading to synaptic dysfunction (Wang and Mandelkow, 2016). Aβ42 aggregates can lead to tau protein accumulation in neurons, inducing abnormal phosphorylation at AD-related sites (Guo et al., 2017). In astrocytes, decreased cholesterol synthesis mediates abnormal phosphorylation of their own tau protein (Chen et al., 2013). Specifically, after lentivirus infection of C8D1A astrocytes, DHCR24 expression was downregulated, leading to disruption of the cholesterol-rich lipid raft structure. This damage activated the lipid raft-dependent Ras/MEK/ERK signaling pathway, which in turn promoted excessive tau phosphorylation at multiple sites in C8D1A astrocytes (Wu et al., 2017).

PIEZO1 is a mechanically sensitive ion channel with a trimeric helical structure consisting of 2100–4700 amino acids and 24–40 transmembrane domains (Saotome et al., 2018). This special structure enables PIEZO1 to act as a mechanically gated cation channel that responds rapidly to various mechanical stimulation methods and senses changes in the mechanical stiffness of brain tissue. It also converts mechanical pressure into current in cells and regulates processes such as angiogenesis, red blood cell volume, and cell migration (Lai et al., 2022, Velasco-Estevez et al., 2020). In astrocytes surrounding rigid Aβ fibrils, the mechanically sensitive channel PIEZO1 is upregulated. Under the action of the agonist Yoda1, PIEZO1 can enhance the phagocytic and lysosomal activities of microglia, coordinating the clearance of Aβ (Lilek et al., 2023). Furthermore, astrocytic PIEZO1 regulates adult hippocampal neurogenesis, long-term potentiation, and cognitive function (Jantti et al., 2022, Lai et al., 2022). PIEZO1 plays a leading role in ultrasonic mechanical conduction. Specifically, it can significantly trigger downstream cell signaling processes through ultrasonic stimulation of neurons, tumor cells, and osteoblasts. It can also regulate neuronal function, inhibit tumor growth, promote osteogenesis, and play a role in brain function and ultrasonic signal transduction (Chi et al., 2022, Zhang et al., 2021, Qiu et al., 2019). Astrocytes exhibit a high degree of mechanical sensitivity and sense local environmental signals via mechanical receptors (Singh et al., 2021). In hippocampal astrocytes, PIEZO1 responds to mechanical stimulation and affects neurogenesis, neural stem cell proliferation, and cognitive function (Fonar et al., 2011). Moreover, PIEZO1 activation induces significant cation currents and transient Ca2+ influx in astrocytes, thereby promoting adenosine triphosphate release and adult neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus (Chen and Qiu, 2022). In astrocytes, PIEZO1 overexpression enhances learning and memory (Chen and Qiu, 2022). Notably, resting astrocytes rarely express PIEZO1, whereas reactive astrocytes exhibit significant upregulation of PIEZO1 expression (Velasco-Estevez et al., 2020, Singh et al., 2021). A1 and A2 are two subgroups of reactive astrocytes, with A1 representing a neurotoxic subtype that drives neuroprotein inflammation, whereas A2 is generally considered neuroprotective (Liddelow et al., 2017). However, it remains unclear whether PIEZO1 expression differs between A1 and A2 reactive astrocyte subsets. Notably, the response of PIEZO1 in astrocytes to Aβ appears to be specific, as PIEZO1 is upregulated in reactive astrocytes around amyloid plaques in both patients with AD and TGF344-AD rats (Velasco-Estevez et al., 2018, Satoh et al., 2006).

Brain cells are structurally supported by the outer matrix; therefore, brain development and neural remodeling are affected by mechanical pressure. Both physiological and pathological processes in the brain are regulated by the mechanical characteristics of the cellular microenvironment (Singh et al., 2021). Brain functional processes, such as axonal migration, neuron-astrocyte communication, and neuron-matrix interactions, can also be altered by external mechanical forces (Koser et al., 2016). For example, during brain development, the direction of migration and growth rate of neuronal axons are affected by substrate rigidity (Liu et al., 2018). PIEZO1 mediates perception of external mechanical signals during neuronal axon growth (Li et al., 2021). In adult mice, the dorsal root ganglion nerves exhibit the greatest extension and traction when the matrix stiffness is 1 kPa (Koch et al., 2012). Within the substrate stiffness range of 0.3–1 kPa, PIEZO1 is more easily activated by decreased substrate stiffness (Singh et al., 2021). However, reduced substrate stiffness impairs the growth and migration of neuronal axons, Singh et al. (2021) suggesting a negative correlation between PIEZO1 activation and axon growth and migration. In 5xFAD mouse models, PIEZO1 can transduce mechanical and electrical stimuli into biochemical signals, mediating the favorable effect of transcranial magneto-acoustic stimulation on synaptic plasticity (Chu et al., 2023).

Changes in the mechanical properties of the ECM play a crucial role in AD progression (Segel et al., 2019). For example, PIEZO1 is activated by sensing the mechanical indentation and tension of the cell membrane. In contrast, its activation can be inhibited by membranes rich in saturated fatty acids, potentially resulting in its long-term inactivation (Romero et al., 2019). In the present study, our GO analysis revealed that the top eight biological processes enriched in the group with high PIEZO1 expression included ECM organization, extracellular structure organization, external encapsulation structure organization, cell–substrate adhesion, synapse organization, and glial cell development. Moreover, the intragroup GSEA results revealed that upregulated PIEZO1 expression was associated with enrichment of pathways involved in AD. This finding suggests a relationship between PIEZO1 and AD, which is consistent with the results of previous studies.

In our characterization analysis, astrocytes treated with tau preformed fibrils (Group B) exhibited higher PIEZO1 expression than control astrocytes, suggesting a regulatory role for PIEZO1 in the pathogenesis of AD. Notably, after treatment with PF-562271 (PF271), the expression of PIEZO1 continued to increase, despite the elimination of tau phosphorylation. PIEZO1 activation leads to changes in the dynamics of the cytoskeleton (Nourse and Pathak, 2017). Specifically, the adhesion zone serves as a central cytoskeletal hub connecting the ECM to the intracellular cytoskeleton, enabling cells to detect and transmit external mechanical forces. Focal adhesion kinase (FAK), a focal adhesion protein, plays a key regulatory role in focal adhesion dynamics and cytoskeletal remodeling. PF-562271 (PF271) is a specific FAK inhibitor that eliminates tau hyperphosphorylation. Activating PIEZO1-transfected cells using laser-induced shock waves and chemical stimulation leads to a sustained increase in calcium and a decrease in FAK activity. In contrast, a reduction in pulse and chemical stimulation results in a more transient increase in calcium and a slight increase in FAK activity. This suggests a bimodal pattern of PIEZO1 activation: transient mode characterized by transient Ca²⁺increase and FAK activation, and a persistent mode marked by persistent Ca²⁺ increase and FAK inhibition (Shannon et al., 2017, Steinhauser and Schmidt, 2014, Quinto-Su et al., 2011). This may explain the upregulation of PIEZO1 expression observed following FAK inhibition. However, the limitation of our research includes the lack of direct experimental evidence to verify the role of PIEZO1 in the pathology of AD, and the detailed mechanism requires further clarification in future investigations. In addition, we noted that the Wnt pathway was inhibited in group F following FAK inhibition. Notably, numerous interactions exist between FAK and Wnt during embryonic development, where FAK regulates Wnt3a gene expression to control cell fate in the developing neural plate (Fonar and Frank, 2011, Cohen et al., 2002). In future studies, we aim to further explore these underlying mechanisms.

Conclusion

Our study found that the upregulation of astrocytic PIEZO1 in an in vitro model of AD is associated with changes in extracellular matrix organization, cell–substrate adhesion, synaptic migration, and glial cell structural remodeling, suggesting a potential role of PIEZO1 in these process.

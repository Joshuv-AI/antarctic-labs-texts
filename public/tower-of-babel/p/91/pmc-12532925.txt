Treg cells mitigate inflammatory responses and symptoms via β2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK signaling in an experimental rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Yan Liu, Xiao-Qin Wang, Jian-Hua Lu, Hui-Wei Huang, Yi-Hua Qiu, Yu-Ping Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundβ2-adrenergic receptor (β2-AR) is widely expressed on immune cells, including T cells and it has a non-canonical signaling pathway, which is β2-AR-β-arrestin 2 (β-Arr2)-extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2). Our previous studies have shown that the β2-AR agonist terbutaline (Terb) can activate β2-AR and significantly inhibit helper T (Th) 17 cell function in collagen-induced arthritis (CIA). However, the effects of β2-AR on regulatory T (Treg) cells in CIA have not been consistently determined. The aim of our research was to explore whether β2-AR-β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling in Treg cells regulates inflammatory responses and signs in CIA.MethodsDBA1/J mice were used to prepare CIA model by intradermal injection of collagen type II (CII). Inducible Treg (iTreg) cells were induced from CD4+ T cells that were isolated from the spleens of normal or CIA mice and treated with Terb, β-Arr2 gene silence or overexpression or the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 in vitro. β2-AR and β-Arr2 expression, pERK1/2 level, interleukin (IL)-10 and transforming growth factor (TGF)-β production and the suppression of effector T (Teff) cell proliferation mediated by nature Treg (nTreg) cells were determined. β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells were intravenously injected into the tail base of CIA mice. Arthritic symptoms were assessed by clinical arthritis scores. Frequencies of Th17 and Treg cells, cytokine production and osteoclast and osteoblast-specific gene expression were estimated.ResultsThe increased β-Arr2 expression and pERK1/2 level induced by Terb in CIA iTregs were reduced by β-Arr2 gene silence. The increased TGF-β and IL-10 production in iTreg cells by Terb and the suppression of Teff cell proliferation mediated by Terb-treated nTregs from CIA mice were downregulated by β-Arr2 gene silence or U0126 and further enhanced by β-Arr2 gene overexpression. Adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice reduced limb inflammation, osteoclast-specific gene expression in joints and Th17 cell cytokine production in joints and serum but increased osteoblast-specific gene expression in joints and Treg cell cytokine production in joints and serum.Conclusionsβ2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK signaling in Treg cells contributes to alleviation of inflammatory responses and symptoms in CIA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13075-025-03659-9.Keywords: β2-adrenergic receptor, β-arrestin2, Extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2, Collagen-induced arthritis, Treg cells

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a progressive autoimmune disease characterized by joint pain and deformity, infiltration of immune cells, and an increase in self-antibodies [1]. A variety of factors are involved in the course of RA. Imbalance of immune cells in the local joint cavity and the immune system are considered to be the most relevant factors in the development of RA [2, 3]. The dysregulation of CD4+ T cells particularly plays a crucial role in the development of RA, and the balance between CD4+ T cells is an important factor in maintaining immune homeostasis and inhibiting the development of autoimmune diseases [4]. Some reports have indicated that as the disease activity of RA increases, the ratio of peripheral blood helper T (Th) 17 cells increases, while the ratio of regulatory T (Treg) cells decreases, leading to an imbalance in the ratio of Th17/Treg cells and a shift towards Th17 cells [5]. Treg cells, also known as inhibitory T cells, have regulatory roles in the innate and adaptive immune system. Tregs are divided into various types, including nature Treg (nTreg) cells, in vitro-derived inducible Treg (iTreg) cells that originate from CD4+ T cells, and other Treg cells. Transcription factor forks head box P3 (Foxp3) is critical for the maturation of Tregs and has significant roles in maintaining the negative regulation of Tregs on effector T (Teff) cells [6, 7]. Interleukin (IL)-10 and transforming growth factor (TGF)-β are the main cytokines produced by Treg cells to inhibit the proliferation and differentiation of Teff cells [8]. Both nTregs and iTregs are involved in immune tolerance and regulation of autoimmune diseases including RA [9–11]. Previous studies have shown that the number of CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ Treg cells in the peripheral blood of early RA patients is much lower than that of normal healthy controls and the difference between the peripheral blood Treg cells of active RA patients and normal controls is more obvious, indicating that the number of Treg cells is related to the development of RA [12]. Studies have also found that the removal of Treg cells by using anti-CD25 monoclonal antibody results in more severe collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) and more self-antibody production [13, 14]. Adoptive transfer of nTreg cells can alleviate the progression of mouse arthritis induced by collagen type II (CII), and the cells could be found in the inflamed synovium soon after transfer, indicating that regulation may occur locally in the joint [15]. On the treatment of spontaneous RA mice (SKG mice) using anti-CD4 monoclonal antibody, increasing the expression of Foxp3 in the synovium and reducing the level of Th17 cells and affecting the ratio of Th17/Treg can prevent SKG mice from developing chronic autoimmune arthritis [16]. These results all indicate that Treg cells play a role in downregulating inflammatory responses, inhibiting autoimmune responses, and maintaining immune homeostasis in the development and progression of RA.

Catecholamine neurotransmitters are secreted by the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) and have an impact on most systems of the body, including the immune system. The SNS also plays a very important regulatory role in autoimmune diseases [17]. Adrenaline and noradrenaline bind to adrenergic receptors (ARs) on immune cells, particularly CD4+ T cells, affecting their function. ARs include β1-AR, β2-AR, and β3-AR, but β2-AR has the most significant regulatory effect on immune cells and has been the most widely studied [18]. β2-AR is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) which is widely expressed on immune cells, including T cells [19, 20]. In general, its signaling pathway has a canonical pathway, which is β2-AR-cAMP-PKA-CREB, and a non-canonical pathway, which is β2-AR-β-arrestin 2 (β-Arr2)-extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2). Among these signaling molecules, β-Arr has four subtypes: β-Arr1-4. β-Arr1 has been shown to link to the canonical cAMP-PKA pathway, while β-Arr2 is known to link to GPCRs and the MAPK pathway [21]. Additionally, some researchers have found that certain cytokine changes in immune cells are not induced by an increase in cAMP, but rather by the MAPK-ERK pathway, both in healthy and diseased states [22]. Many studies have focused on the variation of Treg cell function or the changes in the SNS separately in different stages of RA [23]. The role of the SNS and the activation of β2-AR in RA or RA animal models is not consistent and sometimes contradictory. The activation of β2-AR may have inflammatory effects in the early stages of the disease and anti-inflammatory effects in the later stages [24, 25]. This suggests that the SNS has time-point differences in its effects on mice with arthritis.

In our previous studies on RA, we found that CD4+ T cells can also produce catecholamines and can promote the differentiation of CD4+ T cells towards Th2 cells [26]. In mice with CIA, these catecholamine-like substances have been shown to rebuild the imbalance of Th17/Treg in CIA mice and alleviate joint inflammation and bone damage [27]. Our previous laboratory studies have also shown that the β2-AR agonist terbutaline (Terb) can activate β2-AR and significantly inhibit the proliferation and differentiation of Th17 cells through the cAMP-PKA signaling pathway in CIA [28]. However, it is not known whether the β2-AR-β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling pathway in Treg cells is changeable in CIA. Thus, in the present study, we determined the expression of β2-AR on Treg cells and the change of the β2-AR expression in CIA condition and showed that the β2-AR-β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling pathway in Treg cells is involved in CIA condition. This investigation thereby provides potentially useful information for treatment of RA with β2-AR signaling activators or stimulators targeting Treg cells.

Methods and materials

Mice

Male DBA1/J mice, 8–10 weeks old, were obtained from Center of Experimental Animals (Nantong University, China). Mice were housed in polystryrene cages and bred with food and water ad libitum with standard temperature and a 12-h light/dark cycle. All animal procedures were performed according to the guidelines of the National Institutes of Health (USA) and with the approval of the Animal Care and Ethics Committee of Nantong University.

Induction of CIA

As a primary immunization, DBA1/J mice were intradermally injected in the tail base with 100 µl emulsion containing 100 µg CII (Chondrex, USA) in complete Freund’s adjuvant (Chondrex, USA) on day 0. On day 21, the mice were intraperitoneally boosted with 100 µg CII (1 mg/ml) in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant (Chondrex, USA). Eventually, an intraperitoneal injection with 20 µg lipopolysaccharide dissolved in 20 µl PBS was executed to the mice on day 28. The clinical arthritis scores of mouse limbs were observed by two independently blinded examiners every 2 days from day 21 post-immunization and evaluated using the following scale: grade 0 = no swelling; grade 1 = slight swelling and erythema; grade 2 = pronounced swelling; and grade 3 = joint rigidity. A representative arthritis score was determined by summing the scores of all four paws. Mice were sacrificed on the 41st day after the primary immunization fora separate in vitro experiment.

Induction and analysis of iTreg cells

Primary naive CD4+ T cells were isolated from spleens of mice with CD4 magnetic beads (MiltenyiBiotec, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The purified CD4+ T cells were activated with anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 antibodies (both 1 µg/ml, both from BD Pharmingen, USA) and stimulated with IL-2 (10 ng/ml, R&D, USA) and TGF-β1 (25 ng/ml, R&D, USA) in the incubator with 5% CO2 for 48 h. To analyze iTreg generation, cells were collected in PBS containing 1% fetal bovine serum and fixed/permeabilized using the Transcription Factor Buffer Set (BD Biosciences, USA) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Selected protein markers were stained with FITC-conjugated anti-CD4, PE-conjugated anti-CD25 and APC-conjugated anti-Foxp3 monoclonal antibodies (all from BD Biosciences, USA). Flow cytometry analysis was carried out with BD FACS Calibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA), and acquired data were analyzed using FlowJo, version 10, software. For iTreg analysis, cells positive for CD4, CD25, and Foxp3 were selected and further evaluated. Number of CD4, CD25, and Foxp3 positive cells was approximately 25% (data not shown).

nTreg and Teff cell isolation

CD4+CD25+ T cells, known as nTreg cells, were isolated from the spleens of normal or CIA mice based on their surface expression of CD25 using a magnetic bead based isolation kit (MiltenyiBiotec, Germany) per the manufacturer’s instructions. The CD4+CD25– fraction was used as Teff cells.

Plasmids construction

The sequence of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting mouse β-Arr2 (β-Arr2-shRNA; Gene Bank Accession (NM_145429)) was 5’-GTGCCAAATCAATAGAAGAAA-3’, and the plasmid expressing scrambled shRNA (Scr-shRNA) (5’-TTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGT-3’) was used as a control of the β-Arr2-shRNA. All of the plasmids were generated by Genechem Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Construction of lentiviral vectors expressing β-Arr2

We used a negative control for β-Arr2 (β-Arr2 gene overexpression) with just lentiviral vector. Lentivirus overexpressing β-Arr2 or containing control vector was purchased from Genechem Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Drug treatments

The iTregs were incubated at 37 °C for 48 h and treated with a highly selective β2-AR agonist terbutaline (Terb, 10–5 or 10–4 M, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for 24 h, or treated combined with the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 (10–6 M, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) 30 min earlier and the β2-AR agonist Terb (10–4 M) for 24 h. These iTreg cells were also treated with transfection of lentiviral vectors expressing β-Arr2 for β-Arr2 overexpression or plasmids used for β-Arr2 knockdown and 48 h later the cells were incubated by Terb (10–4 M) for 24 h. Subsequent analyses as described below were performed.

Transfection of plasmids used for β-Arr2 knockdown

The constructed plasmids used for β-Arr2 knockdown were transfected into the activated iTregs or nTregs using nucleofection technology (Amaxa Biosystems, Germany), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In the same way, the control plasmids (Scr-shRNA-expressing plasmid) were transfected. Briefly, iTregs or nTregs were resuspended in 100 µl of cell nucleofector solution after incubating at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 for 48 h. Plasmid of 3 µg was added to Treg cell suspension. The mixtures were subsequently transferred to an electroporation cuvette with aluminum electrodes and placed in the nucleofection device (Amaxa Biosystems, Germany). Nucleofection of these cells was accomplished using X-001 program, and the samples were immediately transferred to pre-warmed media and incubated for 48 h. The transfection efficiency was determined by the fluorescence-positive cells under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Germany). Number of GFP-labeled cells was approximately 50% to 70% (data not shown).

Transfection of lentiviral vectors expressing β-Arr2 for β-Arr2 overexpression

After cell culturing for 48 h, medium containing lentiviruses (1 × 108 transducing-units/ml) and polybrene (8 µg/ml, Hanbio, Shanghai, China) was added at a multiplicity of infection of 10 and mixed with the iTregs or nTregs cells. Polybrene was used to improve infection efficiency. After incubation for 48 h, cell supernatants in the wells were replaced by DMEM containing fetal bovine serum and then cells were cultured with Terb (10− 4 M) for 24 h for subsequent analyses.

Suppression of Teff cell proliferation assay

nTregs were isolated from normal or CIA mice based on their surface expression of CD25 using a magnetic bead-based isolation kit (MiltenyiBiotec, Germany) per the manufacturer’s instructions. The CD4+CD25– fraction was used as Teff cells. Prior to co-plating Tregs and Teffs, the Teff cells were labeled with carboxyfluorescein diacetate succinimidyl ester (CFSE, 5 µM, Invitrogen, USA) in PBS at 37 °C for 10 min. CFSE stock solutions were diluted in DMSO prior to use. Samples were then placed in RPMI + 10% FCS and incubated on ice for 10 min and then washed 2 times. Treg or Teff cells were cultured for 48 h after each stimulation. Tregs and Teffs mixtures (ratio of Tregs to Teffs was as close as possible to 1:3) or control samples consisting of Teffs alone were then incubated with anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 antibodies (both 1 µg/ml, both from BD Pharmingen, USA) in a 37 °C incubator for 48 h. The proliferation of CFSE labeled Teffs was subsequently evaluated by a flow cytometric quantification of CFSE dilutional staining.

Adoptive transfer of non-treated or β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice

nTreg cells were isolated from the spleens of normal mice using magnetic beads (MiltenyiBiotec, Germany). After culturing for 48 h, the cells were infected with lentiviral vectors expressing β-Arr2. 72 h later, non-treated or β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells were then centrifuged to remove the medium, resuspended in sterile D-PBS and intravenously injected into the tail base of CIA mice on day 40 after primary immunization. On day 56 after primary immunization, the CIA mice were sacrificed for the subsequent experiments. Hind paw thickness and ankle joint width were measured by microcalliper before mice were killed on the 56th day after the first immunization.

Histopathology

Ankle joints of mice were removed after sacrifice, fixed in 4% phosphate-buffered formalin for 3 days, and decalcified in 10% EDTA for 15 days. Joint Sect. (15 μm) were prepared in a freezing microtome (Leica CM1900–1–1, Germany) and stained with H&E.

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis

Total RNA of mouse ankle joints or iTreg cells was extracted with Trizol reagent (Life Technologies Inc, USA). RNA was synthesized into cDNA using M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen, USA) and PCR amplification was performed using Rotor-gene 3000 real-time cycle instrument (Corbett Research, Australia). Primers were as follows: IL-22, 5’-GGCCAGCCTTGCAGATAACA-3’, and 5’-GCTGATGTGACAGGAGCTGA-3’ (NM_016971.2); IL-17, 5’-GCTCCAGAAGGCCCTCAGA-3’, and 5’-AGCTTTCCCTCCGCATTGA-3’ (NM_010552.3); IL-10, 5’-GGACAACATACTGCTAACCGAC-3’, and 5’-TGGATCATTTCCGATAAGGCTTG-3’ (NM_010548.2); TGF-β, 5’-GATACGCCTGAGTGGCTGTC-3’, and 5’-GCTGATCCCGTTGATTTCC-3’ (NM_011577.1); collagen type I (Col1) α1, 5’-TGCCTGGCGGACTTCCTGGT-3’, and 5’-CCGGCACCTGGCTTAGGTGG-3’ (NM_007742.3); Col1α2, 5’-ATGCCGGTCGACCTGGGGAA-3’, and 5’-GTCCAGCTGGGCCGATTGGG-3’; (NM_007743.2); Osteocalcin, 5’-TTCTGCTCACTCTGCTGACC-3’, and 5’-TATTGCCCTCCTGCTTGGAC-3’ (NM_007541.2); alkaline phosphatase (ALP), 5’-TTAAGGGCCAGCTACACCAC-3’, and 5’-CCTTCACGCCACACAAGTAG-3’ (NM_007431.2); tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP), 5’-CCAATGCCAAAGAGATCGCC-3’, and 5’-TCTGTGCAGAGACGTTGCCAAG-3’ (NM_001102404.1); cathepsin K (CatK), 5’-GACGCAGCGATGCTAACTAA-3’, and 5’-CCAGCACAGAGT-CCACAACT-3’ (NM_007802.4); calcitonin receptor (CTR), 5’-TCAGGAACCACGGAATCCTC-3’, and 5’-ACATTC-AAGCGGATGCGTCT-3’ (NM_007588.2); matrix metallopeptidase (MMP)-9, 5’-CTGGACAGCCAGACACTAAAG-3’, and 5’-CTCGCGGCAAGTCTTCAGAG-3’ (NM_013599.4); β-actin, 5’-CTGTCCCTGTATGCCTCTG-3’, and 5’-ATGTCACGCACGATTTCC-3’ (NM_007393.5). β-actin was used as an endogenous standard. The 2−ΔΔCt method was used to calculate each gene expression [29].

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from iTreg cells, which were homogenized in lysis buffer. The supernatants were obtained by centrifuging at 4 °C at 12,000×g for 15 min and mixed with loading buffer, which was boiled for 10 min. The protein was separated by polyacrylamide gel and transferred onto a polyvinylidene diflfluoride membrane. After blocking nonspecificbinding with 5% BSA for 1 h, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary rabbit antibodies specific for β-Arr2 (1:100, abcam, USA), β2-AR (1:200, Santa Cruz, USA), pERK1/2 (1:200, Cell signaling, USA), ERK1/2 (1:200, Cell signaling, USA), and mouse antibody specific for β-actin (1:5000, abcam, USA). Following incubation with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature, the membranes were visualized and analysis of the protein bands was detected by Odyssey laser scanning system (LI-COR Inc, USA).

Flow cytometric assay for CD25+Foxp3+(Treg) cells and CD25–IL-17+(Th17) cells in CD4+ cells of CIA mice

The spleens were harvested from the anaesthetized normal mice or CIA mice which were intravenously injected with non-treated or β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells and the CD4+ T cells were isolated and sorted using magnetic beads (MiltenyiBiotec, Germany). The CD4+ T cells were incubated with 50 ng/ml PMA (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), 1 µM ionomycin (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), and 2 µM monensin (BD Biosciences, USA) for 5 h. The cells were fixed and permeabilized witht he Cytofix/Cytoperm kit (BD Biosciences, USA) and then stained with PE-conjugated anti-IL-17 antibody or APC-conjugated anti-Foxp3 antibody (both from eBioscience, USA) at 4 °C for 30 min, followed by surface staining with FITC-conjugated anti-CD25 antibody (eBioscience, USA) or the appropriate isotype control antibodies. Cells were analyzed with FACS Calibur (BD Biosciences, USA).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The ankle joints from mice were pulverized and homogenized in lysis buffer. The supernatants from ankle joints or cultured iTregs and serum from mice were collected to measure cytokine (IL-17, IL-22, TGF-β and IL-10) level. The serum was also collected to test anti-CII IgG level. The samples were assayed using commercially available ELISA kits (eBioscience, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

All results are displayed as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD). Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software. Student’s t test was used to evaluate comparisons between two groups. One-way analysis of variance was used to assess multiple comparisons among the groups, followed by a post hoc analysis. p < 0.05 indicates a significant difference.

Results

Activating β2-AR enhances CIA-induced β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling in Treg cells

CD4+ T cells were isolated from the spleens of normal or CIA mice, and iTreg cells were induced from CD4+ T cells using anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 antibodies, IL-2, and TGF-β1. As shown in Fig. 1A, we observed that β2-AR expression was upregulated in iTreg cells of CIA mice compared to that of intact mice. The β2-AR agonist Terb (10− 5 M and 10− 4 M) further increased β2-AR expression in iTreg cells of CIA mice (Fig. 1A). To investigate the changes and effects of β2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK1/2 signals in Treg cells in CIA, we examined the expression of β-Arr2 and the level of pERK1/2 in iTreg cells which were treated by β2-AR agonist Terb. Expression of β-Arr2 (Fig. 1B) and the level of pERK1/2 (Fig. 1C) were increased in CIA iTreg cells compared to that in control iTreg cells. Terb (10− 5 M or 10− 4 M) further upregulated the expression of β-Arr2 (Fig. 1B) and the level of pERK1/2 (Fig. 1C) in CIA iTreg cells. The increased β-Arr2 expression and ERK1/2 phosphorylation level induced by Terb (10− 4 M) in CIA iTreg cells were reduced by β-Arr2 gene silence (Fig. 1D and E).

Activating β2-AR on Treg cells increases anti-inflammatory effect via β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling

We further examined whether the β2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK1/2 pathway is involved in the function of Treg cells in CIA. We applied the β2-AR agonist Terb (10− 4 M) and silencing or overexpressing β-Arr2 gene or the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 (10− 6 M) to iTreg cells that were activated and polarized from CD4+ T cells using various antibodies and cytokines. TGF-β gene expression and secretion and IL-10 gene expression in iTreg cells were not influenced in CIA mice compared to that in intact mice (Fig. 2A and B). IL-10 secretion in iTreg cells was upregulated in CIA mice compared to that in intact mice (Fig. 2B). The β2-AR agonist Terb further increased TGF-β and IL-10 expression and secretion in iTreg cells of CIA mice (Fig. 2A and B). The increased TGF-β and IL-10 expression and secretion in iTreg cells by Terb was downregulated by β-Arr2 gene silence or the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 (Fig. 2A and B) and was further enhanced by β-Arr2 gene overexpression (Fig. 2D and E). Additionally, we also observed whether the suppression of Teff cell proliferation mediated by nTreg cells was affected by β-Arr2 gene silence or overexpression or ERK1/2 inhibition after β2-AR activation. Teff cell proliferation was inhibited by nTreg cells from normal mice (Fig. 2C1, C2, F1, F2). The suppression of Teff cell proliferation mediated by nTreg cells from CIA mice was downregulated compared with that from normal mice (Fig. 2C1, C2, F1, F2). The inhibition of Teff cell proliferation was enhanced by Terb-treated Treg cells of mice with CIA compared to that by non-treated Treg cells of CIA mice (Fig. 2C1, C2, F1, F2) and this effect was downregulated by β-Arr2 gene silence or the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126-treated Treg cells of mice with CIA (Fig. 2C1 and C2), while β-Arr2 gene overexpression in Treg cells of mice with CIA further enhanced this effect (Fig. 2F1 and F2).

Adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice ameliorates Th17/Treg imbalance

The percentage of CD25–IL-17+ (Th17) cells in CD4+ T cells of CIA mice was increased significantly (Fig. 3A and C), while the percentage of CD25+FOXP3+ (Treg) cells in CD4+ T cells of CIA mice was not changed significantly compared to that in intact mice (Fig. 3B and D). Adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice increased the percentage of Treg cells in CD4+ T cells of CIA mice, and this effect was further increased by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice (Fig. 3B and D). Adoptive transfer of non-treated or β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice did not change the percentage of Th17 cells in CD4+ T cells of CIA mice significantly (Fig. 3A and C).

Adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice increased TGF-β and IL-10 gene expression in ankle joints of CIA mice and this effect was further upregulated by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice (Fig. 3J).

Adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells alleviates arthritic symptoms and osteoclast/osteoblast functional imbalance in CIA mice

In addition, symptoms of arthritis were estimated by clinical arthritis score, which was measured every other day from day 21 to day 56 after primary immunization, as well as by hind paw thickness and ankle joint width on day 56 post-immunization. The clinical arthritis score was continuously elevated in CIA mice from day 28 to day 56 after immunization in comparison with that in intact mice (Fig. 4A). Adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice reduced the elevation of the clinical arthritis score during days 44 through 56 after immunization, and this effect was further decreased by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice during days 48 through 56 after immunization (Fig. 4A). Moreover, the increased hind paw thickness and ankle joint width in CIA mice were also reduced by adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice, and adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice further decreased this effect (Fig. 4B). The histopathology of ankle joints showed synovial hyperplasia and cartilage destruction in CIA mice as well as bone erosion in CIA mice and adoptive transfer of non-treated or β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice relieved these effects (Fig. 4E). Furthermore, CIA mice showed a dramatic increase in anti-CII IgG antibody level in serum on day 56 post-immunization compared with intact mice (Fig. 4F). In this study, we used real-time PCR to evaluate the expression levels of osteoclast and osteoblast-specific genes in CIA mice. Expression of osteoclast-specific genes, TRAP, CatK, CTR and MMP-9 in joints was elevated in CIA mice compared to that in the intact mice (Fig. 4C). Discussion

Treg cells expressed β2-AR both under normal condition and during the occurrence of CIA, and the β2-AR expression on Tregs was upregulated in CIA condition in this study. Catecholamines, including epinephrine and norepinephrine, interact with ARs on immune cells, particularly β2-AR, which can influence the function of immune cells and the secretion of cytokines in both healthy and pathological states [30]. Previous research has shown that the expression of β2-AR gene in T cells is variable, with Th1 cells expressing β2-AR and Th2 cells not expressing it [31–33]. Here, we provide new evidence for the expression of β2-AR on Treg cells. In this study, the results that β2-AR expression was upregulated in iTreg cells of CIA mice and the β2-AR agonist Terb further increased this effect indicated that β2-AR on Treg cells is activated in CIA condition.

In addition, research has shown that β-Arr2, which serves as an adaptive ligand for β2-AR, is widely expressed in most types of cells, including immune cells. When β2-AR is activated, β-Arr2 binds rapidly to the receptor, which can lead to receptor desensitization and endocytosis and the activation of downstream signaling pathways such as MAPK and ERK1/2 [34, 35]. Previous studies have shown that under disease or physiological condition, β-Arr2 molecules can be expressed in different cells and exhibit an anti-inflammatory effect [36–38]. We found that β-Arr2 expression and pERK1/2 level were increased in CIA iTreg cells and Terb further upregulated these effects, while these Terb-induced effects in CIA iTreg cells were reduced by β-Arr2 gene silence. These results fully demonstrated that activating β2-AR enhances CIA-induced β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling in Treg cells.

Treg cells can suppress most immune activities by expressing anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β [39, 40]. Some research has confirmed that Teff cells in an inflammatory environment are resistant to Treg cell-mediated inhibition [41]. Through the introduction of exogenous Treg cells, Teff cell proliferation can be reduced. IL-10 and TGF-β are the main cytokines produced by Treg cells to inhibit the proliferation and differentiation of Teff cells [8]. Further investigation showed that activating β2-AR with the agonist Terb in Tregs upregulated Treg cell-related cytokines, TGF-β and IL-10 production and increased the suppression of Teff cell proliferation mediated by Tregs from CIA mice, and these Terb-induced effects were downregulated by β-Arr2 gene silence or the ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 and further enhanced by β-Arr2 gene overexpression. These findings suggest that activating β2-AR on Treg cells, enhances Treg cell function and thereby has an anti-inflammatory property in CIA. Here, we propose that activating β2-AR-β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling in Treg cells enhances the cell anti-inflammatory response in CIA mice. Thus, an agonist or activator of β2-AR-β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling may be helpful for alleviation of RA.

Th17/Treg imbalance is observed in the development of RA [5, 16, 42] and regulating this balance has become a new target for RA treatment [43–45]. Based on these in vitro results, we wondered if it would be possible to alleviate joint inflammation signs and Th17/Treg imbalance in CIA mice by administering Treg cells overexpressing the β-Arr2 gene. It was found that adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice downregulated differentiation and function of Th17 cells in CIA and this effect was further inhibited by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice in this study. These changes suggest that adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice inhibits the activity of Th17 cells and adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice further suppresses the activity of Th17 cells. In addition, adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice enhanced CIA-induced TGF-β and IL-10 production, and adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice further enhanced these effects in mice with CIA. These results revealed that adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice increased Treg cell activity in CIA mice and adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice further enhanced these effects in mice with CIA. Collectively, adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice alleviates Th17/Treg imbalance induced by CIA in the joints.

Adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice downregulated CIA-induced clinical score, ankle joint and rear paw thickness, synovial hyperplasia and cartilage destruction and the elevated level of anti-CII IgG in serum and these CIA-induced effects were further suppressed by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice. These results show that adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice alleviates arthritic symptoms in mice with CIA. In addition to inhibiting inflammatory T cell responses, Treg cells can also directly maintain bone balance by inhibiting the differentiation of osteoclasts and inhibiting TNF-mediated bone destruction locally and systemically [46]. The elevated expression of osteoclast-specific genes in joints of CIA mice was downregulated by adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice, and this effect was further inhibited by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice. The decreased expression of osteoblast-specific genes in CIA joints was upregulated by adoptive transfer of non-treated Treg cells into CIA mice, and this effect was further increased by adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice. These results suggest that adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice can effectively ameliorate osteoclast/osteoblast functional imbalance and inhibit the destruction of joints in CIA mice.

Conclusions

Treg cells express β2-AR and β2-AR expression on Treg cells is upregulated in CIA condition. Activating β2-AR enhances CIA-induced β-Arr2-ERK1/2 signaling in Treg cells. Silencing β-Arr2 gene or inhibiting ERK1/2 activity in CIA Treg cells reduces the effect of β2-AR activation on the cells, and β-Arr2 overexpression in CIA Treg cells enhances this effect. Adoptive transfer of β-Arr2-overexpressed Treg cells into CIA mice ameliorate arthritic symptoms, Th17/Treg and osteoclast/osteoblast functional imbalance. These findings that β2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK signaling in Treg cells contributes to alleviation of inflammatory responses and symptoms provide a novel insight into the role of β2-AR/β-Arr2/ERK signaling of Treg cells in induction or prevention of RA or CIA and could be applied for new treatment in some autoimmune diseases.

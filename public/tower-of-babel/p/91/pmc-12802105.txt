A scoping review of breakthrough cancer pain: Multidimensional patient needs and influencing factors
Authors: Qingyi Li, Yanlei Sheng, Xinyu Liu, Jie Li, Lisi Zhu, Yang Yang, Luhong Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesBreakthrough cancer pain (BTcP), a common complication among cancer patients, has been confirmed to have extensive impacts on patients' lives. However, existing research remains fragmented, lacking a systematic theoretical framework to integrate these findings. This scoping review aimed to systematically examine the multidimensional needs and influencing factors of BTcP patients based on Kolcaba's Comfort Theory, construct a conceptual mapping of BTcP needs and influences, and explore the theoretical foundations and practical pathways of related interventions.MethodsUsing a scoping review methodology, we retrieved literature on BTcP impact studies from databases including PubMed, MEDLINE, CINAHL, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, AMED, and Google Scholar, deadline for searching the database is January 2025. We qualitatively integrated existing research results using the Comfort Theory's four dimensions (physical, psychological, social, environmental) and three stages (needs assessment, intervention implementation, effect evaluation) as an organizational framework.ResultsA total of 30 studies were included. The findings revealed that BTcP patients' needs are multidimensional, with interactions between different dimensions forming a complex network of needs. Eleven key characteristic categories of BTcP impacts were extracted. Within the Comfort Theory's four-dimensional framework: Physical comfort dimension primarily addressed functional impacts, sleep influences, and treatment experiences; Psychological comfort dimension encompassed psychological impacts, quality of life (psychological aspects), and psychological treatment barriers; Social comfort dimension focused on work and social activity impacts, economic influences, caregiver burden, and communication challenges; Environmental comfort dimension included system support needs and related treatment barriers, Additionally, two intervention studies were discovered, providing preliminary evidence for BTcP management under the Comfort Theory's three-stage framework.ConclusionsThis study is the first to systematically integrate BTcP impact factor research using Comfort Theory, constructing a multidimensional conceptual map of BTcP influences. The findings reveal that BTcP impacts extend far beyond pain itself, involving various aspects of patient life. The Comfort Theory framework facilitates comprehensive assessment and intervention of BTcP in clinical practice. Future research should focus on developing and evaluating interventions addressing comfort needs across different dimensions.

Introduction

Cancer pain is one of the most common and intolerable symptoms among cancer patients, with a prevalence ranging from 54.6% to 69% in patients with advanced, metastatic, and end-stage cancer.1,2 Within this pain spectrum, breakthrough cancer pain (BTcP) represents a unique clinical entity characterized by brief yet intense pain episodes that occur even when background pain is effectively controlled. The Association for Palliative Medicine of Great Britain and Ireland (APM) defines BTcP as "a transient exacerbation of pain that occurs either spontaneously or in relation to a specific predictable or unpredictable trigger, despite relatively stable and adequately controlled background pain".3

Epidemiological data indicates that approximately 50–75% of cancer pain patients experience BTcP.4,5 This pain is typically characterized by sudden onset, high intensity (usually 7–8/10 on the pain scale),6 and transient duration (averaging 30–60 minutes), often manifesting as sharp, burning, or stabbing sensations. Patients experience an average of 2–3 episodes daily, reaching peak intensity within 5–10 minutes. BTcP can be classified into two categories: incident-induced and spontaneous, significantly disrupting patients' daily activities, work, sleep, and emotional states.6 The impact of BTcP on patients' lives is comprehensive, leading to substantial functional impairment, mobility limitations, social isolation, and psychological distress, significantly reducing quality of life.7,8 Unrelieved BTcP may also compel patients to discontinue potentially curative treatments, thereby jeopardizing survival,9 while simultaneously increasing medical resource utilization and associated costs.10 In China, research demonstrates that 53.41% of cancer patients experience more than three BTcP episodes within 24 hours, with over half of patients reporting low satisfaction with pain relief,11 indicating significant deficiencies in BTcP management.

Despite the critical importance of BTcP management, clinical practice continues to face numerous challenges. BTcP characteristics demonstrate significant individual heterogeneity, influenced by multiple factors;12 potential variations in etiology, comorbidities, and pathophysiological mechanisms;13 cultural factors affecting pain expression and assessment difficulties in special populations further increase management complexity.14,15 In China, patients harbor reservations about opioid medications, and healthcare professionals lack unified guidelines for assessment and management strategies.15 Currently, BTcP management primarily focuses on pharmacological treatment, but a single pain control model fails to address patients' comprehensive needs. The International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) proposed a multidimensional pain assessment framework that emphasizes the multidimensionality of pain experiences, necessitating an approach that extends beyond mere physiological dimensions to incorporate psychological, social, and environmental factors.16 The Comfort Theory provides a more comprehensive theoretical framework, expanding patient needs across four dimensions: physical, psychological, sociocultural, and environmental. It advocates a strategic shift from simple pain control to holistic comfort enhancement.17,18 The theory conceptualizes comfort as an immediate state, manifested through experiences of relief, ease, and transcendence across four contextual domains.19,20 Through the lens of Comfort Theory, BTcP is no longer merely a symptom to be eliminated, but a multidimensional challenge affecting patients' overall comfort state.

Based on the aforementioned background, this study aims to systematically explore the multidimensional needs of BTcP patients and factors influencing their comfort by integrating the Comfort Theory framework with the IASP multidimensional pain assessment model. The research seeks to provide theoretical foundations for comprehensive clinical intervention strategies. By identifying comfort gaps across four dimensions—physical, psychological, sociocultural, and environmental—for BTcP patients, this study aims to transform BTcP management from a narrow symptom-control approach to a holistic, multidimensional comfort enhancement pathway. The ultimate goal is to improve patients' quality of life and disease experience.

This study aims to systematically explore the multidimensional needs and influencing factors of BTcP patients based on Kolcaba's Comfort Theory framework, providing theoretical foundations for developing holistic and multidimensional BTcP management strategies. Specific objectives include: (1) Identifying and categorizing the multidimensional needs characteristics of BTcP patients across the four dimensions of Comfort Theory (physical, psychological, sociocultural, and environmental); (2) Analyzing the key factors influencing BTcP patients' comfort experiences.

Methods

This study employs a scoping review methodology, strictly adhering to the guidelines of the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI).21 The selection of the JBI framework is based on its status as an advanced guideline building upon the collective work of Arksey and O'Malley22 and Levac et al.,23 thus presenting the most methodologically robust framework for scoping reviews.24 Consistent with the JBI framework, this study's reporting follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) checklist.25

Search strategy and paper selection

Initial searches were conducted in MEDLINE (EBSCO) and CINAHL to preliminarily identify potential search terms by rapidly reviewing article titles and abstracts. After refining the search terms, a comprehensive second-stage search was performed. On January 4, 2025, a formal search was conducted across eight databases: PubMed, MEDLINE (EBSCO), CINAHL, Web of Science, Scopus, AMED, Embase, and Google Scholar. Following the comprehensive search, reference lists of included literature were quickly reviewed to ensure no potentially relevant studies were overlooked. Chinese databases were also searched to obtain evidence from the Chinese context. The search strategy is outlined in Appendix A. Retrieved literature was imported into Endnote X9 and deduplicated. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts, excluding irrelevant literature. Subsequently, the review team independently examined the full texts of potentially relevant articles. Any discrepancies during the screening process were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer. The search results and literature screening process were documented in a PRISMA-ScR flow chart, with explicit reasons for exclusion noted. A narrative description consistent with the flow chart was prepared to illustrate the selection process.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Population: Patients with confirmed cancer diagnosis; (2) Context: No geographical or environmental restrictions, includes all settings primarily focused on promoting, restoring, or maintaining health; (3) Content: Comprehensive review of BTcP in cancer patients; (4) No restrictions on research design. Both English- and Chinese-language literature were included. Rationale for language selection: The research team is proficient in both English and Chinese. (5) International mainstream databases predominantly feature English-language literature. English-language search maximizes comprehensive coverage of relevant research. Exclusion Criteria: conference abstracts, Duplicate publications, studies with incomplete or unavailable data.

Data extraction

The data extraction for this study was conducted by two nursing graduate students who had completed evidence-based research courses and possessed extensive research experience. The data extraction process followed a standardized methodology, systematically recording included literature information in an Excel spreadsheet. Extracted literature details encompassed key information including authors, publication year, country, study design, study participants, study location, research objectives, assessment tools, primary and secondary research indicators, and study results. To ensure the accuracy and comprehensiveness of data extraction, a second reviewer independently verified the extracted data. This dual-review approach aimed to minimize potential bias and enhance the reliability of data extraction. Through rigorous quality control procedures, the research team ensured the scientific and systematic nature of the literature data extraction process, laying a solid foundation for subsequent data analysis. The meticulous approach to data extraction reflects the study's commitment to methodological rigor and scientific precision. By implementing a comprehensive and structured data collection strategy, the researchers sought to capture the nuanced details of BTcP research across various contexts and methodological approaches.

Statistical analysis

This study employed a qualitative synthesis approach. After data extraction, the content was categorized and organized based on the four dimensions of Comfort Theory to ensure the accuracy of extracted information, with related concepts defined.26 The physical comfort dimension refers to an individual's balanced state of physiological functioning and bodily perception, with its core focusing on the stability of the internal bodily environment and the degree of physiological needs satisfaction, including pain control, symptom management, and physical functional status. The psychological comfort dimension focuses on the individual's emotional state, cognitive function, and psychological adaptation mechanisms. The social comfort dimension indicates the individual's adaptation and integration within interpersonal relationships, social roles, and cultural backgrounds. The environmental comfort dimension concentrates on the impact of external environmental factors on an individual's comfort experience.

Physical Dimension

In terms of physical comfort, patients primarily demonstrated needs focused on maintaining functional status and daily activities, hoping to preserve basic self-care abilities and daily functionality.42,48,51,52,57 They expressed needs related to sleep quality, expecting to obtain sufficient and high-quality rest.41,45,48,54 Patients showed significant attention to pain characteristics, yearning for effective pain assessment and relief.42,54,55 Additionally, they had expectations regarding treatment and medication management, seeking to reduce treatment burden and medication side effects.37,52

Psychological Dimension

Psychological comfort needs were primarily manifested in the desire for emotional and psychological symptom management. Patients hoped to alleviate negative emotional experiences such as anxiety and depression.39,41,45,46,48,50,54,56 They pursued quality of life and life enjoyment, expecting to maintain life satisfaction despite their disease state.31,39,50,56 Additionally, patients demonstrated needs for psychological adaptation and hope, yearning to accept their current reality while maintaining a positive outlook for the future.41,55

Sociocultural dimension

In terms of social comfort, patients focused on the impacts of socioeconomic factors, requiring economic and resource support to alleviate disease burden.40,43 They demonstrated needs for participation in work and daily social activities, hoping to maintain social roles and social functioning.50,54,56 Patients expressed needs for maintaining interpersonal relationships, expecting to preserve family and social connections.41,50,54 Additionally, they showed concern for reducing caregiver burden, hoping that family caregivers would also receive support.39,55

Environmental dimension

Environmental comfort needs were reflected in expectations of support from the treatment system, with patients requiring high-quality and accessible medical services. They emphasized medical communication and relationships, yearning for effective communication and respect from healthcare personnel. Additionally, patients expressed needs for coping resources and environmental support, hoping to obtain a comfortable treatment environment and comprehensive resource support.36,39,55

Physical comfort dimension

Involving physiological sensations, bodily symptoms, and physical function comfort, the research findings integrated the discovery that the degree of physical comfort is related to patients' pain nature, physiological triggers, disease-related factors, and treatment physiological responses. Pain characteristics include the nature, timing, and circadian rhythm of pain episodes; trigger factors encompass activities, disease, and diet; disease-related aspects involve cancer type, disease stage, and disease progression; treatment physiological responses include patient treatment reactions, intervention effectiveness, and duration of response.

Pain characteristics influencing factors

The characteristics of BTcP significantly impact patients' physical comfort. Its episodic features (such as sudden onset and intensity variations) often prevent patients from predicting pain, increasing psychological burden. Time-related characteristics (including duration and frequency of episodes) determine the cumulative pain burden, with different impact patterns between frequent short episodes and continuous pain on quality of life.31,32,35,54 Circadian rhythm characteristics manifest as some patients experiencing BTcP more easily during specific time periods (such as early morning or night), with this rhythmic pattern disrupting patients' normal daily routines and sleep cycles, further reducing physical comfort.29

Physiological trigger influencing factors

Different physiological trigger factors affect patient comfort through various mechanisms. Activity-related triggers cause breakthrough pain during daily activities (such as walking, coughing, defecation), restricting patients' activity capabilities and self-care levels.32,34,37,42 Disease-specific triggers are associated with particular cancer types or pathological processes, such as bone metastasis patients potentially experiencing breakthrough pain due to skeletal loading.34,36 Diet-related BTcP manifests as pain triggered by eating behaviors (like swallowing, digestion), directly impacting patients' nutritional intake and appetite, exacerbating physical discomfort and malnutrition risk.32

Disease-related influencing factors

The characteristics of the disease significantly impact the manifestation and management difficulty of breakthrough pain. Cancer type-related risks indicate that different cancer types (such as pancreatic cancer, bone metastasis) have varying breakthrough pain incidence and characteristic patterns.38,42 Disease stage impact is reflected in the changes of breakthrough pain nature and management complexity as the disease progresses.42 Disease progression-related findings show that late-stage patients typically face more frequent and harder-to-control breakthrough pain, which is closely associated with increased disease burden and declining physiological function.49,50

Treatment physiological response influencing factors

Treatment factors also significantly impact patients' pain experience and comfort level. Treatment response manifests as individual variations in pain medication, including drug efficacy onset speed and duration.33,45,50 Intervention effectiveness is reflected in the differences of various treatment schemes' efficacy, which directly relates to breakthrough pain control level and patient comfort.33,45,49 The duration threshold effect indicates that specific treatments must be maintained for a certain period to reach the pain control threshold, and this time window is crucial for determining optimal medication timing and maintaining patient comfort.51

Psychological comfort dimension

Psychological comfort involves comfort related to emotional states, sense of meaning, self-esteem, and mental health. The integrated research findings reveal that BTcP influencing patients' psychological comfort encompasses emotional responses, cognitive processes, sense of control, and patients' ability to mentally adapt.

Emotional influencing factors

BTcP significantly impacts patients' emotional states and subjective experiences. Psychological impacts manifest as fear, anxiety, and a sense of helplessness experienced by patients after repeated, unpredictable pain episodes, with these negative emotions potentially further lowering pain thresholds, creating a vicious cycle.48,50,55 Life quality impacts are reflected in activity limitations, social isolation, and sleep disorders caused by breakthrough pain. Patients report significantly reduced life satisfaction and sense of well-being, often unable to enjoy normal life even during pain-free periods due to the fear of impending pain episodes.29,40,55

Cognitive influencing factors

Cognitive function and communication abilities are significantly impacted by breakthrough pain. Treatment barriers manifest as patients developing incorrect perceptions about pain treatment, such as excessive concerns about opioid addiction and misguided expectations regarding pain relief medication efficacy, with these cognitive obstacles affecting treatment adherence and outcomes.39,48 Communication challenges are reflected in patients' difficulty accurately describing the complex and variable characteristics of breakthrough pain, resulting in low communication efficiency between patients and healthcare providers. This leads to inaccurate symptom assessment and mismatched treatment plans, increasing patients' feelings of frustration and helplessness.55,57

Sense of control influencing factors

Patients' sense of control over pain and life is a critical determinant of psychological comfort. Predictability distribution refers to the varying degrees of patients' ability to anticipate breakthrough pain episodes, with patients who can predict pain onset demonstrating higher psychological adaptability and sense of control.31,42 Treatment experience involves patients' level of participation and proactivity in pain management plans. When patients can engage in decision-making and understand treatment options, their sense of disease mastery is enhanced, anxiety levels significantly decrease, and subjective comfort improves notably, even when objective pain parameters remain similar.55,56

Spiritual adaptation influencing factors

Individual differences and spiritual adaptation strategies significantly influence the psychological experience of breakthrough pain. Gender differences manifest in distinct patterns of pain responses and coping mechanisms between males and females, with research indicating that women may be more willing to express pain experiences but potentially bear a greater psychological burden.28 Debilitating impacts refer to the influence of previous mental health status and coping abilities on pain adaptation, with patients demonstrating lower psychological resilience facing greater adaptation challenges.40 Belief factors are reflected in spiritual or religious beliefs providing a meaning framework and spiritual support for some patients, helping them more effectively cope with the uncertainty and fear induced by breakthrough pain, thus enhancing psychological resilience.55

Socio-cultural comfort dimension

Socio-cultural comfort involves comfort related to interpersonal relationships, family support, social roles, and cultural backgrounds. The integrated research findings reveal that BTcP impacts encompass social roles, family dynamics, cultural contexts, and economic aspects.

Social role influencing factors

BTcP severely disrupts patients' social functioning and role performance. Work and social activity impacts are manifested by patients reducing work participation and productivity due to unpredictable pain episodes, and in some cases, being forced to abandon work entirely. Simultaneously, social and recreational activities significantly decrease, leading to an increased sense of social isolation.50,56 Functional impacts are reflected in breakthrough pain limiting patients' ability to fulfill family and social roles, such as being unable to continue as the family's primary breadwinner, care for children, or participate in community activities. This role transformation not only affects patients' social identity but also alters their interaction patterns with family and society.40,50

Family influencing factors

Breakthrough pain exerts profound impacts on family systems. Caregiver burden is manifested through family members' need to constantly respond to patients' pain crises and undertake additional caregiving responsibilities. This unpredictability increases caregivers' physical and mental stress and sense of exhaustion, with family caregivers frequently reporting sleep deprivation, anxiety, and professional interruptions.30,41 Social support needs are reflected in patients' and their families' increased dependence on professional support and community resources. However, social support systems often struggle to meet the unique, rapidly changing demands brought about by breakthrough pain, resulting in patients and families experiencing resource insufficiency and systemic support inadequacies.40

Cultural factors influence

Cultural backgrounds significantly impact the expression and management of breakthrough pain. Treatment barriers manifest in patients' varying cognitive and acceptance levels regarding pain medications (especially opioids) across different cultural contexts. Patients from certain cultural backgrounds may refuse adequate analgesic medications due to fears of addiction or cultural expectations of pain endurance.39,48 Communication patterns are reflected in the diverse ways patients express pain across different cultural backgrounds. Some cultures encourage restrained pain expression, while others allow more open communication. These differences affect medical-patient communication efficiency and pain assessment accuracy, thereby influencing treatment outcomes and patient trust in medical systems.55,57

Economic influencing factors

Breakthrough pain brings significant economic burdens and resource allocation challenges. Economic impacts are characterized by the dual pressure of increased treatment costs (including medication expenses, emergency medical visits, and hospitalization fees) and reduced income (due to diminished work capacity), with many patients reporting severe economic difficulties resulting from pain management.40,43,48 Resource allocation involves how families balance breakthrough pain management with other needs under limited resources, including prioritizing time, caregiving resources, and economic resources. This resource allocation stress may lead to internal family conflicts and relationship tensions, further reducing patients' social comfort.48,57

Environmental comfort dimension

The environmental comfort dimension encompasses comfort related to external environments, treatment settings, and the overall care system. The integrated research findings reveal that the environmental dimension of comfort includes physical environments, sleep environments, medical systems, and treatment choice environments.

Physical environment influencing factors

Physical environmental factors directly impact the triggering and management of breakthrough pain. Environmental factors manifest through temperature, humidity, noise, and other physical conditions that can serve as triggers or exacerbating factors. For instance, some patients experience increased breakthrough pain frequency during environmental temperature changes or weather transitions, and pain perception may intensify in noisy environments.27 Comorbidity symptom correlation is reflected in the interaction between breakthrough pain and other symptoms (such as nausea, breathing difficulties, fatigue), where the coexistence of these symptoms creates cumulative environmental demand effects. This requires specific indoor arrangements, assistive devices, or environmental adjustments to simultaneously manage multiple symptoms, thereby increasing the complexity of environmental adaptation.47

Sleep environment influencing factors

A bidirectional relationship exists between sleep and breakthrough pain. Sleep impacts are manifested through nighttime breakthrough pain-induced sleep interruptions, difficulty falling asleep, and decreased sleep quality, which subsequently trigger daytime fatigue and increased pain sensitivity. Simultaneously, sleep environment factors (such as mattress firmness, pillow height, room temperature, and lighting) can potentially serve as triggers for nocturnal breakthrough pain. Many patients require specially adjusted sleep environments to reduce nighttime pain episodes, and these specific needs are particularly challenging to meet in hospital or travel settings, further exacerbating patients' environmental adaptability issues.41,45

Medical system environment influencing factors

Medical system characteristics significantly impact breakthrough pain management effectiveness. System support needs are reflected in patients' dependence on medical resource accessibility, continuity of care, and rapid response mechanisms. Due to the sudden and urgent nature of breakthrough pain, patients require medical support systems that can provide swift assistance. Uneven medical resource distribution and response time delays become critical factors affecting patient comfort.48,49,57 Digital management involves the application of remote monitoring systems, electronic pain diaries, and mobile health applications in breakthrough pain management. These technological tools create new medical environments that enable patients to access support beyond physical space limitations, while simultaneously confronting challenges of digital literacy and unequal technological accessibility.41,46

Treatment choice environment influencing factors

The characteristics of treatment plans constitute the patient's therapeutic environment. The classification system focuses on the impact of breakthrough pain categorization and individualized assessment on the treatment environment. Different types of breakthrough pain (such as spontaneous and triggered) require distinct prevention and management strategies, with accurate classification helping to create a more patient-tailored treatment environment.30,40 Treatment intervention selection is reflected in the impact of available analgesic medications, administration routes, and auxiliary treatment methods on patient environmental comfort. This includes factors such as the accessibility of fast-acting opioid medications, availability of non-pharmacological intervention methods, and the portability and privacy of treatment plans, which ultimately determine patients' ability to maintain comfort across various environments.44

Discussion

Main findings

This study innovatively applies Kolcaba's Comfort Theory to BTcP management, transforming it from a single pain control paradigm to a holistic, multi-dimensional comfort enhancement approach. Through this theoretical framework, we identified the comfort needs of BTcP patients across four dimensions - physical, psychological, social, and environmental - and explored potential influencing factors affecting comfort due to BTcP. This approach provides a theoretical foundation for developing individualized intervention strategies, particularly suitable for managing pain symptoms that cannot be completely eliminated.

Currently, BTcP research is primarily concentrated in Italy and Spain, followed by China and the United States. Italian studies predominantly employ secondary analysis methods, exploring BTcP characteristics from different cancer type perspectives, thereby establishing foundations for cancer-specific BTcP management. Overall, existing research primarily consists of cross-sectional surveys, with a relative scarcity of intervention studies, indicating that the field remains in an exploratory stage. Moreover, this study similarly finds diverse assessment tools for BTcP, with most utilizing numerical rating scales (NRS, 0–10 points), suggesting a continued need for developing specific assessment tools. Among the included studies, only two were intervention-based research,44,46 necessitating further in-depth investigation to more comprehensively address patient needs. Bossi et al.'s research60 demonstrated that adhering to European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) guidelines for standardized BTcP diagnosis and treatment could effectively improve patient pain conditions and quality of life, with significant reductions in pain intensity and duration during treatment progression. Lee et al.61 discovered significant correlations between cancer patients' fatigue symptoms and various quality of life aspects, with pain, fatigue, emotional issues, and sleep quality being closely interrelated. BTcP management strategies should comprehensively consider attack frequency, suffering levels, and patient preferences, adopting personalized approaches.62 Research by Zhang63 and Yu et al.64 revealed significant gaps in healthcare professionals' knowledge and practices of BTcP management, particularly regarding comprehensive assessment and personalized treatment. García-Foncillas et al.'s study39 showed that while most patients were satisfied with pain management, some remained concerned about medication dependence and adverse reactions. Future research can build upon this study to further develop comprehensive nursing interventions and promote patient comfort.

Comfort, as a universal cross-disciplinary cultural concept, is particularly core in healthcare and represents the primary clinical practice goal and ideal health outcome.58 Multiple nursing theorists, such as Nightingale and Mores, have explored the concept of comfort in their theories. In 1991,26 American nursing scholar Dr. Kolcaba established a comprehensive comfort theory through systematic concept analysis, providing a theoretical framework and practical guidance for this concept. Kolcaba defined comfort as an immediate enhanced experience generated by meeting relief, ease, or transcendence needs across four contextual dimensions: physical, psychospiritual, environmental, and sociocultural.59 The comfort theory provides a systematic framework for comprehensively understanding BTcP patients' needs. Within this framework, we discovered that BTcP impacts patients' comfort experiences across multiple dimensions: Physical Dimension, Functional impacts, Sleep experiences, Treatment experiences. Psychological Dimension: Emotional influences, Psychological barriers; Social Dimension: Activity limitations, Economic burden, Caregiver stress; Environmental Dimension: System support needs, Treatment accessibility issues. This multi-dimensional analysis helps develop more comprehensive BTcP management strategies.

However, the core challenge of effectively assessing and managing the multidimensional comfort of BTcP patients in busy clinical environments lies in constructing an assessment and intervention framework that is both scientific and efficient. Future research can further achieve the comprehensive assessment of patients' physical, psychological, social, and environmental comfort in 5–10 minutes through the development of a standardized and rapid multidimensional screening tool;65 and at the same time, establish an urgency-based tiered management strategy, supplemented by an intelligent e-assessment system and an interdisciplinary collaboration mechanism, so that clinicians can accurately identify the most urgent comfort needs of patients within the limited outpatient time, and thus formulate corresponding individualized intervention plans, thus enhancing the overall comfort experience of BTcP patients under the realistic conditions of resource constraints.66

Implications for nursing practice and research

In nursing practice, nurses should prioritize the comprehensive assessment of physical comfort. The results of this study indicate that physical comfort is closely related to the nature of patients' pain, physiological triggers, the stage of the disease, and treatment responses. Nurses need to develop patient-centered care plans, paying special attention to pain characteristics, such as the nature and timing of pain episodes, as well as physiological triggers, including activities and dietary habits. Additionally, nurses should meticulously monitor patients' physiological responses following treatment. This process requires nurses not only to evaluate the effectiveness of pain management but also to design individualized interventions that enhance patients' physical function and overall comfort. Regarding psychological comfort, nurses must proactively identify and assess patients' emotional states and mental health. Breakthrough cancer pain can significantly impact patients' emotions, cognition, and sense of control, underscoring the importance of psychological support. Therefore, nurses should establish trusting relationships with patients, employ effective communication techniques to support their psychological adaptation, and collaborate with mental health professionals to provide the necessary emotional support and psychological treatment, thereby improving patients' quality of life. The social and cultural dimensions highlight the crucial role of patients' social roles, family support, and cultural background in the management of BTcP. In nursing practice, it is imperative for nurses to assess patients' social support systems and the status of caregivers to ensure that patients receive essential support while being able to engage in social and family activities. Furthermore, considering the economic factors affecting patients and their treatment can help provide necessary resources and educational support to both patients and their families, thereby alleviating caregiver burdens and improving the overall care experience for patients. Lastly, the enhancement of environmental comfort should not be overlooked. When developing care plans, nurses should comprehensively consider external environments, treatment settings, and the overall care system to ensure that patients have access to quality medical support. Research indicates that the physical environment of hospitals, sleep settings, and treatment options can significantly affect patients' comfort levels. Therefore, nurses need to focus on these environmental factors and actively advocate for improvements within the healthcare system to provide a higher quality of nursing experience and facilitate access to resources, ultimately optimizing patient treatment outcomes and overall comfort. Given that BTcP research remains in its preliminary exploratory stage, future investigations should deepen and expand across multiple critical dimensions. The primary task is to conduct more high-quality intervention studies, with a specific focus on systematically assessing nursing intervention strategies to provide robust support for clinical practice. Simultaneously, an in-depth analysis of the complex mechanisms by which BTcP influences patients' multidimensional comfort needs becomes a crucial research direction, aiming to construct a more comprehensive and precise assessment system. Considering the geographical limitations of existing research, studies on BTcP characteristics and management across cultural backgrounds urgently need to be advanced. This approach not only helps validate the universality of existing research findings but also broadens the overall understanding of the disease. Furthermore, future research should comprehensively examine the multifaceted influencing factors of BTcP, encompassing physiological, psychological, and social dimensions. Through comprehensive and personalized management strategies, the ultimate goal is to enhance patients' overall quality of life.

Limitations

This scoping review presents several noteworthy limitations. First, we did not conduct a strict methodological quality assessment of the included studies, which, while facilitating a comprehensive analysis of a broader literature range, may potentially compromise the reliability and generalizability of the results. The absence of rigorous quality evaluation standards implies that the academic quality of the included literature may vary, potentially leading to biased and uncertain research conclusions. Secondly, the geographical distribution of the included studies exhibits a significant bias, primarily concentrated in specific regions such as Spain, which limits the applicability of research findings across different cultural contexts. This geographical concentration suggests that the research results may fail to adequately reflect the diversity and complexity of BTcP across different cultural and medical system backgrounds, thereby reducing the universality of the research findings. Moreover, this study only extracted and integrated existing quantitative and qualitative results, with significant heterogeneity among quantitative research findings, potentially affecting the accuracy of comprehensive analysis. Differences in measurement methods, sample selection, and research backgrounds across various studies increase the difficulty of result integration and may lead to uncertain comprehensive conclusions. The currently included studies are predominantly cross-sectional surveys, with a relative scarcity of intervention studies, especially those focusing on nursing interventions, which restricts the exploration of causal relationships. Cross-sectional studies primarily reflect the status quo at a specific time point and struggle to reveal dynamic changes and causal mechanisms between variables. Particularly in the complex field of BTcP management, the lack of intervention studies impedes a deeper understanding of effective intervention strategies. Despite these limitations, this study still provides an important comprehensive perspective and foundation for BTcP research. These limitations not only reveal the current research deficiencies but also point the direction for future research, encouraging researchers to conduct more in-depth and comprehensive investigations in methodological design, geographical coverage, research types, and causal relationship exploration.

Conclusions

The comfort theory provides an innovative paradigm shift in BTcP management, evolving from traditional single-dimensional pain control to a comprehensive, multi-dimensional comfort enhancement approach. This theory systematically identifies and assesses patients' complex comfort needs across physical, psychological, social, and environmental dimensions, conducting an in-depth analysis of the multifaceted factors influencing patient comfort in BTcP, thus offering a more precise and comprehensive theoretical foundation for clinical practice. This theoretical framework extends beyond mere symptom relief, emphasizing patients' holistic experience and quality of life. By integrating multi-dimensional assessment strategies, the comfort theory promotes the development of personalized intervention plans, enabling medical teams to transcend conventional symptomatic treatment and transition towards a more holistic and humanistic care model. Particularly in contexts where BTcP cannot be completely eliminated, this comprehensive comfort management approach provides patients with a more humane and empathetic nursing framework.

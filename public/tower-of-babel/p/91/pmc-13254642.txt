Expanding role of artificial intelligence in gastric neoplasms: moving beyond resource limitations
Authors: Hannah Lee, Jun-Won Chung, Kyoung Oh Kim, Kwang An Kwon, Jung Ho Kim
Published: 2026

The widespread introduction of artificial intelligence (AI) in modern medicine has already had a significant impact and improved clinical outcomes. The field of endoscopy in gastroenterology is no exception, where AI has advanced the endoscopic approach and procedure, serving as an assistant and tutor, ultimately improving the quality of the procedure. AI has enhanced the endoscopic approach and therapeutic outcomes by facilitating early detection on subtle dysplastic endoscopic presentation, reducing the rate of missed lesions and blind spots, and improving quality control feedback and detection rate. In this issue of Clinical Endoscopy, Lee et al.1 demonstrated the impact of a real-time AI-assisted detection system, particularly for gastric neoplasms in a resource-limited environment.

Gastric cancer ranks fifth worldwide in incidence and fourth among leading causes of mortality.2 According to the most recent global cancer statistics, approximately 968,350 new cases of gastric cancer and 659,853 related deaths are reported annually.2 Considering the significant difference in prognosis and 5-year survival rate between early and advanced gastric cancer (90% vs. ~20%), diagnosing gastric cancer at an early stage is essential for favorable clinical outcomes.3

Lee et al.1 reported the expanding role of real-time AI-assisted detection systems, especially for gastric lesions less than 5 mm in diameter, even in a low-definition endoscopy platform environment in Vietnam using the Olympus CV-180 processor and GIF-Q150 endoscope setting. The application of AI has significantly increased the overall lesion detection rate per person from 1.15±0.45 to 1.20±0.57 lesions (p=0.035). Similarly, the detection rate for lesions ≤0.5 cm has increased from 0.18 to 0.20 (p<0.05).

In addition, there was a significant reduction in the rate of biopsies performed on benign lesions after AI application, decreasing from 13.8% to 8.5% (p<0.05), thereby alleviating the economic and procedural burden. The application of AI also significantly reduced the benign detection rate from 12.4% to 5.8% (p<0.05), whereas the malignant detection rate showed no significant change (1.5% vs. 1.0%, p=0.32). Limitation of center source for endoscopic submucosal dissection cases on high-grade dysplasia or early gastric cancer may explain the lack of significant change in the malignant detection rate following the application of AI.

In current well-resourced conventional medical settings, compared with a resource-limited environment challenged by Lee et al.1 challenged, AI research has reached controlling endoscopic quality as well as evaluating premalignant conditions for risk stratification.4,5 Specifically, Fang et al.6 reported a diagnostic approach for individual gastric cancer risk classification using deep learning and OLGA/OLGIM. This approach demonstrated encouraging diagnostic performance for intestinal metaplasia (area under the curve [AUC], 0.884; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.862–0.902) and atrophy (AUC, 0.877; 95% CI, 0.855–0.897) in the external test set. In addition, Wu et al.7 evaluated ENDOANGEL, an AI-based system designed for both lesion detection and endoscopy quality control, in a multicenter randomized controlled trial. ENDOANGEL significantly reduced blind spots (mean±standard deviation (SD), 5.38±4.32 vs. 9.82±4.98; p<0.001) while increasing inspection time (5.40±3.82 vs. 4.38±3.91 minutes, p<0.001), indicating improvement in procedural quality. Clinically, ENDOANGEL achieved a per-lesion accuracy of 84.7%, sensitivity of 100%, and specificity of 84.3% for gastric cancer detection.

On the limited epidemiologic basis of relative lower gastric cancer incidence in comparison with East Asian countries as well as a low-definition endoscopic imaging platform, Lee et al.1 presented “Decent of artificial intelligence to real world” with expansion of its role surpassing medical infrastructure hurdle. In the future, prospective and multicenter studies with larger sample sizes and higher-quality datasets would provide further evidence supporting the application of AI for gastric neoplasms beyond resource limitations.

Finally, a step forward from current AI study outcome including Lee et al.,1 future research directions include expanding AI from lesion detection to comprehensive clinical decision support across screening, surveillance, and treatment planning, with integration into routine workflows, enabling personalized risk stratification and optimized management.

Based on the achievements demonstrated by Lee et al.,1 future studies will seek to use AI-based systems to provide reinforcement not just on human manpower but also limited resources with challenging facilities. It would accelerate the widespread introduction of AI overcoming “field” for application as well as “region” for supplementation reaching technically isolated and medically deprived area.

In addition to the improvement in subtle lesion detection presented by Lee et al.,1 lesion prediction including lesion diagnosis based on explainable AI integration, as well as magnified image–enhanced endoscopy, invasion of depth and risk assessment on vascular bleeding represent future tasks that will be necessary to surpass current limitations. An AI-assisted pathologic diagnosis would enable automatic lesion identification, and a prediction model would further advance the field of therapeutic endoscopy with higher accuracy and personalized treatment.

In conclusion, based on the findings demonstrated by Lee et al.,1 the expanding role of AI in gastric neoplasms is shaping a tailored approach for each patient designed to fit right Time-Place-Occasion, that is, appropriate “Time” for earlier intervention with subtle lesion detection, overcoming “Place” barrier of resource limitation and personalized “Occasion” with prediction. This craftmanship surely clothe future AI in gastric neoplasm with custom-made masterpiece garments.

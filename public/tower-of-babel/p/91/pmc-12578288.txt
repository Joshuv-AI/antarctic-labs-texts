Markers of sudden cardiac death associated with the ventricular-paced 12-lead ECG
Authors: Thien Tan Tri Tai Truyen, Audrey Uy-Evanado, Kotoka Nakamura, Harpriya Chugh, Kyndaron Reinier, Sumeet S Chugh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionECG markers are associated with increased risk of sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) on the sinus rhythm ECG. A sizeable subgroup of patients at risk receives cardiac implantable electrical devices, but there are no known markers of SCA risk on the ventricular-paced (VP) ECG.Methods and resultsWe conducted a case-control analysis within a community-based SCA study in Oregon USA (2002–2020; ∼1 million catchment population) with validation in an identically designed study in California USA (2015–2023; ∼850 000 catchment population). SCA cases included adults (≥18 years) with archived VP ECGs obtained prior and unrelated to their SCA. Controls met the same ECG criteria but without history of ventricular arrhythmias or SCA. The discovery analysis included 158 participants (119 SCA, 39 controls), mean age 76.9 ± 11.8 years. SCA cases had a higher ventricular rate (74.9 ± 16.0 bpm vs. 69.3 ± 12.0 bpm, P = 0.05), longer corrected QT interval (QTc; 525.9 ± 49.9 ms vs. 493.9 ± 31.0 ms, P < 0.001) and longer Tpeak-Tend (Tpe; 111.8 ± 23.3 ms vs. 95.9 ± 20.1 ms, P < 0.001). After adjustment, QTc and Tpe were significantly associated with SCA, with adjusted ORs 6.1 (95%CI: 1.4–26.2) and 7.9 (95%CI: 2.0–31.0) in the discovery population, and 6.1 (95%CI: 2.5–14.8) and 3.7 (95%CI: 1.6–8.6) in the validation population. In pooled analysis, the model combining QTc and Tpe achieved an AUC of 0.752, significantly outperforming each individually. Subjects with both prolonged QTc and Tpe had a 16-fold higher risk (adjusted OR:16.2, 95%CI: 6.0–43.6) compared to those without abnormalities.ConclusionAbnormal myocardial repolarization measured by QTc and Tpe was independently associated with SCA. These findings suggest that the VP ECG could also predict SCA risk.

Introduction

A pacemaker is a cardiac implantable electronic device primarily used to treat bradycardia.1 Between 1993 and 2009, there was a rising trend in permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantations, with approximately 2.9 million cases recorded in the United States (US).2 The prevalence of PPM recipients increases with age, from 40 per 100 000 individuals aged 18–64 to 500 per 100 000 among those older than 65.3 While PPM reduce life-threatening brady-arrhythmias, the deleterious impact of right ventricular pacing (RVP) on left ventricular (LV) function, heart failure progression, and cardiac mortality has been previously documented.4–6 Pacing-induced cardiomyopathy (PiCM), occurring in approximately 10–20% of patients undergoing RVP, results from multiple mechanisms, including RVP-induced intraventricular dyssynchrony, impaired LV contractility, and ventricular structural remodeling.6–9 Notably, PiCM has been linked to an increased risk of malignant ventricular arrhythmias, making sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) the leading cause of mortality among patients with PPMs.10,11 More specifically, while the incidence of SCA among PPM recipients declined from 58.7 cases per 1000 patient-years in 2002–2004 to 13.7 in 2014–2016, this condition still accounts for 18.5% of deaths in patients with cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) and nearly 90% in those with RVP.12,13

Published studies indicate the usefulness of 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs) for prediction of SCA.14 However, due to the unique features of ventricular paced ECGs and relatively smaller numbers of those that suffer SCA, there is a lack of such studies among individuals with implanted cardiac electrical devices. From sinus rhythm ECGs several markers have been identified as predictors of SCA, including resting heart rate, left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) by ECG criteria, delayed QRS transition, QRS-T angle > 90°, prolonged QT corrected interval (QTc), and prolonged T peak to T end interval (Tpe).15–18 This study aims to assess the association between key ECG markers—specifically QRS duration, QTc, and Tpe—and SCA in PPM recipients to enhance SCA prediction in this unique population.

Methods

Study population and design of discovery population

Our discovery population was drawn from the ongoing Oregon Sudden Unexpected Death Study (ORSUDS), a prospective, community-based study that enrols individuals who have experienced out-of-hospital SCA in the Portland, Oregon metropolitan area (population ∼1 million). The study design has been previously described.19 Briefly, upon identifying a potential SCA case, a comprehensive review was conducted, including event circumstances, clinical history, and, when available, autopsy findings. Three physicians adjudicated cases to determine if the cause was likely cardiac in origin. SCA was defined as a sudden, pulseless collapse due to a likely cardiac aetiology, occurring rapidly after symptom onset when witnessed or if unwitnessed, within 24 h of the subject being last seen in their usual state of health.20 Non-cardiac cases, including those due to trauma, drowning, overdose, terminal illness, malignancies not in remission, or extracardiac causes (e.g. pulmonary embolism), were excluded. To establish a comparison group, ORSUDS controls were prospectively recruited from the same geographic area and timeframe. These individuals represented a medium-to-high-risk population with a significant prevalence of coronary artery disease but no history of ventricular arrhythmias or SCA. Controls were selected from individuals transported by EMS for acute coronary symptoms, patients undergoing coronary angiography, outpatient cardiology clinic attendees, and members of a regional health maintenance organization.

For this case–control analysis, we selected SCA cases and controls from the entire study population (1 February 2002 to 31 December 2020) aged ≥18 years who had archived ECGs showing continuous ventricular pacing (for cases, obtained prior to and unrelated to their SCA). Individuals with left ventricular pacing, biventricular pacing, His bundle pacing, fusion rhythm, or on demand PM were excluded from both case and control groups to ensure our final cohort was limited to subjects with continuous RVP on the 12-lead ECG. The assessment of RVP was done solely on the basis of the 12-lead ECG. There was no minimum time requirement between the ECG and the occurrence of SCA.

Ventricular-paced 12-lead ECG markers and measurement

For SCA cases, we analysed the latest ventricular-paced 12-lead ECG available in the medical record before and unrelated to the cardiac arrest event. For non-SCA controls, the most recent ventricular-paced ECG was used. All ECGs were standard 12-lead tracings recorded at a paper speed of 25 mm/s and an amplitude of 10 mm/mV. Ventricular rate and QRS duration were obtained from the ECG reports, while other markers—including QT interval, Tpe, and T wave duration—were manually measured using digital onscreen software (DataInf Measure: DataInf GmbH; Tübingen, Germany). The measurement methodology has been detailed previously.21 Briefly, the QT interval was measured from the earliest onset of the QRS complex to the end of the T wave, determined at the intersection of the isoelectric line (T-R) and the descending slope of the T wave in leads V5, II, and others to identify the longest value.22 The QT interval was then corrected for heart rate using the Bazett formula and the Fridericia formula, alternatively.23,24 Additionally, Tpe was measured in lead V5 (Tpe) and, if unmeasurable, in leads V4 or V6 in that order.25 Tpe was then corrected using Bazett formula (Tpe_c). T wave duration was measured from the onset of the T wave to its endpoint, as defined above. Finally, the interval from Q wave to peak of T wave corrected for heart rate (QTpeak_c) was also calculated by subtracting Tpe_c from QTc.

Study population and design of validation population

To validate our findings, we replicated the analysis using data from 1 February 2015 to 1 February 2023, from the ongoing Ventura PRESTO study (Prediction of Sudden Death in Multi-Ethnic Communities), which covers a catchment population of approximately 850 000 residents. SCA cases in Ventura PRESTO were identified using methods consistent with ORSUDS, including case ascertainment, adjudication, inclusion criteria, data retrieval, and definitions. The control group for Ventura PRESTO was selected from the Observational Study of Cardiac Arrest Risk (OSCAR), an electronic health records cohort study comprising 379 833 individuals.26 These individuals are residents of Los Angeles County receiving regular medical care in the Cedars-Sinai Health System, and thus represent a general population receiving regular medical care. Control subjects were selected from the OSCAR cohort by identifying the subset with paced ECGs during the baseline period and randomly selecting individuals frequency-matched to SCA cases by age (10-year categories) and sex at a 1:1 ratio. For the validation population, ventricular-paced 12-leads ECGs were measured using the same methods.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as means with standard deviations for continuous variables and as counts with percentages for categorical variables. Differences between continuous variables were assessed using the independent samples t-test, while categorical variables were analysed using Pearson’s chi-square test. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to calculate adjusted odds ratios (ORs) for various ECG markers in predicting SCA, adjusting for demographic factors (age, sex), heart rate, and history of heart failure. Unadjusted and adjusted ORs were calculated for significant ECG markers as predictors. Additionally, a pooled analysis was conducted combining both the discovery and validation populations. Unadjusted and adjusted ORs were also calculated in this pooled population. Moreover, in this analysis, the discriminatory ability of the ECG markers for predicting SCA was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC). The optimal diagnostic threshold for each ECG marker was determined using the Youden Index (J = Sensitivity + Specificity – 1), based on the corresponding sensitivity and specificity values. A subgroup analysis was also performed to assess potential differences across sexes and history of heart failure.

Ethical considerations

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, Oregon Health & Science University, and all participating hospitals and health systems.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

In the ORSUDS study, out of 8377 SCA cases, 2642 (31.5%) had an available ECG prior to SCA, 135 (5.1%) of which were paced ECGs. After excluding 16 (11.9%) cases due to left ventricular pacing, biventricular pacing, His bundle pacing, and on-demand pacing, 119 SCA cases with RVP were included in the final discovery population. The PPM was implanted a median of 6.7 [Interquartile range (IQR) 1.3–18.1] months prior to the date of arrest. Among 3074 controls recruited from the same regional area, 39 (1.3%) with an RVP ECG were included. Overall, the discovery population consisted of 119 SCA cases and 39 controls, totalling 158 participants, with a mean age of 76.9 ± 11.8 years and 111 (70.3%) males (Figure 1). The validation analysis included 85 SCA cases and 85 frequency-matched controls with a mean age of 82.5 ± 9.7 years and 138 (81.2%) males. The PPM was implanted a median of 2.9 (IQR 0.8–16.1) months prior to the date of arrest. In the discovery population, individuals with SCA were generally older (78.4 ± 12.1 vs. 72.3 ± 9.7, P = 0.005), predominantly male, exhibited more comorbidities, and had a lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) compared to controls (Table 1).

ECG characteristics of SCA cases and controls in discovery and validation study population

In the discovery population, SCA cases exhibited a higher ventricular rate (74.9 ± 16.0 bpm vs. 69.3 ± 12.0 bpm, P = 0.05), along with prolonged QTc (525.9 ± 49.9 ms vs. 493.9 ± 31.0 ms, P < 0.001), Tpe (111.8 ± 23.3 ms vs. 95.9 ± 20.1 ms, P < 0.001), and Tpe_c (124.3 ± 28.2 ms vs. 103.2 ± 24.9 ms, P < 0.001) compared to non-SCA controls (Table 2). Using the lowest tertile as the reference group, the highest tertile of QTc was associated with an increased risk of SCA, with an unadjusted OR of 11.1 (95% CI: 3.0–40.2). Similarly, Tpe showed an OR of 10.2 (95% CI: 2.8–37.2) in the discovery population. The validation analysis produced comparable results, with ORs of 5.4 (95% CI: 2.4–12.2) for QTc and 3.3 (95% CI: 1.5–7.1) for Tpe (Table 3). After adjusting for sex, age, heart rate, and history of heart failure, QTc and Tpe remained significant predictors of SCA. In the discovery population, the adjusted ORs were 6.1 (95% CI: 1.4–26.2) for QTc and 7.9 (95% CI: 2.0–31.0) for Tpe. In the validation analysis, adjusted ORs for SCA were 6.1 (95% CI: 2.5–14.8) for QTc and 3.7 (95% CI: 1.6–8.6) for Tpe. The corrected QT interval using the Fridericia formula also produced in significant but more modest association, with adjusted ORs of 3.3 (95% CI: 1.1–10.1) for the discovery population and 4.9 (2.0–11.8) for the validation population, as detailed in Table 3. In the discovery population, combining QTc and Tpe as categorical variables (per tertile) in a logistic regression model to predict SCA resulted in good calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit: χ²₍6₎ = 9.414, P = 0.152) and good discrimination ability with an AUC of 0.765 (95% CI: 0.684–0.864). This model was significantly better than using QTc alone (AUC 0.708, 95% CI: 0.623–0.793), with a net AUC difference of 0.057, P = 0.03 (DeLong’s test), but not better than Tpe alone (AUC 0.707, 95% CI: 0.619–0.796) (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1). In the validation population, the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.716 (95% CI: 0.640–0.793), which was not superior to using QTc and Tpe individually (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2).

A subgroup analysis within the discovery population, restricted to individuals whose ECGs were recorded within 3 years prior to the date of arrest (89.1% of the study population), demonstrated similar results. The adjusted ORs were 6.6 (95% CI: 1.5–27.9) for QTc and 8.2 (95% CI: 2.1–32.7) for Tpe. Furthermore, recognizing that SCA cases tended to have lower LVEF compared to controls, we performed an additional multivariable adjustment that included the aforementioned covariates along with LVEF. This analysis showed similar results with ORs of 9.7 (95% CI: 1.7–56.4) for QTc and 11.4 (95% CI: 2.3–57.1) for Tpe, respectively.

Pooled analysis and the AUC of QTc and Tpe in predicting SCA

In the pooled analysis, mean QTc and Tpe were significantly higher in SCA cases compared to controls, with values of 521.8 ± 53.8 ms vs. 490.9 ± 36.0 ms (P < 0.001) for QTc and 109.0 ± 24.5 ms vs. 93.8 ± 18.5 ms (P < 0.001) for Tpe (Figure 2). As QTc and Tpe increased, the proportion of SCA cases relative to controls also increased. The adjusted ORs for the highest tertile (vs. lowest) of QTc and Tpe in predicting SCA were 5.9 (95% CI: 3.1–11.4) and 7.3 (95% CI: 3.8–13.9), respectively, aligning with findings from the discovery and validation populations (Table 3). In the pooled population, the model incorporating both QTc and Tpe achieved an AUC of 0.752 (95% CI: 0.698–0.806), which was significantly better than QTc (AUC of 0.695, 95% CI: 0.638–0.751), and Tpe alone (AUC of 0.688, 95% CI: 0.631–0.745) (Figure 3).

Using the threshold of 517.9 ms, prolonged QTc achieved a sensitivity of 52.9% and specificity of 82.3%. For Tpe, a threshold of 113.6 ms yielded a sensitivity of 39.9% and specificity of 91.9%, corresponding to the highest Youden Index. Using the defined thresholds for prolonged QTc and Tpe, and following multivariable adjustment, we found that subjects with one abnormality (either prolonged QTc or Tpe) had a risk of SCA approximately six times higher (adjusted ORs: 6.0, 95% CI: 3.3–10.7) compared to the reference group without any abnormalities. Furthermore, subjects with both prolonged QTc and Tpe showed a risk 16 times greater (adjusted ORs: 16.2, 95% CI: 6.0–43.6) compared to those without any abnormality.

Subgroup analysis revealed comparable results between male and female individuals. In males, QTc demonstrated an AUC of 0.705 (95% CI: 0.641–0.769), with a threshold of 517 ms yielding a sensitivity of 54.1% and a specificity of 82.6%. In females, the AUC was 0.668 (95% CI: 0.545–0.791), with a threshold of 522 ms resulting in a sensitivity of 48.9% and a specificity of 84.4%. Similarly, Tpe exhibited an AUC of 0.695 (95% CI: 0.630–0.760) in males and 0.671 (95% CI: 0.550–0.792) in females (Figure 4). Finally, the predictive value of QTc and Tpe was comparable for patients with and without heart failure. QTc achieved an AUC of 0.618 (95% CI: 0.505–0.731) in those without heart failure and 0.719 (95% CI: 0.653–0.786) in those with heart failure. Similarly, Tpe showed an AUC of 0.631 (95% CI: 0.517–0.746) in patients without heart failure and 0.712 (95% CI: 0.646–0.778) in those with heart failure.

Discussion

Our study demonstrated that in ventricular paced ECGs, prolonged QTc and Tpe are markers that are significantly associated with increased risk of SCA, even after adjusting for demographic factors and comorbidities. These findings were successfully replicated in both the validation population and pooled analysis. QTc and Tpe exhibited acceptable discriminatory power for SCA, with AUC values of 0.695 and 0.688, respectively. A prolonged QTc, defined as ≥518 ms, showed a sensitivity of 52.9% and a specificity of 82.3%, while a prolonged Tpe (≥114 ms) had a sensitivity of 39.9% and a specificity of 91.9%. The predictive performance of QTc and Tpe remained consistent across different sexes.

Our findings revealed that Tpe, particularly in lead V5, was significantly associated with SCA in both the discovery and validation populations. Repolarization plays a critical role in the occurrence of ventricular arrhythmias, with the T wave representing the apico-basal repolarization gradient.27,28 More specifically, the transmural dispersion of repolarization manifests as the Tpe on a 12-lead ECG.27 Although the precise mechanism of this marker is not fully understood, this phenomenon may be partially explained by the subendocardial M cell theory. According to this theory, M cells are particularly susceptible to prolonged repolarization, characterized by a large late sodium current, increased sodium–calcium exchange, and a weak slow-activating delayed rectifier current.29–32 These factors make repolarization more susceptible to prolongation, increasing the risk of early afterdepolarization, particularly under physiological and pathological conditions such as myocardial injury or exposure to IKr-blocking medications.30 Our findings align with previous studies demonstrating an association between Tpe and sudden cardiac death in the general population,16,21,33 particularly in patients with Brugada syndrome, long QT syndrome, short QT syndrome, and catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia.34,35 However, this study is the first to highlight this association in 12-lead ventricular-paced ECGs of PPM recipients. Furthermore, previous research has shown that a Tpe value exceeding 100 ms in sinus rhythm 12-lead ECGs demonstrates a sensitivity of 27% and a specificity of 97% for SCA discrimination.21 In our study, a Tpe threshold of 114 ms exhibited a sensitivity of 39.9% and a specificity of 91.9%, underscoring the adaptation of ECG markers from normal sinus rhythm to 12-lead ventricular-paced ECGs.

The corrected QT interval using either Bazett or Fridericia formula has also been identified as a significant predictor of SCA in 12-lead ventricular-paced ECGs. Our findings align with previous studies indicating that prolonged QTc may increase the risk of SCD by 1.98–5.89 times in patients with PPM.36,37 As a measure of both the depolarization and repolarization processes of cardiac myocytes, QTc is particularly susceptible to pathological changes, especially in signal conduction from the right ventricle to the left ventricle in cases of RVP. Prolonged QTc may reflect underlying LV dyssynchrony or increased heterogeneity in ventricular conduction.38 Additionally, earlier studies have shown that a prolonged QTc was inversely proportional to E′ velocity (r = −0.54) and was shown to be an independent predictor of left ventricular diastolic dysfunction, with an AUC ranging from 0.82 to 0.84.38,39 Patients with RVP typically exhibit a wide QRS duration with a left bundle branch block pattern. Therefore, we believe the threshold for defining prolonged QTc in these patients should differ from that used in normal sinus rhythm. Previous studies have shown that a prolonged QTc exceeding 490 to 511 ms is associated with a higher risk of cardiac death.36,37 In our study, we identified QTc thresholds of 517 ms for males and 522 ms for females, with sensitivity and specificity values of 54.1% and 82.6% in males, and 48.9% and 84.4% in females, respectively. These thresholds could serve as a replacement for the standard QTc criteria used in normal sinus rhythm ECGs (440 ms for males and 460 ms for females) when assessing patients with ventricular-paced rhythms.

Finally, by identifying and confirming the association between prolonged QTc and Tpe with the occurrence of SCA in patients with RVP PPM, our study provides valuable insights for clinicians. Our findings reinforce the predictive value of prolonged QTc in indicating PiCM in patients with RVP, consistent with previous studies.37 Additionally, we suggest Tpe as a potential novel ECG marker for PiCM, complementing prolonged QTc. These ECG markers can support future research in determining the optimal timing for upgrading from RVP to CRT, which has been shown to reduce cardiac mortality and heart failure compared to RVP.40 Currently, the upgrade is based on an anticipated RVP burden of >40%, but our findings suggest potential refinements to this criterion.41 Furthermore, future studies investigating ICD implantation in patients with RVP may utilize our findings as thresholds for prolonged QTc and Tpe to enhance risk stratification and identify patients who may benefit from ICD placement. Additionally, the proposed QTc thresholds could be applied in studies examining and monitoring the effects of medication-induced QT prolongation in patients with PPM.42

Limitation and strengths

There are limitations that need to be considered before interpreting our findings. Firstly, while this analysis of archived ventricular paced ECGs among individuals who suffered future SCA is the largest experience published until now, the relatively low number of individuals with PPM among SCA cases in the discovery and validation studies may have limited the statistical power of our analysis. Secondly, our findings about the association between ECG factors with SCA specifically may be constrained by the limitations inherent in our case–control study design. Thirdly, specific aetiology of cardiac disease, the indication for RV pacing, site of ventricular pacing, and pacemaker memory could not be assessed. Consequently, the true extent of RV pacing could not be assessed, which may affect our findings. Finally, absence of multivariable adjustments for medications used prior to arrest and electrolytes may introduce potential confounding bias. Despite these limitations, our study has several notable strengths. First, it is the first to investigate ECG markers, particularly Tpe, in 12-lead ventricular-paced ECGs obtained prior to SCA. Second, while most similar community-based studies rely on ICD codes for diagnosing SCA, our comprehensive adjudication process ensures a more accurate determination of SCA, reducing potential bias. Finally, the SCA discrimination capability of ECG markers were validated in an independent population with diverse geographic and baseline characteristics. The results remained consistent across sexes, with comparable AUC values, further reinforcing the robustness of our findings.

Conclusion

Our findings indicate that two ECG markers, specifically QTc and Tpe measured from ventricular-paced ECGs, are also associated with increased SCA risk. Further research is needed to validate these results in other populations and to examine their implications for SCA risk stratification in the previously neglected sub-group of individuals with ventricular-paced rhythms.

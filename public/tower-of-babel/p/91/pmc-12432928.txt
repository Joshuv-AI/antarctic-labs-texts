Survival of hepatectomy in colorectal cancer patients with ten or more liver metastases: a retrospective cohort study of multidisciplinary treatment
Authors: Ping Lu, Yaqi Li, Zhiyu Chen, Jianling Zou, Junjie Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPalliative chemotherapy is the standard first treatment for patients with ≥10 liver metastases, irrespective of resectability. This study aimed to assess the clinical benefits of hepatic resection and identify optimal treatments for colorectal cancer patients with ≥10 liver-limited metastases (LLMs).MethodsA retrospective study of 373 patients with unresectable colorectal cancer and ≥10 liver metastases. Patients were grouped into hepatic surgery ± radiofrequency ablation (RFA) + systemic therapy, RFA + systemic therapy, and systemic therapy alone. Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional hazards methods were used to analyze overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). A nomogram was developed and validated.ResultsThree-year survival rates were 39.2%, 18.6%, and 8.2% for surgery ± RFA + systemic, RFA + systemic, and systemic only groups, respectively. Significant survival differences were noted in both KRAS/NRAS/BRAF wildtype and mutated cohorts. For wildtype LLMs, OS and PFS were 39.3 and 13.8 months for surgery ± RFA + systemic, compared to 23.9 and 9.9 months for RFA + systemic. The nomogram (C-index =0.709) identified independent prognostic factors for OS.ConclusionsHepatectomy, with or without RFA, is associated with significantly improved survival in selected patients with ≥10 liver metastases. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings.Keywords: Hepatic resection, colorectal liver metastases (CRLM), radiofrequency ablation (RFA), overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS)

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study demonstrates that hepatectomy, with or without radiofrequency ablation (RFA), is associated with a significant improvement in overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in colorectal cancer patients with ≥10 liver-limited metastases (LLMs). Among 373 patients, those who underwent surgery ± RFA with systemic therapy had a three-year survival rate of 39.2%, compared to 18.6% for RFA with systemic therapy and 8.2% for systemic therapy alone. The nomogram (C-index =0.709) identified local therapy for liver metastases as an important prognostic factor, highlighting the potential role of hepatectomy in improving patient outcomes.

What is known and what is new?

• Palliative chemotherapy is typically conducted first, for patients with extensive liver metastases, regardless of resectability.

• This study provides evidence that hepatectomy, combined with systemic therapy, is associated with improved OS and PFS in selected patients with ≥10 LLMs.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Hepatectomy should be considered for selected patients with extensive liver metastases, and a multidisciplinary treatment approach integrating systemic therapy, hepatectomy, and RFA should be further investigated to refine patient selection criteria and improve outcomes.

Introduction

A multidisciplinary team approach is the context in which decisions regarding the treatment of colorectal liver metastases (CRLM) are made (1-3). Curative-intent local treatments, such as hepatic resection, are essential for achieving long-term survival in selected patients (4). Advances in systemic therapies have also enhanced outcomes through downsizing or downstaging. Patients with liver-limited metastases (LLMs) from colorectal cancer can achieve long-term survival or even cure with upfront surgical resection and multidisciplinary treatment. However, treatment modalities vary depending on the characteristics of the liver metastases, such as number, location, size, and future liver remnant (FLR) (1) .

Historically, colorectal cancer with LLMs was considered unresectable when the number of liver metastases exceeded five, as seen in the CELIM trial and other studies (5). With developments in surgical methods, the eligibility for hepatic resection has continued to expand. However, most studies have focused on patients with fewer than ten metastases (6,7). In cases with ten or more liver metastases, palliative chemotherapy is typically conducted first, regardless of the technical resectability status.

This study aimed to assess whether patients with ten or more LLMs, initially deemed unresectable, could benefit from local treatments, comparing survival outcomes between those who underwent hepatic resection and other local therapies, stratified by KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jgo.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jgo-2025-221/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC) in an Asian/Pacific Islander population. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (No. 1902197-15) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. Adult colorectal cancer patients with 10 or more liver metastases who received systemic therapy between January 2010 and January 2022 were eligible for inclusion. The following exclusion criteria were applied: (I) pathologically diagnosed with colorectal neuroendocrine neoplasms or (II) with initial extrahepatic metastases or (III) with history of other malignancies or (IV) microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H)/deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) colorectal cancer or (V) clinically diagnosed with resectable CRLM or (VI) receiving no systemic therapy (Figure 1). Patients were classified into three groups according to local liver metastasis therapy: hepatic surgery with radiofrequency ablation (RFA) or not (surgery ± RFA + systemic group), RFA without hepatectomy (RFA + systemic group), and systemic treatment alone without local therapy (systemic only group). Data were collected from the electronic medical database. Missing data were addressed using multiple imputation methods when appropriate, and cases with critical missing values were excluded from the analysis.

Ineligibility for resection

In this study, patients were considered ineligible for hepatic resection based on a combination of technical and oncological factors. The “technical” criteria for ineligibility included: (I) inability to achieve R0/R1 resection; (II) inadequate FLR of less than 30%; (III) the necessity of resecting more than two contiguous hepatic segments; and (IV) compromised vascular inflow and outflow or biliary drainage. Oncological factors contributing to ineligibility for resection included the presence of extrahepatic unresectable metastases and the lack of response to systemic therapy. Notably, a significant proportion of patients with technically resectable CRLM may not benefit from surgery due to these oncological considerations.

Major and minor hepatectomy

The classical definition was applied in this study. Major hepatectomy was defined as the resection of three or more hepatic segments, as described by Couinaud. Minor hepatectomy referred to the resection of two or fewer Couinaud segments. Patients who underwent both anatomical and non-anatomical resections, where the metastasis was removed with a margin of normal liver tissue, were included. The classification of major or minor hepatectomy was based on the number of anatomical segments resected.

Follow-up

The primary endpoint of the study was overall survival (OS), with progression-free survival (PFS) as the secondary endpoint. Follow-up began at the initiation of treatment, and the endpoint was death within the timeframe up to August 31, 2023. Patients were followed mainly through outpatient clinic visits, with tumor assessments based on early tumor shrinkage (ETS) and RECIST v1.1 criteria.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies, and continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Demographic, clinicopathological, and therapeutic characteristics were compared via the Chi-squared (χ2) test or Mann-Whitney U test, depending on the data distribution. OS and PFS were calculated via the Kaplan-Meier method. The Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to identify independent predictors, presented as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables considered clinically relevant or potentially confounding—such as liver metastasis number—were included in the multivariate model irrespective of their significance in univariate analysis. A nomogram was constructed and validated based on the identified independent risk factors. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS statistical software version 20.0 and R software. P≤0.05 (2-tailed) was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the participants are summarized in Table 1 and Table S1. The CRLM cohort consisted of 373 Asian/Pacific Islander patients who received systemic chemotherapy: 103 underwent hepatectomy plus RFA, 15 underwent hepatectomy alone (surgery ± RFA + systemic group, N=118, 31.6%), 106 received RFA alone (RFA + systemic group, N=106, 28.4%), and 149 received only systemic therapy (systemic only group, N=149, 40%) (Table 1). Patients were stratified by KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status (Table S1). There were no significant demographic differences between the groups (Table 1). Patients who underwent primary tumor resection were more likely to receive hepatic resection ± RFA, regardless of their KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status. The median size of the largest liver metastasis was comparable across all groups (Table 1). Minor hepatectomy was performed more frequently than major hepatectomy, with 74.6% of patients in the surgery ± RFA + systemic group undergoing minor resection. Resection was generally performed after disease control, with 50 patients (42.4% of the surgery ± RFA + systemic group) undergoing hepatectomy following a partial response (PR) to chemotherapy (P<0.001). Moreover, patients who achieved a PR or ETS (defined as a ≥20% reduction in tumor size at the first follow-up imaging) were more likely to undergo surgery ± RFA + systemic therapy (Table 1 and Table S1).

Prognostic factors and survival outcomes in patients undergoing different treatment strategies

The median follow-up time for the entire cohort was 42.0 months. The median OS for the three treatment groups was as follows: 27.8 months for the surgery ± RFA + systemic group, 20.0 months for the RFA + systemic group, and 16.4 months for the systemic only group (P<0.001) (Table 1, Figure 2A). The 3-year OS rates for the three groups were 39.2%, 18.6%, and 8.2%, respectively. In terms of PFS, the surgery ± RFA + systemic group had the longest median PFS of 13.5 months, followed by 8.9 months in the RFA + systemic group and 4.7 months in the systemic only group (P<0.001) (Table 1, Figure S1A).

Multivariate analysis revealed several factors associated with worse OS. Right-sided colon cancer (HR =1.30, 95% CI: 1.01–1.66, P=0.04) (Table 2, Figure 2B), KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutated status (HR =1.48, 95% CI: 1.15–1.89, P<0.01) (Table 2, Figure 2C), and stable or progressive disease (SD/PD) (HR =1.39, 95% CI: 1.01–1.89, P=0.03) (Table 2, Figure 2D) were associated with poorer OS in the entire cohort. In contrast, factors such as ≥4 months of conversion therapy (HR =0.76, 95% CI: 0.59–0.96, P=0.02) (Table 2, Figure 2E) and ETS ≥20% (HR =0.64, 95% CI: 0.49–0.84, P<0.01) (Table 2, Figure 2F) were associated with improved OS. Similarly, histology type and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) levels showed significance in univariate analyses (Figure 2G,2H), but weak evidence was observed in the multivariate analyses. The histological type, KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status, and efficacy results according to RECIST, ETS, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), CA19-9 and number of liver metastases were all associated with PFS (Figure S1B-S1H).

Surgery ± RFA + systemic therapy was independently associated with better OS and PFS. Compared with the systemic only group, the surgery ± RFA + systemic group was associated with significantly improved OS (HR =0.42, 95% CI: 0.31–0.57, P<0.001) (Table 2) and PFS (HR =0.26, 95% CI: 0.20–0.34, P<0.001) (Table S2). Improved OS was also observed in patients who underwent RFA alone compared to the systemic only group (HR =0.72, 95% CI: 0.55–0.95, P=0.02) (Table 2, Figure 2A). These findings suggest that both surgery ± RFA + systemic and RFA alone are independent favorable prognostic factors for OS.

A nomogram model was established on the basis of the independent risk factors in the entire cohort

A nomogram was established for CRLM in patients with ≥10 liver metastases. Six independent risk factors—primary tumor location, KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status, duration of conversion therapy, ETS, efficacy based on RECIST, and therapy regimens—were selected to construct the nomogram. The nomogram revealed that the factors contributing the most to the prognosis were therapy regimens and ETS. The C-index (0.709 for the entire cohort) indicated satisfactory discriminative ability of the nomogram (Figure 3A). We determined the cut-off values by regrouping all patients into three subgroups (low-, middle-, and high-risk groups) on the basis of the total scores. The low-risk group had the highest median OS (32.0 months). Significant survival differences were observed across the three subgroups, with each group exhibiting distinct prognoses (P<0.001) (Figure 3B).

Stratified by KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status: prognostic role of hepatectomy in CRLM

In the KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutated cohort, patients who received hepatectomy with or without RFA showed longer median PFS compared to those without surgery (Table S1, Figure S1I). Similarly, in the wildtype cohort, surgery ± RFA + systemic therapy was also associated with favorable PFS outcomes (Table S1, Figure S1J).

Regarding OS, in the KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutated cohort, 45 (24.5%) patients who underwent hepatectomy with or without RFA was associated with improved median OS (surgery ± RFA + systemic vs. RFA + systemic vs. systemic only: 22.7 vs. 15.6 vs. 13.7 months, P=0.001) (Table S1, Figure S2A). Multivariate analysis revealed that hepatectomy was independently associated with longer OS compared to no local treatment (HR =0.49, 95% CI: 0.32–0.75, P<0.01) (Table S3). ETS was also an independent factor for favorable OS (HR =0.41, 95% CI: 0.27–0.62; P<0.001) (Table S3, Figure S2B).

In the KRAS/NRAS/BRAF wildtype cohort, the median OS was 39.3 months for the surgery ± RFA + systemic group, 23.9 months for the RFA + systemic group and 20.0 months for the systemic only group (P<0.001) (Table S1, Figure S3A). Multivariate analysis confirmed that surgery ± RFA + systemic was independently associated with favorable OS (Table S4). Additionally, a conversion therapy duration ≥4 months (Table S4, Figure S3B) was independently associated with favorable OS. Conversely, right-sided CRLM (Table S4, Figure S3C) and tumor response to PD or SD (Table S4, Figure S3D) were associated with worse OS in the wildtype cohort. ETS (Figure S3E) was significant in univariate analyses, but showed weak evidence in the multivariate analyses.

Prognostic factors in surgery ± RFA + systemic therapy for CRLM

Subgroup analysis was conducted in patients who underwent surgery ± RFA + systemic therapy to identify prognostic factors associated with the efficacy of surgery. Right-sided colon cancer (Table S5, Figure S4A), KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutated status (Table S5, Figure S4B) and number of liver metastases ≥20 (Table S5, Figure S4C) were associated with worse OS. In contrast, patients who received ≥4 months of conversion therapy (Table S5, Figure S4D) and those with ETS ≥20% (Table S5, Figure S4E) were more likely to experience improved OS.

Comparison of major and minor hepatectomy outcomes in patients with high tumor burden

Patients with ≥10 liver metastases were more likely to undergo anatomical hepatectomy or non-anatomical resection combined with RFA, particularly for unresectable or deeply situated lesions. Patients who underwent major hepatectomy had similar OS rates to those who underwent minor hepatectomy (Table S5, Figure S4F), regardless of KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutational status (P>0.05, no significant difference) (Tables S3-S5).

Discussion

In the present study, for colorectal cancer patients with 10 or more LLMs, we developed a prognostic nomogram for predicting OS that incorporates clinicopathological and therapeutic factors for these patients. This predictive model was capable of risk stratification and identifying patients with different survival outcomes. Moreover, we evaluated the benefits of different treatment modalities in patients with LLMs and a high tumor burden. For the first time, we found that systemic therapy combined with hepatic resection, with or without RFA, was strongly associated with favorable OS and PFS. While the 3-year OS observed in our cohort appears lower than that reported in surgical registry studies such as Allard et al. (8), this likely reflects differences in patient selection. Our cohort included only initially unresectable patients, encompassing both surgical and non-surgical cases, thereby providing a more pragmatic and real-world assessment of multidisciplinary team-guided treatment strategies. Similarly, Lin et al. reported a comparable 2-year OS in patients achieving conversion, further underscoring the importance of appropriate patient selection and tailored treatment in this high-burden population (9). Our study may help support decision making in clinical practice for LLMs patients with a high tumor burden.

The therapeutic landscape of patients with liver-only colorectal cancer metastases has rapidly changed. The European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) consensus guidelines state that the optimal local therapy should be selected from a ‘toolbox’ (10). However, there are still no concrete criteria for specific patient and tumor characteristics. Our study will help physicians and patients pursue individualized treatment according to risk factors that influence survival expectations.

Curative-intent surgical resection is currently considered the only realistic treatment, and can lead to long-term survival outcomes and even a cure (4,11). With the development of surgical techniques, the eligibility for hepatic resection continues to expand. In 1986, strict resectability criteria were established for patients with CRLM (12). Conversion therapy and high-quality imaging studies of preoperative evaluation appear to expand eligibility for hepatic resection (13-15). In 2006, U.S. colorectal cancer expert organizations issued a consensus statement (16) in which assessment of the resectability of liver metastases should focus on negative margins. Since 2006, various resectability criteria have been proposed in the U.S. and Europe (17,18); however, there are no essential differences. Although the scope of hepatectomy has expanded to approximately 80%, it is not possible to perform surgical excision of all CRLM (19). In cases of a high tumor burden, surgical resection is generally not recommended in the absence of sufficient FLR. Deep-seated CRLM is another issue in hepatectomy. With the availability of ablative methods, the therapeutic options for the combination of surgical resection and RFA in patients with unresectable CRLM have increased considerably.

The EORTC40004 (CLOCC) trial indicated that the survival rate of patients treated with RFA combined with systemic therapy and/or resection was excellent (20). In the COLLISION trial, ablation was reported to be associated with superior per-tumour local control (HR =0.09, 95% CI: 0.01–0.74, P=0.024), with substantially reduced morbidity and mortality (7). Notably, 10 liver lesions were regarded as the limit for RFA in the CLOCC trial, and the total number of CRLM lesions was ≤10 in the COLLISION trial. We focused on therapeutic modalities in patients with at least 10 metastases.

In our study, 106 patients underwent RFA, 103 underwent resection combined with RFA, and 15 underwent resection alone after systemic treatment. A high proportion of RFA in these patients indicates that RFA is prevalent. Furthermore, our investigation revealed that surgery ± RFA was the optimal treatment option after chemotherapy. RFA alone can be considered if the patient is ineligible for resection. Interestingly, the OS of patients who underwent major hepatectomy was similar to that of those who underwent minor hepatectomy. Similarly, Liu et al. reported that the survival rates of patients with resectable CRLM who underwent minor hepatectomy combined with RFA were similar to those of patients who underwent major hepatectomy (21). This finding also suggested that the pattern of combined resection and ablation was well established in both initially unresectable and resectable CRLM.

In our study, RFA played a pivotal role in achieving local control among patients with a high hepatic tumor burden. Another promising locoregional strategy is hepatic arterial infusion (HAI) chemotherapy, which allows for the targeted delivery of high-dose agents directly to liver metastases while limiting systemic toxicity. Several studies have demonstrated the benefit of HAI, particularly in initially unresectable CRLM (22,23). A recent meta-analysis evaluating liver-directed therapies found that the 36-month OS rate in patients treated with HAI combined with systemic therapy was 35%, which was notably higher than outcomes associated with other intra-arterial approaches such as transarterial chemoembolization plus systemic therapy (TACE-S, 14%) or transarterial radioembolization plus systemic therapy (TARE-S, 21%) (24). Although HAI was not utilized in our cohort, these data support its potential as a complementary modality to RFA or surgical resection in multidisciplinary treatment strategies, especially for patients with extensive but liver-limited disease.

Tumor downsizing for hepatectomy and/or RFA has contributed greatly to advances in systemic regimens. Our study indicates that a duration of conversion therapy of 4 months is needed. An objective response or ETS ≥20% was associated with a higher resection rate, with significant differences in OS. Similarly, Blazer et al. reported that the pathological response was correlated with superior survival (25). In fact, the duration of preoperative chemotherapy, ETS, and efficacy results according to RECIST contributed greatly to the prognosis of our predictive nomogram. In light of these data, we believe that the tumor response may represent a tool for selecting patients who will not benefit from resection (26). Although obvious progress has been made in the surgical methods, some patients may not be suitable for surgery because of the aggressive nature of the disease.

There is increasing interest in liver transplantation for unresectable colorectal cancer with LLMs in a selected group of patients, contributing to the emergence of transplant oncology and many trials worldwide (27,28). In 2013, the OS rates reported in the SECA-I trial at 1, 3, and 5 years were 95%, 68%, and 60%, respectively, in liver transplantation patients (29). Later, in the SECA-II trial, the OS rates at 1-, 3- and 5-year were 100%, 83%, and 83%, respectively (30). These results suggest that liver transplantation may serve as a new option for patients with colorectal cancer with unresectable LLMs. However, wider societal implications of liver transplantation, such as the allocation of resources and national transplant waitlists, should be considered (31).

Several limitations of this study should be considered when interpreting the findings. The study was retrospective; therefore, the findings were affected by a selection bias. Furthermore, many patients underwent KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutation analysis outside our institution. Detailed mutation information was not available for a substantial number of colorectal cancer patients. Additional studies on liver lesions and randomized controlled trials are needed to investigate the benefits of different treatment modalities in patients with high tumor burden.

Conclusions

Conversion therapy combined with hepatic surgery ± RFA is associated with improved OS and PFS in patients with extensive, liver-limited, and unresectable colorectal cancer metastases (≥10 liver lesions). RFA alone may be considered for patients ineligible for resection. Further randomized controlled trials and studies are needed to validate these findings.

Identifying candidate genes for trichome traits in grapevine through a genome-wide association study
Authors: Zhongyi Yang, Yuxuan Zhou, Lingling Hu, Qianqian Zheng, Congling Fang, Shuyi Zhou, Chao Yu, Yangshengkai Xu, Liufei Huang, Tao Jiang, Yueyan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As the primary organ for photosynthesis in plants, the leaf surface trichomes play crucial roles in modulating light energy absorption and defending against biotic and abiotic stresses. The characteristic distribution of trichomes on grape leaves serves as an important diagnostic trait for cultivar identification, yet their genetic regulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood. In this study, we performed the first genome-wide association study (GWAS) on seven leaf trichome-related traits in grapevine. Utilizing resequencing data from 153 grape accessions combined with two-year phenotypic observations, we identified 201 significant single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) consistently detected across both years, and further screened 314 candidate genes. These SNPs were distributed across 16 chromosomes, with chromosome 16 harboring the highest number of associated loci. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the candidate genes were primarily involved in transcriptional regulation, ubiquitination modification, signal transduction, and cell wall metabolism. RT-qPCR validation confirmed that several candidate genes were highly expressed in young leaves, with significantly higher transcript levels in densely trichomatous cultivars compared to sparse-trichome varieties. Notably, the expression levels of VIT_18s0001g14070, VIT_05s0077g01370, VIT_05s0020g01710, and VIT_15s0046g01010 showed significant positive correlations with trichome density. This study provides new insights into the genetic basis of leaf trichome formation in grapevine and offers valuable references for breeding stress-resistant grape cultivars. Importantly, we highlight a grapevine-specific signal: a significant association interval enriched with multiple stilbene synthase genes (e.g., the VIT_16s0100g00770 cluster), suggesting a potential mechanistic link between trichome architecture and stilbenoid-mediated chemical defense. Collectively, the stable SNPs and candidate genes reported here provide immediate resources for developing breeder-friendly markers and accelerating marker-assisted selection to improve grapevine resilience to biotic stresses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-13158-z.

Introduction

Leaves are the primary photosynthetic organs of plants [1, 2] and play essential roles in carbon assimilation, transpiration, and environmental adaptation [3]. The leaf epidermis, particularly the abaxial surface, contains specialized structures that contribute to water balance [4], defense, and environmental responses [5]. Among these structures, trichomes are morphologically diverse epidermal appendages that can be classified as glandular or non-glandular types [6]. Leaf trichomes can modulate light absorption, reduce water loss [7], protect stomata from ultraviolet radiation [8], and provide mechanical or microenvironmental barriers against herbivores and pathogens [9]. Therefore, trichome density and distribution are important adaptive traits with potential relevance to stress resistance and cultivar identification in grapevine.

GWAS serves as a powerful approach for linkage mapping and identifying candidate genes associated with specific traits, thereby elucidating relationships between genotypes and phenotypic characteristics. This method utilizes millions of SNPs [10] distributed throughout the genome and aims to identify genetic markers significantly associated with phenotypic variations in species through large-scale genotyping technologies. Over the past decade, GWAS has detected trait-associated loci with higher resolution and has been successfully applied in species such as apple (‌Malus pumila Mill.) [11], tomato (‌Solanum lycopersicum‌ L.) [12], pear (P. pyrifolia) [13], and jujube (Phoenix dactylifera) [14]. The success of GWAS relies on the diversity of its analysis panel, reliable phenotypic data, and densely distributed molecular markers across the genome [15]. GWAS has identified novel candidate genes for agronomic traits in economically important crops; for instance, analysis of 153 mung bean genotypes revealed SNPs and candidate genes associated with phenological and agronomic traits [16]. Through GWAS, scientists have identified specific variants linked to particular traits, such as the association between cold stress tolerance and proline content in tomato, which influences yield. In pear, Zhang [13] et al. investigated 11 agronomic traits and identified trait-associated SNPs using a Mixed Linear Model (MLM). Their GWAS revealed candidate loci for fruit peel color, a key breeding focus, and notably, identified significant SNPs concurrently associated with three traits in P. pyrifolia: fruit surface russet furrowing, russet coverage, and russet distribution. Regarding trichome research, extensive studies have been conducted in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. The team led by Radek Bezvoda [17] identified genes involved in Arabidopsis epidermal development, characterized phenotypic variations in rosette leaf epidermises of 310 accessions, and found correlations between specific trichome morphology, distribution, and cell wall composition with climatic parameters of their geographic origins. Their GWAS identified 1,546 non-synonymous SNPs associated with one or more traits, with some candidate genes exhibiting environment-related allele frequency distributions. Similarly, GWAS has been applied to study leaf trichomes in Brassica napus. An analysis of 290 accessions revealed substantial natural diversity in trichome traits and demonstrated associations between trichome presence/absence genotypes and sucrose, glucose, and auxin response factors in shoot apical meristems [18].

Vitis vinifera, as one of the globally important fruit crops, is widely cultivated and highly valued for its nutritional quality and consumer appeal, possessing significant commercial and economic importance [19]. The abaxial leaf surface of grapevine typically bears characteristic trichomes, whose density and length serve as diagnostic traits for cultivar identification and species discrimination. Grape leaf trichomes are categorized into two distinct types: erect trichomes and prostrate trichomes, with their relative abundance and spatial density varying among different cultivars [20]. Trichomes in grapevine play multiple physiological and ecological defense roles. Acting as a mechanical barrier, they effectively deter herbivorous insects from feeding and oviposition [21]. Through their physical barrier function, they also contribute to resistance against Plasmopara viticola, the causal agent of downy mildew [22]. Furthermore, grape leaf trichomes provide microhabitats for plant-inhabiting mites [23], including predatory species [24], thereby facilitating biological control. Additionally, trichomes enhance grapevine tolerance to abiotic stresses. Despite these important functions, research on leaf trichomes in grapevine remains limited, and to date, no GWAS has been conducted to identify candidate genes associated with trichome traits in grape leaves.

Materials and methods

Grape resources and sample collection

This study utilized 153 grapevine accessions from the uniformly managed Grape Germplasm Repository in Ningbo, China, comprising 43 Vitis vinifera L., 97 Euro-American hybrids (Vitis vinifera × Vitis labrusca), one accession of Vitis davidii, and 12 rootstock genotypes. Samples were transported to the laboratory on the day of collection. For each cultivar, three leaves and shoot tips at comparable developmental stages were collected consecutively. Sampling was conducted between 9:00 AM and 12:00 PM. In July of both 2023 and 2024, leaves and shoot tips of similar maturity and without surface defects were selected for analysis.

Whole-genome resequencing of the 153 grape genotypes had been completed in our previous work, with data publicly available at https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/ (Hu et al. [25]), from which 58,926,380 high-quality single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers were identified. For trichome characterization, leaves and shoot tips at similar developmental stages were collected in July 2023 and 2024 from the Ningbo Grape Germplasm Resource Garden (30.26434°N, 121.40831°E). The detailed list of accessions is provided in Supplementary Material (S1). The garden has an annual average temperature of 18.5 °C, annual precipitation of 1119.3 mm, and sunshine duration of 1879.2 h. The soil is brown, slightly alkaline (pH 8.58), with an organic matter content of 5.44 g/kg, moisture content of 1.6%, and bulk density of 2.56 g/cm³.

For tissue-specific expression analysis of candidate genes, the grapevine cultivar ‘Yinhong’ was used. ‘Yinhong’ was selected because it is a laboratory-developed cultivar with a clear genetic background, stable growth performance, and a well-defined trichome phenotype under our experimental conditions. In addition, plants of ‘Yinhong’ provided sufficient and developmentally comparable tissues, including tendrils, stems, fruits, young leaves, and mature leaves, enabling reliable comparison of candidate gene expression across different organs. All plants were cultivated following conventional vineyard management protocols.

Phenotypic trait evaluation in grapevine

The following trichome-related traits were systematically characterized: density of prostrate trichomes in interveinal areas on the abaxial surface of mature leaves (YPF), density of erect trichomes on the main vein of the abaxial surface of mature leaves (YZL), density of prostrate trichomes in interveinal areas on the abaxial surface of young leaves (YYPF), density of erect trichomes on the main vein of the abaxial surface of young leaves (YYZL), density of prostrate trichomes at shoot apices (SJPF), density of erect trichomes at shoot apices (SJZL), and density of erect trichomes on new shoot internodes (JZL). The evaluation was strictly performed in accordance with the Chinese agricultural industry standard NY/T 2563‑2014 “Guidelines for the Conduct of Tests for Distinctness. All trichome traits were visually scored on the abaxial leaf surface (or shoot apex/internode as specified) under uniform natural light in the field, using the ordinal grades defined in NY/T 2563 − 2014; scores represent categorical pubescence classes (from absent to very dense) rather than absolute counts.Scoring was performed by evaluators using the same criteria across years/replicates. To provide an intuitive visual reference for the aforementioned scoring scales, we have included Supplementary Figure S3 in the supplemental materials, which displays representative images of the abaxial leaf surfaces from four low-trichome cultivars and four high-trichome accessions side by side.

Among these traits, YPF, YZL, YYPF, and SJPF were rated on a 5-grade scale of 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9, whereas YYZL, SJZL, and JZL were rated on a 5-grade scale of 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Each accession was evaluated with three biological replicates. These data constitute the graded phenotypic dataset for grape trichome traits (S2).

Genome-wide association study

Based on the previously determined linkage disequilibrium (LD) decay distance (r²) in our laboratory, an LD decay plot was generated. GWAS was performed using R software to analyze the seven trichome-related traits across the 153 grapevine accessions. The General Linear Model (GLM), a commonly used model in GWAS, was applied to assess the associations between SNPs and grape trichome traits. The Mixed Linear Model (MLM) was also employed; it accounts for both fixed and random effects in GWAS analysis and uses a kinship matrix for correction, primarily to eliminate false positives caused by samples with close familial relationships. Manhattan and quantile-quantile (QQ) plots were generated to represent the GWAS results. The Manhattan plot displays the visualization of GWAS results, with the x-axis representing chromosomes and the y-axis representing the LOD (log of odds) values of SNP markers. A higher LOD indicates a stronger association between the SNP markers and traits. Each point in the Manhattan plot represents a SNP. The QQ plot visually displays the difference between the actual SNP LOD values obtained from the association analysis and the expected values from a uniform distribution of SNP markers. Genome-wide significance thresholds were determined using the Bonferroni correction based on the total number of SNPs tested. Specifically, with 5,848,986 SNPs, the corrected significance threshold was calculated as P_ threshold = 0.05 / 5,848,986 = 8.55 × 10− 9, corresponding to −log10(P_ threshold) = 8.07. SNPs exceeding this threshold were considered genome-wide significant, while SNPs with LOD > 5 were considered suggestive for preliminary screening. To ensure robust year-crossing associations, a SNP was retained for downstream analysis only if it exceeded the suggestive threshold in both the 2023 and 2024 growing seasons for the same trait and was reliably called in our genotype dataset.

Gene annotation

Based on the significant correlated SNPs for each trait across different chromosomes, we extracted the SNP positions of the most significant loci on each chromosome and expanded the genome by a 15 kb window from these positions. We then studied the up- and downstream regions of these important SNPs to identify annotated genes. Subsequently, we selected genes within these regions for functional enrichment analysis to identify genes that are potentially associated with the traits. Functional annotation was performed using the UniProt databases (https://www.uniprot.org/id-mapping) or by downloading existing annotation files to enrich the gene functions in the significant regions.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using the Rnaprep Pure polysaccharide polyphenol plant total RNA extraction kit (HuiLing, China), and cDNA was synthesized using the NovoScript Plus All in one 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (gDNA Purge) reverse transcription kit (Novoprotein, China). Perform qRT-PCR using the Trans start TransStart Tip Green qPCR Super rMixS kit (Novoprotein, China) and q225 fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument (Kubo, Guangzhou).Reaction system (10 µL) Template cDNA 1 µL. 1 forward and 1 reverse primer each µL. 2 x TransStart Tip Green qPCR SuperMix 5 µL. H2O replenishment to 10 µL. Reaction procedure: 95 °C pre denaturation for 60 s; 95 °C for 20 s, 60 °C for 60 s, 45 cycles. Using VvEF1α [26] for internal reference genes, set 3 replicates for each reaction. The specificity of each primer pair was confirmed by melting curve analysis. A single sharp peak in the melting curve for each reaction confirmed the amplification of a specific PCR product without primer-dimer artifacts or non-specific amplification. Adopting 2 − ΔΔCt method was used for data analysis, SPSS 26.0 was used for one-way ANOVA, and software was used to plot gene expression levels. The qRT-PCR primers are shown in Supplementary Table 3.

Data analysis

Phenotypic data of leaf trichomes were organized using Microsoft Excel 2021, with results presented as mean ± standard error (SEM). Data visualization and graphical generation were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 10.1.2 and Origin 2024. All statistical analyses for differences and correlations were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) in IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Correlation analysis of leaf trichome traits in grapevine

Table 1 indicates that all traits exhibited extensive continuous variation across the association population in both years, with coefficients of variation (CV) ranging from 25.01% to 82.70%. The erect trichome density on new shoot internodes (JZL) demonstrated the lowest CV values (31.15% and 25.01%) among all traits, suggesting minimal variation across cultivars and years, and indicating relatively stable expression with limited influence from either genetic background or environmental conditions. In contrast, the prostrate trichome density at shoot apices (SJPF) showed the highest CV values (80.64% and 82.70%), reflecting substantial variation among cultivars and high sensitivity to genetic and/or environmental factors. Overall, prostrate trichome (PF) traits generally exhibited higher coefficients of variation than erect trichome (ZL) traits, implying that PF traits are more susceptible to genetic or environmental influences. All traits displayed characteristic patterns consistent with quantitative inheritance.In addition to the coefficient of variation, analyses of skewness and kurtosis provided further insights into the detailed distribution characteristics of each trait (Table 1). Overall, all traits exhibited positive skewness (skewness > 0), indicating that within the population, the majority of accessions displayed relatively low trichome densities, with only a minority showing high-density phenotypes. For instance, the prostrate trichome density at shoot apices (SJPF) and the erect trichome density on new shoot internodes (JZL) showed the highest skewness values (1.256–1.398 and 2.757–3.167, respectively). This signifies a marked asymmetry in their distributions, with the presence of extreme high-value individuals, which is informative for the selection of germplasm with specific traits.Kurtosis analysis revealed substantial differences in the concentration of distributions among traits. The erect trichome density on new shoot internodes (JZL) and at shoot apices (SJZL) exhibited exceptionally high kurtosis values (> 7), suggesting that the phenotypic values for these traits in most accessions were highly clustered within a narrow range of variation. This pattern implies that their inheritance might be relatively stable or subject to strong genetic constraints. In contrast, traits such as the prostrate (YPF) and erect (YZL) trichome densities on the abaxial surface of mature leaves showed kurtosis values close to or below zero, presenting relatively platykurtic distributions. This indicates that the variation for these traits within the population is more continuous and widespread, potentially controlled by a larger number of minor-effect genes or being more susceptible to microenvironmental influences.

The combination of high positive skewness and high kurtosis observed for traits such as JZL and SJZL indicates that most accessions were concentrated in low-score classes, whereas a small number of accessions exhibited extremely high trichome density. Such distribution patterns are consistent with traits potentially controlled by one or a few loci with relatively large effects. In contrast, traits with lower kurtosis and more continuous distributions, such as YPF, may be influenced by a more polygenic architecture involving multiple minor-effect loci. This interpretation provides a phenotypic basis for the subsequent GWAS results, in which some traits showed clustered association signals while others exhibited more dispersed loci.

As shown in Fig. 1 combined with the corresponding frequency distribution histograms, it is evident that the majority of Vitis vinifera and Euro-American hybrid cultivars exhibit relatively low trichome densities. Specifically, sparse trichome coverage is predominantly observed on shoot tips and leaf veins, with most accessions distributed within the first three grading levels.

Integrated phenotypic analysis based on two-year mean values

Consistent with the results from the two-year analysis, the trichome traits of young leaves and mature leaves exhibited moderate to high correlations, with correlation coefficients ranging from 0.56 to 0.74 (Supplementary Fig. 1). In comparison to the trichome traits of leaves, the two trichome traits measured at the shoot tip showed a relatively high correlation with each other, with a correlation coefficient of 0.43.

The eigenvalues of the first and second principal components were 3.09 and 1.52, respectively, both exceeding the conventional retention threshold of 1, with a cumulative variance contribution rate of 65.87%. This result indicates that the first two principal components alone captured and integrated nearly two-thirds of the total variation from the original seven trichome traits, demonstrating significant representativeness. The cumulative contribution rate of the first principal component reached 44.17%, which showed highly consistent positive loadings (0.45–0.49) across the four leaf trichome density traits (YYPF, YYZL, YPF, YZL). The cumulative contribution rate of the second principal component was 21.70%, which primarily exhibited strong positive loadings on the shoot tip trichome traits (SJPF, SJZL), while showing negative loadings on some leaf traits (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Overall, the coefficients of variation for all traits ranged from 0.26 to 0.80, indicating that the trichome traits exhibited moderate to high relative variability within the population (Supplementary Table). SJPF showed the highest variability (CV = 0.80), suggesting the most pronounced differentiation of this trait among the tested germplasm. The leaf-associated trichome traits demonstrated moderate levels of variability, with YYPF having the highest CV (0.70), while YZL showed a relatively lower CV (0.57). This trend is consistent with the PCA results, where PC1 contributed the highest proportion of variance, indicating that although leaf trichome traits co-vary as an integrated module, considerable variation potential exists among their internal components. Notably, JZL displayed the lowest coefficient of variation among all traits (CV = 0.26), reflecting a high degree of phenotypic stability.

GWAS for leaf trichome traits in grape

GWAS of grape leaf trichome traits was performed using both the GLM and MLM (Fig. 3, Supplementary Fig. 4&5). The analysis revealed a high degree of concordance between the two models. Across both years, the GLM identified 196 significant SNP loci, while the more stringent MLM identified 183. Notably, 178 of these SNPs (approximately 97.3% of the MLM set) were common to both models, indicating that the vast majority of the association signals are robust and not attributable to false positives arising from uncorrected population structure or kinship. The distribution of these SNPs across traits was as follows: for GLM—JZL (4 SNPs), SJPF (8 SNPs), SJZL (147 SNPs), YPF (23 SNPs), YYPF (7 SNPs), YYZL (3 SNPs), and YZL (4 SNPs); for MLM—JZL (4 SNPs), SJPF (5 SNPs), SJZL (140 SNPs), YPF (25 SNPs), YYPF (7 SNPs), YYZL (1 SNP), and YZL (1 SNP). The strong overlap validates the core genetic architecture of trichome traits in our population. The few model-specific SNPs may reflect differences in statistical power or sensitivity to specific genetic backgrounds. We prioritized the 178 high-confidence SNPs identified by both models as the most reliable targets for subsequent candidate gene mining. Notably, chromosome 16 for SJZL and chromosome 5 for YPF exhibited distinct clustered distribution patterns of significant SNPs, suggesting coordinated regulation of trichome traits within these localized genomic regions.

Chromosomal distribution of SNP loci associated with leaf trichome traits

To ensure the reliability of the association signals, we focused specifically on significant SNP loci that were consistently detected across both years as key regions for subsequent candidate gene investigation. As shown in Fig. 4 and Supplementary Table 7, a total of 196 and 183 SNP loci were identified as significantly associated with leaf trichome traits over the two years by the GLM and MLM, respectively. These SNPs were distributed across 16 chromosomes, with the exception of chromosomes 1, 7, and 9. The highest number of SNPs was located on chromosome 16. In contrast, chromosomes 17 and 19 contained the fewest SNPs, with only one significant locus each. The remaining chromosomes harbored between 2 and 31 associated SNPs. Notably, two traits—prostrate trichome density in interveinal areas on the abaxial surface of mature leaves (YPF) and prostrate trichome density in interveinal areas on the abaxial surface of young leaves (YYPF)—shared identical SNP loci at positions 5,064,000 and 3,421,666 on chromosome 5.

Candidate genes for leaf trichome traits

Based on the GWAS results, candidate gene identification was performed within a 15-kb flanking region (upstream and downstream) of the significant SNP markers located in gene regions. After excluding genes encoding uncharacterized proteins, a total of 302 candidate genes were screened. Genes located within the SNP loci exhibiting the most significant P-values for each trichome trait were preliminarily selected as candidate genes. Due to the clustered distribution of these SNPs, a single locus may encompass multiple genes, resulting in the identification of 12 high-priority candidate genes ( Supplementary Table 6). These primarily include transcription factors (e.g., VIT_18s0001g14060 and VIT_18s0001g14070, both annotated as proteins containing Myb/SANT-like domains), ubiquitin ligase-related genes (e.g., VIT_16s0100g00690, a RING-type E3 ubiquitin transferase and VIT_15s0046g00990, a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin transferase), carbohydrate metabolism-related genes (e.g., VIT_15s0046g01010, a trehalose-6-phosphate phosphatase), and other genes associated with RNA helicase activity and regulatory functions. Candidate genes for leaf trichome traits in grape

Tissue-specific expression analysis of candidate genes

To validate the potential functions of candidate genes identified through GWAS of leaf trichomes, we employed quantitative RT-PCR to analyze their spatial expression patterns across different grapevine tissues. We measured the relative expression levels of four genes in stems, tendrils, young leaves, fruits, and mature leaves, using mature leaf expression as the calibrator (Fig. 5).

A pronounced tissue-specific expression pattern was observed for VIT_15s0046g01010. Its transcript abundance in young leaves was significantly higher than in all other examined tissues, reaching approximately 3.7-fold the expression level in mature leaves. Conversely, its expression in stems, tendrils, and fruits was markedly lower, representing only 60–70%, 40–45%, and 60–65% of mature leaf levels, respectively.

While it also showed significant upregulation in young leaves, its expression in stems remained comparable to that in mature leaves. Similar to other genes, its expression was substantially suppressed in tendrils and fruits.

In summary, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that three of the four candidate genes displayed significantly higher expression specifically in young leaves, with VIT_15s0046g01010 showing the most substantial upregulation. In contrast, VIT_05s0020g01710 demonstrated a relatively broader expression pattern, with elevated levels in both young leaves and stems. This expression profile likely reflects that young leaves represent the critical period for trichome development and formation, thus genes regulating this process are highly expressed during this stage.

Comparison of candidate gene expression among different grape cultivars

To further validate their functions, we analyzed the expression patterns of four candidate genes (VIT_18s0001g14070, VIT_05s0077g01370, VIT_05s0020g01710, and VIT_15s0046g01010) in six grape cultivars. These cultivars were selected based on observed trichome phenotypes: three glabrous or low-trichome cultivars (‘Baladuo’, ‘Hongbaladuo’, ‘Puzhimeng’) and three high-trichome cultivars (‘Baiaolin’, ‘Jinhuanghou’, ‘Yongfeihong’).

Clear expression divergence between the two phenotypic groups was observed for VIT_18s0001g14070 (Fig. 6A). The high-trichome cultivar ‘Yongfeihong’ exhibited the highest transcript abundance, with an average expression level approximately 4.3-fold higher than the mean of glabrous cultivars. The other two high-trichome cultivars, ‘Jinhuanghou’ and ‘Baiaolin’, also showed elevated expression compared to the glabrous group. In contrast, expression levels in glabrous cultivars remained consistently low with minimal variation.

Among these, VIT_15s0046g01010 demonstrated the most pronounced and consistent positive correlation between expression level and trichome density phenotype.

Discussion

The present study revealed significant variation in leaf trichome density among grape cultivars, which aligns with the multifaceted roles of trichomes in plant adaptation. Previous studies have demonstrated that trichomes can reduce excessive light absorption by reflecting radiation, thereby protecting chloroplasts from photoinhibition. Additionally, in most plant species, trichomes serve as a primary defense mechanism against herbivores by impeding insect movement and minimizing direct contact with leaf and stem tissues, consequently enhancing plant resistance and reducing herbivore-induced damage [27].

Trichome distribution is influenced by plant genotype, growth conditions, and organ developmental stages, indicating complex regulatory systems. Extensive research has revealed a multifaceted network of external environmental factors and internal mechanisms driving trichome formation and development [28]. For instance, the effect of shading on trichome formation in tea plants highlights the profound influence of environmental signals on trichome initiation [29]. In Arabidopsis thaliana, the research team led by An [30] demonstrated that interactions among hormone signaling pathways also play crucial roles in trichome induction. Furthermore, numerous studies have elucidated the significant impact of cell wall structure and composition on trichome development. For example, in tomato [31], cytoskeletal dynamics are critical for the formation of multicellular trichome structures, while research in Arabidopsis has revealed the regulatory role of plant-specific calcium channels in trichome cell wall biosynthesis and susceptibility to powdery mildew [32].

Leaf trichomes in grapevine serve as a crucial physical barrier, playing a critical role in defending against both biotic and abiotic stresses. However, their genetic basis, particularly in this economically important fruit crop, remains inadequately characterized. In this study, we conducted the first GWAS on leaf trichome traits in grapevine, successfully identifying 196 stable significant SNPs and 302 candidate genes. These genes are primarily enriched in pathways related to transcriptional regulation, ubiquitination modification, signal transduction, and cell wall metabolism, outlining a complex genetic regulatory network.

The central role of transcriptional regulation: from MYB master regulators to epigenetic control

Transcriptional regulation represents a core mechanism underlying plant cell differentiation and organ morphogenesis. Among our candidate genes, we identified multiple transcription factor family members that are likely to function as key regulatory switches in trichome development.

Transcriptional control serves as the cornerstone of epidermal trichome development in plants. The most compelling candidates identified in this study are two genes encoding Myb/SANT-like domain proteins (VIT_18s0001g14060 and VIT_18s0001g14070), whose discovery strongly suggests conservation of trichome regulatory pathways in grapevine. In the model plant Arabidopsis Thaliana, the R2R3-MYB transcription factor GLABRA1 (GL1) has been established as the master regulator that initiates trichome cell fate determination and controls the onset of trichome development. It forms complexes with other proteins including GLABRA3 and TRANSPARENT TESTA GLABRA to activate downstream genes required for trichome formation [33]. Loss-of-function mutants of GL1 exhibit completely glabrous leaf phenotypes. Phylogenetic analyses often classify such genes within the R2R3-MYB subgroup 15, which specifically regulates trichome and root trichome development [34]. We hypothesize that these two VviMyb genes are functional orthologs of AtGL1, playing analogous “master switch” roles in grapevine. This hypothesis finds preliminary support in our expression data: VIT_18s0001g14070 shows significantly higher expression in young leaves and high-trichome cultivars, consistent with the established pattern of trichome initiation during early organ development.

Beyond MYB factors, our study revealed a notable epigenetic regulator: a NERD domain protein (VIT_16s0039g01660). NERD proteins are plant-specific GW proteins that serve as key components of the RNA-directed DNA methylation pathway, participating in epigenetic and transcriptional silencing mechanisms [35, 36]. In Arabidopsis, similar epigenetic regulatory mechanisms have been demonstrated to finely tune the expression patterns of developmental genes. Therefore, we propose a novel hypothesis: VviNERD may spatially control trichome distribution and density by establishing or maintaining specific DNA methylation patterns that silence trichome development repressors, thereby offering new perspectives for understanding the patterning mechanisms of trichome formation.

Fine-tuning by the ubiquitination system: the “timer” of protein stability

Protein ubiquitination and degradation represent a central mechanism for regulating intracellular protein homeostasis and signaling intensity. Our candidate gene list includes as many as seven RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligases (e.g., VIT_16s0100g00690) and one HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase (VIT_05s0077g00470). In eukaryotes, the ubiquitin-proteasome system controls signal transduction events through the selective elimination of regulatory proteins. E3 ubiquitin ligases specifically bind to substrate proteins destined for degradation and mediate their polyubiquitination, functioning as precise “timers” that control the lifespan of key developmental regulators [37].

The role of the ubiquitination system is well-documented in plant research. For instance, in Arabidopsis thaliana, overexpression of BrRING509 significantly enhanced the survival rate and root length of transgenic seedlings under varying salt concentrations. Furthermore, ectopic expression markedly increased the expression levels of salt-responsive genes in Arabidopsis transgenic lines subjected to salt stress [38]. In leguminous plants, Li [39] et al. revealed a finely-tuned symbiotic mechanism wherein a single E3 ligase can undergo a phosphorylation-dependent functional switch to dynamically and precisely regulate the protein levels of Nod Factor receptors. Our findings lead us to propose that a complex regulatory network, composed of multiple E3 ligases, may operate in grapevine. This network could finely tune the initiation, development cycle, and final morphology of trichomes by precisely controlling the abundance of key transcription factors, such as VviMyb proteins, or their co-factors.

Signal transduction and metabolic coordination: bridging internal and external cues

Trichome development does not occur in isolation but requires the integration of endogenous hormonal signals with external environmental factors. This study identified several serine/threonine protein kinases (e.g., VIT_05s0020g01690) as central components in intracellular signal transduction. In cucumber, a gene encoding a C-lectin receptor-like kinase, CsTM, was determined to interact with the aquaporin CsTIP1;1 and regulate trichome development. Genes associated with pathogenesis, calcium binding, and cellulose synthase were significantly enriched, altering multicellular trichome morphology and enhancing resistance to aphids [40].

At the metabolic level, the identification of a trehalose-6-phosphate phosphatase (VIT_15s0046g01000) is particularly significant. Trehalose-6-phosphate serves as a central sugar signaling molecule, acting as a key nexus linking carbon metabolic status with growth and developmental processes [41].

Cell wall remodeling and chemical defense: structural integrity and functional implementation of trichomes

Mature trichomes require rigid cell walls to provide structural support and may additionally possess secretory capabilities. We identified numerous genes encoding pectinases (VIT_16s0039g00260), polygalacturonases (VIT_11s0016g00720), and pectin methylesterase inhibitors (VIT_16s0022g00830). These enzymes mediate dynamic modifications to the polysaccharide network of the cell wall, playing indispensable roles in the polar outgrowth, branching, and final hardening processes of trichome cells [42]. Their regulation directly influences the physical strength and morphology of trichomes.

More uniquely characteristic of grapevine, we observed an enrichment of several stilbene synthase (STS) genes within a significant association interval (VIT_16s0100g00770, VIT_16s0100g00780, etc.), highlighting a grapevine-specific candidate module that may connect epidermal morphology with specialized metabolism. Stilbenoids, such as resveratrol, are renowned phytoalexins in grapevine with well-established roles in plant defense and stress responses [43]. In this context, grapevine glandular trichomes may serve as dynamic biosynthetic sites that not only constitute a physical barrier but also support the in situ synthesis and/or secretion of antimicrobial compounds, thereby establishing an efficient chemical defense front. This biological interpretation is supported by functional and inducible-defense evidence: introducing a grape-derived STS gene can enhance resistance to bacterial infection in transgenic plants [44], and microbial-associated elicitation (e.g., culture filtrate of Aureobasidium pullulans) can induce stilbene phytoalexins in grapevine, consistent with a rapid, surface-proximal chemical defense strategy [45]. Mechanistically, recent studies in wild grape further suggest that upstream transcription factors can interact with MYB regulators to promote stilbene synthesis and concomitantly enhance disease resistance (e.g., NAC–MYB modules) [46], complementing the previously reported WRKY–MYB regulatory framework for stilbene biosynthesis [47]. Together, these findings make it plausible that allelic variation at an STS-enriched locus could contribute to coordinated variation in trichome traits and inducible stilbenoid-based defense capacity in grapevine.

Limitations and future perspectives

Although the GWAS and RT-qPCR results provide useful evidence for prioritizing candidate genes associated with grapevine trichome traits, the present study remains primarily correlative. The higher expression levels of several candidate genes in densely trichomed cultivars support their association with the phenotype, but do not by themselves demonstrate causal roles in trichome initiation or development. In addition, part of the functional interpretation is based on knowledge from model species such as Arabidopsis, and grapevine-specific regulatory mechanisms may differ.

Future studies should therefore focus on direct functional validation of the prioritized genes. First, the major SNPs identified in this study should be validated in independent grapevine populations and converted into breeder-friendly markers such as KASP or HRM assays. Second, haplotype analysis and fine mapping should be performed for the major associated regions, particularly the loci on chromosomes 5 and 16. Third, the spatial expression of key genes should be examined in trichome-bearing epidermal tissues using approaches such as in situ hybridization, promoter-reporter assays, or laser microdissection-based expression analysis. Finally, functional assays, including VIGS, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated mutagenesis, overexpression, or transient expression systems, will be necessary to determine whether these genes directly regulate trichome development in grapevine. For the STS-enriched locus, combined analyses of trichome density, candidate gene expression, and stilbenoid accumulation would help test the proposed link between epidermal morphology and chemical defense.

Conclusion

In this study, we performed the first GWAS of grapevine leaf trichome-related traits based on two-year phenotyping of a diverse panel and identified 201 stable significant SNPs together with 314 candidate genes, outlining a complex genetic network involving transcriptional regulation, ubiquitination, signal transduction, and cell wall remodeling. RT-qPCR validation supported several prioritized candidates, showing higher expression in young leaves and in densely trichomed cultivars, with multiple genes (e.g., VIT_18s0001g14070, VIT_15s0046g01010) exhibiting positive correlations with trichome density.

Beyond conserved developmental regulators, we emphasize a grapevine-specific and biologically intriguing finding: enrichment of stilbene synthase genes within a significant locus (including the VIT_16s0100g00770 cluster). This observation supports the testable hypothesis that trichomes—particularly glandular trichomes—may integrate physical protection with localized stilbenoid-based chemical defense, strengthening leaf-surface resistance. From an applied perspective, the stable SNPs—especially those tightly linked to major trichome-density traits such as YPF—can be converted into breeder-friendly genotyping assays (e.g., KASP/HRM), validated in independent populations, and used for early-stage marker-assisted selection to accelerate breeding of grape cultivars with enhanced resilience.

Association between utilization of community- based chronic disease examination services and disease control outcomes among patients with hypertension and diabetes
Authors: Yingying Bao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCommunity-based chronic disease examination services (CCDES) constitute a critical component of chronic disease management. The utilization of these services may be associated with patients’ disease control outcomes. Nevertheless, empirical evidence in this domain remains limited. This study aims to investigate the relationship between these two factors and elucidate the potential underlying mechanisms.MethodsData for this study were derived from the 2018 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS). A total of 6576 middle-aged and older adults diagnosed with hypertension and diabetes were included. CCDES utilization was assessed using participants’ self-reported service use and frequency, recorded as CCDES_Use and CCDES_Usage_Frequency, respectively. Disease control outcomes were evaluated based on the hypertension and diabetes control status of the participants. Chi-square tests were performed to compare differences across groups. Logistic regression models were utilized to explore the associations between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes. Subgroup analyses were performed to investigate potential heterogeneity stratified by the number of chronic diseases and residence. Additionally, mediation analyses were performed to examine the mediating role of medical treatment and physical activity in the aforementioned associations.ResultsCompared with non‑users and infrequent users of CCDES, users and frequent users were more likely to achieve disease control(p < 0.05). After adjusting for confounders, individuals using CCDES had 34.6% higher odds of achieving disease control than non‑users (aOR = 1.346, 95% CI: 1.151–1.574). Moreover, each one‑unit increase in CCDES utilization was associated with a 6.5% higher odds of disease control (aOR = 1.065, 95% CI: 1.028–1.104). These associations were also observed in both hypertension (CCDES_Use: aOR = 1.361, 95% CI: 1.152–1.609; CCDES_Usage_Frequency: aOR = 1.061, 95% CI: 1.022–1.101) and diabetes (CCDES_Use: aOR = 1.473, 95% CI: 1.098–1.976; CCDES_Usage_Frequency: aOR = 1.087, 95% CI: 1.015–1.164) subgroups. Heterogeneity analyses revealed that CCDES utilization was more strongly associated with disease control outcomes among participants with a single chronic disease and among rural residents, whereas no significant association was observed among those with multimorbidity or urban residents. Mediation analyses indicated that medical treatment acted as a mediator in this association, while physical activity showed no mediating effect.ConclusionsThis study provides evidence for an association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes. The findings underscore the importance of proactive chronic disease management, especially the utilization of examination services provided by primary healthcare facilities, as such strategies may help reduce adverse chronic disease–related health outcomes.

Introduction

With population aging, the global disease burden has shifted dramatically. Historically, malnutrition and infectious diseases were the leading causes of mortality, but they have increasingly been replaced by chronic non-communicable diseases (NCDs), including hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases [1, 2]. Data indicates that over 78% of older adults suffer from one or more chronic diseases [3]. These diseases exert profound impacts on population health outcomes, increasing risks of mortality and disability, causing declines in physical function, and reducing quality of life [4, 5]. Furthermore, chronic diseases not only compromise individual health but also incur substantial and sustained medical expenditures, thereby imposing considerable economic burdens on patients and healthcare systems [6–8]. Chronic diseases have undoubtedly become a major public health threat and a significant pressure on healthcare systems [9], with key strategies to address this burden including strengthening chronic disease management, preventing disease onset, and reducing related harms [10].

Chronic diseases typically require a prolonged treatment course. Effective health management therefore relies on the integration and coordination of resources across multiple stakeholders, including healthcare institutions, communities, families and patients themselves. Such collaboration is essential to deliver continuous, coordinated, and high-quality care [11]. Community-based chronic disease management has demonstrated substantial improvements in health outcomes [12]. With the advent of the “Public Health 3.0” era, chronic disease management has placed increasing emphasis on collaboration between healthcare systems and community-based health organizations [13]. The establishment of an integrated healthcare system incorporating active community participation is essential for optimizing chronic disease care pathways [14]. Indeed, the community-based primary healthcare setting has emerged as a critical determinant of care quality for patients with chronic diseases [15]. Strengthened collaboration between hospital and community-level primary care settings has been demonstrated to enhance the efficiency of chronic disease management, while simultaneously reinforcing care continuity and quality [16]. Community-based chronic disease management services, characterized by enhanced accessibility and long-term sustainability, have been identified as critical contributors to improved population health outcomes and reduced healthcare expenditures [13]. Primary care services encompassing chronic disease examination and treatment play a pivotal role in facilitating patient engagement in scheduled clinical visits, which are essential for the effective disease management [17].

Although numerous studies have explored the relationship between chronic disease management and health outcomes [18–20], notable gaps remain in the existing research. First, most existing studies focus on the association between individual self-management practices and health outcomes, with insufficient attention to chronic disease management strategies from community-based primary healthcare institutions [21, 22]. Second, the mediating role of community-based chronic disease management interventions in promoting self-management behavioral changes is underexplored. Third, heterogeneity in chronic disease management outcomes across participant subgroups has received limited scholarly attention.

To address these gaps, this study examined the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes, with a specific focus on the mediating roles of medical treatment and physical activity. Additionally, heterogeneity in this relationship across different groups was explored. The findings provide valuable insights for policymakers to develop chronic disease management strategies that leverage primary healthcare services, enhance community residents’ disease prevention capacity, and reduce adverse health outcomes.

Methods

Data sources and study population

The data used in this study were derived from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), a nationally representative longitudinal survey initiated and administered by Peking University. Since its 2011 baseline, CHARLS has been conducted every two to three years. It employs a stratified multistage probability sampling design: 150 counties or districts were randomly selected from 30 of China’s 34 provincial-level administrative units, followed by three villages or communities randomly sampled within each selected county or district. At the final stage, 80 households were drawn from each sampled village or community. Data for the survey were collected primarily via face-to-face interviews with individuals aged 45 and above from sampled households. CHARLS has obtained ethical approval from the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of Peking University (IRB00001052-11015). The dataset is publicly available on the official website (http://charls.pku.edu.cn/en). Given the research objectives and data availability, this study employs cross-sectional data from the 2018 wave. Given China’s large geographic scale and substantial regional economic disparities, this study further incorporates regional economic variables to investigate their potential associations with the capacity and effectiveness of community-based chronic disease management. These variables were derived from authoritative sources, including the China Statistical Yearbook and provincial economic statistical bulletins, and were subsequently merged with individual-level data from CHARLS to facilitate a comprehensive analysis.

Prior to empirical analysis, participants were screened based on the following criteria: (i) Only participants diagnosed with chronic diseases were included. (ii) Given existing constraints on community-based chronic disease management capacity in China, this study focused on two highly prevalent conditions: hypertension and diabetes. Notably, the CHARLS dataset only provides information on community chronic disease management for these two conditions. Thus, only participants with hypertension or diabetes were retained. (iii) Participants with missing values for key variables were excluded. These procedures yielded a final analytical sample of 6576 participants. Assessment of disease control outcomes

Disease control outcomes were defined as whether participants’ chronic disease were under control, assessed using two items from CHARLS questionnaire: “Is your blood pressure generally under control?” and “Is your sugar generally under control?” This variable was operationalized as a binary variable: participants who responded “yes” to either item were coded 1 (effective disease control), whereas those who answered “no” to both items were assigned a value of 0 (ineffective control).

Assessment of CCDES utilization

CCDES utilization was measured by relevant items from the CHARLS questionnaire. Specifically, measures included whether participants had used CCDES and the frequency of use. For brevity, these two variables were denoted as CCDES_Use and CCDES_Usage_Frequency.

CCDES_Use was measured directly using the following questions: “During the last year (last 12 months), have you had blood pressure examination by community/village doctors regularly?” and “During the last year (last 12 months), have you had diabetes examination by community/village doctors regularly?” Responses were coded as a binary variable: participants who answered “yes” to either question were coded 1, indicating that they had used CCDES; those who answered “no” to both questions were coded 0, indicating that they had never used CCDES.

CCDES_Usage_Frequency was assessed using the following questions: “How often did you have blood pressure examination by community/village doctors?” and “How often did you have diabetes examinations by community/village doctors?” Response options were “never,” “once a year,” “once half a year,” “once every three months,” “once every two months,” “once a month,” “once half a month,” and “once a week,” coded 1 to 8, respectively. The maximum value of the two responses was adopted. Higher values corresponded to greater utilization of the CCDES.

Medical treatment

Medical treatment, one of the mediating variables examined in this study, was assessed using the following items from the CHARLS questionnaire: “Are you now taking any of the following treatments to treat or control your hypertension? Taking Chinese traditional medicine, taking Western modern medicine?” and “Are you now taking any of the following treatments to treat or control your diabetes?” Responses were coded as a binary variable: participants who reported “taking Chinese traditional medicine,” “taking Western modern medicine,” or “other treatments” were coded 1(indicating receipt of medical treatment), and those who responded “none of the above” were coded 0 (indicating no medical treatment).

Physical activity

Physical activity, another mediating variable, was operationalized using the CHARLS item: “Do you usually engage in this type of activity for at least 10 minutes per week?” This variable was coded as a binary indicator, where 1 = “yes” and 0 = “no”.

Covariables

Building on prior literature, this study selected potential covariates for the analytical model, including demographic characteristics and local economic conditions. Demographic characteristics included gender (1 = male, 0 = female), age group (1 = 45 ~ 60, 2 = 61 ~ 75, 3 = > 75), household registration type(1 = agricultural, 0 = non-agricultural), educational(1 = primary school or below, 2 = junior high school/high school, 3 = college degree or above), marital status (1 = married, 0 = separated/divorced/widowed/never married), medical insurance participation (1 = yes, 0 = no), self-reported health(1 = very poor, 2 = poor, 3 = fair, 4 = good, 5 = very good), smoking (1 = yes, 0 = no), and drinking (1 = yes, 0 = no). Local economic conditions included the natural logarithm of per capita GDP and the share of the tertiary sector in the respondent’s city of residence. It is worth noting that there are two covariates that require explanation here. Household registration type related to China’s household registration (hukou) system, which represents a fundamental administrative framework that legally ties individuals to specific geographic locations (hukou registration areas) and grants distinct legal classifications, primarily agricultural and non-agricultural hukou status. Despite a series of recent reforms, hukou remains closely linked to residents’ access to social welfare benefits and socioeconomic opportunities. The share of the tertiary sector was assessed as the ratio of tertiary sector output value to gross domestic product (GDP) in each respondent’s city of residence. It encompasses economic activities that provide services rather than tangible goods, including wholesale and retail trade, transportation, scientific and technical services, education, healthcare, cultural and entertainment services, public administration, and so on.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed utilizing Stata version 17.0. Descriptive statistics were first used to characterize the demographic profiles of patients with hypertension and diabetes. All demographic variables were categorical, with distributions presented as numbers (n) and proportions (%). Chi-square tests (x2) were performed to compare differences across groups. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to examine the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes, with odds ratios (ORs), 95% confidence intervals (CI), and p-values reported. To control for potential confounding, the logistic regression models were adjusted for the following covariates: gender, age, household registration type, education, marital status, medical insurance participation, self-reported health, smoking, drinking, the natural logarithm of per capita GDP, and the share of the tertiary sector. Subgroup analyses were performed to explore heterogeneity among patients with different chronic conditions, stratified by hypertension and diabetes. Additional heterogeneity analyses were conducted by the number of chronic conditions and residence to examine potential effect differences across subgroups. Mediation analyses were implemented by sequentially including medical treatment and physical activity in the logistic regression models to evaluate potential mediating pathways. Mediation effect was assessed by comparing changes in ORs (and their significance levels) for CCDES utilization with baseline estimates. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 6576 participants were included in this study. The sample was relatively gender-balanced, with males and females accounting for 47.2% and 52.8%, respectively. The majority of participants were aged 61~75 years (51.5%) and held agricultural household registration (hukou) (73.7%). Educational attainment was generally low, with 66.3% of participants having primary school education or below. Regarding marital status, 83.8% were married, and 16.2% were non-married (i.e., separated, divorced, widowed, or never married). Medical insurance coverage was nearly universal (96.9%). Most participants rated their health as “fair” (48.5%), followed by 27.3% who reported “poor” health. The prevalence of smoking and drinking was 23.9% and 24.4%, respectively. Significant differences in disease control outcomes were observed across participants with different household registration types, educational, marital status, medical insurance participation, self-reported health, and smoking status (p < 0.05). Specifically, participants with non-agricultural household registration, higher education, married status, medical insurance coverage, good self-reported health, and non-smoking status were more likely to achieve disease control (see Table 1).

Association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes

Logistic regression was employed to examine the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes (Table 3). After adjusting for gender, age, household registration type, education, marital status, medical insurance participation, self-reported health, smoking, drinking, the natural logarithm of per capita GDP, and the share of the tertiary sector, patients who used CCDES had 34.6% higher odds of disease control than non-users (aOR = 1.346, 95% CI: 1.151–1.574; Model 1). Furthermore, a significant positive association was identified between CCDES utilization frequency and disease control outcomes. Specifically, each one-unit increase in CCDES utilization was associated with a 6.5% increase in the odds of attaining disease control among patients (aOR = 1.065, 95% CI: 1.028–1.104; Model 2).

Associations between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes stratified by disease type

To compare differences in the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes across disease types, participants were divided into hypertension and diabetes groups. Notably, some participants had both conditions, resulting in the combined number of participants in the two groups exceeding the overall sample size of this study. After adjusting for all covariates, patients who used CCDES had 36.1% higher odds of hypertension control compared with non‑users (aOR = 1.361, 95% CI: 1.152–1.609; Model 1). Furthermore, each one-unit increase in CCDES utilization was associated with a 6.1% increase in the odds of achieving hypertension control among patients (aOR = 1.061, 95% CI: 1.022–1.101; Model 2). In the diabetes subgroup, CCDES_Use and CCDES_Usage_Frequency remained significantly associated with higher odds of glycemic control after adjustment for all covariates (CCDES_Use: aOR = 1.473, 95% CI: 1.098–1.976; CCDES_Usage_Frequency: aOR = 1.087, 95% CI: 1.015–1.164; Models 3 and 4). These findings indicate that CCDES utilization is positively associated with disease control outcomes among both hypertensive and diabetic participants (Table 4).

Heterogeneity

Heterogeneity in the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes was primarily examined by the number of chronic conditions and residence.

Heterogeneity analysis of the number of chronic conditions

Table 5 presents the differences stratified by the number of chronic conditions. Differences were observed in the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes across subgroups stratified by the number of chronic conditions. Among participants with a single chronic condition, after adjustment for all covariates, patients who used CCDES had 25.8% higher odds of disease control compared with non‑users (aOR = 1.258, 95% CI: 1.060–1.493; Model 1). Furthermore, each one-unit increase in CCDES utilization was associated with 5.9% higher odds of disease control (aOR = 1.059, 95% CI: 1.019–1.101; Model 2). In contrast, for participants with both hypertension and diabetes, neither CCDES_Use (p > 0.05) nor CCDES_Usage_Frequency (p > 0.05) was significantly associated with disease control outcomes (Models 3 and 4). These findings indicate that CCDES utilization is positively associated with disease control in patients with a single chronic condition, whereas no significant association was observed in those with multimorbidity.

Heterogeneity analysis of residence

Healthcare service provision varies by region, and the capacity for community-based chronic disease management also differs, with particularly pronounced disparities between urban and rural areas. Therefore, this study conducted subgroup analyses to examine differences in this association between urban and rural residents (Table 6). Among urban residents, no significant association was found between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes after adjusting for all covariates (p > 0.05; Models 1 and 2). In contrast, for the rural subgroup, after adjustment for all covariates, both CCDES_Use (aOR = 1.377, 95% CI: 1.158–1.638; Model 3) and CCDES_Usage_Frequency (aOR = 1.067, 95% CI: 1.026–1.109; Model 4) were significantly positively associated with disease control outcomes. Collectively, these findings indicate that CCDES utilization is positively associated with favorable disease control in rural patients, but not in urban patients.

Mechanisms

Mediation analyses were performed to examine the potential mechanisms underlying the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes, with medical treatment and physical activity specified as mediating variables.

Mediation effect of medical treatment

Medical treatment was included as an intermediate variable in baseline logistic regression models (Table 7). Significant associations were observed between CCDES_Use (aOR = 1.956, 95% CI: 1.682–2.274; Model 1) and CCDES_Usage_Frequency (aOR = 1.211, 95% CI: 1.166–1.257; Model 3) with medical treatment. When medical treatment was included in the baseline models, CCDES utilization remained significantly associated with disease control outcomes (Model 2: aOR = 1.297, 95% CI: 1.108–1.519; Model 4: aOR = 1.056, 95% CI: 1.019–1.094). Compared with the estimates in Table 3, the adjusted odds ratios for both CCDES_Use (aOR = 1.346) and CCDES_Usage_Frequency (aOR = 1.065) declined, indicating a mediating role of medical treatment. These findings suggest that CCDES utilization may partially facilitate favorable disease control by increasing patients’ likelihood of receiving medical treatment for chronic conditions.

Discussion

This study comprehensively analyzed the association between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes among patients with hypertensive and diabetic, using cross-sectional data from the 2018 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS). Subgroup analyses were conducted to compare this association between patients with hypertension and diabetes. Additionally, heterogeneity in this association was further explored by stratifying participants by the number of chronic conditions and place of residence. Mediation analyses were performed to investigate the potential mediating roles of medical treatment and physical activity.

The current study identified a positive association between the utilization of CCDES and disease control outcomes. This finding aligns with prior research demonstrating that health examinations are positively correlated with health outcomes [23, 24]. Health management programs provided by community-based primary healthcare teams are positively associated with favorable health outcomes, particularly physical health [25–27]. Health examination programs represent an important public health intervention designed to reduce disease risk factors through early detection and diagnosis [28]. Community-based chronic disease examination services enhance patients’ access to appropriate medical care while mitigating barriers to healthcare utilization [29]. Furthermore, these services are likely to improve patients’ health literacy regarding their own conditions, thereby promoting their engagement in health management practices and facilitating better disease control outcomes [30]. In contrast, several studies have raised concerns about the effectiveness of health examination programs in primary care settings. This skepticism primarily stems from cost-effectiveness analyses, which suggest that such programs often fail to achieve cost savings while simultaneously improving equity [31]. Furthermore, evidence indicates that the frequency of CCDES utilization is positively associated with favorable disease control outcomes. Community-based examination services function to identify eligible patients, who subsequently receive targeted disease management interventions. This approach facilitates regular follow-up visits, thereby supporting the attainment of disease intervention and management goals [32].

With specific reference to hypertension and diabetes, two highly prevalent chronic conditions, utilization of the CCDES has been shown to exert a significant positive impact on the control outcomes of both diseases. Check-up services targeting conditions such as hypertension and diabetes constitute a key component of health examinations delivered by primary healthcare teams [33, 34]. The implementation of these projects is intended to alleviate adverse health effects [35]. Research focusing on hypertension has shown that community health workers provide examinations and management services for hypertension to facilitate early diagnosis and timely intervention, thereby achieving effective disease control [32]. Additionally, general practitioners demonstrate higher detection rates in public health examination programs, which enables the management of risk factors such as blood glucose levels and ultimately improves patient health outcomes [36].

The findings of this study indicate that utilization of the CCDES exerts significantly differential effects on disease control outcomes between patients with a single chronic condition and those with multimorbidity. Patients with multimorbidity often face more complex clinical challenges throughout the diagnostic and therapeutic processes [37]. Compared with patients with a single chronic condition, those with multimorbidity are strongly associated with an elevated risk of mortality, deterioration in physical function, an increased risk of disability, and a reduced overall quality of life [4, 37, 38]. General health examinations in community primary care settings typically include routine physical assessments and periodic health evaluations, which facilitate early identification of chronic diseases and management of modifiable risk factors [39]. Nevertheless, the efficacy of such general health examinations remains controversial [39, 40]. Accordingly, there are substantial opportunities to enhance health management strategies and improve general health examination outcomes for patients with multimorbidity, who have greater healthcare utilization needs.

Additionally, this study found that CCDES utilization is associated with improved disease control outcomes among rural residents, whereas no significant association was observed among urban residents. Compared with urban residents, rural residents receive fewer preventive healthcare services [41]. Due to resource allocation constraints, healthcare service provision in rural areas is limited, leading to lower primary care utilization among residents [42]. Optimizing existing resource allocation can enable rural primary healthcare institutions to effectively increase service utilization rates and potentially improve health outcomes [43]. Addressing barriers to healthcare accessibility in rural areas has the potential to improve health outcomes [44]. Therefore, policymakers should prioritize routine health examinations in these underserved rural regions, as these examinations, an essential tool for chronic disease management, play a key role in enhancing health outcomes.

Regarding mechanism analysis, this study identified medical treatment as a key pathway through which CCDES utilization influences disease control outcomes. Health examination services enable individuals to identify their health status, thereby facilitating access to appropriate medical interventions [45]. Following the identification of elevated risk via examinations, pharmacological or behavioral interventions may be implemented [31]. In the short term, these interventions have been shown to increase disease diagnosis rates among primary care examination participants, improve medication adherence [46], and enhance equity in intervention delivery [47].

Although research indicates that physical inactivity among adults constitutes a significant risk factor for chronic diseases such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease [48], the efficacy of physical activity as a health-promoting intervention for chronic disease management remains contentious, with no consensus reached. While evidence suggests that health examinations are associated with modest improvements in health behaviors, including physical activity and dietary habits [39], findings from some randomized controlled trials show that while preventive services may facilitate increased physical activity, their effects on health outcomes remain inconsistent [49, 50]. Notably, interventions targeting health behavior change showed short-term benefits, but a study indicates these improvements were not sustained at four-year follow-up [50]. Nevertheless, community-based chronic disease examinations, an essential diagnostic tool, may facilitate chronic disease management by enabling early intervention and potentially supporting the maintenance of long-term health outcomes.

Several limitations of this study should be considered. (i) The cross-sectional study design precludes the establishment of causal inference between CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes; (ii) When examining chronic disease management and control outcomes, this study focused exclusively on hypertension and diabetes, two of the most highly prevalent chronic conditions in China, and did not extend to other chronic diseases types; (iii) The measurement of CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes relied on patient self-reported, which may be subject to reporting bias; (iv) Despite adjusting for multiple potential confounders, residual confounding from unmeasured factors (e.g., family support and health self-management behaviors) cannot be excluded.

Conclusions

This study used nationally representative data from China to examine the association between the CCDES utilization and disease control outcomes. Findings indicated that patients who used CCDES and did so more frequently were more likely to have their chronic disease under control, compared with those who did not use it or used it less frequently, and this association was statistically significant in both the hypertension and diabetes subgroups. Heterogeneity analyses indicated that the beneficial effects of CCDES utilization were more pronounced among patients with a single chronic condition and rural residents. Mediation analyses revealed that medical treatment served as a significant mediator in this association, whereas physical activity did not act as a significant mediator.

In summary, chronic disease health management, particularly examination services delivered by primary healthcare teams, plays a critical role in improving disease control outcomes among patients. This study provides novel insights into strategies for preventing and mitigating the adverse health consequences of chronic diseases.

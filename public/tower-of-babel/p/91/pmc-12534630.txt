Introduction

Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) is among the most prevalent gastrointestinal malignancies and is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Despite significant advances in surgical techniques and chemotherapeutic regimens, the prognosis for advanced COAD remains poor [2]. Moreover, the prognosis of early-stage COAD is challenging due to the limitations of current diagnostic and prognostic tools. The TNM staging system, in particular, often fails to accurately predict disease recurrence in these patients. While stage II colon cancer is generally considered to have a favorable prognosis, the 5-year cancer-specific survival rate is only 81–83% in patients who do not receive adjuvant chemotherapy [3]. Recent advances in molecular diagnostics have provided new avenues to overcome these limitations. For example, the development of prognostic models based on methylation-driven genes offers a promising approach to more accurately predict outcomes in COAD. These models enable more personalized prognostication, potentially enhancing the management of early-stage COAD [4]. By identifying novel biomarkers and exploring the tumor microenvironment, particularly immune cell infiltration, we can develop more accurate prognostic tools and personalized treatment strategies for COAD patients, especially those in the early stages of the disease. This highlights the need for a deeper understanding of the factors that influence tumor progression and therapeutic response.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a crucial role in cancer development and progression [5]. It consists of various cellular components, including immune cells, which interact with tumor cells to influence disease outcomes [6]. Among these immune cells, macrophages are of particular interest due to their plasticity and diverse functions [7]. Macrophages, essential components of the immune system, can adopt different phenotypes based on microenvironmental signals. In the context of cancer, macrophages range from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypes [8]. M1 macrophages are typically associated with anti-tumor responses, whereas M2 macrophages support tissue remodeling, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression, thereby promoting tumor progression [9, 10]. The presence of M2 macrophages within the TME has been correlated with poor prognosis in various cancers, including COAD [11–13]. Recent studies have emphasized the critical role of M2 macrophages in modulating the TME, facilitating tumor growth and metastasis [14]. These cells contribute to creating an immunosuppressive environment conducive to tumor progression [15]. However, the precise mechanisms by which M2 macrophages influence COAD progression, and their potential as therapeutic targets, remain inadequately explored.

This study aims to investigate the role of M2 macrophages in the tumor microenvironment of COAD and their association with clinical outcomes. Additionally, we explore the prognostic significance of the LDOC1 gene in COAD, assessing its potential as a novel biomarker for patient prognosis. A comprehensive analysis of immune cell infiltration in COAD is also conducted, utilizing multiple immune infiltration estimation methods to gain deeper insights into tumor-immune interactions. Furthermore, we extend our analysis to perform a pan-cancer evaluation of DMPK and LDOC1, determining their prognostic relevance across a range of cancer types and exploring their broader potential as biomarkers in oncological research.

By investigating macrophage-associated genes and their influence on the COAD microenvironment, this research seeks to uncover new insights into tumor biology and identify potential therapeutic targets. These findings may inform the development of targeted therapies and improve treatment strategies, ultimately enhancing clinical outcomes for patients with COAD.

Methods

Data collection and preprocessing

In this study, we utilized two major databases: TCGA database (The Cancer Genome Atlas, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and GEO database (Gene Expression Omnibus, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The TCGA COAD (Colon Adenocarcinoma) dataset was obtained from the TCGA Data Portal, which included 455 tumor samples and 41 normal tissue samples. For the GEO datasets, we included GSE17536 (177 tumor samples) and GSE39582 (585 tumor samples). To ensure comparability between the TCGA and GEO datasets, all TCGA raw RNA sequencing data were processed and converted to TPM (Transcripts Per Million) format [16]. This normalization approach was chosen to better align the TCGA data with the chip-based expression data from GEO, facilitating cross-dataset analysis. For data filtering, genes with an average expression level below 0.5 across all samples were excluded from the analysis to focus on genes with meaningful expression. The processed data were then used for immune infiltration analysis, survival analysis, and other downstream statistical evaluations.

Quantification and analysis of immune cell infiltration in COAD patients

To analyze the immune microenvironment in COAD patients, we utilized TIMER [17], CIBERSORT [18], QUANTISEQ [19], MCPcounter [20], xCell [21], and EPIC [22] methods to quantify immune cell infiltration. Additionally, we employed the ESTIMATE algorithm to calculate stromal and immune scores for all patients. Samples were categorized into tumor and normal groups, and within each group, patients were sorted based on their ESTIMATE scores. These data were used to create a comprehensive heatmap to visualize the immune infiltration patterns. Furthermore, we extracted M2 macrophage expression data from each method and performed statistical analyses to compare their expression between tumor and normal groups.

Module preservation assessment and WGCNA analysis

To identify co-expression gene modules relevant to immune cell infiltration in COAD, we performed Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) [23]. Module preservation was assessed using Z-summary scores, with thresholds set to categorize modules as strongly preserved (Z >10), weakly preserved (2 < Z ≤ 10), and not preserved (Z ≤ 2). Modules with Z-summary scores below 2 were excluded from further analysis. We subsequently examined the correlation between the preserved modules and the quantified expression of M2 macrophages using various immune infiltration methods. The darkgreen, pink, and greenyellow modules, which exhibited the highest correlations with M2 macrophage expression, were selected for further study. Genes from these modules were aggregated and subjected to differential expression analysis to identify significant differences between tumor and normal tissues.

Prognostic analysis of module genes

To assess the prognostic significance of genes from the darkgreen, pink, and greenyellow modules, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis. A forest plot was generated to display the top 12 genes with the smallest p-values. Subsequently, we conducted a pan-cancer prognostic analysis of the most significant genes using data from TCGA, evaluating their roles as prognostic factors across different cancer types. For COAD patients, Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis was performed based on Disease Specific Survival (DSS) to further explore the prognostic implications of these genes. Additionally, calibration plots were created to compare the predicted and actual probabilities of survival at various time points, and a nomogram was constructed to visualize the combined prognostic impact of these genes.

Single-cell analysis using TISCH2 and Pan-Cancer expression analysis

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis was conducted using the TISCH2 database to evaluate the expression and correlation of DMPK and LDOC1 genes across different cell types in COAD datasets [24]. We analyzed the correlation between gene expression and various cell populations and assessed the expression levels of these genes across different cell types within the datasets. Further, differential expression of LDOC1 across various cancer types was investigated using TCGA data. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed using hallmark gene sets to identify pathways significantly associated with LDOC1 expression in multiple cancers. In COAD samples, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were conducted to compare high and low LDOC1 expression groups.

Assessment of tumor genomic heterogeneity with LDOC1

We investigated the association between LDOC1 expression and copy number variation (CNV) across various cancer types using TCGA pan-cancer data. LDOC1 expression levels were compared between CNV loss and CNV gain groups. Furthermore, we examined the correlation of LDOC1 expression with tumor mutation burden (TMB) [25], microsatellite instability (MSI) [26], neoantigen load (NEO) [27], and tumor purity [28] across multiple cancers, including COAD. Additionally, we assessed the mutation status of LDOC1 in the pan-cancer cohort and performed a comparative mutation analysis in COAD patients, focusing on the top 10 differentially mutated genes between high and low LDOC1 expression groups.

Analysis of tumor microenvironment, and immunotherapy response in COAD

We analyzed the correlation between LDOC1 expression and immune checkpoint genes, including both inhibitory and stimulatory genes, in COAD. The ESTIMATE algorithm was used to assess the correlation between LDOC1 expression and Stromal score, Immune score, and Estimate score. Additionally, differential expression analysis of immune regulatory genes, such as chemokines, receptors, and MHC molecules, was performed between high and low LDOC1 expression groups in COAD patients. To predict immunotherapy response, we conducted TIDE analysis, evaluating the distribution of TIDE scores, Dysfunction scores, and Exclusion scores between high and low LDOC1 expression groups in COAD patients [29]. Submap analysis was performed to further assess potential differences in immunotherapy response between these groups [30]. Drug sensitivity prediction analysis was conducted using the GDSC database, identifying the eight drugs with the smallest p-values for their association with LDOC1 expression [31].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using the software of R in 4.2.2 version. A significance level of p < 0.05 was used for all tests. The Chi-square test was applied for categorical variables, and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for continuous variables. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with the log-rank test was used to assess survival differences, and univariate Cox regression identified significant prognostic genes. The optimal cutoff values for gene expression were determined using the “survminer” package. Gene modules were identified through WGCNA, with soft-thresholding selected to achieve an R² value closest to 0.9.

Results

Differential immune infiltration patterns in COAD patients

Immune cell infiltration in COAD patients was analyzed using six immune infiltration estimation methods: TIMER, CIBERSORT, QUANTISEQ, MCPcounter, xCell, and EPIC. The resulting heatmap (Fig. 1A) revealed significant differences in immune cell presence between tumor and normal tissues. Specifically, patients were categorized based on their ESTIMATE scores, which allowed us to further highlight distinct infiltration patterns in tumor versus normal tissue. This categorization underscores the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in COAD and the crucial role of immune cells in tumor progression and immune evasion mechanisms. We further focused on the analysis of M2 macrophages, which are generally considered immunosuppressive in the TME. Interestingly, M2 macrophages were significantly less abundant in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues (Fig. 1B). This finding contradicts the classical view that M2 macrophages promote tumor growth and progression, suggesting a more complex role for M2 macrophages in COAD. This could indicate potential alternative mechanisms of immune regulation in COAD, which do not solely rely on M2 macrophages, and highlights the importance of exploring this aspect further for therapeutic purposes.

Identification and analysis of key gene modules

Using WGCNA, we identified several co-expression gene modules in COAD. We determined the optimal soft threshold (β) using the ‘pickSoftThreshold’ function from the WGCNA package. The soft threshold value was selected by evaluating a range of power values (from 1 to 20). After assessing the scale-free topology fit, we identified that a soft threshold of 10 resulted in the best fit, with an R² value closest to 0.9 (Supplementary Fig. 1A and 1B). Module preservation analysis indicated that the gold module had a Z-summary score below 2, signifying poor preservation, and it was excluded from further study (Fig. 2A and B). Correlation analysis between the preserved modules and the quantified expression of M2 macrophages, using various immune infiltration methods, revealed that the darkgreen, pink, and greenyellow modules had the highest correlations (Fig. 2C). Genes from these three modules were combined into a single set (Fig. 2D), and differential expression analysis was performed to identify genes with significant expression differences between tumor and normal tissues (Fig. 2E). This approach enabled the identification of key genes potentially involved in M2 macrophage-related immune responses in COAD.

Prognostic significance of DMPK and LDOC1 in COAD

Univariate Cox regression analysis of genes from the darkgreen, pink, and greenyellow modules identified DMPK and LDOC1 as significant prognostic factors. The forest plot illustrates the top 12 genes with the smallest p-values, highlighting the notable significance of DMPK and LDOC1 (Fig. 3A). Pan-cancer prognostic analysis using TCGA data revealed that DMPK and LDOC1 act as distinct prognostic indicators across various cancer types, underscoring their diverse roles in cancer biology (Fig. 3B). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis for COAD patients, based on Disease Specific Survival (DSS), demonstrated significant differences in survival outcomes associated with the expression levels of DMPK and LDOC1 (Fig. 3C). To further validate the prognostic significance of DMPK and LDOC1, we performed additional analysis using datasets from the GEO database, including GSE17536 and GSE39582. The findings indicated that LDOC1 was significantly associated with OS and Disease-Specific Survival (DSS) (Supplementary Fig. 2A), and it also showed prognostic relevance for Relapse-Free Survival (RFS) (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Calibration plots for DMPK and LDOC1 depicted the discrepancies between predicted and actual survival probabilities at different time points, providing insights into the accuracy of prognostic predictions based on these genes (Fig. 3D). The nomogram constructed for DMPK and LDOC1 visualized their combined prognostic impact, offering a practical tool for predicting patient outcomes in COAD based on the expression of these genes (Fig. 3E).

Single-cell and pan-cancer expression analysis of DMPK and LDOC1

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis from the TISCH2 database revealed significant correlations between DMPK and LDOC1 expression and various cell populations in COAD datasets (Fig. 4A, B). These analyses demonstrated that the expression levels of DMPK and LDOC1 were notably higher in specific immune and stromal cells, suggesting their potential roles in modulating tumor-immune interactions within the COAD microenvironment. The expression profiles of these genes across different cell types revealed distinct patterns, which could have important implications for understanding tumor progression and therapeutic responses in COAD (Fig. 4C, D). Pan-cancer analysis using TCGA data indicated significant differential expression of LDOC1 across a variety of cancer types (Fig. 5A). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) demonstrated that LDOC1 expression was positively correlated with multiple oncogenic pathways, including KRAS signaling, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and inflammatory response (Fig. 5B). These findings suggest that LDOC1 may participate in critical cellular processes like tumor initiation, metastasis, and immune evasion. In COAD samples, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses based on LDOC1 expression revealed distinct differences in pathway activation between high and low expression groups, offering new insights into the functional roles of LDOC1 in tumor progression and immune modulation (Fig. 5C).

Correlation of LDOC1 with immune checkpoints and tumor microenvironment

Correlation analysis demonstrated that LDOC1 expression in COAD is significantly associated with both inhibitory and stimulatory immune checkpoint genes (Fig. 7A). Using the ESTIMATE algorithm, we found a strong positive correlation between LDOC1 expression and Stromal score, Immune score, and Estimate score (Fig. 7B). Differential expression analysis of immune regulatory genes revealed significant differences between high and low LDOC1 expression groups, particularly in chemokines, receptors, and MHC molecules (Figs. 7C–E). This indicates that LDOC1 may play a critical role in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment, affecting both immune cell infiltration and immune evasion mechanisms.

Immunotherapy response and drug sensitivity prediction

TIDE analysis showed the distribution of TIDE scores in COAD patients, with significant differences in TIDE, Dysfunction, and Exclusion scores between high and low LDOC1 expression groups (Fig. 8A, B). Pie chart analysis indicated a higher number of immunotherapy responders in the low LDOC1 expression group (Fig. 8C). Submap analysis suggested that the low LDOC1 expression group might respond better to immunotherapy (Fig. 8D). Drug sensitivity prediction using the GDSC database identified the top eight drugs with the smallest p-values associated with LDOC1 expression, suggesting potential therapeutic options (Fig. 8E). These results highlight the potential of LDOC1 as a predictive biomarker for immunotherapy response and drug sensitivity in COAD patients.

Discussion

Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, despite advances in treatment modalities [32]. The tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly immune cells such as macrophages, plays a crucial role in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance [33]. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of M2 macrophages and associated genes within the COAD microenvironment, offering new insights into immune-tumor interactions and potential therapeutic targets.

Our analysis revealed that M2 macrophages were less prevalent in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues, suggesting a more complex role in COAD than previously assumed. Using Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA), we identified key gene modules associated with immune cell infiltration. Univariate Cox regression analysis highlighted the prognostic significance of DMPK and LDOC1, pointing to these genes as potential biomarkers for COAD.

Pan-cancer analysis revealed that LDOC1 serves as a significant prognostic indicator across multiple cancer types, including COAD. LDOC1 expression was positively correlated with several oncogenic pathways, such as KRAS signaling, EMT, and inflammatory response. On one hand, KRAS is a vital signaling protein essential for maintaining intestinal homeostasis and playing a central role in the development and progression of COAD [34]. Currently, there are no effective targeted therapies for KRAS-mutant COAD [35]. Therefore, understanding the downstream networks of KRAS and their relationship with cancer phenotypes is a critical objective for future research. On the other hand, EMT is a cell-biological process that plays a crucial role in both developmental biology and cancer progression [36]. In the context of malignant tumors, EMT endows cancer cells with enhanced tumor-initiating and metastatic capabilities, as well as increased resistance to various therapeutic regimens [37]. By elucidating the pathways and networks downstream of LDOC1, particularly those involving KRAS signaling and EMT, researchers can develop targeted strategies to inhibit these processes and potentially improve patient outcomes. The ongoing investigation into the role of LDOC1 in the tumor microenvironment and its interaction with key oncogenic pathways underscores the importance of this gene in COAD progression and highlights its potential as a target for future cancer therapies.

Our study also explored the correlation of LDOC1 expression with tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), neoantigen load (NEO), and tumor purity. LDOC1 expression showed significant associations with these genomic features, suggesting that it may influence the genetic landscape of COAD. These correlations highlight the complexity of the tumor-immune interplay and the potential of LDOC1 as a biomarker for assessing tumor heterogeneity and immune evasion mechanisms.

TIDE analysis was employed to evaluate the potential response of COAD patients to immunotherapy. The TIDE scores indicated that patients with low LDOC1 expression might have a better response to immunotherapy. This finding is crucial, as it suggests that LDOC1 expression levels could be used as a biomarker to stratify patients who are more likely to benefit from immunotherapy. Further supporting this, Submap analysis was conducted to compare the gene expression profiles of COAD patients with those of known responders and non-responders to immune checkpoint blockade therapies. The results from the Submap analysis reinforced the TIDE findings, indicating that the low LDOC1 expression group had a higher likelihood of responding positively to immunotherapy. These predictive analyses are significant as they provide a method to tailor immunotherapy treatments based on LDOC1 expression levels, potentially improving clinical outcomes.

In this study, we employed a novel approach by identifying M2 macrophage-associated genes as potential prognostic biomarkers for COAD, offering insights into tumor-targeted therapeutic strategies. Unlike previous studies that have typically relied on pre-existing gene signatures [38, 39], our methodology integrates multiple screening techniques to identify genes specifically linked to M2 macrophages, thus enhancing the relevance of our findings to tumor biology. Additionally, our comprehensive analysis of immune cell infiltration, utilizing a broad range of algorithms—such as TIMER, CIBERSORT, QUANTISEQ, MCPcounter, xCell, and EPIC—provides a more nuanced and accurate understanding of the immune microenvironment in COAD compared to studies using a single algorithm [40]. Furthermore, our pan-cancer analysis of DMPK and LDOC1 extends their prognostic significance beyond COAD, incorporating survival outcomes, genomic characteristics, and pathway analyses. This multi-faceted approach strengthens the clinical applicability of our findings, offering a more robust framework for understanding tumor-immune interactions and advancing personalized treatment strategies in COAD and other cancers [41].

This study provides novel insights into the prognostic significance of M2 macrophages and the LDOC1 in COAD. Specifically, we highlight their potential as both prognostic biomarkers and modulators of the tumor microenvironment, offering a novel perspective on their role in immune evasion and tumor growth. While the involvement of M2 macrophages in COAD has been suggested, our work provides a comprehensive analysis of their contribution to immune modulation within the tumor. In addition, we employed a robust multi-platform approach to assess immune cell infiltration using multiple algorithms. This comprehensive evaluation offers a more nuanced understanding of the immune landscape in COAD, with a particular focus on macrophage polarization, a key aspect of tumor-immune interactions that has not been fully explored in previous studies. Furthermore, our study extends beyond COAD by conducting a pan-cancer analysis of DMPK and LDOC1, elucidating their prognostic significance across a variety of malignancies. This broader investigation underscores the potential of these markers as universal prognostic tools, suggesting their utility in clinical settings for multiple cancer types.

Despite the comprehensive nature of our study, there are several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the study relies heavily on publicly available datasets, such as TCGA and TISCH2, which may have inherent biases and variability in data quality. Second, our analysis is primarily based on bioinformatics methods and computational predictions, which require experimental validation to confirm the biological relevance of the findings. Third, the study focuses on correlations and associations, which do not necessarily imply causation. Further mechanistic studies are needed to elucidate the precise roles of M2 macrophages and the identified genes in COAD progression. Lastly, while we identified potential therapeutic targets and predictors of immunotherapy response, clinical trials are necessary to evaluate the efficacy and safety of these proposed interventions in patients.

Conclusion

This study underscores the critical role of M2 macrophages in the progression of COAD and identifies DMPK and LDOC1 as potential prognostic biomarkers. Our findings highlight the significance of immune cell infiltration in COAD and suggest that these biomarkers could offer valuable prognostic insights, both within COAD and across a broader spectrum of cancers. These results lay the groundwork for future research focused on the development of targeted therapies and personalized treatment strategies, ultimately aiming to improve clinical outcomes for COAD patients and potentially extend to other malignancies.

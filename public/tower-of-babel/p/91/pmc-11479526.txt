Network toxicology and molecular docking to investigative the non-acetylcholinesterase mechanisms and targets of cardiotoxicity injury induced by organophosphorus pesticides
Authors: Yongmei Guo, Nan Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Organophosphorus pesticides (OPPs) are widely used in the world, however, OPP poisoning often occurs because of improper use and lack of protective measures. Cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs is insidious, and it does not receive attention until the end stage of OPP poisoning. Heart failure or arrhythmia gradually becomes the main lethal cause of OPP poisoning patients.Methods:In this study, network toxicology and molecular docking were employed to investigate the non-acetylcholinesterase targets and mechanisms of cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs.Results:One hundred twenty-three targets of dichlorvos, 205 targets of methidathion, and 337 targets of malathion were searched from SwissTargetPreict, STITCH and PharmMapper database. Additionally, 1379 targets related to cardiotoxicity injury were acquired from GeneCards and OMIM database. Ninety-six mutual targets between OPPs and cardiotoxicity injury were considered as the potential cardiotoxicity injury targets induced by OPPs. The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using STING database, and 21 core targets were identified by Cytoscape software, such as AKT1, ESR1, HSP90AA1, MAPK1, MMP9, and MAPK8. Gene ontology and KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that cell migration, apoptotic process, protein phosphorylation and signal transduction were the major biological functions associated with OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury, and OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury might be regulated by MAPK, PI3K-Akt, VEGF signaling pathway. Docking results manifested that the best binding target for dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were MAPK9 (−7.1 kcal/mol), MAPK1 (−8.1 kcal/mol) and HSP90AA1 (−8.6 kcal/mol) with the lowest affinity, respectively.Conclusion:The core targets and non-AchE mechanisms were explored by network toxicology and molecular docking, providing a theoretical basis for the treatment of OPP-induced cardiotoxicity injury.

1. Introduction

Organophosphorus pesticides (OPPs), widely used pesticides globally, have been extensively applied in various process of agricultural processes.[1] Productive poisoning, occupational exposure or oral suicide are common routes of OPP intoxication.[2] With the population of mental health education and the enhancement of awareness of poisoning prevention, skin absorption has become the primary approach of OPP intoxication. Dichlorvos, Methidathion, and Malathion are commonly used in agricultural production, and these 3 OPPs were absorbed into bloodstream through skin, leading to cause poisoning symptoms. Thus, 3 OPPs (Dichlorvos, Methidathion, and Malathion) were selected for further research.[3–5] OPP poisoning is a clinical emergency and severe disease with the characteristics of sudden onset, severe disease and high fatality rate.[6] A report released by the World Health Organization revealed that approximately 3 million people worldwide suffered from acute organophosphorus pesticide poisoning every year.[7] OPP poisoning has become a public health problem and attract the attention of all sectors of society.

Clinical researchers have discovered that OPP poisoning causes damage to the lung, brain, liver and kidney, with prominent clinical symptoms of brain and lung damage.[5] The toxicity mechanisms of OPP intoxication involve multiple biological functions, including cytokines, apoptosis, cell signal transduction and gene regulation.[8–10] With in-depth investigation of mechanism, some process has been made in the treatment of OPP poisoning.[4] OPPs also can cause heart damage, potentially leading to sudden cardiac death. However, heart injury induced by OPPs is insidious, and it does not got attention until the end stage of OPP poisoning.[6] Among the patients with OPP poisoning, the proportion of death caused by respiratory failure has decreased, while that caused by heart failure or arrhythmia has increased.[11] Consequently, heart injury induced by OPP poisoning has gradually received more attention. Nevertheless, there have been few studies on OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury, and the exploitation of heart protective agent for OPP poisoning is still in the stage of development.

Traditional toxicological experiments require significant manpower, resources, and experimental animals. Network toxicology can predict the multiple targets for the poison to the body and elucidate the action mechanisms of poison.[12] Molecular docking technology can directly infer the possible binding modes and binding sites between the poison and the target based on their characteristics.[13] Combining network toxicology with toxicology experiment greatly reduces the research cost. Therefore, in this study, non-acetylcholinesterase (AChE) core targets and mechanisms of cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs were investigated using network toxicology and molecular docking, aiming to provide reference for the development of heart protective agents during OPP poisoning.

2. Method

2.1. Selection of organophosphorus pesticides

There are 3 common ways for OPPs to be absorbed into blood, causing toxic symptoms: oral administration, absorption through the skin and mucous membranes, and respiratory absorption. With the increase in mental health education and the enhanced awareness of poisoning prevention, cases of OPP intoxication through oral and respiratory administration have decreased. However, more patients are now poisoned by OPPs through skin absorption, which is more common when pesticides are sprayed on hot days. Consequently, SwissADME and ADMETlab 2.0 were used to screen OPPs caused poisoning through skin absorption.[14] Filter parameters were as follows: molecular weight < 400, 1 < oil–water partition coefficients (log P) < 3, half-life time (T1/2) < 10 hours, and skin permeability coefficient (Kp) > 0.5 × 10−3 cm/h.[15] Finally, 3 OPPs (Dichlorvos, Methidathion, and Malathion, Table 1) were selected as object of study, and their physical, chemical parameters and structure of them are shown in Table 1 and Figure 1.

2.2. Collection the targets of organophosphorus pesticides

The structures and Canonical SMILESs of dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were obtained from PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/, search on November 15, 2023). The Canonical SMILESs of dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were uploaded to STITCH (http://stitch.embl.de/, search on November 15, 2023) and SwissTargetPredict (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/, search on November 15, 2023) to obtain potential targets, with the species limited to “Homo sapiens.” Meanwhile, their structures were input into PharmMapper database (https://lilab-ecust.cn/pharmmapper/, search on November 15, 2023) to search the overlooked targets, limited the species to “Homo sapiens,” conformation generation with generate conformers, and the number of reserved matched targets set to 1000. Based on these results, all the targets were merged and deleted duplicates. Besides, the processed targets were converted to standard gene name using Uniprot database (https://www.uniprot.org/) for further analysis.

2.3. Collection of cardiotoxicity related target

Cardiotoxicity targets were searched form OMIM (https://omim.org/, search on November 15, 2023) and GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/, search on November 17, 2023) database using the keywords of “heart injury,” “heart toxicity,” “cardiotoxicity” and “heart dysfunction.” In order to ensure that the obtained targets were highly correlated with heart injury and toxic effects, the “relevance score” threshold in GeneCards database was set to >10. Afterwards, a Vene diagram between the targets of dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion and heart-toxicity targets were constructed to determine the mutual targets, and these targets were considered as the potential targets causing cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs.

2.4. Construction of protein–protein interaction network and determination of core targets

In order to investigate the interrelations between targets, the mutual targets were uploaded to STING database (https://cn.string-db.org/, search on November 25, 2023). The parameters of STING database were set as follow: organisms with “Homo sapiens,” network display options with “hide disconnected nodes in the network,” minimum required interaction score with “high confidence 0.7.”

The results obtained from STING database were imported into Cytoscape software (version 3.10.1) to construct the protein–protein interaction (PPI) network, and the degree value of each node were calculated using CentiScaPe2.2 plug-in. Meanwhile, the core targets were determined by MCODE plug-in for further research.

2.5. Gene ontology function and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment analysis

To explore the potential mechanism and biological function of the 96 targets in OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury, Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis were performed using DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/, search on November 30, 2023). GO enrichment analysis, composed of biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF), was employed to elucidate the major biological functions. KEGG enrichment analysis was utilized to determine the significant pathway related to the potential targets of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury. Meanwhile, GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of the core targets were also performed in the same manner.

2.6. Molecular docking for organophosphorus pesticides and core targets

Molecular docking was employed to elucidate the intermolecular interactions between OPPs and the pivotal targets predicted in this study. The 3D protein structure of core targets, determined by X-ray, were obtained from RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) database (https://www.rcsb.org/, search on October 10, 2023), and they were <3.0 Å. Then, the protein structures were processed by removing water molecules, ligands, adding hydrogen atoms. The 3D structure of the 6 compounds were obtained through 2D structure transformation by OpenBabel 2.4.1 software. The score of docking between compounds and proteins was calculated by Autodock vina software (La Jolla, CA, USA). The best docking poses models with RMSD ≤ 2 were considered accurate. Visualization of the docking results was performed using the Pymol software.

3. Result

3.1. Determination of organophosphorus pesticides-induced cardiotoxicity targets

After removing the duplicates, 348 targets of 3 OPPs (123 targets of dichlorvos, 205 targets of methidathion, and 337 targets of malathion) were obtained from SwissTargetPreict, STITCH and PharmMapper database (Fig. 2A). Among them, there were 200 mutual targets between malathion and methidathion, 117 mutual targets between dichlorvos and methidathion, and 92 mutual targets between dichlorvos and methidathion, indicating the selected 3 OPPs absorbed through skin might share the same action targets and mechanisms. Besides, 1379 targets related to cardiotoxicity injury were acquired from GeneCards and OMIM database. There were 96 mutual targets between OPPs and cardiotoxicity injury, and these 96 targets were considered as the potential cardiotoxicity injury targets induced by OPPs (Fig. 2B).

The Venn diagram of the 3 OPPs targets (A) and the mutual targets between OPPs and cardiotoxicity injury (B).

3.2. Construction of protein–protein interaction and determination the core targets

The PPI network was constructed using STING database with 70 nodes and 309 edges (except alone targets). The degree of each node was calculated using Cytoscape software, and the nodes were ranked according to their degree (Fig. 3A). Analysis of the PPI was performed, and a total of 21 core targets for OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury were determined by MCODE plug-in (Fig. 3B). According to their degree values, the top 6 core targets for OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury were AKT1, HSP90AA1, MMP9, ESR1, MAPK8, and MAPK1.

The protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks of 96 targets (A) and 21 core targets (B).

3.3. Gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes enrichment analysis

3.3.1. Gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes enrichment analysis of potential targets: The GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of the 96 potential targets were performed using DAVID database, limiting the organisms with “Homo sapiens.” There were 626 statistically significant GO terms (P < .05), including 458 BPs, 71 CCs, and 97 MFs, indicating that BP might be the main biological function for OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury targets. The terms were ranked according to their P-values, and the top 10 terms of BP, CC, MF were selected for visualization (Fig. 4). GO functional analysis indicated that the potential targets were enriched in BPs (apoptotic process, cell migration, and MAPK cascade), CCs (cytosol, extracellular exosome, and plasma membrane), MFs (binding to multiple compound).

Gene ontology enrichment analysis of 96 targets. The bar chart (A) and bubble chart (B) of the top 10 terms for BP, cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) biological functions.

There were 148 statistically significant KEGG pathways (P < .05), which could be divided into 14 super-pathways (such as disease, developmental biology, immune system and signal transduction). KEGG enrichment analysis (Fig. 5) indicated that OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury targets were associated with PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, Foxo signaling pathway, Rap1 signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway and Relaxin signaling pathway.

Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of 96 targets. The bar chart (A) and bubble chart (B) of the top 10 KEGG pathways.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of 21 core targets: The 21 core targets identified for OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury were uploaded into DAVID database to perform GO and KEGG enrichment analysis. There were 203 statistically significant GO terms (P < .05), including 129 BPs, 32 CCs, and 42 MFs, and 114 KEGG pathways. The top 10 GO terms and signal pathways were selected for visualization. The result manifested that the core targets of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury associated with apoptotic process, protein phosphorylation, signal transduction, nucleus, plasma membrane, protein kinase binding, and oxidoreductase activity (Fig. 6A–C), indicating that OPPs might cause cardiotoxicity injury through nucleus and membrane receptor, and apoptotic pathway. For KEGG enrichment analysis (Fig. 6D), the core targets of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury were linked to MAPK, PI3K-Akt, and VEGF signaling pathways.

3.4. Molecular docking for organophosphorus pesticides and core targets

Molecular docking was performed to further investigate the interaction between 3 OPPs and 6 core targets (AKT1, HSP90AA1, MMP9, ESR1, MAPK8, and MAPK1). To investigate the possible binding site and binging energy, the 3 OPPs were chosen as ligands, and 6 core targets were selected as receptors. As shown in Figures 7 to 9, the main forms between ligand and receptor were hydrogen bonding.

The molecular docking between methidathion and AKT1 (A), ESR1 (B), HSP90AA1 (C), MAPK1 (D), MAPK9 (E), and MMP9 (F).

The molecular docking between malathion and AKT1 (A), ESR1 (B), HSP90AA1 (C), MAPK1 (D), MAPK9 (E), and MMP9 (F).

In addition, it is believed that the more hydrogen bond and lower the affinity (kcal/mol) for molecular docking, the stronger binding between the ligand and receptor. Molecular docking binding free energy less than −5.0 kcal/mol indicates strong binding activity, and that less than −7.0 kcal/mol indicates very strong binding activity. The docking results are shown in Table 1. The affinity of the 6 proteins with dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were all less than −5.0 kcal/mol, and the target proteins and ligands were connected by hydrogen bond, which indicated the bindings of the ligands and receptor were very tight. The best binding protein for dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were MAPK9, MAPK1 and HSP90AA1 with the lowest affinity, respectively. Overall, docking results successfully predicted docking between 3 OPPs and the binding pocket of all 6 core target proteins.

4. Discussion

In this study, network pharmacology was employed to screen 96 potential targets associated with OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury according to SwissTargetPredict STITCH, PharmMapper, GeneCards, and OMIM database. Based on the STRING and Cytoscape software, the PPI network was established, and the 21 crucial targets were extracted, including AKT1, ESR1, HSP90AA1, MAPK1, MMP9, and MAPK8, which serve as core targets in the context of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury.

AKT1, a serine/threonine specific protein kinase, is implicated in a variety of biological functions, including glucose metabolism, apoptosis, cell proliferation, cell transcription, and cell migration.[16] Several studies demonstrated that abnormal AKT1 expression was observed in multiple heart disease, indicating that AKT1 might be a pivotal potential therapeutic target.[17,18] This was consistent with the results of this research, which revealed that AKT1 was 1 of the core targets in PPI, and related to multiple signaling pathways. In the short term, lack of AKT1 immediately enhanced injury and apoptosis of cardiomyocytes after ischemic/reperfusion injury, however, in the long term, it could improve heart function and ventricular remodeling after myocardial infarction.[19] In addition, inhibition of AKT1 also regulated cardiomyocyte survival through GSK-3β/β-catenin pathway, and alleviated heart injury by promoting autophagy via AKT1-RPS6KB1 signaling.[20] Nonetheless, the change of AKT1 expression induced by OPPs in heart had not been reported.

HSP90AA1, was considered as the “guardian” of the body.[21] If the body was exposed to heat, cold, infection, disease, and other stressful environments, the synthesis of HSP90AA1 would increase to protect body. HSP90AA1 participated in a variety of biological functions, such as apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptpsis.[22] Increment of HSP90AA1 alleviated myocardial injury caused by heat stress. Besides, ERK and Heme Oxygenase-1 signaling pathways were activated by inhibiting the interaction of HSP90AA1 and CDC37 to reduce infarct volume, fibrosis and macrophage infiltration.[23] In this study, HSP90AA1 was predicted as the 1 of the core targets of heart injury induced by OPPs, which might be related to the rise of body temperature after OPPs poisoning.[24]

Matrix metallo proteinases (MMPs) are a group of proteolytic enzymes that rely on zinc ions and degrade extracellular matrix components as substrates. Studies had found that MMPs were connected with oxidative stress reaction induced ischemic/reperfusion injury, and inhibition of MMPs effectively relieved myocardial damage.[25] MMP9 is a major member of the MMPs family, which is involved in cell proliferation, migration, and differentiation, and plays a role in angiogenesis and tissue repair.[26] Researchers demonstrated that the concentration of MMP9 in serum was positively correlated with the risk of coronary events.[27] The heart injury caused by OPPs might be associated with the elevation of MMP9.

ESR1 could mediate the classical estrogen receptor transduction pathway, that is, estrogen directly bound to ESR1 in the nucleus to promote target gene transcription. Moreover, ESR1 also mediated non-genotypic effects (such as MAPK/ERK or PI3-K/AKT signaling pathways) to promote cell proliferation.[28] The abnormal increase of ESR1 expression in myocardial damage was observed, and decrease of ESR1 expression patently improved the heart injury.[29] The elevation of ESR1 expression induced by OPPs was observed in the liver of Atlantic salmon.[30] Even so, there was no study about the ESR1 aberrant expression caused by OPPs in heart.

MAPK signaling pathway is 1 of the important pathways in eukaryotic signal transduction network, and plays a crucial role in apoptosis, inflammation, cell proliferation, and energy metabolism.[28] MAPK1 and MAPK8 were the key transcription factors in MAPK signaling pathway. MAPK1 and MAPK8 abnormally increased in myocardial injury, which indicated MAPK1 and MAPK8 might be potential key targets for the treatment of myocardial injury.[31] Other researchers reported inhibition of MAPK1 restrained downstream PTEN signaling pathway and activated AKT and ERK signaling pathways to alleviate myocardial injury.[32] OPPs could increase the expressions of MAPK1 and MAPK8 in brain, and induce mitochondrial apoptosis through MAPK/ERK signaling pathway.

GO enrichment analysis of 96 targets revealed the main biological process of heart injury induced by OPPs involved protein phosphorylation, apoptotic process, cell migration, MAPK cascade, and protein kinase B signaling. OPPs promoted cell migration of macrophages and cancer cells, which might be related to their metabolites in the body.[33] Excessive apoptosis was the main pathological reaction of OPP poisoning. ERK/MAPK signaling pathway was involved in mitochondrial cell apoptosis induced by chlorpyrifos, and ginseng extract alleviated chlorpyrifos-induced enterotoxicity by inhibiting excessive oxidative stress, inflammation and cell apoptosis.[34] Besides, OPPs also could phosphorylate AKT1, ERK1/2, MAPK, and other targets, participating in multiple signaling pathways of signal transduction. GO enrichment analysis of core targets designated that cell migration, apoptotic process, protein phosphorylation and signal transduction, which indicated the selected 21 core targets represented the 96 targets of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury. KEGG enrichment analysis of core targets demonstrated that OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury was closely associated with MAPK signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, VEGF signaling pathway, which were related to cardiomyocyte energy metabolism, apoptosis, and angiogenesis.

Molecular docking studies further provided a visual explanation of the interaction between OPPs and its predicted 6 potential core targets related to heart injury. Docking analysis provided evidence that the proteins of the 6 core targets were tightly bound to OPPs (Table 2), and the best binding protein for dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were MAPK9 (−7.1 kcal/mol), MAPK1 (−8.1 kcal/mol) and HSP90AA1 (−8.6 kcal/mol) with the lowest affinity, respectively. In addition, results also showed that hydrophobic interaction, was the main forms of interaction. Consequently, it was possible to reveal the core targets and mechanisms of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury using network toxicology and molecular docking. However, the predicted targets and mechanisms had not been verified in vivo or in vitro, which was a shortcoming of this research. In future studies, the conclusions of this research need to be further verified by cell or animal experiments.

5. Conclusion

In this study, the potential cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs was comprehensively investigated by network toxicology and molecular docking. Ninety-six mutual targets were identified as the targets of cardiotoxicity injury induced by OPPs, and 21 core targets were further determined, such as AKT1, ESR1, HSP90AA1, MAPK1, MMP9, and MAPK8, which represented the 96 potential targets and played a crucial role for the treatment of OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that cell migration, apoptotic process, protein phosphorylation and signal transduction might biological functions were associated with OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury, and OPPs-induced cardiotoxicity injury might be regulated by MAPK, PI3K-Akt, VEGF signaling pathway. Docking results manifested that the best binding target for dichlorvos, methidathion and malathion were MAPK9 (−7.1 kcal/mol), MAPK1 (−8.1 kcal/mol) and HSP90AA1 (−8.6 kcal/mol) with the lowest affinity, respectively.

Study on the construction and verification of intraoperative pressure injury risk prediction model for children undergoing cardiac surgery
Authors: Zhihao Fang, Qiuyu Chen, Sini He, Zhixiong Lin, Weixi Zheng, Pinying Chen, Liu Chen, Qing Zhuo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study applied nomogram to develop an intraoperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) risk prediction model for pediatric cardiac surgery patients, validated its predictive performance, and aims to provide evidence-based guidance for IAPI prevention in pediatric cardiac surgery.MethodsA retrospective analysis of clinical surgical data from 1,179 pediatric patients undergoing cardiac surgery at Fujian Children’s Hospital between August 2022 and November 2024 was conducted to construct a corresponding dataset. Through LASSO analysis and multivariate logistic stepwise regression, we identified high-risk factors for IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery patients and developed a ROC curve prediction model. The model’s fit and predictive performance were evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test and ROC area under the curve (AUC), with internal validation performed via bootstrap.ResultsA total of 1,179 pediatric cardiac surgery patients were included in the study, with 70 cases (5.94%) developing IAPI. LASSO regression analysis identified 10 variables, and subsequent multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that preoperative hematocrit, prothrombin time, fibrinogen, Braden-Q score, and concurrent respiratory tract infection were significant predictors of IAPI in these pediatric patients (P < 0.05). The Hosmer-Lemeshow test yielded a chi-square value of 11.251 (P = 0.188). Internal validation demonstrated the model’s sensitivity at 0.826, specificity at 0.758, and an ROC curve area under the curve (AUC) of 0.833 (0.741–0.925).ConclusionThe machine learning and nomogram-based predictive model for IAPI risk in pediatric cardiac surgery demonstrates significant predictive efficacy, providing a scientific basis for operating room nurses to identify high-risk IAPI patients early and implement timely personalized nursing interventions.Keywords: Cardiac surgery, Child, IAPI, Risk assessment, Machine learning, Predictive model

Background

Intraoperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) refers to any pressure-related tissue damage at the surgical site occurring within 48–72 h postoperatively [1]. In children, the immature skin barrier and delicate subcutaneous tissue make them far less tolerant to stressors like pressure, shear forces, and moisture compared to adults [2]. And children also have a higher body surface area-to-body weight ratio and more pronounced bony prominences. During surgical positioning, the uneven distribution of localized pressure increases the risk of pressure injuries in areas such as the occipital region, sacrococcygeal area, and heel [3, 4]. For children with congenital heart disease, chronic hypoxia, malnutrition, low body weight, inadequate tissue perfusion, and frequent use of vasoactive drugs further compromise the skin’s self-protection mechanisms, making them more susceptible to tissue damage during surgical stress [5–7]. Cardiac surgery is highly complex and invasive, often requiring prolonged extracorporeal circulation, deep anesthesia, and the use of multiple catheters and monitoring equipment. These factors collectively create a unique “multi-dimensional injury risk” [8]. Pathophysiological changes during surgery, including hypothermia, hemodynamic fluctuations, acid-base imbalances, and tissue edema, all can lead to microcirculatory disorders and exacerbate local tissue ischemia and hypoxia [9, 10]. Additionally, to maintain stable vital signs, clinicians often restrict early postoperative position changes, prolonging pressure exposure time and further aggravating the cumulative effect of skin damage. Notably [7], approximately 29.5% of pediatric cardiac surgery patients develop perioperative pressure injuries, with a significant proportion being IAPI. These injuries predominantly occur within 48–72 h postoperatively, characterized by their subtle presentation and early detection challenges. Therefore, given the high incidence of intraoperative pressure injuries and complex pathological mechanisms in pediatric cardiac surgery patients, it is imperative to establish a scientific and systematic management strategy. Through precise risk assessment and predictive interventions, the incidence of IAPI can be reduced, perioperative safety of pediatric patients ensured, and postoperative recovery promoted. While pediatric risk assessment tools like the Braden Q have been developed, their sensitivity and specificity remain inadequate for cardiac surgery subgroups, making it challenging to accurately capture the complex risk profile composed of critical variables such as cardiopulmonary bypass duration, aortic cross-clamping time, intraoperative blood loss, anesthesia depth, and preoperative nutritional status [11–13]. Most existing universal models are built on adult data, lacking consideration for children’s growth characteristics, disease-specific features, and surgical details. This results in a dual dilemma of “high-risk missed diagnoses” or “over-intervention” in clinical practice [14, 15]. Studies indicate that the incidence of hospital-acquired stress injury (HASSI) in pediatric patients with congenital heart disease ranges from 9.7% to 15.6%. The unique nature of surgical procedures—including prolonged positioning, extracorporeal circulation, and postoperative monitoring—significantly elevates the risk of IAPI in these children [6, 16, 17]. More critically, IAPI not only prolongs hospital stays, increases infection risks, and raises medical costs, but may also impede postoperative recovery in pediatric patients, potentially leading to long-term skin dysfunction that profoundly affects their growth, development, and quality of life [18]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a customized IAPI risk prediction model specifically designed for pediatric cardiac surgery patients, integrating multidimensional clinical, surgical, and physiological data [15, 19].

Machine learning, as a powerful data analysis tool, can process complex nonlinear relationships and identify interactions between variables, providing a new approach to overcome the limitations of traditional evaluation tools [20]. By leveraging advanced algorithms such as nomogram to uncover latent nonlinear relationships and interaction effects, this study aims to transition from “empirical judgment” to “precise early warning”, thereby achieving the perioperative safety management goals of “early identification, early intervention, and early prevention”. It addresses the current gap in personalized IAPI risk assessment within pediatric cardiac surgery nursing. Through comprehensive analysis of clinical characteristics, surgical parameters, and pathophysiological conditions using nomogram and big data analytics, the research provides more accurate IAPI risk predictions for pediatric cardiac surgery patients, guiding the implementation of personalized preventive measures. Early identification of high-risk infants allows for targeted interventions to reduce IAPI incidence, improving surgical outcomes and rehabilitation quality. The findings also provide valuable references for developing standardized preventive strategies against IAPI in future clinical practice.

Methods

Study design and participants

This study was approved by the hospital ethics committee, with the approval number: 2022ETKLR08061. This retrospective study included children treated with cardiac surgeries at Fujian Children’s Hospital from August 2022 to November 2024. Participants ranged in age from 1 day old to 16 years, with surgical interventions including major arterial reorientation, reconstruction of the airway during cardiac surgery, and congenital heart valve repair or replacement. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≤ 18 years; (2) surgical treatment. Exclusion criteria included: (1) pre-existing pressure injuries; (2) incomplete surgical records or missing follow-up data.

Observation indicators

The 2019 Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Pressure Ulcers [21], developed by the National Pressure Ulcer Advisory Panel (NPUAP), classify IAPI into four stages (I-IV), non-gradable cases, and deep tissue injuries.The IAPI diagnosis was jointly confirmed by CICU nurses, OR nurses, and anesthesiologists based on standardized criteria. Due to the retrospective nature of the data, formal inter-rater reliability (e.g., Cohen’s Kappa) was not calculated. The evaluation team was aware of the patients’ clinical data and was not blinded.

Data collection methods and tools

General demographics

Through preliminary research [1], we independently developed a clinical data questionnaire comprising four sections: gender, age, Body Mass Index (BMI), ward Braden Q score, and presence of comorbid respiratory diseases;

Laboratory parameters

Preoperative hemoglobin, hematocrit, ejection fraction, C-reactive protein, prothrombin time, fibrinogen; All study data were based on the last valid preoperative measurement recorded in the electronic medical record system to ensure accurate representation of the patient’s preoperative physiological status.

Surgical indicators (American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA)

physical status classification, anesthesia duration, intraoperative maximum temperature fluctuation, lowest invasive mean pressure, total extracorporeal circulation time, aortic cross-clamping duration, intraoperative blood loss, urine output, and lowest intraoperative rectal temperature) [22–24];

IAPI status in pediatric cardiac surgery

The Braden-Q score was determined by preoperative assessment during ward rounds; preoperative hemoglobin and fibrinogen levels were recorded from the final preoperative test; intraoperative rectal temperature was automatically monitored every 5 min via the surgical anesthesia information system, with hypothermia defined as body temperature < 36.0 °C and sustained duration > 30 min; The IAPI status of pediatric cardiac surgery patients is assessed and documented by ward/nursing staff from the Cardiac Intensive Care Unit (CICU), operating room nurses, and anesthesiologists before and after transfer and surgery. All other data are imported through the hospital’s electronic medical records system.

Study quality control

Throughout the planning, execution, and data collection phases of this study, rigorous quality control measures were implemented, with standardized training provided for data collection procedures. To ensure the completeness and accuracy of all survey participant information, data was entered by designated personnel and carefully verified through a two-person review process, effectively preventing data entry errors.

Statistical treatment

Statistical analysis was performed using R4.4.0 and Zstats software. For categorical data, chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was employed, while for quantitative data, t-test or rank-sum test was applied.The cutoff values of continuous variables were determined as follows: preoperative hematocrit ≥ 43% and fibrinogen ≥ 2.8 g/L were based on published literature of pediatric cardiac surgery and pressure injury research [13, 22, 23]. Prothrombin time ≥ 13.4s and LVEF ≥ 60% were determined by the Youden index from ROC curve analysis in this study [32]. A LASSO analysis was conducted to preliminarily screen predictive factors, followed by multivariate stepwise logistic regression to further identify factors influencing IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery patients, with a cumulative logit plot model constructed for prediction. A P-value ≤ 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. All statistical tests were two-sided. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test was used to validate model fit, ROC curve area was utilized to assess predictive performance, and model validation through sensitivity and specificity metrics was performed. Internal validation was achieved via 1,000 bootstrap replicates, with a significance level set at α = 0.05.

Development and Internal Validation of an IAPI risk prediction model for pediatric cardiac surgery patients

Based on the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis, five influencing factors were selected as final predictors. Using the Nomogram function from the Regression Modeling Strategies (RMS) package in R, a risk prediction model for IAP in pediatric cardiac surgery patients was established and presented as a nomogram (Fig. 3). The Hosmer-Lemeshow test showed in the training set, χ2 = 8.803, P = 0.359; in the validation set, χ2 = 11.251, P = 0.188 indicating good model fit (Figs. 4 and 5). In the training set, the model’s AUC was 0.850 (0.791–0.908), with the optimal cutoff value at 0.057, sensitivity at 0.809, and specificity at 0.737 (Fig. 6). In the validation set, the model’s AUC was 0.833 (0.741–0.925), sensitivity at 0.826, and specificity at 0.758 (Fig. 7), indicating that the predictive model demonstrates good discriminative ability. The calibration curve approaches the diagonal line, with average absolute errors of 0.006 and 0.013 for model predicted probabilities and actual occurrence probabilities in both training and validation sets, respectively, demonstrating the consistency between predicted probabilities and actual occurrence probabilities(Figs. 8 and 9).Comparative analysis revealed that the AUC values of the Braden-Q score and our predictive model were 0.687 and 0.850, respectively. Following Delong’s test, the model’s AUC was significantly higher than that of the pressure ulcer score in the ward (P < 0.001)(Figures 10; Table 4 ).

Discussion

Analysis of IAPI incidence in pediatric cardiac surgery patients

This study demonstrates that the incidence of IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery patients is 5.94%, which is considered moderate compared to the 9.7% reported in previous study [6]. This incidence highlights the importance of preventing IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery. Further analysis of IAPI occurrence sites revealed that compressed areas such as the occipital region, sacrococcygeal region, and heel are common high-risk zones, which are related to positional constraints and surgical duration. This may be due to the unique nature of cardiac surgery, where patients require prolonged immobilization postoperatively compared to general surgery cases, thereby increasing the risk of local tissue compression. Based on this, operating room nurses in clinical practice should prioritize proactive preoperative prevention by optimizing body positioning and distributing pressure through support devices such as gel pads and foam pads, particularly for high-risk areas. During surgery, micro-adjustments of body position or pressure relief maneuvers should be performed when feasible to reduce ischemic risk, with continuous monitoring of skin changes. Concurrently, enhanced collaboration with postoperative teams is essential to ensure the continuity of preventive measures. Clinical managers must establish systematic mechanisms to integrate evidence-based guidelines into perioperative pathways, develop standardized risk assessment and prevention protocols, conduct specialized training to enhance team capabilities, and introduce intelligent decompression devices. Regular audits of incidence rates and root cause analysis should be conducted for continuous improvement. Collaboration among surgical, nursing, and rehabilitation teams should be promoted to optimize positioning design and procedural efficiency. Through a comprehensive system at both operational and systemic levels, targeted reduction of injury risks in high-risk areas and prevention at the source can be achieved, which holds practical value for improving surgical safety in pediatric patients.

Analysis of factors influencing IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery

This study systematically screened and validated key predictive factors influencing perioperative acquired pressure injury (IAPI) in pediatric cardiac surgery patients by integrating LASSO regression and multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results reveal an important clinical implication: Although cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) duration and aortic cross-clamping time are widely recognized as critical intervention variables in cardiac surgery, these surgical-specific variables did not demonstrate statistical significance in the multivariate regression model constructed in this study. Additionally, compared to the traditional “surgical procedure duration” metric, patients’ “preoperative physiological status” and “systemic pathophysiological response” may be the core determinants of IAPI risk. These findings suggest that perioperative risk assessment paradigms should shift from “surgery-centered” to “patient physiological status-centered” approaches.This finding diverges from previous consensus [25], likely due to impaired homeostatic functions in anesthetized pediatric patients, where prolonged skin and tissue stress indeed increases IAPI risk. However, in complex congenital heart disease populations, temporal variables alone may fail to accurately reflect surgical trauma impact.In this study, concurrent respiratory tract infection showed a negative regression coefficient (OR = 0.009, P < 0.001), which appeared to be a protective factor statistically. This result may be related to the fact that children with preoperative respiratory infection usually received standardized anti-infection treatment, oxygen support, and perioperative monitoring before elective surgery, which improved tissue perfusion and reduced the risk of pressure injury [4, 6]. Although respiratory diseases are generally considered to reduce tissue tolerance in pathophysiology [26], based on the routine clinical management specifications of our center, patients with preoperative respiratory infection routinely receive standardized anti-infective medication, continuous oxygen inhalation and enhanced perioperative monitoring before elective surgery. Such targeted preoperative interventions are not routinely provided for children without respiratory infection, which may be the core contributor to the paradoxical protective finding in our regression analysis. The statistical results in this cohort were affected by preoperative intervention and perioperative management, leading to a paradoxical result. This finding should be explained cautiously in the specific population of pediatric cardiac surgery.

Furthermore, the study results demonstrated no correlation between preoperative left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥ 60% and reduced IAPI risk. This finding suggests that significant individual developmental differences in pediatric populations lead to marked heterogeneity in the degree of physiological disturbances induced by identical pathophysiological stimuli (e.g., extracorporeal circulation) among patients [27]. One of the most innovative findings of this study is that prothrombin time and fibrinogen serve as significant independent predictors of IAPI. This result not only reflects coagulation dysfunction but also more profoundly reveals the central role of systemic inflammation and microcirculatory dysfunction in the pathogenesis of IAPI. Extracorporeal circulation and surgical trauma are prone to induce systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), leading to a hypercoagulable state in the body. This hypercoagulability promotes fibrin deposition in microvessels and microthrombus formation, subsequently causing ischemia and hypoxia in peripheral tissues such as the skin. As an acute-phase protein, elevated fibrinogen levels are often accompanied by increased interleukin-6 (IL-6) and C-reactive protein (CRP). It exacerbates microcirculatory obstruction by increasing plasma viscosity and erythrocyte aggregation, potentially serving as a “critical point” for tissue injury in compressed areas. Chronic inflammation may further amplify the risk through endothelial damage and repair dysfunction. Therefore, fibrinogen essentially integrates the “inflammation-coagulation-microcirculation” pathological axis [28, 29], demonstrating predictive value that surpasses traditional local pressure assessment. This suggests that clinicians should prioritize perioperative systemic inflammation control as a potential strategy for IAPI prevention.

In terms of clinical evaluation, this study demonstrates that preoperative hematocrit ≥ 43% was an independent risk factor of IAPI, which was related to hyperviscosity and microcirculation disturbance in children with congenital heart disease [9, 10]. In clinical consensus, extremely low hematocrit may also lead to insufficient skin oxygen supply and increased pressure injury risk [5, 18]. In the present cohort, most children with low hematocrit had received preoperative correction such as fluid management or transfusion before surgery, so low hematocrit was not screened as an independent risk factor in multivariate regression. Both high and low hematocrit levels have clinical implications for IAPI risk.Concurrently, the AUC of the Braden-Q score was 0.687, indicating its certain predictive value, which is consistent with previous studies demonstrating the efficacy of Braden-Q as a routine screening tool [30, 31]. However, the predictive model developed in this study demonstrated superior performance, achieving an AUC of 0.850, which significantly outperformed the Braden-Q score. Compared to traditional single-assessment scales, the model constructed in this study integrates multidimensional clinical indicators, enabling more accurate identification of high-risk populations and effectively reducing the risk of missed diagnoses, thereby exhibiting greater clinical application value.These findings collectively indicate that relying solely on operative duration for early warning in pediatric cardiac surgery patients is insufficient. Clinical practice should adopt comprehensive preventive measures, including but not limited to optimizing anesthesia management to maintain hemodynamic stability, improving preoperative cardiopulmonary reserve, and enhancing risk-based individualized skin care protocols.

Effectiveness analysis of IAPI prediction model for pediatric cardiac surgery patients

In The ROC curve area under the curve (AUC) of the risk prediction model for IAPI in pediatric cardiac surgery patients reached 0.833(0.741–0.925) ,The result is higher than the 0.815 in Chen Honglin et al. ‘s thermal study and also higher than the 0.806 in Cai Jiyu et al.’ s study [22, 23], demonstrating the model’s diagnostic value [32]. The calibration curve’s proximity to the diagonal further confirms the model’s reliability. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test yielded a P-value of 0.188, indicating strong agreement between the predicted IAPI occurrence and actual observations, confirming the model’s accuracy in predicting IAPI risk. This nomogram can be utilized for preoperative evaluation and anesthesia consultation to identify high-risk pediatric patients. For high-risk patients, enhanced preventive strategies should be implemented early, such as the use of advanced decompression mattresses, more frequent position adjustments, intensified nutritional support, and optimized oxygenation monitoring. For intermediate and low-risk patients, unnecessary interventions can be avoided to optimize resource allocation. After integrating this model into electronic medical record (EMR) systems, automated risk alerts can be achieved and linked with clinical pathways, providing decision support for operating room and ICU teams, simplifying nursing workflows, and improving compliance with preventive protocols. In clinical practice, this tool enables rapid early IAPI risk stratification prior to cardiac surgery, facilitating targeted preventive measures and precise interventions. Overall, this predictive model offers an efficient approach for IAPI risk assessment, contributing to better protection of pediatric cardiac surgery patients while enhancing nursing efficiency and patient safety.

Strengths and limitations

The key strength of this study lies in its development of an intraoperative pressure injury risk prediction column chart model specifically designed for pediatric cardiac surgery patients. This innovation addresses the gap in specialized applications of existing general assessment tools. Through internal validation using multivariate Logistic regression analysis combined with Bootstrap resampling, the model demonstrated excellent discriminative power and calibration accuracy, with enhanced clinical applicability through intuitive visualization. The retrospective cohort of over 1,000 cases from the past three years, covering diverse cardiac surgical procedures, provides representative data.

However, this study has several limitations that should be considered. First, regarding variable selection, although we collected core cardiac surgery variables such as total extracorporeal circulation time and aortic cross-clamping duration, these mechanical factors were not retained in the final multivariate model. Instead, the model prioritized the child’s physiological reserve (e.g., coagulation function), which may limit the model’s ability to fully capture specific surgical procedural risks. Furthermore, the relatively small sample size in certain subgroups (e.g., specific respiratory comorbidities) may have introduced “healthy user bias” or chance findings, necessitating cautious interpretation of these specific associations.Second, this was a single-center retrospective study. The findings are therefore subject to inherent selection bias, and the generalizability of the model to other clinical settings with different patient demographics or care protocols remains unclear. Finally, due to the reliance on retrospective medical records, we lacked high-frequency continuous intraoperative physiological monitoring data (e.g., real-time blood pressure, temperature, and SpO₂ trends). Consequently, our model, based on static preoperative variables, may obscure the non-linear threshold effects and time-dependent risks associated with anesthesia duration and dynamic hemodynamic changes. Future multi-center prospective studies incorporating dense physiological data are needed to validate and refine this model.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study developed an IAPI risk score prediction model for pediatric cardiac surgery patients, achieving visualized risk assessment through graphical representation. The model enables operating room nurses to identify high-risk cases early and implement targeted preventive measures.

Primary flap coverage with internal fixation in severe open leg fractures: A case series on Gustilo grade III injuries
Authors: Samir Ben Salah, Aymen Benabdellah, Najib Abdeljaouad, Hicham Yacoubi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study presents our experience in managing 10 cases of open tibial fractures (Gustilo type IIIa or IIIb) through a single-stage surgical approach combining internal fixation and soft tissue coverage.Materials and methodsWe conducted a prospective study including all patients with Gustilo type III open tibial fractures. Each case underwent comprehensive clinical and radiological assessment, including standard radiography and computed tomography angiography (CTA). Surgical management consisted of internal osteosynthesis with immediate soft tissue reconstruction using either pedicled fasciocutaneous or muscle flaps in a single operative session.ResultsThe predominant etiology was road traffic accidents, with motorcyclists accounting for 80 % of cases. The mean patient age ranged from 18 to 45 years, and all patients were admitted within 6 h post-injury. Surgical intervention included aggressive wound debridement, vascular and nerve repair, definitive intramedullary nailing, and immediate flap coverage. Radiological bone union was achieved at a mean of 5 months postoperatively.DiscussionThe single-stage approach for open tibial fractures with concomitant soft tissue reconstruction demonstrated significant clinical and economic advantages. By eliminating the need for secondary procedures, this method reduced overall treatment duration, accelerated functional recovery, and minimized healthcare costs. The integration of fracture stabilization and soft tissue coverage in a single surgical session optimizes resource utilization while improving patient outcomes.ConclusionOur findings suggest that single-stage surgical management of Gustilo type III open tibial fractures combining definitive fixation with immediate flap coverage is both clinically effective and cost-efficient. This approach enhances recovery times, reduces financial burdens, and represents a viable strategy for complex open fracture management.Keywords: Case series, Traumatology, Open leg fractures, Gustilo stage III, Primary flap coverage, Internal fixation, Combined internal fixation and primary flap coverage

Introduction

Leg fractures, particularly those involving the tibia, are typically defined as breaks that create communication between the external environment and the fracture site. These injuries most commonly affect the diaphyseal region, whereas fractures of the proximal and distal extremities (epiphyseal-metaphyseal fractures) are more frequently studied in relation to the knee and ankle joints. The severity and complexity of associated soft tissue damage vary considerably and have been well classified by Cauchoix et al. and Gustilo, who established two clinical grading systems for these injuries (with the most severe cases classified as Gustilo type III or Duparc type 3) [1,2].

These fractures present multiple therapeutic challenges, including significant skin loss (often requiring flap coverage), frequent comminution, and associated neurovascular injuries all of which worsen prognosis and complicate management [3,4]. Currently, surgical treatment of these injuries almost universally follows a two-stage approach [5]. Notably, the literature lacks reports of a single-stage strategy combining definitive internal fixation and flap coverage during the initial intervention. Despite our limited cohort, this study aims to evaluate the feasibility and outcomes of this innovative approach.

This case series has been reported in line with the PROCESS guidelines [6].

Results

In all studied cases, the trauma resulted from road traffic accidents, with motorcyclists involved in 80 % of instances. The patients' mean age ranged between 18 and 45 years. The time to hospital admission was under 6 h for all cases and under 2 h for 40 % of patients.

The study exclusively focused on open fractures classified as Gustilo stage III, distributed as 60 % stage IIIa and 40 % stage IIIb, the latter being associated with neurovascular injuries. All wounds were clean upon admission. Initial assessment included standard anteroposterior and lateral radiographs of the leg. According to the AO classification, 50 % of fractures were simple, 30 % had a third fragment, and 20 % were comminuted. A systematic CT angiography of the lower limb was performed, revealing vascular involvement in 40 % of cases (Fig. 1).

A, B: Clinical appearance of the skin opening upon patient admission (arrows).

C, D: Initial standard radiographs in anteroposterior (D) and lateral (C) views showing a fracture of the distal quarter of the tibia associated with a lateral malleolus fracture.

Before admission to the operating room, all patients underwent saline irrigation, compressive dressing, and immobilization with a plaster splint.

The surgical procedure began with extensive wound irrigation using saline and povidone‑iodine solution, followed by meticulous debridement with resection of necrotic tissue. After redraping, a thorough wound exploration was performed to identify vascular and nerve injuries. Repair of these structures was carried out after bone stabilization, except in cases of multiple vascular ruptures, where revascularization was prioritized.

Bone stabilization was achieved via intramedullary nailing of the tibia in all cases. A lateral malleolar plate was used in 70 % of cases, and intramedullary fibular nailing in 20 %.

Coverage of skin defects was ensured by a superiorly based fasciocutaneous flap in six cases. No cases of flap necrosis were observed. Postoperative outcomes were favorable for all patients, both in terms of skin coverage and pedicled flap weaning. A rehabilitation protocol for the knee and ankle was initiated immediately after surgery.

Bone union was achieved at a mean time of 5 months, with 60 % of patients healing at 4 months and 40 % at 5 months.

Discussion

The anteromedial region of the leg, characterized by thin skin coverage and a subcutaneous tibia, presents unique challenges in trauma management [7,9]. This anatomical configuration increases the risk of tissue necrosis and complications, particularly in complex Gustilo stage III open fractures, where bone and vascular exposure are common [10,12]. Traditionally, the management of these injuries follows a two-stage protocol: initial external fixation followed by delayed flap coverage after 4–6 weeks, with possible subsequent conversion to internal osteosynthesis [13,14]. However, this conventional protocol not only significantly lengthens the therapeutic course but also exposes patients to the risks and burdens of multiple surgical interventions.

Recently, innovative alternatives have emerged. Meccariello L et al. demonstrated the efficacy of external fixation combined with VAC therapy and early coverage (3–4 days) in floating knee injuries [15], while Rollo G et al. reported promising results with the S.A.R.A. technique (using resorbable antibiotic-loaded cement) in infection prevention [16,17]. Inspired by these advances, we developed a more radical approach combining definitive internal osteosynthesis via intramedullary nailing and immediate flap coverage during initial management.

Our protocol, applied to 10 carefully selected cases (surgical delay <6 h, favorable wound condition with maintained or restored vascular patency), yielded particularly encouraging results. We observed bone union within normal timeframes without secondary infection, optimal skin healing without surgical revision, and significant benefits in terms of cost (approximately 50 % reduction), treatment duration (shortened by 2–3 months), and patient psychological comfort. Six additional cases, though excluded from this series due to insufficient follow-up, appear to support these positive trends.

While promising, this single-stage approach is not universally applicable. It requires stringent patient selection and advanced surgical expertise in microvascular reconstruction. Comparative prospective studies will be necessary to definitively validate this strategy against conventional protocols. Nevertheless, our findings suggest that this method could redefine the management of complex open fractures by combining clinical efficacy, resource optimization, and improved patient quality of life.

Conclusion

The single-stage management of Gustilo stage III open leg fractures, combining internal fixation and skin coverage using a flap, offers significant advantages. Clinically, it reduces the total treatment duration, enabling faster rehabilitation and an accelerated return to normal life for patients. Economically, it decreases costs by avoiding a second intervention, prolonged hospitalization, and additional postoperative care. This approach thus optimizes care efficiency while alleviating the financial burden on healthcare systems and patients.

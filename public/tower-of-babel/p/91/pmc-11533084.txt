PPARγ and C/EBPα enable adipocyte differentiation upon inhibition of histone methyltransferase PRC2 in malignant tumors
Authors: Jiaqi Zhao, Hui Qian, Yang An, Liping Chu, Dongxia Tan, Chenyang Qin, Qianying Sun, Yunpeng Wang, Wei Qi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Loss of terminal differentiation is a hallmark of cancer and offers a potential mechanism for differentiation therapy. Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) serves as the methyltransferase for K27 of histone H3 that is crucial in development. While PRC2 inhibitors show promise in treating various cancers, the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Here, we demonstrated that the inhibition or depletion of PRC2 enhanced adipocyte differentiation in malignant rhabdoid tumors and mesenchymal stem cells, through upregulation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG) and CEBPA. Mechanistically, PRC2 directly represses their transcription through H3K27 methylation, as both genes exhibit a bivalent state in mesenchymal stem cells. KO of PPARG compromised C/EBPα expression and impeded the PRC2 inhibitor-induced differentiation into adipocytes. Furthermore, the combination of the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone and the PRC2 inhibitor MAK683 exhibited a higher inhibition on Ki67 positivity in tumor xenograft compared to MAK683 alone. High CEBPA, PLIN1, and FABP4 levels positively correlated with favorable prognosis in sarcoma patients in The Cancer Genome Atlas cohort. Together, these findings unveil an epigenetic regulatory mechanism for PPARG and highlight the essential role of PPARγ and C/EBPα in the adipocyte differentiation of malignant rhabdoid tumors and sarcomas with a potential clinical implication.

Epigenetic mechanisms provide a programed framework to canalize cell identities along the differentiation path, playing critical roles in embryonic development. A typical example is the chromatin state of bivalency, characterized by the cooccurrence of histone H3 lysine 4 trimethylation (H3K4me3) and lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3) on the promoters of critical developmental genes (1). H3K4me3 is often associated with transcriptional activation, while H3K27me3 is linked to transcriptional repression. Bivalent promoters are open yet transcriptional poised, ready to initiate transcription swiftly upon H3K27me3 loss. This ensures the concordant and timely regulation of gene transcription during dynamic developmental processes (1, 2). Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) is the sole methyltransferase for monomethylation, dimethylation, and trimethylation of H3K27, and playing a critical role in the establishment of promoter bivalency and differentiation (3). PRC2 comprises EZH1/2, SUZ12, EED, and other accessary subunits, among which EZH1/2 is the enzymatic subunit while EED can bind to H3K27me3 and activate PRC2 allosterically (4, 5). Due to its critical role in suppressing differentiation, gain-of-function mutations, and overexpression of EZH2 have been identified in a variety of cancers, demonstrating their role in driving or facilitating oncogenesis. EZH2 and EED inhibitors have been discovered and showed efficacy in multiple cancer models (6, 7, 8, 9), particularly in B-cell lymphoma with EZH2 mutations, epithelioid sarcoma, and pediatric rhabdoid tumors with SMARCB1 loss (10, 11).

Malignant rhabdoid tumors (MRTs) are aggressive soft tissue cancers of early childhood with a poor prognosis (5-years overall survival <25%). The pathogenesis of MRTs is strongly associated with biallelic loss of SMARCB1 (INI1, hSNF5, or BAF47) in 95% of cases or SMARCA4 (BRG1) loss in 5% of cases, a hallmark of MRTs. SMARCB1 and SMARCA4 are components of mammalian switch/sucrose nonfermentable (SWI/SNF), a critical chromatin remodeling ATPase protein complex (12). The SWI/SNF complex catalyzes the opening of chromatin in enhancers and upon transcription activation. Its subunits are frequently mutated in a variety of cancers, including SMARCB1, ARID1A, or PBRM. Studies have revealed an antagonistic relationship between SWI/SNF and PRC2, and the clinical efficacy of EZH2 inhibition has been observed in treating epithelioid sarcoma (ES), a rare soft tissue sarcoma similar to MRT, where > 90% of cases exhibit loss of SMARCB1 (12). In contrast to other SWI/SNF subunit, the loss of SMARCB1 transforms specific lineages at very early embryogenesis, such as embryonic neural crest, fetal primordial germ cells, or Sox2 positive progenitors, considered the cell-of-origin for MRTs (13). MRTs may adopt different morphological phenotypes, for example, undifferentiated with neural-epithelial or mesenchyme-like characteristics, providing a pathological feature of this cancer. Consistently, transcriptomic analysis revealed these tumors exhibited neural and mesenchymal features (14).

Cancer differentiation therapy has proven to be a clinically effective approach for treating certain types of cancers, exemplified by the successful treatment of pediatric acute promyelocytic leukemia with retinoic acid (RA) (15, 16). Here, we identified the adipocyte differentiation of MRT with PRC2 inhibitor treatment, delineated the mechanism of adipocytes differentiation by PRC2 inhibition, and validated the combination with rosiglitazone as a way to enhance adipocyte differentiation and efficacy of PRC2 inhibitors for treating ES and MRT in vivo. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data confirmed that a high level of adipocyte differentiation markers, including CEBPA, PLIN1, and fatty acid binding protein 4 (FABP4), is associated with a better prognosis in sarcoma patients. Given the reported primary and acquired resistance to the PRC2 inhibitor tazemetostat in SMARCB1-deficient ES (17), our study provides mechanistic insights and highlight the tumor inhibitory role of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) and CCAAT-enhancer binding protein α (C/EBPα) with a potential clinical implication.

Results

PRC2 inhibition induces adipocyte differentiation of MRT

We and others have shown that targeting EED or dual targeting of EZH1 and EZH2 achieves superior efficacy compared to tazemetostat in MRT, likely due to a complete PRC2 inhibition (18, 19). Indeed, treatment with MAK683, an EED inhibitor in clinical study (NCT02900651) (20), significantly inhibited G401 xenograft tumors without inducing body weight loss in mice (Fig. S1, A and B). In the HE analysis of the tumor samples, we observed vacuole-like structures in the tumor tissue slices resembling the morphology of adipocytes. These structures increased with MAK683 treatment (Fig. 1, A and B). To characterize these possible “adipocytes”, we stained the tumor tissues with oil red O for neutral lipid storage and an antibody against perilipin-1, an adipocyte-specific lipid droplet coating protein. The results showed these structures were indeed adipocytes containing large lipid droplets (Fig. 1, C–E). Furthermore, MAK683 treatment significantly increased the number of adipocytes in G401 tumors in quantification (Fig. 1, B, D and E). The expression of four adipocyte markers was also assessed by quantitative PCR (qPCR), and they were all upregulated by MAK683 treatment (Fig. 1F). As our study involved grafting human cancer cells in mice, the next critical question arose: are the adipocytes differentiated from the xenograft sarcoma cells? To clarify the species origin of the adipocytes, we stained the tumor slices using species-specific antibodies against humanhuman leukocyte antigen (HLA)-A or HLA-ABC. As shown in Fig. 1, G and H, the adipocytes expressing human HLA-ABC and containing uniocular lipid droplets were observed in G401 tumors, with a higher frequency in the MAK683-treated tumors. Consistent with the G401 xenograft, MAK683 treatment not only significantly inhibited the tumor growth but also stimulated the adipocyte differentiation in another MRT A204 xenograft (Figs. 1, I–J and S1C–E). The qPCR results further confirmed an increase in the expression level of adipocyte markers following treatment with MAK683 (Fig. 1K). So, the PRC2 inhibitor induced adipocyte differentiation in xenograft MRT models in vivo.

The adipocyte differentiation by PRC2 inhibitor is independent of p16/CDKN2A and enhanced by rosiglitazone

Next, we aimed to identify the modulating factors for the adipocyte differentiation of MRT cells induced by PRC2 inhibition to gain mechanistic understanding. We and others have previously demonstrated that p16/CDKN2A is an important target gene regulated by PRC2 in MRT and other cancers (18). It has also been reported that knocking down p16/CDKN2A potentiate brown/beige adipocyte differentiation (21). So we tried to detect the adipocytes in the tumor sections of a p16-deleted (p16 KO) G401 xenograft following MAK683 treatment. However, MAK683 treatment similarly led to a significant increase in both adipocyte number and the area of Oil Red O staining (Fig. 2, A–C), along with an increase in the level of perilipin-1 and a decrease in H3K27me3 level in immunohistochemistry (IHC) studies (Fig. 2, D and E). Upon PRC2 inhibition, the bivalent promoters losing H3K27me3 may adopt a relaxed conformation (2). We hypothesized that this chromatin state may be prone to regulation by nuclear receptors, which are critical in adipogenesis. A total of 15 compounds affecting nuclear receptors were individually applied in an adipocyte differentiation procedure in the presence of MAK683 (Fig. 2, G and H). The majority of these compounds, when combined with MAK683, induced the upregulation of PPARG, CEBPA, and FABP4 expression in G401 (Figs. 2I, and S2, A and B), while only TMPA, vosilasarm, ML-180, and rosiglitazone induced the expression of PPARG and FABP4 in A204 (Fig. S2, C–D). Especially, the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone showed the strongest effect among all the compounds in both MRT cells, and it was selected for further investigation.

PRC2 inhibition or depletion promotes adipocyte differentiation of mesenchymal progenitor cells

PPARγ has long been recognized as a master regulator of adipogenesis, and its agonist rosiglitazone has been widely used in the induction of preadipocyte differentiation (22, 23). Previously, we and others successfully used the mouse mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) C3H10T1/2 to screen for stimulators of adipocyte differentiation through a procedure similar to that shown in Fig. 2G (24). As MRT has been shown originated from a neural crest-lineage and can be induced into mesenchymal differentiation (14), we next tested PRC2 inhibitors and rosiglitazone in the MSC C3H10T1/2 cells. Similar to MRT, the PRC2 inhibitors EPZ6438 and MAK683 substantially enhanced the lipid accumulation substantially (Fig. 3A). Western blot showed that both PRC2 inhibitors led to higher levels of PPARγ and perilipin-1, although the loading standard CHC was a little low in differentiated samples (Fig. 3B). And qPCR analysis also revealed that PRC2 inhibition resulted in increased expression of Pparg, Cebpα, Fabp4, and Plin1 (Fig. 3C). Primary stromal vascular fraction (SVF) cells isolated from mouse subcutaneous adipose tissue is a widely used model for adipocyte differentiation (25). The adipogenic promoting effect of EPZ6438 and MAK683 was similarly observed in SVF via oil red O staining and qPCR analysis (Figs. 3D and S3, A and B) (26). To exclude the possible off-target effects of chemical inhibitors and ensure the function of PRC2 in adipocyte differentiation, we designed single guide RNAs (sgRNAs) and constructed the C3H10T1/2 cells with Ezh2 KO (Fig. 3E). Both independent Ezh2 KO clones showed a significant enhancement of lipid accumulation upon differentiation induction with rosiglitazone (Fig. 3F). The deletion of Ezh2 resulted in a significant upregulation of multiple adipocyte transcription factors and genes, such as Pparg, Fabp4, Cebpa, and Cebpb (Fig. 3G). In addition, we generated Eed knockdown cells in C3H10T1/2 using shRNAs. Adipocyte differentiation was enhanced by EED depletion in the presence of rosiglitazone (Fig. S3, C–E).

Besides C3H10T1/2, we also constructed PPARG overexpression (PPARG-OE) in G401 to recapitulate adipocyte differentiation in the tumor (Fig. 3H). With the induction of differentiation, MAK683 treatment facilitated the expression of PPARG and FABP4 in G401, and PPARG-OE led to an even more significant increase in FABP4 expression together with MAK683 treatment (Fig. 3I). As a nuclear receptor, PPARγ is activated by ligands and works together with retinoid X receptor alpha (RXRα) to regulate genes in triglyceride metabolism, fatty acid processing and storage, and glucose regulation (27). We also examined the adipocyte marker after treatment with the retinoid X receptor alpha agonists bexarotene and RA in G401 PPARG-OE. Both bexarotene and RA exhibited the capability to further enhance the expression of ADIPOQ (Fig. 3J). Together, the inhibition or depletion of PRC2 facilitated the process of adipocyte differentiation in the presence of PPARγ and/or its agonist rosiglitazone.

It has been reported that PRC2 inhibits beige adipogenic differentiation but promotes white adipocyte differentiation in MSCs and mice (28, 29). We carefully examined the experimental details revealed in the literature and found that rosiglitazone was always included in beige/brown adipocyte differentiation procedures but omitted in white adipocyte differentiation procedures. As the combinatory effect of PRC2 inhibition/deletion and rosiglitazone was clearly observed in MSCs, mouse primary SVF, and even MRT tumor cells (Figs. 3 and S3), it is conceivable that our observation is from a different angle but likely in line with earlier studies.

PRC2 directly represses mesenchymal markers including PPARG and CEBPA

To further investigate the mechanism linking PRC2 inhibition to adipocyte differentiation in MRT, we performed RNA-seq analysis of the tumors in Fig. 1, A and J. Firstly, the overall transcriptome change by MAK683 was dramatic in G401 and A204 xenograft tumors (Fig. 4A), with more upregulated genes than downregulated genes. This is consistent with the notion that PRC2 represses target genes (18, 30). Differentially expressed genes (Log2Fold Change ≥ 1 and p adj ≤ 0.05) analysis revealed a significant number of genes associated with adipogenesis in the upregulated differentially expressed genes in both G401 and A204, such as PPARG, CEBPA, ABCG1, and ABCA3 (Fig. 4B). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) showed that multiple pathways associated with differentiation and the extracellular matrix were enriched in G401 and A204 xenografts after PRC2 inhibition (Figs. 4, C–D and S4A). To focus on the adipocytes, integrated analysis was performed using the H3K27me3 chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-seq and ATAC-seq data from MAK683-treated G401 and those from adipogenic-differentiated MSCs (18, 31). Clustering analysis indicated a common cluster of genes with decreased H3K27me3 in MAK683 treated G401 and elevated H3K27ac in adipogenic-differentiated MSCs (Fig. S4B). We compared the ATAC-seq data for DNA accessibility from MAK683-treated G401 with those from adipogenic-differentiated MSCs (31). About 43% of the peaks with increased accessibility after adipogenic differentiation (3086 out of 7128) overlapped with those peaks with increased accessibility after MAK683 treatment, and GSEA analysis of the overlapped peaks also indicated the function of differentiation (Fig. 4, E and F). The peaks matched to specific genes including PPARG, TWIST1, KLF4, KLF5, ZEB2, KIT, and ACSL1 (Figs. 4G and S4C). HOMER motif analysis was performed on the MAK683-upregulated genes from RNA-seq and ATAC-seq, and identified the enrichment of transcription factors including Zic, PPAR, and ACSL1 (Fig. S4D). Intriguingly, the ChIP-seq data of G401 suggested PPARG is a direct target gene of PRC2, since the promoter H3K27me3 level is as high as the other PRC2 targets (Figs. 4G and S4C). Furthermore, the data from the Encode database indeed support PPARG and CEBPA as bivalent genes in embryonic stem cells and progenitors, which lose H3K27me3 in adult lung and human adipocytes (Fig. S4E). The EZH2 binding on the promoters showed a consistent pattern (Fig. 4H). We validated the upregulation of PPARG, CEBPA, ZEB2, TWIST-1, ACSL1, FABP4, and other genes by qPCR (Figs. 4, I–J and S4F). ChIP-qPCR also validated that the H3K27me3 on PPARG promoter was high in G401 and significantly diminished by MAK683 treatment (Fig. 4K). Together, these results indicate PPARG and CEBPA are directly regulated by PRC2 and the upregulation following PRC2 inhibition is a mechanism driving the adipocyte differentiation in tumors of mesenchymal lineage.

PPARG and CEBPA are required for the adipocyte differentiation by PRC2 inhibition

It is well-established that PPARG plays a crucial role as a transcription activator in adipogenesis and lipid-related processes (32, 33, 34). Following our finding that PPARG is directly regulated by PRC2 in MRT, we proceeded to generate a G401 PPARG-KO cell to assure its role in the observed adipocyte differentiation (Fig. S5A). MAK683 still exhibited significant tumor inhibition on both NT and PPARG-KO tumors in vitro and in vivo (Figs. 5A and S5B). HE staining revealed that PPARG-KO tumor treated with MAK683 exhibited no adipocyte differentiation (Fig. 5, B and C). The PPARG-KO tumor also showed a significant reduction in perilipin-1 levels compared to the NT group with MAK683 treatment (Fig. 5, B and D), indicating the adipocyte differentiation was indeed blocked by PPARG-KO. Combination of PRC2 inhibitor with PPARγ agonist better induces adipocyte differentiation and inhibits Ki67 positivity in tumor

Thiazolidinedione compounds, such as rosiglitazone and pioglitazone, are agonists for PPARγ and display activity to enhance insulin sensitivity and adipogenesis in mice and humans (37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42). However, it is still controversial whether rosiglitazone has antitumor effect. We next performed the combination treatment of MAK683 and Rosi in G401 xenografts. The compound administration did not cause any observable adverse effects or body weight loss (Fig. S5F). MAK683 alone and combination with rosiglitazone both showed clear tumor growth inhibition, although the combination did not show further tumor-size reduction compared to the MAK683 alone (Fig. 6A). The expression of adipocyte-related genes, such as PPARG, FABP4, and ADIPOQ, was upregulated in the combination group (Fig. 6B). The results from HE and oil red O staining and perilipin-1 IHC demonstrated that the combined treatment indeed further enhanced the adipocyte differentiation compared to either treatment alone (Fig. 6, C–F). The quantification of H3K27me3 levels revealed a significant reduction in the MAK683 treated groups, compared to the control groups (Fig. 6, C and G). Notably, the administration of rosiglitazone already resulted in a considerable reduction in the Ki67 level. As MAK683 also decreased Ki67, a further reduction of Ki67 level was detected in the MAK683 and rosiglitazone combination group in G401 xenograft (Fig. 6, C and H). High level of adipocyte differentiation markers associates with better prognosis in sarcoma

Adipocyte differentiation of tumor cells by drug treatment has recently been reported in breast cancer and liposarcoma. The MEK inhibitor induced the conversion of breast cancer to adipocytes and prevented cancer metastasis, whereas in liposarcoma, adipocyte differentiation induction by compounds was considered a therapeutic strategy (36, 43). Our data indicated enhanced adipocyte differentiation induction by PRC2 inhibitor and PPARγ agonist in MRT from mesenchymal lineage. As MRT is a subtype of soft tissue sarcoma, we next expanded the validation to the mesenchymal originated sarcoma (SARC) cohort in TCGA and conducted Kaplan–Meier survival analysis on the expression level of adipocyte differentiation markers. These markers were selected based on literature and they all showed the highest expression in MAK683 and rosiglitazone combination treatment of G401 tumor xenograft (Fig. 7A) (44). This cohort includes 123 cases distributed in more than seven subtypes of sarcoma, with the majority (56.1%) being leiomyosarcoma (Fig. 7B). The analysis revealed a significant positive association between high CEBPA expression and longer overall survival in individuals with sarcoma. Additionally, high levels of PLIN1, FABP4, and ALDH1A1 also associated with a favorable outcome, while COL1A1 did not show the association (Fig. 7, C–G). In recent studies, CEBPA has been identified as a tumor suppressor in dedifferentiated liposarcoma (43) as well as in certain types of solid tumors (45, 46). Moreover, the level of CEBPA is also under the regulation of PPARG, which showed no significant correlation with sarcoma prognosis, likely due to the activation of PPARγ depending on the availability of its ligand (Fig. 7H) (47). Notably, CEBPA protein level was highly upregulated by MAK683 and rosiglitazone combination (Fig. 7I), which may reflect the activation of PPARγ and thus be positively correlated with the overall survival of sarcoma patients. Discussion

This study provides multiple pieces of evidence to illustrate a pharmacological mechanism for PRC2 inhibitors, inducing adipocyte differentiation via upregulating PPARG and CEBPA (Fig. S6). First, adipocyte differentiation was observed and characterized with human origin (human HLA-A and HLA-ABC IHC confirmation) in the xenograft MRT tumors treated with the EED inhibitor MAK683 (Fig. 1). The adipocytes in xenograft tumors were characterized by at least three methods: (1) HE and oil-red O detection of lipid droplets in tissue slices; (2) perilipin-1 antibody detection of this specific adipocyte marker (perilipin-1 is only expressed in adipocytes, whereas other cell types use perilipin-2 and -3 for lipid droplet assembly); (3) the qPCR detection of adipogenic markers with primers for human gene sequences. Adipogenic pathways and genes were also upregulated in MAK683-treated tumors in RNA-seq profiles (Fig. 4). Second, PPARG and CEBPA show the signature of a typical bivalent gene and are direct target of PRC2 in adipocyte differentiation of MRT. The promoter regions of PPARG and CEBPA carry both H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 in embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and embryonic kidney, while losing the H3K27me3 in adult lung and A549 cell (Fig. S4). MAK683 treatment in MRT cells also diminishes H3K27me3, and the expression of PPARG is upregulated (Fig. 4). On the contrary, PPARG deletion, which also led to the decreased expression of C/EBPα, completely abolished the adipocyte differentiation (Fig. 5). Third, PRC2 inhibitor and PPARγ agonist may synergize to enhance the differentiation of tumor cells into adipocytes, especially in MRT and sarcoma with a mesenchymal lineage. The inhibitory role of PRC2 in adipocyte differentiation in the presence of the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone was clarified in MSCs and tumor cells using various chemical inhibitors and genetic depletion/activation (Fig. 3). The combination of MAK683 and rosiglitazone treatment in tumor xenografts induced significant adipocyte differentiation and a decrease in Ki67-marked proliferating tumor cells (Fig. 6).

Previous studies have elucidated that PRC2 inhibitors induce neural differentiation and cell cycle arrest through the upregulation of p16/CDKN2A in treating SMARCB1-deficient ES and MRT (14, 18, 19, 48). It has been recently reported that p16, RB1, or NSD1 loss would enable tazemetostat resistance, and combination with CDK4/6 or Aurora kinase inhibitor would enhance cell cycle arrest and tumor growth inhibition (17, 18, 49). Our work provides a novel direction that PRC2 inhibitors may induce adipocyte differentiation, especially when combined with the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone, which may reinforce adipocyte differentiation to enhance the efficacy of the PRC2 inhibitor. Rosiglitazone has shown to induce cellular differentiation in leukemia (50, 51, 52), although not specifically adipocytes. Our data here focus on adipocyte differentiation and confirm the efficacy of adipocyte-inducing therapy in the in vivo experiments. The levels of Ki67 exhibited a mild but significant decrease in the rosiglitazone-treated tumors (Fig. 6H), suggesting that PPARγ activation alone may block proliferation somewhat already, likely through adipocyte differentiation. Consistently, oil red O positivity, perilipin-1 staining, and adipocyte count were also mildly upregulated in rosiglitazone treatment. The combination of PRC2 inhibitor and rosiglitazone led to a strong decrease of Ki67, likely due to not only the cell cycle arrest by the PRC2 inhibitor but also the enhanced adipocyte differentiation. The results of adipocyte differentiation support this notion (Fig. 6, D–F). One potential caveat is that the directly measured tumor size was not significantly smaller in the rosiglitazone-treated group as vehicle (Fig. 6A), suggesting treating the MRT with Rosi to induce adipocyte differentiation may not inhibit tumor growth. Similarly, the tumor size was not further decreased in combo-treated group comparing with MAK683-treated tumors (Fig. 6A). We reasoned that this may reflect that the differentiated adipocytes are not eliminated although they are less malignant. Further studies are required to strengthen the presence and importance of adipocyte differentiation in more tumor models and the clinical setting.

Our data show that PPARG and CEBPA are bivalent genes in ESCs and MSCs and may be regulated by PRC2 in MSCs and MRT cells. There were only a few reports on the regulation of PPARG and CEBPA by PRC2 previously. In one study, PRC2 was found to be aberrantly recruited to PPARG to silence its expression in myeloma-associated bone marrow adipocytes (53). In another report, it was shown that interferon-γ induced the recruitment of EZH2 to MERTK, PPARG, and RANK loci to repress these antiinflammatory genes in human macrophages (54). Different from these earlier reports, we studied the role of PRC2 in PPARG and CEBPA regulation in adipocyte differentiation. We identified the bivalency of PPARG and CEBPA through searching the Encode database. Considering the mutual regulation of PPARγ and C/EBPα loop, their coherent regulation by PRC2 is perceivable. In one study focusing on bivalency, PPARG was also listed as an experimentally validated bivalent gene in ESCs through ChIP-reChIP study (2). So, our results are consistent with these reports and further expand the functional study of this regulation in adipocyte differentiation of MSCs and sarcomas.

In summary, PRC2 inhibition may block tumor growth via multiple mechanisms and inducing adipocyte differentiation is one mechanism identified in MRTs and sarcomas. Our work also highlights the essential role of PPARγ and C/EBPα in the adipocyte differentiation with a potential clinical implication.

Experimental procedures

Cell culture and reagents

Cells were obtained from American Type Culture Collection and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% (v/v) CO2. G401 and A204 were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM) (Invitrogen, 12100046) with 10% (vol/vol) fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Lonsera, S711–001S), 0.055 mM 2-mercaptoethanol (Sigma-Aldrich, M3148) and 100 U/ml penicillin and 100 μg/ml streptomycin (P/S). C3H10T1/2 and SVF were cultured in DMEM and F-12 Nutrient Mixture (Gibco, 21700075) at 1:1 ratio with 10% FBS and P/S. HEK293T were cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS and P/S. All cells are authenticated by short tandem repeat profiling and confirmed of mycoplasma-free. Chemical compounds EPZ6438, MAK683 and the nuclear receptor compounds listed in Fig. 2G were purchased from MCE and Selleck.

Isolation and culture of primary adipocytes

Inguinal fat tissues were dissected from 6 ∼ 8-week-old male mice, chopped to small pieces and digested in digestion buffer containing 1.3 mM CaCl2, 1 mg/ml collagenase A (Roche, 10103578001) and 1 mg/ml Dispase II neutral protease (Roche, 04942078001) in PBS with 200 rpm shaking for 120 min at 37 °C, followed by washing with DMEM/F12 medium containing 10% FBS. Digested tissue suspensions were filtered through 70 μM cell strainers, and the flow-through was centrifuged at 700 g for 10 min. At this condition, the SVF cells were pelleted, and mature adipocytes were separated as the floating layer. The pellet of SVF was collected and used in the compound treatment and adipocyte differentiation.

Adipocyte differentiation

C3H10T1/2 and SVF cells were maintained in regular culture medium at low density. To induce adipocyte differentiation in C3H10T1/2 and SVF, cells were seeded at high density in regular medium and cultured until 2 days after confluence, and then the cells were switched to DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 60 nM insulin, 1 μM dexamethasone, 0.5 mM isobutylmethylxanthine and P/S with or without 1 μM Rosi (marked as day 0). After 3 days, the cells were changed to fresh medium supplemented with 60 nM insulin and P/S with or without 1 μM Rosi for another 2 days until day 7. To induce G401 cell differentiation, cells were incubated in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 867 nM insulin, 10 μM dexamethasone, and 2 μM Rosi at day 0, and in day 3, the cells were then cultured in fresh medium supplemented with 867 nM insulin and 2 μM Rosi for another 2 days until day 7.

CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing and shRNA

Three pairs of gRNAs targeting human PPARG (NG_011749.1) were designed using Chop-Chop website. The gRNA or shRNA oligos were synthesized, annealed and subcloned into the lentiCRISPR v2 (Plasmid #52961, Addgene). G401 were transfected with the resultant plasmids, cultured for 48 h, and then applied to the single cell sorting for puromycin-resistant cell by flow cytometer. Single cell clones were initially cultured in wells of 96-well plate, and then gradually expanded in larger volume for sequencing validation and further experiments. Two pairs of guide RNAs targeting mouse EED were designed using Sigma-Aldrich.cn. The shRNA oligos were synthesized, annealed, and subcloned into the pLKO.1-TRC Cloning vector (Plasmid #10878, Addgene).C3H10T1/2 were transfected with the resultant plasmids, cultured for 48 h for further analysis. All oligonucleotides used are listed with their sequences in Table S1.

PPARG overexpression cell line construction

To generate G401 PPARG-OE cell line, we amplified the coding region of human PPARG (NM_001330615.4) from G401 cells by standard PCR and cloned into plvx-Ires-Puro lentiviral vectors. G401 were transfected with the resultant plasmids, cultured for 48 h for further analysis. All oligonucleotides used are listed with their sequences in Table S1.

Lentiviral packaging and infection

HEK293T cells were cotransfected with plasmid DNAs of a lentiviral vector, packaging plasmid psA2X and envelope plasmid pMD2G using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) with the ratio of 4:3:1. The supernatant was collected 48 and 72 h after transfection and filtered by 0.45 μm filter. The fresh filtered lentiviruses were used to infect the target cells G401 in the presence of 8 μg/ml polybrene (Merck, TR-1003-G). After 72 h, cells were treated with puromycin in medium and refreshed every 2 days. After about 1 week, puromycin resistant cells were cultured under the selection of 0.7 μg/ml puromycin for 6 days before further experiments.

RNA extraction and real-time qPCR

Total RNA samples were prepared using Trizol reagent (Sigma-Aldrich) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Subsequently, 2 μg of total RNA were reserve-transcribed to complementary DNA using M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase (Promega). And complementary DNA was analyzed by real-time qPCR through the 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Relative mRNA levels were calculated using the comparative cycle threshold method and normalized to the levels of mouse TATA-box binding protein (TBP) or human GADPH. The average of the control group was set to be one and all the results were represented as the relative mRNA expression. All primers used are listed with their sequences in Table S1. All reactions were performed in triplicates.

Western blotting

For Western blotting, whole-cell lysates were prepared in 1 × SDS loading buffer containing protease inhibitor cocktail (TargetMol, C0001) and boiled at 95 °C for 10 min before loading. Then, 10 to 20 μl of lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane using the wet-transfer method. The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk in Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween 20 (TBST) for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated in primary antibodies for overnight at 4 °C. The next day, the membranes were washed in TBST (3 × 10 min) and then incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature. After washes with TBST (3 × 10 min), Pierce enhanced chemiluminescence substrate was added onto the membrane and incubated for 2 min before the chemiluminescent signal detection. The following commercial antibodies were used: H3 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9715), H3K27me3 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9733), PPARγ (Cell Signaling Technology, 2435), β-actin (Cell Signaling Technology, 3700), CHC (BD, 610500), UCP1 (Abcam, 10983), FABP4 (Cell Signaling Technology, 2583), perilipin-1 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9349). All primary antibodies were used at a 1:1000 dilution in 3% bovine serum albumin in TBST. Secondary antibodies were used at a 1:5000 dilution in 5% non-fat milk in TBST.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation

G401 cells (1 × 106) were seeded in 10-cm dishes and treated with dimethylsulfoxide or 3 μM of MAK683 for 3 days. Cells were fixed with 0.7% formaldehyde in culture medium for 10 min at room temperature and the reaction was quenched with 0.125 M glycine sharply. Then remove media and wash the cell twice with cold PBS with protease inhibitor mix. Cells were scraped off the plate, and the pellets were collected, lysed at the volume of 4 × 106 per 300 μl using SDS lysis buffer for 10 min on ice, and then proceed to sonication. Each sample was sonicated for 8 min, 15 s on, and 45 s off with 80% output (Qsonica 4905 Chiller). Sonicated samples were diluted with ChIP dilution buffer, and applied to 1 h preclear with 20 μl protein A + G agarose beads. After that, input samples were collected, and every 300 μl of cell lysate samples were applied into ChIP with 1 μl IgG, 5 μl H3 antibody, and 5 μl H3K27me3 antibody. Antibody-chromatin complex was incubated overnight at 4 °C, and then captured by 60 μl protein A + G agarose beads for 1 h at 4 °C. The beads were washed in low salt buffer once, high salt buffer once, LiCl buffer once, and Tris-EDTA buffer twice. DNA-protein complex was eluted twice with elution buffer (1% SDS and 0.1 M NaHCO3), eluted chromatin was decrosslinked at 65 °C for 4 h with 5 M NaCl and 2 μl Proteinase K. Chromatin DNA was purified with phenol-chloroform method.

Mouse xenograft studies

The animal study design and performance were evaluated and approved by Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of ShanghaiTech University under the document number of 20201225001 and following the internationally recognized guidelines on animal welfare. To establish xenograft models, G401 WT, G401 NT, or PPARG-KO cells were washed and resuspended in cold PBS, mixed with Matrigel at 1:1 (v/v) to reach 2.5 × 106 cells per 200 μl, and then the tumor cell mixtures were injected subcutaneously into the right flank of female Balb/c nude mice at 200 μl per mice (5–6 weeks of age). A204 xenograft tumor was similarly established at the seeding density of 1 × 106 cells per 200 μl.

When tumor volume reached ∼150 mm3, the mice were numbered and random-grouped into vehicle, 20 mg/kg rosiglitazone, 50 mg/kg MAK683 and combined treatment groups. The mice with tumor size out of the range were not included in the study. Empirically, at least six mice were included for each group. For compound daily dosing, rosiglitazone and MAK683 was resolved and suspended in water with 0.5% hydroxypropylmethylcellulose and 0.5% Tween-20. Mice weight and tumor volumes are measured and recorded every 3 days. Tumor volumes were calculated based on perpendicular length and width caliper measurements using the following formula: tumor volume (mm3) = 0.5 × (length × width2).

Oil red O staining, immunohistochemistry, and immunofluorescence

For oil red O staining, freshly isolated tumor tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 24 to 48 h at room temperature. To make oil red O stock solution, 0.14 g Oil Red O powder (Sigma-Aldrich, 00625) is dissolved in 40 ml of isopropanol. When in use, the stock solution is mixed with distilled water in a ratio of 3:2 to make the working/staining solution. Tissues were embedded in optimal cutting temperature embedding compound and frozen sections (6～10 μm thick) were mounted on slides. After air dry, tissues slices are washed in distilled water for three times followed by soaking into 60% isopropanol for 5 min at room temperature. Then the slices are stained in oil red O staining solution, covered, and kept in the dark for 10 min. After wash in distilled water for three times, slides are counter-stained with hematoxylin staining solution for 2 min, washed, and mounted for microscope imaging. For the culture of the differentiated C3H10T1/2 or SVF cells, they were fixed with 4% PFA and then stained with a similar procedure.

For immunohistochemistry staining, freshly isolated tumor tissues were fixed in 4% PFA for 24 to 48 h at room temperature. Tissues were embedded in paraffin, sectioned at thicknesses of 5 μm, deparaffinized, and rehydrated through graded concentrations of ethanol in water. Sections were then stained with HE. For antigen retrieval, sections were boiled in a microwave for 15 min in 10 mM citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for Ki67 (Abcam, ab16667; dilution 1:100), H3K27me3 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9733; dilution 1:200), perilipin-1 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9349; dilution 1:200); or in 10 mM Tris-EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) for HLA-A (Abcam, ab52922; dilution 1:100), HLA-ABC (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA1-80454; dilution 1:100). After cooling down to room temperature, endogenous peroxidase was inactivated with 3% H2O2 in methanol. Then the sections were washed with dH2O twice, blocked with 5% normal goat serum in PBS with 0.25% Triton X-100 for 1 h at room temperature followed by primary antibodies incubation for overnight at 4 °C. Followed by incubation with the biotinylated secondary antibody, the sections were then color rendered by DAB substrate kit (Cell Signaling Technology, 8059s) and visualized by Olympus VS120-S6-W.

For immunofluorescence staining, sections were similarly processed then stained with primary antibodies for HLA-ABC (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA1-80454; dilution 1:100) and perilipin-1 (Cell Signaling Technology, 9349; dilution 1:200) for overnight at 4 °C. The next day, tissue sections were washed in PBS (3 × 10 min) and incubated with fluorochrome-conjugated secondary antibody (1:1000 in PBS with 1% bovine serum albumin) for 1 h at room temperature. Then, the tissue sections were washed in PBS (3 × 15 min) and nuclei were stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (Vector labs, H-1200–10). Images were acquired with a Zeiss LSM 880 confocal microscope.

Next-generation sequencing data analysis

Sequences were aligned to the human genome (hg38). All of the methods were used as previously described (18). For RNA-seq data analysis. mRNA levels of genes in triplicate samples were calculated as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). We determined differential gene expression using R package (https://cran.r-project.org/src/base/R-4/R-4.0.0.tar.gz) DESeq2 (55) (version v1.20.0; https://www.bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/src/contrib/DESeq2_1.20.0.tar.gz/) with a false discovery rate threshold of 0.05 and an Log2 Fold Change threshold of ± 1. About ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq data analysis. Spike-in normalization was performed on G401 H3K27me3 samples. These reads were used to generate binding site with MACS2 (56) (version 2.2.7.1; https://files.pythonhosted.org/packages/1f/a7/973a7867efc40ff87d040f3adac29a26fcde76edbf10ed80733c1caafbf6/MACS2-2.2.9.1.tar.gz) and featureCounts (57) (version v1.5.3; https://downloads.sourceforge.net/project/subread/subread-2.0.6/subread-2.0.6-Windowsx86_64.tar.gz?ts=gAAAAABm9YhfeUv54AVHziKtdcuwv2eYOYIqGX1AYdvhdjFvpem2mSoJhUGfKBhlmHDGfy1uxQ7sNOSOb7GwDaZV_yHsKZTqFA%3D%3D&r=https%3A%2F%2Fsourceforge.net%2Fprojects%2Fsubread%2Ffiles%2Flatest%2Fdownload).

Enrichment analysis

Fisher-exact test

Through Python (version 3.7.0; https://www.python.org/ftp/python/3.12.6/python-3.12.6-amd64.exe) and its Scipy (58) (version 1.2.1; https://files.pythonhosted.org/packages/62/11/4d44a1f274e002784e4dbdb81e0ea96d2de2d1045b2132d5af62cc31fd28/scipy-1.14.1.tar.gz) module, the enrichment level of each gene set was calculated according to Fisher's exact test method. Database.C5.GO gene sets download from Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp, version v7.2).

Motif enrichment analysis

Enriched motifs across cell differentiation genes were determined using HOMER findMotifsGenome.pl against genome-background with modified parameters (-mask -cpg) (59).

Visualization

Data visualization was performed using Python’s Matplotlib (60) (version 3.1.0; https://files.pythonhosted.org/packages/9e/d8/3d7f706c69e024d4287c1110d74f7dabac91d9843b99eadc90de9efc8869/matplotlib-3.9.2.tar.gz) and Seaborn (27) (version 0.9.0; https://files.pythonhosted.org/packages/86/59/a451d7420a77ab0b98f7affa3a1d78a313d2f7281a57afb1a34bae8ab412/seaborn-0.13.2.tar.gz) module, R's Gviz (61) (version 1.30.3; https://github.com/ivanek/Gviz), ComplexHeatmap (62) (version 2.12.1; https://github.com/jokergoo/ComplexHeatmap) and ggplot2 (62) (version 3.4.0; https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2) packages and DeepTools software (version 3.4.3/3.5.1; https://github.com/deeptools/deepTools) (63).

Survival analysis

Gene expression matrix and patients clinical data download from TCGA (TCGA-SARC, phs000178). We retained samples with high EZH2 expression levels (top 50%) and recorded clinical information and obtained 123 patients. We used Python's lifelines (version 0.21.5) (64) module for survival analysis for all genes strongly associated with patient survival. We used log-rank test method to calculate p values and univariate cox regression to calculate hazard ratios. We use the minimum p-value approach to find the cut point in continuous gene expression measurement for grouping patients (65).

Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Statistical analysis for qPCR and tumor tissue section staining were carried out using GraphPad Prism nine (https://www.graphpad.com/scientificsoftware/prism/www.graphpad.com/scientific-software/prism/). Data are shown as mean ± sd or mean ± s.e.m. for qPCR data as noted in the legend. The variance within each group is similar. Statistical significance was determined using the unpaired t test or ANOVA test as annotated in the legend. p < 0.05 was considered significant. All cellular experiments were repeated for multiple (two or more) times with biological repeats.

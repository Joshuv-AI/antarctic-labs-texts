Intelligent optimization of natural gas pipeline compressor stations
Authors: Zixuan Zhao, Lin Li, Zhenxiang Sun, Qian Lv, Xingyu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryNatural gas compressor stations account for a substantial share of energy consumption in long-distance pipeline systems, making operational optimization important for improving efficiency and reducing emissions. This study addresses the load allocation problem of parallel compressor units by formulating a mixed-integer nonlinear optimization model that incorporates operational constraints and thermodynamic characteristics. A hybrid intelligent optimization framework is developed to improve solution quality and robustness for highly constrained operating conditions. In a representative compressor station case, the optimized strategy reduces power consumption from approximately 32 MW–24.58 MW, corresponding to an energy saving of about 23%. These findings demonstrate the potential of intelligent optimization to support energy-efficient and low-carbon operation of natural gas transportation infrastructure.Keywords: CO2 emission reduction, low-carbon operation, energy-environment nexus, compressor stations, natural gas pipeline systems, environmental decision support

Introduction

Since the Industrial Revolution, oil has been one of the world’s dominant energy sources and has substantially driven global economic development.1 With increasing concerns regarding sustainable development and climate change mitigation, the transition toward cleaner and lower-carbon energy systems has become an irreversible trend.2 Among available alternatives, natural gas has been widely regarded as an important transitional energy source due to its higher combustion efficiency and lower pollutant emissions compared with conventional fossil fuels.3 Consequently, natural gas has become increasingly important in electricity generation,4 industrial fuel supply,5 and chemical production,6 contributing to low-carbon industrial development.7 Because natural gas resources are often geographically separated from major consumption centers,8 efficient and reliable long-distance transportation has become essential to ensure energy security and supply stability.9 Among various transportation approaches, pipeline transmission has emerged as the dominant mode for large-scale overland natural gas delivery due to its continuity, reliability, and cost-effectiveness.10,11,12,13

Pipeline transportation is inherently energy-intensive because natural gas experiences substantial pressure losses caused by friction and terrain variations during long-distance transmission.14 To maintain stable flow and operating pressure, compressor stations distributed along the pipeline play a critical role in pressure restoration and system stability.15 Compressor units, particularly electrically driven centrifugal compressors, have become the dominant configuration in modern pipeline systems because of their high efficiency and operational flexibility.16,17 However, compressor stations are also the major energy consumers within natural gas pipeline systems, accounting for a considerable proportion of operational costs and directly affecting both economic performance and environmental impacts.18,19

A typical compressor station consists of multiple compressor units operating in parallel, which gives rise to a compressor load allocation problem.20 The optimization task involves determining compressor start-stop states and allocating load among active units while satisfying complex operational constraints associated with gas transmission, including flow rate, pressure balance, compressor operating ranges, and anti-surge requirements. Consequently, the problem can be formulated as a highly constrained mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem characterized by coupled discrete-continuous decision variables, nonlinear compressor performance, and a nonconvex feasible region with multiple local optima. These characteristics make efficient and reliable optimization particularly challenging for conventional methods.

Because compressor stations dominate energy consumption in long-distance natural gas pipelines, improving load allocation efficiency has become an important strategy for reducing operational costs and environmental impacts. Traditional operating decisions often rely on empirical rules or simplified heuristics,21 which may struggle to achieve satisfactory performance under complex operating conditions. Consequently, advanced optimization approaches are increasingly required to improve decision quality and operational efficiency.

To improve compressor station operation, previous studies have explored multiple optimization paradigms, including mechanistic modeling, uncertainty-aware optimization, and intelligent optimization methods. Early studies mainly focused on developing physics-based models to accurately characterize compressor operation and thermodynamic behavior, thereby establishing the foundation for optimization-oriented research.22 As system complexity and operational uncertainty increased, subsequent research gradually shifted toward robust and uncertainty-aware optimization frameworks to improve decision reliability under realistic operating conditions.23

In recent years, intelligent optimization algorithms have received increasing attention for compressor station operation and energy-efficient scheduling. Existing studies have improved optimization performance through hybrid intelligent algorithms, multi-population metaheuristics, and emerging swarm-based methods, demonstrating enhanced global search capability and operational efficiency.24,25,26,27,28,29 More recently, machine learning and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches have been introduced to compressor and natural gas pipeline optimization, enabling data-driven operational decision-making under dynamic conditions.30,31 Although learning-based methods exhibit strong potential for high-dimensional control tasks, their implementation often depends on large volumes of historical data and extensive retraining under varying operating conditions. In contrast, physics-consistent optimization frameworks remain attractive for constrained industrial applications because they explicitly incorporate thermodynamic relationships, nonlinear compressor characteristics, and operational feasibility.

Despite recent progress, existing intelligent optimization approaches often focus on isolated algorithmic improvements, such as hybridization or enhancement of a single search mechanism, which may limit convergence robustness and solution precision for highly constrained engineering problems. When applied to compressor station load allocation, these limitations become particularly pronounced because of the coupled discrete-continuous decision variables, nonlinear compressor behavior, and strict operational constraints. Furthermore, the application of gray wolf optimization (GWO) and its variants to parallel compressor station load allocation in natural gas pipelines remains insufficiently explored.31

To address these challenges, this study develops a hybrid multi-strategy improved gray wolf optimizer (IGWO) for compressor station load allocation. The proposed framework integrates three complementary strategies: a time-varying nonlinear convergence factor to improve the balance between exploration and exploitation, a dynamic leader perturbation mechanism to reduce premature convergence, and an adaptive weighting strategy to enhance solution stability. The performance of IGWO is evaluated using benchmark functions and a representative compressor station optimization case to assess its effectiveness under constrained operating conditions.

The primary contribution of this study lies in developing a physics-consistent optimization framework for compressor station load allocation under realistic operational constraints. Rather than emphasizing generic algorithm development alone, the proposed approach explicitly considers compressor start-stop decisions, nonlinear efficiency-dependent power consumption, and thermodynamic coupling within a constrained MINLP framework. By improving modeling rigor and solution reliability at the station level, this work provides a scalable decision-support framework for energy-efficient operation in natural gas pipeline systems and may support future extensions to multi-station and network-level optimization problems.

Study implications

This study developed a hybrid intelligent optimization framework for improving the energy efficiency of compressor stations in long-distance natural gas pipeline systems. By integrating a MINLP formulation with a multi-strategy IGWO, the proposed framework addressed compressor load allocation under realistic operational constraints.

In the six-unit compressor station case study, IGWO reduced power consumption to 24.58 MW, corresponding to an energy saving of approximately 23% relative to baseline operation. The framework also demonstrated stable optimization performance across repeated runs, indicating favorable robustness and computational consistency.

Beyond operational improvements, the optimized strategy showed potential environmental benefits through indirect reductions in CO2 emissions associated with electricity consumption. The estimated annual reduction ranged from approximately 35,720 to 50,640 t CO2, depending on electricity emission factors, highlighting the broader implications of intelligent optimization for energy-intensive infrastructure.

Overall, the proposed framework provides a physically consistent and computationally reliable approach for compressor station load allocation and may serve as a foundation for future extensions toward dynamic and uncertainty-aware optimization.

Limitations of the study

Although the proposed framework demonstrated favorable performance under steady-state operating conditions, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the present formulation assumes quasi-steady compressor operation and does not explicitly capture transient pipeline dynamics or short-term load fluctuations. Extending the framework toward dynamic or multi-period optimization represents an important direction for future work.

Second, the current model adopts deterministic operating conditions and does not explicitly account for uncertainties associated with demand variation, gas composition changes, or measurement noise. Future studies may incorporate uncertainty-aware or robust optimization strategies to improve operational reliability.

Third, extending the optimization framework from single-station operation to network-level coordination involving multiple interconnected compressor stations may substantially increase computational complexity and, therefore, warrants further investigation.

Development of the IGWO framework

The standard gray wolf optimizer (GWO) was adopted as the baseline optimization framework for comparison and subsequent improvement.31 GWO is a swarm intelligence algorithm inspired by the cooperative hunting behavior and hierarchical structure of gray wolves, where candidate solutions are updated through interactions among three leading wolves (α, β, and δ). This collaborative mechanism enables adaptive transitions between global exploration and local exploitation. Figure 1 illustrates the basic framework of the standard GWO algorithm.

In the standard GWO, candidate solutions are initialized within predefined search boundaries and iteratively updated through prey encircling, hunting, and attacking behaviors. Compressor station optimization model

To enable energy-efficient operation of parallel compressor stations in long-distance natural gas pipelines, a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) framework was established to optimize compressor start–stop states and load allocation among operating units. The objective of the model is to minimize total station energy consumption while satisfying operational and gas transmission requirements.

The optimization problem is inherently nonconvex because of the coexistence of discrete compressor activation decisions, nonlinear compressor performance characteristics, and coupled operational constraints. In the present steady-state case study, thermodynamic properties of natural gas were determined using the BWRS equation of state under fixed inlet conditions and gas composition. These properties were precomputed prior to optimization and incorporated into the framework as physically consistent inputs.

Decision variables

The optimization problem involves both continuous and discrete decision variables. Continuous variables are defined as the volumetric flow rate assigned to each compressor unit, Q (in units of m3/s), where i = 1,2 …,n and n denotes the total number of parallel compressors. In this study, a six-unit compressor station (n = 6) was selected as a representative case based on a previously reported industrial configuration.29

Discrete variables are introduced to represent the operating status of each compressor unit, uᵢ, where ui = 1 indicates that the compressor is operating and ui = 0 indicates a shutdown state. To enable integrated discrete–continuous optimization, a semi-continuous formulation was adopted by combining compressor operating status and flow allocation into a unified decision structure: {0}∪[Qi,min,Qi,max].

This formulation enables the proposed IGWO framework to simultaneously optimize compressor activation and load allocation while preserving operational feasibility.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Optimization runs: each optimization algorithm was independently executed 30 times under identical computational conditions.

Statistical metrics: Best, Worst, Mean, and standard deviation (Std) were used to evaluate optimization accuracy, robustness, and repeatability.

Statistical tests: two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were conducted to evaluate performance differences between algorithms.

Convergence analysis: mean best objective values over repeated runs were used to characterize convergence behavior.

Software: all simulations and statistical analyses were performed using MATLAB R2019b.

Clinical outcomes following open reduction and internal fixation without primary cruciate ligament reconstruction or avulsion fixation for Schatzker types IV-VI tibial plateau fractures: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Dai Xiaoyu, Chen Kai, Leng Tengfei, Ding Wenge, Lv Shujun, Wang Kejie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSchatzker types IV-VI tibial plateau fractures (TPFs) are frequently complicated by soft tissue injuries. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical and functional outcomes of Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs treated with ORIF alone, and to compare outcomes between patients with and without concomitant anterior or posterior cruciate ligament (ACL or PCL) injuries.Methods316 Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs patients with a mean age of 51.5 ± 13.2 years old who underwent open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) were enrolled. There were 152, 56 and 108 patients with type IV, type V, and type VI fractures. Simultaneous reconstruction was not performed for ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries and the displaced ACL or PCL avulsion fractures were only reduced without additional specific fixation. According to preoperative MRI images and intraoperative explorations, visual analog pain scale (VAS), knee flexion range of motion (ROM), and American Hospital for Special Surgery Knee Joint (HSS) scores in both groups of ACL/PCL mid-substance injuries and avulsion fractures were separately compared to no ACL/PCL injuries group for a comprehensive assessment of the outcomes. Also, we measured KT-1000 side-to-side difference (SSD) to assist in evaluating anteroposterior knee stability.ResultsThe incidence of ACL or PCL injuries was approximately 23.1% or 15.2%. KT-1000 (SSD was more likely to be abnormal or severe in patients with ACL or PCL avulsion fractures (P < 0.05) preoperatively and obviously improved postoperatively (P < 0.05). For patients with ACL avulsion fractures, knee flexion ROM was significantly lower than that in patients without ACL injuries during the follow-up (t = -2.089, P = 0.040; t = -2.659, P = 0.010; t = −2.169, P = 0.033; t = -2.200, P = 0.031). Still, these patients manifested as significantly low HSS scores at postoperative 3 and 6 months preoperatively (t = -2.561, P = 0.013; t = -2.164, P = 0.034).ConclusionsFor Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs combined with ACL or PCL injuries, ORIF without concurrent ACL or PCL reconstruction and additional fixation of ACL or PCL avulsion fractures is feasible and can achieve satisfactory outcomes.

Background

Schatzker types IV-VI tibial plateau fractures (TPFs) are mainly characterized by complex injury mechanisms and fracture patterns, which pose significant challenges in treatment and have a higher incidence of soft tissue injuries [1]. Among these, the overall estimated incidence of lateral meniscus and anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injuries is ranging from 44.4% to 63.0% and 18.9% to 92.6%, respectively. In contrast, injuries to the medial meniscus and posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) occur less frequently [2–6]. The stability and alignment of the knee joint should be restored based on a comprehensive assessment of soft tissue injuries to maximally alleviate the pain and improve functional outcomes [7].

ACL or PCL injuries associated with TPFs are mainly divided into three types: avulsion fractures at the insertion site, partial mid-substance tears, and complete mid-substance tears. Among these, avulsion fractures at the insertion site are relatively more common and typically occur in the tibial intercondylar eminence [8]. Currently, in patients accompanied by ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries, the treatment principally depends on the overall stability of the knee joint after fracture fixation and the condition of soft tissues. Particularly for Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs, the indications for one-stage ACL or PCL reconstruction remain inconsistent and there is still some controversy regarding the timing and criteria for staged reconstruction [2, 9]. Furthermore, simultaneous ligament reconstruction or avulsion fractures fixation using suture loop, Endobutton plate, or cannulated screw may be limited by the spatial constraints imposed by the existing implants in surgical treatment.

Routine use of arthroscopy is not recommended for Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs due to the complexity of the surgery and the risk of irrigation fluid extravasating through the joint capsule or fracture sites into the lower leg fascial compartments [10]. Importantly, MRI combined with intraoperative exploration can provide a preliminary assessment and enhance the diagnostic accuracy of cruciate ligament injuries involved in TPFs. It remains imperative to further explore appropriate management methods for concomitant ACL or PCL injuries and optimize treatment strategies while adhering to the principles of both minimizing surgical procedures and avoiding unnecessary trauma [11]. Accordingly, this study focused on Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs treated by ORIF without simultaneous ACL/PCL reconstruction or additional fixation for ACL/PCL avulsion fractures, and primarily provided a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical outcomes. Also, we aimed to compare outcomes between patients with and without concomitant cruciate ligament injuries fully based on a clear understanding of cruciate ligament injury patterns.

Methods

Patient screening

Patients with Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs who underwent surgical treatment at the Department of Orthopedic Trauma, the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University from August 2017 to May 2023 were consecutively recruitedin this retrospective study. The study protocol was approved by the local institutional review board and written informed consents were obtained from all patients. Based on the major postoperative recovery period, the peak incidence of complications following surgery, and the highest data integrity in different follow-up period ranges, we established the following inclusion and exclusion criteria thoughtfully. Inclusion criteria: (1) unilateral Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs confirmed by X-rays and CT scan with an articular step-off of more than 3 mm; (2) a clear history of knee trauma without any prior ACL or PCL injuries caused by other external factors; (3) the time from injury to surgery of less than 3 weeks; (4) surgical treatment for ORIF with locking plates and screws. Exclusion criteria: (1) complicated by ipsilateral lower limb periarticular fractures; (2) severe osteoporosis or pathological fractures; (3) pre-existing knee flexion-extension dysfunction; (4) a follow-up duration of less than 18 months or loss to follow-up.

Preoperative imaging included X-rays, CT scans, and MRI were used to comprehensively evaluate the fractures and associated soft tissue injuries. Consideirng that more severe bony articular involvement may be associated with both cruciate ligament injury and worse recovery, direct causal attribution is not possible. As a result, patients were generally classified into three categories: ACL/PCL mid-substance injuries, ACL/PCL avulsion fractures, and no ACL/PCL injuries referring to preoperative MRI findings and intraoperative exploration in open incisions. Thereinto, based on the degree of continuity loss of the ACL/PCL mid-substance and the extent of localized thickening or curvature on sagittal MRI images, ACL/PCL mid-substance injuries were classified into two categories: ACL/PCL partial tear and ACL/PCL complete tear. The grouping in this study was not intended to not aim to isolate the effect of concomitant cruciate ligament injuries on fracture prognosis, but rather to evaluate the specific functional differences between patients with and without ACL/PCL injuries.

Surgical methods and postoperative rehabilitation

All patients accepted ORIF surgery under general anesthesia after the local skin wrinkling and significant reduction of swelling in the proximal tibial region. Following positioning the patient in a supine or floating position, an anteromedial, medial, posteromedial, or inverted “L” approach was routinely used. When necessary, we needed to combine the modified anterolateral approach extending above the fibular head to elevate and reduce the collapsed lateral tibial plateau articular surface around the tibial insertions of ACL or PCL. Under fluoroscopic guidance using a C-arm (anteroposterior and lateral views) and direct visualization, the articular surface was confirmed to be adequately reduced and internal fixation was then performed using tibial plateau plates and screws (Kanghui, China). For those with mid-substance injuries involving the body of ACL or PCL, we did not perform primary reconstruction. Through the original open incision, displaced avulsion fractures of ACL or PCL in the tibial insertion were only reduced without additional specific fixation on the premise that no significant fractures displacement following ORIF of the TPFs was observed during knee flexion-extension under direct visualization and fluoroscopy intraoperatively. In terms of some other soft tissue injuries such as meniscus, collateral ligament complex, and posterolateral complex (PLC) injuries, we performed targeted repair, reshaping or reinforcement based on the intraoperative lateral stress and tibial external rotation tests of the knee joint following ORIF surgery. In terms of concomitant meniscal injuries such as peripheral meniscal tears, we consistently performed primary suture repair or reshaping. For combined grade III injuries the mid-substance of the collateral ligament or avulsion fractures at its insertion site, reinforcement sutures or repair using suture anchors or sutures through bone tunnels were performed while grade I and II collateral ligament injuries were left untreated. For injuries to PLC structures such as popliteofibular ligament and popliteus tendon, we generally did a varus stress test at 30° knee flexion after fixation of the TPFs and repair of the injured structures of PLC was performed in patients with a significant increase in external rotation of the tibia and severe varus instability. If necessary, the original incision could be partially extended or an auxiliary incision should be made.

All patients underwent systematic functional rehabilitation that mainly including knee flexion-extension exercises, quadriceps training, and patellar mobilization until 6 weeks postoperatively. For those who were accompanied by cruciate or collateral ligament injuries, functional exercises were proceeded with the assistance of an adjustable knee brace. The brace protection was removed 6–8 weeks postoperatively and partial weight-bearing was initiated depending on the associated ligament injury. Following radiological evidence of obvious callus formation, we allowed the patients to commence full weight bearing with crutches at postoperative 8–10 weeks.

Observation and follow-up indicators

Before surgery and at 3, 6, 12 months after surgery, as well as at the final follow-up, standard anteroposterior and lateral knee X-rays were adopted to assess fracture healing, implant, and soft tissue conditions. Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) was used to evaluate the intensity of knee pain. The overall knee function was determined by the anterior or posterior KT-1000 side-to-side difference (SSD), flexion-extension range of motion (ROM), and the Hospital for Special Surgery Knee (HSS) score. Thereinto, SSD was defined as the absolute value measured under a maximum manual force of 30 lbs (133 N) at 30° of knee flexion. An SSD of less than or equal to 5 mm was classified as normal or near-normal laxity while an SSD of more than 5 mm was classified as abnormal laxity [12]. ACL or PCL avulsion fractures at the intercondylar eminence were graded according to the modified Meyers-McKeever (MM) classification with types I to IV representing varying degrees of displacement and fragmentation of the intercondylar eminence fracture fragment [13].

Statistical analysis

All tests were performed using SPSS 24.0 software (IBM, USA). After Kolmogorov-Smirnov testing, normally distributed continuous data with homogeneity of variance were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). Continuous variables including knee flexion ROM, HSS score, etc., in both groups of ACL/PCL mid-substance injuries and ACL/PCL avulsion fractures were separately compared to no ACL/PCL injuries group using independent samples t-tests. In addition to this, KT-SSD measured at multiple time points were analyzed using repeated-measures methods. Categorical data was expressed as frequencies (cases, %), and intergroup comparisons were performed by Pearson χ2 test and Fisher’s exact test. The significance level (α) was set at 0.05 for two-tailed tests.

Results

A total of 316 patients met the inclusion criteria and were retrospectively enrolled (Fig. 1). The mean age was 51.5 ± 13.2 years old with an average BMI of 24.7 ± 5.8 kg/m2. The primary cause of injury was traffic accident (67.1%, 212/316) while other causes such as fall and sports injuries were relatively less common. According to the Schatzker typing, there were 152 patients of type IV (48.1%), 56 patients of type V (17.7%), and 108 patients of type VI (34.2%) TPFs, respectively. All 316 patients successfully completed the surgery with an average operation time of 121.3 ± 17.4 min and an average intraoperative blood loss of 248.2 ± 65.6 ml. After grouping the patients based on the presence or absence of ACL/PCL injuries, there was no significant differences in the comparisons of the mean age, BMI, operation time, and intraoperative blood loss (P > 0.05). Also, a parallel distribution in gender, cause of trauma, and Schatzker type could be observed in patients with and without ACL/PCL injuries. (Table 1).

Characteristics of soft tissue injuries

There were 73 (23.1%), 48 (15.2%), 84 (26.6%), 118 (37.3%), and 132 patients involved ACL, PCL, medial collateral ligament complex, PLC, and meniscus injuries, respectively. These injuries were most frequently associated with Schatzker type IV TPFs. ACL injuries predominantly involved avulsion fractures at the tibial insertion site (40/73, 54.8%) while PCL (32/48, 66.7%), medial collateral ligament complex (71/84, 84.5%), and PLC injuries (95/118, 80.5%) were mainly manifested as mid-substance tears. In Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs, no significant differences were observed in the distribution of different categories of ligament injuries (P > 0.05). Even in the subgroups of ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries, there was no statistically significant difference in the proportion of ACL or PCL complete and partial tears (P > 0.05) (Table 2). The incidence of modified Meyers-McKeever types III and IV fractures was relatively high, which accounted for 70.0% (28/40) and 75.0% (12/16) in patients with ACL and PCL avulsion fractures, respectively. Still, no statistical significance in the distribution of different modified Meyers-McKeever types could be found among Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs (P > 0.05) (Table 3).

Comparisons of pain, ROM, and function of the knee joint

The average follow-up time was 23.6 ± 2.2 months. After surgery, there was no significant difference in VAS scores between patients with and without ACL or PCL injuries (P > 0.05). Knee flexion ROM was observably less in patients with ACL injuries particularly those with ACL avulsion fractures at 3, 6, 12 months and the last follow-up compared to those without ACL injuries (t = -2.107, P = 0.038, t =- 2.089, P = 0.040; t = -2.260, P = 0.027, t = -2.659, P = 0.010; t = -2.437, P = 0.017, t =-2.169, P = 0.033; t = -2.286, P = 0.025, t = -2.200, P = 0.031). In addition, patients with PCL injuries especially those who had PCL avulsion fractures exhibited significantly lower knee flexion ROM at 6 months postoperatively compared to those without PCL injury (t = -2.042, P = 0.045; t = -2.107, P = 0.039). It was worth noting that patients with ACL avulsion fractures showed lower HSS scores at both 3 and 6 months postoperatively compared to patients without ACL injuries with statistically significant differences (t = -2.561, P = 0.013; t = -2.164, P = 0.034). However, no significant differences were observed at 12 months postoperatively and the last follow-up (P > 0.05). In contrast, no significant differences in HSS scores could be found between patients with and without PCL injuries at any postoperative time point (P > 0.05) (Fig. 2). Meanwhile, patients with ACL or PCL avulsion fractures were significantly more likely to exhibit a higher proportion of SSD measurements exceeding 5 mm compared to those with non-avulsion fractures of ACL or PCL before surgery (χ2 = 4.319, P = 0.038; χ2 = 4.443, P = 0.035) (Table 4). Additionally, KT-SSD showed significant improvement at postoperative 3, 6, 12 months and the last follow-up compared to pre-operative measurements (F = 255.371, P < 0.001; F = 310.370, P < 0.001) (Table 5).

Complications

Eight patients developed incision infections which were successfully managed with antibiotic therapy and wound care. A total of 21 patients including 4 patients with ACL avulsion fractures experienced knee joint stiffness at 6 weeks postoperatively and were treated with manual manipulation to achieve full extension at 0° and flexion beyond 100°. 6 patients with ACL mid-substance injuries exhibited a low-grade laxity on the Lachman and anterior-posterior drawer tests after removing the adjustable brace. Among them, 2 patients demonstrated low-grade positive pivot shift tests with gliding reduction while the other 4 patients showed negative in pivot shift tests. All 6 patients did not undergo secondary reconstruction due to being over 60 years of age with relatively low functional demands. A typical case was presented in Fig. 3.

Discussion

This study further emphasized the critical role of restoring bony stability in surgical treatment of Schatzker types IV-VI type TPFs. Importantly, our findings suggested that ACL or PCL reconstruction, as well as additional fixation of ACL or PCL avulsion fractures, might not be necessary at the time of initial fracture fixation. To a considerable extent, accurate reduction of the TPFs with restoring and maintaining the width of the medial and lateral plateaus may help stabilize ACL or PCL avulsion fractures, thereby reducing the likelihood of re-displacement of fracture fragments at the tibial insertions. More importantly, we wish to emphasize that the observed differences, particularly the early delays in ROM and HSS scores in the ACL avulsion fractures subgroup, must be viewed in the context of the inherently greater bony disruption and soft tissue trauma in these injuries.Given the significant proportion of high-energy mechanisms in Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs [14] and the principle of minimizing unnecessary intraoperative trauma, these results do not prove that ACL/PCL status is unimportant, but rather that this surgical strategy might contribute to optimize a precise treatment of both bone and soft tissue injuries following ORIF of the fractures.

Clinical characteristics of concomitant ACL or PCL injuries

Arthroscopic studies have confirmed that approximately 18.9%-55.4% and 11.3%-21.9% of patients with Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs have concomitant ACL and PCL injuries, respectively [2–4, 15]. In this study, both the incidence of ACL (23.1%) and PCL (15.2%) injuries was comparable to the findings from the above-mentioned studies utilizing direct arthroscopic visualization but lower than the preoperative MRI-diagnosed incidence. Notably, MRI-based occurrence rate of the combined ACL/PCL injuries was reported to be 50.6%/41.8%, 92.6%/70.4%, and 54.5%/15.2% in previous relevant studies [5, 6, 16], respectively. We thought that differences in the inclusion proportions among different Schatzker types of the TPFs [5, 16], extensive hematoma formation and disruption of normal anatomical structures following fractures were likely the primary reasons for MRI overestimation [17]. Nevertheless, MRI could provide important adjunctive guidance for preoperative assessment of meniscal entrapment and obvious displacement of avulsion fractures at the collateral ligament complex insertion, which played a key adjunctive role in determining the surgical approach and the sequence of fracture reduction.

Evidently, we herein noted that the majority of ACL injuries manifested as avulsion fractures which were most commonly observed in Schatzker type IV TPFs. Also, the locations of ACL avulsion fractures were considerably aligned with the heatmap distribution of Schatzker type IV fracture lines [18]. In fact, Schatzker type IV TPFs are typically resulted from the medial femoral condyle impacting the medial tibial plateau due to forces such as varus stress at varying degrees of knee flexion. The axial, rotational, and shear loads applied at the moment of trauma can easily cause excessive tension in ACL and directly lead to ACL mid-substance tears or avulsion fractures with the extension of the force [5, 19, 20]. Unlike ACL, PCL injuries were most frequently seen in Schatzker type VI TPFs and mainly involve partial mid-substance tears which might be attributed to the high-energy trauma and the inherent greater strength of PCL. In addition, PCL tibial insertion is located on the posterior slope of the central intercondylar region and the dense joint capsule can partially counteract the anterior-posterior and rotational stresses generated by high-energy trauma. Consequently, complete mid-substance tears of PCL were relatively rare in our results [21].

Treatment of combined ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries

In Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs with combined ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries, delayed ACL or PCL reconstruction is generally recommended as a secondary procedure following fracture fixation with surgical decisions made based on stability assessment of the knee joint. Notably, a considerable proportion of patients did not undergo subsequent ACL or PCL reconstruction and still achieve satisfactory overall functional outcomes [22–24]. Also, concurrent reconstruction of ACL or PCL has been shown to yield good functional results in the short and medium term. Accordingly, the potential for iatrogenic injury or increased surgical burden warrants cautious consideration and deliberate omission of reconstruction may be a viable option [2, 11, 25, 26]. It was worth mentioning that Sabesan et al. [22] and Wu et al. [27] separately performed ORIF surgeries without addressing cruciate ligament injuries in 3 patients with Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs complicated by multi-ligament injuries. After that, the patients had regained normal knee ROM, stability, and function with satisfactory clinical outcomes at 12–18 months postoperatively. In fact, existing literature evidence on this group of patients is predominantly limited to small-sample case series reports with inadequately addressed details regarding the treatment of ACL or PCL injuries and the stability assessments during subsequent follow-up [2, 27, 28]. In this study, we found a residual laxity rate of 18.2% (6/33) with 6 patients exhibiting positive anterior-posterior drawer tests and neither patient reported any significant functional impairment in daily life. This seemed to indirectly suggest fibrous scarring of ACL could still play an important role in restoring and maintaining the overall stability of the postoperative knee joint in this group of patients. Therefore, for Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs combined with ACL or PCL mid-substance injuries, determining indications for ligament reconstruction for one-stage ACL or PCL reconstruction require a comprehensive evaluation of patient age, pre-injury activity level, functional expectations, surgical trauma, and global knee stability. As a potential alternative option, the mid- to long-term efficacy of non-surgical treatment on combined ACL or PCL injuries necessitates further validation through high-level prospective clinical studies.

Surgical options for combined ACL or PCL avulsion fractures

ACL or PCL avulsion fractures are generally seen in high-energy injury TPFs and often indicate severe soft tissue injuries [18]. Fixing both TPFs and combined ACL or PCL avulsion fractures can achieve a satisfactory prognosis [29, 30], but definitive guidelines remain debatable on both the necessity of performing one-stage fixation separately and the specific fixation methods to be used [31–33]. In Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs, conceivable interference between the additional bone tunnels used for extra fixation of ACL or PCL avulsion fractures and tibial plateau stabilization hardware further complicates surgical treatment. Also, it should be noted that the bone tunnel for fixing ACL avulsion fractures was mostly positioned in the anterior region of the tibial plateau, which might be unsuitable for Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs with hyperextension injury mechanisms [34]. In this study, by reducing and realigning displaced ACL or PCL avulsion fracture fragments under direct visualization and following restoration the overall width of the tibial plateau, plate-screw internal fixation was performed to establish a biomechanical environment where the reconstructed plateau dimensions might exert potential compressive and clamping effects on the bony structures of the intercondylar eminence [34]. Still, concurrent primary repair of meniscus and collateral ligament complex injuries as well as the utilizing of postoperative bracing could exert partial effect in maintaining knee joint stability and mitigating secondary displacement of avulsion fracture fragments [11].

Lv et al. [35] reported that patients with TPFs accompanied by ACL avulsion fractures exhibited inferior knee functional outcomes at 6 months postoperatively in the condition that ACL avulsion fractures were not specifically fixed. Similarly, Konda et al. [31] identified non-intervention for ACL avulsion fractures as an independent predictor of suboptimal knee functional recovery and restricted knee ROM within postoperative 6 months. However, the above findings lacked validation from the comparative follow-up data exceeding one year. Huang et al. [29] pointed out that arthroscopic suture fixation of ACL avulsion fractures combined with ORIF treatment of the TPFs could obtain favorable outcomes maintained for at least postoperative 5 years. In the present study, although the omission of additional fixation for ACL or PCL avulsion fractures delayed the recovery of postoperative knee ROM and function, comparable short-term prognostic results and definitive treatment outcomes can still be acquired. Moreover, this treatment strategy effectively minimized unnecessary injuries, reduced operative time, and thereby partly facilitated enhanced postoperative rehabilitation. Currently, clinical treatment of ACL or PCL avulsion fractures predominantly reference surgical strategies for isolated tibial eminence fractures [36] and we insisted that such treatment decisions must be contingent upon achieving precise reduction and stable fixation of the tibial plateau in patients with Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs.

Addressing potential confounding by fracture severity

It must be acknowledged that Schatzker types IV-VI fractures inherently involve substantial bony injury that could confound the attribution of outcomes to soft tissue status. The observed differences cannot be solely attributed to ligament status due to inherent differences in bony injury severity We fully noted that the ACL/PCL injured groups likely harbored more severe overall trauma, as suggested by the higher proportion of comminuted avulsion fractures and more frequent combined soft tissue involvement. Although we observed no statistical differences in Schatzker type distribution between groups, unmeasured dimensions of fracture complexity (e.g., articular comminution, initial displacement) were not adequately captured. Therefore, the early functional deficits seen in the ACL avulsion subgroup could reflect more extensive osseous insult rather than the ligament injury per se. This inherent confounding constrains the causal interpretation of our group comparisons, and our results should be seen as a real-world characterization of outcomes when a non-interventional ligament philosophy is adopted, stratified by the presence of documented soft tissue damage.

Limitations

The relatively small sample size of ACL/PCL avulsion fractures, the lack of repeated measures analysis of the specific parameters within subgroups might limit a thorough evaluation of the corresponding results due to the insufficient statistical power. Also, considering the inclusion criterion of a minimum 18-month follow-up, patients with insufficient postoperative follow-up or those lost to follow-up were excluded and the potential confounding effects introduced by this exclusion cannot be entirely ruled out. The main findings might be most applicable to patients who successfully undergo a stable recovery during the initial two-year postoperative period. In addition, treatment strategy was depended on individual surgeon judgment rather than a standardized protocol, which might lead to potential surgeon decision bias. and lacking arthroscopic confirmation might lead to misclassification of ligament injuries. Although preoperative MRI was routinely performed, misclassification of ligament injuries due to the absence of arthroscopic confirmation might result in definition errors and negatively impact postoperative outcomes assessment. Overall, we did not perform any specific cruciate ligament repair or avulsion fixation in a comparative group of patients which significantly affected the very clinical strategy under evaluation. More fundamentally, because the severity of bony injury was both a determinant of ligament injury and a driver of functional outcomes, our group comparisons could not distinguish the effect of the ligament injury from that of the underlying osseous trauma. Definitive conclusions regarding the optimal management of combined ACL/PCL injuries require prospective, controlled trials with granular injury severity stratification and a group receiving ligament repair/fixation.

Conclusions

In Schatzker types IV-VI TPFs accompanied by ACL or PCL injuries, the overall clinical outcomes of simultaneous treatment versus non-treatment of the associated ACL or PCL injuries following ORIF for TPFs were consistently favorable. This suggests that a strategy of no specific intervention for ACL/PCL injuries is conceivably feasible and may be considered particularly when surgical factors or patient characteristics limit simultaneous reconstruction or fixation. Owing to inherent confounding by fracture severity and the non-comparative design, these observations should be interpreted with caution and served as a basis for future prospective comparisons.

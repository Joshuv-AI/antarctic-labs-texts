Nordic Respiratory Academy: a historical note and reflection
Authors: Amund Gulsvik, Per Sigvald Bakke, Knut Stavem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe first formal meeting of Nordic professors in pulmonary medicine took place in Gothenburg in December 1995. In Reykjavik in June 2003, the laws and statutes of the Nordic Respiratory Academy (NORA) were approved, and the first board was elected. NORA aimed to improve education, research, and clinical practice and became a meeting place for building friendships and exchange of ideas among academic teachers in pulmonary medicine in the Nordic region. The meetings initiated cooperative research among the university departments of respiratory medicine, and NORA reestablished the Nordic chest physicians’ own pulmonary medical journal with roots in the Nordic region. Until 2025, 20 NORA meetings have been held. Its future depends on the perceived need for such a meeting place and academics that are willing to continue organizing and using this institution as a think tank.KEYWORDS: Nordic, respiratory, organization, pulmonary, journal

Introduction

The Nordic Society of Pulmonologists (‘Nordisk tuberkuloseforening’) was founded on 19 August 1919 in Copenhagen. The first meeting was held in Stockholm in 1920. Later, meetings were organized every other year under the name ‘Nordic Lung Physicians Congress’. Early on, there were only a few professors of tuberculosis and pulmonary medicine at the Nordic universities, as the sanatoriums treating patients with tuberculosis were often located in rural areas, far from the universities. The contact between the few Nordic professors was good, and the Nordic congresses were popular. From 1919 to 2024, a total of 51 meetings were held. The name of the congress changed over time, to ‘Nordic Congress of Pneumonology’, and later to ‘Nordic Lung Congress’ (NLC). It has recently drawn an audience of >500 physicians or nurses [1,2] (Table 1). From several of these meetings abstracts or congress reports have been printed as supplements to respiratory journals [3–7].

In the 1990s, there were two driving forces for the establishment of the Nordic Professors’ Meetings outside the NLC, namely a large group of young professors in pulmonary medicine with common academic interests, and a new threatening organization of university hospitals in the Nordic countries. The hospital function and the university functions were to be separated and financed by different bodies, and the professors in full-time positions would no longer automatically be the head of the affiliated clinical department. This stimulated unity among the Nordic professors of pulmonary medicine. The aim of this paper is to describe the process of establishing a scientific pulmonary community in the Nordic countries and reflect on its future.

The first professors’ meeting in 1995

Professor and chief physician Bengt-Eric Skoogh, MD, PhD at Sahlgrenska Hospital/University of Gothenburg initiated and invited to the first gathering of professors in pulmonary medicine in the Nordic region, ‘Nordiskt ämnesföreträdarmöte i lungmedicin’, which took place 19–20 December 1995 at the Sheraton Hotel, Gothenburg. In total, 9 participants from 4 countries attended the meeting and funded their own travel and accommodation, while the gala dinner and an evening at the Gothenburg Opera’s Christmas concert were sponsored by Glaxo AB. From Denmark, Ronald Dahl and Asger Dirksen participated, from Finland Brita Stenius-Aarniala, from Sweden Gunnar Boman. Claes-Göran Löfdahl, and Bengt-Erik Skoogh and from Norway Leif Bjermer, Jakob Boe, and Amund Gulsvik. The academic program comprised: 1. Nordic cooperation on care programs for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), 2. Pulmonary medicine research and development in the Baltic countries, 3. Nordic cooperation in the European Respiratory Society (ERS) [8], at the NLC and in specialist training, and 4. Status of academic pulmonary medicine in the Nordic countries. These topics have been repeated in the subsequent 28 meetings.

The minutes reported the following outputs of the first meeting: 1. sending a request to the steering group for the international asthma study (Euroscop) about further follow-up of study participants under national supervision [9], 2. preparation of a project protocol on mortality and morbidity of COPD and the prevalence of smoking habits in the Nordic countries, 3. a review of reference values for simple spirometry in the Nordic countries, 4. highlighting the economic aspects of COPD, 5. quality assurance of spirometry in the Nordic countries, 6. helping to strengthen research and teaching in the Baltic countries, 7. cooperating to promote Nordic physicians in the management of the ERS, and harmonizing specialist education in the Nordic countries to the European Union of Medical Specialists (EUMS) requirements in lung diseases, and 9. planning to arrange professor meetings in Oslo 1996, Copenhagen 1997, and Helsinki 1998.

This first meeting in Gothenburg was a great success and inspired to further cooperation between participants. In 1995, a total of 32 professors, lecturers/clinical lecturers were working in academic or teaching positions in pulmonary medicine in the Nordic countries (Table 2).

Later professors’ meetings and the Nordic Respiratory Academy

Meetings and agendas

From 1995 to 2009, meetings were held in early December almost every year. In the beginning, 10–25 participants attended the meetings, typically with most participants from the host country.

The names professors’ meeting and Nordic Respiratory Academy (NORA) meeting were used interchangeably for a time. In Stockholm in 1999, the meeting was instead called the ‘Teachers Forum’. When the criterion for invitation was changed to having a PhD degree in pulmonary medicine, more people were eligible to attend the meetings, although the number of participants did not increase substantially. Hosting of the meeting rotated between Sweden, Norway, Denmark, and Finland, and later meetings were also held in Iceland and once in Estonia.

Until 2002, the professor meetings were always a two-day meeting with dinner and included a cultural experience, such as concerts, theater performances, opera performances, or museum tours. Full minutes from the meetings were until 2002 prepared by meeting hosts and distributed to all participants.

Training of specialists in pulmonary diseases was regularly discussed at the meetings, but proposals for harmonized education in the Nordic region were not prepared. Continuing education courses were arranged by the Nordic Lung Congresses, national associations, universities, or the pharmaceutical industry, without involvement by the Professors’ Meeting.

The participants also reported on research activities in pulmonary medicine at their universities, including epidemiological, clinical, respiratory, and basal physiology studies. Participants also briefed on current PhD theses and distributed them to the attending colleagues. International advisory groups for the departments’ pulmonary medicine research were also on the meeting agenda.

In the 2000 meeting, a joint Nordic research project was initiated on patients with exacerbations of COPD that were admitted to one university hospital in each country in 2000–2001. This was a cross-sectional and observational study in Sweden. Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Iceland [10–14].

In 2001, the professor meeting was held in Iceland for the first time. The agenda included: 1. an overview of recommended textbooks in pulmonary medicine, 2, a new Nordic textbook in pulmonary medicine and allergology, 3. joint Nordic guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of asthma, 4. a Nordic initiative to cooperate with the European Respiratory Society and the International Union Against Tuberculosis and Lung Diseases, 5. centers of excellence for research at regional hospitals, and 6. the possibility of allowing young Nordic pulmonary researchers to present their research results in their own language. Furthermore, the editor of Lungeforum and the editorial staff of Respiratory Medicine informed about their journals.

On 7–11 April 2002, the network of respiratory academics organized a symposium on board the Norwegian Coastal Express (‘Hurtigruten’) ship, MS ‘Nordkapp’, sailing from Bergen to Tromsø. Respiratory physicians from 16 Nordic universities (Helsinki, Tampere, Åbo, Oulu, Umeå, Stockholm, Uppsala, Goethenburg, Lund, Århus, Odense, Copenhagen, Oslo, Trondheim, Tromsø, Bergen) participated. The theme of the meeting was «Health care, management and guidelines of obstructive lung disease in the Nordic countries», and results of the working groups were later published [15].

Formalization of NORA

A meeting in Copenhagen in 1997, chaired by Asger Dirksen, proposed to establish an interdisciplinary Nordic association, ‘The Nordic Respiratory Health Care Association’, but this name was dropped in favour of the ‘Nordic Respiratory Academy’ (NORA). The Norwegian delegates proposed to establish a Nordic respiratory journal that would not compete with the major international English-language journals in Europe, the USA, and the UK. The meeting participants envisioned a journal for members of the Nordic pulmonary associations with review articles, practical guides in the diagnosis and treatment of pulmonary diseases, doctoral theses, abstracts from professional national (Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Iceland, and Norway) and Nordic pulmonary meetings, and information about future meetings in the Nordic region.

The process of establishing NORA with its own statutes took more than 6 years. In Malmö in 2002, it was decided to prepare a proposal for the establishment of NORA at the next Nordic Pulmonary Congress. The requirement for membership was having PhD competence and residing in one of the Nordic countries. Nurses and physiotherapists with PhDs were welcomed. Proposals were prepared for procedures for election of the leadership of NORA, the NLC committee, financial proposals, etc.

On 5 June 2003, the establishment, laws and statutes of NORA were unanimously adopted by the 25 participants in a meeting during the 41st Nordic Congress in Reykjavik, Iceland. The first elected trustees for NORA were: president Ernst Omenaas, Bergen; vice president Gunnar Boman, Uppsala; secretary Jacob Boe, Oslo; treasurer Charlotte Suppli Ulrik, Copenhagen; editor Per Bakke, Bergen; board members Markku Niemenen, Tampere, Thorarinn Gislason, and Dora Lùdviksdòttir, Reykjavik.

NORA meetings after 2003

From 2003, the Nordic Professor Meeting became an organization of academics with PhD qualifications and employment at the 19 universities with medical faculties in the Nordic countries, i.e. Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Norway, Iceland, and later Estonia.

A total of 20 NORA meetings were held from 2003 to 2025. After the founding in 2003, there has been no new election of members to the board. NORA meetings have rotated loosely between various locations (number of times): Copenhagen [4], Uppsala [3], Helsinki [3], Bergen [2], Oslo [2], Reykjavik [2], Aarhus [1], Gothenburg [1], Tartu [1], and Stockholm [1]. There has been no clear plan as to who would arrange the next meeting, and invitations have often been ad-hoc invitations during the previous meeting or afterwards. The meetings were usually of 2 days’ duration, but sometimes of only a few hours’ duration in connection with the NLC and without minutes from the meeting.

NORA meetings have regularly informed participants about the topics for the next NLC, enabling members to provide input on themes for the congress and continuing education courses. The NLC was now planned not only for pulmonologists and doctors in training for the lung specialty but also aimed at general practitioners, nurses, physiotherapists, and others. NORA has not taken any responsibility for the implementation of the NLC, which has been the responsibility of the national pulmonology associations and the local university communities.

Lung cancer has become less prominent in the program at these congresses, as oncology departments in many Nordic countries have taken over some of that activity, such as chemotherapy, while pulmonologists have maintained the focus on cancers diagnosis, asthma, COPD, and interstitial lung diseases.

Another recurrent item at the NORA meetings was the information from the editor about our Nordic journal, the transition from Lungeforum to Clinical Respiratory Journal, and further to the European Clinical Respiratory Journal.

Basic education in pulmonary medicine for medical students has been a recurring topic at NORA meetings, e.g. on new study plans in both Stockholm, Bergen, and Oslo. There has also been dialogue about financing of scientific projects, research education, and cooperation between academia and industry, and how to stimulate young doctors to pursue academic careers in pulmonary medicine. Joint Nordic candidates for high positions of trust in the ERS were sometimes first put forward at the NORA meeting, to improve the odds of a Nordic candidate being elected.

The scientific topics during the NORA meeting often focused on subjects where research expertise was high in the hosting city, such as sarcoidosis in Stockholm, sleep studies in Uppsala, genetics studies in Reykjavik and Bergen, and the COPD registry in Copenhagen.

Other topics discussed included: a) Nordic normal values for spirometry, b) non-invasive monitoring of airway inflammation, c) ultrasound methods in lung cancer diagnostics and radiotherapy. For example, in Gothenburg in 2004 the meeting discussed possible common Nordic spirometric reference values with Asgeir Dirksen (DK), Ansi Sovijärvi (FIN), Amund Gulsvik (N), and Hans Hedenström (S) as moderators.

The relationship between the pharmaceutical industries and the NORA attendees were good, and representatives of the industry were invited to talks in Bergen (2000) on future collaborations and interaction, and in Stockholm (2011) on what was in the pipelines of the drug industry.

After 2004, there is less information available from the NORA meetings. The NORA meetings from 2010 to 2020 were all arranged in the Nordic capitals except for meetings in Bergen (2017), Uppsala (2020) and Visby (2017).

In Oslo in April 2013, the agenda contained items on presentation of individual projects in the Oslo area, orientation on the Clinical Respiratory Journal/European Clinical Respiratory Journal (ECRJ) (Vibeke Backer/Leif Bjermer), discussion about the future need for NORA meetings and the Nordic congresses, and information on the new medical curriculum at the University of Oslo. The first evening there was a guided tour of Munch Museum, ending with dinner.

In the Copenhagen meeting in March 2014, there were presentations on a fixed evaluation package in asthma diagnostic, airway inflammation and hyperresponsiveness, endobronchial ultrasound, a Danish register for COPD, and discussion on how to recruit young clinicians into research.

In Reykjavik in January 2016, there were presentations on the history of NORA, update on the next NLC, the ECRJ, and lessons learned from Icelandic participation in Nordic and International respiratory studies, Nordic collaboration in specialist training in lung medicine, and opportunities for Nordic cooperation in lung rehabilitation and interstitial lung disease. Finally, the future of NORA meetings was discussed, and draft constitution and bylaws were discussed and changes proposed. In addition, a Nordic network of Severe Asthma was established during this meeting.

In the NORA meeting in Bergen in January 2017, there were presentations on the upcoming NLCs in Visby (2017) and Tallinn (2019), respiratory research activity in Norway, update on the Nordic severe asthma network, updated draft bylaws for NORA, update on the ECRJ, and orientation on the new curriculum at the school of medicine, University of Bergen.

In Helsinki in January 2019, the agenda contained presentations on asthma and allergies, tests for bronchial hyperresponsiveness, biologicals in asthma, adult onset asthma, and drug allergies, as well as items on the next NLC, and ECRJ update.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, no regular NORA meeting was arranged, but the 50th NLC in Copenhagen in 2022 had an ad-hoc NORA meeting. Here, participants received information about the forthcoming NLC in Helsinki in 2024. During the congress in Helsinki, there was a short orientation for NORA members from the leaders of the Danish (Ulla Moller Weinreich), Norwegian (Rune Nielsen), Swedish (Cecilie Bredin), Finnish (Paula Kauppi), Estonian (Alan Altraja), and Icelandic (Dora Ludviksdottir) lung medical associations. NORA has recently established a brief web site with some information and the bylaws [16].

At the NORA meeting in February 2025 in Oslo, the host called it a meeting for senior university academics in pulmonary medicine at the Nordic universities. Altogether 13 respiratory acadedemic teachers participated. The agenda contained information about ongoing research projects at Oslo and Akershus University Hospital, teaching in pulmonary medicine, the governance and future of NORA, and orientation about the NLC in Reykjavik in June 2026.

Re-establishing a Nordic journal in respiratory medicine

Until 1980, the pulmonary medical community in the Nordic region had its own journal, Acta Tuberculosea et Pneumologica Scandinavia (Figure 1), which in 1966 became the Scandinavian Journal of Respiratory Diseases. In 1980, this journal became part of the European Journal of Respiratory Diseases, renamed the European Respiratory Journal from 1988.

Title pages of the first two scientific journals of Respiratory medicine in Scandinavia: a) Acta Tubercolosea Scandinavica (1960), and b) Acta Tuberculosea et Pneumologica Scandinavica (1962).

At the 1998 meeting in Helsinki, participants discussed a future Nordic pulmonary journal in terms of market analyses, affiliations to national associations and Nordic universities, financing, editorial function, and possible printing location.

Many pulmonary professors in the Nordic region were at that time associate editors or editorial advisors in peer-reviewed journals: in national medical journals (A. Dirksen, A. Gulsvik), Allergy (R. Dahl, T. Haathela), Sarcoidosis (A. Eklund, O. Selroos), International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease (P. Bakke, C. Jansson, M. Nieminen, J. Vestbo), Respiratory Medicine (R. Dahl, L. Bjermer, K. Larsson, L. Laitinen, K. Mattson, B.G. Simonsson) and in the European Respiratory Journal (K. Larsson).

The professors wanted the editor of the journal to be chosen by a board, with members from each of the 19 medical schools in the Nordic countries and the 5 national pulmonary medicine associations. The editor would be paid in a 25% position and would serve for 4 years, and the appointment could be renewed once. An editorial assistant would be employed in a 50% position. Articles were to be peer-reviewed by volunteers. The goal was to publish 6 issues per year, each with 150 printed pages. The budget in 1998 was estimated at NOK 1 million, to be financed through subscriptions and industry advertisements. Participants at the professor meeting in Stockholm in 1999 contacted the national pulmonary medical associations for support for establishing a new Nordic journal.

From 1992, the Norwegian Society for Pulmonary Medicine had a member journal, Lungeforum (Norwegian Respiratory Journal), with Professor Per S. Bakke in Bergen as editor [2]. It had a small editorial staff, and the annual costs were about NOK 300,000 for four issues per year. At the request of the members of the professor meetings, from 2001, Lungeforum was distributed to all members of the pulmonary medical associations in Sweden, Denmark, Iceland, and Norway, and the editorial staff was expanded with a member from Sweden and Denmark.

In 2002, the journal’s name was changed from Lungeforum to Clinical Respiratory Journal (CRJ), and articles were written in English. In 2005, the ownership of the journal was transferred to NORA.

In 2005–2008, Ronald Dahl in Aarhus and Vibeke Backer in Copenhagen negotiated an agreement with the publisher Blackwell, which published NORA’s journal Clinical Respiratory Journal from 2007 to 2012. Vibeke Backer, Copenhagen, was editor-in-chief until 2013, in addition to a working group with five experienced Nordic associate editors [17,18]. The journal was the official publication site of NORA, but the number of contributors with original scientific manuscripts varied greatly between the Nordic countries. From 2007, the articles were registered on Pubmed. The first supplement of the CRJ was from a Nordic scientific workshop on Respiratory Epidemiologic Research on 18–21 August 2007 in Stamsund, Lofoten, Norway [19].

Blackwell Publishing was acquired by John Wiley & Sons in 2007, to become Wiley-Blackwell [20,21]. The agreement with Blackwell Publishing did not prevent the publisher from transferring the journal to new owners, and Wiley-Blackwell after some time decided to move the journal to China, with a new local editor-in-chief and with mainly Chinese editorial board members. The entire editorial staff from the Nordic countries withdrew because of this change.

This led to the founding of a new journal, which was established in 2013 with financial contributions from Lungeforum, the national Nordic pulmonary associations, and accumulated royalties from the former CRJ. The new journal was called the European Clinical Respiratory Journal (ECJR) with Taylor & Francis as publisher, with professor Leif Bjermer, Lund as editor-in-chief from 2013 to 2021 [22]. From 2014, the ECJR was an e-journal without printed versions.

During the NORA meeting in Reykjavik in 2016, it was clarified that for legal reasons the ownership of the ECJR would need to be with a permanent society of NORA with legal bylaws. After a debate, it was suggested that NORA should be anchored in Denmark, with the Danish Pulmonary Society. In the end, the Danish Pulmonary Society took over that ownership of the journal from 2018 (Ole Hilberg, personal communication 27 February 2025). After 2021, Danish pulmonary physicians have been central editorial staff members, with Professor Jens-Ulrik Jensen, Copenhagen, and currently, Professor Vasiliki Panou, Odense and Barbara Bonnesen Bertelsen, Copenhagen as editor-in-chiefs.

Reflections on NORA and its future

In 1995, Nordic professors in pulmonary medicine created a community for academic teachers and researchers in pulmonary medicine. This community provided insight and solutions that the individual could not get at their own institution. The meetings provided a mutual contact point, led to re-establishing of a Nordic pulmonary medicine journal, and the establishment of NORA.

During the past 30 years, the number of respiratory physicians with PhD qualification in the Nordic countries has increased immensely, as well as the number of professors in pulmonary medicine at the universities. Concurrently, the activity in NORA has decreased, and there is a lack of clear objectives and a formal structure and organization. In recent years, there have been few meetings, and in spite of an increased number of eligible participants, the participation rate has been low.

There are several explanations for this. New information technology, such as the internet and email, have revolutionized the way we work and cooperate, and it is easier to identify and contact potential professional collaborators than before. Other arenas, such as the annual ERS Congress, specialized international scientific meetings, or national meetings might be perceived as satisfactory substitutes. Therefore, the perceived need for a Nordic arena for networking may not be the same as before. In addition, more physicians today have a full-time working partner than before, which also leaves less time for professional travels and networking, and the financial support from the pharmaceutical industry for scientific meeting is more regulated and less abundant than before.

Thirty years after its establishment, the question is being asked whether there, in our global era, still is a need for a regional academic pulmonary medical institution in the Nordic countries. Having a common culture, traditions, similar disease panorama and healthcare system, which initially was perceived as important, may not be enough to awaken NORA. We think there is a potential for fruitful Nordic cooperation, both in education and in research in respiratory medicine, e.g. in prospective registries or large multi-center studies, although it is uncertain if NORA can have a role here.

In order to re-vitalize NORA, we think it is necessary to have a clear focus and differentiate the organization and meetings from other arenas. It is also necessary to involve young, committed, and enthusiastic academic pulmonary physicians in several Nordic countries, who believe in the benefits of Nordic cooperation and are willing to carry out the work. This may be a difficult endeavor, with an uncertain outcome.

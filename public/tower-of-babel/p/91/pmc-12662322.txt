Development and validation of an explainable machine learning model for preoperative prediction of central lymph node metastasis in capsular-invasive papillary thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Jingchao Chen, Yingting Hong, Haolin Shen, Hong Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to develop an explainable machine learning framework integrating dual-modality ultrasonography and thyroid function parameters for preoperative prediction of central lymph node metastasis (CLNM) in capsular-invasive papillary thyroid carcinoma. A retrospective cohort of 382 pathologically confirmed capsular-invasive papillary thyroid carcinoma patients was stratified into CLNM-negative and CLNM-positive cohorts. After comprehensive univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, predictive models were developed using 8 machine learning algorithms (including Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine, Gradient Boosting Machine, eXtreme Gradient Boosting, K-Nearest Neighbors, Adaptive Boosting, Neural Network, and Categorical Boosting [CatBoost]) and rigorously validated through receiver operating characteristic analysis. Multivariate analysis showed irregular margins, tumor location in lower/mid poles, maximum diameter > 10 mm, rich blood supply, heterogeneous enhancement, and elevated thyroid-stimulating hormone were independent CLNM risk factors. Receiver operating characteristic curves demonstrated the CatBoost model achieved optimal performance (training area under the curve: 0.791; test area under the curve: 0.804). SHapley Additive exPlanations analysis revealed maximum diameter > 10 mm, tumor location in lower/mid poles, and irregular margins were the top 3 contributing features. Tumor size > 10 mm is the most important predictor of CLNM. The CatBoost model demonstrated superior performance and, combined with SHapley Additive exPlanations analysis, provides a clinically applicable tool for personalized surgical planning by identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from prophylactic central lymph node dissection.

1. Introduction

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) exhibits lymphotrophic behavior, with cervical lymph node metastases developing in 20% to 90% of cases; central lymph node metastasis (CLNM) accounts for approximately 70% of LNM events.[1] The presence of lymph node metastasis affects prognosis, correlating with tumor recurrence and reduced patient survival.[2] Capsular invasion is a well-established high-risk factor associated with both lymph node metastasis and distant metastasis.[3]

Current guidelines recommend prophylactic central lymph node dissection (CLND) for patients exhibiting capsular invasion. However, preoperative identification of high-risk patients with capsular invasion is challenging, The appropriateness of prophylactic CLND in clinically node-negative in clinically node-negative (cN0, i.e., no preoperative evidence of lymph node involvement) PTC is debated, with controversy persisting across international guidelines.[4,5] This may be due to increased risks of recurrent laryngeal nerve injury and permanent hypoparathyroidism.[6] Consequently, precise preoperative risk stratification is essential to minimize unnecessary dissection while ensuring appropriate surgical management. Conventional ultrasonography is widely employed for CLNM evaluation in PTC owing to its noninvasiveness and high spatial resolution. Diagnostic sensitivity remains limited (38–64%) due to anatomical barriers in the central compartment,[7] Contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) provides real-time visualization of microvascular perfusion dynamics, enabling quantitative assessment of tumor angiogenesis.[8] Studies suggest,[1] CEUS may serve as a potential tool for predicting CLNM in PTC. Thyroid function parameters (thyroid-stimulating hormone [TSH], thyroglobulin, antithyroid peroxidase antibody) provide complementary predictive value with high reproducibility and patient compliance.

Machine learning algorithms have been increasingly integrated into PTC risk stratification frameworks over the past decade. Compared to traditional statistical models, ML integrates larger and more complex features to enhance predictive accuracy, explainable AI techniques (e.g., SHapley Additive exPlanations [SHAP] analysis) enable clinicians to quantitatively interpret feature contributions, thereby bridging the gap between model predictions and clinical decision-making. Therefore, this study aimed to develop and validate an explainable machine learning framework specifically for preoperative CLNM prediction in sonographically confirmed capsular-invasive PTC patients, integrating multimodal data: conventional ultrasonography, CEUS, and thyroid function parameters.

2.3. Statistical analysis

The dataset was first randomly divided into a training set and a test set at a ratio of 7:3. The training set was used for model training and internal validation, while the test set was employed for the final evaluation of the model’s generalization performance. During the training phase, models established by various machine learning algorithms were compared, including Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine, Gradient Boosting Machine, eXtreme Gradient Boosting, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Adaptive Boosting, and Categorical Boosting (CatBoost). Cross-validation was adopted in the model training process to optimize hyperparameters, aiming to avoid overfitting and determine the optimal parameter combination.[10,11]

To minimize potential multicollinearity, we first assessed correlations among all candidate features. Variables with a variance inflation factor >5 were considered to have significant collinearity and were excluded or merged prior to model construction. Furthermore, feature selection was guided by both clinical relevance and statistical significance. Variables identified as independent predictors in multivariate logistic regression were subsequently incorporated into the machine learning models. In addition, variable importance was quantified within the CatBoost framework using SHAP values, which allowed us to rank the contribution of each predictor to the model outputs.

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.3; http://www.r-project.org/). After the Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test, data conforming to a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and comparisons between groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median (lower quartile, upper quartile), and intergroup comparisons were made using the rank sum test. Categorical variables were expressed as cases (rates), and comparisons between groups were performed using the Chi-square test. A P-value < .05 was considered statistically significant. The diagnostic efficacy of the models was compared by calculating indicators such as receiver operating characteristic curve, area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. After obtaining the final prediction model, in-depth interpretive analysis of the model was conducted. The SHAP value analysis method was mainly used to calculate the Shapley additive value for each input feature, thereby quantifying the contribution of the feature to the positive and negative directions of the prediction result.

This retrospective study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (Approval No. 2025LWB228). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

4. Discussion

While PTC typically demonstrates indolent behavior, capsular invasion substantially enhances lymphatic metastatic potential and recurrence risk, thereby adversely impacting long-term survival outcomes. Our investigation specifically targets the high-risk subpopulation with sonographically confirmed capsular invasion, distinguishing itself from prior heterogeneous PTC cohorts through rigorous patient stratification. It specifically predicts CLNM probability to assist surgeons in developing individualized surgical plans (e.g., deciding on prophylactic central dissection), enhancing treatment precision and safety. Integration of SHAP interpretability frameworks with multimodal ML models achieves both high diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.804) and clinical interpretability, addressing the critical “black box” limitation of conventional AI systems in medical applications.

A well-established relationship exists between tumor size and CLNM risk, as consistently reported by Zhao et al[11] and Zhong et al.[12] Further strengthening this association, multiple independent studies have confirmed that tumors larger than 1 cm significantly elevate CLNM risk (a conclusion that aligns closely with our own findings).[13] From a biological perspective, progressive tumor growth induces structurally deficient neovascularization, characterized by discontinuous basement membranes and elevated vascular permeability, which together promote lymphatic invasion.[8,14]

The impact of tumor location on metastatic propensity remains contentious. Although several studies have found no significant correlation between topographic site and CLNM risk,[15,16] others propose that isthmic tumors carry higher susceptibility.[17,18] Zhong et al[19] specifically identified mid/inferior pole lesions as high-risk zones, a finding corroborated by our results. Nevertheless, definitive confirmation of topographic influence requires future prospective, multicenter studies employing standardized mapping protocols.

Elevated TSH levels have also been recognized as an independent predictor of extrathyroidal extension and CLNM.[20,21] Our data reinforce observations by Z.G. et al,[20] who noted significantly higher TSH in patients with central metastasis (P = .014) and extrathyroidal extension (P = .003). Mechanistically, TSH overexpression appears to stimulate cAMP-dependent pathways that enhance thyrocyte proliferation and may facilitate lymphatic spread in capsular-invasive PTC.[22]

CEUS characteristics offer additional predictive value. Zhan et al[23] and Chen et al[1] identified heterogeneous and hyper-/iso-enhancement during the peak phase as significant predictors, with integrated CEUS models achieving an AUC of 0.757. These enhancement patterns are thought to arise from perfusion disturbances caused by microarchitectural disruption during extracapsular invasion.

Likewise, hypervascularity assessed via Doppler ultrasound has been repeatedly linked to CLNM.[19,24] Both Jang et al[24] and Zhong et al[19] documented strong correlations between vascular density and metastatic risk, consistent with our conclusions. The pathophysiological basis may lie in VEGF-driven angiogenesis, which generates abnormal vessel networks and hypoxic microenvironments conducive to invasion.

Investigations by Li et al[16]and Chen et al[1] confirm the prognostic significance of irregular tumor margins for CLNM prediction, aligning with our core conclusion. This morphological trait signifies localized aggressive behavior, where tumor cells breach basal membranes and invade lymphatic channels, initiating metastatic dissemination.

Machine learning represents a cutting-edge artificial intelligence methodology offering distinct advantages in deciphering complex nonlinear relationships beyond conventional linear analytical approaches.[25–27] Unlike traditional linear models, ML algorithms enable effective integration of multimodal data (combining ultrasonographic features, CEUS parameters, and thyroid function biomarkers) to reveal intricate associations imperceptible to univariate analysis and substantially improve predictive capability. We therefore developed an integrated predictive framework incorporating 8 distinct machine learning algorithms, including multivariate logistic regression, eXtreme Gradient Boosting, CatBoost, AdaBoost, and support vector machines. Tree-based ensemble methods, particularly CatBoost, demonstrated exceptional performance in this task, likely due to their inherent capability to handle mixed data types, model complex feature interactions, and maintain robustness against overfitting (characteristics particularly valuable for clinical prediction tasks where variables operate through interdependent pathways).

This investigation pioneers the comparative assessment of multiple ML algorithms for predicting CLNM specifically in capsular-invasive PTC, a high-risk clinical cohort. All 8 integrated models demonstrated clinically relevant accuracy, with CatBoost emerging as the optimal predictive model based on its balanced discriminative capacity and sensitivity metrics. Comprehensive validation via decision curve analysis and calibration curves further substantiated CatBoost’s robustness and clinical utility. The model exhibits significant translatability for central metastasis detection while maintaining exceptional accuracy and reliability, offering substantial decision-support capabilities for personalized surgical strategy formulation.

However, the inherent complexity of machine learning models creates interpretability challenges that have historically hindered their clinical implementation. To address this critical “black box” limitation, we employed SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis to quantify feature-specific contributions to model outputs. This explainable AI framework provides 3 distinct clinical advantages: First, it validates and complements traditional statistical analysis by quantitatively identifying the most influential predictors (e.g., maximum diameter > 10 mm, tumor location), thus bridging machine learning outputs with established clinical knowledge. Second, through visualization techniques including bar plots, dependence plots, and individualized force plots, SHAP analysis illuminates the model’s decision pathways, fostering clinician trust and facilitating adoption into clinical workflows. Most importantly, as demonstrated in Figure 8, this approach enables patient-specific explanation of predictions, allowing surgeons to understand which factors contributed to an individual’s risk assessment (thereby supporting personalized patient communication and tailored surgical decision-making). This integration of robust prediction with model interpretability represents a significant advancement over conventional prediction tools and addresses a fundamental barrier to clinical implementation of AI systems in medical practice.

Compared with previous studies, our work offers several novel contributions. First, while most existing models have investigated heterogeneous PTC populations, our study specifically targeted the high-risk subgroup of sonographically confirmed capsular-invasive PTC patients, thereby addressing a clinically relevant cohort with greater metastatic propensity. Second, we integrated multimodal information, including both conventional and contrast-enhanced ultrasound features along with thyroid function biomarkers, which to our knowledge has not been systematically combined in prior studies. Third, we performed a comprehensive comparison of 8 machine learning algorithms and demonstrated that the CatBoost model, coupled with SHAP interpretability, achieved superior predictive performance while overcoming the “black-box” limitation of traditional AI approaches. Finally, by providing transparent, feature-level explanations, our model not only improves predictive accuracy but also enhances clinical interpretability, supporting personalized surgical planning. These innovations collectively distinguish our work from previous nomogram- or ultrasound-based models and highlight its potential for clinical translation.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, this study was conducted in a single-center retrospective setting, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, although the model demonstrated promising performance, the moderate cohort size without external or prospective validation restricts its robustness and clinical applicability. To address these issues, future studies should prioritize large-scale, multicenter cohorts with prospective designs to validate and refine the predictive framework. In addition, incorporating heterogeneous populations across different clinical settings will be essential to confirm the model’s stability and adaptability. Ultimately, integrating externally validated models into clinical decision support systems or developing user-friendly web-based or mobile risk calculators could enhance clinical translation and assist surgeons in individualized surgical planning for patients with capsular-invasive PTC.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, we identified 6 key preoperative predictors of CLNM and developed an explainable CatBoost model integrating dual-modality ultrasound and thyroid function. This model demonstrated good predictive performance and, crucially, provides transparent insights into the driving factors behind its predictions through SHAP analysis. It holds promise as a clinical decision aid for optimizing the surgical management of patients with capsular-invasive PTC, potentially reducing unnecessary prophylactic CLND while ensuring appropriate intervention for high-risk cases.

Identification and Validation of Genes Involved in Immune and Ferroptosis in Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome Using Bioinformatics and Experimental Approach
Authors: Yang Chen, Liwei Xing, Zhe He, Xiaoqi Wang, Yujing Chen, Xiaowei Yuan, Rong Zhao
Published: 2026

Background

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a complex endocrine and metabolic disorder [1], with an estimated prevalence ranging from 8% to 13% globally [2]. Hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovary morphology constitute the main diagnostic criteria for PCOS in clinical practice [3]. PCOS is associated with comorbidities such as abnormal lipid metabolism, obesity, and an increased long-term cardiovascular and metabolic risk [4, 5]. Although the clinical manifestations of PCOS are highly heterogeneous, its underlying pathophysiological mechanisms remain incompletely understood. The current consensus is that the disease’s pathogenesis is driven by genetic, environmental, and epigenetic factors, which converge on key disruptions including hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis dysfunction, insulin signaling pathway aberration, and a state of chronic low-grade inflammation [6–8].

Emerging evidence implicates the immune-inflammatory response as a key player in the pathological mechanisms of PCOS [9]. Patients with PCOS often exhibit a persistent state of low-grade chronic inflammation, showing increased circulating concentrations of inflammatory markers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and the systemic immune-inflammation index [10, 11]. Meanwhile, patients exhibit increased secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, paralleling an imbalance in immune cell subsets [12, 13]. For example, there is a significant increase in CD14+ and CD16– monocytes with a pro-inflammatory phenotype, and these cells can serve as important precursors of tissue macrophages [14]. These abnormal changes in the systemic and local immune microenvironment further exacerbate insulin resistance and metabolic disorders, promote hyperandrogenism, and impair normal ovarian function and endometrial receptivity [13, 15]. Therefore, a systematic analysis of the key molecular characteristics of PCOS from the perspective of immune inflammation is conducive to a deeper understanding of its pathological mechanisms and aids in identifying potential therapeutic targets and novel strategies.

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent, lipid-peroxidation-driven mode of programmed cell death [16], and its roles in metabolic diseases, reproductive system disorders, and immune regulation have increasingly drawn attention [17, 18]. Manifesting as a systemic metabolic disorder syndrome, PCOS demonstrates pathological links to metabolic imbalance and tissue damage driven by ferroptosis [19]. Studies indicate that in PCOS patients, ferroptosis may participate in the progression of reproductive disorders and metabolic abnormalities by catalyzing oxidative stress and inflammation, and directly or indirectly impairing ovarian and endometrial functions [20, 21]. It may also be related to an increased risk of long-term liver diseases, cardiovascular diseases, and diabetic complications [22, 23]. However, the regulatory network of ferroptosis in PCOS and its key molecular hubs still lack systematic elucidation.

With the rapid development of bioinformatics and multi-omics integrated analysis technologies, mining key disease genes from public databases and combining with experimental verification has become an important strategy for elucidating the mechanisms of complex diseases [24]. Therefore, this study initially screened differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using bioinformatics methods based on the PCOS datasets in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. In combination with multiple algorithms, it identified key candidate genes closely associated with the immune and ferroptosis pathways and evaluated their diagnostic value. Subsequently, a regulatory system for these key genes was established in cell models to detect relevant cell phenotypes and ferroptosis-related indicators. Meanwhile, animal experiments were conducted to analyze the pathological morphology of ovaries and hormone levels. The study systematically clarified the regulatory functions and molecular mechanisms of key genes in the development of PCOS, thus providing a theoretical basis for a more in-depth understanding of its pathophysiological process and the exploration of new therapeutic targets.

Materials and Methods

Screening and Enrichment Analysis of DEGs

Datasets of GSE34526, GSE80432, GSE10946, GSE48031, and GSE84958 were downloaded from the National Center for Biotechnology Information GEO database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Among them, GSE34526 and GSE80432 served as the training set, while GSE10946, GSE48031, and GSE84958 were used as independent validation sets. Batch effects were then corrected using the removeBatchEffect function from the limma package (version 3.60.6), and the batch-corrected matrix was used for subsequent analyses [25]. The limma package (version 3.60.6) in R4.3.2 was utilized to screen for DEGs in PCOS (false discovery rate < 0.05 and |log2 Fold Change| > 0.585). Subsequently, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway, Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment, and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were conducted on the DEGs using the clusterProfiler package (version 4.6.2) in R4.3.2.

Screening of Key Candidate Genes and Evaluation of Diagnostic Efficiency

The weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was carried out employing the WGCNA package (version 1.73) in R4.3.2. Genes selected from PCOS-related modules were intersected with immune- and ferroptosis-related gene sets to obtain respective module genes. The overlapping genes resulting from the intersection of these two module gene sets with PCOS DEGs in the validation set were identified as key candidate genes. Subsequently, three machine learning methods were comprehensively applied for feature selection and importance assessment of the key candidate genes: LASSO regression (glmnet package, version 4.1.8), RandomForest (RandomForest package, version 4.7.1.2), and support vector machine–recursive feature elimination (SVM, e1071 package, version 1.7‑16, and the caret package, version 7.0.1). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted utilizing the pROC package (version 1.18.5) to systematically assess the diagnostic performance of the key candidate genes for PCOS.

Cell Culture

Human ovarian granulosa cell line (KGN cells) was obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China) and cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/F12 (GIBCO-BRL, Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. To mimic PCOS conditions, KGN cells were exposed to 500 nM dihydrotestosterone (DHT, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) for 24 h, as described previously [26, 27]. To inhibit ferroptosis, cells were treated with 10 µM ferroptosis inhibitor (Ferrostatin-1, Selleck) for 24 h.

Cell Transfection

NNMT knockdown (shNNMT) and overexpression (oeNNMT) vectors were constructed. For NNMT knockdown, three specific short-hairpin RNA sequences (shNNMT-1: 5’-CTATGTGTGTGATCTTGAA-3’; shNNMT-2: 5’-AGGTGATCTCGCAAAGTTA-3’; shNNMT-3: 5’-GCTCCTCTCTGCTTGTGAA-3’) were designed, and then ligated into the pLKO.1 vector. For NNMT overexpression, the complete NNMT sequence was ligated into the pLVX-Puro plasmid. The corresponding viral particles were prepared using a lentiviral packaging system. Cells transduced with the blank lentiviral vector or scrambled shRNA (shNC) lentivirus were used as negative controls.

Evaluation of Cell Viability and Cytotoxicity

Following seeding and attachment of KGN cells in a 96-well plate, the culture medium was gently replaced along the well wall with Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) solution prepared in fresh medium. The plate was incubated in the dark for 1.5–2 h, after which cell viability was evaluated via absorbance measurement at 450 nm. Using the lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) cytotoxicity assay kit (Beyotime, C0016) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, LDH cytotoxicity was assessed in the collected cell culture supernatant, with absorbance measured at 490 nm.

Detection of Ferroptosis-Related Indicators

The Fe²⁺ level was quantified using an iron colorimetric assay kit (Abcam, ab83366). The malondialdehyde (MDA; Elabscience, E-BC-K025-M) concentration was determined by a lipid peroxidation assay kit. Lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) were assayed using the C11-BODIPY fluorescent probe. The C11-BODIPY probe was co-incubated with the cell suspension for 30 min. After washing with phosphate-buffered saline, the fluorescence intensity was detected by flow cytometry for quantitative analysis.

Detection by Real-Time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-qPCR)

Following RNA extraction from KGN cells and ovarian tissues with TRIzol reagent, reverse transcription was performed using the Qiagen one-step RT-qPCR kit to generate cDNA. The amplification reaction was executed on the ABI 7500 system with SYBR Green premix. The primer sequences for the genes analyzed are detailed in Table S1.

Western Blot Analysis

Total protein concentration was assayed with the BCA protein assay kit. Separation of equal protein aliquots was achieved via SDS-PAGE electrophoresis. The transferred membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST buffer, and then incubated with the corresponding primary antibodies, anti-NNMT (Proteintech, 15123-1-AP), anti-SLC7A11 (Abcam, ab307601), anti-GPX4 (Proteintech, 30388-1-AP), anti-ACSL4 (Abcam, ab155282), or anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, 60004-1-Ig). After incubating the membrane with horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary antibodies (ZSGB-BIO, ZB-2301, ZB-2305) for 2 h, ECL chemiluminescent substrate was used for development. After image acquisition with the Tanon 4600 fully automatic chemiluminescence imaging system, the band gray values were quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software.

Establishment of PCOS Mouse Model

From the Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center, four-week-old female C57BL/6 mice were used in this study. The PCOS mouse model was induced via daily subcutaneous injection of dehydroisoandrosterone (DHEA; LKT Laboratories, 60 mg/kg body weight) for 20 days. Ovarian tissues and whole blood were obtained from the mice at the end of the experiment, following euthanasia by an overdose of pentobarbital sodium anesthesia. The animal experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Hainan Medical University (Approval number HYLL-2024-823).

Hematoxylin and EOsin (HE) Staining

For paraffin embedding, the 4% paraformaldehyde-fixed ovarian tissues were subjected to dehydration through graded ethanol, followed by clearing in xylene. Serial sections with a thickness of 4 μm were dewaxed, hydrated, stained with HE, and then mounted with neutral balsam.

Determination of Serum Hormone Levels

The whole blood of mice was collected into ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid-contained tubes and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C to obtain plasma samples. Plasma samples were analyzed by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) to determine the concentrations of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH; Elabscience, E-EL-M0511), luteinizing hormone (LH; Elabscience, E-EL-M3053), and anti-mullerian hormone (AMH; Elabscience, E-EL-M3015), following the kit instructions precisely. A standard curve and quality control samples were set during the determination process, and all samples were tested in duplicate.

Statistical Analysis

Using GraphPad Prism 8.4.2 software, all experimental data were statistically analyzed and expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between two groups were performed using the unpaired Student’s t-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were assessed by the one-way analysis of variance followed by the Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. A P of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification and Functional Enrichment of PCOS-Related DEGs

To identify stable DEGs related to PCOS, this study conducted differential expression analysis on two training datasets respectively and took the intersection. A total of 1102 genes were found to be up-regulated, while 654 genes were down-regulated in the PCOS samples (Fig. 1A). Cluster analysis of the DEGs demonstrated that the molecular profiles of the PCOS group versus the control group were markedly distinct, and the expression levels of the DEGs could be clearly differentiated across the two cohorts (Fig. 1B), indicating a strong correlation between the dysregulation of these genes and the pathogenesis of PCOS. Enrichment analysis of the GSE34526-derived DEGs revealed remarkable enrichment in the “immune response regulating signaling pathway” (Fig. 1C), and the “ferroptosis” also exhibited significant enrichment characteristics (Fig. 1D). The results of this enrichment analysis suggested that abnormal immune regulation and disordered ferroptosis processes may jointly participate in the pathogenesis of PCOS. In addition, enrichment analysis of the GSE34526 dataset using GO and KEGG pathways revealed that the DEGs in PCOS exhibited marked functional heterogeneity. Immune-inflammation-related functions emerged as the primary categories enriched among the up-regulated genes, involving biological processes such as “positive regulation of cytokine production” and “leukocyte migration”, and were clustered in the “phagosome” and “NOD-like receptor immune signaling pathway” (Fig. 2A-D). The down-regulated genes were found to be mainly implicated in metabolic regulation and signal transduction, including the “insulin secretion involved in cellular response to glucose stimulus” and “negative regulation of Wnt signaling pathway”, and were enriched in the “calcium signaling pathway” and “fatty acid metabolism” (Fig. 2E-H). These results further revealed that the pathological mechanism of PCOS may involve both immune-inflammation activation and metabolic signal disorders.

Identification of Key Candidate Genes by WGCNA and Cross-Validation

To systematically screen for key candidate genes closely associated with the diagnosis of PCOS, we first employed WGCNA. When the soft threshold was 12, the scale-free topological fit index R² reached 0.8, meeting the scale-free network condition (Fig. 3A). The average connectivity results further supported the rationality of this parameter selection (Fig. 3B). Based on this, the constructed clustering dendrogram showed that genes were divided into multiple co-expression modules (Fig. 3C). An intersection analysis was performed on PCOS-related module genes and ferroptosis-and immune-related gene sets (Fig. 3D), obtaining 37 module genes related to ferroptosis and 402 module genes related to immunity. The union of these two sets formed a gene set, among which 19 genes were common to all three (Fig. 3D). Further, the module gene set was intersected with the up-regulated DEGs in PCOS samples from three validation datasets respectively, and 5, 1, and 6 common genes were screened out in sequence. Finally, 12 key candidate genes (RNF144B, C3AR1, GLIPR1, S100A8, NNMT, GBP2, DNAJC3, CD36, FTL, GPAM, PELI3, and VSIG4) related to PCOS diagnosis were obtained.

Diagnostic Value of Key Candidate Genes

To systematically evaluate the diagnostic performance of candidate genes, we integrated three machine-learning models, SVM, RandomForest, and LASSO, and analyzed them in a multi-cohort including the training set and three independent validation sets. The results showed that in the training set, the RandomForest model exhibited the best diagnostic discrimination ability (AUC = 0.75, Fig. 4A). In external validation, the performance of each model showed a certain degree of cohort-dependence (Fig. 4B-D). The SVM model exhibited a higher diagnostic efficacy in GSE84958 dataset, and RandomForest model exhibited higher diagnostic efficacy in GSE48031 and GSE10946 validation cohorts.

NNMT Expression is up-Regulated by DHT and Induces Ferroptosis of KGN Cells

To examine the impact of DHT treatment on the expression of 12 key candidate genes, we exposed KGN cells to DHT for varying durations. RT-qPCR results showed that after DHT treatment for 0, 12, and 24 h, the mRNA expressions of RNF144B, C3AR1, GLIPR1, S100A8, NNMT, GBP2, and FTL genes were significantly up-regulated over time, while the expressions of DNAJC3, GPAM, and VSIG4 were unchanged over time. Among them, the mRNA expression of NNMT was up-regulated most significantly within 24 h (Fig. 5A, B). Western blot further confirmed that with the prolongation of 500 nM DHT treatment time, the protein expression of NNMT increased significantly, consistent with the mRNA expression trend (Fig. 5C). Moreover, the protein expression of NNMT was increased significantly with the increased concentrations of DHT (Fig. 5D). Based on this, an NNMT overexpression vector was constructed and transduced into KGN cells. Both RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis confirmed that the mRNA and protein levels of NNMT were markedly increased in the overexpression group relative to the blank vector control group (Fig. 5E, F).

To explore the effects of NNMT overexpression on ferroptosis, we performed measurement of lipid peroxidation, iron accumulation, and expression levels of related proteins. As depicted in Fig. 5G, the oeNNMT group exhibited a significant elevation in MDA level when compared to the vector group. At the same time, the intracellular Fe²⁺ level in the oeNNMT group also increased significantly (Fig. 5H). Using flow cytometry, we further observed that the oeNNMT group exhibited markedly elevated lipid ROS levels compared to the vector group (Fig. 5I), verifying the promoting effect of NNMT overexpression on lipid oxidation. Western blot analysis revealed that ferroptosis-inhibitory proteins SLC7A11 and GPX4 were significantly down-regulated in the oeNNMT group, whereas ACSL4, a ferroptosis-promoting protein, exhibited marked up-regulation (Fig. 5J). These results indicated that NNMT overexpression could induce ferroptosis.

NNMT Overexpression Induces Ferroptosis-Associated Cell Death in KGN Cells

NNMT overexpression could significantly reduce cell viability, increase the cytotoxicity, elevate intracellular Fe²⁺ level, increase lipid ROS levels, down-regulated expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4, and up-regulated ACSL4 expression (Fig. 6A-F). Notably, after treatment with the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1, these performances were reversed. These findings suggested that the cell death induced by NNMT overexpression was related to the ferroptosis pathway.

Knocking Down NNMT Alleviates DHT-Induced Ferroptosis in KGN Cells

To clarify the regulatory function of NNMT in DHT-induced ferroptosis in KGN cells, we constructed a shNNMT knockdown vector. Using RT-qPCR and Western blot, we demonstrated that shNNMT significantly reduced both the mRNA and protein expression of NNMT (Fig. 7A-C). Functional experiments showed that after DHT treatment, we observed that cell viability was markedly restored in the shNNMT group relative to the shNC + DHT group (Fig. 7D), and the cytotoxicity was significantly reduced (Fig. 7E). Further detection of ferroptosis-related indicators found that shNNMT significantly inhibited DHT-induced MDA production (Fig. 7F), Fe²⁺ accumulation (Fig. 7G), and lipid ROS increase (Fig. 7H). Western blot results demonstrated that shNNMT reversed the down-regulation of SLC7A11 and GPX4 expression and the up-regulation of ACSL4 expression triggered by DHT (Fig. 7I). These results indicated that knocking down NNMT could alleviate DHT-induced ferroptosis in KGN cells by regulating the expression of ferroptosis-related proteins, inhibiting iron ion accumulation, and lipid peroxidation.

DHEA Induces Ovarian Pathological Changes and Abnormal Expression of Ferroptosis-Related Genes in PCOS Mice

To validate the involvement of NNMT and the ferroptosis-related pathway in PCOS pathogenesis in vivo, we established a PCOS mouse model via DHEA injection. As shown in Fig. 8A-C, the number of cystic follicles in the ovaries of PCOS group mice increased significantly, and the number of corpora lutea decreased significantly. Using ELISA, we observed that the plasma concentrations of LH and AMH were markedly increased, while FSH levels showed a significant decrease (Fig. 8D-F). At the molecular level, significant upregulation of NNMT and ACSL4 mRNA expression, along with downregulation of SLC7A11 and GPX4, was detected in the ovaries of the PCOS group by RT-qPCR (Fig. 8G-J). Western blot analysis further verified the up-regulation of NNMT and ACSL4 protein expressions and the down-regulation of SLC7A11 and GPX4 expressions (Fig. 8K). These results indicated that in the DHEA-induced PCOS mouse model, the ovarian tissue shows typical pathological changes and endocrine disorders, accompanied by increased NNMT expression and coordinated abnormal expression of genes and proteins related to the ferroptosis pathway.

Discussion

PCOS accounts for 90–95% of anovulatory infertility cases among women presenting to fertility clinics [28]. Current first-line management strategies emphasize a high-calorie breakfast and low-calorie dinner pattern, alongside targeted micronutrient supplementation and resistance training [29]. PCOS presents with typical ovarian dysfunction closely related to chronic low-grade inflammation, metabolic disorders, and oxidative stress [30]. However, the molecular mechanisms linking these pathological processes have not been fully elucidated. To deeply explore the molecular basis of PCOS and identify potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets, this study integrated bioinformatics, machine learning, and multi-level experimental validation. It revealed the central role of NNMT in driving ferroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells in a hyperandrogenic environment, thus establishing a functional connection among “hyperandrogenism-metabolic disorder-oxidative stress-cell death”. This not only provides a new perspective for understanding the cytological basis of follicular developmental arrest in PCOS but also offers potential precise targets for intervening in this disease.

Immune dysregulation and ferroptosis are closely related in the pathogenesis of PCOS. Transcriptome analysis first confirmed that DEGs are related to “positive regulation of cytokine production”, “leukocyte migration”, and the “NOD-like receptor signaling pathway” in PCOS patients, directly corresponding at the molecular level to the characteristic of their clinical manifestation as a chronic low-grade inflammatory state [31, 32]. The significant enrichment of DEGs in the “ferroptosis” signaling pathway further confirmed that immune-inflammation activation may trigger ferroptosis, ultimately leading to ovarian granulosa cell dysfunction and abnormal follicular development [33, 34].

The up-regulation of NNMT expression was established as a key mechanistic node connecting PCOS-associated hyperandrogenemia and ferroptosis in ovarian granulosa cells. By using WGCNA combined with cross-validation of multiple datasets, 12 key candidate genes were screened out, with a focus on NNMT, whose expression was significantly up-regulated after androgen treatment. As a core metabolic enzyme, overexpression of NNMT consumes a large amount of the methyl donor S-adenosylmethionine, thereby reshaping the cellular state at both the epigenetic modification and basic metabolism levels, creating a microenvironment that promotes oxidative stress [35, 36]. This disrupted metabolic background may directly undermine the cell’s core defense system against ferroptosis [37]. By enhancing the iron-ion-dependent Fenton reaction, it leads to the accumulation of intracellular Fe²⁺ and a marked elevation of lipid peroxidation markers, including lipid ROS and MDA, ultimately driving the cells towards ferroptosis [38, 39].

In the PCOS mouse model, the classic endocrine disorder pattern of elevated LH and AMH and decreased FSH is a core manifestation connecting the dysfunction at multiple levels of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis. Abnormally high pulsatile secretion of LH is a hallmark of PCOS [40]. Its neuroendocrine origin is attributed to the dysfunction of the gonadotropin-releasing hormone pulse generator in the hypothalamus [41]. AMH is regarded as a marker of ovarian reserve. High levels of AMH can act on the tanycytes in the median eminence of the hypothalamus and directly contribute to neuroendocrine disorders at the hypothalamic level [42]. In addition, the decrease or relative deficiency of FSH levels directly affects the normal development of follicles and the selection of dominant follicles, leading to follicular developmental arrest, the formation of polycystic-like changes, and ovulatory dysfunction [43, 44]. DHEA, an androgen of adrenal origin, has been demonstrated to induce PCOS in experimental animals [45]. In this study, DHEA-induced PCOS model mice demonstrated increased number of cystic follicles and corpora lutea and levels of LH, AMH, and decreased FSH level, recapitulating cardinal features of PCOS. Previous studies have reported that high-fat diet combined with DHEA or letrozole has been shown to induce metabolic changes, making it a more reliable PCOS model [46]. Therefore, further studies are required to understand whether the changes in NNMT and ferroptosis pathways are exhibited in alternative models.

In multiple organ injury and disease models, the combined expression pattern of down-regulated SLC7A11/GPX4 and up-regulated ACSL4 has been confirmed as a common molecular signature of ferroptosis in cells [47]. SLC7A11 is a key transporter for cells to uptake cystine for the synthesis of glutathione [48]. A decrease in its expression leads to the depletion of the intracellular antioxidant glutathione, thus directly weakening the activity of GPX4 [49]. As the only enzyme capable of specifically reducing membrane lipid peroxides, the loss of GPX4 function means the collapse of the cell’s last line of defense against lipid peroxidation [50]. At the same time, the up-regulation of ACSL4 expression can promote the esterification of long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids, greatly increasing the substrates in membrane phospholipids that are prone to oxidation, and thus significantly enhancing the sensitivity to lipid peroxidation [51]. For example, in the cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury model, neurons and glial cells generally show decreased expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4, increased expression of ACSL4, along with the accumulation of the lipid peroxidation product MDA and a decrease in glutathione levels [52]. This series of changes together constitute the typical pathological features of ferroptosis.

Conclusion

This study, through a systematic research combining bioinformatics and experimental validation, revealed the synergistic mechanism of immune dysregulation and ferroptosis in the pathogenesis of PCOS. Transcriptome analysis revealed that DEGs in PCOS were enriched in both immune response and ferroptosis signaling pathways. Twelve key candidate genes screened through multi-dataset analysis have potential diagnostic value. Mechanistic studies indicated that androgens could induce ferroptosis in ovarian granulosa cells by significantly upregulating NNMT expression, leading to exacerbated lipid peroxidation, iron ion accumulation, and disordered expression of ferroptosis-related proteins. Knocking down NNMT could effectively alleviate this process. In-vivo experiments further confirmed that the PCOS mouse model simultaneously exhibited typical endocrine disorders and abnormally activated ferroptosis pathways mediated by NNMT. This study systematically clarified the crucial hub role of NNMT in linking endocrine disorders in PCOS with ferroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells, and provided a theoretical basis for targeted therapeutic strategies targeting the NNMT-ferroptosis axis.

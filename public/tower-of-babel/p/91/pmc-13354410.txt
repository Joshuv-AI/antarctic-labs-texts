Suzetrigine (Journavx): first-in-class NaV1.8 sodium-channel blocker offering non-opioid oral relief for moderate-to-severe acute pain
Authors: Abdullah Imtiaz, Ehtisham Haider, Hashir Atiq, Sakan Binte Imran, Joshua Ekouo
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Tissue injury, inflammation, or surgical trauma causes acute moderate-to-severe pain, which is a major cause of healthcare utilization worldwide. Pathophysiologically, nociceptive signaling is stimulated by the activation of peripheral sensory neurons, and the NaV1.8 voltage-gated sodium channel plays an important role in action potential conduction and the amplification of pain; it belongs to the NaV1 channel family. In patients, severe pain is often accompanied by functional disability, psychological suffering, and poor management, which increases the risk of chronic pain. The burden of pain is enormous worldwide, causing high levels of morbidity, disability, and disability-adjusted life years, while indirectly contributing to opioid-related harm. One of the main emerging concepts is selective peripheral sodium-channel blockade to achieve analgesia without central opioid effects. Existing interventions, such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAIDs), acetaminophen, regional anesthesia, and opioids, are constrained by safety concerns. Suzetrigine (Journavx), a first-in-class oral NaV1.8 blocker, provides an alternative method of non-opioid analgesia, offering a substitute for opioid analgesics and NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors[1]. This study adheres to the TITAN 2025 guidelines to ensure transparent and reproducible methods[2].

Suzetrigine, whose trade name is also known as Journavx, is a revolutionary step in managing acute pain as the first-of-its-kind oral non-opioid analgesic that selectively targets voltage-gated sodium channel NaV1.8 (Table 1). In contrast to classic opioid drugs, which interact centrally and carry a high risk of addiction and respiratory depression, suzetrigine blocks the NaV1.8 channels, which are primarily located on peripheral nociceptive neurons, thereby inhibiting the transmission of pain signals to the central nervous system. Pharmacologic and clinical features of suzetrigine (Journavx) for acute pain.

Suzetrigine showed significant pain intensity elimination over placebo and equivalent results to a hydrocodone/acetaminophen mixture during 48 hours in Phase 3 clinical trials of post-surgical pain models, including abdominoplasty and bunionectomy, with good tolerability and a low incidence of serious adverse events. The most frequent side effects documented are mild itching, muscle spasms, and rash, without any indication of probable addiction potential, which is a major plus in terms of the current opioid crisis[1].

Journavx was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in January 2025 for moderate to severe acute pain in adults, a gap unoccupied by a new pain drug in decades. It has shown therapeutic promise due to its emergence as a non-addictive and safer option compared to opioids as a means of treating short-term postoperative pain through the inhibition of peripheral sodium channels. Nevertheless, its long-term efficacy, cost-effectiveness, and possible use in chronic pain are still unclear and require further research[4,5].

Suzetrigine (Journavx) is a pioneering, non-opioid method for the management of acute pain that involves creating a selective blockade of sodium channels (NaV1.8) in peripheral regions. This addresses an important, untapped clinical need, given that it produces opioid-comparable analgesia with no risks of dependence or respiratory depression. Its approval by the Food and Drug Administration highlights its potential in reducing opioid use in acute pain clinical environments. Suzetrigine is a potentially valuable innovation in contemporary analgesic treatment, despite the fact that additional research is required to elucidate its long-term safety, cost-effectiveness, and use during chronic pain management.

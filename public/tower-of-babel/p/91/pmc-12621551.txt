An intelligent active polylactic acid/gelatin nanofibrous membrane with Janus structure for meat freshness monitoring and preservation
Authors: Xingzi Lan, Xinyu Chen, Jian Zhang, Yue Qiu, Wei Lan, Yadong Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Real-time monitoring of food freshness and extending its shelf life are crucial for reducing food waste and enhancing food quality and safety. In this study, an intelligent active food packaging with Janus structure was fabricated by sequentially electrospinning a hydrophobic polylactic acid (PLA) nanofibrous membrane and a hydrophilic PLA/gelatin/cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB)/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The incorporation of CTAB enhanced both the hydrophilicity and antibacterial activity. The water contact angle of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin membrane decreased to 0° within 3 s, confirming the directional water transport capability. The membrane with 0.5% w/w alizarin achieved the highest antioxidant activity at 59.48%. The alizarin-loaded membranes exhibited significant colorimetric response under alkaline conditions, with ΔE above 5. In meat freshness monitoring applications, the bilayered membrane turned purple by Day 7, which was consistent with the change of pH. These results indicated the bilayered membrane has significant potential for application in intelligent active food packaging.

Introduction

High-protein meat products are highly susceptible to bacterial contamination and subsequent spoilage during transportation and storage (Li, Wang, et al., 2025). This not only leads to substantial food waste and economic losses but also poses severe threats to human health (Wang, Zhao, et al., 2025). Food packaging has emerged as an effective strategy to extend the shelf life of food, thereby reducing waste and safety risks. However, traditional petroleum-based food packaging materials are non-biodegradable, lack active functions, and are incapable of real-time freshness monitoring. Hence, developing biodegradable, intelligent, and active food packaging is urgently needed to ensure food quality and safety.

pH-responsive intelligent food packaging offers a simple, fast, and cost-effective approach that enables real-time monitoring of food freshness (Xia et al., 2025). In meat products, microbial contamination and endogenous enzymatic degradation generate volatile biogenic amines including ammonia, methylamine, dimethylamine, and trimethylamine. This subsequently elevates environmental pH, thereby triggering color changes in pH-responsive intelligent food packaging (Lv et al., 2025). Currently, anthocyanins have been widely employed in intelligent food packaging. However, they exhibit low stability and are susceptible to degradation by light and temperature (Bian et al., 2024). Alizarin is a natural pigment with good photostability (Gam, Kalita, Gogoi, Bora, & Roy, 2025). Alizarin exhibits distinct pH-responsive behavior: yellow under acidic conditions, red at neutral pH, and purple in alkaline environments (He et al., 2025). Furthermore, alizarin imparts antioxidant, antibacterial and UV-blocking properties to the intelligent food packaging (Cui et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2024). However, alizarin exhibits limited antibacterial activity, which cannot be utilized to effectively extend the shelf life of food. Therefore, it is necessary to enhance the antibacterial effect of the intelligent active food packaging.

Bacterial growth and metabolism require an optimal aqueous environment, as nutrient absorption, enzymatic activity, and waste excretion all depend on adequate moisture conditions (Huang et al., 2025). Meat products contain abundant exudates that create favorable conditions for bacterial proliferation, thereby accelerating spoilage (Jiao et al., 2023). Developing food packaging with directional water transport function to absorb exudates and maintain a dry environment might be a strategy to inhibit bacterial growth. Beyond physical bacteriostatic approaches, incorporating chemical antibacterial agents might suppress the growth of external bacterial contaminants. CTAB, a cationic surfactant composed of a hydrophilic quaternary ammonium group and a hydrophobic alkyl chain, exhibits favorable biocompatibility and broad-spectrum antibacterial activity (Cui, Wan, Wang, Zhao, & Sun, 2023). CTAB has been integrated into food packaging to impart antibacterial property (Li et al., 2024). Due to the cytotoxicity of high-concentration CTAB, the concentration should be carefully optimized to reduce adverse effects while maintaining strong antibacterial efficacy (Ke et al., 2024). Consequently, integrating physical bacteriostatic methods with chemical antibacterial agents in food packaging might achieve a dual antibacterial effect.

Electrospinning is a straightforward, convenient, mild and versatile electrohydrodynamic technology to prepare nanofibers (Aman Mohammadi et al., 2024). Electrospinning enables encapsulation of bioactive compounds while largely preserving their activity (Zhang et al., 2024). Compared to cast films, electrospun nanofibrous membranes exhibit higher porosity and specific surface area, leading to enhanced pH-response sensitivity (Chen et al., 2025). Importantly, electrospinning provides a primary strategy for fabricating Janus nanofibrous membranes with asymmetric wettability, achieving directional water transport (Ao et al., 2023). A wide range of synthetic and natural polymers can be used for electrospinning. Through appropriate selection of hydrophobic and hydrophilic materials, Janus nanofibrous membranes with asymmetric wettability can be readily prepared (Si et al., 2025). PLA is a synthetic polymer with good hydrophobicity and biodegradability (Li, Zhang, et al., 2025). Gelatin is a natural polymer that exhibits good hydrophilicity, biocompatibility, and biodegradability (Liang et al., 2025). PLA and gelatin have attracted great attention in fabricating food packaging.

In this study, a multifunctional nanofibrous membrane integrating pH-responsive, antibacterial, antioxidant, UV-blocking and directional water transport properties was fabricated to monitor meat freshness and extend its shelf life (Fig. 1). To construct Janus nanofibrous membrane with asymmetric wettability, hydrophobic PLA nanofibrous membrane was deposited on the surface of hydrophilic PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The Janus membrane with directional water transport capability maintained a dry environment for the meat, thus potentially inhibiting bacterial growth. The incorporation of CTAB into the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane not only imparted antibacterial activity but also enhanced its hydrophilicity. The combination of physical bacteriostatic method and chemical antibacterial method might inhibit microbial proliferation. Alizarin was also loaded into the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane to achieve pH-responsive, antioxidant, and UV-blocking functions. The morphology, chemical composition, water contact angle, wetting behavior, antigravity water transport behavior and UV-shielding property of the nanofibrous membrane were evaluated. Furthermore, the antioxidant, antibacterial, and pH-responsive properties of the nanofibrous membrane were investigated. Finally, the potential application of the nanofibrous membrane was explored through monitoring chicken breast meat freshness.Fig. 1Schematic illustration showing the preparation processes, structures and functions of nanofibrous membrane.Fig. 1

Materials and methods

Materials

PLA (4032D) was obtained from NatureWorks Co., Ltd. (USA). Type A gelatin from porcine skin (G2625, gel strength: 175 g Bloom) and 1, 1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). CTAB (99%), alizarin (97%) and methylene blue were provided by Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP, ≥99.5%) was supplied by Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Ethanol absolute was bought from Guangdong Guanghua Sci-Tech Co., Ltd. Muller Hinton broth and nutrient agar were procured from Guangdong Huankai microbial Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (China). pH buffer solution was ordered from Fangqi Instrument Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Fresh chicken breast meat with a one-day shelf life, maintained under refrigerated storage, was purchased from a local supermarket (Guangzhou, China) on the morning.

Preparation of electrospinning solutions

The 28% w/v PLA solution was obtained by adding 0.56 g PLA into 2 mL HFIP and stirring at room temperature until complete dissolution (Xu et al., 2025). The PLA/gelatin solution was prepared by dissolving 0.45 g PLA and 0.135 g gelatin into 3 mL HFIP, yielding PLA at 15% w/v and gelatin at 30% w/w relative to PLA (Bogdanova et al., 2023). The PLA/gelatin/CTAB solution was prepared by dissolving 0.45 g PLA, 0.135 g gelatin and 0.0135 g CTAB into 3 mL HFIP, yielding CTAB at 3% w/w relative to PLA. 15 mg alizarin was dissolved in 1 mL HFIP by ultrasonication for 10 min to yield 15 mg/mL alizarin solution. Subsequently, the PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin solution was prepared by adding 150 μL alizarin solution into 3 mL PLA/gelatin solution, yielding alizarin at 0.5% w/w based on PLA. 30 μL, 90 μL and 150 μL of alizarin solution were added into 3 mL PLA/gelatin/CTAB solution to prepare three composite solutions termed: PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin, respectively.

Fabrication of monolayer nanofibrous membranes

The 28% w/v PLA solution was loaded into a 2.5 mL syringe equipped with a 22 G blunt steel needle. The applied voltage between the needle and the collector and flow rate were set to 12 kV and 1.5 mL/h, respectively. The PLA nanofibrous membrane was deposited on a grounded rotating drum wrapped with a silicone paper. The rotating drum speed was maintained at 1 r/s. To prepare PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane, the applied voltage and flow rate were adjusted to 12 kV and 2 mL/h, respectively. To prepare PLA/gelatin/CTAB nanofibrous membrane, the electrospinning processes was conducted at applied voltage of 12 kV and flow rate of 1.8 mL/h. The PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes were fabricated at applied voltage of 16 kV and flow rate of 2.5 mL/h.

Fabrication of dual-layer nanofibrous membranes

The dual-layer nanofibrous membranes were fabricated by sequentially electrospinning. Firstly, the PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin solutions were electrospun for 50 min. Then, the hydrophobic PLA nanofibrous membrane was deposited on the surface of above alizarin-loaded nanofibrous membranes to form composite nanofibrous membranes. The electrospinning time of the PLA nanofibrous membrane was set at 2 min. The fabricated dual-layer nanofibrous membranes were named PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin, respectively.

Morphology observation

The surface morphology of the nanofibrous membrane was examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, TM3030, Hitachi, Japan) at magnification of 2000 times. The average diameter was determined using Image J software by measuring 50 randomly selected nanofibers.

Chemical characterization

The functional groups and chemical structure of the alizarin, CTAB, gelatin and a series of nanofibrous membranes were determined by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, Nicolet iS50R, ThermoFisher Scientific) in attenuated total reflection mode. The samples were scanned 32 times at a wavenumber resolution of 4 cm−1 in the wavenumber scanning range of 500–4000 cm−1.

Water contact angle measurement

An automatic contact angle system (OCA100, Dataphysics, Germany) was carried out to measure the water contact angles of the PLA, PLA/gelatin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes. 3 μL deionized water was dropped on the surface of the nanofibrous membrane. Specially, the deionized water was dropped on the PLA layer for the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The change in water contact angle was recorded on video and measured using Image J software.

Wetting behavior test

The wetting behavior was quantitatively explored by the spreading diameter of water droplet. The PLA nanofibrous membrane and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane were chosen as the representative nanofibrous membranes to evaluate the wetting behavior. A water droplet stained with methylene blue with volume of 10 μL was dropped onto the surface of PLA nanofibrous membrane. Moreover, the dyed water droplet was dropped onto the PLA layer of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. A mobile phone was used to record the spreading process of water droplet. Image J software was utilized to analyze the spreading diameter of water droplet.

Antigravity water transport behavior assessment

To further verify the directional water transport property of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane, the antigravity water transport experiment was carried out. 3 μL of water droplet dyed with methylene blue was fed upward to contact the PLA layer of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The antigravity water transport process was recorded using a mobile phone.

UV-shielding property evaluation

The UV-shielding property of the nanofibrous membrane was studied using a UV-induced color change card (Wu et al., 2024). A piece of nanofibrous membrane was placed on the middle part of the UV-induced color change card, which was followed by exposure to a 365 nm UV lamp (RSD-0078, Royalstar) for 10 s. Photographs of the UV-induced color change card were captured both before and after removal of the nanofibrous membrane. Furthermore, a piece of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) membrane was used as the control. Moreover, the transmittance of the PVC film and the nanofibrous membranes in the 200–400 nm wavelength range was investigated using an Ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometer (TU-1901, Beijing Purkinje General Instrument Co., LtD).

Antioxidant activity evaluation

The antioxidant activity of the nanofibrous membranes was investigated using the DPPH free radical scavenging method (Jiang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025). A DPPH ethanol solution was prepared by dissolving DPPH in anhydrous ethanol via vigorous vortex mixing to achieve complete dissolution. 10 mg of the nanofibrous membrane was transferred to a 5 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 2 mL of the freshly prepared DPPH ethanol solution. The mixture was then incubated for 30 min, 5 h, and 24 h at room temperature in the dark. At predetermined time intervals, the absorbance of DPPH ethanol solution was detected at 517 nm using an Ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometer. The DPPH ethanol solution was served as the control. The radical scavenging activity was determined by the following formula:(1)Radical scavenging activity%=Acontrol−AsampleAcontrol×100where Acontrol represented the absorbance of the control and Asample represented the absorbance of the DPPH ethanol solution containing nanofibrous membrane.

Antibacterial activity evaluation

The antibacterial activity of the nanofibrous membranes was assessed using the plate count method. Gram-negative bacteria Escherichia coli (E. coli, ATCC 25922) and Gram-positive bacteria Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus, ATCC 29213) were used as two model pathogens. The bacterial suspension was adjusted to 108 CFU/mL according to the result of optical density. The bacterial suspension was further diluted to 105 CFU/mL using physiological saline. 0.3 mL of bacterial suspension with a concentration of 105 CFU/mL was added in 2.7 mL of broth culture medium, followed by the addition of 30 mg of nanofibrous membrane. The group without adding nanofibrous membrane was served as the control. The inoculum was incubated in a shaking incubator at 37 °C with 100 rpm for 9 h. Then, the bacterial suspension was diluted to appropriate dilution factors. 50 μL of dilution was evenly spread onto agar plate and then incubated at 37 °C for 12 h. At last, viable bacterial colonies on the agar plate were calculated by Image J software.

Color response of alizarin solution

pH 3–11 buffer solution (1 mL) was added to individual glass vials. Subsequently, 1 mL of 0.2 mg/mL alizarin ethanol solution was added to each vial, and the mixtures were mixed thoroughly. Then, 100 μL aliquots of each solution were pipetted into a 96-well microplate. The solutions were scanned with a multi-mode microplate reader (Varioskan LUX, Thermo Fisher Scientific) in the range of 300–800 nm.

Color response of electrospun nanofibrous membranes

The color response of electrospun nanofibrous membranes under different pH buffer solutions were evaluated by a colorimeter (LS 170, Linshang). The nanofibrous membranes (1 × 1 cm) were immersed into the buffer solution (1 mL) with different pH levels for 10 min. A mobile phone was used to capture the color changes of the nanofibrous membranes. Then, the nanofibrous membranes were removed from the buffer solutions, blotted dry with filter paper to remove surface moisture, and subsequently their color parameters (L*, a*, and b* values) were measured using a colorimeter. L*, a*, and b* represent lightness, redness/greenness, and yellowness/blueness, respectively. The total color difference (ΔE) was calculated using the following formula (Zhang et al., 2025):(2)∆E=∆L∗2+∆a∗2+∆b∗2where ∆L=L∗−L0∗, ∆a=a∗−a0∗, ∆b=b∗−b0∗; L0∗, a0∗ and b0∗ were the initial color parameters of the nanofibrous membranes; while L∗, a∗ and b∗ were the color parameters of the nanofibrous membranes under different pH buffer solutions.

Freshness indication of chicken breast meat

The colorimetric performance of the nanofibrous membranes was evaluated using chicken breast meat as a model food. The experimental methodology was based on previous study (Li, Wang, et al., 2025). A piece of fresh chicken breast meat was placed in the bottom of Petri dish with diameter of 3 cm. Then, the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane was attached to the inner side of the lid. Subsequently, the Petri dish was sealed by Parafilm and stored at 4 °C. At predetermined time intervals (0, 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 days), the Petri dish was photographed without removing the Parafilm. Besides, the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membranes was served as the control.

Determination of pH

The pH of the chicken breast meat during storage was measured. 2 g of chicken breast meat was homogenized with 28 mL of physiological saline to prepare the sample solution. Then, the pH of the sample solution was detected using a digital pH meter (PHS-3E, Shanghai Yidian Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd.).

Results and discussion

Morphology of monolayer nanofibrous membranes

The morphology of the nanofibrous membrane significantly influences the stability and functionality in food packaging (Xu et al., 2025). SEM was employed to investigate the morphology of the nanofibrous membranes. The morphology and diameter distribution are shown in Fig. 2. The PLA nanofibrous membrane exhibited large pore size and a small number of beads. The average fiber diameter of the PLA nanofibrous membrane was 836 ± 162 nm, indicating a broad diameter distribution. The PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane exhibited bead-free uniform nanofibers, with the average fiber diameter of 979 ± 110 nm. After incorporation of CTAB into the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane, the average fiber diameter decreased to 736 ± 94 nm, which was likely due to a decrease in solution flow rate. Moreover, CTAB is a cationic surfactant that can reduce the surface tension and increase the conductivity of the solution, leading to a decrease in fiber diameter (Bonakdar, Hamdi, Nazarenko, Ariya, & Rodrigue, 2023). The PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane displayed a uniform and smooth morphology, with the average fiber diameter of 875 ± 100 nm. Of note, the flow rate was increased to 2.5 mL/h for the fabrication of PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. This was likely due to the addition of alizarin leading to the increase of the viscosity of the spinning solution. The PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibers exhibited bending morphologies with smooth surfaces. Their average fiber diameters were 743 ± 106 nm, 702 ± 130 nm, and 736 ± 89 nm, respectively. Water contact angle analysis

The pictures and change curves of the water contact angles of four kinds of nanofibrous membranes are shown in Fig. 4a and Fig. 4b, respectively. The initial water contact angle of the PLA nanofibrous membrane was 96.2°. The droplet remained relatively stable for 50 s, demonstrating the good hydrophobicity of the PLA nanofibrous membrane. The water contact angle of the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane decreased from 131.1° to 0° within 25 s. The incorporation of gelatin improved the hydrophilicity, resulting from the presence of hydrophilic groups in gelatin. The further incorporation of CTAB into the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane resulted in a rapid decrease in water contact angle from 83° to 0° within 1.5 s. This was attributed to CTAB being a cationic surfactant, which significantly enhanced the hydrophilicity of the nanofibrous membrane (Bonakdar et al., 2023). When a droplet was dropped on the PLA layer of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane, the water contact angle gradually decreased from 117.1° to 0° within 3 s. The results suggested that the nanofibrous membrane with asymmetric wettability facilitated the transport of droplets from the hydrophobic side to the hydrophilic side. Therefore, the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane can maintain a dry environment around food and thereby inhibiting bacterial growth.Fig. 4(a) Dynamic water contact angle picture of PLA, PLA/gelatin, PLA/gelatin/CTAB, and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes, indicating that water droplets can transfer from the hydrophobic side to the hydrophilic side. The mechanism of directional water transport for the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane was proposed. Fig. 5e displays the cross-sectional view of the bilayered nanofibrous membrane. The hydrophobic PLA layer was positioned on top of the hydrophilic PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer. When a droplet was dropped on the PLA layer, the droplet encountered opposing interactions: hydrophobic force (HF) and hydrostatic pressure (HP). When HP exceeded HF, the droplet penetrated the hydrophobic layer and reached the hydrophilic layer. Subsequently, the droplet spread along the hydrophilic layer due to capillary force (CF) (Jiao et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2026).

Antigravity water transport behavior

The high moisture environment in food will result in water accumulation. Therefore, the antigravity water transport behavior of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane was also investigated. As shown in Fig. 6a, the water droplet was fed upward to touch the PLA layer of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The water droplet was able to penetrate through the PLA layer and spread into the PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer by overcoming gravity and hydrophobic resistance, driven by the capillary force of the hydrophilic PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer (Huang et al., 2025).Fig. 6(a) Antigravity water transport behavior of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. (b) Antigravity directional water transport mechanism of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. The antigravity directional water transport mechanism of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane was illustrated in Fig. 6b. For the single-layer membrane, upward droplet penetration occurs when capillary force surpasses gravitational force (Wang et al., 2019). The generation of capillary force is governed by the Laplace pressure (PL), as described by the following equation:(3)PL=4γ×cosθDwhere θ is the water contact angle, γ is the surface tension and D is the diameter of capillary channels.

According to the above equation, capillary force exhibits an inverse relationship with pore diameter. In the bilayered PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane, the PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer exhibited smaller pore diameter than the PLA layer. On the other hand, the PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer exhibited higher cosθ value than that of the PLA layer, attributed to its superior hydrophilicity. Consequently, the PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer exhibited greater capillary force than those of the PLA layer. A capillary pressure difference (ΔP) was generated as follows (Qi et al., 2021):(4)∆P=PL2−PL1=4γ×cosθ2D2−4γ×cosθ1D1where PL1 and PL2 are the capillary force of the PLA layer and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer, respectively. θ1 and θ2 are the water contact angle of the PLA layer and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer, respectively. D1 and D2 are the diameter of capillary channels of the PLA layer and PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin layer, respectively.

Based on the above analysis, PL2 was larger than PL1. Therefore, ∆P acted as the driving force for water droplet transported from the lower to upper layer.

UV-shielding property

UV irradiation induces accelerated lipid oxidation and nutrient degradation in meat products, thereby shortening their shelf life (Lv et al., 2025). Food packaging with UV-shielding property effectively inhibits lipid oxidation, discoloration, and nutrient loss (Wu et al., 2024). Thus, the UV-shielding property of the nanofibrous membrane was evaluated using a UV-sensitive test card. As shown in Fig. 7a, the initial color of UV-induced color change card was white. After exposure to a 365 nm UV lamp for 10 s, the UV-induced color change card appeared deep purple. After removal of the PVC film, the covered area remained deep purple, indicating the lack of UV-shielding capability. The area covered by the PLA/gelatin/CTAB nanofibrous membrane exhibited a pale pink coloration, showing limited UV-shielding capacity of the membrane. This phenomenon is likely attributable to light reflection caused by its micro/nanostructure. It was observed that the area covered by the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane appeared light white, suggesting certain UV-shielding capability. This performance was attributed to UV-light absorption by alizarin (Ezati, Rhim, Moradi, Tajik, & Molaei, 2020; Wu, Ma, Gao, Liu, & Gao, 2022). The incorporation of alizarin into the nanofibrous membrane shielded against UV irradiation and mitigated oxidative food deterioration.Fig. 7(a) Photographs of the UV-induced color change card. Antioxidant activity

The antioxidant activity represents a critical feature of active food packaging, functioning to scavenge free radicals and thereby prevent food discoloration, rancidity, and odor formation (Wu et al., 2022). Therefore, the antioxidant activity of the nanofibrous membranes was investigated by DPPH radical scavenging method. Fig. 8a displays the photographs of the DPPH solution after reaction with different nanofibrous membranes for 5 h and 24 h. After 5 h of incubation, the DPPH solution in the PLA/gelatin group retained its purple color, indicating that the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane lacked antioxidant activity. The incorporation of CTAB and alizarin into the nanofibrous membrane changed the solution color from purple to yellow. As the content of alizarin increased, the yellow color deepened. Furthermore, the yellow color deepened progressively with prolonged incubation time, attributable to the continuous release of alizarin.Fig. 8(a) Photographs of the DPPH solution after reaction with different nanofibrous membranes for 5 h and 24 h. (b) DPPH radical scavenging activity of the nanofibrous membranes. The antioxidant capacity of the nanofibrous membranes increased with higher alizarin content and longer duration.Fig. 8

(a) Photographs of the DPPH solution after reaction with different nanofibrous membranes for 5 h and 24 h. (b) DPPH radical scavenging activity of the nanofibrous membranes. The antioxidant capacity of the nanofibrous membranes increased with higher alizarin content and longer duration.

The DPPH radical scavenging activity of the nanofibrous membranes was quantificationally demonstrated in Fig. 8b. After 30 min of incubation, the radical scavenging activities of the PLA/gelatin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes were 2.24 ± 0.92%, 10.65 ± 11.48%, 17.67 ± 0.88%, and 19.29 ± 0.62%, respectively. The antioxidant activity gradually enhanced with the increment of alizarin content. Alizarin possesses multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups within its chemical structure, enabling it to donate hydrogen atoms that quench DPPH radicals, thereby inducing color fading in the solution. On the one hand, these results confirmed successful incorporation of alizarin into the nanofibrous membranes. On the other hand, they demonstrated retention of alizarin's antioxidant activity under electrostatic field conditions. When the incubation time increased to 5 h, the radical scavenging activities of the PLA/gelatin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes improved to 4.15 ± 2.03%, 19.96 ± 2.85%, 32.43 ± 1.06%, and 36.5 ± 2.00%, respectively. With incubation time extended to 24 h, the radical scavenging activities of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.1 alizarin, PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.3 alizarin, and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes enhanced to 35.83 ± 1.08%, 55.76 ± 1.67%, and 59.48 ± 2.24%, respectively. These results demonstrated that the alizarin-loaded nanofibrous membranes possessed long-lasting antioxidant capacity, suggesting their potential in preventing oxidative deterioration in meat products. The fabricated PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane demonstrated higher antioxidant activity than previously reported anthocyanin-loaded nanofibrous membranes. For example, the DPPH radical scavenging activity of the PLA/quaternized chitosan/2% anthocyanin nanofibrous membrane was around 40% (Xu et al., 2025). Moreover, Ezati et al. found that the DPPH radical scavenging activity of alizarin was higher than that of the natural antioxidant ascorbic acid at a low concentration (Ezati and Rhim, 2020).

Antibacterial activity

The antibacterial activity of food packaging plays a critical role in inhibiting the growth of bacteria, thus maintaining food quality and prolonging shelf life (Xu et al., 2025). E. coli and S. aureus are prominent bacterium responsible for food spoilage. The antibacterial activity of the nanofibrous membranes against E. coli and S. aureus was evaluated. Fig. 9a shows the pictures of surviving E. coli colonies in an agar plate. The control group and the PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin group exhibited abundant bacterial colonies. The PLA/gelatin/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane did not exhibit inhibitory effect against E. coli. After exposure to PLA/gelatin/CTAB and PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membranes, almost no viable colonies were found on the agar plate. The antibacterial property of the nanofibrous membranes was mainly related to the antibacterial effect of CTAB. With further increased in pH from 7 to 11, the purple color of the alizarin solution gradually deepened. The UV–visible absorption spectra of the alizarin solution at pH 3–11 are shown in Fig. 10b. Under acidic conditions at pH 3–5, the alizarin solution exhibited the maximum absorption peak at approximately 433 nm (He et al., 2025). At pH 6, the alizarin solution exhibited two absorption peaks, at approximately 433 nm and 535 nm. In an alkaline environment, the maximum absorption peak of the alizarin solution shifted to approximately 535 nm. The color change of alizarin under various pH conditions was ascribed to the reversible transformations in its chemical structure, as shown in Fig. 10c. Under acidic conditions (pH 3–5), alizarin predominantly adopted neutral form (Zou et al., 2024). At pH 6, alizarin predominantly existed in the mono-anionic form (He et al., 2025). At pH 7–11, the phenolic hydroxyl group of the alizarin exhibited a secondary deprotonation behavior. Freshness indication of chicken breast meat

To demonstrate the practical applicability of PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane in freshness monitoring, the color change of the membrane during chicken breast meat storage was monitored. As shown in Fig. 12a, the initial color of the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane was white. With prolonged storage time, the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane gradually became wetted and was completely wetted by Day 5, due to the condensation of water vapor into liquid at low temperatures. From Day 5 to Day 9, the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane maintained a stable light gray color, revealing a lack of pH response ability. The PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane exhibited a bright yellow initially. From Day 0 to Day 5, the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane progressively wetted while transitioning to light yellow. By Day 7, the color of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane changed to purple. With increasing storage time, the purple color of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane deepened. Wang et al. observed that fresh fish stored at 4 °C were about to begin spoilage by Day 6 (Wang, Liang, et al., 2025). The color change in the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane was primarily attributable to volatile alkali compound release during protein degradation in chicken breast meat, resulting in pH elevation. The pH of the chicken breast meat during storage is shown in Fig. 12b. During the 5-day storage period, no noticeable change was observed in the pH value of the chicken breast meat. By Day 7, the pH increased to 5.78. When the storage period was extended to Day 9, the pH value of the chicken breast meat increased rapidly to 6.50. The change trend of pH was consistent with the color change of the PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane. Conclusion

In summary, a multifunctional nanofibrous membrane integrating pH-responsive, antibacterial, antioxidant, UV-blocking and directional water transport properties was successfully prepared, which was used for freshness monitoring and preservation. The incorporation of CTAB into the PLA/gelatin nanofibrous membrane significantly enhanced hydrophilicity, thus enabling a water droplet to transfer from the hydrophobic to the hydrophilic layer within 3 s. Furthermore, the load of CTAB effectively inhibited the growth of E. coli and S. aureus. The PLA@PLA/gelatin/CTAB/0.5 alizarin nanofibrous membrane possessed UV-shielding capability and antioxidant activity owing to the encapsulation of alizarin. The antioxidant activity reached 59.48% after 24 h of incubation. The bilayered nanofibrous membrane exhibited a color change from yellow to purple as the pH increased from 3 to 11. In meat freshness monitoring applications, the color change of the membrane corresponded to the pH variation in meat. In the future, the use of multiple-needle electrospinning and needleless electrospinning could enhance production efficiency, making large-scale production and application of Janus membrane feasible. As a limitation, the concentration of CTAB requires future optimization due to its potential cytotoxicity. Moreover, the stability of the nanofibrous membrane in real packaging environments remains to be validated. Overall, the work paves the way for developing intelligent active food packaging.

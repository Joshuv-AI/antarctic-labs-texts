Malaria vaccine - A solution to the endemic burden of malaria in the Democratic Republic of the Congo; a call for action
Authors: Chrispin Biamba, Mayar Moustafa Budair, Hazem Mohamed Mousa, Excellent Rugendabanga, Faizullah Jafar, Elie Kihanduka, Samson Hangi, Jones Onesime, Aymar Akilimali
Published: 2024

Dear editor

Malaria poses a significant public health challenge. It is commonly found in sub-Saharan Africa where about half of the population is exposed to the disease. Democratic Republic of the Congo (DR Congo) is economically poor and its health system is weakened by the epidemic of infectious diseases [1]. Malaria prevention through vaccines is fundamental and has proven to be extremely effective. The vaccine against Plasmodium Falciparum malaria- RTS, S/AS01- has reduced the incidence of the disease by 51 % in individuals receiving three vaccinations on a 0-1-2-month schedule [2]. This vaccine was recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2021, however currently the R21/Matrix-M vaccine is the number one recommended vaccine by the organization. It reduced symptomatic cases of malaria by 75 % following the three-dose series. A fourth dose is given a year later to maintain efficacy [3]. Approximately 28 countries in Africa plan to introduce the WHO-recommended malaria vaccine into their disease control programs. Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, has approved providing technical and financial support for the deployment of these vaccines that provide evident results when administered just before the high transmission season [3,4]. The request for malaria vaccines from the DR Congo to Gavi was approved in April 2023 and the start of vaccination is scheduled for the beginning of 2024. The DR Congo has requested millions of doses of the vaccine to reach thousands of children between the years 2024–2026 in 13 provinces. However, access to the vaccines for most of the Congolese population will be complicated due to the lack of road infrastructure in certain regions of the country. With the funds from the 2024 budget and additional support in planning, vaccine deployment should be feasible [4].

In the DR Congo, many challenges prevent its people from fighting against the expansion of the disease. Armed conflicts constantly displace the population and there is a lack of access to healthcare services. The country also acts as a hospitable environment for the transmission of malaria due to its humid climate and vegetation [5]. The lack of funds will also hinder the people's ability to receive the vaccine to completely eradicate the disease. A recent study showed that 21.4 % of malaria affected patients did not seek proper health consultations even when a diagnosis was made in a medical setting [4]. This is because self-medication is quite common in the DR Congo due to low household income. Consistency is also required by the public when it comes to the utilization of preventative measures. This furthermore demonstrates the importance of providing the vaccine as it is a quick and efficient one-time solution [5]. The elimination of malaria in the country requires the implementation of immediate interventional strategies. The strategy of upmost importance is the provision of the malaria vaccine to the population, free of charge and in accessible facilities. Secondly, the use of insecticide-treated mosquito nets should be distributed to the entire population during mass campaigns. Moreover, the use of Indoor Residual Spraying of Insecticide as recommended by the National Malaria Control Program should be encouraged. There also should be preventative management during pregnancy such as the administration of Sulphadoxine-pyrimethamine- a drug given twice antenatally in areas of high transmission. In addition to the national and international measures currently in place, the mass vaccination campaign against malaria is the ultimate solution. Although the cost is a major concern, the potential benefits prove that it is the most effective strategy especially when it comes to large scale implementation. Vulnerable groups such as pregnant women and children could be targeted with this preventative measure in a more feasible manner. Governmental support would be required in terms of improving the country's health infrastructure and budget allocations which could be a challenge [1,5]. The DR Congo has the potential to be on track for a “Zero Malaria” status like neighboring Rwanda, which is among the top 5 countries on this path, or even better like Cape Verde which was recently declared “Malaria-Free” in the year 2024. Therefore, the Congolese government and various non-governmental organizations are recommended to make further efforts to materialize the initiatives already launched, for a Congo without malaria before 2030.

Systematic review of the changing land to people relationship and co-evolution of land administration
Authors: Achamyeleh Gashu Adam
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Land to people interaction is an old phenomenon that has occurred throughout human history. The scope and nature of the relationship has been changing over time as a result of continuous and disruptive socio-cultural and technological changes. Human civilizations observed in the past as well as the current rapid technological advancements and high rate of urbanization, along with the need for sustainable development are driving for changes in land to people interactions. However, studies and scientific discourses on examining the changing people-land relationships and the co-evolving need to manage or govern land are scarce. This paper, therefore, aimed to offer an understanding on how the concept of land administration evolved from the complexities involved in the changing people to land relationships. To achieve the objective of the paper, desk review research approach was employed. The review demonstrates that various forms, designations, functions of land administration have been evolved over the history of human being as a response to manage the changing people to land relationships. Thus, it is plausible to assume that the notion of land administration is deeply founded in human history and is one of the oldest professions that support humanity. Finally, this study underlines that land administration as a subject or concept is constantly evolving due to the changing nature of people's perception towards land and it is also expected to continue to co-evolve and respond to address societal needs at a particular time.

Introduction

Land is not only just the earth that people walk on and use, it is fundamentally also the way people think about place [1]. This cognitive approach of describing land confirms that land can be an abstract concept connected to peoples' feelings and everyday practices [2]. This implies that people have a living spiritual, physical, social and cultural connection to land [3]. Here, people to land relationship refer to the interaction and feedback between human being and nature on the earth surface. This interaction has begun long time ago through simple production activities for survival and enhanced through accumulation of knowledge about the surrounding [4]. The idea of people to land relationship also includes human beings’ dependence on nature as well as their active role to exploit nature. Hence, the content and connotation of the people to land relationship is changing overtime in the history of human being due to the differences in human abilities to understand, utilize and reconstruct the nature with the development of social and technological advancement [4]. More specially, the scope and nature of the relationship varies depending on the level of human civilization. For instance, in the earlier times of human civilization, when hunting and gathering was the bases for human livelihood, land resource was abundant and it was freely accessible for everyone who wants to gather and hunt. This implies, during old times land was a common property of the community and there was no restriction to extract resources. Later on, due to population growth competition to control resources has started between groups or individuals. So as to avoid conflicts between tribes over land resources, boundaries created between different tribes and traditional rules established to govern land resources which finally led to the emergence of well-defined economic structures and systems [5,6]. Empirical evidences show that it was during feudalism that the first well defined and structured socio-economic system was created in human history. During feudalism, agriculture was the dominant economic activity and land was considered just only a wealth and it was only owned by landlords for perpetuity [7].

The perception of people towards land had also continued to change after industrial revolution which begun in Europe and the growth of modern cities in the 1800s. Land, during industrial revolution, was regarded as a tradable commodity and this new perception fueled the development of land market [8]. As a result, land became private property or commodity transferable in the market. Further changes in the global context in the late 20th century and beginning of the 21st century have again driven for changes in the perception of people land relationships. For instance, the idea of sustainable development emerged in 1980′ and introduced within the context of a growing awareness of an imminent ecological crisis, seems to be the leading development thought and driving force for change in human history [9]. Sustainable development as a new development thought is intended to balance three competing interests: economic, social and ecological interests of development [10]. This new thought led to a significant shift in people to land relationships aggravated by competing interests over land by diverse actors. Moreover, the multiple aspects of land itself such as economic aspect, social aspect, ecological aspect and spiritual aspect attracts various actors from diverse background with different objectives [11,12]. Hence, the current competing interests and pressures on land, including the land grabbing phenomenon, that have been growing globally, particularly in Africa, place land administration at the center of addressing social and environmental problems including food insecurity and climate change-induced displacements or climate refugees [13]. Thus, it is critical to recognize that land administration plays a crucial role in addressing the current multiple challenges associated with human beings’ growing desire to control land and other natural resources, as well as the corresponding pressures upon it.

More importantly, attempt has been made to discuss the dynamic nature of the man to land relationship and the role of land administration in addressing multiple challenges of the society by Lisa and Williamson in 1999 in their famous article [8]. However, the time frame of the article covers only perceptions of people towards land that were seen after agricultural revolution mainly after 1700s and with a focus only on the history of Western society. Hence, the article failed to explain the perception of people towards land that was seen before agricultural revolution. As a result, conducting a more in-depth review on the dynamic nature of people-to-land relationships seen throughout the history human being is important to understand how land administration has been evolving and responding to the challenges connected to it. In the review process and as a foundation to this study, it is assumed that people to land relationship came into being along with the history of human being [4]. It is also plausible to assume that land administration as a means to handle people-to-land relationships has been started as early as human history. On the contrary, studies or literature explaining the changing people to land relationship and the co-evolving responses of land administration seems to be very limited. Moreover, well-structured definitions and conceptualization of land administration has been provided only after the 1990s [1]. But still, a substantial quantity of literature has a narrower claim on land administration, as the activities and duties associated to cadaster and land register, with little attention to link and explain the changing people to land relationship to it. In view of this quandary, this study aimed to explore how people to land relationship which has been constructed through time in the history of human social and technological development contributed to the co-evolution of land administration. This study also intended to initiate ideas for future research with a more comprehensive understanding of the notion of people to land relationship and evolution of land administration. Desk review research approach was employed to achieve the objectives of this paper. This paper comprises of six sections. The next section details with the research materials and methods. The subsequent section presents detail reviews on land administration. Section four is devoted to a survey of empirical and theoretical literature on the dynamic nature of people-land relationship and land administration responses in different eras. The fifth section is attempted to summarize the key perceptions of people towards land and also attempted to develop a harmonized land administration concept. Finally, conclusions and issues for further research are provided in the last section of the paper.

Research methods and process

This research is a desk review research that aims to review and compiles previous empirical and theoretical literature on the evolution and development of land administration as a response to the changing people-land relationship. A systematic desk review research approach was employed to achieve the objectives of the research. Systematic desk review is a deliberate, explicit, and repetitive way for identifying, evaluating and synthesizing a set of related studies available in the bulk of literature in the field of land administration [14]. It is also an efficient and reproducible method for identifying, evaluating and synthesizing the existing body of knowledge compiled and produced by researchers, scholars, and practitioners in a particular field of study [14]. That is why systematic desk review approach was considered as the most appropriate method to synthesize the results of previous related studies [15]. Most often, systematic desk review has three key stages and activities to be performed: the search plan and identification stage, the survey and synthesis stage, and the result explanation and presentation stage (see Fig. 1).Fig. 1Research Process (Author produced).Fig. 1

Research Process (Author produced).

The first stage in the review process mainly consists of searching and identifying relevant and eligible documents and developing the inclusion and exclusion criteria [16]. In the search stage, an extensive search was initially conducted from all important scientific databases as much as possible. The databases include Science Direct, Scopus, Springer, Wiley & sons, Elsevier, Taylor & Francis, Sage and other sources. For search purpose, combination of key words and phrases related to the theme of the research was applied. The key words or phrases used mainly include people to land relationship and land administration. Three different mechanisms were also applied to filter the search engine before executing the searches. These filtering mechanisms were: (a) the area filter, which was specified as “land administration research papers (both empirical and theoretical papers) to ensure the suitability of the studies found; (b) the date filter, which was set to limit the search to publications from 1980 to 2022 to ensure the timeliness of the studies; and (c) the type of document, research articles published in scientific journals, books and book chapters were considered for review. Finally, about 150 documents related to the subject of study were gathered.

After gathering data, screening was conducted to determine inclusion and exclusion. The key criteria used to screen were time interval, impact factor for journal publications, title and abstract of the papers. Accordingly, the abstracts and titles of the gathered documents were evaluated and analyzed, taking into account the 40-year time period, and the impact factor of the journals were also considered and impact factor of 0.5 to above was considered. Finally, 50 documents were selected and included in the final list of documents for systematic review.

The second stage of the review process was the survey and synthesis stage. After selection of the final documents for actual review, the review process was followed by studying the title, abstract, and the entire text in the material/document. At this stage, the key activities were reading the material, learning, communication, and reflection/translation of key issues in each document. In the reading and communication phase, the text of the selected materials was critically assessed and their relationship with each other was examined to extract key concepts. The key issues considered during the reading stage were the concept of land administration, dynamic nature of people to land relationship, the link between sustainability and land administration and other issues and each issue was coded and recorded. Later on, the codes were classified in the form of categories to examine more precisely and each falls into three major categories such as: concept and development of land administration, dynamism in people to land relationship, the link between sustainability and land administration. Finally, at this stage extracted concepts were summarized and synthesized to generate new knowledge.

The third stage in the review process was result presentation phase and it mainly focused to explain people to land relationship in different eras of human history. In this stage, critical analysis and explanation towards production of new knowledge was conducted. Explanation was carried out based on key classification derived from the fundamental concepts extracted from the previous stage on the evolution and development of land administration, dynamism of people-land relationship and the link between sustainability and land administration. Finally, interpretation of the findings was made to present a balanced and impartial summary. Moreover, recommendations for practice and policy-making were also provided. Directions for future research were also suggested to fill existing gaps in knowledge or to strengthen the body of evidence in the field.

Land administration as an evolving concept

Land administration is a term commonly used by land sector professionals such as natural resources management, surveying and mapping, land use planning, land law and valuation and related fields. It is also a discipline – practiced by professionals – mostly in the public sector - and studied by academics that tend to be connected to engineering and/or social sciences or administration sciences. Despite attempts made by various scholars to define land administration in the last three decades, standalone and harmonized definition of land administration has not been provided yet. It appears that existing definitions and conceptualizations are tainted by professional bias. That is why, most often, people tend to mix-up the concept of land administration with land management and some others link it to cadaster and land register. For instance, in some countries like Ethiopia, the two terms seem to be synonymous and used interchangeably. In some other national states these two concepts are treated as two different things. For more clarification, selected definitions of land administration are reviewed and summarized below.

The first well-structured definition of land administration was provided by Nichols and McLaughlin in 1990 as the operational function of land management for making and implementing land policies [17]. According to this definition, land administration is considered as a tool for implementing land policies. The key activities of land administration include protecting, acquiring, using, transferring, and distributing rights and interests of land; and carrying out the distributive functions by planning, organizing, coordinating, directing, and controlling the use of land. Nichols again in 1993 defined land administration as the management of the land tenure systems which mainly plays a distributive function of land resources [18]. According to this definition key activities of land administration include the determination of the right to use the land and creation and enforcement of land use restrictions. Moreover, according to the above definition, the objective and function of land administration is expected to be guided by the broader objectives of land management policies [18]. For instance, the objective of land management is to facilitate economic development through inventory, extraction, and sale of natural resources. As a result, the objective of land administration need to be promoting efficient systems to allocate rights to these resources, ensuring security for private investments and introducing efficient dispute resolution system. More importantly, if the specific land management objective is to minimize the conversion of agricultural lands to urban land use, then the land administration objective might be to monitor land transactions to enforce compliance with land use and development regulations.

The United Nations Economic commission for Europe (UNECE) in 1996 has also provided the most commonly used definition of land administration as the process of recording and disseminating information about ownership, value, and use of land when implementing land management policies [19]. In this definition, ownership, value, and use of land are stated as the three key attributes of land. In addition, the issue of land information is stated as key aspect of land administration systems which should be considered as part of the wider land policy. Land administration is simply stated as a policy implementation tool intended to promote land market economy in transitioning countries from state controlled economy to market led economy.

Later on in 1999, land administration was defined as the process of regulating land and property development and the use and conservation of land, gathering of revenues from the land through sales, leasing, and taxation, and resolving of conflicts concerning the ownership and use of the land [20]. The key issues entertained in this definition are public sector activities required to support the alienation, development, use, valuation, and transfer of land. Again, the focus of this definition is promoting land market economy. In addition, public administration is considered to be the component of this definition.

A very different view of land administration is captured in the land management paradigm of Enemark, Williamson [21]. Here, land administration is described as systems and processes to manage land tenure, land use, land value and land development. According to the land management paradigm, countries are expected to improve governance, e-democracy, and knowledge management so as to improve land and food security of the society. It emphasizes that improving governance help to build effective land market and efficient land use management and it ultimately supports to achieve the goals of sustainable development.

The revised definition of land administration was presented again by United Nations Economic Commission for Europe in 2005. In the revised definition, land administration is elaborated as the process of determining, recording and disseminating information about ownership, value, and use of land when implementing land management policies [22]. Such process includes:the determination (sometimes known as the “adjudication”) of rights and other attributes of the land, the cadastral surveying and description of the rights and attributes, and the detailed documentation and provision of relevant information in support of land markets and good governance (ibid). The key aspects here are emphasis on system maintenance and e-services.

The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) in its part also defined land administration as the set of systems and processes for making land tenure rules operational [23]. The key functions, according to this definition, include land rights, land use regulations, and land valuation and taxation. The focus of this definition and explanation is land tenure security in the developing countries. Again, both formal institutions administered by official government agencies and informal institutions under customary laws and leaders are given equal weight.

In the book titled as ‘Land Administration for Sustainable Development’, which was written in 2010 by Williamson et al. land administration was defined as the process of managing land and resources from sustainable development perspectives [1]. Central to this perspective is the land management paradigm that considers the land administration functions of land tenure, use, value, and development within the context of land policy framework and land information infrastructure in a particular country context. Moreover, land administration systems are not static entities but revolve mainly around five land tenure core processes. These five core tenure processes common to most nations are: formally titling land; transferring land by agreements (buying, selling, leasing); transferring land by social events (death, birth, marriage, divorce, and exclusion and inclusion among the managing group); forming new interests or properties (subdivision and consolidation); and determining boundaries [1]. The focus of the definition of land administration contributed by these scholars is managing land tenure and the key aspect of land, the institutions and agencies people build, and the processes these institutions and agencies manage.

Scholars in the field have also initiated the development of a land information conceptual framework or data model that facilitates the quick and efficient set up of land administration system [24]. This international standard or data model for land administration refers to Land Administration Domain Model (LADM). The model is intended to assist the alignment of land administration design with societal demands embedded in national and state land polices and has been available since December 2012 [25]. It is believed that land administration systems themselves benefit only from proper data standards like LADM. LADM covers basic information related to components of land administration which includes water and other elements above and below the earth's surface and people. The components particularly include and concerned with party related data; data on RRRs and the basic administrative units; and data on spatial units and on surveying and topology/geometry [24]. From LADM perspective, thus, land administration can be defined as the process of managing information related to party which refers to people and organizations; basic administrative units which includes rights, responsibilities, and restrictions over land and spatial units (parcels) [24]. The idea of LADM is very much connected with the latest definition and description of land administration is presented by UN-GGIM [26], which defines land administration as the process of informing the ‘how’, the ‘what’, the ‘who’, the ‘when’ and the ‘where’ of land tenure, land use, land value, and land development. In this definition an attempt is made to determine record and disseminate information about the relationship between people and land. Overall, the reviews above on the conceptualization of land administration are not exhaustive. But it is possible to understand that a harmonized definition and function of land administration accepted by a wider scientific and academic community do not exist yet. The key issues and concepts in the definitions are still changing overtime and consensus on the conceptual understanding has not reached yet [27,28]. The data in Table 1 show that the key aspects in the definitions of land administration have been changing through time and much work is required from the scholars come up with harmonized understanding of land administration. The review shows that the narrow definition focusing on the operational function of land policy [17] has grown into a broader land management concept whereby the goal moves from underpinning land markets towards supporting sustainable development and good governance with the purpose to meet the needs of the people [1]. Meeting peoples’ needs has become the central issue in latest definition of land administration. As also stated by McLaren, Enemark [29] land administration should be designed to meet the needs of people and their relationship to land, to support security of tenure for all and to sustainably manage land use and natural resources. More specifically, land administration systems need to be responsive enough to the emerging new drivers and inspirations of human beings [30]. Therefore, the review shows that the contemporary concern of land administration has been shifted from managing land only to managing people-land relationship and the issue is given more attention in the series of latest land administration researches and publications.

Changing people to land relationship throughout human history

It is not difficult to conceive that people-land relationship came into being along with the history of human being. However, due to the past and ongoing socio-economic and demographic changes as well as differences in human abilities to understand and utilize land/nature, the content and connotation of people-land relationship has been changing in both scope and depth. In this section, therefore, emphasis is given to examine the structure and content of people to land relationship in six different eras or human civilizations. The review starts with examining man-land relationship during prehistory of human being: era of hunters and gatherers, and ends with the current and modern digital era. Along with the long journey of human being, the content of the people-land relationship and interaction has changed significantly. Moreover, this section is emphasized to explore how land administration is co-evolving as a response to changing people to land relationships over time.

People to land relationship in the era of hunters and gathers: Paleolithic age

The hunting and gathering dominated economic activity was practiced during the earlier history of human being until around 10000 BCE That period is referred as the ‘Paleolithic Age’ or ‘Stone Age’. During that era, land served as a major source of food through gathering and haunting and made it accessible to everyone. All food, collected by hunters and gatherers, was shared equally by everyone in the community [31]. A shared property system was the main attribute of the hunting and gathering societies. As a social norm, every hunted and gathered food was commonly used and consumed based on customary rules set by the group. Everyone in the group had equal rights to share the hunted and gathered food and it did not matter who gathered and hunted the food. Hence, from a property rights perspective, during hunters and gatherers era, people were free to move to any direction they need. Perfect freedom of movement was practiced for better hunting and gathering to satisfy their livelihood during that time. This implies that people at that time had practiced full (100%) land rights without any restriction. In other words, there was no restriction on the movement of people at individual or group level [6]. Consequently, the freedom of movement was expected to result in a spreading of people in all directions, all over the globe.

Gradually, during late Paleolithic era, people started to use and improve tools made of crude stone and wooden implants. The use of tools gradually improved the hunters' and gatherers’ economy. Through time, people learned to sharpen and shape stones, to make better weapons and cutting edges for hunting and gathering purposes. Moreover, the practice of speech started by the ancient people encouraged more group cooperation resulting in the transmission of technical knowledge to improve the hunting and gathering tools. The development of speech contributed for the evolution of different human groups in different locations with quite varied belief and social systems which ultimately resulted to the formation of different tribal groups or communities with common interest governed by social rules [5].

Later in the Paleolithic era, exclusive communal territories and property boundaries controlled by tribal groups began to emerge [6]. Following this, common resource bases started to be fenced and consequently communal properties have started to emerge replacing open access resources under the control of customary or tribal management. During that time, resource was abundant and conflict over the resources was almost non-existent or so minimal and everything was shared among the community. Traditional institutions were there to manage the fairness of forger sharing among the community and to resolve tribal conflicts over communal territories. Finally, later on in the Paleolithic era, land cultivation and domestication of animals gradually evolved out which ended the Stone Age in the entire world.

People to land relation after agricultural revolution: Neolithic era (10000BC-1000DC)

The improvement of tools used by the end of the Paleolithic era led to improvement in farm productivity which in turn resulted in Agricultural revolution or Neolithic revolution. The magnitude of socio-economic change involved and the overall shift in the utilization of land by Neolithic people demonstrated the first economic revolution in human history [32]. As a result of this economic revolution, the economic system/activity of human kind changed from hunting and gathering to cultivation of corps and livestock rearing as a source of food. Evidences show that by 9000 BCE in certain parts of the world, people were becoming increasingly dependent on regular harvests of crops or plants on a particular parcel of land [33].

The deliberate planting of grains and domestication of animals were probably triggered by population increase, stemming from improved climate after the ice age [6]. The invention of agriculture and its improvement in agricultural productivity has clearly moved the human species toward more elaborate social, cultural patterns and systems [5]. With agriculture, humans were able to settle in one spot and focus on particular economic, political, and religious goals and activities and encouraged people to settle more permanently in one particular area. Even agriculture itself requires more regular work at a particular place which consequently had supported far more people per square mile of land than hunting ever could.

The dramatic transformation from a hunting and gathering based economy to sedentary agriculture significantly strengthened the direct control and dependence of land for survival and livelihood [33]. It is also at this point that the sense of individualized property evolved. According to existing empirical evidence, there are two assumptions for emergence of individualized property system during the agricultural revolution [34]. First, farming came with a vast expansion of the realm over which individually/privately controlled and managed goods begun to replace the forager social norms of sharing food upon acquisition. A common explanation is that farming increased labor productivity, which then encouraged the adoption of private property by providing incentives for the long-term investments required in a farming economy. The second assumption for the emergence of private property right is related to the improvement of the climate change after the Ice Age (Ibid). A favorable climate for agriculture supported by improved farming tools contributed to the introduction of individualized and privately controlled and managed property for the first time in history of human being. Sets of individualized possessions in the early private property system included dwellings, private storage, and possession or ownership of garden plots by families or lineages [35,36]. More importantly, the discovery of bronze and metal as a farming tool during the later ages of Neolithic era, contributed a lot for the improvement of agricultural productivity and food security [5,36]. Metal hoes and other tools allowed farmers to work on the ground more efficiently. Metal weapons were obviously superior to those made from stone and wood. The invention of metal tools has resulted in a dramatic change for people to land relationship and has increased the attachment of man with a particular parcel of land. The increased productivity of land has also helped man to establish settled and stable way of life by creating abundant access and control on a piece of land for food which later on developed into novel individualized property. This shows that the invention of metal tools has contributed a lot for the emergence of individualized land possession which can serve as resource to extract food for the family. The role of social institutions was mainly resolving conflicts among the possessors of land for farming who can be individuals or tribal groups.

People to land relationship during feudalism (1000BC-1700)

The invention of metal in about 1000BCE triggered agricultural productivity and settled way of life and followed by the emergence of urban centers. Following this radical shift in human's history, kingdoms began to emerge as units of government system referred as Feudalism [37]. A fully developed Feudalism can be described as a socio-economic and political domination through the medium of land in which the power flow from the king down to the lowest unit/landholder or peasant. The king owned all land directly or indirectly and the king granted use of these lands to his subjects (and their heirs) in return for the rendering of military or other services. Thus, the socio-economic and political relation during feudalism was bound together by obligation of service and defense through land: the lord to protect his vassal, the vassal to do service to his lord [38].

The state unit system during feudalism was also dependent on the upward flow of land tribute from peasants (commoners) to the king in exchange for the downward flow of protection [7]. Each state structure stretched between the king and peasants claim portion of the land tribute at each level (can be grain, meat, leather, wool, wine, etc.) and keep its portion and transmit the rest to the next level of nobility and so on up to the royal court. The upward flow of land tribute sustained the entire linage of aristocracy, including the royal family. Thus, state power structure and authority during feudalism was based on ownership of land and the system was tied by loyalty and duty of the actors involved in the structure [39].

From the explanation above, feudalism as a new social and economic system resulted in a shift of property system from novel individualized property system to monarchical property system. Land, which begun to be private property of everyone after agricultural revolution, was pulled and controlled by few ruling class members and the majority were landless tenants [7]. During feudalism, all land in a given country was held by the ruling monarch or emperor and the citizens granted to practice only usufruct right in return for the rendering of military or other services or pay tribute [7]. In other words, the whole land was in the hands of few nobility and royal family members. On the other hand, the majority of the people, commoners/tenants, did not own land in almost all civilizations. More importantly, land was undisputedly considered as the primary source of wealth and power which was also primarily in the hands of very few. The legal and institutional systems established during feudalism attempted to tie the nobility and royal family members as landowners and their future generations as closely and perpetually as possible to their land [7,40]. The level of authority and power of nobility was reflected by the size of acreages/hectare/of land owned and controlled as a landlord and the amount of land tribute, in a modern sense tax, collected from citizens. Evidence also shows that the institutional arrangement in the state structure during feudalism paid more attention on the land tribute/tax collection. This laid a foundation for the establishment of a land administration function focused on fiscal aspects of land. Finally, the feudal system which considered land as a basis of all wealth and power and land tax as the basis for raising funds to maintain society and state stayed until beginning of the 18th century, industrial revolution.

People to land relationship during early capitalism (from 1700 to 1945)

The continued improvement in agricultural productivity and invention of industrial equipment in the beginning of 18th century AC led to advancement in manufacturing which also referred to as industrial revolution [41,42]. The outbreak of industrial revolution transformed the agricultural societies into more industrialized and urban societies [41]. This socio-economic transformation signaled a shift in the people-land relationship. Following the industrial revolution, the philosophy and thought that land is the basis of all wealth was changed due to the rise of capital in the economy. The rise of capital due to advancement in manufacturing encouraged industrialist and company owners to purchase land as any commodity to increase production [33,43]. Land as a commodity and factor of production has taken various forms in its definition: from agricultural land to commercial real estate to the resources available from a particular piece of land.

The rise of capital and capitalism as an economic system usurped the perception of people towards land which used to be the primary source of wealth and power during feudalism. Capitalism as an economic system allowed private actors to own and control land for their own interest and benefit like any exchangeable commodity by which the pricing determined by the interaction of supply and demand in market [44]. During early capitalism, the emphasis moved from the physical ownership of land to the conversion of land to capital for mobility of assets [33]. Thus, industrial revolution has pushed man to change his feeling towards land. Humans considered land as an exchangeable commodity which in turn also changed the functions of land administration. After the industrial revolution, land administration systems and functions geared to focus on managing land transfers with the aim to avoid frauds between the transacting parties [40].

People to land relationship during Post WWII reconstruction period (1945–1980)

The end of WWII in 1945 was another turning point for another shift to people to land relationship. After this war, the direction of global development mainly focused on the recovery and reconstruction of the economy and infrastructure destructed by the war. The global development direction and economic growth induced rapid urban expansion into the rural territories. Most scholars concede this era as the most remarkable period of economic and social progress in human civilization [45], and it is seen as a period of economic growth and cultural stability [46].

The economic recovery and reconstruction of the infrastructure boomed the global economy [47], which in turn resulted in a huge demand of land for different development and reconstruction purposes [45]. This increased competition for land for multiple land uses and ecosystem services exerted pressure on the carrying capacity of land resources. The multiplicity of actors interested in limited land resource needs proper allocation of land for the intended purposes without leaving it for market to balance the supply and demand of land [47,48]. Managing the increasing competition for land by different actors, requires efficient and effective planning allocation of land resources [49]. Thus, employing appropriate land use planning tool was considered as a mechanism to balance the competing interests over land.

The fundamental notion of a limited carrying capacity and scarcity of land resources which largely discussed during 1960's and early 1970s became the turning point for shift in people-land relationship [50]. This shift in people to land relationship observed after the new global development direction necessitated for better land use planning. This implies that land sector institutions have given more attention to land use planning supported by land information system [40]. Thus, land use planning and allocation of land for different purposes supported by suitability analysis was added to the existing land administration functions as another new function followed by new land laws, policies and institutions to address subdivisions and redistribution of land for development. Moreover, more detail land use regulations have also been enacted with the aim to allocate public amenities, minimizing spillover effects on contiguous land and increase efficiency by guiding development and redevelopment of land.

People to land relationship in the current era since 1980's

Global population boom seen after 1980's accompanied by huge demand for food has exerted pressure on world's limited land resources. The new global challenge or societal problem again calls on how to balance the resource consumption with that of environmental concerns. With this dual need, a new concept called sustainable development emerged, officially for the first time in 1987 [51]. The idea of sustainable development is that human societies must live and meet their needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. This implies that the wellbeing must be sustained over many generations while maintaining the quality of the environment and natural resources system. The idea of sustainable development explicitly refers to s balance the three competing needs: economic growth, social equity and environmental protection [52]. It is also described as set of three interconnected pillars standing to balance economic, social, and environmental goals simultaneously or a mix of three intersected places [53]. Economic growth refers to the increase in goods and services produced by using natural resources and environmental requirements need to be recognized in the process of economic production [54]. Social component of sustainable development can be measured by how far the distribution of resources or growth benefits are fair or just to the society. That is why, sustainable development is presented in most literature in the form of three intersecting circles of society, economy and environment or ecology at which sustainability being achieved at the intersection (see Fig. 3). Therefore, sustainability as a development concept and thought has underpinned the policy objectives of most countries globally, and countries have adopted as national development strategy and policy framework.Fig. 3Three intersecting pillar elements of sustainability [53].Fig. 3

Three intersecting pillar elements of sustainability [53].

As explained above, the concept of sustainability not only connects the current and future generation with land or natural resources, it also creates many possibilities in rethinking land resource management practices [2]. This new development approach clearly manifests a change in the perception of man towards land. The incompatibility between the rapid population growth and the growing demand for food and land for shelter which also calls for fairness and equity in the distribution of land resources for both current and future generations were the driving factors for the birth of the idea of sustainable development. In the current era, therefore, it is clearly realized that land is scarce community resource which needs to be shared between the current and future generations. That is why better and proper management or administration of land resources gains more urgency in this era than ever before [1]. This global development guideline is also evident in the international instruments such as Agenda 21 and the Habitat II Global Plan of Action [55].

It is also evident that the trading patterns of land as a commodity have also undergone substantial changes during this era [56]. It is also realized that land is complex and transactable commodity accommodating various and competing stakes and interests over it [57]. This shift calls again for a system designed to support a modern land market that supports to trade this complex commodity such as mortgage backed certificates, leasing, selling, buying, water rights, land information, development instruments and vertical villages. Supporting modern land markets through clear structures or functions of land administration help to achieve sustainability [21]. The necessity and pressure for modern market and proper land use management has resulted in new, modern and global perspective of land administration [58] composed of four key functions such as land tenure, value, use and development integrated by land information/cadaster (see Fig. 4). Moreover, authoritative and fundamental information about the four core functions of land administration are expected to be provided by the spatial information infrastructure of land administration.Fig. 4Land Management Paradigm Perspective of land administration functions [58].Fig. 4

Land Management Paradigm Perspective of land administration functions [58].

The new and modern perspective of land administration is primarily derived by the need for sustainable development and as a tool for it. The new perspective is primarily expected to support land market and land use systems properly and effectively [21]. Supporting a land market includes activities such as executing cadastral surveys in order to identify and subdivide land, maintaining land registry systems to support land trading (buying, selling, mortgaging and leasing land) and managing land information systems to facilitate access to the relevant information [59]. On the other hand, supporting effectiveness of the land use system focuses on the proper use and development of land guided by proper land use planning. Land use planning has a distributive role on scarce land resource compatible with the growing demand for it resulted from rapid population growth. Land use planning also helps to limit natural resource degradation for the benefit of the wider community [49]. All these developments in the functions of land administration revealed that core and sub-core functions of land administration have been rapidly expanding over the last couple of years and it seems the change will continue in the future.

The ongoing innovation and development of information technology have also been enhancing the land administration services in quality and quantity. The changing people to land relationships, the need to pursue sustainable development and the increasing need to administer complex commodities within an ICT (information and communications technologies) enabled to create virtual world in land sector, and offer new opportunities for evolution and development of land administration. Computerization and accompanied innovations in the information technology has transformed the land administration functions particularly the cadastral system rapidly. Further efforts and studies on the evolution of land administration from technology perspective have been made and explored five stages of change. The first stage recognizes that historical cadastral systems as core functions of land administration which were manually operated with all maps and indexes focusing on ensuring tenure security. In the second stage, computerization in 1980s contributed for the creation of digital cadastral data bases (DCDBs) and computerized indexes and this major change initiated to bring the separate functions of surveying, mapping and cadaster and land registration together as land information infrastructure. In the third stage, with the growth of the internet in 1990's, the web enabled land administration services started to be delivered. This development again has facilitated digital lodgment of cadastral data and opened up the era of e-Conveyancing and e-Land administration. The SDI concept, together with web enablement, stimulated the integration of different data sets, particularly the natural and the built environmental data sets. With these integrated data sets now, it is considered as critical infrastructure for any nation state. Thus, cadastral data in the future will be seen as information and a new concept called iLand will become the paradigm for the next decade (see Fig. 5). iLand is a vision of integrated, spatially enabled, land information available on the Internet that is freely and easily accessible.Fig. 5Evolution of new land administration services [1].Fig. 5

Evolution of new land administration services [1].

The overall advancement of information technology has contributed for the introduction of multi-functional land administration system with the purpose to accommodate and balance competing needs on scarce community resources. Information technology has made easier to integrate all of those key functions of land administration: land tenure, value, use and development. Thus, land information infrastructure (often referred to as cadasters and land registers) represent the core function of land administration, designed to record all competing interests, described as 3RRRs (rights, responsibilities and restrictions) associated with land and with the purpose of achieving sustainability. Land administration, thus, is conceptualized as managing the rights, responsibilities and restrictions burdened over the land user/owner with the purpose to balance and reconcile competing interests (Bennett et al., 2005). These competing interests can be ownership right/interests, land development or environmental requirements which restrict not to do with the purpose to preserve natural system and other forms of property regulations. Supporting the idea of 3RRRs conceptual framework, land administration domain model (LADM) was developed by the scholars in the field [24]. LADM is a land information conceptual model developed to support full functionality of land administration system in a given country with three packages: parties (people and organizations); basic administrative units, rights, responsibilities, and restriction; spatial units as components of land administration. Overall, the review above reveals that the current land administration functions became significantly more comprehensive than any other time in history. More importantly, the need to integrate multiple functions of land administration through land information system is primarily driven by the need for sustainable development.

Land to people relationship and future research focus towards a harmonized understanding of land administration

Proper understanding of land to people relationship particularly in the current era of high dependency of people on land resources is supposed to be crucial to utilize wisely or sustainably. As stated in numerous existing literatures, land is an ultimate resource having special importance to the survival of people. Although great progress is made so far to conceptualize and theorize land administration, there are still limitations in theoretical methodology of linking it with the idea of people to land relationship explicitly. Hence, it is crucial to re-understand the concept of land administration from the perspective of people to land relationship.

The details in section 4 show that people to land relationship are changing overtime and becoming more intense and thicker than ever before. This necessitates careful management of the relationship, which draws the attention towards the issue of land administration. Hence, it is important to consider that the idea of land administration does not exist in abstraction, but it co-evolves with the change in people to land relationship. Empirical evidences show that efforts have been made to understand the trends of people to land relationship and its cadastral responses starting from the era of feudalism to the present time [8,40]. The structure of the connection is continually changing and at the same time governing and administering land issues have evolved over centuries, in line with the cultural and economic development in the history of human civilization [60]. Accordingly, people to land relationships are changing over time as a response to general trends in societal development. Again, as a response to this societal need for better livelihood, the content and emphasis of land administration is also changing over time simultaneously. As a result, currently, attention is given to introduce ‘responsible and fit-for-purpose’ approaches of land administration that fit and responsive to societal needs [30]. The new construct of responsible land administration encourages shared responsibilities between citizens and government and placing community participation at its central place in the process of planning and decision-making process [30,61].

It is also argued in this study that the re-understanding of land administration needs to be tuned to address the current people-to-land relationship, intensified by the competing and conflicting interests overland. It is also important to understand that the current intense people land relationship and accompanied ecological crisis has been threatening the survival of mankind. Thus, the conflicting and competing interest between man and nature urges us to re-examine our activities and conception of land administration in a way that can help to achieve the goal of sustainability. In the effort made to balance the competing interests over land between individuals and the community, the 3RRRs conceptual framework was introduced by scholars in the field [59]. The 3RRRs conceptual framework is primarily intended to clarify the benefits or rights allowed to be exercised by land user or owner and accompanied restrictions and responsibilities as burdens imposed on the land user for the benefit of the larger community [62]. Thus, the volume or thickness of interests overland have been shrinking or decreasing due to the continuous regulations and restrictions enacted by states as controlling mechanisms to ensure fairness in the distribution of resources for the community. On the other hand, the volume of restrictions and responsibilities has been increasing from time to time to ensure environmental requirements and to meet future needs of the society.

It is also important to understand that people to land relationship will continue to change and it will keep on influencing how to handle land issues. For instance, in the early times of human civilization, land resources were abundant and land was freely accessible for everyone who wanted to gather and hunt almost without any restriction. That means during ancient times land was a common property of the community used without fight. But, later on due to population growth, competitions to control land resources has been started and different communities or tribes begun to devise customary rules to govern land resources and so as to avoid conflicts between tribes. After wards, invention of tools leads to agricultural revolution which was followed by evolution of private property system and feudalism as social-economic system. Consequently, during feudalism land was considered just only wealth and the focus of land institutions was tying landowners and their future generations as closely and perpetually as possible to their land which continued until the outbreak of industrial revolution in the beginning of 18th century [7]. After the outbreak of the Industrial Revolution and growth of modern cities, land was considered to be tradable commodity and reduced to be as private property or commodity which can be tradable in the market as any commodity [40]. The role of land institutions was facilitating land markets so as to avoid frauds in the transaction process. Again, after mid of the 20th century and beginning of 21st, the idea of wise use of resources for the benefit of community and future generations has taken wider coverage in the national and international development agendas. This shift has revealed a change in people to land relationship in that land is a scarce community resource which should have to be shared between the present generation and the future generation. The shift has also revealed the need for multi-functionality of land administration's role incorporating the social, economic and environmental aspects of land (see the details in section 4). Moreover, an attempt to separate cadaster as an independent entity as displayed in the existing bulk of literature and legislations is argued as a misleading conception. Rather, it is argued in this paper that cadaster/land information is part or component of land administration which helps to link man to land, as it is just simply refers to record of parcel information in a given area. In its broad sense, cadaster also refers to information about people to land relationship with all details such as where the location of the parcel is, what the size of the parcel is, who the owner of a particular parcel of land is or who is benefiting the rights attached to a particular parcel, what rights are allowed which ones prohibited how the parcel land acquired, and other details. However, the existing understanding and conceptualization of land administration failed to show the link between cadaster and land administration, rather it is largely considered as separate entities. Overall, the reviews above reveal that comprehensiveness in the concept of land administration is lacking and manifested by fragmentation of sub-process. The conceptualization is also loosely linked with people-to-land relationships. Therefore, this gap demands more fitting shift on the concept of land administration.

The above argument therefore demands a new, fitting and harmonized understanding of land administration derived from combination of key co-evolving concepts/aspects which are also extracted from scholarly reviews. The key building blocks of the proposed definition of land administration include: people to land relationship, functions of land administration, land information, and management for sustainable development. The new conceptualization is also expected to be responsive to the current and future demands of the global academic and research community of land sector. Therefore, land administration can be re-conceptualized as an operationalization of information about land tenure, value, use and development for the proper and appropriate management of the changing people to land relationship for public benefit, both to the present and future generation. Thus, it is important to place people-to-land relationships at the center of land administration systems which also need to be enhanced by land information system. People-to-land relationship is reinforced and introduced in the above conceptualization of land administration with the emphasis to prioritize community interests over land and contribute to sustainable development. Finally, people to land relationship as the cognitive aspects land and the co-evolving concept of land administration is one of the most underexplored themes, the researcher encourages for further research on the issue.

Conclusion

The core literature on land administration is reviewed and tested how the existing scholarly concepts of land administration is responsive to the needs of the current academic and research community. The review shows that conceptualizations of land administration lack consistency and harmony in the scholarly definitions of land administration formulated by different scholars since 1980's. The issue of people to land relationship supposed to be at the center of land administration discussions is loosely linked with the concept of land administration. On the other hand, history shows that the nature of people to land relationship has been changing over time and it has been also influencing on the content and description of land administration. For instance, in the earlier times of human civilization, land resources were abundant and land was freely accessible for everyone who wanted to gather and hunt almost without any restriction, the role of customarily established institutions were mainly focused to ensuring fairness in food sharing with in the community. Currently, especially after global population boom 1980's, land is considered as scarce community resource which needs to be used wisely and shared between the present and future generations. This shift in people to land relationship clearly revealed the need for multi-functionality of land administration's role incorporating the social, economic and environmental aspects of land. However, still some of the land administration sub-process and functions such as cadaster or land information in most instances have been considered as separate and independent entities, ignoring the multiple aspects of land.

This paper, finally, attempted to shed light on new and harmonized concept of land administration by linking the idea of people to land relationship as an important element of land administration. The new and reinforced concept focused on cognitive aspects of people to land relationship and the key functions required to be included in a given land administration system designed to enhance sustainability or continuity of community development. Moreover, this paper has highlighted that sustainability of the land administration system can only be realized when all core functions of the system are integrated by cadastral or land information system. As a result, all of the various components of land concerns, including cadaster, are expected to be integrated and viewed as parts of land administration and need to be managed in a cohesive manner so as to address the multiplicity of land aspects and its relation to people depending on the national context.

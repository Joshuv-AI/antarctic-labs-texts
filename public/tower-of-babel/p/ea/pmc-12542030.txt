Drug-coated balloon angioplasty for catastrophic left main STEMI in a 24-year-old with antiphospholipid syndrome: a case report
Authors: Yufeng Jiang, Feifei Cui, Liangping Zhao, Yafeng Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAntiphospholipid syndrome (APS), characterized by antiphospholipid antibody (aPL)-driven thrombosis, is increasingly recognized as a critical etiology of catastrophic coronary events in young adults lacking traditional cardiovascular risk factors. This report delineates a 24-year-old male with left main coronary artery (LMCA) ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), managed through a novel strategy combining drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty and APS-targeted pharmacotherapy (anticoagulation and immunomodulation). The case underscores the necessity of etiological exploration in young STEMI populations and challenges the conventional stent-based revascularization paradigm in hypercoagulable states. Background

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), an autoimmune thrombotic disorder driven by pathogenic antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL), is a rare but critical etiology of acute coronary syndromes (ACS), particularly in young adults without conventional cardiovascular risk factors. APS-associated thrombosis results from aPL-mediated endothelial activation, complement dysregulation, and impaired anticoagulant mechanisms. These disruptions culminate in catastrophic vascular occlusion. While APS is classically linked to venous thromboembolism, emerging evidence highlights its role in arterial thrombosis, with coronary involvement accounting for 1.2–3.8% of APS-related events [1]. Left main coronary artery (LMCA) ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) in this context represents a life-threatening manifestation, carrying mortality rates exceeding 80% without immediate revascularization [2].

Current guidelines for LMCA-STEMI prioritize primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with drug-eluting stent (DES) implantation [3]. However, APS introduces unique challenges: hypercoagulability increases stent thrombosis risk, while chronic endothelial inflammation accelerates neoatherosclerosis. Young patients face additional dilemmas—lifelong stent-related complications, need for repeated interventions, and interactions between anticoagulation and antiplatelet regimens. Recent studies suggest that drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty, which avoids permanent implants, may mitigate these risks by reducing thrombogenic surfaces and preserving vascular anatomy. Yet, data on DCB for APS-related LMCA occlusion remain absent, reflecting a critical knowledge gap in managing this high-risk population.

This case report describes a 24-year-old male with APS-associated LMCA-STEMI successfully treated with DCB angioplasty combined with triple-pathway therapy (anticoagulation, immunomodulation, and metabolic control). By integrating pathophysiology-driven revascularization and targeted secondary prevention, we propose a paradigm shift in managing autoimmune-mediated coronary thrombosis, emphasizing the importance of individualized strategies over conventional stent-based algorithms.

Case presentation

A 24-year-old male with no prior cardiovascular history presented with sudden-onset crushing substernal chest pain radiating to the left arm, accompanied by diaphoresis and dyspnea. Initial electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed ST-segment elevation in leads aVR, I, and aVL (≥ 2 mm), with diffuse ST depression (1–3 mm) across precordial leads V1-V6 (Fig. 1A), accompanied by right bundle branch block (RBBB). This pattern is highly suggestive of left main or proximal left anterior descending artery occlusion, though it may also occur in triple-vessel disease. The presence of RBBB underscores the extensive myocardial injury and is associated with worse prognosis in STEMI, necessitating urgent revascularization. Bedside echocardiography demonstrated severe hypokinesis of the anterior and lateral left ventricular walls (ejection fraction 42%), while laboratory findings included elevated high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (34.2 ng/L; normal ≤ 14 ng/L), hyperglycemia (17.8 mmol/L; normal fasting 3.9 ~ 6.1 mmol/L), and lactic acidosis (4.1 mmol/L; normal 0.5 ~ 1.6 mmol/L). Notably, the patient lacked traditional atherosclerotic risk factors (non-smoker, BMI 23.76 kg/m², LDL-C 2.15 mmol/L[normal ≤ 3.37 mmol/L]) except for newly diagnosed hyperglycemia.

Emergent coronary angiography confirmed thrombotic occlusion of the LMCA (Fig. 1B), with TIMI-0 flow and high thrombus burden. The angiography also showed a lack of collateral circulation from the right coronary artery, supporting the acute presentation of the left main occlusion without evidence of chronic compensatory adaptation (Additional file 1). Despite hemodynamic instability (blood pressure 96/64 mmHg, requiring norepinephrine infusion), conventional stenting was deferred due to concerns about recurrent thrombosis in this young patient with suspected hypercoagulability. Revascularization was achieved through thrombus aspiration followed by intracoronary tirofiban (25 µg/kg bolus + 0.15 µg/kg/min infusion) and DCB angioplasty (3.5 × 20 mm SeQuent Please NEO) at 8 atm for 60 s, restoring TIMI-3 flow with < 30% residual stenosis (Fig. 1C). Post-procedural stabilization necessitated intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) support for 72 h and vasopressor weaning over 48 h.

Serial aPL testing at baseline and 12-week follow-up revealed persistent positivity for lupus anticoagulant and anti-β2-glycoprotein-I IgG (77.58 RU/mL, 81.62 RU/mL; >99th percentile, normal ≤ 20 RU/mL), fulfilling the 2023 ACR/EULAR APS classification criteria. Transient hyperglycemia was attributed to stress-induced insulin resistance, with HbA1c 6.7% (normal 4.0 ~ 6.0%) confirming new-onset type 2 diabetes. ​Warfarin (target INR 2–3) was initiated to counteract APS-related hypercoagulability, supported by evidence that vitamin K antagonists reduce thrombotic recurrence by 80% versus antiplatelet monotherapy in APS. Hydroxychloroquine (200 mg bid) was prescribed to suppress aPL production via Toll-like receptor (TLR) inhibition and NETosis modulation. Dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin 100 mg + clopidogrel 75 mg) plus warfarin was maintained for 1 months, and clopidogrel 75 mg plus warfarin for the next 2 months, followed by warfarin with a targeted INR of 2.0–3.0, with empagliflozin (10 mg/day) added to mitigate hyperglycemia-induced endothelial dysfunction. One year later, a follow-up coronary angiography revealed no significant stenosis (Additional file 2).

Discussion and conclusions

This case highlights several diagnostic and therapeutic dilemmas, particularly in the context of autoimmune-mediated coronary thrombosis. The pathogenic interplay between antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) and acute left main coronary occlusion reflects autoimmune dysregulation intersecting with catastrophic thrombosis. Distinct from atherosclerotic STEMI, APS-mediated thrombosis stems from endothelial activation, complement hyperactivity, and impaired endogenous anticoagulation.

In this context, LMCA involvement reflects the severity of the hypercoagulable state rather than conventional plaque rupture. The absence of coronary calcification or significant stenosis distal to the thrombus in our patient underscores the distinct pathophysiology. Such cases challenge traditional risk stratification models, emphasizing the need for systematic hypercoagulability evaluation in young STEMI patients without atherosclerotic markers. Early aPL testing and multidisciplinary collaboration between cardiologists and rheumatologists are paramount, as delayed APS diagnosis increases the risk of recurrent thrombosis by 35% within the first year.

The management of APS-related LMCA occlusion demands a paradigm shift from conventional stent-based approaches. While drug-eluting stents (DES) remain standard for atherosclerotic lesions, their utility in APS is limited by dual risks of acute stent thrombosis and late neo-atherosclerosis [4, 5]. The proinflammatory endothelial environment in APS accelerates neointimal hyperplasia and reduces stent strut endothelialization. Historical data indicate up to 40% incidence of stent thrombosis in APS patients despite adequate anticoagulation, often culminating in fatal outcomes [6].

Drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty emerges as a physiologically tailored alternative. By delivering antiproliferative paclitaxel without permanent implants, DCB mitigates three critical APS-related risks: (1) eliminating niduses for platelet adhesion from stent struts, (2) preserving native coronary anatomy for future interventions, and (3) reducing long-term endothelial inflammation [4, 7]. The immediate antithrombotic effect of glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors (tirofiban) coupled with DCB’s sustained suppression of smooth muscle cell proliferation addresses both acute and chronic phases of APS thrombosis.

Long-term management of APS-related coronary thrombosis necessitates simultaneous targeting of coagulation cascades and autoimmune drivers. Vitamin K antagonists (VKA) remain the cornerstone, with randomized trials confirming 46% relative risk reduction in thrombotic recurrence compared to direct oral anticoagulants in APS [8]. Maintaining an INR of 2.0–3.0 achieves optimal inhibition of aPL-induced thrombin generation while minimizing bleeding risks.

However, anticoagulation alone fails to address the upstream autoimmune pathology perpetuating endothelial dysfunction. The addition of hydroxychloroquine introduces multidimensional immunomodulation. By blocking Toll-like receptor (TLR) 7/9 signaling, hydroxychloroquine disrupts aPL-induced interferon-α production and NETosis, thereby reducing thrombogenic microparticle release. Additionally, lysosomal alkalinization inhibits antigen presentation and anti-β2GPI antibody synthesis. The integration of empagliflozin, while primarily addressing hyperglycemia, may provide ancillary vascular benefits. SGLT2 inhibitors attenuate endothelial inflammation via NLRP3 inflammasome suppression and mitigate ischemia-reperfusion injury through ketone-driven metabolic reprogramming.

This case redefines APS-related STEMI management by integrating mechanical revascularization with pathogen-targeted pharmacotherapy. DCB angioplasty avoids stent-related risks while providing effective revascularization. The combination of warfarin and hydroxychloroquine addresses both thrombotic and autoimmune components. This paradigm emphasizes etiology-guided therapy, balancing acute intervention with chronic immunothrombosis control, and highlights the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration in managing rare yet catastrophic cardiovascular events. However, in this case, the unavailability of IVUS precluded histopathological characterization of the thrombus and vessel wall. Future cases should incorporate intracoronary imaging when hemodynamically feasible, particularly to distinguish APS-specific endothelial erosion from classic plaque rupture.

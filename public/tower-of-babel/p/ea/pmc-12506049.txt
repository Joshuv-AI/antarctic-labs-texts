Impact of video-assisted preoperative patient information on patient-reported outcomes after pancreatic resection (INFORM trial): a study protocol for a randomised controlled pilot trial

Introduction

Rationale of the trial

Pancreatic resection is the preferred treatment for a range of premalignant and malignant diseases of the pancreas and its neighbouring organs. In patients with pancreatic cancer, it is the only treatment option offering the chance for long-term survival.1 2 Recent advances in surgical techniques and perioperative management have enabled experienced surgeons to perform highly demanding and extended pancreatic resections with low mortality rates in specialised institutions.3 4 However, overall postoperative morbidity remains at approximately 60%, depending on the definitions for complications used, the type of resection performed and the individual patients’ risk profile.5 6 The reduction of surgery-related morbidity and improvement of patients’ postoperative quality of life (QLQ) while prolonging survival outcomes presents one of the foremost goals of pancreatic surgery.2

Undergoing surgery represents an exceptional situation for the individual patient which is frequently associated with stress, anxiety and the fear of loss of self-control.7 8 Especially in major abdominal surgery such as pancreatic operations, patients suffer from increased anxiety levels which are evidently associated with higher morbidity.9 10 Thus, there is an increased need to prepare patients appropriately for the peri- and postoperative processes aiming at the reduction of negative patient-reported outcomes and morbidity.11 12 However, surgeons’ and anaesthesiologists’ lack of time make it difficult to inform patients well about their surgical procedure and hospital stay.13 Additionally, in daily practice, there is little room to answer all questions and eliminate all ambiguities.14 Preoperative patient-focused interventions addressing patient information and education are therefore needed to reduce patients’ QLQ and satisfaction.11 15

Preliminary data

In the area of major abdominal surgery and particularly in pancreatic surgery, only a few studies have investigated the effect of patient-centred interventions on patient-reported and surgical outcomes so far.16 A previous randomised controlled trial on preoperative patient education in patients undergoing major visceral surgery could not show a reduction in postoperative morbidity but increased patient satisfaction with any type of additional preoperative patient information (standardised preoperative patient seminar and/or preoperative information brochure in addition to the standard procedures).12 A significant reduction of patient-reported outcomes such as anxiety and depression was not achieved; however, this might be due to a relevant inhomogeneity of the study cohort. The results from the PEDUCAT trial also indicated that preoperative patient education is a helpful instrument not only for teaching patients but also for training the nursing staff in standardised patient education.12 No other interventional trial so far has focused on preoperative patient education in patients undergoing pancreatic surgery. Due to the demanding surgical procedure, the associated risks and the severity of the underlying disease with potentially severe impact on patients’ prognosis and QLQ, the assessment of patient-centred interventions in pancreatic surgery is of particular importance. The INFORM trial is the first to investigate video-assisted preoperative patient information on patient-reported and postoperative outcomes after pancreatic resection.

Methods and analysis

Study population and eligibility criteria

Patients scheduled for any type of elective pancreatic resection (laparoscopic, robotic and open approach) at the Department of General Surgery, Division of Visceral Surgery, Medical University of Vienna, Austria, will be screened for meeting the eligibility criteria. Inclusion criteria comprise any type of indication requiring elective partial or total pancreatic resection, ≥18 years of age, ability to understand character and individual consequences of the clinical trial, good knowledge of German language and written informed consent. Patients with understanding or language problems, inability to comply with the study and follow-up procedures and patients participating in a concurrent interventional clinical trial with potential interference with the trial outcome will be excluded.

Sample size

In this feasibility trial, the focus is on the feasibility of the procedure and on the generation of first outcome data. As there are no data from previous studies with comparative trial intervention, no formal sample size calculation has been performed. Thus, we have chosen a sample size which is considered valid to obtain first data on feasibility and efficacy of the intervention. 80 patients are planned to be enrolled in this trial (40 patients in each group; see figure 1). Data will serve as a basis for further planning of a confirmatory randomised controlled trial.

Trial flow.

Trial design

This is an investigator-initiated, single-centre, open-label randomised controlled clinical trial with two parallel trial groups. The sponsor of the trial is the Medical University of Vienna, Austria. The sponsor had no role in the design of this study and will not have any role during its execution, analysis of the data, interpretation of the findings or the decision to submit the results for publication. The site of this trial is the Department of General Surgery, Division of Visceral Surgery, Medical University of Vienna, Austria. See online supplemental material for the CONSORT 2025 checklist of this trial.

Recruitment and trial timeline

The estimated time frame for randomisation of 80 eligible patients is 8 months. The total duration of the trial is expected to be 14 months, including analysis. Recruitment of participants started in February 2025. Patients are followed up for 30 days after surgery. The actual overall duration or recruitment time may differ depending on the recruitment progress.

Randomisation

Recruitment and treatment of patients will be performed at one single trial centre. Patients will be screened during consultation hours in the outpatient clinic of the trial centre. Eligible patients will be informed about the clinical trial and will be provided with the patient information and informed consent document (see online supplemental material for the model consent form). After inclusion into the trial, patients will be randomised 1:1 into the intervention and control groups by means of an unstratified block randomisation with fixed block sizes using established randomisation software. The block size is kept confidential until completion of recruitment.

Trial intervention and perioperative management

Intervention group

Patients in the intervention group will receive access to the video sequences after randomisation, which will be within 2 weeks before surgery. They will be instructed how to use the video sequences and will be asked to watch the entire video sequences at least once. Each patient will only receive video sequences that are applicable to his/her planned surgical procedure. Videos encompass general information on the planned surgery and the postoperative recovery phase, as well as more detailed information depending on the planned procedures, the indication for surgery, the planned approach (open, laparoscopic or robotic-assisted surgery) and the type of pancreatic resection (distal pancreatectomy, partial duodenopancreatectomy, total duodenopancreatectomy).

Patients are permitted to watch the videos as often as they wish prior to their admission to hospital. Besides the videos, patients in the intervention group will receive standard preoperative preparation and treatment. Access to the videos will be password protected and with an individually generated QR code. The videos are produced by medudoc AG in close cooperation with the principal investigator of the INFORM trial. On admission, patients are asked whether videos were watched completely, in parts or not seen at all.

As there is now cluster randomisation, there is a potential risk for contamination. However, patients included will not share their room. Potential risk will be monitored and analysed if appropriate (eg, if patients included would share their room).

Control group

Patients in the control group will not receive access to the videos but will undergo the standard procedures. During preoperative consultations with a surgeon and anaesthesiologist, the planned surgical procedure and perioperative management, possibly associated complications and the individual patient’s questions will be discussed.

Surgical procedures

Surgical procedures will be carried out according to the institutional standard procedures as open, laparoscopic or robotic partial or total pancreatectomies. After exclusion of distant tumour spread and after confirming local resectability, pancreatic resection will be performed depending on the localisation of the disease. Extended resections such as vascular resections will be carried out as indicated.

Risks

No additional risks for study patients are anticipated. In accordance with the institution’s ethical committee, a Data Monitoring Committee is not required for this feasibility trial of a low-risk intervention.

Trial objectives and endpoints

Primary objective and primary endpoint

The main purpose of this pilot trial is to evaluate the feasibility of video-assisted preoperative patient information before pancreatic resection. Therefore, the primary feasibility endpoint of the study is the proportion of patients watching the complete video sequences.

Secondary objectives and secondary endpoints

The secondary objectives are to evaluate QLQ in terms of (i) satisfaction with information on disease and treatment as assessed by the validated questionnaire by the European Organisation For Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) QLQ – INFO25 and (ii) disease symptoms as assessed by EORTC QLQ C30 and PAN26.

Furthermore, the extent of usage of the videos in the intervention group, as well as clinically relevant postoperative complications, durations of intensive care unit and total hospital stay, and readmissions to hospital will be assessed. Postoperative complications will be graduated as proposed by the International Study Group of Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) and Clavien–Dindo17 as applicable. The following morbidity endpoints will be assessed until postoperative day 30 after the index operation: Overall rate and severity of postoperative pancreatic fistula according to the updated ISGPS definition,18 overall rate and severity of delayed gastric emptying according to the ISGPS definition,19 overall rate and severity of post-pancreatectomy haemorrhage according to the ISGPS definition,20 overall rate and severity of post-pancreatectomy acute pancreatitis as defined by the ISGPS,21 overall rate and severity of lymphatic fistula as defined by the ISGPS,22 intra-abdominal fluid collection/abscess, burst abdomen, reinterventions/operations due to any cause and mortality (death due to any cause).

Assessment of safety

Considering that the trial intervention is watching videos, trial intervention-related adverse events are not expected. Nevertheless, during the period of observation from visit 1 to the last study visit 30 days after surgery, serious adverse events will be documented. In the case of any occurring serious adverse event, investigators must ensure adequate medical care in close consultation with the patient’s case manager.

Schedule of trial procedures and follow-up visits

During the initial trial visit, patients’ eligibility is checked and confirmed. Afterwards, they are randomly assigned to the trial groups, and their demographics and baseline clinical data are collected. Subsequent visits are scheduled for the day of admission to the hospital, the day of surgery as well as on days 7, 14 and 30 after surgery. Postoperative visits are conducted to assess the primary and secondary endpoints of the study including QLQ, postoperative morbidity and mortality endpoints (see table 1).

Statistical analysis

All analyses will be of descriptive character and will be performed with the software programmes SPSS (IBM, New York, USA) and R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). All endpoints will be analysed descriptively by tabulation of the measures of the empirical distributions. According to the level of the variables, means, SD, medians, first and third quartiles as well as minimum and maximum or absolute and relative frequencies will be reported, respectively. For comparison with the control group, the non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis test will be used to compare continuous parameters. For categorical parameters, absolute and relative frequencies will be calculated and compared using the X2 or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Scoring procedures for the analysis of the QLQ questionnaires will be carried out according to the corresponding manual. Missing data are expected to be rare, so no imputation will be performed. Two-sided P values will be used, and a difference will be considered statistically significant at p<0.05. To account for the potential impact of cancer treatment on the outcome parameters, a subgroup analysis including only patients without malignancy will be performed. In addition, potential influence factors on QLQ scores (ie, postoperative complications) will be investigated by comparing QLQ scores among appropriate subsets of patients. The strength of the relationship between the number of complications per patient and QLQ scores will be determined by Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. For visualisation, box plots and radar charts will be generated. Due to the problem of multiple testing, statistically significant P values must be interpreted with caution.

Ethics and dissemination

This trial was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Medical University of Vienna (reference number 1479/2024) on 10 August 2024. All trial procedures are performed in accordance with the ICH harmonised tripartite guideline on Good Clinical Practice and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Once the study has been completed, the results will be published in due course.

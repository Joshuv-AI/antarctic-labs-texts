Utility of Xpert MTB/RIF using bronchoalveolar lavage samples for the diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis in negative or sputum-scarce adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Valentina L Sabella-Jiménez, Vanessa Sabella-Jiménez, Andrés Felipe Estupinan-Bohorquez, Jorge Acosta-Reyes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTuberculosis is the leading cause of death globally from a single infectious agent. Early diagnosis is critical to reducing morbimortality. In cases of negative smear microscopy or limited sputum production, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) samples offer an alternative for diagnosis. Culture, the gold standard, requires a high bacterial load, extensive infrastructure and is time-consuming. Xpert MTB/RIF provides faster results with a higher cost. Previous systematic reviews present substantial limitations, including significant heterogeneity. Therefore, the diagnostic utility of Xpert MTB/RIF using BAL samples in adults with negative or scant sputum for pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) needs to be reassessed.Methods and analysisA systematic search of MEDLINE, Embase, LILACS and Web of Science will be conducted without language or publication date restriction. Cross-sectional diagnostic studies of negative or sputum-scarce adults with presumptive PTB who underwent bronchoscopy to obtain samples for Xpert MTB/RIF and culture will be included. Screening and data extraction will be performed independently. Methodological quality will be assessed using the QUADAS-2 tool. A bivariate hierarchical random-effects model will synthesise sensitivity and specificity. Meta-analysis will be performed using Meta-DiSc 2.0. Heterogeneity will be assessed using I2 and Cochrane thresholds. Subgroup analyses will be performed based on study design, population differences, country, culture method and risk of bias. Publication bias will be investigated using a funnel plot. The certainty of evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach. Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is a preventable disease caused by the bacillus Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB).1 In 2023, TB became the leading cause of death globally from a single infectious agent, claiming nearly twice as many deaths as HIV / AIDS.1 Over 10 million people develop TB annually, with cases rising since 2021, and an estimated 1.25 million deaths worldwide in 2023.1 To reduce mortality and morbidity, early diagnosis and the prompt initiation of effective treatment are essential, in line with efforts to end the global TB epidemic by 2030.1

The confirmation of TB diagnosis is achieved by detecting the presence of MTB through essential laboratory tests,2 with samples obtained either spontaneously or via sputum induction. Ensuring the availability of diagnostic tools is crucial for timely detection and early treatment. Although sputum smear microscopy remains one of the basic methods for identifying MTB in developing countries,3 sputum analysis can be limited by several factors, including patients’ inability to produce spontaneous sputum,4 difficulty in obtaining a sample of adequate quality and volume,4 the low sensitivity of sputum smear microscopy3 and its inability to differentiate between MTB and non-tuberculous mycobacteria.4 In cases of negative smear microscopy or scant sputum production, bronchoscopy is a valuable alternative for obtaining high-quality biological samples through bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL).2 5

Culture is the WHO-recommended gold standard for the diagnosis of TB, as MTB isolation is crucial not only for disease diagnosis but also for detecting drug resistance.3 However, TB culture methods can be time-consuming,6 as solid media may take 4–8 weeks to yield results,6 while liquid media takes 2–4 weeks.7 Additionally, culture methods require a minimum of 10 bacilli per millilitre to identify bacterial colonies8 and demand extensive infrastructure, which is often lacking in resource-limited settings.9 In contrast, Xpert MTB/RIF (Cepheid, Sunnyvale, California, USA) is a WHO-recommended automated nucleic acid amplification test that detects MTB and rifampicin susceptibility within 2–3 hours, particularly in individuals infected with HIV.6 10 However, Xpert MTB/RIF relies on the availability of complex instruments and has a high cost.6

Although a previous systematic review evaluated Xpert MTB/RIF using BAL for the diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB), the study presented important limitations (included both children and adults, as well as smear-positive sputum patients; lacked a protocol, PROSPERO registration and a list of excluded studies; used a composite reference standard as the gold standard; showed significant heterogeneity in the pooled sensitivity and specificity among the included studies; did not evaluate the certainty of evidence and lacked a summary of findings table).6 Therefore, the present study will systematically review and synthesise the available evidence on the diagnostic accuracy of the Xpert MTB/RIF assay using BAL samples for detecting MTB, compared with solid and/or liquid culture media as the reference standard. This review aims to assess the role of Xpert MTB/RIF as a potential rapid diagnostic tool in clinical practice.

Methods and analysis

The protocol of this systematic review was developed according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines,11 and was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) database (registration number CRD42025639440) on 27 January 2025.

This review will include all prospective and retrospective cross-sectional diagnostic accuracy studies of adults older than 18 years of age, with presumptive PTB, defined by the presence of symptoms or signs suggestive of TB, and who have negative sputum results and/or are unable to produce sputum (sputum-scarce). No restrictions will be imposed regarding the clinical setting in which the tests were performed. Diagnostic accuracy studies, in which participants were subjected to both the index test (Xpert MTB/RIF) and the reference standard (MTB solid and/or liquid culture media), using BAL samples obtained through bronchoscopy procedure, will be included. Studies reporting information on the outcomes, such as sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values as well as likelihood ratios, will be included. We will exclude studies with incomplete data regarding true positives, false positives, true negatives and false negatives or those which used inadequate or less accurate reference tests (smear microscopy or clinical diagnosis) and/or only implemented non-Xpert methods as index tests for the detection of PTB.

We are interested in Löwenstein-Jensen (LJ), Ogawa-Kudoh, Petragnani, Middlebrook 7H10/7H11 agar, Gruft Modification of LJ and Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube (MGIT) solid medium as solid culture media. Likewise, we will include studies with liquid culture media as reference standard, such as BACTEC MGIT 960 System, Middlebrook 7H9 broth, BacT/ALERT 3D and VersaTREK Myco System. We considered a positive culture as the visible growth of MTB colonies on solid media or a positive signal in liquid culture systems. Additionally, Xpert MTB/RIF has a detection threshold of approximately 100–130 colony-forming units per millilitre and was useful in detecting smear-positive and smear-negative PTB.12 13 We considered a positive Xpert MTB/RIF test result if MTB was identified and reported as ‘detected’.

We will search MEDLINE (via Ovid), Embase, Literatura Latinoamericana y del Caribe en Ciencias de la Salud (LILACS) and Web of Science, from inception to search date, without language restriction, and limit to human studies. To build the search strategy, MEDLINE and Embase were used to identify a list of keywords and Medical Subject Headings terms related to ‘tuberculosis’, ‘pulmonary tuberculosis’, ‘bronchoalveolar lavage’, ‘diagnostic tests’ and ‘culture’. The search strategy was revised and approved by all authors. A draft MEDLINE search strategy is available in online supplemental appendix 1. The MEDLINE strategy will be adapted to the syntax and subject subheadings of the other databases. The PROSPERO database was also searched for ongoing or recently completed systematic reviews. Grey literature (conference abstracts, reference citation, reference lists of included studies and previous systematic reviews) will also be searched. The full search strategies and study selection process will be presented in a PRISMA flow diagram and be updated towards the end of the review.

The search results from all databases will be merged and will be exported into EndNote V.21 to remove the duplicates. The remaining records will then be exported to a Microsoft Excel (V.16.30, Redmond, Washington, USA: Microsoft, 2019) spreadsheet. Reviewers (VLS-J, VS-J) will independently screen title and abstract of each study to assess eligibility. When one or more of the reviewers identifies a potentially eligible study, we will retrieve it for full-text stage screening, which will be reviewed independently and in duplicate (VLS-J, VS-J). Disagreement in the full-text stage will be resolved by consensus or by a third reviewer (JA-R) to determine inclusion. We will contact the authors of the studies to obtain missing data, clarify study details or for additional information. In the absence of a response, available data will be used as reported. Studies lacking sufficient information will be excluded from the meta-analysis but retained for qualitative description. Missing data will not be imputed.

Two reviewers (VLS-J, VS-J) will independently perform data extraction with a pre-piloted format on a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. The following data will be extracted from all studies: study characteristics (first author, year of publication, country, study design, type of initial sputum, reference test culture method, population characteristics, bronchoscopy details, sample size), operative test results (true positives, false positives, false negatives and true negatives), reference and index test details, diagnostic accuracy measures (sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative predictive values and positive/negative likelihood ratios with 95% CIs (will be abstracted where available or calculated)) and information for the risk of bias assessment.

The methodological quality of included studies will be assessed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2) tool.14 Each of the four domains (patient selection, index test, reference standard and flow and timing) will be scored as ‘yes’ (low degree of bias or good applicability), ‘no’ (high degree of bias risk or poor applicability) or ‘unclear’ (lack of relevant information or uncertainty of bias risk). The QUADAS-2 tool will be pilot tested on two articles and will be adapted to align with the specific characteristics of the Xpert MTB/RIF and PTB population as well as the context of our review. Two independent reviewers (VLS-J, VS-J) will also perform the risk of bias assessment. Disagreements between the two researchers on the QUADAS-2 tool and risk of bias appraisal will be resolved by consensus or via a third party (JA-R).

The characteristics of all included studies will be summarised in a table, as well the reasons for excluding studies in the full-text stage. Data will be extracted using tables and will be tabulated as true positives, false positives, false negatives and true negatives. A bivariate hierarchical random-effects model1517 will be used to jointly synthesise sensitivity and specificity, accounting for their correlation and between-study heterogeneity since Xpert MTB/RIF applies a fixed and uniform positivity threshold across studies. The bivariate model provides a direct and interpretable summary of diagnostic accuracy, in accordance with current methodological recommendations for diagnostic test accuracy meta-analyses. The hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic (HSROC) framework is mathematically equivalent and may be used for graphical representation of the summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curve; however, the primary analysis will be conducted using the bivariate hierarchical model. We will implement Meta-DiSc 2.0 (Clinical Biostatistics Unit, Hospital Ramón y Cajal, Madrid, Spain) to perform the diagnostic meta-analysis.

Heterogeneity will be evaluated using the I2 statistic and interpreted according to the Cochrane Handbook,18 where 0–40% might not be important, 30–60% may represent moderate heterogeneity, 50–90% may represent substantial heterogeneity and 75–100% is considerable heterogeneity. Where possible, the potential sources of heterogeneity will be explored through sensitivity analysis. Depending on the availability of the data, we will perform the following subgroup analyses: study design characteristics (retrospective or prospective studies); patient population differences (prevalence of HIV-positive individuals; sputum-negative and sputum-scarce individuals); country (developed or developing country); variations in the reference standard method used (Mycobacterium liquid or solid culture method); risk of bias.

If the number of studies included in the meta-analysis is 10 or more, we will assess publication bias and generate the Deeks’ funnel plot by using the diagnostic OR as the dependent variable and the reciprocal of the square root of the effective sample size as the independent variable. We will use the funnel plot to assess the publication bias based on the plot’s symmetry. The analysis will be performed using RStudio on Posit Cloud.

The confidence in the estimates for each outcome and certainty of the evidence will be independently assessed by two reviewers (VLS-J, VS-J) using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) Working Group approach for diagnostic tests.19 20 Discrepancies will be resolved through consensus. We will use GRADEpro Guideline Development Tool (McMaster 148 University 2015, developed by Evidence Prime) to create the evidence tables for the diagnostic question, which includes risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision and publication bias as domains, and to generate the summary of findings. Overall certainty of evidence will be classified as high, moderate, low or very low depending on the assessment of the domains.

Patient and public involvement

There was no patient or public involvement in the development of the systematic review protocol.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required as this study will use publicly available data. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication.

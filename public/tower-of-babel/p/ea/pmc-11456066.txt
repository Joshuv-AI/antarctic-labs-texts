Changes in Early-Phase Clinical Trials in China During 2013–2022: A Review
Authors: Jianxiong Zhang, Peng Zhang, Haixue Wang, Ruihua Dong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractClinical trials on innovative drugs in China have witnessed a stage of rapid development in recent years. The introduction of a number of government policies has stimulated enthusiasm for research and development in the pharmaceutical industry. We analyzed the data of the early-phase clinical trials registered in the Chinese Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) from September 6, 2013, to December 31, 2022. All related registration information disclosed on the CDE website, including posted time, drug classification, dosing formula, indications, trial design, and trial status, were summarized and analyzed. A total of 5336 early-phase clinical trials were identified. The quantity and growth rate of early-phase clinical trials have increased substantially each year. Chemical drugs accounted for the largest proportion of early-phase clinical trials, although it dropped from 85.7% in 2013 to 59.5% in 2022. Moreover, the number of oncology drugs has increased yearly, accounting for 49.2% of all early-phase clinical trials in 2022. We can conclude that during 2013–2022, the development of early-phase clinical trials has progressed due to government support and advanced development techniques. To enhance the efficiency and success rate of early-phase clinical trials in the future, it is necessary to acquire advanced technical support and improve standardized supervision systems.

Key Points

Analysis of early-phase clinical trial information in CDE of China.Early-phase trials progress by the government support and techniques development.Necessity of advanced support and intensified supervision for future advancement.

Introduction

During the period between 2010 and 2015, the number of applications for clinical trials of innovative drugs in China started to increase [1]. In July 2015, the introduction of China's regulatory reform measures eased the backlog of applications, while China's new drug development system and clinical trial system have increasingly improved and developed, and the number of first investigational new drugs (INDs) for innovative drugs that were applied for and approved increased significantly [1, 2]. During that period, the State Council has successively issued policies, such as the Opinions on Deepening the Reform of the Examination and Approval System to Encourage the Innovation of Drugs and Medical Devices [3] and Regulations on the Administration of Drug Clinical Trial Institutions [4], aiming to encourage pharmaceutical innovation and reform. At the same time, China joined the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH), which led to an increase in cooperation with leading international institutions. Therefore, the demand for clinical trials is expanding rapidly.

On September 6, 2013, the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) issued Notice No. 28, 2013 on its official website, stating that it is mandatory that all clinical trials (including bioequivalence tests, PK tests, and phase I, II, III, and IV trials, etc.) approved by the State Food and Drug Administration and conducted in China must registered and information must be disclosed on the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) clinical trial registration platform. In this study, we aimed to summarize and analyze early-phase clinical trial information registered in CDE in China from 2013 to 2022, and to further elucidate the trend of innovative drug research and development in clinical trials in China.

Search Methodology

We collected the data of early-phase clinical trials registered with the CDE from September 6th, 2013, to December 31st, 2022. The data was collected on March 22, 2023. The phase I clinical trials were included, and the bioequivalence trials were excluded. The clinical trial registration information published on the CDE website, including posted time, drug classification, dosing formula, indications, trial design, and trial status, were recorded.

The classification of drugs was categorized into three types, "chemical drugs," "biological products," and "traditional Chinese medicine/natural medicine." According to different indications, we classified these trials according to different specialties of treatment, including "oncology,” "cardiovascular,” "respiratory,” "orthopedics,” "general surgery/transplantation,” "hematology,” "urinary,” "neurology,” "psychiatry,” "pediatrics,” "endocrine,” "otolaryngology,” "traditional Chinese medicine,” "dermatology,” "anesthesia/pain,” "vaccine,” "gastroenterology,” "autoimmune,” "infectious diseases,” "vascular,” "ophthalmology,” and "oral cavity.” For example, a new drug on the CDE website is registered as a third-generation cephalosporin. Its indications are lung infection and urinary infection. And it is classified as “infectious diseases”, instead of “respiratory” or “urinary”. A trial can only be classified into one category and will not be classified repeatedly. If a trial had multiple indications, we recorded the first indication.

The clinical trial status included "ongoing," "completed," "paused," and "stopped." According to the protocol design, the early-phase clinical trials included "single-ascending dose (SAD),” "multiple ascending doses (MAD),” "food effect (FE),” "drug-drug interaction (DDI),” "mass balance study,” "hepatic impairment," and "renal impairment." Information not published was not included in this study.

In this study, the data published in clinical trials from 2013 to 2022 were collected, analyzed, classified and recorded by two researchers. When a consensus on certain information could not be reached by two researchers, a third researcher was invited for discussion and voting. Statistical software R (version 3.6.2) was used for data processing and analysis. Descriptive analysis used numbers (%) to represent qualitative variables. To ensure that relevant trials were not overlooked, a simple random sampling method was adopted, and 20% of all clinical trials were selected for analysis to ensure the inclusion of all relevant trials.

Results

A total of 19,685 drug clinical trials were identified between September 6, 2013, and December 31, 2022, including 5,336 early-phase clinical trials.

Posted Time

Among all the early-phase clinical trials registered on the CDE registration platform, 2013 year had the least number of registered early-phase clinical trials, followed by a substantial increase during the succeeding years (Fig. 1). Early-phase clinical trials in 2018 increased dramatically, with 49.4% higher than that in 2017. In 2021, early-phase clinical trials increased by 41.2% at 974 when compared with those in 2020.

Drug Classification

Chemical drugs accounted for the largest proportion of early-phase clinical trials, although it dropped from 85.7% in 2013 to 59.5% in 2022. Although the number substantially increased each year, the proportion has been declining gradually (Fig. 2). However, the number of early phase trials on biological products has been increasing annually, and the proportion has also gradually risen, from 9.5% in 2013 to 39.9% in 2022. Simultaneously, early-phase clinical trials on traditional Chinese medicine and natural medicine decreased steadily. In 2014, the proportion of early-phase clinical trials on these products occupied 11.5% of all trials but then declined consistently. The proportion fell to 3.6% in 2017 and was as low as 0.6% in 2022.

Annual Classified Statistics of Drugs in Early-Phase Clinical Trials

Dosing Formula

Trials on tablets, injections, capsules, vaccines, granules, suspensions, inhalants, sprays, oral liquids, eye drops, creams, patches, suppositories, and pills were analyzed, and the top five formulas per year are shown in Fig. 3. In the early-phase clinical trials from 2013 to 2022, the number of trials on tablets trials increased sharply in 2017 and 2018 and stabilized in 2019. Injection trials increased rapidly since 2018. In 2022, the proportion of injections accounted for 50.2% of all trials. In terms of capsule trials, the number of early-phase clinical trials reached 134 in 2021, which was 48.9% more than that in 2020.

Annual Statistics of Drug Dosage Forms in Early-Phase Clinical Trials

Indications

Innovative oncology drugs accounted for the largest number of early- phase clinical trials, followed by endocrinology, hematology, infectious diseases, and gastroenterology, respectively (Fig. 4). The number of oncology drugs increased each year, accounting for 49.2% of all early- phase clinical trials in 2022. Endocrine and hematologic trials also increased steadily, accounting for about 8.5% and 5.3% of all early-phase clinical trials in 2022. Simultaneously, infectious diseases, communicable diseases, gastroenterology, and neurological trials each accounted for about 3%–5% of all trials annually.

Annual Statistics of Indications for Early-Phase Clinical Trials

Trial Status

The number and proportion of ongoing early-phase clinical trials rose from 2013 to 2022 (Fig. 5). Among the completed clinical trials, the percentage of completion was from 49.2% in 2013 to 59.4% in 2018, and the number of stopped clinical trials was higher in 2019 (2.9%) and 2020 (3.2%). The number of paused clinical trials was higher in 2017–2018 and decreased gradually after 2019.

Annual Statistics of Status of Early-Phase Clinical Trials

Trial Design

The design of early clinical trials mainly included SAD, MAD and FE, which accounted for the largest proportion. The number of DDI trials increased each year from 2013 to 2022. In addition, the number of mass balance studies also increased yearly and reached 12 in 2019, which was 300% higher than in 2018. Moreover, the number of clinical trials in special populations with hepatic dysfunction also increased each year (Fig. 6). Clinical trials of hepatic dysfunction and renal dysfunction became gradually fewer each year from 2014 to 2018. The number of early-phase clinical trials on hepatic impairment trials increased from four in 2019 to eight in 2021, and there were 13 renal impairment trials in 2022.

Discussion

Since 2016, the number, as well as the growth rate, of new clinical trials launched in China has been increasing every year [5]. This coincided with the introduction of China’s regulatory reform measures in July 2015, which alleviated the backlog of applications and encouraged pharmaceutical innovation [1]. The government has adopted a series of measures, including simplifying the approval process and giving more responsibility to enterprises, institutions, and markets while ensuring safety. In July 2018, the NMPA adjusted the approval process for commencing drug clinical trials to an implied license system [1]. This system implies that within a certain period after accepting the application for clinical trials, if the drug regulatory department does not give negative feedback or does not have questions, then the applicant can carry out clinical trials in accordance with the submitted protocol [3]. Concurrently, the government has enhanced the approval system and therefore clinically urgent drugs can be approved under specific conditions. By encouraging investment, the government built more clinical trial institutions to meet the rapidly growing demand for clinical trials.

Chemical drugs account for the largest proportion in all early-phase clinical trials, which likely stems from the advantages of traditional research and development models for the preceding processes, such as pre-clinical and early phases [6, 7]. In recent years, the proportion of drug clinical trials have gradually increased. From 2019, clinical trial institutions have shifted from a qualification system to a record system. As a consequence, qualified medical institutions could carry out the clinical trials after registration and filing on the Food and Drug Regulatory Department website, and through the relevant departments on-site verification. This leads to a greatly enhanced capacity and enthusiasm for medical institutions to participate in clinical trials. [4, 8, 9]. The extraction, research, and development of traditional Chinese medicine (which refers to the collection, processing, preparation, and clinical application of drugs guided by the theory of traditional Chinese medicine) and natural medicine (which refers to animal drugs, plant drugs, and mineral drugs that have been proved to have certain pharmacological activity by modern pharmaceutical system) have been challenging, as there is no universal consensus on what constitutes evidence [10, 11], resulting in a decreasing number of clinical trials every year. The composition of natural medicines is complex and may encompass various active ingredients, while the small molecules are the sole ingredient of chemical medicines that are synthesized through artificial synthesis or chemical approaches. Research and development on traditional Chinese medicine and natural medicine deserve more attention and further support from the government.

The number of early-phase clinical trials has increased steadily since 2018. Because of the COVID-19 pandemic outbreak in 2020, the growth rate of early-phase clinical trials slowed down. According to the 2021 Annual Report on the Progress of Clinical Trials of New Drug Registration in China, the total number of clinical trials registered as drug clinical trials surpassed 3000 for the first time, increasing by 29.1% compared with the number registered in 2020 [12]. Among them, the number of new drug clinical trials was 2033, an increase of 38.0% compared with that in 2020 [12]. The number of early-phase clinical trials soared to more than 1000, which required more qualified clinical trial centers [8]. The number of clinical trial centers has increased from 375 in 2015 to 1072 in 2019 [8]. The participation of more qualified clinical trial centers will facilitate the completion of more clinical trials.

In recent years, the guidelines of innovative drug development have highlighted the requirements to improve clinical studies, such as DDI and hepatic/renal impairment trials. Correspondingly, the number of early-phase clinical trials in these areas has increased greatly. Since more evidence is required for the application of specific prescriptions in post-market clinical trials, clinical trials such as DDI, mass balance study, and hepatic/renal impairment deserve more attention for higher efficiency of drug clinical trial registration. In early exploratory clinical research, early-phase clinical trials need to be furnished with supporting facilities, equipment, and corresponding technical systems, including negative pressure wards, isotope wards, and DDI platforms. Therefore, clinical researchers are required to undergo more professional training for better evaluation of innovative drugs and the accumulation of relevant experiences.

According to the 2021 Annual Report on the Progress of Clinical Trials of New Drug Registration in China [12], an analysis of the clinical trials of new drugs registered for the first time indicated that over half of the trials (51.4%) in 2021 could commence subject recruitment within 6 months [12]. The average initiation time decreased from 6.4 to 3.8 months from 2019 to 2021, and the proportion of clinical trials initiated within 6 months rose each year, reaching 85.7% in 2021 [12]. This was attributed to the solicitation of comments on September 30, 2018, on the Administrative Measures for Communication and Exchange of Drug R&D and Technical Review [13]. Later, it was revised on December 10, 2020 [14], which greatly expedited clinical trial initiation, enhanced the efficiency of clinical trials, and saved time in pre-marketing applications.

With the increase in the number of clinical trials, the demand for early-phase clinical trial facilities increased, and therefore, brought more pressure on the recruitment of healthy volunteers. Early-phase clinical trials and bioequivalence studies are usually carried out in phase I clinical research centers, need a large number of inpatient wards, and require full-time researchers. To ensure that more drugs enter phase II and III studies, higher investment in preclinical research and human resources is essential to facilitate the entry of more candidates into phase I, thereby enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of the drug discovery and development process [15].

The number and proportion of oncology drugs in trials have increased steadily every year during 2013–2022, which might be due to the advancement of basic sciences such as understanding of disease pathogenesis and the discovery of related targets, as well as clinical needs. Since the high incidence of cardiovascular and endocrine diseases attracted an increasing investment, a rising number of endocrine and cardiovascular trials took place each year. Currently, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, coronary disease, and obesity are the main focus of innovative drug research and development. Furthermore, we may witness an upward trend in the coming years due to the increase in metabolic diseases, especially in developing countries.

The data accuracy of this report may be affected by a small number of omissions, delays in the trial registration by sponsors or contract research organization companies, or the failure to update the trial information on the website in a timely manner after the trial information is changed. When the number of trials differs from that published by the drug clinical trial institution, the institution's announcement shall prevail.

The study shows that government support and advanced technology have led to drug development, facilitating increasingly innovative drugs to enter early-phase clinical trials. Accordingly, clinical trials need long-term and constant advanced technical support and a standardized supervision system to improve the efficiency and quality for entering phase II and further.

Conclusion

During the period of 2013–2022, the development of early-phase clinical trials has progressed due to government support and advanced development techniques. In order to improve the efficiency and success rate of early-phase clinical trials in the future, it is necessary to acquire advanced technical support and improve standardized supervision systems.

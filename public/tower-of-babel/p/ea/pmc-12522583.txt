Effect of esketamine vs sufentanil adjunct to propofol deep sedation on the incidence of oxygen-desaturation in elderly patients undergoing urologic surgeries: a randomized, double-blind, controlled trial
Authors: Si-Qi Hao, Xue Zhang, Fang-Yu Zhang, Li-Xin An, Xin Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPropofol deep sedation/anesthesia combined with analgesics is always used in minimally invasive urologic surgeries in elderly patients.Oxygen-desaturation is one of the most frequent respiratory complications susceptible to other life-threatening accidents for elderly patients during propofol sedation. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of low-dose esketamine adjunct to propofol sedation on the occurrence of oxygen-desaturation, hypotension, propofol requirements and other related adverse events compared to sufentanil for elderly patients undergoing urologic surgeries.MethodsNinety four elderly male patients undergoing transurethral bladder lesion resection or ureteroscopic ureteral laser lithotripsy were randomly enrolled into esketamine/propofol group (SK group, n = 47) or sufentanil/propofol group (SF group, n = 47). The patients in SK group received 0.25 mg/kg esketamine and 1.5 mg/kg propofol for anesthetic induction and SF group received 0.1 μg/kg sufentanil and 1.5 mg/kg propofol. Sedation was Maintained with 4-6 mg/kg/h propofol infusion. The primary outcome was the incidence of oxygen-desaturation during the surgeries. Second outcomes included induction time, hemodynamic changes, intraoperative propofol dosage, operation duration and anesthesia duration, recovery time, other related adverse events and rescue medication.ResultsThe incidence of oxygen-desaturation was 34.0% (16 of 47) in SK group compared to 68.1% (32 of 47) in SF group with a significant difference (risk ratio, 0.50; 95% confidence interval, 0.32–0.78; P < 0.001). The overall severity of hypoxiemia was mild in SK group (22/9/16 vs 6/9/32, P = 0.00072). Propofol requirements, induction time were all much lower in SK group than those in SF group (P < 0.001). More hemodynamic stability was observed in SK group compared to SF group. The protective factors of oxygen-desaturation were esketamine (OR:0.26, CI: 0.08 to 0.86, P = 0.027), transurethral bladder lesion resection (OR:0.14, CI: 0.03 to 0.64, P = 0.011). Weight was a hazard factor of OD (OR:1.07, CI: 1.00 to 1.15, P = 0.048). Postoperative sedation score, MMSE, VAS score, incidence of nausea and vomiting were similar in both groups (P > 0.05).ConclusionsEsketamine combined with propofol sedation used in minimal invasive urologic surgeries, could reduce the incidence of oxygen-desaturation, decrease propofol requirements, stabilize hemodynamics and didn’t affect the postoperative recovery and neurological function of elderly patients.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry, No: ChiCTR2300077170. Background

As the proportion of elderly population in China has a growing trend, the number of elderly patients requiring urological surgery has also increased [1, 2]. Most urological surgeries are performed using minimally invasive techniques or cystoscopy, and most patients undergoing urological surgery are elderly individuals with co-morbidities. Therefore, while providing adequate anesthesia, anesthesiologists should also consider various factors such as age, complications, functional status, and surgical duration to optimize surgical outcomes [2–5].

When performing some common minor urological surgeries, such as transurethral bladder resection or ureteroscopic lithotripsy, propofol based intubation free general anesthesia is often the first choice for anesthesiologists due to its mild surgical stimulation and short operation time [6]. However, side effects associated with propofol based sedation, such as hypotension or desaturation frequently observed in elderly patients, still gained priority attention of anesthesiologists [7–10]. Opioids, as a common adjuvant to propofol sedation, not only worsen the inhibition of circulatory respiration, but also lead to adverse reactions such as shivering, postoperative nausea and vomiting [11]. Thus, an optimal weakly opioided anesthesia scheme, based on routine propofol administration with less side effects, requires to be designed and detected with caution for elderly patients undergoing endoscopic urological interventions.

Esketamine, a promising anesthetic acting as sedation and analgesia, has anti-inflammatory and mood-improving effects with less respiratory depression and hypotension than other anesthetic agents [12, 13]. Some previous available studies indicate that the addition of esketamine to anesthesia or sedation regimen in patients undergoing gastrointestinal endoscopic procedures and hysteroscopic procedures can provide satisfactory anesthetic effect, produce stable hemodynamics and maintain constant breathing without desaturation or hypoventilation under intravenous general anesthesia [14, 15]. Additionally, a recent study summarizes that esketamine can effectively enhance the potency of sedation and analgesia and improve the safety for adults and pediatric patients undergoing endoscopic procedures, especially with propofol combined anesthesia or sedation [16]. However, there have been limited clinical studies detecting effects of esketamine adjunct to propofol-based sedation on the incidence of oxygen-desaturation and the postoperative neurological function on elderly patients. undergoing urological surgeries. Therefore, this double-blinded, randomized controlled trial was designed and investigate the hypothesis that low-dose esketamine adjunct to propofol sedation could achieve a desirable anesthetic effect, decrease the incidence of oxygen-desaturation, hypotension and not influence neurological function in elderly patients after urologic surgeries.

Materials and methods

Our single-center, randomized, double-blind trial was conducted in Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University, and approved by the Ethics Committee of the hospital (Approval No: 2023-P2-246–02). This trial was registered at Chictr.org.cn on November 1, 2023, by the principal investigator, Lixin An (ChiCTR2300077170), prior to the first participant enrolled. All participants provided written informed consent prior to participating in the trial.

Study population

From November 2023 to December 2024, we screened hospitalized male patients, aged ≥ 60 years, scheduled to undergo transurethral bladder lesion resection or transurethral ureterourethral laser lithotripsy who were designed American Standards Association (ASA) classification I-III and body Mass index between 18 and 28 kg/m2. Patients were excluded if they had severe liver, renal dysfunction or coagulation abnormalities, uncontrolled hypertension, severe cardiovascular diseases, acute respiratory infections or other serious respiratory diseases, neurological disorders, psychiatric conditions or a history of cognitive impairment, allergy to experimental medications, elevated intracranial or intraocular pressure. If the operation time was expected > 1 h, and the laryngeal mask would be placed to establish an artificial airway, which would also be excluded from this study.

Randomization and blinding

A total of 94 participants were randomly assigned 1:1 by a computer-generated codes to SK group (esketamine group, n = 47) or SF group (sufentanil group, n = 47). After the patients were enrolled, the envelope containing group information was opened. The specialized nurse responsible for configuring the experimental drug would prepare 10 ml of the experimental drug in a colorless syringe (2.5 mg/ml of esketamine or 1 μg/ml of sufentanil), and gave the syringe to the anesthesiologist. And then anesthesiologist would gave the medicine in 0.1 ml/kg to all patients according to our anesthesia regimen. Therefore, the anesthesiologists, participants, detectors and urological surgeons were all blinded to grouping.

Standard anesthetic procedure

Patients were required to fast and abstain from water for at least 8 h before surgery. After being admitted to the operating room, patients received continuous monitoring including non-invasive blood pressure (BP), pulse oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate (HR), and electrocardiogram (ECG). Patients were laid in the lithotomy position and upper limb venous access was opened. Before initiation of anesthesia induction, patients inhaled oxygen continuously via a Mask at 6 L/min for at least 5 min, followed by the intravenous injection of 0.3 mg atropine.

Esketamine and sufentanil were both diluted into a volume of 10 ml and prepared by a trained nurse, who then handed them to the anesthesiologists for drug administration. Patients were intravenously administered a single dose of 0.25 mg/kg esketamine (registration number H20193336, Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Jiangsu, China) in SK group or 0.1 μg/kg sufentanil (registration number H20054171, Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co., Hubei, China) in SF group. The dose of esketamine, set at 0.25 mg/kg, was based on our previous study which demonstrated adequate sedation and analgesia for urologic surgeries, without inducing involuntary body movement and cardiorespiratory adverse events especially in elderly male patients [17]. After three minutes following experimental drugs administration, anesthesia was induced with 1.5 mg/kg propofol for both two groups, which was injected over 30–60 s. After patients lost consciousness, anesthesia is maintained with a continuous infusion of propofol (registration number HJ20150655, Beijing Fresenius Kabi Pharmaceutical Co., Beijing, China) at a rate of 4–6 mg/kg/h. If intraoperative movement occurs, a single dose of 30 mg will be administered intravenously and the infusion speed will be adjusted based on the BIS index.

Sedation levels in patients were evaluated using the Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness and Sedation (MOAA/S scale) [18] (0: No response after painful trapezius stimulation; 1: Reacts only after painful trapezius squeeze; 2: Reacts only after mild prodding or shaking; 3: Reacts only after name is called loudly and/or repeatedly; 4: delayed response to normal verbal prompting; 5: Responds promptly to name spoken in a normal tone). Recovery of consciousness was generally defined as a MOAA/S score of 5; moderate sedation was usually regarded as a MOAA/S score range of 3–4; deep sedation was typically considered as MOAA/S ≤ 2. Ureteroscopy was performed by 2 fixed urological surgeons after the MOAA/S score reached ≤ 2.

Intraoperative hypoxia events and management:

If SpO2 drops below 95%, a head strap is utilized and oxygen was administered via a closed face mask;

If SpO2 drops below 95%, a head strap is utilized and oxygen was administered via a closed face mask;

If SpO2 falls below 90%, chin is supported or an oral-pharyngeal airway was inserted to perform ventilation;

If SpO2 falls below 90%, chin is supported or an oral-pharyngeal airway was inserted to perform ventilation;

If SpO2 falls below 85%, the airway is manually opened and assisted ventilation was provided;

If SpO2 falls below 85%, the airway is manually opened and assisted ventilation was provided;

If SpO2 drops below 70%, a laryngeal mask airway (LMA) was inserted to establish the airway.

If SpO2 drops below 70%, a laryngeal mask airway (LMA) was inserted to establish the airway.

Hypotension was defined as a mean arterial pressure (MAP) drop lower than 65 mmHg or a decrease by more than 30% of basal value. Once MaP starts to drop, 100 ml of crystalloid fluid should be administered rapidly. If the hypotension occurs, 6 mg ephedrine or 1 mg methoxamine will be given. It was considered as bradycardia if HR dropped below 45 beats/min, and 0.25–0.5 mg atropine was intravenously injected if needed. All relevant therapy interventions were documented. Other maintenance-phase management decisions were made by the anesthesiologists and all kinds of adverse events occured during surgery were treated according to relevant clinical guidelines.

Data collection

Patients baseline characteristics including age, BMI, vital signs, medical history, ASA status, and clinical symptoms were recorded. BP, HR and SpO2 were measured and recorded at before induction (T0), anesthesia induction (T1), ureteroscope insertion (T2), ureteroscopy withdrawal (T3) and wake up time (T4). We also recorded anesthesia details, fluid management, postoperative pain treatment and antiemetics.

Primary and secondary outcomes

The primary outcome of this trial was the incidence of oxygen-desaturation. The SpO2 was continuously monitored and recorded in all enrolled patients. Oxygen-desaturation is defined as SpO2 falling below 90% for a duration of 10 s or longer [19].

Secondary outcomes included the degree of hypoxemia and different management of hypoxemia. The hemodynamic parameters (MBP, HR) and SpO2 at different time points were measured. Usage of vasoactive drugs during procedure, duration of procedure, intraoperative propofol dosage, the recovery time (from the end of surgery to Steward score > 9), Visual analog scale (VAS) scores at 4 h after surgery, postoperative cognitive status (3 min Confusion Assessment Method, 3D-CAM scale), postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) scores were also documented.

Sample size estimation and statistical methods

We used PASS 15.0 software to estimate the sample size. The sample size was calculated based on Our pre-trial results. Before the study, we found that the oxygen-desaturation incidence in the sufentanil group was 68%, while in the esketamine group, it was 35%. With α set to 0.05, a β set to 0.9, and a drop rate of 10%, the final sample size was adjusted to 47 participants per group, a total of 94 participants.

The absolute standard mean difference (ASD) is calculated between the groups with the mean difference of the two group estimates divided by the mean standard deviation (MD/SD). Any variables with ASD > 0.124 were defined as imbalanced and were adjusted based on primary outcome.

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD), with intergroup comparisons performed using the independent t-test. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), with intergroup comparisons conducted using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Categorical variables were presented as counts or percentages, and group comparisons were determined using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis models were used to study the relationship between reduced oxygen saturation and its contributing factors, calculating risk ratio and 95% confidence interval (CI). In the multivariate logistic regression analysis, we followed the results of the univariate regression analysis, including potential covariate group (SK group), weight, transurethral electrical resection of the prostate, propofol unit time dose and number of ephedrine usage. For patients with preoperative concomitant asthma or COPD, record their grading and perform subgroup analysis. Lastly, all data was analyzed using SPSS software (version 26.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and a p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant except for the baseline characteristics of the patients.

A CONSORT flowchart of patient randomization and exclusion is presented in Fig. 1. From March 2024 to September 2024, 105 elderly patients scheduled for transurethral bladder lesion resection or ureteroscopic ureteral laser lithotripsy were screened for eligibility. Seven patients were excluded because of the following reasons: three for failing to participate in the VAS score, three for declining to participate, and one for other studies. Then 98 patients were randomly enrolled in esketamine/propofol group (SK group, n = 49) or sufentanil/propofol group (SF group, n = 49), forming the intention-to-treat population. In SK group, one changed the surgical procedure, and one was lost to follow-up. In SF group, one developed frequent premature ventricular beats before anesthesia induction and one requested a withdrawal from the study. Finally, 94 patients were included and 47 patients in each group completed the study.

Discussion

This study was designed and aimed to validate the hypothesis that the adjuvant of esketamine (0.25 mg/kg) to propofol anesthesia regimen could certainly reduce the incidence of oxygen-desaturation, provide an admirable analgesic effect and sustain hemodynamic stability in elderly patients after urological endoscopy. In the result of this study, the addition of esketamine halved the incidence of intraoperative oxygen-desaturation (34.0% vs.68.1%) with a significantly lower dose of propofol consumption in comparison to propofol-sufentanil combination. Moreover, shorter induction time and more stable hemodynamics were observed when propofol infusion was administered with a single dose of esketamine applied intravenously, which was confirmed to our original hypothesis.

Elderly patients gradually experience a reduction of metabolic capacity and organ degeneration over time. These physiological characteristics not only have a negative influence on success and safety of surgical interventions, but also contribute to a higher occurrence of morbidity or mortality during the perioperative period [20]. As minimally invasive endourological surgeries gain prevalence, more and more elderly patients should receive effective surgical treatment, shorter hospitalization and fewer associated complications such as post-operative pain, urinary tract infection and even sepsis [5, 6]. Due to bladder tumors and urinary system stones are easy to recur, repeated endourological treatments are always performed combined with regular follow-up [2–4]. Given the shorter duration of urologic surgeries and demand of rapid postoperative recovery, similar to other kinds of endoscopic treatments, procedural sedative and anesthesia (PSA) with short-acting and highly safe anesthetic agents are recommended to utilize for clinical promotion. However, deeper sedation and significant cardiopulmonary depression usually occur in elderly patients. Intravenous propofol and opioids, used together or alone, can certainly inhibit the respiratory system function which may result in respiratory depression or even different degrees of oxygen-desaturation [8, 21]. Numerous studies have confirmed that IV esketamine can provide sufficient analgesic and favorable anesthetic effects, reduce propofol and opioids dosage and accelerate postoperative recovery for elderly patients during endoscopic procedure sedation [22–24]. In our study, the total propofol dosage and propofol unit time dosage were still significantly decreased (about 18.3% and 14.3%). The lower occurrence of oxygen-desaturation by esketamine results from acting as an alternative of opioids and reducing propofol requirement, which is consistent with previous studies.

The incidence of oxygen-desaturation in Our study was 34.0% in SK group vs. 68.1% in SF group with a higher occurrence compared to other studies [14, 25]. However, previous similar studies have focused on painless gastroenteroscopy or bronchoscopy. For example, Song et al.’s [14] found that 0.15 mg/kg esketamine could significantly decrease the incidence of deoxygenation events and propofol consumption during endoscopic procedures. Nie et al.’s [25] confirmed that 0.2 mg/kg esketamine added to propofol sedation in fiberoptic bronchoscopy could decrease the incidence of transient oxygen-desaturation events, reduce propofol dosage and elevate bronchoscopists’ satisfaction compared to co-administration with remifentanil. The differences between our results and other studies may be related to the higher dose of esketamine administered without sufentanil or remifentanil, aging population and more stimulation of the urological surgeries in our study. Similar to the promising marketed S-enantiomer of ketamine, esketamine can stimulate breathing effectively attributed to activation of the sympathetic nerve on bronchial smooth muscle, which can encounter respiratory inhibition resulting from anesthetic medication [26–28]. The effective dose of esketamine added to PSA is ranging from 0.1 to 0.5 mg/kg [23, 29]. In Our previous study, intravenous 0.25 mg/kg esketamine can prevent unpredictable movement, facilitate urological surgeries and promote more safety for elderly patients’ propofol sedation [17]. Thus, the same dose of esketamine was selected and injected according to our experience. In addition, multivariate logistic regression was performed for analysis. It indicated that IV esketamine and operative style (transurethral bladder lesion resection) were the protective risk factors whereas weight was the non-protective risk factor for oxygen-desaturation. IV esketamine has been proven to be an incontestable protective risk factor in a previous article [14]. Age over 65 years and higher BMI are certainly linked to increasing the incidence of oxygen-desaturation. New discovery is that the operative style, transurethral bladder lesion resection, might play a positive role on reducing the occurrence of oxygen-desaturation. This type of operation is prone to producing more intense stimulation and patients may suffer stronger intraoperative and postoperative pain, which can prevent deep anesthesia-related depression to some extent.

The relatively high incidence of oxygen-desaturation observed in both groups should be interpreted in the context of several clinical and procedural factors. First, the study population consisted predominantly of elderly patients, whose reduced pulmonary compliance, diminished ventilatory drive, and increased prevalence of subclinical obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) increase their vulnerability to hypoxemia during sedation. Secondly, both groups received deep sedation with propofol at 4–6 mg/kg/h, a regimen known to depress respiratory drive and reduce tidal volume. Third, the majority of procedures were transurethral resections (TUR), which involve lithotomy positioning, increased intra-abdominal pressure, and large-volume irrigation—all of which can compromise respiratory mechanics. The control group received sufentanil, a μ-opioid agonist with strong respiratory depressant effects, which may have compounded the risk of desaturation is another cause of high incidence of oxygen-desaturation. Despite these factors, none of the events required airway interventions, indicating that desaturation episodes were clinically manageable under the study protocol. In this study, patients with severe respiratory diseases such as active infections or significant pulmonary impairment were excluded to minimize confounding risk. The baseline SpO2 levels in all included patients were ≥ 95% on room air and no cases of bronchospasm or delayed recovery were recorded, which show a relatively favorable safety profile in patients with underlying respiratory conditions. Future studies may benefit from integrating standardized pulmonary risk scores or spirometry-based assessments to stratify respiratory vulnerability and enhance methodological rigor.

The average total dosage reported in the results exceeded of 4–6 mg/kg/hour in some patients. This discrepancy can be attributed to inter-individual variations in anesthesia duration and the administration of intraoperative infusion dose and intermittent bolus doses during the procedure. Additional propofol was given in certain cases to manage involuntary movements or to deepen sedation in response to stronger intraoperative stimulation—particularly during TUR procedures. These supplemental doses may result in a higher-than-expected hourly average in some patients. The significant shorter induction time was found in SK group indicating that esketamine could exert a synergistic analgesic and sedative effect in combination with propofol. We also found that lower frequency of hypotension (5(10.6%) vs 13(27.7%), risk ratio: 3.212; CI: 1.041 to 9.904; P = 0.032) and sustainable hemodynamic stability in SK group. High-dosage propofol consumpiton is still the main cause of significant hemodynamic fluctuation and cardiovascular adverse events during PSA, especially for elderly patients. Propofol has the direct negative inotropic properties and peripheral vasodilation thereby eliciting a series cardiovascular stress responses [7]. In our study, MAPs and HRs at time of anesthesia induction, ureteroscope insertion, ureteroscopy withdrawal, and wake up time decreased significantly in SF group, while MAP and HR in SK group were more stable without significant fluctuation. On the one hand, esketamine exerts a cardiovascular excitation effect through its sympathetic efficacy on circulation. On the other hand, decreased dosage of propofol related to esketamine also absolutely has a positive effect on circulatory stability. The elevation of MAPs and HRs in SK group was well tolerated and did not require any vasoactive medication. The sympathetic effect of esketamine can elevate MAP and HR levels without other significant adverse reactions, which was susceptible to contribute to greater safety and good outcomes for elderly patients.

Moreover, differences in surgical techniques may also have contributed to the variation in oxygen-desaturation rates. Compared to URS, transurethral resection (TUR) procedures often require larger volumes of irrigation fluid, which may increase the risk of pulmonary fluid overload and oxygen desaturation, particularly in elderly patients with reduced cardiopulmonary reserve. While our study did not stratify desaturation incidence by procedure type in the main analysis, multivariate regression indicated that TUR was independently associated with a reduced risk of oxygen-desaturation (OR: 0.14; 95% CI: 0.03–0.64; P = 0.011). This paradoxical finding may reflect the greater surgical stimulation associated with TUR, which helps maintain spontaneous respiration and counteracts deep sedation-related hypoventilation. Future studies should further explore the interplay between procedural factors and respiratory complications during sedation in elderly patients.

In the present study, there were no life-threatening adverse events observed and no dose related mental side effects of esketamine appeared, including psychic symptoms and dizziness.. Notably, Mini-mental state examination (MMSE) values did not reach statistical significance both before anesthesia induction and after procedure completion and only 1 patient sustained postoperative delirium (POD) in SK group. The available evidence indicates that a lower single dose of esketamine whether combined with a continuous infusion dose or not had a positive effect on relieving postoperative pain and decreasing the incidence of delirium in elderly patients with surgical treatment [30, 31]. However, Ma et al.’s [32] confirmed that repeated infusion of low-dose esketamine had no notable influence on the incidence of POD during three postoperative days in elderly patients under general anesthesia. More clinical explorations detecting esketamine effects on POD or other postoperative mental disorders of elderly patients undergoing several types of surgeries should be given high priority and conducted further. However, it is true that for the secondary outcome of POD incidence, the sample size of this study is relatively small, and larger sample studies may be needed to confirm the reliability of this result.

Although the population involved in this study was only elderly male patients, the main results obtained from this study showed that the combination of ketamine and propofol for intravenous general anesthesia exhibited advantages of less respiratory inhibition and less circulatory inhibition. It can also be extended to patients of other genders, such as elderly women undergoing gynecological surgeries such as hysteroscopy, and even some elderly patients with high ASA grades and comorbidities of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, who may also have the same advantages. And these are expected to be confirmed by us in clinical applications and other studies.

There are several limitations should be noted in Our study design. First, a single dose of 0.25 mg/kg esketamine was designed in our study, thus this study cannot reveal whether the addition of different esketamine dosing protocols to PSA medication regimen may achieve adequate sedation efficacy and dramatically affect safety for elderly patient in urological endoscopic procedures and the optimal sedation regimen for urological endoscopic procedures is still required further investigation. Thus, we will explore dose–response relationships in future studies. Second, as the sample size was relatively small which may be limited powered to determine significant differences in a part of secondary outcomes, such as the frequency of adverse cardiovascular events and POD. Third, this study specifically enrolled male patients aged ≥ 60 years with an ASA physical status I-III. These inclusion criteria allowed us to conduct the treatment effects of esketamine in this specific study population, wherea they do not completely capture same effects in other age groups. Thus, the findings of this study should not easy to be extrapolated to these populations. To address above issue, we believe that extend the applicability of esketamine for broadering patient populations undergoing endourological surgeries are still needed.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that compared with sufentanil, esketamine combined with propofol can provide adequate sedative and analgesic effect, stabilize hemodynamics and reduce the incidence of adverse cardiorespiratory events, such as transient oxygen-desaturation, for elderly patients during urological endoscopy.

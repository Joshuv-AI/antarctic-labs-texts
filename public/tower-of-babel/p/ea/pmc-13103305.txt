MultiScaleWave: a wavelet-based multiscale framework for univariate time series forecasting
Authors: Canjie Zheng, Heng Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate forecasting of time series data is essential in many fields. However, real-world time series are often characterized by noise, non-stationarity and multiscale temporal dependencies, which collectively reduce forecasting performance. To address these challenges, MultiScaleWave, a deep learning framework based on time series decomposition, is proposed for univariate forecasting. The MultiScaleWave model first applies multi-level discrete wavelet transforms to decompose the series into multiscale temporal components. Each component is modeled by a granularity-adaptive module, and the outputs are then fused to generate an informative representation for final forecasting. The MultiScaleWave model has been validated on benchmark datasets and achieves superior performance compared to competitive baselines. The results demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed approach.Keywords: Time series forecasting, Deep learning, Wavelet transformSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Sequential data are ubiquitous in real-world environments, reflecting the evolution of patterns within complex systems1. Time Series Forecasting (TSF), which focuses on predicting subsequent data from historical sequential data2, is extensively used in diverse fields, including pandemic spread modeling3, financial forecasting4, and weather prediction5, as exemplified in Fig. 1. Real-world time series data exhibit noise contamination, non-stationarity, and multiscale coupling6, which pose serious challenges to accurate forecasting.

Examples of real-world time series data in different domains.

Extensive research over the past decades has yielded a diverse set of methodologies for Time Series Forecasting (TSF). These methodologies can be categorized into three classes: statistical-based approaches7, machine learning-based methods8, and deep learning (DL) models9. Statistical approaches such as ARIMA10, though widely used in TSF11, typically assume stationarity and linearity, limiting the ability to model complex and nonlinear patterns in real-world data.

The emergence of Machine Learning (ML) has driven substantial progress in TSF, particularly for complex nonlinear systems. The machine learning methods, including Support Vector Machines (SVM)12 , random forests13 , and Bayesian methods14 have improved forecasting for non-stationary time series. These methods enable robust nonlinear modeling and adaptive learning, which enhance prediction accuracy within data-driven modeling frameworks. Ghosh et al.15 utilized random forests to predict directional movements of stock prices in the highly nonlinear market, demonstrating the efficacy of tree-based ensemble methods. Kurani et al.16 discussed the effectiveness of combining classical machine learning techniques such as SVM with neural networks for stock market forecasting. Motivated by the concept of synergistic integration, Fang et al.17 further proposed a BO-based approach to optimize the hyperparameters of a CNN-LSTM model. The model achieves better accuracy and generalization performance compared to conventional networks by leveraging BO’s capability to navigate high-dimensional parameter spaces. Although ML methods achieve competitive accuracy in some conditions, the effectiveness declines when applied to noisy and strongly coupled datasets.

The development of foundational DL architectures, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)18, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks19, and Transformers20, has profoundly improved the modeling capabilities for TSF. In terms of CNNs, these methods are well-suited for capturing local temporal features and high-frequency variations in time series data21. For instance, Wang et al.22 proposed a Multi-scale Isometric Convolution Network to better extract local features and global correlations by introducing a multi-scale branch structure. Luo et al.23 leveraged CNN’s feature extraction strengths to develop a modern pure convolutional architecture for time series analysis, demonstrating significant efficiency and performance. Despite effectiveness for local patterns, CNNs show reduced effectiveness in capturing long-range and global relationships in sequences, which has prompted the adoption of recurrent and attention-based models. In terms of LSTM, Xuan et al.24 proposed a new prediction model based on Empirical Mode Decomposition, Long Short-Term Memory and Cubic Spline Interpolation to improve the accuracy of stock price trend prediction. In terms of attention-based models, the Transformer architecture has demonstrated promising results in long-term forecasting tasks due to their global modeling capabilities. In particular, Autoformer25 advanced time series forecasting by incorporating a decomposition architecture into Transformers, achieving competitive performance for long-term predictions. Through segmentation of time series into subseries-level patches, Nie et al.26 proposed PatchTST, a Transformer-based architecture that significantly enhanced long-term forecasting accuracy. However, the effectiveness of Transformers in time series forecasting remains to be fully validated. The architectures do not always fully leverage historical patterns and exhibit limited sensitivity to temporal dependencies27.

Current research in DL focuses on decomposing time series into interpretable components and processing these components separately to capture the complex temporal patterns28. CEEMDAN-TCN29 proposed an approach that decomposes time series into multi-frequency components and processes them with parallel temporal convolutional networks (TCNs). Liu et al.30 developed a new stock price prediction model based on improved ensemble empirical mode decomposition and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network. In addition, TimeMixer31 introduced a multiscale mixing architecture that decomposes time series into multiscale microscopic and macroscopic components, leveraging complementary patterns across scales to enhance forecasting robustness. Inspired by prior research, TimeKAN32 decoupled time series into distinct spectral components for independent learning and significantly improved forecasting accuracy. The above DL research generally employs decomposition methods to decouple multiscale temporal patterns. However, the current methods typically employ average pooling for time series decomposition, resulting in irreversible loss of high-frequency signals. Besides, these methods apply identical prediction modules in all temporal scales, which neglect the scale-specific characteristics. An important fact is that existing approaches mostly focus on multivariate forecasting, while univariate problems are common in real-world applications. The univariate problems exhibit greater forecasting difficulty due to the absence of auxiliary covariates, requiring models that extract robust representations from noisy, information-scarce temporal data.

Recent research have explored the use of Wavelet Transform for time series analysis33 . Wavelet Transform effectively decomposes a series into multiple frequency components while preserving temporal information, enabling the capture of both high and low frequency patterns. This multi-scale decomposition also facilitates inherent noise reduction, making it well-suited for modeling complex and non-stationary time series.

The aforementioned challenges inspire the presentation of MultiScaleWave: a wavelet-based univariate forecasting framework with multi-scale specialized processing. The MultiScaleWave employs a cascaded discrete wavelet transform to decompose time series into multi-scale temporal components. Each component is processed by specialized neural modules according to its temporal granularity. The fine-grained components, rich in high-frequency information, are modeled through multi-scale convolutional filters and an LSTM layer with channel-wise attention. The medium-grained components, which contain mid-level frequency patterns, are processed by dilated causal convolutions to capture long-range dependencies. The coarse-grained components, which represent slow-changing trends, are processed by MLPs to efficiently extract features. The refined features are then reconstructed through an inverse wavelet transform, followed by a prediction head for final forecasting. MultiScaleWave is evaluated on multiple real-world datasets, including stock market, solar power34, and weather35 data. Compared with existing baselines, the model achieves higher accuracy, greater stability, and improved generalization. The performance of the proposed model is also evaluated in the presence of input noise, demonstrating strong noise robustness. In addition, the results on the Solar Power and Weather datasets highlight its long-horizon forecasting capability and computational efficiency, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed multi-scale design.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

(1)Wavelet-domain High-Fidelity Processing: A wavelet-based decomposition-reconstruction architecture is proposed, effectively preserving high-frequency details while separating multi-scale components.

(2)Granularity-Adaptive Modules: Dedicated modules are designed for multiple temporal scales, effectively extracting features across diverse scales.

(3)Univariate Forecasting Framework: A specialized univariate predictor is developed, achieving competitive forecasting accuracy on temporal datasets.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 describes the architecture of the method proposed in this research. Section 3 shows the forecasting results and performance analysis. Section 4 summarizes the proposed research method and discusses future research directions on modeling strategies for different temporal scales.

Methods

Time series forecasting is affected by noise contamination, multiscale coupling, and non-stationarity, which increase the difficulty of accurate prediction. To address these challenges, this paper introduces MultiScaleWave, a wavelet-based forecasting framework tailored for time series prediction. Figure 2 illustrates the overall architecture of the proposed framework, which comprises three modules: Series Decomposition Module, Multiscale Processing Module, and Temporal Fusion & Prediction Module. The Series Decomposition Module employs a cascaded wavelet transform to decompose the series into multiple temporal granularity series. Each series is then processed by the Multiscale Processing Module to model temporal patterns at different scales. The processed features are subsequently integrated in the Temporal Fusion & Prediction Module and ultimately used for forecasting. Detailed descriptions of each module are provided in the following subsections.

Work Flow of MultiScaleWave model.

Series decomposition module

Conventional decomposition methods such as average pooling often result in the loss of high-frequency signals, which are critical for capturing rapid changes and fine-grained temporal patterns. Besides, real-world time series are frequently contaminated with noise, which makes accurate predictions more challenging. By decomposing the series into multiple frequency bands, the wavelet transform offers an effective solution for analyzing signals at different frequencies. This process also enables inherent noise suppression by isolating signal variations at different temporal scales. The above benefits motivate the adoption of wavelet transform in the Series Decomposition Module.

In the Series Decomposition Module, the raw series is first mapped to a higher-dimensional space through an embedding layer, enabling richer feature representations for subsequent temporal decomposition. As illustrated in the Series Decomposition Module of Fig. 2, the embedded data is first processed by a discrete wavelet transform using the Haar wavelet, producing a pair of high- and low-frequency components, termed D1 and A1, respectively. The A1 is then further decomposed into D2 and A2 via a second wavelet transform. Through the hierarchical decomposition, the original time series is progressively separated into components of varying temporal scales. This structure enables the model to capture both fine-grained fluctuations and long-term trends effectively. The decomposed sub-series are individually passed to the Multiscale Processing Module, which captures temporal patterns at different scales.

Multiscale processing module

The Multiscale Processing Module is designed to effectively model temporal patterns at different granularity by employing specialized processors for each frequency band. As illustrated in Fig. 2, this module comprises three distinct processors: Fine-Grained Processor, Medium-Grained Processor and Coarse-Grained Processor.

The Fine-Grained Processor is employed to process the undecomposed sequence. This processor combines a multi-scale convolutional block and a BiLSTM layer to jointly capture short-term variations and long-range dependencies. As depicted in Fig. 3, the input series is first passed through a multi-branch convolutional module comprising three parallel CNN Blocks with kernel sizes of 3, 5, and 7. Each branch is responsible for extracting temporal features at a specific scale. The outputs are merged on the channel dimension and passed through a 11 CNN Block followed by ReLU activation. In parallel, the input is processed by a BiLSTM layer to capture temporal dependencies, followed by a linear layer. The outputs of the convolutional and BiLSTM branches are summed and further refined by a channel attention mechanism, which emphasizes important features for improved forecasting. Within the hybrid feature extraction mechanism, the Fine-Grained Processor effectively captures detailed temporal features from the fine-scale sequence. Although specific attention weights vary across samples, this mechanism highlights the features which are most important for forecasting.

Architecture of the fine-grained processor.

The Medium-Grained Processor is developed to handle the medium-scale sequence obtained from the first-level wavelet decomposition. As shown in Fig. 4, the decomposed sequence A1 and D1 are independently processed by a causal convolutional network composed of stacked 1D convolutional layers with dilation rates of 1, 2, and 4. This multi-dilation design enables the model to effectively capture patterns at varying temporal ranges within the medium scale, enhancing its ability to model complex temporal dependencies. In order to ensure the temporal causality, left-padding is applied before each convolution, allowing the model to access only past time steps. A gating structure is then applied to adaptively control information flow across time steps for enhancing the expressiveness of the learned features. This design enables the processor to capture medium-range dependencies while preserving the temporal order of the input.

Architecture of the medium-grained processor.

The Coarse-Grained Processor is designed for processing sequences at the largest temporal scale obtained from the second-level wavelet decomposition. At the current decomposition level, with the sequence length substantially reduced and the temporal resolution becoming coarse, capturing detailed temporal patterns is less effective. A simple multilayer perceptron (MLP) is used to efficiently extract semantic features from the coarse representations. The network consists of three fully connected layers stacked sequentially. The first two layers are followed by ReLU activations to introduce nonlinearity, while the final layer produces the transformed feature representation. This simple architecture effectively captures abstract temporal features while maintaining computational efficiency.

Separate processing of fine, medium, and coarse grained sequences enables the model to capture temporal patterns at different scales effectively. Fine-Grained Processor captures short-term fluctuations, Medium-Grained Processor models intermediate-range dependencies, and Coarse-Grained Processor extracts long-term trends. This hierarchical processing enables MultiScaleWave to handle complex, multiscale time series more accurately.

Temporal fusion & prediction module

The Temporal Fusion & Prediction Module is proposed to hierarchically integrate the multi-resolution features for accurate time series forecasting. The Module firstly reconstructs the sequence by applying an inverse wavelet transform (using the Haar wavelet). In this step, the high- and low-frequency components generated by the Coarse-Grained Processor are first combined through inverse wavelet transform to produce a reconstructed sequence. The sequence is then added to the output of the Medium-Grained Processor, which takes the low-frequency component as input. The combined sequence is further sent to the second inverse wavelet transform together with the high-frequency component processed by the Medium-Grained Processor. The result is subsequently fused with the Fine-Grained Processor’s output. This hierarchical integration completes the integration of multi-scale representations and reconstructs the sequence back into the time domain. The reconstructed sequence is subsequently transformed by a two-layer fully connected network to produce the final forecast. By combining the outputs of each processor through sequential inverse wavelet transforms, the model effectively integrates features across multiple temporal scales, producing accurate forecasts that leverage both short-term variations and long-term trends.

Experiment

A series of experiments are conducted on four real-world time series datasets to evaluate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed model. This section begins with a description of the datasets and preprocessing methods, followed by details of the experimental setup. Comparative results with multiple baseline models are then presented, along with a discussion of the model’s forecasting performance. The robustness of the proposed model is further examined by injecting Gaussian noise with varying intensities into the input sequences. Besides, dedicated ablations are conducted to validate the contribution of the wavelet decomposition module. In addition, long-horizon forecasting tasks and computational-efficiency analyses are included to further demonstrate the robustness and practicality of the proposed framework.

Data preparation and training settings

Daily closing price time-series data from the Shanghai Composite Index(ticker: 000001.SS), Ping An Bank Co., Ltd.(ticker: 000001.SZ), Citigroup Inc.(ticker: C), and Mercedes-Benz Group AG(ticker: MBGAF, OTC market), sourced from Yahoo Finance(accessed on 5 September 2025), are selected as experimental datasets in this research. The data extend from May 30, 2016, to May 30, 2025, providing ample historical information for model evaluation. As illustrated in Fig. 5, the four datasets represent diverse domains–including financial markets, banking, and the automotive industry–and exhibit distinct temporal patterns. In addition to the financial datasets, two environmental datasets are used to further broaden the evaluation scope. The Weather dataset, provided by the Max Planck Institute for Biogeochemistry, contains meteorological observations collected throughout 2020 at a 10-minute resolution. The Solar Power dataset records photovoltaic power generation and related environmental variables, with measurements collected every 15 minutes from 2019 to 2020. These datasets originate from different application areas and exhibit varied temporal behaviors, enabling a comprehensive assessment of the proposed model’s generalization capability and adaptability to diverse time series patterns.

Time series patterns of the Shanghai Composite Index, Ping An Bank, Citigroup, and Mercedes-Benz datasets.

A series of preprocessing steps is applied to prepare the collected datasets for model training. Each dataset is first divided into training, validation, and test sets in a ratio of 6:2:2, ensuring a balanced distribution for model development and evaluation. Min-Max scaling is then applied to normalize the features to the range [0,1], with the scaling parameters calculated from the training set. These parameters are subsequently applied to transform the validation and test sets. The Sliding Window Segmentation is applied to transform series into a format suitable for time series forecasting, with the look-back window set to 12, the forecast horizon to 1, and the step size to 1.

Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) is employed as the loss function, while model parameters are optimized using the Adam algorithm with a learning rate of 0.001. Model performance is assessed based on four widely used evaluation metrics: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), RMSE, Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and the coefficient of determination (). Before computing these metrics, the predicted results are transformed back to the original scale through inverse normalization, which aligns them with the true labels for accurate evaluation. The chosen hyperparameter, including the sliding window size, forecast horizon, step size, learning rate, and batch size, are determined based on preliminary experiments that balance model complexity and forecasting performance, ensuring reliable and robust results.

Conclusion

Accurate forecasting of time series data remains an essential challenge in diverse fields. This paper proposes MultiScaleWave, a univariate time series forecasting framework that combines wavelet-based multiscale decomposition, tailored modeling and feature fusion. The MultiScaleWave consists of three modules: the Series Decomposition Module, Multiscale Processing Module, and Temporal Fusion & Prediction Module. Extensive experiments on multiple real-world datasets have been conducted to assess the effectiveness of the proposed method. The accuracy enhancements observed over several baseline models demonstrate the stability and adaptability of MultiScaleWave. The results indicate that the decomposition–modeling–fusion strategy provides a robust and generalizable solution for univariate time series forecasting. In conclusion, MultiScaleWave offers a powerful and versatile framework that effectively addresses the fundamental challenge of capturing complex temporal dependencies across multiple scales, enabling more reliable predictions across diverse applications. While the proposed approach has demonstrated strong performance in diverse datasets, future research is focusing on exploring alternative modeling strategies for different temporal scales to further enhance the accuracy and applicability of the framework.

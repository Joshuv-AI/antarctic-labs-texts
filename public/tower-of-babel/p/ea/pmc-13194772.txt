The antibacterial, Cytotoxic/photocatalysis efficacy of g-C3N4 and CS is influenced by various parameters, such as its specific surface area, fast electron-hole recombination, and optical absorption characteristics. To enhance the efficacy of the antibacterial, Cytotoxic/photocatalysis activities of g-C3N4 and CS and broaden its applicability, several nanocomposites and nanoparticles have been synthesized and assessed. While hence, it will be changing the higher death rate found at the higher concentration and the result is concluded Ag-NPs (K4), Ag-CS (K5) & Ag-gC3N4 (K6) NCs are found to be less toxic than others. The photocatalytic reaction for Ag-NPs (K4) at 2% 35 ± 1.20%, while Ag-CS (K5) at 6% 49 ± 1.10%, and Ag-gC3N4 (K6) at 6% 51 ± 1.10% against degradation of 4-NP. All the synthesized nanoparticles and nanocomposites were characterized through various spectroscopic techniques such as UV-Visible, X-ray diffraction and Scanning electron microscopy. Introduction

Nanotechnology is a rapidly evolving and interdisciplinary domain with several applications in science and technology. The development of environment friendly techniques for nanoparticle synthesis is a crucial advancement in nanotechnology. Over the past decade, extensive efforts have been made to develop environmentally friendly synthesis procedures to eliminate toxic byproducts1–4. Traditional methods for nanoparticle synthesis are costly, hazardous, and environmentally unfriendly. To address these issues, researchers have identified specific green pathways, namely the naturally existing sources and their derivatives suitable for the creation of nanoparticles5–7. Plant-based nanoparticle green synthesis is now considered the gold standard among green biological approaches because of its simplicity and the variety of available plants8–10. The escalating problem of germ resistance, aggravated by lethargic antibiotic research, demands new solutions. Microbes, prevalent in the environment, contribute to several infectious and non-infectious disorders11,12. Photocatalytic materials exhibit potential for antimicrobial applications; nonetheless, they encounter obstacles including limited effectiveness under visible light, possible toxicity, and elevated prices13. Concerns over water pollution have increased day by day and got its significant attention recently14,15. Dye-contaminated wastewater significantly contributes to water contamination from different industries such as textile dyeing, paper manufacturing, food processing, paints, and cosmetics, discharge dye-contaminated effluents into the environment, leading to dye-contaminated wastewater16,17. The dyes are released in wastewater are highly poisonous, carcinogenic, and xenobiotic to living creatures. The dyes in wastewater adversely impact on the aquatic ecology, as their strong coloration obstructs sunlight penetration into the water body18,19. Dyes have intricate structures and are categorized into many classes according to their molecular configurations, like direct dyes, basic dyes, solvent dyes, reactive dyes, and vat dyes20. Moreover, environmental pollution resulting from industrialization and population expansion has exacerbated contamination, underscoring the necessity for advanced materials21 to tackle both microbial resistance and environmental deterioration22–25.

Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) have emerged as particularly significant entities within the extensive range of nanomaterials. The attraction of these compounds emerges from a complex combination of remarkable optical, electrical, catalytic, and notably, potent antibacterial properties. Silver nanoparticles are significant due to their remarkable chemical, physical, and biological properties, which facilitate various uses. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) exhibit various anti-inflammatory and antibacterial characteristics that promote rapid wound healing. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) exhibit exceptional characteristics; nevertheless, their amalgamation with other functional materials to create nanocomposites presents a sophisticated approach to enhance their efficacy, introduce novel synergistic qualities, and expand their range of applications26–31.

The amino and hydroxyl functional groups on the chitosan backbone serve as efficient chelating agents for silver ions, facilitating their reduction and subsequently stabilizing the AgNPs, so preventing aggregation and regulating their size distribution32. The integration of AgNPs into a chitosan matrix is expected to improve the stability and dispersibility of the AgNPs while also generating a synergistic impact that may enhance their antibacterial and cytotoxic properties33–35. Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) is a compelling material for the advancement of innovative nanocomposites. g-C3N4, a metal-free polymeric semiconductor, exhibits remarkable thermal and chemical stability, a moderate band gap of around 2.7 eV, and a distinctive electronic band structure, rendering it highly advantageous for diverse photocatalytic applications36–38. The layered structure, similar to graphite, and nitrogen-rich composition enhance its notable qualities, such as visible-light absorption, large surface area potential, and effective charge separation capabilities39–41. The concrete use of pure g-C3N4 is frequently constrained by its comparatively low surface area, swift recombination of photo-generated electron-hole pairs, and restricted light absorption spectrum42.

This study thoroughly examines the eco-friendly synthesis of pure AgNPs, AgNP@Chitosan, and AgNP@g-C3N4 nanocomposites. The primary aim is to methodically examine these synthesized materials for their effectiveness in various applications, such as antibacterial properties, cytotoxic effects, and photocatalytic destruction of pollutants. This comprehensive strategy seeks to develop sustainable and efficient nanomaterials to address significant issues in health and the environment.

Materials and methods

Materials

The chemicals and reagents used in this study were analytical grade and purchased from Sigma Aldrich and Merck. Melamine, gold (III) chloride trihydrate, silver nitrate, deionized water, chitosan, crucible, muffle furnace, plant leaves (Haloxylon salicornicum and Diospyros kaki), vacuum oven and all other necessary glassware are also analytical grades.

Material characterization

All the synthesized material are characterized through various spectroscopic techniques such as UV-Vis., SEM and XRD spectroscopy. Also, these synthesized NPs and NCs are assessed their antibacterial as well as cytotoxic analysis carried out by using different bacterial strains and Brine Shrimp for cytotoxic study.

Preparation of g-C3N4

It was prepared through thermal decomposition of melamine. Muffle furnace was kept under atmospheric control condition applied for the preparation of g-C3N4 from melamine (7.0 g) was taken in a crucible. 3 °C/min. temperature is raised up to 450 °C maintained for the period of 2 h and finally set the temperature 550 °C by the increase 3 °C/min and it was monitored for the period of 4 h. After this the g-C3N4 was obtained through this process, and material was allowed to cool at room temperature then it was weighed and grinded. The prepared material was washed with distilled water and filtered and then dried it in the over for overnight at 85 °C.

Preparation of leaves extract (Diospyros kaki)

Diospyros kaki (green leaves, immature pods and leaflets) sample were collected from Swat valley Barikot, Pakistan with the help of two research students from the Department of Chemistry, Hazara University Mansehra, Pakistan and the these were washed thrice with distil water to remove the dust and sand particles. After that these are drying with the help of vacuum filtration placed on the Whattman filter paper. The 10.0 g of leaves were taken and grind with the help of pestle and mortar and then added in to the 500 mL beaker and boiled in 100 mL deionized water (60 °C) for the period of 50 min and after that the product was separated from the biomass by using vacuum filtration. The filtrate was centrifuged for 5 min at 4000 rpm to separate the upper part of centrifuged material (supernatant) and stored at 4 °C until the formation of NPs.

Green synthesis of Ag-oxide nanoparticles

Plant extract (5.0 mL), were taken and metal precursor 10 mM AgNO3 (0.4 mL) added and 30 mL of distill water added and put at stirring with the speed of 220 rpm at 40 °C for the period of 7 h. Upon the change in color the formation of nanoparticles was confirmed. Furthermore, the purification of supernatant was removed through centrifugation and dried in oven at 110 °C overnight. These dried nanoparticles were ground to avoid the aggregation of the NPS. At the end these NPs were rehydrated in DI water vortex and sonicate to prepare NPs dried and further used for nanocomposites and analysis.

Synthesis of Ag-NPs@g-C3N4 nanocomposites

The Ag-NPs base nanocomposites were prepared loaded g-C3N4 by the addition of Ag-NPs (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5%) with various concentration in ultrapure water to prepare the nanocomposites. The solution was put into the magnetically controlled string 500 rpm/min for the period of 4 h. After that the solution was put into vacuum oven at 120 °C overnight and then cool at room temperature too and then grind with the help of pestle and mortar to avoid aggregation of NCs. At the end these NPs were rehydrated in DI water vortex and sonicate to prepare NCs and used for further analysis.

Synthesis of Ag-NPs@Chitosan nanocomposites

Chitosan was dissolved in the 2% formic acid solution and stirred for the period of 1 h at room temperature until the homogenous mixing and added the Ag-NPs (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5%) to make the different formulations and stirrer for 1 h until the complete mixing. After that it was put into the petri dishes and placed overnight in oven at room temperature for the formation of thin films which are used for further characterization.

Assay protocol for anti-bacterial activity

The antimicrobial efficacy of the prepared Au-NPs (K1), Au@CS (K2), and Au@g-C3N4 (K3) was assessed using the most used well diffusion technique against B. subtilis, E. coli, and K. pneumoniae43. The cultivated culture was evenly distributed into sterilized nutrient agar plates, and wells with a diameter of 100 µL were formed in the agar plates using a sterile cork-borer. The wells were filled with different concentrations (1.0 mM, 1.5 mM, 2.0 mM, and 3.0 mM) of Au-NPs (K1), Au@CS (K2), and Au@g-C3N4 (K3). A control group was included with 1.0 mgL− 1 of gentamycin. Further, the plates were placed at a temperature of 37 ᴼC for a duration of 24 h. The antibacterial efficacy of the Au-NPs (K1), Au@CS (K2), and Au@g-C3N4 (K3) was calculated by measuring the areas where bacterial growth was inhibited zone of inhibition (ZOI) ± SD (n = 3) around the well that was infused with Au-NPs (K1), Au@CS (K2), and Au@g-C3N4 (K3).

Assay protocol for cytotoxic analysis of Au-NPs, Au@CS and Au@g-C3N4 through Brine Shrimp method

The cytotoxicity of Ag-NPs, Ag@CS and Ag@g-C3N4 was analyzed due to their ability to readily infiltrate the human body. While, the experiment aimed to establish the lethal concentration of the sample by exposing brine shrimp eggs (Artemia salina) to assess the toxicity percentage of leather coated with Ag-NPs, Ag@CS and Ag@g-C3N444.

In this study 0.5 g sample was taken and put into the vial. Then, 1mL of methanol was put in vial with the help of pipette. Furthermore, the solution was vigorously agitated until the test portions completely dissolved. 250 µl, 25 µl, and 2.5 µl of the stock solution were transferred into separate vials containing samples using a micro pipette. This resulted in the preparation of solutions with concentrations of 1000 µg/mL, 100 µg/mL, and 10 µg/mL, respectively. At the end the samples mixture was kept undisturbed for period of 24 h to evaporate the solvent. Meanwhile, eggs of Artemia salina were added into the hatching area and sea water to maintain it for 24–48 h to help the production of shrimp larvae from the eggs.

Three drops of dimethylsulphoxide (DMSO) and 2 ml of seawater were added to each of the solutions with concentrations of 1000 µg/ml and 100 µg/ml, respectively. The solution with a concentration of 10 µg/ml also had seawater added to it. The experiment was conducted in triplicate manner. With the help of dropper, a total of 10 larvae of Artemia Selina were delivered into individual sample vials containing the aforementioned mixture. Seawater was introduced into each sample vial to precisely achieve a content volume of 5 ml. Subsequently, each vial was left undisturbed for a duration of 24 h, during which the number of surviving entities was tallied.

Assay protocol for photo-catalysis activity

The photocatalytic degradation of 4-Nitrophenol (4-NP) dye carried out by utilizing Ag-NPs (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%), Ag@CS (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%), and Ag@g-C3N4 (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%) under UV light irradiation. In summary, 10 mg of Ag-NPs (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%), Ag@CS (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%), and Ag@g-C3N4 (1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5%) were mixed with 50 mL of the corresponding pollutants and then 10 ppm solutions of 4-nitrophenol. The sample mixture was subjected to sonication for 3 min and subsequently mixed in the absence of light for 3 min. The reaction solutions were continually agitated and exposed to UV light (300 W) for 5 h. The absorbance of the 4-NP solution at λmax of 620 nm and 316 nm was measured hourly to monitor the photo-catalysis progress over a duration of five hours in different intervals. Results and discussion

UV-visible spectroscopic analysis of Ag-based NPs and NCs

In this study UV-Visible analysis in the range of 200 nm to 800 nm carried out of prepared Ag-NPs (K4), Ag@CS (K5) and Ag@g-C3N4 (K6) nanocomposites. UV-Vis., spectroscopy is the basic employed to record the optical properties and electrical configuration of nanoparticles and nanocomposites. This is because the absorption bands observed in this method provide information about the size and shape of metal nanoparticles. At the nanometer scale the electron cloud showed oscillations on the surface of a particle and absorbed EMR of a targeted energy. The formation of surface plasmon resonance or plasmon absorption in nanoparticles/nanocomposites is due to small size. In this study different factors can influence this resonance with the solvent and surface functionalization being particularly occur in influencing the precise frequency and intensity of the band. The surface effects render the surface plasmon a highly effective indication of adsorption into particle surfaces while facilitating the application of nanoparticle/nanocomposites used in various applications. Those NPs/NCs having their size below 1–2 nm do not display this phenomenon because their electrons inhabit discrete energy levels. Unlike nanospheres, bulk silver exhibits a consistent absorbance across the UV-vis., range which ultimately merges into a singular plasmon absorbance.

In this research work Ag-NPs (K4) have showed peak at 423 nm and these are spherical in shape while Ag@g-C3N4 (K6) showed 362 nm & Ag@CS (K5) 393 nm while Ag-NPs (K4) strong peak which are attributed to the surface plasmon resonance in Fig. 1. While in this case the relationship between plasmon band and size & form of nanoparticles/nanocomposites is widely published reports. In the small NPs and NCs quantum size effects neglect, but big bulk-like nanoparticles are constrained by the electron mean free path.

Photocatalytic degradation of 4-NP by using Ag-NPs (K4), Ag-CS (K5) & Ag-gC3N4 (K6) NCs nanocomposites

The photocatalytic degradation of 4-NP was examined by applying different newly developed catalyst Ag-NPs (K4), Ag-CS (K5), and Ag-gC3N4 (K6) nanocomposites under UV light irradiation, as shown in Table 4; Fig. 4. At every hour, the absorbance of the treated 4-NP solution was recorded to assess the photocatalytic reaction. The measurements were conducted thrice to obtain consistency. The photocatalytic response of Ag-NPs (K4), Ag-CS (K5), and Ag-gC3N4 (K6) NCs under UV light irradiation was anticipated throughout the experiment. In the presence of Ag-NPs enhance the interfacial charge transfer with the photocatalyst surface and pollutants absorbed. The electrons transferred also react with the dissolved oxygen to generate the superoxide radical and the other remaining holes can oxidize water to or ions to form the hydroxyl ions. The result is concluded that these species are also responsible for the oxidative degradation of organic contaminants. The K4 exhibited the lowest response among the samples, around 35 ± 1.20% in photocatalytic degradation of 4-NP. The incorporation of 2% Ag into K5 resulted in a modest enhancement of the photocatalytic response (43 ± 1.50%), indicating the influence of Ag doping. The photocatalytic reaction for 4% K5 was approximately 45 ± 1.30%, while 6% K5 exhibited around 49 ± 1.10% degradation of 4-NP. The photocatalytic response of 2% Ag doped in K6 exhibited a small increase (44 ± 1.30%), indicating the influence of Ag doping. An increased percentage of Ag doping has demonstrated enhanced photocatalytic degradation of 4-NP. The photocatalytic reaction for 4% K6 was approximately 48 ± 1.00%, while 6% K6 exhibited around 51 ± 1.10% degradation of 4-NP (Table 4). Overall, in these three schemes 6% of K6 found to be the most potent which showed highest degradation of the 4-NP which is used as organic dye in this work while rest of all the others found to be lower degradation of 4-NP.

Conclusion

This study illustrates the effective synthesis of Ag-NPs, Ag@CS, and Ag@gC3N4 nanocomposites via environmentally friendly methods. The thorough characterization of these nanocomposites by several analytical methods yields significant insights into their characteristics and prospective uses. The antibacterial behavior of the Ag-NPs (K4) at different concentration zone of inhibition is measured and remarkable activity is observed at 2.5% having (B. subtilis, E. coli and K. pneumoniae and standard drug, 10.16 ± 0.02, 10.85 ± 0.02, 11.16 ± 0.02 and 14 ± 0.06) while in case of composites higher activity found at 2.0% of Ag-CS (K5) (B. subtilis, E. coli, K. pneumoniae standard drug gentamycin, 10.40 ± 0.10, 12.05 ± 0.10, 11.70 ± 0.10 and 11 ± 0.06) & Ag-gC3N4 (K6) NCs B. subtilis, E. coli, K. pneumoniae and standard drug (9.40 ± 0.08, 15.05 ± 0.05, 7.70 ± 0.10 and 11 ± 0.06) in different ratios was performed against the standard drug gentamicin. While hence, it will be changing the higher death rate found at the higher concentration and the result is concluded Ag-NPs (K4), Ag-CS (K5) & Ag-gC3N4 (K6) NCs are found to be less toxic than others. The photocatalytic reaction for Ag-NPs (K4) at 2% 35 ± 1.20%, while Ag-CS (K5) at 6% 49 ± 1.10%, and Ag-gC3N4 (K6) at 6% 51 ± 1.10% against degradation of 4-NP. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) examination validated the existence of Ag-NPs, and Ag@CS, Ag@gC3N4 nanoparticles inside the nanocomposites, exhibiting variations in peak intensity and accuracy.

Palladium-Catalyzed Enantioselective [4 + 2] Annulation with Allenes: Direct Access to Chiral Silatetralins
Authors: Hang Zhou, Yan Sun, Rui Xu, Tao Chen, Liuzhou Gao, Guofeng Zhao, Junliang Zhang, Yidong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chiral tetralins represent privileged scaffolds in medicinal chemistry, and the incorporation of silicon as a bioisostere offers a valuable strategy for molecular design. Nevertheless, efficient synthetic approaches to enantiopure silicon-bridged tetralin analogues remain highly desirable and underdeveloped. Herein, we present the strategy for constructing chiral silatetralins via a highly enantioselective palladium-catalyzed [4 + 2] annulation between benzosilacyclobutenes and a wide range of allenes by employing a chiral phosphoramidite ligand. The reaction is distinguished by exclusive proximal CC bond insertion of the allene and selective Si–C­(sp2) bond activation of benzosilacyclobutene. This method exhibits a robust substrate scope, accommodating allenes with alkyl, alkenyl, aryl, silyl, alkoxy, and complex bioactive substituents to deliver enantioenriched functionalized silatetralins. Moreover, the protocol provides direct access to valuable exocyclic methylene-containing silatetralins, serving as versatile intermediates for further synthetic diversification.

Introduction

Silacycles, as pivotal structural motifs in medicinal chemistry, have attracted considerable attention due to their advantageous properties, such as enhanced lipophilicity, improved biological activity, and reduced toxicity, compared to their all-carbon analogues. Representative examples, including sila-tacrine, sila-mesembranol, sila-venlafaxine, and sila-tetrahydroisoquinoline, demonstrate the significant therapeutic potential of silacyclic scaffolds (Scheme a). − Despite these advantages, progress toward structurally diverse and complex organosilicon frameworks has been limited by a scarcity of efficient synthetic methodologies, which, in turn, constrain the exploration of their biological properties. Consequently, the development of general strategies for the synthesis of organosilicon analogues of well-established bioactive molecular scaffolds represents an urgent priority.

Research Background and Our Work

Silacyclobutenes (SCBs) have garnered significant interest as sila-4C synthons, enabling access to structurally diverse silacycles in a wide range of transformations, driven by their inherent ring strain. − In contrast to the well-established reactions of SCBs with alkenes, − alkynes, − carbonyl groups, − and other coupling partners, − their engagement with allenes − remains notably underdeveloped, likely due to the formidable challenge of controlling the regio- and stereoselectivity of the allene insertion (Scheme b). ,− The pioneering cycloaddition of SCBs with 1-phenylallene was disclosed by Utimoto and co-workers in 1991, which notably affords a mixture of regioisomers. A significant advance in enantioselectivity was later made by the Song group in 2022, whose reaction with allenyl esters proceeds via exclusive distal π-bond insertion. Recently, our group has developed the regioselective palladium-catalyzed cycloaddition of silacyclobutanes with N-allenamides. A key limitation of these prior works is their restriction to specific allenes (aryl allenes, allenyl esters, or N-allenamides), with the reactivity of general allenes remaining underdeveloped. Moreover, the development of asymmetric catalytic versions of such transformations remains elusive.

Motivated to access chiral silatetralins as valuable bioisosteres of chiral tetralins, − we proposed a regio- and enantioselective [4 + 2] annulation, in which the proximal π-bond of general allenes engages in the ring-opening of the benzosilacyclobutene (Scheme c). Nevertheless, several synthetic and selectivity challenges remain to be addressed: First, regiocontrol in the engagement of proximal vs distal CC bonds of general allenes constitutes a major obstacle, as it is sensitive to the allene’s steric and electronic properties; , second, controlling the selective activation of the Si–C­(sp2) vs Si–C­(sp3) bond of benzosilacyclobutenes; , finally, achieving high enantioselectivity, which necessitates the chiral ligand provides a perfect steric environment to simultaneously govern the stereochemical outcome and accelerate the desired pathway, thereby suppressing racemic background process.

During the final stage of our manuscript preparation, a study by Shen and colleagues reporting a related concept for aryl allenes came to our attention. While their work is commendable, its scope is limited to aryl allene. We disclose herein a highly chemo-, regio-, and enantioselective palladium-catalyzed [4 + 2] annulation between benzosilacyclobutenes and diverse allenes by employing a chiral phosphoramidite ligand (Scheme c). The methodology demonstrates remarkable generality, compatible with a wide array of allenes bearing alkyl, alkenyl, aryl, silyl, and alkoxy substituents, including those functionalized with complex bioactive molecules, thus enabling efficient access to diverse and enantioenriched silatetralin derivatives. The key to this process is the selective insertion of the proximal CC bond of allenes and the Si–C­(sp2) bond cleavage of benzosilacyclobutenes. Additionally, the method efficiently constructs silatetralins featuring an exocyclic methylene group, a functional handle that enables further synthetic diversification.

Results and Discussion

Optimization of Reaction Conditions

At the outset, alkyl allene 2a was selected as the model substrate to evaluate the [4 + 2] cycloaddition with benzosilacyclobutene 1a in Table . Initially, various chiral phosphine ligands were screened in the presence of Pd­(dba)2 (5 mol %) in (CH2Cl)2 at −20 °C (Table ). The results revealed that privileged bisphosphine ligands such as BINAP (L1) and Segphos (L2) were ineffective in promoting this reaction. We then turned our attention to a series of phosphoramidite ligands. Notably, desired product 3a was obtained, resulting from selective insertion of the proximal CC bond of allene 2a in benzosilacyclobutene 1a. Simple phosphoramidite ligands L3 and L4 exhibited good reactivity; however, only low enantioselectivity was obtained. Strategic modification of the substituents at the 3,3′-positions of the binaphthyl backbone (L5–L9) significantly influenced both the yield and enantiocontrol. In particular, electron-withdrawing groups at these positions were found to be beneficial for both yield and enantioselectivity (L8 and L9). Remarkably, phosphoramidite ligand L9 bearing pentafluorophenyl substituents was identified as the optimal choice, delivering cycloadduct 3a in 98% yield with excellent enantioselectivity (93% ee). Adjustment of the substituents on the amine (L10) did not lead to a further improvement in enantioselectivity.

Subsequently, we examined other reaction conditions with L9 as the optimized ligand. First, raising the temperature significantly compromised the enantioselectivity (Table , entries 2 and 3). We then screened various solvents (entries 4–8) and found that dichloromethane afforded a further improvement in enantioselectivity (94% ee) (entry 8). Finally, the evaluation of palladium catalysts revealed that Pd2(dba)3 delivered superior performance with 98% yield and 95% ee (entry 9), whereas [Pd­(allyl)­Cl]2 and Pd­(PPh3)4 performed poorly (entries 11 and 12). Notably, even under ligand-free conditions, target product 3a could be obtained in 87% yield at 25 °C (Table , entry 13). This result indicates that enhancing enantioselectivity requires effectively suppressing the competing non-enantioselective background pathway while accelerating the rate of the main catalytic cycle.

Scope of the Reaction

Under the optimized conditions, we next evaluated the scope of the two reaction partners. As summarized in Table , a series of enantioenriched silatetralins were successfully synthesized in good yields, with excellent enantioselectivity and regioselectivity (>20:1). Initially, the scope of allenes was evaluated. A series of readily available allenes bearing synthetically useful functional groups, such as N-tosyl amine (3a and 3g), phthalimides (3b and 3c), alkyl tosylate (3d), ester (3e and 3i), and alcohol (3f) could all be tolerated, providing the desired products in high yields (90–99%) with excellent enantioselectivities (90–96% ees). Other unique functional groups such as cyano (3h), thioether (3q), and boronic acid pinacol ester (3r) were found to be compatible under the same conditions. We next examined substrates (3j–3m) with other types of unsaturated C–C bonds to investigate the selectivity of annulation with benzosilacyclobutene 1a. Gratifyingly, all substrates exhibited exclusive selectivity (>20:1) for the proximal CC bond of allenes over that of alkenes and alkynes, as evidenced by 1H NMR analysis of the crude reaction mixtures. Furthermore, allene substrates bearing a variety of heterocycles, such as thiophene (3n), furan (3o), tetrazole (3p), and piperidine (3t) gave the chiral silatetralins in good yields (91–99%) with good enantioselectivities (92–94% ees). In addition, aryl or alkenyl allenes (3u–3x) were also suitable substrates, proceeding with consistently high levels of enantioselectivity (90–99% ees). The absolute configuration of product 3w was determined by X-ray crystallography (CCDC 2490610). Finally, this catalytic system was also effective for allenyl silanes and allenyl ethers, affording the corresponding products 3y and 3z in high yields with moderate to good enantioselectivities.

Subsequently, the variations of benzosilacyclobutenes 1 were evaluated with allene 2f as the reaction partner (Table , bottom). Substitution at the 3-position of the aryl ring in benzosilacyclobutenes was well tolerated in the ring-expansion process, affording products 3aa and 3ab in good yield with high enantioselectivity. Notably, benzosilacyclobutenes with other symmetrical substituents on the silicon atom (3ac–3ae) reacted smoothly, affording the corresponding products in high yields without a loss of enantioselectivity (91–96% ees). Finally, unsymmetrical benzosilacyclobutenes bearing a methyl group along with a varied substituent (such as n-hexyl or phenyl) also proved to be suitable substrates, affording the desired products (3af–3ag) with excellent yields (95–98% yields) and enantioselectivity (92–98% ees), albeit with low diastereoselectivity.

We further extended the applicability of our method to allenes functionalized with natural products and bioactive compounds, as summarized in Table . For instance, the allene derivative of febuxostat, a therapeutic agent used to treat hyperuricemia, underwent a smooth reaction with benzosilacyclobutene 1a, affording the corresponding product 4a in 99% yield with excellent enantioselectivity (94% ee). Similarly, the allene analogue of perphenazine, a phenothiazine-class antipsychotic drug, served as an effective substrate, yielding product 4b in 97% yield with 94% ee. Notably, this catalytic system also tolerated allenes containing pre-existing stereocenters and sensitive functional groups, as demonstrated with derivatives of fenofibric acid (4c), isoxepac (4i), repaglinide (4j), (1S)-(−)-camphanic acid (4k), artesunate (4l), and D-biotin (4m). In all cases, the reactions proceeded smoothly without the erosion of reactivity, regioselectivity, or enantioselectivity.

Synthetic Application

To demonstrate the synthetic utility of this method, we conducted a scale-up reaction between 1a and allene 2f on a 3 mmol scale, which furnished the corresponding enantioenriched silatetralin 3f with good efficiency and excellent regio- and enantioselectivity (Scheme ). The resulting silatetralin 3f was then subjected to a series of downstream transformations.

Scale-Up Synthesis and Synthetic Application

Initially, hydrogenation of the terminal alkene moiety in 3f provided derivative 6 in 83% yield with a diastereomeric ratio of 1.5:1, while maintaining the enantiopurity. Furthermore, the CC bond of 3f readily underwent an iron-catalyzed reaction with acrylonitrile and phenylsilane to afford adduct 7. Additionally, olefin metathesis was successfully conducted on silatetralin derivatives 3j and 3k, which bear terminal alkenyl groups, leading to the formation of a seven-membered ring in products 8 and 9, respectively.

Drawing from previous mechanistic studies on Pd-catalyzed silacycloadditions of silacyclobutenes with allenes, , a catalytic cycle is proposed in Scheme . The active Pd(0) catalyst PdL* is generated in situ via ligand exchange of the precatalyst Pd2(dba)3 and the chiral phosphoramidite ligand L* (L9). Coordination of PdL* to benzosilacyclobutene 1a yields complex Int1, which undergoes oxidative addition of the Si–C­(sp2) bond to afford palladacyclic intermediate Int2. Upon coordination of allene 2 to palladium, intermediate Int3 is formed. The enhanced electron density of the proximal CC bond relative to the distal CC bond directs exclusive silylation at the β-position of the allene, furnishing Int4 as the favored intermediate over Int4′. Subsequent Cα–C­(Ar) reductive elimination from Int4 represents the stereoselectivity-determining step, producing the β,α-regioselective cycloadduct-coordinated intermediate Int6-R. Finally, ligand exchange between Int6-R and another molecule of 1a releases the desired product (R)-3 and regenerates the substrate-coordinated Pd(0) species Int1.

Conclusions

In summary, we have developed an asymmetric palladium-catalyzed ring-expansion reaction of benzosilacyclobutenes with a diverse array of allenes, delivering enantioenriched silatetralins with high efficiency and excellent enantioselectivities (up to 99% ee). Notably, the reaction proceeds with exclusive insertion of the proximal CC bond of the allene and selective cleavage of the Si–C­(sp2) bond within the benzosilacyclobutene. This method also directly installs a synthetically valuable exocyclic methylene group, enabling further diversification of the chiral silatetralin products. Given the medicinal relevance of chiral tetralins, we anticipate that this strategy for installing stereogenicity adjacent to silicon will contribute to bioisosteric optimization strategies aimed at enhancing the metabolic stability and target engagement.

Zanthoxylum bungeanum leaf enhanced yolk color and egg flavor in laying hens by altering yolk lipid composition
Authors: Shanchuan Cao, Hanshu Lin, Xinglai Li, Jianfei Zhao, Qiaobo Lei, Jianchuan Zhou, Jianping Wang, Jingbo Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present study was conducted to investigate the effects of different levels of dietary Zanthoxylum bungeanum leaf (ZBL) on productive performance, egg quality, flavor, and yolk lipidomic of laying hens. In total, 640 healthy Lohmann Pink laying hens (age, 58 weeks) were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups: control (CON), 1 % ZBL, 2 % ZBL, or 3 % ZBL. The entire experiment lasted for eight weeks. The results demonstrated that dietary supplementation with ZBL did not compromise the laying rate in laying hens (P > 0.05). The egg yolk color had increased with ZBL levels (P < 0.05). As compared to the CON group, 3 % ZBL strengthened sourness, richness, saltiness and bitterness of E-tongue analysis and strengthened overall acceptance of sensory evaluation (P < 0.05). In total, 1,533 lipid species in 33 subclasses were detected, including 392 differential lipid species between the CON and 3 % ZBL groups. Glycerophospholipids and sphingolipids were the most abundant lipid species. Principal component analysis and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis showed that the addition of 3 % ZBL resulted in significant changes to the lipid profiles of egg yolks. Moreover, “sphingolipid metabolism” and “glycerophospholipid metabolism” were the key metabolic pathways altered by the addition of 3 % ZBL. The content of sphingolipids in egg has been found to be strongly positively correlated with the saltiness, sourness, and bitterness of eggs. In conclusion, dietary supplementation with ZBL did not reduce the laying rate, but enhanced yolk color and flavor. Sphingolipids were key lipids that influence the flavor of eggs.Keywords: Zanthoxylum bungeanum leaf, Egg yolk, e-tongue, Lipidomic

Introduction

As a pivotal dietary source of nutrients for the human population, eggs are rich in proteins, lipids, vitamins, and minerals (Applegate, 2000). The lipids of eggs are mainly present in the yolk and provide a significant amount of energy in the form of triglycerides (TGs), phospholipids, and cholesterol (Anton, 2013). In addition, phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), and sphingomyelin (SM) present in egg yolks possess antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory activities, in addition to regulating lipid metabolism (Xiao et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2021). In the context of diet and health promotion, there has been a growing interest in producing egg products with elevated concentrations of these lipids.

Zanthoxylum bungeanum belongs to the Zanthoxylum Linn. genus of the Rutaceae family. It is distributed across Asia, America, Africa, and Oceania, with approximately 250 species worldwide, including 45 species and 13 varieties in China. Zanthoxylum bungeanum leaf (ZBL), a by-product of Zanthoxylum bungeanum harvesting, are rich in flavonoids and polyphenols and usually discarded after harvesting, is rich in flavonoids, volatile oils, and polyphenols with antioxidant, anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory, anti-bacterial, and anthelmintic properties (Wu et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2021; Agnihotri et al., 2023). Plant-based polyphenols and flavonoids, which are generally harmless and residue-free, possess anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities, and can improve lipid metabolism (Alagawany et al., 2016; Farahat et al., 2017; Untea et al., 2020). However, little is known about the effect of ZBL on the lipid composition of eggs. Based on these considerations, we investigated the effects of different amounts of dietary ZBL on productive performance, egg quality, flavor, and yolk lipid composition.

Experimental design, diets, and management

In total, 640 healthy Lohmann Pink laying hens (age, 58 weeks) were randomly assigned to four treatment groups. Each treatment group was performed in eight replicates with 20 hens per replicate (two hens per cage, ten cages adjacent to each other in the same row constitute a repetition). The groups were as follows: control (CON), 1 % ZBL, 2 % ZBL, or 3 % ZBL. The diets were formulated by substituting ZBL for wheat bran (Table 2). All the feeds were in mash form and were mixed at the feed factory. The trial period was 8 weeks. The hens were provided with ad libitum access to feed and were housed in an open-ventilated layer house equipped with A-shaped four-tier stainless-steel cages. During the treatment period, the hens were maintained at a constant temperature of 20-22°C under a 16:8-h light: dark cycle. The hens were provided with adequate drinking water.

Production performance and sample collection

Weight and number of all eggs were recorded daily for calculating hen-day egg production (total number of eggs produced / (number of hens x number of days) × 100). Feed intake and egg mass data were collected weekly to determine the average daily feed intake (ADFI) and the feed conversion ratio (FCR). At end of the experiment, the serum was obtained by centrifuging the wing vein blood samples at 3,000 × g, and then the obtained was immediately stored at −80°C. On the day of the end of experiment, to maintain the freshness of the eggs, 32 eggs were collected from each treatment group (four eggs from each repetition) and egg quality analysis was completed. Additionally, 32 eggs were collected from each treatment group (four eggs from each repetition) for sensory evaluation. In addition, 24 eggs were collected from each treatment group (three eggs from each repetition) and stored in the refrigerator at 4 ℃ for the measurement of the electronic tongue the next day. When determining the quality of eggs, separate intact egg yolk samples were respectively placed in sample tubes and stored in −80 ℃ to measure lipidomic.

Nutrient analysis

To determine the dry matter content, ZBL was dried in an oven at 135 °C for 2 h (AOAC method 930.15). The crude protein (CP) concentration was determined in accordance with AOAC method 990.03 using an elemental microanalyzer (CHNS-932; Leco Corporation, St. Joseph, MI, USA). The Ca and P contents of the diets and ZBL were determined by inductively-coupled plasma spectroscopy (AOAC method 985.01) after wet-ash sample digestion (AOAC method 975.03). The neutral detergent fiber content of amylase-treated ZBL was determined as described in AOAC method 2002.04. The acid detergent fiber content of ZBL was determined in accordance with AOAC method 973.18. The amino acid concentrations in the ZBL was determined following 24-hour liquid hydrolysis at 110°C in 6 mol HCl. The amino acids were then analysed using Waters' AccQ-Tag Ultra chemistry on a Waters Acquity UPLC.

Serum biochemistry

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), urea, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), albumin (ALB), cholesterol (CHO), triglycerides (TG), and total protein (TP) were measured using a fully automated serum biochemical analyzer (HITACH-7020, Hitachi, Japan).

Egg quality analysis

The eggshell color, Haugh units, albumen height, and yolk color were determined using an automatic egg quality analyzer (EMT-5200; Robotmation Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). After separating the albumen, the yolk weight was determined using an electronic scale. The strength of the eggshell was determined using an eggshell strength tester (ETG-1601A; Robotmation Co., Ltd. Tokyo, Japan). The thickness at the blunt end, middle, and tip of the eggshell was measured using a micrometer and then averaged. The egg shape index (transverse and longitudinal measurements) was calculated using an electronic Vernier caliper (CR-2032; Mahr GmbH, Göttingen, Germany).

Sensory evaluation of eggs

The eggs were boiled at 95-100°C for 10 min, cooled to room temperature, peeled, and cut into four equal parts containing both egg albumen and yolk. The color, aroma, taste, texture, and overall acceptance of the egg albumen and yolk were separately evaluated by 15 trained panelists. Three training sessions were conducted before the formal sensory evaluation. Each sensory evaluation included one sample from the CON and three ZBL-treated groups. The panelists evaluated the standard sample first and then the ZBL-treated samples labeled with random numbers using a nine-point hedonic scale, where 1 = dislike extremely, 2 = dislike very much; 3 = dislike moderately, 4 = dislike slightly, 5 = neither like nor dislike, 6 = like slightly, 7 = like moderately, 8 = like very much, and 9 = like extremely.

Electronic tongue (E-tongue) analysis of egg yolks

The egg samples were boiled at 95-100°C for 10 min. Afterward, the yolks were separated and homogenized in 100 ml of distilled water. Then, the upper layer of the mixture was extracted by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 min. The various flavors of the supernatant were determined using an E-tongue device (ASTREE; Alpha MOS, Toulouse, France). The different flavors were determined by six sensors each (AHS, sourness; PKS, richness; CTS, saltiness; NMS, umami; CPS, astringency; ANS, sweetness; and SCS, bitterness).

Yolk lipidomic analysis

Based on data on production performance, egg quality, sensory evaluation, and electronic tongue, we analyzed only the egg yolk lipidomic of the CON and the 3 % ZBL group. For each treatment, eight egg yolks of similar weight were selected for lipidomic determination. The samples were pre-treated to remove proteins and impurities, and extract lipid metabolites. Liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry was conducted in positive and negative modes, and the obtained data (peak extraction, alignment, identification, retention times, peak areas, mass-charge ratios, and identification information) were analyzed with LipidSearch™ Software (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA USA).

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SAS 9.3 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). The egg quality and sensory evaluation data were normally distributed. The significance of differences among the groups was determined by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with linear and quadratic regression analyses. Differences between pairs of means were analyzed with the Duncan multiple range test. The results are expressed as the mean and standard error of the mean. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, while 0.05 ≤ P < 0.1 indicated a trend. The differential lipid metabolites were screened by the t-test and variable influence on projection (VIP) score (orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis [OPLS-DA]). Correlation, cluster, chemical bonding, and saturation analyses were used to assess the biological data of the differential lipids.

Results

Laying rate, egg quality, sensory evaluation, and E-tongue analysis

As demonstrated in Table 3 and Fig. 1, dietary 3 % ZBL increased the egg yolk color (P < 0.05), but had no significant impact on other metrics of egg quality and laying rate (P > 0.05, Table 3). Egg production tends to increased linearly as the amount of ZBL added increased (P = 0.074). Dietary 1 % ZBL and 3 % ZBL increased the ALB concentration of serum compared with the CON group and 2 % ZBL group (P < 0.05). The E-tongue and sensory evaluation results (Fig. 2A, B, Table 5) revealed that 3 % ZBL increased sourness, richness, saltiness, bitterness, and overall acceptance of egg (P < 0.05). Based on these results, lipidomic analyses were limited to the 3 % ZBL and CON groups.

Lipidomic profiles of egg yolk lipids

As demonstrated in Fig. 3A, 5 classes and 33 subclasses of 689 lipid species consisting of glycerophospholipids (GPs), glycol esters (GLs), sphingolipids (SPs), steroidolipids (STs), fatty acyls (FAs), TGs, PE, PC, diglycerides (DGs), SM, ceramides (Cer), lysophosphatidylcholines (LPCs), and methyl phosphatidylcholine (MePC) were detected in positive ionization mode. TGs accounted for the largest proportion of lipid species. Signals in negative ionization mode mainly belonged to 4 classes and 33 subclasses of 844 lipid species, including GPs, SPs, GLs, FAs, cardiolipin (CL), PE, PC, Cer, dimethyl phosphatidylethanolamine (DMPE), and phosphatidylserine (PS) (Fig. 3B). Of these, CL was the most abundant lipid species. The 33 subclasses consisted of 1533 lipid species. The 903 principal lipids had carbon chains varying in length from 32 to 44 carbon atoms (Fig. 3C). Other components included 1218 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), 959 saturated fatty acids, 856 monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs), and 388 odd-numbered fatty acids (Fig. 3D).

Screening of significantly different lipids

Volcano plots were generated to illustrate the differential lipids (P < 0.05; VIP_pred OPLS-DA > 1) between the two groups (Fig. 5A). In total, 392 differential lipid species (226 up-regulated; 166 down-regulated) were identified in the 3 % ZBL group as compared to the CON group. The top 10 up-regulated lipid species were Cer (t18:1/20:3), Cer (t18:0/18:2), Cer (t18:0/20:4), Cer (t18:0/20:3), TG (4:0/10:2/16:0), TG (4:0/8:0/16:0), TG (4:0/10:3/16:0), PE (8:1e/17:1), ZyE (0:0), and PS (20:2e/22:4), while the top 10 down-regulated lipid species were CL (22:3/18:0/20:0/20:4), PE (19:0/20:4), PC (19:0/18:2), CL (20:4/18:0/18:1/24:1), PE (19:1/18:0), Ld DMPE (20:2), LPE (19:0), MLCL (19:0/15:1/18:1), PC (15:0/16:1), and CL (16:0/14:1/16:0/16:0) (Table S1). The top 30 differential lipid species (VIP_OPLS-DA > 1) are listed in Fig. 5B. Furthermore, the differential unsaturated lipid species (Fig. 6 and Table S1) were compared based on the number of double bonds (Fig. S1) and carbon chain length (Fig. S2). Dietary 3 % ZBL significantly increased the relative abundances of unsaturated fatty acids (101 MUFAs and 178 PUFAs were up-regulated). Furthermore, the carbon chain of the up-regulated lipids types was composed of 18–46 carbon atoms and contained 0–7 double bonds. Collectively, these results confirm that the addition of 3 % ZBL significantly altered the lipid species of egg yolks.

Overall, 132 distinct lipids were associated with 22 metabolic pathways (Fig. 7A), which included “lipid metabolism”, “endocrine and metabolic disease”, “cardiovascular disease”, “nervous system”, “endocrine system”, “signal transduction”, and “cell growth and death”. Topology analysis in reference to the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) found that these lipids were associated with seven primary metabolic pathways: namely, “sphingolipid metabolism”, “glycerophospholipid metabolism”, “linoleic acid metabolism”, “alpha-linolenic acid metabolism”, “glycerolipid metabolism”, and “grachidonic acid metabolism” (Fig. 7B).

Discussion

The ZBL samples used in this study had crude protein, crude fiber, total flavonoid, and total polyphenol contents of 14.23 %, 13.44 %, 53.31 g/kg, and 45.37 g/kg, respectively. These components of the majority of plant species have been linked to physiological functions in animals (Ma et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2021). The present study focused on the effects of different levels of dietary ZBL on productive performance,egg quality, flavor, and lipid composition of yolk.

The laying rate determines the production efficiency of laying hens. This study demonstrated that dietary supplementation with ZBL did not compromise the laying rate in laying hens. However, it should be noted that as the amount of ZBL added increases, there was a linear upward trend in laying rate. From this, we can infer that ZBL may have a certain potential role in promoting egg production in hens. Egg quality is an important indicator that is tested in the factory. The average weight of eggs in this study ranged from 59.07 to 60.63 g, which was comparable to the same breed of laying hens (Zhu et al., 2020). The mean Haugh units were all greater than 84, which was consistent with the results of a previous study (Wen et al., 2019). In this study, dietary 2 % and 3 % ZBL improved yolk color without reducing egg quality. The high levels of pigmenting carotenoids in various herbs are responsible for the coloration of egg yolks (Hammershøj and Johansen, 2016). Despite the absence of data pertaining to the pigment content, the ZBL utilized in this study was freshly collected and subsequently dried to preserve the most of pigment content (Wang et al., 2023). Furthermore, as demonstrated in earlier research, consumers have a stronger preference for egg yolks with a darker hue (Bovšková et al., 2014). Therefore, eggs produced with the addition of 2 % ZBL and 3 % ZBL may be even more preferred by consumers. Moreover, the cost of ZBL encompasses solely the expenses incurred during collection and drying, excluding the cost of the raw materials themselves, which are typically discarded. Therefore, dietary supplementation with cost-effective ZBL in laying hens’ diets not only enhanced yolk color but also reduces feed costs.

Egg texture and flavor are also significant indicators of quality. Sensory assessment is a scientific discipline that involves the evocation, measurement, analysis and interpretation of responses to stimuli perceived through the senses of sight, smell, touch, taste, and hearing (Margeta et al., 2019). In this study, the eggs were subjected to a rigorous evaluation process that involved the training of testers to assess various sensory attributes, including color, aroma, taste, and texture. In a similar manner, the utilization of obtainable lipids (palm butter, grape seed, and flax seed), n-3 PUFAs (flax seed and marine algae), and rosemary did not alter the organoleptic evaluation of eggs or compromise taste (Parpinello et al., 2006). However, participants exhibited higher acceptance of eggs from the 3 % ZBL group during the tasting sessions.

The E-tongue devise employs a range of sensors to emulate the human sense of taste, thereby minimizing any inaccuracy arising from subjective evaluation (Ha et al., 2015). The E-tongue devise utilized in this study possesses the capacity to discern six distinct taste sensations (sourness, richness, saltiness, umami, astringency, sweetness, and bitterness). The results revealed that 3 % ZBL had strengthened sourness, richness, saltiness, and bitterness. The improvement in flavor may explain the higher acceptance of eggs from the 3 % ZBL-supplemented diet group among participants. A previous study utilizing an E-tongue devise reported that egg flavor differs among breeds of chickens (Dong et al., 2021). Furthermore, egg flavor is influenced by the polyphenol content (Tang et al., 2025). The inclusion of ingredients containing polyphenols and flavonoids in the feed of laying hens has been demonstrated to substantially enhance antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacities (Goliomytis et al., 2019; Abd El-Hack et al., 2023). The oxidation and accelerated decomposition of fats and proteins of eggs processed at elevated temperatures produces odorous volatiles. However, the presence of polyphenols and flavonoids, which are natural antioxidants, has been shown to mitigate odor production (Tang et al., 2025). Therefore, the enhanced flavor of eggs in the ZBL group was attributed to the polyphenols and flavonoids of ZBL (Agnihotri et al., 2023).

However, this study did not directly investigate the effect of ZBL flavonoids on egg flavor, but rather further explored egg yolk lipidomic. After the above analysis, we found that the egg yolks in the 3 % ZBL group were darker in color and had more significant differences in sensory evaluation and electronic tongue evaluation compared to the control group. Therefore, we only used 3 % ZBL to analyze egg yolk lipidomic.

Layer diets high in polyphenols obtained from extra virgin olive oil improved the fatty acid quality of egg yolks and simultaneously reduce yolk cholesterol (Laudadio et al., 2015). Moreover, dietary supplementation of laying hens with flavonoids (hesperidin and quercetin) reduced yolk cholesterol and increased yolk protein content. Flavonoid supplementation of the chicken diet has been reported to positively alter to the fatty acid profile of meat and eggs, while reducing the cholesterol and TG contents (Kamboh et al., 2019). These findings suggest that dietary flavonoids and polyphenols influence lipid metabolism in laying hens. Similarly, lipidomics analysis was employed in the present study to explore the effect of 3 % ZBL on the lipid composition of egg yolks. In total, 1,533 lipid species in 33 subclasses were detected, including 392 differential lipid species. PCA can provide intuitive explanations of complex datasets and reveal groupings, trends, and outliers within the dataset (Guo et al., 2022). OPLS-DA is a supervised model that uses partial least squares regression to model the relationship among expressions, quantities, and sample categories (Lu et al., 2021). The results of both PCA and OPLS-DA revealed a large difference in lipid composition between the CON and 3 % ZBL groups. The classification parameters R2Y and Q2 of the positive and negative groups were 0.998/0.848 and 0.998/0.891, respectively, confirming that the OPLS-DA model is reliable with a good fit and predictability.

As compared to the control group, the top 10 up-regulated lipid species of the 3 % ZBL group were Cer (t18:1/20:3), Cer (t18:0/18:2), Cer (t18:0/20:4), Cer (t18:0/20:3), TG (4:0/10:2/16:0), TG (4:0/8:0/16:0), TG (4:0/10:3/16:0), PE (8:1e/17:1), ZyE (0:0), and PS (20:2e/22:4). The Cer species are the main lipid components of lamellar sheets present in the cellular interstices of the stratum corneum and have the ability to maintain the skin barrier, moisturize, prevent aging, and whiten the skin (Coderch et al., 2003). TG is the major form of fatty acid storage and transport in the cell and plasma. Overnutrition can lead to accumulation of TGs and cardiovascular disease (Nordestgaard and Varbo, 2014; Alves-Bezerra and Cohen, 2017). PE in egg yolk has high free radical scavenging activity due to the high unsaturated fatty acid content (N. Sun et al., 2019). Moreover, dietary 3 % ZBL up-regulated the contents of MUFAs and PUFAs in egg yolks (Figs. S1–3, Table S1). MUFAs and PUFAs can protect human health and promote development, while reducing the risks of cardiovascular diseases, cancers, Alzheimer's disease, skin diseases, asthma, allergic diseases, and rheumatoid arthritis (Öz et al., 2022). Furthermore, alterations in yolk lipid composition influenced the textural properties of eggs. Specifically, an increase in lipid content contributes to enhanced creaminess, while elevated levels of unsaturated fatty acids improved sensory attributes in consumer evaluations(Aro et al., 2011; Gao et al., 2022). Dietary supplementation with vitamin E, an antioxidant, enhances egg textural quality by inhibiting lipid oxidation (Meluzzi et al., 2000). ZBL are also rich in diverse antioxidant compounds. The observed modifications in lipid composition may primarily account for the altered flavor profile of eggs from the 3 % ZBL group.

Metabolic pathways consist of a series of biochemical reactions that involve a variety of substances and multiple steps (Guo et al., 2022). Dietary 3 % ZBL mainly affected sphingolipid and glycerophospholipid metabolism. Sphingolipids have been demonstrated to regulate signaling pathways associated with cell proliferation and growth (Hannun and Obeid, 2018). A previous study has shown that polyphenols in food have disease-preventive effects mediated through sphingolipids (Dei Cas and Ghidoni, 2018). Flavonoids affect sphingolipid metabolism and reduce elevated ceramide levels in the aged liver (Babenko and Shakhova, 2008). The same results were obtained in this study by dietary ZBL enriched with polyphenols and flavonoids. In our study, sphingolipid metabolism was mainly related to up-regulation of SM (d18:1/20:0), Cer (d18:1/24:0), SM (d18:1/12:0), Cer (d18:1/20:0), Cer (d18:1/22:0), Cer (d18:1/18:0), Cer (d18:1/26:0), and Cer (d18:1/25:0) (Table S2). Glycerophospholipids are the most abundant class of phospholipids in the membranes of eukaryotic cells and main components of bile and membrane surface active substances (Tocher, 1995). It has been hypothesized that Tartary buckwheat extract could play a role in preventing alcoholic liver disease by modulating hepatic glycerophospholipid metabolism (Cao et al., 2022). In our study, glycerophospholipid metabolism was mainly related to up-regulation of PC (22:5/22:6) and down-regulation of LPC (20:2), PC (22:0/20:4), PC (15:0/16:1), and PC (14:0/20:4) (Table S2).

A correlation analysis was performed between the top 10 metabolites with the most significant differences between the 3 % ZBL group and the control group, as well as serum biochemistry and electronic tongue. The results of this analysis are shown in Fig. 8. We can see that different lipids in eggs mainly affect the saltiness, sourness, and bitterness of eggs. The most critical lipids are Cer (t18:0/20:3), Cer (t18:1/20:3), and PS (20:2e/22:4)

Following the conduction of a correlation analysis between differential metabolites and serum biochemical and e-tongue indicators, it was determined that Cer (t18:0/20:3), Cer (t18:1/20:3), and PS (20:2e/22:4) were the three most critical differential lipids affecting the flavor of eggs. Following the integration of KEGG analysis, it was determined that Cer (t18:0/20:3) and Cer (t18:1/20:3) represent the pivotal lipids that influence on the egg's color and flavor when 3 % ZBL were added into the diet.

Conclusions

In the present study, dietary ZBL increased the yolk color without reducing egg quality and laying rate. Egg yolks with ZBL had a stronger flavor as measured with an E-tongue device. Sensory evaluation revealed significantly higher consumer acceptance of eggs from the 3 % ZBL group. Changes to the lipid profile of egg yolks were observed by the addition of 3 % ZBL, and a total of 392 differential lipids were identified. MUFAs and PUFAs were up-regulated. Sphingolipid and glycerophospholipid metabolism were the key metabolic pathways. Cer was the key metabolite.

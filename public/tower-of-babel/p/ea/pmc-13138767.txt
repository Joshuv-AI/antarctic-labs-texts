Evaluation of mycobacterial microscopy and culture results from clinical samples: A five-year analysis
Authors: Ahmet BALIKÇI, Mehmet ERBAKAN, Aylin BABALIK
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Evaluation of mycobacterial microscopy and culture results from clinical samples: A five-year analysis Introduction Ehrlich-Ziehl-Neelsen (EZN) staining and culture methods are often used to diagnose tuberculosis. This study aimed to determine the acidfast bacteria (AFS) positivity rates in various clinical samples sent to our laboratory over five years and the growth and resistance rates in two different (solid and liquid) cultures and compare them with the data from Türkiye and the world. Materials and Methods A total of 62.456 clinic samples were accepted in the microbiology laboratory between 2019 and 2024. The mycobacterial culture was performed by searching for acid-resistant bacilli microscopically and parallel inoculation media [solid Löwenstein-Jensen (L-J) and MGIT 960 liquid]. Those growing in the MGIT 960 system were identified using BD MGIT TBC Identification test kits that detect the MPT64 antigen. AFS and MPT64 antigenpositive samples were identified as Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex (MTBC) while AFS-positive samples and MPT64 antigen-negative results were classified as non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM). Drug susceptibility testing was performed with the BACTEC MGIT 960 SIRE kit. Susceptibility to NTM samples was not performed. Results Out of a total of 120.829 samples, 95.101 were lung samples and 25.728 were extrapulmonary samples. AFS positivity was detected in 2961 (2.4%) samples. MTBC grew in 6854 (5.6%) samples, and NTM grew in 1506 (1.24%) samples. Contamination was detected in 7171 (5.9%) media. Two thousand one hundred and sixty-nine susceptibility tests were performed. Considering antibiotic resistance rates, isoniazid resistance was detected in 154 (7%), rifampicin resistance in 140 (6.4%), ethambutol resistance in 18 (0.8%), and streptomycin resistance in 120 (0.5%) samples. All four-drug resistance was observed in 91 (4.1%) samples. AFP positivity and resistance rates for rifampicin have decreased significantly, while there have been no significant changes in NTM rates over the years. Conclusion When our data was determined, the sensitivity of microscopy was low. It is understood that mycobacterial culture and microscopy must be evaluated together to exclude tuberculosis infection. The high mycobacterial culture positivity rate, which is 5.6%, is due to the high number of follow-up patients and new referrals. It is seen that the change in sensitivity rates is due to the period of the COVID-19 epidemic, and it is similar to World Health Organization (WHO) data. Keywords: Tuberculosis, Ehrlich-Ziehl-Neelsen, auramin-rodamin, BACTEC MGIT 960

INTRODUCTION

Tuberculosis (TB) is a disease that affects 41 million people annually in the world, kills nearly two million people, and continues to be an essential problem in society with an increasing rate of multidrug-resistant TB (MDR-TB) (1). According to the World Health Organization (WHO) Global Tuberculosis 2022 report, the incidence of tuberculosis in Türkiye in 2022 was reported as 14 per 100.000 and 185 per 100.000 worldwide. The total number of cases in Türkiye is 12.000, and 155 are resistant tuberculosis cases. The mortality rate has been reported as 42 per 100.000 when individuals with HIV infection are omitted (2).

Microscopic examination with Ehrlich-Ziehl Neelsen (EZN) staining is used to diagnose tuberculosis. Fluorescence staining with auramine-rhodamine is widely used in centers that study many samples. Recently, molecular methods have come to the fore. Culture, on the other hand, is a standard method that should be done to give results with fewer bacilli (10-100 bacilli/mL) (3). Both specieslevel identification and antimicrobial susceptibility tests can be performed using the culture method. It is recommended that culture be done on solid Löwenstein-Jensen (L-J) and liquid medium (Middlebrook). Liquid media provide standardized results in a shorter time (4). On the other hand, molecular methods have been rapidly introduced recently, but it is only possible to perform them in some laboratories due to the high cost and training (5,6).

This study aimed to determine the results of acidresistant bacteria (AFS), culture growth, and susceptibility in various clinical samples sent to our hospital’s laboratory for five years and compare them with the data of the world and our country.

MATERIALS and METHODS

The study included 62.456 clinical samples (sputum, tracheal aspirate, bronchoalveolar lavage, sterile body fluid, tissue, urine, etc.) accepted in the microbiology laboratory between January 2019 and January 2024.

The classical decontamination concentration method was applied to the samples taken from the patients. Microscopically, the results of the samples grown by AFS and culture cultivation were evaluated.

Microscopic examination

After being evaluated with a fluorescent dye (auramine-rhodamin), positive ones were confirmed by EZN staining. The preparations were classified as 1+ (1-9 bacteria in 100 areas), 2+ (1-9 bacteria in 10 regions), and 3+ (1-9 bacteria in each location) (7).

Culture

All samples were inoculated simultaneously on L-J and MGIT liquid medium. L-J media were evaluated for reproduction twice in the first week and once weekly for the next eight weeks. Microscopic examination was performed with the EZN staining method from liquid media grown in an MGIT device or samples grown in L-J to evaluate whether there was genuine positivity or contamination in mycobacteria.

Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex (MTBC) was defined by standard methods, colony shape, presence of cord factor, and immunochromatic method (MGIT card test) in L-J medium (8). Those who grew in the MGIT 960 system were accepted as BD MGIT TBC Identification Test (Becton Dickinson, USA) kits. Positive samples within the 15 minutes specified in the kit procedure were accepted as MTBC, and negative results were accepted as non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM).

Sensitivity

Antimicrobial susceptibility of M. tuberculosis isolated samples was evaluated with the MGIT 960 SIRE kit (Becton Dickinson, USA) SIRE kit. In quality control, M. tuberculosis ATCC 27294 (H37Rv) isolate, known to be sensitive to four drugs, was used. It was made using the BACTEC MGIT 960 system. Isolates with isoniazid (INH) and rifampicin (RIF) resistance were accepted as MDR-TB.

Statistical Analysis

Qualitative data are indicated as percentages. Other data were calculated using the chi-square test in the SPSS 17 program. According to this, resistance rates for rifampicin have decreased significantly over the years (p= 0.040). In the sub-analyzes, it was seen that the difference was due to the difference between 2019-2023 (p= 0.009) and 2020-2023 (p= 0.003).

AFS positivity rates decreased significantly over the years (p= 0.0001). In the sub-analyzes, it was seen that the difference was due to the difference between 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023 (p= 0.001; 0.001; 0.001). There is a significant difference between the years regarding the total sample produced. The years from which this difference originated were analyzed, and the difference originated between 2019 and 2020. There has been no significant change in NTM rates over the years (p= 0.001).

DISCUSSION

Tuberculosis is one of the most important bacterial diseases that threaten human health. Early diagnosis is instrumental in preventing the spread of the disease by active tuberculosis cases.

Samples for the diagnosis of tuberculosis are mostly lung samples, including sputum and bronchoalveolar lavage. However, the most common extrapulmonary sample is pleural fluid (Table 1). When the examples were examined in our study, of the 62.456 samples, 60.923 (97.5%) were lung samples and 1533 (2.5%) were extrapulmonary samples.

For diagnosis: Although new methods (especially molecular) are being developed, microscopic examination continues to be of diagnostic value, especially in most countries with insufficient resources. Microscopic sputum thinning is easy and inexpensive, but its sensitivity is low, and the bacillus detection rate varies between 50-80% (9). Many factors that affect sensitivity such as the decontamination process, dye method, spreading method, and examiner experience can be given as examples (10).

In our study, culture was used as the standard method, and the positivity rate of AFS staining was 43%. In addition to the previously mentioned reasons, such as decontamination and investigator experience, preanalytical errors in samples from many hospitals are effective in excluding the diagnosis of tuberculosis, especially from patients who do not have specific symptoms. In our laboratory, a sample examination was started with fluorescent staining in the last five years, and those who were positive for fluorescent staining were confirmed by staining with EZN.

When analyzed by year, it was 63% in 2019, 52% in 2020, 41% in 2021, 41% in 2022, and 26% in 2023.

As can be seen in the statistical analysis, this significant decrease is especially due to the increase in the number of extrapulmonary samples.

Annam et al. have found microscopic positivity rate as 44.11% with EZN and 81% with fluorescence staining (11). Similarly, Özkütük et al. have found the sensitivity and specificity of microscopic examination as 56.7% and 98.7%, 63.2% and 98.6% for lung samples, and 41.8% and 99% for extrapulmonary samples (12). Habit et al. have found microscopy sensitivity and specificity to be 50% and 99.7%, respectively (13).

In evaluating the clinical features and epidemiology of tuberculosis, it is necessary to culture using two different media, preferably one liquid and one solid, to detect and identify the agent and perform drug susceptibility tests. Tests conducted with these classical methods give results in approximately 45-60 days (7). In their study with 6814 samples, Alışkan et al. have found culture positivity as 5.4% (13). In the study of Özbey et al. including 2.5 years of data, culture positivity has been found to be 7.44% with the BACTEC MGIT 960 system (14). In our study, mycobacterium culture positivity was 5.6% in total.

According to the National Tuberculosis Diagnostic Guidelines (UTTR), contamination rates should be at least 5-10% (7). Our contamination rates were found to be within acceptable limits of 5.9%. While our contamination rates have not varied much over the years, they increased, especially in 2019-2020, when we were in the central laboratory and the samples were transported. Regular monitoring of contamination rates by each institution and taking precautions to detect changes are essential parameters for the continuity of laboratory quality.

Strains that show resistance to INH and RIF are defined as multidrug-resistant (MDR). Therefore, the right treatment plan and prevention of resistance spread are critical.

According to WHO’s Global Tuberculosis 2023 report, 410.000 MDR cases were detected in the world in 2022, and it was observed that there was an increase again after the decrease in 2015-2019 and 2020. In the same report, the rate of new MDR cases for Türkiye in 2022 was 2.6% (2).

In the Tuberculosis War in Türkiye, 2020 report published by the Ministry of Health, the most common resistance rates in our country in 2018 were INH (6.0%) and ST (4.7%) resistance, INH+SM resistance (2.8%) as a dual drug, and INH+RIF+SM (1.1%) resistance as a triple-drug. The total resistance rate was 19.2% in patients who underwent IDT. The MDR-TB ratio was found to be 2.6%, and the firstchoice anti-TB drug INH was the first choice with the highest resistance rate (15).

In our study, resistance rates in breeding isolates were 7.1% for INH, 6.4% for RIF, 5.5% for SM, and 0.8% for ETM. The MDR-TB ratio was found to be 4.1%. Among anti-TB drugs, the highest resistance rate was found in INH, and the MDR-TB ratio was found to be higher than the average in Türkiye. This situation is due to the large number of patients coming from countries where resistant tuberculosis is expected, who have come to our hospital in recent years because our hospital is a chest disease hospital. In our study, 1564 (72.1%) of 2169 cases were sensitive to all drugs. Studies were carried out in different regions of our country between 2002 and 2017. When these studies were examined in terms of susceptibility to all drugs, the highest rate was found to be 88.3% in the survey conducted by Özmen et al. in Erzurum in 2017, and the lowest rate was 59.2% in the study conducted by Orhan et al. in Gaziantep in 2002 (12, 16,17). These rates differ in various regions of our country due to socioeconomic status and level of development. In addition, reasons such as method differences and patient population may cause different results.

When our data for these five years, including various applications, are examined, it is seen that microscopy sensitivity is low in all years, and evaluation should be performed together with mycobacterial culture to exclude tuberculosis infection. All specimens, especially extrapulmonary specimens that reduce microscopy sensitivity, should be supported by molecular methods, especially in the initial diagnosis, aligning with WHO recommendations. Our laboratory has worked intensively on molecular techniques for the last two years.

The culture positivity rate of 5.6% is due to the high number of follow-up patients admitted with new admissions, especially in our hospital. It is seen that the change in sensitivity rates is in line with the WHO data due to the period of the COVID-19 epidemic. It is essential to regularly monitor data such as microscopy sensitivity, culture positivity rates, resistance rates, and contamination values of tuberculosis laboratories and to evaluate their compliance with laboratory quality standards and surveillance studies.

Assessment of Efficacy of Erector Spinae Plane Block Versus Subcostal Transversus Abdominis Plane Block for Postoperative Pain Management in Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy Under General Anaesthesia: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Deepak Vijaykumar Kadlimatti, Mohammed Salim Iqbal, Syed Adeeba Nousheen, S. S. Harsoor, Peddapelli Abhishekh Vijaymohan, K. Harshitha, Ankitha Jagadeesh Hiremath
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LCY), although a routine minimal invasive surgery, still leads to considerable pain post-operatively, especially in first 24 hours. Regional anaesthesia like erector spinae plane (ESP) and subcostal TAP (STAP) blocks are used to reduce this pain, but limited data exists comparing them directly in LCY patients.Materials and Methods:A total of 100 American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) I/II patients (aged 18–75) undergoing LCY under general anaesthesia were randomly divided into ESP and STAP groups (n = 50 each). Blocks were given pre-op under ultrasound guidance. Pain was recorded using Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for 24 hrs. Rescue analgesia was given if VAS >3.Results:ESP group had significantly longer analgesia (671.39 ± 41.77 min) compared to STAP (535.50 ± 230.28 min), with P < 0.001. No adverse effects seen in either group.Conclusion:The ESP block showed more effective and longer pain relief than STAP block in LCY patients. Hence, ESP may be preferred for better post-op pain control in similar settings.

INTRODUCTION

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LCY) often causes considerable post-op pain due to trocar wounds, visceral handling and referred shoulder pain.[1] While opioids were used earlier, their side effects limit recovery.[2] So regional techniques like subcostal TAP (STAP) and erector spinae plane (ESP) blocks are now more used.[3] STAP mainly covers T6 and gives good supraumbilical pain relief, while ESP spreads widely and works for both somatic and visceral pain.[4567] But not many direct comparisons exist, especially for LCY. This study compares bilateral STAP and ESP blocks with bupivacaine-dexamethasone, focusing on demographic matching and pain duration outcomes post-LCY.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This randomized study included 100 American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) I-II patients (aged 18–75) scheduled for elective LCY under general anesthesia, conducted from June to October 2023 after ethical clearance (EC327) and CTRI registration (CTRI/2023/07/054768). Patients with coagulopathy, chronic analgesic use, diabetes, or psychological illness were excluded. Patients were randomly assigned into two groups (n = 50)-Group STAP and Group ESP. Both blocks were given preoperatively using ultrasound guidance with 20 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine plus 4 mg dexamethasone on each side. For STAP the probe was placed subcostally, for ESP it was aligned over T7 to target the plane deep to erector spinae muscle.

Standard GA was induced with propofol (2 mg/kg), atracurium (0.5 mg/kg) and fentanyl (2 μg/kg), followed by intubation and maintenance with isoflurane (1–2 MAC). Intra-abdominal pressure was kept at 12–14 mmHg. HR, MAP and SpO2 were monitored every 5 min. Post-op pain was assessed using VAS at regular 2-hour intervals till 24 hours. Rescue analgesia (IV paracetamol 15 mg/kg) was given when VAS ≥3. Primary outcome was time to first analgesic request; complications were also noted.

Sample size was calculated using: n = (Zα + Zβ)2 × σ²/d², where σ = 5.22, d = 2.2, Zα = 1.96, Zβ = 0.84.[8]

RESULTS

Demographic and surgical parameters were compared between groups. The ESP group had a significantly lower mean age (39.24 ± 12.78 years) than the STAP group (44.92 ± 13.65 years; P = 0.034). Mean weight was also significantly lower in the ESP group (62.26 ± 7.12 kg) compared to STAP (72.18 ± 12.48 kg; P < 0.001). No significant differences were noted in height (P = 0.061) or BMI (P = 0.134). Pneumoperitoneum time and surgical duration were longer in the STAP group, but these differences were not statistically significant (P = 0.116 and 0.235, respectively).

Analgesic efficacy was notably better in the ESP group. Mean duration of analgesia was significantly longer in ESP (11.19 ± 0.70 hours/671.39 ± 41.77 min) versus STAP (8.93 ± 3.84 hours/535.50 ± 230.28 min), with P < 0.001 in both cases. This indicates superior and more consistent pain control with ESP block postoperatively.

DISCUSSION

This study compared the analgesic efficacy of ESP and STAP blocks in patients undergoing LCY. A key observation was the significantly higher age and weight in the STAP group (P = 0.034 and < 0.001, respectively), while height and BMI were comparable between groups [Table 1]. Despite these differences, pain outcomes favored the ESP group. ESP block provided significantly longer pain relief (11.19 ± 0.70 hour vs. 8.93 ± 3.84 hour) and higher analgesic duration in minutes (671.39 ± 41.77 vs. 535.50 ± 230.28), suggesting its superiority in postoperative pain control [Table 2]. Similar findings were seen in earlier studies, where ESP block showed extended time to rescue analgesia and reduced opioid needs.[910]

The ESP block, due to its deeper fascial injection and multi-dermatomal spread, offers both somatic and visceral analgesia, making it effective in abdominal procedures like LCY.[1112] In our results, ESP patients reported lower Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores and required fewer rescue analgesics, echoing results from Altıparmak et al.[13] The pain progression trend and earlier onset of analgesia were also favorable in the ESP group [Figure 1].

Adjuvant use of dexamethasone further enhanced block duration, aligning with findings from Chen et al.[14] and Deshpande et al.[15] No complications were noted in either group. Although the STAP block was effective, its shorter analgesic span and higher analgesic demand indicate ESP may be a better choice in LCY. A limitation includes exclusion of diabetic patients, which could affect generalizability. Long-term follow-up beyond 24 hours was also not included.

CONCLUSION

The erector spinae plane block under ultrasound guidance with bupivacaine and dexamethasone provided more effective and longer-lasting postoperative pain relief than the subcostal transversus abdominis plane block in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Patients who received the ESP block required fewer additional pain medications suggesting that this technique could be a better choice for improving comfort and recovery in the immediate postoperative period.

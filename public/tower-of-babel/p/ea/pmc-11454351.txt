7746 Developing An Ambulatory Endocrinology, Diabetes And Metabolism Curriculum With On-Site Fellow Involvement
Authors: Javier Ocampo Mascaro, Caitlin A White, Deepa R Nandiwada
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: J. Ocampo Mascaro: None. C.A. White: None. D.R. Nandiwada: None. Background: Traditionally, Internal Medicine (IM) residency programs have favored inpatient learning opportunities, with limited exposure to outpatient subspecialties like Endocrinology, Diabetes and Metabolism (EDM). Understanding EDM concepts is critical for IM physicians. Over the last decade, programs have been able to increase outpatient component in their curriculum. Thoughtful educational interventions that bolster exposure to EDM topics for IM residents are necessary to both reduce gaps in medical knowledge and address dwindling numbers of applicants interested in pursuing a career in EDM. On-site EDM fellow participation presents a unique opportunity to achieve these goals. Methods: An electronic needs assessment was delivered to Primary Care (PC) IM residents at a large academic medical center. The survey included questions gauging their degree of confidence in diagnosing, managing, and counseling patients with frequent EDM concerns. All questions were answered using a Likert scale. Data was collected from August to October 2023 and used to design an educational intervention by a senior EDM fellow who is physically present at the primary care IM clinic monthly. This intervention encompassed didactic lectures, direct observation of patient counseling, and on-site curbside consultation. Results: 12 of 24 PC IM residents completed the survey. Most felt somewhat or extremely comfortable evaluating patients with diabetes mellitus (DM) type 2 (100%) and hypothyroidism (83%). When evaluating adrenal insufficiency, osteoporosis, adrenal incidentalomas and primary aldosteronism (PA), 91%, 67%, 67% and 67% of residents reported being extremely or somewhat uncomfortable, respectively. Residents reported being somewhat or extremely comfortable addressing lifestyle interventions for weight loss (83%), medications for DM2 (83%), and instructing patients how to take levothyroxine (67%). Residents felt most uncomfortable counseling patients about osteoporosis treatment, adrenal incidentaloma, osteoporosis screening, fall prevention, and insulin teaching, with 83%, 67%, 58%, 58%, and 58% of residents reporting being somewhat or extremely uncomfortable, respectively. Conclusion: This needs assessment highlighted PC IM residents at our institution were uncomfortable evaluating adrenal insufficiency, osteoporosis, adrenal incidentalomas, and PA. They were also uncomfortable counseling patients on these topics as well as fall prevention and insulin teaching. We describe an intervention by an on-site EDM fellow utilizing pre-clinic conferences, direct observation, and on-site curbside consultations. As IM training pathways have increased exposure to outpatient subspecialties, it is important to thoughtfully assess and design interventions to maximize trainees' learning potential, which can be accomplished with subspecialty fellow direction.

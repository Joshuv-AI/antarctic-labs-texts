Appropriateness of oral anticoagulant therapy and associated factors among hospitalized atrial fibrillation patients with the CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 (beyond sex)
Authors: Gebretekle Gebremichael Hailesilase, Gebru Hagos Atsbha, Haylay Araya Gebrezgabiher, Abrahaley Mulu Kidane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAtrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common cardiac rhythm disturbances associated with increased risks of mortality. Oral anticoagulant (OAC) reduces AF-related strokes. However, the pattern of OAC use among AF patients is unknown in Ethiopia. This study evaluated the appropriateness of OAC therapy and associated factors among hospitalized AF patients.MethodsA retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted among AF patients hospitalized in medical ward of Adigrat general hospital. Previously validated CHA2DS2-VASc and HAS-BLED scores were used to predict risk of stroke and bleeding, respectively. Multivariate logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with OAC therapy. Data was analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences, setting p-value < 0.05 as a statistically significant.ResultsOf the total 84 AF patients, more than half (52.4%) were females and their median age was 64.50 (75–39.5). About 42.9 and 31.0% of the patients were at high risks of stroke and bleeding, respectively. Thirty seven (44.0%) of the patients received no antithrombotics and almost half (48.8%) of them were not prescribed OAC. On the other hand, 51.2% of patients received OAC containing regimen, 40.5% were prescribed with OAC monotherapy and 4.8% received aspirin monotherapy. Among patients at high risk of stroke, 36.1% of them received no antithrombotic drug. Regarding risk of bleeding, the prescription of OAC containing regimen among patients at high risk of bleeding was lower than in those at low risk of bleeding (41.9% versus 56.6%). Almost two third (61.9%) of AF patients were prescribed inappropriately, dominantly being underprescribed (44.0%). Admission time for AF was significantly associated (p < 0.001) with prescription of OAC (Adjusted Odds ratio = 6.587, CI: 2.273−19.091).ConclusionsSubstantial proportions of AF patients at high risk of stroke were inappropriately prescribed with OACs. Inappropriate prescription was characterized by underprescription. The cardiologists of the hospital should follow AF guidelines to improve prescription patterns with an ultimate goal of improving treatment outcomes.Keywords: Antithrombotic drug, OAC, Inappropriate prescription, Underprescription

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a supraventricular tachyarrhythmia with uncoordinated atrial activation and consequently ineffective atrial contraction [1]. It is the most common serious cardiac rhythm disturbances with a complex etiology [2,3]. AF affects the elder and women individuals disproportionally [[2], [3], [4]]. It is estimated that about 1% of AF patients are <60 years of age, while up to 12% of patients are 75 to 84 years of age. Moreover, more than one third of AF patients are ≥ 80 years old [1]. Studies have reported that there were about 2.3 million AF persons in 1997 in the United States, which is projected to be 5.6 million by 2050 [3,4]. The disease showed a worldwide progressive increase in prevalence between 1990 and 2010. Globally, the estimated number of persons with AF was 33.5 million in 2010 [5]. AF increases risks of mortality and morbidities due to thromboembolic complications [3]. Ischemic strokes associated with AF are more severe and carry high rate of mortality. Previous study showed that AF was associated with 5.9-fold, 5-fold and 2-fold increased risks of heart failure, stroke and mortality, respectively [2]. A study from Ethiopia reported that AF is third most commonly identified risk factor for stroke, next to hypertension and structural heart disease [6].

The main goal of therapy in the management of AF is to prevent AF-related cardiovascular events. Antithrombotic drugs prevent strokes and systemic emboli in AF patients by reducing the formation of thrombosis [1]. Antithrombotic drugs are classified as oral anticoagulant (OAC) and antiplatelet agent (e.g. aspirin and clopidogrel) [1,7]. OACs reduce AF-related ischemic strokes by two-thirds. Antithrombotics in the OAC class include vitamin-K antagonists, mainly warfarin, and non-vitamin K antagonist OAC (NOAC) such as dabigatran, rivaroxaban and apixaban. Warfarin and other vitamin-K antagonists were the first anticoagulants used in AF patients [8]. Warfarin reduces AF associated stroke by 64% [9]. However, warfarin has narrow therapeutic index, necessitating frequent monitoring [8]. The antiplatelet antithrombotic agents (notably aspirin and clopidogrel) also reduce risk of stroke in AF patients to lesser extent than vitamin K antagonists agents while risk of bleeding remains similar [9,10].

The NOACs have been introduced to the clinical practice in order to overcome these problems associated with vitamin-K antagonists. They have the advantages of a predictable effects in terms of onset and offset without need for regular anticoagulation monitoring and are safer than warfarin [8]. International trends in the treatment of AF patients indicate that NOAC use increased to 43% in 2016, whereas use of antiplatelet monotherapy decreased from 36% to 17% [10]. Studies also show that the proportions of warfarin users decreased from 77.9% in 2011 to 12.7% in 2017, while NOACs accounting for 87.3% of claims, of which apixaban and rivaroxaban accounted for 60.1% and 23.4%, respectively, by the end of 2017 [11].

In spite of the existing evidences showing the net clinical benefit of OAC therapy in AF patients, the utility pattern remains suboptimal [12]. Several studies have shown that 33.6%−58.5% of the AF patients who were at high risk of stroke did not receive OAC therapy [[13], [14], [15], [16]]. Bleeding events of any severity, a perceived ‘high risk of bleeding’ on anticoagulation, and frequent monitoring as well as dose-adjusting of vitamin-K antagonists therapy are among the most common reasons for withholding of OAC therapy [17]. Besides, increasing age increases the risk of both stroke from AF and hemorrhage from OAC therapy, which led to under-use of warfarin in older population [14].

Determining the patterns of OAC use and the associated factors in AF patients can help improve OAC medication prescription patterns in order to prevent AF-related stroke and systemic embolism. However, the current OAC medication prescription and pattern of use among AF patients is unknown in Tigrai, Ethiopia. To our best literature review, there is no published study evaluating the OAC use among patients AF in Tigrai. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to evaluate the appropriateness of oral anticoagulant therapy and associated factors among hospitalized atrial fibrillation patients with the CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 (beyond sex).

Methods

Study design, setting and period

A retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted from 01 October 2024 to 30 November 2024 among AF patients who were hospitalized in the medical ward of Adigrat general hospital from 01 September 2023 to 31 August 2024. Adigrat general hospital is found in Adigrat town, eastern zone of Tigrai, 120 km away from Mekelle (the capital city of Tigrai region) and 903 km away from Addis Ababa (the capital city of Ethiopia). The hospital has four major wards viz. pediatric ward, surgical ward, medical ward and obstetrics/gynecology ward; two major operation rooms and one central emergency clinic.

Eligibility criteria

The study included all patients with the diagnoses of AF and heart failure examined by the cardiologists of Adigrat general hospital hospitalized from 01 September 2023 to 31 August 2024. The AF was diagnosed by electrocardiogram according to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guideline [18]. The patients were then defined to have AF and heart failure if the diseases were discharge diagnoses [19]. AF patients with medical files lacking data such as drug prescriptions or discharge summaries were excluded from the study.

Study variables

The prescription of OAC and other antithrombotic drug s is the dependent variable of the study. Patient's demographic characteristics: sex, age, residence and discharge against medical advice; comorbidities, times of admission for AF, CHA2DS2-VASc score and HAS-BLED score are the independent variables of the study.

Data collection tool, procedure and management

The data was collected by hospital pharmacists of Adigrat general hospital under the supervision of the investigators. Structured data abstraction tool developed by reviewing literature [12,13,[19], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24]] was used. The tool was pretested on 5% of the sample size in Adigrat general hospital medical ward (which were excluded from the final study) to ensure its intended purpose. Patients' medical charts were used to abstract data. The data abstraction tool had five sections; the first section assessed socio demographics of patients, such as sex, age and residence. Clinical characteristics of the AF such as electrocardiogram records for AF and date of AF diagnosis were included in the second section. Questions assessing medical history (major bleeding history, excess alcohol use [≥ 8 units/week], previous stroke, previous transient ischemic attack, previous thromboembolism and previous vascular diseases) and comorbidities (congestive heart failure, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and hepatic and kidney diseases) were contained in the third section. The fourth section of the tool consisted of questions assessing laboratory values (such as alanine aminotransferase [ALT] and aspartate aminotransferase [AST]). The use of OCA and antiplatelet as well as other drugs was recorded in the last section.

Risk stratification of stroke

The risk of stroke can be estimated using risk stratification schemes that have been validated in previous studies, of which the CHADS2 and CHA2DS2-VASc scores are recommended by ESC Guidelines [8]. The acronym CHADS2 stands for Congestive heart failure, Hypertension, Age > 75, Diabetes mellitus, and prior Stroke or transient ischemic attack whereas CHA2DS2-VASc stands for Congestive heart failure, Hypertension, Age ≥ 75, Diabetes mellitus, and prior Stroke or transient ischemic attack, Vascular disease, Age 65–75, Sex category i.e. female sex. The CHA2DS2-VASc score is more effective to identify the ‘actually low-risk’ population (in a patient with a CHA2DS2-VASc score = 0) in which no thrombo-prophylaxis is required [24].

Both the ESC and the jointed American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology /Heart Rhythm Society (2014 AHA/ACC/HRS) state that AF patients with CHA2DS2-VASc score of ≥2 for males and with CHA2DS2VASc ≥ 3 for females are higher-risk groups and need OAC therapy [1,8]. However, the ESC guideline favors NOACs for AF patients with CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 with alternative being warfarin [4], whereas the 2014 AHA/ ACC/HRS AF guidelines recommend ‘no antithrombotic therapy’ for these patients [1]. For each patient, stroke risk score was calculated using the CHA2DS2-VASc score. The CHA2DS2-VASc score assigns 1 point each for heart failure, hypertension, age between 65 and 74 years, vascular disease and diabetes mellitus, and 2 points each for prior stroke or transient ischemic attack or thromboembolism, and age ≥ 75 years. Generally, patients with CHA2DS2-VASc risk score of ≥2 in men or ≥3 in women are at high risk for stroke, and benefit from OAC while patients with one clinical risk factor (i.e. CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 for men, and 2 for women) are at moderate risk and are likely to benefit from OAC therapy [18].

Risk stratification of bleeding

Stroke and bleeding risk factors overlap. While OACs reduce the risk of stroke from AF, they are associated with hemorrhagic events [25]. Thus, several bleeding risk scores were developed [22,[26], [27], [28]]. Of these, HAS-BLED score [22] is commonly used in previous studies [13,29] and is recommended by AF guidelines [1,18]. HAS-BLED is an acronym for Hypertension (1point), Abnormal renal/liver function (1 point for presence of renal or liver impairment, giving a maximum of 2 points), Stroke history (1point), Bleeding history or predisposition like anemia (1point), Labile international normalized ratio (INR) [1point]), Elderly (>65 years [1point]), Drugs and alcohol concomitantly (antiplatelet agents, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; 1 point for drugs plus 1 point for alcohol [Alcohol use ≥8 units/week], maximum 2 points) [22]. The maximum point of HAS-BLED bleeding score is 9. Abnormal kidney function was defined as the presence of dialysis-dependent kidney disease or renal transplantation [18]. Abnormal liver function was defined as the presence of chronic hepatic disease [22] or biochemical evidence of significant hepatic disorder (ALT ≥41 U/L and AST ≥33 U/L) [21].

The HAS-BLED score is a simple bleeding risk score that provides a practical tool to assess in clinical decision-making regarding antithrombotic therapy in AF patients. It is suitable for routine activity as it contains only seven risk factors all of which are either readily available from the clinical medical history or routinely tested in new AF patients [22]. In the current study, the HAS-BLED scores were calculated for each patient to categorize the risk of bleeding. Generally, a HAS-BLED score of ≥3 indicates potentially “high risk” for bleeding and may require closer observation of a patient for adverse risks, closer monitoring of INRs, or differential dose selections of OACs or antiplatelet medications [1].

Criteria for prescription appropriateness

To determine the CHA2DS2-VASc and HAS-BLED scores, the patients were diagnosed to have the comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus and heart failure only if the diseases were discharge diagnoses or confirmed at least twice in an outpatient department or when the patients were on medication for these specific diseases [19,23]. Specifically, hypertension was defined if the patient had a systolic blood pressure of ≥140 mmHg or a diastolic blood pressure of ≥90 mmHg on each of two readings or the patient was on antihypertensive drug. Similarly, diabetes mellitus was diagnosed if either the patient had a fasting glucose level of ≥126 mg/dL or was on an antidiabetic agent [30].

We used the ESC guidelines in order to determine appropriateness of the prescribed antithrombotic agents. According to the ESC guidelines recommendation, men with a CHA2DS2-VASc score of ≥1, and women with a score of ≥2 were considered to be at moderate or high risk, and likely to benefit from OAC therapy. Thus, the patients were considered as ‘appropriate’ for nonprescription only if they reported a contraindication to OAC treatment, such as previous adverse drug reaction or bleed, risk of poor drug adherence or potential drug–drug interaction. As all patients included had heart failure, no low risk patients (CHA2DS2-VASc score of 0) were present in this study. Furthermore, patients having CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥ 1 (males) or ≥ 2 (females) and prescribed antiplatelet drugs were considered as inappropriately prescribed.

A co-prescription of OAC medications with antiplatelet agents increases the risk of major hemorrhage. An OAC monotherapy is recommended in AF patients with stable coronary artery diseases but without acute coronary syndrome and/or coronary intervention in the preceding 12 months. In patients treated for acute coronary syndrome and in those receiving coronary stent, short-term triple combination therapy of OAC, clopidogrel and aspirin may be warranted [8]. So, we considered the combination of OACs with antiplatelet agents as ‘inappropriate’ unless prescribed within 1–12 months after coronary stenting. For patients whose INR values were not assessed, we assumed these patients had labile INR values, giving a HAS-BLED score of at least 1. The ESC Guideline recommends that high bleeding risk score should not result in withholding OAC [24]. So, patients having HAS-BLED score of ≥3 and high risk of stroke based on CHA2DS2-VASc score, but not prescribed OAC were defined as not appropriately prescribed.

Data entry and analysis

Data was analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences version 21. Binary logistic regression analysis was performed to determine the association of each independent variable with prescribing antithrombotic drugs. Multivariable logistic regression was conducted for variables with p-value of <0.25 in the univariable analysis. A p-value <0.05 was taken as statistically significant in the multivariate analysis. Categorical variables (such as age category, CHA2DS2-VASc score category, HAS-BLED score category, prescriptions of antithrombotic drugs) are presented as frequencies (n) and percentages (%) while quantitative variables (values of liver function tests, age, CHA2DS2-VASc score) are presented as median and interquartile range. The AF patients are grouped into two categories on the basis of appropriateness of their antithrombotic therapy at the time of discharge as: those who were prescribed with OACs antithrombotic agents appropriately and those who were prescribed inappropriately (OACs and antiplatelet dual therapy or antiplatelet monotherapy or those who were not prescribed any antithrombotic agent or those who were prescribed antithrombotic despite contraindication).

Results

Socio-demographic and clinical characteristics of patients

A total of 84 AF patients were included in this study. More than half (52.4%) of the patients were females. The median age of the patients was 64.50 (75−39.5) while 51.2% of them were in the age of <65 years old. Majorities (59.5%) of the patients were admitted for the first time for AF and 14.5% of patients left the hospital against medical advice. Concerning the comorbidities, 17.9% of the patients had abnormal liver functions, 15.5% hypertension, 6.0% previous stroke/transient ischemic attack/thromboembolism and 4.8% major bleeding history (Table 1).

Prescription patterns of antithrombotic drugs versus the CHA2DS2-VASc and HAS-BLED scores

Among the total of 36 patients with CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥2 (male) or ≥3 (female) who are at high risk of stroke, more than one third (36.1%) of them received no antithrombotic drug at all. On the other hand, 21 (58.3%) out of these patients with the CHA2DS2-VASc score≥2 for male or ≥3 for female were prescribed OAC (warfarin) monotherapy. Moreover, the proportion of warfarin monotherapy prescription for patients at high risk to those patients at moderate risk of stroke was more than twice (58.3% versus 27.1%). Similarly, the proportion of prescription of OAC containing regimen among the AF patients at high risk of bleeding (with the HAS-BLED score ≥ 3) was much lower than in those at low risk of bleeding (with the HAS-BLED score < 3) (41.9% versus 56.6%). Besides, 15 (48.4%) and 22 (41.5%) of the patients with high risk and low risk of bleeding, respectively, received no antithrombotic drug at all (Table 3).

Appropriateness of prescription of antithrombotic therapy

The current study showed that almost two third (61.9%) of the total AF patients were prescribed inappropriately for different reasons. Of those with inappropriate prescriptions, underprescription (no antithrombotic drug at all) was the leading problem, which was identified in 44.0% of the patients. Among the 38.1% patients who were appropriately prescribed with antithrombotic drug, 36.9% were prescribed with warfarin while 1 (1.2%) patient was not prescribed with OAC and was considered as appropriate therapy because the patient had contraindication for anti-coagulation due to major bleeding history (Fig. 2).

Factors associated with OAC antithrombotics prescriptions

The analysis of the data using the univariable analysis showed that age; admission times for AF, discharge against medical advice (self-discharge), abnormal liver functions and CHA2DS2-VASc score were associated with the underprescription of OAC antithrombotics (P-value <0.25). However, multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that only admission times for AF was significantly associated with the underprescription of OAC antithrombotics (p < 0.001). Accordingly, the multivariate analysis revealed that AF patients admitted for the second or more times were more likely to be prescribed with OAC medications compared to those patients admitted for the first time (Adjusted Odds Ratio = 6.587, CI: 2.273−19.091), (Table 4).

Discussion

This study revealed that large proportion (44.0%) of AF patients at risk of stroke from AF received no antithrombotic drug at all. Besides, almost half (48.8%) of the total patients at risk of stroke were not prescribed any OAC containing regimen and none of them were prescribed with NOAC drugs. On the other hand, out of the 47 (56.0%) patients who were prescribed with any antithrombotic drug, 43 (51.2%) patients received OAC containing regimen while 34 (40.5%) patients were prescribed with OAC monotherapy, warfarin. Besides, 4.8% of the AF patients were on aspirin monotherapy. Many studies have also shown that OACs are underprescribed in AF patients with various degrees of risks of stroke from AF [35.6−87%, [10,13,15,21,31,32]].

Despite the underprescription trends of OACs antithrombotics shown by many studies, the usage of OAC in our study is lower as compared to the previous findings, where prescription of OAC varied from 66 to 87% in AF patients with CHA2DS2VASc score ≥ 1 [10,21,32]. The age class of the patients, sample size and study design may be some of possible reasons for the variations in prescribing rates of OACs. Franchi et al [21] (67.4%) included AF patients ≥65 years older while Henrard et al [16] (66%) included patients ≥75 years older. As age is one component of the CHA2DS2VASc score, increasing age increases risk of stroke from AF which encourages prescription of more drugs. But, our study included all age groups where 51.2% of AF patients were < 65 years old. Besides, a study conducted by Steinberg et al used prospective study design, which found 87% of AF patients with CHA2DS2VASc scores ≥2 received OAC [10]. The retrospective study design in our study, coupled with small sample size, might have introduced bias. Furthermore, our study showed that the proportion of OAC (warfarin) monotherapy for those patients at high risk to those patients at moderate risk of stroke was more than twice (58.3% versus27.1%).

In line with our result, Waldo et al [31] and Holt et al [12] found that 55% and 53.0% of AF patients at high risk of stroke received warfarin, respectively. Nevertheless, AF patients with low risks of stroke were included in the study conducted by Waldo et al [31], which may be one possible reason for the comparable prescription of OAC with our results. A longitudinal series of cross-sectional study also showed that 50.7% of AF patients with the CHA2DS2-VASc ≥2 scores were given OAC treatments [12], which is very close to our findings. But, inconsistent with our result, use of OAC in AF patients was lower in previous study done in China (35.6%) [13]. All AF patients included in our study had heart failure and so all were at risk of stroke while the study done in China included low risk patients for stroke. This could be one reason for the variations of OAC use observed between these studies.

Unlike to our study, the NOAC antithrombotic drugs have been prescribed in previous studies to considerable proportions among other OAC antithrombotic agents [10,13,21,33]. A multi-center cross-sectional study from China found that among AF patients with high risk of stroke, 11.1% of them were prescribed NOAC while 24.8% were prescribed OAC medication, warfarin [13]. The “no” usage of the NOAC antithrombotic drugs in our study may be attributed to non-availability of these drugs due to their inaccessibility and expensiveness. In the current study, prescription of OAC in AF patients with the HAS-BLED score ≥ 3 (41.9%) was much lower than in those with HAS-BLED score <3 (56.6%). This may come from inclination of physicians to overestimate the risk of bleeding among patients with HAS-BLED score of ≥3 to prescribe OAC. This result is inconsistent with a previous study where bleeding risk did not influence OAC use [13]. Besides, AF Guidelines recommend that high bleeding risk score should not be guarantee for withholding OAC therapy; rather, bleeding risk factors should be addressed [25] and differential dose selections of OACs should be considered [1].

In the present study, the prescription of OAC antithrombotic agents dropped from 59.5% in patients in the age category of 60 years old to 45.5% in patients aged ≥75 years old. Increasing age leads physicians to dilemma as it increases the risk of both thromboembolic stroke from AF and hemorrhagic complication from the OAC therapy [14]. Nevertheless, the ESC recommends that elderly AF patients are at higher risk of thromboembolic stroke and, thus, are more likely to benefit from OAC than younger patients [18].

The current study showed that almost two third (61.9%) of the AF patients were prescribed inappropriately, while the rest (38.1%) patients were appropriately prescribed with OAC (warfarin). Of those with inappropriate prescriptions, underprescription (no antithrombotic drug use) was the leading problem identified in 44.0% of patients. This is different from previous study report where 44.0% of the patient were inappropriately prescribed with OAC, which is higher than our result [29]. The potential differences in the levels of knowledge of clinicians about the thromboembolic stroke risks may be the possible reason for the variations in usage of OACs observed in the studies. But, consistent with our study, the same study reported that inappropriate prescription was mainly due to underprescription.

In this study, combination therapy of OAC (warfarin) with aspirin or heparin was identified in 10.7% AF patients. Besides, about 4.8% of the patients were prescribed aspirin monotherapy. However, combining antiplatelet drug with OAC in AF patients without additional indication is inappropriate. Patients on OAC and aspirin dual therapy experiences higher hemorrhagic complications, which is not outweighed by reduction in thromboembolism [34]. A meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials showed that warfarin was found to be 40% more efficacious than antiplatelet agent [9]. Moreover, aspirin was found to be ineffective in preventing strokes in those >75 years old and did not prevent stroke [1]. Accordingly, the ESC guideline does not recommend antiplatelet agents for stroke prevention in patients with AF [8].

Our multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that the times of admission for AF was significantly associated with underprescription of OAC antithrombotics (p < 0.01). Accordingly, AF patients who were admitted for the second or more times were more likely to be prescribed with OAC compared to those patients who were admitted for the first time (Adjusted Odds Ratio = 6.587, CI: 2.273−19.091). This is inconsistent with previous studies, which found that the prescription of antiplatelet drugs, age ≥75 years old and perceived bleeding risk were associated with an increased risk of OAC underuse in AF patients [15,16,31].

Conclusions

The present study has revealed that substantial proportions of the hospitalized AF patients who were at high risk of thromboembolic stroke were underprescribed with OACs. Moreover, inappropriate prescription of OACs characterized by underuse and wrong drug selections as well as inappropriate combinations of antithrombotic was common among the hospitalized patients with atrial fibrillation. The study also indicated AF patients admitted for the second or more times for AF case had less probability of being underprescribed with OAC medications. Therefore, the cardiologists of the hospital should follow standard AF guidelines and give emphasis about the significance of OACs in order to improve the prescription patterns of OAC drugs with ultimate goal of prevention of AF-related stroke and systemic thromboembolism.

Comparative production of recombinant capripoxvirus in Vero cells using microcarrier- and macrocarrier-based dynamic cell culture systems under serum-free conditions
Authors: Zhanat Amanova, Zhanna Sametova, Olga Chervyakova, Sholpan Turyskeldy, Alina Kurmasheva, Ruslan Abitayev, Abdurakhman Ussembay, Zhanat Kondibayeva, Dariya Toktyrova, Dana Mazbayeva, Kuandyk Zhugunissov, Yerbol Bulatov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractBrucellosis remains one of the most significant zoonotic diseases worldwide, and the absence of licensed human vaccines highlights the need for novel vaccine platforms and reproducible laboratory-scale manufacturing strategies. In the present study, the replication efficiency of a recombinant capripoxvirus expressing a Brucella antigen was evaluated in Vero cells cultured under serum-free conditions using two 3D carrier-based cultivation systems. Vero cells were cultivated in serum-free medium either on Cytodex 1 microcarriers in a 500-mL reusable spinner flask operated in repeated-batch mode or on BioNOC II® macrocarriers in single-use 500-mL BelloCell™ 500AP vessels integrated into the BelloStage™-3000 system and operated according to the Tide Motion principle with medium recirculation. Cells were infected with recombinant SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 at a multiplicity of infection of 0.1, and total cell yield, metabolic parameters, and virus production were subsequently monitored throughout cultivation. The microcarrier and macrocarrier systems achieved maximum total cell yields of 7.0 × 10⁸ and 3.1 × 10⁹ cells, respectively. Peak virus titers reached 5.75 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 120 h post-infection in the microcarrier system and 7.25 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 168 h post-infection in the macrocarrier system, corresponding to a 1.5 log₁₀ increase in virus titer (p < 0.01). After normalization to the working culture volume, the macrocarrier system exhibited 4.4-fold higher volumetric cell productivity. In addition, virus productivity normalized to the total number of cells present at the time of infection was approximately sevenfold higher than that observed in the microcarrier system.These findings demonstrate that the macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation system enables superior total cell yield and enhanced recombinant virus production under serum-free laboratory-scale conditions.Key points• BioNOC II® macrocarriers increased volumetric cell productivity by 4.4-fold compared with Cytodex 1.• BioNOC II® macrocarriers increased peak virus titers by 1.5 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL.• Cell-specific viral productivity was approximately sevenfold higher in the macrocarrier-based bioreactor system.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00253-026-13934-7.Keywords: Brucellosis, Viral vector production, Adherent cell culture, 3D cell culture, Process intensification, Tide Motion

Introduction

Brucellosis remains a globally significant zoonotic disease caused by bacteria of the genus Brucella, with more than 500,000 human cases reported annually worldwide (Freire et al. 2024; Dagnaw et al. 2024; Wen et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2025; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention 2023). Despite long-standing control strategies based on livestock vaccination and culling, the disease continues to persist in many endemic regions (Qureshi et al. 2023; Lalsiamthara et al. 2017). Importantly, no licensed vaccines are currently available for human use, highlighting the need for the development of novel and safe vaccine platforms (Hou et al. 2019; Arenas-Gamboa et al. 2009).

Recent advances in vaccine development have increasingly focused on recombinant approaches, including viral vector-based systems capable of delivering heterologous antigens and inducing protective immune responses (Heidary et al. 2022). Among these, capripoxvirus-based vectors represent promising candidates due to their genetic stability and capacity for insertion of foreign genes. However, the successful implementation of such platforms requires not only rational vector design but also robust and reproducible manufacturing processes capable of ensuring consistent virus yields.

Animal cell culture technologies form the foundation of modern viral vaccine production. Traditionally, vaccine manufacturing has relied on two-dimensional (2D) adherent cell cultures supplemented with serum. These systems are associated with limitations in scalability and process control, particularly when large production volumes are required (Lee et al. 2020). The transition to serum-free cultivation systems has enabled improved standardization of culture conditions and more controlled process operation, while maintaining efficient growth and virus replication in Vero cells (Ji et al. 2025; Kiesslich et al. 2021).

To address the limitations of conventional 2D systems, three-dimensional (3D) cultivation technologies based on carrier-supported growth of adherent cells have been developed. Two main approaches are currently used: microcarrier-based stirred systems and macrocarrier-based fixed-bed systems. Microcarrier-based cultivation enables the expansion of adherent cells in stirred environments and is widely used in vaccine production. However, under stirred conditions, cell growth and productivity may be influenced by hydrodynamic effects such as shear stress and limitations in mass transfer, particularly at increased cell densities (Rourou et al. 2007; Fang et al. 2022).

In contrast, macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation systems provide a structured environment that supports cell attachment and retention while reducing exposure to hydrodynamic stress. Such systems have been shown to support high cell densities and efficient virus production, particularly when operated under intensified conditions involving continuous or semi-continuous medium exchange (Lennaertz et al. 2013; Lesch et al. 2021; Guo et al. 2024).

Process intensification has become an important concept in viral vaccine manufacturing, combining high-cell-density cultivation with optimized operation modes such as repeated-batch or perfusion-like processes to improve volumetric productivity (Fang et al. 2022). However, the effects of carrier type and process operation mode are often difficult to distinguish, as these parameters are typically investigated separately or under non-comparable experimental conditions.

In our previous study, serum-free production of recombinant capripoxviruses in Vero cells was demonstrated using BelloCell™ 500 A culture vessels containing BioNOC II® macrocarriers operated in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system according to the Tide Motion principle. Vero cells were seeded at 1.5 × 10⁷ cells/mL, cultivated for 6 days until reaching total cell number of approximately 3.2 × 10⁹ cells, and infected at an MOI of 0.1 following a 1 h adsorption phase under controlled Tide Motion conditions. Under these batch-mode laboratory-scale conditions, peak virus titers of 7.5–7.7 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL were achieved (Amanova et al. 2025a). Building upon these findings, the present study further extends this approach by employing BelloCell™ 500AP cultivation vessels integrated with the CelFeeder™ module and operated in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system, enabling controlled medium recirculation during virus production. In addition, to our knowledge, this study represents the first systematic comparison of recombinant capripoxvirus production in microcarrier-based stirred cultivation and macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation systems under standardized serum-free laboratory-scale conditions.

Therefore, the present study was designed to comparatively evaluate the production of recombinant capripoxvirus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 in Vero cells cultured under serum-free conditions using two laboratory-scale dynamic carrier-based cultivation systems. The first system consisted of microcarrier-based cultivation in reusable glass spinner flasks with a working volume of 500 mL, operated on a Techne MCS-102L stirring platform under repeated-batch conditions. The second system consisted of a macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation system designed for high-density expansion of adherent cells under low hydrodynamic stress conditions. Vero cells were cultured on 5.5 g of macroporous BioNOC II® carriers housed in a single-use BelloCell™ 500AP culture vessel with a nominal working volume of 500 mL and operated using the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system. Dynamic cultivation was maintained in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system, operated according to the Tide Motion principle, whereas automated nutrient feeding and medium recirculation through an external 2 L medium reservoir were performed using the CelFeeder™ peristaltic pump module, ensuring efficient mass transfer, stable nutrient supply, and continuous removal of metabolic by-products. Particular emphasis was placed on evaluating how carrier architecture and cultivation mode jointly influence cell expansion and viral productivity in laboratory-scale recombinant virus production systems.

Materials and methods

Virus

Recombinant viruses SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 and SPPV(TK −)-EGFP were obtained from the Microorganism Collection Laboratory of the Research Institute for Biological Safety Problems (RIBSP), Republic of Kazakhstan, as frozen master stocks stored at − 80 °C. The approach used to generate the recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16, based on the SPPV NISKHI strain with targeted replacement of the thymidine kinase gene (SPPV066) by a heterologous expression cassette via homologous recombination, has been described previously (Chervyakova et al. 2021; Amanova et al. 2025b).

The recombinant virus SPPV(TK −)-EGFP (Chervyakova et al. 2021) was used as a model control strain for comparative evaluation of the replication and biological characteristics of SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16. For the preparation of virus stocks, Vero cells were grown to 80–90% confluence and infected with SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1. At 96 h post-infection, culture supernatants were harvested and clarified by low-speed centrifugation to remove cellular debris. The virus suspension was aliquoted and stored at − 40 °C until use. In all experiments, single-use thawed aliquots of the virus suspension were employed to prevent repeated freeze–thaw cycles and minimize the loss of infectious activity.

Cell line and culture medium

For the replication of the recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 and determination of its titer, the Vero cell line was used, provided by the Laboratory of Cell Biotechnology of the Research Institute for Biological Safety Problems (RIBSP), Republic of Kazakhstan. Cells were cultured in serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM (Gibco, Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA) medium supplemented with 4 mM L-glutamine (Gibco, Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Prior to use in the experiments, cell cultures were tested for the absence of mycoplasma contamination using the fluorescence-based MycoFluor™ mycoplasma detection assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Life Technologies Holdings Pte Ltd, Singapore). The assay was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions and was based on the selective binding of DNA-specific fluorochromes to mycoplasmal nucleic acids, followed by fluorescence microscopic evaluation. Positive and negative controls were included in each assay, as described in ESM1.

Preparation of Cytodex 1 microcarriers and inoculation of Vero cells

Cultivation of Vero cells on Cytodex 1 microcarriers (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden) was performed using a previously described cultivation approach with modifications introduced in the present study (Amanova et al. 2025b). Vero cells were expanded in 300 cm2 culture flasks (Techno Plastic Products AG, Switzerland) in serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM supplemented with L-glutamine until reaching 80–90% confluence. The monolayer was washed twice with PBS (Gibco, Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA), followed by enzymatic dissociation using 0.25% trypsin–EDTA (1X) (Gibco, Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA) for 3 min at 37 °C. Cells were pelleted by centrifugation (180 × g, 5 min) and resuspended in fresh medium to a concentration of ≥ 5 × 105 cells/mL.

For microcarrier cultivation, 50 mL of cell suspension was transferred into reusable glass Techne spinner flasks (Bibby Scientific Ltd., Stone, Staffordshire, UK), and 450 mL of fresh medium was added to obtain a final culture volume of 500 mL. Cytodex 1 microcarriers were added at a final concentration of 2 g/L.

The attachment phase was carried out for 2 h at 37 °C under intermittent agitation (2 min of stirring followed by a 10 min pause). Following cell attachment, continuous stirring at 60 rpm was maintained using a Techne MCS-102L stirring platform (Bibby Scientific Ltd., Stone, Staffordshire, UK).

Cultivation was performed at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. The system was operated in a repeated-batch mode, with partial medium replacement (70% of the working volume) every 48 h. During cultivation, cell concentration, pH (7.0–7.4), and glucose concentration (≥ 1.0 g/L) were monitored daily.

Replication of recombinant virus on Cytodex 1 microcarriers

Infection of Vero cells cultured on Cytodex 1 microcarriers was performed at a cell density of ≥ 1.0 × 10⁶ cells/mL using SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1.

Prior to infection, agitation was stopped for 10 min to allow microcarrier sedimentation, and approximately two-thirds of the culture medium was removed. Virus adsorption was carried out for 30 min at 37 °C under intermittent agitation (2 min stirring followed by 10 min pause). After adsorption, continuous agitation at 60 rpm was resumed.

Cultivation of infected cells was performed at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ under repeated-batch conditions, with partial medium replacement (70%) every 48 h. During cultivation, cell concentration, pH (7.2–7.6), and glucose concentration (≥ 1.0 g/L) were monitored daily.

Virus harvest was performed when cytopathic effect reached approximately 90%. The culture supernatant was collected and stored at 2–8 °C. To recover cell-associated virus, microcarriers were treated with 2 M NaCl (1:1 v/v) at 20–25 °C for 30 min under gentle agitation (50 rpm), followed by sedimentation and centrifugation (900 × g, 10 min, 4 °C). The resulting supernatant was combined with the initial culture medium and filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane (NEST Biotechnology, Wuxi, China).

Cultivation of Vero cells on BioNOC II® macrocarriers

The cultivation of Vero cells on BioNOC II® macrocarriers was adapted from previously published protocols (Amanova et al. 2025a, 2025b) with modifications introduced in the present study. Single-use BelloCell™ 500AP cultivation vessels containing 5.5 g of BioNOC II® macrocarriers (Esco Micro Pte. Ltd., Singapore) were pre-hydrated with 100 mL of serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM medium. Vero cells were inoculated in a volume of 50 mL corresponding to a total cell number of ≥ 2.0 × 10⁸ cells.

Following inoculation, the culture vessels were sealed with airtight caps and incubated at (37 ± 0.5) °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ for 5 h to allow cell attachment to the macrocarriers. During the attachment phase, gentle manual mixing was performed every 15 min during the first hour and every 30 min during the subsequent 4 h to ensure complete immersion of the macrocarriers in the cell suspension. After completion of the attachment phase, 350 mL of serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM medium supplemented with L-glutamine (4 mM) was added. Airtight caps were replaced with vented caps equipped with 0.22 μm membrane filters.

Subsequent cultivation was carried out in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system operated according to the Tide Motion principle (Esco Micro Pte. Ltd., Singapore), which provides cyclic upward and downward medium displacement for periodic carrier immersion and aeration, under the following parameters: upward movement rate 1.0 mm/s, top holding time (T_H) = 10 s, downward movement rate 1.0 mm/s, and bottom holding time (B_H) = 10 s.

Medium replacement was performed daily starting from day 2 of cultivation until the total cell number reached ≥ 3.0 × 10⁹ cells. Cells were harvested for virus infection upon reaching a total cell number of ≥ 3.0 × 10⁹ cells, corresponding to day 7 of cultivation under stable metabolic conditions (pH 7.0–7.4; glucose 1.0–1.5 g/L).

Replication of recombinant virus on BioNOC II® macrocarriers

Replication of recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 on BioNOC II® macrocarriers was performed after Vero cells reached a total cell number of ≥ 3.0 × 10⁹ cells. Prior to infection, the culture was washed with 500 mL of serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM medium to remove residual metabolites. The viral inoculum was then added at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1, corresponding to 500 mL of virus-containing medium.

Virus adsorption was carried out for 1 h at 37 °C in the BelloStage™−3000 system under controlled Tide Motion conditions with the following parameters: upward movement rate 2.0 mm/s, top holding time (T_H) = 20 s, downward movement rate 2.0 mm/s, and no holding time at the bottom position (B_H) = 0 s. These conditions ensured effective contact between the virus and cells immobilized on the macrocarriers. After the predefined 1 h adsorption period, performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommended protocol, the single-use BelloCell™ 500AP cultivation vessels were aseptically connected to external 2 L medium reservoirs containing 2.2 L of OptiPRO™ SFM medium prewarmed to 37 °C and supplemented with glucose and L-glutamine to maintain target concentrations (≥ 1.0 g/L glucose, 4 mM L-glutamine).

Post-infection virus cultivation was performed in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system operated under standard operating conditions based on the Tide Motion principle (upward movement rate 1.0 mm/s, top holding time (T_H) = 10 s, downward movement rate 1.0 mm/s, and bottom holding time (B_H) = 10 s) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Medium feeding and recirculation between the BelloCell™ 500AP cultivation vessels and the external 2 L medium reservoirs were controlled using the CelFeeder™ module (Esco Micro Pte. Ltd., Singapore). Recirculation was initiated 24 h post-infection at a flow rate of 1200 mL/day (24 cycles/day) and was gradually increased to 1992 mL/day by day 4 post-infection.

During the cultivation process, key parameters were monitored daily, including total cell number, pH (maintained within 7.2–7.4), and glucose concentration (maintained at ≥ 1.0 g/L). Virus-containing supernatants were collected at 72, 96, 120, 144, 168, 192, 216, and 240 h post-infection by aseptically replacing the medium reservoirs containing virus-containing supernatants with reservoirs containing fresh culture medium.

Determination of virus infectious titer

The infectious activity of virus samples was determined by the endpoint dilution assay in Vero cell culture as previously described (Amanova et al. 2025a). Briefly, tenfold serial dilutions of the virus were prepared and inoculated into 96-well plates containing Vero cells. Cytopathic effect (CPE) was evaluated after 7 days of incubation. The infectious virus titer (TCID₅₀/mL) was calculated using the Reed–Muench method and expressed as log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL (Reed and Muench 1938).

The recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-EGFP, carrying the EGFP gene, was used as a positive control strain. GFP expression in Vero cells infected with SPPV(TKΔ)-EGFP was monitored by fluorescence microscopy throughout the cultivation period to confirm viral infection and replication.

Determination of cell concentration on BioNOC II® macrocarriers

At each sampling time point, six BioNOC II® macrocarriers were aseptically removed from each BelloCell™ 500AP vessel. The carriers were washed three times with 1.0 mL of PBS/EDTA solution (0.02%), after which cells were detached by incubation in 1.0 mL of trypsin–EDTA solution (0.05%/0.02%) at 37 °C for 10 min. Following trypsinization, the cell suspension was centrifuged (180 × g, 5 min), and the resulting pellet was resuspended in OptiPRO™ SFM. Cell concentration and viability were determined using the trypan blue exclusion method with either a hemocytometer or an automated cell counter TC20 (Bio-Rad Laboratories Pte. Ltd., Singapore).

Monitoring of glucose concentration

Glucose concentration in the culture medium was measured using the GlucCell™ system (Esco Micro Pte. Ltd., Singapore) glucose monitoring system, which is based on an electrochemical biosensor principle involving oxidation of glucose by glucose oxidase, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Optimal metabolic conditions were maintained by adjusting glucose levels through supplementation with a sterile D-glucose solution (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). During Vero cell cultivation, glucose concentration was maintained at ≥ 1.0 g/L. When glucose levels decreased below 1.0 g/L during the virus replication phase, additional glucose was added to achieve a final concentration of 3.0 g/L.

Monitoring of pH

The pH of the culture medium was measured using a calibrated edge® HI 2030 pH meter (Hanna Instruments, Inc., Woonsocket, RI, USA) based on potentiometric measurement with a digital glass electrode, according to a previously described method (Guo et al. 2024). In cases of deviation from the established range, pH was adjusted by regulating the CO₂ concentration in the incubation chamber and by adding sterile 7.5% NaHCO₃ solution (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software version 8.0.1 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Differences in cell concentration and virus titers obtained under different cultivation conditions were analyzed using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Attachment and proliferation of Vero cells on Cytodex 1 microcarriers

Changes in Vero cell concentration on Cytodex 1 microcarriers were monitored throughout the entire cultivation period by light microscopy. Cell attachment efficiency, morphological characteristics, distribution across the microcarrier surface, and formation of a confluent monolayer were evaluated. Active cell spreading on the surface of Cytodex 1 was observed approximately 16 h after inoculation, whereas pronounced cell proliferation was noted at 24 h of cultivation. By 96 h, a dense confluent monolayer with typical epithelial-like morphology was formed, resulting in nearly complete coverage of the microcarrier surface (Fig. 1A).

Glucose concentration was maintained at no less than 1.0 g/L throughout the entire cultivation period (Fig. 1B). The pH was stabilized within the range of 7.0–7.4 by periodic addition of NaHCO₃ solution and regulation of CO₂ concentration in the incubation atmosphere (Fig. 1C). Maintenance of these parameters ensured stable conditions for cell growth and proliferation. Cell concentration was quantified every 12 h throughout the 120 h cultivation period (Fig. 1D).

The initial seeding density of Vero cells cultured on Cytodex 1 microcarriers was 5 × 105 cells/mL. Cell concentration increased over the first 5 days of cultivation, reaching a maximum density of 1.4 × 10⁶ cells/mL. Further cultivation did not result in a significant increase in cell concentration, indicating that the culture had reached a plateau phase and maximum cell density under the given conditions.

Replication of recombinant virus in Vero cells cultured on Cytodex 1 microcarriers

Infection of Vero cells cultured on Cytodex 1 microcarriers was performed upon reaching a cell density of 1.4 × 10⁶ cells/mL using the recombinant strain SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 (MOI = 0.1). Following infection, a decrease in the rate of cell density increase was observed compared to the pre-infection period, accompanied by an increase in the proportion of infected cells and the development of cytopathic effect (CPE) (Fig. 2A).

In the control Vero cell culture infected with recombinant SPPV(TKΔ)-EGFP, viral replication was qualitatively monitored by direct fluorescence microscopic observation of GFP expression every 12 h throughout the post-infection period, confirming successful infection and progressive viral spread (Fig. 2B).

Throughout the post-infection cultivation period, glucose concentration and pH were maintained within predefined physiological ranges, providing stable metabolic conditions for viral replication (Fig. 2C,D). Cell density and virus production were monitored every 12 h throughout the post-infection period (Fig. 2E).

Viral replication exhibited a progressive increase during the productive phase, reaching a peak titer of 5.75 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 120 h post-infection, followed by a gradual decline that coincided with reduced viable cell density, indicating completion of the productive infection cycle.

Attachment and proliferation of Vero cells on BioNOC II® macrocarriers

Cultivation of Vero cells on BioNOC II® macrocarriers in BelloCell™ 500AP vessels within the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system resulted in a high total number of cells. At an early stage after seeding, a high efficiency of cell attachment to the carrier surface was observed (up to 98% within 3 h), facilitating uniform colonization of the macrostructures and the establishment of a stable cellular substrate (Fig. 3A,B).

Cell growth progressed steadily and reached a peak at 168 h of cultivation, after which the total cell number gradually declined to 2.74 × 10⁹ cells at 240 h. Total cell number was monitored at 12 h intervals throughout cultivation, allowing detailed characterization of cell expansion under BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor conditions (Fig. 3C). Throughout cultivation, glucose concentration and pH were maintained within predefined physiological ranges, ensuring stable metabolic conditions and supporting the expansion of the total cell population (Fig. 3D,E).

Replication of recombinant virus in Vero cells cultured on BioNOC II® macrocarriers

Vero cell cultures were infected with the recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 (MOI 0.1) after reaching a total cell number of 3.1 × 10⁹ cells. At the time of inoculation and daily from day 2 to day 10 post-infection, the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium, and virus-containing supernatants were simultaneously collected.

Following infection, the total cell number gradually decreased from 3.1 × 10⁹ cells at the time of infection to 2.74 × 10⁹ cells at 240 h post-infection, with the decline becoming evident approximately 48 h post-infection and coinciding with active viral replication (Fig. 4A). In the control Vero culture infected with recombinant SPPV(TKΔ)-EGFP (Fig. 4B), GFP expression was used to visualize viral replication in real time, confirming the functional activity of infection. The virus titer increased from 4.5 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at the onset of the productive phase to a peak value of 7.25 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 168 h post-infection, representing a 2.75 log₁₀ increase. Thereafter, the virus titer declined to 5.92 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 240 h post-infection, representing a 1.33 log₁₀ decrease, concomitant with reductions in viable cell concentration.

Cultivation of SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 was continued for 10 days post-infection, with total cell number and virus production monitored every 12 h (Fig. 4C). Throughout the post-infection period, glucose concentration and pH were maintained within predefined physiological ranges, ensuring stable metabolic conditions and supporting sustained recombinant virus replication (Fig. 4D,E).

Comparison of cell expansion and viral productivity in microcarrier and macrocarrier systems

Comparative analysis demonstrated that Vero cell expansion was strongly dependent on the carrier type. In the Cytodex 1 microcarrier system, the maximum viable cell concentration reached 1.4 × 10⁶ cells/mL, corresponding to a total cell yield of 7.0 × 10⁸ cells. In contrast, cultivation on BioNOC II® macrocarriers resulted in a maximum total cell yield of 3.1 × 10⁹ cells. Relative to the initial inoculum, these values corresponded to approximately 2.8-fold and 15.5-fold increases in cell population, respectively. To enable direct comparison between the two laboratory-scale systems, total cell yield in the BioNOC II® system was normalized to the working culture volume. After normalization, cultivation on BioNOC II® macrocarriers corresponded to approximately 6.2 × 10⁶ cells/mL, compared with 1.4 × 10⁶ cells/mL in the Cytodex 1 system, representing an approximately 4.4-fold increase in volumetric cell productivity. The differences were highly significant according to two-way ANOVA (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 5A).

Comparison of microcarrier and macrocarrier systems in terms of cell expansion and viral productivity. A Normalized Vero cell concentration; B Peak titers of recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Statistical significance was determined using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Significant differences were observed for cell productivity (p < 0.0001) and virus titers (p < 0.01)

A similar trend was observed for replication of the recombinant virus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16. On Cytodex 1 microcarriers, the maximum virus titer reached 5.75 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 120 h post-infection, whereas cultivation on BioNOC II® macrocarriers yielded a peak titer of 7.25 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL at 168 h post-infection, representing a 1.5 log₁₀ increase in peak virus titer. These differences were statistically significant according to two-way ANOVA (p < 0.01) (Fig. 5B). When peak virus production was normalized to the total number of cells present at the time of infection, the BioNOC II® system exhibited approximately sevenfold higher virus productivity than the Cytodex 1 system.

Overall, the obtained results indicate a substantial advantage of BioNOC II® macrocarriers in both increasing cell yield and enhancing viral productivity.

Discussion

One of the major trends in modern vaccine biotechnology is the transition from conventional 2D adherent cultures to 3D cell culture systems, which enable higher cell densities and efficient virus production. Recent reviews indicate that the use of bioreactor-based cultivation technologies has become an important component of modern viral vaccine process development, particularly in the context of increasing global demand for vaccines (Fang et al. 2022).

Unlike our previous studies, which compared macrocarrier-based cultivation with conventional static 2D systems, the present study provides the first direct comparison of recombinant capripoxvirus production in Vero cells using microcarrier-based stirred cultivation and macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation operated according to the Tide Motion principle under serum-free conditions and harmonized infection parameters. Two carrier-based cultivation systems were evaluated: Vero cells grown on Cytodex 1 microcarriers in Techne spinner flask cultures under continuous agitation, and Vero cells cultivated on BioNOC II® macrocarriers in BelloCell™ 500AP vessels operated within the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system according to the Tide Motion principle.

The efficient proliferation of Vero cells in serum-free OptiPRO™ SFM observed in this study supports the current trend toward the use of chemically defined serum-free culture systems in viral vaccine production. The use of such media improves process reproducibility, reduces the risk of contamination with animal-derived components, and facilitates standardization of production protocols. Furthermore, numerous studies have demonstrated the suitability of Vero cells for cultivation under serum-free conditions and their widespread application in the production of viral vaccines and next-generation viral vectors (Ji et al. 2025; Morrow and Sha 2013; Frazatti-Gallina et al. 2004; Bourigault et al. 2025; Kiesslich et al. 2021).

In the Cytodex 1 microcarrier-based cultivation system, Vero cells reached a maximum density of 1.4 × 10⁶ cells/mL, which falls within the range of values previously reported for stirred microcarrier cultures. According to the literature, Vero cell density during cultivation on Cytodex microcarriers typically ranges from 1 to 4 × 10⁶ cells/mL, depending on cultivation conditions, bioreactor type, and agitation parameters. For example, Vero cells cultured on Cytodex-1 in spinner flasks reached a maximum density of 1.85 × 10⁶ cells/mL, whereas in bioreactor systems, cell densities may increase to 2.6 × 10⁶ cells/mL or higher (Rourou et al. 2007).

In addition, several studies have reported that, under optimized cultivation conditions and when using different types of microcarriers (Cytodex-1 or Cytodex-3), Vero cell density generally falls within the range of 1–3 × 10⁶ cells/mL. These findings indicate that the cell densities obtained in the present study are consistent with those typically observed in microcarrier-based culture systems (Kiesslich et al. 2021; Logan et al. 2022; Berrie et al. 2020; Fang et al. 2022).

Compared to the Cytodex 1 microcarrier-based cultivation system, the use of BioNOC II® macrocarriers in BelloCell™ 500AP vessels resulted in a significantly higher yield of Vero cells. In the present study, after 7 days of cultivation, the total cell number reached 3.1 × 10⁹ cells per vessel at a working volume of 0.5 L. Comparable results have been reported in the literature, where cultivation in BelloCell™ 500AP vessels yielded 2.2–2.5 × 10⁹ Vero cells per vessel, corresponding to a normalized cell concentration of approximately 4.4–5.0 × 10⁶ cells/mL (Offersgaard et al. 2021).

The high productivity of this system may be partially attributed to the structural characteristics of BioNOC II® macroporous carriers, which have a specific surface area of up to 2730 cm2/g and enable the generation of cell biomass reaching 2–3 × 10⁹ cells per 0.1 L of carrier material (Esco VacciXcell 2026).

Similarly, Rhazi et al. (2021) demonstrated efficient high-density cultivation of Vero cells in CelCradle™−500A (currently BelloCell™ 500 A) culture vessels containing macrocarriers and operated according to the Tide Motion principle. In that study, Vero cells were inoculated at 1.5 × 10⁸ cells per vessel, allowed to attach for 3 h, and subsequently cultivated under programmed Tide Motion conditions following confirmation of > 90% cell attachment. Under these conditions, total Vero cell numbers reached 3.04 × 10⁹ cells within 5 days of cultivation, corresponding to a 19-fold increase in total cell number relative to the initial inoculum, whereas conventional cell factory cultures achieved only 9.45 × 10⁸ cells (3.7-fold expansion). Moreover, cultivation of lumpy skin disease virus, another member of the Capripoxvirus genus, demonstrated improved cell-specific virus productivity in CelCradle™ (currently BelloCell™) macrocarrier cultivation vessels operated according to the Tide Motion principle, further supporting the suitability of Tide Motion-based macrocarrier cultivation systems for high-density Vero cell expansion and efficient capripoxvirus production (Rhazi et al. 2021).

Similarly, Toriniwa and Komiya (2007) demonstrated serum-free cultivation of Vero cells for Japanese encephalitis virus production at the 500 mL laboratory scale, comparing microcarrier-based cultivation in stirred spinner flasks with macrocarrier-based cultivation in BelloCell™ 500 A culture vessels operated according to the Tide Motion principle. Under these conditions, cultivation in BelloCell™ 500 A culture vessels achieved a maximum cell yield of 2.8 × 10⁹ cells, whereas cultivation in a microcarrier-based spinner flask with a working volume of 500 mL yielded 9.0 × 10⁸ cells, corresponding to an approximately threefold increase in cell output (Toriniwa and Komiya 2007). Furthermore, Guo et al. (2024) reported serum-containing cultivation of Vero cells in BelloCell™ 500 A culture vessels operated on the CelCradle™ tidal bioreactor system (currently marketed as BelloStage™−3000). Cells were inoculated at an initial concentration of approximately 3 × 10⁶ cells/mL, corresponding to a total inoculum of 3 × 10⁸ cells in 100 mL, followed by a 1.5 h attachment phase at 37 °C and subsequent expansion to a final working volume of 500 mL under programmed Tide Motion conditions. Under daily metabolic monitoring and medium replacement from day 2, approximately 85% cell attachment was achieved, and the total cell number reached 2.49 × 10⁹ cells by day 7 of cultivation (Guo et al. 2024).

Consistent with these findings, our previous laboratory-scale studies on serum-free Vero cell cultivation using BelloCell™ 500 A culture vessels containing BioNOC II® macrocarriers operated in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system according to the Tide Motion principle demonstrated similarly high cell yields. Specifically, Vero cell numbers reached 3.2 × 10⁹, corresponding to an approximately two-order-of-magnitude increase compared to conventional static flask cultures (Amanova et al. 2025a). In another study conducted under comparable serum-free macrocarrier-based cultivation conditions and benchmarked against Cytodex 1 and Cytodex 3 microcarrier systems, total Vero cell numbers reached 2.8 × 10⁹ cells, exceeding those obtained in the microcarrier systems under the corresponding experimental conditions (Amanova et al. 2025b).

These findings are further supported by additional studies employing CelCradle™/BelloCell™ macrocarrier-based cultivation systems containing BioNOC II® carriers and operated according to the Tide Motion principle under controlled medium exchange or recirculation conditions. In a subsequent study, Offersgaard et al. (2022) demonstrated high-titer production of hepatitis C virus (HCV) in CelCradle™ 500AP culture vessels containing 5.5 g of BioNOC II® macrocarriers, where Huh7.5 cells were inoculated onto the macrocarriers, cultivated under Tide Motion-based dynamic conditions with regular medium replacement, and infected after establishment of a high-density cell population at an MOI of 0.006. Under these conditions, peak infectivity titers of up to 7.2 log₁₀ FFU/mL were achieved, with a cumulative virus yield of 5.9 × 101⁰ FFU per culture vessel (Offersgaard et al. 2022).

In addition, Choi et al. (2023) reported production of a PRRSV vaccine candidate in a CelCradle™ macrocarrier-based cultivation system, where MARC-145 cells were seeded at an initial density of 8.6 × 104 cells/mL, cultivated for 5 days under programmed Tide Motion conditions with scheduled medium exchange until reaching 4.47 ± 0.63 × 10⁸ cells per vessel, and subsequently infected at an MOI of 0.01. Under these conditions, the maximum virus titer reached 2.04 × 10⁸ TCID₅₀/mL, with a total virus yield of 1.02 × 1011 TCID₅₀ per 500 mL culture (Choi et al. 2023). Collectively, these studies further confirm that Tide Motion-based macrocarrier cultivation systems provide favorable conditions for high-density expansion of adherent cells and efficient production of both human and veterinary viral vaccines. Beyond process performance, the genetic stability of the recombinant virus was also confirmed throughout 3D cultivation. Genetic analysis confirmed the stable integration and retention of the heterologous antigen-encoding sequences within the recombinant SPPV genome. The presence of the inserted sequences was verified by PCR (Fig. S1 in ESM2) using gene-specific primers and universal primers targeting the thymidine kinase (TK) locus. These results confirm the genetic stability of the recombinant virus during cultivation on 3D carriers.

From a process performance perspective, the higher normalized cell concentration achieved in the macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation system containing BioNOC II® macrocarriers was directly associated with enhanced viral productivity. Specifically, the maximum titer of recombinant SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 reached 7.25 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL, compared with 5.75 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL obtained in the microcarrier-based stirred system, representing a 1.5 log₁₀ increase in peak virus titer. This improvement is likely associated with the higher available surface area for cell attachment, reduced hydrodynamic stress, and more stable metabolic conditions provided by the macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation system.

The observed increase in total cell number and virus productivity in the BioNOC II® macrocarrier-based cultivation system may also be interpreted from the perspective of process intensification, since the combination of cell retention within macroporous carriers, Tide Motion-based dynamic cultivation, and CelFeeder™-mediated medium recirculation likely improved nutrient availability and facilitated metabolite dilution compared with the repeated-batch microcarrier-based stirred cultivation process. An important observation was the stable maintenance of key metabolic parameters throughout cultivation. Glucose concentration and pH were maintained within physiologically optimal ranges (glucose ≥ 1 g/L, pH 7.0–7.4), ensuring favorable conditions for Vero cell growth and viral replication. The selection of pH and glucose concentration as primary monitoring parameters is justified by their key role in maintaining cell viability and ensuring stable cultivation conditions.

In this study, ammonia and lactate concentrations, as well as oxygen uptake rate (OUR), were not evaluated, as the primary objective was to assess cell expansion and viral replication under stable cultivation conditions. The macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation system, consisting of a BelloCell™ 500AP culture vessel operated in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system according to the Tide Motion principle and integrated with CelFeeder™-mediated medium recirculation, likely contributed to improved nutrient availability and facilitated metabolite dilution, thereby supporting a more stable culture environment. Furthermore, previous studies have demonstrated that during serum-free Vero cell cultivation in BioNOC II® macrocarrier-based dynamic cultivation systems, glutamine did not become a limiting substrate, while ammonia accumulation remained within non-inhibitory ranges under controlled feeding and medium exchange conditions (Offersgaard et al. 2021). In the present study, stable cultivation conditions were maintained through regular medium replacement, performed daily from day 2 of cultivation until a total cell number of ≥ 3.0 × 10⁹ cells was achieved.

Despite the promising results obtained, the present study has several limitations. First, the experiments were conducted at a laboratory scale, and therefore further evaluation in pilot- and larger-scale dynamic bioreactor systems is required to confirm the broader applicability of the proposed technology. From a translational perspective, a formal techno-economic comparison between the two laboratory-scale systems was beyond the scope of the present study. Nevertheless, future investigations should address medium consumption, carrier utilization, labor intensity, and cost per infectious dose.

In addition, the present study primarily focused on cell expansion and virus titer, whereas other important parameters, including recombinant antigen expression, viral stability, downstream purification efficiency, immunogenicity, and protective efficacy of SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 produced under optimized cultivation conditions, require further investigation.

Conclusion

In the present study, the feasibility of serum-free cultivation of Vero cells for the production of recombinant capripoxvirus SPPV(TKΔ)-OMP16 expressing a Brucella antigen was demonstrated using a three-dimensional carrier-based culture system. Comparative analysis showed that cultivation on BioNOC II® macrocarriers in the BelloStage™−3000 bioreactor system resulted in substantially higher total cell yield and viral productivity than cultivation on Cytodex 1 microcarriers under stirred conditions. The maximum virus titer reached 7.25 log₁₀ TCID₅₀/mL, representing a 1.5 log₁₀ increase compared with the microcarrier-based cultivation system. After normalization of virus productivity to the total number of cells present at the time of infection, the macrocarrier system demonstrated approximately sevenfold higher cell-specific viral productivity.

These findings highlight the potential of Tide Motion-based macrocarrier cultivation as an efficient platform for intensified production of recombinant viral vectors under serum-free conditions. The combination of high cell density, enhanced virus productivity, and operational simplicity supports the broader application of this technology for laboratory-scale process development and future scale-up of recombinant viral vaccine manufacturing. Future studies should focus on further optimization of bioprocess parameters, validation of the proposed cultivation strategy in pilot-scale bioreactor systems, and evaluation of the immunogenicity, protective efficacy, and techno-economic feasibility of recombinant virus production under scaled-up conditions.

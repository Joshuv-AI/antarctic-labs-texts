Mean platelet volume and long-term cardiovascular outcomes in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus
Authors: Shani Dahan, Ygal Plakht, Arthur Shiyovich, Eyal Ben Assa, Farrukh Almazzada, Guy Melamed, Barak Nahir, Sivan Gazit, Tal Patalon, Eli Lev
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAltered platelet function contributes to the prothrombotic state in type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM), potentially influencing long-term cardiovascular risk. Mean platelet volume (MPV), a readily available marker of platelet size and activity, has been linked to adverse cardiovascular outcomes, yet its prognostic value in DM is less defined. This study evaluated the association between MPV and the composite MPV × platelet count (MPV × PLT) with long-term cardiovascular outcomes in patients with DM without prior myocardial infarction (MI) or stroke.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study used anonymized data from 88,710 patients with DM and no prior history of MI or stroke. Patients were classified into three equal-sized groups based on MPV and MPV*PLT values, with additional analysis of the lowest and highest 5 % of values. The primary outcome was major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), a composite of all-cause mortality, MI, and stroke.ResultsDuring a median follow-up of 8.9 years, a total of 8,828 (10.0 %) MACCE events occurred. MACCE rates were highest in the lowest (13.1 %) and highest (10.0 %) MPV groups (p < 0.001). Both low and high MPV × PLT values were independently associated with greater MACCE risk versus the middle group (adjusted hazard ratio 1.22, 95 % CI 1.11–1.33; and hazard ratio 1.47, 95 % CI 1.33–1.63; both p < 0.001).ConclusionsExtreme MPV and MPV × PLT values were independently associated with adverse long-term cardiovascular outcomes in patients with DM. These findings suggest that MPV and MPV × PLT may serve as cost-effective, readily available biomarkers for improving cardiovascular risk stratification in this high-risk population.Keywords: Mean platelet volume (MPV), Platelet count (PLT), Platelet function, Type 2 diabetes mellitus, Prevention, Cardiovascular risk, Major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE)

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM), even in the absence of prior cardiovascular events, despite advancements in evidence-based treatments. Identifying patients at increased risk for cardiovascular events is therefore essential for optimizing preventive strategies in this patient population.

Platelets play a pivotal role not only in primary hemostasis and endothelial repair but also in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and arterial thrombosis [1,2]. Mean platelet volume (MPV), a routinely available index of platelet size obtained from complete blood counts, reflects platelet activation and reactivity. Larger platelets are metabolically and enzymatically more active, and prior studies have linked higher MPV values to increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [3]. In patients with diabetes, MPV has also been associated with microvascular complications, such as retinopathy and nephropathy [4]. However, data on the long-term prognostic significance of MPV in diabetic populations remain limited.

Because platelet size (MPV) and platelet count (PLT) capture distinct yet complementary aspects of platelet biology, combining them into a composite index (MPV × PLT) may yield a more integrated measure of platelet activation and overall thrombotic potential. MPV represents individual platelet reactivity, while PLT reflects total circulating platelet mass and hemostatic capacity. Emerging evidence suggests that the combined MPV × PLT parameter better reflects the interplay between platelet activity and inflammatory burden, potentially providing stronger predictive value for cardiovascular and thrombotic events than either variable alone [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. This concept aligns with the biological principle that platelet functionality depends not solely on size or count, but on their aggregate contribution to thrombogenesis and vascular inflammation. Clinically, elevated MPV × PLT values could indicate a heightened platelet-driven prothrombotic state, which may predispose to adverse cardiovascular events, particularly in the context of endothelial dysfunction, plaque rupture, or systemic inflammation [3,9].

The aim of our study was to investigate the relationship between MPV and long-term cardiovascular outcomes in patients with type 2 DM without a prior history of myocardial infarction (MI) or stroke, using a large community-based healthcare database. Additionally, we assessed whether incorporating the MPV × PLT index improves cardiovascular risk stratification beyond established clinical predictors.

Methods

Patients and data collection

This population-based cohort study utilized anonymized data from the electronic medical records of Maccabi Healthcare Services (MHS), the second-largest healthcare organization in Israel, providing care to over 2.6 million individuals nationwide. The MHS database contains longitudinal clinical and administrative information on all members, continuously linked to national registries, enabling comprehensive follow-up.

Patients aged 40–70 years who received a new diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM) between 2000 and 2022 were identified using International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) codes. The index date was defined as the first recorded date of definite type 2 DM diagnosis. To ensure internal validity, included patients were required to be registered in the validated MHS Diabetes Registry [10]. Exclusion criteria included patients with hematological malignancies and patients with any prior history of documented MI or stroke, based on MHS respective validated registries. Baseline mean platelet volume (MPV) and total platelet count (PLT) were obtained from the first available complete blood count within 30 days of the index date. A composite parameter, MPV × PLT, was calculated for each patient. Participants were categorized into tertials according to MPV and MPV × PLT values; the lowest and highest 5 % of each distribution were analyzed separately to account for non-linear associations and extreme values.

Demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors, comorbidities, and medication use were retrieved from the electronic records. Laboratory data, including hemoglobin A1C (HbA1C), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, were extracted from the first available test within 30 days of diagnosis.

Follow-up and study outcomes

Patients were followed for up to 10 years from the index date. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), defined as a composite of all-cause mortality, MI, and stroke. Events were identified from validated MHS registries, which undergo routine quality assurance. Mortality data were derived from national death records. To minimize reverse causation, patients experiencing an event within 90 days of diagnosis were excluded.

Statistics

Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages; continuous variables as means ± standard deviation (SD) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). Comparisons among MPV and MPV × PLT groups were performed using the chi-square test for categorical variables and analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables. Survival probabilities were estimated using Kaplan–Meier curves and compared with the log-rank test. Associations between MPV or MPV × PLT groups and clinical outcomes were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models. Results were expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) and adjusted HRs (AdjHRs) with 95 % confidence intervals (CIs). Multivariable models included baseline characteristics that were both clinically and statistically relevant. Model improvement with the addition of MPV × PLT or MPV was evaluated using the −2 log-likelihood (–2LL) comparison [11]. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics, version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethics

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Maccabi Healthcare Services and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design and use of fully anonymized data.

Results

Study cohort

The initial study cohort included a sample of 200,000 patients with type 2 DM. After applying the exclusion and inclusion criteria (Fig. 1), the overall study cohort included 88,710 patients. The mean MPV and total platelet count values were 10.97 ± 1.00 fL and 252.75 ± 65.53 X 109/L, respectively. Baseline characteristics stratified by MPV are summarized in Table 1. Patients with the lowest MPV (<9.3 fL) had higher prevalence of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, renal failure, and higher LDL levels., whereas patients in the highest MPV group (≥12.7 fL) were more likely to be males and had a higher prevalence of smoking.

Clinical outcomes

During a follow up period of up to 10 years, with a median duration of 8.9 years (IQR, 4.7–10 years), a total of 8,828 (10.0 %) MACCE were identified, including 2096 (2.4 %) MIs, 2,076 (2.3 %) strokes and 4,656 all-cause deaths (5.2 %).

MPV and clinical outcomes

As shown in Table 2A and Fig. 2, patients in the lowest MPV group had the highest rates of all-cause death and MACCE. In unadjusted analyses, both low and high MPV were associated with greater MACCE risk compared with the middle group (HR 1.30, 95 % CI 1.19–1.42, p < 0.001 and HR 1.12, 95 % CI 1.01–1.24, p = 0.03, respectively). After multivariable adjustment, only low MPV remained independently associated with increased MACCE risk (AdjHR 1.12, 95 % CI 1.02–1.23, p = 0.02). Other independent predictors included age, male sex, renal failure, PAD, smoking, atrial fibrillation, elevated HbA1C and LDL, while BMI >24.9 kg/m2 was protective (Supplemental Table 1).

MPVxPLT groups

MPVxPLT were distributed between 880.0 and 10,396.0 (fL*109/L), with mean MPVxPLT of 2751.71 ± 665.18 and median of 2691 (IQR: 2300.4–3124.8). Baseline characteristics of the patients by MPVxPLT groups are shown in Supplemental Table 2.

MPVxPLT and clinical outcomes

The rates of MI, stroke, all-cause deat and MACCE were significantly higher in the lowest MPVxPLT group, compared to the other 4 groups (Table 2B and Fig. 2).

Patients in the higher MPVxPLT group had increased risk of MACCE (HR = 1.36, 95 % CI: 1.23–1.50, p < 0.001), as well as patients in the lowest MPVxPLT group (HR = 1.59, 95 % CI: 1.46–1.73, p < 0.001), compared to the middle group. In multivariable analysis, low MPVxPLT (AdjHR 1.22, 95 % CI 1.11–1.33, p < 0.001) and high MPVxPLT (AdjHR 1.47, 95 % CI 1.33–1.63, p < 0.001) were independently associated with increased MACCE risk (Table 3). Incorporating MPVxPLT into the multivariable model significantly improved predictive performance compared with baseline variables alone (p < 0.001); the addition of MPV alone also provided a modest but significant improvement (p = 0.041).

Discussion

This large, community-based cohort of patients with type 2 DM without prior MI or stroke demonstrates that both low and high MPV, as well as extreme MPV × PLT values, are associated with increased long-term risk of major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events. These findings suggest that readily available platelet indices may add incremental prognostic information for cardiovascular prevention in DM, beyond traditional risk factors.

MPV as a marker of cardiovascular risk

The association between MPV and adverse outcomes aligns with platelet biology and prior clinical data. Platelets are central to atherothrombosis, and larger platelets exhibit greater metabolic activity and pro-aggregatory potential [1]. The effectiveness of antiplatelet drugs in reducing cardiovascular events highlights the significant role of platelets in atherothrombotic processes [12].

Consistent with prior studies, increased MPV has been linked to worse outcomes in acute myocardial infarction [[8], [9], [10], [11], [13], [12], [14]], patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention [15], and ischemic stroke [16,17]. Elevated MPV has also been associated with reduced responsiveness to antiplatelet therapy [18]. Furthermore, higher MPV is observed in patients with hypertension [19], hypercholesterolemia [20], smoking [21] and obesity [22], suggesting these factors may share platelet-related mechanisms that increase cardiovascular disease risk. Conversely, some cohorts have observed that low MPV correlates with higher rates of major adverse cardiac events or critical limb ischemia, suggesting that MPV reflects complex hematologic and inflammatory processes [23,24].

MPV in diabetes mellitus

In patients with diabetes, elevated MPV levels have been consistently observed relative to non-diabetic individuals [4,25], and higher MPV correlates with enhanced platelet activation, aggregation, and production of vasoactive mediators [[26], [27], [28]]. Elevated MPV has also been associated with microvascular complications, including retinopathy and nephropathy [29], and correlates with higher HbA1C levels, reflecting poor glycemic control and greater platelet activation [30]. In contrast, lower MPV (<9.8 fL) has been independently linked with the occurrence of critical limb ischemia in DM [31], showing that both extremes may convey risk.

Notably, MPV is also a component of the Intermountain Risk Score (IMRS), a validated hematologic-based mortality prediction model derived from routine laboratory tests. IMRS predicts both short- and long-term mortality across cardiovascular populations, including ST-elevation myocardial infarction and cardiogenic shock [32,33]. The inclusion of MPV in IMRS underscores the prognostic value of platelet-derived indices as accessible markers of systemic risk.

Physiological implications

Both low and high MPV and MPV × PLT values exhibited a U-shaped association with MACCE risk (Fig. 3), with the lowest and highest 5 % of the distribution showing the greatest event rates. Higher MPV reflects larger, metabolically active platelets with greater thrombogenic potential [9], whereas lower MPV may indicate platelet consumption, impaired bone-marrow responsiveness, or chronic inflammation, where cytokine-mediated suppression of megakaryopoiesis can result in smaller circulating platelets [34,35]. In addition, age-related changes in hematopoietic reserve and platelet reactivity may contribute to lower MPV in older individuals, consistent with reduced bone marrow function and diminished physiologic resilience [24]. These mechanisms are plausible but not directly tested in our dataset and require further validation in mechanistic studies. By incorporating both platelet size and count, MPV × PLT provides a more integrated measure of platelet “activity load.” The superior predictive performance of MPV × PLT compared with MPV alone likely reflects its ability to capture the interplay between platelet reactivity and circulating platelet mass.

Clinical implications

The identification of MPV and MPV × PLT as independent predictors of MACCE highlights their potential as accessible, cost-effective biomarkers for cardiovascular prevention and risk stratification in DM. Integrating these parameters into cardiovascular risk models may facilitate the identification of patients who would benefit from intensified preventive measures—such as tighter blood pressure or lipid control, targeted lifestyle interventions, or closer follow-up. However, therapeutic adjustments based on these parameters should remain hypothesis-generating until prospective studies confirm causality.

Future perspectives

Beyond platelet indices, other emerging cardiometabolic biomarkers, such as the Triglyceride–Glucose (TyG) index, have demonstrated prognostic value for long-term cardiovascular events and mortality in high-risk and heart failure cohorts [[36], [37]]. Combining metabolic and platelet-based indices, such as TyG and MPV × PLT, may offer a more comprehensive risk stratification framework by integrating metabolic and thrombotic mechanisms underlying adverse cardiovascular outcomes in DM.

Limitations

Despite the strengths of a large cohort and robust multivariable adjustments, this study has limitations. First, inherent limitation arises from the study's retrospective design. However, Maccabi Healthcare database is based on regular quality assessments to ensure accuracy and reproducibility, and offers the benefit of enabling extensive data evaluation and the analysis of real-world, all-comers participant data. MPV and platelet count were obtained at a single time point, precluding analysis of temporal trends. The study period (2000–2022) encompassed the COVID-19 pandemic, which is associated with platelet activation, hyperreactivity, and prothrombotic changes that could affect observed associations [[38], [39], [40], [41], [42]]. COVID-19 is also linked to increased risk of acute and long-term adverse cardiovascular events, even in patients without prior cardiovascular disease [43,44]. This could represent a confounding factor influencing the observed outcomes, although detailed analysis of the impact of COVID-19 was beyond the scope of our study. Finally, the single healthcare-system setting may limit generalizability to other populations with different ethnic and demographic compositions.

Conclusions

In patients with DM and no history of MI or stroke, both low and high MPV and MPV × PLT were independently associated with increased long-term cardiovascular risk. These findings emphasize the role of platelet-related markers in cardiovascular prevention and support further investigation into MPV and MPV × PLT as simple, widely available, and cost-effective tools for enhancing risk stratification in diabetes.

Conflicted and healthy behavioural profiles in adolescents: a latent profile analysis of traditional sports attitude, physical activity attitude, and digital game addiction
Authors: Şinasi Özsaydi, Özlem Sinem Uslu, Banu Kabak, Ali Erdoğan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to identify latent profiles of adolescents based on their traditional sports attitudes, digital game addiction levels, and physical activity attitudes using a person-centered approach. MethodA total of 634 adolescents (female = 50.5%, n = 320; male = 49.5%, n = 314; mean age = 15.8 years; Grades 9–12) from state high schools in Antalya and Osmaniye provinces were assessed using the Traditional Sports Attitude Scale, the Digital Game Addiction Scale, and the Physical Activity Attitude Scale. Data were analysed via Latent Profile Analysis (LPA); between-profile differences were examined through ANOVA and post-hoc tests.ResultsLPA yielded three meaningful profiles (Entropy = 0.816). The Active-Healthy profile (n = 467, 73.7%) is characterised by high traditional sports attitude, low digital game addiction, and positive physical activity attitude. The Conflicted-Addicted profile (n = 101, 15.9%) exhibits an unexpected internal conflict pattern, carrying the highest digital game addiction in the sample (M = 3.31) despite high traditional sports attitude. The At-Risk profile (n = 66, 10.4%) displays the lowest values across all positive variables. Post-hoc analyses showed that traditional sports attitude failed to differentiate two profiles (|d| = 0.01 to 0.16; p = .314–0.994); whereas negative physical activity attitude was the strongest discriminating variable (η² = 0.621).ConclusionA high traditional sports attitude cannot be considered universally protective against digital addiction risk. Intervention programs should directly target negative physical activity attitudes, beyond the dimension of cultural participation.Keywords: Adolescent, Digital game addiction, Latent profile analysis, Physical activity attitude, Traditional sports

Introduction

Digital game addiction has begun to occupy an increasingly prominent place in the global health agenda following its inclusion in the World Health Organization’s 11th International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11) [1]. Global gaming industry data indicate a player base exceeding three billion and a market volume expected to surpass 300 billion dollars by 2027 [2]. Meta-analytic findings report the rate of transition from widespread use to addiction in the range of 3–9% [3]; in recent years it has been demonstrated that adolescents occupy a position deserving attention within the risk group [4, 5]. Problematic digital game use has been consistently associated with low physical activity levels, declining academic motivation, and negative body image perceptions [3]. Meta-analytic evidence demonstrating that adolescent physical activity levels declined markedly on a global scale during the pandemic period [6, 7] reinforces this picture; indicating that the problem of a substantial proportion of adolescents being unable to meet the physical activity levels recommended by WHO guidelines [8, 9] persists. Against this background, the central problem addressed in the present study is how three constructs that are usually examined separately, namely traditional sports attitude, digital game addiction, and physical activity attitude, combine within individual adolescents to form distinct subgroups. The sections that follow first introduce each construct in turn and then bring them together within a person-centered framework guided by Self-Determination Theory and the Displacement Hypothesis, in order to keep this central question in focus throughout the review.

When evaluated specifically for Turkey, this pattern becomes more pronounced. According to the current data of the (Information and Communication Technologies Authority [ICTA]; Turkish acronym BTK) [10], the great majority of children and young people who are internet users in Turkey are in regular contact with digital games, with this rate showing a marked increase over the last five years. Adolescents referred to as digital natives born between 1997 and 2012 [11] have grown up immersed in digital technology, rendering them particularly susceptible to digital game addiction from a developmental standpoint. The Ministry of National Education Physical Education Course Curriculum [12] positions traditional Turkish sports as an integral component of the curriculum, granting them a special place in terms of national identity, cultural heritage, and meaning-making functions in physical education lessons [13]. In this context, Guler [14] examined the similarities and differences between digital games and e-sports and traditional sports comparatively, emphasising that the cultural identity transmission function of traditional sports has no equivalent in digital games.

Traditional sports are positioned as a multidimensional phenomenon that influences the holistic development of individuals through shared cultural meanings, emotional experiences, and social integration dimensions [15]. These sports offer life experiences that profoundly shape an individual’s personality and serve as a living medium of cultural heritage transmission. This dual role makes traditional sports worthy of examination within the school curriculum context [16]; they also remain a variable carried on the research agenda with their character development and national identity dimensions [17]. Lavega-Burgues et al. [18] further argued that traditional sporting games serve as a mirror of society, reflecting cultural and pedagogical dimensions that digital environments cannot replicate. In a limited number of Turkey-specific studies, moderate traditional sports attitude mean scores have been reported, and attention has been drawn to the positive relationship between cultural awareness and sports participation intention [19, 20].

Previous studies examining the relationship between physical activity attitude and digital game addiction have generally reported an inverse association [21, 22]. However, the conditions regulating the magnitude and direction of the relationship have not yet been fully understood; findings at the attitude level and profile-based findings remain scarce [13]. While Zhu et al. [23] demonstrated the discriminating function of physical activity level on internet addiction profiles through a person-centered analysis, Bayir and Eskiler [24] reported that school-based programmes aimed at improving physical activity attitude significantly reduced digital addiction risk. In the Turkish context, Marufoglu and KutluTurk [25] demonstrated that digital game addiction negatively affected physical activity habits, and Turktas and Esen [26] revealed the negative relationship between digital game addiction and physical activity level. Demir and Cicioglu [27] found that physical activity participation motivation and digital game playing motivation were negatively related among Turkish adolescents. Namli and Demir [28] showed that digital game attitude and attitude toward sports were positively related; Tuver and Gumus [29] found that digital game motivation restricted leisure-time physical activity in secondary school students. However, studies examining how these variables should differ in subgroups through a person-centered approach have received limited attention.

The theoretical framework of this study consists of two complementary theories. The first is Ryan and Deci’s Self-Determination Theory (SDT) [30, 31]. The theory argues that when an individual’s needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are met, autonomous motivation is strengthened; and that autonomous motivation predicts the sustainability of health-supporting behaviours such as physical activity. Within SDT, these three basic psychological needs are defined as autonomy (the sense of volition and acting in accordance with one’s own values), competence (the sense of being effective and capable in one’s activities), and relatedness (the sense of connection and belonging with others). The satisfaction of these needs is theorised to foster autonomous forms of motivation, whereas their frustration is associated with controlled motivation and amotivation. Prior research framing participation in physical activity and sport through SDT has reported that contexts supporting these needs are linked to more autonomous motivation and more sustained engagement, whereas need-frustrating contexts are associated with disengagement and compensatory behaviours. However, uncontrolled digital game addiction can be understood as a compensatory process that comes into play following the failure of basic psychological needs to be met in physical activity environments [32]. From the SDT perspective, a reverse-directional relationship is expected between traditional sports, which the individual participates in with autonomous motivation, and an exclusive addiction profile; and how this relational pattern is shaped at the profile level remains a question still awaiting an answer in the literature.

The second theoretical framework is the Displacement Hypothesis of Carlson et al. [33]. This framework argues that the individual has a fixed time budget; that as the time allocated to digital screens increases, the time remaining for physical activity will necessarily decrease [33]. Lizandra et al. [34], however, in their three-year longitudinal cohort study conducted with Spanish adolescents, demonstrated that the displacement effect was valid not across the entire sample but only in certain subgroups. This finding indicates that the hypothesis may be a conditional mechanism; no Turkey-specific study testing at the profile level in which subgroups it operates has been encountered.

When SDT and the Displacement Hypothesis are evaluated together, a positive-directional association is expected between traditional sports attitude and positive physical activity attitude; the autonomous motivation component of SDT feeds both. A negative-directional relationship is expected between digital game addiction and physical activity attitude; both the displacement mechanism and the findings of Gulbetekin et al. [21] support this orientation. A negative-directional relationship can also be predicted between traditional sports attitude and digital game addiction, consistent with Kardefelt-Winther’s [32] compensatory model. However, studies testing this dual theoretical framework together with a person-centered analysis remain underexplored in the literature. Variable-centered techniques report average relationships across the sample, rendering individual differentiation patterns and hidden subgroups invisible. Although these three variables have been addressed together in a limited number of LPA studies in the international literature, profile-level findings covering Turkish adolescent samples are still rare.

This study aims to identify, through latent profile analysis (LPA), the hidden profiles that differ qualitatively from one another based on traditional sports attitude, digital game addiction, and physical activity attitude in 634 adolescents attending state high schools in the provinces of Antalya and Osmaniye. Empirical studies that jointly test SDT and the Displacement Hypothesis within a single person-centered design remain scarce. The study makes two distinct contributions. At the theoretical level, it examines whether the configurations predicted by SDT and the Displacement Hypothesis are recoverable as distinct latent profiles in an adolescent sample, thereby clarifying how these frameworks may operate at the subgroup level. At the practical level, the resulting profiles provide a descriptive basis for tailoring physical education and prevention efforts to qualitatively different adolescent subgroups. These two contributions are kept conceptually separate, as the theoretical aim concerns profile structure whereas the practical aim concerns the implications such a structure may hold for intervention. Accordingly, the following hypotheses have been specified:

H1: At least three qualitatively distinct latent profiles will emerge, including (a) a normative profile combining high traditional sports attitude, low digital game addiction, and favourable physical activity attitudes, and (b) at least one risk-oriented profile characterised by less favourable physical activity attitudes.

Method

Research model

The study was conducted with a cross-sectional survey design [35]. Latent Profile Analysis (LPA) was adopted as the basic analytical framework; this preference stems from the fact that it enables the identification of hidden subgroups that cannot be detected by variable-centered approaches [36] in the person-centered domain. LPA places individuals in their profiles through a probabilistic assignment method and can define each profile as a qualitatively different motivational configuration [37]. Considering that variable-centered analyses reflect average relationships across the entire sample, LPA offers a complementary methodological perspective with its capacity to concretize how these relationships may differ in subgroups.

Participants

The participant group of the study consists of 634 adolescents attending in high schools in the provinces of Antalya and Osmaniye during the 2025–2026 academic year. The sample was approximately balanced in terms of gender, comprising 320 female (50.5%) and 314 male (49.5%) participants. The mean age was 15.8 years (SD = 1.21; range = 14–18), which is representative of the high school age band examined in this study rather than the general adolescent population. The grade distribution was as follows: 9th grade 195 (30.8%), 10th grade 131 (20.7%), 11th grade 161 (25.4%), and 12th grade 147 (23.2%). The inclusion of two provinces was intended to enhance socioeconomic and geographical variability within the sample while increasing statistical representation; however, institutional access constraints precluded random assignment, and convenience sampling was adopted. The sample size of N = 634, which is well beyond the N = 300 threshold recommended by LPA simulation studies [37], indicates that sufficient power for analytical stability has been achieved. An a priori power analysis was conducted with G*Power 3.1 software; the minimum required sample size was calculated as 210 under K = 3, medium effect size (f = 0.25), alpha = 0.05, and 1-beta = 0.95. Data collection instruments

Traditional Sports Attitude Scale (TSAS)

The TSAS, developed by Evli et al. [38], is a 21-item five-point Likert scale (1 = Disagree, 5 = Completely Agree) comprising Psychological Development, Physical Development, Social Development, Cognitive Development, and Cultural Development subscales. CFA fit indices were reported as good-level in the original study (χ²/df = 2.47; CFI = 0.96; RMSEA = 0.067). Total Cronbach alpha was 0.864 in the original study; in the current study the internal consistency coefficient was 0.872, with subscale coefficients ranging from 0.758 to 0.831. The subscales assess the extent to which adolescents endorse the developmental and cultural functions of traditional sports. Representative items include “Traditional sports support the transmission of our cultural heritage” (Cultural Development), “Traditional sports contribute to the development of my thinking and decision-making skills” (Cognitive Development), “Traditional sports strengthen my sense of belonging to a group” (Social Development), “Traditional sports help me cope with stress” (Psychological Development), and “Traditional sports improve my physical fitness” (Physical Development). Higher scores reflect a more favourable attitude toward the developmental and cultural value of traditional sports.

Digital Game Addiction Scale (DGAS)

The DGAS, developed by Hazar and Hazar [39], consists of six single-factor items scored on a five-point Likert scale. The internal consistency coefficient was reported as 0.82 in the original study; Cronbach alpha in the current study was 0.804. Higher scores reflect greater addiction tendency. A representative item is “I neglect my other responsibilities because of digital games.”

Physical activity attitude scale for children and youth, Turkish form (PAAS-T)

The original scale developed by Simonton et al. [40] was adapted into Turkish by Uyhan et al. [41]. The scale comprises two subscales, Positive Attitude (7 items) and Negative Attitude (5 items), scored on a five-point Likert scale; negative items are reverse-coded. The Turkish adaptation demonstrated acceptable CFA fit (CFI = 0.97; RMSEA = 0.04). In the current study, Positive Attitude Cronbach alpha was 0.819 and Negative Attitude Cronbach alpha was 0.827. A representative Positive Attitude item is “Being physically active makes me feel good,” and a representative Negative Attitude item is “Physical activity is boring for me.” To clarify the scoring logic, the five Negative Attitude items were reverse-coded so that, after recoding, higher scores on both subscales consistently indicate a more favourable orientation toward physical activity. Accordingly, throughout the Results a higher PA Negative Attitude score reflects the relative absence of unfavourable attitudes (that is, a more positive standing), not a higher level of negative attitude. This recoding ensures that all four indicators share a common interpretive direction in the latent profile analysis.

Procedure

Data collection was carried out between 15 February and 30 March 2026. Approval was obtained from the Bartın University Social and Human Sciences Ethics Committee (Protocol No: 2025-SBB-1240, Date: 11.02.2026). In accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, institutional permission was obtained from school administrators, written consent from parents, and assent from participants. Participation was entirely voluntary. Scales were administered in classroom settings under researcher supervision, requiring approximately 15–20 min.

Data analysis

Analyses were conducted in R 4.6.0. Missing data screening revealed no missing observations (N = 634). Univariate normality was assessed via skewness and kurtosis (all |skewness| < 2.0; |kurtosis| < 7.0) [42]; no multivariate outliers were identified using Mahalanobis distance (p < .001 threshold). LPA models were estimated through the tidyLPA 2.0.2 [43] and mclust 6.1.2 [44] packages. Indicator variables were TSAS, DGAS, PAAS Positive Attitude, and PAAS Negative Attitude total scores; TSAS subscales were addressed in post-hoc comparisons after the K = 3 solution was determined. EEI (equal variances, zero covariances) was selected as the covariance structure. BIC-based systematic comparison through mclust (EEI: −4926.24; EEV: −4912.27; VEI: −4847.33; VVI: −4821.27; VVV: −4771.27; VEV: −4750.87) revealed that EEI was the most appropriate structure for K = 3. The VEV structure was rejected on the grounds of insufficient entropy (Entropy = 0.588); EEI with high classification quality was adopted [5, 37]. Models between K = 1 and K = 6 were compared using AIC, BIC, BLRT significance (p < .01), entropy (≥ 0.80), and minimum profile size (≥ 5%) criteria.

After profiles were determined, one-way ANOVA was applied to test between-profile differences. Welch ANOVA and Games-Howell post-hoc test were applied for variables where the variance homogeneity assumption was violated; Tukey HSD was applied otherwise. Effect sizes were reported as eta-squared (η²) and Cohen d; statistical significance threshold was set at alpha = 0.05. Post-hoc power analysis (G*Power 3.1, k = 3, N = 634, alpha = 0.05) showed that a power level of 1 − β = 1.000 was achieved even for the variable with the smallest effect (DGA; η² = 0.105).

Examining Table 4, BIC shows a marked improvement at K = 3 (ΔBIC = 84.94), with rapidly slowing improvement from K = 4 onwards. The K = 3 solution offers good classification quality with entropy = 0.816. In models with K ≥ 4, minimum profile size falls below 5%, suggesting possible weakening of parameter stability. When all criteria are jointly evaluated, the K = 3 solution was concluded to be the most appropriate and interpretable model. The decision to retain the three-profile solution over the four-profile alternative rested not on statistical indices alone but on the joint consideration of statistical, parsimony, and substantive criteria. Although the four-profile model produced a marginally lower BIC, the additional fourth profile contained only 29 cases (4.6%), falling below the recommended minimum profile size of 5% and thereby raising concerns about parameter stability and replicability. Substantively, the fourth profile did not represent a theoretically distinct configuration; rather, it split the Active-Healthy group into two profiles that differed only in degree along the same indicators, without introducing a qualitatively new motivational pattern. The three-profile solution, by contrast, yielded profiles that were each interpretable in light of Self-Determination Theory and the Displacement Hypothesis, including the theoretically informative Conflicted-Addicted profile. Following established recommendations that profile enumeration should balance statistical fit with parsimony and theoretical meaningfulness, the three-profile solution was therefore preferred.

Examining Table 5, the Active-Healthy profile stands out with excellent classification quality (mean posterior probability = 0.948). The Conflicted-Addicted and At-Risk profiles also remain above the acceptable classification quality threshold (0.820 and 0.870, respectively). The fact that mean posterior probabilities in all three profiles exceed the 0.80 threshold indicates that profile assignments are reliable.

Post-hoc comparisons

Levene’s test showed that the variance homogeneity assumption was not met for TSAS Psychological, TSAS Physical, PA Positive Attitude, and PA Negative Attitude variables; Games-Howell test was applied for these. Tukey HSD was used for other variables. Post-hoc comparisons are presented in Table 7.

Table 7 highlights three critical findings. No statistically significant difference is found between the Active-Healthy and Conflicted-Addicted profiles across all TSAS subscales (|d| = 0.01 to 0.16; p = .314–0.994); traditional sports attitude cannot differentiate the two profiles. However, both profiles show marked differentiation from the At-Risk profile (TSAS subscales: mean diff. = −1.04 to − 1.01; p < .001). The second critical finding appears in DGA: the difference between Active-Healthy and At-Risk profiles remains at the boundary of statistical significance (difference = 0.248; p = .120); DGA functions only as the variable differentiating the Conflicted-Addicted profile from the other two. The third and notable finding belongs to PA Negative Attitude, which showed the largest effect size across all pairwise profile comparisons (η² = 0.621); this points to the central position of negative attitude in profile differentiation.

Profile membership and demographic variables

The association of profile membership with gender and grade level was examined using chi-square tests of independence in order to assess whether the identified profiles reflected a demographic structure rather than a behavioural one. As presented in Table 8, profile membership did not differ significantly by gender, χ²(2, N = 634) = 0.92, p = .633, Cramer’s V = 0.038, with female and male adolescents distributed in broadly similar proportions across the three profiles. Likewise, the association between profile membership and grade level was not statistically significant, χ²(6, N = 634) = 9.24, p = .161, Cramer’s V = 0.085. These results indicate that the three profiles were largely independent of the demographic composition of the sample, supporting the interpretation that the profiles capture qualitatively distinct behavioural and attitudinal configurations rather than artefacts of gender or grade distribution.

Discussion

Profile structures and comparison with the literature

The main finding indicates that traditional sports attitude, digital game addiction, and physical activity attitude among adolescents do not form a single homogeneous type; rather, they appear to form three qualitatively distinct, internally consistent latent profiles. This finding may contribute to a more nuanced methodological understanding of profile differentiation: variable-centered analyses report average relationships rendering individual-level heterogeneity invisible. Profile definition through LPA concretely reveals which adolescents are at risk, which have protective characteristics, and where the critical differences lie. While H1 and H2 are supported, the existence of the Conflicted-Addicted profile presents an original pattern under H3 allowing questioning of how SDT and the Displacement Hypothesis operate at the profile level: high traditional sports attitude and high digital game addiction can coexist within the same profile.

The Active-Healthy profile (73.7%), displaying high traditional sports attitude, low digital game addiction, and positive means across both physical activity dimensions, is consistent with the general findings of the literature. Considering the findings revealing that traditional sports attitude in Turkey is at a moderate level [19], the fact that approximately three-quarters of the sample is represented by this profile is evaluated as a positive picture. It is stated that developing positive attitudes and behaviours toward physical activity is important in reducing digital game addiction [24]; similarly, Gulbetekin et al. [21] found a significant negative-directional relationship between physical activity attitude and digital game addiction. The pattern in the Active-Healthy profile concretises these relational findings at the profile level. Skripkauskaite and Fazel [45] reported that the majority of intensive gamers in a large English adolescent sample did not experience negative health effects; this finding is a global parallel of the Active-Healthy profile. The concentration of approximately three-quarters of the sample in this profile may indicate that physical education and sports participation culture in the provinces of Antalya and Osmaniye follows a relatively positive course; however, this interpretation needs to be tested by studies covering different provinces and school types.

The Conflicted-Addicted profile (15.9%) is one of the most noteworthy findings of the study. This profile carries nearly identical traditional sports attitude to the Active-Healthy profile alongside the highest digital game addiction in the sample (M = 3.31). It should be noted that motivational constructs were not measured in this study; therefore, the following SDT-based reading is offered as an interpretive account rather than a tested account. Viewed through the SDT lens, a high attitude toward traditional sports may be read as broadly aligned with autonomous motivation, while high digital game addiction may reflect a behavioural orientation more consistent with amotivation or external regulation [30]. SDT argues that the satisfaction of basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) is domain-specific for individuals; a need met in one domain does not eliminate the compensation for a deficiency in another domain. Indeed, Kardefelt-Winther [32] directly addresses this: an individual’s autonomous participation within a specific context does not close the psychological need gap in a different context, and therefore compensatory seeking in another platform can continue. Because the present design did not directly measure autonomous or controlled motivation, need satisfaction, or need frustration, SDT is drawn upon here as an interpretive lens rather than as a framework empirically tested in this study. With this caveat in mind, the coexistence observed in the Conflicted-Addicted profile is consistent with, and may be read as illustrative of, this theoretical prediction at the profile level, though it does not by itself constitute empirical confirmation of the proposed mechanism. Avoidance motivation is a prominent mechanism in the background of this coexistence. In a multi-center study conducted with adolescents aged 14–18 in Turkey, it was revealed that problematic internet use could fulfil an avoidance function from real-life stressors and that this avoidance tendency was associated with social media addiction [46]. The cultural and social belonging provided by traditional sports cannot always protect an individual from this avoidance process in the digital environment. Indeed, it has been reported that digital game addiction levels can be high in individuals who engage in regular sports [22]. Zhang et al. [47] classified gaming disorder in Chinese adolescents through LPA into four profiles; the most marked distinction between profiles emerged not in DGA severity but in uncontrolled gaming behaviours. This finding indicates that DGA level alone is not a profile determinant; the large effect of PA Negative Attitude in profile differentiation in the current study (η² = 0.621) is consistent with this suggestion.

The At-Risk profile (10.4%) represents the group with the lowest values on all positive life variables and the highest negative physical activity attitude. Notably, digital game addiction did not differentiate this profile from Active-Healthy (p = .120), which suggests that addiction level alone may be insufficient as a profile criterion. This central function of negative PA attitude can be understood through Bandura’s [48] self-efficacy theory. As self-efficacy was not measured in this study, the following account is presented as a theoretical extrapolation consistent with this framework rather than as a mechanism tested by the present data. Within this interpretive frame, belittling feedback, peer comparison, or repeated failure experiences in physical education lessons may consolidate low self-efficacy perceptions, which could in turn contribute to the establishment of a negative PA attitude. This attitude can become a decisive profile characteristic facilitating avoidance of physical activity and directing leisure time toward digital environments. Gulu et al. [49] reported that physical activity was an independent predictor reducing digital game addiction risk by 1.51 times; Parker et al. [50] reported that profile membership remained stable above 83% throughout high school. Zhu et al. [23] demonstrated that physical activity level markedly differentiates internet addiction profiles in the same three-profile LPA structure; the current finding reinforces this evidence base at the attitude level.

The existence of the Conflicted-Addicted profile also presents important findings from the perspective of the Displacement Hypothesis. The time budget argument of Carlson et al. [33] argues that as digital screen time increases, physical activity time will necessarily decrease. It should be emphasised that the present design did not include time-use or screen-time measures and therefore did not directly test displacement mechanisms. With this limitation in mind, the Conflicted-Addicted profile appears difficult to reconcile with a simple displacement account: the observation that adolescents with high traditional sports attitudes can simultaneously report the highest digital game addiction level may suggest that, if displacement operates, it does so under certain conditions rather than uniformly across the sample. This possibility is offered as a hypothesis for future longitudinal or time-use studies rather than as a conclusion established by the present data. Lizandra et al. [34] revealed that SEM analysis with Spanish adolescents did not support the displacement hypothesis for the entire sample; only a delayed effect was observed in males. Nowotarski et al. [51] longitudinally demonstrated that intense physical activity significantly reduced internet gaming disorder risk; this effect was not significant for moderate-level activity. The fact that profile membership was not examined by gender in the current study limits the testing of this conditionality; future studies focusing on this dimension have the potential to strengthen the theoretical contribution.

Post-hoc analyses show that the contribution of PA Negative Attitude to profile differentiation is larger than that of DGA (η² = 0.621). It is worth noting that, within the present findings, digital game addiction did not function as a general or universally differentiating factor across the profile solution; its discriminative role was largely confined to the Conflicted-Addicted profile and did not separate the Active-Healthy and At-Risk groups, so it is more accurately described as having a profile-specific, conditional role than as a general risk indicator. This finding highlights that the negative dimension of physical activity attitude may warrant attention as an independent variable in digital addiction research. It should also be acknowledged that positive and negative physical activity attitudes need not be simple opposites; they may coexist within the same individual and reflect qualitatively different motivational or experiential processes. Negative physical activity attitude may therefore be better understood as an indicator of ambivalence or controlled motivation rather than merely as the inverse of positive attitude. Although the direction of the relationship between PA Negative Attitude and digital addiction is open to debate, the current cross-sectional data are consistent with a bidirectional reading: the negative attitude consolidated during physical education experiences may push the individual toward inactivity and toward seeking compensation in the digital environment, just as increasing digital addiction may over time affect PA attitude. The findings of Altinisik et al. [52] reporting that attitude decreases as addiction increases support the second direction, though determining the direction of causality requires longitudinal data. Helvaci and Tayhan [13] reveal that the relationship between digital addiction and physical activity enjoyment shows different modes of operation in some subgroups. The strong differentiation of PA Negative Attitude of both the At-Risk and Conflicted-Addicted profiles from Active-Healthy indicates that this variable needs to be positioned as a central profile-constituting dimension in digital addiction research. Longitudinal data are critically important for determining the direction of this relationship.

Theoretical contributions

The theoretical contribution of this study is shaped at three levels. First, the existence of the Conflicted-Addicted profile tests the literature discussion related to SDT and Kardefelt-Winther’s [32] compensatory model at the profile level. SDT [30] predicts that autonomous motivation is domain-specific; autonomous participation in one context does not automatically close the psychological need gap in a different context. The present profile structure is consistent with this prediction in the person-centered domain with a Turkish adolescent sample; because motivational constructs were not directly measured, this is best read as an interpretive alignment rather than direct empirical support. Second, the findings raise the possibility that the mechanism of the Displacement Hypothesis [33] is valid not across the entire sample but in certain subgroups. This result is consistent with the variable-centered findings of Lizandra et al. [34] and the person-centered findings of Skripkauskaite and Fazel [45] and Parker et al. [50], contributing to the growing body of evidence that the Displacement Hypothesis is a conditional mechanism in the Turkish context. Similarly, Zhao et al. [53] classified Chinese adolescents’ motivation profiles with LPA in a K = 3 solution and showed that the profile with high autonomous motivation was more strongly associated with PA level. Third, negative PA attitude is established as a profile-constituting dimension not merely a relational variable that separates adolescents into different risk categories. This finding is a methodological directive for the systematic addressing of PA attitude as an independent variable in digital addiction research.

Practical contributions

The findings suggest several complementary profile-based implications for physical education policy and clinical intervention; because the effectiveness of the practices outlined below was not tested within this study, they are presented as potential implications and hypotheses for future evaluation rather than as evidence-based recommendations. For the Conflicted-Addicted profile, a plausible intervention objective is to reduce avoidance motivation while maintaining cultural participation. Physical education teachers and school counsellors can conduct individual assessments aimed at understanding what adolescents in this profile are seeking in the digital environment. In line with the findings of Yildirim Demirdogen et al. [46], cognitive behavioral psychoeducational studies targeting avoidance motivation and digital literacy programmed integrated with peer-led traditional sports activities are of application value for this profile [12]. For the At-Risk profile, priority should be placed on transforming negative PA attitude at an early age. In line with Bandura’s [48] self-efficacy theory, mastery-focused physical education practices minimizing belittling comparisons and supporting individual competence awareness can be functional in breaking the low self-efficacy spiral [48, 49]. Considering the finding of Parker et al. [50] that profile membership remained stable above 83% throughout adolescence, it is understood that opening the intervention window in the last grades of primary school and during the middle school period is of critical importance in terms of long-term impact.

Limitations

Several limitations should be taken into account. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference; why individuals in the Conflicted-Addicted profile display this unique coexistence should be examined through longitudinal studies. Second, convenience sampling from two provinces limits generalizability to the broader Turkish adolescent population; rural areas and different socioeconomic contexts may yield different profile structures. In particular, the relatively high prevalence of the Active-Healthy profile (73.7%) may partly reflect the specific regional and school culture of the two provinces sampled, and should not be assumed to generalise to the national adolescent population; this prevalence estimate therefore warrants cautious interpretation. Third, the mean posterior probability for the Conflicted-Addicted profile (0.820) is at the lower boundary of acceptable classification quality; the classification uncertainty of this profile’s members at the individual level may be relatively high. Fourth, several potentially relevant variables were not modelled; in particular, socioeconomic status, parental monitoring, screen-time duration, and sleep quality were not assessed, and their omission may limit the completeness of the profile characterisation. Future studies could incorporate these variables and determine profile membership predictors using the R3STEP or BCH method. Fifth, the identification of negative physical activity attitude as the strongest discriminating variable reflects its between-profile separating power rather than a confirmed causal driver of digital addiction risk; the present design does not establish causal priority, and this variable should not be interpreted as a demonstrated causal antecedent. Finally, the study is based on self-assessment scales; objective measurements regarding digital game playing duration (screen time records) are not available.

Recommendations for future research

The findings present four concrete directives. First, the stability of the Conflicted-Addicted profile over time should be monitored through longitudinal studies; whether this profile is a transient developmental stage or a stable direction will directly affect the timing of the intervention window. Second, the role of the gender variable on profile membership should be examined; it is worth illuminating how the differentiation observed between female and male adolescents in digital game habits and physical activity attitude is reflected in profile structures. Third, the link between the three profiles and distal outcomes such as sleep quality, academic motivation, and psychological well-being should be tested with three-stage LPA designs. Fourth, experimental school-based programmed directly targeting PA Negative Attitude should be tested with randomized controlled designs to clarify the causal role of this variable and contribute to evidence-based intervention development.

Conclusion

This study indicates that traditional sports attitude, digital game addiction, and physical activity attitude among adolescents are organised into three qualitatively different profiles. While the Active-Healthy profile covers the majority of the sample, the Conflicted-Addicted profile displays an unexpected pattern carrying the highest digital game addiction level despite high traditional sports attitude, and the At-Risk profile represents the most vulnerable position across all positive life variables. Two findings are noteworthy: traditional sports attitude does not meaningfully differentiate the Active-Healthy and Conflicted-Addicted profiles, whereas negative physical activity attitude emerged as the strongest variable in profile differentiation. Taken together, these findings suggest that a high attitude toward traditional sports cannot be considered a universally protective indicator against digital addiction risk, and that negative physical activity attitude, having emerged as the strongest discriminating variable between profiles, may be a promising focus for intervention efforts beyond the cultural participation dimension. As the present design is cross-sectional, this is offered as a direction for future evaluation rather than as a confirmed causal target. The profile-based perspective expands the possibility of addressing digital addiction risk during adolescence by considering individual differences and points subsequent studies toward longitudinal and experimental designs.

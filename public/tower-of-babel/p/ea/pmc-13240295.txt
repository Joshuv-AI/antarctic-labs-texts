Beyond the Triple‐Negative Label: A Case Report on Metaplastic Breast Carcinoma
Authors: Samana Oli, Ayush Sapkota, Sajjad Ahmed Khan, Asmita Rayamajhi, Dhiraj Gupta, Vivek Ghosh, Diksha Karki, Rikesh Acharya, Prajina Shrestha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMetaplastic breast carcinoma (MpBC) is a rare, aggressive breast malignancy with poorer prognosis than conventional subtypes. Predominant hematogenous spread, frequent triple‐negative phenotype, chemoresistance, rarity, and poorly defined treatment protocols render MpBC a significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenge for oncologists in contemporary clinical oncology practice worldwide.

Introduction

First described by Huvos et al. in 1974 [1], metaplastic breast carcinoma (MpBC) represents an extremely rare and highly heterogeneous subgroup of invasive breast cancers. Histopathologically, MpBC is characterized by the presence of mixed epithelial and mesenchymal components, encompassing a wide spectrum that includes squamous differentiation as well as mesenchymal elements, such as spindle cells, bone, or cartilage. This marked morphological diversity contributes significantly to its biological aggressiveness and clinical unpredictability [2]. MpBC was formally recognized as a distinct pathological entity by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2000, with subsequent refinement of its classification based on histopathological patterns in 2012 [3]. The most recent WHO classification update in 2019 further reinforced MpBC as a special type of invasive carcinoma, clearly distinct from conventional invasive ductal carcinoma, and associated with an aggressive disease course and unfavorable prognosis [4].

The pronounced histopathological heterogeneity of MpBC often leads to diagnostic challenges, particularly on conventional imaging modalities, such as ultrasonography and mammography. Tumors may lack typical radiological features of malignancy and instead mimic a broad range of benign and malignant entities, from fibroadenomas to primary breast sarcomas, resulting in delayed or incorrect diagnosis [5]. At the molecular level, this heterogeneity is largely attributed to dysregulation of key cell‐cycle and signaling pathways, including alterations in the PI3/AKT pathway, aberrant activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway, and frequent TP53 mutations, all of which are implicated in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance [6].

Although over 90% of MpBCs exhibit a triple‐negative immunohistochemical profile [7], their clinical behavior differs substantially from conventional triple‐negative breast cancers. MpBC demonstrates a propensity for early hematogenous dissemination rather than lymphatic spread, leading to early distant metastasis even in the absence of axillary lymph node involvement [8]. Due to its rarity and the consequent lack of large prospective studies, there are currently no standardized treatment guidelines specific to MpBC [9]. As a result, therapeutic strategies are often extrapolated from protocols designed for triple‐negative breast cancer, despite accumulating evidence suggesting limited responsiveness to standard chemotherapy and the need for tailored treatment approaches.

Case History and Examination

A 56‐year‐old postmenopausal woman with no comorbidities and no family history of malignancy presented with a rapidly enlarging lump in the right breast over a duration of 3 months. There were no associated symptoms, such as erythema, nipple retraction, pain, or nipple discharge. Clinical breast examination revealed a firm to hard mass measuring approximately 4 × 4 cm in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast, with associated thickening of the overlying skin. The lesion was fixed to the chest wall. No palpable axillary or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy was noted.

Differential Diagnosis, Investigations and Treatment

Based on the clinical presentation of a rapidly growing breast mass with fixation to the chest wall and absence of lymphadenopathy, differential diagnoses included invasive ductal carcinoma, metaplastic carcinoma, primary breast sarcoma, phyllodes tumor, and squamous cell carcinoma of the breast.

Ultrasonography of both breasts and axillae demonstrated a well‐defined heterogeneous solid lesion measuring 3.8 × 3.8 cm in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast, with internal vascularity in the solid component, internal echoes, and a cystic component extending from the 7 to 12 o'clock position toward the nipple–areolar complex, suggestive of malignancy (BIRADS category 4B). The left breast and bilateral axillae were unremarkable. Subsequent mammography confirmed BIRADS category 4B for the right breast and BIRADS Category 1 for the left breast. Core needle biopsy revealed features consistent with metaplastic carcinoma. Immunohistochemical profiling showed triple‐negative status (ER–, PR–, and HER2–).

The patient was initiated on neoadjuvant chemotherapy following protocols for triple‐negative breast cancer. She received four cycles of dose‐dense doxorubicin (60 mg/m2) and cyclophosphamide (600 mg/m2) administered at 2‐week intervals, followed by four cycles of paclitaxel (175 mg/m2) given every 2 weeks. Despite the traditionally reported chemoresistance of MpBC, the tumor demonstrated a partial clinical response.

Following completion of neoadjuvant chemotherapy, the patient underwent a modified radical mastectomy, and the surgical specimen was submitted for histopathological evaluation. Microscopic examination revealed a cystic structure lined predominantly by dysplastic squamous epithelium, with focal areas of granulation tissue, multinucleated giant cells, and histiocytes (Figure 1). The dysplastic tumor cells were arranged in solid sheets, nests, and as scattered individual cells infiltrating the subepithelial fibrocollagenous stroma. Tumor cells exhibited moderate pleomorphism, increased nucleocytoplasmic ratio, round to ovoid nuclei with irregular nuclear membranes, vesicular chromatin, prominent eosinophilic nucleoli, and abundant pale, glassy cytoplasm with intercellular bridges. Binucleated and multinucleated tumor cells, frequent atypical mitotic figures, and keratin pearl formation were noted. The surrounding stroma was fibrotic and infiltrated by chronic inflammatory cells, including lymphocytes, histiocytes, plasma cells, and foreign body–type giant cells, along with dilated and congested blood vessels. Based on these findings, the tumor was diagnosed as metaplastic carcinoma with squamous differentiation and staged as ypT3pN0.

Outcomes and Follow‐Up

Postoperatively, the patient received adjuvant radiotherapy to the ipsilateral chest wall and regional lymphatics. A total dose of 45 Gy was delivered in 15 fractions over 3 weeks using a hypofractionated schedule with external beam radiotherapy to achieve locoregional disease control. At 3 months of follow‐up after completion of radiotherapy, the patient was clinically asymptomatic, had an ECOG performance status of 0, and showed a well‐healed surgical scar with no evidence of residual or recurrent disease, skin thickening, or locoregional lymphadenopathy. She is currently enrolled in a structured survivorship program with planned regular clinical assessments and radiological surveillance.

Discussion

The principal challenge in the management of metaplastic breast carcinoma (MpBC) stems from its extremely low incidence among breast malignancies. This rarity results in statistical underrepresentation, which in turn contributes to the absence of standardized, evidence‐based treatment protocols [10]. Consequently, treating oncologists are often compelled to extrapolate therapeutic strategies from conventional triple‐negative breast cancer (TNBC) or invasive ductal carcinoma regimens. However, such approaches may inadequately address the intrinsic biological differences of MpBC, which is widely recognized for its relative resistance to standard chemotherapeutic agents [11].

Among the various histological subtypes, the squamous variant of MpBC has been reported to be associated with a comparatively better prognosis than other metaplastic subtypes [12]. In one of the largest population‐based analyses, Polamraju et al. demonstrated that in non‐metastatic disease, the 5‐year overall survival (OS) for MpBC was 60.5%, compared with 65.2% for TNBC [13]. This survival disparity was even more pronounced in the metastatic setting, where the 5‐year OS was 42.7% for MpBC versus 56% for TNBC [13]. Several studies have further highlighted that different histological subtypes of MpBC exhibit variable responses to chemotherapy, underscoring the biological heterogeneity of this entity [14].

Despite these differences, there remains broad clinical agreement on the use of dose‐dense, sequential anthracycline‐ and taxane‐based neoadjuvant chemotherapy as the foundational systemic treatment for MpBC, largely due to the lack of superior alternatives and limited prospective data [6]. Given the increased propensity of MpBC for local recurrence, surgical management continues to be the cornerstone of treatment, with an emphasis on achieving optimal locoregional disease control through aggressive local clearance [15].

In recent years, a notable shift in management has emerged, with growing consensus supporting the routine use of adjuvant radiotherapy in MpBC. Evidence from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database analyzing MpBC cases from 1973 to 2015 demonstrated that radiotherapy was associated with a significant improvement in overall survival (hazard ratio = 0.64; p < 0.001) [16]. Although immunotherapy remains an investigational approach in MpBC, emerging molecular data suggest potential future applications. Notably, Joneja et al. reported frequent overexpression of programmed death‐ligand 1 (PD‐L1) in MpBC, indicating a possible role for immune checkpoint inhibitors in selected patients and highlighting the need for further translational and clinical research in this area [17].

The principal strength of the present case lies in the comprehensive multimodality management and detailed histopathological characterization of a rare MpBC subtype with squamous differentiation, along with documentation of a partial response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which is uncommonly reported in this disease entity. However, this report is limited by its single‐case design, short duration of follow‐up, and the inherent inability to generalize outcomes or establish causal associations between treatment modalities and survival. The primary takeaway from this case is that, despite its recognized chemoresistance, MpBC may still demonstrate clinically meaningful responses to standard TNBC‐based systemic therapy when combined with optimal surgery and adjuvant radiotherapy. This underscores the importance of aggressive multimodal treatment and highlights the pressing need for larger prospective studies and molecularly guided therapeutic strategies to refine and personalize the management of MpBC in the future.

Conclusion

Metaplastic breast carcinoma is an uncommon and highly aggressive variant of breast cancer, distinguished by significant histopathological diversity, resistance to conventional chemotherapy, and an overall unfavorable prognosis. This case demonstrates that, even in the absence of well‐established treatment protocols, a comprehensive multimodal strategy comprising neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgical intervention, and adjuvant radiotherapy may result in effective locoregional disease control and encouraging short‐term outcomes. The favorable clinical response observed underscores the necessity for tailored, patient‐specific management approaches and highlights the value of documenting such cases to enhance the existing body of evidence. Further prospective research, particularly focusing on molecularly targeted therapies, is crucial for refining treatment strategies and improving long‐term survival in patients with metaplastic breast carcinoma.

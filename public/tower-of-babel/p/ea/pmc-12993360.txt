Bimekizumab longer-term safety profile in adult patients with axial spondyloarthritis or psoriatic arthritis: an updated analysis of six phase IIb/III clinical studies
Authors: Philip J Mease, Joseph F Merola, Marina Magrey, Peter Nash, Denis Poddubnyy, Mark Lebwohl, Rajan Bajracharya, Barbara Ink, Alexander Marten, Myriam Manente, Luke Peterson, Katy White, Lianne S Gensler
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo present updated integrated safety analyses of bimekizumab in patients with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) or psoriatic arthritis (PsA).MethodsSafety data pooled from six phase IIb/III studies in axSpA and PsA (data-cut: July 2023 for phase III) reported for patients who received ≥1 dose of bimekizumab 160 mg every 4 weeks. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported using exposure-adjusted incidence rate per 100 patient-years (EAIR/100 PY).Results848 patients with axSpA (total exposure: 2513.8 PY) and 1409 patients with PsA (3655.9 PY) were included. TEAE incidence (EAIR/100 PY) was 129.6 in axSpA and 126.9 in PsA. Study discontinuations due to TEAEs were infrequent (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 2.4; PsA: 2.9). The most frequent TEAEs were SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) infection (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 9.9; PsA: 9.9), nasopharyngitis (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 8.4; PsA: 6.8) and upper respiratory tract infection (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 5.0; PsA: 5.7). EAIR/100 PY for oral candidiasis was 3.5 in axSpA and 3.8 in PsA; most cases were mild/moderate, with few leading to study discontinuation (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.2; PsA: 0.3). Serious opportunistic infections were infrequent (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0; PsA: 0.1), with no active tuberculosis. EAIR/100 PY for hepatic events was 5.3 in axSpA and 5.0 in PsA. EAIRs for adjudicated definite/probable inflammatory bowel disease, uveitis, adjudicated major adverse cardiovascular events and adjudicated suicidal ideation/behaviour were low.ConclusionTEAE EAIRs were similar between axSpA and PsA. Bimekizumab demonstrated tolerability up to 5 years (2 years in phase III); no new safety signals were identified.Trial registration numbersNCT02963506; NCT03355573; NCT03928704; NCT03928743; NCT04436640; NCT02969525; NCT03347110; NCT03895203; NCT03896581; NCT04009499.

Bimekizumab, a humanised monoclonal IgG1 antibody that selectively inhibits interleukin (IL)-17F in addition to IL-17A, has demonstrated superior efficacy versus placebo in patients with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) and psoriatic arthritis (PsA). Bimekizumab has also been found to be generally well tolerated across indications. WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

With an additional 1 year of bimekizumab exposure from the ongoing phase III open-label extension studies, the long-term safety profile of bimekizumab in patients with axSpA or PsA remained consistent with prior analyses, therefore demonstrating the long-term tolerability of bimekizumab treatment. Incidence rates of treatment-emergent adverse events were comparable between the axSpA and PsA patient populations. No new safety signals were identified in this analysis.

Introduction

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) and psoriatic arthritis (PsA) are chronic, immune-mediated diseases.1 2 AxSpA, which encompasses both radiographic (r-)axSpA and non-radiographic (nr-)axSpA, predominantly affects the axial skeleton and often manifests as inflammatory back pain, peripheral arthritis, enthesitis and extra-musculoskeletal manifestations, which commonly include inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), psoriasis and acute anterior uveitis. In addition to these clinical symptoms, patients with r-axSpA have definite radiographic damage in the sacroiliac joints, in contrast with patients with nr-axSpA.1 3 Meanwhile, predominant peripheral symptoms, including distal interphalangeal joint involvement and dactylitis, are characteristic of PsA.3 AxSpA and PsA are grouped under spondyloarthritis (SpA), as they have shared aetiopathogenesis, overlapping clinical characteristics, such as joint inflammation, pain, peripheral arthritis, psoriasis and reduced functional ability, and similar treatment options.3 4 Due to the chronic nature of axSpA and PsA, and their impact on daily activities as well as mental health,5 6 it is important for treatments to demonstrate an acceptable long-term safety profile.

Bimekizumab is a humanised monoclonal IgG1 antibody that selectively inhibits interleukin (IL)-17F in addition to IL-17A; both cytokines contribute to chronic inflammation and have been implicated in the pathogenesis of axSpA,7 PsA,8 9 psoriasis9 and hidradenitis suppurativa.10 Bimekizumab has demonstrated superior efficacy versus placebo in axSpA, PsA and hidradenitis suppurativa,1114 as well as versus placebo and comparator IL-17 inhibitors (IL-17i), IL-12/23 inhibitors (IL-12/23i) and tumour necrosis factor inhibitors (TNFi) in patients with plaque psoriasis.1518 Bimekizumab has also been found to be generally well tolerated across indications for 160 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W) in patients with axSpA19 20 and PsA,21 22 and at higher doses of 320 mg Q4W or Q8W in patients with psoriasis23 and 320 mg Q2W or Q4W in patients with hidradenitis suppurativa.24 Although an increased incidence of mild to moderate oral candidiasis has been observed with bimekizumab treatment,141924 this is expected due to the role of IL-17 signalling in antifungal immunity25 26; the safety profile of bimekizumab has otherwise been shown to be in line with other biologic and targeted synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs).27 28

Evaluating integrated safety data can provide useful insights into the management of patients across the SpA spectrum. A previous integrated safety analysis has demonstrated a consistent and favourable safety profile across phase IIb/III studies in patients with axSpA and PsA.29 The previous analysis reported data from phase IIb/III studies, including 52 weeks of study participation for patients in ongoing phase III open-label extension (OLE) studies.29 Continually evaluating the safety of treatments over a longer duration is valuable for physicians’ and patients’ decision-making.

This analysis presents updated integrated safety analyses across phase IIb/III studies of bimekizumab in axSpA and PsA. Data are reported using the same analysis set,29 but with up to an additional 1 year of bimekizumab exposure from the ongoing phase III OLE studies; this corresponds to at least 104 weeks of total study participation for patients in the ongoing phase III OLE studies. This integrated analysis consolidates data from previously published axSpA and PsA-specific datasets while reporting additional safety outcomes that benefit from a large patient pool.1922 These data will provide a comprehensive and updated overview of bimekizumab’s safety profile to support clinical decision-making.

Methods

Patient population

Safety data were pooled from phase IIb/III studies, and their respective OLE studies, of bimekizumab in axSpA and PsA (online supplemental figure 1). Included patients and pooling methods align with the previously reported pooled safety data across patients with axSpA and PsA29; however, patients in the current analysis had up to 1 additional year of bimekizumab exposure relative to the previous data cut (total of up to 5 years of bimekizumab treatment for axSpA and up to 3 years for PsA; both including up to 104 weeks from the ongoing phase III studies). At the time of the data cut reported here (July 2023), all phase IIb studies and their OLEs had completed. Patients in the phase III studies had completed their final study visit, and the respective OLEs for patients with axSpA and PsA were ongoing.

Full inclusion and exclusion criteria have been reported previously.1213 3032 In brief, patients were ≥18 years of age and clinically diagnosed with moderate to severe axSpA (BE AGILE (NCT02963506), BE MOBILE 1 (NCT03928704), BE MOBILE 2 (NCT03928743)) or PsA (BE ACTIVE (NCT02969525), BE OPTIMAL (NCT03895203), BE COMPLETE (NCT03896581)). In BE MOBILE 1, patients had nr-axSpA. In BE AGILE and BE MOBILE 2, patients had r-axSpA (ie, ankylosing spondylitis). In BE OPTIMAL, patients were biologic DMARD (bDMARD)-naïve. In BE COMPLETE, patients had inadequate response or intolerance to treatment with 1 or 2 TNFi for either PsA or psoriasis (TNFi-IR). Patients were eligible to enter the OLEs (axSpA: BE AGILE 2 (NCT03355573), BE MOVING (NCT04436640); PsA: BE ACTIVE 2 (NCT03347110), BE VITAL (NCT04009499)) if they completed the relevant feeder studies without meeting any discontinuation criteria.

Study design

Full study designs have been reported previously (online supplemental table 1).1922 Following study completion or early discontinuation, patients attended a safety follow-up visit 20 weeks after their final dose of study medication.

Safety evaluations

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were defined as adverse events (AEs) that occurred on or following the first dose of study treatment through the ≤140 days after the final dose (covering the 20-week safety follow-up period). For patients initially randomised to placebo, adalimumab or other bimekizumab dosing regimens, only TEAEs that occurred during treatment with bimekizumab 160 mg Q4W are reported. TEAEs were coded according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA), V.19.0; the intensity of TEAEs was assessed by the investigators as ‘mild’, ‘moderate’ or ‘severe’, independently from seriousness.

Safety topics of interest were pre-specified in the study protocols; these were chosen based on findings from the clinical development programme to date, potential risks generally associated with biological immunomodulators, or comorbidities and risk factors prevalent in the study population. These topics included infections (serious, fungal and opportunistic (including active tuberculosis)), elevated liver enzyme/liver function test results (hepatic events), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), malignancies, hypersensitivity (including anaphylactic and injection site reactions), neutropenia and suicidal ideation or behaviour (SIB). MACE, SIB and IBD events were adjudicated by relevant independent adjudication committees. Although not a pre-specified safety topic of interest, uveitis events (including the preferred terms ‘Autoimmune uveitis’, ‘Iridocyclitis’, ‘Iritis’ and ‘Uveitis’), were also analysed, owing to the frequency with which uveitis presents in patients with axSpA.

Mental health evaluations

Mental health evaluations, assessing suicidal ideation and depression severity, are reported from the phase III studies. SIB was assessed using the electronic Columbia‍-‍Suicide Severity Rating Scale (eC-SSRS), a patient-reported, self-administered, standardised, validated instrument.33 34 Questions 1–5 measure suicidal ideation and questions 6–9 measure suicidal behaviour.

Depression severity was assessed in the phase III studies using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), a patient-reported measure. Scores range from 0 to 27; scores of 15 and 20 demarcate the lower limits of moderately severe and severe depression.35

The schedule of assessment for all phase III studies, and their respective OLE studies, can be found in online supplemental table 2.

Statistical analyses

TEAEs, serious AEs and safety topics of interest are reported across all treatment periods for all patients who received ≥1 dose of bimekizumab 160 mg Q4W, including those not initially randomised to bimekizumab, in six pooled phase IIb/III studies in axSpA and PsA and their OLEs to the most recent data-cut (July 2023). TEAEs were evaluated based on exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs). EAIRs are reported per 100 patient-years (EAIR/100 PY) with associated 95% CIs. The number and percentage of patients reporting TEAEs are also reported.

Overall and new-onset positive eC-SSRS and PHQ-9 responses are reported for all patients who received ≥1 dose of bimekizumab 160 mg Q4W, including those not initially randomised to bimekizumab, in four pooled phase III studies in axSpA and PsA and their OLEs to the July 2023 data-cut. New-onset responses are defined as a positive post-baseline result with no positive results during screening or at baseline. Positive responses to the eC-SSRS question 1 (‘wish to be dead’), PHQ-9 question 9 (‘thoughts you would be better off dead or of hurting yourself’), or both questions are reported, due to the similarity between these questions. Additionally, the proportions of patients with a PHQ-9 score ≥15 and ≥20 are reported. Mean PHQ-9 scores are reported for placebo-randomised and bimekizumab-randomised patients in BE MOBILE 1, BE MOBILE 2, BE OPTIMAL, BE COMPLETE by study to 2 years. Multiple imputation (MI) was used to impute missing data. Data analyses were conducted using SAS V.9.4 (SAS Institute LLC).

Results

Patient disposition and baseline characteristics

Overall, 848 patients with axSpA and 1409 patients with PsA received ≥1 dose of bimekizumab across the phase IIb/III studies. Median total duration of exposure to bimekizumab across the phase IIb/III studies was 1036.0 (minimum: 17.0, maximum: 1809.0) days for patients with axSpA and 980.0 (minimum: 12.0, maximum: 1470.0) days for patients with PsA (table 1). Total exposure to bimekizumab in the phase IIb/III studies was 2513.8 PY for patients with axSpA and 3655.9 PY for patients with PsA.

Baseline demographics and disease characteristics were generally similar between patients with axSpA and PsA; however, in line with the general population, patients with axSpA were younger, with a lower mean body mass index, and a greater proportion were male, compared with patients with PsA (online supplemental table 3).4 Additionally, a greater proportion of patients with axSpA had a history of uveitis compared with patients with PsA, and a greater proportion of patients with PsA reported concomitant conventional synthetic DMARD use compared with patients with axSpA.

At the time of the data cut reported in this manuscript (July 2023), 456/848 (53.8%) patients with axSpA and 271/1409 (19.2%) patients with PsA had completed their respective phase IIb/III studies, and 223 (26.3%) patients with axSpA and 912 (64.7%) patients with PsA were ongoing in their respective phase III OLEs. 169 (19.9%) patients with axSpA and 226 (16.0%) patients with PsA had discontinued their respective phase IIb/III studies at the time of the data-cut; 60 (7.1%) patients with axSpA and 92 (6.5%) patients with PsA discontinued due to AEs (online supplemental figure 2).

Overall safety

The incidence rates for the majority of TEAEs remained stable with 1 additional year of exposure to bimekizumab in the phase III studies (online supplemental figure 3). The incidence rate (EAIR/100 PY) of ≥1 TEAE was 129.6 for patients with axSpA and 126.9 for patients with PsA (table 1). Most TEAEs were mild or moderate, and the incidence rate (EAIR/100 PY) for severe TEAEs was 3.2 for patients with axSpA and 3.5 for patients with PsA. Study discontinuations due to TEAEs were infrequent (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 2.4; PsA: 2.9). For serious TEAEs, the incidence rate (EAIR/100 PY) across the phase IIb/III studies was 5.3 for patients with axSpA and 5.8 for patients with PsA.

Up to the data cut reported previously (July 2022), there were 3 deaths reported in patients with axSpA, and 3 in patients with PsA. Details of these deaths have been reported previously.29 Two additional deaths occurred in patients with PsA between the previous analysis and the data cut reported here (July 2023). The causes of death, reported by MedDRA V.19.0 preferred term, were cardiac arrest and hepatobiliary neoplasm, and neither death was considered drug-related by the investigator. No additional deaths occurred in patients with axSpA within this period. As such, a total of 3 deaths occurred in patients with axSpA and 5 deaths in patients with PsA (EAIR/100 PY of 0.1 in both indications) to the present data-cut (July 2023).

The most frequently reported TEAEs across the phase IIb/III studies, defined as occurring in ≥5% of patients in either the axSpA and PsA patient pools, are reported in table 1. The 3 most common TEAEs were SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) infection (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 9.9; PsA: 9.9), nasopharyngitis (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 8.4; PsA: 6.8) and upper respiratory tract infection (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 5.0; PsA: 5.7).

Infections

The overall EAIR/100 PY of infections and infestations was 52.3 for patients with axSpA and 53.6 for patients with PsA. The incidence rate (EAIR/100 PY) of serious infections was 1.4 for patients with axSpA and 1.3 for patients with PsA (table 2). Serious opportunistic infections were infrequent (EAIR/100 PY for patients with axSpA: 0; PsA: 0.1); there was 1 serious case of herpes zoster meningoencephalitis, and 1 of oropharyngeal candidiasis, among patients with PsA. No cases of active tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis or blastomycosis were reported in any study. Few patients discontinued the study due to infections and infestations: 13 (1.5%) patients with axSpA and 28 (2.0%) patients with PsA (EAIR/100 PY for patients with axSpA: 0.5; PsA: 0.8).

Fungal infections

In the phase IIb/III studies, the EAIR/100 PY of fungal infections was 8.4 among patients with axSpA and 7.9 for patients with PsA. No systemic fungal infections were reported and fungal infection events were generally mild or moderate in severity across both indications; there was 1 severe case of oral candidiasis and 1 severe case of oesophageal candidiasis among patients with axSpA, and 1 severe case of an otitis media fungal infection in a patient with PsA.

Most fungal infections were Candida infections (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 4.3; PsA: 4.7), the majority of which were reported as oral candidiasis (EAIR/100 PY for patients with axSpA: 3.5; PsA: 3.8); most cases of oral candidiasis were mild to moderate in severity and resolved with standard antifungal therapy (table 2). In the phase III studies, 20/574 (3.5%) patients with axSpA and 34/1211 (2.8%) patients with PsA reported more than 1 occurrence of oral candidiasis (figure 1). Study discontinuation due to oral candidiasis was infrequent across the phase IIb/III studies: 5 (0.6%) patients with axSpA and 10 (0.7%) patients with PsA discontinued (EAIR/100 PY for patients with axSpA: 0.2; PsA: 0.3). Across the phase III studies, incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) for oral candidiasis decreased between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with axSpA (year 1: 8.0, year 2: 5.4) and PsA (year 1: 6.8, year 2: 4.5) (online supplemental figure 3).

Hepatic events

The EAIR/100 PY of hepatic events across the phase IIb/III studies was 5.3 in patients with axSpA and 5.0 in patients with PsA (table 2). Few patients discontinued the study due to hepatic TEAEs: 3 (0.4%) patients with axSpA and 5 (0.4%) patients with PsA (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.1; PsA: 0.1). Most hepatic TEAEs were non-serious transient elevations of liver enzymes. Elevations of alanine transaminase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST)>3×upper limit of normal (ULN) occurred at an EAIR/100 PY of 2.0 for patients with axSpA and 1.9 for patients with PsA. Elevated levels of ALT or AST>5×ULN occurred at an EAIR/100 PY of 0.8 for patients with axSpA and 0.6 for patients with PsA. Across the phase III studies, incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) for hepatic events decreased between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with axSpA (year 1: 10.4, year 2: 4.7) and patients with PsA (year 1: 8.8, year 2: 3.9) (online supplemental figure 3).

Uveitis

In the phase IIb/III studies, the EAIR/100 PY of uveitis was 1.3 for patients with axSpA and 0.1 for patients with PsA. Among patients with axSpA, 18/130 (13.8%) with a prior history of uveitis and 13/718 (1.8%) with no prior history reported a uveitis event (EAIR/100 PY: 4.8 vs 0.6, respectively) (table 2). Among patients with PsA, 1/21 (4.8%) with a prior history of uveitis and 2/1388 (0.1%) without a prior history of uveitis reported a uveitis event (EAIR/100 PY: 2.4 vs 0.1, respectively). Across the phase III studies, incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) decreased between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with axSpA (year 1: 2.6, year 2: 1.3) and PsA, with no events reported in year 2 among patients with PsA (year 1: 0.1, year 2: 0.0) (online supplemental figure 3).

Inflammatory bowel disease

The incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) of adjudicated definite or probable IBD were low across phase IIb/III studies: 0.7 for patients with axSpA and 0.2 for patients with PsA (table 2). IBD events were classified as either Crohn’s disease (EAIR/100 PY for patients with axSpA: 0.4; PsA: 0.1) or ulcerative colitis (axSpA: 0.2; PsA: 0.1); all other events were considered unclassified/as microscopic colitis/no further differentiation was possible (axSpA: 0.2; PsA: 0.1). Among patients with axSpA, 3/15 (20.0%) with a prior history of IBD and 15/833 (1.8%) with no prior history reported an adjudicated definite or probable IBD event (EAIR/100 PY: 6.6 vs 0.6, respectively). Among patients with PsA, 1/13 (7.7%) with a prior history of IBD and 7/1396 (0.5%) without a prior history reported an adjudicated definite or probable IBD event (EAIR/100 PY: 2.9 vs 0.2, respectively). Across the phase III studies, incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) remained similar between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with axSpA (year 1: 0.9, year 2: 0.8) and PsA (year 1: 0.5, year 2: 0.1) (online supplemental figure 3).

Major adverse cardiovascular event

Across the phase IIb/III studies, the incidence rate (EAIR/100 PY) of adjudicated MACE was 0.2 in patients with axSpA and 0.3 in patients with PsA. Considering only the phase III studies, there were no adjudicated MACE events reported in patients with axSpA (year 1: 0.0, year 2: 0.0) and incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) of adjudicated MACE decreased between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with PsA (year 1: 0.7, year 2: 0.2) (online supplemental figure 3).

Other safety topics of interest

Overall, 10 (1.2%) patients with axSpA and 24 (1.7%) patients with PsA experienced a malignancy (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.4; PsA: 0.7). Excluding non-melanoma skin cancer, 9 (1.1%) patients with axSpA and 19 (1.3%) patients with PsA experienced a malignancy (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.4; PsA: 0.5). There were no serious hypersensitivity or anaphylactic reactions in any study. Additional safety topics of interest are reported in table 2.

Mental health

This integrated safety analysis provided insight into the mental health of patients treated with bimekizumab. Across the phase IIb/III studies, few patients with axSpA or PsA had adjudicated SIB (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.12; PsA: 0.05) (online supplemental table 4). All individuals who experienced an adjudicated SIB event had a history of psychiatric disorders, ongoing traumatic and stressful circumstances or other additional confounding factors. Further, the incidence rates for depressive disorders were low across both indications (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.32; PsA: 0.41) (online supplemental table 4). Additional evaluation of incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) of adjudicated SIB in patients in the phase III studies by year demonstrated that rates remained low between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in patients with axSpA (year 1: 0.4, year 2: 0.0) and patients with PsA (year 1: 0.1, year 2: 0.0) (online supplemental figure 3).

Up to the data cut reported previously (July 2022), 2 events were adjudicated as suicide attempts: 1 in a patient with axSpA reported as intentional self-injury and 1 in a patient with PsA reported as suicidal behaviour.29 There were no new adjudicated suicide attempts in either indication to the data cut reported here (July 2023) (online supplemental table 4). Overall, incidence rates (EAIR/100 PY) of adjudicated suicide attempts were low across the phase IIb/III studies (EAIR/100 PY for axSpA: 0.04; PsA: 0.03). There were no cases of completed suicide reported in either indication across the phase IIb/III studies, during a total bimekizumab exposure of 2513.8 PY for patients with axSpA and 3655.9 PY for patients with PsA.

Mental health was also assessed in the phase III studies using the eC-‍SSRS for SIB and PHQ-9 for depression severity. A total of 4/574 (0.7%) patients with axSpA and 12/1211 (1.0%) patients with PsA reported new-onset positive eC-SSRS responses; the majority of these were positive responses to Q1 (‘have you wished you were dead or wished you could go to sleep and not wake up’), the least severe of the questions (online supplemental table 5). Further, 19 (3.3%) patients with axSpA and 47 (3.9%) patients with PsA reported new-onset positive responses to PHQ-9 Q9 (‘thoughts that you would be better off dead, or thoughts of hurting yourself in some way’). Despite the similarity of these questions, only 2 (0.3%) patients with axSpA and 2 (0.2%) patients with PsA reported a new-onset positive response to both eC-‍SSRS Q1 and PHQ-9 Q9 across the phase III studies.

Within the individual phase III studies for axSpA and PsA, mean PHQ-9 scores initially improved from baseline to week 16 among bimekizumab-treated patients in both indications, and these improvements were sustained to 2 years; thus, mean PHQ-9 scores remained stable and low to 2 years (online supplemental figure 4).

Discussion

This analysis presents pooled safety data from an integrated analysis of phase IIb/III studies of bimekizumab in axSpA and PsA, and serves as an update to a previous pooled analysis, including 1 additional year of treatment exposure from the ongoing phase III OLE studies.29 Additionally, this is the first report of long-term mental health data collected using eC-SSRS and PHQ-9 from the phase III bimekizumab programme.

Incidence rates of TEAEs were generally similar in patients with axSpA and PsA across the phase IIb/III studies, including the incidence of ≥1 TEAE. The incidence rates of severe TEAEs and TEAEs leading to study discontinuation were low across indications, supporting good tolerability of bimekizumab in patients with axSpA and PsA.

SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19), nasopharyngitis and upper respiratory tract infection continued to be the most frequently reported TEAEs with bimekizumab 160 mg Q4W, in line with findings from the previous integrated safety analysis.29 To note, the conduct of the phase III studies overlapped with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic.

There were no serious opportunistic infections among patients with axSpA and very few for patients with PsA, highlighting the consistent safety profile of bimekizumab. Notably, there were no cases of active tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis or blastomycosis.

Oral candidiasis continued to be a commonly reported TEAE, in line with previously published safety data for bimekizumab,141924 and as expected due to the role of IL-17 in antifungal activity at the oral mucosa.25 However, incidence rates of oral candidiasis decreased between year 1 and year 2 of bimekizumab treatment in the phase III studies. Additionally, most reported cases were mild to moderate in severity and resolved with standard antifungal treatment, and the associated recurrence and study discontinuation rates were low. Furthermore, there were no systemic fungal infections reported for either indication.

In the current analysis, incidence rates for hepatic events were low across both patients with axSpA and PsA; these findings were consistent with safety signals associated with other IL-17A inhibitor therapies in PsA.36 Furthermore, there was a reduction in the incidence of hepatic TEAEs between year 1 and year 2 of the phase III studies. The lower frequency of liver function tests conducted between year 1 and year 2 may have contributed to this trend1922: as liver enzymes fluctuate, less frequent testing may mean AEs are less likely to be captured. Overall, the low incidence rates for hepatic TEAEs support the tolerability of long-term bimekizumab treatment.

The management of uveitis is particularly integral to the care of patients with axSpA, due to the higher frequency in this population compared with patients with PsA, and its negative impacts on quality of life.37 38 A previous exploratory analysis of uveitis incidence in phase IIb/III studies of axSpA, which evaluated the differences in incidence rate among patients receiving bimekizumab versus placebo in the double-blind treatment period, suggested that bimekizumab may confer protective effects for uveitis flares among patients with axSpA.39 Indeed, in the current analysis, with longer exposure to bimekizumab treatment, incidence rates decreased among patients with axSpA and remained stable and low among patients with PsA. Although further investigation is needed, including with a control population, these findings provide longer-term support for this hypothesis.

IBD is a common comorbidity among patients with axSpA and PsA and is noted in the EULAR recommendations as a factor to consider when selecting treatment for both axSpA and PsA.38 40 The incidence rates of adjudicated definite or probable IBD TEAEs were higher among patients with a prior history of IBD compared with patients with no prior history. Additionally, incidence rates were slightly higher in patients with axSpA compared with PsA, consistent with general patient populations,4 and safety signals reported in patients treated with IL-17A inhibitors such as ixekizumab and secukinumab.41 42 This suggests the inhibition of IL-17F in addition to IL-17A with bimekizumab treatment does not increase the risk of IBD.

Anxiety and depression are common in patients with axSpA and PsA.43 44 Furthermore, given that all phase III trials reported in the manuscript coincided, at least in part, with the COVID-19 pandemic, during which local lockdown measures and health issues may have impacted mental health, it is especially relevant to evaluate and report mental health outcomes. Importantly, there were no cases of completed suicide in any study. The incidence rate of adjudicated SIB events remained low across patients with axSpA and PsA, consistent with the original pooled integrated safety manuscript,29 and all individuals who experienced an event had a history of psychiatric disorders or confounding life circumstances. Furthermore, rates of adjudicated SIB and depressive disorder TEAEs were low among both patients with axSpA and PsA, in line with what has been observed with bimekizumab treatment across other indications, including psoriasis,45 suggesting a consistent safety profile of bimekizumab. Additionally, few patients reported new-onset positive responses to either eC-SSRS Q1 or PHQ-9 Q9, supporting the finding that SIB and depression were infrequent among both patients with axSpA and PsA. The limited number of new-onset positive responses to both questions also highlights inconsistencies in how patients answered similar items between the two questionnaires.

Incidence rates of other TEAEs were low and similar between indications, demonstrating good tolerability of bimekizumab. These safety results across axSpA and PsA, taken together with published efficacy data,1922 suggest that bimekizumab has a favourable overall benefit-risk profile.

Strengths and limitations

This analysis pools patients from multiple clinical studies of bimekizumab in axSpA and PsA, which results in maximum patient number and captures the longest duration of treatment exposure across these patient populations to date. This large safety pool aims to capture less frequent safety events, though rare safety events may still not be observed in this population. Additionally, to the authors’ knowledge, the phase III bimekizumab programme is the first programme of IL-17i in PsA and axSpA to collect eC-SSRS and PHQ-9 data at every clinical visit.

Furthermore, these results span patients with nr-axSpA and r-axSpA, and bDMARD-naïve and TNFi-IR patients with PsA, providing a comprehensive overview of the safety of bimekizumab in patients across the SpA spectrum. However, the included patient population may not be reflective of the wider clinical population, which may be more heterogeneous with regards to demographics, disease characteristics and comorbidities.

It will be important to assess the safety profile across a more representative population of patients. Additionally, future research should consider the safety profile in subgroups of patients that may have higher risk of AEs of interest. For example, methotrexate use has previously been found to be associated with an increased risk of liver-related AEs.46 Further, patients with both PsA and psoriasis have been found to be at higher risk for MACE.47 Although outside of scope for the current analysis, such subgroup analyses may warrant further exploration in the future. Real-world data should be collected to demonstrate the safety and tolerability of bimekizumab treatment in routine clinical practice.

Conclusion

Incidence rates of TEAEs with bimekizumab treatment were generally similar between axSpA and PsA patient populations, and bimekizumab demonstrated long-term tolerability. The overall safety profile of bimekizumab was consistent with previously reported data in axSpA, PsA, psoriasis and hidradenitis suppurativa. In combination with previously reported efficacy data, these results support a positive benefit-risk profile of bimekizumab for the treatment of axSpA and PsA.

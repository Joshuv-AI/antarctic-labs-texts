Research on the evaluation of the manual manipulation level of a student pilot in a typical scene
Authors: Yu Qian, Jiwen Tai, Shengxiang Tang, Zijie Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe traditional evaluation method of student pilots ' manual manipulation level relies too much on teachers' subjective judgment. A quantitative evaluation method based on flight data is proposed to compensate for the shortcoming of the traditional method. According to the theory of the core competence of a pilot, the evaluation index system of manual manipulation level of student pilots in a typical scene is constructed. The Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) categorizes pilot manipulation levels into four grades. The improved support vector machine (SVM) model with particle swarm optimization (PSO) is trained using index data and rank labels as inputs. The data is split into training and verification sets with the cross-validation method. The training data for Guanghan Airport are taken as an example to verify the model's reliability. The model's prediction accuracy is compared with that of K-nearest neighbor and random forest models. The results show that the PSO-SVM model has higher prediction accuracy. The research can provide a theoretical reference for quantitatively evaluating manual manipulation levels and improving flight training quality.Keywords: Flight training, Manual manipulation, Typical scene, Machine learning, Evaluation model

Introduction

Safety is the eternal theme of civil aviation construction, and ensuring flight safety is the basis for the survival and development of civil aviation. As the operator of an aircraft, the pilot directly affects the running state of the aircraft during flight. According to the analysis report of Comac China, in the next 20 years, China is expected to have an annual demand of approximately 4500 pilots, which shows the importance of speeding up the pace of training high-level pilots. In the process of training, evaluating the level of manual manipulation of student pilots can help to determine whether the student pilots have a good technical foundation and can operate aircraft safely, and to test the reaction ability and the accuracy of manual manipulation of the aircraft to ensure the safety of aircraft operation. The traditional evaluation method relies on the subjective experience of teachers, and different teachers may have different evaluation criteria and preferences, which may lead to inconsistencies in the evaluation results. In addition, traditional assessment methods are costly to the teacher in terms of time and effort. Due to the limited time and attention of the instructors, they may not be able to give equal attention to all student pilots, which leads to uneven distribution of resources and affects the training effect of some students. Long-term observation, recording, and evaluation work may lead to teacher fatigue, affect their judgment and teaching quality, and even increase the risk of incorrect assessment. Quantitative evaluation can help pilots identify their weaknesses and deficiencies so that they can carry out targeted training and improvement. Through quantitative evaluation, flight schools and airlines can allocate training resources more effectively to ensure that each pilot receives appropriate training. The quantitative evaluation method provides a standardized way to measure the skill level of pilots and helps to ensure that all pilots meet certain training standards.

The quality of flight training is a topic of great interest that many scholars have paid attention to in recent years. Considering the leadership and organizational culture of teachers, Gokalp et al. [1] investigated how leadership style affected the performance of student pilots. Zhang et al. [2] simulated the flight environment of carrier-based aircraft at sea and established a pilot training evaluation method with the help of electromyography (EMG). Wright et al. [3] used different cockpit displays in simulation training to measure the flight performance of novice flight students. Ahmadi et al. [4] developed an intervention method based on gaze that improved the training quality of flight students during the transition from visual flight to instrument flight. Moesl et al. [5] evaluated the landing training effect of flight students based on an augmented reality (AR) method, and they found that the use of AR training was beneficial to bridge the gap between theory and practice teaching. Sun et al. [6] established an evaluation system based on behavioral indicators and established an accurate quantitative evaluation of the core competence of flight students. Tian et al. [7] used a deep variational self-encoder network and an adaptive dynamic time-warping algorithm to explore the important value of existing airborne combat data to establish a comprehensive evaluation system for military training pilots. Jirgl et al. [8] used pilot behavior parameters to explore the objective evaluation method of a military flight training level. Yang et al. [9] developed an eye movement training course for the flight task of a no-power stall and compared this with traditional training methods. They found that eye movement training effectively improved the flight performance of flight students, and they verified the important role of attention distribution training in improving the quality of flight training. Zhu et al. [10] considered the type identification of a maneuver and constructed an automatic evaluation method for pilot flight training quality, which avoided the subjective problem of flight instructors in the training process, made the evaluation results more scientific, and generated a new concept for flight training quality evaluation. Kelemen et al. [11] combined fuzzy set theory and the system method to develop a mixed expert model to accurately evaluate the quality of the personal results of student pilots in an aviation education and teaching system. Perfect et al. [12] developed an objective method to effectively evaluate the performance of novice pilots in typical personal aircraft training tasks. Lin et al. [13] established a competency evaluation model for flight students and pointed out that the most important factors affecting the competency of civil aviation flight students were self-confidence, optimism, attention distribution, and situational awareness, which provided a reference for aviation universities to improve training methods and improve training quality.

As a classic machine learning algorithm [[14], [15], [16], [17]], support vector machines (SVMs), which are widely used in prediction-related classification [18], have essential application value in solving flight-related evaluation and prediction problems [[19], [20], [21]]. Jiang et al. [22] detected and estimated the probability of multi-aircraft collision in free-flight airspace based on the SVM method. Chen et al. [23] used the improved SVM model to fit and supplement the missing values of QAR data in a plateau area. Based on the classification method of an SVM, Chen et al. [24] established a real-time evaluation model for mission load that met the requirements of an actual working environment. Mohanavelu et al. [25] used an SVM algorithm to classify the cognitive load level of fighter pilots in different flight stages and achieve continuous monitoring of pilot performance in the take-off, cruise, and landing stages. Ke et al. [26] investigated the problem of pilot selection and constructed a classification model based on SVM to distinguish professional pilots from novice students from ordinary universities, which was helpful for selecting outstanding potential pilots. It should be noted that the prediction effect of an SVM model is greatly influenced by the parameter settings, and selecting the appropriate parameters is the key issue in the application of an SVM. As an algorithm based on swarm intelligence [[27], [28], [29], [30]], the core idea of particle swarm optimization (PSO) is to simulate the group behavior of birds. Each particle represents a bird [31]. Using the memory of each particle and the knowledge obtained by the entire group [32], the optimal solution is found through cooperation and information sharing among individuals in the group [33]. PSO has fast calculation speed and strong optimization ability [34]. It is suitable for improving the machine learning model, evaluating the quality of different solutions through the fitness function [35], and iterating repeatedly to improve the evaluation accuracy [36]. PSO algorithm has attracted the attention of many scholars in recent years, and it has been introduced into the SVM model to optimize the penalty factor coefficient and kernel function parameters [[37], [38], [39], [40]]. In the field of civil aviation, the application of PSO-SVM helps to ensure the safe operation of aviation. Zhou et al. [41] used the PSO-SVM method to accurately identify and predict hazards for civil aircraft. Dai et al. [42] successfully diagnosed the fault state of an airborne fuel pump by using the PSO-SVM model. Nieto et al. [43] combined SVM with PSO technology to accurately predict the remaining service life of aircraft engines. Zhao et al. [44] established an SVM model based on PSO to predict air traffic flow management delay (ATFM delay).

In summary, although the support vector machine optimized by particle swarm optimization (PSO-SVM) has been applied in civil aviation, it is rarely used to evaluate the manual manipulation level of student pilots. The existing evaluation method mainly relies on the subjective observation and empirical judgment of the teacher, which may lead to inconsistencies and deviations in the evaluation results. The existing evaluation methods often lack in-depth analysis of flight data, which cannot fully reflect the students ' manipulation skills and potential problems. Compared with previous studies, this study applies the PSO-SVM model to the quantitative evaluation of the manual manipulation level of student pilots. It provides a more objective and accurate evaluation method. With the help of the intelligent machine learning method, the effective information in the flight data is fully excavated. In addition, by comparing with the two classical classification models of K-nearest neighbor and random forest, the advantage of the PSO-SVM model in accuracy is verified. The research provides a theoretical reference for the training and evaluation of student pilots, which helps improve the quality and effect of flight training.

Evaluation index of manual manipulation level of student pilots

Observable behavior of student pilots in manual flight

According to the core competence theory, the ability of manual flight in flight path management has seven Observable Behaviors (OBs), namely OB4.1–OB4.7, as shown in Table 1 [6].

Table 1Observable behavior of manual flight.Table 1OBObservable behaviorOB4.1Controls the aircraft manually with accuracy and smoothness as appropriate to the situationOB4.2Monitors and detects deviations from the intended flight path and takes appropriate actionOB4.3Manually controls the aeroplane using the relationship between aeroplane attitude, speed and thrust, and navigation signals or visual informationOB4.4Manages the flight path safely to achieve optimum operational performanceOB4.5Maintains the intended flight path during manual flight while managing other tasks and distractionsOB4.6Uses appropriate flight management and guidance systems, as installed and applicable to the conditionsOB4.7Effectively monitors flight guidance systems including engagement and automatic mode transitions

Observable behavior of manual flight.

In OB4.1–OB4.7, OB4.1 and OB4.2 require student pilots to accurately and gently achieve common flight states such as ascending, descending, level flying, and turning through manual manipulation, complete mutual conversion operations, and find and correct deviations in time. Navigation information and visual information in OB4.3 cannot be obtained with SD card data. Effective information related to OB4.4 and OB4.5 cannot be obtained solely by observing flight data. OB4.6 and OB4.7 are the requirements for the use of autopilot and flight guidance systems in the automatic flight state, which are not considered in this study. Therefore, this study mainly considers the requirements of OB4.1 and OB4.2 to construct the evaluation index of the manual manipulation of student pilots.

After determining the optimal value according to the index category, all of the types of indexes are transformed forward and normalized to obtain the standardized matrix. The method of TOPSIS is used to synthesize the distances between each student pilot and the optimal value and the worst value, and the closeness of the distance for each student pilot is obtained. The manual manipulation level of a student pilot is divided into four grades: Excellent, Good, Medium, and Qualified.

TOPSIS is widely used in the field of multi-attribute decision-making. This method can be utilized to score each evaluation object by integrating multiple indicators, thus ranking the evaluation objects. The results can accurately feed the distance between each evaluation object back, and a large amount of effective information in the data can be retained, so it is widely used. In contrast to the distribution-based evaluation method, TOPSIS comprehensively considers the distance between each evaluation object and the best and worst scheme to obtain more reasonable evaluation results.

The TOPSIS method needs to ensure that the evaluation index is in the same direction. Generally, the larger the value of the evaluation index is, the higher the evaluation is. This is because, if it is better if the value of one indicator is larger and it is better if the value of another indicator is smaller, the dimension will be chaotic and the final result will be affected.

Data preprocessing

Garmin1000 is a universal integrated avionics system designed and developed by Garmin. By applying the Garmin1000 avionics system, the Cessna172 training machine owned by Guanghan Branch stores a large amount of data generated during flight training in the airborne SD card in the avionics system, as shown in Fig. 4, Fig. 5. The flight data used in this study are generated and stored in the airborne SD card when the student pilots of the Guanghan Branch manipulate the Cessna172 in the scene of the traffic pattern.

Garmin1000 integrated avionics data system.

Airborne SD card.

Because the Garmin1000 system is designed with multiple redundancy and fault detection mechanisms to ensure that the system can still provide accurate data even when a single sensor has a problem, the data measurement error in this study is negligible. Considering the integrity of the data, only the data of student pilots who have completed all stages of the traffic pattern are used, and the data of student pilots who have only flown partial stages of the traffic pattern are not used. There is no specific restriction on student pilots' flight duration and age. Only the flight duration requirement of the flight school for students for the solo flight should be met. This is to ensure the generalization ability of the model and improve the applicability of the model to student pilots of different ages and experience levels.

For the original data, it is necessary to preprocess the abnormal data first, as shown in Fig. 6. For abnormal points, a Lagrange interpolation polynomial is used for data preprocessing. Optimization of SVM parameters by PSO

The evaluation index and the evaluation grade of student pilots obtained by TOPSIS in each stage are used as the input data of PSO-SVM for machine learning. Overall, 76 pieces of data from students 1 to 76 are used as a training set and a verification set, and fourfold cross-verification is adopted; that is, 3/4 of the 76 pieces of data are used as the training set, 1/4 are used as the verification set and 19 pieces of data from students 77 to 95 are used as the test set.

The performance of the SVM mainly depends on the type of kernel function K (x,xi). When the number of features is much smaller than the number of samples, an RBF (Radial Basis Function) kernel function is generally used. Because the number of features extracted in this study is far less than the number of samples, an RBF kernel function is selected to construct the SVM model. The RBF kernel function has strong generalization ability and learning ability and can achieve nonlinear mapping by only adjusting one parameter. γ is the RBF kernel parameter, representing the influence range of a single training sample on the model.

The parameters of the SVM are optimized with PSO. The selection method for parameters c1 and c2 of PSO is mature, and their sum is generally less than four. According to experience, c1 is set to 0.9, c2 is set to 0.9. The inertia weight ω is set to 0.8, and the population size is set to 50; that is, the value of i is an integer between 1 and 50. The maximum evolution generation of the population is set to 200, and r1 and r2 are randomly generated in the range of [0,1]. The value boundary of c is set to 0.01–100, and the value boundary of γ is 0.01–100. The classification error rate of the SVM is taken as the particle fitness value of the PSO. Conclusion

To accurately judge the manual manipulation level of student pilots, an evaluation model of the manual manipulation level of student pilots based on the PSO-SVM is proposed. This model can provide a reference for objectively understanding the training situation and manual manipulation skill level of student pilots. The model has good recognition performance and can maintain high accuracy in different stages of a typical scene, which shows that the model has good generalization ability and is suitable for the evaluation of the manual manipulation level of student pilots in different stages of the route. Compared with the prediction results of the KNN and random forest models, those of the PSO-SVM model have high accuracy, which proves that the PSO-SVM has certain advantages in evaluating the manual manipulation level of student pilots. The implementation of the PSO-SVM model is of great benefit to the saving of teachers ' labor costs and the optimization of training resources in the training process. The PSO-SVM model realizes the automation of the evaluation process, reduces the time teachers need to observe and evaluate students' performance in person, and saves the physical and mental costs teachers need to pay. The teacher can focus on training the trainees, which helps improve the training effect. In addition, optimizing resource allocation can ensure that training resources are used where they are most needed, reduce unnecessary repetitive training, and ensure that students are strengthened where needed rather than wasting time on the skills they have mastered, thereby improving overall training efficiency and cost-effectiveness.

This research can provide a reference for developing an automatic pilot training evaluation system on applying a machine learning classification algorithm. The fully automatic evaluation system can learn from the experience of this research and tap the influential role of machine learning models in fully automatic evaluation. This research can be used as an optional method for the automatic evaluation system to evaluate student pilots' skills in manual manipulation. Concerning this method, we can explore the method of automatically evaluating student pilots' performance levels in other aspects, which is helpful to realize the full coverage of the automatic evaluation system in all aspects of student pilots. This research helps make human instructors accessible from the role of evaluators. It reduces the burden on instructors to perform repetitive assessments and record work, and they can focus on complex teaching and mentoring tasks. Instructors can use the data and feedback provided by the automatic evaluation system to guide students more effectively, communicate more deeply and pertinently with students, and improve the quality of teaching. However, even if the evaluation process is automated, instructors still need to monitor the evaluation results to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the system. The research on the PSO-SVM model provides an important reference for the development of an automatic evaluation system for pilot training and also profoundly impacts the role of human instructors. The quality and efficiency of flight training can be improved by reasonably integrating automation technology and the professional knowledge of human instructors.

In order to improve the PSO-SVM model, efforts can be made in the following aspects in the future: (1) Research and develop more efficient particle swarm optimization algorithms to improve the convergence speed of the model. (2) By training the model on more diverse data sets, the generalization ability of the model can be further improved to make it more suitable for different flight conditions and student characteristics. (3) Design and implement a long-term follow-up study to evaluate the application effect and long-term impact of the PSO-SVM model in actual flight training. In the future, it is meaningful to cooperate with experts in psychology, pedagogy, and other fields to study further how to combine the PSO-SVM model with the pilot's psychological cognition and theoretical learning process to improve the training effect in an all-around way.

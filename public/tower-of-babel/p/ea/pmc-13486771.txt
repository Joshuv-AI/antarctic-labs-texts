Impact of delayed medical consultation on dengue severity and mortality during the 2023 outbreak in Burkina Faso
Authors: Dramane Ouedraogo, Eric Arnaud Diendere, Absatou Ky/Ba, Rose Kabore, Souleymane Ouédraogo, Ismael Diallo, Apoline Ouedraogo/Sondo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Delayed medical consultation (>3 days) was frequent among hospitalized patients with dengue.•Delayed consultation (>3 days) was associated with severe dengue.•Delayed consultation (>3 days) was independently associated with mortality.Keywords: Dengue, Delayed consultation, Outbreak, Burkina Faso

Introduction

Dengue is an emerging viral disease whose spread has intensified considerably in recent years. It is endemic in more than 100 countries, mainly in tropical and subtropical regions, and affects over 400 million people worldwide each year [1]. Although most patients with dengue develop a mild form, some cases can progress to severe disease, which may lead to potentially life-threatening complications, such as hemorrhage, organ failure, and shock syndrome. According to the World Health Organization, more than 7300 dengue-related deaths have been reported since the beginning of 2023 [2]. Currently, there is no curative treatment for dengue.

Research on dengue is often limited by the fact that patients tend to seek medical attention late, usually after the viral phase, which lasts for the first 5-7 days of the illness. At this stage, the inflammatory phase is already established, and antiviral treatments are ineffective. Consequently, delays in seeking medical care are a major factor influencing disease outcomes. In Africa, patients often consult late for various reasons, such as the use of traditional medicine or herbal remedies, self-medication, geographic inaccessibility of health centers, especially during the rainy season and lack of financial resources [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. For instance, in Guinea, a survey conducted between 2022 and 2023 among 3300 patients with malaria showed that the median delay between symptom onset and initial medical care was 3.04 days [7]. Such delays in accessing healthcare can worsen disease progression and increase the risk of complications.

Dengue is a major public health concern in Burkina Faso, with successive epidemic outbreaks observed since 2013. Burkina Faso has reported the highest number of dengue cases on the African continent. In week 52 of 2023, 154,867 suspected cases of dengue fever were reported in Burkina Faso, with 70,433 probable cases and 709 deaths (0.4%) [8]. These figures highlight an alarming increase in dengue incidence despite ongoing control efforts including vector control, public awareness campaigns, and standardized clinical management protocols.

This study aimed to identify factors associated with delayed consultation among patients diagnosed with dengue during the 2023 outbreak in Burkina Faso. The findings may contribute to improving early consultation and management of dengue cases within healthcare facilities.

Methods

Type, period, and study setting

This was a descriptive and analytical cross-sectional study based on data collected from January 1 to December 31, 2023, at the University Hospital Centre (UHC) of Bogodogo. The UHC is one of the six university hospitals in Burkina Faso and one of the four located in Ouagadougou, the capital city. It serves as a referral center for the management of highly transmissible diseases. Data were collected from the Departments of Infectious and Tropical Diseases, Rheumatology, Medical Emergencies, and Nephrology.

Study population and sampling

The study population consisted of patients hospitalized for dengue during the study period at the UHC of Bogodogo. Patients managed in the four departments were included in the study. Dengue was defined as the presence of antigenic or serological markers suggestive of dengue infection in hospitalized patients. Therefore, patients aged 15 years and older who were hospitalized in one of the four departments and had a positive NS1 antigen test, with or without anti-dengue immunoglobulin M (IgM) and/or immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies, were included.

The isolated positivity of anti-dengue IgG on a rapid diagnostic test (RDT), in the absence of titration techniques, was considered indicative of a previous dengue infection. Such patients were excluded from the study.

Data collection and studied variables

The data sources included the patients’ medical records and hospitalization registers. Data were collected using standardized questionnaires covering sociodemographic characteristics (age, sex, education level, residence, occupation), admission characteristics (date of symptom onset, date of consultation), clinical characteristics (history and comorbidities, clinical signs), biological parameters, and disease outcomes.

Characteristics of dengue diagnostic tests

Dengue diagnosis was primarily based on rapid diagnostic tests detecting the NS1 antigen and/or IgM antibodies, according to national diagnostic guidelines. A probable dengue case was defined as a patient presenting clinical signs compatible with dengue and a positive dengue RDT validated by national standards. A positive dengue RDT was defined as positive serology for IgM and/or IgG and/or positive NS1 antigen.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables were expressed as means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, as appropriate. Missing values were excluded from the denominator when calculating percentages.

New variables were generated from raw variables, including the following:

Early medical consultation: This was the main dependent variable and was categorized as yes/no. It was defined as an interval of ≤3 days between the onset of symptoms reported by the patient and the first medical consultation. This threshold was chosen based on the average duration of 3-4 days between the febrile and critical phases of dengue, as commonly reported in the literature [9]. Patients with a consultation delay of more than three days were classified as the “delayed consultation” group.

Warning signs included severe abdominal pain or tenderness, persistent vomiting, fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion, or pericardial effusion), mucosal bleeding, lethargy or restlessness, hepatomegaly >2 cm, and a progressive increase in hematocrit associated with a rapid decrease in platelet count.

Severe dengue was defined according to the 2009 World Health Organization dengue classification as the presence of at least one severity criterion [9]. The following criteria were considered:

-Plasma leakage: defined by hemoconcentration with hematocrit > 45% or by fluid accumulation in serous cavities in the absence of known underlying disease.

For the analytical approach, bivariate analysis was performed using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate, to assess associations between categorical variables and delayed consultation. Comparisons of continuous variables between groups were performed using the Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. A significance level of 5% was used for statistical comparisons. Univariable binary logistic regression was then performed to estimate crude odds ratios (ORs) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and P values. Subsequently, a multivariable binary logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify factors independently associated with the outcomes. Results were reported as adjusted ORs (aORs) with 95% CIs. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 22.

Ethical considerations

The study was conducted after obtaining prior authorization from the Ethics Committee (Deliberation No. 2024-09-286) and the General Director of the UHC (Authorization No. 2024/0162/MS/SG/CHU-B/DG). Data confidentiality was strictly maintained. All information collected during data collection was anonymized and treated as confidential. Data were used solely for the purposes of this study, in compliance with current ethical and regulatory requirements.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics

Of the 377 eligible patients, 332 were included in the study (Figure 1). The median age was 35 years (interquartile range, 25-49). Patients aged 15-40 years accounted for 62% (206) of the cases. The male-to-female ratio was 1.04. Housewives and informal sector workers represented 25.3% and 23.8% of patients, respectively. A total of 317 patients (95.5%) resided in urban areas, mainly in Ouagadougou.

Admission characteristics

The median duration between symptom onset and the first consultation at a health facility was 5 days, with a range of 0-19 days. Two hundred and seventeen patients (65.4%) first consulted in primary health centers. Three hundred patients (90.4%) were transferred or referred to the university hospital (Table 1).

Clinical, biological, and outcome characteristics

Before admission, 95 patients (28.6%) had already received treatment, including antimalarials (n = 37, 11.1%), antibiotics (n = 22, 6.6%), and analgesics (n = 36, 10.9%). Hypertension and diabetes were present in 10.5% and 3.6% of cases, respectively.

Clinically, fever was observed in 98.2% of patients, myalgia in 91.3%, headache in 74.7%, nausea and vomiting in 42.8%, and asthenia in 31.6%. Among the warning signs at admission, mucosal bleeding (gingivorrhagia, hematemesis, epistaxis, hematuria) and lethargy were observed in 25% and 8.1% of patients, respectively. Severe clinical signs included hemorrhagic manifestations, shock, and respiratory distress, observed in 25.0%, 8.4%, and 23.2% of patients, respectively (Table 1).

Biologically, 10.8% of patients (62/297) had elevated transaminases >1000 IU/l, and 7.8% (23/293) had thrombocytopenia <20,000/mm³ (Table 1). The NS1 antigen test was positive in 303 (91.3%) patients.

Severe dengue was diagnosed in 197 patients (59.3%). Clinical outcomes were assessed in 326 patients, of whom 76 (23.3%) died.

Analytical findings

Among the 332 patients included, 225 (67.8%) consulted more than three days after symptom onset.

Univariate analysis showed that the occurrence of myalgia (P = 0.01), hypoxia (93.4% vs 95.6%; P = 0.002), respiratory distress (P < 0.001), and hemorrhagic signs (P = 0.004) were associated with consultation delay >3 days. Biologically, severe acute kidney injury (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes 3; 6 vs 32; P = 0.015), elevated transaminases >1000 IU/l (P = 0.04), and thrombocytopenia <20,000/µL (P = 0.01) were significantly associated with delayed consultation.

Patients with a consultation delay >3 days had significantly more severe forms of dengue (45 vs 152; P < 0.001) (Table 2) and a significantly higher risk of death than those who consulted earlier (OR = 2.87; 95% CI, 1.49-5.51; P = 0.003). Similarly, patients aged >40 years had an increased risk of death compared with those aged ≤40 years (OR = 2.72; 95% CI, 1.61-4.60; P < 0.001) (Table 3).

Discussion

In this hospital-based study conducted during the 2023 dengue outbreak in Burkina Faso, delayed medical consultation was frequent, with nearly two-thirds of patients (67.8%) seeking care three or more days after symptom onset. Notably, this delay was independently associated with a significantly increased risk of death (adjusted OR = 2.99; 95% CI, 1.54-5.80).

Consistent with data from Benin, where Allanonto et al. [10] documented a mean delay of four days between symptom onset and hospital presentation during the 2019 dengue outbreak, with only 13.3% of patients presenting within the first three days, our findings suggest that delayed healthcare-seeking is a recurring challenge during dengue outbreaks in West Africa.

Furthermore, in our study, 59.3% of patients presented with severe dengue. A consultation delay of more than three days was significantly associated with severe dengue. Specifically, delayed consultation was associated with respiratory distress, severe acute kidney injury, profound thrombocytopenia, and marked hepatic dysfunction, all of which are recognized indicators of dengue severity. These findings are consistent with those reported in Thailand [11], Myanmar [12], and Indonesia [13]. The clinical impact of this delay is biologically plausible: the critical phase of dengue, characterized by increased vascular permeability, plasma leakage, hemorrhage, and circulatory collapse, typically follows the initial febrile phase [[14], [15], [16]]. Consequently, late presentation often coincides with established organ dysfunction, limiting the effectiveness of early fluid resuscitation and supportive care and increasing mortality risk. Taken together, these findings emphasize the importance of timely intervention to improve clinical outcomes.

Several factors likely contribute to these delays in Burkina Faso. The clinical overlap between early-stage dengue and endemic malaria often leads to diagnostic confusion, prompting patients to rely on self-medication, particularly with oral antimalarials, rather than seeking prompt medical evaluation. This confusion, coupled with limited public awareness of dengue warning signs and economic barriers to healthcare access, remains a critical challenge in early clinical management.

In the face of these challenges, prevention remains the most effective strategy for dengue control. It relies primarily on vector control, while vaccination may provide an additional preventive strategy where available. Two dengue vaccines are currently licensed, CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia, Sanofi) and TAK-003 (Qdenga, Takeda), which differ in their indications, dosing schedules, and immunological response profiles [17]. However, these vaccines are not available in Burkina Faso. In this context, strengthening awareness campaigns on both vector control and the recognition of warning signs, particularly during epidemic periods, is essential to reduce dengue-related morbidity and mortality in the country.

This study had several limitations. First, although dengue virus circulation during the 2023 outbreak was confirmed by reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction at the National Reference Laboratory for Viral Hemorrhagic Fevers (Centre Muraz, Bobo-Dioulasso), co-circulation of serotypes was observed, predominantly DENV-3 [8]. In routine clinical practice, however, diagnosis was primarily based on RDT, in accordance with national guidelines implemented to manage the high patient load. While reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction remains the reference standard for dengue diagnosis and serotyping, its use was limited in routine hospital settings. Second, as a cross-sectional study conducted in a single tertiary care university hospital, the findings may not be fully generalizable to other healthcare settings in Burkina Faso. Finally, owing to the retrospective nature of data collection from medical records, some clinical information may have been subject to documentation bias.

Conclusion

This study showed that delayed medical consultation (>3 days) was associated with increased dengue severity during the 2023 outbreak in Burkina Faso and remained an independent predictor of mortality. These findings highlight the importance of early healthcare-seeking and timely clinical management in reducing dengue-related mortality. Strengthening community awareness of dengue warning signs and improving access to healthcare services should be prioritized to improve patient outcomes and reduce the disease burden.

Fusing non-textual cues with classical NLP for enhanced multimodal fake news spread detection
Authors: Jing Dang, Yichen Sun, Chengzhi Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe increasing spread of misinformation on social platforms has made it necessary to develop automated and accurate fake news detection systems. Traditional methods that focus solely on textual content are ineffective in dealing with more complex fake news that mimic authentic writing styles. This research introduces a novel multi-modal deep learning architecture for fake news detection that achieves a more comprehensive understanding of the content by intelligently integrating non-textual clues (including statistical and behavioral features) and deep textual features. In the proposed model, news features are extracted from distinct modalities: statistically engineered features and behavioral-communicational characteristics of news publishers, character-level features (to deal with misspellings and words outside the vocabulary), as well as word-level semantic features using the Word-level features (Word2Vec) model. Each of these information modalities is processed by a dedicated deep network to produce compact and rich representations of each one. Then, in an innovative step, these representations are intelligently merged using a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanism to dynamically determine the weight and importance of each modality. Finally, the fused feature vector is used by a SoftMax classifier to finally detect whether the news is fake or authentic. The evaluation results of the proposed method, which were performed on two valid GossipCop and PolitiFact datasets, demonstrated remarkable efficiency. On the GossipCop dataset, accuracy values of 0.99 and F-measure of 0.98 were achieved by the proposed method, demonstrating the high ability of the model in identifying news accurately and completely. Similarly, on the PolitiFact dataset, values of accuracy of 0.96 and F-measure of 0.95 were acquired. This high performance on both datasets indicates an evident dominance over the comparative approaches and confirms the validity and performance of the proposed multi-modal technique for detecting fake news.Keywords: Fake news detection, False information spread, Social network analysis, Multi-modal deep learning, Multi-head self-attentionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Internet changed methods of interaction and communication through cheap, convenient access, and rapid distribution of information. Consequently, social media and internet portals have become favored alternatives for searching news and reading for the majority of individuals as opposed to traditional newspapers. Social media is harmful to society as it affects major events even though it has become a powerful source of information. Especially after the 2016 U.S. presidential election, the issue of internet fake news has gained widespread popularity1,2. The electorate might easily be swayed by deceptive political messages and claims, as believed by Zhang and Ghorbani3. Inspection demonstrates that deceptions or false news propagate quicker through human beings than authentic information and have a vast impact4. The words rumor and false news go hand in hand5. Disinformation or fake news is created intentionally. Rumors, however, refer to unsubstantiated and dubious facts that are shared without intent to deceive6. On social media sites, the motives of the spreaders might be difficult to discern. Thus, any incorrect or misinformation is normally referred to as misinformation on the Internet. True and false information is difficult to distinguish5.

However, various techniques have been used to counter this. Some machine learning methods have been used for the detection of misinformation spread online under knowledge verification7, Natural Language Processing (NLP)7–9 and sentiment analysis10. The early work was directed towards leveraging textual data derived from the article content, such as statistical text features11 and affective information12–14.

Previous works on identifying fake news are usually prone to weaknesses that are addressed in this study. To begin with, most methods are unimodal, based on text content analysis alone. Such methods neglect important cues in metadata and user behavior patterns (i.e., repost percentages, comment rates, and publisher social network profiles), which might indicate a coordinated attempt to spread misinformation. Second, in existing multimodal research, the process of feature integration is often performed by simple methods such as concatenation. These methods are unable to understand the relative importance and complex interactions between different aspects of information (such as statistical, character-level, and word-level features) and cannot dynamically adjust the weight of each aspect in the final decision-making. Finally, the vulnerability of text models to misspellings, slang, and out-of-vocabulary words that are common in social media content is another major challenge. Word-based models alone cannot effectively handle these issues and require more robust solutions such as character-level analysis. This research will take a step forward in this area by providing an integrated architecture that covers these three research gaps simultaneously.

The innovations of this research can be categorized into two main axes. The first innovation refers to the proposed three-facet feature extraction architecture. In this research, a comprehensive model for feature extraction is presented that analyzes three different aspects of the news in parallel. These aspects include: engineered features that include a combination of text statistical features and user-interactional characteristics; character-level features used for noise resistance and understanding writing patterns; and word-level features used for semantic understanding of the content. The second innovation includes dedicated representations for each aspect and intelligent fusion based on a self-attention mechanism. Instead of using a single model for all features, a dedicated deep learning network is designed for each aspect to extract an optimal representation that is appropriate for the nature of each aspect of information. Also, the use of a MHSA mechanism to merge the extracted representations is a key innovation that allows the model to dynamically weigh the importance of each facet for each instance and provide far better performance than conventional linear combinations.

The architectural novelty is that simultaneous projection of different types of data (discrete patterns of character, continuous patterns of words, and non-linear patterns of behavior) into a common latent space is done. Our structure is based on the MHSA mechanism as a dynamic feature gatekeeper unlike conventional ensembles which average results. This enables the architecture to address a root issue of fake news detection: the fact that semantic content (text) and dissemination behavior (meta-data) can give conflicting information. Our architecture offers a conceptual plan of information-theoretic complementarity, an architecture which allows one modality to compensate the noise or lack of another in real-time.

Related work

In this section, fake news detection methods in recent years have been reviewed. Sahoo and Gupta15 suggested a deep learning-based automatic fake news detection in the Chrome setup, where they examined Facebook user behavior along with news content. The suggested technique exhibited improved accuracy over existing techniques, highlighting the need for automatic fake news detection on social media, as online social network arrival had led to the dissemination of fake news. Braşoveanu and Andonie16 presented a semantic fake news detection method that employed machine learning, semantics, and natural language processing to assess speaker-subject relationships and credibility factors. Experiments showed a dramatic boost in accuracy in the detection of fake news with the addition of semantic features.

Mishra et al.17 investigated the identification, features, taxonomy, data types, categories, and identification methods of spurious news based on probabilistic latent semantic analysis as human verification became more complex with the growth of social media. Ali et al.18 proposed a deep ensemble fake news detection model using sequential deep learning methods. It extracted features from news text, pre-processed them, and encoded them by using n-gram and term frequency–inverse term frequency techniques. The model improved F1-score by 2.41% and achieved 100% accuracy on the ISOT dataset.

Zhang et al.19 also suggested a model of fake news detection in cyber-physical social services based on deep learning for Chinese text. It employed a convolution-based architecture of neural computing with efficient processing and detection at reduced training time and enhanced classification accuracy compared to baselines. Jain et al.20 developed a model and approach for identifying false news with the assistance of machine learning and natural language processing to combat rumors and false news on social media. The model was able to yield a 93.6% accuracy rate, which was particularly relevant in developing countries like India, where genuine news focus was of utmost concern.

Khanam et al.21 explored identifying fake news with traditional machine learning models. It recommended a product model using Python scikit-learn and NLP for text processing, which performed feature extraction and vectorization. The model further explored feature selection methods for greatest precision, addressing the destructive consequences of fake news on social media. Sharma et al.22 aimed to recognize disinformation on Twitter using user attributes and tweet text content through machine learning and deep learning methods. It proposed a novel framework that used news content and social contexts to predict fake news, analyzing their validity and effectiveness. Comito et al.23 examined deep learning methods for multimodal social media fake news detection, with implications and future directions for research introduced for improving explainability and cross-domain fake news detection models.

Mallick et al.24 proposed a cooperative deep learning system that used user feedback to predict the trust level of news and ranked the news based on these scores. The proposed system was 98% accurate and highly efficient compared to state-of-the-art methods, addressing the issue of fake news on social media. Jouhar et al.25 applied machine learning algorithms for false news detection to improve decision-making and information correctness. It used precision and accuracy metrics for identifying the most appropriate algorithm to classify articles, to the benefit of news media, social media, and the government, boosting trust, legitimacy, and credibility against disinformation.

Abdullah et al.26 formulated a machine learning model for identification of social media data as fake news using Twitter data, Naïve Bayes, decision tree, and support vector machine. The decision tree classifier was better than Naïve Bayes and support vector machine when ordered based on precision, recall, and F1-score metrics and best for detection of Twitter fake news. Nasser et al.27 developed reliable methods for detecting and eliminating imitated news using deep learning methods like Transformer models and recurrent neural networks. One study compared 121 deep learning models for multimodal fake news detection in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F-scores.

Xu et al.28 suggested a new detection scheme using Large Language Models (LLMs) to identify fake news, enhancing the F1 score by 1.5%, and offering a scalable solution towards a more reliable online information environment. Zhou and Cai29 designed a novel false news detection system relying on Electra, a multi-scale recurrent convolutional neural network, self-attention mechanism, adaptive gated residual connections, and focal loss function. The method effectively embedded news texts, extracted contextual features, and balanced few-sample and multi-sample data, enhancing information authenticity and public interest.

More advanced architectures have been considered recently in order to reflect the multifaceted nature of disinformation. Ahmed et al.30 proposed a model based on GCNN, which combines psycholinguistic features with user interaction and semantic propagation graphs and emphasizes the significance of content topologies. On the same note, Nadeem et al.31 introduced the framework of SSM, which concentrates on stylometric textual features through a Hyperbolic Hierarchical Attention Network and integrates visual forgery detection to multimodal news. In order to utilize the power of Transformer, Nadeem et al.32 designed HyproBert, a hybrid architecture that integrates DistilBERT with BiGRU and Capsule Networks to extract deep hypercontextual features. Moreover, the EFND model suggested by Nadeem et al.33 applies multimodal factorized bilinear pooling to combine social, visual, and textual data with a high level of accuracy on benchmark datasets. These models can deliver state-of-the-art performance, but they may have a significant computational cost because of graph convolutions or huge Transformer backends, or they do not have any particular mechanisms to address the character-level noise and out-of-vocabulary problems that are common to social media streams. Limitations of Existing Approaches and Research Gap

Weakness of Unimodal Analysis: According to18,20, text-centric models are very effective on certain datasets (e.g., ISOT). Nevertheless, they are becoming more and more flouted by advanced falsehoods, which make use of genuine language patterns. Such models do not have the dissemination context (who is disseminating it and how) that non-textual cues offer.

Research methodology

This research focuses on the identification of fake news through the integration of knowledge acquired from the analysis of both textual and non-textual information. In this section, the characteristics of the datasets used in this study are first described, followed by the details of the proposed method for multimodal fake news detection based on the fusion of non-textual features with features extracted from textual content.

Data

Two databases that contain both real and false news from the social network Twitter, GossipCop and Politifact, were employed in this investigation34. In the realm of Twitter’s spread of false political information, the Politifact data is quite modest. Of the 1,270 news items in this collection of data that were posted on Twitter, 473 were categorized as fake news about politics and 797 as real news. On the other hand, a broader training data source for Twitter false news detection that covers a variety of news topics is offered by the GossipCop dataset. Out of the 22,153 samples, 16,818 have been classified as real information and 5,335 as untrue. Four characteristics are present in every instance in the aforementioned collections: an identifier, the post’s URL, the title, and the publisher’s Twitter ID. A crawler was used to gather the content of the news for all samples in this study, and the data was then used for the investigation. Following the principles of data privacy, all behavioral data of users (e.g., Twitter IDs and metrics of interactions) were anonymized at the preprocessing phase. Proposed algorithm

This section describes the stages of the proposed method for identifying content related to fake news by combining text processing techniques and non-textual data analysis. In this process, text processing techniques describe properties related to the presence of inaccurate information in the message text. Meanwhile, non-textual data analysis techniques represent the behavioral characteristics of the user/users associated with news dissemination. By integrating these two sets of features, namely the content being disseminated and the characteristics of the disseminators, a more complete understanding of the news’s falsity can be achieved. From a general perspective, the proposed method can be decomposed into the following stages:

These stages are presented as a block diagram in Fig. 1. The input dataset includes the textual information of each message alongside the ID of its publishing user. In the first stage, the textual information of the message in each record is pre-processed. This step, through operations such as text segmentation, normalization, and the replacement of stop words, attempts to adapt the textual content for subsequent processing steps. In the second stage, a multimodal model is used to describe and represent message-related features. The first informational modality targets engineered statistical features of the message and the communication characteristics of its disseminators. This informational modality can provide deeper insight into the behavioral patterns of individuals in disseminating fake news and other content. In the second informational modality, the description of content features at the word level is targeted, for which the Word2Vec technique is utilized. Based on this informational modality, a general understanding of the pattern of word usage in generating fake content can be achieved. The third informational modality in the proposed model is organized based on character-level features and the Character N-Gram (CNG) technique. This informational modality represents the smallest extractable informational component from the textual content and can be useful in identifying patterns related to text writing in fake news. After organizing the three aforementioned informational modalities, deep learning models are used to represent the features of each modality. In this regard, the informational modality of engineered features is processed by an Auto-Encoder (AE) network; while other informational modalities are represented by specialized Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Each of these deep learning models converts its corresponding informational modality into an intermediate state so that, at the beginning of the third stage, they are intelligently integrated using a Multi-Head Self Attention mechanism (MHSA). The integrated features are classified by a SoftMax layer. Thus, the output of this classifier at the end of the proposed architecture will determine the type of news (fake/normal).

Pre-processing

The purpose of initial processing of message content is to analyze and segment them into a standard format, where unnecessary information is removed and content is displayed in an understandable form. This process begins by dividing each message into sentences, as a sentence is considered the smallest unit of meaning transfer. Following that, the text’s punctuation and spaces are processed to fix other elements including consecutive spaces, the spacing characters’ placement in relation to the punctuation symbols, and new line signs.

During implementation, Text Analytics Toolbox™ has been employed for initial processing. After these operations, keywords are added to the text to highlight latent concepts. Specific concepts can be represented by content elements like currency amounts, web addresses, and numbers. In many instances, these things’ very existence—rather than their worth—is significant. (B)CNG Feature Extraction.In the second informational modality for describing textual content characteristics, character-based features are utilized via CNG. Character trigrams have been used as the feature representation technique to precisely identify character-level structures in textual information. This approach is robust to spelling errors and handles out-of-vocabulary words nicely, which is essential when working with social media data where such variations are common. The following steps are followed to generate character trigram representations:Text Padding: First, to accurately represent trigrams at word boundaries, padding is applied to the pre-processed text. Specifically, a special character, in this case ‘#’, is added to the beginning and end of each textual sequence. This padding step ensures that trigrams at the edges of words, such as ‘#me’ and ‘ia#’, are captured for words like “media”, preventing the underrepresentation of beginning and end sequence of characters.Extracting Trigrams and Building Vocabulary: After prep-rocessing, character trigrams are extracted by sliding a window with the size of 3 throughout the text. For example, the padded word “#media#” yields the trigrams ‘#me’, ‘med’, ‘edi’, ‘dia’, and ‘ia#’. Subsequently, a comprehensive vocabulary is constructed that includes all unique trigrams extracted from the entire corpus. Each unique trigram in this vocabulary is dedicated by a integer index to facilitate its numerical representation.Using TF-IDF for Text Vectorization: The TF-IDF weighted method for character trigram representation has been used for vectorization. By taking into account both the relevance of trigrams throughout the entire database and how often they appear within an item, this technique enhances the bag-of-trigrams approach. CNG Feature Extraction.

In the second informational modality for describing textual content characteristics, character-based features are utilized via CNG. Character trigrams have been used as the feature representation technique to precisely identify character-level structures in textual information. This approach is robust to spelling errors and handles out-of-vocabulary words nicely, which is essential when working with social media data where such variations are common. The following steps are followed to generate character trigram representations:

Text Padding: First, to accurately represent trigrams at word boundaries, padding is applied to the pre-processed text. Specifically, a special character, in this case ‘#’, is added to the beginning and end of each textual sequence. This padding step ensures that trigrams at the edges of words, such as ‘#me’ and ‘ia#’, are captured for words like “media”, preventing the underrepresentation of beginning and end sequence of characters.

Extracting Trigrams and Building Vocabulary: After prep-rocessing, character trigrams are extracted by sliding a window with the size of 3 throughout the text. For example, the padded word “#media#” yields the trigrams ‘#me’, ‘med’, ‘edi’, ‘dia’, and ‘ia#’. Subsequently, a comprehensive vocabulary is constructed that includes all unique trigrams extracted from the entire corpus. Each unique trigram in this vocabulary is dedicated by a integer index to facilitate its numerical representation.

The second stage of the multimodal feature extraction phase in the proposed method involves representing each modality using a specialized deep neural network. The AE network performs well in compressing statistical data. In contrast, CNN models can more efficiently process complex feature patterns.

Additionally, methods suggested in20–22 were used as compared approaches. All of the models were trained and evaluated by the same data. In evaluating classification models, there are four key concepts: True Positive (TP) is when the model correctly identifies a positive (fake news) case. True Negative (TN) refers to the case when the model correctly predicts a negative case. False Negative (FN) represents positive cases that the model incorrectly classified as negative. And finally, False Positive (FP) represents cases where the model incorrectly identified negative samples as positive. These four metrics are the basis for analyzing the model’s performance in correct detection and classification errors.

In comparing the performance of various classification models, various measures are used, each of which evaluates a different aspect of the model’s ability. All accurately identified samples (including TPs and TNs) divided by the total number of instances is known as accuracy. Contrarily, precision is concerned with positive estimates and determines the percentage of TPs among all cases that the model predicts would be positive. The percentage of TPs over all actual positive instances is identified by recall. Precision and Recall are balanced by the F-measure, which is the harmonic average of the two measures and offers a more comprehensive evaluation for the model’s efficacy. Furthermore, Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), a correlation measure with a range from − 1 to + 1, provides an overall evaluation of the accuracy of binary classification by considering true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, as well as class imbalance. Critical Success Index (CSI) is a measure that evaluates the effectiveness of a model’s positive predictions by considering both false positive and false negative errors. Together, they provide an overall view of the strengths and weaknesses of a classification model under different scenarios.

This is an especially informative performance gap: RoBERTa is very effective at recognizing linguistic patterns of fake news, but it is blind to the non-textual attributes of news dissemination. The fact that the proposed model can integrate semantic knowledge with behavioral metadata (e.g., repost-to-comment ratios and publisher metrics) enables it to identify advanced fake news that resembles the authentic writing styles but presents abnormal spreading patterns. Moreover, the suggested model attains this high performance with about 1.8 million parameters, which is a much more efficient architecture than RoBERTa-base (~ 125 million parameters), and therefore, a more scalable solution to real-time monitoring.

To overcome the black box characteristics of deep learning models and to offer Explainable AI (XAI)40 information, this paper examines the weights produced by the MHSA layer. These weights are the dynamic significance that the model gives to each modality (Behavioral, Character-level, and Word-level) in the fusion process. The distribution of average attention in the GossipCop data is shown in Fig. 9a. One important fact is that in the case of Authentic News, the model has a fairly balanced dependency on semantic word-level features and statistical consistency. Nonetheless, in the case of Fake News detection, the MHSA mechanism puts a considerable emphasis on the Behavioral/Statistical branch (mean contribution 0.55). This implies that the model does not simply recognize fake content based on what is said, but also the interaction of the users with the content and the pattern of metadata of the publisher.

Statistical significance analysis

In order to strictly assess the statistical significance of the performance improvements of the proposed multimodal model over the baseline architectures and the current state-of-the-art techniques, a sequence of statistical hypothesis tests were performed. The main goal is to make sure that the observed accuracy increase is a direct consequence of the proposed MHSA-based fusion and multimodal feature extraction, and not a random change in the dataset or training procedure.

The paired-sample t-test was used, with the results obtained after a 10-fold cross-validation process. The accuracy of the proposed model was compared with ablation baselines, and literature baselines, in each fold. The null hypothesis (H0) presupposes that there is no significant difference in the mean performance of the proposed model and the comparative models. A p-value of below 0.05 is said to be statistically significant.

The findings in Tables 7 and 8 indicate that in both datasets, the suggested method is much better than all the comparative models (p < 0.05 in all instances). Interestingly, the p-values of CNNCNG and CNNW2V are extremely low, which means that both the character-level and word-level features are not sufficient on their own, but intelligent combination of them is mathematically better.

Computational complexity and efficiency

One of the strong points of the suggested multimodal architecture is that it is computationally efficient compared to the existing Large Language Models (LLMs). With specialized deep networks (1D-CNNs in the case of textual modalities and a compact AE in the case of statistical features) the overall number of parameters is around 1.8 million, which is many orders of magnitude lower than transformer models such as BERT (110 M+ parameters).

The mean inference time on the GossipCop dataset was estimated to be (12 ms) per sample on the hardware in “Experimental setup and reproducibility”. This performance shows that it is possible to monitor social media streams in real-time. Although the existing model offers a good trade-off between accuracy and speed, additional optimizations include weight pruning (eliminating unnecessary neural connections) and quantization (cutting the precision of weights down to 8-bit) are known to be worthwhile. These methods would further decrease the memory footprint and the power consumption, making it easy to deploy in the edge devices and mobile platforms.

Model robustness analysis

Since misinformation is adversarial, a detection system should be able to perform well when under sub-optimal conditions. In this section, the proposed architecture is tested on noisy inputs, adversarial perturbation with intent to attack, and testing on a different dataset (domain shift).

(A) Robustness to Noisy and Adversarial Inputs.The content of social media is noisy by nature, with the use of slang, abbreviations, and deliberate misspellings meant to evade automated filters. In order to test the strength of our model, we performed a stress test by adding artificial noise to the test sets of GossipCop. We applied adversarial noise by randomly replacing 10% and 20% of characters in the news titles with adjacent keys on a QWERTY keyboard, and simulated human typing errors and deliberate obfuscation. Our proposed multimodal model was compared to the CNN(W2V) baseline that is based on word-level embeddings only. Table 9 summarizes the results.As it can be seen, the word-level model (W2V) loses accuracy dramatically as a result of out-of-vocabulary (OOV) errors, whereas the proposed model is very robust. This strength is directly credited to the CNG modality that records sub-word patterns that are recognizable even when the individual characters are changed. In addition, the Behavioral/Statistical modality is not affected by textual noise, and it gives the classifier a consistent second opinion.

Conclusion and future work

The results of the current research confirm the effectiveness and usefulness of the suggested multi-modal deep learning model in detecting fake news. The model is capable of analyzing the news content efficiently by the thorough, but intelligent combination of textual and non-textual features, such as the statistical to behavioral, character-level to semantic word-level perspective. One of the strengths of this study is the fact that it is resistant to noisy social media text through the CNG modality, which is also more resistant to misspellings and OOV terms than traditional word-level models. Moreover, the application of specific deep networks to each task and then a new MHSA fusion mechanism allows the system to balance dynamically and use exclusive sources of information (especially the behavioral fingerprint of news disseminators which text-only models do not pay much attention to).

Assessment of the GossipCop and PolitiFact datasets confirmed the desirable results of the proposed model with the accuracy of 0.99 and 0.96 and the F-measure of 0.98 and 0.95. These findings support the effectiveness of the suggested strategy and emphasize its potential as an effective solution to be applied in practice. In particular, this architecture is intended to be used as a first-pass automated filter in social media monitoring systems. The fact that it can process behavioral metadata in addition to text enables it to flag suspicious content with high accuracy which can be escalated to human fact-checking, thus lowering the number of content moderators.

Limitations and future works

Although these findings have been achieved, the given method has a number of limitations that can offer future research directions:

Computational and Energy Efficiency: The model has a small representation although concurrently running many deep networks and attention heads raise the computational cost. More practical enhancements of the green-computing profile of model pruning or distillation should be considered in future work, as well as a formal energy audit.

Comparison to LLMs and Graph Models: Although the present paper is devoted to the integration of classical deep learning with behavioral cues, the recent development of Large Language Models (LLMs) and graph-based detectors has other strengths. Future versions will seek to compare this architecture to LLaMA or GPT-4-based detectors and investigate Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to have a more detailed model of the relational structure of news propagation.

Temporal Dynamics and Interpretability: The existing model derives a fixed point of news interactions. The dissemination of news is however a dynamic process. Further studies are necessary to include time dynamics and concentrate on XAI to enable users to comprehend why a particular news was labeled as fake.

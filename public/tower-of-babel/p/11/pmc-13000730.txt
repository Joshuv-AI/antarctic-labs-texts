Ion-activated mucoadhesive in situ gel incorporating terbinafine-loaded NLCs promotes ocular penetration and retention for enhanced fungal keratitis therapy
Authors: Fudan Dong, Zhen Liang, Xiaping Yao, Zhen Zhang, Yuan Tao, Hui Chen, Ping Lu, Huiyun Xia, Junjie Zhang, Guojuan Pu, Tianyang Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fungal keratitis (FK) is a sight-threatening infection with poor outcomes due to rapid tear clearance and insufficient corneal penetration. To overcome the limitations, we developed an ion-activated mucoadhesive in-situ gelling system (TH-NLC-ISG) integrating terbinafine-loaded nanostructured lipid carrier (TH-NLC) into a gellan gum/carboxymethyl chitosan matrix. The optimized formulation exhibited a particle size of 25.27 ± 0.75 nm. Upon contact with simulated tear fluid, the formulation underwent rapid sol-gel transition, achieving a 70-fold viscosity increase (34 °C, shear rate 1 s−1), critical for prolonged ocular residence. Fluorescence imaging demonstrated ocular retention of fluorescein sodium-labeled TH-NLC-ISG exceeding 60 min, significantly longer than the non-gelling control. In vivo pharmacokinetic studies revealed markedly improved ocular bioavailability, with higher area under the concentration in ocular tissues-time curve of TH in the TH-NLC-ISG group compared to TH-NLC. Corneal penetration studies confirmed that TH-NLC-ISG exhibited longer corneal retention time and higher corneal drug retention compared to TH-NLC. The formulation demonstrated excellent ocular biocompatibility in vitro and in vivo. TH-NLC-ISG exhibited potent in vitro antifungal activity against Fusarium solani. and Aspergillus flavus., with a lower MIC50 value than TH suspension. In rabbits and mice FK models, TH-NLC-ISG group achieved complete corneal healing, reduced clinical scores and corneal perforation rates, displaying efficacy superior to conventional natamycin eye drops. By synergistically augmenting retention and corneal permeation, TH-NLC-ISG provides a transformative strategy for enhanced fungal keratitis therapy.

Introduction

Fungal keratitis (FK), a vision-threatening ocular infectious disease, is primarily caused by pathogenic fungi such as Aspergillus spp., Fusarium spp., and Candida spp. [1,2]. FK constitutes a major cause of blindness throughout the world, resulting in approximately one million cases of vision loss annually and posing significant clinical challenges [3]. Although topical eyedrops remain the cornerstone of FK treatment, favored for its non-invasiveness, good biocompatibility, and high patient compliance [4], conventional antifungal eyedrops suffer from rapid precorneal elimination and the inefficient penetration across corneal barriers [5,6]. Consequently, the ocular bioavailability of topical eyedrops is typically less than 5% [7], greatly restricting their therapeutic effectiveness.

To address the dual challenges of the corneal barrier and rapid tear clearance, nanostructured lipid carrier (NLC) [8] and in situ gelling system (ISG) [9] offer promising strategies. NLC, comprising surfactant-stabilized, blended solid and liquid lipid matrix, enhances the solubilization and stability of poorly water-soluble drugs [10]. Furthermore, the nanoscale dimension of NLC facilitates the drug deep trans-corneal penetration and improves the ocular bioavailability [11,12], yet rapidly tear clearance persists [13,14]. Stimuli-responsive ISG, administered as low-viscosity solutions9, undergoes rapid sol-gel transition upon ocular exposure to physiological stimuli including temperature [15,16], pH variations [17], or ionic strength changes [18], forming a viscoelastic gel network to prolong residence time [9]. Encapsulating drug-loaded NLCs into stimuli-responsive ISGs synergistically addresses the dual challenges of precorneal elimination and corneal permeability and enhances ocular delivery efficiency.

Gellan gum (GG), a water-soluble polysaccharide, responds to physiological cations in tear fluid (Na+, K+, Ca2+) by undergoing sol-gel transition [19], significantly enhancing precorneal drug retention [20]. Carboxymethyl chitosan (CMCS), a chitosan derivative, preserves intrinsic mucoadhesive properties and a zwitterionic character [21,22]. GG combined with CMCS may not only enhance ocular stimuli responsiveness and enable more stable gelation but also leverage the protonated amino groups of CMCS to mediate strong electrostatic adhesion to negatively charged mucin glycoproteins on the corneal/conjunctival epithelium, further extending ocular residence time and improving ocular bioavailability.

Terbinafine hydrochloride (TH), an allylamine antifungal drug, inhibits fungal squalene epoxidase, a key enzyme in the fungal early ergosterol biosynthesis [23]. This blockade disrupts fungal cell membrane formation and suppresses fungal growth [23]. While, concomitant squalene accumulation leads to fungal death [23]. Natamycin, the only US Food and Drug Administration-approved topical antifungal (5% w/v suspension), has long been considered the main drug for treating FK despite its limitations, including short residence time, poor corneal penetration, and low bioavailability (<5%), which often lead to high dosing frequency and patient discomfort [24]. Notably, TH demonstrates potent activity against ocular pathogenic Fusarium spp. isolates, with the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values ranging from 0.125 to 4 μg/mL, which is lower (indicating higher potency) than that of natamycin [25]. However, the therapeutic potential of TH in FK treatment is hindered by poor aqueous solubility and limited corneal permeability [26]. Therefore, it is essential to develop novel ocular delivery systems for TH to treat fungal keratitis.

Herein, this study developed a novel ion-activated mucoadhesive in situ hydrogel integrating TH-loaded NLC (TH-NLC) into a GG/CMCS matrix (TH-NLC-ISG) for enhanced fungal keratitis therapy (Scheme 1). This system synergistically combines: (1) TH-NLCs to improve corneal penetration; (2) GG enabling ion-activated gelation; and (3) CMCS providing mucoadhesion; thereby enhancing corneal adhesion and prolonging ocular residence time for effective FK treatment. Materials and methods

Animals and cells

Health New Zealand white rabbits weighing 2.0∼2.5 kg (3 months of age) and C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks) without eye diseases were used, which were purchased from Huaxing Laboratory Animal Company, Huiji District, Zhengzhou, Henan, China. All animal care and experimental protocols were approved by the Ethical Committee of Experimental Animal Care of Henan Eye Institute (HNEECA-2024-17) and complied with National Institutes of Health guidelines. The human corneal epithelial cell line HCE-2 (HCECs) and the conjunctival epithelial cell line CCL-20.2 (ConECs) were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA).

Preparation and characterization of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG

Preparation of TH-NLC

TH-NLC with TH concentration of 6 mg/mL was prepared using a microemulsion method [12]. Briefly, the lipid phase including glyceryl monolaurate (0.701 g) and oleic acid (0.304 g) was melted at 80 °C. CRH 40 (4.235 g) and glycerin (0.721 g) were added and mixed with the molten lipid. 0.6 g TH was incorporated into the above mixture at 80 °C. Double distilled water was added until a homogeneous, transparent microemulsion formed. This microemulsion was cooled to room temperature, and double distilled water was added to adjust to a final volume of 100 mL for the target drug concentration (6 mg/mL). The resulting NLC dispersion was stored at room temperature. Courmarin-6-labeled NLC and Fluorescein sodium-labeled NLC were prepared with a similar method.

Optimization of TH-NLC-ISG formulation

To optimize gel network stability and mechanical properties, the concentrations of GG (0.3-0.5%) [27] and CMCS (0.2-2%) were systematically screened. Table S1 presents the CMCS and GG levels used in the design. Formulations were prepared by mixing equal volumes of 2 × concentrated TH-NLC and 2 × concentrated gel matrix (containing target GG/CMCS), yielding final desired concentrations. Simulated tear fluid (STF) was used to activate gels at 34 °C (7:25 v/v ratio) [28]. Viscosity was measured at 5 rpm (Brookfield DVNX, CP-51 spindle) for initial state (η0, 25 °C) and STF-activated state (ηt, 34 °C post-homogenization).

Preparation of TH-NLC-ISG formulation

1g CMCS was dissolved in 50 mL double distilled water. 0.4 g GG was added and dissolved in above solution with continuous stirring for 15 min at room temperature and followed by 30 min at 80 °C (1000 rpm). The polymer solution was cooled to room temperature and mixed 1:1 (v/v) with 2 × concentrated TH-NLC, adjusting to a final volume of 100 mL to form TH-NLC-ISG. TH-NLC-ISG was stored at room temperature. Courmarin-6-labeled NLC-ISG and Fluorescein sodium-labeled NLC-ISG was prepared with a similar method.

Characterization of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG

Particle size and zeta potential

Particle size, polydispersity index (PDI) and zeta potential of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG were determined by dynamic light scattering (Zetasizer, NanoZS90, Malvern Instruments, Worcestershire, UK).

Encapsulation efficiency

The encapsulation efficiency (EE%) of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG were quantified by HPLC [29]. Briefly, 667 μL TH-NLC was added 20 mL volumetric flask and dilute to the mark with methanol. The free TH in NLC was separated by centrifugation (4000 rpm, 30 min). The filtrate was assayed by HPLC. The above samples were analyzed on an Eclipse Plus C18 column (150 × 3.0 mm, 5 μm) with mobile phase (0.03 M KH2PO4 (0.2% triethylamine)-methanol (8:92, v/v)) at 0.6 mL/min, 40 °C, and 282 nm detection. EE quantification procedure for the TH-NLC-ISG aligned with the NLC methodology. EE%were calculated based on the following formula.(1)EE(%)=W0−W1W0×100Where W0 was weight of the drug added, W1 was weight of the drug free in the TH-NLC.

Morphology analysis by scanning electron microscopy

Surface morphology of TH-NLC-ISG and STF-activated gel (25:7 v/v) was analyzed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM–6060 JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Samples were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, lyophilized for 24 h, and platinum-coated for conductivity, mounted on brass stubs with conductive tape, imaging was performed at 10 kV accelerating voltage.

Rheological evaluation of TH-NLC-ISG

TH-NLC-ISG viscosity was analyzed at 34 °C before and after being mixed with STF (25:7 and 50:7, v/v) under 0.1 to 1000 s−1 shear rates by the DHR-1 rotational rheometer [13]. Under low-shear conditions (1 s−1, 1 min), TH-NLC-ISG simulated static ocular equilibrium during eyelid opening. TH-NLC-ISG was placed at a high shear rate (1000 s−1, 1 s) to simulate blink events. Ten cycles were executed with viscosity measurements at shear extremes.

The viscoelastic properties of STF-activated TH-NLC-ISG were evaluated at 34 °C. Initial dynamic strain sweep tests were conducted at a fixed angular frequency of 10 rad/s while varying strain amplitudes from 0.1% to 450% [13]. Subsequent frequency sweep measurements maintained a constant 1% strain while scanning angular frequencies across 0.1-200 rad/s [13]. Time-resolved structural evolution was then monitored through time sweep experiments at 10% constant strain and 10 rad/s frequency, recording modulus changes over time. The self-healing capability was probed by subjecting the STF-activated TH-NLC-ISG to five consecutive cycles of alternating mechanical states: 20 s intervals at 1% low strain followed by 20 s high-strain phases at 100%, simulating repeated damage-recovery events under physiologically relevant conditions.

Drug release of TH-NLC-ISG

The in vitro drug release of TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp was assessed [30]. Samples (1 mL containing 6 mg TH) were sealed in pretreated dialysis bags immersed in 50 mL dissolution medium (STF with 1% Tween 80) at 34 °C under shocking. At predetermined intervals, 1 mL aliquots were withdrawn and replaced with fresh medium. Quantification of released TH via HPLC enabled calculation of cumulative release. Release kinetics were modeled using zero-order, first-order, and Korsmeyer-Peppas equations.

Short-term storage stability

Short-term storage stability of TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp was evaluated at 25 °C for 4 weeks. Critical parameters of the above formulations, including particle size, zeta potential, and drug concentration were detected.

Ocular residence time in rabbit eyes

Fluorescein sodium-labeled formulations including Susp, NLC, and NLC-ISG were prepared to investigate the effect of ISG on ocular retention time. A volume of 50 μL of Fluorescein sodium-labeled NLC or NLC-ISG were instilled into the conjunctival sac of New Zealand White rabbits (3 rabbits per group). At predetermined time intervals, the ocular surface was observed and photographed under cobalt blue illumination.

Bioadhesive force evaluation

The bioadhesion force was evaluated by the method described by Han-Gon Choi [31]. Corneal tissue from rabbit eyes was mounted onto each vial and securely fixed using a rubber band and an aluminum cap. The vial containing the corneal tissue was equilibrated at 35 °C for 10 min and subsequently positioned on a height-adjustable platform. A second vial was then attached to a precision balance. The test formulation was applied directly onto the corneal tissue. The height of the vial was carefully adjusted to ensure uniform contact between the corneal surfaces of the two vials. The applied weight was gradually increased until separation occurred. The bioadhesion force, expressed in dyne/cm2, was recorded as the minimum weight required to detach the vials. The corneal tissue was replaced for each replicate measurement.

Ocular pharmacokinetics in rabbit eyes

Sixty-three New Zealand White rabbits (ocular diseases-free) were randomized divided into three groups (TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp). Each group comprised seven time-point subgroups (5, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, and 180 min, three animals in each subgroup). Following bilateral instillation of 50 μL formulation into the inferior conjunctival sac, eyelids were gently held closed for 15 s. Before sampling, clean the periocular area and remove adhering formulation. Tear fluid samples were collected at the predetermined time and weighed immediately. After euthanizing the experimental animals, ocular surfaces were irrigated with saline. Corneal and conjunctival tissues were excised and weighed. All samples were stored at −80 °C pending HPLC analysis after extraction [30].

Corneal permeation study via Valia-Chien diffusion cells

Transcorneal permeation of TH-NLC and TH-Susp was assessed using Valia-Chien diffusion cells mounted with freshly excised New Zealand White rabbit corneas [32]. Systems were maintained at 34 °C with stirring (50 rpm). Donor chambers received 0.2 mL formulation and 4.8 mL STF, while receptor chambers contained 5 mL STF. At designed intervals (15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, and 240 min), Receptor phase samples (1 mL) were withdrawn and replenished with 1 mL preheated fresh medium. TH in the samples was analyzed by HPLC. Corneal permeation study via two-photon laser scanning microscopy

Following bilateral ocular administration of 50 μL Coumarin-6-labeled formulations including C6-Susp, C6-NLC, and C6-NLC-ISG, New Zealand White rabbits underwent euthanasia at scheduled intervals (30, 60, and 120 min) [33], and each time point has six eyes of three rabbits (n = 6). Enucleated eyeballs were subjected to real-time in vivo tracking via two-photon laser scanning microscopy (Zeiss LSM780 NLO microscope) to map fluorescent tracer distribution. Excitation wavelength was 700 nm, and emission wavelength was 530 nm.

Hens egg test-chorioallantoic membrane assay

The experiment assessed formulation irritation using Hens egg test-chorioallantoic membrane (CAM) assay [34]. Freshly fertilized eggs ethically were incubated at 37.8 °C and 60% humidity for 10 days, with tri-daily rotation to prevent embryonic adhesion. Embryos were divided into six groups (n = 8) including negative control (0.9% saline), Blank-NLC ISG, TH-Susp, TH-NLC, TH-NLC-ISG, and 0.1 M NaOH. After carefully removing the shell over the air chamber, 0.5 mL of each test formulation was applied around major embryonic blood vessels. Following 5-min exposure and saline rinsing, the embryos were photographed and evaluated for irritation.

Ocular irritation studies

Ocular irritation of TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp was evaluated in New Zealand White rabbits (n = 4) using the Draize test [35]. After confirming baseline ocular health, 0.1 mL of each formulation ( TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp) was instilled into the left conjunctival sac followed by 15-s eyelid closure, with contralateral eyes receiving saline as controls. Slit-lamp microscopy was used to assess ocular responses (hyperemia, discharge, chemosis, corneal/iris status) at 1, 2, 4, 24, 48, and 72 h under white/cobalt blue illumination. The left and right eyes of the rabbits were dissected and fixed in eyeball fixative. After paraffin embedding and sectioning, H&E staining and microscopic pathological observation and analysis were performed.

Following 24 or 48 h incubation, the drug-containing medium was removed. Each well received 100 μL serum-free medium with 10% CCK-8 solution, followed by 3 h incubation protected from light. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations

To elucidate the selective binding mechanism of TH toward fungal squalene epoxidase (fSQLE) over its human ortholog (hSQLE), molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina1.1.2. The tertiary structure of squalene epoxidase was retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) and converted to PDBQT format using OpenBabel and AutoDockTools. For docking simulations targeting the antifungal agent TH, a grid box (25 × 25 × 25 Å, 1.0 Å spacing) was centered on the enzyme's catalytic site, with coordinates derived from PyMOL-based alignment of its native ligand or co-crystallized substrate. AutoDock Vina 1.1.2 executed the docking, and the lowest-energy terbinafine pose was selected to quantify binding affinity. Subsequent pose analysis in PyMOL characterized key interactions including hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic contacts, and steric complementarity within the squalene-binding pocket.

All ligand-protein complexes underwent molecular dynamics simulations leveraging the GPU-accelerated PMEMD implementation in AMBER 22. For force field parametrization, the TH ligand received atomic partial charges via the RESP approach (executed in ANTECHAMBER) alongside GAFF assignment, whereas protein systems adopted the FF19SB force field. Initial system construction using the LEaP module encompassed: incorporation of missing atoms within heterodimers, charge neutralization through Na+/Cl− counterions, and solvation within a cubic TIP3P water environment maintaining a 10 Å solute buffer. Electrostatic interactions at range were treated with the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) technique, complemented by a 10 Å direct-space cutoff for van der Waals forces. Prior to dynamics, each system experienced sequential energy minimization: 5000 steps of steepest descent proceeded by 5000 steps of conjugate gradient optimization, resolving initial atomic clashes. Temperature equilibration followed within the NPT ensemble: systems heated progressively from 0 to 300K across 50ps, governed by a Langevin thermostat and subject to harmonic positional restraints (5.0 kcal mol−1 Å−2) on solute atoms. Subsequent pressure equilibration spanned 5 ns at 300 K and 1 bar (Berendsen barostat). SHAKE constraints were applied universally to bonds involving hydrogen. Unrestrained production MD ensued for 100ns per complex under maintained conditions (300K, 1 bar). Conformational sampling for subsequent MM/GBSA binding affinity calculations extracted 250 temporally equidistant frames exclusively from the terminal 50 ns of each production trajectory.

In vivo evaluation of antifungal activity in rabbits

We established a rabbit model of Fusarium solani-induced fungal keratitis as follows [36]. Fifty rabbits received preoperative and postoperative gatifloxacin (GTFX) ophthalmic drops in the right eye. Following intravenous anesthesia with diazepam and butorphanol tartrate, the periorbital area was disinfected with iodophor, topical proparacaine provided local anesthesia, and povidone-iodine ocular disinfection was performed prior to saline irrigation. Under microscopic guidance, a central corneal pocket was marked using a sterilized 3.5 mm trephine. A two mm incision was created with a 300 μm calibrated knife, followed by lamellar dissection to establish a pocket. After injecting 20 μL spore suspension via Hamilton syringe, 0.1 mL triamcinolone acetonide was injected subconjunctivally and GTFX eye gel topically applied. Postoperatively, alternating applications of GTFX eye and diluted dexamethasone sodium phosphate were administered four times daily (8:00, 12:00, 16:00, and 20:00) for two days with 10 min intervals between agents. Model validation occurred 24 h after treatment cessation. Bacterial contamination was excluded through parallel PDA slant and blood agar cultures (24-72 h incubation). Slit-lamp examination scored corneal pathologies [37] (infiltration, edema, ulcer size, transparency, neovascularization, and hypopyon), excluding subjects with anterior chamber penetration or bacterial positivity. Animals with homogeneous lesions scoring 4-7 points were randomized into five groups: negative control (saline), positive control (natamycin ophthalmic suspension), TH-NLC, TH-NLC-ISG, and TH-Susp. All groups received hourly treatments from 8:00 to 20:00 daily. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed via slit-lamp photography and clinical scoring on days 0, 3, 7, and 10. Upon treatment completion, rabbit corneal tissues were harvested and subjected to Grocott's methenamine silver (GMS) staining to assess fungal hyphal morphology for infection status confirmation.

In vivo evaluation of antifungal activity in C57BL/6 mice

We established a mice model of Aspergillus fumigatus-induced fungal keratitis as follows [33,38]. Sixty healthy C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks old) received intraperitoneal anesthesia with 4% sodium pentobarbital (0.1 mL/10 g), with anesthetic depth verified by suppressed respiration and absent corneal reflexes. Following topical ocular anesthesia using 0.5% proparacaine hydrochloride (10 μL) and a 1-min exposure period, residual agents were irrigated with sterile saline. Under surgical microscopy, a 34G microinjection needle delivered 2.5 μL Aspergillus fumigatus spore suspension via intrastromal injection at the central cornea. Immediate postoperative prophylaxis involved erythromycin ointment application. Animals recovered on a 37 °C warming pad until ambulatory, then underwent single-housing with sterile feed and water ad libitum. Slit-lamp examination 12 h post-inoculation confirmed model establishment; subjects exhibiting conjunctival hyperemia, corneal neovascularization, or systemic pathologies were excluded. Eligible mice underwent stratified randomization by clinical score into five cohorts (n = 9): Saline control (Saline), Natamycin eye drops (Natamycin), TH-NLC, TH-NLC-ISG, and TH-Susp. Treatment administration followed an hourly regimen (08:00–20:00) for seven consecutive days. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed on days 1, 3, 5, and 7 post-treatment initiation through: slit-lamp white light imaging (evaluating lesion area, surface regularity, transparency, and neovascularization), sodium fluorescein staining (corneal epithelial defects), standardized clinical scoring [39], and documentation of corneal perforation incidence.

Results and discussion

Preparation and characterization of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG

To address the dual challenges of TH's poor solubility and limited corneal permeability, we prepared TH-NLC. For prolonged ocular retention, TH-NLC was integrated into a composite matrix of GG and CMCS, leveraging their synergistic ion-responsive properties to construct a crosslinked ocular in situ gel (TH-NLC-ISG). To optimize gel network stability and mechanical properties, the concentrations of GG and CMCS were systematically screened, with initial viscosity (η0, viscosity measured at 25 °C) and STF-activated viscosity (ηt, viscosity measured at 34 °C after homogenization with STF serving as critical evaluation metrics. Through systematic screening (Table S1), the optimal formulation (0.4%GG/1%CMCS, w/v) exhibited a significantly enhanced viscosity response to STF compared to GG alone, attributed to ionic cross-linking between GG and CMCS. Notably, higher GG concentration (0.5%, w/v) resulted in excessive pre-gelation viscosity and compromised fluidity, potentially causing ocular irritation.

As schematized in Fig. 1A, the dual functional TH-NLC-ISG was successfully prepared. Key physicochemical properties of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG were summarized in Table S2 and illustrated in Fig. 1B-D. TH-NLC significantly improved the aqueous solubility of TH to 6.00 ± 0.01 mg/mL (Table S2), representing a 312.5-fold increase over the intrinsic solubility of TH (0.0193 mg/mL) [40]. This ultrasmall size (<20 nm) is substantially smaller than the corneal intercellular space (∼20-50 nm) [41], suggesting a strong potential for enhanced trans-corneal permeation. Upon integration into the GG/CMCS matrix to form TH-NLC-ISG, the particle size increased to 25.27 ± 0.75 nm (PDI of 0.32 ± 0.01, Table S2 and Fig. 1C), indicating partial aggregation or polymer adsorption, yet retaining a favorable nanoscale dimension for ocular delivery. The zeta potential of TH-NLC was moderately negative (−7.64 ± 0.21 mV, Fig. 1D). Following incorporation into the anionic GG/CMCS gel matrix, the zeta potential of TH-NLC-ISG shifted significantly to −26.17 ± 1.00 mV (Fig. 1D), confirming the dominant contribution of the polyanionic GG component to the surface charge. The significant shift in zeta potential and the retention of nanoscale dimensions upon incorporation into the GG/CMCS matrix suggest the formation of a stable hybrid assembly. As shown in Fig. 1E, the TH-NLC-ISG underwent a rapid sol-to-gel transition upon mixing with STF at a 25:7 (v:v) ratio. SEM images revealed that the TH-NLC-ISG without STF displayed an amorphous, non-porous morphology, whereas the STF-activated TH-NLC-ISG formed an interconnect, porous three-dimensional network (Fig. 1F and G). The structural reorganization suggests that ionic crosslinking between GG/CMCS carboxylate groups and physiological cations present in STF, demonstrating the core mechanism for in situ gelation and sustained drug retention on the ocular surface.

The performance of TH-NLC-ISG was evaluated through comprehensive rheological characterization. As shown in Fig. 1H, TH-NLC-ISG without STF activation exhibited low pre-gelation viscosity, ensuring excellent fluidity for facile instillation and uniform corneal coverage. Upon activation with STF, TH-NLC-ISG displayed pronounced shear-thinning behavior, characterized by decreased viscosity with the increasing shear rate (Fig. 1H). This property is critical for ocular compatibility as viscosity reduction under high shear rates (simulating blinking) minimizes irritation, while elevated viscosity under low shear rates (simulating interblink periods) resists tear drainage.

Cyclic shear rate tests (Fig. 1I) revealed rapid thixotropic recovery of TH-NLC-ISG. Viscosity was restored within seconds upon reversion to low shear after high-shear exposure, demonstrating structural resilience. This reversible behavior enables the gel to maintain high static viscosity for retention [43] while permitting comfortable administration via transient shear-induced liquefaction. The dynamic strain scanning test was performed with a strain amplitude of 0.1% and 100% (Fig. 1J). When a 100% high dynamic strain (simulating mechanical stress during blinking) was applied, the value of storage modulus G′ below loss modulus G″, indicating temporary gel disruption into a sol state. Upon strain reduction to 0.1% (mimicking ocular rest), G′ recovered above G″, suggesting gel state restoration. This reversible sol-gel transition persisted through five alternative cycles, indicating the self-healing ability essential for maintaining ocular residence under repeated blink-induced shear. The time scanning measurements at simulated blink condition (10% strain, 10 rad/s) further validated the stability of hydrogel structure. As shown in Fig. 1K–G′ consistently exceeded G″, indicating stable network structure during the gelation phase. Therefore, TH-NLC-ISG integrates shear-thinning fluidity, thixotropic recovery, and strain-triggered self-healing to ensure prolonged ocular retention while enabling patient-friendly administration [43], thereby addressing a key challenge in topical antifungal therapy.

Fig. 1L shows the cumulative release profiles of TH from different formulations. TH-NLC-ISG group exhibited the slowest drug release, with a cumulative release of only 42.01 ± 3.75% at 96 h, significantly lower than TH-NLC (52.43 ± 2.32%), and TH-Susp (70.64 ± 6.89%) groups. The extend release profile may be due to the nanostructured lipid matrix retarding drug diffusion, and the ionically crosslinked hydrogel network restricting molecular mobility [13]. As shown in Table S3, the TH release profile from TH-NLC-ISG fitted well with the Korsmeyer-Peppas kinetic equation (R2 = 0.99), with a release exponent of 0.4383, indicating Fickian diffusion governed by both lipid matrix erosion and hydrogel swelling [44]. Finally, the storage stability of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG was investigated by monitoring the variation of size, zeta potential, and drug concentration at 25 °C. As displayed in Fig. 1M and Figs. S1–S2, TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG were stable for 4 weeks, indicating their short-term stability.

Ocular pharmacokinetics studies of TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG

Ocular pharmacokinetics behavior following a single dose topical administration of TH-NLC-ISG, TH-NLC, and TH-Susp was evaluated (Fig. 2A). As shown in Fig. 2B, fluorescein sodium was employed as a model drug to evaluate the ocular retention of the NLC-ISG formulation in rabbits. The fluorescence on the corneal surface diminished rapidly within 20 min post-administration for both the NLC and Susp groups. In contrast, the NLC-ISG group exhibited a significantly prolonged fluorescence signal, which remained clearly observable at 60 min. These results indicate that the ISG formation effectively extended the ocular residence time. Furthermore, Fig. 2C suggests that the enhanced retention is attributable to the improved bioadhesion of the ISG system to the corneal tissue. Fig. 2D depicts the TH concentration-time profiles in tear. TH-NLC achieved rapid peak concentration (Cmax) at 5 min but exhibited rapid clearance. Notably, TH-NLC-ISG maintained significantly higher tear TH concentrations than TH-NLC throughout the sampling period. Consistent with above observation, area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) in ocular tissues and tears for TH-NLC-ISG was greater than that of TH-NLC (Table S4), indicating substantially enhanced precorneal residence time. Fig. 2E displayed that TH-NLC-ISG produced significantly higher drug concentration in conjunctiva than TH-Susp and TH-NLC, with Cmax elevated 1.59-fold higher than TH-NLC and 11.43-fold higher than TH-Susp. Consistently, AUC0-120min of TH-NLC-ISG was exceed than TH-NLC (1.30-fold) and TH-Susp (92.06-fold). Corneal TH concentrations were significantly elevated in the TH-NLC-ISG group versus both TH-NLC and TH-Susp at all timepoints (Fig. 2F). As summarized in Table S4, pharmacokinetics analysis revealed a 4.42-fold higher corneal AUC0-120min for TH-NLC compared to TH-Susp in corneal. Strikingly, TH-NLC-ISG further enhanced this effect, achieving a 7.78-fold AUC increase over TH-Susp, corresponding to a 4.13-fold improvement relative to TH-NLC alone. Enhanced corneal permeability is crucial for the efficacy and bioavailability of ophthalmic drugs [45]. Corneal permeability studies via Valia-Chien diffusion cells demonstrated the superior trans-corneal permeation of TH from TH-NLC compared to TH-Susp. As shown in Fig. 2G, the trans-corneal permeability coefficient (Papp) of TH-NLC (2.04 ± 0.27 × 10−8 cm/s) was significantly higher than that from TH-Susp (0.52 ± 0.07 × 10−8 cm/s), representing 3.92-fold enhancement, respectively. Similarly, the steady-state flux (Jss) of TH-NLC was 2.53-fold higher than TH-Susp, respectively (Fig. 2H). Critically, TH-NLC also achieved significantly higher corneal drug penetration than TH-Susp (Fig. 2I). These results indicate that TH-NLC enhances corneal penetration of TH due to the extremely small nanoscale dimension [45]. Furthermore, corneal hydration rates for TH-NLC remained below 80%, significantly lower than TH-Susp (about 90%), indicating enhanced safety of the nanoparticle-based formulations (Fig. S3).

Corneal penetration in rabbits was directly visualized using two-photon laser scanning microscopy following administration of C6-labeled formulations. At 30 min post topical ocular instillation, intense green fluorescence (C6) was observed in the corneal epithelium for both TH-NLC-ISG and TH-NLC, whereas TH-Susp exhibited only faint signals (Fig. 2J). TH-NLC exhibited diffusion into the corneal stroma by the 30 min. Although TH-NLC-ISG also penetrated the stroma by 60 min, it demonstrated significantly stronger fluorescence intensity — an effect attributable to its sustained-release properties. Critically, at 120 min, green fluorescence was detected in the corneal endothelium with TH-NLC-ISG, while minimal signal persisted at corneal epithelium for TH-NLC and TH-Susp. Collectively, these findings suggest that TH-NLC enhances trans-corneal permeability, and its integration into the ISG matrix further amplifies penetration by sustained drug release and significantly prolonging ocular residence time. To further investigate the distribution and accumulation of formulations within the corneal epithelium at 30 min, we visualized their localization across the squamous, wing, and basal cell layers using two-photon microscopy. Results revealed that both TH-NLC and TH-NLC-ISG exhibited significantly stronger green fluorescence intensity in all three epithelial layers compared to TH-Susp, with TH-NLC-ISG demonstrating the most robust signal (Fig. 2K). These results establish that TH-NLC-ISG simultaneously enhances drug permeation and prolongs ocular retention through synergistic integration of NLC and ISG technologies, resulting in the enhanced corneal drug distribution.

A proposed mechanism of action for the antifungal effect is schematically illustrated in Fig. 4A. Meanwhile, the antifungal activity was evaluated using both agar diffusion and microdilution assays. To further elucidate the antifungal mechanism of TH, molecular docking was employed to examine its binding to squalene epoxidase. 3D structural simulation (Fig. 4H) reveals that TH embeds tightly within fSQE hydrophobic active site cavity. The interaction map between TH and the binding pocket residues of fSQE demonstrates that the molecular interactions of the TH core scaffold with enzyme residues primarily involve characteristic CH-π interactions, π–π stacking, and hydrophobic forces, with the latter being predominant. Subsequent 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations demonstrated distinct binding stability TH exhibited higher conformational flexibility in fSQLE (RMSD 1.70 Å, Fig. 5C) than in hSQLE (RMSD1.25 Å, Fig. 5D), indicating greater flexibility within the fungal binding pocket. Consistent with this, binding pocket residues in fSQLE showed substantially higher conformational fluctuations compared to hSQLE (Fig. 5E and F), while TH displayed greater positional variance within fSQLE's binding pocket than hSQLE (Fig. 5G and H). Although molecular docking indicated highly similar initial binding conformations in residue and ligand conformation superposition for TH in both enzymes, molecular dynamics simulations revealed more pronounced conformational rearrangements of both TH and its surrounding residues in fSQLE. These results suggest that the higher flexibility of fSQLE's binding pocket may facilitate adaptive binding of TH, potentially contributing to its selective antifungal activity. Although the flexibility of the fSQLE binding pocket may allow conformational adaptations and decreased drug binding efficacy, the firm inhibition by TH ensures potent enzyme inactivation [46,47] and remains the principal mechanism underlying the antifungal potency of TH-NLC-ISG.

Superposition analysis of binding pocket residues from hSQLE and fSQLE, along with TH conformations from molecular docking, revealed significant conformational differences in two key residues within the binding pockets of hSQLE and fSQLE. Residue 74 in hSQLE exhibited an RMSF of 0.5016 nm, while its fungal counterpart (residue 101 in fSQLE) showed an RMSF of 0.5784 nm. Similarly, residue 203 in hSQLE had an RMSF of 0.6124 nm, compared to 0.7486 nm for the corresponding residue 230 in fSQLE (Table S6). These elevated RMSF values indicate that both critical residues possess higher conformational flexibility in fSQLE than in hSQLE. Furthermore, throughout the entire simulation sampling period, the RMSD values for the backbone of these two distinct binding pocket residues were significantly higher in hSQLE compared to fSQLE (Fig. 5I and J). This observation suggests that the binding pocket of fSQLE may exhibit enhanced structural complementarity with TH, resulting in less perturbation to the protein backbone during ligand-protein binding, thus facilitating the binding process (Fig. 5K).

Conclusion

This study developed a dual-functional ion-activated mucoadhesive ocular in situ gel (TH-NLC-ISG) that synergistically integrated TH-loaded NLC with GG/CMCS polymer matrix, enhancing corneal retention and trans-corneal penetration for efficient fungal keratitis therapy. To solve the problem of poor solubility of TH, NLC was used, which significantly improved the solubility by 312 times. To sustain the drug release and prolong the corneal residence time, GG/CMCS was used to mix with drug loaded NLC, which achieved sol-to-gel transition immediately when got in contact with tears. The in situ formed gel showed shear thinning and good self-healing property, which empowered the gel with reduced foreign body sensation in the eye during blinking and enough viscosity after blinking. Besides, the NLC-gel significantly improved the corneal permeability of TH and extended the retention time on the ocular surface. In the in vivo FK models, TH-NLC-ISG significantly impeded the FK development, reduced the FK-related corneal perforation ratios. In summary, TH-NLC-ISG could safely and efficiently deliver TH to the cornea, significantly improved the FK therapy. In conclusion, TH-NLC-ISG represents a promising, patient-friendly platform for topical ocular delivery of poorly soluble drugs, demonstrating strong potential for clinical translation in the management of fungal keratitis.

The Use of Contralateral AEA‐Pedicled Septal Flap Combined With HBF for Complex Skull Base Defects
Authors: Shichang Li, Bao‐Feng Wang, Ping‐Ping Cao
Published: 2026

Endoscopic repair of posterior frontal sinus wall defects remains technically demanding due to anatomical constraints. While the Hadad‐Bassagasteguy flap (HBF) has transformed midline defect reconstruction, 1 its inability to adequately cover posterior frontal sinus defects frequently necessitates open approaches, which carry inherent risks. We present the first documented case utilizing bilateral pedicled nasal septal flaps (NSFs)—combining conventional HBF with a contralateral anterior ethmoid artery (AEA)‐pedicled septal flap—to simultaneously address large ethmoid roof and frontal sinus defects while preserving frontal sinus physiological drainage and avoiding external incisions.

Case Report

A 38‐year‐old male with a history of post‐traumatic skull base fracture presented with recurrent meningitis despite prior cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak repair. The previous CSF rhinorrhea was repaired using fascia lata graft from the thigh at a local hospital; however, the specific surgical technique and site of repair were not clearly documented. The patient reported new‐onset intermittent clear rhinorrhea originating from the right nasal cavity. Biochemical analysis of the nasal discharge, coupled with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) cisternography, confirmed CSF leakage. High‐resolution computed tomography (CT) and MRI identified two skull base defects with associated meningoencephaloceles: (1) a 2.08 × 1.60 cm2 defect in the right posterior frontal sinus wall, and (2) a 1.63 × 1.43 cm2 defect in the ethmoid roof (Figure 1).

Computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reveals two anterior skull base defects (A and B) with associated brain tissue herniation into the right ethmoid sinus (C and D). And a left ocular prosthesis is noted (B and D).

Surgical intervention proceeded as follows: the herniated meningoencephalocele tissue was meticulously coagulated using bipolar radiofrequency until it fully retracted into the skull. Radical excision of the surrounding mucosa was performed to create fresh vascularized wound beds, thereby mitigating the risk of postoperative mucocele formation (Figure 2A,D). Multilayer reconstruction was accomplished using an underlay fascia lata graft followed by an overlay NSF. The defect in the ethmoid roof was reconstructed using an HBF (Figure 2B) as previously described. 1 For the frontal sinus defect, a contralateral AEA‐pedicled NSF was harvested and transposed through an intentionally widened preexisting septal perforation (Figure 2E). To optimize exposure, a Draf 2b frontal sinusotomy was performed. Critical to preserving flap vascularity, two vertical incisions were made: one 1 cm anterior and another 1 cm posterior to the projection of the middle turbinate axilla to the septum. They were connected inferiorly by a horizontal incision along the nasal floor. 2 The septal perforation was carefully enlarged anteriorly to permit tension‐free flap transposition. The recipient site was prepared by radical mucosal excision to prevent mucocele development. Fibrin glue and gelatin sponges were applied to stabilize the flaps, followed by iodoform gauze packing for structural support. Postoperative endoscopic surveillance at 3 months confirmed complete healing, with a patent frontal sinus outflow tract and no evidence of CSF leakage (Figure 2C,F).

Two defects—right ethmoid roof (A) and frontal sinus wall (D)—were repaired with a Hadad‐Bassagasteguy flap (HBF) (B, C) and a contralateral septal flap (E, F), respectively, with ▲ and ★ marking flaps and sinus drainages.

Discussion

The distinction between the contralateral AEA‐based flap and the HBF lies in their respective vascular pedicles, which dictates their distinct clinical applications. The HBF, based on the posterior septal artery, serves as the versatile and reliable first‐line workhorse for reconstructing the majority of mid‐to‐posterior skull base defects. 1 , 3 , 4 In contrast, the contralateral AEA flap represents a specialized salvage option. Sourced from the opposite AEA, its unique cross‐nasal rotational arc enables it to reach challenging defects in the anterior skull base and lateral frontal sinus. This flap is reserved for specific scenarios where the HBF is either unavailable or insufficient for coverage. This study presents the first documented use of bilateral pedicled NSFs—combining HBF with a contralateral AEA‐based septal flap—to simultaneously reconstruct large ethmoid roof and posterior frontal sinus defects. This approach addresses two major challenges in endoscopic skull base surgery: (1) the avoidance of open approaches and potential morbidities, and (2) the limited availability of vascularized flaps for posterior frontal sinus defects following HBF harvest.

Compared to traditional methods, this approach offers three distinct advantages: (1) a minimally invasive salvage option for failed prior repairs or patients contraindicated for open surgery, (2) dual‐flap synergy, enabling concurrent reconstruction of ethmoid (HBF) and frontal (contralateral NSF) defects, and (3) active drainage pathway preservation, reducing long‐term mucocele risks (reported incidence: 6%‐25%), 5 though extended follow‐up remains essential.

Although our results are encouraging, larger cohort studies are needed to quantify long‐term patency rates and mucocele prevention efficacy, refine patient selection criteria based on defect characteristics (eg, defect size/location), and compare outcomes with pericranial flaps or free grafts. This technique meaningfully expands the endoscopic armamentarium for complex anterior skull base reconstruction, aligning perfectly with modern morbidity‐reducing paradigms.

Conclusion

The contralateral AEA‐pedicled NSF technique meaningfully expands the endoscopic armamentarium for complex anterior skull base reconstruction, aligning with modern paradigms prioritizing morbidity reduction and minimally invasive solutions. Future multicenter studies should validate these findings in larger cohorts.

The role and prognostic value of mitochondria-related gene PARK7 in breast cancer
Authors: Shuxian Yu, Yiming Zhou, Shuangyi Zhang, Fengwu He, Leting Jiang, Xiaoai Lv, Xidong Gu, Xiaohong Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer is the most common malignant tumor among women globally, with incidence and mortality rates ranking first among female cancers. Mitochondrial dysfunction plays a crucial role in the occurrence, development, and metastasis of breast cancer, but the specific mechanisms remain unclear. This study aims to explore the mechanisms and prognostic value of the mitochondrial-related gene PARK7 in breast cancer.MethodsThis study integrated summary data on the methylation levels, expression levels, and protein abundance of mitochondrial-related genes. The discovery phase data were sourced from the IEU GWAS database, while the validation phase data came from the FinnGen study dataset. Mendelian randomization and colocalization analyses were conducted to explore the potential associations between the methylation, expression, and protein abundance of mitochondrial-related genes and breast cancer risk. Clinical and pathological data, as well as gene expression data from breast cancer patients, were obtained from the TCGA database. Cox regression analysis was used to assess the relationship between PARK7 expression and clinical pathological features, and a prognostic model was constructed. Additionally, in vitro experiments were performed to verify the effects of PARK7 on triple-negative breast cancer cells.ResultsThe study found that high expression of the PARK7 gene is closely related to a reduced risk of breast cancer (OR 0.91, 95% Cl 0.88–0.95; PPH4 = 0.90), which was also validated in the FinnGen dataset. Patients with high PARK7 expression had longer survival times and higher survival probabilities in the TCGA database. In vitro experiments showed that overexpression of PARK7 significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and colony formation of triple-negative breast cancer cells, while promoting apoptosis and significantly increasing mitochondrial membrane potential, indicating altered mitochondrial function. Furthermore, Western blot analysis revealed that overexpression of PARK7 led to increased PTEN protein expression and decreased p-PI3K and p-AKT protein expression, suggesting that PARK7 may exert its tumor-suppressive effects by modulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway.Conclusion High expression of the PARK7 gene is closely related to reduced breast cancer risk and good prognosis, and it may exert its tumor-suppressive effects by regulating the expression of apoptosis-related proteins and migration-related proteins, as well as by affecting mitochondrial function and modulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. It can serve as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of breast cancer.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-025-02153-z.Keywords: Breast cancer, PARK7, Mitochondria, Mendelian randomization, In vitro experiments

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common cancer type among women globally. In 2022, it accounted for 25% of new female cancer cases, with 2.3 million new cases and 670,000 deaths worldwide [1]. The occurrence and development of breast cancer is a complex, multifactorial and multistage process, involving the interplay of genetic factors, environmental factors and lifestyle factors [2, 3]. In recent years, with the deepening of research on breast cancer, more and more studies have shown that mitochondrial dysfunction plays a key role in the occurrence, development and metastasis of breast cancer.

Mitochondria, as the powerhouses of the cell, are not only involved in cellular energy metabolism but also closely related to various biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress [4, 5]. Under normal conditions, mitochondria maintain cellular energy metabolism balance and regulate the oxidative-reduction state within the cell to ensure normal cellular physiological functions. However, in breast cancer cells, mitochondrial function is often disrupted, characterized by changes in mitochondrial membrane potential, increased oxidative stress levels, and abnormal expression of mitochondrial-related genes [6]. These changes not only promote the proliferation and survival of breast cancer cells but also enhance their invasive and metastatic capabilities, thereby driving the occurrence and development of breast cancer [7]. Furthermore, mitochondrial dysfunction is also closely related to drug resistance in breast cancer, further complicating the treatment of the disease [8, 9].

This study aims to systematically explore the mechanisms of action and prognostic value of mitochondrial-related genes in breast cancer by integrating multi-omics data, in the hope of providing new targets and ideas for the diagnosis, treatment, and prognostic assessment of breast cancer.

Methods

Association screening of mitochondrial genes with breast cancer initiation and progression

Research design

To identify mitochondrial dysfunction characterized by genetic predisposition in the mitochondrial-related gene set, we extracted a list of 1136 known mitochondrial-related genes from the human MitoCarta3.0 database [10]. These genes are all nuclear-encoded mitochondrial proteins, while the 13 genes in the mitochondrial genome were not included in this study. We then extracted instrumental variables (IVs) for these mitochondrial-related genes at the levels of methylation, gene expression, and protein abundance, respectively. Subsequently, Mendelian Randomization analysis was conducted for breast cancer at each biological level, and colocalization analysis was applied to strengthen causal inference.

Data sources

In this study, we utilized the IEU GWAS database [11] as the primary discovery dataset and validated the levels of gene methylation, gene expression, and protein abundance using the FinnGen study dataset [12]. Through the integration and analysis of multi-omics data, we revealed a potential molecular network associated with mitochondrial dysfunction. Additionally, QTL analysis further elucidated the associations between single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and levels of DNA methylation, gene expression, and protein abundance.

The dataset ieu-a-1126 from the IEU GWAS database includes 228,951 individuals with breast cancer (122,977 cases and 105,974 controls).The samples involved in this study included SNP-CpG associations in blood extracted from mQTL data of 3560 individuals of European descent. The methylation data were normalized using a generalized linear model with a logistic link function and adjusted for variables such as chip type, sex, and age. The eQTL data covered 52,498 individuals, while the pQTL data included 65,325 individuals. For the protein levels in the pQTL data, we performed rank-based inverse normal transformation and adjusted for factors such as age and sex to eliminate potential confounding effects.

This study also utilized data from the GTEx portal (https://gtexportal.org/home/), which is a dataset containing various unique tissue types from 960 donors. Among these donors, there are 211 normal breast tissue samples; additionally, there are 396 individuals who have both genotype data and expression data from breast tissue (337 of European descent; 47 of African descent).This resource facilitates the study of gene expression across different tissues, thereby providing deeper insights into the role of specific genes in the pathogenesis of breast cancer.

In this study, the replication dataset was derived from the FinnGen study, which included 8401 breast cancer-related cases and 115,178 controls. The discovery phase utilized data from the IEU GWAS database, and the replication phase utilized data from the FinnGen study.

Summary-data Mendelian Randomization was employed to assess the associations between mitochondrial-related gene methylation, expression, and protein abundance and breast cancer risk.The HEIDI test is employed to detect pleiotropy in genetic instrumental variables, with a HEIDI value less than 0.01 indicating possible pleiotropy, which could lead to the exclusion of these variables from the analysis. The SMR method and the SMR software tool (version 1.3.1) are used to perform Mendelian randomization analysis to evaluate the causal relationship between genetic variations and disease. P-values are adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate (FDR) at a level of 0.05. Associations that have FDR-adjusted p-values less than 0.05 and HEIDI test p-values greater than 0.01 undergo colocalization analysis to assess whether the genetic signals might originate from the same causal variant.

Colocalization analysis

Colocalization analysis was performed using the coloc R package (version 5.2.3) to identify common causal variants between breast cancer and mitochondrial-related mQTLs (methylation quantitative trait loci), eQTLs (expression quantitative trait loci), or pQTLs (protein quantitative trait loci). The colocalization analysis was based on Bayesian inference and considered five hypotheses: (1) H0: Neither trait has a causal variant; (2) H1: Only gene expression has a causal variant; (3) H2: Only disease risk has a causal variant; (4) H3: The two traits are driven by different causal variants; (5) H4: The two traits share the same causal variant.

For colocalization of pQTL-GWAS, eQTL-GWAS, and mQTL-GWAS, the colocalization region windows were set to ± 1000 kb, ± 1000 kb, and ± 500 kb, respectively. The prior probabilities for causal variants being associated only with trait 1 (e.g., gene expression or methylation) and only with trait 2 (breast cancer) were set at 1.0 × 10⁻⁴, 1.0 × 10⁻⁴, and 1.0 × 10⁻⁵, respectively. Evidence for colocalization was considered present when the posterior probability of H4 (PPH4) was greater than 0.70, with this threshold corresponding to a false discovery rate (FDR) of less than 0.05, thereby strengthening the evidence for causal inference.

Expression profile and clinical relevance of PARK7 in breast cancer

Download of breast cancer patient data from TCGA

In this study, clinical pathological and gene expression data of 1232 breast cancer patients were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The inclusion criteria were as follows: ① pathologically confirmed breast cancer; ② complete clinical pathological information (age, gender, tumor stage, TNM stage, etc.); ③ available PARK7 gene expression data. After data cleaning, a total of 1203 patients were finally included and divided into high and low PARK7 expression groups based on the median expression level.

COX regression analysis

The Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to evaluate the relationship between PARK7 expression and clinicopathological features in breast cancer patients. Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated, and both univariate and multivariate analyses were performed. Univariate analysis included variables such as age, gender, tumor stage, T stage, N stage, and M stage. Variables with statistical significance in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis to verify the independent association of PARK7 expression. The results were visualized using a forest plot. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.0), with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Clinical correlation analysis

The “limma” R package was used to analyze the correlation between PARK7 expression and clinicopathological factors, with results visualized using “ggplot2”. The “survival” and “survminer” R packages were employed to generate Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves for breast cancer patients. The “timeROC” R package was used to plot ROC curves and evaluate the predictive accuracy of TMOD3 in prognosis. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of TMOD3 and clinicopathological features were performed using the “survival” R package, with results presented as forest plots. Finally, based on the Cox regression results, a nomogram model was constructed using the “rms” and “survival” R packages, and calibration plots were generated to validate the model’s accuracy.

Single-cell analysis

In this study, the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE161529 was downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds), comprising 69 samples from 52 breast cancer patients. Data preprocessing and quality control were performed using the Seurat R package. After filtering high-quality cells, principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted, and the top 20 principal components were selected based on an elbow plot for downstream analysis. UMAP was employed for dimensionality reduction and clustering to identify distinct cell types, which were then annotated using established marker genes. The expression of the PARK7 gene across different cell types was analyzed in detail.

Validation through in vitro experiments

Cells and culture conditions

The main cell lines used include the human triple-negative breast cancer cell lines MDA-MB-231 and Bcap 37, and the human normal breast epithelial cell line MCF-10 A. Bcap 37 cells are cultured in RPMI-1640 medium, while MDA-MB-231 cells are cultured in high-glucose DMEM medium. The culture conditions for these cells are 90% basal medium + 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) + 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution. MCF-10 A cells are directly cultured in MCF-10 A specific medium. All cells are cultured in a cell incubator (temperature: 37 °C; CO₂ concentration: 5%).

Detection of PARK7 mRNA expression by RT-qPCR

A variety of breast cancer cell lines (MCF-7, BT-474, MDA-MB-453, Bcap-37, MDA-MB-231, KPL-4) and normal breast cells (MCF-10 A) were collected. Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method. After chloroform extraction, isopropanol precipitation, and ethanol washing, the RNA was dissolved in DEPC-treated water. The RNA concentration was measured using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (A260/A280 ratio of 1.8–2.1), and the integrity was verified by agarose gel electrophoresis. One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using PARK7-specific primers synthesized by Sigma Biotech (Table 3). The 20 µL reaction mixture contained SYBR Green Mix, 2 µL of cDNA, and 0.5 µL of primers. The qPCR program was as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. The specificity of the amplification was confirmed by a melting curve analysis. GAPDH was used as an internal reference gene. The relative expression levels were calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method. No template control (NTC) and reverse transcription-negative control (-RT) were included. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Western blot experiment

Logarithmic phase Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control group and PARK7 overexpression group) were collected. After washing with PBS, RIPA lysis buffer (containing protease inhibitors) was added and the cells were lysed on ice for 30 min. The lysates were then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. The protein concentration was measured using the BCA method. Subsequently, 5× loading buffer was added to the supernatant, and the mixture was boiled at 100 °C for 5 min to denature the proteins. Thirty micrograms of protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and then transferred to PVDF membranes using a wet transfer method. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk at room temperature for 1 h, followed by incubation with primary antibodies (PARK7, Bax, Bad, Bcl-2, MMP9, Vimentin, Slug, Snail, and GAPDH, etc.) at 4 °C overnight. The membranes were washed three times with TBST, followed by incubation with secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. After washing three times with TBST again, the ECL chemiluminescent substrate (mixed 1:1 from solutions A and B) was applied, and the bands were visualized using a Bio-Rad chemiluminescent imaging system.

Overexpression of PARK7 gene

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in 6-well plates. When the cell confluence reached 70%–80%, the transfection was performed according to the Lipofectamine 2000 protocol. Specifically, 2 µg of the PARK7 overexpression plasmid and 5 µL of Lipofectamine 2000 were each diluted in 250 µL of serum-free Opti-MEM medium. After incubation at room temperature for 5 min, the two dilutions were mixed and incubated for an additional 20 min to form the transfection complex. The complex was then added to the cells. After 6 h of incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, the medium was replaced with complete culture medium, and the cells were cultured for an additional 48 h. The cells were then collected for subsequent experiments.

Detection of cell proliferation by cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control group and PARK7 overexpression group) were used as experimental subjects. After trypsinization was terminated with complete culture medium, the cells were resuspended by pipetting and transferred to a 15 mL centrifuge tube. The cells were centrifuged at 800 rpm for 5 min, the supernatant was discarded, and the cell pellet was resuspended in 4 mL of culture medium. After counting, the cells were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 3 × 10³ cells per well, with 100 µL of culture medium per well. Five time points were set: 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 96 h, and 120 h, with three replicate wells for each time point. The cells were cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ until the designated time. Then, the culture medium was replaced with 100 µL of medium containing 10% CCK-8 solution, and the cells were incubated for 0.5 h. The absorbance (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader. The proliferation curves were plotted using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Detection of cell viability and toxicity by Calcein/PI fluorescent double staining

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control group and PARK7 overexpression group) were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of approximately 1 × 10⁴cells per well. After the cells reached an appropriate density, the staining working solution was prepared according to the instructions of the Calcein/PI Cell Viability and Cytotoxicity Assay Kit (containing 2 µM Calcein-AM and 1 µg/mL PI). The culture medium was removed, and the cells were gently washed once with PBS. The staining working solution was then added to the wells, and the cells were incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 15–30 min. The cells were observed and imaged under a fluorescence microscope. Live cells exhibited green fluorescence, while dead cells exhibited red fluorescence.

Colony formation assay on petri dishes

Logarithmic phase Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control and PARK7 overexpression groups) were washed with PBS, digested with 0.25% trypsin, and resuspended in complete culture medium to create a single-cell suspension. After adjusting the cell density to 500 cells/mL, 2 mL of the suspension (about 1000 cells) was seeded into each well of a 6-well plate, with three replicates per group. The cells were cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, with medium changes every 3 days for 10–14 days until colonies formed. The medium was then discarded, and the cells were washed twice with PBS, fixed with methanol for 15 min, air-dried, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min, washed with PBS, and air-dried again. The colonies were photographed, and the data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Flow cytometry detection of mitochondrial membrane potential changes

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control and PARK7 overexpression groups) were seeded in 6-well plates. After reaching an appropriate density, the cells were collected, resuspended in PBS, and centrifuged to wash. The supernatant was discarded, and 500 µL of 2 µM JC-1 staining solution was added. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 20 min, then centrifuged for 5 min and washed twice with PBS. The supernatant was discarded again, and the cells were resuspended in 500 µL of PBS for detection by flow cytometry. The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Transwell assay

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control and PARK7 overexpression groups) were serum-starved for 12 h in serum-free medium. The cells were then digested with trypsin, collected, washed with PBS, and resuspended in serum-free medium at a density of 2 × 10⁵cells/mL. A volume of 200 µL of the cell suspension was added to the upper chamber of the Transwell insert, while 600 µL of complete culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum was added to the lower chamber. The cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h. After incubation, the insert was removed, washed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. Non-migrated cells in the upper chamber were removed with a cotton swab. The migrated cells were counted under a microscope, and the data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Wound healing assay

Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control and PARK7 overexpression groups) were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of approximately 5 × 10⁵ cells per well. After the cells reached 90% confluence, a scratch was made vertically using a 200 µL pipette tip. The detached cells were removed by washing with PBS, and then serum-free medium was added. The cells were observed and photographed under a microscope at 0 h and 48 h to measure the width of the scratch. The data were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Flow cytometry detection of apoptosis

Logarithmic phase Bcap-37 and MDA-MB-231 cells (control and PARK7 overexpression groups) were collected. 100 µL of cell suspension was added to a flow cytometry tube. According to the instructions of the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit, 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL of PI were added. The mixture was gently vortexed and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. After incubation, 400 µL of Binding Buffer was added and mixed well. The samples were then analyzed using a flow cytometer. Data were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Results

Analysis of the correlation between methylation of mitochondrial-related genes and the risk of breast cancer

Based on the causal effects of methylation of mitochondrial-related genes on breast cancer, 247 CpG sites near 115 unique genes passed marginal significance (p < 0.05) after excluding associations with P-HEIDI < 0.01. After multiple testing correction (FDR < 0.05), 43 CpG sites near 18 unique genes were identified (Fig. 1). Among the 43 identified signals, the following CpG sites were supported by strong evidence of colocalization (PPH4 > 0.70): GCDH methylation at cg21050076 (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.04–1.12; PPH4 = 0.954), NSUN4 methylation at cg02459555 (OR 1.11, 95% CI 1.05–1.18; PPH4 = 0.700), PC methylation at cg21628794 (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.05–1.14; PPH4 = 0.892), and TRMT1 methylation at cg12014333 (OR 1.15, 95% CI 1.09–1.22; PPH4 = 0.968) and cg22237401 (OR 1.13, 95% CI 1.08–1.19; PPH4 = 0.972). Gene prediction showed that increased methylation levels at these CpG sites—cg21050076 of the GCDH gene, cg02459555 of the NSUN4 gene, cg21628794 of the PC gene, and cg12014333 and cg22237401 of the TRMT1 gene—were associated with an increased risk of breast cancer. Among these identified CpG sites, the cg21050076 site of the GCDH gene was replicated in FinnGen (OR 1.04, 95% CI 1.02–1.06), and the cg02459555 site of the NSUN4 gene was replicated in FinnGen (OR 1.03, 95% CI 1.01–1.05).The colocalization analysis results of the CpG sites cg21050076, cg02459555, cg21628794, cg12014333, and cg22237401 with breast cancer indicate that these methylation sites are significantly associated with the occurrence of breast cancer and the regulation of gene expression (Fig. 2).

The correlation analysis of mitochondrial gene expression and breast cancer risk

The results of the causal effects of mitochondrial-related gene expression on breast cancer are shown in Fig. 3. A total of 102 associations were identified at the nominal significance level with breast cancer (P < 0.05). After multiple testing correction and colocalization analysis, gene prediction showed that elevated expression levels of CASP8, PICK1, OCIAD1, MRPL2, PARK7, GTPBP3, DAP3, SETD9, FDPS, MRPS16, C15orf61, and others were associated with a reduced risk of breast cancer. Elevated expression levels of genes such as HSCB, GCDH, PMPCB, MRPS30, NIT2, NSUN4, SIRT5, MUTYH, KYAT3, MPC2, AUH, MRPS18C, COX11, MTX1, YBEY, UQCR10, NDUFA13, GPX1 were associated with an increased risk of breast cancer (Figs. 3 and 4A).

Among these identified genes, PARK7 (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.88–0.95; PPH4 = 0.90) was supported by strong evidence of colocalization (PPH4 > 0.70). The association of PARK7 was replicated in FinnGen, with predicted high expression levels being negatively correlated with breast cancer risk (OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.83–0.97). The colocalization analysis results of the PARK7 gene with breast cancer indicated that genetic variants of PARK7 are significantly associated with the occurrence of breast cancer and the regulation of gene expression (Fig. 4B).

Mitochondrial-related gene-encoded proteins and breast cancer risk

A total of 11 proteins encoded by mitochondrial-related genes were identified as being independently associated with an increased risk of breast cancer, with P < 0.05. After multiple testing correction, elevated protein expression levels of KYAT3 (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.04–1.14) and NIT2 (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.13–1.50) were found to be associated with an increased risk of breast cancer (Fig. 5). Unfortunately, among these two identified signals, no mitochondrial-related gene-encoded proteins were supported by strong evidence of colocalization (PPH4 > 0.70).

Mitochondrial associated protein analysis and its association with breast cancer risk

Tissue validation

In breast tissue, we further explored the causal relationships between the expression of identified genes and breast cancer risk. The study results showed that the expression levels of CCBL2, MRPS30, COX11, GCDH, and YBEY genes, as genetically predicted, were significantly associated with an increased risk of breast cancer (Fig. 6). In contrast, the expression levels of PARK7, NSUN4, CASP8, OCIAD1, SETD9, and PICK1 genes, as genetically predicted, were not associated with breast cancer risk.

Nevertheless, since the PARK7 gene was identified in the previous study as a gene supported by strong evidence of colocalization, this study will further determine the specific impact of this gene on breast cancer.

Association analysis of PARK7 and clinical features of breast cancer

By comparing the expression levels of the PARK7 gene in normal breast cells and breast cancer cells, it is evident that the expression of this gene is significantly higher in breast cancer cells than in normal breast cells, with a statistically significant difference (P < 0.001) (Fig. 7A, B).

When conducting univariate COX regression analysis of PARK7 expression with age, weight, gender, pathological grade, and clinical stage as continuous variables, it was found that the expression level of PARK7 is significantly associated with age, weight, pathological grade, and tumor stage (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). In contrast, when conducting multivariate COX regression analysis of PARK7 expression with age, weight, gender, pathological grade, and clinical stage as continuous variables, the results showed that the expression level of PARK7 is significantly associated with age, weight, and tumor stage (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7D). The correlation between PARK7 expression levels and clinical pathological features of breast cancer patients is shown in Fig. 7E. Box plots show no significant correlation between PARK7 gene expression levels and age, gender, or tumor stage of breast cancer patients (P > 0.05) (Fig. 7F–H). Scatter plots display the correlation between PARK7 gene expression levels and the weight of breast cancer patients, showing a slight but statistically significant negative correlation between PARK7 gene expression levels and weight (R = − 0.11, P < 0.001) (Fig. 7I).

The upregulation of PARK7 in breast cancer cells and its correlation with clinical features suggest its potential role in the progression of breast cancer.

PARK7 gene expression and prognostic and predictive model evaluation in breast cancer

In this study, samples were divided into high-expression and low-expression groups based on the expression levels of the PARK7 gene. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis revealed that breast cancer patients with high PARK7 expression had longer survival times and higher survival probabilities in the TCGA database, indicating a protective effect against breast cancer (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8A). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis showed that the area under the curve (AUC) for PARK7 gene expression was 0.699, demonstrating its potential for prognostic prediction (Fig. 8B). Additionally, scatter and linear plots revealed a negative correlation between PARK7 expression levels and risk scores, with high expression corresponding to low risk scores and low expression corresponding to high risk scores (Fig. 8C, D).

Based on these data, a nomogram was constructed to evaluate the impact of PARK7 gene expression on prognosis. The nomogram indicated that high PARK7 expression corresponded to lower scores, further confirming its association with favorable prognosis (Fig. 8E). Calibration curves showed that the nomogram had high accuracy in predicting recurrence at 1, 3, and 5 years, making it a reliable reference for clinical decision-making (Fig. 8F). Decision curve analysis (DCA) curves demonstrated that the predictive model had significant clinical utility in predicting recurrence at 1, 3, and 5 years (Fig. 8G). ROC curves further validated the nomogram’s discriminative ability at these time points, with the strongest predictive power at 1 year (Fig. 8H).

In summary, PARK7 gene expression level is an important prognostic indicator, with high expression levels being closely associated with favorable prognosis in breast cancer patients.

Impact of PARK7 on immune infiltration and prognosis in breast cancer

Figure 9A illustrates the distribution of infiltration levels of various immune cells in breast cancer, which correlate with different states of copy number variation (including deep deletion, arm-level deletion, diploid/normal, arm-level gain, and high amplification). Figure 9B shows a negative correlation between the expression levels of the PARK7 gene and the infiltration levels of different immune cells; the higher the expression level of PARK7, the lower the infiltration level of immune cells. Survival analysis reveals that among patients with low PARK7 expression, B cell infiltration is positively correlated with survival rates (P < 0.05), while no significant association is observed with other types of immune cells (Fig. 9C).

Although B cell infiltration is associated with better survival rates in cases of low PARK7 expression, this does not imply that PARK7 improves prognosis by promoting immune cell infiltration. In fact, PARK7 may exert its tumor-suppressing effects through other mechanisms, such as directly inhibiting the growth of tumor cells or influencing other aspects of the tumor microenvironment. Therefore, the relationship between PARK7 and the prognosis of breast cancer may be more complex than currently understood, involving multiple biological pathways and mechanisms. Future research is needed to further explore the specific role of PARK7 in the development and treatment of breast cancer.

In vitro experiments to verify the relationship between PARK7 and triple-negative breast cancer cells

Using q-PCR technology, we measured the mRNA expression levels of PARK7 in various breast cancer cell lines, including Luminal A (MCF-7), Luminal B (BT-474), triple-negative (MDA-MB-231, Bcap37), HER2-overexpressing (MDA-MB-453), inflammatory breast cancer (KPL-4), and normal breast epithelial cells (MCF-10 A). The results showed that compared to MCF-10 A, PARK7 mRNA expression was significantly reduced in the Bcap37 cell line, with no significant difference observed in the MDA-MB-231 cell line (Fig. 11A). Western blot analysis further confirmed the reduced PARK7 protein expression in MDA-MB-231 and Bcap37 cell lines (Fig. 11B). After overexpressing PARK7 in these triple-negative breast cancer cell lines (Fig. 11C, D), the CCK-8 assay indicated that cell proliferation was significantly inhibited (P < 0.001) (Fig. 11E). Microscopic observation revealed that PARK7 overexpression led to abnormal cell morphology and increased cell death (Fig. 11F). Cell viability assays demonstrated that PARK7 overexpression significantly reduced cell viability (Fig. 11G). Clonogenic assays also showed that PARK7 overexpression significantly decreased the number of colonies formed (P < 0.01) (Fig. 11H–I). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that PARK7 overexpression significantly reduced the mitochondrial membrane potential in MDA-MB-231 and Bcap37 cell lines, indicating mitochondrial dysfunction (Fig. 11J–K). These results suggest that PARK7 may play a role in inhibiting tumor growth in triple-negative breast cancer cells.

The regulatory effect of park7 on migration and apoptosis of triple-negative breast cancer cells

According to the experimental results shown in Fig. 12, overexpression of PARK7 significantly inhibited the migration ability of MDA-MB-231 and Bcap37, two triple-negative breast cancer cell lines. This conclusion was drawn from the Transwell assay and is statistically significant (P < 0.01) (Fig. 12A, B). Furthermore, the wound healing assay indicated that overexpression of PARK7 also significantly suppressed the cells’ wound healing capacity, demonstrating its inhibitory effect on cell migration (P < 0.05) (Fig. 12C, D). Flow cytometry analysis further revealed that PARK7 overexpression significantly increased the apoptosis rate of these two cell lines (P < 0.05) (Fig. 12E, F), suggesting that PARK7 may inhibit tumor growth by promoting cell apoptosis.

Western blot analysis showed significant changes in the expression of apoptosis and migration-related proteins between the control and PARK7 overexpression groups (Fig. 12G–I). Specifically, overexpression of PARK7 leads to increased expression of pro-apoptotic proteins Bax and Bad, while the expression of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 and proteins related to cell migration, including MMP9, Vimentin, Slug, and Snail, is decreased.These results suggest that PARK7 overexpression may inhibit the migration of triple-negative breast cancer cells and promote their apoptosis by regulating the expression of these key proteins, thereby exerting its tumor-suppressive effects.

The PI3K/Akt pathway validation study of PARK7’s impact on triple-negative breast cancer cells

We conducted a comprehensive pathway enrichment analysis of the PARK7 gene and presented in Fig. 13A the ten biological pathways significantly associated with the PARK7 gene. The identification of these pathways provides crucial clues for understanding the function of the PARK7 gene and its mechanisms of action within cells. Further analysis revealed that the PARK7 gene exhibits significant enrichment in the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway (Fig. 13B), with this enrichment being statistically significant (P < 0.05), suggesting that PARK7 may play a pivotal role in this signaling pathway.

Using Western blot technology, we observed significant changes in the expression levels of proteins related to the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway between cells overexpressing PARK7 and control cells (Fig. 13C–E). Specifically, overexpression of PARK7 led to an increase in PTEN protein expression, along with a decrease in the expression of p-PI3K and p-AKT proteins. These findings suggest that overexpression of PARK7 may influence cellular function by modulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, which could have significant implications for therapeutic strategies in triple-negative breast cancer.

Discussion

Mitochondria, as the powerhouses of the cell, play a crucial role in normal cellular physiological functions [13]. However, mitochondrial dysfunction plays a key role in the occurrence and development of breast cancer [14]. Studies have shown that mitochondrial dysfunction can lead to increased levels of oxidative stress within cells, disrupting the cellular redox balance and subsequently altering gene expression patterns. These changes may activate a series of signaling pathways related to cell proliferation, apoptosis inhibition, and invasion and metastasis, thereby providing a favorable microenvironment for the malignant phenotype of breast cancer cells and accelerating their occurrence and development [8, 15]. Therefore, in-depth research into the mechanisms of mitochondrial dysfunction in breast cancer is extremely important for the development of new diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for breast cancer.

In this study, through Mendelian randomization analysis and colocalization analysis, we found that the methylation, expression, and protein abundance levels of multiple mitochondria-related genes are significantly associated with breast cancer risk. These results further support the importance of mitochondrial dysfunction in breast cancer, especially in the energy metabolism and metastasis of tumor cells.

The key gene PARK7 identified in this study stands out in breast cancer. The high expression of the PARK7 gene is closely related to a reduced risk of breast cancer (OR 0.91, 95%Cl 0.88–0.95; PPH4 = 0.90), and this association was validated in the FinnGen dataset. Further analysis showed that breast cancer patients with high PARK7 expression had longer survival times and higher survival probabilities in the TCGA database, indicating its protective effect against breast cancer.

In vitro experimental results showed that the expression of PARK7 in triple-negative breast cancer cells was significantly lower than that in normal breast cells, while overexpression of PARK7 significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and colony formation ability of triple-negative breast cancer cells and promoted apoptosis. These results suggest that PARK7 may exert its anticancer effects by regulating the expression of apoptosis-related and migration-related proteins. In addition, overexpression of PARK7 also led to a significant increase in mitochondrial membrane potential, indicating a change in mitochondrial function. This change may lead to mitochondrial dysfunction, thereby affecting cellular energy metabolism and survival. Therefore, the regulation of mitochondrial membrane potential by PARK7 may be closely related to its regulation of cell proliferation and apoptosis.

In this study, we observed significant changes in the expression levels of proteins related to the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway between cells overexpressing PARK7 and control cells through Western blot technology (Fig. 13C). Specifically, overexpression of PARK7 led to an increase in PTEN protein expression, along with a decrease in the expression of p-PI3K and p-AKT proteins. These findings suggest that overexpression of PARK7 may influence cellular function by modulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, which could have significant implications for therapeutic strategies in triple-negative breast cancer. The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway plays a key role in various biological processes, including cell proliferation, survival, and metabolism, and its abnormal activation is closely related to the occurrence and development of many cancers. By regulating this pathway, PARK7 may inhibit the proliferation and survival of triple-negative breast cancer cells, thereby exerting its anticancer effects.

Despite the important role of the PARK7 gene in breast cancer revealed by this study through various analytical methods, there are still some limitations. First, this study mainly relies on data from public databases, lacking detailed clinical information and long-term follow-up data at the individual patient level. Second, although the high expression of the PARK7 gene is associated with a reduced risk of breast cancer, its specific mechanism of action still needs to be further verified through in vitro and in vivo experiments. In addition, this study only focused on mitochondria-related genes, and future research can further explore the role of other organelle-related genes in breast cancer.

Conclusion

This study systematically revealed the significant causal association between the mitochondrial-related gene PARK7 and breast cancer through Mendelian randomization and colocalization analyses. The findings showed that high expression of the PARK7 gene is closely related to reduced breast cancer risk (OR 0.91, 95% Cl 0.88–0.95; PPH4 = 0.90), and this association was validated in the FinnGen dataset. Moreover, in the TCGA database, breast cancer patients with high PARK7 expression had longer survival times and higher survival probabilities, indicating a protective effect of PARK7 against breast cancer. In vitro experiments further confirmed that overexpression of PARK7 significantly inhibits the proliferation and migration of triple-negative breast cancer cells while promoting apoptosis. Additionally, this study found that overexpression of PARK7 may influence cellular function by modulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, which could have significant implications for therapeutic strategies in triple-negative breast cancer. These results suggest that the PARK7 gene can serve as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of breast cancer.

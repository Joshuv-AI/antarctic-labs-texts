Studies on the effect of replacing wheat straw with coriander straw in different proportions in the diet on liver health status in Awassi lambs
Authors: Y M Almohsen, Abdul Nasser Al-Omar, Mohamed Alrez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study was carried out on 40 Awassi lambs with an average weight 40.15 kg and an age of 218 days at the Sheep Research Station of Hama Agriculture Research Center, with the aim to find out the effect of replacing wheat straw with coriander straw in different proportions in the diet on liver health status in Awassi lambs. The experimental lambs were divided into four groups- the first group (control) was fed only 100% wheat straw, while the second was fed with 25% coriander straw and 75% wheat straw, the third with 50% coriander straw and 50% wheat straw and the fourth with 75% coriander straw and 25% wheat straw. Blood samples were taken from all experimental lambs in four blood draws, the first was before the start of the experiment, the second was after one month, the third was after two month and the fourth was after three months from the start of the experiment.The results showed a significant increase (P ≤ 0.01) in glucose concentration in lambs of the second, third and fourth groups. As for the concentration of total proteins, it showed a significant decrease (P ≤ 0.01) one month after the start of the experiment in lambs of the second, third and fourth groups, and its concentration increased significant at the end of the experiment (P ≤ 0.01) in the third and fourth groups (P ≤ 0.01). Albumin concentration did not show any significant change during the experiment period and in all study groups.It may be concluded from this study that alternative feeding with coriander hay led to positive changes in blood glucose and total protein concentration, indicating a beneficial effect on metabolic processes and metabolism in Awassi lambs.Keywords: Awassi lambs, Blood glucose level, Coriander straw, Liver health status, Serum total protein, Wheat straw

Background

Serum total proteins are good and important indicators for characterizing the health status of the animal [1]. Therefore, any increase in dietary proteins can be a causal factor in this total increase in serum [2], with the possibility of affecting the levels and concentrations of albumin and globulin [3]. Amino acids lead to an increase in blood protein [4]. They are considered a good source for improving metabolism, increasing milk production, regulating the thyroid gland, regulating blood sugar, reducing cholesterol, and regulating sex hormones [5]. Adding amino acids to feed concentrates affects rumen fermentation and the digestibility of nutrients in lambs [6], and hinders the intestinal absorption of both cholesterol and fats [7]. The normal state of the hepatocytes responsible for albumin synthesis and high concentrations of globulin indicate a good immune status that develops in Resistance to many pathological conditions that pregnant ewes are exposed to [8]. Some types of herbs and medicinal plants have been used in ruminant feeds in order to improve and develop productive performance, as they contain active substances that improve the environmental conditions of the digestive tract and increase the beneficial microorganisms for the animal [9], leading to increased and developed growth, raising the efficiency of feed materials, and improving the productive properties of the animal [10].

Coriander (Coriander straw) is a medicinal plant whose seeds are used to treat numerous conditions. Its active ingredients have multiple effects on the body [11], including a dual biological effect as an antibacterial and antioxidant, and its ability to inhibit free radicals [12]. Its positive effects in improving production and increasing the economic return of fattening sheep may be due to its ability to stimulate appetite, expel gas, strengthen the stomach, and act as an antispasmodic. It also regulates gastrointestinal motility [13], lowers blood pressure, and helps control blood sugar [14, 15].

Al-Qaisi and Shuwail [16]. observed no significant differences in albumin and urea concentrations. Adding coriander seeds to ewes’ diets had a positive effect on blood concentrations or parameters [17].

The results of a study conducted by Mohammed [18] showed that adding these seeds to a low-concentration diet led to a significant increase in daily weight (0.162 g/day) compared to the control (0.047 g/day). Earlier study indicated that an aqueous extract of coriander helped reduce blood sugar levels, insulin levels, and cholesterol [19]. Another finding envisaged that coriander that coriander seeds may help lower total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL cholesterol levels, raise HDL cholesterol levels, increase bile acid production, and break down cholesterol by excreting it in the feces and converting it to sterols [20]. A study by Al-Omar et al. [21]. , showed the possibility of using coriander straw as an unconventional filler feed in fattening rations for Awassi lambs instead of burning or destroying it. The second group containing 25% coriander straw and 75% wheat straw was found to be the best compared to the rest of the experimental groups, as it gave a conversion factor equivalent to wheat straw without showing any apparent health problems in the fattening indicators. They indicated that it provides an important economic return for fattening Awassi males for breeders and reduces some of the financial burden that the breeder loses. The study conducted by Al-Mohsen and Al-Omar [22] aimed to evaluate some properties of rumen fluid when using different proportions of coriander straw instead of wheat straw in fattening male Awassi lambs. The study was conducted in four groups and over three time periods (one, two, and three months). The study examined all physical and chemical changes in rumen fluid, as well as pH values. The three-month period resulted in dense and intense rumen viscosity in all groups of experimental lambs. It was recommended that further research be conducted to investigate the health, metabolic, and hematological effects. The objective of the study was to find to the effect of replacing wheat straw with coriander straw in different proportions in the diet on liver health status in Awassi lambs.

Materials and methods

Location of the study

The experiment was conducted at the Sheep Improvement Research Station of the Agricultural Scientific Research Center in Hama, located 3 km south of Hama city at latitude 35°N longitude 62°E, at an altitude of 886 feet above sea level.

Animals and duration of the experiment

The study was conducted using 40 Awassi lambs with an average initial weight of 40.15 kg and an average age of 218 days over a period of three months.

Management of animals

The percentage (%) consists of: barley kernels 53%, wheat bran 15%, unhulled cottonseed meal 20%, soybean meal 10%, dicalcium phosphate 1%, Natrium chloride 1%, feed supplements, and an antifungal (0.04%), achieving a protein content of 16.5%.

The chemical analysis of the straws used in the experiment (wheat and coriander) for all studied groups was conducted in the laboratories of the Animal Wealth Research Department of the General Authority for Agricultural Scientific Research, and the results of the analysis were as shown in Table 1.

Feed was provided to the lambs to cover the animals’ needs according to the nutritional requirements tables for sheep NRC [23]. Concentrated and filler feeds were provided according to groups twice daily, morning and evening, and the remainder was collected to calculate the amount of feed consumed by each group, and the weight of the feed provided and the remainder of it daily in order to limit the quantities of feed consumed.

Drinking water was provided to the study lambs Ad libitum, and feed was provided to the pens in two batches for each group, at 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. The lambs were kept at a suitable temperature of 25–27 °C, with a relative humidity of 50–75%.

The necessary veterinary healthcare was provided according to the program proposed by the Syrian Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform. The experimental animals were subjected to daily health checks to record disease cases by the veterinary technical staff.

Experimental design

The lambs were divided into four groups, each containing ten lambs. All environmental, rearing, and health conditions were identical. These groups were placed under as similar housing, care, and maintenance conditions as possible. These conditions were as follows:

Group 1 (control): Awassi lambs were fed a 100% wheat straw residue with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 1 (control): Awassi lambs were fed a 100% wheat straw residue with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 2: Awassi lambs were fed non-traditional residues (25% coriander straw + 75% wheat straw) with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 2: Awassi lambs were fed non-traditional residues (25% coriander straw + 75% wheat straw) with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 3: Lambs were fed a 50% coriander straw + 50% wheat straw with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 3: Lambs were fed a 50% coriander straw + 50% wheat straw with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 4: Lambs were fed a 75% coriander straw + 25% wheat straw with a standardized concentrate ration.

Group 4: Lambs were fed a 75% coriander straw + 25% wheat straw with a standardized concentrate ration.

Data recording

Blood samples were collected from the jugular vein of the lambs, after fasting for 12 h prior to each blood draw, over four time periods: the first draw before the start of the experiment, the second draw at the end of the first month, the third draw at the end of the second month, and the fourth draw at the end of the third month. These samples were transported in a container on ice to the Physiology Laboratory at the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine at Hama University after being centrifuged. The following tests were performed:

They were analyzed using total protein concentrations in g/L, albumin in g/L, and blood glucose concentrations in g/l. Analyses were performed using a BioSystem BTS-310 spectrophotometer (made in Spain).

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were statistically analyzed using SPSS version 26, employing a one-way ANOVA and Duncan’s multi-range test [24], which assessed the significance of differences between groups at a significance level of P ≤ 0.01.

Results and discussion

Table 2 shows the results of adding different percentages of coriander straw (25, 50, 75) % to the ration of improved Awassi lambs on changes in glucose, total protein, and albumin concentrations in four blood draws before the start of the experiment and every month thereafter. A significant increase (P ≤ 0.01) was observed in the glucose concentration of lambs in the second, third, and fourth groups compared to its concentration recorded in the control group. These results showed different concentrations from the studied measurements and ratios. It is worth noting that the glucose concentration recorded in the control group, which was fed only wheat straw (100%) in addition to its concentrated ration, during the experiment period reached, respectively, (106.21 ± 10.42, 110.64 ± 9.62, 100.64 ± 5.95, and 105.25 ± 11.57) mg/dl at the known ratios for the first, second, third and fourth blood draws, and this indicates the positive effect of coriander, which is an aromatic plant, on blood serum glucose concentration, which may affect carbohydrate absorption or change the activity of metabolic enzymes, in addition to the effect of its active and antioxidant compounds on insulin sensitivity and cellular glucose transport, which is reflected in its concentration in the blood [18].

As for the concentration of total proteins, as shown in Table 2, they took a different path. Their concentrations were recorded in the control group of lambs fed only wheat straw in addition to its concentrated feed during the first, second, third, and fourth blood draws, respectively, as 45.32 ± 8.71, 41.49 ± 6.84, 27.02 ± 10.94, and 35.39 ± 6.94, according to the known ratios. These values ​​were lower than those recorded by [21], as they provided a conversion factor equivalent to wheat straw without any apparent health problems in the fattening indicators, which will provide a significant economic return for fattening Awassi males for breeders. The addition of coriander straw showed a significant decrease (P ≤ 0.01) in the second draw, i.e., one month after the start of the experiment, in lambs from the second, third, and fourth groups, compared to the concentrations recorded in the control group. The concentration of total proteins in the serum of experimental lambs increased significantly at the end of the experiment (P ≤ 0.01) in the serum of the third group and (P ≤ 0.01) in the serum of the fourth group, while no significant change was observed in the concentration of total proteins in the serum of the lambs of the second experimental group compared to its concentration in the control group. The percentages of total proteins in [22] increased in the rumen fluid, when different proportions of coriander straw were used instead of wheat straw in fattening Awassi male lambs, across four groups and for three time periods, the physical, chemical, and (pH) values ​​of the rumen fluid showed significant changes in viscosity, resulting in thicker and more viscous rumen in all lamb groups. The increase in total protein may be attributed to the coriander straw’s effect on improving metabolic processes and utilization of protein in the body [14].

As for the concentration of albumin, which is produced in the liver and is part of the total proteins, which also includes immunoglobulins produced by the liver and prothrombin, Table 2 show that albumin concentrations in lambs in the first control group, which were fed wheat straw in addition to their concentrated diet during the study stages, were, respectively: 29.38 ± 9.31, 24.37 ± 2.84, 26.84 ± 2.24, and 23.53 ± 1.35. Albumin concentrations did not show any significant changes during the experimental period and in all study, groups fed different percentages of coriander (25, 50, and 75%). Al-Qaisi and Shuwail [16] also indicated no significant differences in albumin concentration when fed coriander seeds.

This indicates the positive effect of adding coriander straw in varying proportions on the liver’s ability to produce proteins. The increased concentration of total proteins in improved fattening lambs at the end of the experiment, with the replacement of coriander straw at 50% and 75%, and the fact that albumin production in the liver is not affected due to the lack of differences in its concentration compared to the control may reveal the role of coriander straw in improving metabolic processes in the body, protein digestion, and amino acid absorption by modifying the activity of digestive enzymes or stimulating the growth of rumen microflora and improving fluid balance in the rumen, which increases the animal’s utilization of dietary protein and affects the total protein concentration in the blood [18].

Conclusions

The study concluded that feeding Awassi lambs with varying proportions of coriander straw as an alternative to wheat straw led to positive effects on blood glucose and total protein concentrations, due to coriander’s role in improving digestive enzyme activity and metabolic processes in the body, and in utilizing nutrients for the growth and repair of body tissues.

Experimental and simulation investigation on failure characteristic of granite considering the effect of intermediate principal stresses
Authors: Feiyue Sun, Xiaobing Zhang, Jiaqi Guo, Xiliang Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study performs one-sided lateral unloading- and three-way five-sided force-vertical continuous loading experiments with different intermediate principal stresses (IPS) to investigate the effect of the IPS on deformation, strength, failure modes, and acoustic emission (AE) signals of rock and to elucidate the role of the IPS on the rock strength. The inoculation, occurrence, development, and failure of rockburst as well as the AE evolution characteristics are discussed. The findings indicate that the rockburst ejection includes localized ejections of particles, spalling of rocks into plates, shearing of rocks into fragments, and ejections of rock fragments. The formation mechanism of rockburst involves three progressive processes, i.e., tensile, shear, and tensile-shear composite destruction. The peak stress of sample increases with increasing IPS, while the deformation modulus decreases exponentially as the unloading progresses. The sample properties under true triaxial unloading condition with different IPS can be described by the Mogi-Coulomb criterion. The evolution curves of ringing impact ratio (CH) and cumulative absolute energy can be divided into four stages. The crystal-scale refined model that considers mineral components efficiently simulate the rockburst. The simulated stress-strain (SS) curves of samples are in good agreement with those obtained from laboratory tests.Keywords: Rock mechanics, True triaxial test, Intermediate principal stress, Failure characteristic, Numerical simulation

Introduction

Currently, there is an increasing demand for major infrastructures such as transportation, water conservancy, and energy, and a large number of deep underground projects have emerged. The complex and changeable geological conditions and the extremely special properties of rock mass provide spatial and dynamic conditions for the occurrence of hazards in deep underground projects. Among them, rockburst hazards caused by high stress and strong unloading are more common and extremely harmful, which seriously threats their construction safety and long-term stability. Therefore, understanding the failure characteristic, mechanical properties (MPs), and AE characteristics of granite under unloading condition is of great significance for design and construction of deep underground projects, and also provides a certain reference for solving geologic hazards, such as rockburst.

Extensive experimental and simulation studies have been performed on MPs, damage behavior, and AE characteristics of rocks. Vavryčuk et al. [1] experimentally studied the AE signals of the sandstone and revealed its bi-modular behaviour. Yahaghi et al. [2] proposed a hybrid finite-discrete element method (FDEM) to investigate the sandstone damage. Khadivi et al. [3] studied the AE evolution characteristics and the development of micro-macro cracks of the sandstone, coal, and granite under different sizes and loading configurations. Tian et al. [4] optimized the prediction model for rocks based on improved clustering algorithm. Zhang et al. [5] conduced uniaxial cyclic loading tests on layered phyllite samples to expound their MPs and energy evolution. Liu et al. [6] studied the MPs and failure modes of drilled coal under different stress paths. Wang et al. [7] discussed the behaviors of stress-induced AE signals. Kallimogiannis et al. [8] experimentally investigated the wave velocity, strength, deformability, and degradation characteristics of rocks. Ghasemi et al. [9] clarified the impact of cyclic loading parameters on the behaviours of granite and its constituent minerals. Li et al. [10] performed true triaxial rapid unloading simulations and triaxial tests based on the prediction of the post-peak curves of uniaxially compressed rocks with the stress-displacement surface model. The above research results mainly focused on the uniaxial and conventional triaxial tests and simulations. However, there is always a non-equal confining pressure state for the excavated underground projects. Given the significant effect of IPS on the MPs and failure modes of rocks, it is difficult to effectively capture the actual boundary conditions of excavated rock mass before and after unloading without considering the influence of IPS. In terms of the condition of traditional true triaxial compression, Zhai et al. [11] clarified the anisotropy of deep laminated sandstones and discussed the correlations between AE characteristics and brittleness index. Ma et al. [12] pointed out that granite samples under true triaxial compression underwent a transition from shear failure to tensile-shear composite failure as the temperature and σ2, and developed a modified Mogi-Coulomb criterion that introduces varying temperature-dependent coefficients. Zalooli et al. [13] investigated the petrographic and physico-mechanical properties of greenschist. Pérez-Rey et al. [14] experimentally assessed the reproducibility and repeatability of direct tensile tests. Deiminiat et al. [15] developed a complete stress-displacement surface model to reveal the behaviors of rock under differential loads. Yadav et al. [16] simulated the post-peak SS behaviors of the coal rock. Pham et al. [17] explored the thermal-mechanical coupling behaviors of slate base on the DEM. Corkum et al. [18] explained rock failure during triaxial shearing with the aid of the 3DEC software. Yang et al. [19] conducted numerical simulations based on an impact-induced rockburst model. Li et al. [20] explored energy evolution and excess energy mechanism in rockburst. Jiang et al. [21] obtained AE feature parameters of rock specimens experiencing uniaxial compression. Regarding the influence of IPS on failure characteristic, MPs, and AE properties of deep rocks, it is difficult for traditional true triaxial tests and simulations to replicate stress state change in deep excavated rock masses. In addition, the research on the identification method of rockburst precursor warning information based on AE signal parameters is still relatively limited. Therefore, it is urgent to clarify the impact of IPS on the failure modes, MPs, and AE characteristics of granite under one-sided lateral unloading and three-way five-sided force vertical continuous loading condition.

Based on the self-designed multi-axial loading-unloading stress path, this paper performs one-sided lateral unloading and three-way five-sided force vertical continuous loading experiments with different IPS with the aid of the multifunctional high-voltage servo true triaxial test system. The failure process of granite specimen is recorded by the high-speed camera, while the AE signals of granite specimen during the whole failure process are captured by the AE monitoring system in real time. The impact of the IPS on deformation, strength, failure modes, and AE signals of granite is analyzed. The role of the IPS on the rock strength is discussed based on the Mogi-Coulomb strength criterion. According to test results, the 3D block DEM and polycrystalline modeling system are combined to establish a 3D crystal-scale numerical simulation model that can consider the mineral components. The rockburst ejection process and AE evolution characteristics are macroscopically and microscopically studied. The findings are informative for prediction and prevention of hazards in deep underground engineering.

Testing system and programme

Testing equipment and rock sample preparation

The multifunctional high-voltage servo true triaxial test system is composed of a power loading control system, a hydraulic system, an oil source system, and a data acquisition system (Fig. 1). The testing device is equipped with a high-precision servo-controlled single-sided pendulum unloading device. The one-sided rapid unloading can be performed at any point during the test. It allows for the collection and editing of test data in real time.

True triaxial testing system.

The specimens used for tests are prepared from the intact granite block. The specimen is 100 mm in length, 100 mm in width, and 200 mm in height (Fig. 2). The main mineral components are potassium feldspar, quartz, plagioclase, and biotite, which are unevenly distributed in the rock, showing granitic texture and block structure (Sun et al. [22]). The processing accuracy of the specimen strictly follows the requirements of International Society for Rock Mechanics to ensure that the specimen is uniformly loaded.

Tested sample.

Testing methodology

As depicted in Fig. 3, the cavern excavation boundary is characterized as a free face and five stress faces. Thereafter, a stress path of one-sided lateral unloading and three-way five-sided force vertical continuous loading is designed in this study, as presented in Fig. 4.

Testing method and stress path.

The specific testing procedure is as follows:

Step 1: Field measured data demonstrate that the initial stresses in three directions are σ1 = 74.58 MPa, σ2 = 54.96 MPa, and σ3 = 39.51 MPa (Ge et al. [24]). In the test, the three principal stresses are 75 MPa, 55 MPa, and 40 MPa, respectively. To simulate initial geo-stress, a stress of σ3 = 40 MPa is horizontally applied to the left and right boundaries of specimen, a stress of σ2 = 55 MPa is horizontally applied to the front and rear boundaries, and a stress of σ1 = 70 MPa is vertically applied to the upper and lower boundaries.

Three different IPS values of 40, 55, and 70 MPa are designed in this paper.

Analysis of test results

Deformation and strength characteristics of specimen

Deformation characteristics

SS curves

Fig. 5 presents the SS curves of specimens under varying IPS.

SS curves of tested specimen for varying IPS.

As depicted in Fig. 5, (a) the SS curves for varying IPS are basically the same. The peak strength of specimen shows an increasing trend as the IPS increases, which indicates that the severer the damage of specimen, the greater the intensity of rockburst. This indicates that during the crack unstable fracture stage, both the growth rate and number of cracks within each group of specimens are different. There are significant differences in the characteristics of the macroscopic cracks, which causes the samples to exhibit different peak strengths. Further, the double Poisson's effect resulting from the first principal stress and IPS causes an increased rockburst stress level and allows the rock specimens to store a large amount of energy before rockburst occurs, which provide a direct energy source for rockburst. The SS curves for varying IPS can be divided into the initial compaction stage (stage I), elastic deformation stage (stage Ⅱ), crack stable fracture stage (stage Ⅲ), and crack unstable fracture stage (stage Ⅳ). (b) In the stage Ⅰ, the deformations of each group of specimens are similar. In the stage Ⅱ, the specimens exhibit approximately straight SS curves. In the stage Ⅲ, the curves exhibit slight nonlinear characteristics, while the cracks initiate inside the specimen and then stably expand and develop with a large strain growth rate. In the stage Ⅳ, the σ3(y1) is maintained at a constant unloading rate, and the IPS σ2 remains unchanged. Numerous newly-generated cracks begin to initiate within the specimen and gradually extend to unloading surface, while internal deformation damage intensifies. The rapid expansion and penetration form a macroscopic failure plane on the unloading surface. At this time, specimen loses its load-bearing capacity and rockburst occurs. (c) The volumetric strain εv curve shows a significant segmentation phenomenon. Once the load reaches the threshold, the curve undergoes a reverse curvature trend. The evolution of volumetric strain gradually shifts from linear to nonlinear, and the volumetric rotation phenomenon appears. The turning point of the curve shifting from linear to nonlinear gradually decreases with increasing IPS. The volumetric strain of specimen decreases with increasing IPS, showing a dilatancy.

The frictional force that hinders the sliding between cracking planes increases with increasing IPS. In other words, both the cracking resistance and strength of the rock increase.

The failure process, variations of CH and cumulative absolute energy of specimen with different IPS is shown in Fig. 10.

Failure process, variations of CH, and cumulative absolute energy of specimen under different IPS.

As depicted in Fig. 10, according to the time sequence, the rockburst ejection for hard rock with different IPS includes local ejection of grains, spalling of rock into plate, shearing of rock into fragment, and ejection of rock fragment. The above four stages can also characterize the whole process of “incubation-occurrence-development-failure” of rockburst. The evolution characteristic curves of the CH and cumulative absolute energy can be divided into microcrack closure and initial compaction stage (stage I), microcrack initiation and elastic deformation stage (stage II), microcrack stable expansion and elastic-plastic transition stage (stage III), as well as microcrack unstable development and destruction stage (stage IV). (a) Stage I: The CH value of specimen at the IPS of 40 MPa and 55 MPa is almost 0, and its evolution curve is essentially a straight line. The CH value of specimen at the IPS of 70 MPa experiences a small sudden increase and then gradually stabilize. This indicates that the AE ringing count in this stage is less, and the AE impact signals are relatively low in amplitude. The cumulative absolute energy of specimen at the IPS of 40 MPa is also almost 0. In contrast, the cumulative absolute energy of specimens at the IPS of 55 MPa and 70 MPa shows a stepwise increase and is at a lower level in this stage. It suggests that the AE signal is less active,

The initial cracks inside the specimen are compacted and closed, and local ejection of grains occurs on the unloading surface of specimen. (b) Stage II: The CH value and cumulative absolute energy remain low and have similar patterns. It shows that the IPS has little effect on both of them in this stage, and the AE signal is remains less active with a low amplitude. The initial and newly-generated microcracks develop slowly, while the unloading surface of specimen bulges to form a thin rock plate. (c) Stage III: Under different IPS, the CH value shows a significant increase in the early stage and returns to a lower level in the later stage. Meanwhile, the cumulative absolute energy also undergoes a long-term stepwise growth. It reveals that microcracks continuously initiate and expand, leading to accumulated damage. The cracks expand rapidly to merge and penetrate, while the unloading surface destroys, resulting in the fracture of rock plate. (d) Stage IV: The CH values of the three groups of specimens under different IPS increase sharply with a multi-peak phenomenon. It shows that the AE signal is abnormally active, and that the AE impact signal oscillates and has a high amplitude. The macroscopic fracture plane is gradually formed. The rock plate is broken and ejected onto specimen unloading surface, forming a rockburst pit. In addition, the cumulative absolute energy of three groups of specimens suddenly increases to the maximum value, and then the change is no longer obvious. It shows that the energy accumulated in the specimen is released instantaneously, i.e., rockburst occurs. (e) From the microcrack stable expansion stage (stage III) to the microcrack unstable development stage (stage IV), the CH value increases sharply, and there is a multi-peak phenomenon, which can serve as warning evidence for rockburst.

Evolution characteristics of cracks in granite based on RA-AF value

The AE monitoring system identifies different types of cracks in rock by characterizing different AE signal parameters. The RA value (ratio of rising time to amplitude) and the AF value (ratio of ringing counts to duration) provide an access to clarify the features of cracks in rock from a microscopic perspective (Ohtsu et al. [30]). According to the available studies (Aggelis et al. [31]; Jia et al. [32]), a low AF value and a high RA value indicate shear failure (i.e., mostly shear cracks), while the opposite indicates tension failure (i.e., mostly tension cracks).

The failure mode and variation of RA-AF values of specimens under varying IPS are displayed in Fig. 11.

Failure mode, variation law of RA-AF value of specimen under different IPS.

As depicted in Fig. 11, the variations of RA-AF values of granite specimens under different IPS are basically the same. Shear failure occurs mainly within the specimen. The energy values of shear and tension cracks are mostly concentrated in the ranges of 260 × 102-360 × 102 mV/μs and 60 × 102-180 × 102 mV/μs, respectively. It is shown that the penetrating shear oblique cracks in granite specimen are caused by the propagation and penetration of substantial shear cracks. The splitting crack is formed by the initiation and propagation of partial tension cracks. During the test, the deformation and rock damage generates considerable AE signals, which results in a wide distribution range of RA-AF values. The distribution range and number of RA-AF values of specimen gradually increase as the IPS increases. Meanwhile, the distribution range and number of shear cracks gradually increase. It shows that shear cracks become the main form of microcracks in specimen as the IPS increases. This further confirms that the damage and crack expansion within the specimen during unloading resulted in the violent ejection of fragments from the unloading surface. This suggests that considerable energy is released from the specimen, which is also a typical feature of rockburst occurrence. (c) The failure mode of specimen gradually transforms from shear and tensile destruction to shear destruction as the IPS increases. The formation mechanism of rockburst in hard rock involves three progressive processes.

The proportion of shear cracks in specimen under different IPS during the deformation stage is shown in Fig. 12.

Proportion of shear cracks in the deformation stage of specimen under different IPS.

As depicted in Fig. 12, the initiation and propagation of shear cracks are mostly clustered in crack unstable expansion stage. In elastic and crack stable expansion stage, as the IPS increases, the initiation, propagation, and penetration of new and original cracks in the specimen increase, and the proportion of shear cracks increases. In crack unstable expansion stage, with the unloading of σ3(y1), the principal stress difference is increasingly large, the old cracks expand and penetrate rapidly, while the new cracks initiate and develop rapidly, and the crack propagation is extremely unstable. The cracks gradually penetrate to form a macroscopic failure plane, which triggers the rockburst. (c) The above analyses indicate that the microcracks generated within the specimen are mainly tensile cracks on the eve of the rockburst, with a small proportion of shear cracks. The microcracks produced near the rockburst failure are mainly shear cracks, and the proportion of tension cracks decreases significantly. The change in AE signal characteristics provides early warning information for rockburst.

Analysis of numerical simulation

3D crystal scale numerical simulation model

The 3D block DEM and the polycrystalline modeling system (NEPER) are coupled to establish a 3D crystal-scale numerical simulation model (Fig. 13(a) and (b)) that can reflect the composition of mineral particles (consistent with experimental results). It overcomes the deficiency in FISH language-based modeling and improves the modeling efficiency and computation accuracy. A set of coupled DEM-NEPER data interface calculation program is developed independently, and the numerical model is embedded into a 3D block DEM platform.

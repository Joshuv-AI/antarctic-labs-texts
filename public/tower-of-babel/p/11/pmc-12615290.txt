Clinical utility of the exercise optically pumped magnetocardiographic stress test in young adults: an exploratory study☆
Authors: Lanxin Feng, Xin Zhao, Shuwen Yang, Linqi Liu, Zhao Ma, Huan Zhang, Jianping Xiang, Ming Ding, Chenchen Tu, Xiantao Song, Hongjia Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo assess the clinical feasibility and safety of exercise magnetocardiographic stress test based on optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs).MethodsSubjects without previous cardiovascular disease were enrolled in this study. All subjects underwent exercise electrocardiographic stress test (EST) and both rest and stress OPM-based magnetocardiography (MCG).ResultsA total of 26 healthy subjects finally enrolled in this study. None of the subjects reported chest pain, dyspnea or any other discomfort during the test. The mean interval between the completion of EST and the initiation of OPM-based MCG signal acquisition was 60.3 ± 8.4 s. One subject observed obvious deflection of magnetic field (Unstable-MCG group) during stress OPM-based MCG, whereas 25 subjects did not (Stable-MCG group). In Stable-MCG group, the rest and stress magnetocardiographic parameters were phenotypically similar (P > 0.05).ConclusionWe demonstrated an innovative diagnostic technology with broad potential application value for diagnosing myocardial ischemia and assessed its clinical feasibility and safety. This is a critical step toward making the examination widely used in clinical practice.Keywords: Magnetocardiography, Myocardial ischemia, Coronary artery disease, Electrocardiography, Coronary microcirculatory dysfunction

Introduction

Despite guidelines recommending initial non-invasive functional assessment of myocardial ischemia for patients with suspected coronary artery disease (CAD), its practical clinical application remains highly limited [1]. Exercise electrocardiographic stress testing (EST) as a noninvasive functional method for the evaluation of patients with new-onset angina, has been extensively utilized in clinical practice. Integrating various imaging modalities with exercise can provide higher diagnostic accuracy compared to EST alone. However, several clinical studies have shown that even exercise single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) still has low sensitivity and accuracy in diagnosing coronary stenosis [2]. Magnetocardiography (MCG) is a non-invasive and radiation-free multi-channel technology to record cardiac electromagnetic signal. Several studies have demonstrated its high sensitivity and specificity in diagnosing myocardial ischemia [[3], [4], [5]].

Traditional MCG technology is mainly based on superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs) [6]. Due to its high cost and various application limitations, this technology has not gained widespread clinical acceptance. The new generation of MCG based on optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) provides substantial cost reductions compared to traditional SQUID MCG. Additionally, it eliminates the need for liquid helium and magnetic shielding rooms, enabling its widespread deployment in medical institutions [7,8]. However, there are currently no clinical applications and reports of the OPM-based MCG stress test. Therefore, we present the examination process and preliminary results of the OPM-based stress test, as well as investigate its feasibility and safety in healthy individuals.

Methods

Study population

This study was conducted as a prospective registry at the Capital Medical University affiliated Anzhen Hospital, Beijing, China with approval from the institutional review board. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects. We prospectively enrolled 30 healthy subjects with no previous history of cardiovascular disease for further assessment (Fig. 1). The detailed exclusion criteria are shown in Table 1. Fig. 2 depicts the stepwise examination protocol in subjects we recruited. All subjects were required to undergo electrocardiogram (ECG) and MCG measurements at rest as part of the initial assessment. Stress MCG test must be completed following the EST to accurately assess myocardial ischemia during exercise. An independent investigator conducted quality evaluation and analysis of the ECG and MCG.

MCG recording

The rest and stress MCG recordings were conducted using a 36-channel OPM-based MCG system (Miracle MCG, Beijing X-MAGTECH Technologies Ltd.). Each subject underwent a continuous recording with an arrayed sensors (6 × 6 grid) above the chest, with a total recording time of 90 s. The measurement sensitivity of the 36-channel atomic magnetometers is consistently below 30fT/Hz1/2. The direct-current (DC) residual magnetic field in the MCG measurement area is kept below 5nT. Following completion of MCG data collection, the software automatically reconstructs high-precision magnetic field maps and pseudo current density maps depicting spatiotemporal distribution, and outputs the corresponding parameters.

EST protocol

EST was conducted using a CARDIOVIT AT-104PC system (Schiller, Switzerland) according to the practice guidelines of the American College of Cardiology and American Heart Association, employing a standard Ramp protocol [9,10]. The target heart rate was set at 85 % of the age-adjusted maximum exercise heart rate (calculated as 220–age). Termination of the EST would be considered upon reaching the target heart rate. Continuous recording of the 12‑lead ECG, heart rate, and blood pressure was conducted at regular intervals during the EST. All exercise stress tests were supervised by two experienced cardiologists who were blinded to the results.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were presented as frequency (percentage), and continuous data were expressed as mean ± SD. Differences between groups were evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (for continuous variables) and chi-square test (for categorical variables). Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics version 26 (IBM) and Prism version 9.0 for Windows (GraphPad Software).

Results

A total of 30 subjects (all male) were recruited for this study, with the mean age of 33.3 ± 7.4 years. Three subjects with complete right-bundle branch block (RBBB) on resting ECG, which could interfere with the interpretation of the exercise test, were excluded from the study. The exclusion criteria are provided in Table 1. Twenty-seven subjects underwent the rest OPM-based MCG, and 26 subjects were included in the final analysis. One subject was unable to undergo the EST and stress OPM-based MCG test due to significant magnetic field interference in the MCG image. All 26 subjects reached their age-adjusted target heart rate during the EST. Fig. 3 shows the blood pressure and heart rate values of all subjects at baseline and during the stress test. The mean interval between the completion of EST and the initiation of OPM-based MCG signal acquisition was 60.3 ± 8.4 s. No subjects reported chest pain, dyspnea or any other discomfort during the test.

Compared to the resting condition, both the magnetic angle and current angle of the stress OPM-based MCG in the Unstable-MCG group showed significant deflection, exhibiting obvious positive unipolar characteristics (Fig. 6). This subject experienced atypical angina symptoms following exertion 2 months ago, and underwent coronary angiography at another hospital, which revealed no obvious epicardial coronary artery stenosis. Due to personal reasons, this subject declined our recommendation to undergo additional imaging examinations for further assessment of myocardial ischemia. To further investigate the cause of magnetocardiographic abnormalities, we computed the angio-based index of microcirculatory resistance (AccuIMR) using the subject's previous coronary angiography images (ArteryFlow Technology, Hangzhou, China). As shown in Fig. S1, the AccuIMR in the left anterior descending coronary artery was 29.12 (>25), 19.19 in the left circumflex artery and 18.38 in the Right Coronary Artery. This subject's coronary microcirculatory resistance increased significantly, combined with his long-term smoking history suggesting a higher likelihood of coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD).

Discussion

In this study, we presented the process and preliminary results of the OPM-based MCG stress test for the first time. Given that this study recruited a young cohort with no previous history of cardiovascular disease, it is not surprising that the majority of subjects had negative results on both EST and OPM-based MCG. Only one subject, considered for diagnosing CMD, had a positive stress OPM-based MCG result while the EST was negative. None of the subjects experienced chest pain, dyspnea or other uncomfortable symptoms during the test. Our study has preliminarily shown the safety and feasibility of the OPM-based MCG stress test and its potential application value for assessing myocardial ischemia.

Both MCG and ECG signals are derived from the electrical activity of myocardial cells [11]. Myocardial cell hypoxia due to the imbalance of oxygen supply and demand often causes abnormal ion currents of myocardial action potential in the early stage [12,13]. However, due to the obstruction by body tissues, detecting such weak electrical signal changes with ECG is always challenging. Owing to the physical characteristics of the heart's magnetic field, MCG is significantly less influenced by variations in conductance compared to electric currents in body tissues [14]. Several clinical studies have shown that the MCG exhibits superior sensitivity and specificity for detecting ischemic myocardium [4,5].

Exercise testing has been widely utilized for provocation and identification of myocardial ischemia in contemporary practice [15]. Aerobic exercise as a common physiological stress that can induce cardiovascular abnormalities not observed at rest. Integrating stress exercise into various imaging modalities can enhance diagnostic utility for cardiovascular disease. Several studies have demonstrated that stress exercise can enhance the sensitivity and specificity of MCG in diagnosing myocardial ischemia [[16], [17], [18]]. In this study, we optimized the process of stress MCG test in comparison with previous studies, considering the actual clinical scenario. Due to clinical accessibility considerations, we opted not to use the pharmacological stress test such as dobutamine, and instead employed the traditional exercise stress method, enhancing the generalizability of our findings. Our study utilized a widely employed model to standardize the exercise load status of the subjects (85 % of the age-adjusted maximum exercise heart rate). However, given the high sensitivity of the MCG, the feasibility of using a lower exercise dose remains to be explored in the future. Aggarwal et al. [19] showed that abruptly placing the patient in the supine position after exercise testing, resulting in increased cardiac work due to heightened venous return, could induce the appearance of ST-segment depression. In our study, as depicted in Fig. 2, the subjects underwent supine-position OPM-based MCG immediately after completing the exercise stress protocol. Such examination arrangements could additionally enhance the diagnostic capability of stress MCG for detecting myocardial ischemia to some extent.

Due to the high cost and various application limitations, traditional SQUID MCG equipment is predominantly utilized as clinical research equipment in select medical centers. The new generation of MCG based on OPMs differs significantly from traditional SQUID MCG. OPM-based MCG eliminates the need for liquid helium for cryogenic cooling and removes cumbersome magnetic shielding rooms, significantly expanding the clinical applications of magnetocardiography [20]. However, this presents new challenges. The presence of the magnetic shielding chamber associated with OPM-based MCG devices (a person-sized magnetic shield) prevents subjects from undergoing exercise during the MCG process, which contrasts with SQUID MCG equipment. To facilitate subjects reaching the target heart rate during stress OPM-based MCG, we combined the processes of EST and MCG. Given that electrophysiological abnormalities in the ischemic myocardium can persist for hours after exercise and some abnormal electrocardiographic responses might manifest only during the recovery period [15,21], it is reasonable to conduct MCG test after stress exercise in our study.

From a technical standpoint, the most innovative aspect of our study was the development of an open inspection system based on OPM-based MCG. The open system improved subject comfort during examinations, optimized equipment deployment, and streamlined the examination process, thereby reducing anxiety and the risk of claustrophobia. However, at the same time, this also enabled environmental magnetic interference to enter the shielding, making the inspection results more uncertain. However, gratifyingly, except for one subject in this study, all other subjects collected clear magnetic cardiac image data that can be further analyzed. This study also established the groundwork for future clinical research in the field of stress OPM-based MCG.

In terms of clinical application prospects, stress OPM-based MCG is helpful for accurately assessing myocardial ischemia in patients with new-onset angina, thereby shortening the diagnostic pathway for coronary heart disease. On the other hand, the study by La et al. [22] showed that only 6 % of angina patients were discharged from hospital with a clear mechanism of non-obstructive myocardial ischemia, while the majority of patients were only diagnosed with “nonspecific chest pain”. In this context, stress OPM-based MCG may be a potential test with high clinical application value. Its combination with CTCA or CAG can expedite the diagnosis of CMD and reduce the need of complex and expensive invasive physiological techniques or stress perfusion cardiac magnetic resonance imaging. The efficacy of this proposed clinical strategy requires validation in future clinical studies.

While our findings provide encouraging evidence supporting the feasibility and safety of the novel stress OPM-based MCG approach, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center study with a limited sample size. Although the inclusion criteria did not restrict age or gender, all enrolled participants were relatively young males, which limits the generalizability of our findings to broader and more diverse populations. This study was designed as a proof-of-concept investigation to assess system performance in a low-risk cohort with minimal cardiac comorbidities, enabling clearer evaluation of technical feasibility. Future studies should therefore involve larger and more heterogeneous populations with a higher prevalence of cardiovascular disease to validate the diagnostic utility and clinical relevance of this technique.

Additionally, our study focused exclusively on exercise-based stress protocols due to their non-invasive nature and broad clinical accessibility. However, we recognize that this approach inherently introduces a time delay between peak stress and MCG acquisition, despite our efforts to minimize the interval. In contrast, pharmacologic stress protocols allow for signal acquisition during active drug-induced stress and may offer superior temporal resolution for ischemia detection. While such protocols require intravenous access and pharmacologic agents—reducing some of the practical advantages of exercise testing—they may be more appropriate for patients with limited exercise capacity or elevated cardiovascular risk. Future research directly comparing pharmacologic and exercise-based stress MCG protocols will be valuable in evaluating their respective diagnostic performance and clinical applicability across different patient populations.

Moreover, we excluded individuals with known complete conduction abnormalities, such as RBBB. This decision was based on the consideration that such conduction delays may lead to baseline MCG abnormalities even in the absence of ischemia, potentially confounding the statistical analysis of MCG parameters in this exploratory study. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that patients with bundle branch block represent a clinically important subgroup. The potential diagnostic utility of stress OPM-based MCG in these patients warrants dedicated investigation in future studies. Despite these limitations, our study represents the first clinical evaluation of stress OPM-based MCG in an unshielded environment and provides an important foundation for further translational development and clinical integration of this novel technology.

It is important to note that the current findings are preliminary and should be interpreted with caution. As an early-stage feasibility study, the primary aim was to explore the technical performance and procedural safety of the stress OPM-based MCG system under real-world, unshielded conditions. While our results are encouraging, they are not sufficient to support definitive conclusions regarding diagnostic accuracy or clinical utility. Substantially more work is needed to validate the proposed approach in larger, more diverse patient populations, and to establish standardized criteria for MCG-based ischemia detection under stress. Therefore, the current study should be viewed as a foundation for further research rather than a conclusive evaluation of clinical efficacy.

Conclusion

We demonstrated the safety and feasibility of exercise MCG stress test system based on OPM technology, showing significant potential for diagnosing myocardial ischemia. The clinical application value of this system needs to be further explored in patients in the future.

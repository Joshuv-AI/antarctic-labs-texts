Immunohistochemical Expression of Basement Membrane Laminin in Histological Grades of Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma: A Semiquantitative Analysis
Authors: Prakhar Mangal, Adarsh Sanikop, Vikas Pandey, Tanushree Satpathy, Ripudaman Sharma, Shilpi Sharma, Dipooja Patil
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is a prevalent malignancy characterized by varying histological grades. Basement membrane laminin expression plays a crucial role in tumor invasion and progression. This study aimed to assess the immunohistochemical expression of basement membrane laminin in different histological grades of OSCC.Materials and Methods:Archival tissue samples of OSCC from 60 patients were categorized into well-differentiated, moderately differentiated, and poorly differentiated grades. Immunohistochemistry was performed using anti-laminin antibodies, and staining intensity was semiquantitatively analyzed using a scoring system.Results:The immunohistochemical analysis revealed varying levels of laminin expression across different histological grades of OSCC. Well-differentiated tumors showed an average staining intensity score of 7.2 (SD ± 1.5), moderately differentiated tumors had a score of 5.8 (SD ± 1.2), and poorly differentiated tumors exhibited a score of 4.3 (SD ± 0.9). Statistical analysis indicated a significant difference in laminin expression between histological grades (P < 0.001).Conclusion:Our findings demonstrate a progressive decrease in basement membrane laminin expression with an increasing histological grade of OSCC. This suggests that laminin may be a potential biomarker for assessing tumor aggressiveness and prognosis in oral squamous cell carcinoma.

INTRODUCTION

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) represents a significant burden in oncology, accounting for most oral cavity cancers worldwide.[1] The histological differentiation of OSCC is a crucial determinant of prognosis, with well-differentiated tumors generally exhibiting better outcomes than poorly differentiated counterparts.[2] The basement membrane, a specialized extracellular matrix structure underlying epithelial tissues, plays a pivotal role in tumor invasion and metastasis by influencing cell adhesion, migration, and differentiation.[3]

Laminins, a family of heterotrimeric glycoproteins predominantly found in the basement membrane, contribute significantly to its structural integrity and biological functions.[4] In cancer, alterations in laminin expression have been implicated in tumor progression and invasion in various malignancies, including OSCC.[56] Specifically, changes in laminin expression patterns have been associated with different histological grades of OSCC, reflecting varying degrees of tumor aggressiveness and metastatic potential.[7]

Despite these observations, the specific role of basement membrane laminin in differentiating between histological grades of OSCC remains incompletely understood. Understanding the immunohistochemical expression of laminin across different grades of OSCC may provide valuable insights into its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in oral cancer management.

Study design and sample selection

This study utilized archival tissue samples obtained from 60 patients diagnosed with oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) at [Specify your institution or source]. The samples were categorized into three groups based on histological grading: well-differentiated (n = 20), moderately differentiated (n = 20), and poorly differentiated (n = 20) OSCC.

Immunohistochemical staining

Sections of formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue blocks (4 μm thick) were prepared and mounted on glass slides. Immunohistochemical staining was performed using a standard avidin-biotin complex method. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) and microwave heating. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using 3% hydrogen peroxide. The primary antibody used was an anti-laminin antibody (dilution [Specify dilution], [Specify company]), followed by incubation with a biotinylated secondary antibody and streptavidin-peroxidase complex. Visualization was achieved using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB), and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin.

Semiquantitative analysis

Stained slides were evaluated under a light microscope by two independent observers blinded to clinical data. Laminin expression was assessed semiquantitatively based on staining intensity and distribution. A scoring system ranging from 0 to 10 was employed, where 0 represented no staining, 1-3 represented weak staining, 4-6 represented moderate staining, and 7-10 represented strong staining. Discrepancies in scoring were resolved by consensus.

Statistical analysis

The Kruskal-Wallis test revealed a statistically significant difference in laminin expression scores among the different histological grades of OSCC (P < 0.001). Post hoc analysis with Bonferroni correction showed significant differences between all pairs of histological grades (P < 0.05).

The results demonstrate a progressive decrease in basement membrane laminin expression with the increasing histological grade of OSCC. Well-differentiated tumors exhibited the highest mean laminin expression score (7.2 ± 1.5), followed by moderately differentiated tumors (5.8 ± 1.2), and poorly differentiated tumors showed the lowest mean score (4.3 ± 0.9). This finding suggests that reduced laminin expression may correlate with more aggressive tumor behavior and a poorer prognosis in oral squamous cell carcinoma.

DISCUSSION

The present study investigated the immunohistochemical expression of basement membrane laminin in varying histological grades of oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). Our findings indicate a significant decrease in laminin expression with increasing tumor differentiation, suggesting a potential role for laminin as a prognostic biomarker in OSCC.

The observed decrease in laminin expression aligns with previous studies that have reported alterations in basement membrane components in various cancers, reflecting changes in tumor invasiveness and metastatic potential.[12] Laminins, as key components of the basement membrane, are integral to maintaining tissue architecture and regulating cellular behavior, including adhesion and migration.[3]

The higher laminin expression observed in well-differentiated OSCC tumors than poorly differentiated ones support the hypothesis that laminin may influence tumor differentiation and aggressiveness.[4] This finding underscores the importance of basement membrane integrity in modulating tumor behavior and clinical outcomes in OSCC.

The implications of reduced laminin expression in OSCC extend to potential therapeutic strategies targeting basement membrane components to mitigate tumor progression and improve patient outcomes.[5] Future research could explore the mechanistic roles of laminin in OSCC pathogenesis, including its interactions with other extracellular matrix components and signaling pathways.

Limitations of our study include its retrospective design and reliance on archival tissue samples, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of findings. Additionally, while immunohistochemistry provides valuable insights into protein expression patterns, further studies incorporating molecular techniques could elucidate underlying genetic and epigenetic mechanisms driving laminin dysregulation in OSCC.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, our study contributes to the growing body of evidence highlighting the significance of basement membrane laminin in OSCC progression. By elucidating its differential expression across histological grades, this research underscores the potential utility of laminin as a biomarker for predicting tumor behavior and guiding therapeutic interventions in oral cancer management.

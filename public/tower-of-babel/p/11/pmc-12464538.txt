Emergence of the SARS-CoV-2 variant P.1 (Gamma) in Ecuador: A retrospective report of the first confirmed case
Authors: Katty Ojeda-Gutiérrez, Fernando Serrano-Tamay, David Zúñiga, Sully Márquez, Belén Prado-Vivar, Mónica Becerra, Juan José Guadalupe, Patricio Rojas-Silva, Verónica Barragán, Paúl Cárdenas, Paola Dalgo Aguilar
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by severe acute respiratory coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), was first reported in Wuhan, China, in December 2019 [1]. Since its emergence it has had a disastrous health, social and economic impact. As of February 20, 2025, it has resulted in 777 million confirmed cases and 7.1 million reported deaths worldwide [2].

Like other RNA viruses, SARS-CoV-2 undergoes a high degree of genomic mutation in the process of adaptation to the host, which contributes to the emergence of a wide variety of SARS-CoV-2 variants [3]. On January 2021, a new variant of concern (VOC) P1 (Gamma), was identified in China in samples of tourists from Manaus-Brazil [4], has been associated with an increase in transmissibility and high viral load [5].

The first case of severe COVID-19 associated with the P.1 lineage (VOC Gamma) was reported in Loja, Ecuador in April 2021. The virus was isolated from a nasopharyngeal swab sample collected from a patient hospitalized in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) at Manuel Ygnacio Monteros hospital in Loja, Ecuador with a suspected of COVID-19 and respiratory distress syndrome, returned a positive SARS-CoV-2 result through PCR. Upon arrival at the emergency department, the patient presented temperature 37.2 °C, blood pressure 120/80 mmHg, heart rate 91 beats/min, respiratory rate of 30 breaths/min and an SpO2 of 80 %. Due to respiratory failure, the patient required high-flow oxygen and was transferred to the ICU. Management included two cycles of prone position, each lasting three days, and required several days of vasopressor support. After three days, the patient developed complications such as cholestatic syndrome, acute kidney injury (AKI), thrombocytopenia, liver failure, septic shock of presumed pulmonary or abdominal origin, and multiple organ failure.

Approximately nine days after admission, the patient experienced fever spikes an increase in acute phase reactants. New cultures revealed infection by carbapenems resistant Klebsiellla oxytoca, for which targeted antibiotic therapy was initiated with good clinical response. The patient remained intubated for 14 of 19 days in the ICU. Following a favorable clinical course, the patient was extubated and then transferred to the general ward with oxygen support via nasal cannula at 3 L/min and remained hemodynamically stable. After 25 days of hospitalization, he was discharged without complications, with a blood pressure of 103/77 mmHg, heart rate 88 beats/min and an SpO2 of 91 %. The patient continues physical therapy for polyneuropathy in their city of residence. None of the family members reported symptoms associated with COVID-19.

For the molecular analysis, the samples were transported at 4 °C to the Institute of Microbiology at Universidad San Francisco de Quito (USFQ). SARS-CoV-2 sequencing was performed using the MinION (Oxford Nanopore Technologies), following the ARTIC Network protocols. The viral sequence from the ICU patient was classified as belonging to lineage P.1 and is available in the EpiCov database maintained by the GISAID initiative with accession code: EPI ISL 1540707; collection date: 2021-04-06. Whole genomes of SARS-CoV-2 P.1 from South America were searched, a seventy-six complete genomes sequences were obtained and compared with the reference sequence (EPI_ISL_402124) and the sequence identified in Loja, Ecuador. Sequence alignment was performed using the MAFFT program, and the phylogenetic analyzes were carried out using the IQ-TREE web server. Subsequently, whole genomes sequences were analyzed using the CoV-GLUE online resource to identify characteristic mutations. The genome from the first reported SARS-CoV-2 P.1 linage case in Loja, Ecuador, showed few differences compared to other South America P1 sequences, and clustered closely with sequences from patients in Peru and Brazil (Fig. 1). The first documented case of the Gamma variant (lineage P.1) in Loja, Ecuador presented new challenges for controlling the COVID-19 pandemic. This letter emphasizes the importance of continuous monitoring and genomic epidemiological investigations in mitigating the impact of SARS-CoV-2 and its variants. Continued efforts to understand the evolutionary dynamics and transmission patterns of SARS-CoV-2 are crucial for mitigating the global impact of COVID-19. Furthermore, sharing information in scientific databases fosters research on emerging viruses, accelerating the development of diagnostics, vaccines, and treatments to more effectively prevent future outbreaks.

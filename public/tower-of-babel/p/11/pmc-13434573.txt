Widely linear precoding with interference exploitation for improper signals in MU-MISO systems
Authors: Qiusheng Gao, Jiaju Zhang, Xianglong Meng, Sinuo Jiao, Junpeng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe evolution of dedicated power communication networks requires higher capacity and reliability for services like high-definition video and real-time data transmission. Traditional systems assume proper (circularly symmetric) signals, but improper signals—prevalent in modulations such as binary phase shift keying (BPSK) and amplitude shift keying (ASK)—offer asymmetric statistics that can be exploited for improved performance in multi-user multiple-input single-output (MU-MISO) systems. This paper addresses the gap by proposing widely linear precoding and reception techniques that integrate interference exploitation for improper signals. Two schemes are introduced: Widely Linear Zero-Forcing (WLZF) and Widely Linear Minimum Mean Square Error (WLMMSE), paired with optimal widely linear receivers. Simulations show significant improvements in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and bit error rate (BER) over conventional methods, providing valuable insights for high-reliability, low-latency networks.Keywords: Improper signal, Dedicated power communication network, Widely linear precoding, Widely linear reception, Interference exploitationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of smart grids, the Internet of Things (IoT), and the new industrial Internet, the dedicated power communication network—serving as the core infrastructure for information flow within the power system—is facing unprecedented service load pressures1.

The traditional service model, which primarily involved monitoring data and simple control commands, is evolving toward a variety of high-bandwidth, low-latency applications, including high-definition video transmission, real-time monitoring, coordinated control of distributed energy resources, and precise location services. These emerging services impose stringent requirements on data rate, reliability, interference resistance, and system capacity.

Traditional communication system designs predominantly assume that transmitted signals are proper (circularly symmetric complex Gaussian), where the real and imaginary parts are uncorrelated and have equal variance2. However, in many practical modulation schemes—such as BPSK and ASK—the signals are improper, exhibiting correlation between real and imaginary parts and/or unequal variances3. Improper signals contain additional second-order statistical information (pseudo-covariance) that can be exploited for performance gains. Recent advancements in precoding techniques for mmWave systems further highlight the importance of hybrid beamforming and spectral efficiency optimization in emerging applications, such as finite-blocklength communications4, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)5, and multi-beam localization in connected autonomous vehicles6. Additionally, regularized approaches in RIS-aided cognitive radio systems provide insights into robust interference management7.

Widely linear processing, which jointly processes the signal and its complex conjugate, has been shown to effectively capture this redundancy in improper signals8. Meanwhile, constructive interference (interference exploitation) precoding techniques allow inter-user interference to be steered to enhance rather than degrade the desired signal9, 10, 11. This paper combines these two paradigms to address multi-user interference in improper-signal MU-MISO systems.

Compared to conventional ZF/MMSE that assume proper signals and suppress interference, and existing widely linear precoding without constructive interference13, the proposed WLZF and WLMMSE explicitly steer interference to reinforce the desired symbol while respecting decision regions of improper constellations.

The saturation observed in conventional schemes is primarily due to the breakdown of the proper signal assumption and the lack of coordinated interference processing. Traditional ZF strictly nullifies all interference, which may result in signal gain degradation when the system is dimensionally constrained (e.g., ). Although traditional MMSE accounts for noise, it fails to exploit constructive interference, thus limiting signal enhancement. In contrast, both WLZF and WLMMSE jointly process and , effectively leveraging the redundant structure of improper signals to recover valuable signal dimensions overlooked by conventional designs. The WLZF scheme explicitly incorporates the concept of constructive interference, allowing certain interference components to reinforce the desired signal by pushing the decision value closer to the correct symbol decision region, thereby reducing the probability of detection error. WLMMSE further seeks a global minimum MSE by jointly optimizing transmit power, noise, and covariance information, making it particularly well-suited for practical communication scenarios with moderate SNR and strong interference.

Figure 2 illustrates average received SNR. Widely linear schemes achieve steeper gains due to exploitation of improper signal redundancy and constructive interference. All schemes exhibit a linear increase in received SNR as transmit SNR rises, but the rates of increase differ. The average SNR at the receiver for conventional ZF and MMSE schemes is relatively low and increases slowly with transmit SNR. In contrast, the WLZF and WLMMSE schemes achieve significantly higher received SNRs, as reflected in their steeper curve slopes. WLMMSE slightly outperforms WLZF across the entire SNR range, with particularly better stability in the low-SNR region.

The average received SNR reflects the “signal clarity” of the received symbols. Widely linear schemes, by simultaneously processing the signal and its complex conjugate, yield a more complete “reception projection,” thereby increasing the signal energy of and effectively enhancing . Moreover, since the unmodeled interference embedded in the error term is partially canceled, the variance is reduced, resulting in an improved SNR. WLZF aims to achieve interference nulling and constructive interference steering. While it excels at signal enhancement in high-SNR scenarios, its performance gains are less pronounced at low SNR due to the dominance of noise. In contrast, WLMMSE, by minimizing the mean square error, balances interference suppression, noise resilience, and signal strength. As a result, it consistently maintains a high received SNR across all SNR regimes and represents a more balanced reception strategy.

Conclusion

This paper focuses on the application of improper signals in multi-user multiple-input single-output (MU-MISO) communication systems, motivated by the growing demand for high reliability, low latency, and strong interference resistance in future dedicated power communication networks. A comprehensive study on widely linear precoding and reception techniques for improper signals is conducted, yielding the following key contributions: First, in terms of system modeling, a downlink MU-MISO system model is established to accommodate the transmission characteristics of improper signals. A widely linear processing structure is adopted to model the transmit signals, taking into account both the covariance and pseudo-covariance information, thereby laying the theoretical foundation for the design of subsequent precoding and reception algorithms. Second, two widely linear precoding schemes based on the principle of interference exploitation are proposed. The WLZF scheme leverages the idea of steering interference to become constructive, enabling the beneficial use of inter-user interference. The WLMMSE scheme minimizes the overall system mean square error by dynamically balancing interference, noise, and transmit power. Third, an optimal widely linear receiver is designed to match the proposed precoding strategies. Based on a complex-augmented vector structure, a closed-form expression for the widely linear MMSE receiver is derived, which effectively exploits the statistical properties of improper signals. Through simulation experiments, the paper compares the BER and received SNR performance of the proposed widely linear schemes against conventional ZF/MMSE approaches. The results demonstrate that the proposed methods offer significant performance advantages under improper signal conditions, especially in medium-to-high SNR scenarios, where they achieve higher reception accuracy and lower bit error rates.

In conclusion, by leveraging the widely linear signal processing framework, this work successfully integrates the characteristics of improper signals with interference exploitation mechanisms, leading to enhanced performance in multi-user communication systems. This paper proposed widely linear precoding and reception techniques that exploit both the redundancy of improper signals and constructive interference in MU-MISO systems. The WLZF and WLMMSE schemes, combined with optimal widely linear receivers, demonstrate substantial BER, received SNR, and spectral efficiency improvements over conventional methods. These results provide promising directions for high-reliability communication in dedicated power networks and beyond.

The TOPSIS method: Figuring the landslide susceptibility using Excel and GIS
Authors: Jonmenjoy Barman, Brototi Biswas, Syed Sadath Ali, Mohamed Zhran
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The current study introduced Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) to enhance landslide susceptibility. It determines the relative distance of each alternative from the ideal best and ideal worst value. The ArcGIS environment was used to prepare eleven landslide conditioning factors, while raster values were extracted for the decision matrix preparation. We utilized subjective expert judgment to create a weighted matrix that considers the roles of each conditioning component. In addition, a Euclidean distance was measured from each alternative to the ideal best and worst values. The relative closeness value (Ri) has been used to prepare the landslide susceptibility index by the inverse distance weighting (IDW) interpolation. Furthermore, the precision of the landslide susceptibility was justified by area under curve-receiver operating characteristic (AUC-ROC) which was 0.987. Hence, multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) techniques like the TOPSIS method are very useful for natural hazard mapping.•The simplified TOPSIS approach described by Hwang and Yoon (1981) is applied in this study. The criteria have been categorized and assigned weights based on expert judgment and previously published material.•The TOPSIS approach and GIS integration has significantly enhanced the creation of a landslide susceptibility map for a sensitive area.•The method is easiest and suitable for short term operation research.

Background

The northeastern Indian state of Mizoram facing rainfall indued frequent shallow landslide during monsoon. The most of the slope formed by tertiary soil deposition over the sand, silt, and sandstone admixture [1,2]. According to state disaster management authority [3], the state shared very high and high susceptibility by 8.65% and 20.22% respectively. Landslide affected over 1085 villages of the state and it caused 54 human deaths. The landslide susceptibility mapping which is suitable scientific techniques to mitigate landslide risk [4]. Landslide susceptibility and hazard assessment pose challenges due to the complexity of factors causing mass instabilities. Still, they can be more comprehensively analysed and regionally evaluated using modern programming tools to mitigate risks [5]. The development of remote sensing and GIS provide a different approach including heuristic, statistical, deterministic, and multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) [6,7]. Although each approach has advantages and disadvantages, researchers often use them because they lack universal standards and consistent guidelines [7]. Among all, the heuristic approach is more site-specific and laboratory oriented. The extensive literature reviews [[8], [9], [10], [11]] summarized that statistical models establish a relationship between landslide likelihood and landslide spatial distribution or presence/absence in the geo-environmental condition [12]. However, the lack of inventories or uneven distribution of landslide inventories is a major disadvantage of statistical models [[13], [14], [15]]. A lack of thorough historical landslip archives and high-resolution satellite data sometimes limits the reliability of susceptibility maps [2,16,17]. Meanwhile, the MCDM procedures work with hierarchical decision structures that allow multiple groups of domain specialists and any evaluator, including non-expert participants like members of the local community, to participate in a decision-making process [18]. The superiority of the MCDM model over other models is that it can be run without landslide inventories. In literature there are a long list of MCDM techniques used for operation research like Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) [19,20], Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) [20], VIseKriterijumska Optimizacija I Kompromisno Resenje (VIKOR) [19], Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) [21], Weighted Aggregated Sum Product Assessment (WASPAS) [22], Multi-Objective Optimization on the basis of Ratio Analysis (MOORA) [23,24], Preference Selection Index (PSI) [25], COmbined COmpromise Solution (CoCoSo) [[26], [27], [28]]. As per the study conducted by Chakraborty [29], shown that TOPSIS model consider the less method's processing time than others MCDM models (AHP, MOORA, VIKOR, PROMETHEE, and ELECTRE) and found best choice base on simplicity, computations, stability, and information delivered. In the recent years, the use of TOPSIS model was used in natural hazard and risk mapping. TOPSIS eliminates the need for paired comparisons, potentially reducing the impact of capacity limitations. As a result, it would be appropriate for instances involving a wide variety of criteria and options, particularly when objective or quantitative facts are provided [30]. Besides that, TOPSIS model can be integrated with any types of weightages like subjective, objective, and hybrid while AHP/Analytic Network Process (ANP) provide subjective weightage of conditioning factors. How ever, TOPSIS model was previously used by a number of scholars for flood susceptibility mapping in district level studies [[31], [32], [33]]. It is the first studies use of TOPSIS model in the Indian scenario. The Aibawk block of Aizawl district made a connection between northern and southern Mizoram and received a huge amount (312.77 mm) of rainfall by the influence of Southwest Monsoon and 57.71% days of a year is rainy days [34]. The recently cyclone Remal induced landslides claimed 9 lives within the block [35]. However, maximum landslides are shallow and not reported and difficult to identify in freely available large-resolution satellite images which show a failure of statistical models. The objective of current study is to landslide susceptibility in a the Aibawk block of Mizoram using TOPSIS model. As per our knowledge, the block has not micro level landslide susceptibility zones map which can be used for risk reduction. In this regard, the current study aimed stepwise application of TOPSIS model to prepare landslide susceptibility zones of the study area.

Method details

The TOPSIS is a compensatory aggregating function developed by Hwang and Yoon [36]. It is an extension of ELimination Et Choix Traduisant La REalité ((ELECTRE) and the ideal solution is the maximum distance from the ideal worst solution and the minimum distance from the ideal best solution [37]. The ideal worst solution tried to minimize the beneficial criteria and maximize the non-beneficial criteria. Meanwhile, the ideal best solution tried to minimize the non-beneficial criteria and maximize the beneficial criteria [38]. Though it may not always be the closest to the perfect solution, the option that ranks highest in TOPSIS is considered the best according to the ranking index [39]. The TOPSIS method can be intergrade with subjective or objective weightage and solve real life decision problem well. Recently, it has been widely used in wide field of watershed management, flood mapping, landslide hazard mapping and others viz. Kumar et al. [40], Tabarestani and Afzalimehr [41], Gu et al. [42]. Limitations

TOPSIS organises items in a linear order using a set of indicators. The TOPSIS algorithm is one of the most well-known ranking methods [63]. However, the limitations of TOPSIS model are below:a.TOPSIS's fundamental drawback is that it lacks weight elicitation and consistency verification for judgements [30].b.The selection of beneficial and non-beneficial factor varies one decision makers to others.c.One of the difficulties with TOPSIS is that when used by the prescribed process, it might induce a phenomenon known as rank inversion. The order of alternative preferences changes when an alternative is removed or added from the decision issue. Reversing ranks could occur in very uncommon situations where the greatest alternative becomes the worst. This occurrence may be unacceptable in many situations [64].

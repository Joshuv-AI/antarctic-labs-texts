The effects of time-restricted eating in comparison with energy-restricted diet on expression of BMAL-1 and miRNA-let-7f-1-3p genes, circadian rhythm status, sleep quality, metabolic and anthropometric markers in obese women: a study protocol for a randomized controlled clinical trial
Authors: Atefeh Tavakoli, Neda Kermani, Parastou Zamani, Bahram Pourghassem Gargari, Parvin Dehghan, Khadijeh Mirzaei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionOne of the most common chronic diseases is obesity. Disturbances in circadian rhythm and sleep quality are common in these people. Genes related to circadian rhythm control are also disrupted. One of the methods of controlling and reducing obesity is to intervene in the individual’s diet, and one of the alternative methods to the conventional low-calorie diet is the use of fasting diets, including time-restricted eating (TRE), which can have effects on the expression of a circadian rhythm genes, the circadian rhythm status, and the quality of sleep. This randomized controlled trial (RCT) will be aimed to examine the effect of the TRE diet in comparison with an energy-restricted diet (ERD) on the expression of basic helix-loop-helix ARNT-Like-1 (BMAL-1) and micro ribonucleic acid (miRNA)-let-7f-1-3p genes, circadian rhythm status, and sleep quality in women with obesity.Methods and analysisThis study will be conducted on 42 obese women. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either 16:8 TRE (fasting from 8 to 12 PM the next day) or an ERD (55% carbohydrate, 30% fat, 15% protein) diet for 12 weeks. Gene expression, circadian rhythm status, sleep quality, anthropometric measurements, and metabolic evaluations will be assessed before and after the intervention. Dietary evaluations will be performed at baseline, middle, and end of the study.DiscussionSince circadian rhythm-related gene expression has a role in circadian rhythm and ultimately obesity regulation, it seems that TRE, as a dietary approach, can affect these gene expressions.Trial registrationIranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20100123003140N25), which was registered and approved on 28 July 2025 (https://irct.behdasht.gov.ir/user/trial/84484/view).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-026-09905-y.

Introduction

Obesity is a multifaceted chronic disease characterized by excessive fat buildup that negatively affects health. The World Health Organization (WHO) defines adult obesity as having a body mass index (BMI) of 30 or higher [1]. In 2022, approximately 44% of women over 18 years old were overweight, and global projections indicate that by 2035, there may be around 3.3 billion adults with a high body mass index (BMI) [1]. Its pathogenesis is multifactorial, arising from complex interactions among lifestyle, environmental influences, genetic, epigenetic changes such as miRNA and deoxyribonucleic (DNA) methylation, and disruptions in the endocrine system [2]. Obesity is associated with a variety of chronic diseases, including metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance (IR), cardiovascular diseases (CVD), non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and certain types of cancer. Additionally, obesity may lead to alterations in physiological rhythms, such as the circadian rhythm, resulting in individuals with obesity experiencing fragmented sleep patterns of lower quality compared to non-obese individuals [3].

The mammalian circadian clock is an internal timing system present in all cells and organs, primarily regulated by a transcription-translation feedback loop involving genes like BMAL-1, circadian locomotor output cycles kaput (CLOCK), period (PER), and crystal (CRY). Post-translational modifications of BMAL-1 are key to maintaining biological oscillations and regulating various functions. Deletion of BMAL-1 disrupts circadian rhythms and is linked to the development of several diseases [4]. Studies have shown a relationship between the circadian clock and NF-κB activation in human visceral fat, with this interaction being more pronounced in obese individuals. Hyperactivation of NF-κB prevents BMAL-1 from binding to the PER-2 promoter region, thereby inhibiting its transcription [5]. MicroRNAs are small molecules that regulate gene expression and can be measured in blood, making them potential biomarkers for diseases like obesity, diabetes, and heart conditions. Several miRNA families are linked to obesity [2]. A novel miRNA, miRNA-let-7f-1, has recently gained attention and was observed in a 2022 study to be able to affect BMAL-1 [6]. In a study conducted by Zhan C and colleagues, the effects of a high-fat diet on both BMAL-1 deficient and wild-type mice were examined. The results indicated that the absence of this gene may serve as a protective factor against the development of obesity [7]. Also, in a study performed in 2025 on 20 mice fed a high-fat diet, the group lacking the BMAL-1 gene in the thyroid exhibited lower levels of total cholesterol (TC) and high-density lipoprotein (HDL) compared to the other group [8]. Light is a key environmental factor that influences biological rhythms, but other cues, like diet, also play a significant role in regulating circadian rhythms. Research highlights that diet composition, meal timing, and dietary restrictions can impact metabolic balance and circadian patterns at multiple levels [9]. ERD, involving a daily energy deficit of 500 to 750 kcal, has historically been advised for weight loss or management. In recent years, intermittent fasting (IF), also known as intermittent energy restriction, has gained popularity as an alternative to traditional weight-loss approaches. IF is a broad term that encompasses various fasting approaches, including TRE, where individuals usually fast for 14 to 16 h daily and have a feeding window of 4 to 12 h [10]. In a clinical trial conducted on obese adults, an 8-week TRE diet had no effect on sleep quality, duration, severity of insomnia, or risk of obstructive sleep apnea [11] but in another clinical trial the TRE diet resulted in better sleep quality and duration [12]. Also, a 2025 study on 30 adults found that an 8-week TRE diet regulated appetite and improved some sleep characteristics, such as earlier sleep onset and later wake-up time and fewer movements during sleep [13].

Considering the conflicting evidence regarding the effects of time-restricted fasting on sleep and circadian rhythms, the limited animal studies on its impact on BMAL-1 gene expression, and the absence of human studies on this subject, as well as the potential influence of this regimen on miRNA-let-7f expression and subsequent effects on BMAL-1 gene expression in overweight and obese individuals, this study is necessary.

Methods

Study design and participants

A diagram of the study design is demonstrated in Fig. 1. A flow chart of the study process is demonstrated in Fig. 2. We will conduct a randomized, controlled, parallel-group superiority trial to compare the effects of diets, TRF and ERD, on anthropometric and metabolic markers, sleep quality, circadian rhythm status, and expression of BMAL-1 and miRNA-let-7f-1-3p genes in obese women over 12 weeks, with a 1:1 allocation ratio in 2025–2026. Participants will be registered based on inclusion criteria, in this study, and they will be referred to Tehran University of Medical Sciences. A total of 42 women will be randomly allocated in to an intervention group with TRF diet (21 women) and a control group with the ERD diet (21 women) for 8 weeks. We estimate that the recruitment period for participants will last from September 6, 2025 to February 4, 2026 and after that until February 20, 2026; data collection will be completed. We predict that the results will be available on June 30, 2026. The protocol of this study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences (IR. TBZMED. REC. 1404.231) and was registered at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20100123003140N25). To attract participants, we will mention the benefits of this project, such as providing a program and diet to reduce weight and improve sleep quality, metabolic factors, and body composition, as well as assessing the expression of target genes and assessing sleep and circadian rhythms, in our calls. Each participant will be promised that after the study is completed and data is collected, the results of metabolic tests, gene expression, and changes related to body composition will be reported.

Inclusion criteria include obese women (BMI: 30–40 kg/m2) with an age range of 19–49 years who have not undergone menopause and exclusion criteria include a history of any disease such as hypertension and CVD, liver and kidney diseases, any type of cancer, mental and psychological diseases and in general any type of disease that affects the individual’s diet, drug abuse such as smoking or alcoholism, taking any medication and dietary supplements that affect appetite such as antidepressants-steroids or contraceptives, conditions such as pregnancy or breastfeeding, having a special and unusual diet plan within the last 3 months, having weight loss plans within the last 3 months, and having a job that requires working the night shift. All eligible individuals will be included in the study. All information regarding the purpose of the study, the type of interventions, and the duration of the study will be provided to all participants. Then, written informed consent will be obtained from individuals. All participating women will be aware that they can leave the study at any time. Randomization

Participants will be randomized using stratified permuted blocks. A block size of 2 will be used, and stratification factors are the participant’s age (19 to 34 and 34 to 49 years) and BMI (30 to 35, 35 to 40 kg/m2). Two intervention groups intervention and control will be considered for participants. We will use a computer (https://www.sealedenvelope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists) to do stratified block randomization. In this site, we specify the intervention groups (A: treatment and B: control), the size of each block [4], the number of sample subjects (42 persons), and the factors we intend to stratify (age: less than 34, greater than or equal to 34, and BMI: less than 35, greater than or equal to 35), and finally it gives us a list of the results of this stratified block randomization, based on which subjects will enter the study. Patients, doctors, and researchers will not be blinded. In our study, the intervention in question is diet for both treatment and control groups, which requires us to fully explain the diet to the participants, so the participants will be aware of the type of intervention they are participating in, and this will not allow blinding of the participants. This study will be carried out by principal investigator and her colleagues, so they will be aware of the process, and on the other hand, the final analysis will be carried out by the researcher, who is fully aware of the process and details of the project, so there will be no possibility of blinding the researchers and statistical analyst, so we will not have blinding in this study. Naturally, studies in which blinding will be carried out will have fewer errors and will have purer results because blinding of one or more parties is done to prevent observer bias. This refers to the fact that most (if not all) researchers will have some expectations regarding the effectiveness of an intervention. Blinding of observers provides a strategy to minimize this form of bias. About participants, if they do not know what the type of intervention is, they will be less biased towards compliance with the intervention.

Main researcher, AT, will generate the allocation sequence, enroll participants, and assign participants to interventions.

Study hypothesis

We hypothesized that the TRF diet has a beneficial effect on gene expression, circadian rhythm status, sleep quality, and clinical symptoms in obese women.

Intervention

Individuals are divided into two groups, intervention (TRF diet, n: 21) and control (ERD n: 21). We will prescribe the TRF diet as 16:8 diet, such that individuals have a 16-h hunger phase (from 8:00 PM to 12:00 PM) and are not allowed to eat any food combination except tea, coffee, water, or any beverages without energy-containing sweeteners during this period. In the remaining 8 h (from 12:00 PM to 8:00 PM), they will have their usual diet, such that the participants will not have any restrictions on energy intake or restrictions about the type and amount of food. Another dietary approach, ERD, is defined as consuming 500 kcal less than the daily energy requirement (55% carbohydrates, 30% fat, 15% protein), which is received during three main meals and three snacks.

Intervention safety

No important side effects have been reported with the fasting diet. However, participants are asked to report any side effects during the study via weekly telephone interviews.

Assessment of outcomes

Primary and secondary outcomes

In this study, the primary outcome is the changes in expression of BMAL-1 and miRNA-let 7f-1-3p genes from baseline values. We expect the results of this variable will be determined by July 20, 2026. Because we have primary correlated outcomes, to consider less conservative alternatives to maintain statistical power, we will use a Bonferroni correction. The secondary outcomes will be considered as changes from baseline in variables including anthropometric measures such as weight, BMI, waist circumference (WC), hip circumference (HC), waist to hip ratio (WtHR), and body composition, and metabolic markers including fasting blood sugar (FBS), insulin, TC, triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), HDL, sleep quality, circadian rhythm status, and dietary intakes.

Assessment of dietary intake

In order to assess nutritional status and dietary adherence, participants will be asked to complete two dietary recalls (one regular day and one off day) by telephone at the beginning, weekly, and at the end of the study. To track and increase adherence to the diet, participants will be contacted every week, food recalls will be taken, and text messages will be sent to them during these 3 months. A 168-item semi-quantitative food frequency questionnaire (FFQ) will be administered at the beginning of the study, which includes Iranian food items and assesses dietary intake over the past 1 year. Its validity and reliability have been validated in Iran [14]. This questionnaire will be used to assess changes in dietary intake over the past 1 year, as well as to assess background diet, which is very important in dietary intervention studies. This FFQ will be taken at the beginning and food recall will be assessed weekly, but we will use recalls from the beginning and mid-time and endpoint for statistical analysis. The mean intake of food items during these three recalls and one FFQ will be calculated in grams per day. The nutrient intake of individuals will be calculated using N4 software (Nutritionist4).

Assessment of gene expression

To examine the expression of BMAL-1 and miRNA-let-7f genes, a total of 10–7 ml of whole blood samples will be collected from all participants at the beginning and end of the study in a fasting state. Seven to 10 ml of whole blood will be centrifuged at room temperature for 10 min at 3000 rpm to separate the plasma. After separating the plasma, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) will be separated using 3.5 cc of Ficoll solution for each sample and centrifuged at room temperature for 20 min at 2300 rpm. Then, they will be washed twice with PBS solution and centrifuged at 4 °C for 7 min at 4000 rpm. They will be immediately frozen with the plasma at − 80 °C until further testing.

Total RNA, including miRNA, is isolated using TRIZOL reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality and quantity of extracted RNA are confirmed using the Nanodrop method, and qPCR is used to assess gene expression. For this purpose, cDNA must be synthesized using the relevant kit, and green master mix and required primers with specific sequences will be synthesized and used [15, 16]. These assessments will be conducted twice, at the beginning and end of the study.

Assessment of circadian rhythm status

The morning-evening questionnaire (MEQ) will be used to assess the circadian rhythm status at the beginning and end of the study. This questionnaire consists of 19 items that focus on the patterns and habits related to waking up and sleeping. The range of scores is between 16 and 86 points, and the classification of scores is as follows: completely evening (16–30), relatively evening (31–41), intermediate (42–58), relatively morning (59–69), and completely morning (70–86). People whose circadian rhythm status is assessed as morning based on this questionnaire are considered to be morning people, whose efficiency begins with sunrise, reaches its maximum in the middle of the day, and decreases with sunset. They often have acceptable sleep quality at night and fall asleep before midnight. Individuals whose circadian rhythm status is assessed in the evening are considered to be night owls, who, unlike the morning group, are most efficient after sunset and often suffer from sleep quality disorders at night [17]. The reliability and validity of the MEQ have also been investigated and confirmed in the Iranian population [18].

Assessment of sleep quality

The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) questionnaire will be used to assess sleep quality [19] at the beginning and end of the study, which includes 19 items related to 7 components including sleep quality, sleep-related disorders, sleep duration, sleep latency, sleep efficiency, use of sleeping pills, and disruption of daily activities due to poor sleep quality. This questionnaire also contains 5 other questions that will be answered by the referring companions, which will not be considered in the scoring process. The scoring range is between 0 and 21 points (each component between 0 and 3 points), with scores higher than 5 being considered as poor sleep quality, and the validity and reliability of this questionnaire have been reviewed and confirmed in Iran [20].

Biochemical assessments

Biochemical factors will be assessed in the TUMS Nutrition Faculty Laboratory. To assess blood glucose and lipid profile, we require a 10 ml blood sample, which is taken from participants after a 10–12-h fast. The blood samples are kept in the laboratory for 30 min to clot and then centrifuged for 10 min at 3000 rpm to extract the serum. Serum lipid profile, including TC, HDL-C, LDL-C, and serum TG, will be measured using commercial kits and enzymatic methods (Pars Azmoon kit, Tehran, Iran). The enzymatic glucose oxidase method will be considered for FBS assessment by using a glucose-specific kit. Anthropometric assessment

Anthropometric assessments (weight, height, BMI, WC, HC, WtHR, and body composition) will be performed at the beginning and end of the study. A digital scale with a precision of 100 g will be used for body weight assessment in minimal clothes and without shoes. A standard stadiometer with a precision of 0.5 cm will be used for standing height assessment without shoes. WC will be measured by measuring the distance between the suprailiac bone and the last rib using a measuring tape with a precision of 0.5 cm. BMI will be calculated using the measured height and weight (weight in kilograms/height in square meters) [23].

Statistical analysis

Analyses according to the intention-to-treat analysis (ITT) method will be done. Therefore, all participants who entered the study will be included in the analysis. The last data will be used in participants who will be dropped; then, re-analyses will be performed based on the protocol-based analysis method (per-protocol analysis), so that the changes in the results can be seen. Patients who finished the duration of the study and will be visited by the researcher will be included in the per-protocol analysis. Multiple imputations with chained equations will be used to assign any missing values. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test will be used to check the normality of the data. If a variable does not have a normal distribution, then a logarithmic transformation will be used. Variables will be reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The statistical tests used in the study will include paired t-test, independent t-test, ANCOVA, repeated measurement, chi-square, and linear regression. All participants’ information who will complete the study will be used.

Data management

The researchers of this study will adhere to Tabriz University of Medical Sciences regulations for collecting participant information. To keep privacy, all documents will be anonymized using ID numbers, and any identifying participant characteristics data will be securely safeguarded in the database. Access to the login passwords will be restricted to the trial researchers only. The principal investigator, AT, is responsible for verifying the accuracy of coding, security, and storage of data, and performing double checks of data entry and data values to ensure accuracy. This means that AT will personally enter the data into the software and will double-check the data entry so that the data is stored securely and accurately and will not provide this database to anyone else to ensure the security of the collected data. Each of the qualitative or quantitative variables will be entered according to the assessments made (laboratory or by questionnaire) and the variables that require scoring will be calculated and finalized according to the protocol of each questionnaire, which, as mentioned, will all be double-checked by the researcher.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete

We will call for participants, and in the same call, we will mention that participating in the study will have benefits such as complete and accurate anthropometric examinations, biochemical assessments including lipid profiles, glucose and insulin, evaluation of the expression of the aforementioned genes, and examination of sleep quality and circadian rhythm status. On the other hand, each participant will receive a weight-loss diet, and their food intake will be evaluated at the beginning, during the study (weekly), and at the end. This explanation will be given to each volunteer that if they follow their diet, they will benefit from the benefits of weight loss, such as improving anthropometric and biochemical factors, sleep quality, and even the expression of the aforementioned genes. After each participant enters the study, all the above-mentioned items will be done for her based on the study entry criteria, and the participant will not be abandoned in any way during the study, and she will be contacted weekly and text messages will be sent, and the participant will be assured that any problems and challenges she may encounter during the study can easily be discussed with the researcher and the necessary guidance will be provided. Finally, after the completion of the interventions, the results of the biochemical genetic tests will be provided to the participant.

Plans for auditing trial conduct

There is no plan to audit the relevant tests in this study. The main researcher should ensure that the trial steering committee and the ethics committee are kept informed of any potential risks that may arise during the course of the study.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties

Interim analyses

Discussion

Obesity is defined as a chronic and multifactorial disease characterized by excessive fat accumulation in the body, which can endanger an individual’s health [24]. High caloric intake is one of the contributing factors to the development of obesity [25]. Recent evidence suggest that meal timing also plays an important role in metabolic regulation and obesity. TRE is a dietary strategy for preventing and treating glucose and lipid metabolic disorders. Molecular studies have showed that cycles of fasting and feeding effectively interact with cellular processes such as metabolism, cell survival, and inflammatory and mitochondrial activities [26]. In addition, TRE appears to counteract circadian rhythm disturbances, which are known to play a role in metabolic changes associated with obesity [27]. Several mechanisms have been proposed for the effects of TRE: it typically involves a 4–10-h eating window, which may unintentionally reduce daily caloric intake by approximately 350–500 kcal [28]. TRE affects dietary patterns and significantly reduce meal frequency and snacking [29]. Synchronization of TRE with circadian rhythms enhances the efficiency of metabolism [30]. TRE regulates lipogenesis and lipolysis by aligning the rhythms of metabolic gene expression across various tissues. Specifically, the timing of food intake activates or suppress fat synthesis and breakdown processes during specific times of the day, promoting metabolic balance [31]. TRE can influence peripheral biological clocks via the BMAL1/PER, CRY feedback loop, thereby affecting the circadian rhythm [32]. It has also been shown that TRE can influence microRNA expression [33]. Recent studies have found that microRNA hsa-let-7f-1-3p can directly target the BMAL-1 gene and plays a critical role in circadian regulation and related processes [6]. In a study conducted on obese women, TRE did not result in significant changes in self-reported sleep quality compared to the control group, as measured by questionnaires. However, objective data obtained from a wearable device (Oura ring) demonstrated improvements in sleep parameters in the TRE group, including increased sleep efficiency, reduced sleep latency, and decreased wake time after sleep onset [34]. Studies that have assessed sleep quality subjectively using the PSQI have generally not reported significant improvements following TRE. However, due to the weak correlation between subjective and objective measures of sleep quality, definitive conclusions about the ineffectiveness of TRE cannot be drawn. Therefore, further investigations using more objective methods, such as polysomnography, are warranted to accurately evaluate the effects of TRE on sleep quality [35].

Evidence regarding the effects of TRE on anthropometric changes is inconsistent. de Melo JM et al. showed no significant difference in weight loss between the TRE and control groups in overweight and obese individuals [36]. However, Feehan J et al. reported that TRE led to significant reductions in body weight, waist circumference, and BMI compared to the control group. A significant reduction in lean body mass was also observed relative to baseline. However, TRE had no significant effects on LDL, total cholesterol, or triglycerides [37]. A study by Liu D et al. demonstrated no significant differences between TRE and calorie restriction groups in terms of weight loss, body fat, and metabolic markers. A similar finding was reported by Lin S et al. in a 12-month intervention involving obese adults. TRE compared to ERD showed no significant differences between the two groups in terms of weight loss. Additionally, no significant differences were observed in lipid profiles, fasting glucose, insulin resistance, or HbA1c between the groups. No significant changes were reported in self-reported sleep quality, sleep duration, insomnia severity, or risk of obstructive sleep apnea in the TRE and ERD groups compared to controls [38, 39]. A study by Pescari D et al. showed that both calorie-restricted diets and TRE led to reductions in weight and waist circumference, although the weight loss was significantly greater in the calorie-restricted group [40]. A 2024 meta-analysis comparing caloric restriction and fasting (including TRE) found that both approaches produced similar effects on lean body mass, waist and hip circumference, and blood pressure. Both methods reduced body weight and fat mass, but fasting led to significantly greater weight loss and improved insulin sensitivity compared to caloric restriction [41]. In a study on rats, caloric restriction was shown to regulate the expression of peripheral clock genes such as Clock and Bmal1, and lipid metabolism genes including Pgc1a, Pparg, and Ucp2 in peripheral tissues like adipose tissue, through nutrient-sensing pathways such as Sirt1, AMPK, and Nampt [42]. In a study by Yang et al. on gerbils, TRE induced changes in the expression of rhythm and inflammation-related genes in both central (hypothalamus) and peripheral tissues (liver and colon). Gerbils on a daily restricted feeding schedule exhibited reduced expression of central clock repressors and increased Bmal1 expression in the liver and colon [43].

Strengths and limitations

Considering the limited and often contradictory findings in the existing literature—and the clear lack of human studies exploring how TRE influences gene expression—we were compelled to carry out this study to help fill these important gaps in understanding. This protocol outlines a randomized clinical trial that, for the first time, compares the effects of TRE with traditional calorie restriction on metabolic, anthropometric, circadian rhythm, and gene expression outcomes, including miRNA-let-7f-1-3p and BMAL1. This approach allows for the exploration of complex molecular and genetic interactions with observable metabolic and anthropometric outcomes. The use of a defined female population increases the relevance and applicability of findings to a high-risk group vulnerable to obesity. Limitations of this study include challenges with adherence to dietary protocols, inter-individual differences in circadian rhythm, and potential confounding factors such as physical activity and light exposure. Another limitation is that we will not have blinding in this study. Additionally, the small sample size and the study’s focus on one gender limit the generalizability of results.

Trial status

We estimate that the recruitment period for participants will last from September 6, 2025 to February 4, 2026 and after that until July 20, 2026; data collection will be completed. We predict that the results will be available on September 30, 2026.

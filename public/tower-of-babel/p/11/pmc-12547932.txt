Ganciclovir-resistant CMV encephalitis in a mantle cell lymphoma patient on pirtobrutinib: Case report
Authors: Rebecca Unterborn, Karrine Brade, Steven M. Bair, Chia-Yu Chiu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection in patients with hematologic malignancies who have not undergone stem cell transplantation remains poorly understood. Reports of clinically significant CMV infections in patients receiving Bruton Tyrosine Kinase inhibitors (BTKi) are rare. We present a case of ganciclovir-resistant CMV encephalitis in a patient with mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) treated with pirtobrutinib (a third generation BTKi). This case highlights the importance of maintaining a high index of suspicion for CMV infection in patients with hematologic malignancies, including those receiving therapies traditionally considered to carry a lower risk of opportunistic infections.

Introduction

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection is common in allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (SCT) recipients. However, much remains unknown about CMV disease in patients with hematologic malignancies who did not undergo SCT, though some data support high mortality in this population [1]. Patients with lymphoma have a unique infection risk due to their underlying malignancies and cancer therapies. Bruton Tyrosine Kinase inhibitors (BTKi) are approved to treat indolent lymphoma. There are only a few reported cases of CMV reactivation in patients receiving BTKi, mainly with ibrutinib (first-generation BTK inhibitor) [2], [3]. Second-generation BTKi (acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib) and third-generation BTKi (pirtobrutinib) are generally considered less immunosuppressive due to fewer off-target effects [4]. Therefore, they appear to have a lower infectious risk and there were no explicit reports of CMV infection in major clinical trials [4], [5], [6]. Thus far, only one case of CMV viremia in a patient who received pirtobrutinib prior to CAR-T was reported in preliminary results from a clinical trial (incidence: 9 % [1/11]) [7]. Herein, we report a case of CMV encephalitis in a patient with mantle cell lymphoma treated with pirtobrutinib.

Case presentation

A 71-year-old man with mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) was diagnosed in 2010 and underwent splenectomy. He received multiple lines of therapy due to disease relapse, including VR-CAP (bortezomib, rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, prednisone), acalabrutinib, R-DHAX (dexamethasone, cytarabine, oxaliplatin, rituximab) and R-BAC (rituximab, bendamustine, cytarabine). Ultimately, he underwent CAR-T in April 2023. Six months after CAR-T, he experienced isolated CNS relapse of MCL and received treatment with obinutuzumab and intrathecal methotrexate followed by pirtobrutinib monotherapy resulting in a complete remission of the MCL (Fig. 1). At the time he was diagnosed with CNS lymphoma, he developed diarrhea with four non-bloody bowel movements per day. He was presumed to have CMV colitis based on positive qualitative stool CMV polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assay, although biopsies of the colon were not performed. A whole blood CMV PCR was detected with 5.01 log IU/mL (102,000 IU/mL). He was started on ganciclovir 5 mg/kg every 12 h (weight was 90 kg) for four days and transitioned to induction dose valganciclovir (900 mg twice daily). His viral load decreased with treatment but remained detectable (<3 log IU/mL; <1000 IU/mL). Following 8 months of pirtobrutinib, he was admitted with worsening confusion. Laboratory evaluation was unremarkable with normal cell counts, renal function, liver function tests and urine analysis. Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), varicella zoster virus (VZV), herpes simplex virus (HSV), human herpes virus 6 (HHV-6), and adenovirus blood PCRs were not detected, but CMV whole blood PCR was elevated at 4.48 log IU/mL (30,000 IU/mL). He underwent lumbar puncture on hospital day three with cerebral spinal fluid (CSF) analysis showing 16 total nucleated cells with 77 % lymphocytes, protein 109 mg/dL, and glucose 50 mg/dL. CMV PCR from the CSF was detected at 5.85 log IU/mL (700,000 IU/mL). CSF work-up for other pathogens (EBV, VZV, HSV, HHV6, adenovirus, enterovirus, West Nile virus, cryptococcus) were negative. The CSF culture and cytology were negative. The MRI brain showed bilateral non-specific confluent periventricular white matter changes. He was initially started on ganciclovir 5 mg/kg every 12 h hospital day three, but resistance testing sent from the blood showed a UL97 mutation, conferring high-level ganciclovir resistance [8]. He began foscarnet 90 mg/kg every 12 h on hospital day four (which was appropriately adjusted for worsening renal dysfunction throughout his stay) and on hospital day twenty-five, ganciclovir was added back for synergy due to lack of robust clinical response. After completing four weeks of therapy, his whole blood CMV PCR became undetectable, but CSF CMV PCR remained elevated on subsequent lumbar puncture (5.66 log; 460,500 IU/mL) and the patient’s mentation did not significantly improve. He transitioned to comfort care and died after thirteen days on home hospice. No autopsy was performed.

Discussion

This was a case of ganciclovir-resistant CMV encephalitis from CMV reactivation in the setting of pirtobrutinib monotherapy. This patient did complete multiple rounds of therapy prior to pirtobrutinib, including other BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib), anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies (rituximab, obinutuzumab) and CAR-T. The concept of cumulative immunosuppression in lymphoma patients who receive multiple lines of therapy has been described but is not fully understood and may be challenging to assess clinically [9]. It is possible this patient’s initial presentation with presumed CMV colitis was related to obinutuzumab exposure, as CMV infection with this agent has been described in the literature [10].

T lymphocyte function is crucial for CMV control [11]. The impact of BTK inhibitors on CMV risk is controversial. In addition to reducing B lymphocyte production, studies of ibrutinib showed altered ratios of T regulatory cells and CD4 T lymphocytes, which can hamper CMV control [12]. Conversely, animal studies showed BTK inhibitors may increase CD4 effector memory cells, which could help fight CMV infections [11]. Thus far, there are two CMV cases reported in patients who received BTK inhibitors (Table 1). Additionally, one retrospective study (n = 23) showed 30 % of CMV seropositive individuals developed CMV reactivation after ibrutinib initiation (median 30 days, range 15 – 45). However, none of the patients developed CMV disease and viremia cleared spontaneously without treatment in all cases [2], [12]. No clear guidance on the management of ganciclovir-resistant CMV encephalitis in patients with hematologic malignancies exists. The data are extrapolated from advanced HIV and transplant literature, though generally foscarnet, with consideration of ganciclovir for synergy, is considered common practice [8], [13], [14]. The outcome is poor (median survival three months) [14], though clinical symptoms and antiviral response do not differ based on CSF CMV levels [14]. This presents challenges in attempting to predict disease trajectory using CSF CMV PCRs, as reflected in our case, where CSF CMV remained elevated despite clearance of viremia. Based on current evidence, blood or CSF CMV PCR do not significantly predict the prognosis of CMV encephalitis [14].

This rare case of CMV encephalitis in a patient with MCL on a third-generation BTKi emphasizes the need to consider CMV infection in patients with hematologic malignancies, even those receiving therapies commonly associated with lower infectious risks. Further research is needed to better understand cumulative immunosuppression in these patients and to develop guidelines for CMV treatment in patients with hematologic malignancies that are not SCT recipients.

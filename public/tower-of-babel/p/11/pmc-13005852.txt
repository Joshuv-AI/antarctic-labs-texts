Usability and feasibility of INFORMA platform for computerized cognitive stimulation in mild cognitive impairment and early dementia: an 8-week multicenter study
Authors: Cristina Basso, Andrea Zanella, Marco Simoni, Carlo Fantozzi, Donata Gollin, Cristina Ruaro, Silvia Tiozzo Netti, Alessandra Zapparoli, Roberta Padoan, Maurizio Gallucci, Livia Gallo, Luca Valentinis, Moira Ceci, Pierluigi Dal Santo, Anna Ceccon, Samantha Pradelli, Michela Marcon, Laura De Togni, Anna Maria Musso, Antonino Riolo, Giuseppe Gagni, Carlo Gabelli, Pierangelo Spano, Therapists Informa Group (TIG)
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMild cognitive impairment (MCI) and dementia represent significant global health challenges requiring innovative intervention approaches. Computerized cognitive training (CCT) offers promising alternatives to traditional face-to-face interventions, providing flexibility and personalized care for individuals with cognitive disorders.AimsThis study evaluated the usability of INFORMA, a novel software platform based on Cognitive Activation Therapy (CAT), for delivering telerehabilitation to participants with MCI and mild-to-moderate dementia.MethodsA prospective, non-interventional multicenter study enrolled 241 participants (MMSE 20–30, CDR 0.5-1) from 12 Italian Centers for Cognitive Disorders and Dementia. Participants engaged in an 8-week tablet-based telerehabilitation program with weekly supervision. Usability was assessed using the System Usability Scale (SUS), Task Completion Rate (TCR), and Relative Task Completion Time (R-TCT), supplemented by participant and caregiver questionnaires.ResultsOf 219 completing participants (mean age 73.5 years), the mean SUS score was 82.0 (95% CI: 80.3–83.8), with 84% exceeding the usability threshold (SUS score > 68). Mean TCR reached 97.8%, while 70.3% demonstrated improved R-TCT. Higher SUS scores correlated with education and MMSE scores but decreased with age. Satisfaction ratings exceeded 87% for participants and 95% for caregivers.DiscussionINFORMA demonstrated high usability across all educational and cognitive levels, with the hybrid model combining automated exercises and professional supervision being well-received.ConclusionsINFORMA showed significant promise as a user-friendly computerized cognitive stimulation tool, supporting its potential efficacy for MCI and dementia interventions and warranting further therapeutic efficacy studies.

Introduction

Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) and dementia are significant global health challenges with a substantial burden on individuals, families, and healthcare systems. MCI involves subtle but noticeable cognitive decline beyond normal aging, whereas dementia is characterized by a progressive cognitive decline that significantly affects daily functioning [1, 2].

In the United States, approximately 22% of adults aged 65 years and older have MCI, while dementia affects approximately 10% of this population, with the prevalence increasing significantly with age and varying across demographic groups [3]. In Europe, the prevalence of MCI varies widely, with studies reporting rates between 5.1% and 24.5% [4, 5] and a prevalence rate standardized for age and sex of 7.1% [6]. In Veneto, Italy, the MCI prevalence is estimated at 5.4%, translating to over 78,000 cases according to the Osservatorio Demenze ISS-Report [7]. As longevity increases and the baby boomer generation ages, the burden of cognitive impairment and dementia will rise dramatically in the coming decades, and Alzheimer’s disease Europe estimates that the number of affected people will nearly double by 2050 [8].

As pharmacological treatment options for MCI and dementia are limited [9] and given the profound impact of these conditions, there is a growing interest and an imminent need for cognition-focused interventions to mitigate cognitive decline. Traditionally, cognitive interventions for mild cognitive impairment (MCI) are administered face-to-face by trained professionals. However, with technological advancements, these interventions can now be computerized, commonly referred to as Computerized Cognitive Training (CCT), and utilized as an alternative or complementary approach. Computerized methods are particularly promising because they offer flexibility in intervention design and ease of access for individuals who can participate at home [10]. These approaches also allow for personalized interventions [11] and adaptive difficulties [12] that cater to each individual’s unique cognitive profile and needs.

In recent years, various CCT solutions have been developed and investigated: Cogmed, a program that has shown improvements in non-verbal working memory and subjective memory complaints in older adults with MCI after 5–7 weeks of Training; CogniFit, a training app that demonstrated improvements in global cognitive ability, particularly in individuals with mood-related neuropsychiatric symptoms, and CogniPlus, a training platform aimed at improving memory, attention, and problem-solving skills. An RCT found that participants receiving computerized Training with CogniPlus performed better on cognition, attention, and quality of life measures than those in a traditional group-based program [13].

INFORMA, a modular software platform developed at the Centro Regionale Invecchiamento Cerebrale (CRIC) (University Hospital of Padua) in collaboration with the Department of Information Engineering of the University of Padua [14] (Veneto Region, Italy), offers a distinct approach to computerized cognitive stimulation and training. It is unique because it is entirely based on Cognitive Activation Therapy (CAT) [15], a proven cognitive stimulation intervention that has been digitalized and integrated. INFORMA emphasizes broad cognitive domains rather than isolated cognitive domains. The platform is specifically designed for individuals with MCI and mild-to-moderate dementia and offers personalized activities that can be adapted to individual needs and interests. Moreover, INFORMA adopts a hybrid model, blending automated in-app guidance with remote therapist supervision and interaction to enhance user engagement. Additionally, therapists are also involved in planning, implementing, assigning, and adjusting activities to improve adherence and deliver a personalized activity plan.

Notably, supervised interventions have shown significant cognitive benefits [12]. While the evidence for CCT in delaying the progression of dementia remains inconclusive, several studies have reported promising results in improving specific cognitive functions in MCI patients [9, 16, 17]. A recent review demonstrated CCT effectiveness in MCI and dementia individuals [12]. The meta-analysis included 35 eligible studies with 37 comparisons of CCT interventions. Of these 35 studies, 26 used supervised CCT and 9 used home-based unsupervised CCT. This highlights the potential efficacy of CCT in improving cognitive function, showing particularly significant improvements in memory (verbal, visual, and working) in individuals with MCI or dementia. The articles covered a variety of study designs, interventions, and technologies, ranging from computerized software and tablets to gaming consoles and virtual reality. This review highlights the need for more rigorous and standardized methodologies for future studies [12].

Most studies have primarily focused on the therapeutic efficacy of interventions, often overlooking the application’s usability, acceptability, and suitability from the users’ perspective [18, 19]. However, these aspects are fundamental for correctly interpreting the outcomes of studies intended to assess the effectiveness of CCT and ensure long-term engagement with cognitive interventions.

This study aims to address this gap by conducting a large-scale usability evaluation of the INFORMA system from the users’ perspective. The research objectives encompassed assessing feasibility, user acceptance, engagement, and persistence using standardized measures such as the System Usability Scale (SUS) and other usability and adherence criteria, including the Task Completion Rate (TCR) and Relative Task Completion Time (R-TCT). This investigation seeks to provide valuable insights into the factors that influence the successful implementation and sustained utilization of computerized cognitive training interventions in populations with neurocognitive disorders, including MCI and mild-to-moderate dementia.

Materials and methods.

The present study was a prospective, non-interventional usability study conducted at 11 Centers for Cognitive Disorders and Dementia in the Veneto Region and one Center in the Friuli Venezia Giulia Region (Italy).

Local ethical approval was obtained from all participating sites, and central approval was granted by the Ethics Committee for Clinical Research of the Province of Padua (Study Code: DSOG-REG-VEN-PROT 01/22; URC Code: AOP2842; Ethics Committee Code: 5629/AO/22). The study followed established ethical guidelines, including the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and adhered to the protocols for obtaining written informed consent, ensuring voluntary participation, and maintaining participant confidentiality.

Study population

Patients attending the Cognitive Disorders and Dementia Centers (CDCD) in the Veneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia regions (Italy) were screened for eligibility and recruited by a multidisciplinary team consisting of Medical Specialists in neurology, geriatrics, neuropsychologists and speech therapists who had previously completed theoretical and practical training on the study protocol.

Inclusion criteria were: adults with MCI or mild-to-moderate major neurocognitive disorder (Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) 20–30 raw score; Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) Scale 0.5-1). The exclusion criteria encompassed significant visuoperceptual, ideomotor, attentional, or behavioral impairments.

INFORMA cognitive stimulation software

The study was performed using INFORMA software. The platform comprises a Planning Module for therapists to customize rehabilitation paths and monitor participants’ progress; a User Module that allows participants to access INFORMA activities via a tablet app and communicate with therapists via written or vocal messages; and a backend that synchronizes data and supports multiple users across different sites.

Study procedures

The participants were provided tablets preloaded with the INFORMA app and received comprehensive training on their use, involving caregivers when indicated. The training was delivered by a certified therapist across two 45-minute sessions during the week preceding the initiation of the home-based activities. Participants were instructed to engage in cognitive stimulation tasks for approximately 20 min per day, 5 days per week (Monday to Friday) for over 8 weeks. To ensure adherence and foster therapeutic relationships, participants received weekly follow-up phone calls from their therapist to monitor their progress and address any challenges. The number of cognitive tasks assigned increased incrementally over the intervention period: 45 activities per week during weeks 1 and 2, 80 activities per week during weeks 3 to 5, and 115 activities per week during weeks 6 to 8. The tasks included a diverse set of exercises, namely ‘Quiz’, ‘Crosswords’, ‘Puzzle’, ‘Sentence’ ‘Reordering’, ‘Spot the Detail’, and ‘Categorization’. These exercises were organized into three difficulty levels (easy, medium, and difficult), assigned based on the participants’ cognitive profile and functional capabilities.

Usability assessment

The primary objective of the investigation was to evaluate the usability of the INFORMA User Module, which was assessed using the following metrics:

System Usability Scale (SUS): A validated 10-item questionnaire with a score ranging from 0 to 100, wherein an SUS score > 68 is considered the benchmark for acceptable usability/performance, in accordance with established literature [31]. The score is calculated as follows: for each of the odd numbered questions, 1 point was subtracted from the user’s score; for each of the even numbered questions, 5 points were subtracted from the user’s score. The algebraic sum of adjusted values were then multiply by 2.5 [20–23].

Task Completion Rate (TCR): the ratio between the number of activities completed and the total number of activities assigned over an 8-week period. For each participant, TCR was calculated as follows: TCR = (number of completed activities/total assigned activities) * 100, with a performance standard of ≥ 50% of the assigned activities completed, adopted as a pragmatic indicator of minimal task engagement and feasibility in an exploratory usability context.

Relative Task Completion Time (R-TCT) (or improvement index): The ratio between completion times from different periods, calculated as: Relative TCT = Mean time required to complete an activity during the first week / Mean time required to complete an activity during the last week.

In this case, a value greater than 1 indicates improvement (the subsequent time is less than the initial time).

Adherence assessment

Adherence to the study was evaluated based on the interaction data. The TCR is a direct measure; a decrease in the R-TCT indicates that participants demonstrated improved performance, suggesting sustained adherence. High SUS scores indicated that the participants perceived the tool as accessible, facilitating adherence.

User-reported assessment

The evaluation of the INFORMA User Module was supplemented by participant-reported (6 questions) and caregiver-reported (7 questions) outcome questionnaires (PRO & CRO) administered during the final study visit,

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data. The relationships between outcomes and participants’ characteristics (age, sex, years in school, and cognitive scores) were examined using t-tests, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), chi-square tests, and regression analyses, as appropriate. A 95% confidence interval (CI) was computed for the relevant estimates.

All statistical analyses were performed using Stata 18 software (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

Patient and public involvement

The study directly involved participants in the evaluation of the usability of the INFORMA App, capturing the user’s perspective.

Improvement of Relative Task Completion Time (R-TCT) based on years in school

SUS scores were positively correlated with higher education levels (p < 0.001) and MMSE scores (p < 0.001) but demonstrated a negative correlation with age (r = -0.31). Participants with higher educational levels and MMSE scores exhibited faster task performances (Tables 3 and 4).

These findings suggest that, although SUS scores varied according to participant characteristics, the overall task completion rates remained consistently high.

Multiple linear regression analysis was employed to investigate the relationship between System Usability Scale (SUS) scores and participant characteristics, including age, years of education, and Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores. The model revealed that age was significantly associated with lower SUS scores (β = -0.425, 95% CI [-0.647, -0.203], p < 0.001), indicating that for each additional year of age, SUS scores decreased by 0.425 points. Conversely, years of education demonstrated a significant positive association with SUS scores (β = 0.419; 95% CI [0.040–0.799], p = 0.03), suggesting that each additional year of formal education was associated with a 0.419-point increase in SUS scores. MMSE scores were also significantly associated with SUS scores (β = 1.227, 95% CI [0.601, 1.852], p < 0.001), indicating that higher cognitive function correlated with higher usability scores. The constant was 77.86 (95% CI [53.49, 102.22]), representing the theoretical baseline SUS score. These findings suggest that age, education, and cognitive function significantly predicted perceived system usability.

Adherence assessment

The mean Task Completion Rate (TCR) remained above 96% across all eight weeks, indicating that participants maintained consistent participation without prematurely terminating sessions. The total time spent to complete the activities remained stable or decreased across the activity intervals (weeks 1–2, 3–5, and 6–8). The time per activity (R-TCT) decreased significantly from week 1 to week 8 (p < 0.0001). In 63% of the participants, R-TCT decreased, reflecting improved task efficiency and supporting sustained engagement and adherence.

High SUS scores and reasonably low dropout rates further demonstrated a strong adherence throughout the study.

User-reported assessment

A total of 230 participants and 194 caregivers completed questionnaires regarding their experiences with the INFORMA App-based cognitive stimulation program. Among the participants, 96.6% rated the tablet’s usability as feasible to very easy (47.4% feasible, 34.4% easy, and 14.8% very easy), with only 3.4% reporting difficulties. Regarding family assistance, 56.5% required minimal support, whereas 33.5% occasionally required it. The INFORMA App activities were evaluated positively by 96.6% of the participants, and 94.3% appreciated the flexibility in organizing activities. Overall, 98.3% of the participants rated their experience as positive, with 87.4% rating it ≥ 8 on a 10-point scale.

From the caregivers’ perspective, 93.3% found the initial training sufficient. The majority of caregivers (47.4%) reported minimal supervision requirements, and 89.2% observed increased independence in their care recipients’ use of tablets and applications. Caregivers infrequently experienced difficulties, with 82.0% reporting no difficulties. The majority (99.0%) positively considered home-based cognitive stimulation using the INFORMA application. Overall, 99.5% of the caregivers rated their experience positively, with 95.5% rating it ≥ 8 on a 10-point scale.

Discussion

The results of this multicenter usability study demonstrate that the INFORMA software platform, based on Cognitive Action Therapy, has established the feasibility of effectively delivering personalized computerized telerehabilitation to individuals with neurocognitive disorders including MCI and mild-to-moderate dementia.

The usability of the platform is substantiated by several key metrics. The mean SUS score significantly exceeded the standard usability threshold of 68, with 84% of participants scoring above this benchmark. This indicates that INFORMA provides high user-friendliness and accessibility to users with diverse demographics and varying cognitive abilities and it may be especially accessible even for older adults, who traditionally face more challenges with technology adoption. The consistently high Relative Task Completion Rate (R-TCR) across all education levels and MMSE classes proves excellent adherence to the proposed intervention, surpassing the adherence levels typically observed in traditional cognitive rehabilitation approaches [23].

The observed reduction in Relative Task Completion Time (R-TCT) within the three blocks of activity (weeks 1–2, 3–5, 6–8) for most participants suggests enhanced tool learning, operational efficiency, and interface acceptance. The finding that participants with lower education levels and MMSE scores demonstrated more significant improvements in R-TCT is particularly noteworthy. This trend may reflect a ceiling effect in high-performing users, who possibly reached optimal performance from the beginning, leaving little room for improvement. In contrast, individuals with greater initial difficulties benefited more from repeated exposure and gradual adaptation to the tasks.

While our study focused exclusively on usability rather than therapeutic efficacy, existing literature demonstrates the efficacy of computerized cognitive training in supporting traditional cognition-oriented treatments [13, 24, 25]. Moreover, high usability is a fundamental prerequisite for the effectiveness of any digital intervention. A well-designed and easily navigable interface is essential to ensure patients can fully engage with cognitive stimulation programs, regardless of therapeutic efficacy level.

A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis by Li et al. (2022) examined 18 studies involving 1,059 participants with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). They found a modest yet statistically significant enhancement in global cognitive function following computerized cognitive training (CCT) interventions, as compared to control groups (standardized mean difference = 0.54, 95% CI 0.35–0.73). Subtle improvements were observed in domain-specific cognition [26]. Further evidence comes from a 2024 meta-analysis encompassing 55 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with 4,455 participants across various cognitive states, including normal cognition, MCI, and neurodegenerative dementia. This study demonstrated a significant cross-domain effect of cognitive training (Hedges’ g = 0.286, 95% CI: 0.224–0.348), underscoring the importance of engagement, persistence, and adherence in maximizing training efficacy [27]. Moreover, a meta-analysis by Chan et al. (2024), focused on memory functions in individuals with MCI or dementia, analyzing 28 studies with 1,489 MCI participants. The results indicated substantial improvements in verbal episodic memory, visual episodic memory, and working memory following CCT [12].

High satisfaction ratings from participants and caregivers provided robust support for the acceptability of INFORMA as a telerehabilitation tool. Although not formally stratified, therapist observations indicated that participants with lower cognitive scores tended to require more assistance, highlighting the importance of maintaining a human element in digital interventions. This finding aligns with emerging research on digital brain health interventions. As highlighted in a recent perspective paper by Bond et al. (2023), digital tools should enhance, rather than replace, human interaction in healthcare delivery, emphasizing the importance of integrated approaches that combine digital capabilities with human support [28]. This ‘blended care’ approach is particularly relevant for cognitive stimulation, where therapist guidance and human interaction remain crucial components of effective intervention delivery.

Furthermore, a study on the limitations of using the MMSE for capacity assessment (Marrodan et al., 2018) [ 29] found that cognitive impairment, as measured by the MMSE, affected the participants’ performance in understanding and reasoning tasks [31]. This suggests that individuals with lower MMSE scores may require additional support when engaging in digital interventions, reinforcing the importance of maintaining human assistance, especially for those with cognitive challenges. This underscores the value of INFORMA’s design, which incorporates remote therapist supervision and automated assistance, striking a balance between the scalability of digital interventions and the personalized care of traditional therapy.

The high concordance between caregiver and participant assessments of usability underscores the reliability of the reported satisfaction levels and reinforces the potential of INFORMA as a tool that can be integrated into a participant’s daily life with caregiver and therapist support.

The results of this usability study position INFORMA as an innovative platform in the landscape of computerized cognitive interventions for neurocognitive disorders.

As detailed by Paggetti et al. (2025) [30], cognition-oriented treatments (COTs) serve distinct purposes, with cognitive stimulation (CS) and cognitive training (CT) representing different paradigms in neurorehabilitation. INFORMA bridges these approaches by integrating Cognitive Activation Therapy (CAT) into a digital platform combining computerized cognitive stimulation and training. Unlike CT platforms reviewed by Paggetti et al. that focus on isolated cognitive functions, INFORMA emphasizes broader cognitive intervention through personalized activities. INFORMA incorporates supervised, interactive elements of CS interventions while leveraging technology for accessibility. This hybrid model, combining in-app guidance with remote therapist supervision, addresses the methodological heterogeneity identified by Paggetti et al.

Strength and limitations

This study provides a comprehensive evaluation of the usability of a cognitive stimulation app from the user’s perspective, addressing a significant gap in the current literature. This research has several methodological strengths, including adherence to an approved research protocol and consistent data collection procedures that enhance the reliability and replicability of findings. It examines an innovative hybrid platform that combines personalized, adaptable, and automated exercises with professional support, potentially leading to increased user engagement and program adherence.

The study design features a relatively large, diverse sample within a multicenter framework, employing a comprehensive usability assessment with a high retention rate. The standardized implementation protocol enhances the generalizability of the findings to populations with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and mild-to-moderate dementia. These elements collectively contribute to the study’s robust nature and potential to provide valuable insights into the factors influencing the successful implementation and sustained utilization of computerized cognitive training interventions in populations with neurocognitive disorders.

However, this study had several limitations. The study’s single-country focus may limit its applicability, while the 8-week intervention period may not capture long-term usability aspects. The absence of a control or comparative group limits comparison with other cognitive stimulation platforms. The sample size and representativeness may constrain the broader applicability of these findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the INFORMA software platform demonstrates promising results in terms of usability, adherence, and user satisfaction for cognitive telerehabilitation. High completion rates, improved operational efficiency over time, and positive user experiences across varying levels of education and cognitive impairment indicate its potential as a versatile and user-friendly tool for delivering personalized cognitive stimulation.

Participant and caregiver satisfaction, combined with appreciation for therapist support among several participants, underscores the validity of INFORMA’s hybrid model. This approach effectively combines automated exercises with professional supervision, balancing digital scalability with personalized care.

Future research should focus on evaluating INFORMA’s therapeutic efficacy, examining its impact on global cognitive function, functional abilities, long-term benefits in mitigating cognitive decline, potential impact on quality of life, and effect on reducing healthcare burden. Additionally, investigating methods to further personalize the platform to individual needs and preferences could enhance user experience, potentially contributing to sustained adherence. Randomized controlled trials are needed to assess the efficacy and determine optimal intervention durations and frequencies. Moreover, research on the platform’s cost-effectiveness and integration into existing healthcare settings could lead to broader implementation strategies.

Overall, in terms of usability, INFORMA shows significant promise as a computerized cognitive stimulation platform for individuals with MCI and mild-to-moderate dementia. Its successful implementation across multiple centres demonstrates broad acceptability and usability, paving the way for larger-scale efficacy studies.

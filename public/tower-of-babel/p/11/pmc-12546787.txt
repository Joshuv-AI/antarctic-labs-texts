Are There High-Risk Prognostic Factors for Isolated Locoregional Recurrence in Patients With Early-Stage Breast Cancer Treated With Mastectomy Without Adjuvant Radiation Therapy?
Authors: Giuseppe Facondo, Chiara Reverberi, Agnese Prisco, Tino Ceschia, Gianluca Vullo, Giuseppe Aprile, Alessandro Marco Minisini, Luca Seriau, Serena Bertozzi, Carla Cedolini, Marco Trovò
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to investigate whether there are subgroups of patients with early-stage breast cancer (BC) treated with radical mastectomy that are at high risk of locoregional recurrence (LRR) and could benefit from postmastectomy radiation therapy (PMRT).Methods and MaterialsWe retrospectively reviewed patients with early-stage BC treated with mastectomy at our institution between December 2009 and December 2018. Tumors were classified according to molecular subtype and known prognostic factors. Outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Univariate analysis was performed using the log-rank test, while Cox proportional hazards regression was applied to estimate hazard ratios for evaluating associations between prognostic factors and survival. A P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsA total of 670 patients who met the selection criteria were identified. Median age was 59.4 years (IQR, 48-72), and the median follow-up was 107.1 months (IQR, 80.3-138.6). Among the cohort, 257 patients (38.3%) had T2 tumors, 249 (37.1%) were pN1, 186 (27.8%) had grade 3 tumors, 134 (20%) presented with lymphovascular invasion (LVI), 116 (17.3%) were HER2-positive, and 55 (8.2%) had triple-negative disease. LRR rates at 2, 5, and 8 years were 1.4%, 2.9%, and 3.5%, respectively. On univariate analysis, the presence of LVI, G3, tumor size (T), nodal involvement (pN1), estrogen receptor-negative status, triple-negative phenotype, and Ki-67 expression were significantly associated with an increased risk of LRR. Notably, LVI-positive patients had significantly higher locoregional and regional recurrence rates at 8 years (9.5% and 9.4%, respectively) compared to LVI-negative patients (1% and 1.1%). Multivariate analysis confirmed LVI as a strong and independent predictor of recurrence across all models.ConclusionsThis study confirms the prognostic relevance of several pathologic factors in predicting LRR, with particular emphasis on the independent role of LVI, in patients with early-stage BC treated with mastectomy and not receiving PMRT. In patients with early-stage BC with T1-T2 tumors and 1-3 positive axillary lymph nodes with the presence of LVI, PMRT should be considered.

Introduction

According to current guidelines from the American Society of Clinical Oncology and the American Society for Radiation Oncology, adjuvant chest wall irradiation following mastectomy is recommended for patients with 4 or more positive axillary lymph nodes, tumors >5 cm, or stage III disease. However, the role of postmastectomy radiation therapy (PMRT) in patients with early-stage breast cancer with node-negative disease or 1-3 positive lymph nodes (stage I-II) remains controversial. There is generally no indication for PMRT in node-negative patients because of their low risk of locoregional recurrence (LRR).1

Nonetheless, the role of PMRT continues to be among the most debated issues in the adjuvant management of breast cancer. Decisions regarding adjuvant radiation therapy are based primarily on the estimated risk of local recurrence, taking into account several clinicopathologic risk factors, including the number of positive axillary lymph nodes, tumor size, Ki-67 proliferation index, and lymphovascular invasion (LVI). Additional important considerations include histologic grade, hormone receptor status, and molecular subtype [eg, HER2-positive or triple-negative (TN) breast cancer].

Some retrospective studies have examined the controversial role of PMRT in patients with 1-3 positive axillary nodes, showing that radiation therapy may be beneficial, especially when additional adverse prognostic factors are present—such as young age, high histologic grade, LVI, extracapsular extension, or hormone receptor negativity.2, 3, 4, 5

A comprehensive understanding of the interaction between prognostic variables is essential for personalizing treatment strategies. The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the locoregional failure rate in patients with stage I-II breast cancer with node-negative or 1-3 positive axillary lymph nodes treated with mastectomy and adjuvant systemic therapy. A secondary aim is to identify specific subgroups that may be at sufficiently high risk of recurrence to benefit from PMRT.

Methods and Materials

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board. Eligible patients had histologically confirmed breast cancer and underwent mastectomy between December 2009 and December 2018. Inclusion criteria were: female sex, age ≥18 years, stage I-II disease (specifically T1-T2 and N0-N1 with a maximum of 3 positive lymph nodes), and ≥6 months of follow-up. Concerning the surgical procedure, all patients had to undergo mastectomy with or without axillary dissection. Regarding the histologic examination, all patients were required to have documented histology, tumor grade, unifocal or multifocal status, immunophenotype, presence or absence of LVI, and Ki-67 count. Patients were excluded if they had T3-T4 tumors, received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, had synchronous or metachronous breast cancer, bilateral disease, distant metastasis at diagnosis, BRCA mutations, positive margin or had received PMRT. Follow-up status was updated through review of electronic medical records.

Clinical and pathologic data

Recorded data were: age at diagnosis, laterality, tumor size (pT), histology, number of positive lymph nodes, presence of LVI, Ki-67 index, and immunohistochemical expression of estrogen, progesterone, and HER2 receptors. Tumor grade and adjuvant systemic therapies were also recorded. Ki-67 and nodal status were categorized into subgroups to assess prognostic thresholds. All recurrences were confirmed via radiologic or histologic evidence. Local recurrence (LR) was defined as relapse in the ipsilateral chest wall; regional recurrence (RR) involved the ipsilateral axillary, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, or internal mammary nodes. LRR was defined as either LR or RR. Distant metastasis was defined as recurrence outside these sites. Follow-up data were collected from medical records or direct contact with patients or their families. Time-to-event outcomes were calculated from the date of surgery to the occurrence of the event of interest or censored at the last follow-up in event-free patients.

Statistical analysis

The Kaplan-Meier method was used for survival estimates. Univariate analysis was performed using the log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards regression was used to compute hazard ratios (HRs) for the association between prognostic factors and recurrence. A multivariate model was constructed using backward stepwise elimination. A P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 25.0 (SPSS Inc).

Outcomes analysis

The median follow-up was 107.1 months (IQR, 80.3-138.6). A total of 22 patients (3.3%) experienced LR, with cumulative rates of 0.6% at 2 years, 1.7% at 5 years, and 2.9% at 8 years. RR occurred in 21 patients (3.1%), with rates of 0.9% at 2 years, 2.3% at 5 years, and 2.7% at 8 years. In total, 32 patients (4.7%) experienced LRR corresponding to rates of 1.4% at 2 years, 2.9% at 5 years, and 3.5% at 8 years (Fig. 1). Eleven patients (1.6%) had both LR and RR. Prognostic factors associated with LRR

In the univariate analysis, several clinicopathologic variables were significantly associated with an increased risk of LR, RR, and LRR (Table 2). LVI demonstrated the strongest association with recurrence across all outcomes: patients with LVI had an HR of 9.98 for LR, 6.23 for RR, and 8.97 for LRR (all P < .01). At 8 years, LVI-positive patients showed markedly higher recurrence rates, 9.5% for LR and 9.4% for RR—compared to 1.0% and 1.1%, respectively, in LVI-negative patients (Fig. 2). Other significant prognostic factors associated with an increased risk of both LR and RR were: grade 3 tumors, estrogen receptor-negative disease, TN disease, and Ki-67 index.

Discussion

The risk of LRR in patients with early-stage breast cancer undergoing mastectomy without PMRT remains a critical issue in oncologic management. While mastectomy is a standard and effective treatment for early-stage breast cancer, it does not guarantee absolute locoregional control. Identifying high-risk subgroups within this population is essential for tailoring adjuvant strategies, particularly in the absence of PMRT.

Over time, data have increasingly supported the use of PMRT in patients with T1-2 tumors and 1-3 positive lymph nodes.3,4,6, 7, 8 However, the decision to administer PMRT remains nuanced, especially when absolute risk reduction must be balanced against potential treatment-related morbidity.

In this retrospective cohort study, we evaluated clinical outcomes in a large population of patients with pT1-2 pN0-1 early breast cancer, treated with mastectomy, without PMRT. Patients with positive margins, BRCA mutations, or bilateral/metachronous tumors were excluded to minimize confounding. With a median follow-up of over 107 months, our data likely captured the majority of relevant LRR events, as these are uncommon beyond the first decade after treatment.

Although there was a low overall LRR rate reported, we identified a subgroup of women at high risk of LRR after mastectomy for small, node-negative breast cancers. LVI emerged as the most consistent independent prognostic factor, with HRs of 9.98 (LR), 6.23 (RR), and 8.97 (LRR) (P < .01) confirmed with the multivariate analysis. In our study, G3, TN, T, N, estrogen receptor, and Ki67 were identified as negative prognostic factors for LRR rate in the univariate analysis.

These findings are consistent with previously published studies, such as the KROG 14-23 study—a large retrospective analysis that identified close surgical margins as a significant independent risk factor for LRR. In multivariate analysis, close resection margin (P = .001) emerged as the only independent predictor of LRR. Furthermore, multivariate analysis for any first recurrence revealed additional independent risk factors, including age under 35 years (P = .025), T2 tumor stage (P = .004), high tumor grade (P = .032), close resection margin (P = .035), and TN biological subtype (P = .031).9

Several studies, including those by Trovo et al10 and Leonardi et al,11 have examined also LVI as a prognostic factor for LRR. In the study by Trovo et al, several variables were significantly associated with an increased risk of LRR, including premenopausal status (P = .004), estrogen receptor-negative status (P = .02), high pathologic grade (grade 3, P = .02), and notably, the presence of LVI (P = .001).10 Similarly, Leonardi et al demonstrated that the presence of 2 or more risk factors—such as LVI, high Ki-67 index, and young age—was linked to a significantly elevated risk of LRR. These findings support a risk-adapted approach to adjuvant therapy, in which LVI plays a key role in identifying patients at higher risk of recurrence.11

Our findings are also consistent with those of Chen et al, who found that LVI, grade 3 tumors, and nodal burden were independently associated with LRR in patients with TN breast cancer not receiving PMRT.12

Other studies have demonstrated that vascular invasion, particularly LVI, is associated with an increased risk of recurrence. However, these analyses often evaluated LVI in conjunction with other adverse prognostic factors, without isolating its individual contribution or quantifying its specific prognostic weight.13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18

Recent evidence underscores the significance of LVI as a prognostic factor for LRR in breast cancer. Koleoso et al19 demonstrated a progressive increase in LRR risk across 3 LVI categories: no LVI, nonextensive LVI, and extensive LVI (E-LVI). At 5 and 10 years post treatment, patients with E-LVI experienced LRR rates of 9.6% (95% CI, 7.1%-13%) and 13% (95% CI, 10%-17%), respectively, which were significantly higher than those with nonextensive LVI, who had rates of 6.8% (95% CI, 5.7%-7.9%) and 10% (95% CI, 8.8%-12%) (P < .01).19 These findings highlight E-LVI as a substantial and often under-recognized risk factor for LRR.

Supporting these results, Jagsi et al20 examined a cohort of patients undergoing mastectomy and axillary nodal dissection without PMRT. Their analysis revealed that the presence of LVI was significantly associated with increased LRR, with a 10-year LRR rate of 19.8% in patients with LVI compared to 5.0% in those without (P < .01).20 Together, these studies emphasize the critical role of LVI, particularly extensive LVI, in stratifying risk and guiding treatment decisions to reduce LRR in patients with breast cancer.

These data are consistent with the results of our study, where LVI remained statistically significant across all models, with HRs ranging approximately from 4.9 to 7.7 and P values consistently <.01, thus demonstrating that it can have a significant independent role.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design introduces potential selection bias and unmeasured confounding variables, particularly regarding heterogeneity in systemic therapies. Second, the absence of a PMRT control group prevents direct assessment of radiation therapy's potential benefits in high-risk subgroups. Lastly, although our cohort included a robust sample size of 670 patients, only 20% exhibited LVI, reflecting the population's natural heterogeneity rather than representing a homogeneous sample. Although we controlled known prognostic factors through multivariable modeling, residual confounding from unaccounted variables (eg, detailed margin assessment, treatment compliance) may persist.

These limitations are balanced by several notable strengths: the substantial sample size of 670 patients, which is significant for a single-institutional study, the extended median follow-up of 9 years, and the comprehensive data collected, including detailed histologic characteristics for all selected patients. Finally, it should be emphasized that the lack of randomized data in the current literature still limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding the potential benefits of PMRT in patients positive for LVI. The current study focused on patients who had pT1-T2N0-N1 disease, demonstrating that there is a subgroup of patients having a high risk of LRR when several clinicopathologic variables are present; despite that, the overall LRR rate for the entire population is relatively low.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that LVI is an important prognostic factor for LRR in patients with early-stage breast cancer treated with mastectomy and adjuvant systemic therapy without radiation therapy. The presence of LVI was associated with a significantly increased risk of both LR and RR, consistent with findings from contemporary literature.

Given its prognostic impact, LVI status should be incorporated into treatment planning and multidisciplinary discussions, particularly when evaluating the potential benefit of PMRT. While current guidelines emphasize nodal status and tumor size, our findings support a more nuanced risk stratification that includes LVI.

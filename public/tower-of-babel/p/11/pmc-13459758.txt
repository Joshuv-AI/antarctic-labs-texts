FMultifunctional magnetic resonance imaging for the assessment of renal pathological alterations in IgA nephropathy
Authors: Aiqun Chen, Ban Zhao, Ying Sun, Fang Fang, Jinsong Zhang, Yonghui Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe aimed to evaluate the application value of multiparametric diffusion weighted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) for the assessment and risk stratification of renal histopathological injuries in patients with IgA nephropathy (IgAN), as an exploratory study.Methods95 patients were prospectively enrolled, intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM), diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) were performed, and a renal biopsy was performed within 3 days after MRI examination. Finally, 31 patients with pathologically confirmed IgAN were selected for analysis, and glomerulosclerosis index (GSI) and tubulointerstitial fibrosis index (TBI) were assessed by two experienced pathologists who were blinded to the clinical and multiparametric diffusion weighted imaging MRI data.ResultsCorrelation analysis showed that eGFR was negatively correlated with age, GSI, and TBI. Both GSI and TBI were negatively correlated with the true diffusion coefficient (D, cortex), mean diffusivity (MD, cortex and medulla), fractional anisotropy (FA, medulla), and axial diffusivity (Da, cortex and medulla), and positively correlated with the mean kurtosis (MK, cortex) and axial kurtosis (Ka, cortex and medulla). Compared with the normal control group, the moderate-to-severe GSI/TBI groups showed significant decreases in most diffusion parameters and increases in kurtosis parameters, while there was no significant difference in all MRI parameters between the mild injury groups and the control group. Multivariate regression and model validation showed that medullary FA was the independent imaging predictor of moderate-to-severe GSI in the DTI-based model (p = 0.013), and the clinical+DTI combined model had a high diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.932, optimism-corrected AUC = 0.858); eGFR was the only significant predictor in the DKI-based model (p = 0.023), with the clinical+DKI combined model AUC reaching 0.891 (optimism-corrected AUC = 0.801).ConclusionsAs a preliminary exploratory study,DTI and DKI may provide useful non-invasive information for assessing renal histopathological alterations in IgAN patients. Medullary FA is a promising imaging biomarker for glomerulosclerosis, and its combination with clinical variables shows potential for identifying patients with moderate-to-severe disease. The originally hypothesized superiority of DKI-derived MK was not clearly demonstrated. Background

IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common glomerulonephritis worldwide, accounting for 30–45% of primary glomerular diseases in Asians, 30–40% in Caucasians, and a lower proportion in Blacks [1]. In China, IgAN is also the most common primary glomerular disease [2, 3]. Studies have found renal pathological lesions were one of the main factors related to renal prognosis [4, 5]. Currently, tissue biopsy is the gold-standard modality for the assessment of pathological changes of the kidneys; however, monitoring pathological lesions progression through repeated renal biopsies is not practical, only 25% of all patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) had undergone a biopsy in large CKD cohorts, and biopsy is an invasive examination with sampling errors and observer variability [6]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to find a non-invasive, relatively sensitive, and repeatable method for multidimensional assessment of renal pathological changes.

To date, several common multiparametric diffusion-weighted imaging magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) techniques have been used to assess renal function, perfusion, oxygenation, and the degree of fibrosis. Togao et al. first used apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) to assess renal fibrosis in a murine model of unilateral ureteral obstruction [7]. And since then, ADC has widely been used to assess renal function [8, 9] and histopathological fibrosis [10, 11]. Intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM) is an improved bi-exponential MRI approach, the derived parameter ADC was used to detect the diffusion of water molecules in tissues; furthermore, the parameter true diffusion coefficient (D) can minimize the influence of fast diffusion on the estimated true water diffusion in tissues, showing good application in assessing the renal function and pathological injury of renal transplant patients and CKD patients [12–14]. As IVIM reflects the diffusion movement only in the direction of the diffusion-sensitive gradient field, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) is a sequence that applies a diffusion-sensitive gradient field in multiple directions to fully reflect the diffusion of water molecules in various directions and accurately detect the diffusion anisotropy of water molecules in each voxel [15]. However, the renal lesions in IgAN patients are complex, and conventional IVIM and DTI are each based on the assumption that water molecules diffuse by Gaussian motion. Thus, diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) is an extension of diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) technology; the derived parameters mean kurtosis (MK), axial kurtosis (Ka), and radial kurtosis (Kr) reflect the non-Gaussianity of water molecule diffusion, which may be related to tissue complexity and heterogeneity [16].

Previous promising findings of multiparametric diffusion weighted imaging MRI have been reported in the evaluation of renal function and various pathophysiologic alterations in patients with CKD. However, the conclusions were inconsistent, possibly due to the absence of histopathologic examinations, or the majority of studies were evaluated by a single sequence, making it difficult to determine the optimal approach. Performing multiple functional MRI approaches simultaneously results in a higher economic burden and is time-consuming; furthermore, some patients may be intolerant of the prolonged scanning, necessitating the selection of optimal sequences for clinical application.

Based on the above background, we proposed the following exploratory, testable hypothesis: Among multiparametric DWI MRI sequences (IVIM, DTI, DKI), DKI-derived parameters (especially MK) may have superior diagnostic performance for glomerulosclerosis (>25% vs ≤ 25%) and tubulointerstitial injury (>25% vs ≤ 25%) risk stratification in IgAN patients, with renal pathological lesions as the reference standard. The primary aim of this exploratory study was to preliminarily test this hypothesis by evaluating the utility of these three sequences for such risk stratification. By synchronously acquiring the sequences, comparing their diagnostic efficacy, and linking imaging parameters to clinical indicators, we aim to explore the potential optimal non-invasive MRI sequence for IgAN pathological assessment, provide preliminary insights into addressing prior study limitations, and offer initial clinical evidence for non-invasive IgAN progression monitoring.

Methods

Study design

Selection and description of participants

A total of 95 patients with CKD aged ≥ 18 years who were hospitalized in the Department of Nephrology from March 2018 to September 2023 were prospectively selected. Additionally, 10 volunteers with normal renal function (7 males and 3 females, with an average age of 47 years) were recruited as controls. Inclusion criteria: Patients ≥ 18 years old who fulfilled renal biopsy indications and were scheduled for renal biopsy, and who voluntarily participated in this clinical study. Exclusion criteria: the presence of MRI-incompatible devices, claustrophobia, or pregnancy; acute infection, trauma, or any major surgery or intervention (excluding the scheduled renal biopsy) within two weeks prior to MRI examination; psychosis or refusal; and other serious medical conditions such as coronary heart disease, emphysema, asthma, or other serious medical disease or the inability to tolerate breath-holding owing to other reasons. We selected 31 patients with IgA nephropathy for analysis (Fig. 1). The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital, with the ethics number 2018BJYYEC-058–01, and written informed consent was obtained from all patients. And this study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (registration number: ChiCTR1800020390, the registration date is December 28, 2018).

The participant flow diagram of the study

General information

Participant characteristics, including age, sex, height, and weight, were recorded. In addition, detailed medical history was obtained, including smoker status (patients who had stopped smoking for >5 years were classed as non-smokers), diabetes mellitus, hypertension (including primary and renal hypertension), cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases, and dyslipidemia, etc.

Biochemical analyses

Venous blood samples were collected in a fasting state, and serum creatinine levels were measured with the enzymatic isotope dilution mass-spectrometry traceable standardized method (Cobas C501 Biochemical Analyzer; Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany). Serum concentrations of uric acid, albumin, and 24-hour urine protein quantification (24-h UPRO) were measured using the serum sample, while hemoglobin was measured using the whole blood sample according to standard methods at the hospital’s central laboratory.

24-h UPRO is the gold standard for measuring urinary protein excretion. It involves collecting all urine produced over 24 hours to determine the total amount of protein excreted daily. Normal protein excretion is <150 mg/day; values above this indicate proteinuria, which is a critical marker for kidney disease progression.

Assessment of kidney function

Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation, for the White or other [17]:

For women:eGFR = 141×min(Scr/0.7,1)−0.329×max(Scr/0.7,1)−1.209×0.993age × 1.018

For men:eGFR = 141×min(Scr/0.9,1)−0.411×max(Scr/0.9,1)−1.209×0.993age

MRI examinations

MRI examinations were performed on a 3.0-Tesla MRI scanner (GE Signa Discovery 750 HD; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA), with a standard 8-channel phase array upper torso surface coil (GE Healthcare). Conventional sequences included axial LAVA Flex T1-weighted imaging and axial and coronal fat-suppression fast spin echo T2-weighted imaging, but these were not assessed as part of this study. The subsequent functional sequences, including IVIM, DTI, and DKI, were detected. Detailed MRI protocols of functional sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1. All patients underwent a standardized hydration protocol before their MRI examinations: on the day of examination, the patient drank 3 mL/kg of water at 8 a.m., then 1 mL/kg of water at 9 a.m., 10 a.m., 11 a.m., and 12 p.m., respectively, before undergoing MRI at 12:10 p.m. The control group was subjected to the same MRI protocols as the CKD patients.

Imaging analysis

All imaging data were processed on an Advantage Windows Workstation (version 4.4; GE Healthcare) using Functool software, which performs a voxel-by-voxel analysis and can automatically output the values per voxel from each region-of-interest (ROI) measurement. The metrics of IVIM, DTI, and DKI were automatically calculated and displayed on corresponding maps (Fig. 2).

And fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (Da), and radial diffusivity (Dr) were obtained from the DTI sequence. MK, Ka, and Kr were derived from the DKI approach. Images were accessed by a radiologist with 15 years of experience who was blinded to the clinical and pathological results. For the IVIM, DTI, and DKI images, three similar ROIs of 30–50 mm2 were positioned at the anterior, middle, and posterior parts of the renal medulla on the central slice of a kidney. The ROIs of the renal cortex were drawn by hand with a free curve and a mean size of 150–250 mm2. The IVIM, DTI, and DKI values of the split renal cortex and medulla were calculated by separately averaging the three ROIs for each of the renal cortex and medulla, whereas the total renal cortex and medulla parameters were calculated by averaging the six ROIs for the renal cortex and medulla.

Renal histopathological analysis

Renal biopsy tissues taken from all participants underwent standard histopathological processing. Pathologic diagnoses and scoring were performed by two experienced pathologists who were blinded to the clinical and multiparametric diffusion weighted imaging MRI data. The renal pathologic score included the glomerulosclerosis index (GSI) and tubulointerstitial fibrosis index (TBI) (Supplementary Figure S1). All stained slides used for renal histopathological analyses by light microscopy (×400 magnification) were digitally scanned using a digital pathology platform (Eclipse Ni; Nikon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The GSI was determined in all glomeruli from each renal biopsy specimen on slides stained with periodic acid–Schiff stain under an optical microscope; a semi-quantitative scoring system with a range of 0–4 was used (0, normal; 1, 1–25% glomerular area affected; 2, 25–50% affected; 3, 50–75% affected; 4, 75–100% affected), and the results were averaged [18]. The TBI was assessed under the light microscope following Masson’s trichrome staining, with the following criteria: the Oxford MEST-C scoring system was used to evaluate interstitial injury, and the renal tubulointerstitial fields were observed continuously under an optical microscope (at 400x magnification) using a semi-quantitative scoring method from 1 to 3 (scores of 1, 2, and 3 corresponded to ≤25%, 26–50%, and >50% tubulointerstitial injury, respectively), with the results averaged subsequently [19].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 20.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of raw data was assessed with the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally and non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and median values with 25th and 75th percentiles, respectively. Interobserver repeatability in measurements of diffusion parameters was evaluated by using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) (range between 0 and 1) with 95% confidence interval (CI). The MRI parameters between the left and right kidneys were compared by the Wilcoxon paired rank-sum test. Differences between groups were evaluated with Student’s t test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Spearman’s or Pearson’s method was used to analyze the correlation between eGFR, histopathological score, and renal parenchymal MRI parameters, respectively. For all analyses, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Univariate analysis: To evaluate the association between individual predictors and the outcome, we performed univariate logistic regression using Firth’s penalized maximum likelihood method (logistf in R) to address potential issues of separation in small samples. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for each variable.

Multivariate analysis and model building: significant predictors from univariate analyses, together with established confounders (age, gender, eGFR, hypertension), were included in multivariate Firth logistic regression models. A backward elimination procedure (p > 0.1 for removal) was applied to select the most parsimonious model. Final models were refitted using Firth logistic regression, and coefficients (β), ORs, 95% CIs, and p-values were reported. Univariate analysis, Multivariate analysis, and model building were conducted in R (version 4.3.1). Data preprocessing and visualization were performed using the tidyverse package (version 2.0.0). Firth logistic regression was implemented with logistf (version 1.26.1). Model performance metrics and internal validation were performed using rms (version 6.3.0) and pROC (version1.18.5).

Analysis of MRI parameters

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test revealed no significant differences in the MRI sequence parameters between the left and right kidneys of the enrolled patients; consequently, the mean values of the bilateral kidneys were used for subsequent analyses. Consistency testing showed that the intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) of the renal cortical ADC, D, FA, MD, Da, Dr, MK, Ka, and Kr values ranged from 0.860 to 0.985, and for the renal medulla, they ranged from 0.860 to 0.948 (Supplementary Table S2). The cortical ADC, D, MD, Da, and Dr values were significantly higher, and the FA and Ka values were significantly lower compared with those of the renal medulla (p < 0.05).

Comparisons of MRI metrics between patients with IgAN and the control group

The 31 participants were divided into mild and moderate-to-severe damage groups according to the GSI (mild group: 0–1, moderate-to-severe group: 2–4) and TBI score (mild group: 0–1, moderate-to-severe group: 2–3), respectively. Compared with the control group, the GSI moderate-to-severe group showed significantly reduced ADC (medulla), D (medulla and cortex), MD (medulla and cortex), Da (medulla and cortex), and FA (medulla and cortex), while Ka (cortex and medulla) was significantly increased, and there was a trend for the cortical MK to be higher than in the normal control group (p = 0.065). The TBI moderate-to-severe damage group showed significantly lower ADC (medulla), D (medulla), MD (medulla and cortex), Da (medulla and cortex), and FA (medulla and cortex), higher MK (cortex), and Ka (medulla and cortex) compared to the control group. There were no significant differences in all MRI parameters between the mild damage (GSI and TBI) groups and the control group. The details are shown in Supplementary Table S3.

Relationships between the basic eGFR, MRI parameters, renal GSI, and TBI

Correlation analysis showed that GSI was negatively correlated with eGFR, ADC (cortex and medulla), D (cortex), MD (cortex and medulla), Da (cortex and medulla), and FA (medulla), and positively correlated with Ka (cortex and medulla) and MK (cortex). Similarly, TBI was negatively correlated with eGFR, ADC (medulla), D (cortex), MD (cortex and medulla), Da (cortex and medulla), and FA (medulla), and positively correlated with Ka (cortex and medulla) and MK (cortex). Clinical and MRI (DKI/DTI) metrics for predicting moderate-to-severe GSI and TBI

Univariate Firth logistic regression was performed to address small-sample separation and identify predictors of moderate-to-severe GSI and TBI (Supplementary Table S4). For moderate-to-severe GSI, significant predictors included eGFR, Medulla Da, Cortex D, Medulla FA,Cortex MK, Medulla Ka, and Cortex Ka (p < 0.05). For moderate-to-severe TBI, significant predictors included eGFR, Medulla Da, Medulla FA, Cortex Da, Medulla MK, Cortex MK, Medulla Ka, and Cortex Ka (p < 0.05).

Significant univariate predictors plus established clinical confounders (age, gender, eGFR, hypertension) were included in DKI- and DTI-based multivariable Firth logistic regression models for moderate-to-severe GSI and TBI (Table 2). For moderate-to-severe TBI, eGFR was the only significant predictor in the DKI-based model (p = 0.034), and showed a non-significant protective trend in the DTI-based model (p = 0.070), with no DKI/DTI MRI metrics achieving significance and no incremental predictive value from imaging; thus, no further model construction was performed for TBI. For moderate-to-severe GSI, eGFR was the only significant predictor in the DKI-based model (p = 0.023), while Medulla FA emerged as the significant imaging predictor in the DTI-based model (p = 0.013). All other clinical confounders and non-target imaging metrics were non-significant in the respective GSI models (p > 0.05), justifying the development of parsimonious GSI models via backward elimination.

Discussion

In the present study, using renal biopsy pathology as the standard, we conducted IVIM, DTI, and DKI for the assessment of renal histopathological injuries in 31 patients with IgAN, and constructed clinical-imaging combined models to evaluate the diagnostic value of each sequence for moderate-to-severe renal pathological damage. Our findings suggest that DTI and DKI may provide useful non-invasive information for evaluating renal function and stratifying the severity of renal pathological damage in patients with IgAN, whereas the clinical value of IVIM appears limited in this population. These preliminary observations may help inform the selection of non-invasive MRI sequences for IgAN pathological assessment.

Kidney is a well-perfused organ; parameters reflecting the diffusion of water molecules, including ADC, D, MD, Da, and Dr, have significantly higher values in the renal cortex than in the renal medulla, which indicates that there was more free water molecule diffusion in the renal cortex compared with the medulla. Furthermore, the motion of water molecules in the kidney is influenced by the components of renal tissues, we found that ADC, D, MD, Da, and Dr, were positively correlated with eGFR, and ADC, D, MD, and Da were negatively related to renal pathological damage, and this could potentially be explained by the renal histopathological changes-including tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and glomerular sclerosis-limiting the movement of water molecules [10].

The microscopic motion of water protons in the kidneys includes a fast diffusion component attributed to microvascular perfusion, glomerular filtration, tubular flow, and a slow diffusion component secondary to true water diffusion [20]. Low b-values render a greater contribution to the ADC of the rapid diffusion component, while high b-values give greater weight to the slow diffusion component [21]. It is suggested to use b-values exceeding 200 m/mm2 to assess the true water diffusion in the kidney. IVIM, a unique type of bi-exponential imaging, was proposed to distinguish between estimated fast diffusion and slow diffusion. The application of IVIM in the evaluation of renal function and pathological injury has been demonstrated in renal transplant patients and CKD patients [16, 18]. In our study, the IVIM sequence included 8 b-values and 3 orthogonal directions, and we found that cortical ADC and D were positively correlated with eGFR and negatively correlated with histopathological scores, which was congruent with the results of Sułkowska et al. [14]. However, IVIM parameters did not yield significant predictive value in the predictive models, the possible reasons include: first, the hydration status of individual patients may affect the microperfusion component of the kidney, thus interfering with the IVIM parameter measurement; second, the pathological damage of IgAN is a diffuse lesion involving glomeruli and tubulointerstitium, and the single bi-exponential model of IVIM cannot fully reflect the complex tissue microstructure changes; third, the b-value setting and post-processing method of IVIM in this study may need further optimization for the IgAN population. It should be noted that the lack of predictive value of IVIM in this study is specific to our cohort and protocol, and cannot be generalized to all IgAN populations or IVIM applications

The preferential diffusion or diffusion anisotropy of water molecules in certain directions of biological tissues reflects the microstructure of the tissue. Renal lesions exhibit diffuse damage in patients with CKD, and the arrangement of the renal medulla is anisotropic. The three orthogonal directions of IVIM cannot fully and accurately describe the diffusion of water molecules in the kidney; DTI can reflect the diffusion of water molecules in various directions and accurately detect the diffusion anisotropy of water molecules in each voxel [15]. The derived parameter FA reflects the anisotropic diffusion of water molecules in the tissues. Previous studies have observed that renal parenchymal FA was valuable for assessing renal function and pathological injuries in patients with CKD [22, 23]. In our study, DTI used 30 independent diffusion gradient directions, showed well performance in the assessment of IgAN pathological damage: medullary FA was identified as a significant imaging predictor for moderate-to-severe GSI in the DTI-based multivariate model (p = 0.013), and the DTI-only model had the same diagnostic efficacy as the clinical model for moderate-to-severe GSI (AUC = 0.882). After combining with clinical factors, the diagnostic efficacy was further improved (AUC = 0.932), with optimism-corrected AUC (0.858) and goodness of fit (Hosmer-Lemeshow p = 0.628). Similarly, Li et al. found medullary FA was closely related to tubular atrophy and interstitial fibrosis in renal transplant patients [24]. In addition, Saini et al. also observed a significant correlation between medullary FA and renal function in patients with diabetic nephropathy [25]. In particular, our findings demonstrate that medullary FA exhibits superior sensitivity compared to cortical FA in detecting renal dysfunction and pathological injury, which is closely related to the pathological characteristics of IgAN and the microstructure of the renal medulla. Although glomerulosclerosis represents the primary insult, downstream tubulointerstitial injury inevitably develops through tubuloglomerular feedback and hemodynamic disturbances, establishing a pathological cascade of glomerulosclerosis, tubular atrophy, and interstitial fibrosis. The highly anisotropic microstructure of the renal medulla amplifies these microstructural alterations, rendering medullary FA particularly sensitive to disease progression. In contrast, glomeruli reside in the cortex as nearly spherical isotropic structures, so cortical FA remains relatively stable despite glomerular sclerosis. Furthermore, the cortical microstructure contains a complex intermixture of glomeruli, proximal tubules, and vasculature that yields inherently lower signal-to-noise ratios for diffusion tensor measurements. These observations suggest that medullary FA may serve as a surrogate marker of overall renal pathological severity.

DKI, as an extension of DWI technology, the derived parameters Ka, Kr and MK represent the axial, radial, and mean diffusion kurtosis of water molecules, which reflect the non-Gaussian nature of water molecule diffusion. Renal fibrosis raises additional obstacles against the diffusion of water molecules, thus increasing the diffuse kurtosis in patients with CKD [16]. In this study, cortical MK and cortical/medullary Ka were significantly positively correlated with GSI and TBI, and were significant predictors in the univariate logistic regression for moderate-to-severe GSI/TBI, which is consistent with the previous research results [26, 27]. Liu et al. have confirmed that MK performed effectively in detecting renal fibrosis and renal function in IgAN patients [26], and Mao W et al. also found that MK is closely related to eGFR and renal fibrosis in patients with CKD [27]. The DKI-only model had a certain diagnostic efficacy for moderate-to-severe GSI (AUC = 0.764), and the clinical+DKI combined model further improved the diagnostic ability (AUC = 0.891), with good optimism-corrected (AUC = 0.801) and goodness of fit (Hosmer-Lemeshow p = 0.284). In the DKI-based multivariate model, eGFR was the only significant predictor (p = 0.023), while kurtosis parameters lost their statistical significance, which may be due to the strong correlation between eGFR and kurtosis parameters, and eGFR, as a classic clinical index for evaluating renal function, has a strong predictive ability for pathological damage, thus covering the predictive value of kurtosis parameters to a certain extent. This finding suggests that DKI parameters may not provide incremental value beyond eGFR in predicting moderate-to-severe GSI in this cohort, but this may vary in larger or more heterogeneous populations.

Notably, our results were not fully consistent with our predefined exploratory hypothesis, which proposed DKI-derived parameters (especially MK) would have superior diagnostic performance for GSI and TBI risk stratification in IgAN patients. Instead, DTI-derived medullary FA was a significant imaging predictor for moderate-to-severe GSI (p = 0.013). We analyzed three potential reasons for this discrepancy: First, the strong correlation between eGFR and DKI parameters (MK, Ka) may have masked MK’s independent predictive value. Second, the small sample size limited statistical power, potentially leading to unstable DKI parameter estimates and obscuring MK’s true superiority. Third, IgAN’s unique pathology explains medullary FA’s superiority,in the anisotropic renal medulla where DTI (30 diffusion directions) is more sensitive to microstructural changes than DKI. Our cohort’s median GSI of 1.63 (moderate injury) may not have fully manifested the non-Gaussian diffusion that MK detects, suggesting DTI/DKI utility may varies with pathological severity.

This study had several important limitations that should be fully acknowledged and considered when interpreting the results, and which limit the generalizability of our findings. First, the investigation relied on a relatively small sample size, which limits the statistical power and increases the risk of model overfitting, as suggested by the optimism-corrected AUC. Second, because numerous MRI parameters, renal regions, correlation analyses, and group comparisons were performed without correction for multiple comparisons, all correlation and groupwise results should be interpreted as exploratory and with caution due to the potential for type I error inflation. Therefore, the models and correlations identified here require robust validation in larger, independent cohorts. Third, although all patients followed a standardized hydration protocol, we could not fully control for individual variations in hydration status, particularly in patients with nephrotic syndrome and edema. This could be a potential confounding factor for perfusion-sensitive MRI parameters. Fourth, the ROI measurement does not match the kidney biopsy points, which may result in bias. Nonetheless, the bias was controllable as the diffuse nephropathy could affect the entire kidney of CKD patients. Finally, this study focused exclusively on patients with IgAN; therefore, the diagnostic accuracy and optimal MRI parameters identified in this study may not be directly generalizable to other forms of chronic kidney disease. Future multicenter studies with larger, diverse CKD populations are needed to validate these multiparametric MRI techniques, address these limitations, and clarify DTI/DKI’s incremental value over conventional clinical indicators.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this preliminary exploratory study suggests that DTI and DKI may provide useful non-invasive information for assessing renal histopathological alterations in IgAN patients. Medullary FA (DTI) represents a promising imaging biomarker for moderate-to-severe glomerulosclerosis, and its combination with clinical indicators improves risk stratification. The originally hypothesized superiority of DKI-derived MK was not clearly demonstrated. These preliminary findings highlight the potential of advanced diffusion MRI techniques and warrant further validation in larger, multicenter prospective studies.

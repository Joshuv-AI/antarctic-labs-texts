Recurrence of hydatid cyst post thoracoscopic excision
Authors: Urvashi S. Paliwal, Tejas R. Shah, Bijal H Prajapati, Umang C. Shah, Arpan C. Shah
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Hydatosis, an infection caused by the larvae of Echinococcus, is a parasitic disease primarily affecting the liver and lungs. The larvae enter the body through ingestion, break through the intestinal wall, and circulate via the bloodstream to form cysts in the various organs. Pulmonary involvement can lead to the symptoms like persistent cough, chest pain, and difficulty breathing, while hepatic cysts, often cause pain in the right upper abdomen, nausea, jaundice, and enlargement of the liver. If cysts rupture, severe allergic reactions or infections may occur. This report focuses on a rare case of recurring hydatid cysts in a 27-year-old woman from India, who had previously undergone thoracoscopic adhesiolysis.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

The patient, a 27-year-old woman, presented with swelling on her right back, accompanied by a dry cough, chest pain, intermittent fever, and vomiting.[1] She had a history of right-sided pleural effusion and multi-septate cysts in the liver, with one cyst extending into the pleural space. Six months earlier [Figure 1], she had undergone thoracoscopic adhesiolysis and was prescribed Albendazole (400 mg BD).[2] Physical examination revealed dullness and reduced breath sounds in the right chest, along with a palpable, immobile swelling on the right side of her back. Computed Tomography (CT) imaging of the chest showed multiple hydatid cysts of various sizes in the right lung, mediastinum, liver, and subphrenic region. A prominent cyst in the right liver lobe appeared to communicate with a cyst in the posterior basal lung through the diaphragm.

CECT CHEST

The patient was scheduled for the surgical removal of hydatid cysts from both the right lung (apical and lower lobe segments) and the liver.[3] Open surgery was preferred over minimally invasive techniques due to the cyst size and anatomical location [Figure 2].[4] The procedure was successful, with no complications, and the intercostal chest drain was removed once lung expansion was confirmed.

Histopathological examination of the cysts confirmed their recurrent nature, showing the distinctive chitinous wall typical of hydatid cysts.

Following surgery, the patient was put on Albendazole (400 mg BD) and Praziquantel (1200 mg BD) for 3 months to prevent recurrence. Her recovery was uneventful, and she was discharged in stable condition.

DISCUSSION

Hydatid disease primarily affects the liver and lungs.[5] Pulmonary cysts are often asymptomatic until they result in symptoms such as chest pain or persistent cough. Accurate diagnosis requires imaging (e.g., CT, X-ray) and serological tests (such as ELISA). Surgical removal of the cysts is essential to prevent rupture, complications, or recurrence. Several techniques, including cystotomy, capitonnage, and peri-cystectomy, are utilized based on the cyst’s size and location. While minimally invasive methods like Video Assisted Thoracospic Surgery (VATS) are suitable for smaller, peripheral cysts, open surgery is typically necessary for larger or centrally located cysts to ensure complete removal and avoid complications.

This case emphasizes the possibility of recurrent hydatid cysts even after thoracoscopic surgery, stressing the importance of thorough cyst removal to prevent relapse.

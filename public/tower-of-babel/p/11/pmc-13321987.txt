Synergy of a Low-methionine Diet and Reduced-dose Cisplatinum Eradicates Experimental Lung-cancer Bone Metastases in a Nude-mouse Model
Authors: YOHEI ASANO, KOHEI MIZUTA, YUTA MIYASHI, QINGHONG HAN, SHUKUAN LI, BYUNG MO KANG, JIN SOO KIM, NORIO YAMAMOTO, KATSUHIRO HAYASHI, HIROAKI KIMURA, SHINJI MIWA, KENTARO IGARASHI, TAKASHI HIGUCHI, SEI MORINAGA, HIROYUKI TSUCHIYA, SATORU DEMURA, ROBERT M. HOFFMAN
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimBone metastasis of lung cancer is highly recalcitrant, and current treatment is palliative. Methionine addiction is a fundamental hallmark of cancer, known as the Hoffman effect, and is targeted by methionine restriction (MR). The present study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of MR combined with low-dose cisplatinum on an experimental nude-mouse model of bone metastasis of lung cancer.Materials and MethodsThe A549 human lung-adenocarcinoma cell line was used in the present study. An experimental bone-metastasis model was established by implanting A549 cells into the tibia of nude mice. The mice were then randomly assigned to four groups: untreated control; standard-dose cisplatinum (6 mg/kg, intraperitoneally, weekly); combination of low-dose cisplatinum (3 mg/kg, intraperitoneally, weekly) and an MR diet; and an MR diet only. Tumor growth and body weight were monitored during the 3-week treatment period.ResultsThe combination of low-dose cisplatinum and an MR diet had the most tumor inhibition efficacy of all treatment groups, with tumors essentially being eradicated by the end of the treatment period. The combination of MR and low-dose cisplatinum showed superior efficacy compared to standard-dose cisplatinum. Furthermore, weight loss was observed only in the mice treated with standard-dose cisplatinum, whereas mice treated with the combination of low-dose cisplatinum and an MR diet maintained body weight comparable to that of the MR-alone group.ConclusionMR enhanced the efficacy of low-dose cisplatinum, while mitigating treatment-associated toxicity in a lung-cancer experimental bone-metastasis model. The present findings suggest that metabolic targeting of methionine addiction is a promising therapeutic strategy enabling effective chemotherapy dose reduction in bone-metastatic lung cancer.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide (1). In particular, bone metastasis is recalcitrant and has a poor prognosis (2, 3), for which treatment is palliative. Despite recent advances in molecular-targeted therapies and immune checkpoint inhibitors, improvements in the prognosis of patients with bone metastases remain limited. Furthermore, with the aging of the global cancer population, the number of elderly patients presenting with lung-cancer bone metastases is expected to increase (4). Therefore, therapeutic strategies that can enhance antitumor efficacy while reducing treatment-related toxicity are urgently needed for lung-cancer bone metastases.

Methionine addiction is a fundamental metabolic hallmark of cancer termed the Hoffman effect (5-11). Methionine restriction (MR) enhances the efficacy of chemotherapy and suppresses tumor progression in numerous primary and metastatic tumor models (12-16). A combination of MR and ultra-low-dose cisplatinum was shown to be effective against lung cancer cells in vitro and in vivo (17).

The present study aimed to investigate the potential synergistic efficacy of combining MR with low-dose cisplatinum on an experimental lung-cancer bone-metastasis model established by intra-tibial implantation of A549 lung-cancer cells into nude mice.

Materials and Methods

Cell culture and reagents. The human lung-adenocarcinoma cell line A549 was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 μg/ml streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

MR diet and cisplatinum. The MR diet (TD.90262) was obtained from Inotiv, Inc. (West Lafayette, IN, USA). Cisplatinum was obtained from WG Critical Care, LLC (Paramus, NJ, USA).

Mice. Five- to six-week-old athymic nu/nu nude mice were obtained from AntiCancer Inc. (San Diego, CA, USA) and maintained in a barrier facility on high-efficiency particulate air-filtered racks under standard conditions with a 12-h light/dark cycle and free access to food and water.

All experimental procedures were approved by the AntiCancer Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (approval number: 061944-25) and were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (18). The study design and reporting complied with the ARRIVE guidelines version 2.0 (19).

To minimize animal discomfort, all procedures were performed under anesthesia using a mixture of ketamine (20 mg/kg), xylazine (15.2 mg/kg), and acepromazine maleate (0.48 mg/kg), administered at a dose of 0.02 ml per mouse.

Establishment of lung-cancer bone-metastasis model. An experimental model of lung-cancer bone metastasis was established by intratibial implantation of A549 cells. Briefly, a hole was created in the right tibial tuberosity using an 18-gauge needle, followed by curettage of the medullary cavity. Cultured A549 cells (3 × 106 cells) were suspended in 100 μl of Matrigel, and 5 μL of the cell suspension was injected into the tibial medullary cavity (Figure 1) (20, 21). After injection, the injection site was compressed with sterile cotton to prevent leakage, and the skin was closed with 5-0 nylon sutures.

Procedure for establishing an experimental nude-mouse model of lung-cancer bone metastasis. A hole was created in the right tibial tuberosity of nude mice using an 18-gauge needle, followed by curettage of the medullary cavity. Cultured A549 cells (3×10⁶) suspended in 100 μl of Matrigel were prepared, and 5 μl of the suspension was injected into the tibial medullary cavity.

Treatment protocol. Two weeks after tumor implantation, 24 mice with established tumors were randomly assigned to four treatment groups (n=6 per group) and treated for 3 weeks as follows (Figure 2):

Treatment schema. Group 1: Weekly intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection with vehicle (phosphate-buffered saline), with a normal diet. Group 2: Treated weekly i.p with 6 mg/kg cisplatinum (CDDP), with a normal diet. Group 3: Treated weekly i.p with 3 mg/kg CDDP combined with a methioninerestricted (MR) diet. Group 4: Treated with the MR diet only. All mice were sacrificed 21 days after the start of treatment.

1) Control: Normal diet with vehicle (phosphate-buffered saline, intraperitoneal injection weekly).

2) Standard-dose cisplatinum: Normal diet with cisplatinum injected intraperitoneally, weekly at 6 mg/kg;

3) Low-dose cisplatinum + MR diet: MR diet combined with low-dose cisplatinum injected intraperitoneally, weekly at 3 mg/kg;

4) MR diet alone.

Tumor size was measured twice weekly using calipers, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: tumor volume (mm3)=(long axis × short axis2)/2. For relative tumor volume analysis, tumor volume at each time point was normalized to the initial tumor volume measured at the start of treatment (defined as 1). Body weight was also recorded twice weekly as an indicator of systemic toxicity.

Statistical analysis. All data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean. Differences among groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Statistical analyses were performed using EZR software (Jichi Medical University, Saitama Medical Center, Saitama, Japan). A value of p≤0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Efficacy of standard-dose cisplatinum alone compared with MR combined with low-dose cisplatinum. Low-dose cisplatinum combined with an MR diet was the only treatment that significantly inhibited tumor growth compared with all other groups (all p<0.05). The tumors were essentially eradicated by the end of the treatment period (Figure 3). Standard-dose cisplatinum or the MR diet alone did not significantly inhibit tumor growth compared with the untreated control (p=0.09 and p=0.78, respectively). No significant difference in tumor growth was observed between the standard-dose cisplatinum treatment and the MR diet alone (p=0.98).

Effect of cisplatinum (CDDP) and methionine restriction (MR) on tumor growth in an experimental nude-mouse model of lung-cancer bone metastasis established using A549 cells. (A) Relative tumor volume over time after treatment initiation (day 1). Tumor volume at each time point was normalized to the volume at the start of treatment (defined as 1). (B) Representative images of tumors at the end of the treatment period. *Significantly different at p<0.05.

Body weight change due to treatment. Changes in body weight during the 3-week treatment period are summarized in Figure 4. No significant differences were observed between any of the treatment groups. Only the standard-dose cisplatinum reduced body weight at the end of treatment compared with baseline. In contrast, mice treated with low-dose cisplatinum combined with the MR diet had a body weight gain comparable to that observed in mice in the group receiving the MR diet alone. However, no significant changes in body weight from baseline were observed in any group.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that combining MR with low-dose cisplatinum was effective in a nude-mouse model of lung-cancer bone-metastasis, while mitigating treatment-related toxicity by reducing the cisplatinum dose. The present findings suggest that MR is a promising approach against lung-cancer bone metastasis.

Methionine addiction, also known as the Hoffman effect, is a fundamental and general metabolic hallmark of cancer (5-11). Targeting this metabolic vulnerability through MR inhibited tumor growth in all major types of malignancies in combination with other agents in vitro and in vivo (12-16, 22-26). Furthermore, MR has been reported to reduce the effective dose of cisplatinum in osteosarcoma, bladder cancer, and colon cancer models (17, 27-29). Recent studies further support the therapeutic relevance of methionine metabolism, demonstrating that methionine deprivation induces cancer cell death and alters methylation of cell-cycle-related genes, and that targeting methionine-dependent pathways enhances the cytotoxicity of DNA-damaging agents in other malignancies (30, 31).

To the best of our knowledge, the present study is the first to demonstrate that MR enhances the efficacy of low-dose cisplatinum in lung-cancer bone metastasis. The main limit of the study is only one lung-cancer cell line was tested.

Given that patients with lung adenocarcinoma and bone metastasis continue to have limited long-term survival despite advances in targeted therapies (32), adjunctive metabolic strategies such as MR may provide additional clinical benefit.

Restricting methionine induces a cancer-specific cell-cycle arrest in the late S/G2 phase (12, 33, 34). Because cisplatinum exerts its cytotoxic effects primarily during the same phase of the cell cycle (35), the observed synergistic antitumor activity is likely attributable to coordinated enhancement of cell-cycle-dependent DNA damage.

Future studies will evaluate the efficacy of MR in combination with other cytotoxic agents, molecular-targeted therapies, and immune checkpoint inhibitors. In addition, more comprehensive assessments of systemic and organ-specific toxicities, beyond body-weight changes, will be required to facilitate clinical translation. Patient-derived orthotopic xenograft mouse models of lung-cancer bone metastasis (36) will also be tested with MR and low dose cisplatinum. Methionine restriction is effective because it targets a fundamental and general hallmark of cancer (5-13, 22-26, 37-40).

Conclusion

MR enhanced the antitumor efficacy of low-dose cisplatinum, while mitigating treatment-associated toxicity in an experimental model of lung-cancer bone-metastasis. These findings suggest that targeting methionine addiction may enable effective chemotherapy dose-reduction and represent a promising therapeutic strategy for lung cancer with bone metastasis.

A unified time series classification framework via adaptive Gaussian image representation
Authors: Xinyue Ren, Duo Li, Xiang Gao, Wai Kin Victor Chan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Real-world time series classification (TSC) is frequently hindered by significant data heterogeneity, characterized by complex multivariate dependencies and variable sequence lengths. While image-based representations offer a promising direction, existing methods often rely on rigid, deterministic mappings that fail to adapt to diverse input structures or capture intrinsic statistical uncertainty. To address these challenges, we propose TS2Vision, a unified framework driven by Adaptive Time Series Gaussian Mapping (ATSGM). Unlike static encodings, ATSGM transforms temporal signals into 2D images by leveraging Gaussian statistics to robustly model local fluctuations and employing an adaptive “circle packing” optimization to dynamically arrange spatial layouts for multivariate channels. Theoretically, we prove that this mapping satisfies Lipschitz continuity, ensuring representation stability against input perturbations. We conduct a comprehensive evaluation on 158 datasets from the UCR and UEA archives. The results demonstrate that TS2Vision achieves Rank-1 consistency across both univariate and multivariate benchmarks, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines. By bridging sequence modeling with computer vision, our framework offers a robust and generalizable solution for intelligent decision-making systems.

Introduction

Time series data are foundational to data-driven decision-making process in diverse application domains, including healthcare monitoring1–5, financial forecasting6,7, and smart transportation systems8. However, as data collection sources become increasingly ubiquitous, the resulting time series exhibit significant heterogeneity. For instance, real-world data often manifest as multivariate sequences with complex inter-channel correlations and varying lengths due to irregular sampling or sensor anomalies. Accurately classifying such heterogeneous data requires robust representation learning capable of handling these intrinsic inconsistencies.

While many approaches have been proposed for time series classification (TSC)9–12, a central question is how to construct representations that are discriminative, unified, and stable. Traditional machine learning methods often rely on manual features derived from statistical13–15, frequency16–18 or shapelet19–21. Although interpretable, these approaches are limited by domain expertise and scalability. Deep learning models for TSC such as Convolution Neural Networks (CNNs)22–24, Transformers25,26 and Linear-based models27 offer automatic feature learning but often require extensive hyper-parameter tuning and exhibit limited generalization across datasets with varying sequence structures.

These limitations motivate a shift towards a representation-centric perspective, focusing on encoding time series prior to classification. Among various representations, transformation-based methods have emerged as promising strategies28,29, which reformulate time series into alternative data structures, such as graphs30–32, images33, to extract informative features. In particular, image-based representations have gained attention due to their compatibility with powerful computer vision models34,35. While existing image representation methods have shown promise, they lack a unified mechanism to handle the complexity of real-world data. Specifically, they rely on rigid mappings that struggle to adaptively encode multivariate dependencies without excessive dimensionality expansion and cannot naturally accommodate variable-length inputs. Moreover, most existing mappings are deterministic, failing to explicitly model the statistical uncertainty inherent in noisy temporal signals. Consequently, there is a lack of a theoretically grounded, adaptive framework capable of unifiedly encoding variable-length, multivariate time series (MTS) into robust image representations for advanced vision classifiers.

To address these challenges, we propose TS2Vision, a robust TSC framework driven by a novel Adaptive Time Series Gaussian Mapping (ATSGM). Unlike static encoding, ATSGM leverages the statistical properties of Gaussian distributions to robustly characterize temporal fluctuations. Crucially, it employs an adaptive spatial optimization strategy (modeled as a circle packing problem) to dynamically arrange multivariate channels, ensuring a unified representation regardless of variable dimensionality. Vision Transformer (ViT) is then adopted as the classifier to capture long-term dependency characteristics. This design enables a single image representation paradigm to accommodate univariate time series (UTS) and MTS, including sequences with unequal lengths. Overall, the contributions of this work can be summarized as follows: Adaptive Time Series Gaussian Mapping (ATSGM) module is proposed as a cornerstone of the framework for robust time series encoding to 2D images, with theoretical validation ensuring its robustness under data perturbations.The proposed TS2Vision is an end-to-end unified framework that delivers a unified image-based solution for both UTS classification (UTSC) and MTS classification (MTSC) with unequal lengths across different domains, indicating its inherent generalization ability.We validate the effectiveness of our approach based on all 158 public datasets from the UCR and UEA archives, demonstrating consistent and significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

Related work

Time series transformation methods

To enhance the expressiveness of time series, more and more transformation-based methods have been proposed. These approaches aim to reformulate time series into alternative structures such as symbols, graphs, frequency domains, or image to enable more effective feature extraction and classification.

Graph-based transformation method is a flexible framework to capture complex interrelationships and dependencies within a time series36,37. Techniques such as Visibility graph38,39, Recurrence network40, and Transition network41 are available for time series modeling. Under this framework, graph theory and network science can be adopted for further tasks but constructing a graph is computationally expensive, especially for long time series data. Moreover, symbolic sequence representation aims to simplify continuous time series data into discrete symbols based on predefined rules. A Method like Symbolic Aggregation approXimation (SAX)42 is proposed for representation, which allows the utilization of symbolic analysis, but it will inevitably lose detailed information and the selection of the parameters is subjective. In the meantime, numerical transformation includes Fourier Transform43, Wavelet Transform44–46, etc. endeavor to execute mathematical operations for spectral component capturing or features from different scales, but the estimation and selection of suitable transformation functions can also be subjective.

In addition to the above methods, image-based representation has gained popularity in recent years with the development of computer vision33,47. Existing image-encoder methods48–50 for time series include Gramian Angular Field (GAF), Markov Transition Field (MTF), Recurrence Plots (RP), Relative Position Matrix (RPM) etc. Phase relationships, recurrence patterns, and frequency-related features can be captured through current techniques. While classical time series image representation methods have pioneered visual representations for time series, they typically rely on predefined pairwise transformations that generate fixed \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N \times N$$\end{document} matrices. Furthermore, existing approaches often treat UTS and MTS as isolated modeling scenarios. Despite their promise, most of these representations are designed for fixed-length series, and lack scalability to variable-length inputs. These limitations motivate the need for a more flexible and generalizable representation that can effectively encode multivariate, variable-length time series with a competitive performance for downstream tasks.

Image classification

A key motivation for image-based time series representation is the opportunity to leverage advances in computer vision for feature extraction and classification. Deep learning has revolutionized image classification, with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) achieving remarkable success through local receptive fields and hierarchical feature extraction. Representative models such as ResNet23 has been widely applied to structured data including time series images48.

When it comes to image classification, various deep learning architectures have emerged as state-of-the-art models for image classification47,51. Existing architectures can be concluded into 2 categories: Convolutional Neural Networks(CNNs)48,52,53 based models and Transformer based models54. CNNs have revolutionized this field, achieving remarkable results by effectively capturing local spatial dependencies through convolutional layers and hierarchical features via pooling and stacking operations, of which ResNet is a typical model of CNN-based models. In recent years, attention mechanisms have gained attention in image classification research. After that, the emergence of ViT from Google proposed in 2021 indicates that the transformer-based models have officially entered the field of image classification. ViTs have shown impressive results on large-scale image benchmarks and offer strong theoretical alignment with temporal data modeling due to their long-term dependency modeling capability. In this work, we bridge this gap by transforming time series into image representations for ViT, allowing for end-to-end learning to classification.

Data preprocessing

Data preprocessing plays a critical role in preparing the time series data for classification tasks. In this framework, the data preprocessing involves two techniques, which are standardization and resizing. For time series data of each channel in MTS, standardization is first conducted separately to align data to a common scale and distribution so as to ensure different time series from different channels are comparable, as indicated in Eq. 1.1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} S^{n}_{d,t} = \frac{X^{n}_{d,t}-\mu _{X^{n}_{d}}}{\sigma _{X^{n}_{d}}} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma$$\end{document} are the mean and standard deviation of the time series, respectively.

After that, to handle unequal sequence lengths, we utilize cubic interpolation to resize all time series to a unified length L (set to 196 in this study). Compared to linear methods, cubic interpolation utilizes smooth polynomials, providing a more accurate approximation for complex temporal variations.

Adaptive time series gaussian mapping (ATSGM)

ATSGM is a crucial component of our proposed framework for time series image representation, which transforms the preprocessed 1D time series \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S \in \mathbb {R}^{D \times L}$$\end{document} into a 2D image by leveraging the statistical properties of Gaussian distributions. This process ensures that the inherent uncertainty and fluctuations of the time series are robustly preserved. The module operates in two integrated stages: constructing the local Gaussian representation and adaptively optimizing the spatial configuration.

Classification model

Following ATSGM, we adopt Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) as the classifier. ViT is selected for its ability to model long-range dependencies through the multi-head self-attention mechanism. The generated time-series images are divided into sequences of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$16 \times 16$$\end{document} patches, which are linearly projected and processed by Transformer encoders to predict the final class.

Baselines

To facilitate a comprehensive evaluation of our proposed algorithm, we conducted extensive experiments encompassing all representative models feasible for both UTS and MTS across current four classification paradigms. Specifically, we chose DLinear27 for linear-based models, InceptionTime22 and ResNet23 for CNN-based models, and TimesNet60 and Informer26 for Transformer-based models. In addition, to show the effectiveness of the ATSGM of our framework, we also experimented to replace our following classifier from ViT to ResNet to find out the performance of the current two typical classification architectures from computer vision.

Implementation

We adopt the standard ViT-B/1654 architecture as the backbone classifier. Consistent with the patch configuration described in Sect. 2, the patch size is set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$16 \times 16$$\end{document} pixels. Consequently, to fit the input requirements of ViT, all time series are resized to a unified length of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L=196$$\end{document} via cubic interpolation, resulting in a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$14 \times 14$$\end{document} grid of patches (totaling 196 patches) for the final image representation 1. The Gaussian mapping range is effectively truncated at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[-3\sigma , +3\sigma ]$$\end{document} based on the adaptive strategy, ensuring numerical stability.

To ensure a fair and rigorous comparison, all baseline models were implemented using the optimal dataset-specific hyperparameter configurations and optimizer settings recommended in their respective original publications. For our proposed TS2Vision, we initialize the ViT backbone with weights pre-trained on the massive ImageNet-21k dataset. The TS2Vision framework is explicitly optimized using the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer with a base learning rate of 0.01 and a batch size of 16. The training process is conducted for 100 epochs.

Evaluation indicator

We report the average accuracy on the test set over 5 independent runs for each dataset to measure individual model performance. In addition, we report the mean rank and pairwise win/tie/loss counts for each algorithm to compare TS2Vision against baselines, where win, tie, and loss indicate outperforming, equivalent, and underperforming performance, respectively.

Ablation studies and parameter sensitivity

To disentangle the contributions of our encoding strategy, the classifier architecture, and the data preprocessing, we conduct a comprehensive ablation study to explore the effectiveness of the interpolation strategies, the ATSGM, and the model architectures.

To investigate the potential impact of time-series resizing on classification performance, we conduct an ablation study comparing different interpolation strategies. Specifically, we evaluate three commonly used approaches, which are nearest-neighbor, linear, and cubic interpolation across seven diverse datasets that include both univariate (UTS) and multivariate (MTS) time series with varying sequence lengths.

The results are presented in Fig. 5. Across all datasets, the overall performance differences between interpolation strategies remain relatively limited, indicating that the proposed framework is not overly sensitive to the specific resizing method. Notably, even when nearest-neighbor interpolation is used, which preserves the original piecewise structure without introducing additional smoothness, the model still maintains competitive accuracy. Nevertheless, cubic interpolation consistently achieves the best overall performance across most datasets. This result is expected, as cubic interpolation provides a smoother approximation of the temporal signal while preserving the underlying trend structure, thereby producing more stable spatial patterns for the subsequent ATSGM encoding. These observations suggest that while interpolation quality can influence performance to some extent, the effectiveness of TS2Vision primarily arises from the ATSGM representation rather than from the resizing strategy itself.Fig. 6Average accuracy of different image representation methods.

Average accuracy of different image representation methods.

To strictly isolate the source of performance gains and verify whether the improvements stem from the representation itself or the strong ViT classifier, we conducted a controlled comparative ablation. As shown in Fig. 6, multiple classical time series image representations including GAF, MTF, RP, and RPM were evaluated under the identical ViT backbone and training protocol. ATSGM consistently achieves the highest classification accuracy. Furthermore, when the ViT backbone is replaced by a standard CNN (i.e., ResNet), ATSGM continues to demonstrate significant improvements over baseline models. These findings collectively confirm that the performance gains fundamentally originate from the topological and representational advantages of ATSGM, while the advanced vision backbones serve to further amplify these inherent benefits.

Robustness analysis under noise perturbations

In Sect. 3.4, we established that the ATSGM mapping possesses a Lipschitz continuous representation property, implying a theoretically smooth transformation from 1D signals to 2D geometric densities. In this section, we further investigate how this mathematical smoothness relates to the robustness of the framework in real-world environments where sensor noise is common.

To empirically validate the practical relevance of the representation stability, we evaluate the framework’s robustness against input perturbations. Gaussian noise was systematically injected into the raw temporal signals across six diverse datasets spanning multiple domains, including power demand, motion tracking, audio sensing, and medical ECG. By embedding discrete temporal amplitudes into bounded 2D probability density fields through Gaussian kernels, ATSGM introduces a smoothing effect that reduces the amplification of high-frequency noise. Consequently, small temporal perturbations in the original signal are less likely to produce large structural distortions in the resulting spatial representation, providing a stable geometric prior for the downstream vision backbone.

Representation effectiveness via adaptive geometry

The superior performance of TS2Vision across 158 datasets validates the core hypothesis of this study, which is geometric adaptation is key to unified representation. Traditional transformation methods often fail on Multivariate Time Series (MTS) because they apply rigid mappings that ignore inter-channel relationships. In contrast, our ATSGM module leverages the “circle packing” mechanism to dynamically optimize the spatial topology of variables. This explains why TS2Vision achieves the “Rank-1 Consistency” in both UTS and MTS tasks . Furthermore, the theoretical guarantee of Lipschitz continuity ensures that small fluctuations in noisy sensor data (e.g., EEG/MEG) do not result in catastrophic shifts in the image space, providing the stability required for high-stakes decision-making.

Visual interpretability and transparency

Beyond accuracy, a critical advantage of TS2Vision is its inherent visual interpretability. As designed in the ATSGM, each spatial region corresponds to a specific variable, and pixel intensity directly reflects signal fluctuation strength . For instance, in healthcare monitoring, a clinician can visually identify an anomaly in a specific ECG lead by observing a high-intensity “hotspot” in the corresponding image region. This spatial transparency allows domain experts to validate model predictions against their medical knowledge, bridging the gap between AI performance and clinical trust.

Applicability and practical guidelines

As indicated in the scalability analysis, TS2Vision exhibits performance degradation in extremely low-data regimes. This behavior can be attributed to two interacting factors. First, transforming 1D sequences into 2D ATSGM images expands the representational space and increases feature flexibility. While this enables modeling richer spatial-temporal structures, it typically requires sufficient training data to learn stable statistical patterns. Second, the Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone has relatively weaker inductive biases compared to convolutional architectures and relies more heavily on data-driven self-attention mechanisms.

To further examine this issue, we analyze the MTS datasets where substituting the ViT backbone with a ResNet improves classification accuracy(see Supplementary material S4.). Among these datasets, 6 out of 8 have relatively small training sets (fewer than 300 samples). This empirical observation is consistent with the interpretation that CNNs’ built-in structural priors provide greater robustness in data-scarce settings. However, across the broader dataset archive, replacing ViT with ResNet leads to an overall performance reduction, suggesting a trade-off between data efficiency and the ability to model long-range temporal dependencies.

Consequently, TS2Vision is highly recommended for medium-to-large scale datasets, particularly in complex MTS tasks where explicitly mapping cross-channel correlations provides a decisive advantage. Conversely, in strict data-scarce scenarios or more straightforward univariate tasks, lightweight 1D algorithms without dimensionality expansion might be more practical.

Limitations and future work

Despite its robustness and effectiveness, this algorithm also has limitations. As indicated in the scalability analysis in Fig. 8, the Vision Transformer backbone is data-hungry. Performance gains are less pronounced on extremely small datasets (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$<300$$\end{document} samples) compared to lightweight CNNs.

Beyond the data-scale requirements, another limitation of this framework lies in its computational footprint which introduces challenges for millisecond-level real-time inference on resource-constrained edge devices. Future work will explore integrating advanced model compression strategies to mitigate this issue. Model compression techniques63,64 such as structured pruning to reduce redundant attention heads65, or low-rank tensor decomposition for the feed-forward networks66, hold significant potential.

Conclusion

In this work, we presented TS2Vision, a unified framework that redefines time series classification by bridging the gap between temporal sequence modeling and computer vision. Addressing the challenges of heterogeneity in real-world data, we introduced the Adaptive Time Series Gaussian Mapping (ATSGM), a novel mechanism that transforms diverse univariate and multivariate time series into theoretically grounded, Lipschitz-continuous image representations. Our comprehensive evaluation on 158 datasets demonstrates that TS2Vision achieves state-of-the-art performance, distinguished by its unique ability to maintain Rank-1 consistency across both univariate and multivariate benchmarks. By leveraging adaptive geometric optimization to capture complex inter-channel dependencies, our approach bridges the gap between sequence modeling and computer vision, offering a stable solution that paves the way for general-purpose data-driven decision support systems.

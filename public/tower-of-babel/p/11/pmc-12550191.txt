Impact of tea green leafhopper (Empoasca onukii) puncturing on the aroma profile of high-aroma oolong tea cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan
Authors: Baoyu Kong, Qiufang Zhu, Hang Wei, Qifu Liang, Mengzhu Shi, Ling Fang, Sun-an Yan, Jianwei Fu, Wenquan Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHigh-aroma Camellia sinensis cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan are usually used to make black tea and lightly fermented oolong tea. The Oolong tea produced has a rich, lingering aroma and high quality. However, tea green leafhoppers can significantly reduce both yield and quality. Therefore, this study investigated the impact of varying degrees of tea green leafhopper damage on the volatile compounds of Oolong tea which made from Jinguanyin and Jinmudan according to the heavy-fermentation beauty tea process. The volatile profiles were analyzed by GC–MS. Results indicated that medium degree of puncturing by tea green leafhoppers enhanced the formation and accumulation of volatiles in Oolong tea made from Jinguanyin. We also found that Linalool, Geraniol, Dihydrolinalool and (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid) were identified as key volatile compounds contributing to unique floral, fruity and sweet aroma of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan oolong tea. And the contents in JGYTM were the highest at 2749.98 μg/kg, 24751.63 μg/kg, 17131.17 μg/kg and 8172.35 μg/kg, respectively. The OAV values were 1833.32, 3300.22, 155.74 and 24,517.1, respectively. The content of geraniol in JMDTH was the highest at 22973.28 μg/kg, with an OAV value of 3063.1. The contents of linalool, dihydrolinalool and (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid) were the highest in JMDTL, at 2207.25 μg/kg, 10080.36 μg/kg and 7441.64 μg/kg, respectively, with OAV values of 1471.5, 91.64 and 22,324.9, respectively. Therefore, the medium and low levels of puncture caused by the tea green leafhoppers respectively impart unique floral and fruity scents as well as sweet aromas to the Jinguanyin and Jinmudan Oolong teas. This study provides technical reference for the cultivation and management of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan, and provides theoretical basis for the improvement of raw materials and aroma of Oriental beauty tea in oolong tea.Keywords: Tea green leafhopper, High-aroma, Oolong tea, Oriental beauty tea, Puncturing, Aroma, Influence

Introduction

Tea green leafhopper (Empoasca onukii (Matsuda)) (Hemiptera: Cicadae) is a major pest affecting tea production in China (Pu, Feng, & Shi, 2005), and is widely distributed in important tea producing areas of Fujian, Guangdong, Yunnan and Taiwan. Female tea green leafhoppers prefer to lay eggs in the tender buds and leaves of tea plants, and their nymphs and adults suck sap from fresh tea buds, causing yellowing and curled leaves of tea plants in the early stage of infestation. As the infestation intensifies, the leaves gradually turn brown, wilt and die, eventually being engulfed (Fu, Han, & Xiao, 2014; Jin, Chen, Backus, Sun, & Xiao, 2012), which seriously affects the yield and quality of tea leaves (Idris et al., 2020; Roy et al., 2016). In Datian County of Sanming, Fujian Province, China, there are also large areas of tea gardens that suffer from the harm of tea green leafhoppers every year, including tea cultivar Jinguanyin, Jinmudan, Meizhan in addition to Jinxuan cultivar. Tea cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan are new clones bred produced by natural hybridization by the Tea Research Institute of Fujian Academy of Agricultural Sciences with Tieguanyin as the female parent and Huangdan as the male parent. They passed the national cultivar examination in 2002 and 2010 respectively and became the national excellent tea cultivars. In China, oolong tea made of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan has a rich and strong aroma and is recognized as a high-aroma tea cultivar (Tang et al., 2025).

Oriental beauty tea, also called Pengfeng tea or Beauty tea, originated in Taiwan Province of China. It is a kind of deeply fermented variety of oolong tea (60–80% fermentation). Legend has it that after tasting it, Queen Victoria of England was full of praise and personally conferred the name “Oriental Beauty Tea,” a title by which it has been known ever since. Oriental beauty tea is favored by consumers, because of it has a rich amber-colored infusion, with floral, fruity and sweet flavor (Zeng et al., 2022). The unique flavor of beauty tea is not only related to the processing technology (withering, rotating, fermentation, enzyme inactivation, softening, rolling, drying) (Cho et al., 2007; Ding et al., 2024), but also closely related to raw materials, which must come from leaves subjected to puncture of tea green leafhoppers (Li, Zhang, Yan, et al., 2023; Mei et al., 2017). The unique quality of Beauty tea is attributed to the tea green leafhopper, a kind of cicada insect that feeds on tea leaves. After puncturing by tea green leafhopper, leaf cells were damaged, resulting in complex chemical changes in the leaves, producing some volatile substances, such as causing GPP to come into contact with linalool synthases (CsLIS), which lead to the production and release of linalool (Zeng, Watanabe, & Yang, 2019). These volatile compounds not only repelled tea green leafhopper, but also attracted the natural enemy of tea green leafhopper (Arimura, Ozawa, & Maffei, 2011; Erb, Meldau, & Howe, 2012; Hilker & Meiners, 2010; Wu & Baldwin, 2010). It also has an important effect on the aroma of tea (Zeng, Watanabe, Yang, et al., 2019). The varying degrees of puncturing intensity by the tea green leafhopper differentially influence the metabolic profile of tea leaves. Both excessive and insufficient puncturing are detrimental to the formation of characteristic quality compounds. Moderate puncturing stimulation induces the biosynthesis and accumulation of unique flavor metabolites, contributing to the distinctive sensory attributes of the tea(Chien et al., 2020). Previous studies have found that continuous puncturing by the tea green leafhopper increased the expression of linalool synthases1 (LIS1) and linalool synthases2 (LIS2) in tea leaves, promoted the synthesis of geraniol diphosphate (GPP) to (S)-linalool, and promoted the release of linalool (Mei et al., 2017). Moreover, linalool, geraniol, benzeneacetaldehyde, and dihydrolinalool have been identified as characteristic volatile compounds released by the Jinxuan cultivar in response to varying intensities of puncturing damage by the tea green leafhopper. They also found that Oriental beauty tea quality under low degree of puncturing by tea green leafhopper was the best, which contained more tea polyphenols, lipids and carbohydrates(Li, Zhang, Yan, et al., 2023). The composition and concentration of volatile compounds in tea are significantly influenced by both the tea cultivar and the processing techniques. For instance, there were 42 important volatile compounds have been identified in black tea (Schuh & Schieberle, 2006), while 24 key odor-active compounds were characterized in green tea(Yin et al., 2022). Furthermore, the content of volatile compounds such as linalool, geraniol, and acetaldehyde has decreased after fresh leaves are processed into green tea (Flaig, Qi, Wei, Yang, & Schieberle, 2020). The aroma of tea produced from the same variety but using different processing methods varies. For example, the main aroma compounds expect for geraniol, were different among the various types of tea, which used Jinmudan variety to process into black tea, oolong tea, yellow tea, white tea, and green tea(Tang et al., 2025). The high-aroma tea varieties Jinguanyin and Jinmudan are mainly processed into light-fermented Wuyi Rock Tea and black tea, and they are endowed with unique quality and flavor characteristics. Traditional beauty tea is generally processed from Taiwanese tea varieties such as Qingxindamao and Jinxuan, et al. At present, few studies have explored Oolong teas made from the high-aroma tea cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan in accordance with the processing techniques of beauty tea, and research specifically investigating their impact on tea aroma is even more limited. Therefore, in this study, the high-aroma tea plant cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan, which were punctured by tea green leafhoppers, were used as raw materials to make Oolong tea. The influence of different degrees of puncturing by tea green leafhoppers on the aroma was measured by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS). We also combined this analysis with human olfactory receptor protein predictions to explore the potential mechanisms behind the perceived aroma profiles. The aim of this study was to determine the optimal degree of tea green leafhopper puncturing, identify the key volatile compounds influencing the aroma of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan Beauty teas, and reveal the potential mechanisms of aroma formation. It provides theoretical support for the selection of high-quality raw materials for beauty tea, and offers scientific basis for expanding the adaptability of Jinguanyin and Jindanmu.

Materials and methods

Preparation of the oolong tea

One bud and two leaves from high-aroma tea cultivar Jinguanyin, Jinmudan with different degrees of tea green leafhopper puncturing were picked in the ecological tea garden of Ruibian Dragon Tea Factory, Heyang Village, Wushan Town, Datian County, Sanming City, Fujian Province, China, on September 9,2022. The fresh leaves were processed into Beauty tea by professional tea producers according to previous Beauty tea processing technology (Cho et al., 2007) which made slight adjustments:

The fresh leaves were picked in the morning, then withered outdoors under natural light for about 2 h. After withering, leaves were moved indoors for tossing, maintaining the temperature at 26 °C and the humidity at 75% during the tossing process. Then, pour the leaves into a dense bamboo sieve, with a thickness of 20 cm. Covered it with a breathable cotton cloth for fermentation. The fermentation temperature should be controlled at 28 °C, and the relative humidity at 75%. When 80% of the leaves changed from green to red, the grass smell disappeared, and the floral and fruity aroma began to emerge, it indicated that the fermentation is moderate. The fermentation process lasted approximately 2 h. Next, the leaves were fixated by roller fixating machine, which added about 10 kg of leaves and kept for 8 min at a temperature of 250 °C. When you pinched the leaves with a prickly feel, a warm and moist sensation in your palm, a slight elasticity, the floral and fruity aroma became prominent, it indicated that the fixation was moderate. Then there were about 6.5 kg leaves were put into a wet cloth bag. After tying the bags into balls, put them into plastic bags and tied them tightly. Then placed them in a plastic bucket, covered the bucket lid to keep warm, and promoted the redistribution of moisture in the tea. This process called softening and should lasted for 20 min. Next process is rolling. The leaves were putted into a rolling machine with 25 r/min of the rotational speed. After pressing and rolling for 3–5 turns, we released the pressure. Then, we changed the angle of tea ball with pressing and rolling for 3–5 turns. Repeated the above method of rolling. The final process is dying. The initial drying temperature was set as 80 °C, the thickness of the tea-spreading was 1.5 cm, and the duration is 6 h. The re-drying temperature was 80 °C, lasting for 3–10 h, when the moisture content of the tea was measured to be lower than 6% by a moisture meter.

Extraction of volatile components

Solid-Phase Microextraction (SPME) was used to extract volatile compounds from tea, which with solid phase microextraction probe (50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS, Merck Instrument, USA). Firstly, tea was weighed 3.0 g and put it into 40 mL extraction flask. Secondly, ethyl caprate standard solution was added 10 μL with concentration of 86.5 μg/mL. Finally, we sealed it and balanced it in a water bath (SYG-2, Changzhou Langyue instrument, China) at 70 °C for 10 min.

Determination of volatile components

Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC–MS) (GC–MS-QP2020NX, Shimadzu Instrument, Japan) was used to determine the contents and components of the volatile substances punctured by tea green leafhopper. Ethyl caprate standard (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology co.,ltd) is used as an internal standard. Firstly, the extraction head was aged at 250 °C for 2 h, then it was inserted into the extraction flask and pushed out, adsorbed for 50 min and took out, finally analyzed for 10 min by inserting it into the GC–MS injection port. The test was performed on Shimadzu column with Rxi-5 Sil MS (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm) without shunt injection, and the inlet temperature was 250 °C. The column flow rate was 1.0 mL/min. The initial temperature of the column temperature chamber was 40 °C for 2 min, then the temperature was increased to 170 °C at 4 °C/min and maintained for 1 min. After that, it was raised to 250 °C at 20 °C/min and kept for 2 min. Finally, it was raised to 300 °C at 25 °C/min and kept for 2 min. The interface temperature was 260 °C, and the ion source temperature was 200 °C. The scanning mode is full scanning, and the quality scanning range is 40–600 m/z. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate.

Quantitative analysis of volatile components

The NIST17.0 spectrum library was utilized in combination with the retention time and retention index calibrated by normal alkanes (C7-C33) for manual analysis. Compounds with a similarity of ≥80% were selected for subsequent experimental analysis. Molecular docking of aroma active compounds with olfactory receptor

Molecular docking is employed to investigate the intermolecular interactions between ligands and biological macromolecules, predicting their binding conformations and binding energies. PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) was used to obtain the three-dimensional structure of aroma active compounds. The three-dimensional structure models of the olfactory receptors were obtained from Uni-Prot(https://www.uniprot.org). CB-Dock2 (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/php/index.php) was used to hydrogenated and charged the human olfactory receptor protein (receptor) and aromatic compounds (ligand). 3D and 2D visualizations of protein and compound binding were analyzed using BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2019 Client software.

Statistical analysis

One-way analysis of variance (One-way ANOVA) is used to determine whether there are statistically significant differences among the means of three or more independent samples. In this study, One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed with SPSS (P < 0.05) to analyze the significant differences among the various treatment groups, providing an intuitive reflection of the differences between the treatments. Tortadiagram was drawn by EXCEL to explore the distribution of Esters, Alcohols, Ketones and Other substances in different samples.

OPLS-DA is a regression modeling method that transforms multiple dependent variables into multiple independent variables, and it can be used to identify differential metabolites between different groups. In this study, SIMCA 14.1 was used for orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) to reflect the distribution of different samples and separate different variable indicators to the greatest extent. Using the variable importance in the project (VIP) to screen the characteristic volatile components with the importance index of variables (VIP value>1) as significant differences. Prediction parameters for evaluating OPLS-DA model are mainly R2Y (cum) and Q2 (cum), R2Y (cum) represents the model's ability to explain separation, and Q2 (cum) represents the model's ability to predict. The closer these two indicators are to 1, the more stable and reliable the model is.

Results

Composition of volatiles in high-aroma oolong tea after puncturing by tea green leafhoppers

The degree of tea fermentation significantly affects the composition of volatile compounds in tea leaves. A total of 79 volatile compounds were identified in unfermented green tea, among which alcohols, esters, aldehydes and ketones constituting the predominant categories (Shi et al., 2022). Approximately 125 volatile compounds have been identified in semi-fermented oolong tea, with alcohols and olefins account for a high proportion (Li, 2022). A total of 140 volatile compounds were identified in fully-fermented black tea, among which alkanes, alcohols and esters accounted for a high proportion (Zhu, Zhou, & Wen, 2025). In this study, 82 volatile compounds were detected in high-aroma tea cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan oolong tea after being punctured by tea green leafhoppers (Table S1). Among them, there are 68 kinds of volatile compounds were detected in Jinguanyin oolong tea (JGYT) and 68 kinds of volatile compounds were detected in Jinmudan oolong tea (JMDT) (Fig. 1). Further classification analysis revealed that in Jinguanyin oolong tea, alkanes accounted for 35.29%, followed by alcohols (22.06%), esters (16.18%), and olefins (11.77%). In Jinmudan oolong tea, alkanes constituted 29.41%, followed by alcohols (26.47%), esters (17.65%), and olefins (11.77%). These results indicate that higher fermentation degrees correlate with increased diversity of volatile compounds, particularly in alkane accumulation. The alkanes compounds in tea do not contribute much to the aroma. The high proportion of alkanes might be cause by the presence of waxy substances composed of alkanes in the leaves of tea plants. The proportions of various volatile compounds in different types of tea, and these are the main factors contributing to the distinctive aromas of different tea categories. The aroma compounds in tea mainly include alcohols, aldehydes, esters, etc. The differences in the types and contents of these compounds result in the variations in the aroma types of tea. Some alcohols in tea have floral and fruity scents, such as benzoic alcohol with bitter almond scent, phenylethanol with rose fragrance, linalool with floral and fruity scents, et al. Some aldehydes mainly have fatty, floral and fruity scents, such as octanal and nonanal, which have fatty scents, and phenylacetaldehyde with honey-like fragrance. Some esters in tea also have floral and fruity scents, such as Butanoic acid, 2-methyl-, 2-methylbutyl ester and cis-3-Hexenyl-.alpha.-methylbutyrate, which have sweet apple scents, and Butanoic acid, 3-methyl-, 3,7-dimethyl-2,6-octadienyl ester, (Z)- with sweet orange scents.

Previous studies compared the types of aroma compounds in green tea and black tea made from five varieties (Chunyu2, Chunyu1, Longjing43, Zhenong117, Jinguanyin). Their findings revealed that green tea had higher proportions of alcohols, aldehydes, and esters, while black tea had higher proportions of alcohols and aldehydes (Fang et al., 2022). Through analyzing the composition of aroma compounds in Rougui rock tea from different regions of Wuyi Mountain (Zhengyan, Banyan and Zhou tea), and found that the types of aroma compounds in different regions varied greatly. The proportion of alcohol compounds was high in Rougui in the core production area (Wu et al., 2024). The proportion of olefins, alkanes, alcohols and esters in Wuyi Shuixian Rock tea from different production regions is relatively high (Yuan et al., 2024). These findings corroborate that the compositional profiles of volatile compounds exhibit significant variation across both tea cultivars and processing categories.

Analysis of volatile component differences in high-aroma oolong tea after different degrees puncturing by tea green leafhopper

According to the OPLS-DA score chart (Fig. 2A), the R2X (cum) and R2Y (cum) of the Jinguanyin oolong tea (JGYT) model was 0.896 and 0.986 respectively. It was illustrated that the interpretation rate of the model for X matrix was 0.896 and that for Y matrix was 0.986. Q2 (cum) is 0.961, indicating that the model had good predictive power and the model was reliable. Jinguanyin oolong tea under low degree of puncturing (JGYTL), Jinguanyin oolong tea under medium degree of puncturing (JGYTM) and Jinguanyin oolong tea under high degree of puncturing (JGYTH) treatment groups were located in the fourth, third and first quadrants of the principal component diagram, respectively, indicating that there were differences in volatile compounds in Jinguanyin oolong tea after different degrees of puncture by tea green leafhoppers, which could clearly distinguish the three types of Jinguanyin oolong tea. The permutation plot (Fig. 2C) showed that the original model Q2 value at the right end was higher than the Q2 value after permutation, indicating that the model was not overfitted.

Screening of key volatiles in JMDT after puncturing by tea green leafhoppers.

Notably, Jinguanyin and Jinmudan oolong teas share 7 key volatile compounds: dihydrolinalool, linalool, (Z)-linalool oxide (furanoid), 2,6-dimethyl-3,7-octadiene-2,6-diol, methyl salicylate, geraniol, and (E)-linalool oxide (furanoid). These 7 key volatile compounds could clearly distinguish between the Jinguanyin and Jinmudan Oolong teas, which have different levels of damage caused by the tea green leafhoppers. Tea green leafhopper puncturing upregulated the expression of linalool synthases (CsLIS1 and CsLIS2) in fresh leaves of Jinxuan tea plants and released linalool, which was conducive to the formation of the characteristic aroma of oolong tea (Mei et al., 2017). Moreover, the tea green leafhopper could activate the conversion of linalool into linalool oxide and their glucosides in tea leaves (Xiao et al., 2022). Four characteristic aroma compounds were also identified in the tea leaves after being punctured by tea green leafhopper, namely linalool, geraniol, benzeneacetaldehyde, and dihydrolinalool (Li, Zhang, Yan, et al., 2023). It is indicated that geraniol, linalool, and their oxides are key aroma compounds in high-aroma oolong tea punctured by tea green leafhopper.

Different varieties and processing techniques can also affect the composition and content of volatile compounds in tea. There are approximately 30 volatile compounds in unfermented green tea that are the main contributors to its aroma (Kumazawa & Masuda, 2002). While in fermented black tea, 42 volatile compounds are regarded as important contributors to the aroma of black tea (Schuh & Schieberle, 2006; Yang, Baldermann, & Watanabe, 2013). During the processing of Xinyangmaojian green tea, there were 24 key aroma active compounds were identified, such as trans-nerolidol, geranylacetone, nonanal, (+)-δ-cadinene, linalool, (Z)-jasmone, cis-3-hexenyl butyrate, cis-3-hexenyl hexanoate, methyl jasmonate, β-ocimene, β-Myrcene, Copaene, cis-β-Farnesene, d-Limonene, (E)-β-Ocimene, Dihydroactinolide, M-Cymene, (Z,E)-α-Farnesene, Methyl salicylate, Geranial, Phytol acetate, trans-Furan linalool oxide, cis-3-Hexenyl valerate and (−)-α-Terpineol. Moreover, most of them were significantly reduced during fixating process in Xinyangmaojian (Yin et al., 2022). And the contents of linallol, geranol and acetaldehyde in green tea were higher in fresh leaves than in dry tea (Flaig et al., 2020). While geraniol, (E)-2-hexenal, 4-methoxy-benzaldehyde, guaiacol, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (E,Z)-2,6-nonadienal, (E)-2-nonenal, 1-hexanol, furanoid, 2,6,6-trimethyl-1-cyclohexene-1-carboxaldehyde and benzyl alcohol gave the white tea made from albino tea cultivar “Ming guan” with flowers, fruit, sweet, grass and wood incense (Huang et al., 2025). Dehydrolinalool, linalool oxide II, linalool, α-farnesene, linalool oxide I, β-ocimene, nerolidol, cis-3-butyric acid folate, myrcene, and (Z)-hexanoic acid-3-Hexenyl ester are the characteristic components that distinguish the Fenghuang Dancong, Tieguanyin, and Dahongpao oolong tea (Li, 2022). The main volatile compounds in Rucheng Baimao (Camellia pubescens) Black tea produced in spring, summer and autumn are alcohols, esters and alkanes. The spring tea exhibits the best aroma, primarily attributed to the accumulation of geraniol, nonanal, methyl salicylate, and (E)-citral (Zhu et al., 2025). The processing technology significantly influences the volatile compounds in black tea. The main aroma active compounds of sweet aroma in black tea are linalool, methyl salicylate, β-cyclocitral, dimethyl sulfide,β-myrcene, 3-methylbutanal, 2-methylbutanal,β-ocimene,β-citral, β-ionone, and citral (Yang et al., 2024). And trans-2-hexenal and methyl salicylate are the main components that distinguish semi-fermented and fermented tea (Wang et al., 2008). The study found that geraniol, phenethyl alcohol, (E)-β-ocimene and methyl salicylate are the key aroma compounds in Jinmudan black tea; Linalyl valerate, indole and phenethyl alcohol are the main aroma compounds in Jinmudan oolong tea; Geraniol, methyl salicylate and terpinolene are the dominate aroma profile of Jinmudan yellow tea and Jinmudan white tea; while geraniol, indole and (E)-β-ocimene are the characteristic aroma compounds in Jinmudan green tea (Tang et al., 2025). Integrally, it is concluded that linalool or geraniol are the main contributors to the aroma of tea. However, the aroma profiles differ among various tea types, which may be due to the different contents of these two compounds and the different other aroma active compounds produced by different processing techniques. It was also found that the tea green leafhopper's puncture significantly altered the aroma active compounds and aroma types of the oolong tea.

OAV analysis of differential volatile components

Different volatile compounds have different aroma types and aroma thresholds (OAVs). OAV is a common indicator for measuring the contribution of volatile compounds to the aroma of tea. OAV > 1 means that volatile compounds make a significant contribution to the aroma. Using The Good Scents Company Information System (https://www.thegoodscentscompany.com) and Compilations of Flavor Threshold Values in Water and Other Media (Second Enlarged and Revised Edition) (van Gemert, 2015) to identify the aroma characteristics and odor thresholds in water for 22 key volatile compounds in Jinguanyin oolong tea and 13 key volatile compounds in Jinmudan oolong tea. The results show in Tables 2 and Tables 3.

There were 12 volatile compounds that have aroma charateristics were identified in Jinguanyin oolong tea, such as Geraniol (loral, sweet), Dihydrolinalool (floral, fruity), Benzylalcohol (bitter almond-like, fruity), Methylsalicylate (holly oil, minty), (E)-linalooloxide(furanoid) (sweet, floral), (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid) (floral, sweet), Phenethylalcohol (floral, honey-like), Linalool (Floral, fruity), 3-Hexen-1-ylhexanoate (fruity), linalooloxide(pyranoid) (floral, sweet), Cedrol (cedarwood-like), Hex-3(Z)-enylbutyrate (fruity). Except for Cedrol and (E)-linalooloxide(furanoid), for which no corresponding odor thresholds were found, all other compounds had OAV>1. Notably, geraniol, dihydrolinalool, methylsalicylate, (Z) -Linalool Oxide (furanoid), phenethylalcohol, linalool, 3-Hexen-1-ylhexanoate, linalool Oxide (pyranoid), Hex-3(Z)-enylbutyrate exhibited OAVs >50, indicating that these 9 compounds are the key aroma-active compounds of Jinguanyin oolong tea. It endowing that Jinguanyin Oolong tea with the characteristics of floral, fruity, honey-like and woody scents. Interestingly, the content of these 9 compounds in JGYTM was significantly higher than that in JGYTL and JGYTH, indicating that moderate puncturing by tea green leafhopper is conducive to the formation and accumulation of the aroma of Jinguanyin oolong tea. This showed that as the degree of puncturing caused by the tea green leafhopper increases, the concentrations of these 9 compounds first rise and then fall. Previous studies had found that the low degree puncture of the tea green leafhopper is beneficial to the formation of oolong tea quality, which is inconsistent with our results (Li, Zhang, Yan, et al., 2023). This might be due to the uniqueness of the varieties. Previous studies used the Jinxuan and Tieguanyin varieties, while our research employed the high-aroma tea varieties, Jinguanyin and Jinmudan.

There were 10 aroma active compounds that possess aroma charateristics have been identified in Jinmudan oolong tea, including geraniol (floral, sweet), dihydrolinalool (floral, fruity), (E)-linalooloxide(furanoid) (sweet, floral), (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid) (floral, sweet), methylsalicylate (holly oil, minty), hexylisovalerate (fruity), hexylhexanoate (fruity), nerol (rose-like, floral), linalool (floral, fruity), hexadecane (orchid-like). Among them, the OAV of geraniol, dihydrolinalool, (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid), methylsalicylate, hexylhexanoate, and linalool is greater than 1. Moreover, the OAV of geraniol, dihydrolinalool, (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid), and linalool is greater than 45, indicating that these four volatile compounds are the main aroma active compounds of Jinmudan oolong tea, which endows Jinmudan oolong tea with the characteristics of floral, sweet and fruity aromas. The contents of dihydrolinalool, (Z)-Linalooloxide(furanoid) and linalool were highest under the low degree of puncturing by tea green leafhoppers, and its content decreases as the degree of puncture intensifies. It is indicated that the low degree puncture of tea green leafhopper is conducive to the formation and accumulation of the aroma of Jinmudan oolong tea.

Geraniol, Dihydrolinalool, (Z)-Linalool oxide (furanoid), and Linalool are key aroma active compounds in two kinds of high-aroma oolong tea. It is speculated that these four compounds are the main contributors to the aroma of high-aroma oolong tea. Moreover, these four volatile compounds are influenced not only by tea cultivar but also by the degree of puncture by tea green leafhopper. In Jinguanyin oolong Tea, the concents of these four compounds exhibited an initial increase followed by a decrease as the intensity of tea green leafhopper. The concent of geraniol in Jinmudan oolong tea increased with the intensification of the puncture degree of the tea green leafhopper, while the concents of Dihydrolinalool, (Z)-linalooloxide (furanoid), and Linalool decreased. Previous studies have demonstrated that linalool, geraniol, phenylacetaldehyde and dihydrolinalool were characteristic volatile compounds with the floral and fruity aroma in Jinxuan oolong tea (Li, Zhang, Yan, et al., 2023). Meanwhile, dihydrolinalool and cis-linalool oxide have been identified as characteristic aroma compounds in tieguanyin oolong tea (Li, Zhang, Chen, et al., 2023). These four volatile compounds are also common and important aroma active compounds in tea. Geraniol is the most abundant volatile compound in Longjing tea (Wang et al., 2020). Geraniol, cis-linalool oxide (pyranoid) and (Z)-3-hexen-1-ylhexanoate were detected in Keemun, Assam, Darjeeling and Ceylon black teas, which have a strong aroma and a pleasant scent (Kang et al., 2019). Linalool and geraniol were the main contributor to Wuyirougui (Liang et al., 2024). The contribution of geraniol and nerol to the aroma of Wuyishuixian tea was 96.28%, that geraniol endows it with floral fragrance, while nerol endows it with fruity aroma (Ye et al., 2023).

Linalool, dihydrolinalool, geraniol and (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid) endow high-aroma oolong tea with floral, fruity and sweet scents

In order to further clarify the characteristic aroma compounds in the high-aroma oolong tea of Jingyanyin and Jinmudan, we used CB-Dock2(https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/php/index.php) for molecular docking of key aroma compounds with olfactory receptor proteins. Six human olfactory related proteins: Olfactory receptor 1A1(OR1A1), Olfactory receptor 1A2(OR1A2), Olfactory receptor 3A1(OR3A1), Olfactory receptor 6A2(OR6A2), Olfactory receptor 2 W1(OR2W1) and Olfactory receptor 5 M3(OR5M3) were obtained from UniProt(https://www.uniprot.org). The 3D structural formulas of 7 compounds [Linalool, Geraniol, Dihydrolinalool, (Z)-linalooloxide(furanoid), Phenethylalcohol, 3-Hexen-1-ylhexanoate and Linalooloxide (pyranoid), respectively] were obtained from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The molecular docking of 6 olfactory related proteins with 7 aroma compounds was performed. The key aroma characteristic compounds were screened and identified by binding energy |n| ≥ 5.

The results showed that Linalool, Geraniol, Dihydrolinalool, (Z)-linalooloxide (furanoid), Phenethylalcohol, 3-Hexen-1-ylhexanoate, Linalooloxide (pyranoid) were bound to 6 olfactory receptor proteins, and the binding energy |n| ≥ 5 (Fig. 4, Table S2). It was indicated that high-aroma Jinguanyin and Jinmudan oolong tea showed floral, fruit and sweet aroma, mainly due to the rich flower and fruit characteristic compounds of linalool, dihydrolinalool, the rich flower characteristic compound of geraniol and the rich flower and sweet characteristic compound of (Z)-linalooloxide (furanoid). And these aromatic compounds are recognized and perceived by human by binding to the human olfactory receptor proteins. The floral, fruit and honey fragrance in Jinguanyin oolong tea also came from Phenethylalcohol (floral, honey), 3-Hexen-1-ylhexanoate(fruity) and Linalooloxide (pyranoid) (sweet, floral), which can also be recognized and perceived by human olfactory proteins, endowing it with floral, fruity and honey-like scents (Fig. 4). Molecular docking analysis was also used to confirm that both linalool and geraniol can bind to human olfactory receptors, contributing to the floral aroma characteristics of Wuyirougui tea (Liang et al., 2024).

The potential mechanism of key aroma formation in high-aroma Jinguanyin and Jinmudan oolong tea

The fresh leaves of high-aroma cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan were punctured by tea green leafhoppers, and then processed through oriental beauty tea processing technology to produce oolong tea which presented floral, fruity and sweet aroma. The floral fragrance was mainly composed of Geraniol, Linalool, (Z)-linalooloxide (furanoid) and Dihydrolinalool. The fruity aroma was mainly composed of Linalool and Dihydrolinalool, and the sweet aroma was mainly composed of Geraniol and (Z)-linalooloxide (furanoid).

Based on literature retrieval, we sorted out the potential metabolic pathways of key aroma active compounds and revealed the formation of different aroma types in high-aroma oolong tea after the tea green leafhopper's puncture (Fig. 5) (Liang et al., 2024; Saeed et al., 2025; Zeng, Watanabe, Yang, et al., 2019; Zeng, Xiao, Zhou, et al., 2021). Garbodydrates were used to synthesize isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP) through glycolysis and the methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway(Barja & Rodriguez-Concepcion, 2021). Both of them were catalyzed by geranyl diphosphate synthase (GPPS) to generate geranyl diphosphate (GPP)(Pichersky, Noel, & Dudareva, 2006; Zeng, Watanabe, Yang, et al., 2019). On one hand, GPP was converted into geraniol by geraniol synthase (GES) derived from the tea green leafhopper (Zeng, Watanabe, Yang, et al., 2019; Zhou, Liu, & Yang, 2019). On the other hand, sustained puncturing by the tea green leafhopper activated linalool synthase (LIS) in tea leaves, which in turn catalyzed the conversion of GPP into linalool(Mei et al., 2017; Zeng, Watanabe, Yang, et al., 2019). Linalool was activated and transformed into 6,7-Epoxylinalool under the conditions of light and punctured by tea green leafhopper. Under acidic conditions, 6,7-epoxylinalool underwent a non-enzymatic reaction to form (Z)-linalool oxide (furanoid)(Xiao et al., 2022). Linalool could also be converted into Dihydrolinalool through complex reactions such as hydrogenation. And linalool also underwent oxidation to form (Z)-linalool oxide (furanoid) and (E)-linalool oxide (furanoid)(Zeng, Xiao, Zhou, et al., 2021). Therefore, we speculate that after puncturing by the tea green leafhopper, geraniol is primarily synthesized via the insect's endogenous GES gene, while linalool production is enhanced through upregulation of linalool synthase (LIS) expression in tea leaves. Additionally, (Z)-linalool oxide (furanoid) is likely formed through the synergy of environmental factors such as light and non-enzymatic reactions, and dihydrolinalool may also be generated via a series of complex reactions. These four aroma active compounds ultimately endow the oolong tea of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan with distinctive floral, fruity, and sweet aroma characteristics.

Conclusions

This study revealed that medium degree of puncturing by tea green leafhopper enhances aroma formation and accumulation in Jinguanyin oolong tea. Specifically, linalool, geraniol, dihydrolinalool, and (Z)-linalool oxide (furanoid) were identified as the key aroma active compounds in high-aroma tea cultivars Jinguanyin and Jinmudan following varying degree of tea green leafhopper puncturing. These compounds endow oolong tea with floral, fruity and sweet aromas. In future cultivation of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan, if infestation by the tea green leafhopper occurs, the damage status can be monitored. Fresh leaves with appropriate levels of infestation can be selectively harvested and processed into oolong tea using the Beauty tea production process. This approach may improve the final tea quality and reduce economic losses caused by the tea green leafhopper. Beauty tea is traditionally produced from Taiwanese tea cultivars, which are relatively scarce in Fujian tea-growing regions, limiting the development of this tea style. This study explores the potential of using highly aromatic cultivars such as Jinguanyin and Jinmudan to produce Beauty tea, providing a theoretical basis for raw material selection and aroma improvement of beauty tea, and a new idea for coping with the invasion of tea green leafhopper in the cultivation of Jinguanyin and Jinmudan tea plants.

Efficacy and tolerability of CDK 4/6 inhibitors in HR-positive HER2-negative de novo metastatic breast cancer patients aged ≥ 70 years
Authors: Muhammed Mustafa Atci, Mert Erciyestepe, Kayhan Erturk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCurrent guidelines recommend endocrine therapy with CDK 4/6 inhibitors as the first and most strongly evidenced treatment for patients with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, unless they are experiencing visceral crisis or resistance to endocrine therapy. CDK 4/6 inhibitors can cause hematological and gastrointestinal toxicity. In case of toxicity, drug doses need to be reduced. Sometimes, depending on the degree of toxicity, taking a break or even stopping treatment may be considered.MethodsAll patients included in our study were 70 years of age or older, had de novo metastatic disease, were hormone receptor-positive and HER2-negative, and were receiving CDK 4/6 inhibitors as part of their metastatic first-line treatment. Recorded common side effects of CDK 4/6 inhibitors. Survival analyses were conducted on patients who had dose reductions due to these side effects. Additionally, OS and PFS were compared according to the type of CDK 4/6 inhibitor the patients received and the Charlson comorbidity index. Our study was conducted retrospectively and as a single-center study.ResultsMedian OS was 33.0 months (27.0-38.9). Median OS was statistically significantly better in patients with a Charlson comorbidity index < 10 (patients aged 70–79 years with no comorbidities other than metastatic breast cancer) compared to those with a Charlson comorbidity index ≥ 10 (patients aged 80 and over or with comorbidities other than metastatic breast cancer) (41.5 vs. 15.0 months, p < 0.001). CDK 4/6 inhibitor selection and dose reduction did not significantly affect median OS. Median PFS was 27.0 months (22.5–31.4). Similar to variables affecting OS, PFS was significantly worse in patients with a Charlson comorbidity index ≥ 10 and in patients with more than 3 bone metastases.ConclusionsA review of the literature reveals that studies on the efficacy and tolerability of CDK 4/6 inhibitors have primarily focused on a limited number of patients aged 70 years or older. The results of these studies, similar to our study, generally indicate that efficacy and survival are similar to those in younger patients, and that dose reductions do not significantly impact survival. Furthermore, there are no studies in the literature yet that examine the relationship between the Charlson comorbidity index and survival in elderly patients with multiple comorbidities who are receiving CDK 4/6 inhibitors.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common type of cancer detected worldwide and is the second most common cause of mortality in women. Risk factors for breast cancer are gender, age, race/ethnicity, genetic predisposition, family and personal history of breast cancer, high breast density, benign breast disease, early menarche or late menopause, obesity, ionizing radiation, alcohol and smoking, use of oral contraceptives/hormone replacement therapy. About 30% of cases with early-stage breast cancer progress to metastatic disease. Approximately 5–10% of patients may be metastatic at the time of diagnosis.

Approximately 70% of breast cancer patients are hormone receptor (HR)-positive [1]. Endocrine therapy is preferred for HR-positive HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer patients without visceral crisis or endocrine therapy resistance [2], and is applied according to the premenopausal, perimenopausal, and postmenopausal conditions of the patients. Response to endocrine therapy alone diminishes after the first year as patients develop resistance to treatment. In this case, other endocrine treatment options should be considered. Endocrine therapy can be used in combination with targeted therapies to overcome endocrine resistance and increase response time.

Cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) 4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib), mTOR inhibitors (everolimus), and phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase inhibitors (alpelisib) are targeted therapies for hormone receptor-positive patients. In addition to these agents, treatment options such as elacestrant in patients with ESR-1 mutations and capivasertib in patients with AKT1-activating mutations or PTEN alterations have also been used in recent years [3]. In postmenopausal patients with ER-positive and HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, the addition of CDK 4/6 inhibitor drugs to aromatase inhibitors prolonged both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of patients [4]. CDK 4/6 inhibitors can cause hematological and gastrointestinal toxicity [5]. While neutropenia and leukopenia are more common, thrombocytopenia and anemia are less common [6]. Side effects such as fatigue, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea may also occur during treatment with these agents. In addition, ribociclib can prolong QTc and increase liver enzymes [7]. In case of toxicity, drug doses need to be reduced. Sometimes, depending on the degree of toxicity, taking a break or even stopping treatment may be considered.

Studies investigating the efficacy of these agents in the geriatric population, especially those aged 70 and older, are limited in the literature. We planned this study to evaluate the efficacy, toxicity, and effect of dose reduction on survival of CDK 4/6 inhibitor use in the treatment of ≥ 70 years old with metastatic breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Our study was done retrospectively. 109 patients who were followed up and treated in our clinic between June 2020 and January 2024 were included in our study. All patients included in our study were de novo metastatic, HR-positive, HER2-negative, and received CDK 4/6 inhibitors in their metastatic first-line treatment. Patients’ age, comorbidities, treatment response, dose reduction, side effects, and metastatic sites were recorded. Hemoglobin levels, MCV, leukocyte count, neutrophil count, and platelet count were determined at the beginning of treatment and during follow-up. All of our patients received letrozole as endocrine therapy in addition to a CDK 4/6 inhibitor.

OS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the date of death or last follow-up. 18 F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography-computed tomography was used to assess treatment responses. All patients underwent imaging at initial presentation and every 3 months. Radiologic response was evaluated by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors [8]. PFS was defined as the time from the start of treatment to progression. Survival analyses for both OS and PFS were performed using the entire follow-up period available for each patient. Reported 2-year survival percentages are presented as descriptive milestone estimates and do not represent abbreviations of survival analyses. The Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) was calculated for all patients [9]. Side effects were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) [10]. Patients who were lost to follow-up, refused or discontinued treatment, had incomplete or inaccessible data, or had multiple malignancies were excluded from the study. Patients aged 70 years and older were included in the study. Patients received CDK 4/6 inhibitor treatments as specified in the drug information: 3 weeks on, 1 week off. All patients started with a ribociclib dose of 600 mg/day and a palbociclib dose of 125 mg/day.

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Saglik Bilimleri University Prof. Dr. Cemil Taşcıoğlu City Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval date and number: October 6, 2025, 355). Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics are presented as n and % for categorical variables, and as Mean ± SD and Median (min-max) for continuous variables. The Wilcoxon test was used to compare baseline and 3-month measurements of various hematological parameters. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to compare survival and PFS times between clinical groups. Finally, multivariate Cox regression results were presented on the risk of death and progression of various clinical variables. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In metastatic first-line patients, 47.7% received palbociclib and 52.3% received ribociclib. Dose reductions were performed in 48.6% of patients. 56% of patients died. The median follow-up was 30.0 months (range, 3.0–50.0 months). Dose reduction was carried out in a total of 53 patients. Patient OS comparisons are presented in Table 3. Median OS was 33.0 months (27.0-38.9). Median OS was statistically significantly better in patients with CCI < 10 (patients aged 70–79 years with no comorbidities other than metastatic breast cancer) compared to those with CCI ≥ 10 (patients aged 80 and over or with comorbidities other than metastatic breast cancer) (41.5 vs. 15.0 months, p < 0.001). CDK 4/6 inhibitor selection and dose reduction did not significantly affect median OS. In patients receiving ribociclib, no significant difference was found in OS (p = 0.072) and PFS (p = 0.081) between patients who underwent dose reduction and those who did not. In patients receiving palbociclib, no significant difference was found in OS (p = 0.067) and PFS (p = 0.102) between patients who underwent dose reduction and those who did not. While having 3 or fewer bone metastases did not significantly alter OS, having 3 or more bone metastases had a statistically significant negative impact on OS (p < 0.001). Discussion

CDK 4/6 inhibitors inhibit the progression from the G1 phase of the cell cycle to the S phase by blocking the phosphorylation of the Rb protein. Thus, they slow or stop the growth and division of cancer cells. Current guidelines recommend endocrine therapy with CDK 4/6 inhibitors as the first and most strongly evidenced treatment for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, unless they are experiencing visceral crisis or resistance to endocrine therapy [3].

The first study demonstrating the contribution of these agents to survival was the randomized phase 2 PALOMA-1 trial, published in January 2015, which demonstrated a significant survival benefit of palbociclib + letrozole compared to letrozole alone. Median progression-free survival was 10.2 months (95% CI 5.7–12.6) for the letrozole group and 20.2 months (13.8–27.5) for the palbociclib + letrozole group [11]. In recent years, numerous studies have been published in the literature regarding the efficacy of these agents. In particular, the PALOMA-2 study, conducted in 666 postmenopausal ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer patients, showed similar results to our study. The median PFS was 24.8 months in the palbociclib + letrozole group, compared with 14.5 months in the placebo + letrozole group (hazard ratio for disease progression or death, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.46 to 0.72; p < 0.001) [12]. Our study has similar results to this study in terms of determining the median PFS as 27.0 months, and all patients were postmenopausal. In this study, grade 3–4 neutropenia was observed in 66% of patients receiving palbociclib + letrozole, grade 3–4 leukopenia was observed in 24.8%, and grade 3–4 anemia was observed in 5.4%. Febrile neutropenia was observed in 1.8% of patients receiving palbociclib + letrozole. Our study also shows similar side effect frequencies. In our study, grade 3–4 side effects were observed in 60.6% of our patients. Grade 3–4 neutropenia was observed in 57.8% of our patients, grade 3–4 leukopenia was observed in 23.9%, and grade 3–4 anemia was observed in 6.4%. The incidence of febrile neutropenia was 2.75% (3 patients).

Studies with ribociclib, another CDK 4/6 inhibitor, have also shown a positive effect on survival. When used in combination with letrozole in the first-line setting in postmenopausal, HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic patients, it has been shown to significantly prolong median OS compared to letrozole alone (63.9 months vs. 51.4 months) [13]. In this study, the median OS for patients receiving ribociclib + letrozole was calculated as 63.9 months. The shorter median OS in our study (33.0 months) may be due to all patients being 70 years of age or older or the short follow-up period.

In our study, all patients were started on a CDK 4/6 inhibitor at the maximum recommended dose, namely 125 mg/day for palbociclib and 600 mg/day for ribociclib, but dose reduction was performed in 48.6% of patients due to side effects. No statistically significant difference was found in terms of OS and PFS between patients who underwent dose reduction and those who did not. A recently published study showed that patients starting treatment with 400 mg ribociclib were similar to patients starting with 600 mg in terms of overall response rate and PFS [14]. Another study, with nearly the same patient number and characteristics as ours, examined 108 h-positive, HER2-negative patients aged 70 years and older. Dose reductions were not significantly impacting OS and PFS in patients receiving abemaciclib and ribociclib. Dose reductions were associated with worse OS and PFS in patients receiving palbociclib [15].

A clinical trial published in 2025 also showed that PFS was not affected by dose adjustment in patients receiving palbociclib + aromatase inhibitor, but OS was prolonged [16]. In a similar study, it was shown that dose reduction in patients receiving CDK 4/6 inhibitors did not affect PFS [17]. In a systematic review, rates of palbociclib dose changes, dose reductions, and discontinuations were higher in patients aged 65 years and older compared to younger patients; however, these differences did not appear to adversely affect survival [18]. Similarly, in a real-world multicenter study examining patients over the age of 75 who received aromatase inhibitor and palbociclib treatment, dose reduction was shown not to affect survival [19]. In this context, our study is generally consistent with the existing literature. In other words, studies in general have shown that CDK 4/6 inhibitors are tolerable and effective in elderly patients, similar to our study [20].

A review of the literature reveals that studies on the efficacy and tolerability of CDK 4/6 inhibitors have focused on a limited number of patients over the age of 70 [21]. The results of these studies, similar to our study, generally indicate that efficacy and survival are similar to those in younger patients, and that dose reductions do not significantly impact survival [22]. In our study, we evaluated the frequency of hematological toxicities and liver enzyme elevations. Furthermore, because we included many patients with multiple comorbidities, we examined the relationship between CCI and PFS and OS. Studies examining the relationship between this index and survival in older patients receiving CDK 4/6 inhibitors are rare in the literature [19, 23]. In our study, we observed that patients with CCI < 10, that is, patients aged 70–79 years and without comorbidities other than cancer, had better results in terms of both OS and PFS compared to patients with CCI ≥ 10, that is, patients aged 80 years and over or with comorbidities other than cancer. However, the CCI also has some limitations. Some diseases are not included in this index, and performance status, which is very important for oncology patients, is not included in the CCI. The CCI mainly scores chronic diseases, and acute conditions closely related to survival (such as febrile neutropenia, sepsis) are not scored.

Limitations of our study include being a single-center study, having a relatively limited number of patients, and being a retrospective study. Multicenter studies with larger patient numbers and longer follow-up periods are needed. Although OS analysis was performed using the entire available follow-up period in this study, the retrospective nature of the study and the relatively limited median follow-up period may restrict the maturity of long-term overall survival predictions. Our study is generally consistent with the literature and demonstrates the efficacy of CDK 4/6 inhibitors, their side effects, and the effects of dose reduction and comorbidities on survival, especially in the age group of ≥ 70 years.

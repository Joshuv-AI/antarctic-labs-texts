Research on energy-saving renovation of old oceanarium based on energy consumption monitoring technology
Authors: Shi Hua, Jian Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study takes an old oceanarium in Jiangsu Province, China, as a case study and monitors the energy consumption values related to electricity, water and gas consumption for the whole year before the renovation of the oceanarium. Based on the energy consumption monitoring data, an in-depth analysis of the energy consumption defects of the oceanarium before the renovation is conducted using energy audit technology. We combine the characteristics of the oceanarium itself according to: (1) the results of the building energy audit; (2) the results of the envelope calculation; and (3) the latest energy-saving retrofit technology. We developed a more scientific building energy-saving retrofit programme. Using this method, we can accurately monitor the energy consumption problems in old oceanarium buildings and find building energy consumption defects. In this study, we monitor the real-time energy consumption of nine old government office buildings in Jiangsu Province, China using energy monitoring technology. We analyse and study the monitoring results and finally target the scientific energy-saving retrofits. This method can be used to carry out targeted energy-saving building renovation in the future and put forward new methods and ideas for studying the energy consumption of existing public buildings.Keywords: Existing buildings, Energy monitoring, Energy audits, Energy efficiency, Efficiency renovation, Energy consumption

Introduction

Since the oil crisis in the 1970s, energy consumption has become a major concern [1]. According to statistics, buildings worldwide account for 30 % of total energy consumption, 60 % of electricity consumption, one billion litres of water per day, and 30 % of the total solid waste generation [[2], [3], [4]]. Therefore, energy analysis and retrofitting of existing buildings have garnered significant attention globally. By 2000, the total floor area of buildings in China reached 51.9 billion square meters, and by 2030, the floor area will be twice the 2000 vol [[5], [6], [7]]. In China's existing public buildings, the energy consumption per unit area of the exterior walls and roofs is three to five times that of developed countries. In terms of the energy consumption of windows, it is 2–3 times that of developed countries [8]. Consequently, the development potential for the energy-saving renovation of the envelope of existing buildings in China is 50–80 % [9]. The total number of existing public buildings in China is huge, most of which were built in the last century. Therefore, their envelope structures do not use thermal insulation materials, the insulation effect is poor, the HVAC equipment is relatively old, energy consumption is large, the lighting system does not use energy-saving lamps and lanterns, and the energy-saving performance is not good. So, there are generally low energy utilisation and high energy consumption defects that do not measure up to the current building energy-saving standards [[10], [11], [12]]. China has 43 billion square metres of existing public buildings that need energy-saving renovation [13], and the cost of demolishing or rebuilding them is considerable. Therefore, studying the energy consumption of existing public buildings has attracted significant attention. In 2005, there were a total of 43 oceanariums in China, mainly located in coastal areas, whose geographical location can effectively reduce costs. As of 2017, after more than a decade of development, there are now as many as 200 oceanariums across the country [[14], [15], [16]]. Currently, developed countries are carrying out targeted energy-saving retrofits of old venues with high energy consumption on a large scale. Most of the building energy retrofitting efforts in developed countries are as follows: as long as the buildings are still available for human use, they are not demolished and rebuilt, but the actual energy consumption of existing buildings is used as the basis for systematic energy retrofitting [17]. Some countries in Northern and Central Europe completed energy efficiency retrofitting of existing buildings by the end of the 20th century [18]. The Japanese government has set up a special fund to subsidise energy-saving retrofitting of existing venue buildings. It aims to reduce CO2 emission from retrofitted buildings by 20 % [19]. The government will subsidise one-third of the total retrofitting cost for buildings that make energy-saving improvements to HVAC and lighting systems, and some financial institutions will also provide related financing incentives [20]. At present, the comprehensive environmental performance evaluation of low-energy buildings in Japan after energy-saving retrofits has been recognised worldwide [21].

Oceanariums, a new tourism industry that integrates tourism and marine science [22], have been favoured by investment groups and local governments. Since 2000, many regions across the country have begun building oceanariums, which have greatly promoted local tourism development. A growing number of oceanariums are facing significant energy consumption problems. Consider an oceanarium with a floor area of 40,000 m2 as an example. The building consumes approximately 1156.71 tonnes (standard coal) of energy per year [23]. In addition to oceanarium air conditioning, ventilation systems, and lighting systems, most of the energy consumption for the oceanarium vivarium system (vivarium system is short for life maintenance systems) uses modern aquarium technology to mimic the natural environment and create an artificial water environment for marine life [24]. It includes the operation of six parts: gravity filter pool, heat and cold exchanger, pressure filter, trickling filter, treatment of ozone, and protein separator. The energy consumption used for temperature control accounts for 30–40 % of the total energy consumption of the system, which is often attributed to the energy consumption of the air conditioning system [25,26]. However, most mainstream building energy analysis and energy-saving renovation techniques currently use modelling software and simulation calculation results as the primary basis for analysis and renovation [27,28] and lack measures such as quantitative analysis and tailoring of building energy consumption. Therefore, studying building energy auditing based on accurate energy consumption monitoring data is of great significance and practical value.

Building energy audit technology

The main purpose of building energy audits is to objectively examine the building energy use efficiency, consumption level, and economic effect in energy-using units to quantitatively analyse the building energy use status of energy-using units. The potential for building energy efficiency and the location of energy consumption deficiencies can be identified by auditing, monitoring, diagnosing, and evaluating the efficiency of building energy use, consumption levels, energy economy, and environmental effects.

Building energy auditing is a method for the scientific management and analysis of building energy efficiency. The principle is to account for and evaluate the efficiency and consumption level of building energy use and the economic effect of energy use in energy-using units. To quantitatively analyse the status of building energy use in energy-using units and to audit, monitor, diagnose, and evaluate the efficiency, consumption level, energy economy, and environmental effects of building energy use to discover the potential of building energy efficiency. It is primarily based on the principles of building energy balance and energy ladder utilisation, energy cost analysis, engineering economic and environmental analysis, and the optimal allocation of energy use systems. When building submeasurement data are available, the submeasurement results can be used to conduct energy audits. When sub-measurement data are not available, schematic diagrams and operation records of distribution and substation systems, equipment operation records, on-site measured energy consumption of major equipment and major branch circuits, equipment nameplate information, and statistics on the number of typical annual working and non-working days of buildings can be used to calculate energy audits. Building energy audits can be divided into eight indicators: total building energy consumption, total conventional energy consumption, total special area energy consumption, HVAC system energy consumption, lighting system energy consumption, indoor equipment energy consumption, comprehensive service system energy consumption, and total building water consumption.

Energy efficiency measurement simulation technology

Building energy efficiency measurements use the CAD 3D modelling method to simulate and calculate the energy-saving rate (%), heating heat load index [W/m2], air conditioning cooling load index [W/m2], annual energy consumption [kWh/m2], and other building parameters by setting the building HVAC parameters, building envelope parameters, and building lighting parameters. It can analyse the changes in building energy consumption parameters before and after renovation and can be used for energy-saving labelling, programme revision, and optimisation of energy-saving renovation.

The PKPM building energy efficiency evaluation software has building energy analysis, energy consumption analysis, and energy-saving rate calculation functions. It has a large built-in database of building materials and related industries and can select the construction of walls, roofs, beams, columns, and floor slabs according to the actual situation of buildings. It can identify the setting parameters of doors, windows, walls, roofs, beams, columns, and rooms and calculate parameters such as body shape coefficients and window-wall ratios of buildings. It can select the climate parameters of the region to which the building belongs. It can analyse and calculate the annual energy consumption and relative energy-saving rate of the evaluated buildings and establish the building energy efficiency label grade with reference to the building energy efficiency evaluation standards.

Results and discussion

Research on the application of energy consumption monitoring technology

Taking the energy-saving renovation of an oceanarium in Jiangsu as an example, we elaborate on the application of an energy-consumption monitoring system. First, we assessed the basic situation of the building, which was built in 1996, before renovation was introduced. Because of the early age of the building, no energy-saving measures were considered. The building area was 17,647.31 m2, with one underground floor and six aboveground floors, a total height of approximately 20 m, a brick and concrete structure, and a seismic intensity of 7°. In terms of electrical and HVAC systems, two water-cooled screw central air conditioners are used as the cooling source, and 61 split- and one-drag multi air conditioners are used as the cooling and heating sources for the rest of the area. One gas boiler is used as the heat source for the oceanarium.

Overview of energy consumption monitoring before project renovation

From energy consumption statistics in recent years, it was found that a building's energy consumption greatly exceeded the existing energy-saving building standards, and the building's energy consumption had to be monitored to design a renovation plan. According to preliminary research, the oceanarium is equipped with ten low-voltage main distribution cabinet fed by two circuits and installed with two three-phase multi-functional meters. Twenty-five energy meters are installed in the low-voltage outlet circuit of the distribution cabinets, but they are not divided according to energy-using areas such as bright sockets, power, air conditioning, and special, so it is impossible to monitor the sub-energy consumption. With reference to the actual measurement of key parameters such as building energy use, energy management units, and capacity, combined with the requirements of relevant laws and regulations such as the Design standards for electricity submetering systems in public buildings (DB33/1090–2017) [33]. The electricity consumption of business hall lighting, corridors and emergency lighting, air-conditioning water-cooling units, VRV units, ventilation fans, pumps, elevators, fire protection, data rooms, and the natural gas consumption of the building's water system and boilers were categorised and measured.

Research on the application of building energy auditing techniques

A building energy audit is the process of inspecting, diagnosing, and auditing some or all of a building's energy activities, evaluating the reasonableness of energy use, and proposing improvement measures and recommendations. The goal of energy auditing is to identify and locate unreasonable energy uses, analyse energy-saving potential, and provide guidance for the energy-saving renovation of public buildings.

Diagnosis and analysis of HVAC system energy consumption

(1)Air conditioning system diagnostic analysis

Air conditioning system diagnostic analysis

The air-conditioning system comprises chillers, pumps, other equipment, and related valves and pipelines. First of all, according to the Qualified Value of Energy Efficiency and Energy Efficiency Class of Chiller Unit (GB 19577-2004) [36], when the rated cooling capacity of the water-cooled chiller unit is greater than 1163 kW and the COP is greater than 5.10, it belongs to Grade 3 energy efficiency, so the energy efficiency class of the chiller unit has not reached the requirement of Grade 2 energy efficiency.

Based on the calculation of the rated efficiency analysis of the cooling water circulation pump in Section 2.2, the cooling water pump configured in the chiller unit is highly energy-consuming elimination equipment and should be transformed into an energy-saving pump.

Further, full heat recovery fresh air units can be used in the centrally set areas of the building to effectively use natural heat and cold sources and reduce air conditioning energy consumption; split air conditioners adopt speed-controlled room air conditioners to meet the requirements of the energy efficiency ratio of Class 2 as stipulated in the Energy Efficiency Limiting Value and Energy Efficiency Class of Speed-Controlled Room Air Conditioners (GB21455-2013) [37]. A frequency conversion adjustment control is adopted for air conditioning refrigeration units, circulating pumps, and other equipment to adjust the speed according to the temperature condition and reduce the electricity consumption of the air conditioning equipment. According to the set value and deviation of the return air temperature, the coil-regulating valve was controlled to ensure the accuracy of the return air temperature.

(3)VRV air conditioning unit diagnostic analysis

VRV air conditioning unit diagnostic analysis

The integrated performance coefficient of refrigeration [IPLV(C)] of the original VRV air conditioning unit in the building = 3.25, and according to the Energy Efficiency Limiting Value and Energy Efficiency Rating of Multi-connected Air Conditioning (Heat Pump) Units (GB21454-2008) [38], the integrated performance coefficient of refrigeration [IPLV(C)] of the VRV air conditioning unit has met the requirements of the energy efficiency limiting value. However, the cooling integrated performance coefficient [IPLV(C)] of the VRV air-conditioning units does not meet the requirements of the energy efficiency evaluation value.

An on-site survey found that the actual operation of the VRV air conditioning unit, air conditioning noise, high current, and air conditioning effect were not good. Owing to the perennial lack of maintenance, the pipeline insulation layer fell off, which greatly increased the loss and attenuation of the refrigerant liquid tube cold and increased the possibility of pipeline corrosion, reducing the service life of the equipment.

Because it is difficult to detect the actual operating efficiency and can only be speculated from the usage conditions, the energy-saving performance of this unit can hardly meet the requirements of the Energy Conservation Design Standard for Public Buildings (GB 50189-2015) [39]. Thus, it is included in the scope of renovation.

According to Fig. 1 in the Energy Efficiency Limiting Value and Energy Conservation Evaluation Value of Clear Water Centrifugal Pumps [31], the uncorrected efficiency value η is 79.51 %, and the target limiting value is 77.51 % when the flow rate of the multi-stage single-suction clearwater centrifugal pump is 85 m3/h.

Table 1 in the Energy Efficiency Limiting Value and Energy Conservation Evaluation Value of Clear Water Centrifugal Pumps [31] shows that when the specific speed ns = 74.64, the efficiency correction value Δη = 4.07 %.

Pump specified point efficiency value η0 = uncorrected efficiency value η - efficiency correction value Δη = 79.51 %–4.07 % = 75.44 %.

Pump target energy efficiency limit value η2 = target limit value η- efficiency correction value = 77.51 %–4.07 % = 73.44 %.

Pump specified point energy efficiency limit value η1 = pump specified point efficiency value η0- 4 % = 75.44 %–4 % = 71.44 %.

Pump energy efficiency evaluation value η3 = pump prescribed point efficiency value η0 +1 % = 75.44 % + 1 % = 76.44 %.

After checking the nameplate of the project boiler hot-water circulation pump, we can observe that its rated efficiency is 66 %, and the energy efficiency level is lower than the target limit of 73.44 %, which is high-energy consumption elimination equipment.

From the above calculations, it can be observed that the gas boiler of the oceanarium and its equipped hot-water circulation pump are both highly energy-consuming equipment and should be replaced by energy-saving pumps.

Furthermore, adjustable heating end heat and heat metering devices can be used, and energy-saving effects of 30–50 % can be achieved by applying heat network control, regulation, and frequency conversion technology.

Conclusion

Energy consumption monitoring technology for existing public buildings is an important initiative for reducing urban energy consumption in line with national energy conservation and emission reduction policies. In response to the shortcomings of energy-saving renovations of existing buildings, which are mostly based on qualitative analysis, high reliance on energy-saving calculation software, and insufficient accuracy and quantification, a scientific study of an existing public building based on energy consumption monitoring and a building energy audit of its energy consumption monitoring results throughout the year was conducted, with the following specific work and results.

(1)In this study, the monitoring results of a building energy consumption monitoring system were subjected to a scientific building energy audit, and the quantitative analysis results of the energy consumption of existing buildings were obtained through an in-depth analysis of the energy consumption monitoring data. The average electricity consumption per unit area is 108.67 kWh/(m2•a), and the annual energy consumption is 19,17588 kWh, i.e., 44.72 kgce/(m2•a), which is far more than the prescribed value of energy consumption of corresponding energy-saving buildings.

In this study, the monitoring results of a building energy consumption monitoring system were subjected to a scientific building energy audit, and the quantitative analysis results of the energy consumption of existing buildings were obtained through an in-depth analysis of the energy consumption monitoring data. The average electricity consumption per unit area is 108.67 kWh/(m2•a), and the annual energy consumption is 19,17588 kWh, i.e., 44.72 kgce/(m2•a), which is far more than the prescribed value of energy consumption of corresponding energy-saving buildings.

(2)The energy consumption of the water, electricity, and gas systems of the oceanarium before the renovation is calculated, analysed, tested using energy audit technology, and other related studies are conducted. The conclusions of the energy audit are given as follows: the chiller operation should be used to strengthen daily energy-saving management; split- and one-tow multi air conditioners are not up to standard; heating and steam boilers need to be replaced; the external envelope is not up to standard; the lighting system needs to be replaced; and the pressure of the water supply system at the lower floors needs to be reformed.

(3)Energy efficiency measurement techniques were used to model the oceanarium before and after the renovation, and simulations were calculated. The relative energy saving rate of the building before and after the retrofit increased from −15.6 % to 5.24 %, the annual energy consumption (electricity) decreased from 1427700.88 kWh to 1176294.88 kWh, the discounted energy consumption decreased from 172.26 kgce to 164.09 kgce, and the annual energy consumption converted to standard coal decreased from 498.89 tonnes to 475.22 tonnes, among other changes in the simulated values.

The relative energy saving rate of the building before and after the retrofit increased from −15.6 % to 5.24 %, the annual energy consumption (electricity) decreased from 1427700.88 kWh to 1176294.88 kWh, the discounted energy consumption decreased from 172.26 kgce to 164.09 kgce, and the annual energy consumption converted to standard coal decreased from 498.89 tonnes to 475.22 tonnes, among other changes in the simulated values.

In summary, this study combines the three technologies of building energy consumption submetering technology, building energy audit technology, and building energy efficiency measurement technology to develop the most scientific and reasonable energy-saving renovation plan for the existing oceanarium. This approach is more relevant, effective, and practical than the software calculation and blind renovation of oceanarium buildings by the design institute.

Comparsion of CURB 65, PSI and SMART-COP Systems as Predictors of 30-Day Mortality in Community Acquired Pneumonia
Authors: Tintu Jacob, Jai Prakash Yadav, Asit Kumar Pati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCAP continues to pose a significant healthcare challenge in India, particularly in vulnerable populations such as the elderly and those with comorbidities. The choice of scoring systems is crucial in risk stratification of CAP patients, especially in resource-limited settings. The need for early identification of high-risk patients and timely ICU management cannot be overstated, as delays in care often lead to poorer outcomes and higher mortality rates.ObjectivesThis study has compared the prognostic accuracy of the CURB-65, PSI, and SMART-COP scoring systems in predicting 30-day mortality and clinical outcomes, such as the need for intensive respiratory support and ICU admission, in adult patients with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP).MethodsThis cross sectional prospective observational study was conducted from May 2023 to April 2024 in the medical wards and ICU of a tertiary care hospital, enrolling 82 consecutively recruited adult patients diagnosed with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) based on Joint ICS/NCCP(I) criteria. Detailed sociodemographic, clinical, laboratory, radiological, and microbiological data were collected using structured questionnaires, and each patient's severity was assessed using the CURB-65, PSI, and SMART-COP scoring systems. Patients were followed daily for 30 days to document outcomes including mortality, ICU stay, ventilation days, vasopressor use, and duration of hospitalization and antibiotics. Statistical analysis included comparisons of clinical variables, correlation of severity scores with outcomes, and ROC analysis to evaluate the predictive performance of the three scoring systems for 30-day mortality.ResultsThe results demonstrate that the PSI scoring system exhibited the highest predictive accuracy for 30-day mortality (p = 0.002), followed closely by CURB 65 (p = 0.024) and SMART COP (p = 0.001). The patients classified in the severe categories of these scoring systems had significantly higher mortality rates, underscoring the importance of early and accurate risk stratification in clinical practice. the study found that these scoring systems could effectively predict the need for intensive respiratory support (p < 0.0001) and vasopressor therapy (p < 0.0001), as well as the likelihood of ICU admission (p < 0.0001). This is crucial for optimizing resource allocation and patient management in critical care settings. Notably, comorbidities such as coronary artery disease (CAD) (unadjusted odds ratio [OR] = 6.04, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.223–29.877, p = 0.034) and findings like pleural effusion (unadjusted OR = 12.321, 95% CI: 2.168–70.028, p = 0.004) were associated with significantly increased mortality risks,ConclusionsA key takeaway from this research is that implementing these scoring systems and identifying some clinical parameters and comorbidities can enhance clinical decision-making processes, allowing healthcare professionals to identify high-risk patients promptly particularly in resource-limited environments.Keywords: Scores, Mortality, Community acquired pneumonia, ICU admission, Respiratory and ventilatory support

Prevalence and epidemic pattern of ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis during 2012–2022 in Hangzhou, China: implication for public health strategies
Authors: Qingchun Li, Yifei Wu, Qinglin Cheng, Min Lu, Yinyan Huang, Xuexin Bai, Qingjun Jia, Zijian Fang, Liyun Ai, Nan Jiang, QiuFeng Lao, Li Xie, Junfang Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo assess the prevalence and epidemic pattern of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis in Hangzhou City, Zhejiang Province, China during 2012–2022.MethodsAll the tuberculosis cases undergoing drug susceptibility testing during 2012–2022 were included in this study. De-identified information was extracted from the electronic database Tuberculosis Information Management System for analysis of drug resistance prevalence in Hangzhou and ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis which originated from other regions. Chi-square tests were used to compare drug resistance rates between different groups, while Chi-square tests for trend were used to evaluate the change of drug resistance rates over the years of 2012–2022. The sources and destinations of ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis were illustrated using a Sankey diagram.ResultsOf 21,127 cases included in this study, 1119 (5.3%) were multidrug-resistant tuberculosis. A significant decline in multidrug-resistant tuberculosis rates was observed during 2012–2022. There was a significant difference in multidrug-resistant tuberculosis rates among immigrant population and local residents in Hangzhou City. Of 1119 multidrug-resistant tuberculosis cases, 515(46%) were ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis cases, of which 277(53.8%) were from other parts of Zhejiang Province and 238(46.2%) were from other provinces in China. Anhui, Jiangxi and Sichuan were among top three provinces which were the source of ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis cases. Three districts including Xiaoshan, Shangcheng and Linping districts had the most cases in Hangzhou. The proportion of ecdemic multidrug-resistant tuberculosis cases in Binjiang, Xiaoshan, Qiantang and Linping districtalso exceeded 30% of total cases.ConclusionsMultidrug-resistant tuberculosis prevalence has been declining in Hangzhou. Migrant population contributed to a significant potion of cases in Hangzhou. Interventions should be tailed to local and migrant residents.Keywords: MDR, TB, Ecdemic, Prevalence, Hangzhou, China

Introduction

According to a 2022 report from the World Health Organization (WHO) [1], the COVID-19 pandemic has had significant impacts on tuberculosis (TB) disease burden and access to TB diagnosis and treatment. Due to the quarantine policies and social distancing measures implemented globally during the COVID-19 pandemic, individuals were unable to seek medical care in a timely manner. Consequently, numerous TB patients did not receive prompt diagnosis and treatment, leading to a decrease in the reported global TB case numbers in 2019 and 2020. The lack of prompt diagnosis and treatment in these patients resulted in an increased number of TB-related deaths and enhanced community transmission of the infection, which was subsequently followed by a rise in the incidence of TB [2]. The progress that had been made prior to 2019 has now slowed down, stagnated or even regressed, causing global TB targets to be off track.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, there was a 17% decrease in the number of people receiving treatment for Rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB) and Multidrug-resistant TB (MDR-TB), which could potentially result in higher mortality rates and the spread of drug-resistant tuberculosis [1, 2]. In 2022, only 43% of MDR-TB/RR-TB patients were diagnosed and treated, which was lower than that before the COVID-19 pandemic in 2019. The situation regarding the prevention and control of MDR-TB/RR-TB is even worse. MDR-TB is characterized by a prolonged treatment duration, high treatment costs, low cure rates, and limited availability of drugs [3, 4]. It requires more attention [3, 5], particularly in the post-COVID-19 era [6].

In recent years, the strategy for MDR-TB control and prevention has been strengthened in Hangzhou city [7], including the following measures: (1) The eligibility for drug susceptibility testing (DST) has been expanded from smear-positive TB patients and high-risk patients to include all bacteriologically positive TB patients, in order to improve MDR-TB case detection [8]; (2) The introduction of the GeneXpert MTB/RIF rapid DST assay, which combines testing for Mycobacterium tuberculosis with screening for rifampicin resistance to provide same-day results. The costs for this test are either fully or mostly covered by the government; (3) Enhanced reimbursement rates for medical insurance for tuberculosis, especially for MDR-TB patients; (4) Increased governmental investment in TB-designated hospitals located in Hangzhou. These measures have gradually produced positive impacts on MDR-TB control, with a significant decrease in the prevalence of MDR-TB from 11.6 to 8.0% from 2011 to 2015 [9], and a significant increase in the treatment success rate of MDR-TB from 66 to 85% [8]. However, there are continuous challenges in providing TB services to vulnerable groups such as migrants [7, 10]. Hangzhou, as the capital of Zhejiang province, has experienced a growing migrant population in recent years, with the number of long-term residents increasing from 7.0 million in 2012 [11] to 12.37 million in 2022 [12]. The migrant population has contributed to the majority of the growth during China’s rapid urbanization. Considering the overall high treatment success rate in Hangzhou, lack of effective vaccines for MDR-TB [13], and limited availability of new treatment regimens and drugs for MDR-TB [14, 15], understanding the prevalence and pattern of MDR among the migrant population can help in the timely detection and treatment of endemic MDR-TB patients, thereby controlling the spread of MDR and addressing the shortcomings in MDR-TB treatment and management in Hangzhou, particularly in the context of the high prevalence of primary drug resistance due to transmission that remains high in Hangzhou [9]. However, little is known about the situation of MDR among the migrant population in Hangzhou. The present study aims to uncover the prevalence and pattern of endemic MDR during 2012–2022 in Hangzhou.

Methods

Study setting, design, database, and participants

Hangzhou is the capital city of Zhejiang Province in eastern China. It comprises 11 districts, one county-level city, and two counties, with a population of 12.37 million long-term residents. There are 13 hospitals that provide TB care services, including two hospitals with the capacity to treat drug-resistant TB.

This retrospective analysis utilized de-identified information extracted from the Tuberculosis Information Management System (TBIMS), a national electronic database maintained by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention [16]. Since TB is a notifiable infectious disease in China, all diagnosed cases are reported to TBIMS, which serves as a central repository for TB diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring data. All TB cases undergoing DST during 2012–2022 in the designated TB hospitals in Hangzhou were included in this study. The eligibility for DST during 2012–2016 included all smear-positive TB cases and high-risk individuals, such as cases with recurrent TB, close contacts of MDR-TB cases, cases who experienced treatment failure, relapse, or retreatment, and cases who remained smear-positive at the end of the second or third month of initial treatment. The eligibility was expanded to include all bacteriologically positive TB cases during 2017–2022. Socio-demographic information (gender, age, occupation, current residency, registered household or “Hukou”, ethnicity) and clinical information (previous TB treatment history, results of sputum microscopy, DST results) were abstracted from TBIMS. Cases with incomplete information or logical errors were excluded from this study.

Definitions

Migrants were defined as cases whose registered household was not in Hangzhou. MDR-TB is defined as resistance to the two most common anti-TB drugs, isoniazid (H) and rifampin (R). ORR-TB refers to resistance to rifampicin only, while HR-TB refers to resistance to isoniazid only. SHR indicates susceptibility to both isoniazid and rifampicin. According to the WHO’s report, extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (XDR-TB) was defined as resistance to any fluoroquinolone and at least one of three second-line injectable drugs (capreomycin, kanamycin, and amikacin), in addition to multidrug resistance. Pre-extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (Pre-XDR-TB) was defined as additional resistance to second-line drugs, either a fluoroquinolone or at least one of the three second-line injectable drugs (amikacin, kanamycin, and capreomycin), before 2021. From 2021 onwards, XDR-TB is defined as TB caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis strains that fulfill the definition of MDR/RR-TB and are also resistant to any fluoroquinolone and at least one additional Group A drug. Pre-XDR-TB is defined as TB caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis strains that fulfill the definition of MDR/RR-TB and are also resistant to any fluoroquinolone [17].

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using SPSS 19.0 software. Frequencies and proportions were calculated for categorical variables, while Means and Standard deviation were calculated for continuous variables. Chi-square tests were employed to compare the differences in drug resistance rates between different groups. Chi-square tests for trend were applied to compare the differences in drug resistance rates from 2012 to 2022. The Sankey diagram was utilized to illustrate the sources and destinations of ecdemic MDR-TB. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics of TB cases

During 2012–2022, a total of 27,572 TB cases in Hangzhou underwent species identification of mycobacteria and DST, 464 were diagnosed with ORR-TB, 1,119 with MDR-TB, 1,135 with HR-TB, 17 with XDR-TB, 12 with Pre-XDR-TB, and 18,380 with SHR. A total of 6,446 cases were excluded from the study, including 1,418 cases of non-tuberculosis mycobacteria and 4,964 cases with incomplete DST results or logical errors. Consequently, the study included a total of 21,127 cases.

Among the 21,127 cases included in the study, the mean age was 51.5 ± 20.9 years. Of these cases, 14,716 (69.7%) were male and 6,411 (30.3%) were female. The majority of cases, 20,927 (99.1%), belonged to the Han ethnicity, while 200 (0.9%) belonged to other ethnic minorities. In terms of occupation, 13,028 (61.7%) were workers, farmers, or migrant workers, and 8,099 (38.3%) had other occupations. Regarding registered residence, 15,353 (72.7%) had Hangzhou Hukou (registered residence), while 5,774 (27.3%) did not. In terms of TB treatment history, 3,488 (16.5%) had previous TB treatment, while 17,639 (83.5%) had no previous TB treatment history.

Drug resistance patterns of TB cases in Hangzhou

Out of the 21,127 cases, 1,119 (5.3%) were diagnosed with MDR-TB, 464 (2.2%) with ORR-TB, 17 (0.08%) with XDR-TB, 12 (0.06%) with Pre-XDR-TB, and 1,135 (5.37%) with HR-TB. Significant declines were observed for rates of MDR, ORR, XDR, Pre-XDR and HR from 2012 to 2022 (P < 0.01) (Table 1).

Ecdemic and local MDR-TB in Hangzhou City during 2012–2022

Among the 515 ecdemic MDR-TB cases, 277 (53.8%) were from other prefectures in Zhejiang Province, 35 (6.8%) were from Anhui Province, 33 (6.4%) were from Jiangxi Province, 21 (4.1%) were from Sichuan Province, and 149 (28.9%) were from other provinces in China.

Of the 515 ecdemic MDR-TB cases, 207 (40.2%) were currently residing in Hangzhou. Among these cases, 25 (12.1%) were from Anhui Province, 25 (12.1%) were from Jiangxi Province, 23 (11.1%) were from other prefectures in Zhejiang Province, 15 (7.2%) were from Sichuan Province, and 119 (57.5%) were from other provinces in China (Fig. 1).

Sources and destinations of ecdemic MDR-TB in Hangzhou during 2012-2022

Among the 207 ecdemic MDR-TB cases residing in Hangzhou, 36 (17.4%) lived in Xiaoshan District, 30 (14.5%) lived in Shangcheng District, 25 (12.1%) lived in Linping District, 24 (11.6%) lived in Yuhang District, 20 (9.7%) lived in Gongshu District, and 72 (34.6%) lived in other districts in Hangzhou. There were no ecdemic MDR-TB cases in Chunan County and West Lake Scenic District (WLSD) (Fig. 1).

Among the 207 ecdemic MDR-TB cases, those from Anhui province were distributed across multiple districts in Hangzhou city. Cases from Jiangxi province predominantly flowed into Linping District, Yuhang District, and Binjiang District. Cases from Sichuan province predominantly flowed into Shangcheng District and Xiaoshan District. Cases from other prefectures in Zhejiang province predominantly flowed into Gongshu District and Xihu District (Fig. 1).

Ecdemic MDR-TB accounted for 52.4% of all MDR-TB cases in Binjiang District, the proportions were 38.1%, 37.9%, and 35.2% for Qiantang District, Xiaoshan District, and Linping District, respectively (Table 4).

Discussion

Our study revealed that the overall MDR rate during 2012–2022 in Hangzhou City was 5.3%, decreasing to 2.2% in 2022. It is lower than those from other studies conducted in other parts of China or other countries. For example, a systematic review and spatio-temporal analysis of studies in China reported MDR rates of 12.8% among all cases, 5.4% among new cases, and 28.5% among previously treated cases in 2012 [18]. A study conducted in Zhejiang province found an MDR rate of 6.4% in 2013 [19]. Another study in Beijing from 2002 to 2014 reported an MDR rate of 16.7% [20]. A study in Shenzhen involving 19,951 participants showed an MDR rate of 5.7% from 2012 to 2020 [21]. In Liberia, a study from 2013 to 2016 revealed an MDR rate of 23.5% [22]. A systematic review in Ethiopia indicated an MDR rate of 7.24%, with some regions reaching 18.5% [23, 24]. A systematic review in India reported an MDR rate of 27.9% between 2006 and 2015 [25]. Despite the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on TB during 2020–2022 [26–28], considering previous research findings [9], we can still conclude that the MDR prevalence in Hangzhou City was in the lower range of the epidemics in China and globally.

Although the MDR rate among retreatment cases was higher than that among newly diagnosed cases, previous research conducted in Hangzhou has shown that nearly half of the drug resistance cases are due to primary drug resistance rather than acquired drug resistance [9, 29]. Therefore, efforts should be focused on screening for drug resistance during case finding and reducing transmission by improving the treatment and management of drug-resistant cases. It is also important to restore critical tuberculosis healthcare services and implement policies for sustained mask-wearing in the post-COVID-19 era [30].

Significant declines were observed for rates of MDR, ORR, XDR, Pre-XDR and HR from 2012 to 2022 in this study. Combined with previous study conducted in Hangzhou [9], in which, significant declines of resistance to any first-line drugs and MDR rates from 2011 to 2015 were observed, we can have a conclusion that the drug resistance rates of TB in the Hangzhou has been showing a declining trend. These results were attributed to a series of prevention and control measures implemented in Hangzhou over the years, including: the high-level implementation of the DOTS-Plus program, expanding the eligibility for DST, and increased government investment. Additionally, the higher MDR treatment success rates contribute to reducing the transmission of MDR and thereby lowering the drug resistance rates [8].

In our study, ecdemic MDR-TB accounted for 46% of the total MDR-TB cases, which is relatively high compared to other regions. A study in Shanghai reported that 53% of MDR-TB cases were internal migrants [31], while a study in South Korea from 2013 to 2018 found that immigrants accounted for only 4% of the total MDR-TB cases [32]. Another study in Portugal from 2014 to 2017 showed that immigrants accounted for 32.9% of the total MDR-TB cases [33]. Unlike cross-border immigrants in developed countries, internal immigrants are more transient and difficult to access by the city’s healthcare system, posing challenges in controlling transmissible diseases such as MDR-TB. The high proportion of ecdemic MDR-TB cases and the elevated MDR rate among immigrants, along with previous research highlighting immigrants as a high-risk population for MDR-TB [31, 32, 34], underscore the importance of prioritizing targeted MDR prevention and control strategies for the migrant population [35].

The study findings revealed that 53.8% of the ecdemic MDR-TB cases originated from other prefectures in Zhejiang Province, but the majority of them did not reside in Hangzhou. This is because Hangzhou is home to the Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital, the designated tuberculosis diagnosis and treatment center for the entire province. Many cases from other prefectures in Zhejiang Province seek medical treatment in Hangzhou due to the presence of this specialized hospital. The remaining ecdemic MDR-TB cases primarily came from economically underdeveloped areas such as Anhui Province, Jiangxi Province, and Sichuan Province, as people from these regions migrate to Hangzhou in search of employment opportunities. These findings underscore the need to differentiate between cases from other prefectures in Zhejiang Province and those from other provinces when designing MDR prevention and control strategies. Moreover, it is crucial to strengthen screening efforts for cases from Anhui Province, Jiangxi Province and Sichuan Province.

Among the 207 ecdemic MDR-TB cases residing in Hangzhou, the main destinations were Xiaoshan District, Shangcheng District, and Linping District. Additionally, ecdemic MDR-TB cases in Binjiang District accounted for over half of the total MDR-TB cases in that area between 2012 and 2022. Moreover, Qiantang District, Xiaoshan District, Linping District, Xihu District, and Yuhang District had a relatively high proportion of ecdemic MDR-TB cases, all surpassing 30%. On the contrary, Chun’an County and the West Lake Scenic District had no ecdemic MDR-TB cases, while Tonglu County and Jiande City had a proportion of MDR-TB cases below 10%. These findings highlight the importance of developing distinct strategies for MDR prevention and control tailored to the specific districts and counties in Hangzhou.

This study also had limitations. First, we did not assess whether the cases were infected with MDR-TB before or after coming to Hangzhou, which could affect the interpretation of the results. Second, the economic landscape of Hangzhou is changing, which may result in variations in the composition of future immigrant populations. This could impact the implications of the study for future applications. Future studies that capture comprehensive information on all migrant cases, including their MDR infection status before and after arrival in Hangzhou, as well as their movement over time and space, would provide a more accurate understanding.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study revealed an overall declining trend in MDR rate in Hangzhou during 2012–2022. Immigrants contributed to a significant portion of MDR-TB cases in the city, targeted prevention and control strategies should be implemented for TB cases from different regions, as well as for different districts within Hangzhou where the proportion of ecdemic MDR cases is relatively high, in order to reduce the spread of drug resistance.

Analysis of the Spatial Distribution Characteristics of Crude Oils with Different Freezing Points and the Genetic Mechanism of High-Freezing-Point Crude Oils in the Dongying Sag
Authors: Zicheng Niu, Yongshi Wang, Zheng Li, Ru Wang, Xin Wang, Xiuhong Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The freezing point of crude oils is an important factor affecting the process of exploration, development, and transportation of crude oils. In the Dongying Sag, the freezing point of crude oils varies greatly in space, and high-freezing-point crude oils have been found in many structural belts. The freezing point of crude oils in the Dongying Sag is generally high, ranging from −40 to 80 °C. The distribution of high-freezing-point crude oils in the Dongying Sag is scattered, and they have been found at different depths, layers, and structural belts, with relatively more occurrences in specific areas. Based on the relationship between the wax content and the resin + asphaltene content, the high-freezing-point crude oils in the Dongying Sag can be classified into three types (type A, type B, and type C). Type A, type B, and type C high-freezing-point crude oils are mainly found in the gentle slope zone, depression zone, and uplift zone, respectively. The contents of wax and resins + asphaltenes in crude oils are the key factors controlling the freezing point of crude oils in the Dongying Sag. Oil source correlation using biomarkers and n-alkane carbon isotopes demonstrates that different types of high-freezing-point crude oils are generated from different source rocks, indicating that the source of crude oils is not the only factor controlling the freezing point of crude oils. The hydrocarbon-generating parent material of hydrocarbon source rocks developed in different depositional environments of the Dongying Sag is generally characterized by the dominance of bacteria and algae and is supplemented by terrestrial higher plants. This lays the material foundation for the formation of waxes during the maturation of hydrocarbon source rocks. The change of temperature and pressure conditions during the process of oil expulsion from source rocks and migration in the carrier bed will lead to phase fractionation and composition changes of oils, thus forming type A freezing point crude oils in the slope zone and type B freezing point oils in the depression zone. The increase in resin and asphaltene content caused by biodegradation and water washing in shallower buried oil reservoirs is the reason for the formation of type C high-freezing-point crude oils. The research results indicate that the organic matter composition and hydrocarbon alteration during migration and accumulation jointly control the formation of high-freezing-point crude oils in lacustrine basins. The extensive input of terrestrial organic matter into the source rocks of the lacustrine basin laid the foundation for the formation of high-freezing-point crude oils, and the migration fractionation and biodegradation of the crude oils further promoted the formation of high-freezing-point crude oils.

Introduction

The freezing point of crude oils is an important parameter for evaluating their physical characteristics. It is the critical temperature at which crude oil changes from a liquid to a solid state and the temperature at which it loses its fluidity during the process of temperature reduction.1,2 The freezing point of crude oils has an important effect on their exploitation and transportation process.3,4 For crude oils with high freezing points, solidification is more easily to occur as the temperature decreases, which not only reduces the efficiency of crude oil extraction and transportation but also causes pipeline blockage or burst accidents.5 According to the Chinese industry standard SY/T 5735-1995,6 crude oils are divided into three types based on their freezing point. Crude oils with a freezing point greater than 40 °C are defined as high-freezing-point (HFP) crude oils, those with a freezing point between 0 and 40 °C are defined as medium-freezing-point (MFP) crude oils, and those with a freezing point below 0 °C are defined as low-freezing-point (LFP) crude oils. High freezing points are often associated with high wax contents as waxes in crude oils tend to crystallize and precipitate out during temperature reduction, reducing the fluidity of crude oils.7 The formation of HFP crude oils is also always attributed to the enrichment of the waxy components in the crude oils.8−10 However, crude oil is a hydrocarbon mixture containing paraffin wax, aromatic hydrocarbon, resins, asphaltenes, and other components.11,12 Besides the wax, which is the main component that tends to solidify during temperature reduction, other heavy components such as resins and asphaltenes may experience a similar process.13,14 On the other hand, resins and asphaltenes of different polarities and at different concentrations have different roles in wax precipitation characteristics, yielding different effects on the freezing point of the crude oils.15−17 In general, the formation of HFP crude oils is a complex process, and the geological mechanism of their formation has not been systematically analyzed.

The Dongying Sag is a subtectonic unit within the Jiyang Depression of the Bohai Bay Basin and is one of the most productive oil- and gas-producing areas in eastern China.18−20 The sag is rich in hydrocarbon resources and has a full range of reservoir types, but the extent of hydrocarbon discovery within the depression varies widely in space. After about 60 years of oil and gas exploration and development, dozens of oil fields have been built in the Dongying Sag, with a cumulative proven oil geological reserve of over 2.5 billion tonnes.21,22 Oil found in the Archean, Paleozoic, Mesozoic, and Cenozoic reservoirs exhibits great variation in physical properties such as crude oil density, viscosity, wax content, freezing point, etc.23,24 In the past, a lot of work has been carried out on the formation and accumulation of oil and gas in the Dongying Sag, and the development characteristics of source rocks,25,26 the types and sources of oil and gas,27,28 and the migration and accumulation process of oil and gas have been fully recognized and determined.29−31 However, the physical properties of crude oils, especially the freezing point characteristics of crude oils, the spatial distribution characteristics of crude oils with different freezing points, and the geological reasons for the formation of such characteristics, have not been thoroughly analyzed. The aim of this study is to determine the spatial variation characteristics of the freezing point of crude oils in the Dongying Sag and the distribution pattern of high-freezing-point crude oils. On this basis, the mechanism of the formation of HFP crude oils developed in different structural belts is determined. The research results can improve our understanding of the development patterns of HFP crude oils and can also provide better guidance for the exploration of hydrocarbons in lacustrine basins.

Abundant crude oil samples have been collected, and corresponding geochemical experimental analyses have been carried out from different oil-bearing layers and structural zones in the Dongying Sag. These massive experimental analysis data ensure that we can systematically study the spatial distribution characteristics of crude oil freezing points in the Dongying Sag and the origin of such distribution characteristics. In this study, all of the data of the Dongying Sag crude oil samples with physical property test data from the Shengli Oilfield were collected and statistically analyzed to study the distribution of the crude oil freezing point. On this basis, the types of HFP crude oils are divided, and the sources of different types of HFP crude oils are determined through oil source correlation. Going a step further, starting from the solidification mechanism of crude oils, the formation mechanism of different types of HFP crude oils is systematically analyzed to reveal the factors controlling the formation of HFP oils in the Dongying Sag.

Geological Background

The Dongying Sag is located in the southeast region of the Jiyang Depression with an asymmetrical half-graben morphology and covers an area of 5700 km2. The Dongying Sag is bounded by the Chenjiazhuang and Guangrao uplifts on its north and south, respectively (Figure 1). It has experienced a complex tectonic evolution process, including (1) formation, deformation, and consolidation of the platform basement from the Archean to the early–middle Proterozoic; (2) stable platform development from the late Proterozoic to the Paleozoic; and (3) rift-basin development in the Cenozoic.32−34 A series of secondary structural units developed in the Dongying Sag, including the Northern steep slope zone, the Central uplift zone, the Niuzhuang subsag, the Lijin subsag, the Boxing subsag, the Minfeng subsag, and the Southern gentle slope zone.35 The Northern steep slope zone, the Central uplift zone, and the Southern gentle slope zone consist of a series of fault belts (Figure 1).

Geographic and structural setting of the Dongying Sag. 1–12 represent the Linnan fault belt, Binnan fault belt, Lijin fault belt, Shengtuo fault belt, Central fault belt, Chenguanzhuang fault belt, Wangjiagang fault belt, Bamianhe fault belt, Shicun fault belt, Boxing fault belt, Jinjia fault belt, and Gaoqing fault belt, respectively. I–VI represent the Boxing subsag, Lijing subsag, Minfeng subsag, Niuzhuang subsag, Lizezhen subsag, and Qingnan subsag, respectively.

The Dongying Sag is mainly infilled with the Paleozoic (Pz), Mesozoic (Mz), and Cenozoic (Cz) strata. The Cenozoic strata include Kongdian Formation (Ek), Shahejie Formation (Es), Dongying Formation (Ed), Guantao Formation (Ng), Minghuazhen Formation (Nm), and Pingyuan Formation (Qp), of which Shahejie Formation is further divided into four members (Figure 2). Three sets of source rocks are developed in the Dongying Sag: the lower part of the third member of the Shahejie Formation (Es3L), the upper part of the fourth member of the Shahejie Formation (Es4U), and the second member of the Kongjiang Formation (Ek2). The Es3L was deposited in a freshwater lacustrine setting, while the Es4U was deposited in a brackish-to-saline lacustrine setting.36 A total of 12 sets of oil-bearing strata with burial depth ranging from 700 to 5200 m, such as the Archean, the Cambrian, the Ordovician, the fourth member of the Shahejie Formation, and the Kongdian Formation, have been confirmed, and several oil fields such as Shengtuo, Binnan, Le’an, Wangjiagang, and Bamianhe have been discovered.37 Various types of oil reservoirs are widely developed in the study area, such as structural oil reservoirs formed by forward and reverse fault blocks,38 buried hill oil reservoirs in the Lower Paleozoic,39 lithological oil reservoirs formed by beach bar sand and turbidite sand, and stratigraphic oil reservoirs in nappe structure. The physical properties of crude oils found in the study area vary greatly. Reservoirs with special oil properties such as low-maturity oil,40 high-wax-content oil,41 and high-sulfur-content oil have developed, reflecting the complexity of crude oil sources and accumulation processes. The crude oils in the reservoirs of different layers in the Dongying Sag have experienced multiple episodic charges of distinctive maturities from different source rocks.42 In shallow buried areas, especially at the edge of the depression, the accumulated crude oils generally suffer from different levels of biodegradation, which significantly changes the physical properties of the crude oils in the reservoirs.43−45

Samples and Methods

Physical property data including gravity, viscosity, wax content, and freezing point of more than 3000 crude oils samples with varying ages and depths were collated from Shengli Oilfield Limited, China Petroleum & Chemical Corporation. The SARA (saturate, aromatic, resin, asphaltene) contents of about 300 samples among them were determined by open-column liquid chromatography and gravimetry. These analyzed samples were collected in the Binnan fault belt, Lijin fault belt, Shengtuo fault belt, Chenguanzhuang fault belt, Wangjiagang fault belt, Bamianhe fault belt, Shicun fault belt, Boxing fault belt, Jinjia fault belt, Gaoqing fault belt, Boxing subsag, Lijing subsag, Niuzhuang subsag, etc. The sampling layers included An∈ (Precambrian), Pz, Ek, Es, and Ng. The representative samples were selected for saturated hydrocarbon chromatography–mass spectrometry and n-alkane carbon isotope analyses. All data were measured using standard methods, so only brief descriptions of the analytical methods are given here. The freezing points of crude oils were determined according to SY/T 0541-2009 “Determination of Freezing Point of Crude Oils”.46 Oil samples for GC–MS analyses were fractionated into saturated hydrocarbons, aromatic hydrocarbons, and polar fractions using an aluminum/silica gel column sequentially eluted with hexane, dichloromethane/hexane (2:1), and chloroform/ethanol (1:1) after the asphaltenes were precipitated. The saturated fraction was subjected to GC–MS analysis using an Agilent 7890-5975c instrument fitted to an HP-5MS 60m fused silica capillary column. The gas chromatography–isotope ratio mass spectrometry (GC–IRMS) analyses were performed on a VG Isochrom II instrument. A standard mixture of individual n-alkanes (nC12, nC16, nC20, nC22, nC24, nC27, and nC32) with known isotopic composition was used daily to test the performance of the instrument.

Results

Distribution Characteristics of Crude Oils with Different Freezing Points

The exploration and development history of the Dongying Sag covers a wide range of time, and the crude oil samples obtained represent different periods. To eliminate the adverse effects of sampling and testing time, the obtained physical property data of crude oils were systematically analyzed and screened. From this data, 3190 effective crude oil freezing point data points were screened and their distribution characteristics were analyzed. From the distribution histogram of crude oil freezing points in the Dongying Sag (Figure 3), it can be seen that the crude oil freezing points are widely distributed, ranging from −40 to 80 °C. The distribution characteristics of freezing points show that the number of MFP crude oil samples is the largest, followed by LFP crude oils, and HFP crude oils. However, among the MFP crude oil samples, a large number of samples have freezing points close to the standard of HFP crude oils. Overall, the samples with a freezing point greater than 40 °C account for about 4%, while the samples with a freezing point between 30 and 40 °C account for more than 50%, reflecting that the crude oils in the Dongying Sag have a high freezing point as a whole.

Distribution of the freezing points of crude oils in the Dongying Sag.

The enrichment levels of the LFP, MFP, and HFP crude oils at different depths display obvious differences (Figure 4). The distribution depth range of LFP crude oils is relatively concentrated, which is generally shallower than 2000 m. In contrast, the distribution depth of MFP crude oils and HFP crude oils is very scattered, and they are developed in almost every depth range. Considering that oil reservoirs with different depth ranges often experience different oil and gas accumulation processes and secondary alteration, the wide depth distribution range of HFP oil samples indicates the nonsinglicity and complexity of its genesis.

Histogram of freezing point distribution at different depths in the Dongying Sag.

In addition to the difference in crude oil enrichment, the distribution range of freezing points of crude oils in different layers of the Dongying Sag also shows obvious differences. Overall, the crude oil freezing point of the shallow layers is lower than that of the deeper layers. The medians of freezing points of crude oils in Ng, Ed, Es1, Es2, Es3, Es4, Ek, Mz (Mesozoic), PZ2 (upper Paleozoic), O (Ordovician), ∈ (Cambrian), and An∈ are 3, −1, 12, 20, 32, 30, 24, 33, 39, 29, 27, and 29 °C, respectively. The LFP crude oils account for a relatively high proportion in the shallow layers and a relatively small proportion in the deep layers. The MFP crude oils generally occur in large quantities in all layers. The HFP crude oil samples are concentrated in the Es3, Es4, and Ek, with only sporadic distribution in other layers (Figure 5).

Box diagram of the distribution of freezing points of crude oils at different layers in the Dongying Sag.

From the perspective of the spatial distribution characteristics of crude oils with different freezing points, on the one hand, the occurrence of high-freezing-point crude oils is not limited to a specific structural zone and depth range; on the other hand, the HFP crude oils are more enriched in some areas. In general, the Es3 in the Niuzhuang subsag and Boxing subsag, the Es4 in the Zhengjia-wangzhuang oilfield (Binnan fault belt), and the Ek and pre-Paleogene in the Wangjiagang fault belt are the most developed layers of HFP crude oils (Figure 6).

Distribution characteristics of LFP, MFP, and HFP crude oils in the Dongying Sag.

Classification of High-Freezing-Point Crude Oil Types

Crude oil solidification is predominantly a thermodynamic process, which mainly depends upon its composition. The components that tend to solidify during temperature reduction are mainly waxes and other heavy components such as resins and asphaltenes. The waxes in crude oils are mainly composed of paraffinic hydrocarbons (C18–C36) and cycloalkanes (C30–C60). Both types of waxes will crystallize as the temperature and pressure decrease, with paraffinic hydrocarbons forming large crystalline waxes and cycloalkanes forming small crystalline waxes.47 The asphaltenes and resins that comprise aromatic and naphthenic molecules of molecular weights ranging from several hundred to several thousand grams per mole affect the solubility of paraffins in crude oils.48,49 Therefore, the wax content and resin + asphaltene content are the main factors affecting the freezing point of crude oils. According to the relationship between wax content and resin + asphaltene content, the HFP crude oils in the Dongying Sag can be clearly divided into three types (Figure 7). Of these, type A HFP crude oils have a wax content greater than 20% and a resin + asphaltene content less than 20%, type B HFP crude oils have wax and resin + asphaltene contents between 20 and 40%, while type C HFP crude oils have a wax content less than 20% and a resin + asphaltene content greater than 40%. There are significant differences in the distribution of the three HFP crude oil types, with type A crude oils mainly found in the gentle slope zone, type B crude oils mainly found in the depression zone, and type C crude oils mainly found in the uplift zone.

Classification scheme diagram of HFP crude oils based on the wax content and resin + asphaltene content.

There are significant differences in the spatial distribution, such as structural zones, layers, and depth ranges of different types of HFP crude oils (Table 1). In addition to the differences in the wax content and resin + asphaltene content, the other physical properties of the HFP crude oils in each tectonic zone also show different values. The HFP crude oils in the Niuzhuang subsag are mainly developed in Es3, with crude oil densities ranging from 0.87 to 0.90 g/cm3 and viscosities mostly less than 100 mPa·s. The HFP crude oils in the Boxing subsag are mainly developed in Es3 and Es4, with densities ranging from 0.83 to 0.89 g/cm3 and viscosities less than 50 mPa·s. The HFP crude oils in the Wangjiagang fault zone are developed in Ek and Paleozoic (Pz), with the crude oil density ranging from 0.82 to 0.93 g/cm3 and viscosities mostly below 100 mPa·s. The HFP crude oils in the Binnan fault belt are mainly located in Ng, Es3, and Es4, with crude oil densities greater than 1.0 g/cm3 and viscosities more than 5000 mPa·s. The HFP crude oils in the Gaoqing fault belt are mainly developed in An∈, Pz, and Ek, with crude oil densities ranging from 0.87 to 1.0 g/cm3 and viscosities mostly below 300 mPa·s. In faulted basins, reservoirs in different tectonic zones always undergo different processes of hydrocarbon migration, accumulation, and secondary alteration; thus, the differences in the wax content, resin + asphaltene content, and other physical properties of the three types of HFP crude oils reflect the differences in their origin and formation processes.

Sources of Different Types of High-Freezing-Point Crude Oils

The three sets of source rocks (Es3L, Es4U, and Ek2) developed in the Dongying Sag experienced different tectonic settings and depositional environments during their formation, resulting in obvious differences in their geochemical characteristics. A large number of studies on the geochemical characteristics of source rocks and crude oils have been carried out, and the criteria for identifying different sources of crude oils using biomarkers and isotopes have been established.50−53

Crude oils originated from Es3L have relatively highest values of Pr/Ph, C27Dia/C27St, 4-Me/C29St, C19/C23TT, and C24TeT/C26TT ratios and lowest values of C29St/C30H, G/C30H, and C35/C34 22S H ratios.52 Crude oils originated from Es4U display high C29St/C30H, G/C30H, and C35/C3422S H ratios and low Pr/Ph, C27Dia/C27St, 4-Me/C29St, C19/C23TT, and C24TeT/C26TT ratios.52 Crude oils originated from Ek2 are characterized by abnormally high concentrations of C29 steranes, 24-nordiacholestane, 27-nordiacholestane, C28 triaromatic steroids, C27 4α-methyltriaromatic steroids, and C29 4α-methyl-24-ethyltriaromatic steroids; low concentrations of 24-norcholestane, 27-norcholestane, and triaromatic dinosteroids; and 13C depletion.54 However, the widely varying distributions of steranes and terpenes in mixed oils suggest complexity in the sources of organic matter.51−53

In order to explain the complexity of the sources of different types of HFP crude oils, we selected some typical samples for systematic analysis (Figure 8). The sample from W130 (typical sample of type A HFP crude oils) that is located in the southern slope zone is characterized by a high Pr/Ph ratio, a high long-chain tricyclic terpene and gammacerane content, and an inverse L-shaped C27–C29 regular sterane distribution pattern, indicating a Ek2 source rock origin. The samples from Well N876 (with freezing point of 47 °C) and Well F144 (with a freezing point of 41 °C) both belong to type B HFP crude oils in the depression zone and are derived from Niuzhuang and Boxing subsags, respectively. The sample from Well N876 is characterized by a low Pr/Ph ratio, a low content of long-chain tricyclic terpenes, a high content of gammacerane, and a low content of rearranged steranes. Based on these characteristics, it can be determined that this sample belongs to a typical crude oil generated from the source rock of Es4U. The relatively high Pr/Ph ratio and rearranged sterane content and the relatively low long-chain tricyclic terpene and gammacerane content indicate that the sample from F144 is a mixture of crude oils generated from the Es3L and Es4U source rocks. The sample from Well ZhX41 located on the Binnan fault belt belongs to type C HFP crude oils, and its total ion chromatogram (TIC) shows obvious characteristics of n-alkane degradation, but the degree of degradation is relatively low (Figure 8). The sample exhibits a low Pr/Ph ratio, a low content of long-chain tricyclic terpenes, a low content of rearranged steranes, and a high content of gammacerane, indicating that the sample originated from the Es4U source rock. Like the sample from Well N876, the sample from Well N24 is also located in the Niuzhuang subsag. From the comparison of the characteristics of normal alkanes, terpenes, and steranes, it can be seen that the biomarker characteristics of the two samples are very similar. However, the freezing point of the sample from Well N24 is only 34 °C, which is significantly lower than the sample from Well N876, and it belongs to MFP crude oils.

Total ion chromatograms (TICs) and m/z 191 and m/z 217 mass chromatograms for representative crude oil samples.

In addition to clear differences in biomarker characteristics among different types of HFP crude oils, the carbon isotopic distribution of individual n-alkanes also exhibits significantly different characteristics. Generally, the type A HFP crude oils have the lightest carbon isotopes of individual n-alkanes, with δ13C values between −25.6 and −34.4‰, while the type C HFP crude oils have the heaviest carbon isotopes of individual n-alkanes, with δ13C values between −25.6 and −34.4‰ (Figure 9). Type B HFP crude oils have carbon isotopes of individual n-alkanes intermediate between those of type A and type C, with δ13C values between −25.6 and −34.4‰ (Figure 9). Since the organic matter input of the source rock is the most important factor controlling the carbon isotope of individual hydrocarbons in crude oils, the difference in the carbon isotope of individual hydrocarbons among different types of HFP crude oils indicates their distinct origin.55−57

Discussion

Composition of Crude Oils Controls the Freezing Point

Unlike the solidification of conventional substances, solidification in the petroleum industry refers to the critical temperature point at which crude oils lose their fluidity during temperature reduction. When crude oils are at the freezing point, they do not really solidify but become a plastic substance without fluidity under the conditions at that time. Crude oils are complex mixtures that contain many types of compounds with different structures, such as normal alkanes, cycloalkanes, aromatic hydrocarbons, resins, and asphaltenes.11,12 In general, crude oils belong to a colloidal solution in which the dispersed phase is solid hydrocarbons and asphaltenes, while the continuous phase is liquid hydrocarbons and colloids.58 The structural characteristics and relative contents of different structural compounds in crude oils are key factors in controlling the freezing point of crude oils.

Depending on the contents of wax, resins, and asphaltenes, there are two mechanisms by which crude oils solidify as the temperature decreases. For crude oils with a high content of wax, as the temperature decreases, the wax gradually crystallizes and precipitates. With the wax crystals continuing to precipitate, they eventually form a spatial network structure and ultimately lose fluidity.59 For the crude oils with a low wax content but a high resin and asphaltene content, the viscosity increases rapidly during the process of temperature reduction because the resins and asphaltenes are viscous aromatic macromolecules, eventually becoming an amorphous, viscous glassy substance that loses its fluidity.60,61 The crude oils solidified under the two different solidification mechanisms exhibit different characteristics in their apparent appearance (Figure 10). The obvious feature of crude oils that solidify due to their high wax content is their lighter color, and the surface of the solidified crude oils has a frosted particle-like texture. The solidified crude oils are easily scooped up with a spoon, and during the process of scooping up, the crude oils appear in a lump shape and do not produce wire drawings (Figure 10a). In contrast, the color of crude oils that solidify due to their high content of resins and asphaltenes is mainly black. The solidified crude oils show a smooth glass mirror surface feature, and the solidified crude oils are viscous and difficult to scoop up with a spoon, and during the process of scooping up, they will form wire drawings (Figure 10b).

Pictures showing the characteristics of crude oils that solidify under different solidification mechanisms: (a) typical crude oil with a low wax content but a high resin and asphaltene content and (b) typical crude oil with a high wax content.

The two solidification mechanisms coexist and interact with each other. The resins and asphaltenes act as nuclei during the crystallization of wax crystals, thus facilitating the crystallization and precipitation of wax crystals. At the same time, resin and asphaltene molecules can also directly participate in the crystallization of wax crystals, thus changing the morphology and structure of wax crystals, thus further affecting the physical properties of crude oils.60 Of course, in a crude oil sample, the solidification process is inevitably influenced by both mechanisms simultaneously. Depending on the specific composition characteristics of crude oils, there may be primary and secondary differences in the roles played by the two mechanisms in the solidification process.

In the Dongying Sag, the freezing point of crude oils has a complex relationship with the content of waxes, resins, and asphaltenes. Overall, most crude oils with a wax content greater than 10% and a resin + asphaltene content less than 40% have a freezing point above 30 °C. When the wax content of crude oils is less than 10% and the resin + asphaltene content is more than 30%, with the increase of the resin + asphaltene content, the freezing point of crude oils linearly increases and finally reaches or approaches the standard of high-pour-point crude oils (Figure 11). Therefore, the content of wax, resins, and asphaltenes in crude oils controls the freezing point and the formation of high-freezing-point oils. Analysis of the enrichment of the above components in crude oils can reveal the origin of HFP crude oils in this study area.

Plot of freezing point versus resin + asphaltene content of crude oils with different wax contents in the Dongying Sag.

Organic Matter Composition Is the Material Basis for Wax Formation

Many factors such as the organic matter input, thermal maturation, migration process, and biodegradation control the composition of petroleum. Waxes are the most important component controlling the freezing point of crude oils. They are complex mixtures of high-molecular-weight or high-carbon-number alkanes that consist of straight, branched, and cyclic chains.62 The formation of wax is essentially related to the source of organic matter, and it is widely believed that the wax in crude oils is mainly from terrestrial higher plant sources.63,64 In recent years, many scholars have recognized that aquatic organisms, such as bacteria and algae, can also generate high-wax-content oils.65−67 Meanwhile, Tissot and Welte pointed out that terrestrial organic matter strongly modified by microorganisms can also form high-wax-content oils.68 Although there are differences in the tectonic background and water body characteristics of the source rocks developed in the Dongying Sag during their deposition, different types of fungi and algae are always important components of organic matter in the source rocks. At the same time, the terrestrial source organic matter has variedly contributed to the organic matter enrichment of different sets of source rocks. Source rocks in Es4U and Es3L are the most important ones in the Dongying Sag.40,50 During deposition from Es4U to Es3L, the salinity of the water body experienced a process of gradual reduction. Well-preserved algal structure and amorphous humus can be seen under a microscope in both sets of source rocks, indicating abundant algae-derived organic matter represented by dinoflagellates, green algae, and cyanobacteria.69,70 Ek is a set of effective source rocks confirmed in recent years,71 which is characterized by obvious contributions of organic matter from terrestrial higher plants. A large number of studies based on biomarkers, carbon isotopes, and maceral composition have confirmed the differences in the organic matter composition of these source rocks in the Dongying Sag.40,42,51 The geochemical characteristics of different types of HFP crude oils in Figure 8 further illustrate the difference in the composition of organic matter among these source rocks. Considering the wax generation conditions, all of these source rocks can generate waxy crude oils when reaching a certain thermal evolution stage, which lays a foundation for the formation of HFP crude oils.

Hydrocarbon Migration and Accumulation Leads to the Enrichment of Waxes

In addition to the composition of organic matter, the migration and accumulation of crude oils are other important factors affecting the wax content. Phase fractionation occurs due to the reduction of the temperature and pressure in deeply buried layers during hydrocarbon discharge and migration. The fractionation of hydrocarbons leads to changes in the relative abundance and distribution of the chemical composition. Generally, the light components increase and the heavy components decrease in the migrating oil, while the residual phase demonstrates a distinctive increase of heavier components. The upward hydrocarbon migration from deep layers along the fault is a favorable condition for the occurrence of phase fractionation, as there will be a significant decrease in the temperature and pressure during this process. The invasion of relatively dry gas into oil reservoirs promotes phase fractionation by dissolving the light components and bringing them out of the reservoirs.

The source rocks in the Dongying Sag have experienced multiple periods of hydrocarbon generation and expulsion, and the oil generated by deeply buried Ek source rocks in the early stage migrated toward the slope belt. During the migration of oil in slope zones with faults developed, phase-controlled fractionation occurs continuously, resulting in the formation of a series of oil reservoirs with a high wax content along the migration path. According to the wax content versus fraction index [FI = nC10/(nC16+nC25)] plot proposed by Zhang,72 migration fractionation caused the generation of high-wax-content HFP crude oils in the slope zone (such as in the Wangjiagang fault belt). Natural gas generated by Ek source rocks in the depression zone in the later stage invaded the earlier formed reservoirs, dissolved the low-molecular-weight alkanes into the vapor phase, and then escaped, resulting in the further enrichment of wax in the slope zone oil reservoirs. Gas deasphalting occurs as a result of gas injection, forming solid bitumen while changing the composition of crude oils.73 The existence of solid bitumen and single-phase gas inclusion74 also supports the explanation of the genesis of type A HFP crude oils in the slope zone. In addition to the formation of HFP crude oils with high wax contents in the slope zone that far away from the source kitchen in the depression zone, the continuous expulsion of light components also leads to the enrichment of relatively long chain waxes, resins, and asphaltenes in the depression zone. The migration distance of oil reservoirs in depression zones is short, and they mainly exist in the form of residual oil during phase-controlled fractionation. In the process of phase-controlled fractionation, the light components such as short-chain waxes migrate from these reservoirs to the reservoirs farther away, resulting in the enrichment of long-chain waxes, resins, and asphaltenes.

Migration fractionation and gas washing have occurred continuously in different structural zones during the multiphase hydrocarbon generation and expulsion of the three sets of source rocks. Also, the HFP crude oils with different chemical components formed in both the depression zone and slope zone as a result of the different formation processes. The HFP crude oils formed in the depression zone not only have a high wax content but also have a relatively high content of asphaltenes and resins. In comparison, the high-freezing-point crude oils formed in the slope zone have a higher wax content, but the content of asphaltenes and resins is relatively low.

Secondary Alteration of Reservoirs Causes Concentration of Resins and Asphaltenes

Different from the depression zone and slope zone, the uplift zone develops HFP crude oils with high asphaltene and resin contents. The resins and asphaltenes are heteroatomic compounds with a macromolecular structure, mainly derived from the secondary alteration of petroleum. In the Dongying Sag, the content of resins + asphaltenes in crude oils shows an increasing trend from the center of the sag to the edge and from deep to shallow formations. HFP crude oils characterized by high resin + asphaltene contents are mainly developed in the depth range of 1000–2000 m, where biodegradation generally occurs. Biodegradation has an impact on the physical properties and molecular composition of crude oils. Biodegradation typically causes a decrease in the relative proportion of alkanes and aromatics, while the resin and asphaltene content increases with the enhancement of biodegradation. During the biodegradation process, there are differences in the order in which different compounds are degraded, resulting in significant differences in the compositional characteristics of crude oil compounds with different degrees of biodegradation. In the Dongying Sag, reservoirs that have undergone biodegradation in different regions have varying degrees of degradation, but overall, they have reached a moderate-to-severe degradation level.75,76 Although the HFP crude oils developed in the uplift zone have high resin + asphaltene contents, not all crude oils with high resin + asphaltene contents have high freezing points (Figure 12). One possible explanation for this phenomenon is that different resins and asphaltenes have different molecular structures and interact with other components such as waxes in the crude oil system in different ways, leading to different solidification characteristics of crude oils. In future research, if the structural characteristics of resin and asphaltene molecules can be more accurately characterized, the influence of resin and asphaltene molecules with different molecular structures on the freezing point of crude oil can also be more accurately determined.

Conclusions

Based on the systematic analysis of physical properties, SARA contents, biomarkers, and carbon isotopes of individual n-alkanes of crude oils in the Donying sag, the following conclusions can be drawn:(1)The freezing point of crude oils in Dongying Sag varies greatly, but overall, it is relatively high. The HFP crude oils are commonly developed in the sag and appear densely in local areas.(2)According to the relationship between the wax content and resin + asphaltene content, the HFP crude oils in the Dongying Sag are divided into type A, type B, and type C. The three types of HFP crude oils are mainly found in the gentle slope zone, depression zone, and uplift zone, respectively.(3)The hydrocarbon-generating parent material that can generate waxes commonly developed in source rocks; phase-controlled fractionation during oil migration, biodegradation, and water washing in shallow reservoirs combine to lead to the formation of different types of HFP crude oils in the Dongying Sag.

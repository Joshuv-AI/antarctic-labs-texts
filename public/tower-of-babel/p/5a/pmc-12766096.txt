Valorization of proteins from plant and agri-food by-products using emerging food processing technologies (EFPT)☆
Authors: Xiaohong Sun, Rotimi E Aluko
Published: 2025

Emerging food processing technologies (EFPT) are continuously evolving to address critical challenges in food chemistry and food nutrition. This special issue aims to provide state-of-the-art knowledge on the valorization of proteins from plant and agri-food by-products using EFPT, contributing to addressing United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. Key aspects covered in this issue include the role of advanced processing technologies, biochemical modifications, and innovative extraction methods in enhancing the functionality, stability, digestibility, and bioactivity of food components.

Several studies in this issue focus on improving protein techno-functional properties for diverse applications. Wang et al. (2024) demonstrated that supplemented black rice extract significantly enhanced the properties of oxidized myofibrillar protein gels, providing a theoretical basis for its application as an antioxidant in meat products. Two studies employed different approaches to enhance the emulsifying properties of proteins: one formed a protein-carbohydrate binary complex using egg white ovalbumin and fucoidan (Li, Wu, et al., 2024), and the other created a protein-polyphenol conjugate using myosin and chlorogenic acid (Li, Zhong, et al., 2024), underlining the importance of synergistic interactions in food formulation. Additionally, researchers demonstrated that multi-frequency ultrasound-assisted thawing at a power level of 220 W enhanced the flesh quality and protein stability of large yellow croaker during the defrosting process (Yang et al., 2024). Another study demonstrated that limited enzymatic hydrolysis not only significantly enhanced the functionality (e.g., solubility, foaming, emulsifying, and water-holding capacity) of a soy protein/corn zein mixture but also improved protein digestibility and antioxidant capacity (Wu et al., 2024). Meanwhile, the pH-shifting technique used to conjugate trehalose with quinoa proteins enhanced protein digestibility and water solubility, providing a novel method for modifying plant proteins (Alrosan et al., 2024).

The novel combination process of microwaves and subcritical extraction was developed by Zhang et al. (2024), which is promising in producing fragrant sesame oil with superior qualities. Meanwhile, the regulation of postharvest quality using magnetic fields and low-temperature storage provides valuable insights into maintaining freshness and structural integrity in Clausena lansium Skeels (Sun et al., 2024).

The biological activity of food-derived components remains a key focus. This issue includes three comprehensive reviews: one exploring self-assembly peptides from seafood and their potential applications in functional foods and biological materials (Xu, Han, et al., 2024), and another examining food-derived peptides for osteoporosis prevention (Xu, Zhang, et al., 2024). In addition to peptides, carbohydrates also gained attention for their diverse biological activities such as antioxidant, anticancer, and antimutagenic effects. Song et al. (2024) discussed the impact of processing technologies on the physicochemical and biological properties of carbohydrates, emphasizing the need to understand the underlying mechanisms and offering insights into future research directions to maximize their health benefits for consumers.

In summary, the studies featured in this special issue including both original research and review articles collectively underscore the importance of multidisciplinary approaches in food science. From emerging food processing technologies, novel extraction to various modification methods, the findings presented here pave the way for further innovations in food chemistry, functional foods, and sustainable processing technologies. Lastly, we extend our gratitude to all the authors and reviewers for their contributions to this special issue.

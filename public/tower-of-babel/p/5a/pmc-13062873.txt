Development and validation of an MRI radiomics-based model for predicting progression risk in prostate cancer after endocrine therapy
Authors: Ke Ding, Qiong Chen, Lifeng Huang, Ruisui Huang, Manrong Liu, Mofeng Gong, Haibo Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProstate cancer often progresses to castration-resistant disease despite initial response to endocrine therapy, necessitating better predictive tools like magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) radiomics. This study aimed to develop a predictive model using MRI radiomics and clinicopathological factors to assess tumor progression risk after endocrine therapy in prostate cancer patients, and to create a nomogram for evaluating progression-free survival (PFS).MethodsA total of 136 prostate cancer patients receiving endocrine therapy were retrospectively analyzed and randomly split into training (n=95) and internal validation (n=41) sets (7:3). A radiomics-clinical nomogram was developed and validated internally and externally (n=52). Performance was assessed for discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility.ResultsIndependent predictors for tumor progression included time to prostate-specific antigen (PSA) nadir, Gleason score, tumor T stage, and bone metastasis. The combined prediction model achieved C-index values of 0.884, 0.839, and 0.795 in training, internal validation, and external validation sets, respectively. Calibration curves indicated accuracy; decision curve analysis confirmed clinical utility. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that using a nomogram score of 79.44 as the cutoff effectively stratified prostate cancer patients into high-risk (>79.44) and low-risk (≤79.44) groups, with significantly shorter PFS in the high-risk group (log-rank test, P<0.001).ConclusionsThe model incorporating MRI radiomics features with clinicopathological factors effectively predicts progression risk post-endocrine therapy in prostate cancer patients, aiding personalized clinical decisions to improve prognosis.

Highlight box

Key findings

• A combined predictive model integrating magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) radiomics features with clinicopathological factors was developed to assess tumor progression risk in prostate cancer patients undergoing endocrine therapy.

• Independent predictors of progression-free survival included time to prostate-specific antigen (PSA) nadir, Gleason score, tumor T stage, bone metastasis, and specific radiomics features.

• The combined model achieved superior predictive accuracy (C-index up to 0.884) compared to clinical or radiomics-only models and was successfully validated in an external cohort.

• A constructed nomogram effectively stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, with significantly shorter progression-free survival observed in the high-risk group.

What is known and what is new?

• Prostate cancer patients often progress to castration-resistant disease despite initial responses to endocrine therapy. While factors like Gleason score and PSA kinetics are known prognostic markers, accurate prediction of progression remains a clinical challenge.

• This study uniquely integrates MRI radiomics (specifically shape and texture features) with the time to PSA nadir and other clinical factors to predict post-endocrine therapy progression. It provides a validated nomogram that outperforms standard clinical models, demonstrating that combining radiomic phenotypes with clinical data yields more robust prognostic stratification.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The developed nomogram serves as a practical, non-invasive tool for personalized risk assessment in prostate cancer management. Clinicians can utilize this model to identify high-risk patients early, warranting more aggressive treatment strategies, closer monitoring, or enrollment in clinical trials to improve overall prognosis.

Introduction

Prostate cancer is the fourth most common type of malignant tumor globally, accounting for 7.3% of all cancers (1). Due to the insidious onset and lack of health screening awareness, most patients with prostate cancer are already in the middle to late stages at initial diagnosis, having missed the opportunity for radical surgery. For these patients, endocrine therapy is the primary treatment modality (2). However, most patients inevitably progress to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) after an initial response to endocrine therapy, despite significant reductions in prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels and clinical symptoms (3,4).

Several clinical and pathological factors have been associated with the risk of progression to CRPC, including initial PSA level, Gleason score, extent of bone metastases, time to PSA nadir (TTN), and PSA kinetics (5,6). Additionally, incorporating radiomics with clinicopathological factors has shown promising potential in improving prediction accuracy across various aspects of prostate cancer management (7,8). Radiomics extracts quantitative features from medical images using data-characterization algorithms, and offers a promising approach to capture tumor phenotypic information that may not be apparent to the human eye (9).

The aim of this study was to develop and validate an magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) radiomics-based model for predicting the risk of tumor progression after endocrine therapy in prostate cancer patients. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-1-912/rc).

Methods

Patients

This was a retrospective study analyzing prostate cancer patients who underwent MRI examinations at our hospital from October 2015 to September 2023. The inclusion criteria were: (I) prostate cancer confirmed by biopsy; (II) T2-T4 stage; (III) regular endocrine therapy with complete clinical, pathological, and imaging data; (IV) MRI performed within 2 weeks before biopsy, with visible and completely delineable tumors. The exclusion criteria were: (I) patients who also received other treatments such as surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or targeted therapy; (II) development of other malignancies; (III) poor quality of MRI images or missing clinical data.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committees of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (Y2022101). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. All patient data were anonymized and handled according to relevant data protection regulations.

Sample size calculation

Sample size calculation was performed based on the rule of thumb suggesting a minimum of 10 events per variable for Cox regression models. With an estimated 8 potential predictors in our final model and an expected event rate of approximately 80% of our population, we determined that a minimum of 100 patients would be required in the training set.

Based on these inclusion and exclusion criteria, 136 patients were ultimately included in this study. Patients were randomly divided into a training set (n=95) and an internal validation set (n=41) at a ratio of 7:3 (Figure 1). An additional 52 patients from another hospital served as an external validation set. The development set and the external validation set shared the same eligibility criteria, outcome, and predictors.

Flow chart of patient selection.

Data collection

Pre-treatment MRI images were collected for all patients, including T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps, as well as clinicopathological data including sex, age, body mass index, pre-treatment PSA level, TTN, Gleason score, T stage, presence of bone metastasis, and tumor location.

Treatment and follow-up

Within 1 week after biopsy, all diagnosed patients received endocrine therapy consisting of leuprorelin or goserelin (3.75 mg monthly) plus either bicalutamide (50 mg daily) or abiraterone (1,000 mg daily). Patients were monitored monthly via serum PSA and testosterone tests, with imaging as needed. Those without progression were followed for at least 3 years or until the final follow-up, with non-progressors recorded as censored data.

The endpoint of this study was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the period from the start of endocrine therapy to the occurrence of CRPC or death from any cause. According to the European Association of Urology guidelines (10), the diagnosis of CRPC must simultaneously meet the following two conditions: (I) serum testosterone reaches castration level (<50 ng/mL or 1.7 nmol/L); (II) PSA progression on follow-up, defined as three consecutive increases in PSA levels, each at least 1 week apart, with a >50% increase from the lowest PSA value; or progression on imaging examinations, defined as the discovery of ≥2 new bone metastatic lesions on bone scan, or enlargement of the primary cancer lesion on MRI follow-up. No blinding was used in the outcome assessment.

MRI acquisition

All patients in this study underwent MRI examinations using a United Imaging uMR790 3.0T, Siemens Verio 3.0T, or Siemens Skyra 3.0T MRI scanner. Scanning sequences included sagittal T2WI, coronal T2WI, axial T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), T2WI, and diffusion weighted imaging (DWI). ADC maps were obtained through post-processing workstations using an exponential model.

T2WI and ADC maps were specifically selected for radiomics extraction as they provide a synergistic assessment of tumor morphology and microenvironmental heterogeneity. Shape features from T2WI and texture features from ADC maps were both independent predictors of progression, capturing the complex phenotypic changes associated with resistance to endocrine therapy.

Image segmentation

Original MRI images were imported into the open-source software 3D Slicer (Version 4.11.0). Images were resampled to 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm voxels for standardization to minimize the impact of different MRI scanners and reconstruction parameters. Tumor region of interest (ROI) was manually delineated slice by slice close to the tumor margins. Example images of ROI delineation are shown in Figure 2.

Extraction and selection of radiomics features

To evaluate the reproducibility of radiomics feature selection, 30 patients were randomly selected for intra-observer (radiologist A) and inter-observer (radiologists A and B) reproducibility analysis. Two weeks after the initial delineation, radiologist A re-delineated the ROIs for these 30 patients to assess both intra-observer consistency (by comparing radiologist A’s two delineations) and inter-observer consistency (by comparing radiologist A’s first delineation with radiologist B’s delineation). For the remaining patients, ROIs were delineated by radiologist A, with uncertain cases jointly evaluated by both radiologists. Radiomics features were extracted from the tumor ROIs using Pyradiomics (version 3.0.1), an open-source Python package. The extracted features included: (I) shape and size features; (II) first-order statistical features; (III) second-order texture features; and (IV) higher-order filter features. No blinding was used during the selection of radiomics features.

The extracted quantitative radiomics features were normalized using Z-Score normalization [Z=(x−μ)/σ, where x is the original value, µ is the mean, and σ is the standard deviation]. The consistency between radiologists A and B, as well as between radiologist A’s two delineations, was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). Features with ICCs >0.75 were retained for further analysis.

Subsequently, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-Cox regression with 10-fold cross-validation was applied to select the optimal radiomics features. Development and evaluation of predictive models

In this study, three distinct predictive models were developed and evaluated: a clinical model, a radiomics model, and a combined model that integrated both clinical and radiomics features. The C-index, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA) curves were used to assess the predictive performance of the models, which were validated in the internal and external validation sets. The comparison of C-index values between different models was performed using the DeLong test. Calibration curves were generated using bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations to plot the relationship between predicted and observed survival probabilities. A visual nomogram of the combined model was constructed, with the median nomogram score as the cutoff value to divide patients into high-risk and low-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed, with comparison using the log-rank test. Both Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and the Cox proportional hazards model are used to handle censored data.

Statistical analysis

R software (Version 4.1.2) was used for statistical analysis. For continuous variables with normal distribution, mean ± standard deviation was used, and differences among groups were compared using the one-way ANOVA test, followed by the Tukey’s test. For continuous variables with non-normal distribution, median and interquartile range were used, and comparisons were made using the Kruskal-Wallis test. For categorical variables, frequencies were used for description, and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was applied to compare differences among groups. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical tests were two-sided.

The clinical model

In the training set, univariate Cox regression analysis of clinicopathological data showed that PSA, TTN, Gleason score, T stage, bone metastasis, and tumor location were factors related to PFS (P<0.05). These indicators were further analyzed using multivariate Cox regression, which revealed that TTN [hazard ratio (HR), 0.870; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.821–0.922; P<0.001], Gleason score (HR, 1.524; 95% CI: 1.229–1.890; P<0.001), T stage (HR, 1.917; 95% CI: 1.268–2.898; P=0.002), and bone metastasis (HR, 2.225; 95% CI: 1.306–3.790; P=0.003) were independent predictors of PFS (Table 2, Figure 3). These were used to construct a clinical prediction model, which achieved C-index values of 0.843 (95% CI: 0.812–0.874) and 0.771 (95% CI: 0.681–0.861) in the training and internal validation sets, respectively.

The radiomics model

A total of 2,394 radiomics features were extracted from MRI T2WI and ADC maps. After retaining 1912 features with ICCs greater than 0.75, the LASSO-Cox regression ultimately selected 6 optimal features and their corresponding coefficients (Figure S1, Table 3), including 3 T2WI features and 3 ADC features. The Rad-score was calculated for each patient, and a radiomics model was constructed. This model achieved C-index values of 0.828 (95% CI: 0.789–0.867) in the training set and 0.796 (95% CI: 0.718–0.874) in the internal validation set.

The combined model

A combined model was constructed based on the radiomics signature, TTN, Gleason score, tumor T stage, and bone metastasis. This model achieved C-index values of 0.884 (95% CI: 0.862–0.906) and 0.839 (95% CI: 0.776–0.902) in the training and internal validation sets, respectively. In the training set, the combined model’s C-index was significantly higher than the radiomics model and the clinical model (P=0.002, P=0.004). In the internal validation set, the combined model’s C-index was significantly higher than the clinical model (P=0.3) but not the radiomics model (P=0.25) (Table 4). The combined model achieved a C-index of 0.795 (95% CI: 0.721–0.869) in the external validation set.

Risk stratification analysis

Using the median nomogram score (79.44) of the combined model as the cutoff value, all patients were stratified into high-risk (nomogram score >79.44) and low-risk (nomogram score ≤79.44) groups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted (Figure 5). In the training set, the median PFS for the high and low-risk groups were 12.2 months (95% CI: 10.621–15.517) and 46.4 months (95% CI: 41.267–52.336), respectively (P<0.001). In the internal validation set, the median PFS for the high and low-risk groups were 16.8 months (95% CI: 13.424–20.003) and 32.9 months (95% CI: 27.819–NA), respectively (P<0.001). In the external validation set, the median PFS for the high and low-risk groups were 18.8 months (95% CI: 15.828–24.167) and 34.2 months (95% CI: 28.642–40.585), respectively (P<0.001).

Discussion

Early and accurate prediction of tumor progression risk after endocrine therapy in prostate cancer patients represents a critical clinical need that can significantly impact treatment decisions and patient outcomes. In this study, we developed and validated a novel prediction model combining MRI-derived radiomics features with key clinicopathological factors, which significantly outperformed either the clinical-only or radiomics-only models in predicting progression after endocrine therapy. This finding aligns with the growing consensus that integrating multiple data types often yields more robust and clinically relevant models (11). The combined model achieved impressive C-index values of 0.884, 0.839, and 0.795 in the training, internal validation, and external validation sets, respectively, indicating good discriminative ability across different patient populations.

Among the clinicopathological factors evaluated, TTN, Gleason score, T stage, and bone metastasis emerged as independent predictors of progression risk. The inverse relationship between TTN and progression risk (HR =0.870) suggests that patients with longer time to reach their PSA nadir have better outcomes, possibly reflecting a more sustained response to hormonal therapy. This finding is consistent with previous studies reporting TTN as a valuable prognostic marker in advanced prostate cancer (12). Similarly, higher Gleason scores (HR =1.524) and advanced T stages (HR =1.917) were associated with increased progression risk, confirming their established role in prostate cancer prognostication (13).

From the radiomics perspective, we identified six key features with strong prognostic value. Notably, three of these were shape-based features (major axis length, maximum 2D diameter-row, maximum 2D diameter-slice), suggesting that tumor geometry plays an important role in disease progression. Our analysis revealed that larger tumor dimensions were associated with higher progression risk and shorter PFS. This finding is biologically plausible, as larger tumors may harbor greater cellular heterogeneity and have a higher likelihood of containing therapy-resistant cell populations (14). Similar associations between tumor size parameters and outcomes have been reported in other malignancies (15,16).

The remaining three selected features were texture-based, derived from wavelet-transformed images. Texture features such as GLCM inverse difference normalized (Idn) and NGTDM Busyness quantify the spatial relationships between pixel intensities and may reflect underlying tumor heterogeneity at the cellular and microenvironmental levels (17,18). While the biological interpretation of these complex features remains challenging, they have shown prognostic value across multiple cancer types (19-21). Daniel et al. (22) demonstrated that in prostate cancer patients undergoing endocrine therapy, texture analysis based on biparametric MRI exhibited higher sensitivity and specificity than conventional histogram parameters in differentiating tumors from non-tumor tissues. The advantage of texture features was also confirmed in our study. We found that as the risk of tumor progression increased after endocrine therapy, certain texture feature values also rose accordingly. This suggests that images from high-risk prostate cancer patients exhibit coarser textures and greater tumor heterogeneity, which is often associated with poorer prognosis.

Interestingly, the appearance of “NA” in the confidence interval for the low-risk group in the internal validation set (median PFS 32.9 months, 95% CI: 27.8–NA) warrants specific mention. This occurs because the upper limit of the confidence interval could not be estimated due to insufficient events in the low-risk group during the follow-up period. Rather than representing a statistical limitation, this finding actually reinforces the effectiveness of our risk stratification approach, as it indicates that many patients in the low-risk group had favorable outcomes with censored follow-up before progression events could occur (23). This pattern is consistent with effective risk stratification, where low-risk patients are expected to have substantially better outcomes than high-risk patients.

The nomogram we developed offers a practical tool for clinicians to estimate individualized progression risk after endocrine therapy. The clear separation of survival curves between high-risk and low-risk groups across all three sets (training, internal validation, and external validation) demonstrates the model’s robust stratification ability. This differentiation has important clinical implications, as high-risk patients might benefit from more aggressive treatment strategies, closer monitoring, or consideration for clinical trials with novel agents.

Machine learning is an emerging technology in radiomics that significantly enhances the ability to extract and interpret complex data for clinical use. While manual slice-by-slice segmentation by experienced radiologists was employed here to ensure high-quality data for model development, the integration of machine learning-assisted or semi-automated segmentation algorithms represents a vital next step to improve clinical scalability and minimize inter-observer variability. Furthermore, situating our model within the evolving landscape of texture-driven machine learning—as seen in recent studies utilizing T2WI parameters for ISUP grade prediction—demonstrates that these phenotypic signatures are not only diagnostic but also highly prognostic for treatment response (24).

Our study has several strengths compared to previous work. First, we performed both internal and external validation, demonstrating the generalizability of our model across different institutions and patient populations. Second, our feature selection process was rigorous, including ICC analysis to ensure feature reproducibility. Third, we systematically compared three different models, providing clear evidence for the added value of integrating radiomics with clinical information.

Despite these strengths, our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design introduces potential selection bias and limits the evidence level compared to prospective studies. Second, the MRI images used in our study were obtained from three different MRI types and the variations in hardware and reconstruction algorithms between different manufacturers can introduce non-biological ‘noise’ into radiomic features. Although we implemented voxel resampling and feature normalization, residual scanner-related variability remains a potential source of bias (25). Third, the high event rate in our training cohort (~83%) can lead to an inflated C-index by reducing outcome uncertainty, which should be considered when generalizing the results. Especially, the external validation cohort is relatively small (n=52) and derived from a single center, which may not fully account for the broad variability in MRI acquisition protocols and patient demographics encountered in global practice, thus limiting robustness and transportability. Fourth, we only utilized T2WI and ADC sequences for radiomics analysis. Including additional MRI sequences, such as dynamic contrast-enhanced imaging, or alternative feature fusion strategies, such as multi-channel deep learning or early-stage data integration, might further enhance the model’s performance.

Conclusions

The prediction model developed in this study, combining MRI radiomics features with TTN, Gleason score, tumor T stage, and bone metastasis, demonstrates good performance in predicting the risk of tumor progression after endocrine therapy in prostate cancer patients. By effectively identifying patients at high risk for early progression, this model could help clinicians optimize treatment strategies, intensify monitoring for high-risk patients, and potentially improve overall outcomes.

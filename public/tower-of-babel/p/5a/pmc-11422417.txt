Epidemiological investigation of different types of Crohn’s disease patients undergoing first abdominal surgery in the era of biological agents: a population-based cohort study
Authors: Lichao Yang, Baojia Yao, Zhixian Jiang, Yawei Zhang, Qiang Wu, Hengchang Yao, Liangxin Peng, Lianwen Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBefore the era of biological agents, most Crohn’s disease patients required at least one intestinal resection surgery after diagnosis. However, clinical data regarding the abdominal surgery rates for Crohn’s disease patients in the era of biological agents is not yet fully clear and needs to be updated.Materials and methodsWe retrospectively collected clinical data from 1115 Crohn’s disease patients diagnosed and treated medically at The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University from January 2016 to January 2024. Using abdominal intestinal resection surgery as a clinical outcome, propensity score matching was employed to eliminate confounding factors. We explored the timing and proportion of abdominal surgery in patients with different Montreal classifications of Crohn’s disease during the natural course of the disease, as well as the impact of the duration of the natural course and the use of biological agents on surgical outcomes.ResultsMontreal classification type B had the greatest impact on Crohn’s disease surgery, especially with a higher proportion of type B3 patients undergoing surgery. Type A1 Crohn’s disease patients underwent surgery earlier than types A2 and A3. The occurrence of behavior changes (B Change) during the natural course of the disease is a poor prognostic signal, indicating a significantly increased likelihood of surgery. The duration of the natural course from the onset of gastrointestinal symptoms to diagnosis and clinical observation outcomes did not directly affect the likelihood of surgery in Crohn’s disease patients. Compared with Crohn’s disease patients who did not receive biological agents, the surgery rate was significantly lower in patients who used biological agents. Additionally, Crohn’s disease patients who received biological agents within 1 month of diagnosis had a significantly lower likelihood of undergoing surgical intervention. Moreover, Crohn’s disease patients who received biological agent treatment within 19 months of the onset of gastrointestinal symptoms also had a significantly lower likelihood of undergoing surgery than other Crohn’s disease patients.ConclusionsIn the era of biological agents, the risk of surgical intervention varies among Crohn’s disease patients with different Montreal classifications, particularly when there is type B3 disease or a B Change. Clinicians should pay closer attention to surgical indications in such cases. For Crohn’s disease patients, shortening the natural course before diagnosis and early use of biological agents after diagnosis can significantly reduce the risk of abdominal surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00384-024-04725-5.

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory bowel disease that can affect the entire gastrointestinal tract from the mouth to the anus [1]. The clinical natural course of Crohn’s disease is lengthy and is associated with multiple factors, including genetic predisposition, lifestyle habits, dietary changes, immune dysregulation, and gut microbiota disturbances [2]. Previous studies have found that 50% of Crohn’s disease patients require surgery within 10 years of diagnosis, and the probability of disease recurrence after surgery is very high [3, 4]. With the deepening understanding of Crohn’s disease pathogenesis, a variety of medications that can be effectively applied to the clinical treatment of Crohn’s disease have been developed, including biological agents [5]. Although the clinical benefits of biological agents for Crohn’s disease have been demonstrated in several randomized controlled trials [6, 7], it is currently unclear whether these medications affect the long-term natural course of Crohn’s disease.

To our knowledge, the biological agents first promoted and used in China for treating Crohn’s disease include adalimumab (2016) and infliximab (2017), followed by ustekinumab, vedolizumab, and certolizumab [5, 8, 9]. With the improvement of people’s material living standards and strong support from China’s healthcare insurance policy, an increasing number of Crohn’s disease patients can more easily afford biological agent treatment [10]. Therefore, this study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of Crohn’s disease patients diagnosed and treated medically at The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University since the introduction of biological agents in 2016. The primary clinical endpoint was the first abdominal surgery. Based on the Montreal classification, we explored the natural course variation of different types of Crohn’s disease patients leading to the primary clinical endpoint.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective real-world cohort study gathered clinical data from an inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) center in the Central South region of China (The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University). Ethical approval was obtained prior to data collection (approval no. 2022-155), and the study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. All collected data were anonymized. This study included Crohn’s disease patients diagnosed and medically treated at The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University from January 2016 to January 2024. The work has been reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Cohort Studies in Surgery (STROCSS) guidelines [11]. Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report and accompanying images.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Patients diagnosed with Crohn’s disease according to current IBD diagnostic and treatment guidelines [12, 13]; (2) Patients who received medical treatment (including inpatient and outpatient medication) at The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University from January 2016 to January 2024.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Patients who underwent abdominal intestinal resection surgery (excluding appendectomy) prior to diagnosis or treatment at our hospital; (2) Patients who underwent intestinal resection surgery at another hospital due to an acute exacerbation of Crohn’s disease during treatment; (3) Incomplete clinical data; (4) Missing follow-up data; (5) Death due to other diseases or natural causes.

Data collection

Baseline data and follow-up data of CD patients were exported from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. First, the target population was screened according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Then, a standardized data collection form was used for retrieval, and the process was independently completed by two physicians. If there were inconsistencies in the data collected by the two physicians, discussions were held to reach a consensus.

The study ultimately collected clinical data from 1115 Crohn’s disease patients, of which 213 patients underwent their first abdominal intestinal resection surgery at our hospital. The data collected included gender, smoking history, Montreal classification, extraintestinal manifestations, CDAI score at diagnosis [14], malignancy, height, weight, history of appendectomy, history of biological therapy, biological agent treatment, time of symptom onset, time of diagnosis, time of first surgery, time of biological agent treatment, date of birth, and surgical indications.

Due to the difficulty in distinguishing between ileal and jejunal lesions and the similarity in treatment methods, we defined the small intestine type of Crohn’s disease as L1 in the L classification of Montreal, and L4 as primary lesions located in the stomach and duodenum [15]. Clinically, the behavior of Crohn’s disease often changes, so we defined “B Change” as the presence of a change in disease behavior, including B1 transitioning to B2 or B3, or the coexistence of B2 and B3. In addition, biological agent treatment was defined as the regular administration of biological agents, excluding discontinuation or irregular medication during treatment.

Outcomes

This study considered the first abdominal intestinal resection surgery as the primary clinical endpoint, including both emergency and elective surgeries. All surgical patients, except for emergency surgeries, underwent a multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion at our hospital before deciding whether to proceed with surgical intervention. The MDT team, established in 2016, comprised professors from departments such as surgery, internal medicine, pathology, pediatrics, nutrition, and imaging [16].

Surgical indications included recurrent intestinal bleeding, acute perforation, chronic intestinal fistula, recurrent bowel obstruction, malignancy, ineffective medical treatment, unclear diagnosis, and pediatric growth failure [17]. Crohn’s disease patients could present with multiple surgical indications concurrently.

This study conducted regular follow-up for all Crohn’s disease patients. For those who did not undergo surgery, follow-up was conducted every 3 months via phone to determine whether they had undergone an abdominal intestinal resection surgery (outcome). For Crohn’s disease patients who had already undergone surgery, follow-up was conducted every 6–12 months post-surgery. For Crohn’s disease patients who did not undergo abdominal surgery, the time of outcome occurrence was defined as the time of the last follow-up.

Propensity score matching (PSM)

To reduce selection bias, propensity score matching was implemented for the exposure data, which consisted of categorical data [18]. When the categorical data consisted of two categories, we used the “MatchIt” R package to process the data. This package estimated the propensity score for each participant based on a predefined set of covariates (p < 0.05) [19].

We used a 1:1 nearest neighbor matching algorithm without replacement to ensure that each individual in the treatment group was matched to the control group with the most similar propensity score. After matching, the balance of covariates was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD), and all SMD values were found to be less than 0.1, indicating good balance.

When the categorical data consisted of three categories, we used the “pm3” R package to minimize confounding factors as much as possible [20]. We selected variables with a pre-PSM p-value of less than 0.05 as covariates. The effectiveness of the matching was confirmed when all covariates in the post-PSM data had SMD values significantly lower than those before PSM, and when the p-values were larger than before.

Statistical analysis

All reported statistical significance levels were 2-sided, with a significance threshold set at 0.05. All figures were generated using Adobe Illustrator software (version 2022). All statistical analyses and visualizations were conducted using R software version 4.2.0. The R code can be found in the supplementary document. Continuous variables were described as mean and standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were described as case numbers and proportions. Comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using ANOVA to assess intergroup differences; comparisons of categorical variables were conducted using the chi-squared test. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to explore the relationship between the time Crohn’s disease patients underwent surgery and specific exposure factors (e.g., biological therapy, Montreal classification). Survival time was defined as the time from Crohn’s disease diagnosis to the first surgery. We used the log-rank test to compare the survival curves between different exposure groups to determine whether the exposure factors significantly affected the time patients underwent surgery. All reported statistical significance levels were two-sided, with a significance threshold set at 0.05. All figures were generated using Adobe Illustrator software (version 2022).

Result

Baseline characteristics of the CD patients

Data for patients who received inpatient treatment at The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University from January 2016 to January 2024 were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 1115 Crohn’s disease patients were included in the study. This included 902 non-surgical Crohn’s disease patients (control group) and 213 Crohn’s disease patients who underwent their first intestinal resection surgery (surgical group). Table 1 shows the baseline data of the two groups of patients. While there were no significant differences in gender, perianal lesions, extraintestinal manifestations, and height between the two groups, the proportion of smokers in the surgical group was significantly higher (31% vs. 19%, p < 0.001), and the CDAI score at diagnosis was also higher (p < 0.001). Additionally, the natural disease course was relatively longer. The impact of different Montreal classifications on the first abdominal surgical intervention in Crohn’s disease

Data for the A, L, B, P, B Change, and L4 variables of the 1115 Crohn’s disease patients were summarized in Supplementary Table 1 (S1–S6). To increase the credibility of the study conclusions, data were propensity score matched (PSM) based on different Montreal classifications. Standardized mean difference (SMD) values before and after PSM can be found in Supplementary Table 2 (S1–S6), and the results showed that differences between groups were significantly reduced after PSM. Since there were few cases of primary L4-type Crohn’s disease patients (6 cases), PSM could not be completed for this group, and secondary L4-type Crohn’s disease patients showed collinearity with other L-type Crohn’s disease patients. Therefore, primary L4-type Crohn’s disease patients were not included in the subsequent PSM analysis of L-type Crohn’s disease patients. A

After PSM, a total of 61 patients from each of the A1, A2, and A3 subtypes were included in the subsequent study. In all three groups of Crohn’s disease patients, the number of male patients was significantly higher than that of female patients. A1 and A2 subtype Crohn’s disease patients were more likely to have perianal lesions. Compared to other A-type Crohn’s disease patients, A3 subtype patients were more likely to have extraintestinal manifestations. A1 subtype patients had lower smoking rates and lower BMI values, but a higher proportion of them received biological therapy. The proportion of A1 and A2 subtype patients undergoing surgical intervention was significantly higher than that of A3 subtype patients, and A1 subtype patients underwent surgical intervention sooner after being diagnosed with Crohn’s disease. L

After PSM, a total of 50 patients from each of the L1, L2, and L3 subtypes were included in the subsequent study. In all three groups of Crohn’s disease patients, the proportion of male patients was higher than that of female patients. L2 and L3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients were more likely to have perianal lesions. Compared to other L-type Crohn’s disease patients, L3 subtype patients were more likely to have extraintestinal manifestations. L1 subtype patients had a significantly higher smoking rate than the other two groups. L2 subtype Crohn’s disease patients had rarely undergone an appendectomy in the past, and L3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients had significantly lower BMI values than the other two groups. The proportion of L1 subtype Crohn’s disease patients receiving biological therapy was significantly lower than that of the other two groups. From the time of diagnosis, L2 subtype Crohn’s disease patients required surgical intervention at the earliest, while L3 subtype patients required it at the latest. Additionally, the proportion of L2 subtype Crohn’s disease patients undergoing surgery was the highest, while it was the lowest for L1 subtype patients. Kaplan-Meier plot results showed that there was no statistically significant difference in the likelihood of surgery between different L-type Crohn’s disease patients after being diagnosed (Fig. 1B).

B

After PSM, a total of 170 patients from each of the B1, B2, and B3 subtypes were included in the subsequent study. In all three groups of Crohn’s disease patients, the proportion of male patients was higher than that of female patients. B1 and B3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients were more likely to have perianal lesions and extraintestinal manifestations. The proportion of B2 subtype patients who smoked was significantly higher than that of the other two groups. B3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients had the highest proportion of prior appendectomies, while B1 subtype patients had the lowest proportion of prior appendectomies. B3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients had lower BMI values than the other two groups. The proportion of B2 subtype Crohn’s disease patients receiving biological therapy was the highest, followed by B3 subtype patients. From the time of diagnosis, B3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients required surgical intervention at the earliest, while B1 subtype patients required it at the latest. Additionally, the proportion of B3 subtype Crohn’s disease patients undergoing surgery was significantly higher than that of the other two groups, consistent with the results from the Kaplan-Meier plot (Fig. 1C).

P

Patients were divided into two groups based on whether they had perianal lesions. After PSM, a total of 348 patients from each group were included in the subsequent study. In both groups, approximately 80% of the patients were male. Crohn’s disease patients with perianal lesions were more likely to have extraintestinal manifestations. The two groups showed no significant differences in the proportion of smokers, prior appendectomies, or biological therapy usage. The BMI values of patients without perianal lesions were higher than those of Crohn’s disease patients with perianal lesions. From the time of diagnosis, Crohn’s disease patients with perianal lesions required surgical intervention earlier, but their overall proportion of surgeries was lower than that of patients without perianal lesions. Kaplan-Meier plot results showed that there was no statistically significant difference in the likelihood of undergoing the first abdominal surgery between the two groups of Crohn’s disease patients after diagnosis (Fig. 1D).

B Change

Patients were divided into two groups based on the presence of B Change. After propensity score matching (PSM), 148 patients from each group were included in the subsequent study. In both groups, over 75% of the patients were male. There were no significant differences between the two groups in the proportions of perianal lesions, extraintestinal manifestations, and smoking history. Crohn’s disease patients with B Change were more likely to be concentrated in the B3 subtype, and they had lower BMI values, higher proportions of prior appendectomies, and higher proportions of biological therapy. From the time of diagnosis, Crohn’s disease patients with B Change required surgical intervention earlier and had a significantly higher overall proportion of abdominal surgeries. The Kaplan-Meier plot results were consistent with these findings (Fig. 1E).

L4

Patients were divided into two groups based on whether they had L4-type CD lesions. After PSM, 215 patients from each group were included in the subsequent study. In both groups, approximately 85% of the patients were male. There were no significant differences between the two groups in the proportions of perianal lesions and smoking history. L4-type CD patients were more likely to have extraintestinal manifestations, but they had lower proportions of prior appendectomies. The two groups did not differ significantly in BMI, and L4-type CD patients had higher rates of biological therapy. From the time of diagnosis, L4-type CD patients required earlier abdominal surgical interventions, but their overall proportion of surgeries was lower than that of patients without L4-type CD lesions. The Kaplan-Meier plot results were consistent with these findings (Fig. 1F).

Discussion

Currently, the treatment strategy for CD patients not only aims for clinical remission but also includes deeper endoscopic mucosal healing to further alter disease progression [21]. Despite the significant progress made with medication in the treatment of CD, surgery remains an inevitable and critical part of the treatment course for most CD patients [22]. With the widespread use of biologics in CD treatment, clinical remission rates and endoscopic remission rates among CD patients have improved overall, and the proportion of patients undergoing surgery has decreased [23]. Since the likelihood of surgery varies among different classifications of CD patients, retrospective analysis summarizing the potential links between different CD classifications and surgery in the era of biologics is important for predicting the natural course of CD and determining the timing of surgical intervention.

In this study cohort, the intergroup differences in baseline data were effectively reduced after PSM. Statistical analysis of the data after PSM showed that behavior in the Montreal classification had the greatest impact on CD surgery, particularly for B3 patients who had a higher proportion of undergoing surgery. This is related to the nature of the disease; penetrating disease often requires surgical intervention, whereas medication is more effective for B2 or B1 patients. Additionally, the appearance of B Change in the natural course signals a poorer prognosis and indicates a higher likelihood of undergoing surgery. Age at diagnosis was also related to the surgical course of the disease; A1 patients underwent surgery earlier than A2 and A3 patients, which could be due to CD causing malnutrition in adolescents, leading to stunted growth [24], thus advancing the timing of surgical intervention. Similarly, the location of the disease influences CD surgery decisions; patients with L2 CD, limited to the colon, undergo surgery earlier, while surgery for small bowel involvement requires more caution due to the high recurrence rate of CD and the risk of short bowel syndrome. For gastric and duodenal involvement, surgery should also be more cautious, but early surgical intervention is necessary when internal fistulas form or bowel obstruction persists [25].

A recent large retrospective study found that a longer diagnosis time was associated with an increased risk of emergency hospital admissions related to IBD but was not related to IBD-related surgery [26]. The results of this study are consistent with these findings, indicating that the natural course from the onset of gastrointestinal symptoms to diagnosis and clinical outcomes does not directly impact the surgical outcomes of CD patients. However, with the use of biologics, the treatment course of CD is linked to abdominal surgery outcomes [27]. Compared to CD patients who did not receive biologics treatment, those who used biologics had a significantly lower proportion of undergoing surgery and delayed the time for surgical intervention. Moreover, one study showed that starting biologics within 3 months after diagnosis reduced the absolute risk of surgery by 13% over 10 years compared to starting biologics 2–3 years after diagnosis [28]. The results of this study showed that CD patients who started biologics treatment within 1 month after diagnosis had a significantly lower likelihood of undergoing surgery, and it was also found that CD patients who started biologics treatment within 19 months after the onset of gastrointestinal symptoms had a significantly lower likelihood of undergoing surgery than other CD patients. These data support the rationale for early biologics treatment strategy in CD patients, which helps reduce surgical risk and delay the timing of surgical intervention.

This study has several limitations. First, it included clinical data from a single IBD center in central and southern China, and all patients were of Han ethnicity. Secondly, biologics treatment in this study was not deeply classified, nor were treatment cycles described due to the variety of biologics and the possibility of medication changes during treatment. Additionally, the postoperative recurrence rate of CD patients undergoing surgery was not described, which awaits further follow-up for reporting. Despite these limitations, the study data included over 1000 CD patients, and PSM between two and three groups eliminated the influence of confounding factors, making our conclusions more credible.

In conclusion, this study shows that different Montreal classifications of CD patients have different surgical risks, especially when B3 type disease or B Change is present, clinicians should pay more attention to surgical indications. For CD patients, shortening the natural course before diagnosis and starting early biologics treatment after diagnosis can significantly reduce the risk of abdominal surgery.

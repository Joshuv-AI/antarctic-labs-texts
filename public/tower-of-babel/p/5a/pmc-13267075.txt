Confinement-Induced Dielectric Behavior of Water in Polymeric Nanofiltration Membranes: A Molecular Dynamics Study
Authors: Ryo Arii, Hayato Higuchi, Masaya Miyagawa, Hiromitsu Takaba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Water confined within nanofiltration (NF) membrane pores experiences a dielectric environment that differs substantially from that of bulk water, and this pore-scale dielectric constant is a key parameter in NF transport models such as the Donnan and steric partitioning model with dielectric exclusion (DSPM-DE). However, real polyamide NF membranes possess highly irregular and tortuous pore structures, and the dielectric properties of water under such realistic confinement remain poorly quantified. In this study, molecular dynamics simulations were performed using five polyamide models based on piperazine-TMC and diethylenetriamine-TMC to clarify how nanoscale pore morphology influences the dielectric response of confined water. In all models, confinement significantly reduced the dielectric constant relative to bulk water due to suppressed dipole moment fluctuations and hindered rotational mobility. Analysis of directional dielectric components and orientational distributions revealed that pore geometry and connectivity, rather than polymer hydrophilicity, predominantly govern the dielectric behavior. These findings provide physically grounded dielectric parameters for improving NF transport models and offer molecular-level insight into polarization phenomena within realistic polymeric membrane pores.

Introduction

Nanofiltration (NF) membranes are characterized by pore diameters typically ranging from 1 to 2 nm and molecular weight cutoff (MWCO) values between 100 and 2000 Da, placing them between ultrafiltration (UF) and reverse osmosis (RO) membranes in terms of selectivity and transport behavior. − Because of their unique separation characteristics, NF membranes have been widely employed in applications such as potable water production, seawater pretreatment, and industrial wastewater treatment, where they enable the efficient removal of multivalent ions and small organic molecules.

Early theoretical descriptions of solute and solvent transport in NF membranes were developed based on nonequilibrium statistical mechanics. , However, these frameworks did not explicitly account for key membrane features, such as pore structure and surface charge. To address this limitation, fixed charge density was introduced to represent the charged nature of membrane pores, and the relationships among electric potential, ion concentration, and flux were described using Donnan equilibrium and the Nernst–Planck equation. The extended Nernst–Planck model, incorporating additional transport contributions, provided a foundation for later NF transport models. ,,

One of the earliest theoretical approaches, the Teorell–Meyer–Sievers (TMS) model, ,, treated electrostatic interactions arising from fixed charges and successfully described ion-exchange membrane behavior. Subsequently, combining the extended Nernst–Planck framework with the TMS theory enabled accurate predictions of rejection behavior for mixed electrolyte solutions containing monovalent and divalent ions. , In 1965, Oesterle and co-workers proposed the space-charge (SC) model, , which assumed radial distributions of electric potential and ion concentration within membrane pores. Although these classical theories provided essential insight, they treated ions and pore charges as point charges and neglected steric constraints imposed by finite ion and pore sizes.

To overcome these limitations, models incorporating both electrostatic and steric effects were established, generally referred to as electrostatic and steric-hindrance (ES) models. A notable development was the Donnan and steric partitioning model (DSPM) introduced by Bowen et al., , which enabled accurate prediction of monovalent ion and dye rejection. As interest expanded toward divalent ion separation, solvent dielectric effects became increasingly important to consider. Reduced dielectric permittivity within nanopores destabilizes the solvation energy, resulting in dielectric exclusion. , In 2005, Szymczyk and Fievet proposed the SEDE (Steric, Electric, and Dielectric Exclusion) model, , which simultaneously accounts for steric exclusion, electrostatic interactions, and dielectric exclusion. This model assumes cylindrical or slit-like pore geometries and represents ion–interface interactions using an image charge formalism to describe the discontinuity in dielectric permittivity between the pore interior and the membrane matrix.

The DSPM-DE model further extended the DSPM framework by incorporating dielectric exclusion. , Like the SEDE model, DSPM-DE considers steric exclusion, Donnan exclusion, and dielectric exclusion; however, it does not explicitly include the image force. Notably, studies applying the SEDE framework revealed partial redundancy between dielectric exclusion and image-charge contributions, explaining why DSPM-DE remains effective for many NF systems despite neglecting image forces, and consequently has become the most widely adopted NF transport model.

Experimental approaches have been used to estimate pore-scale dielectric properties. Escoda and co-workers determined the effective dielectric constant inside NF pores by fitting diffusion potentials for NaCl, CaCl2, and mixed electrolytes using the SEDE model under high ionic strength conditions, where Donnan exclusion diminishes. Likewise, ζ-potential measurements at the isoelectric point, where membrane charge approaches zero and dielectric exclusion dominates, have been used to estimate pore permittivity via the DSPM-DE framework. , Cevallos-Cueva et al. further investigated Na+/Mg2+ and NO3 –/SO4 2– separation by fitting experimental rejection data to the DSPM-DE model using nonlinear least-squares optimization. ,

In parallel, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations have been employed to probe the dielectric behavior of confined water. Prior work has focused primarily on idealized nanoscale environments such as carbon nanotubes, − graphene channels, , and silica pores. , Although these studies provide valuable mechanistic insight, real NF membrane pores exhibit far more complex, heterogeneous morphologies and chemical environments, and systematic simulation studies of such systems remain scarce.

In this study, we estimate the dielectric constant of water confined within realistic NF membrane nanopores using MD simulations and elucidate the molecular mechanisms governing dielectric behavior under nanoscale confinement. We constructed polymeric NF membrane models based on polymerization structures of piperazine (PIP) with trimesoyl chloride (TMC) and diethylenetriamine (DETA) with TMC. PIP-TMC are widely employed in commercial nanofiltration membranes due to their high solute rejection. Since pore hydrophilicity of polymer might influence the dielectric constant of confined water, DETA–TMC, which are expected to exhibit higher hydrophilicity than PIP–TMC, were selected for comparison. By bridging nanoscale molecular simulations with established NF transport theory, this work provides a fundamental understanding of the dielectric properties of confined water and offers insights for the rational design of next-generation NF membranes.

Methods

To evaluate the influence of polymer molecular structure on the dielectric properties of confined water, two representative polyamide systems were considered: one formed by the polymerization of trimesoyl chloride (TMC) with diethylenetriamine (DETA) and the other with piperazine (PIP) (Figure ). Structural Parameters of the Polymer-Chain Models

Initial polymer structures were generated by randomly inserting polymer-chains into a cubic unit cell (34–38 Å per side) under periodic boundary conditions using Amorphous Cell module for Monte Carlo (MC) simulation, as supplied by BIOVIA Inc. In the DETA/TMC system (b), two polymer chains were placed in the simulation box, while three chains were placed in the other models. The initial configurations were equilibrated via molecular dynamics (MD) in the NPT ensemble at 298 K and pressures of 1–10 atm for 1 ns using a Nosé–Hoover thermostat , and Berendsen barostat. We used the Forcite Plus module for the MD simulations, as supplied by BIOVIA Inc. After confirming density convergence from approximately 1.0 to 1.2 g/cm3, the equilibrated cells were isotropically expanded by 3-4 Å in each dimension to generate nanoscale pores of approximately 10 Å, representing hydrated NF membrane pore environments with complex geometries.

Subsequently, the expanded polymer matrices were further relaxed for 5 ns in the NVT ensemble, where all the polymer chains moved freely to equilibrate the structure of the pore at 298 K, allowing local structural reorganization and stabilization of pore topology.

Water molecules were introduced into the polymer pores using grand canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) sampling with the Metropolis algorithm at 100 kPa. Following water insertion, a 3 ns MD simulation in the NVT ensemble at 298 K was performed with polymer atoms fixed, allowing confined water molecules to relax within the pore environment without polymer deformation. Immersion in water may cause slight changes in the pore structure and minor rearrangements compared to the dry state. However, in this study, the effect is assumed to be small, and the structure is treated as fixed.

Production MD trajectories were collected for 30 ns in the NVE ensemble to sample dipole-moment fluctuations of confined water for dielectric constant evaluation. This sampling duration was chosen to exceed the 20 ns production run established as a modern benchmark in recent comparative studies. The extended window aims to further enhance the statistical convergence of collective orientational correlations and cooperative molecular motions, which are essential for the accurate determination of macroscopic dielectric properties. Subsequently, NVE MD sampling was employed to eliminate artifacts introduced by the thermostat and to better approximate realistic system conditions. Currently, to explicitly report temperature stability, statistical data for all models are provided in Table S1.

The dielectric constant of water confined within the polymer pores was evaluated along the three Cartesian directions (x, y, and z) based on the statistical fluctuations of the total dipole moment. To define this volume, we used a probe sphere with a diameter of 3 Å, corresponding to the size of water molecule, based on the Connolly surface method. This method determines the surface located between polymer atoms and imagines a probe sphere rolling over the pore surface. While the absolute value of the dielectric constant may exhibit some sensitivity to the definition of the accessible volume, the method employed here provides physically grounded results by considering the volume actually accessible to water molecules. Electrostatic interactions were evaluated using the Ewald summation technique, and a cutoff distance of 10 Å was applied for short-range interactions. The integration time step was set to 1 fs for all calculations. The CVFF force field was used for the polymer matrix, whereas water molecules were modeled using the flexible bond-and-angle/ε (FBA/ε)-model recently reported in the literature. , To further investigate the rotational dynamics of confined water, the dipole autocorrelation function (DACF) was calculated. The DACF C r(t) is defined as , Cr(t)=<μ(t0)·μ(t0+t)> 3

The decay of C r(t) was fitted to obtain the rotational relaxation time τ, which characterizes the reorientation dynamics of water molecules: τ=∫0∞Cr(t)dt 4

The relaxation time and dielectric constant were then analyzed to elucidate confinement-induced restrictions on water rotational motion. In general, a smaller value of τ indicates that water molecules undergo relatively unrestricted rotational motion, whereas a larger τ reflects hindered reorientation due to structural confinement or strong interactions within the system.

In practice, the decay of the DACF was fitted using the following equation to determine the relaxation time: Cr(t)=exp⌊−(tτ)β⌋ 5 where β is the stretching exponent that accounts for nonsingle-exponential relaxation behavior commonly observed in confined or heterogeneous environments.

The orientational behavior of confined water molecules was analyzed. In bulk water, molecular dipoles undergo nearly unrestricted rotational motion, resulting in an approximately isotropic orientational distribution. By contrast, in nanoconfined environments such as polyamide membrane pores, water reorientation may be hindered by several factors, including electrostatic interactions with charged polymer functional groups and geometric confinement imposed by the pore walls. Consequently, the orientational distribution of confined water is expected to deviate markedly from that of bulk water, potentially exhibiting anisotropy or preferential alignment along specific directions. To quantify this effect, the angle θ between the xz plane of the simulation cell (vector a) and the dipole moment vector of each water molecule (vector b) was calculated, as illustrated in Figure . The orientational angle was obtained using the following relation: cos(θ)=a→·b→|a→||b→| 6 Here, θ = 0° corresponds to water molecules whose dipole moments lie parallel to the z-direction, whereas θ = 90° represents dipole oriented perpendicular to the z-vector. This metric provides a direct measure of dipole alignment with respect to the pore framework, thereby enabling a quantitative evaluation of confinement-induced orientational ordering.

To quantitatively evaluate the relationship between dielectric constant and diffusivity, the anisotropy of water diffusion was examined. The diffusion coefficient is a key indicator of molecular mobility, reflecting the influence of nanoscale confinement and direction-dependent intermolecular interactions. Directional diffusion coefficients were calculated from the mean squared displacement (MSD) obtained from molecular dynamics trajectories, using Einstein’s relation: Dα=limt→∞⁡<|rα(t)−rα(t0)|>22t(α=x,y,z) 7 Here, D α is the diffusion coefficient along direction α, r α(t) is the molecular position along direction α at time t, and t 0 denotes the initial time. This formulation allows decoupling of directional transport contributions and enables direct comparison with anisotropic dielectric behavior under confinement.

Results and Discussion

Five different polymeric NF membrane models were constructed. Figure shows the unit cells of these models after the infiltration of water molecules. Water molecules were mainly adsorbed in relatively large pores and were randomly distributed within the irregular pore morphologies. The pore structures vary among the models; however, no systematic trend was observed between the DETA/TMC and PIP/TMC systems. For all models, water molecules aggregated to form clusters in larger pore regions resembling cavities, whereas some molecules were isolated in small, confined spaces. From subsequent MD simulations, these isolated water molecules were found to exhibit very limited diffusion within the cell. They accounted for only 1–4% of the total water molecules depending on the models.

Figure shows the temporal evolution of the total dipole moment of water molecules in the system. The amplitude of dipole fluctuations in confined water is markedly smaller than that observed in bulk water. In the bulk phase, the total dipole moment fluctuates within a range of approximately ±250 D, whereas under confinement the fluctuation amplitude decreases to ∼ ±50 D. In bulk water, the hydrogen-bond network undergoes continuous formation and rupture driven by thermal motion, resulting in highly dynamic molecular rearrangements. In contrast, within the nanopores, rotational freedom is restricted by interactions with the pore walls and limited spatial degrees of freedom. Consequently, the molecular reorientation dynamics are significantly suppressed under confinement, resulting in reduced dipole moment fluctuations compared to bulk water. This confinement-induced suppression of dipole dynamics underpins the lower dielectric response observed in the following section.

We considered that trapped or isolated water molecules might bias the calculated dielectric constant. To evaluate their effect on the dielectric properties and dynamics of the system, we recalculated the dielectric constant after excluding these trapped/isolated molecules. For model (a), the resulting difference was only about 0.2%. This result indicates that the collective fluctuations of water molecules in the connected pathways contribute more significantly to the overall dipole fluctuation than the small number of trapped/isolated molecules. Similar behavior has also been reported in systems with a limited number of water molecules.

The average dielectric constants were 28.9 and 28.7 for the DETA/TMC and PIP/TMC polymeric NF models, respectively. Although the difference is small, DETA/TMC membranes are known to be more hydrophilic than PIP/TMC ones because the DETA possesses two hydrophilic amine groups. In principle, stronger water–polymer interactions in more hydrophilic pores suggest that they restrict molecular reorientation near the pore surface, which could lead to a lower dielectric response. In the present systems, there may be some differences in the interactions between water molecules and the DETA/TMC and PIP/TMC polymeric NF models. However, nearly identical dielectric constants were obtained.

For comparison, previous molecular dynamics studies on water confined within carbon nanotubes (CNTs) with pore sizes ranging from 0.83 to 1.93 nm reported dielectric constants that are slightly larger and nearly comparable to the bulk value of approximately 70 in their MD results. Because CNTs are considerably more hydrophobic than the polymeric NF membrane systems examined here, water molecules in CNT pores experience relatively unrestricted rotational motion due to weak interactions with the pore walls. In the present study, the employed potential model yields a bulk water dielectric constant of approximately 60.9, which is lower than the experimental value of 78.25 at 25 °C. In contrast, the MD study on water in CNTs used the SPC/E water potential, which gives a bulk dielectric constant of about 70 , indicating that the absolute dielectric value strongly depends on the chosen water model. In addition to dependence of the model, other factors may contribute to the observed discrepancy. While previous studies often employed the Parrinello–Rahman barostat under NPT conditions, , the present study employed the Berendsen barostat, maintaining a cubic unit cell throughout the simulation. Second, the treatment of long-range electrostatic interactions differs from that in prior studies, which commonly utilized the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) or PPPM methods. Since PME is not implemented in the Forcite module used here, the conventional Ewald summation method was adopted instead. These methodological differences may also influence the computed dielectric constant.

To further clarify the relationship between the observed decrease in dielectric constant and the restricted rotational dynamics of water molecules within the pores, the dipole autocorrelation function (DACF) of water molecules was calculated. The DACF provides direct insight into the rotational relaxation behavior of molecular dipoles. Figure shows the temporal evolution of the DACF for each directional component in both bulk water and water confined within the nanopores, calculated according to eq . It is evident that the DACF of confined water decays more slowly than that of bulk water, indicating that the rotational relaxation of water molecules is suppressed by interactions with the pore walls and spatial confinement. In particular, model (b) exhibits an extremely slow decay of the DACF along the x- and z-directions, suggesting limited pore connectivity or the presence of narrow pores comparable in size to a water molecule. When comparing the DETA/TMC and PIP/TMC systems, no significant differences in DACF behavior are observed. For all models, the DACF exhibits strong isotropy, suggesting that the nanopores are irregularly connected and lack a uniform directional orientation in the investigated polymeric NF membrane models.

To further elucidate the mechanisms underlying the restricted rotational motion of water molecules within the pores, an analysis focusing on molecular orientation was conducted. Specifically, the spatial distribution of the dipole moment vectors of water molecules was statistically evaluated using eq , and the results are presented in Figure . The analysis revealed that, in bulk water, the molecular orientation is isotropic, showing no preferential direction. In contrast, water confined within the pores (models (a)–(e)) exhibits a nonuniform distribution of dipole moment orientations, indicating orientational bias under confinement. It should be noted that, in a randomly connected pore network, the angular distribution evaluated with respect to a fixed global axis does not necessarily correspond to a unique pore direction. Therefore, the present analysis is intended only to qualitatively illustrate confinement-induced orientational bias rather than to determine a specific preferred orientation.

To elucidate the relationship between the dynamic behavior of water molecules within the pores and the anisotropy of the dielectric constant, directional differences in the diffusion coefficients were analyzed. Figure presents the diffusion coefficients of water confined within the pores along the x-, y-, and z-directions, together with the corresponding dielectric constants. The analysis revealed moderate correlations between the directional diffusion coefficients and the dielectric constants. When water molecules interact strongly with the pore walls, their orientational mobility is hindered, leading to reduced diffusivity. This assumption is generally supported by the trend observed in Figure ; however, in the range of low diffusivity, the dielectric constants exhibit a relatively wide distribution and the slope for model (a) is different from other models. We attribute this deviation to the fact that model (a) possesses the largest pore volume and a distinct pore size distribution, where the effect of spatial volume may outweigh the constraints imposed by tortuosity. The anisotropy of the diffusion coefficient suggests anisotropic pore tortuosity; however, direct quantification of structural anisotropy remains difficult. In addition, anisotropy in pore size distribution may also contribute to the observed behavior. These factors indicate that the dielectric constant exhibits only a moderate dependence on molecular diffusivity.

Conclusions

To evaluate the influence of polymer molecular structure on the dielectric properties of confined water, two representative polyamide systems were considered: one formed by the interfacial polymerization of trimesoyl chloride (TMC) with diethylenetriamine (DETA) and the other with piperazine (PIP). For each chemistry, multiple polymer configurations were constructed to capture realistic structural variability, yielding five distinct models with different cross-linking sites, cross-linking densities, and molecular weights. Rather than aiming to determine a statistically averaged dielectric constant for a specific material, these variations were strategically introduced to systematically assess how specific features of pore morphology and polymer heterogeneity influence the dielectric response of water confined within nanofiltration-scale pores.

By employing five distinct models, we covered a range of structural variations that are representative of the heterogeneous nature of polyamide networks. This approach allowed us to move beyond simple statistical averaging and instead perform a comparative analysis to isolate the specific morphological features, such as pore connectivity that governs the dielectric response of confined water.

The results revealed that the dielectric constant of water inside the pores is significantly lower than that of bulk water. This reduction arises primarily from the restriction of rotational motion and limited orientational freedom of water molecules due to spatial confinement and interactions with the pore walls. The dipole autocorrelation function (DACF) analysis confirmed slower rotational relaxation in confined systems compared to bulk water, indicating hindered dipole reorientation within the pore space. The orientational distribution analysis further showed that water dipoles exhibit weak to moderate anisotropy, with preferential alignment along certain directions depending on the pore structure.

Such orientational bias was found to be highly model-dependent, reflecting the irregular and randomly connected morphology of the simulated NF membranes. A comparison between DETA/TMC and PIP/TMC membranes showed only a minor difference in the average dielectric constants (28.9 vs 28.7), suggesting that the effect of pore geometry and connectivity is more dominant than polymer hydrophilicity.

The relationship between dielectric constant and relaxation time (τ) revealed an approximately inverse correlation, indicating that slower molecular reorientation corresponds to lower dielectric constant. Furthermore, the dielectric constant exhibited a moderate dependence on the diffusion coefficients of confined water, implying that dynamic constraints and dielectric response are coupled through the degree of molecular mobility within the pores. Overall, this study provides molecular-level insights into how nanoscale confinement and polymer–water interactions govern the dielectric properties of water in NF membranes.

The results obtained in this study provide quantitative dielectric values that can enhance the predictive accuracy of NF membrane transport models, such as DSPM-DE, which have traditionally relied on fitted parameters. In addition, these findings offer transferable insights applicable to other polymeric membrane materials, thereby supporting the broader development of NF membranes with tailored electrostatic and separation performance.

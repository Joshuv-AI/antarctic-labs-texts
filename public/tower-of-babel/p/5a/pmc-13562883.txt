Low-Dose Radiation-Induced Expression of Exosomal miRNAs in the Serum of Medical Radiation Workers
Authors: Ping Wang, Leshi Wei, Yu Gao, Xianfei Niu, Jie Li, Shasha Du, Yuanbo Cheng, Yunfei Zhang, Siqi Fang, Xiaohao Li, Lin Han, Yumin Lyu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective: Long-term low-dose ionizing radiation may alter genomes and cause diseases. This study examined serum exosomal miRNA differences among radiation-exposed medical workers by work types. Methods: High-throughput sequencing screened serum exosomal miRNAs in diagnostic radiologists, interventional radiologists, and nuclear medicine doctors. qRT-PCR validated differentially expressed miRNAs. Results: 410 differentially expressed miRNAs, including 102 downregulated and 308 upregulated miRNAs, enriched in 15 KEGG pathways. Four miRNAs were validated. miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p and miR-486-5p differed significantly among groups(P<0.01).The expression levels of these three miRNAs were lower in the nuclear medicine group than in the diagnostic group (0.25±0.05vs3.89±8.62,0.10±0.15vs7.03±15.75,0.37±0.39vs4.46±9.64;Z=3.542,3.335,9.859;P<0.01), whereas miR-155-5p was lower in the interventional group than in the diagnostic group(1.32±2.43vs 7.03±15.75;H=11.889,P=0.003), miR-486-5p was higher(9.54±19.39vs4.46±9.64; H=9.859,P=0.007). All four miRNAs showed higher expression in the 14-31-year work-experience group, and all four also showed age-dependent differences, with miR-30d-5p and miR-486-5p showing the age-related differences (P<0.01). Combined detection of the four miRNAs improved discrimination among occupational categories (AUC=0.784). Multiple linear regression analysis showed that sex was associated with all four miRNAs, whereas work type was associated with miR-30d-5p and miR-155-5p (P<0.05). Conclusion: Serum exosomal miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-486-5p are promising biomarkers for monitoring radiation-induced damage, supporting occupational protection and health management.Keywords: low-dose radiation, radiation workers, exosomes, miRNAs, high-throughput screening

Introduction

The risk of ionizing radiation exposure among medical workers has attracted increasing attention because of the expanding use of radiation equipment in clinical practice. In recent years, sensitive biomarkers for evaluating damage induced by low-dose ionizing radiation have become an important research focus in this field. Cytogenetic indicators, such as chromosomal aberrations (CA) and micronucleus (MN) frequency, are commonly used as biomarkers of radiation-induced DNA damage. These indicators provide direct insights into genotoxic effects by reflecting structural chromosomal changes without major confounding interference.1-3 However, these cytogenetic markers have notable limitations. Their dose-effect relationships are typically nonlinear, with a threshold dose rate below which the frequency of abnormal cells remains stable but above which damage increases sharply, complicating low-dose assessment. 4 Moreover, responses may be influenced by non-targeted effects and genetic variability, reducing their reliability for personalized risk prediction in low-dose scenarios where inter-individual differences are pronounced5-7.8,9 These limitations highlight the need to explore biomarkers with better sensitivity and specificity. Exosomal microRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as promising candidates because they mediate radiation responses and may serve as tissue-specific diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in cancers and radiation-induced injuries.10-15 For instance, dysregulated exosomal miRNA profiles, such as miR-142-5p in radiation protection and miR-21 in tumor proliferation, can reflect cellular damage and repair mechanisms.16,17 Despite this potential, few studies have investigated the changes of exosomal miRNAs of medical radiation workers exposed chronically to low-dose radiation. Therefore, it is necessary to systematically analyze the changes of serum exosomal miRNAs from medical workers in different radiation-related occupations, including diagnostic radiologists , interventional radiologists, and nuclear medicine doctors by using high-throughput sequencing, hoping to find possible potential markers and better determine their health status.

Methods

Study Subjects

All study participants in the screening and validation experiments underwent occupational health examinations at the Third People’s Hospital of Henan Province. The study was conducted from January 2024 to October 2025. A total of 29 participants were enrolled for sequencing in the screening experiment and were divided into three groups according to work type: diagnostic radiologists (DRs), interventional radiologists (IRs), and nuclear medicine doctors (NMDs). For the validation experiment, 86 radiation workers were selected and divided by work type into the DRs, IRs, and NMDs groups. The IRs (n=29) and NMDs (n=27) groups were assigned as experimental groups, and the DRs group served as the age- and sex-matched low-dose exposed reference group.

Personal Dose Measurement

In accordance with the GBZ 128-2019 standard for occupational external exposure personal dose monitoring, thermoluminescent dosimetry (TLD) was used for dose assessment. The dosimeters were worn for quarterly periods, totaling four monitoring cycles annually. For interventional radiology staff, simultaneous dose measurements were performed both inside and outside lead aprons. When a single monitoring cycle result exceeded 1.25 mSv, the individual was issued a “High Dose Verification Registration Form”. Erroneous values were excluded, and nominal doses were applied for subsequent statistical analysis to calculate both the average annual effective dose per capita and the collective effective dose. The photon dose was calculated as Hp (10) = ( x¯ 0 - x¯ ) ×Cf, where Hp(10) represents the personal dose equivalent at 10 mm depth (mSv); x¯ denotes the mean reading of worn detectors (mGy range); x¯ 0 indicates the mean reading of background control detectors (mGy range); Cf is the calibration factor of the TLD reader (mSv per reading, mGy range). In this study, the frequency of individual dose monitoring was set according to exposure risk level (monthly, quarterly, and semi-annually for high-, medium-, and low-risk groups, respectively). Monitoring data were organized and verified within 7 working days after each monitoring period, and traceable individual dose archives were established. To account for seasonal variations in radiation exposure, measurement accuracy was ensured through preliminary investigation, quarterly calibration, and stratified data analysis. A standardized protocol was established for the identification (absolute anomalies: single dose exceeding 1/10 of the annual dose limit specified in GB 18871-2002 or 3 standard deviations above the mean dose of the same group in the same period; relative anomalies: consecutive dose increase >50% without clear cause) and management (early warning, cause investigation, classified processing, and record archiving) of abnormal dose values. Meanwhile, a comprehensive quality control system was implemented, including instrument verification and calibration, standardized operating procedures, two-person data verification, and third-party review every six months.

Isolation and Identification of Serum Exosomes

Four milliliters of peripheral blood were collected from radiation-exposed workers and placed in sterile blood collection tubes. The samples were then centrifuged at 3000 r/min (centrifugation radius 16.7 cm) for 10 min. The upper serum layer was carefully separated, transferred into 1.5 mL sterile Eppendorf tubes, sealed, and stored at -80°C in an ultra-low-temperature freezer until further analysis. Exosomes were extracted using the Serum Exosome Isolation Kit (EIQ3-01001, H-Wayen, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Finally, 150 µL of suspension solution was obtained and stored at -80°C. Size and concentration measurements of the purified exosomal samples were performed using a ZetaView Nanoparticle Tracking Analyzer (NTA) (Particle Metrix, USA). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to observe the morphological and ultrastructural characteristics of exosomes. Western blot (WB) analysis of CD63, Alix, and TSG101 was used to confirm the protein expression profile of exosomes.

RNA Extraction and miRNA Sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using an miRNeasy Micro Kit (217084, Qiagen, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The quantity and quality of the RNA were determined using the RNA ScreenTape assay on an Agilent 4200 TapeStation system (Agilent, CA, USA). miRNA library preparation for high-throughput sequencing was performed using a QIAseq miRNA Library Kit (Qiagen, Germany). Briefly, the constructed libraries were quantified using the Agilent High Sensitivity DNA Kit. The libraries were then diluted as required and sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The quality of the RNA-seq data was assessed using FastQC.

Differential miRNA Expression Analysis

After raw miRNA-seq data (FASTQ format) were preprocessed to remove low-quality reads and adapter sequences, sequence alignment and miRNA identification were performed. A raw read-count matrix was generated for each miRNA across all samples. The raw sequencing data were normalized using counts per million (CPM) transformation to reduce technical variation caused by differences in sequencing depth across samples. Differential expression analysis was performed using statistical methods, with the selection criteria set as adjusted P value < 0.05 and |log2-fold change (FC)| > 1.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Pathway Enrichment Analysis

For target genes of the selected differentially expressed miRNAs, the hypergeometric distribution algorithm in the clusterProfiler package was used to analyze Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment. GO annotation of differentially expressed miRNA target genes was used to identify enriched GO categories and functional changes associated with these target genes. 10 Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the same approach.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

To verify the expression of these differentially expressed miRNAs in radiation workers, four miRNAs (hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-30d-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p) were selected for qRT-PCR analysis between the experimental and control groups. First, exosomal miRNAs were extracted using the BIOG Exosome miRbNA Easy Kit, and 30 µL of miRNA was ultimately obtained from 150 µL of exosomes. cel-miR-39 was added as an external reference. Second, the Bulge-Loop miRNA qRT-PCR Starter Kit (C10211-2, RIBOBIO, Guangzhou, China) was used for cDNA synthesis with stem-loop RT primers. All primers were synthesized by RIBO Biotech Co. (Guangzhou, China). The exosomal miRNAs (hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-30d-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p) were then detected by real-time PCR using the Bulge-Loop miRNA qRT-PCR Starter Kit (C10211-2, RIBOBIO, Guangzhou, China). Three replicates were performed for each miRNA, with a blank control. The 2-ΔΔCt method was used to normalize the Ct value of each miRNA to the Ct value of cel-miR-39-3p in each sample.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 software. Continuous variables were tested for normality. If the data followed a normal distribution, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used; otherwise, the Kruskal-Wallis H test was applied. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate diagnostic performance, and the optimal cut-off value was determined by maximizing Youden’s index. The 95% confidence intervals for the area under the curve (AUC) were calculated using DeLong’s nonparametric method. Multiple linear regression analysis was used to examine associations between miRNA expression and factors such as age, sex, work type, work experience, and personal dose. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Characterization of Serum-Derived Exosomes

After vesicles were extracted from the peripheral blood serum of the study subjects, exosome morphology was examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Figure 1a and 1b). The exosomes exhibited typical cup-shaped morphology and a bilayer phospholipid membrane structure. Nanoparticle Tracking Analyzer (NTA) was used to determine particle-size distribution and concentration. The concentration was 1.5×1011/ml, and the mean serum particle size was 139.5 nm. A particle-size peak of approximately 100 nm was observed (Figure 1c). To further characterize the isolated exosomes, the exosomal protein markers CD63, Alix, and TSG101 were used for identification. As shown in Figure 1d, the exosomes were positive for CD63, Alix, and TSG101 and negative for Calnexin.

High-Throughput Sequencing and Bioinformatics Analysis of Serum Exosomal miRNAs Among Radiation Workers

In this study, serum exosomal miRNA expression profiles were evaluated by miRNA sequencing and bioinformatics analysis in 29 workers from three matched groups. To display overall variation in miRNA expression among the three groups, an integrative heatmap was constructed using 410 selected differentially expressed miRNAs according to |log2(fold change)|>1 and padj<0.05 (Figure 2a). In the figure, blue indicates low expression, red indicates high expression, and the connecting lines represent clustering results. Among these 410 selected miRNAs, 102 were downregulated and 308 were upregulated. In the comparison between the IRs and DRs groups, 115 differential miRNAs were identified, including 62 downregulated and 53 upregulated miRNAs. Between the NMDs and DRs groups, 1117 differential miRNAs were identified, including 735 downregulated and 382 upregulated miRNAs. To visualize these results, a volcano plot was generated (Figure 2b). Blue represents downregulated genes, and red represents upregulated genes. The selected differentially expressed miRNAs were predicted and annotated using the miRTarBase database, and the clusterProfiler R package was used to perform GO functional enrichment analysis of their target genes. For visualization, the top 10 enriched GO terms (P<0.05) were selected and are presented in Figure 2c. In the biological process category, target genes were mainly enriched in cellular macromolecule localization and protein localization. In the cellular component category, target genes were mainly associated with intracellular vesicles and cytoplasmic vesicles. In the molecular function category, the primary enrichments were small molecule binding and anion binding. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed to identify the pathways in which target genes of the differential miRNAs were significantly enriched. The 15 entries with the smallest P values were selected to generate a dot plot (Figure 2d) in which dot color represents the P value and dot size represents the count. The results showed significant enrichment in the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, Hippo signaling pathway, signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells, Alzheimer disease, pathways of neurodegeneration in multiple diseases, and pathways in cancer.

GO and KEGG Analyses of Four miRNA Expression Changes

The four differential miRNAs (hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-30d-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p) were selected to identify associations between miRNA expression and low-dose radiation, based on the smallest P values derived from comparisons with the DRs group. In the screening experiment, all four miRNAs showed increased expression. GO functional enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway analysis were then conducted to predict the target genes of these four miRNAs. In the biological process category, the target genes were mainly enriched in negative regulation of nitrogen compound metabolic processes and cellular response to stress. In the cellular component category, the target genes were mainly associated with chromosomes and catalytic complexes. In the molecular function category, the primary enrichments were enzyme binding and protein-containing complex binding (Figure 3a). KEGG pathway analysis indicated that low-dose ionizing radiation was significantly associated with involvement of the four differential miRNAs in the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications and pathways in cancer (Figure 3b).

Relative Expression of Serum miRNAs in the Three Occupational Groups

qRT-PCR was used to observe the expression profiles of the four differentially expressed miRNAs in populations exposed to low-dose radiation. hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p and hsa-miR-486-5p differed significantly overall among the three groups (H=12.815,11.889,9.859, P<0.01). As shown in Table 3, hsa-miR-30e-5p and hsa-miR-155-5p were significantly downregulated in the NMDs group compared with the DRs group (Z=3.542,3.335, P<0.01). The expression of hsa-miR-486-5p was significantly lower in the NMDs group than in the IRs group (Z=3.064, P<0.01). No significant difference in hsa-miR-30d-5p expression was observed among the three groups. In male radiation workers, hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p and hsa-miR-486-5p also differed significantly overall among the three groups, whereas hsa-miR-30d-5p did not show any significant differences. In female radiation workers, hsa-miR-486-5p only differed significantly overall among the three groups. It is worth noting that has-miR-486-5p expression levels in IRs group were higher than those in DRs group among men, women and overall population.

Relative Expression of Serum miRNAs in Different age Groups

In this study, radiation workers were divided into four age groups: 28-32, 33-36, 37-40, and 41-54 years. The grouping was based on the equal-interval method, and sample sizes were broadly balanced across groups. The expression of miR-30d-5p, miR-30e-5p and miR-486-5p showed significant differences in four age groups(H=11.537,6.871,11.902, P<0.01,Table 6). In addition, the expression level of miR-30e-5p increased with age (r=0.177, P=0.022), whereas the expression of other miRNAs was not associated with age. Among male radiation workers, the expression levels of miR-30d-5p and miR-486-5p differed across the four age groups(H=10.452,6.583, P<0.05).However, among female radiation workers, none of these four miRNAs showed significant differences. Furthermore, these four miRNAs expression levels in radiation of different sex were not associated with age(Male: r=0.140,0.142,0.113,0.212,P>0.05; Female: r=0.070,0.253,-0.044,0.014,P>0.05).

Validation of the Diagnostic Performance of the Four miRNAs Across Different Work Types

The diagnostic performance of individual miRNAs was as follows: miR-30e-5p (AUC=0.701) showed moderate discriminative performance, miR-155-5p (AUC=0.656) and miR-30d-5p (AUC=0.564) showed limited performance, and miR-486-5p (AUC=0.513) exhibited negligible diagnostic value. In contrast, the multi-miRNA combination (AUC=0.784) showed better performance than the individual miRNAs (Figure 4).

Discussion

The vesicles isolated from serum in this study were identified as exosomes using three core criteria, consistent with established findings from multiple cell sources. 18 First, their particle size ranged from 80 to 150 nm, which is within the typical size range of exosomes. 19 Second, marker detection showed positive expression of the exosomal proteins CD63, TSG101, and Alix and negative expression of Calnexin. This ‘three-positive and one-negative’ profile is an important criterion for distinguishing exosomes from other extracellular vesicles. 20 Based on these results, the vesicles extracted from serum were confirmed as exosomes according to their size, morphology, and exosomal protein marker expression.

Many studies have shown that radiation affects exosomal miRNA expression, and such changes may provide important information for research on diseases such as cancer and diabetic complications, with potential relevance to neuropathological mechanisms.21,22 In the present study, four differentially expressed exosomal miRNAs (hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-30d-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p) were identified in radiation workers from three different work types. As differentially expressed miRNAs in serum exosomes, these may be associated with low-dose ionizing radiation and, following further research, could help assess the risk of diabetic complications and cancer in radiation workers. Studies have shown that in X-ray-resistant cell lines, the expression of hsa-miR-30e-5p expression is dysregulated and modulates the MAPK signaling pathway by targeting MAPK1. 23 MiR-30e-5p inhibits inflammatory responses by targeting NLRP3 and is regulated by melatonin in radiation-induced lung injury.24,25 Array-based miRNA expression studies showed increased expression of miRNAs has-miR-30e-5p in gingiva affected by periodontal inflammation. 26 Our findings show that miR-30e expression levels in the nuclear medicine group were significantly lower than those in the diagnostic group, and the results for male radiation workers were consistent with the overall findings. miR-30e expression levels were higher among radiation workers aged 37–40 than in other age groups. Analysis of influencing factors revealed that miR-30e is associated with sex, which is consistent with the results from the job category analysis. This indicates that miR-30e expression levels in radiation workers are associated with being male and working in NMDs.

miR-155-5p was also sensitive to long-term occupational exposure, in line with its reported roles in inflammation and aging.27-29It has been demonstrated that low-dose radiation can induce upregulation of microRNA-155-5p expression in both normal breast cells (MCF-10A) and breast cancer cell lines (MCF-7). 30 However, the results showed that the serum exosomal miR-155 expression levels among radiation workers in the NMDs were significantly lower than those in DRs; the same results were observed among male radiation workers. This discrepancy may be attributable to differences in cumulative radiation doses between cell lines and radiation workers: cell lines typically receive higher acute doses, while radiation workers are exposed to lower chronic doses, resulting in distinct biological effects. In addition, there were no statistically significant differences in miR-155-5p expression levels among different groups based on year of service or age. A potential mechanism is that radiation promotes miR-155-5p expression, thereby reducing inflammatory responses and promoting apoptosis.31,32

In thyroid tumors, hsa-miR-486-5p is downregulated after gamma-ray radiation exposure. 33 The results showed that there were statistically differences in miR-486-5p expression levels among different work type groups. Amang male, female and all radiation workers, miR-486-5p expression levels were significantly higher in IRs than in DRs. The expression level of miR-486-5p increased with work-experience of radiation workers. Importantly, miR-486-5p expression was most markedly increased in the long-term radiation exposure group (14-31 years). These results indicated that miR-486-5p expression levels was associated with IRs, maleand age; so miR-486-5p expression may be associated with dose gradient of low-dose radiation. These results consistented with multiple previous studies.34,35 In IRs, higher miR-486-5p expression may correlate with DNA damage repair.The relevant mechanism was Targeting MAPK pathway genes, downregulated hsa-miR-486-5p boosts radiosensitivity, inhibits tumor DNA repair and drives radiation-induced cell death, while its post-radiation reduction in mesenchymal stem cell exosomes weakens tissue repair and aggravates radiation injury, a phenomenon linked to chronic inflammation, cellular senescence and tissue repair.26,29,34-36

Current research has focused on the regulatory role of miR-30d-5p in tumors, diabetes, autoimmune diseases, and metabolic disorders. Our study found that there was stisstatistically differences in miR-30d-5p expression levels among different work type groups in female radiation workers. Age-dependent upregulated miR-30d-5p, which correlates with the NFATC signaling pathway, may mirror aging-related chronic inflammation accumulation. 36 Chronic inflammation can exacerbate tissue damage after radiation exposure, and miR-30d-5p may alleviate inflammatory responses by inhibiting the NFATC pathway.36,37 Similar to miR-486-5p, miR-30d-5p expression was most markedly increased in the long-term radiation exposure group (14-31 years), linking its expression to occupational exposure duration and associated biological processes, such as chronic inflammation, cellular senescence, and tissue repair.26,29,36 miR-30d-5p is also associated with work type and sex. Collectively, miR-155-5p and miR-486-5p and may be key regulators of radiation responses by modulating inflammation, cell death, and DNA repair pathways, making them valuable molecular targets for occupational health risk assessment and precision radiation medicine.

Low-dose ionizing radiation can significantly modulate miRNA expression, and these changes may show dose-dependent characteristics. 38 Low-dose radiation alters DNA damage repair-related miR-21 and miR-625, which modulate post-radiation cell survival via DNA damage response pathways, while chronic low-dose radiation exposure puts radiation workers at risk of DNA damage and tumorigenesis.39-42These miRNAs may therefore serve as sensitive indicators of cumulative radiation effects and associated pathophysiological processes. Notably, in the annual effective dose analysis, only miR-30d-5p showed a statistically significant between-group difference, whereas miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-486-5p did not reach statistical significance. This finding suggests that individual miRNAs may have limited standalone value for dose-related assessment and that a multi-indicator combined detection system is needed. Among these miRNAs, miR-30d-5p and miR-486-5p showed the most significant increases in the long-term exposure group (14-31 years), consistent with previous reports of their high expression in tissue repair and disease.43,44

Age-related changes in miRNA expression were observed for all four miRNAs, consistent with their roles in age-related immune regulation and inflammatory processes.27,36,45 Sex also influenced miRNA responses to radiation, with relative miRNA expression being more sensitive to radiation exposure in male workers. In males, higher expression of miR-486-5p and miR-155-5p may be associated with DNA damage repair; these findings were consistent with a previous reports. 45

In the NMDs group, downregulation of hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-486-5p, and hsa-miR-155-5p was more pronounced. This may be because nuclear medicine work involves radionuclide therapy, in which internal exposure to continuous low-dose ionizing radiation can accumulate more DNA damage than external exposure, as internal contamination has been shown to induce higher levels of DNA damage.46,47 In the IRs group, upregulation of has-miR-486-5p was seen. These results suggest that different radiological working environments may regulate the expression of hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p. Furthermore, radiation exposure may downregulate miR-30e-5p and miR-155-5p, implying that the differential expression of miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p, and hsa-miR-486-5p may have potential as biomarkers for classifying radiation exposure. Notably, the expression trends of certain miRNAs verified by qRT-PCR were inconsistent with those obtained from high-throughput sequencing. This discrepancy may be partly attributable to the relatively small sample size, which could limit the stability and reproducibility of the high-throughput sequencing data. In addition, future studies should consider more robust and standardized normalization procedures, including multiple stable reference miRNAs and correction for technical and biological variability, before differential expression analysis. A total of 410 differentially expressed miRNAs were identified by high-throughput sequencing; therefore, future studies should expand the number of validated differential miRNAs to further consolidate the reliability of the screening results.

This study used the diagnostic group as the control group, which has limitations because the diagnostic group was also occupationally exposed to low-dose ionizing radiation, albeit at very low doses. Therefore, future studies should include healthy individuals with no history of occupational or medical radiation exposure as a more appropriate control cohort. Furthermore, future research should examine the mechanisms and differential diagnostic applications of these miRNAs, particularly their interactions with signaling pathways, to develop new strategies for reducing risks associated with occupational exposure. The development of differential diagnostic tools based on these miRNAs would help improve strategies for reducing occupational radiation exposure risks. In addition, combined miRNA detection systems that address the limited performance of individual markers will be critical for advancing clinical utility in occupational health risk assessment and precision radiation medicine. Future studies should also incorporate formal interaction-term analyses or sex-stratified analyses within each occupational subgroup to clarify the modifying role of sex in radiation-associated miRNA dysregulation.

Conclusion

In summary, high-throughput sequencing of peripheral blood serum exosomal miRNAs from diagnostic radiologists, interventional radiologists, and nuclear medicine doctors characterized differential miRNA expression profiles, and identified 410 differentially expressed miRNAs. In radiation workers, the relative expression levels of miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-486-5p differed significantly among the three occupational groups, whereas miR-30d-5p did not differ significantly. Work type and sex significantly affected the relative expression levels of these miRNAs. Based on the combined relative expression levels of these miRNAs, radiation workers with different work types can be distinguished (AUC=0.784). This study confirmed that molecules in serum exosomes, including miR-30e-5p, miR-155-5p, and miR-486-5p, have potential as sensitive indicators for monitoring radiation-induced damage in medical radiation workers. These findings provide an early monitoring method with clinical application prospects for workers with occupational exposure. They may enable early warning of health risks before clinical symptoms develop and provide a scientific basis for occupational protection and health management.

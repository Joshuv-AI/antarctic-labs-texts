Albuminuria in Type 2 Diabetes and Risk of Cardiorenal Outcomes
Authors: Saaima Parveen, Morten Malmborg, Anojhaan Arulmurugananthavadivel, Nicholas Carlson, Charlotte Andersson, Camilla Fuchs Andersen, Jesper Jensen, Deewa Zahir Anjum, Mariam Elmegaard, Talip Eroglu, Emil Fosbøl, Gunnar Gislason, Lars Køber, Morten Schou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlbuminuria is strongly associated with cardiorenal morbidity and mortality. However, real-world prevalence and the associated risks remain unclear.ObjectivesThis study aims to investigate the prevalence of patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) with and without albuminuria and the subsequent risk of cardiorenal outcomes, and the eligibility for treatment with cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs.MethodsUsing the Danish nationwide registers, we identified patients ≥18 years with T2D at index January 1, 2015, with a urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and a creatinine level measured within 365 days prior. Patients were grouped by albuminuria (UACR ≥30 mg/g) and normoalbuminuria (UACR <30 mg/g). The primary endpoint was a composite of heart failure, myocardial infarction, stroke, or all-cause death, and the secondary endpoint was a composite of end-stage renal disease or a sustained decrease in the estimated glomerular filtration rate ≥50%. Absolute 4-year risks were calculated using the Kaplan-Meier and Aalen-Johansen estimators.ResultsWe included 74,014 patients, of whom 29,581 (40%) had albuminuria. Patients with albuminuria had a longer duration of diabetes (8.2 vs 6.9 years), lower estimated glomerular filtration rate (76 vs 83) and males were over-represented (62.6% vs 50.5%). The absolute 4-year risk of the primary outcome was 28.6% (95% CI: 28.1%-29.1%) vs 18.7% (95% CI: 18.4%-19.1%) for albuminuria vs normoalbuminuria, and for the secondary outcome, it was 8.7% (95% CI: 8.4%-9.0%) vs 2.9% (95% CI: 2.8%-3.1%), respectively.ConclusionsThe prevalence of patients with T2D and albuminuria was 40% and may benefit from cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs, whereas 60% with normoalbuminuria still have a high residual risk of cardiovascular disease, warranting increased focus.

Albuminuria is a key biomarker strongly associated with adverse cardiovascular and renal events as well as an increased mortality.1, 2, 3 Despite its clinical importance, the extent to which unselected and consecutive patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) undergo the guideline-recommended annual screening for urinary albumin excretion, and thus the prevalence of albuminuria in patients with T2D, is not well established. New classes of cardioprotective and renoprotective therapies, such as sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, and nonsteroidal selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (nsMRAs), have demonstrated outcome benefits in patients with elevated urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR).4, 5, 6, 7, 8 However, recent pathophysiological studies show that early renal and vascular damage is already present within the normoalbuminuric condition (UACR <30 mg/g).9 Current clinical guidelines do not focus on such residual risks in patients with normoalbuminuria, and the risk of cardiovascular and chronic kidney disease in this subgroup is poorly elucidated.

The aims of this national study were, therefore, to investigate the prevalence of albuminuria and normoalbuminuria in patients with T2D, and to highlight gaps in albuminuria testing in routine practice. Furthermore, to identify patients potentially eligible for treatment with new cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs, for example, nsMRAs, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors. In addition, the study aimed to determine the 4-year risk of heart failure (HF), myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, and all-cause death in a cohort of patients with T2D with albuminuria and without albuminuria. And secondly, to determine the risk of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and a sustained decrease in the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥50% in the 2 groups.

Methods

Data sources

Each resident in Denmark has a unique and permanent personal identification number that enables individual-level linkage between nationwide registries. Data were obtained from 5 administrative registries through Statistics Denmark. The Danish Civil Registry provides information about age, gender, date of birth, immigration, and emigration since 1968.10 The Danish National Patient Registry contains information on all hospitalized patients. Each hospital contact is registered with a primary diagnosis and up to several contributing secondary diagnoses at discharge according to the 10th revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) since 1994.11 The Danish National Causes of Death Registry contains information on the time of death from 1970 onwards.12 The Register of Laboratory provides information of all samples from general practitioners, out-patient clinics, and hospitalized patients from 4 out of 5 regions in Denmark from January 1, 2014, to March 20, 2019, according to the Committee on Nomenclature, Properties and Units in Laboratory Medicine.13 The Danish National Prescription Registry contains information of all prescriptions claimed since 1995 according to the Anatomical Therapeutic Classification (ATC).14

Population

We identified all patients ≥18 years of age with prevalent T2D at index date January 1, 2015 with an UACR and a creatinine level measured within 365 days before the index date. The study population was separated into 2 groups consisting of patients with T2D with UACR ≥30 mg/g (albuminuria) or UACR <30 mg/g (normoalbuminuria), respectively. Patients were included from the 4 of 5 regions in Denmark with access to laboratory samples, thereby entirely excluding patients from the fifth region before the study start. Patients with a cancer diagnosis <1 year before the index date or known HF were excluded from the study. Other key exclusion criteria included patients with an eGFR <25 mL/min/1.73 m2 and patients with ESRD (kidney transplant, chronic dialysis or eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m2) at index (Figures 1A and 1B). Prevalent T2D was defined as a treatment with an antidiabetic medication (ATC = A10 excluding Saxenda; positive predictive value 95%, sensitivity 72% in the Danish registries15) or discharge diagnosis of T2D (ICD-10 code DE11). Patients with assumed type 1 diabetes (monotherapy of insulin, ATC codes A10 A, before age 30 or any antidiabetic therapy, ATC codes A10, before age 18) were excluded. Patients with antidiabetic drug dispensations, that were presumed prescribed for possible polycystic ovary syndrome or gestational diabetes, were excluded.16 If the only prescribed medication was metformin (ATC = A10BA02) in women <40 years of age, the individual was considered to potentially have polycystic ovary syndrome, and these dispensations were excluded from the analysis. However, for women diagnosed with possible polycystic ovary syndrome, any prescriptions claimed after the 40th birthday were considered diabetes medications and included from the first purchase date following their 40th birthday. For women without a diabetes diagnosis before pregnancy (defined as 42 weeks before childbirth, ICD-10 codes O80-84), those who claimed an insulin prescription between 42 weeks before and 30 days after childbirth were classified as having gestational diabetes, and any glucose-lowering medication dispensed during and around pregnancy were not considered.16Figure 1Flowchart and Inclusion of Patients(A) Flowchart. Comorbidity, medication, and laboratory samples

For each patient, the last value of UACR, eGFR, sodium, potassium, hemoglobin, glycosylated hemoglobin, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, registered 365 days before the index date were used. The chronic kidney disease Epidemiology Collaboration formula was used to calculate eGFR from creatinine.17 All samples measured during an overnight hospital stay were excluded to ensure clinical accuracy. Comorbidity was obtained using primary and secondary inpatient and outpatient diagnosis codes before the index date, except for hypertension. Hypertension was defined as a treatment with at least 2 classes of antihypertensive drugs within 180 days before the index date, as previously validated.18 Further details of the definition of comorbidities (atrial fibrillation, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, ischemic heart disease, MI, and stroke) are presented in Supplemental Table 1. Medications at baseline were defined as dispensed prescriptions within 180 days before the index date. ATC- and Nomenclature, Properties and Units in Laboratory Medicine-codes for the definition of baseline medication and laboratory samples are also presented in Supplemental Table 1.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was defined as a primary or secondary inpatient diagnosis of HF hospitalization, MI, stroke, or death from all causes. Each component of the primary outcome was also analyzed individually. The secondary outcome was a composite of first occurrence of ESRD or a sustained decrease in the eGFR from baseline, where sustained was defined as 2 consecutive eGFR measurements of at least 50% decrease in the eGFR compared to the eGFR at index for a period of at least 28 days. The start date of the outcome was the date of the last measurement of eGFR (Supplemental Figure A). ESRD was defined as the first occurrence of either a sustained eGFR of <15 mL/min/1.73 m2, or initiation of dialysis (at least 12 dialysis within 90 days) or kidney transplantation. A sustained decrease was defined as 2 consecutive eGFR measurements of <15 mL/min/1.73 m2 for a period of at least 28 days, where the date of the outcome was the date of the last measurement of eGFR (Supplemental Figure B). Each component of the renal composite endpoint was also analyzed individually. Further details of the definition of outcomes are described in Supplemental Table 1. All diagnosis codes used have been estimated and validated with high positive predictive values in the Danish National Patient Registry.19, 20, 21

Statistics

Baseline characteristics at index were reported as percentages for categorical variables and continuous variables with approximately normal distributions (assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, skewness, and visual inspection of histograms) were presented as mean ± SD, whereas skewed variables were presented as medians with IQR. Missing values were quantified for all biomarkers and are reported in Table 1. These variables (potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, glycosylated hemoglobin, low-density lipoprotein, and high-density lipoprotein) were only presented descriptively in Table 1 and were not included in subsequent analyses; therefore, no imputation strategy was applied. Patients were followed from the index date until the outcome of interest, emigration, death, or end of the study (December 31, 2018), whichever came first. Absolute risks of all-cause death and the primary and secondary outcome were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier estimator and Aalen-Johansen estimator, respectively. Absolute risks of the individual outcomes (HF, MI, stroke, ESRD, and sustained decrease of ≥50% in the eGFR from baseline) were estimated using the Aalen-Johansen estimator which incorporated the competing risk of death. Additional analyses were performed using restricted cubic spline multivariable Cox regression analysis with 3 knots to calculate HR curves and evaluate the relationship between UACR as a continuous variable and: 1) all-cause death; 2) HF, MI, or stroke; 3) ESRD or sustained decrease of ≥50% in the eGFR; and 4) HF. Cox regression analysis for each outcome was performed with and without adjustment. Variables in the adjusted model included sex, age, diabetes duration, eGFR, hypertension, atrial fibrillation and ischemic heart disease, and the use of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (RASi) and statins. The reference value in restricted cubic spline model was an UACR of 30 mg/g and served as a baseline when fitting the model, and knots were allocated at UACR of 6, 20, and 232 mg/g. The number of knots for the spline models were selected based on standard recommendations, visual inspection of the data distribution, and model performance metrics (Akaike Information Criterion, Bayesian Information criterion, and Concordance Index). P value for nonlinearity was P < 0.0001 for all models. The level of statistical significance was set at 5%. Ethics

Retrospective registry-based studies do not require ethics committee approval in Denmark. This study was approved by the Capital Region of Denmark (approval number: P-2019 to 348) in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 181,241 patients with T2D were identified at index; of which, 88,352 (49%) had UACR assessed within 1 year (Figure 1A, Supplemental Figure 1). Of those, who met the inclusion criteria, 74,014 had UACR assessed within 1 year before the index. Of these, 29,581 (40%) patients fulfilled the criteria for albuminuria and 44,433 (60%) were classified as normoalbuminuric. Baseline characteristics for both groups are presented in Table 1. The median age in the study population was 67.5 years (25th-75th percentile 58.0-74.8 years), and patients with albuminuria were older than those with normoalbuminuria, 68.7 years (59.2-76.2) vs 66.8 years (57.1-73.8), respectively. Males with albuminuria were over-represented (62.6% vs 50.5%). Patients with albuminuria had a longer duration of diabetes (8.2 vs 6.9 years), lower eGFR (76 vs 83), and higher level of glycosylated hemoglobin (52.0 vs 50.0). The presence of comorbidities at baseline were higher for patients with albuminuria, and dispensed prescriptions of heart medication were higher as well. The use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists was 3.6% in the study population with no significant difference between the 2 groups. Patients with T2D, who did not have UACR assessed, were relatively younger, with a lower burden of comorbidities, and a reduced number of prescribed medications (Supplemental Table 2).

Cardiovascular outcomes

Figures 2A-E illustrates the absolute risk of the primary outcome, including each individual component in patients with albuminuria and normoalbuminuria. The absolute 4-year risk of the primary outcome in patients with albuminuria and normoalbuminuria was 28.6% (95% CI: 28.1%-29.1%) vs 18.7% (95% CI: 18.4%-19.1%), respectively. The absolute 4-year risks for the individual components were as follows: HF 7.0% (95% CI: 6.7%-7.3%), MI 4.4% (95% CI: 4.2%-4.6%), and stroke 7.6% (95% CI: 7.3%-7.9%) for patients with albuminuria vs HF 4.0% (95% CI: 3.8%-4.2%), MI 3.2% (95% CI: 3.1%-3.4%), and stroke 5.5% (95% CI: 5.3%-5.7%) for patients with normoalbuminuria. Patients with albuminuria displayed a higher risk of the primary outcome, and similar results were observed for each individual outcome. Renal outcomes

The absolute 4-year risk of the secondary outcome was 8.7% (8.4%-9.0%) for patients with albuminuria and 2.9% (2.8%-3.1%) for patients with normoalbuminuria (Figure 3A). The absolute 4-year risk of sustained decrease of ≥50% in the eGFR from baseline was 8.6% (8.3%-8.9%) and 2.9% (2.8%-3.1%) for albuminuria and normoalbuminuria, respectively (Figure 3B). For both groups, the 4-year absolute risk of ESRD was the lowest of all the assessed outcomes. Patients with albuminuria had a higher risk of 2.5% (2.3%-2.7%) compared to 0.4% (0.3%-0.4%) for normoalbuminuria (Figure 3C). Discussion

Main findings

This nationwide cohort study of patients with T2D yielded several major findings. First, of the 74,014 included patients, 40% had albuminuria and were potentially eligible for treatment with cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs including nsMRAs based on UACR and eGFR inclusion criteria from landmark trials; however, only 3.6% of the cohort received a steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone or eplerenone). Second, patients with albuminuria had the highest risk of experiencing cardiorenal events, but the association between UACR and renal outcomes was stronger than for cardiovascular events, and patients with normoalbuminuria still displayed a high risk of experiencing a composite cardiovascular outcome of HF, MI, stroke, or all-cause death. Furthermore, only 88,352 (49%) of 181,241 patients with T2D had UACR measured within a year.

Albuminuria and risk of cardiorenal disease

Awareness of albuminuria as a risk marker for cardiorenal damage has increased since approval of nsMRAs.23, 24, 25 Updated Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes-guidelines recommend nsMRAs and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists in addition to RASi and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors as a stepwise approach in managing patients with T2D and chronic kidney disease.26 These therapies are now available, and although both guideline-directed therapies and combination therapies have shown favorable cardiorenal outcomes, implementation gaps have previously been observed for cardiovascular and chronic kidney disease.7,27 Only a total of 3.6% patients used steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists at baseline in our study indicating that use of eplerenone and spironolactone as an antihypertensive is rarely occurring, which may be explained by the concern for risk of hyperkalemia and worsening of kidney function with steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists.25 Based on our findings 40% of patients with T2D, who follow the national recommendations, are eligible for treatment with cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs in Denmark. This could potentially serve as a benchmark for implementation success internationally. It is essential to consider that not all patients in the present cohort were treated optimally with RASi and that not all patients had UACR measured. Similarly, a subanalysis of the FIDELITY trial suggests that nearly three-quarters of the population in the United States is eligible for treatment with nsMRAs.4

Patients with albuminuria had a 4-year absolute risk of 28.6% for a composite cardiovascular outcome with a continuous increase in HR with increasing UACR. For normoalbuminuria the 4-year absolute risk for a composite cardiovascular outcome was 18.7%, whereas the 4-year absolute risk of a composite renal outcome was only 2.9%. These findings emphasize the clinical significance of albuminuria in progression and prevention of cardiac damage even within the normal range. The association between UACR and adverse cardiorenal outcomes has been reported previously in patients with T2D.28, 29, 30, 31 UACR above 30 mg/g is common in patients with HF and is associated with congestion and worse clinical outcomes.32,33 However, even UACR of 10 to 29 mg/g has been associated with HF.28,34,35 In the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities study, compromising 10,975 patients without prevalent HF, an increased HR of the development of HF was reported using a spline model of continuous UACR with knots at 10, 30, and 300 mg/g.34 Our study supports the conclusion that elevated UACR levels are a risk marker for HF in patients with T2D and demonstrates a continuous association between albuminuria and increased HR for the development of cardiorenal disease, even after adjusting for known cardiovascular risk factors including eGFR. Similarly, Fox et al. conducted a meta-analysis in 13 chronic kidney disease cohorts (N = 38,612) and reported an increased risk of ESRD with increasing UACR in patients with T2D above the same cutpoints as in the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities study,30 highlighting the role of albuminuria as a marker of kidney damage.3

Lack of assessment of albuminuria: Prevention of cardiorenal disease

In the 4 of 5 regions in Denmark, only 49% of the patients with T2D had UACR measured within a year, indicating lack of adherence to the national T2D guidelines.36 Insufficient implementation of guidelines in primary care in Denmark has previously been reported.37 In a cohort consisting of 2,057 patients with T2D, who were randomly selected from 64 primary care practices, only 57.2% had been screened for albuminuria with any method; of those, only 41.9% had UACR measured during the preceding 12 months.38 Similar patterns of underutilization of UACR testing in primary care practices have been reported in the United States, where only 47% of 9,339 patients with T2D had UACR measured during a 15-month period.39 In a worldwide study with >80 participating cohorts, adherence to guidelines for UACR measurement was as low as 35%.40 Causes for underutilization of UACR testing include the lack of awareness and understanding of testing guidelines among physicians, infrequent physician visits due to lack of patient compliance or avoidance of treatment, logistical issues regarding UACR screening,41,42 as well as methods for measuring albuminuria not being standardized, including the use of urine dipsticks.25 The low percentage of patients with UACR measurements could potentially be attributed to incomplete data collection in primary care practices. This missing data may result from inconsistent testing or reporting practices, thereby affecting the observed prevalence of albuminuria in the study cohort. Furthermore, the baseline characteristics of the patients, who did not have albuminuria assessed, suggest that healthier individuals tend to have a lower likelihood of having UACR measured. Our data support the unmet need for widespread albuminuria assessment in clinical practice to identify patients with early cardiorenal disease and prevent further irreversible cardiorenal damage.

Strengths and limitations

The main strengths of this nationwide study include the large sample size and comprehensive patient information in the registers including no lost to follow-up. Furthermore, all registers used in this study contained complete data on all patients ensuring full follow-up and they have previously been validated and proven to be reliable. The FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials demonstrate high internal validity, as study participants received all guideline-recommended therapy. In contrast, the current study emphasizes high external validity by using real-world data and including a broader population with T2D. Therefore, the findings of this study provide valuable insights into the potential applicability of new cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs in a real-world T2D population. Limitations of this study include a potential effect of unmeasured confounders in the restricted cubic spline analysis that cannot be completely excluded. Possible unmeasured confounders are lifestyle factors (smoking, body mass index, or frailty) which were not accessible for the present study. The classification of albuminuria in our study was based on a single UACR measurement, which may lead to misclassification due to biological variability. Clinical guidelines recommend confirming albuminuria with at least 2 of 3 elevated UACR measurements. However, repeat measurements were available in only a minority of patients. In a sensitivity analysis among the subgroup with a second UACR measurement within 1 year, 88.9% had a repeat value confirming the diagnosis of albuminuria, supporting the validity of our overall findings. In addition, when using UACR as a continuous variable, the lack of repeated measurements may limit the precision of risk modeling across the UACR spectrum.

Clinical perspectives

Albuminuria is strongly associated to clinical cardiorenal events, particular renal outcomes. Although less than half of patients with T2D have a recent measurement of albuminuria, 40% of patients with a measurement are potentially eligible to treatment with new cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs, including nsMRAs, based on UACR and eGFR criteria. Implementation of guideline-directed medical therapies and new scientific discoveries like nsMRAs in patients with albuminuria are therefore warranted. Patients with T2D and albuminuria within the normal range have a substantial risk of cardiovascular disease and these patients are not considered at risk in current guidelines with the risk of remaining untreated. Conclusions

Of the 74,014 patients with T2D, 29,581 (40%) had albuminuria and were eligible for treatment with new cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs, and 44,433 (60%) had normoalbuminuria. Patients with albuminuria have the highest risk of cardiorenal outcomes, but patients with normoalbuminuria still display a substantial risk of experiencing cardiovascular outcomes. Routine screening for albuminuria is underutilized in the care of patients with T2D. Whether optimized control with cardiovascular and renoprotective drugs, for example, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, and nsMRAs, in this population could translate into lowered risks remains to be studied.

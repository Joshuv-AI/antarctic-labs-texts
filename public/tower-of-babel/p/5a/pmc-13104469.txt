Research highlight: “It’s changing our lives, not for the better. It’s important that we have a say”. The role of young people in informing public health and policy decisions about gambling marketing
Published: 2026

The gambling industry uses positive commercial marketing messages to attract customers, and this has become a public health concern, especially for children and young people. With new technologies, gambling advertisements are everywhere, from TV to social media. This study looks at young Australians’ perceptions of current policy responses to gambling advertising, what they think should be done to protect young people from these advertisements, and the young people’s view about their involvement in discussions and decisions about gambling marketing regulations.

The authors conducted online discussions via Zoom with 64 young Australians aged 12–17 years old from Victoria and New South Wales, in groups of 2–4 people.

Participants discussed their exposure to gambling advertisements, especially on social media, and shared their thoughts on how these advertisements influence their views on gambling. They emphasized the need for more stringent regulations, and suggested embedding education about the risks of gambling in school curricula.

The study found that young people are very aware of gambling marketing and its potential harms. They believe current regulations are outdated and don’t do enough to protect them. Many suggested banning gambling advertisements altogether, or at least making them less appealing by showing the negative consequences of gambling. They also emphasized the importance of including young people in decision-making processes about gambling policies, as they are directly affected by these advertisements. They also proposed using social media platforms like TikTok to engage their peers with impactful content highlighting the risks associated with gambling.

The authors conclude that incorporating young people’s perspectives into policy decisions could lead to more effective strategies for minimizing the negative impact of gambling marketing on youth. This study underscores the importance of empowering young people, and integrating their insights into public health initiatives aimed at protecting them from harmful industry practices.

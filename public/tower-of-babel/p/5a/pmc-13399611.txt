The Biologically Enhanced Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction With Combined Injection of Harvested Platelet‐Rich Plasma, Thrombin, and Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate
Authors: Akram Aldawoudy, Micheal T Hirschmann, Ahmed Abdalla Ahmed Abdelgawad, Hesham Mohamed Gawish
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite continuous advances, anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction still struggles to restore full native knee function, as most techniques improve mechanical stability but overlook biological healing. The described superenhanced anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction technique aims to address this biological problem through a quadruple approach. The native anterior cruciate ligament stump is preserved, autologous bone “biosticks” are used to enhance tunnel osteointegration, and incorporates platelet‐rich plasma, and the bone marrow aspirate concentrate in a gel‐like structure to boost graft revascularization.

Despite decades of innovation and surgical refinement, anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction (ACLR) continues to fall short of fully restoring native knee function. Most techniques focus on improving the mechanical stability, and ignore the biology. This is reflected in suboptimal clinical outcomes as retears and residual laxity are persistent findings in the literature. Improving mechanics of the graft and fixation methods fails to improve the survival of the graft due to persistent biological insufficiency in graft ligamentization and tendon‐to‐bone healing. 1 , 2

Despite the increasing use of orthobiologics such as platelet‐rich plasma (PRP) and bone marrow aspirate concentrate (BMAC), the optimal biological additive to enhance the graft's integration within the knee's hostile environment is still unknown. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 This technical note describes a superenhanced ACLR technique which employs a quadruple biological synergistic approach. The procedure preserves the native ACL stump to maintain proprioceptive fibers and vascular supply. Bone plugs “biosticks” prepared from autologous cancellous bone are inserted into the femoral and tibial tunnels to enhance osteointegration. Additionally, PRP and BMAC are injected directly into the knee, creating a rich biological environment that supports early graft revascularization and reduces postoperative inflammation.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

In this technique, a biologically superenhanced ACLR is performed using a quadruple synergestic biologic approach combining ligament stump preservation, bone plug insertion, and PRP & BMAC gel injection (Video 1). Advantages and disadvantages of the technique are discussed in Table 1, and Pearls and pitfalls are discussed in Table 2.

In this technique a biologically superenhanced anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction is performed using a combined biologic approach of stump preservation, bone plug insertion, and platelet‐rich plasma & bone marrow aspirate concentrate gel injection. Hamstring tendon graft is then harvested and prepared on a side table. The graft is quadrupled and prepared using adjustable button fixation (ToggleLoc Device with ZipLoop Technology, Zimmer Biomet, Warsaw, IN) on the femoral side, and a metal loop graft holder on tibial side. Tunnels are then created in the most isometric points of the anatomic areas of the femoral and tibial anterior cruciate ligament attachment sites using core reamers to harvest bone plugs. The tibial C‐guide is positioned at the center of the native anterior cruciate ligament stump to ensure that once the graft is delivered, it will be completely enveloped by the remaining stump tissue. After graft passage and fixation, the bone plugs extracted from both tunnels are reinserted back by a special applicator, compressing the graft against the wall of the tunnels. About 30 mL peripheral blood is collected and centrifuged to get 6 mL platelet‐rich plasma and 4 mL thrombin. About 60 mL of the bone marrow is obtained to get a final volume of 6 to 8 mL of concentrated bone marrow after centrifugation. They are all then mixed and waited till they coagulate into a gel‐like structure to be injected inside the knee with through a trochar after wound closure to prevent gel escape from the knee joint. Graft Harvest

ACLR is performed under general anesthesia using a high thigh tourniquet. Initial arthroscopic knee evaluation is performed first to assess and measure the residual stump of native ACL along with management of associated meniscal and cartilage injuries. Hamstring tendon graft is then harvested and prepared on a side table. The average graft harvested for this technique should be 10 mm in diameter.

Graft Preparation

The graft is quadrupled and prepared using adjustable button fixation (ToggleLoc Device with ZipLoop Technology, Zimmer Biomet, Warsaw, IN) on the femoral side, and a metal loop graft holder on tibial side. Planning graft length is critical and should be carefully adjusted. The whole length of the graft, including the length of the metal loop graft holder device, is adjusted to be 80 to 85 mm. Approximately 40 mm of this length is to be inserted in the tibial tunnel, around 20 to 25 mm intra‐articularly, and around 20 to 25 mm in the femoral tunnel. After preparation, the graft is left in a saline solution containing vancomycin as a prophylactic measure against infection.

Stump Preservation ACLR

Tunnels are then created in the most isometric points of the anatomic areas of the femoral and tibial ACL attachment sites, starting with the femoral side and preserving as much of the native ACL remnants as possible (Figure 1).

A femoral tunnel is created while the knee is flexed to 120° using a core reamer (Dannoritzer Medizintechnik GmbH & Co. Germany) over a guide to create the socket part of the tunnel nearer to the joint cavity which correlates with the size of the quadrupled graft 7 (Figures 2 and 3). The femoral tunnel is created through anteromedial portal.

The femoral tunnel preparation and plug extraction set. (Dannoritzer Medizintechnik GmbH & Co. Germany).

An extractor (Dannoritzer Medizintechnik GmbH & Co. Germany) is used to extract the bone plug created by the core reamer. This part of the tunnel should extend to 25 to 30 mm in length (a little longer than the planned length of the femoral part of the graft) (Figure 4).

Plug applicator and impactor. (Dannoritzer Medizintechnik GmbH & Co. Germany).

The far part of the tunnel is created with a 4.5 drill pit over a passing pin. A suture shuttle is used for passing the sutures of the adjustable button (ToggleLoc Device with ZipLoop Technology, Zimmer Biomet, Warsaw, IN) and left clamped until the tibial tunnel is created.

The tibial tunnel is then created as one part by a cannulated ACL tibial guide (Dannoritzer Medizintechnik GmbH & Co. Germany), correlating in width with the diameter of the quadrupled graft. The tibial C‐guide is positioned at the center of the native ACL stump to ensure that, once the graft is delivered, it will be completely enveloped by the remaining stump tissue. A bone plug with the same dimensions is extracted with the same technique (Figures 5, 6, 7, 8, 9).

Graft Passage

The ACL graft is then passed into the tunnels until the adjustable button is flipped on the outer femoral cortex. Then, toggling starts to drag the graft proximally until the planned lengths of each part of the graft settle in their respective locations, and the metal loop graft holder abuts firmly against the tibial cortex. Final tensioning by toggling is performed in full extension of the knee (Figure 10).

Arthroscopic view of the left knee showing the ACL graft erupted through and completely covered with the original ACL tibial stump after its passage from tibia to the femur. (ACL, anterior cruciate ligament; LFC, lateral femoral condyle; MFC, medial femoral condyle.)

Bone Plugs “Biosticks”

The bone plugs extracted from both tunnels are then measured and prepared (Figures 11 and 12). The knee is then flexed to 120° flexion, and the bone plug obtained from the femur is reinserted back anterior to the graft by a special applicator, compressing the graft against the wall of the femoral tunnel (Figure 13).

Bone plug inside the graft extractor.

Both plugs are prepared and measured to be inserted inside the tunnels.

Arthroscopic view of the left knee through anteromedial portal showing the femoral bone plug inserted anterior to the graft. (ACL, anterior cruciate ligament; LFC, lateral femoral condyle; MFC, medial femoral condyle.)

Finally, the bone plug obtained from the tibia is reinserted in the tibial tunnel, either as 1 piece or as multiple fragmented pieces around the metal loop graft holder. In this way, the tunnel is sealed to avoid the escape of biologics from the knee joint after injection (Figure 14).

Intraoperative Mini Biolab

The operating room should be equipped by a mini biolab for sterile PRP and BMAC preparation and to decrease handling and risk of infection. The mini lab should be equipped by a centrifugator (swing out centrifuge, Lab Line, India), water bath (NS Biotec Egypt), Laminar flow hood (NS Biotec, Egypt), Vortex mixer (Lab Line, India), and sterile tubes (Figure 15).

Operating room mini biolab for biologics preparation.

Autologous Platelet‐Rich Plasma (PRP)

Approximately 30 mL of peripheral blood is collected in vacutainer tubes containing acid citrate dextrose in a ratio of 9:1, then the blood is centrifuged at 200 g for 10 minutes in a swing‐out centrifuge (Lab Line, India) (Figure 16).

Peripheral blood is collected in vacutainer tubes under complete aseptic technique.

The supernatant plasma is harvested under the laminar flow hood (NS Biotec, Egypt) using sterile syringes and is injected into another sterile plain vacutainer tubes and is centrifuged again at 300 g for 20 minutes. The supernatant excess platelet poor plasma is discarded and the pellet of platelets is resuspended in 4 to 6 mL of the remaining plasma to obtain platelet‐rich plasma 8 (Figure 17).

Illustration showing the steps of preparation of the PRP. (PRP, platelet‐rich plasma.)

Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate (BMAC)

Approxiamately 60 mL of bone marrow is obtained under aseptic conditions from the iliac crest using a bone marrow harvest needle (T‐loc Argon medical devices, Athens, TX) using acid citrate dextrose as an anticoagulant in a ratio of 9:1 (Figures 18 and 19). The bone marrow is centrifuged in a swing‐out centrifuge (Lab Line, IIT Bombay, India) at 200 g for 10 minutes, and then the buffy coat is harvested under a laminar flow hood (N.S Biotec, Alexandria‐Egypt) using sterile syringes. A final volume of 6 to 8 mL of concentrated bone marrow is obtained for injection 9 (Figure 20).

The bone marrow harvest needle. (Argon medical devices‐USA).

Intraoperative photo showing bone marrow aspiration from the left iliac bone.

Illustration showing the steps for the preparation of BMAC. (BMAC, bone marrow aspirate concentrate.)

Thrombin Generation

Acid citrate dextrose anticoagulated blood (20 mL) is centrifuged at 200 g for 10 minutes and then the supernatant plasma is collected and transferred to glass vacationer tubes. To each 1 mL plasma, 100 μL calcium gluconate is added. Then the tubes are left to clot in a water bath at 37°C for 15 to 20 minutes, after which the tubes are centrifuged at 400 g for 5 minutes and the supernatant that is rich in thrombin is collected and used as activator 10 (Figure 21).

Illustration showing the steps for the preparation of activated thrombin. (PRP, platelet‐rich plasma.)

Injecting the Magic Gel

The platelet‐rich plasma (6 mL), bone marrow concentrate (8 mL), and the generated thrombin (4 mL) are then mixed and left until they coagulate into a gel‐like structure to be injected intra‐articularly through a trochar after wound closure to prevent gel escape from the knee joint. Injecting this cocktail in a gel‐like form is very important in this step as it acts as a protective shield that shelters the graft from the surrounding hostile millieu (Figures 22, 23, 24, 25).

The triple biofuel cocktail (PRP + BMAC + Thrombin). (BMAC, bone marrow aspirate concentrate; PRP, platelet‐rich plasma.)

The 3 biofuels are collected inside one syringe under complete aseptic technique.

Postoperative Protocol

The rehabilitation program starts from week 1 focusing on restoring full knee extension and range of motion then advances through strengthening and neuromuscular training, and culminates in sport‐specific conditioning and return‐to‐play preparation by 6 to 9 months.

DISCUSSION

There is growing evidence supporting the use of biological additives with ACLR. Some rotator cuff tears have shown that PRP can accelerate graft maturation. 5 , 11 Park et al. 12 found that BMAC augmented ACLR had significantly higher International Knee Documentation Committee scores in 24 months. Forsythe et al. 13 found that patients in the BMAC group had greater patient‐reported outcomes (International Knee Documentation Committee) at 9 months postoperatively when compared with those who underwent a standard surgical procedure. Lin et al. 14 examined the effect of mixing PRP and BMAC together. They found that BMAC + PRP group had the lowest laxity after 24 weeks of follow‐up. Moreover, the BMAC + PRP group showed significantly wider tendon‐bone interface in the femoral bone tunnel at 24 weeks compared with the other groups.

In this technique, the preservation of the original tibial stump along with the biocharge of ACL graft with PRP‐BMAC gel intra‐articularly and bracing the tunnels with bone plugs were performed together as a biological booster that may ignite the ACL graft maturation and enhance its integration within the tunnels. ACL grafts are subjected to a very hostile environment once seeded inside the knee joint.

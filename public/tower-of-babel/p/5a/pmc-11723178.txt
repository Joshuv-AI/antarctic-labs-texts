Streptococcus pneumoniae endophthalmitis after exposed glaucoma drainage implant
Authors: Dylann Fujimoto, Philina Yee, Stephanie Noh, Kapil Mishra, Sameh Mosaed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A patient with juvenile glaucoma status post 2 glaucoma drainage implants in each eye presents with painful vision loss in the left eye and is found to have streptococcus pneumoniae endophthalmitis from an exposed inferior glaucoma drainage implant. The infection is managed medically with intravitreal antibiotics and surgically with a glaucoma drainage device revision, anterior chamber washout and pars plana vitrectomy.

Hello, my name is Dylann Fujimoto I am a PGY2 ophthalmology resident at UC Irvine. I will be presenting this case of streptococcus pneumoniae endophthalmitis after an exposed glaucoma drainage implant with a video of the operating surgeons Philina Yee, Kapil Mishra and Sameh Mosaed.

This is a 63-year-old male with a history of severe stage juvenile glaucoma, presenting for sudden vision loss and pain in the left eye. He is being managed medically, and has had surgical intervention with 2 anterior chamber tubes in each eye. The superotemporal tubes were placed with outside providers. The inferonasal tubes are both Baerdvelt glaucoma implants with scleral patch grafts. The baseline vision is 20/25; 20/30. He has no history of intravitreal injections, chronic ocular inflammation, nor usage of steroids or non steroidal antiinflammatory eye drops.

Examination of the right eye is unremarkable. Examination of the left eye has a visual acuity of hand motion and intraocular pressure (IOP) of 27 mmHg. Crusted mucopurulent discharge on lashes. 3+ diffuse conjunctival injection. The superotemporal tube is difficult to visualize given severe pain. The inferonasal tube has a 0.5cm overlying conjunctival erosion. There's 3+ corneal edema, 3+ Descemet membrane folds. A crescent shaped hypopyon ∼1.5mm in height in 6 oc position, extending from 2 oc to 10 oc. The view of the anterior chamber cell is limited by corneal edema. There is a posterior chamber intraocular lens. We are unable to directly visualize the fundus. There is a B-scan performed which demonstrates vitreous debris.

The diagnosis of bacterial endophthalmitis was made. The immediate tretment of intravitreal tap and intravitreal injection of ceftazadime and vancomycin was performed. Patient was consented for surgical intervention. Two days later, glaucoma drainage implant revision with scleral patch graft, anterior chamber wash out, pars plana vitrectomy and intravitreal injection of ceftazadime and vancomycin was performed. Four days later intravitreal injection on vancomycin was performed based on culture data.

The inferonasal tube is first further exposed, removed from the anterior chamber. The superotemporal tube was noted to be well covered however not visible in the anterior chamber due to hypopyon and corneal edema. The previous tunnel is sutured with 8-0 vicryl on a spatulated needle. The tube and drainage device is flushed with povidone-iodine. The tube is amputated from the drainage plate. The drainage plate could be removed however it is left in place for the purpose of re-inserting a tube in the future if needed. After, a scleral patch graft, per surgeon preference, is utilized for closure of the prior tube track given its friability during surgery. The anterior chamber wash out is then performed. Two trochars with an infusion line and microfoceps are inserted into the anterior chamber. Trocars are used to limit the corneal edema around the wounds. The microforcepts dissect the ring of fibrinous infectious material from the iris. After which, the vitrector from Alcon Constellation, is inserted and aspirates the dissected fibrinous materal. The pars plana vitrectomy follows. During the vitrectomy, extensive infectious debris was visualized in the vitreous as well as adhered to the retina. It was carefully dissected and aspirated. Intraretinal hemorrhages were noted intraoperatively and likely represented retinitis.

The vitreous culture resulted as streptococcus pneumoniae, susceptible to ceftriaxone, levofloxacin, vancomycin. At post operative month 1, the visual acuity was light perception only, the intraocular pressure was 9 mmHg. There is no residual infectious debris visualized in the anterior chamber. No residual infectious debris visualized on the B-scan. At this time the active infection was resolved, however there was a guarded visual prognosis. Vision limited by retinal damage and nerve damage in the setting of end stage glaucoma.

This case demonstrates the prompt and aggressive treatment must be initiated for endophthalmitis from glaucoma drainage devices. Glaucoma drainage device associated endophthalmitis is more often caused by streptococcus infections which are more virulent compared to coagulase negative staphylococcus. Compared to cataract associated endophthalmitis, earlier surgical intervention may be considered given the differences in microorganisms causing the infection. Endophthalmitis vitrectomy study recommends pars plana vitrectomy for cataract associated endophthalmitis with light perception. However in that study majority of patients had coagulase negative staphylococcus infections.

Based on our video case we offer the following suggestions for glaucoma drainage implant associated endophthalmitis. Removal of the tube should be considered given the possibility of residual mircoorganisms remaining on the hardware. In our case, we removed the inferonasal tube that had eroded through the conjunctiva and was visualized to have purulent material within. However, we kept the superotemporal tube as it was well covered, and the risk of residual microorganisms were outweighed by the benefits of a remaining drainage device for the patient with end-stage glaucoma. Removal of the plate can be up to the discretion of the treating physician based on the patient's case. If the plate is left in place, it can be used in the future for IOP control if needed. In our case, we kept the plate in place given the limitations of future intraocular pressure lowering procedures.

Another glaucoma surgery, trabeculectomy, has shown to have a higher rate of endophthalmitis when performed inferiorly.

It is possible that inferior glaucoma surgeries may have higher rates of endophthalmitis based on their location in the inferior conjunctiva. Inferiorly located trabeculectomies are hypothesized to be higher risk for infection given submersion in the tear film which may contain bacteria, less coverage from the eyelid, and higher risk of mechanical trauma from the eyelid.

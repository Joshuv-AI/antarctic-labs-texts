Early Safety and Efficacy Outcomes of a Novel 360° Canaloplasty Device in Open-Angle Glaucoma: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Kelvin S Y Shi, Emily Aashna Dorairaj, Melanie Wang, Ryan Y Thé, Pranav Vasu, Yazan Abubaker, Bryan Chin Hou Ang, Syril K Dorairaj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the safety profile and clinical efficacy of the VIA360™ Surgical System (VIA360) for ab-interno canaloplasty in the management of glaucoma.MethodsThis retrospective, noncomparative chart review included 64 eyes undergoing standalone VIA360 ABiC (VIA360-Alone) or VIA360 combined with cataract surgery (VIA360–Cataract) between March 13, 2025, and January 7, 2026. Surgical success was defined as ≥20% intraocular pressure (IOP) reduction at the last follow-up without surgical reintervention. Changes in IOP, glaucoma medications, and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) were evaluated through 6 months.ResultsAt 6 months, mean IOP decreased from 18.46 to 14.07 mmHg (−4.39 mmHg; −23.78%; P < 0.0001). In subgroup analysis, VIA360–Cataract eyes showed a reduction from 17.61 to 13.48 mmHg (−4.13 mmHg; −23.45%; P = 0.0011), while VIA360-Alone eyes decreased from 20.61 to 16.15 mmHg (−4.46 mmHg; −21.69%; P = 0.0549). Glaucoma medication use also decreased from 1.46 to 1.13 medications (−22.6%; P = 0.1331) overall at 6 months, with greater reductions observed in the combined group compared with standalone cases. Surgical success was achieved in 15 of 29 eyes (51.72%) with available follow-up. No intraoperative complications or vision-threatening adverse events were observed.ConclusionThese findings suggest that VIA360 provides clinically meaningful IOP reduction with an excellent safety profile in early postoperative outcomes, demonstrating greater consistency when performed in conjunction with cataract surgery. Larger prospective studies are warranted to further define long-term durability.Keywords: glaucoma, canaloplasty, ab-interno canaloplasty, MIGS

Introduction

For many years, trabeculectomy has been considered the gold standard surgical treatment for glaucoma.1 While this procedure is highly effective in reducing intraocular pressure (IOP), it is also associated with notable risks and potential postoperative complications, including bleb leaks and hypotony.2,3 Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) has emerged as an important evolution in the surgical management of glaucoma. Characterized by smaller incisions, reduced tissue disruption, and faster recovery times, MIGS has gained increasing adoption by serving as a safer surgical option that bridges medical therapy and more invasive procedures for many patients.2,4

Among the various MIGS strategies, one of the most common methods described in this group of procedures is canaloplasty.5 The concept of canaloplasty emerged from the use of a flexible microcannula to achieve circumferential dilation of Schlemm’s canal during viscocanalostomy, a technique pioneered by Dr. Robert Stegmann and further refined by Dr. John Kearney.6–8 Following its introduction into clinical practice, canaloplasty enabled surgeons to achieve postoperative IOP comparable to those obtained with traditional incisional surgeries while offering a superior safety profile.9

In February 2025, VIA360TM Surgical System (VIA360; New World Medical Inc., Rancho Cucamonga, CA, USA), a novel canaloplasty device received clearance from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) (Figure 1). The device’s microcatheter is designed to advance up to 360 degrees with micro-channels configured to deliver viscoelastic both forward and tangentially ensuring the delivery of ophthalmic viscoelastic fluid to targeted areas. The technology’s rotatable cannula provides enhanced positioning and flexibility for a single entry without requiring full device rotation. The device’s proprietary ActiveInject™ Technology enables on-demand viscoelastic delivery independent of catheter movement, and its Smart Prime System optimizes viscoelastic volume ensuring efficiency throughout the procedure. VIA360 can be operated in combination with cataract surgery or as standalone procedure.10

VIA360™ Surgical System (VIA360) Device. The VIA360 device is shown in detail, demonstrating the rotatable cannula designed to enhance intraoperative positioning and maneuverability, as well as the handheld delivery mechanism that permits controlled, surgeon-directed viscoelastic administration. The device configuration supports single-incision access and circumferential viscodilation of Schlemm’s canal to augment the conventional aqueous outflow pathway.

Currently, given the recent introduction of the VIA360, there remains a lack of early real world clinical data evaluating its safety and efficacy in the management of glaucoma, whether as a standalone procedure or in combination with cataract surgery. To address this gap, the present study aims to evaluate the early safety and efficacy of VIA360 in a real-world clinical cohort of patients with glaucoma. To our knowledge, this is the first report assessing early clinical outcomes associated with the use of VIA360 in open-angle glaucoma.

Methods

A retrospective, single surgeon, noncomparative study was conducted at the Mayo Clinic. The study protocol was reviewed and waived by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board (IRB) on February 28, 2018 (IRB Number: 18–000006). This study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. All patient data were deidentified, and informed consent was waived by the Mayo Clinic IRB. Patients aged ≥ 18 years diagnosed with glaucoma that received canaloplasty with the VIA360 between March 13th, 2025 and January 7th, 2026 were included within this study. All open-angle glaucoma subtypes were eligible for inclusion.

Surgical Technique

All canaloplasty procedures using VIA360 were performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s guidelines. The patient’s head was rotated 35 degrees away from the surgeon, and the surgical microscope was rotated 35 degrees toward the surgeon, resulting in a net viewing angle of 70 degrees. A gonioprism was placed on the eye to visualize the trabecular meshwork. The VIA360 microcatheter was then carefully inserted into Schlemm’s canal and advanced counterclockwise to the furthest extent possible, up to 360 degrees. The catheter was subsequently withdrawn in a controlled manner while viscoelastic was injected to achieve circumferential viscodilation of Schlemm’s canal, expansion of the collector channels, and focal disruption of the trabecular meshwork to enhance aqueous outflow. Approximately 9 to 10 clicks of viscoelastic were delivered during the procedure, with each click delivering 3 µL of viscoelastic. Upon completion of viscodilation, the catheter was removed from the eye. No trabeculotomy was performed in conjunction with our canaloplasty procedures.

For cases performed in combination with cataract surgery, standard phacoemulsification was completed prior to the canaloplasty procedure. A temporal paracentesis was first created, followed by intracameral injection of non-preserved lidocaine. Air and trypan blue were then used to stain the anterior capsule, and excess dye was removed with balanced salt solution. Viscoelastic was used to maintain the anterior chamber, and a 2.75 mm clear corneal incision was created. A continuous curvilinear capsulorrhexis was performed, followed by hydrodissection. The nucleus was removed using a divide-and-conquer technique, and residual cortical material was aspirated. A posterior chamber intraocular lens was then implanted and positioned appropriately within the capsular bag. Following cataract extraction and intraocular lens implantation, the above described canaloplasty procedure was then performed through the existing corneal incisions.

Data Collection

Demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, race, lens status, glaucoma type and severity, and history of prior glaucoma surgery, were obtained from the electronic medical record. Patients were evaluated preoperatively, intraoperatively, and postoperatively at day 1, week 1, and months 1, 3, and 6. Preoperative and postoperative assessments included IOP, number of glaucoma medications, and BCVA. Glaucoma medications included alpha agonists, beta blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, prostaglandin analogs, pilocarpine, and combination formulations. IOP was measured using Goldmann applanation tonometry. Baseline measurements were obtained one day prior to surgery. If available, BCVA, slit lamp biomicroscopy, and dilated fundus examination were collected at preoperative and postoperative time points. Glaucoma severity was classified according to the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision.11

Statistical Methods

All data were de-identified and independently analyzed by two investigators using GraphPad Prism Version 10.2.2 (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA, USA) and RStudio (Posit PBC, Boston, MA, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics and demographics, with continuous variables presented as means with standard deviations and categorical variables reported as frequencies and percentages. Outcomes assessed included changes from baseline in IOP, number of glaucoma medications, and BCVA at each postoperative visit, as well as intraoperative and postoperative complications and the proportion of eyes achieving ≥20% reduction in IOP from baseline at each follow up time point. Normality of continuous data was first assessed. For comparisons involving paired observations with normally distributed data, two tailed paired t tests were used, while Wilcoxon signed rank tests were applied for paired data that were not normally distributed. For comparisons involving unpaired observations with unequal sample sizes and non-normal distributions, unpaired t tests were used. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Complications

The procedure was completed without intraoperative complications in all eyes. Mild hyphema, defined as circulating red blood cells without a layered blood level or a blood layer measuring less than 1 mm in the anterior chamber, was not classified as a major complication given its self-limiting nature. Six cases of hyphema were noted but they were all self-limiting and resolved by the subsequent follow-up visit.

No vision-threatening complications were observed throughout the follow-up period, and there were no glaucoma surgical reinterventions required.

Surgical Success

At 6 months, 15 of the 29 remaining eyes (51.72%) met surgical success, as defined by the success criteria of > 20% IOP reduction from baseline with no surgical reintervention. When considering the entire baseline cohort, 33 of 64 eyes (51.56%) achieved surgical success at their last available follow up. Discussion

Canaloplasty was originally developed as an ab-externo procedure, involving circumferential catheterization of Schlemm’s canal with viscoelastic injection and placement of a tensioning suture to distend the trabecular meshwork and enhance aqueous outflow.5,12 However, in recent years, ab-interno canaloplasty (ABiC) has become increasingly favored over ab-externo canaloplasty.5 While ab-externo canaloplasty has demonstrated slightly greater efficacy in IOP, it is also associated with inherent disadvantages, including longer operative time, conjunctival suturing, tensioning suture placement, intrascleral lake formation, and a higher complication rate.5,13,14 In contrast, ABiC provides a favorable safety profile through a minimally invasive, conjunctiva-sparing technique while still achieving meaningful IOP reduction, making it one of the preferred options in canaloplasty treatment.15,16

In this retrospective study, we report early clinical efficacy and safety outcomes from a cohort of patients who underwent VIA360 ABiC surgery, performed either as a standalone procedure or in combination with cataract surgery. All procedures were performed at Mayo Clinic Jacksonville campus by a single surgeon, ensuring consistency and validity of the surgical technique and results. To the best of our knowledge, this analysis represents the first published data regarding VIA360 outcomes to date.

Safety Profile

The most prominent finding in this study was the favorable safety profile of the VIA360. No intraoperative complications were observed. Postoperative complications were all mild and self-limiting, consistent with those reported in prior canaloplasty literature. In previous studies, hyphema has been identified as the most common postoperative complication following canaloplasty, with reported incidences ranging from 6.1% to 70% of eyes.9 In the present cohort, six cases of immediate hyphema (9.4%) were observed; two were classified as severe, and all were resolved by postoperative week 1. Additionally, previous literature reports that Descemet’s membrane detachment (DMD) occurs in 1.6% to 9.1% of eyes undergoing canaloplasty.17,18 In our cohort, no cases of DMD were reported. Moreover, no postoperative IOP elevations or serious vision threatening complications, including choroidal detachment, suprachoroidal hemorrhage, or endophthalmitis, were observed in the VIA360 cohort. While these findings suggest a favorable safety profile, they should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size and duration of follow-up.

In contrast to VIA360, the iTrack Advance (Nova Eye Medical, Fremont, USA), another FDA-approved-360-degree canaloplasty device, has been reported to have cases of immediate postoperative transient IOP spikes.9 In fact, postoperative IOP spikes are among the most frequently reported complications associated with the iTrack devices.19 Prior authors have suggested that retained viscoelastic within the anterior chamber may contribute to these postoperative IOP spikes.9 Anatomically, the Schlemm’s canal measures approximately 36 to 40 mm in length, with a cross-sectional area ranging from 4064 to 7164 μm2, corresponding to a total luminal volume of approximately 0.146 to 0.287 μL.20 While the iTrack Advance delivers approximately 100 µL of viscoelastic, VIA360 allows surgeon controlled delivery of typically 24 to 30 µL (8 to 10 clicks), less than half the volume administered with iTrack Advance. This reduced and controlled delivery may minimize viscoelastic retention within the canal and anterior chamber, thereby potentially lowering the risk of immediate postoperative IOP spikes. However, this proposed mechanism remains speculative and requires further investigation in larger cohorts with longer follow up to establish a definitive causal relationship.

IOP-Lowering Efficacy

Compared with the existing literature on 360 degrees canaloplasty device outcomes, similar IOP reductions were observed in the present VIA360 cohort. Across all eyes, regardless of whether the procedure was performed as a standalone intervention or combined with cataract surgery, mean (SD) IOP decreased from 18.45 (5.53) mmHg at baseline to 14.07 (2.69) mmHg at 6 months, representing a 23.73% reduction. Similarly, in Ondrejka and Koerber’s study evaluating the iTrack Advance, mean (SD) IOP decreased from 18.3 (4.3) mmHg at baseline to 14.2 (3.1) mmHg at 12 months, corresponding to an approximately 22% reduction.21 Although the follow-up intervals differ, the overall magnitude of IOP reduction observed in our early postoperative cohort appears consistent with previously reported outcomes following 360-degree ABiC.

Consistent outcomes have also been reported with study evaluating the OMNI Viscosurgical System (OMNI; Sight Sciences Inc., Menlo Park, CA, USA) performed without trabeculotomy. The mean (SD) IOP decreased from 22.5 (5.3) mmHg at baseline to 15.3 (4.6) mmHg at 6 months (28.4% reduction).13 While the OMNI system typically performs 180-degrees of canal viscodilation per pass, circumferential treatment can be achieved through sequential passes by the surgeon, whereas VIA360 enables a single-pass 360-degree viscodilation. Despite these procedural differences, comparisons remain informative because both approaches share the same underlying mechanism of enhancing aqueous outflow through viscodilation of Schlemm’s canal. Taken together, these findings suggest that the degree of IOP lowering achieved with VIA360 falls within the range reported for current available ABiC devices.

In subgroup analysis, the combined VIA360–cataract cohort achieved a 23.48% reduction in IOP at 6 months, whereas the standalone VIA360 group demonstrated a 21.69% reduction. This difference is not unexpected. Although the precise physiological mechanism underlying IOP reduction after phacoemulsification remains incompletely understood, cataract surgery alone has been consistently associated with modest but clinically meaningful IOP lowering.22 This effect is similarly reflected in the OMNI without trabeculotomy study, in which the combined cataract cohort demonstrated a 30.3% IOP reduction compared with a slightly lower 27% reduction in the standalone group.13 Therefore, the additional reduction observed in the combined cohort likely reflects an additive effect of lens extraction.

Importantly, both subgroups demonstrated sustained pressure reduction at 6 months, supporting the independent IOP-lowering contribution of the VIA360 procedure. When contextualized within the existing literature on ABiC devices, the magnitude of IOP reduction observed in our cohort appears comparable to currently utilized platforms. Variability in percentage reduction across studies is likely influenced by differences in baseline IOP. Additionally, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size at the 6-month time point and the degree of attrition within our VIA360 cohort.

Glaucoma Medication Burden Reduction

With respect to glaucoma medication burden reduction, mean (SD) medication use across all eyes decreased from 1.48 (1.08) at baseline to 1.13 (1.16) at month 6, representing a 23.64% reduction. Meanwhile, the iTrack Advance cohort reported by Ondrejka and Koerber demonstrated a larger decrease in medication burden, with mean (SD) medication use declining from 1.87 (0.83) at baseline to 0.08 (0.47) at 12 months (95.7% reduction).21 The greater magnitude of reduction observed in that study may in part reflect the longer follow-up duration compared with the present cohort. Similarly, in the OMNI study without trabeculotomy, mean (SD) medication use decreased from 3.2 (1.0) at baseline to 1.7 (1.4) at 6 months (43.3% reduction).13 Although the magnitude of reduction with VIA360 is bit lower, the overall trend toward decreased medication reliance is consistent with that observed in other 360 degree ABiC platforms, supporting a comparable pattern of clinical effectiveness in reducing postoperative medication dependence.

Additional subgroup analysis revealed a more pronounced reduction in the combined VIA360–cataract cohort, with mean (SD) medication use decreasing from 1.37 (1.12) to 1.05 (1.16) at month 6 (−32.00%). In contrast, the standalone VIA360 group demonstrated a 19.66% reduction, from 1.78 (0.94) to 1.42 (1.13). A similar pattern was observed in the OMNI study when stratified by procedure type, in which canaloplasty alone demonstrated a 43.3% reduction in medication use, while OMNI combined with cataract surgery achieved a greater 50% reduction at 6 months.13 The greater medication reduction observed in the combined cohort may again reflect the additive IOP-lowering effect of cataract extraction, potentially allowing for more aggressive medication tapering in the early postoperative period.

It is important to acknowledge that some patients missed their 3-month follow-up visits, which may have introduced variability in medication adjustment and adherence patterns. Because medication tapering decisions are often individualized and influenced by interim IOP measurements, incomplete follow-up could have affected the statistical power to detect sustained differences at later time points, rather than reflecting diminished device efficacy. Nonetheless, the overall trend toward reduced medication burden supports the role of VIA360 as a meaningful adjunct in decreasing treatment dependence, particularly when performed in conjunction with cataract surgery.

Health Related Quality of Life and Patient Outcomes

The daily use of hypotensive medications is associated with ocular surface disease (OSD), discomfort, and poor compliance with many patients failing to adhere to their prescribed regimen.23 Our reduction in medication burden indicates a positive step forward in improving patient quality of life and downstream health outcomes. By incorporating a semi-permanent amelioration of long-term pressure readings via a surgical approach, surgeons are able to reduce complications and long-term detrimental damage to the optic nerve with reduced need for direct patient action. In the long term, the literature has shown that a reduction in medication burden can increase compliance rates by nearly 20% over just 3 months.24,25

Ultimately, the ability of the VIA360 to provide sustained pressure control while decreasing reliance on topical polypharmacy suggests it may not only preserve visual function but also significantly enhance the daily quality of life for patients who would otherwise struggle with the physical and psychological toll of chronic medical regimens.

Limitations

The main limitation of this single center retrospective study is that it was not randomized and is therefore subject to potential selection bias. In addition, many eyes underwent combined cataract and canaloplasty surgery (71.88%), highlighting the need for greater focus on standalone VIA360 cases to better evaluate its efficacy as an independent procedure. Additional emphasis on the standalone cohort will be important to further clarify long term durability and to allow more direct comparison with other minimally invasive glaucoma surgery platforms.

Another major limitation of this study is its attrition. Of the 64 eyes initially enrolled, only 29 had reached the 6 month follow up at the time of analysis. This apparent attrition is not due to loss to follow up, but rather reflects the ongoing nature of the study, with many eyes still awaiting their scheduled follow-up visits. This limited number at later time points may reduce statistical power and likely explains why the reduction in medication use at 6 months did not reach statistical significance, despite representing a clinically meaningful change. The degree of attrition is therefore an important factor to consider when interpreting these results.

Given that this is a newly introduced device, we believe it is important to report these early findings, as results from 29 eyes still provide meaningful clinical insight. Furthermore, this is an ongoing study, with additional eyes continuing to be enrolled and follow-up data being collected. As a result, we anticipate that statistical power and robustness of conclusions will improve in future analyses with more complete follow up and potentially standardized medication titration protocols.

Conclusion

VIA360 has demonstrated favorable safety profile, whether in combination with cataract or as a standalone procedure. All postoperative events that occurred were consistent with the normal postoperative course, mild in severity, and transient in nature. Significant and sustained IOP lowering was observed in the overall cohort and was more consistent in eyes undergoing combined surgery, while standalone cases demonstrated meaningful but less statistically robust reductions, likely due to the limited number of eyes undergoing the VIA360 standalone procedure. Although early postoperative reductions in medication burden were observed, longer-term medication use remained variable.

Overall, these findings suggest that VIA360 may represent a safe and effective minimally invasive glaucoma surgery option, particularly when combined with cataract surgery. However, these results should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size, relatively short follow up duration, and degree of attrition within the cohort. Additional studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up will be important to further evaluate long term durability and comparative performance, particularly in standalone canaloplasty cases. When considered in the context of previously reported outcomes for other canaloplasty devices, VIA360 appears to demonstrate similar early results; however, this observation is based on indirect comparison across studies rather than a direct head-to-head evaluation and should be interpreted accordingly. Additionally, despite the encouraging early data presented here, further investigation is warranted to better characterize extended outcomes and potential late complications.

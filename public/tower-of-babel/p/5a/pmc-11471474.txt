Studies on language teachers' beliefs and emotions: Current status and future directions
Authors: Yang Gao, Yanchen Liu, Yuqian Zeng, Xiaochen Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper provides a historical overview of research on language teachers' beliefs and emotions, examining how studies on teacher emotions have influenced research on beliefs and classroom practices. Using bibliometric analysis, we mapped trends and paradigms in the literature, focusing on significant developments in this field. Our findings reveal a notable increase in publications on language teacher beliefs, particularly since 2018, indicating growing academic interest. Highly cited studies predominantly emphasize the emotional aspects of teaching, signaling a broader shift in research focus from cognitive to emotional dimensions, commonly referred to as the “emotional turn.” The analysis also identifies influential authors and institutions, with the USA, China, and the UK leading in contributions. Research themes have evolved over time, moving from foundational beliefs and teaching practices to more complex areas like teacher psychology, with particular attention to emotional regulation, identity, and agency in language teaching. Finally, we offer insights for future research, aiming to further explore the intricate relationship between teachers' beliefs and emotions.

Introduction

Background and significance

In recent years, there has been a notable surge in research on language teachers' beliefs ([1]; Latif, 2021; [[2], [3], [4]]). This growing interest is part of a broader shift within the field of language teacher education, moving from a focus on language teacher cognition to an emphasis on the emotional dimensions of teaching.

Initially, research in language teacher education predominantly centered on cognitive aspects, particularly on language teachers' beliefs. Pioneering studies in this area, such as those by Borg (2003), were grounded in the assumption that understanding teachers' beliefs could lead to better educational outcomes, as these beliefs were seen as key predictors of classroom practices. Gao [5], in analyzing the literature on teachers' beliefs across decades, highlighted that these beliefs are complex, non-linear, and unpredictable. They may encompass various theoretical orientations related to teachers’ subject matter, forming intricate matrices that guide educators in their practices.

Over time, studies on teacher beliefs have transitioned from a positivist paradigm—focused on exploring linear relationships between specific language skills to a pragmatic paradigm that emphasizes the intricate tensions among different constructs [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. This evolution reflects a growing recognition of the complexity and context-dependency of teachers' beliefs, which are influenced by various personal, social, and cultural factors.

Concurrently, there has been a growing interest in the emotional dimensions of teaching, marked by what White [9] describes as the “emotional turn.” This shift acknowledges that teaching is not only a cognitive endeavor but also an emotional one, where teachers' feelings, identities, and psychological well-being play crucial roles. Research in language teacher psychology, as highlighted by Mercer and Kostoulas (2018), explores how emotions, well-being, and resilience interact with teachers' professional identities and classroom practices.

The integration of research on teachers' beliefs and emotions is essential because these constructs are deeply interconnected. Teachers' beliefs about education, students, and their roles significantly influence their emotional responses to various classroom situations [10]. Conversely, teachers' emotional experiences can reinforce or challenge their beliefs, leading to changes in teaching practices [11]. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of language teaching requires an integrated approach that considers both cognitive and emotional aspects.

Research questions and objectives

In this study, we retrieved 306 papers about language teachers' beliefs and emotions published in core journals (SCI, SSCI, AHCI and ESCI) from 1987 to 2023. Through a systematic collection and analysis of these articles, we attempted to offer guidance to scholars interested in the realms of language teachers’ beliefs and emotions. The identification of research hotspots and clusters would offer a clear roadmap for future inquiries, helping to direct scholarly efforts towards areas of high impact and relevance. Literature review

Overview of language teacher beliefs

A belief is a form of subjective and experience-derived knowledge [12] and constitutes any proposition initiated with the expression “I believe that” ([13,14]). Beliefs interconnected with other beliefs are considered core or central beliefs and hold significance in all aspects associated with human behavior and the process of learning [15,16]. In the context of education, teachers' beliefs significantly influence educational sustainability by shaping their commitment, innovation, and adaptability [3]. Their belief in the transformative power of education drives them to instill values of environmental stewardship, social responsibility, and lifelong learning in students ([4]; Wang et al., 2024a). This fosters a culture of sustainability, ensuring the continuity of educational efforts towards a more equitable, resilient, and environmentally conscious society [17]. According to Kindsvatter, Willen and Ishler [18] and Abdi and Asadi [19], the origins of teachers’ beliefs can be categorized as follows: 1) personal experience as language learners, 2) insights gained through teaching experiences, 3) influence of teachers' personality, where some educators may favor a specific method aligning with their character, and 4) principles derived from education or research.

Over the past few decades, scholars have investigated teacher beliefs from various perspectives, yielding numerous findings. Firstly, it has undergone extensive examination over the decades from various perspectives, encompassing cognitive orientation [20], sociocultural theory [21], reflective practices [22], and complex dynamic systems theory [5,23]. In addition, research on this topic has delved into methodological approaches [24,25], review or historical analysis [6,26], and tensions between beliefs and actual practice [5,27,28]. Also, it has been studied in specific language skills, including reading [29], writing [30], pronunciation [31] and grammar [32].

Overview of language teacher emotions

In the field of education, teaching processes have long been regarded as cognitive activities that demand a high level of expertise and teaching skills from educators, with little consideration for emotion [33]. It was not until the 1980s that some scholars began to advocate for the importance of emotions (e.g., Ref. [34]), gradually bringing emotion-related concepts into the research arena, such as emotional intelligence, emotional labor, and emotional regulation [[35], [36], [37]].

Emotion is a natural human experience, and we constantly react emotionally to external stimuli [38]. During the early period, studies on teachers’ emotions primarily focused on fragmented emotions of teachers themselves, often neglecting the connection between emotion and the external environment. After the 1990s, influenced by social constructivism, scholars such as Hargreaves [39] proposed that teacher emotions are a product of socio-cultural contexts. They argued that the study of teacher emotions should not only focus on teachers themselves but also pay more attention to the environment that triggers these emotions, adopting a holistic perspective to explore emotions. From a research perspective, recent studies on emotions have undergone a “positive turn” [40]. The findings of positive psychology [41,42] have influenced teacher emotion research, shifting the focus from emotional issues (such as anxiety and burnout) to exploring positive factors that promote individual or collective development and enhance well-being, with attention given to positive emotions themselves, the positive role of negative emotions, and emotional intelligence.

Interrelationship between language teacher beliefs and emotions

The complex interrelationship between language teacher beliefs and emotions is fundamental to understanding how educators approach teaching and interact with their students. Beliefs and emotions are not isolated constructs; they influence and reinforce each other in ways that can synergistically enhance educational outcomes. Both beliefs and emotions are situated within specific contexts and are dynamic in nature, though they remain distinct concepts. The interaction between language teacher beliefs and emotions often leads to synergistic effects that significantly improve both teaching practices and student outcomes. Despite their differences, similar definitions and empirical findings suggest that emotions and beliefs are components of the same developmental process. Emotions play a crucial role in validating and potentially transforming teachers’ pedagogical beliefs. According to Meijers [43], this interpretative process, which is both personal and social, draws from personal history and the discursive resources available in the environment, enabling teachers to authenticate their beliefs and emotions. As noted by Barcelos [44], Social psychologists have explored the relationship between beliefs and emotions, describing their intersection as interdependent and mutually reinforcing [45]. Emotions provide evidence for beliefs and direct our attention to information relevant to our goals [46], thereby shaping, creating, modifying, or reinforcing the resilience of these beliefs [47]. In summary, the relationship between beliefs and emotions is dynamic, interactive, and reciprocal, with cognitive processes influencing emotions and vice versa ([48,49]; Parkinson, 1995).

However, the interplay between language teacher beliefs and emotions is not always harmonious. While their interrelationship can enhance educational practices, it can also lead to conflicts that challenge both teaching efficacy and teacher well-being. The roles that beliefs and emotions play in these conflicts, and their broader interrelationship, have only recently been investigated. Gill and Hardin [50] emphasized that neglecting emotional aspects leads to an incomplete and potentially flawed understanding of teachers’ beliefs. To fully comprehend the development of language teachers, it is essential to explore how these conflicts arise and are managed. These conflicts often occur when there is a misalignment between teachers' beliefs and their emotional experiences in the classroom. Teachers may find themselves in situations where their deeply held pedagogical beliefs are challenged by the realities of their teaching environment, leading to emotional distress. This distress can affect how teachers interpret and respond to their professional experiences, potentially disrupting the validation process of their beliefs and emotions. For instance, when teachers face institutional constraints that conflict with their beliefs about best teaching practices, they may experience significant emotional dissonance, which can hinder their ability to teach and engage with students effectively. Moreover, these conflicts can lead to a re-evaluation of beliefs, where emotions either resist or propel changes in teachers' pedagogical approaches. This dynamic, though challenging, is a critical aspect of teacher development. Understanding how teachers navigate these conflicts—how they reconcile or adjust their beliefs in response to emotional challenges—is vital for supporting their ongoing professional growth and well-being. In exploring the implications of these conflicting perspectives for teacher education, concepts such as “emotional spirals” [49], “indexicality of emotions” [51], and “teacher vulnerability” [52,53] offer valuable insights into the dynamic, interactive, and reciprocal bond between beliefs and emotions in language teacher development.

Research gaps

There are only a few review articles about teachers' or language teachers' beliefs and emotions in recent years. For example, Fang [54] conducted a review that scrutinized the theoretical frameworks guiding research on teacher beliefs and practices, which synthesized recent research on teacher beliefs and practices, addressing pivotal issues relevant to the findings. Basturkmen [55] presented an interpretive review of a collection of studies, aiming to investigate potential factors influencing the correlation between language teachers' beliefs and their actual teaching practices. Gao [6] conducted a historical review of language teachers' beliefs, particularly in language teaching. Barcelos and Aragão [56] is, in essence, one of the few review articles describing interconnection between language teacher beliefs and emotions; however, their specific context is only limited in Brazil. In conclusion, these reviews could not spot the latest research trends through their limited scopes. Therefore, we used CiteSpace to conduct a review of language teachers' beliefs and spot the recent trends in them. The findings obtained are expected to contribute significant insights into the language teachers’ beliefs trend with existing evidence base as reference for future research and development.

Methodology

Research instrument

CiteSpace is a Java-based tool designed to link and visually represent the citation history and structure of publications in a graphic format [57]. The software provides evidence from documented publications, aiding in identifying pivotal points in scientific research, which enhances researchers’ ability to form informed judgments and viewpoints. In this paper, CiteSpace is utilized to generate visualization graphs depicting knowledge, encompassing variables such as the number of publications, keyword clusters, zones, and citation bursts of keywords. Through this tool, the paper describes certain data and parameters associated with identifying knowledge maps.

Data collection and analysis

Data for analyzing the teachers’ beliefs studies was extracted from the Web of Science (WOS), which covers most critical international journals [58,59]. To be exact, we chose articles or review articles that were written in English from 1987 to 2023 and were published in SCI/SSCI/AHCI/ESCI journals. As depicted in Fig. 1, we set 1987 as the beginning of our date range because the first relevant article appeared in the database in 1987. In addition, we set “Language Teacher Belief” or “L2 Teacher Belief” or “English Teacher Belief” or “Language Teacher Emotion” or “L2 Teacher Emotion” or “English Teacher Emotion” as the keywords to search. In this sense, a total of 306 documents were ultimately retrieved from the database with no duplication. The data were downloaded from WOS in BibTeX format, including full records, and subsequently compressed and imported into CiteSpace for analysis.Fig. 1The flowchart of this study.Fig. 1

During the initial phase, yearly publications remained under ten, indicating early interest in the field. In the growth phase, the number of studies consistently hovered around ten annually, suggesting an increased focus on this area of research. The rapid phase, however, has seen publications soar above ten each year, highlighting a significant spike in interest and research activity.Fig. 2Annual publication on language teachers' beliefs and emotions.Fig. 2

This increase reflects a promising trend in exploring language teacher beliefs, with the potential for rich academic inquiry. The recent surge in publications may suggest that this field is gaining momentum, possibly due to recognizing its importance in shaping educational practices. And the field appears poised for further growth, likely bringing forward new theories and research approaches.

In summary, the escalating number of publications over the years points to the expanding scope and significance of language teacher belief research. This pattern suggests that the area will continue to be an important subject of study, with expectations of new developments and contributions to the field in the future.

As shown in Table 1, the top five most cited studies are Benesch [62], Greenier [65], Wolff [66], Miller [67], and Gkonou [68]. Notably, these studies predominantly focus on language teacher emotions rather than beliefs, which can be attributed to the broader shift within language teacher education research from the cognitive to the emotional dimension. This transition, often referred to as the “emotional turn” [9,69,70], marks a significant change in focus from earlier work that primarily explored teachers' cognition (Borg, 2003).

Benesch [62] took a critical approach to examine the relationship between English language teaching and teacher emotions in a publicly-funded US university. This exploration provided a comprehensive and nuanced perspective on English language teaching, which introduces teachers' emotional labor as a framework for critical theorization of emotions and as a tool for teacher agency and resistance. Greenier (2021) utilized a sequential explanatory mixed-methods approach to examine the predictive role of emotion regulation and psychological well-being in work engagement. The analysis revealed categories and themes that help elucidate differences between British and Iranian teachers. Wolff (2017) sought to expand the scope of language teacher identity research by exploring the emotional demands placed on teachers-in-training, with a particular focus on non-native English-speaking teachers. It concluded with a call for new pedagogical models that facilitate teachers in developing reflexivity and navigating potential challenges related to emotions. Miller (2018) utilized language teachers’ questionnaires and semi-structured interview responses to identify the most prevalent emotions experienced by these teachers. It also investigated how their relationships with students lead to emotion labor and emotional rewards. It delves into the co-constitutive effects of agency and emotion for language teachers, examining the role of emotion labor in generating emotional rewards. Gkonou (2019) explored the efforts of eight teachers of English as a foreign language in Greece as they discuss addressing their students' language anxiety. It aimed to establish positive relationships with students as a strategy to mitigate the language anxiety experienced by their students.

And the top six to ten most cited literature include Benesch (2018), Council of Europe [71], Haukas [72], Fathi [73] and Richards JC [64]. Benesch [63] investigates feeling rules and emotion labor as instruments of language teachers' agency and decision-making. The study concludes by discussing the implications of feeling rules and emotion labor for the future of language teacher education. Council of Europe [71] encapsulated the essential messages of the CEFR in an easily understandable format, incorporating all CEFR illustrative descriptors. The updated CEFR Companion volume serves as a more accessible resource for teachers and teacher educators engaging with the framework for learning, teaching, and assessment purposes. Haukas (2016) delved into the beliefs of Norwegian language teachers regarding multilingualism and their application of a multilingual pedagogical approach in the third-language classroom. It shed light on the implications of examining Norwegian language teachers’ beliefs concerning multilingualism and the degree to which teachers have implemented a multilingual pedagogy in their classrooms. Fathi [73] investigated the predictive role of teacher self-efficacy and emotional regulation in teaching stress among Iranian EFL teachers. It provided additional insights into the relationships among psychological factors affecting teachers in the context of teaching English as a Foreign Language. Richards [64] delved into the emotions experienced by both teachers and learners, examining their causes and the potential impact on teaching and learning. It offered suggestions on how teachers and learners can cultivate emotional competence, which involves the ability to understand and effectively manage emotions in the context of language learning and teaching.

In summary, the top 10 most cited studies underscore the central role of emotions in language teaching. While early research focused on cognitive aspects, the emotional turn has brought attention to how emotions impact teachers' decision-making, psychological well-being, and the overall learning environment. These studies highlight themes such as emotional labor, teacher agency, and the importance of emotional regulation, offering new pedagogical models and strategies that integrate both cognitive and emotional dimensions of teaching. The shift in focus from beliefs to emotions reflects a growing recognition of the complexity of teaching, where emotions play a crucial role in shaping both teacher and student experiences in the classroom.

By moving from a narrow focus on specific teaching practices to a broader and deeper examination of the cognitive, experiential and psychological aspects of teaching, the research reflects a maturation that is both intellectually rigorous and highly relevant to the realities of contemporary education.

Burst detection serves as an effective analytical tool for identifying crucial events and significant information that occur during a specific time interval [83]. Research hotspots can be reflected by the keywords with strong citation bursts (Bicheng et al., 2021). In this regard, the burst of keywords indicates that a potential topic has become or is attracting much attention from researchers in a certain period. The explosion of citations indicates that the researchers are paying special attention to the possibility of the article's potential contribution [84]. Discussion

Interpretation of findings

The findings from the Citespace analysis highlight the dynamic evolution and growing importance of research on language teachers' beliefs and emotions. First, the rapid development of this field, particularly since 2018, reflects the burgeoning interest and relevance of this area of inquiry in recent years. This trend underscores the growing recognition of the critical role teacher beliefs play in shaping language education outcomes [3,4,19]. Second, the identification of key contributors and institutions reveals a concentrated effort by a select group of scholars and research centers, predominantly from the USA, China, and the UK, which have been instrumental in steering the discourse in this domain. Notably, Benesch (2017) emerged as a central figure, indicating her significant contributions to the discourse on language teachers' beliefs and emotions. Third, the clustering of research themes into belief systems, teacher development, language systems, and emotion regulation further reflects the multifaceted nature of language teachers’ beliefs and emotions, indicating that these aspects are deeply interwoven with broader educational practices and teacher professional development [5,30,85]. Furthermore, the temporal analysis, which divides the research into four distinct stages, illustrates how the focus of studies has shifted over time, moving from specific teaching practices to more holistic considerations of internal belief systems and teacher psychology [6,44]. This evolution mirrors the broader trends in educational research, where there is a growing recognition of the complex, contextual nature of teaching and learning.

Implications for theory and practice

The findings of the study have significant implications for both theoretical frameworks and practical applications in language education. The identification of core research clusters suggests that future theoretical models should integrate concepts from belief systems, teacher development, language systems, and emotion regulation to provide a more comprehensive understanding of teacher cognition and behavior. This integration could lead to the development of more robust frameworks that account for the interplay between teachers' internal beliefs and their external teaching practices. The theoretical understanding of language teachers' beliefs and emotions has evolved significantly, moving from initial explorations of specific teaching practices to more comprehensive frameworks that integrate psychological and emotional dimensions. This shift in focus suggests that future theoretical models of teacher beliefs must account for the interplay between cognitive and affective factors, as well as the broader socio-cultural context in which teachers operate. The identification of emotion regulation as a key research cluster highlights the importance of considering teachers’ emotional well-being in studies of belief systems, suggesting that emotions are not merely a peripheral concern but are central to understanding how beliefs are formed, maintained, and altered.

In practice, these findings emphasize the need for teacher training programs to be more holistic, addressing not only pedagogical techniques but also the emotional and psychological challenges teachers face. Institutions should consider developing professional development programs that support teachers in managing their emotions and understanding their belief systems in relation to their teaching practice. Moreover, policymakers and educators can use this research to inform curriculum design and educational reforms, ensuring that teachers are equipped with the tools to reflect on and adapt their beliefs to better serve their students.

Limitations of the study

This study, leveraging the capabilities of CiteSpace to analyze and visualize language teachers' beliefs and emotions, still faces several notable limitations that may affect the comprehensiveness of its conclusions. Firstly, despite incorporating a broad array of literature, it potentially fails to fully capture the diversity of teachers' beliefs and emotions across different geographical and cultural contexts, particularly from under-researched or economically disadvantaged regions where access to academic publishing may be limited. Secondly, the reliance predominantly on quantitative data analysis may not adequately explore the complex, evolving nature of teachers' beliefs and emotions, thus missing out on deeper qualitative insights into the personal, subjective, and contextual factors that significantly influence these beliefs and emotions. Moreover, the analysis might not account for the impact of recent educational technology advancements and policy changes on teachers’ beliefs and emotions.

Future directions and recommendations

To address the gaps identified, future research should prioritize a more inclusive approach by integrating qualitative studies that explore the nuanced experiences and beliefs of language teachers in a variety of cultural and economic contexts. Employing mixed-methods research designs could provide richer, more layered insights into the dynamic interplay between individual teacher experiences and the broader educational systems in which they operate. Furthermore, there is a critical need for longitudinal studies that examine the trajectories of teacher beliefs over time, offering insights into how beliefs shift in response to ongoing changes in educational policies, pedagogical innovations, and classroom challenges. This approach would not only enhance our understanding of the stability and malleability of teacher beliefs but also support the development of more targeted, effective teacher training programs that respond to the specific needs of teachers in diverse educational settings.

Conclusion

Significance and impact

The significance of this study lies in its comprehensive overview of the current landscape of research on language teachers' beliefs and emotions. By systematically analyzing the evolution, key contributors, and emerging trends in this field, the study provides a valuable resource for researchers and educators alike. The identification of research hotspots and clusters offers a clear roadmap for future inquiries, helping to direct scholarly efforts towards areas of high impact and relevance.

From a practical perspective, the findings have the potential to significantly influence the way language teacher education and professional development are approached. By highlighting the importance of emotion regulation, belief systems, and teacher development, this study underscores the need for more holistic and context-sensitive training programs. These insights can lead to the design of interventions that not only enhance teachers' instructional capabilities but also support their emotional well-being, thereby fostering a more sustainable and effective teaching workforce. Moreover, the study's emphasis on the global distribution of research productivity reveals the central role of certain countries and institutions in advancing the field. This has implications for international collaboration and knowledge sharing, suggesting that fostering connections between high-yield research centers and underrepresented regions could lead to more inclusive and diverse perspectives in language education research.

Final thoughts and call for action

In conclusion, this study has illuminated the dynamic and multifaceted nature of language teachers' beliefs and emotions, providing both a retrospective analysis and a forward-looking agenda for future research. The exponential growth in publications, coupled with the identification of key research themes and clusters, indicates that this is a vibrant and rapidly evolving field with significant implications for both theory and practice. However, the study also highlights critical gaps that need to be addressed. The limited representation of diverse cultural and geographical contexts, along with the predominant reliance on quantitative analysis, calls for a more inclusive and holistic approach to future research. There is a pressing need for studies that incorporate qualitative methods, explore underrepresented regions, and consider the impact of contemporary educational challenges, such as technological advancements and policy changes, on teacher beliefs.

As we move forward, it is essential for researchers, educators, and policymakers to collaborate to address these gaps. By doing so, we can develop a more comprehensive understanding of language teacher cognition, one that is sensitive to the diverse and evolving realities of teachers around the world. This will not only advance the field theoretically but also lead to more effective and contextually relevant teaching practices, ultimately benefiting learners globally. We must strive for greater inclusivity in research, embrace methodological diversity, and remain responsive to the changing landscape of education. By answering this call, the field of language teacher beliefs and emotions will continue to grow in relevance and impact, contributing to the advancement of language education in increasingly meaningful ways.

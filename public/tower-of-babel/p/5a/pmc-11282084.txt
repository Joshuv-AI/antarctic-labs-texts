A new single magnetic core coupled-inductor based active switched Quasi Z-source inverter
Authors: Mohammadreza Zarrinehbafan, Ali Seifi, Ali Nadermohammadi, Seyed Hossein Hosseini
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a novel topology for Z-source inverters (ZSI). The new Z-Source network is based on the coupled-inductors and active switched boost. Features of the topology include high voltage boost ability, single magnetic-core, low voltage stress on the active switch, and capacitors. The principles of operation, analysis, voltage and current equations of each component, and the converter voltage gain in the steady-state are presented. A comparison with other topologies has also been performed to determine the advantages and disadvantages of the proposed ZSI. Finally, experimental results of the laboratory prototype are presented to confirm the performance of the proposed topology.

Introduction

In recent years, the use of renewable energy, especially photovoltaic systems, has received much attention. However, due to the low power of photovoltaic panels and their DC form, the use of power electronic converters to convert power to AC and increase the voltage of photovoltaic panels becomes more important1–3. Impedance source converter Based on the idea of impedance source networks for the converter, called Z-source inverter, has been proposed to overcome the common problems of voltage source inverters (VSI) and current source inverters (CSI)4. The impedance source network can be used in all power converters such as dc-dc, ac-dc, ac-ac, and dc-ac. The Z-source converter provides single-stage power conversion with buck-boost capability. Because the impedance source converter uses a shoot-through (ST) state to increase the voltage, they achieve wide voltage and current ranges with high reliability. In addition, due to single-stage power conversion, the cost and volume of the converter are reduced and the efficiency of the converter is increased. These advantages increase their use4–7.

Despite the above advantages, the basic structure of ZSI has disadvantages such as high voltage stress on the capacitor, large inrush current flowing from the input source, discontinuous input current, and no common ground. In8, Quasi-ZSI is introduced by changing the arrangement of conventional ZSI components. QZSI is more popular than conventional ZSI due to the reduction of capacitor voltage stress and lower inrush current with continuous input current and common ground between source and inverter.

A review of recent research on ZSI and impedance source networks in general shows that the goal of most research is to achieve higher voltage gain using fewer passive elements and a smaller shoot-through duty cycle ratio; Because the small ST duty cycle ratio reduces the output voltage distortion, increases the power quality and reduces the current stress of the switching elements. various voltage gain techniques are used in9–33 which can be classified into 5 categories. (i) Active switched Z-source inverters, which in9, are proposed to make changes to the conventional and quasi Z-source network by replacing a diode and a power switch with an inductor and a capacitor. These changes reduce the passive components and volume of the converter. In10, an overview of most Z-source inverter structures is based on the active switched network. (ii) Structures using a switching inductor cell and a switching capacitor cell are provided in the impedance source networks to increase the voltage gain. These structures include SL-ZSI11, SL-qZSI12, L-ZSI13, VL-qZSI14, SC-ZSI15. (iii) Using the transformer and coupled-inductor technique, TSI16, trans-ZSI17, Improved trans-QZSI18 with continuous input current, LCCT-ZSI19 with the characteristic of preventing saturation of the transformer core, TZ-source inverter20 by replacing two transformers instead of two inductors in the classic Z-source network, Γ-ZSI21, Y-ZSI22 with an additional winding compared to Γ-ZSI, A-ZSI23 with continuous input current, ∆-ZSI24 with reduced the ohmic losses, achieve a higher boost factor25 presents a Z source inverter using two transformers in the impedance network, which has the capability of continuous input current and high voltage gain. (iv) Using the cascade extension technique with the assistant diode/capacitor, DAC-qZSI and CAC-qZSI are presented in reference26. Despite the higher voltage gain than the quasi Z-source structure, these structures increase costs, losses, and converter volume due to the increase in elements. The main purpose of these structures is to increase the output voltage gain, which could be increased by the implementation of the cascaded quasi Z-source network. (v) Despite the provision of Z-source inverters with different techniques of voltage increase, in recent years to increase the efficiency of impedance source networks and increase the voltage gain required in some applications, especially renewable sources, Combining different techniques of increasing the voltage with each other in the form of a combined Z-source network has been used. For example, in27, ASC/SCL ZSI is presented using a combination of switched-coupled-inductor and Active-switched-capacitor voltage boosting techniques, with the ability to increase the voltage gain28–30, structures based on the combination of the Active Switched technique with the voltage boosting technique developed by auxiliary capacitor and diode, have been proposed to further increase the voltage gain of Z-source inverters. However, the number of components increases in these structures31 presents a structure called SL-Y-source with high voltage gain using a combination of switched-inductors cell and coupled-inductors technique. voltage-double Quasi-Y-source, combining the coupled-inductor technique with the auxiliary capacitor and diode technique, is presented in32, which has a high voltage gain and a continuous input current and common ground current. In33, a structure based on the combination of active switched network technique with switched inductor cell is presented, which has a continuous input current and high voltage gain. In34, using a combination of switched-capacitor inductor technique and active-switched boost technique, a new structure called SCL-ASBI is presented with the ability to increase the high voltage with fewer components. For further improvement, it can expand to n cells. The EB/SCL-ASBI topology is introduced by adding a cell to the SCL-ASBI to achieve a higher voltage gain. In35, to develop new impedance source networks for DC-AC and DC-DC power conversion systems with high efficiency and high reliability, several impedance source networks including S-qZSI, switched capacitor qZS, TICSC, MYSC It is presended that it can be used in DC-AC and DC-DC conversion systems36. Proposes a new family of high-gain Z quasi-source inverters (qZSIs), named rASLB-qZSI and cASLB-qZSI, with a hybrid active switched inductor boost network. The proposed inverter provides continuous input current and higher boost voltage inversion capability.

Proposed topology and operation principles

Proposed topology

As shown in Fig. 1, the proposed topology of an active quasi Z- source network containing an active switch (S0), a three-winding coupled-inductor (N1, N2, and N3), five diodes (D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5) and five capacitors (C1, C2, C3, C4, and C5) are formed. As can be seen, only one magnetic core is used in this structure.Figure 1Proposed Z-source network.

Proposed Z-source network.

The inverter bridge consists of four switches (S1, S2, S3, S4), while the impedance network utilizes one switch (S0). Figure 2 illustrates the gate pulse signals for these switches, as well as the method employed to obtain them37.Figure 2The method to generate gate pulse signals.

The method to generate gate pulse signals.

H1 and H2 are obtained by comparing the triangular signal (Vtri) with two DC signals (VST1 and VST2) so that, if VST1 is greater than Vtri, H1 is set to 1 and otherwise equal to 0. Also, H2 has a value of 1 when VST2 is smaller than Vtri otherwise, it is equal to 0. During the intervals when Vstep is equal to 0 and H1 is equal to 1, the value of the ST1 signal is 1. On the other hand, when both Vstep and H2 are equal to 1, the value of the ST2 signal is 1. The gate pulse signal of switches 1 and 4 (GS1,4) is generated by combining Vstep with ST1. Similarly, the gate pulse signal of switches 2 and 3 (GS2,3) is generated by combining the logical NOT of Vstep with ST2. The integration of ST1 and ST2 results in the generation of the gate pulse signal for switch 0 (GS0).

Conclusion

This paper presents a novel Z-source inverter that utilizes an active switch and only one coupled-inductor, offering a high voltage boost factor capability. The use of a single magnetic core in this proposed structure has resulted in a reduced converter volume and lowered costs associated with additional magnetic cores. Furthermore, the number of turns in the coupled inductor winding provides an additional degree of freedom for controlling the boost factor of the converter. The modulation method employed for generating gate pulse switches is described in the article. To assess the benefits and drawbacks of the proposed Z-source inverter, a comparison has been made with other impedance source structures in terms of boost factor and maximum voltage stress of capacitors, diodes, and active switches. The results clearly indicate that the proposed design present a higher voltage boost factor compared to other structures. Additionally, the maximum voltage stress on capacitors, active switches, and diodes is lower for the proposed design in comparison to most of the compared structures. The theoretical analysis and design of passive elements are presented, and confirmed by the construction of an experimental prototype and the presentation of experimental results, theoretical analysis, and obtained equations.

Treatment trends in patients with de novo metastatic prostate cancer in the era of upfront combination therapy
Authors: Hikari Miura, Shingo Hatakeyama, Ryuji Tabata, Daiji Fujimori, Yohei Kawashima, Shingo Moriyama, Takuya Oishi, Hirotaka Horiguchi, Osamu Soma, Daisuke Noro, Toshikazu Tanaka, Teppei Okamoto, Hayato Yamamoto, Satoshi Sato, Chikara Ohyama
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe objective of this study is to assess the trends in treatment selection for patients with de novo metastatic castration‐sensitive prostate cancer in the era of upfront combination therapy.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective study included 595 patients treated with either upfront combination therapy (upfront novel hormonal therapies and taxane‐based chemotherapy) or vintage therapy (androgen deprivation therapy with or without bicalutamide) between 2016 and 2021. High tumor burden metastatic disease was defined when a patient met the CHAARTED or LATITUDE criteria. We evaluated trends in treatment selection and reasons for selecting vintage therapy.ResultsOf the 595, 123 and 472 patients were classified as having low and high tumor‐burden disease, respectively. The Use of upfront combination therapy was found to be rapidly increasing with utilization rates of 72% and 54% in 2021 for high and low tumor‐burden disease, respectively. Multivariable logistic regression analysis found older age, poor performance status, and nonacademic center were significantly associated with the selection of vintage therapy. Of the 163 patients who received vintage hormone therapy after approval of upfront therapy, 74.2% had a specific reason for avoiding upfront therapy. The reasons for selecting vintage therapy included refusal (39.8%), older age (67.6%), frailty (56.3%), and comorbidity (40.8%). Furthermore, 16.9% of patients declined upfront combination therapy due to cost concerns.ConclusionUpfront combination therapy use has 72% and 54% prevalence among patients with high and low tumor burden diseases, respectively, in this current practice. Older age, poor performance status, and facility bias were negatively associated with the use of upfront combination therapy.Keywords: castration‐sensitive prostate cancer, metastasis, selection, trends, upfront therapy

INTRODUCTION

Prostate cancer (PC) is the most common malignancy in men worldwide. 1 , 2 Approximately 6%–15% of patients with PC have metastatic disease at the time of diagnosis, although prostate‐specific antigen (PSA) screening has reduced the incidence of metastatic PC. 3 , 4 , 5 Due to the high mortality associated with metastatic PC. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Upfront combination therapy comprising androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) plus novel hormonal therapies or taxane‐based chemotherapy has been developed through large‐scale clinical trials of regimens including ADT plus docetaxel (DTX), ADT plus abiraterone acetate (ABI), ADT plus enzalutamide (ENZ), and ADT plus apalutamide (APA). 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 This rapid paradigm shift has led to a major change in the standard of care for metastatic castration‐sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC). However, despite its efficacy, there are reports that the utilization of upfront combination therapy remains low in clinical practice. 15 , 16 , 17 Concerns regarding side effects and medical costs are thought to be the main reasons underlying its low prevalence. Hesitation regarding the use of upfront combination therapy in patients with low tumor burden disease may be due to the effectiveness of vintage therapy (ADT ± bicalutamide) alone in selected cases. However, current trends in patient selection and use of vintage therapy in clinical practice have not been clarified. Accordingly, we conducted a multicenter retrospective study to investigate trends in treatment selection among real‐world patients with mCSPC in the era of upfront combination therapy.

METHODS

Study population and patient selection

The present study protocol was approved by the ethics review boards of the Hirosaki University School of Medicine (authorization number: 2019‐099‐1, 2021‐241) and all participating hospitals. All study activities were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki.

We used the Ageo and Hirosaki Urological Cancer Database to retrospectively evaluate 1190 patients with de novo mCSPC who were initially treated with vintage therapy (ADT ± bicalutamide) or upfront combination therapy using ADT + DTX, ADT + ABI, ADT + ENZ, or ADT + APA between April 2016 and December 2021. This database included one academic center (the Hirosaki University) and 12 community hospitals. Study inclusion criteria were (1) histologically proven adenocarcinoma or clinically diagnosed PC and (2) treatment with ADT alone, ADT plus bicalutamide, or upfront combination therapy as standard of care between January 2016 and December 2021. Patients with insufficient baseline clinical information were excluded. The final study cohort comprised 595 patients with mCSPC (Figure 1). Patients were classified into two groups: treatment with vintage therapy including ADT alone or ADT plus bicalutamide (vintage group); and treatment with upfront combination therapy (upfront group).

Patient selection. We enrolled 595 patients with newly diagnosed metastatic castration‐sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC) in our study. ABI, abiraterone acetate; APA, apalutamide; DTX, docetaxel; ENZ, enzalutamide; vintage, androgen deprivation therapy ± bicalutamide.

Evaluated variables

The following variables were recorded at diagnosis: age (years), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG‐PS), Gleason score, serum PSA (ng/mL), and visceral metastasis. Tumor stage and grade were assigned based on the 8th edition of the TNM classification. 18 Metastatic status was evaluated via chest and body computed tomography and bone scintigraphy before initiating ADT. Bone metastatic volume was evaluated using the extent of disease (EOD) score on bone scintigraphy. High‐volume and high‐risk metastatic disease were defined according to the CHAARTED or LATITUDE criteria, respectively. 10 , 19 Those who meet CHAARTED high volume or LATITUDE high‐risk criteria are considered as a high tumor burden metastatic disease.

Treatment protocol

Before 2016, patients were initially treated with ADT alone or ADT plus bicalutamide. After 2016, urologists in Japan started providing upfront DTX therapy to patients diagnosed with high tumor burden mCSPC. Upfront ABI for LATITUDE high‐risk disease and upfront ENZ/APA for all‐risk groups were approved in February 2018 and May 2020, respectively. The choice of therapeutic agent was determined by the attending physician. All patients were treated by an urologist. The treatment plan is discussed with each patient regarding the intensity, effectiveness, and cost of treatment. The final decision is made by consensus among the patient, family, and health care providers. Patients who developed metastatic castration‐resistant PC were treated with sequential therapy, including second‐generation androgen receptor pathway‐targeted agents and/or taxane‐based chemotherapy.

Outcomes

The primary aim of the present study was to evaluate trends in treatment selection among patients with mCSPC in the era of upfront combination therapy. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the effect of clinical factors on treatment selection who were potential candidates for upfront combination therapy. The secondary aims were to evaluate reasons for declining vintage therapy. The reasons for declining vintage therapy were classified into refusal, older age, frailty, and comorbidity. Multiple choices were allowed as factors may overlap. Comorbidity included cerebral diseases, cardiovascular disease, other cancers, dementia, severe renal impairment, uncontrolled diabetes, chronic pulmonary disease, and neuropathy with paralysis. Frailty was determined by comprehensive assessment by primary physicians using ECOG‐PS, cognitive function, and general state of health.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 7.00 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), BellCurve for Excel (Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan), and R 4.0.2 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) software. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher's exact test. Quantitative variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Statistical difference between groups was compared using Student's t‐test for normally distributed data or the Mann–Whitney U test for non‐normally distributed data. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to investigate the effect of clinical factors on treatment selection. We calculated odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) after controlling for potential confounders, including patient age (>75 years), ECOG‐PS (>1), Gleason score (9, 10), EOD (3, 4), and visceral metastasis. p‐Values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics of patients

Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Of the 595 patients with mCSPC, 123 and 472 were classified as having low and high tumor burden disease, respectively (Figure 1). The median age in this cohort was 74 (IQR, 69–79) years. Of the 123 patients with low tumor burden disease, 87 and 36 were divided to the vintage and upfront groups, respectively. Of the 472 patients with high tumor burden disease, 247 and 225 were divided to vintage and upfront groups, respectively. Of the 334 patients in the vintage therapy group, 163 were treated after the approval of upfront combination therapy. Significant differences were observed in age, ECOG‐PS, Gleason score, and EOD due to uncontrolled selection bias (Table 2). Of the 595 patients, 106 (17.8%) and 489 (82.2%) were treated at the academic center and community hospitals, respectively. Of the 261 patients treated with upfront combination therapy, 91 (34.9%) were treated at the academic center. There was a significant difference in the proportions of patients treated with upfront combination therapy in the academic center (n = 91/106, 85.9%) and community hospitals (n = 170/489, 34.8%) (p < 0.001) (Figure S1). We identified 424 patients who were able to offer upfront combination therapy (upfront group, n = 261; and vintage group, n = 163) after February 2018.

Outcomes

The use of upfront combination therapy was found to have rapidly increased in patients with high tumor burden disease after 2019, reaching 72% (n = 54/75) in 2021 (Figure 2a). The most prescribed treatment was upfront ADT + ABI (n = 113) followed by ADT + APA (n = 47) (Figure 2b). The utilization of upfront combination therapy was seen to have rapidly increased in patients with low tumor burden disease after 2020, reaching 54% (n = 12/22) in 2021 (Figure 2c). The most prescribed treatment was upfront ADT + APA (n = 14) followed by ADT + ABI (n = 11) (Figure 2d). Trends of agent use by high‐risk or high‐vol. are shown in Figure S2. Multivariable logistic regression analysis for treatment selection (ref. vintage therapy) found age >75 years (OR, 0.32; 95% CI, 0.20–0.50; p < 0.001) and ECOG‐PS >1 (OR, 0.44; 95% CI, 0.23–0.83; p = 0.012) were significantly associated with selection of vintage therapy. Conversely, the academic center (OR, 4.76; 95% CI, 2.48–9.15; p < 0.001) was significantly associated with the selection of upfront therapy (Figure 3). Gleason score 9–10 (OR, 1.56; 95% CI, 0.96–2.55; p = 0.075), EOD 3–4 (OR, 0.94; p = 0.810), and visceral metastasis (OR, 1.20; p = 0.516) were not significantly associated with treatment selection.

We selected 163 patients in the vintage group who were treated after the approval of upfront combination therapy (treatment initiation after February 2018 and May 2020 in patients with high and low tumor burden disease, respectively) and investigated the reasons for selecting vintage therapy (Figure 4a). The proportion of patients with negative reasons for treatment was 72.4% (n = 118), respectively. Of the 118 patients with negative reasons, 60.2% (n = 71) did not select upfront therapy as their physicians considered them ineligible. The major reason for refusal from patients/families was cost concerns (n = 20, 16.9%) (Figure 4b). The detailed reason for ineligibility by physicians is shown in Figure 4c. DISCUSSION

A rapid paradigm shift has led to a major change in the standard of care for patients with mCSPC. However, despite its efficacy, the use of upfront combination therapy in clinical practice remains low (up to 59% in 2019–2020). 17 Thus, we aimed to investigate selection trends and reasons for the selection of vintage therapy in the era of upfront combination therapy in our clinical practice. As we expected, the use of vintage therapy dropped over time, whereas the percentage of patients treated with upfront combination therapy increased to 72% for high tumor burden disease and 54% for low tumor burden disease in 2021. We focused on tumor burden because many patients (79.3%) are high tumor‐burden in this cohort. We needed to analyze low and high tumor burden separately because patients with high tumor burden disease tend to be treated with an upfront agent, but not in low tumor burden disease. The low usage of upfront therapy for low tumor burden disease may be based on the favorable efficacy of vintage therapy, particularly in the Asian population. 5 , 20 , 21 However, the rate of disease progression among patients with low tumor burden disease reportedly exceeds 50% in both large clinical trials and in real‐world practice. 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 It is important to recognize that the term “low‐risk/volume” indicates a more favorable prognosis compared with “high‐risk/volume”; however, it does not mean low grade disease. Upfront combination therapy should be administered following careful assessment of the balance between risk and benefit. Of the 163 patients who were potential candidates for upfront combination therapy, we found 45 patients (27.6%) were treated with vintage therapy with no specific reasons provided. We believe such patients should be treated with upfront combination therapy. However, change in clinical practice may take time as clinicians have become accustomed to the use of vintage treatment for many years.

Optimal agent selection in PC can be challenging due to a lack of studies comparing upfront combination therapies. In high tumor‐burden disease group, ABI was the most prescribed regimen as it was first approved in February 2018. The use of DTX has reached a plateau as chemotherapy is a less patient‐friendly strategy in clinical practice compared with ABI. The use of APA was slightly more common in both the high and low tumor‐burden disease groups. This finding may simply be due to APA not being approved for use after the progression of metastatic castration‐resistant PC. Further studies are required to determine the benefits of prior treatment with APA.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed the selection biases for upfront combination or vintage therapy. We found older age, poor ECOG‐PS, and community hospitals (nonacademic centers) were negatively associated with the selection of upfront combination therapy. These identified factors are clinically relevant. Given limited tolerability and capacity, it is unclear whether upfront combination therapy is beneficial in elderly or more frail patients. Our challenge is to extend the use of upfront combination therapy to older and/or frail patients. When we evaluated the 163 patients who were potential candidates for upfront combination therapy, three negative factors (older age, frailty, and comorbidity) were found to be associated with lower use of upfront combination therapy. The efficacy of upfront combination therapy in this patient population remains unclear as older and frail patients are less likely to be included in clinical trials. Considering the large number of older PC patients, frailty assessment is an important component of treatment selection strategies. 22 Further studies are required to determine the benefits of upfront combination therapy in older and/or frail patients with mCSPC.

Our results should be cautiously interpreted as the present study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective study with a small sample size that included several selection biases. Our study describes the trends in treatment in the real‐world practice, but we cannot conclude whether our choices were correct or not. Second, our results may not be adaptable to other countries due to racial and insurance system differences. Third, information regarding comorbidities was not available in patients receiving upfront combination therapy. Finally, the factors analyzed in this study were factors used by the treating physician to make decisions, not by the patient. It should be noted that these are not factors from the patient's perspective. Despite these limitations, our results demonstrate current trends in the selection of upfront combination therapy for patients with mCSPC.

In conclusion, the prevalence of combination therapy use is 72% and 54% in patients with high and low tumor burden disease, respectively, in current practice. Older age was the most common reason for not selecting upfront combination therapy.

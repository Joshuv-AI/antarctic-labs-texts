TGF-β1 promotes collagen synthesis in systemic sclerosis via upregulating P4HA3
Authors: Zhaopeng Chen, Yinru Lin, Yayi Huang, Zhixian Chen, Yao Gong, Zhiduo Hou, Ling Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveSystemic sclerosis (SSc) is a complex autoimmune disease resulting in fibrosis of skin and internal organs. Despite progress in treatment, SSc carries high mortality due to organ fibrosis. This study aimed to identify a potential antifibrotic therapeutic for the treatment of SSc.MethodsSSc bioinformatics data (GSE181549 and GSE138669) were obtained from the public Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Subsequently, we conducted differential analysis, Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction, gene enrichment analysis to identify a key fibrotic gene, followed by verification.ResultsA total of 107 differentially expressed genes were identified through analysis between SSc patients and healthy controls. P4HA3 (α subunit of Collagen prolyl 4-hydroxylases, C-P4Hs) was identified by PPI network analysis. P4HA3 was the sole upregulated gene and positively correlated with the modified Rodnan skin score. PI3K/AKT signaling pathway was enriched by GSEA for single-gene to obtain P4HA3-related pathways. Single-cell analysis detected predominant TGF-β1 upregulation in immune cells, particularly in CD14+CD16− monocytes, and P4HA3 showed elevated expression in SFRP2high fibroblasts. Immunohistochemistry indicated that P4HA3- and TGF-β1-positive cell numbers were elevated in the dermal layers of SSc patients. P4HA3 was also elevated in bleomycin-treated mice and TGF-β1-induced human fibroblasts. Inhibition of C-P4Hs ameliorated experimental fibrosis in bleomycin-treated mice and TGF-β1-induced human fibroblasts. Hydroxyproline levels were reduced following P4HA3 knockdown in human fibroblasts.ConclusionOur results suggest that P4HA3 is upregulated by TGF-β1 secreted from immune cells to promote collagen synthesis in SSc. Inhibition of C-P4Hs is a potential therapeutic approach for SSc fibrosis.

Introduction

Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is a chronic autoimmune disease characterized by immune dysregulation, microvascular damage, and fibrosis of multiple organs [1]. Skin fibrosis is the hallmark of SSc and is considered to be caused by the overproduction of extracellular proteins [2]. Several profibrogenic cytokines have been reported to activate fibroblasts in SSc. Among these, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) has emerged as a key cytokine of fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis in SSc [3,4]. However, the molecular mechanisms of excessive collagen synthesis remain incompletely understood [5]. Despite progress in treatment, SSc carries the highest mortality among all rheumatic diseases [1,6]. Thus, there is a need to develop more effective antifibrotic therapeutics for the treatment of SSc.

Fibroblasts/myofibroblasts are pivotal effector cells in SSc, playing a central role in fibrotic tissue remodeling. As such, the extracellular matrix, which is predominantly composed of collagen, is considered a pathological product of their persistent activation [7,8]. Prolyl 4-hydroxylases (P4Hs), the key enzymes in collagen biosynthesis, stabilize the collagen triple helix by catalyzing the formation of 4-hydroxyproline. P4Hs from all known vertebrates are α2β2 tetramers in which the α subunit contains a substrate binding and catalytic domain [9]. Three isoforms of the α-subunit have been identified in humans and mice: P4H alpha polypeptide I (P4HA1), P4H alpha polypeptide II (P4HA2), and P4H alpha polypeptide III (P4HA3) [10]. P4HA3 mRNA expression is detectable in placenta, adult liver, and fetal skin, but at much lower levels than P4HA1 and P4HA2 transcripts [11].

The most prevalent collagen posttranslational modification is the hydroxylation of collagen strands by collagen P4Hs (C-P4Hs), which requires Fe(II), α-ketoglutarate (AKG) and molecular oxygen. AKG mimics can inhibit P4Hs, such as dihydroxybenzoic acid, pyridine 2,5-dicarboxylate (PDC), and N-oxalyl glycine [12]. Hypoxia-inducible factor P4Hs (HIF-P4Hs), the important enzymes in low oxygen responses, also require Fe(II), AKG and molecular oxygen for catalytic activity [13]. PDC exhibits high selectivity for C-P4Hs and shows no inhibitory effect on HIF-P4Hs [12,14].

In the present study, we used bioinformatics to perform a comparative analysis of skin biopsy samples from individuals with SSc and healthy controls to characterize differentially-expressed genes and identify novel targets for antifibrotic therapeutics. Our analysis revealed P4HA3 as the sole collagen-modifying enzyme upregulated in SSc. This finding was consistently validated using single-cell RNA sequencing, immunohistochemistry, murine models, and fibroblast cultures.

Methods

Data source

We obtained bioinformatics data from the public Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The skin bulk RNA sequencing information and the corresponding clinical features from 113 SSc patients and 44 matched healthy controls were downloaded from GSE181549 (GPL13497) [15]. The skin single-cell RNA sequencing information of 12 SSc patients and 10 healthy controls was downloaded from GSE138669 (GPL18573) [16].

Screening of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The raw counts of gene expression data in GSE181549 were normalized using the R package “Limma” (read.maimages, backgroundCorrect, and normalizeBetweenArrays) [17]. DEGs were filtered using the R package “Limma”. The threshold was determined as the adjusted p-value of <0.05 and |log2 fold change| >1.

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction and hub gene recognition

To evaluate the relationships between DEGs, we constructed a PPI network of DEGs using the STRING database (version 12.0) [18]. A minimum required interaction score ≥0.4 was regarded as significant. Cytoscape software (version 3.10.1) was used to screen the nodes of the PPI network [19]. Ten hub genes were identified by cytoHubba (a plugin of Cytoscape).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

In order to explore target gene-related pathways, we performed GSEA for single genes using GSEA software (Broad Institute, version 4.3.2) [20]. The hallmark gene set profile “h.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt” from MSigDB was used [21]. The threshold for enrichment significance was determined as a false discovery rate <0.25 and NOM p-value <0.05.

Single-cell data processing

The count matrices were combined by the R package “Seurat” (version 5.2.1) [22]. For quality control, the cells expressing between 300 and 5000 genes were selected for analysis. Next, the gene expression matrix was processed with normalization. The highly variable genes were identified and principal component analysis method was performed for linear dimensionality reduction. We performed Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) algorithm to visualize the clusters. Differential gene expression analysis between SSc and healthy controls was performed by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, using a threshold of P < 0.05, log2 fold change (log2FC) > 1.5 and detectable expression of each gene >25 % in SSc group. The differential genes were used to perform Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment by the R package “clusterProfiler” [23]. The threshold for enrichment significance was determined as the adjusted p-value of <0.05.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF)

Skin biopsy samples were collected from 12 patients who fulfilled the 2013 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for SSc [24], and 4 healthy controls. Skin biopsy samples were obtained from all SSc patients at initial diagnosis, prior to the initiation of any drug therapy. Four sex-matched and age-matched healthy subjects were selected as controls. Informed consent was obtained from each enrolled patient. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shantou University Medical College (Approval No. B-2023-105). After heating paraffin-embedded skin biopsy sections at 60 °C for 2 h, 4-μm-thick tissue sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated. Antigen retrieval was performed for 10 min at room temperature in enzymatic antigen retrieval solution (AR0026, Boster).

IHC was performed using an IHC kit (KIT-9710, MXB) and a DAB detection kit (DAB-0031, MXB) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Tissue slices were incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-P4HA3 (HPA007897, Sigma, 1:150) or TGF-β1 (21898-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:200). Tissue sections were counterstained with hematoxylin.

Slides for IF were blocked using 10 % goat serum (10 min). Tissue slices were incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-P4HA3 antibody (HPA007897, Sigma, 1:150) and mouse anti-SFRP2 antibody (66328-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:100), followed by staining with CoraLite®555 conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (RGAR003, Proteintech, 1:100) and CoraLite®647 conjugated goat anti-mouse secondary antibody (SA00014-10, Proteintech, 1:100). DAPI was used as a nuclear marker. Following IF imaging by confocal microscopy, the same tissue sections were subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining.

Murine SSc model

BALB/c female mice (6–8 weeks old) were obtained from Zhuhai Bestest Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Mice were randomly assigned to each group. Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (100 μl/day) was injected subcutaneously into mice in the control group once daily for 4 weeks. BLM (Nippon Kayaku) (1 mg/kg/day) was injected subcutaneously into mice in the other groups once daily for 4 weeks. LY294002 (T2008, TargetMol) (10 mg/kg/day), an inhibitor of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K), was injected intraperitoneally once daily for 4 weeks. PDC (P106867, Aladdin) (10 mg/kg/day), a C-P4Hs inhibitor, was injected intraperitoneally once daily for 4 weeks. The injected skin sites were punch-biopsied (6 mm) for hydroxyproline analysis and stored at −80 °C. Skin samples for Masson’s trichrome staining were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde. Surplus skin samples harvested for analyses of protein and mRNA were stored at −80 °C. The protocol was approved by The Medical Animal Care & Welfare Committee of Shantou University Medical College (Approval No. SUMC2023-258).

Cell culture

Human dermal fibroblasts were isolated from the skin of 3 healthy controls. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (D5194, Solarbio) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (40130ES76, Yeasen), 1 % penicillin/streptomycin (60162ES76, Yeasen). After 3–5 passages, the cells were starved for 24 h, followed by stimulation with 10 ng/ml TGF-β1 (100-21, PeproTech) for 24 h. To inhibit PI3K/AKT pathway or C-P4Hs activity, cells were pre-treated with 10 μM LY294002 or 7.2 μM PDC for 1 h prior to TGF-β1 stimulation [25]. Cells were harvested for analyses of protein and mRNA. The culture media were harvested for analyses of hydroxyproline.

Short interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection was performed to knock down P4HA3 in fibroblasts. The cells were transfected with a premix containing siRNA and GP-transfect-Mate (G04008, GenePharma). The medium was replaced with fresh medium after 48 h of transfection. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by real-time PCR. The siRNA sequences were: P4HA3 siRNA sense: 5′-GGACGUGUACAUGCUCAAUTT-3′; P4HA3 siRNA antisense: 5′-AUUGAGCAUGUACACGUCCTT-3′; negative control sense: 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′; negative control antisense: 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3′.

Western blot analysis

Mouse skins were homogenized and cultured cells were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (20101ES60, Yeasen). The proteins were quantified by BCA protein assay kit (23227, Thermo Fisher). The protein extracts were separated by 10 % SDS-PAGE and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride microporous membranes. After blocking with 5 % skimmed milk powder, the membranes were incubated with anti-P4HA3 (1:1000, 23185, Proteintech), anti-Collagen I (1:800, ab88147, Abcam), anti-GAPDH (1:5000, 5174, CST), anti-AKT (1:2000, 4691, CST), or anti-phospho-AKT (1:2000, 4060, Ser473, CST) overnight at 4 °C. The next day, membranes were incubated with the secondary antibody (1:10000, 7074 or 7076, CST) at room temperature for 1 h. Proteins were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (MA0186, Meilunbio). GAPDH was used as the housekeeping protein.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was isolated from cells and mouse skins using the TRNzol Universal reagent (DP424, Tiangen). The total RNA was reverse-transcribed with Hifair Ⅲ SuperMix (11141, Yeasen). Quantitative real-time PCR was then performed using Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (11201, Yeasen) on a StepOnePlus real-time PCR instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific). GAPDH was used as the housekeeping gene. Primer sequences for P4HA3 and GAPDH were: P4HA3-forward, 5′-AAGTGGAGTACCGCATCAGC-3′; P4HA3-reverse, 5′-TTGGTGACGTAGCATGGTCAA-3′; human GAPDH-forward, 5′-GACCACTTTGTCAAGCTCATTTC-3′; human GAPDH-reverse, 5′-CTCTCTTCCTCTTGTGCTCTTG-3′; mouse GAPDH-forward, 5′-AACTTTGGCATTGTGGAAGGGCTC-3′; mouse GAPDH-reverse, 5′-TGGAAGAGTGGGAGTTGCTGTTGA-3′.

Hydroxyproline assay

The hydroxyproline content of the skin tissue and culture medium was measured using a hydroxyproline content assay kit (BC0255, Solarbio) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using R version 4.4.2 and GraphPad Prism 10. Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was applied for comparisons between two groups. One-way ANOVA was applied for more than two groups. Correlation analysis between two groups was performed using the Pearson correlation method or Spearman rank correlation coefficient. P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

P4HA3 is the sole upregulated gene among collagen-modifying enzymes in SSc

One hundred seven DEGs were identified by comparing the gene expression of primary biopsy samples from 113 SSc patients and biopsy samples from 44 matched healthy controls. The expression levels of DEGs in limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis (lcSSc) patients fell between those of healthy controls (Ctrl) and diffuse cutaneous systemic sclerosis (dcSSc) patients (Fig. 1A). We constructed a PPI network to investigate the interaction of the DEGs. Collagen family genes, P4HA3 and IL6 were identified by cytoHubba (Fig. 1B). To investigate the expression of genes involved in collagen synthesis, the gene set (a set of gene names) for collagen biosynthesis and modifying enzyme genes was obtained from the Reactome database. We used a volcano map to show the expression of this gene set by comparing the gene expression of 113 SSc patients and 44 matched healthy controls. P4HA3 was the sole upregulated gene among collagen-modifying enzymes in SSc, rather than P4HA1 and P4HA2 (Fig. 1C). Moreover, correlation analysis revealed that the expression level of P4HA3 from all SSc biopsy samples was positively correlated with the mRSS (r = 0.664, P < 0.001, Fig. 1D). We performed GSEA for single gene to obtain P4HA3-related pathways. Multiple signaling pathways were enriched, including but not limited to TGF-β, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Kras, IL-6/JAK-STAT3, and IL-2/STAT5 signaling. (Fig. 1E). Numerous studies have demonstrated that TGF-β has emerged as a key cytokine of fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis in SSc [3,4,26], and PI3K is one of the components of the TGF-β non-SMAD-related pathway. The skin cell samples, obtained from 12 SSc patients and 10 healthy controls, comprised 50,496 cells after quality control. We determined 9 primary cell types through overlapping the cluster marker genes with canonical cell type-defining signatures (Fig. 2A and B). CD14+CD16− monocytes were predominantly observed in the SSc group on UMAP plots (Fig. 2D and E). P4HA3 is upregulated in SSc model mice skin

P4HA3 is the α subunit of C-P4Hs, which is the key enzymes in collagen biosynthesis [27]. To antagonize C-P4Hs, we injected pyridine 2,5-dicarboxylate (PDC, an inhibitor for C-P4Hs) intraperitoneally into BLM-treated mice. LY294002, a PI3K inhibitor, was injected intraperitoneally into BLM-treated mice to explore the relation between P4HA3 and PI3K/AKT signaling. Expression of P4HA3 was increased in the SSc model mice at both the mRNA and protein levels. Inhibition of C-P4Hs and PI3K alleviates skin fibrosis in BLM-treated mice

Masson’s trichrome staining revealed increased dermal thickness in BLM-treated mice compared to untreated controls. PDC and LY294002 partly alleviated fibrosis induced by BLM (Fig. 5E and F). Dermal collagen content was determined via hydroxyproline assay using 6-mm punch biopsies isolated from mouse injection sites. Hydroxyproline levels were significantly elevated in BLM-treated mice compared to controls, but this increase was attenuated by PDC or LY294002 treatment (Fig. 5G). Type I collagen is the predominant collagen in the skin. We found that its expression was significantly elevated in BLM-treated mice compared to controls. Notably, this increase was attenuated by treatment with either PDC or LY294002 (Fig. 5H and I).

TGF-β1 drives P4HA3 expression in human dermal fibroblasts to enhance collagen production

In order to understand the relationship between TGF-β1 and P4HA3 in vitro, human dermal fibroblasts were obtained from 3 healthy controls (Fig. 6A). Fibroblasts were pre-treated with LY294002 (10 μM) or PDC (7.2 μM) for 1 h prior to being stimulated by TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml). Unsurprisingly, P4HA3 was upregulated in fibroblasts at both the mRNA and protein levels after induction by TGF-β1. Blockade of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway by LY294002 inhibited TGF-β1-mediated induction of P4HA3 (Fig. 6B–D). Collagen content of culture medium was assessed by measuring hydroxyproline. After fibroblasts were treated with TGF-β1, the levels of hydroxyproline in the culture medium were elevated, and this elevation could be blocked by PDC or LY294002 (Fig. 6E). To further elucidate the role of P4HA3 in fibroblasts, we knocked down its expression using siRNA. Successful knockdown was confirmed 48 h after transfection (Fig. 6F). Discussion

In the present study, we found that P4HA3 may play an important role in the skin fibrosis progression of SSc by bioinformatics analysis. Immune cells secreted TGF-β1 to promote collagen synthesis through upregulation of P4HA3 expression (Fig. 7). P4HA3 is the α subunit of C-P4Hs that catalyzes the formation of 4-hydroxyproline residues in the endoplasmic reticulum, which can be blocked by PDC. 4-Hydroxyproline plays a critical role in stabilizing the collagen triple helix prior to procollagen secretion into the extracellular matrix. There is broad agreement that C-P4Hs are the key enzymes in the synthesis of collagen [9,14,25]. The expression level of P4HA3 mRNA in the human is far lower than that of P4HA1 and P4HA2 [11]. However, P4HA3 was the sole upregulated gene among collagen-modifying enzymes, rather than P4HA1 and P4HA2. Therefore, we speculated that P4HA3 might serve as a regulatory point for collagen synthesis in the pathological fibrosis of SSc.Fig. 7Immune cells secrete TGF-β1 to promote collagen synthesis. One of the pathological features of SSc skin is the deposition of extracellular matrix, together with activated fibroblasts/myofibroblasts and coarse collagen bundles [26,28]. The activation of fibroblasts by inflammatory profibrogenic cytokines, particularly TGF-β1, is regarded as the primary cause of excessive extracellular matrix accumulation [3,4]. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed that TGF-β1-positive cells in SSc skin displayed polymorphic morphologies, indicating TGF-β1 upregulation in various cell types. One study has shown that IL-10 induces pleural thickening in systemic lupus erythematosus by upregulating P4HA1 expression [29]. Separately, P4HA2 has been implicated in promoting hepatic ductular reaction and biliary fibrosis [9]. In contrast, our study demonstrates that TGF-β1 upregulates P4HA3 expression in SSc. Furthermore, the expression levels of P4HA1 and P4HA2 remain unaltered in SSc. This disparity suggests that the regulation of P4H isoforms may be disease-specific. It should be noted that the current scarcity of reports on P4HA3 in other dermatological conditions may pose a limitation to its specificity as a diagnostic indicator for SSc.

Like P4HA3, SFRP2 is a gene upregulated by TGF-β1 in human fibroblasts [30]. SFRP2high fibroblasts exhibited a small, elongated morphology, and were distributed between collagen bundles [31], which is similar to the morphology seen in P4HA3 staining. Additionally, we found that SFRP2high fibroblasts exhibited higher activity in collagen and extracellular matrix synthesis compared with other fibroblast types. Another study has indicated SFRP2high fibroblasts’ potential to differentiate into myofibroblasts in SSc [32].

Human monocytes are classified into classical (CD14+CD16−), intermediate (CD14+CD16+), and nonclassical (CD14−CD16+) subsets based on surface marker expression [33]. The study identified CD14+CD16− monocytes as exhibiting the highest TGF-β1 expression levels among immune cells in SSc. Differentiation of CD14+CD16− monocytes into monocyte-derived macrophages and monocyte-derived dendritic cells (mo-DCs) constitutes a pivotal mechanism governing tissue inflammation dynamics [34]. Pseudotime trajectory analysis positioned CD14+CD16− monocytes proximal to type 2 conventional dendritic cells in SSc, indicating their differentiation into mo-DCs [16].

PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is one of the central signaling pathways that regulate cell growth, motility, proliferation, metabolism and survival. Studies have suggested an association between the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and fibrotic diseases [[35], [36], [37]]. Moon et al. used bioinformatic methods to suggest that activity of the PI3K-AKT pathway in SSc correlates most closely with the mRSS and shifts in parallel with changes in the mRSS [37]. The results of the present study demonstrate that inhibition of the PI3K/AKT pathway by LY294002 ameliorates experimental fibrosis of SSc both in vivo and in vitro. It is worth noting that other signaling pathways, such as TGF-β/Smad signaling and IL-6/JAK signaling, have also attracted considerable attention in the pathogenesis of SSc. Furthermore, JAK inhibitors have been shown to inhibit fibrosis in SSc [38]. The relationship between P4HA3 and other signaling pathways remains to be elucidated. Another limitation of this study is that the generalizability of the fibroblast findings is constrained by the limited number of healthy donors.

SSc is a complex connective tissue disease resulting in progressive fibrosis of multiple organs. The organ systems most frequently include the skin, digestive system, and lungs [1]. Approximately 90 % of SSc patients develop esophageal involvement in systemic sclerosis [39]. Hu et al. suggested that P4HA3 is part of the network that regulates metastasis of gastric cancer by modulating extracellular matrix remodeling and vascular invasion [40]. P4HA3 was also over-expressed in colon cancer [41,42]. It is unclear whether P4HA3 is highly expressed in organs other than the skin in SSc, which remains to be investigated in the future.

The use of PDC is a limitation of this study. PDC compound inhibits all C-P4H isoforms and some enzymes, rather than selectively targeting P4HA3. Pathological hallmarks of SSc skin include excessive extracellular matrix deposition and the presence of activated fibroblasts/myofibroblasts. However, the proportion of fibroblasts within the lesional skin does not increase significantly [43]. Thus, while strategies aimed at depleting P4HA3 to suppress proliferation may be effective in targeting tumor cells [44], fibroblast hyperproliferation is not a dominant pathological driver in SSc. HIF-P4Hs control cellular responses to hypoxia through regulation of HIF. Vascular occlusion leads to hypoxia in SSc skin tissues, and direct inhibition of P4HA3 may disrupt cellular hypoxic responses. Selective inhibition of C-P4Hs can avoid the side effects associated with HIF-P4H suppression. Targeting C-P4Hs may offer a superior therapeutic strategy for SSc.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study used bioinformatics to identify a target for SSc antifibrotic therapeutics. P4HA3 was upregulated by TGF-β1 secreted from immune cells to promote collagen synthesis. Inhibition of C-P4Hs is a potential therapeutic approach for SSc fibrosis.

A comparative observational study on the effect of two different doses of fentanyl on the effect site concentration of propofol during induction of anesthesia using clinical assessment score and bispectral index
Authors: Anu David, Lini Chacko, Binu P. Simon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and Aims:Opioids are known to reduce the propofol dose required to blunt movement and hemodynamic responses to noxious stimuli. However, the optimal propofol dosage for smooth induction remains unclear, particularly in the Indian population where pharmacokinetic data is limited. This study aims to evaluate the effect site concentration of propofol in conjunction with varying fentanyl doses, with relation to loss of consciousness and bispectral index (BIS) values during anesthesia induction using target-controlled infusion (TCI).Material and Methods:Sixty patients aged 20–50 years, with American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I and II, scheduled for elective surgeries under general anesthesia were included. Anesthesia induction was performed using a TCI pump with propofol based on the Schnider model, starting at 1.5 µg/ml and increasing at 0.5 µg/ml until loss of verbal response, followed by 0.2 µg/ml till the final clinical endpoint was reached. Patients received either 1 or 2 µg/kg fentanyl or no fentanyl. Endpoints were assessed using the BIS monitor and Modified Observer Assessment of Alertness/Sedation Scale score.Results:Hemodynamic parameters were stable across groups. The effect site concentration of propofol for loss of response to noxious stimuli was significantly lower in the fentanyl groups (fentanyl 1 µg/kg- 2.90 ± 0.1947 µg/ml, fentanyl 2 µg/kg- 2.445 ± 0.23 µg/ml) compared to the propofol alone group (4.205 ± 0.909 µg/ml) (P = 0.0001). Similar results were observed for loss of response to verbal commands. The effect site concentration at BIS 60 corresponded to loss of response to noxious stimuli.Conclusions:Propofol effect site concentrations were significantly higher without fentanyl. The findings contribute valuable data for refining pharmacokinetic models tailored to individual patient needs.

Introduction

Propofol is an intravenous (IV) sedative hypnotic agent used for the induction and maintenance of anesthesia. Physiologic characteristics that determine the appropriate dose to induce anesthesia are age, lean body mass, and central blood volume. Since the estimation of plasma propofol concentration is resource-intensive and time-consuming, the anesthesiologist often substitutes predicted concentration of propofol in the blood and at the effect site derived from established pharmacokinetic models to titrate administration of propofol.[1] Target controlled infusion (TCI) represents a technique of IV anesthetic drug administration where predictable concentration of the drug is targeted in a specific body compartment. This is achieved with an initial loading dose of propofol based on the central volume of distribution and then an exponential decrease in propofol infusion based on the volume of distribution of each peripheral compartment and the rate constants connecting those compartments.

Effect site targeting in TCI pumps uses a first-order rate constant, keo, in describing the rate of plasma and effect site (brain) equilibration. keo defines the proportional change of concentration gradient between plasma and effect site in unit time.[2] When the effect site target concentration is increased, a bolus of drug is administered, raising the plasma concentration higher than the effect site target, to hasten the increase in effect site concentration.[3] The Schnider model includes age, gender, total body weight, and height as covariates and is used in effect site targeting mode.[3] The models derived from one set of the population may not apply to a population of different ethnicity as well as body structure, and there is no universally acceptable optimal pharmacokinetic model for propofol infusion yet.[4] The measured blood concentrations might vary from model-predicted values. We need to evaluate such models in the individual setups to confirm their pertinence. In spite of India having world’s second largest population, there is very limited Indian pharmacokinetic data of anesthetic drugs. So far, the studies conducted in India and other countries have shown that Indians are more sensitive to propofol.[56]

Ethnicity can be a major factor in the pharmacokinetics and dynamics of drugs including sedatives.[7] Opioids are known to reduce the propofol dose required to produce loss of consciousness and blunt hemodynamic responses to painful stimuli.[3] In the presence of fentanyl, loss of consciousness occurred at a lower effect site concentration of propofol and at a higher bispectral index (BIS) value than with propofol alone.[8] Surprisingly, we have limited data on the decrease in propofol induction dose or Ce at various fentanyl concentrations. In this study, we aim to quantify the difference in Ce of propofol with various fentanyl doses.

Prudent use of analgesia with opioids can have a propofol-sparing effect[9] and potentially minimizes the suppression of brain electrical activity than when propofol is used alone.[10] With this background, the study is performed to titrate propofol doses individually and maintain hemodynamic stability. Our hypothesis was that fentanyl can significantly decrease the effect site concentration of propofol during induction of anesthesia.

In this study, we aim to find out the effect site concentration of propofol with various concentrations of fentanyl among Indian population with relation to loss of consciousness and BIS values during induction of anesthesia. The objectives are to determine the effect site concentration of propofol required to abolish the response to verbal commands and noxious stimulus when used alone compared to a bolus dose of fentanyl given before propofol administration. Our secondary objective is to determine the relation between the effect site concentration of propofol and the objective endpoint of sedation, that is, BIS of 60, during propofol anesthesia with or without fentanyl.

Material and Methods

The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee (30/IEC/19/AIMS -20; 11-20-2019) and registered with the clinical trial registry 2022. This was a prospective cohort observational study that was conducted in a tertiary health-care facility for a period of 2 months from December 2021 to February 2022. Sixty patients of Indian origin aged between 20 and 50 years with American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I and II scheduled for elective surgery under general anesthesia were included in the study. The study protocol was explained to patients, and informed written consent was obtained from them. Patients allergic to propofol and fentanyl, patients with a history of diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, or hypertension, patients on psychotropic drugs, pregnant women, deaf and dumb patients, patients with any known neurologic disorder, those with difficult intubation, and females with a body mass index (BMI) >35 and males with BMI more than 40 were excluded from the study.

In the operating room, baseline values of noninvasive arterial pressure, heart rate, and peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2) using pulse oximetry were recorded and continuously measured throughout the study period. A suitable large-bore IV line was secured, and slow IV drip with Ringer’s lactate was started. BIS was monitored via a four-electrode sensor (BISXP Quatro; Covidien LLC, Mansfield, MA, USA), which was placed on the forehead. Commercial infusion pump (Alaris™ PK, BD Switzerland Sàrl, Switzerland, Romania)[11] was used for propofol infusion. TCI was programmed with Schnider effect site[12] concentration model infusion (TCI). The infusion pump was attached to the peripheral line using a three-way cannula and primed with 1% propofol. Fentanyl was made ready in 10 ml syringe at a dose of 10 µg/ml.

The anesthesiologist unaware of the study outcome prepared the infusion and programmed and operated the TCI pump. After insertion of a peripheral venous line, the following measurements were recorded: arterial pressure, heart rate, SpO2, BIS,[9] and sedation score (Modified Observer Assessment of Alertness/Sedation Scale [MOAA/S]), which reduced the observer bias[13] [Table 1]. Consecutive sampling method was used. Patients who had received fentanyl 1 µg/kg + propofol were enrolled in the group and those who had received fentanyl 2 µg/kg + propofol were enrolled in the FA group. Those patients who received propofol alone were enrolled in the P group. Five minutes before commencing propofol infusion, fentanyl was administered. The propofol pump was initially programmed with an effect site concentration target of 1.5 µg/ml, which continuously displays the predicted effect site concentration. The investigator assessed the patient’s response after the target concentration was reached. When the effect site concentration of propofol reached the target, the investigator assessed the response to “say your name” and “open your eyes” using a constant force and tone until the subject responded appropriately. Those who did not respond to any verbal command and responded only after mild prodding or shaking were considered to be unconscious (MOAA/S score- 2). The values were recorded simultaneously by another person unaware of the study outcome.

If the patient responded appropriately, the propofol effect site target concentration was then increased by 0.5 µg/ml. Once the effect site concentration reached the target, the patient’s response to the above commands were reassessed. If the patient was still responsive, this process was repeated until the patient reached our first endpoint of loss of response to verbal stimuli (MOAA/S score- 2). Loss of response to a painful stimulus (e.g., very firm pressure on the angle of the mandible)[3] (MOAA/S score- 0) was then as sessed by increasing the target site concentration by 0.2 µg/ml from the value of loss of response to verbal stimulus. The propofol effect site concentration at which 1) loss of response to verbal stimulus (MOAA/S score- 2), 2) loss of response to noxious stimulus (MOAA/S score- 0), and 3) BIS at 60[14] persisted for 1 or 2 min was recorded. Vital signs – heart rate, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and SpO2 – were recorded 1 min after induction. 100% oxygen was given via a facemask simultaneously. The values at the desired study endpoints from the TCI pump were recorded simultaneously by another person unaware of the study outcome. After the desired study endpoints were achieved, the drug doses were adjusted and further drugs were administered, as required, to secure the airway and maintain anesthesia for surgery. Ventilation and oxygenation were maintained throughout the study using a facemask, 100% oxygen, and manual ventilation if required.

Based on a previous study,[13] the sample size was calculated as 60, from the standard deviation and by assuming the power as 80%, alpha error as 5%. (20 in each group). The data was entered into Excel worksheet, and analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 23.0. The results on continuous measurements were presented as mean ± SD, and the results on categorical measurements were presented as number (%). Significance was assessed at 5% level. Normality of data was tested. For normally distributed variables, the difference in BIS value and MOAA/S score in three groups was analyzed by unpaired “t”-test. For non-normally distributed variables, Mann–Whitney test was used. P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Interestingly, the time to reach the clinical endpoints of loss of response to verbal command and painful stimuli was not statistically significant among fentanyl groups [Table 3] and was significant with propofol alone with fentanyl group [Tables 4 and 5], despite the fact that the effect site concentrations and propofol dosages differed significantly across the groups.

Discussion

We have ample evidence on the apparent propofol-“sparing” effect of opioids in reducing response to stimulus,[910] a well-established concept. We conducted this study to examine the above fact and to determine the effect site concentration and dose of propofol at induction with and without fentanyl, especially in the Indian population.

An objective endpoint like BIS was included in this study, which appears to work poorly in older adults (>60) as this cohort tends to have lower amplitude oscillations, which the BIS algorithm can interpret as an awake state or state of unconsciousness.[14] Similarly, the BIS algorithm inaccurately reflects the children’s anesthetic state because they generally have much more power across a broader range of frequency bands when appropriately anesthetized compared to adults in the age groups of 18–59 years.[1516]

Patients with diabetes, coronary disease, and hypertension were excluded to avoid confounding factors. Autonomic neuropathy predisposes to hypotension.[17] As this study involved assessing loss of response to verbal commands, deaf and dumb patients were excluded. Hemodynamic parameters did not change significantly throughout the study [Tables 6,7 and Figures 2,3], which reflects the beneficial effect of slow incremental propofol administration with TCI pump during induction, whereby a rapid overshoot in blood concentration which happens with manual bolus dosing and intersubject drug response variability were avoided.[18] Irwin et al.,[19] in their study during induction of anesthesia with IV propofol delivered by TCI, have observed similar stable hemodynamics. All the three groups had received propofol infusion through the TCI pump, which allows continuous effect site measurement advantage of titration to the target effect site. Doyle et al.[20] described cardiovascular stability and rapid emergence associated with remifentanil infusions.

We used the Schnider model in effect-site targeting mode, with a keo with a keo of 0.456 min-1. In Schnider model, the lean body mass is calculated using the James formula,[21] which performs satisfactorily in normal and moderately obese patients, but paradoxically in severely obese patients, that is, with increasing total body weight, the calculated LBM (Lean Body Mass) decreases paradoxically. A similar finding was observed in females when BMI is approximately 37kg/m2.[22] So, we selected BMI <35 for females and <40 for males. A major benefit of the Schnider model is that it adjusts doses and infusion rates according to patient age. This provides a strong argument for using the Schnider model in elderly and unwell patients, in whom smaller bolus doses will be given after target increases, and this may improve hemodynamic stability and safety.

At the clinical endpoint of loss of response to noxious stimulus, the effect site concentration of propofol in propofol alone group was 4.205 µg/ml (95% CI [3.77–4.63]), which was significantly higher than the fentanyl groups, where with fentanyl 1 µg/kg, the Ce of propofol was 2.9 µg/ml (95% CI [2.809–2.991]) and with fentanyl 2 µg/kg, the Ce of propofol was 2.445 µg/ml (95% CI [2.33–2.55]).[Figure 4] The mean time (in min) from the start of induction to the clinical endpoints was significantly less in fentanyl groups when compared to the propofol alone group. This suggests that the addition of fentanyl allows for a reduction in propofol requirements without compromising the time to clinical endpoints.

The recommended Ce of propofol for maintaining adequate depth of anesthesia when combined with opioid varies from 2.5 to 8 µg/ml.[6] However, the various values observed using the model were lower than the recommended dose, which could be due to ethnic variations.[6] In propofol alone group, the loss of response to verbal command was 3.425 ± 0.877 µg/ml and to loss of response to pain was 4.205 ± 0.909 µg/ml, which corresponds to the values from the previous Indian studies[56] and is lower than the propofol concentrations in Chinese population (4.4 µg/ml)[19] and in Caucasian[23] population (5.2 µg/ml).

The incorporation of BIS at BIS 60 has impacted objectivity to loss of consciousness. A patient is appropriately anesthetized (i.e., unconscious) when the value of BIS is between 40 and 60.[24] Loss of response to verbal command denoted unconsciousness and loss of response to painful stimulus indicated anesthesia. In our study, the mean propofol concentration at BIS 60 in the propofol alone group was 3.425 µg/ml at loss of response to verbal commands. Before induction, there is a paradoxical excitation in brain activity, referred to as drug’s biphasic effect.[25] This could explain why higher effect site concentrations of propofol are required at loss of consciousness as more doses of propofol are required to overcome the biphasic effect and to act on cortical inhibitory neurons.

In case of propofol, peak effect concentration is obtained after about 4 min of IV administration, in which 80% of final target concentration is achieved in effect site within about 6 min.[26] In our study, the propofol concentration was started at 1.5 µg/ml as awakening usually occurs at concentration levels less than 1.5 µg/ml and then increased in small increments, allowing some time for equilibration and to minimize the discrepancy between blood and the effect site. So, the time interval to achieve equilibration between target concentration and its effect was kept as 5 min till loss of response to verbal commands and 2 min thereafter till loss of response to noxious stimuli, to allow careful titration of the propofol concentration to the desired endpoint.

The effect site concentration and propofol doses administered during induction of anesthesia were significantly different between each pair of group (Student’s t-test- P value 0.0001). The mean Ce of propofol at loss of response to verbal command was 2.075 ± 0.183 and at loss of response to noxious stimuli was 2.445 ± 0.23 in the FB group, which was much lower than the other two groups. In all three groups, it was observed that the effect site concentration of propofol at BIS 60 matched the concentration attained at loss of response to a painful stimulus. The addition of opioid analgesic ablates the autonomic responses and reduces the concentration of propofol required to produce anesthesia.[19] Kazama et al.,[27] based on the results of their study, showed evidence to suggest that both hypnosis and analgesia are important components of surgical anesthesia. The unconsciousness produced by propofol results in arousal or awakening as a result of noxious stimuli if the stimulus is not inhibited from reaching higher centers. Opioids act at spinal cord receptors to attain this.[28] Scott et al.[10] found in their study that remifentanil markedly reduced the amount of propofol required to obtund response to pain [Figure 5].

Few shortcomings of our study were as follows. Firstly, we could not include obese patients as Schnider pharmacokinetic models did not include severely obese patients. The observer could have been blinded using a placebo saline solution in the propofol alone group, but being purely an observational study and not a randomized study could be a constraint. We had used predicted effect site concentration, which is a virtual compartment., Without concurrent measurements of plasma drug levels, accuracy will always be a concern. Use of tetanic electrical stimulation to a fixed current could have been more objective than mandibular pressure, and hence could have reduced bias. The sample population size was significant, but still small, and more patients could have been included.

Methods to enable precise and individualized dosage estimation and titration are in high demand. Overdosing might be associated with hemodynamic compromise and prolonged recovery. Underdosing is particularly undesirable since it can result in unintended awareness during general anesthesia, with severe psychologic consequences.[29] The data may be used as samples for developing signal processing algorithms and would be better suited for a training dataset for machine learning and prediction of pharmacokinetic models suited as per individual requirements.

Conclusions

It may be reasonable to conclude from the study that the effect site concentration of propofol was significantly high in propofol alone group compared to fentanyl groups at the desired clinical endpoints, implying that fentanyl significantly reduces the target controlled propofol requirements at the induction of anesthesia. The effect site concentration of propofol at BIS 60 corresponds to loss of response to painful stimuli in all three groups. The effect site concentration of propofol at BIS 60 corresponds to loss of response to painful stimulus, which indicates the adequate depth of anesthesia. The effect site concentration of propofol was lower in our population, as demonstrated in previous Indian studies. Induction using TCI pump provides a stable hemodynamics and reduces the side effect of propofol, which is proven to be safe and effective.

The effect site concentrations of propofol at loss of response to verbal stimulus and noxious stimulus were much lower in patients with fentanyl 2 µg/kg compared to the other two groups.

It was found that in all three groups, the effect site concentration of propofol at BIS 60 corresponded to the concentration attained at loss of response to noxious stimulus.

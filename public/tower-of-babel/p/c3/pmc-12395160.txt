Mediating effect of resilience on untreated dental caries among adults in Hyderabad, India: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Jagadeeswara Rao Sukhabhogi, Dolar Doshi, Pothulapally Priyanka, Ellora Reddy Thimmanagari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundResilience, a psychological trait associated with adaptability and stress management, has been increasingly recognized as a determinant of health outcomes. However, its role in oral health, particularly concerning untreated dental caries, remains under-explored. This cross-sectional study examines the mediating effect of resilience on untreated dental caries among adults visiting a Government Dental Hospital in Hyderabad.Materials and methodA cross-sectional study was conducted among adult patients attending a government dental hospital. Resilience was assessed using the Adult Resilience Measure – Revised (ARM-R), while dental caries was evaluated using the WHO Oral Health Assessment Form. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was employed to analyze the mediating effects of sociodemographic factors and oral health behaviors in the relationship between resilience and untreated dental caries.ResultsA significant negative correlation was observed between resilience and untreated dental caries (p = 0.001), indicating that individuals with lower resilience levels had 12 times higher odds of developing dental caries. Employment status emerged as a key mediating factor (∗∗β = 0.30, p = ∗∗∗), with higher resilience levels being associated with better employment outcomes, which in turn influences access to dental care. Additionally, increased brushing frequency was identified as another mediator (β = 0.08, p = 0.02), suggesting that individuals with greater resilience are more likely to engage in positive oral health behaviors.ConclusionThe findings highlight the protective role of resilience in oral health by influencing employment status and oral hygiene practices, thereby reducing the risk of untreated dental caries. These results underscore the need for integrating psycho-social interventions aimed at enhancing resilience in oral health promotion programs.

Introduction

Dental Caries is a result of several interplaying factors such as biological, behavioural, psychosocial, and environmental factors.1 Psychosocial factor is defined as the interrelation between social factors and individuals mind in influencing behaviours, health, and wellbeing. These factors may have their independent effect on health outcomes or may attenuate the relationship between a risk factor and health.2 In addition to having an impact on the health of a person, they have also been shown to improve health-promoting behaviour like toothbrushing and self-care-related dental visits.3 Recent systematic reviews suggests that psychosocial factors are related to periodontal disease, burning mouth and health-related behaviours.3,4 Psychosocial factors are also identified as potential determinants of oral health behaviours in children and adolescents in previous studies.5,6

Resilience being one of the psychosocial factors is described as innate ability of an individual to respond positively to stress and adversity, adapt to difficult circumstances, and maintain positive mental well-being.7,8 In context of oral health, resilience is a critical factor as to how people deal with obstacles connected to dental health, uphold good oral hygiene habits, and seek the right dental care. Studies have suggested that higher resilience is associated with better coping strategies, which may lead to more consistent oral health practices and a greater likelihood of seeking routine dental care.7,9 Additionally, greater resilience may protect against stress-related behaviors such as excessive sugar consumption or neglect of oral hygiene—factors known to increase the risk of dental caries.2,4

It is still unclear whether resilience directly affects untreated dental caries or indirectly through influencing important mediators like oral hygiene habits and socioeconomic position. Therefore, the present study aimed to examine both the direct and indirect effects of resilience on untreated dental caries among adults aged 18 years and above, by considering mediating pathways through sociodemographic and behavioral factors. Considering the emerging nature of this topic and the limited availability of prior theoretical models in adult populations, an exploratory structural equation modelling (SEM) approach was used to identify and test plausible pathways.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a cross-sectional study conducted to assess the mediating effect of resilience on untreated dental caries in adults.

Study setting

The study was conducted at the Government Dental College and Hospital, Hyderabad, and the data collection was carried out for four months, from January 2024 to April 2024, during which eligible patients attending the outpatient department were recruited for participation.

Participants

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

The present study included adults aged 18 years and above, who were proficient in english and willing to provide informed consent.

Exclusion criteria

Individuals with systemic illnesses affecting oral health (e.g., diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular diseases), with cognitive impairments or communication difficulties and unwilling to participate in the study were excluded.

Sampling technique and recruitment

A systematic random sampling method was used wherein every third eligible patient visiting the outpatient department was invited to participate in the study. The recruitment was carried out by principal investigator. Prior to enrolment, participants were provided with detailed study information and were required to sign a written informed consent form.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated using the formula for a single population proportion:n=(Z2×p×(1−p))/d2Where:

Z = 1.96 (standard normal deviate at 95 % confidence).

p = expected prevalence of untreated dental caries = 0.28

d = precision (0.05).

n = ((1.96)2 × 0.28 × 0.72)/(0.05)2 ≈ 310.

The expected prevalence (28 %) was based on findings from Eid SA et al.,1 who assessed the prevalence of untreated dental caries among adolescents in Egypt.

Data collection tools and measures

Resilience was assessed using the Adult Resilience Measure – Revised (ARM-R),10 developed by the Resilience Research Centre (RRC). This validated instrument comprises 17 items rated on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“Not at all”) to 5 (“A lot”), with total scores ranging from 17 to 85. Higher scores indicate greater resilience. The questionnaire was provided in English.

The RRC manual was followed in contextualising the ARM-R tool. It was examined for cultural relevance by a group of public health dentists and a clinical psychologist. Clarity and appropriateness were evaluated in a pilot study involving 30 participants. With a Cronbach's alpha of 0.86, high reliability was indicated. Mean substitution was used for up to two missing items, and participants with more than two missing responses were eliminated as per ARM-R guidelines.

Dental caries was assessed through a clinical oral examination using the WHO Oral Health Assessment Form for Adults (2013).11 The examination was carried out by a single trained and calibrated examiner. The armamentarium used for the examination included a ball-ended CPI probe, a mouth mirror, sterile gauze, disposable gloves, and either a headlamp or natural light depending on availability.

The study employed a structured, pre-tested, self-administered English questionnaire to gather sociodemographic and behavioural data. Age, gender, marital status, education, work, frequency of brushing, tobacco use, and history of dental visits were among the variables. Based on sample distribution, age was divided into four groups (≤20, 21–30, 31–40, and ≥41 years) to ensure balanced representation. Resilience scores obtained from the ARM-R were categorized into low, moderate, and high levels using the ±1 standard deviation rule from the mean score.

Statistical analysis

Data were entered and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0. Descriptive statistics included frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, and means with standard deviations for continuous variables. Chi-square tests assessed associations between categorical variables. To determine the independent effect of sociodemographic variables and resilience on the presence of untreated dental caries, multiple logistic regression analysis was carried out separately (i.e., independent of the SEM model). Spearman's rank correlation was used to assess associations between resilience, caries, and other variables due to the non-parametric nature of some data (such as resilience scores and brushing frequency). Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) was conducted using IBM SPSS AMOS version 26.0 to explore direct and indirect pathways between resilience, employment, brushing frequency, and untreated dental caries.

The model fit was assessed using standard indices:

Root Mean Square Residual (RMR): Acceptable if < 0.08.

Goodness-of-Fit Index (GFI): Acceptable if > 0.90.

Incremental Fit Index (IFI): Acceptable if > 0.90.

Normed Chi-square (NC/χ2 or NCP): Acceptable if < 3.

FMIN: Lower values indicate better fit.

A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Ethical and reporting standards

This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Osmania Medical College (IEC-BHR/OMC/M.NO(05)/P-60). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.12 The reporting followed the STROBE guidelines for cross-sectional studies.13

Results

More than half of them were females (53.23 %) and almost three-fourths of them were married (77.74 %). Most of the subjects completed their graduation (42.90 %), and nearly half were employed (56.13). Vast majority of the subjects had not consumed any form of tobacco (80 %) and brushed their teeth once daily (78.06 %). Furthermore, almost half of the subjects visited the dentist (53.13 %). In this study, the majority of subjects had moderate resilience (62.90 %); whereas comparable percentages of participants had low and high resilience (18.9 % and 18.17 % respectively). Discussion

Traditionally, research on dental caries has predominantly centred on biological risk factors, however, as the understanding of this global health concern continues to advance, there is an increasing recognition of the need to explore a broader range of determinants and mediators beyond these conventional factors. Emerging evidence suggests that psychosocial factors play a crucial role in shaping oral health outcomes.14,15 One such factor that needs further exploration is resilience, often defined as the ability of an individual to maintain healthy psychological functioning during exposure to stressful life events.16 In simpler terms, resilience reflects a person's capacity to recover and adapt in the face of adversity. While resilience has been widely recognized for its positive impact on overall health and health-promoting behaviours,17,18 its implications of the role of this psychosocial factor in the context of dental caries remain underexplored. Therefore, the present study aimed to contribute to the literature by investigating the effect of resilience on untreated dental caries among adults visiting the Government Dental Hospital in Hyderabad.

Resilience was measured using Adult Resilience Measure – Revised version (ARM-R) which has been found to have good validity and reliability.19 It captures both internal strengths (e.g., coping skills, self-efficacy) and external supports (e.g., social relationships, community resources) that contribute to resilience. Additionally, its shortened format allows for more efficient data collection, reducing the respondent burden. WHO Oral Health Assessment Form for adults11 was used to record the data on dental caries status. The structured framework allows for uniform data collection and facilitates both national and international comparisons.

The results of this study showed a significant negative correlation between resilience and dental caries while identifying various mediators influencing this relationship. This relationship was further substantiated through path analysis, which reinforced the negative association between resilience and dental caries (∗∗β = −0.25, p = ∗∗∗). Similar findings were reported by Matsuyama Y et al.20 who demonstrated that children with higher resilience levels tended to have lower incidences of dental caries. This association may be attributed to the mediating effect of higher resilience on positive oral health behaviours, as evidenced by the increased brushing frequency observed in individuals with higher resilience (β = 0.08; p = 0.02).

Further supporting this, research by Tsironis C et al.9 highlighted the significant role of resilience in enhancing the oral health-related quality of life among older adults. Individuals with greater resilience were found to be more proficient in managing their oral health needs, maintaining a heightened sense of agency, and exerting greater control over their circumstances. These findings underscore the potential impact of resilience not only in promoting oral health behaviors but also in fostering better overall dental outcomes.

Another factor explaining the positive association between resilience and dental caries was found to be employment status. Individuals with higher resilience levels were found to have better employment status compared to their counterparts, as demonstrated by the Structural Equation Model (beta estimate = 0.30, p = ∗∗∗). Employment status plays a pivotal role in shaping access to healthcare resources, financial stability, and overall well-being, all of which have a direct impact on oral health outcomes. This is consistent with the findings from the study by Li A et al.21 who found a statistically significant association between low SES and poor oral health. The effect of SES on oral health encompasses factors such as dental health utilisation, health diffusion and psychological stress. Additionally, a well-structured work environment may support healthier lifestyle choices, such as maintaining good oral hygiene practices, accessing dental education programs, and reducing stress-related behaviours like smoking or poor dietary habits.22

The present study elucidates both the direct and indirect effects of resilience on untreated dental caries, highlighting its multifaceted role in psychological adaptability, behavioural regulation, and overall oral health outcomes. Individuals exhibiting higher resilience demonstrate an enhanced capacity to recover from stressors, facilitating greater psychological adjustment and stability. This adaptive advantage may contribute to improved socioeconomic positioning, including better access to education and employment opportunities. Consequently, these factors foster health-promoting behaviours, such as adherence to optimal oral hygiene practices, ultimately resulting in better oral health outcomes. This suggests that resilience serves as a critical psychosocial determinant influencing oral health through complex, interdependent pathways.

Several inconsistencies emerged that merit consideration despite the model's overall fit and coherence. Contrary to previous research, there was a negative correlation between education and dental visits. This could be because highly educated people postponed visits due to hectic schedules or sought private care outside of the study environment. There was also a negative correlation between tobacco use and untreated dental caries, which could be the result of underreporting brought on by social desirability bias or confounding variables like oral hygiene or diet. Furthermore, the inverse relationship between age and education most likely reflects disparities in educational access between generations.

Strengths of the study

The strengths of the present study include the use of validated tools, namely the Adult Resilience Measure–Revised (ARM-R) for assessing resilience and the WHO Oral Health Assessment Form for evaluating dental caries. Additionally, a robust statistical path analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between resilience and dental caries.

Limitations of the study

Nevertheless, despite the standard methodology employed, certain limitations of this study warrant consideration. Firstly, the use of cross-sectional data precludes the establishment of clear temporal relationships between variables. Secondly, given the self-reported nature of the resilience questionnaire, some degree of social desirability bias is anticipated. Poor oral health itself may contribute to psychological stress and lower resilience, potentially resulting in a bidirectional relationship that is challenging to disentangle within the framework of an observational study. Generalisability may be limited by the exploratory application of SEM in the absence of a predetermined theoretical model. Although the novelty of the field of study justifies this deviation, SEM is typically confirmatory, so it should be interpreted with caution. Hence, further longitudinal studies are required to elucidate the complex causal pathways between these variables.

Conclusion

The findings of this study highlight the protective role of resilience in oral health, demonstrating both direct and indirect effects on untreated dental caries. Resilience was found to positively influence employment status and oral hygiene behaviours, which in turn contributed to reduced caries prevalence.

Proposed model for future validation

The results of this exploratory SEM analysis lead us to suggest a conceptual model in which tobacco use, education, and employement status have an indirect impact on untreated dental caries by influencing resilience and oral hygiene practices like brushing frequency. Future research employing confirmatory SEM techniques to validate these pathways in various populations can build upon this model.

5-Year Follow-Up After Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement Using the Cardiovalve System
Authors: Rimantas Benetis, Matteo Saccocci, Egle Rumbinaite, Vaida Mizariene, Ramunas Unikas, Antanas Jankauskas, Michel Zuber, Francesco Maisano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSevere mitral regurgitation (MR) in high-risk patients often precludes surgical repair, and suboptimal anatomy may limit edge-to-edge repair. Transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) has emerged as an alternative; however, long-term durability data remain limited.First in Human/Early Reports SummaryWe report a 75-year-old man with ischemic MR (Carpentier IIIb), treated with transfemoral TMVR using the Cardiovalve device within the Cardiovalve FIM study (NCT03958773). The procedure was successful, with no MR postprocedural and only minor periprocedural complications.DiscussionAt 5-year follow-up, the patient remained clinically stable (NYHA functional class I) with preserved valve function, and absence of degeneration, thrombosis, or left ventricular outflow tract obstruction. This represents the longest reported follow-up of Cardiovalve to date and suggests sustained clinical and hemodynamic benefit.NoveltyThis is the first report of a 5-year follow-up after transfemoral TMVR with Cardiovalve, demonstrating excellent durability and safety.Take-Home MessageCardiovalve TMVR offers a promising long-term option for high-risk MR patients with sustained clinical and echocardiographic success over 5 years.

History of Presentation

A 75-year-old man with a history of chronic severe mitral regurgitation (MR) presented to our hospital for further diagnostic work-up. At admission, the patient was suffering from effort dyspnea (NYHA functional class III), which had progressively worsened in the past few months. During physical examination, a 3/6 systolic murmur was documented, with mild peripheral edema. A bradycardic and arrhythmic pulse was appreciated (60 beats/min), compatible with his history of known long-standing atrial fibrillation. Heart failure therapy (according to guidelines and at maximal tolerated dosage) included the following: metoprolol 50 mg/d, perindopril 2.5 mg/d, and spironolactone 25 mg/d. Moreover, diuretic therapy with torasemide 10 mg/d was present.

Laboratory tests at admission revealed a moderate renal insufficiency, with serum creatinine of 1.5 mg/dL.

Past Medical History

The patient had a history of chronic congestive heart failure and ischemic cardiomyopathy. In 2009, the patient underwent percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty + stent of the left anterior descending artery due to an acute non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction, which required repeated revascularization 2 years later for an intrastent restenosis. The remaining vessels showed 50% stenosis of the mid portion of the right coronary artery and a chronic occlusion of the distal circumflex artery, without a technical option for further revascularization. In 2011, a permanent DDDR pacemaker was implanted.

Other comorbidities included arterial hypertension, recurrent upper gastrointestinal bleeding secondary to peptic erosions, and previous right nephrectomy due to kidney cancer, with consequent chronic renal insufficiency.

Imaging Baseline

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) and transesophageal echocardiography confirmed the diagnosis of severe secondary MR, with asymmetrical tethering due to ischemic left ventricular remodeling (Carpentier IIIb type) and moderate to severe left ventricular systolic dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction: 30%). Management

Considering his age and several comorbidities, the interdisciplinary heart team opted for an interventional catheter-based treatment. Due to the large coaptation gap with asymmetrical tethering and the risk of mitral stenosis in case of transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) (mitral valve area: 3.1 cm2), the patient was considered a suboptimal candidate for TEER. Moreover, due to logistical and geographic reasons, there was no commercially available and approved TEER device in Lithuania at that time. After the addition of a cardiac computed tomography scan, the patient was confirmed to be anatomically suitable for transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) using the Cardiovalve device (Venus Medtech) and to meet all the inclusion criteria of the Cardiovalve Transfemoral System–FIM Study (NCT03958773). After providing informed consent, the patient agreed to participate in the study.

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the safety and performance of the Cardiovalve System in high-risk patients with symptomatic MR. Data collected in this clinical study included 30-day safety and performance of the device and delivery system, and long-term clinical outcomes over a follow-up of up to 2 years. The case here described successfully completed the 2-year follow-up within the study and was then followed-up outside the trial in the outpatient clinic as per clinical practice with yearly clinical and echocardiographic controls.

After the inclusion in the trial, the patient underwent successful transfemoral transseptal TMVR using a Cardiovalve Large implant (Figures 1 and 2). MR was successfully eliminated after the procedure (only minimal paravalvular leak was present), with a mean intraprosthetic gradient of 6 mm Hg. No left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction was observed (mean gradient: 6 mm Hg). The iatrogenic atrial septal defect was close by the implantation of an atrial septal defect Occluder (Occlutech 16.5 mm). The postprocedural course was complicated by major bleeding at the venous access, which required surgical treatment and blood transfusions, and by transitory thrombocytopenia. At discharge, TTE demonstrated the absence of MR, with a left ventricular ejection fraction of 20%. The patient was discharged 7 days postprocedure. Follow-Up

At 30 days, TTE showed a stable optimal functioning of the Cardiovalve, without relevant paravalvular leak or LVOT obstruction and a left ventricular ejection fraction of 25%. Thereafter, the patient regularly attended the clinic's outpatient department for yearly follow-up visits. During the first 6 months, the patient had pneumonia and was rehospitalized due to heart failure. No further major events occurred thereafter.

At the last follow-up, 5 years after the index procedure, the patient maintained his improved clinical conditions, with only mild symptoms (NYHA functional class I). TTE did not show any sign of structural valve deterioration (Figure 3, Video 1), with stable intraprosthetic gradient (<10 mm Hg), absence of intra- or paraprosthetic leak, and absence of LVOT obstruction; left ventricular ejection fraction was 30%. Discussion

TMVR represents a relatively new therapeutic option for patients suffering from MR who are not eligible for open heart surgery and have suboptimal anatomy for transcatheter repair, like TEER.1 Although TMVR has been shown to be feasible with different fully percutaneous devices, which are delivered through a transseptal approach, all of them are still under investigation, and there is a scarcity of data regarding follow-up after index implantation.2 The only TMVR device that is currently commercially available is the Tendyne valve (Abbott Vascular), which is delivered through a transapical approach. Two years of data have showed, however, that mortality is relatively high, partially due to the high-risk clinical profile of the patients treated so far and to the invasiveness of transapical access.3 Valve thrombosis, degeneration, and LVOT obstruction remain an open challenge after TMVR.4

The feasibility of transseptal mitral valve implantation with the Cardiovalve device has been recently reported, with a promising safety and efficacy profile.5 Currently, an enhanced delivery system has been developed and is already in clinical use to improve vein access and complication rate, allowing smooth insertion, navigation, and ease of use.

This case report shows good durability of the Cardiovalve implant, 5 years after implantation, with good functioning of the valve, in the absence of degeneration, thrombosis, or infection; this is the longest follow-up ever reported after transseptal TMVR to our knowledge.

The optimal performance of the valve was associated with a favorable clinical course of the patient over the follow-up period.

In comparison with transapical TMVR, which can guarantee a more controlled coaxial alignment and procedural stability, a transseptal approach is associated with a low procedural risk and potentially with faster recovery.

However, the further clinical course after the initial implantation is of utmost importance to ensure a favorable outcome, especially if patients with longer life expectancy are treated. In this regard, durability and risk of valve thrombosis are crucial. Although 5 years represent only a mid-term follow-up if compared with the entire lifespan of a patient, it is extremely promising to report a complete absence of valve deterioration. This is probably related to the surgical-like design of the Cardiovalve, which replicates the surgical standard of care for mitral replacement (bovine pericardium with anticalcification treatment mounted on a very low-profile stent, to ensure excellent hemodynamic, durability, and minimal protrusion in the LVOT). Moreover, the risk of valve thrombosis is relatively high after TMVR, especially in the absence of precise recommendations regarding the optimal anticoagulation regimen after the procedure. The patient (who already had a preexisting indication to oral anticoagulation, like most potential TMVR candidates) did not experience any thrombotic or thromboembolic complications related to the valve under vitamin K antagonist and therapeutic international normalized ratio. This aspect is extremely reassuring, especially if compared with other MVR devices.

Conclusions

TMVR using the Cardiovalve system is associated with significant clinical improvement and excellent valve performance 5 years after the procedure. No echocardiographic signs of prosthetic degeneration or thrombosis were detected over this mid-term follow-up time, suggesting an acceptable valve durability after TMVR.

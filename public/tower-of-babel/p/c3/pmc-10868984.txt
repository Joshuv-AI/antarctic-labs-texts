Effect of Boron on Sympathetic Skin Response in Rats
Authors: Gulbahar Boyuk, Nazan Dolu, Busra Aksoy
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Boron effects on reproduction and growth have been extensively studied in animals. Electrodermal activity (EDA) reflects the activity of eccrine sweat glands stimulated by the release of acetylcholine from sympathetic nerves.Aim:In the presen study, it was aimed to examine the effect of boron, which was turned into cream, on sweat glands.Methods:A cream form mixed with thyme oil was prepared for EDA recording. Our groups were formed as EDA recording gel (Group 1), cream with thyme oil (Group 2), cream containing 10% boron (Group 3) and cream containing 30% boron (Group 4). In each group, 3 months old, 10 male rats were used, and creams were applied to the soles of the hind extremities of the rats, EDA was recorded from this region after half an hour, and skin conductivity levels (SCL) were recorded as tonic (at rest) and phasic (with auditory sound stimulation).Results:EDA results recorded in the morning were analysed with tonic and phasic recordings. In the morning SCL measurements, tonic SCL value of Group 4 was higher than the other groups (P < 0.001). Although the phasic SCL value was measured, it was significantly higher in Group 4 than in all groups (P < 0.0s).Conclusion:EDA measurements showed that boron increased sweat gland activity by increasing sympathetic nerve activity.

Introduction

Boron is a trace element that occurs mainly in soil, ocean and freshwater. The dominant forms of boron contain borates, boric acid and boric oxide. Because of its physiological and pharmacological characteristics, including antioxidant actions and metabolic arrangement, the effects of boron on breeding and growth have been extensively reported in animals.[1] There is some demonstration that dietary boron is significant for regular bone development and function.[2]

Until now, the effect of boron on metabolism has been mentioned by two possible mechanisms. First, it has been emphasized that boron can play a role in the cell membrane, in the genesis of transmembrane[3] signals and the passing of ions owing to the cell membrane, and secondly, as a metabolic organizer in varied enzymatic systems.[45]

Different formulations of boron have started to be used in many areas, and research on its usage areas is continuing. For example, it has been demonstrated that the boron hydrogel formulation gives successful results in the healing of burns. This borate formulation is thought to positively affect the healing of burns through complex mechanisms such as stimulation of cell migration, immune response, vascularization and increase in growth factor expressions.[6] To examine wound and burn healing, sodium pentaborate penhydrate compounds were applied as a gel.[78] The use of boron nitride in 643 cosmetic formulations has been reported. The highest usage concentration is in eye shadow formularization. Products include boron nitride can be performed to the eye area (25% in eye product formularization), swallowed (2% in rouge formularization) or breathed (up to 16% and 0.9% in powders). Actually, 95% to 99% of the particles let off cosmetic sprays have aerodynamic tantamount bores from 10 to 110 mm. Therefore, most particles incidentally breathed from these sprays are precipitated in the nasopharyngeal area.[9] In the literature review, boron was not used as an armpit deodorant or antiperspirant.

Sympathetic skin response (SSR) or electrodermal activity (EDA) projects the results of attention, emotional and incentive procedure inside the central nervous system that affects the body.[10] SSR is examined as modify in the skin’s strength to a little electrical flow or as distinction in electrical potential among dissimilar parts of the skin. It measures neutrally mediated effects on the skin’s sweat gland permeability. The SSR signal shows the effect of sympathetic nerve stimulation on the eccrine sweat glands. Moreover, SSR projects only sympathetic activity and SSR signal occurs independently of circulating adrenaline and noradrenaline levels.[11] It is an especially beneficial parameter among autonomic nervous system responses because it can easily elicit SSRs reliably. After all, they are much more difficult to measure and suppress. Therefore, SSR is a useful method to measure sweat gland activity.

In our study, a cream form mixed with thyme oil was prepared to measure the effect of boron on sweat gland activity.[6] We used thyme oil to dissolve because of good solvent for boron.[12] Thymus vulgaris L. (thyme), a member of the Lamiaceae family, is a plant found in many areas of the world. And also, it is aromatic and rich in oil. Essential oils of thyme occur thymol and carvacrol, and other chemical components like flavonoids are qualified by their antimicrobial activity, whichever be attached to the thymol content of the oil; otherwise, extracts of thyme and its chemical component have demonstrated action in opposition to diverse pathogenic bacteria and fungi in vitro experiments.[13] The phenolic monoterpenes in oregano, thymol and carvacrol are the fundamental combination declared to have excellent antioxidant efficiency.[14]

Due to its physiological and pharmacological properties, including passing effects of ions to the cell membrane,[45] antioxidant effects, healing of wounds and burns[78] vascularization and metabolic regulation, the study of cosmetic formulations other than antiperspirant effects, we studied the effect of boron on skin and sudomotor function. For this, boron cream was applied to rats in different doses, and its effect on sympathetic skin response was measured by electrodermal activity, and it was investigated whether boron could be used as an antiperspirant.

Materials and Methods

Animals and experimental groups

Our research used Wistar albino (250-300 g) 3-month-old male rats. The experiments were applied in relevance with protocols confirmed by the Baskent University Local Ethical Committee of Laboratory Animals (19/17). Rats were kept in cages under a 12-hour light and 12-hour dark cycle, discontinuous to organized rat chow and tap water. In the experiments, 40 rats were used totally as only EDA recording gel (control group, Group 1, n = 10), only theme oil cream (Group 2, n = 10), 10% boron-containing cream (low-dose group, Group 3, n = 10), 30% boron-containing cream (high-dose group, Group 4, n = 10).

Cream application

In the experimental group, a special cream will be prepared to examine the effects of boron on sweat glands. Boron and thyme creams were prepared through Material Science and Engineering Department at Anadolu University.[6] Boron dissolved in thyme oil with two concentrations (30% and 10%).

The previously prepared boron and thyme creams were applied to the rats 30 minutes before their feet, and then EDA recordings were performed.

SSR recordings

SSR was measured using the MP35 system (MP35; Biopac Systems Inc., Santa Barbara, CA), and the physiological recordings took place in a dimly lit, electrically and acoustically shielded experimental room. Before the recordings, rats were subjected to a habituation period of 5 minutes. SSR was recorded, employing a constant voltage technique and sampling the absolute, direct current skin conductance at the rate of 20 samples per second, from the plantar surface of the posterior extremities of each rat using Ag/AgCl electrodes. Animals were conscious during recording, and the multipurpose gel was used between the skin and the electrodes. Electrodes were connected to the MP35 system. The incoming signals of skin response were converted to digital signals through an MP35 data acquisition unit and processed with offline analysis.

Before the experiment, rat was placed in the immobilizer for cream application. Cream was applied to the plantar surfaces of the rear two extremities and kept for 30 minutes. SSR was recorded immediately after completing the period of applying the cream. The recordings were taken by applying SSR recording gel. Two Ag/AgCl electrodes were attached to the same places with the cream application.

Tonic SSR

A period of 2 minutes was allowed at the start of recording to measure non-specific skin conductance level (SCL) (μmho/cm2) during a no-stimuli period. Tonic SSR is useful for investigating general states of arousal and alertness. The mean of skin conductance (SC) was expressed as SCL [ln (μmho)/cm2 per electrode area] and calculated.

SC fluctuations (SCFr) were defined as peaks with minimum amplitudes of 0.02 μmho and a slope rate <2 μmho/s. The SCFr value was defined as the number of SC fluctuations during the 2 minutes. These analyses were applied as previously reported.[1516]

Phasic SSR

The 15 auditory stimuli were presented at the end of the tonic SSR period (no-stimuli period). All were 1-s, 90 dB, 1000-Hz tones with 50-ms rise and fall times. They occurred at pseudo-random intervals ranging from 30 to 65 seconds, averaging 45 seconds. The mean SCL values were also calculated offline for phasic SSR. Phasic SSR is useful for studying multifaceted attentional processes (related to novelty and intensity).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed by using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) for MacBook. Repeated measurements analysis of variance and post hoc Tukey’s multiple comparison tests were used to determine the differences between SSR and SCFr levels within the group. Tonic and phasic SSR values were compared with paired t-test within groups. Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Differences with P values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Sweating is a physiological reaction of our body. The eccrine sweat glands are mainly responsible for regulating body temperature and immune defence. They are common throughout the body but are more concentrated on the face, armpits, palms and soles of the feet.[17] The epidermis of the skin is composed of glycolytic tissue, and it is known that water and ions play an important role in maintaining the homeostasis of the epidermis. Impaired hydration of the skin can lead to severe skin diseases. Sympathetic sudomotor dysfunction might be related to the permeability barrier function of the stratum corneum of the skin.[18]

SSR is a test that accurately evaluates eccrine sweat gland function. The test focuses on cholinergic unmyelinated postganglionic sympathetic C fibres assessment that innervates these glands. The degeneration of these small-nerve fibres reduces and impairs sudomotor function. SSR measures the ability of the sweat glands to release chloride ions in response to an electrical stimulus over the palmar and plantar surfaces (areas with the highest sweat glands density).

In the literature, several studies have suggested the utility of the SSR parameter as a tool of the investigation of sudomotor functions. For example, SSR was studied in atopic dermatitis patients,[19] hyperhidrosis[20] by clinicians. Atopic patients have a higher incidence of hyperhidrosis. The group of atopic patients shows a statistically significant prolonged SSR latency period, indicating insufficient sympathetic innervation. There is a dysfunction of sudomotor activity in the sympathetic nervous system of atopic patients.[19]

Our study is the first to investigate the effect of boron on sweat gland activity. Boron was applied as a cream on the skin of rats, and its effects on sweat glands were measured by SSR. According to our results, tonic SCL value measured at rest was found to be significantly higher in the boron groups than in the control group. Skin conductivity in the low-dose boron group was significantly lower than in the high-dose boron group and had almost the same skin conductivity as the thyme oil group. In phasic skin conductivity recorded with auditory sound stimulus, boron groups had higher skin conductivity than the control group, as in tonic SCL. In the phasic recording, the thyme oil group had lower skin conductivity than the high-dose boron group and higher than the control group.

The increase in skin conductivity results from an increase in sympathetic activity and eccrine sweat gland activity.[1521] The resting SCL reflects the autonomic and corticoreticular wakefulness time.[16] Since changes in sympathetic activity will affect sweat gland activity, the SSR is also used as an indirect measurement method of emotional changes, cognitive status and attention in people.

No studies were found related to boron and SSR. When studies in humans with boron were examined, it was generally observed that 0.25 mg/day for 63 days or 0.23 mg/2000 kcal boron-poor diet for 119 days,[22] followed by 3 mg/day for 28-49 days.[23] Some studies add sodium borate tablets in the amount corresponding to boron. Experimental animal studies were generally carried out with oral (with the addition of boric acid to the drinking water of rats or mice)[24] or intraperitoneal administration. Sodium pentaborate pentahydrate compounds were applied as gels to examine wound and burn healing.[625]

In our study, we used a boron cream mixed with thyme oil, and none of our rats died due to dermal boron application. Richold’s study (1998) stated that 68% of infants and children who were dermally exposed to boron died due to burns, wounds and skin rashes after treatment.[26] But, we could not find any definitive publication on dose and penetration rates. Dermal penetration can be assessed by measuring boron levels in blood and urine.[27] When boric acid or borax is applied to intact skin in humans, approximately 0.2% of the administered dose is absorbed.[28] In most human studies, toxic effects are related to the oral administration or penetration of borate through damaged skin.[29] Although boron excretion seems to occur mainly through urine, it has also been detected in bile and gastrointestinal contents when applied to the skin in toxic doses.[27]

Intact skin creates an effective barrier for boron, while damaged skin creates a weaker barrier than intact skin. The form of boric acid or borax applied to the skin also affects absorption from the skin. In Nielsen’s study, three different formulations of boric acid were applied to rats, two oily ointments and one aqueous jelly. When applications of boric acid to intact and damaged skin were compared, the concentration in urine was 34 times higher in jelly form and 4 to 8 times higher in ointment form.[30] In another study, the water-based jelly form of boric acid caused increased boron levels in the blood and urine levels of infants.[31] In addition, no increase in the plasma level of boron was observed after the topical application of boric acid ointment to the napkin-covered area.[32] In our study, no systemic effect is expected since boron dissolved in thyme oil was used, and it was a cream form.

Looking at the effect of boron on the nervous system, there are studies suggesting that dietary boron benefits the central nervous system and that boron is a beneficial trace element for humans.[33] However, no study has been found regarding its effect on the autonomic nervous system or its transmission via the nerve pathway. In our study, there is a need for further studies on whether the effect of boron is directly through the sweat gland or the nerve.

To elucidate the perspiration-increasing effect of boron, it is necessary to first look at the mechanism of perspiration. Eccrine sweat glands comprise a single tubule subdivided into a coiled secretory portion (coil) located in the deeper dermis and a primarily salt-absorbing straight duct opening directly onto the skin surface.[3435] The secretory clear cell of the eccrine sweat gland is the probable source of excess sweat production in hyperhidrosis.[36] Triggered by cholinergic stimulation with the acetylcholine and by the subsequent release of intracellular Ca2+ as a second messenger, a cascade of ion transport processes is activated.[37] The most important ions in this secretion are sodium (Na+), chloride (Cl−) and potassium (K+).[38] The skin surface ductal epithelial cells reabsorb Na+ and Cl− ions from the sweat preventing the profuse loss of salts. The final sweat is hypotonic.[3539]

Cosmetically, antiperspirant activity is described as the physical blockage of the gland by precipitated different gels, for example, the use of aluminium, thereby reducing sweat secretion. In our study, the sweating-increasing effect of the boron may be due to its facilitating effect of Na, K and Cl flow, or its effect to increase local acetylcholine release. In Capati et al.[40] studies (2016) demonstrate the acceleration of Ca2+ flux by boron supplementation in osteoblastic cells. They found that a very low concentration of boron promoted the proliferation and differentiation of mammalian osteoblastic cells in vitro. Osteoblastic cells accelerated the osteoblastic function after 3-day culture in 0.1 mM of boron-supplemented medium. They also demonstrated that boron stimulates the activity of Na+/K+ -ATPase. This study supports our results.

The hypothesis that boron can increase sweating by stimulating the release of acetylcholine is supported by the study of Soriano-Ursúa et al. in this regard, boron-containing compounds with medicinal purposes have been clearly demonstrated to be potent ligands of some of the proteins involved in the disease and then potential inducers of excitotoxicity through interactions with calcium channels as well as acetylcholine.[4142]

Conclusion

This is the first study to measure the effect of boron on the SSR. SSR measurements showed that boron increased sweat gland activity. The sweating-increasing effect of the boron may be due to its facilitating effect of Na, K and Cl flow, or its effect to increase local acetylcholine release. In this context, boron can be used as a moisturizer or for therapeutic purposes in diseases where sweating secretion is reduced. We need further studies on the mechanism of boron on sweating.

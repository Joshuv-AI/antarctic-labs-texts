Low-dose ticagrelor combined with aspirin in the prevention of early neurological deterioration of high-risk non-disabling ischemic cerebrovascular events: a PROBE clinical study
Authors: Mingli Wang, Rui Chen, Xinting Yu, Xing Gong, Xidan Li, Xiaofei Ji, Jingshun Shen, Hua Cao, Lanlan Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly neurological deterioration (END) seriously endangers the clinical prognosis of high-risk non-disabling cerebrovascular events (HR-NICE), and strong antiplatelet therapy is an effective means of preventing the occurrence of END. Studies have confirmed that ticagrelor can avoid the low efficacy of clopidogrel in antiplatelet therapy caused by CYP2C19 gene dysfunction and that its combination with aspirin can improve the clinical prognosis of patients with HR-NICE. However, the overall risk of bleeding events is relatively high. In this protocol, we compared the prevention and safety of END in patients with HR-NICE using aspirin combined with low-dose ticagrelor and aspirin combined with clopidogrel.Methods and analysisThis multicenter, prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded endpoints (PROBE) clinical study intended to enrol 240 patients with HR-NICE who have not undergone reperfusion therapy for block randomization. Ticagrelor combined with aspirin group (120 cases): the patients were given 120 mg of ticagrelor and 100 mg of aspirin orally on the first day of enrollment, and from the next day onwards, ticagrelor 60 mg twice daily orally and aspirin 100 mg daily orally. The clopidogrel combined with aspirin group (120 cases) was administered 300 mg clopidogrel and 100 mg aspirin orally on the first day of enrollment, and starting from the next day, 75 mg clopidogrel and 100 mg aspirin were administered orally. After 21 days, all patients were changed to aspirin 100 mg daily orally. The main efficacy outcome is the early neurological deterioration within 7 days. Safety outcomes include any bleeding events and death.DiscussionThis non-inferiority trial will explore whether low-dose ticagrelor combined with aspirin is superior to clopidogrel combined with aspirin for the prevention of END in patients with HR–NICE patients.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (Number Project ID PJ-KS-KY-2023-03(X)). Introduction

Early neurological deterioration (END) refers to the deterioration of neurological function within 24 h to 7 days after the onset of acute ischemic cerebrovascular disease, with an incidence ranging from 13.3% to 43% [1–3]. END is associated with permanent neurological impairment and is an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in patients with ischemic stroke [1, 4]. At present, there is no unified standard for END. The more widely recognized diagnostic criteria are the appearance of new neurological deficits or an increase of ≥2 points in the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score [2, 5].

High-risk TIA (ABCD2 score ≥ 4) and minor stroke are considered as high-risk patients with acute non-disabling cerebrovascular events (HR-NICE) [6]. Unlike severe stroke, which usually involves large vessel occlusion, TIA and mild stroke are typically caused by ischemia in small vessels. When blood pressure rises and cerebral perfusion rapidly recovers, or when an embolus quickly undergoes autolysis after embolization, it presents as TIA. When the degree of atherosclerotic lesions is relatively mild and vascular occlusion is temporary or partial, it may manifest as mild stroke. Meanwhile, some patients with mild stroke or TIA have concomitant large vessel stenosis or occlusion, with the pathological basis being atherosclerotic plaques blocking perforator mouths or a combination of large artery atherosclerosis and perforator artery atherosclerosis. These patients have a higher risk of disease deterioration. After an ischemic event, vascular inflammation, unstable plaque rupture, and platelet aggregation based on atherosclerosis are the main pathological basis of END; thromboembolism is the predominant mechanism [7, 8]. Acute recanalization therapy such as intravenous thrombolysis can restore blood perfusion in time and reduce the occurrence of END. While approximately 10% of patients still experience END after intravenous thrombolysis [9], the majority of minor strokes exceed the time window of intravenous thrombolysis, with the incidence of END in these patients can be as high as 41% [10]. Strong antiplatelet therapy such as clopidogrel combined with aspirin is an effective means to prevent the occurrence of END [11–13]. However, clopidogrel is a precursor drug and the CYP2C19 gene polymorphism severely limits clopidogrel efficacy. Genetic subgroup analysis of the CHANCE trial found that the carrier rate of CYP2C19 loss-of-function (LOF) alleles in the Chinese population was as high as 58.8%, and the risk of stroke recurrence was 92% higher than that of non-carriers [14]. Clopidogrel and aspirin resistance are independent risk factors for END [11, 13, 15].

Ticagrelor can avoid the low efficacy of clopidogrel in antiplatelet therapy caused by the loss of CYP2C19 gene function and exert stronger antiplatelet effects [16, 17]. It itself has pharmacological activity and can exert antiplatelet effects without undergoing liver metabolism [18]. The main active metabolite AR-C124910XX has the same antiplatelet activity as ticagrelor [19, 20]. Meanwhile, ticagrelor has good pharmacokinetics, reaching the peak blood concentration within 1.5–3 h and exerting antiplatelet effect within 2 h. Binding of the P2Y12 receptor is reversible and platelet function can be restored 3–5 days after drug discontinuation [21, 22].

The antiplatelet effect of ticagrelor in acute coronary syndrome has been confirmed by multiple clinical studies and has become the first-line or preferred antiplatelet drug for the treatment of acute coronary syndrome [23–28]. For acute ischemic stroke (AIS), especially HR-NICE, multiple clinical studies have confirmed that ticagrelor can significantly improve the prognosis of high-risk TIA and mild stroke at 30 to 90 days. The CHANCE-2 trial confirmed that the 90-day stroke recurrence rate was significantly lower in the ticagrelor + aspirin group than in the clopidogrel + aspirin group in the HR-NICE acute phase with CYP2C19 LOF alleles [17]. This finding confirmed that ticagrelor combined with aspirin is superior to clopidogrel combined with aspirin in HR-NICE with CYP2C19 LOF alleles and provides stronger evidence for the use of ticagrelor in secondary prevention of AIS. In the SOCRATES trial using ticagrelor alone, the PRINCE, THALES, and CHANCE-2 studies using ticagrelor in combination with aspirin, ticagrelor has shown excellent potential in reducing the risk of mid-to-long-term stroke recurrence and improving prognosis [17, 29–31].

However, several important clinical concerns have not been addressed. First, the CHANCE-2 study relied on rapid bedside genetic testing. Owing to the limitations of current rapid genotyping technologies and tools, application scenarios in primary hospitals are limited, and the cost-effectiveness of genotype-oriented strategies is not yet explicit. Second, all of the above studies used standard doses of ticagrelor, and the experimental results suggested an increased risk of bleeding. Although there was no significant difference in the risk of moderate to severe bleeding between the two groups in the CHANCE-2 study, the ticagrelor group had a significantly higher risk of mild bleeding and an overall higher risk of bleeding than the clopidogrel group [17]. However, the PEGASUS trial, which explored the potential benefits of dual antiplatelet therapy 1 year after myocardial infarction, found that the efficacy of ticagrelor 60 mg twice daily was similar to that of 90 mg twice daily, and that lower doses were more tolerable and safe in terms of bleeding and breathing difficulties [32]. Thirdly, in the THALES and CHANCE-2 trials, the curves representing the cumulative hazard of stroke showed the greatest difference at 7–10 days, indicating that the maximum effect of dual antiplatelet therapy occurs mainly in the acute phase of ischemic stroke at approximately 7–10 days. Though the dual antiplatelet therapy duration in the above two studies was 21 days and 30 days, the risk of major bleeding was higher in the latter study. The risk of bleeding gradually increased over time, suggesting that there was no additional benefit from prolonged dual antiplatelet therapy; however, it increased the risk of bleeding [31, 32]. Therefore, we speculate that reducing the single dose of ticagrelor and shortening the duration of dual antiplatelet therapy may maximize the benefits and further reduce the risk of bleeding.

At present, there are no studies on the use of ticagrelor combined with aspirin to prevent early END in HR-NICE patients. Since HR-NICE is an important window for the prevention and control of stroke and END is closely related to poor prognosis, it is of great clinical significance to study the prevention of END in HR-NICE patients. We aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of low-dose ticagrelor combined with aspirin for the prevention of END in HR-NICE patients, with the aim of reducing the risk of END in high-risk patients in the acute phase of AIS and improving clinical prognosis.

Method and analysis

Study design

This trial is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, open-blind, endpoint (PROBE) clinical study. It aims to evaluate the prevention of END in patients with HR-NICE within 24 h treated with ticagrelor combined with aspirin. In this study, patients with HR-NICE within 24 h of onset of AIS were selected as clinical research participants. The patients were randomly divided into a ticagrelor combined with aspirin treatment group (TA group) and a clopidogrel combined with aspirin treatment group (LA group) in a 1:1 ratio. TA group: patients were randomly assigned to the TA group to receive oral administration of 120 mg of ticagrelor and 100 mg of aspirin on the first day, followed by oral administration of 60 mg twice daily of ticagrelor and 100 mg daily of aspirin from the next day, and then switched to 100 mg daily of aspirin 21 days later. LA group: patients randomly assigned to the LA group were administered clopidogrel 300 mg and aspirin 100 mg orally on the first day, followed by clopidogrel 75 mg daily and aspirin 100 mg daily orally from the next day, and switched to aspirin 100 mg daily orally after 21 days. All patients were followed up on 1 st, 7th, 30th, and 90th days after enrollment.

Based on our retrospective observation of 30 HR-NICE patients’ use of combination of clopidogrel and aspirin, hospitalized at the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University from October 2022 to January 2023, a total of 7 patients exhibited END within 24 h to 7 days of disease onset, accounting for 23.3%. During the same period, among 30 HR-NICE patients who were treated with combination of ticagrelor and aspirin within 24 h of onset, 4 patients experienced END, accounting for 13.3%. Assuming the incidence of END in the intervention and control groups are 13.3% and 23.3%, respectively, with a two-sided α level of 0.05 and 80% power, we used R software version 4.3.2 to calculate the sample size. Each group requires 100 patients. Considering a 20% loss to follow-up, both groups will need 120 patients, making a total of at least 240 patients across both groups. The methods and procedures for collecting data are as follows: Upon first receiving the subject, demographic information, mRS, NIHSS, symptoms, current medications, and other data can be obtained through inquiry and physical examination. After admission, the subject will undergo relevant examinations including head CT, head MRA/SWI, carotid color Doppler ultrasound, electrocardiogram, and dynamic electrocardiogram. At the same time, laboratory tests such as blood and urine will be conducted on the subject to obtain biochemical indicators, cytological indicators, microbiological indicators, and other information.

Randomization

The HR-NICE patients are sorted and then randomized into the intervention or control groups in a 1:1 balanced ratio. Allocation into randomized study groups is conducted via a computer-generated random number table and determined during the pre-study procedures during allocation when informed consent and baseline measurements are made. The number generation is facilitated by R4.3.2, a computer random number generator typically used by the Scientific Research Department of the hospital.

Blinding

The PROBE study was designed as a non-double-blind study. The clinical evaluators and independent clinical event adjudication committees (ruling on primary and secondary efficacy outcomes and bleeding events) were unaware of the treatment allocation.

Interventions

Two hundred forty HR-NICE patients were randomly divided into two groups and immediately received the following treatment after randomization:

Ticagrelor–aspirin group (120 cases):

A total of 120 mg of ticagrelor and 100 mg of aspirin were administered on the first day, followed by ticagrelor 60 mg twice daily and aspirin 100 mg daily the next day; aspirin was switched to 100 mg daily after 21 days.

Clopidogrel combined aspirin group (120 cases):

A 300 mg of clopidogrel and 100 mg aspirin were administered on the first day, followed by clopidogrel 75 mg and aspirin 100 mg daily the next day, and then switched to aspirin 100 mg daily after 21 days.

Participants receive a normal treatment including drugs of antihypertensive, lipid-lowering, hypoglycemic throughout the duration of the study, if applicable to the individual. Other drugs that prevent platelet aggregation or increase the risk of bleeding are prohibited.

Data collection and management

An electronic data-management system is used in this study. The researchers construct eCRF according to the study protocol. They are collected in the form of case reports in a pseudo-anonymous manner and stored in accordance with current legal requirements. The data entry personnel have the authority to enter, modify, and resolve queries; researchers have the authority to modify, browse, question solve, and review; data management personnel have the authority to browse, send queries, and lock data. The experimental data are collected to ensure authenticity, completeness, and accuracy. eCRF data can only be modified by researchers. Original medical records of the trial data should be stored for at least 5 years.

Participants and participant family members will be given a reminder (via e-mail and phone call) a week before to schedule the follow-up.

Adverse events

Adverse event (AE) refers to any clinical manifestation, syndrome, or deterioration of the underlying disease that affects the health of the patients during the clinical study period. However, it is not necessarily related to ticagrelor treatment and may be the occurrence of a new disease accompanied by the worsening of symptoms.

If a subject experiences a serious adverse event (SAE), such as permanent damage to human functions or organs, life-threatening death, or requiring medical treatment during the clinical research process, the investigator will immediately stop the clinical study and report it to the academic and ethics committee of the institution within 24 h. The agency then reports to the national and provincial health and family planning administrative, and food and drug regulatory departments.

After the occurrence of an SAE, investigators should promptly and properly handle the patients and report the subsequent work to the academic and ethical committees of the institution within 15 days of the end of treatment to explain the reasons for the event and the measures taken. During the study period, if participants suffer an injury related to this study, they will receive active treatment, and the sponsor will bear the corresponding medical expenses and make financial compensation in accordance with the relevant laws and regulations.

Clinical management of hemorrhagic events is as follows. Mild bleeding: continued antiplatelet therapy, dynamic changes in the patient’s condition, and no additional disposal; intracranial asymptomatic hemorrhagic transformation: continued antiplatelet therapy, dynamically observing changes in the patient’s condition, and re-examining head CT 7 days later; symptomatic intracranial hemorrhagic transformation: suspend antiplatelet therapy, hospitalization, and symptomatic treatment based on the location and volume of bleeding; severe and fatal bleeding: suspend antiplatelet therapy, seek emergency or hospitalization treatment, and contact relevant departments for surgical treatment if necessary.

Statistical analyses

Analysis of participants

The demographic characteristics, general conditions, and baseline conditions (before treatment) of the two groups will be statistically analyzed. The measurement data are described as the mean, standard deviation, and maximum, minimum, and median values. The grade and classification data are listed in a frequency table to calculate the constituent ratios. The t-test or non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test will be used for count data, and the chi-square test for categorical data.

Analysis of effectiveness

All randomized participants with more than one treatment record will be included in the full analysis set (FAS), and intention-to-treat (ITT) will be performed on all data after the study. Missing data and any data collected not adhering to the protocols will be removed from data analysis. Statistical analyses are planned using SPSS or R. Due to the use of randomized controlled trial, the test standard is set to be bilateral, α = 0.05.

Safety analysis

Safety evaluation data include adverse events observed during the study and changes in laboratory parameters before and after treatment. Adverse events during the treatment period, significant adverse events during the treatment period, unexpected adverse events during the treatment period, adverse events of special concern during the treatment period, serious adverse events during the treatment period, adverse events during the treatment period related to the study drug, and serious adverse events during the treatment period related to the study drug are summarized.

If multiple AEs occur in the same subject, the incidence is recorded as 1 case. When the subject experiences the same AE multiple times, it is recorded as one case in the calculation of the AE incidence rate.

A list of participants with AE and SAE is provided. In addition to the comparison of means before and after treatment, the laboratory data is mainly analyzed and lists the specific situations of cases that were normal before treatment but abnormal after treatment, or abnormal before treatment but worsened after treatment.

Interim analysis

An interim analysis is planned when approximately 50% of the participants complete the 90th day of follow-up. The goal of the interim analysis is to re-estimate the sample size based on the main efficacy indicators. The increase in sample size can be up to 1.5 times the original planned sample size, with no reduction in the sample size.

The sample size re-estimation for this interim analysis used a promising conditional power-based zone approach. If the time node of the interim analysis, the upper limit of the sample size, increases and the appropriate lower limit of the promising zone is set in advance, the type I error probability of the study population would not be inflated, and the statistical method of the final analysis would not be adjusted.

Multiplicity

The false positive rate for testing the primary efficacy hypothesis is controlled at 0.025 (one-sided test). No additional multiplicity adjustments are made.

Oversight and monitoring

The Department of Neurology at the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University will serve as the trial coordinating centre. The Trial Steering Committee (TSC) will consist of one principal investigator and two co-investigators. The TSC will be responsible for ensuring the trial is conducted according to the protocol and for providing oversight throughout the study. This will include approving the final protocol, monitoring study progress, making decisions regarding any protocol amendments, and ensuring the overall integrity of the trial. The committee will meet bi-monthly to review progress and address any issues that may arise.

The Trial Management Committee (TMC) will consist of two co-investigators, two master’s students, and an independent scientific assistant. The TMC will be responsible for the day-to-day management of the trial, including planning, coordination, and execution of the study. Their duties will encompass overseeing patient recruitment, ensuring protocol compliance, managing data collection and quality control, and maintaining participant safety and trial integrity. The independent scientific assistant will be solely responsible for generating the random allocation sequence, ensuring unbiased assignment of participants. The TMC will hold weekly meetings to review trial operations and address any immediate concerns.

In consideration of the relatively low-risk profile of the trial as determined by the hospital’s Ethics Committee and the study’s Steering Committee, there will be no formal Data Monitoring Committee (DMC). Instead, an internal trial monitor, independent of the study team, will be responsible for regular onsite monitoring according to the established monitoring plan.

The Ethics Committee of the hospital conducts audits quarterly (typically every 3–6 months) to check on progress and ensure adherence to the established protocol. If the study passes its previously determined set timeline, then a re-application to obtain permission for continuation of the study is required. If this is necessary, then the trial manager is responsible for re-applying and obtaining committee approval. In addition, the Steering Committee has scheduled meetings and check-ins every other month between the audits from the hospital’s Ethics Committee. This meeting is used to inform investigators, research physicians, and sponsors of updates, issues, and necessary changes to the study to be reviewed and agreed upon.

Discussion

The clinical trial of CHANCE-2 has shown that under the condition of rapid genetic testing, patients with moderate to slow metabolism of clopidogrel can benefit better from the combination of ticagrelor and aspirin, but the overall risk of bleeding remains high. In the design of this experiment, low-dose ticagrelor was selected to provide a feasible dual antibody treatment plan for some medical institutions that cannot achieve rapid bedside genetic testing while reducing the risk of bleeding, in order to avoid clopidogrel insensitivity caused by slow metabolism. This study will evaluate the effectiveness and safety of short-term low-dose ticagrelor combined with aspirin in preventing early neurological deterioration in HR-NICE patients and explore the clinical efficacy of early optimization of antiplatelet therapy plans in patients with AIS. Strong antiplatelet therapy is an effective means of preventing END in the HR-NICE population. However, the efficacy of clopidogrel is influenced by CYP2C19 polymorphism. Ticagrelor is a direct P2Y12 receptor inhibitor that improves the low efficacy of clopidogrel antiplatelet therapy caused by CYP2C19 gene dysfunction. It has consistent and strong antiplatelet activity in the general population. Studies have shown that ticagrelor combined with aspirin is more effective than clopidogrel combined with aspirin in reducing the risk of stroke recurrence in HR-NICE patients with CYP2C19 (LOF) allele, and that the benefits mainly occur shortly after stroke. However, ticagrelor was associated with a higher risk of bleeding. At present, there is a lack of clinical evidence for the efficacy of ticagrelor combined with aspirin in reducing END events in patients with HR-NICE. Therefore, we attempted to investigate whether short-term low-dose ticagrelor combined with aspirin is superior to clopidogrel combined with aspirin in the treatment of acute non-disabling ischemic stroke and conduct safety assessments to provide a basis for more optimized and accurate antithrombotic drug regimens for HR-NICE patients.

However, our study still has some limitations as follows: 1. This clinical study is a small, non-double-blind study, and there may be bias caused by the subjective factors of the investigators or patients. 2. Our study will be conducted in six regions of Dalian, and whether similar effects will be observed in other ethnic groups and regions remains uncertain. 3. The follow-up period of this clinical study is 90 days; therefore, long-term prognostic monitoring is inadequate.

Associated Data

Data Availability Statement

The study regimes, consent forms, assent forms, and study-related materials are accessible from the corresponding author. The final trial dataset will be available to all investigators. The corresponding author will make the de-identified datasets available upon reasonable request. Trial results are planned to be predominantly communicated via conferences and manuscript publication in relevant stroke-related organizations. A formal summary of the study’s results is provided to sponsors who will then produce a publicise copy and manage the dissemination at that time. The attending physicians at the hospital will receive the study’s details, findings, and directions on implementation if they wish to utilise it with their patients. Data sharing arrangements can be made upon reasonable request. Confidentiality is not an issue in the data collecting country; however, the data will be stored on the cloud server provided by the hospital which is already secured to ensure strict access. Only primary researchers who are enrolling participants, collecting or analyzing data and other principal members of the research team will be able to access the cloud database. Before the data is uploaded to the cloud and organised based on trial identification number instead of group and individuals’ names. Access to the database may be granted upon request from other researchers, study entities, or miscellaneous government-associated groups who wish to conduct prospective meta-analyses. For international entities that request for data access, video recordings are processed to de-identify the participants.

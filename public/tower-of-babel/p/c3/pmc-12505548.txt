Determination of the 50% and 95% effective dose of esketamine combined with propofol for intravenous sedation in pediatric circumcision: study protocol for a randomized controlled, double-blind, dose-finding clinical trial
Authors: Junqin Mao, Kang Lin, Xiang Liu, Jie Liu, Gang Liang, Chao Jiang, Zhimin Sheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPediatric circumcision is a common yet often distressing procedure due to the associated pain and emotional response it can elicit in children. Adequate sedation and analgesia are critical to mitigate these effects and ensure the procedure’s success. The combination of propofol and opioids continues to be the most commonly utilized intravenous anesthetic regimen for pediatric circumcision procedures. However, due to its narrow therapeutic index, propofol may cause notable hemodynamic and respiratory depression. Esketamine, an S(+)-isomer of ketamine, is recognized for its analgesic potency and minimal respiratory impact. Furthermore, its sympathomimetic properties offer a counterbalance to the hemodynamic inhibition of propofol. Therefore, the aim of this study is to determine the optimal dose of esketamine combined with propofol for pediatric circumcision.MethodsThis prospective, randomized controlled, dose-finding clinical trial will enroll 100 children aged 3–9 years who undergo circumcision. Participants will be randomly assigned to five groups to receive varying doses of esketamine (0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, and 1.50 mg/kg) combined with 3 mg/kg propofol for achieving adequate sedation. An effective dose of esketamine will be defined as non-occurrence of physical movement, frowning, or Ramsay Sedation Scale (RSS) score ≥ 5 during skin incision. The values for median effective dose (ED50), 95% effective dose (ED95), and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) of esketamine will be determined using probit regression.DiscussionThe results of this study seek to determine the ED50 and ED95 values of esketamine combined with propofol for intravenous sedation in pediatric circumcision using probit regression analysis, which will provide further references for precise medication in pediatric circumcision.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2400090035. Registered on September 23, 2024.

Introduction

Background andrationale {6a}

An extended foreskin in children can not only impede the development of the reproductive system but also lead to local adhesion and infection [1]. While the circumcision procedure may appear straightforward and brief, it is crucial to note that without adequate sedation and analgesia, most children cannot tolerate the intense pain and discomfort associated with the surgery [2]. The anesthesia schemes commonly employed for pediatric circumcision consist of local anesthesia, nerve block, or general anesthesia [3, 4]. Due to the traction caused by surgery or children’s emotional resistance, local anesthesia or nerve block can easily cause pain or injury, disrupting the operation and potentially leading to psychological impact on children. Taking into account the distinct physiological and psychological traits of children, it is imperative to devise suitable anesthesia plans that assist them in smoothly navigating the perioperative period.

The combination of propofol and opioids continues to be the most commonly utilized intravenous anesthesia for pediatric circumcision procedures [5]. However, due to its narrow therapeutic index, propofol may cause notable hemodynamic and respiratory depression when its effect site concentration approaches the levels required for procedural sedation [6]. Esketamine, as the S+ isomer of ketamine, possesses twice the potency of ketamine. It stands out for its mild respiratory depression and also provides more reliable analgesia and sedation with fewer mental side effects compared to ketamine, especially suitable for short-term surgical anesthesia in children with non-tracheal intubation while maintaining spontaneous breathing [7, 8]. Furthermore, its sympathomimetic properties offer a counterbalance to the hemodynamic inhibition of propofol, thus reducing the risk of respiratory depression and cardiovascular depression during sedation [9].

Prior research has indicated that ketamine combined with propofol provided superior hemodynamic stability and sedation quality compared to ketamine alone in pediatric circumcision operations. Therefore, ketamine combined with propofol appears to be a safe and effective sedation method for circumcision operations [10]. It has also been demonstrated that in children undergoing painless gastrointestinal endoscopy, MRI examinations and surgical procedures, the bolus injection of esketamine at 0.15–1.00 mg/kg along with propofol prior to the examination or treatment can play a good role in sedation and analgesia, reduce the required dosage of propofol, significantly decrease the incidence of postoperative agitation, improves the postoperative comfort of children without elevating the incidence of adverse reactions [11–14].

Objectives {7}

While the utilization of esketamine in pediatric anesthesia is gaining popularity, the optimal effective dose of esketamine when combined with propofol in pediatric circumcision has not been determined. Therefore, the aim of this study is to find the median effective dose (ED50) and 95% effective dose (ED95) of esketamine combined with propofol for intravenous sedation in pediatric circumcision, which will provide additional guidance for the precise administration of esketamine combined with propofol in pediatric circumcision.

Trial design {8}

This is a prospective, randomized-controlled, double-blind, dose-finding trial. A total of 100 children will be randomly assigned to five groups to receive esketamine doses of 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, and 1.50 mg/kg, respectively. We report the study protocol according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials statement [15].

Intervention

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

This study will adopt a grouping method to investigate the ED50 and ED95 of intravenous different doses of esketamine combined with propofol for preventing intraoperative physical movement reactions in children undergoing elective circumcision.

Definition of binary endpoint

Different doses of esketamine combined with propofol for preventing intraoperative physical movement reactions and were simplified to a binary endpoint (positive and negative). A positive response is defined as a subject showing a physical movement, frowning, or Ramsay Sedation Scale (RSS) score < 5 during skin incision, while a negative response is defined as a subject having no physical movement, no frowning, or RSS score ≥ 5 during skin incision.

Dose allocation and dose spacing

In this study, dose allocation follows a structured method, whereby esketamine doses are spaced in uniform intervals across five groups. This arrangement ensures that the study adequately covers a range of doses to accurately calculate the ED50 and ED95.

Previous literature has indicated that the dose range of esketamine used in diverse pediatric surgeries fall between approximately 0.25 mg/kg and 1.99 mg/kg [11–14, 16]. Based on prior studies and our clinical experience, the initial dose of esketamine in this study was set at 0.5 mg/kg, the dose interval was 0.25 mg/kg, and the maximum dose was 1.50 mg/kg. Including five levels: 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, and 1.50 mg/kg.

Preoperative preparation and monitoring setup

Prior to anesthesia, all children will adhere to standard fasting guidelines, with a minimum fasting period of 6 h for solids and 2 h for clear fluids. In the holding area, the child will sit on a guardian’s lap and one of the arms will be inserted with a venous catheter of 22- or 24-gauge by a nurse. Standard monitoring (IntelliVue MP50 Vision M8004A Monitor, Philips, Germany) including pulse oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate (HR), noninvasive blood pressure (NIBP), electrocardiograms (ECG), end-tidal carbon dioxide (PetCO2), and respiratory rate (RR) will be established before induction, and the following parameters will be recorded every 1 min during the procedure, then the child will receive 0.1 mg/kg of midazolam intravenously. After 3 min, record the HR, NIBP, and SpO2 of the child in a quiet state as baseline values. To prevent intraoperative awareness, the RSS score will be used to assess sedation levels in this study. The RSS score categorizes sedation into six levels, with each score reflecting a distinct clinical sedation depth [17] (Table 1). An RSS score ≥ 5 is considered effective sedation [18, 19]. Anesthesia induction

Intervention description {11a}

Different doses of esketamine (0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, and 1.50 mg/kg) (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., China) will be prepared and diluted to a total volume of 10 ml with normal saline, and then will be administered intravenously to Group S1 (0.50 mg/kg), Group S2 (0.75 mg/kg), Group S3 (1.00 mg/kg), Group S4 (1.25 mg/kg) and Group S5 (1.50 mg/kg), respectively. The esketamine dose will be injected over a duration of 30 s to avoid any sudden cardiovascular changes. Immediately following esketamine, an initial bolus of propofol (3 mg/kg) (Aspen Pharma Co. Ltd., Ireland) will be administered slowly over 60 s. Subsequently, the procedure will start. If the child shows a positive reaction (defined as a subject showing a physical movement, frowning, or RSS score < 5 during skin incision), additional boluses of propofol (0.5 mg/kg per time) will be administered, with a top-up interval of more than 1 min until the surgery is completed. All the procedures will be performed by the same surgeon with more than 5 years of work experience. During the procedure, all children will receive supplemental oxygen via a mask with a gas flow rate of 2–3 L/min. When the procedure is complete, a 0.3 g acetaminophen suppository will be administered rectally, and lidocaine will be sprayed onto the surgical wound to induce local anesthesia for postoperative analgesia. The children will then be transferred to the post anesthesia care unit (PACU), and their recovery time will be observed and recorded [defined as the time from the end of intravenous esketamine injection to the children reaching the standard for discharge from PACU to the surgical ward (Aldrete score ≥ 9)] (Table 2). Specification of primary, secondary, and safety outcomes according to the five-element framework

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the positive and negative responses of physical movement response during skin incision in children. If the subject has a physical movement, frowning, or RSS score < 5 during skin incision, it will be considered a “positive response”; otherwise, it will be recorded as a “negative response.” Based on this definition, the ED50, ED95, and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for esketamine will be determined using probit regression analysis.

Secondary outcomes (including safety outcomes)

Secondary outcomes include the demographic data, hemodynamic index (T0–T4), initial and cumulative doses of propofol, time of disappearance of eyelash reflex after administration, operation duration, recovery time from anesthesia. Safety outcomes are also considered secondary outcomes and include the incidence of adverse events, such as respiratory depression, desaturation, apnea, hypertension, hypotension, bradycardia, nausea, vomiting, visual disturbance, dizziness, and agitation during the perioperative period.

Sample size {14}

The sample size was calculated using the Cochran–Armitage trend test in PASS software (Version 11.0; NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, Utah, UT). Based on several previous studies [10–12] and our clinical experience, we estimated the intravenous doses of esketamine were 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, and 1.50 mg/kg corresponding to the probability of success for preventing physical movement response during skin incision in children were 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9, and 0.95, respectively. To achieve 90% power at a 0.05 significance level for detecting a linear trend in the proportion of physical movement responses among groups, a Z test with continuity correction indicated a sample size of 65 (13 per dosage) was necessary. Accounting for potential dropouts and to narrow confidence intervals, we planned to increase the sample size to 100 (20 per dosage).

Recruitment {15}

The Wenling Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital is a tertiary hospital in China, where a large number of pediatric circumcisions are performed annually. Furthermore, the recruitment period is approximately 12 months, which will ensure an adequate sample size.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Before starting the study, All enrolled children will be sequentially allocated into different esketamine dose groups: Group S1 (0.50 mg/kg), Group S2 (0.75 mg/kg), Group S3 (1.00 mg/kg), Group S4 (1.25 mg/kg) and Group S5 (1.50 mg/kg) using a double-blinded randomization system at a 1:1:1:1:1 ratio. The randomized block 5 design will be generated by an independent statistician using MedCalc software (version 18.2.1, Ostend, Belgium).

Concealment mechanism {16b}

One code for each participant will be placed into an opaque, sealed, sequentially numbered envelope by a research assistant who is not involved in data collection or patient management. On the day of surgery, all different doses of esketamine will be diluted with normal saline to 10 ml in a batch of identical 10-ml syringes, labeled as study drugs by a nurse anesthetist not involved in the study, and handed over to the anesthesiologist responsible for recording intraoperative data who will be unaware of the dosage of esketamine administered to the participant.

Implementation {16c}

After obtaining written informed consent, a research assistant will randomly assign participants to one of five groups according to the randomization table. Only enrollment details will be shared with the research team, excluding group allocation. On the surgery day, the nurse anesthetist will open a sealed envelope with allocation details to prepare the anesthetic according to the study protocol.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Anesthesia implementation researchers, participants, surgeons, and postoperative assessment researchers will be blinded to the randomized information. Unblinding will not occur until the research is completed, the statistical analysis plan has been confirmed, and all data have been inputted.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Nevertheless, a structured unblinding protocol exists for specific circumstances: (1) Emergencies—if a participant suffers a significant adverse event, the attending physician must be informed of the specific treatment assigned (dosage of medication) to manage the condition appropriately; (2) Inspections or data validation—representatives from regulatory agencies or the study sponsor may need to unblind to confirm the integrity and proper execution of the trial.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Data will be analyzed using IBM SPSS 22.0 for Windows (Corp, Armonk, NY,USA) and GraphPad Prism Software 8.0.2 (San Diego, CA, USA), and P-values < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Continuous data will be tested for normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov method, and values will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), median and interquartile range or percentages (n, %) according to different distribution types. Normally distributed data among groups will be analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with trend analysis. Non-normally distributed data will be analyzed with the Kruskal-Wallis test, and categorical data with the Cochran-Armitage χ2 test for trend. Probability unit regression will be used to analyze the dose-response relationship. After logarithmic transformation of dose values, success proportions at each dose level will be converted to probit and analyzed via regression. The Pearson goodness-of-fit chi-square will test the null hypothesis that the regression model fits the data adequately. Values for ED50, ED95, and 95% CIs of esketamine combined with propofol for preventing intraoperative physical movement response will be derived by interpolation.

The probability of a negative response must consistently increase or decrease with an increasing dose. Interim analyses {21b}

After completing sedation and recovery assessments in approximately 25%, 50%, and 75% of the target sample size, an independent DMC will review unblinded safety data and conduct interim safety analysis, and provide recommendations to the Trial Steering Committee (TSC).

We have defined dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), including airway obstruction requiring airway support, laryngospasm requiring intervention, sustained hypoxemia despite oxygen, hemodynamic instability requiring pharmacological treatment, and any serious adverse event attributable to study drugs. The trial will be paused if two or more DLTs occur in any cohort of 10 patients or if a suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction is observed, and it will be stopped if cumulative DLT rates exceed predefined thresholds (such as overall over 10% or any dose group over 20%) or if unacceptable risks emerge.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

This trial will not include stratification. The effects of different esketamine doses will be analyzed during post hoc analysis, with no additional subgroup analyses planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

The prospective study design will minimize missing data. If any data is missing, imputation will be conducted in collaboration with a statistician.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code {31c}

The full protocol is accessible to the public on the ChiCTR.gov website (ChiCTR2400090035), excluding participants' personal information. The database permits the corresponding author to access the final trial dataset upon reasonable request.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee {5d}

The coordinating center (CC), which is the Department of Anesthesiology of Wenling Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital, Taizhou, China, will be staffed with the PI, three anesthesiologists, a surgeon, a nurse, and an independent statistician, and is responsible for managing trial-related operations including: trial setup and training, randomisation system administration, data management and query resolution, pharmacovigilance processing, and maintenance of the Trial Master File (TMF). The CC meets biweekly to review recruitment, protocol adherence, and data completeness. Strategic oversight is provided by the TSC, comprising the PI, three anesthesiologists, and a statistician (with at least two members independent of the trial team); the TSC meets every two months (bimonthly) to review progress, protocol adherence, and operational issues.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

The DMC will be independent of the trial team and sponsor, consisting of two statisticians with expertise in clinical trial methodology and one clinician experienced in pediatric anesthesia. Operating under a predefined charter, the DMC will review unblinded safety data monthly (and ad hoc if needed), assess data integrity and protocol adherence from a safety perspective, and make recommendations to the Trial Steering Committee and Sponsor on whether the trial should continue, be modified, or stopped. Blinded summary reports will be shared with the TSC and Sponsor, while detailed unblinded analyses will remain within the DMC to maintain trial integrity.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

The anesthesia protocols for patients in this project have undergone extensive clinical validation and will be administered by experienced physicians, ensuring manageable risk. Adverse events will be promptly addressed following standard procedures, with patients monitored until full resolution or treatment termination. Severe adverse approximatelyevents will be reported to the ethics committee immediately.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The Project Management Group, composed of two anesthesiologists (responsible for daily operations) and one research nurse (responsible for participant recruitment), will meet biweekly to review trial progress, including recruitment status, adherence to intervention protocols, and completeness of data records. The TSC will meet bimonthly to review trial progress, protocol adherence, and operational issues. The Independent DMC will review safety and data integrity on a monthly basis. Although this is a low-risk intervention, the DMC will provide independent oversight to ensure participant safety and data quality. All audit reports will be documented and filed in the TMF.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties {25}

Any substantive protocol change will first be submitted to the Sponsor for review and approval, followed by Ethics Committee/IRB review as required. Once approved, the PI will ensure that the revised protocol, including version number and date, is filed in the Investigator Site File and that all relevant study staff are trained accordingly. Any deviations from the protocol will be fully documented using a breach/deviation report form. In addition, the protocol record in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) will be updated within 15 days of approval.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The study results will be presented at local or international anesthesia conferences and be published in peer-reviewed journals. The dataset analyzed in the current study will be obtained from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Discussion

This randomized controlled, double-blind, dose-finding trial will determine the ED50 and ED95 values of esketamine combined with propofol for intravenous sedation in pediatric circumcision. This will provide further reference for the precise administration of this combination in pediatric circumcision procedures.

Circumcision is a painful procedure, and often conducted in children [2]. Due to the children’s inadequate cooperation during the treatment process, general anesthesia is frequently employed to ensure procedural success. Propofol is a sedative with advantages of rapid onset, brief duration and ease of titration, currently considered a commonly used short-acting intravenous anesthetic for general anesthesia in children [22, 23]. However, propofol exhibits a pronounced inhibitory effect on the respiratory and circulatory systems, thereby restricting its sole application scope [24].

Combined anesthesia has the potential to enhance strengths, overcome weaknesses, and complement each agent's effects effectively. Esketamine, the right-handed structural variant of ketamine, is renowned not only as a sedative drug but also for its potent analgesic properties. Furthermore, its sympathomimetic characteristics effectively counteract propofol’s inhibitory effects on hemodynamics, thereby minimizing risks to the cardiovascular and respiratory systems. This versatility makes esketamine a valuable option for anesthesia and analgesia in various clinical procedures and brief examinations [9, 25]. In recent years, several researchers have utilized a combination of esketamine and propofol for pediatric anesthesia, yielding impressive clinical outcomes [11, 26]. However, the optimal effective dose for combining esketamine and propofol in pediatric circumcision has not yet been explored. The findings of this study are expected to validate the efficacy and safety of combining esketamine with propofol in pediatric circumcision, while also establishing an optimal initial dosage.

Regarding the assessment of sedative and analgesic responses, this study will use the Ramsay scale, which offers advantages such as simplicity, reliability, immediate feedback, and wide applicability compared to other assessment tools [27]. In addition, this study will employ a group allocation method for dose-effect comparison. Compared to the Dixon sequential method, the group allocation approach, with its larger sample size, will ensure balanced sample distribution across groups, thereby minimizing experimental errors. This approach will enhance the reliability and accuracy of the experimental outcomes, providing a robust framework for evaluating dose-response relationships [28].

Given the extensive sample size and sufficient dose gradient in this study, a probit regression approach provides a strong statistical framework for determining ED50 and ED95. Probit regression enables precise estimation of effective doses through linear fitting of the dose-response curve, significantly enhancing the accuracy of dose-effect relationship descriptions. Based on a cumulative probability model, this method not only generates reliable confidence intervals but also effectively minimizes bias that may arise from uneven dose distribution, thus strengthening the robustness of statistical results. As it accommodates binary response variables, probit regression is highly suitable for handling efficacy differences across dose groups in grouped experimental designs, making it a dependable dose assessment tool for pharmacological and clinical studies where positive and negative responses are common [29, 30].

The BIS (bispectral index) can accurately forecast the level of sedation and hypnosis by conducting a stepwise regression analysis of electroencephalogram (EEG) waveforms observed during the transition from wakefulness to sleep, serving as a reliable indicator for assessing the depth of anesthesia in children [31]. However, it has been shown that intravenous ketamine administered under general anesthesia may affect the BIS value of children in a dose-dependent manner [32]. Furthermore, according to certain scholars, the brain development of children reaches maturity and the formation of complete synapses persists until the age of 5. Consequently, variations in EEG activity among children differ significantly from those observed in adults, and these differences are also evident among children of varying ages. Currently, there is inadequate evidence to support the use of BIS and clinical depth of anesthesia in pediatric anesthesia [33]. Therefore, this study will not use BIS to monitor sedation levels.

Several limitations of this study warrant attention. Firstly, according to previous studies, the initial dose of intravenous propofol in this study will be set at 3 mg/kg [11, 34]. It has been shown that the ED50 value of propofol decreased when co-administered with increasing doses of esketamine [12, 35]. Therefore, the equilibrium point between the optimal clinical effect and minimal adverse effects across varying doses of propofol and esketamine needs to be further explored. Secondly, this study primarily focuses on the sedative and analgesic effects of esketamine in the short-term perioperative period and does not address longer-term outcomes (such as 1–3 days postoperatively) or potential adverse effects. Extended follow-up may provide additional insights into the durability of the sedation regimen and potential long-term side effects.

In conclusion, this dose-finding study using a group allocation method will determine the ED50 and ED95 values of intravenous esketamine combined with propofol for sedation in pediatric circumcision. These findings will provide valuable clinical data for routine practice.

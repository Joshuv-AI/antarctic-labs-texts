Enhancing renewable energy utilization and energy management strategies for new energy yachts
Authors: Zhe Wang, Mingyu Li, Ning Cao, Pengzhi Liao, Yulong Ji, Fenghui Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHydrogen energy, due to its clean and efficient nature, has shown great potential during the current transition period in the shipbuilding industry. However, the application of hydrogen energy in ship energy systems is influenced by variations in operational load and the integration of new energy sources during actual navigation. To address these issues, this paper focuses on optimizing and scheduling the operation of ships under various navigation conditions, considering the distributed nature of hydrogen energy. System simulations were conducted to model the photovoltaic (PV), proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs), lithium batteries (LIBs), electrolytic cells (ECs), and energy storage modules of yacht energy systems. Component boundaries and objective functions were set, and two cases (excess photovoltaic state and constant power state) were designed to optimize and regulate the energy balance of hydrogen-powered yachts, enhancing their comprehensive utilization of renewable energy. By comparing the changes in ship energy under the two cases, it was concluded that case 1 ensures the maximum utilization of renewable energy. When photovoltaic power generation is insufficient, the PEMFC and LIB in the system provide the required power to achieve a supply-demand balance. Moreover, when PV power generation is sufficient, hydrogen energy is used to store renewable energy. The optimization method designed in this study can, to some extent, maximize the application of renewable energy in new energy yachts, ensuring the efficiency of the comprehensive energy system of new energy yachts, reducing emissions, and improving the sustainability and economic efficiency of the ships.Keywords: New energy yachts, Hydrogen-powered, Proton exchange membrane fuel cell, Photovoltaic, Energy management

Introduction

In 2023, the global shipping industry emitted over 1 billion tons of CO2, accounting for approximately 3 % of the world's total greenhouse gas emissions [1]. If effective measures are not taken, the industry's carbon dioxide emissions are projected to exceed 5 billion tons by 2050 [2]. To address this challenge, the development of efficient and clean ship energy systems has become crucial for the green development of the shipping industry. Traditional ship energy systems often operate multiple independent energy systems, suffer from low efficiency at various energy utilization levels, and face challenges in integrating renewable energy sources such as wind and solar power [3]. These issues significantly affect the effectiveness of energy conservation and emission reduction efforts. Therefore, it is of great practical significance to strengthen the optimization of comprehensive energy systems for new energy ships and to explore optimal energy dispatch strategies [4]. This can enhance the energy utilization efficiency during ship operation and contribute to the sustainable development of the maritime industry.

Hydrogen energy, as a clean and efficient energy source, shows great potential in the application of comprehensive ship energy systems [5]. As the core technology for hydrogen utilization, hydrogen fuel cells can directly convert hydrogen energy into electrical energy, providing continuous and stable power for ships [6]. Additionally, hydrogen storage systems can store and regulate energy, further enhancing the flexibility and reliability of ship energy systems [7]. However, the optimization and regulation of comprehensive energy systems in hydrogen-powered ships is a complex and critical issue. During navigation, ships are influenced by various factors such as speed, load weight, and sea conditions, all of which directly affect energy consumption and distribution [8]. Therefore, developing optimized control strategies to achieve efficient hydrogen utilization, reduce energy consumption and emissions, and enhance the performance and economic efficiency of ships has become a current research focus and challenge.

Given the prosperity of the marine economy and the sustainability of the marine ecological environment, the importance of comprehensive energy systems for ships is increasingly prominent [9]. In recent years, research on the theoretical frameworks and practical methods of these systems has deepened and expanded from various perspectives, becoming a focal point and new trend in the shipping industry [10]. As an innovative solution, comprehensive energy systems integrate multiple energy networks, facilitating the production, conversion, storage, and consumption of various energy forms [11]. The design concept of such systems aims to achieve the coordinated operation of diverse heterogeneous energy sources, meeting the diverse energy demands of users and promoting sustainable energy development [12]. Thirunavukkarasu et al. [13] proposed the concept of comprehensive energy systems and implemented it in certain regions, providing a model for the research and development of such systems. Angelidis et al. [14] summarized the future trends of energy networks, indicating a shift towards the close interaction and coupling between various energy forms such as electricity, natural gas, cooling, and heating. Li et al. [15] analyzed and discussed integrated energy systems involving electricity, heat, and electricity-gas interactions. Zhang et al. [16] focused on regional-level comprehensive energy systems, aiming to integrate and optimize regional energy supply systems with information systems and transportation systems. Wang et al. [17] through their work on multi-energy complementary integration optimization and electric power substitution combined with policy support and demonstration projects, have made the development direction of energy more clear.

In the context of the rapid growth of the shipping industry and the marine economy, the carbon emissions generated by ships during long-distance operations pose unprecedented challenges to the marine ecological environment [18]. This phenomenon underscores the importance of research on optimizing and scheduling ship energy systems, especially new energy systems for ships [19]. However, current research in this field is relatively sparse, and the task of energy conservation, consumption reduction, and emission control is urgent.

Currently, ship propulsion systems can be mainly divided into four categories: steam turbines, diesel engines, gas turbines, and electric propulsion [20]. Among them, diesel engines dominate, accounting for 95 % of ship propulsion systems. However, the combustion of traditional fossil fuels by diesel engines produces pollutants that severely damage the marine ecological environment [21]. Consequently, scholars in the field of ship energy have shifted their research focus towards innovative energy supply models, striving to apply more renewable energy sources to ship energy systems [22].

Ben et al. [23] conducted systematic research on ship sail technology, adding sail-assisted propulsion to existing power systems, which can be adjusted based on wind direction to reduce energy consumption. Dolatabadi et al. [24] developed a new energy ship system that combines wind and solar energy. Sánchez et al. [25] used zero-carbon fuels such as hydrogen and ammonia in combination with fuel cells to power electric ships. Iqbal et al. [26] analyzed the power configuration of electric ships and the effective integration of renewable energy sources.

However, despite the various applications of renewable energy such as solar and wind energy in ship energy systems, relying solely on renewable energy as the primary energy supply mode for ships cannot fundamentally provide continuous high-quality energy supply [27]. The integration of different energy sources is currently the optimal energy supply model. By integrating renewable energy and hydrogen energy through energy storage devices, the mutual conversion between renewable energy and hydrogen energy can be promoted, achieving the stepwise utilization of different quality energy and reducing greenhouse gas emissions [28]. Therefore, optimizing the scheduling of ship comprehensive energy systems to reduce energy consumption, enhance energy efficiency, and achieve green navigation is crucial. However, due to the significant distributed nature of hydrogen energy in ships, the centralized optimization scheduling models and methods suitable for traditional ship energy systems are no longer applicable [29].

To address these issues, this paper focuses on optimizing and scheduling the operation of ships under various navigation conditions, considering the distributed nature of hydrogen energy. The operational characteristics of core equipment are explored, and an optimization scheduling model for the hydrogen-powered yacht energy system is established, aiming for the lowest total energy consumption and the highest energy operation efficiency. Effective scheduling strategies are also investigated. The study primarily addresses how to maximize the utilization of renewable energy and hydrogen energy while meeting the load demands of critical equipment, thereby enhancing overall energy efficiency. The theoretical analysis in this paper delves into the deeper aspects of energy system optimization by integrating advanced mathematical models and simulation techniques. By considering real-time data and dynamic operational conditions, the research identifies the optimal points for energy conversion and storage, ensuring that renewable sources are utilized to their fullest potential. Furthermore, the paper introduces innovative scheduling algorithms that adapt to varying environmental and operational scenarios, providing a robust framework for energy management in hydrogen-powered yachts.

Innovation in this research is highlighted through the development of a novel multi-objective optimization approach that balances energy consumption, emissions, and operational performance. This includes the integration of photovoltaic (PV) systems, proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs), lithium batteries (LIBs), and electrolytic cells (ECs) into a cohesive energy management strategy. The paper also explores the synergy between these components, demonstrating how their coordinated operation can significantly reduce the carbon footprint of maritime activities. Additionally, the study provides actionable insights into energy scheduling and load management, ensuring that the hydrogen-powered yachts operate efficiently under different conditions. This comprehensive approach not only enhances the theoretical understanding of energy optimization in marine applications but also offers practical solutions for achieving sustainability and efficiency in the maritime industry.

System and model

Yacht energy system

To achieve the effective application of renewable energy in ships, this paper explores a renewable energy power system for yachts, as depicted in Fig. 1. The system comprises photovoltaic panels (PV), lithium-ion batteries (LIB), a yacht energy management system (YEMS), hydrogen production electrolysis cells (HPEC), hydrogen storage tanks (HST), and proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFC). Together, these components provide the necessary power load for the yacht. The primary objective is to maximize the use of renewable energy and achieve net-zero emissions. The study aims to determine optimal control strategies for hydrogen-powered ships, ensuring efficient hydrogen energy utilization, reducing energy consumption and emissions, and enhancing the performance and economic efficiency of the vessel.

Schematic diagram of the yacht energy management system.

The ample deck space on yachts provides an excellent opportunity for installing PV panels. These panels capture solar energy and convert it into direct current (DC) electricity. The DC electricity is then transformed into alternating current (AC) suitable for onboard equipment and systems via inverters and other devices. During cruising, yachts can utilize solar power for propulsion. By installing electric motors compatible with the PV system, yachts can navigate using solar energy, reducing reliance on traditional fuels, lowering operating costs, and decreasing carbon emissions. In addition to direct propulsion, the PV system can store excess electricity in LIBs. The high energy density, long lifespan, and rapid charge-discharge rates of LIBs ensure a stable and reliable power supply for onboard systems, whether the yacht is docked or operating under extreme conditions. When it comes to HPEC and PEMFC power generation, yachts typically require only a small amount of electricity when docked or operating under low load. During these times, excess solar power can be used for water electrolysis to produce hydrogen. The electrolysis equipment converts electricity into hydrogen, which is stored in HST. When solar output is low or during extreme conditions, the PEMFC can combine hydrogen with air to generate electricity, providing power for the yacht. This method not only enhances energy utilization efficiency but also improves the yacht's energy self-sufficiency.

To provide a comprehensive understanding of our advanced energy management approach for yachts, it's essential to contextualize it within traditional energy management strategies. Traditional maritime energy management typically includes load following and peak shaving, economic dispatch, time-of-use (TOU) pricing, demand response programs, use of backup generators, and hybrid systems integration. Load following adjusts power generation based on real-time demand, while peak shaving reduces power consumption during peak periods, optimizing energy use and minimizing costs. Economic dispatch allocates generation resources to minimize operational costs while meeting load demands, ensuring efficient power system operation. TOU pricing varies electricity costs based on the time of day, encouraging off-peak usage and balancing grid load. Demand response programs incentivize reduced or shifted electricity usage during peak periods through financial incentives or time-based rates, contributing to grid stability and efficiency. Traditionally, diesel or gas generators serve as backup power sources to ensure reliability and continuous operation during outages or high demand periods. Hybrid systems integration, combining traditional energy sources with renewables, enhances energy security and reduces emissions, adapting traditional strategies for modern sustainability goals.

However, these traditional strategies face significant challenges when integrating renewable energy sources. Reliance on fossil fuels results in higher emissions and environmental impact, while the inflexible nature of traditional systems makes it difficult to incorporate intermittent renewables like solar and wind. Moreover, integrating renewables into existing energy management systems increases complexity and requires advanced management techniques to maintain stability and reliability. This research aims to address these limitations by optimizing and scheduling the operation of yachts under various conditions, focusing on efficient use of hydrogen energy, reducing energy consumption and emissions, and improving performance and economic efficiency. This advanced approach demonstrates significant improvements over traditional methods, ensuring a sustainable and eco-friendly operation for new energy yachts.

Model verification

To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the electrical models for lithium-ion LIB and PEMFC, a detailed validation process was conducted by comparing their U-I characteristics with empirical data [32,36]. For the LIB, U-I characteristics were obtained under various charge and discharge conditions, and the simulated U-I curves were compared with data sourced from current latest literature [32]. The high correlation observed, with deviations consistently within a 6 % margin, confirms the robustness of the LIB model. Similarly, for the PEMFC, the model's U-I characteristics were validated by simulating its performance under different operating conditions, including varying temperature, pressure, and hydrogen flow rates. The simulated U-I curves were then compared with data from existing PEMFC literature [36], revealing a strong agreement with discrepancies also within a 7 % range. This validates the accuracy of the PEMFC model. These validation results emphasize the reliability of the models, ensuring that the predicted performance and efficiency of the energy management system are grounded in accurate and credible data. Incorporating validated U-I characteristics reinforces the theoretical foundation of the study and enhances the credibility of the proposed energy management strategies.

The model's accuracy was validated through implementation in the MATLAB environment. Utilizing data and constraints from Ref. [41], which encompass the 24-h power load of a specific sea area vessel, the performance of the model was compared, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The simulation results demonstrated that the error was consistently within 5 %, showcasing the model's robustness in accurately predicting the energy dynamics of the vessel. This high level of accuracy indicates that the model effectively balances the input and output energy, providing a reliable representation of the vessel's energy management system. In addition to verifying the energy balance, the model also accounted for various operational scenarios, including periods of high and low energy demand. During these periods, the model successfully demonstrated the system's ability to maintain energy equilibrium, leveraging renewable energy sources and energy storage mechanisms effectively.

1)Excess photovoltaic case

Excess photovoltaic case

Fig. 6 depicts the power balance of excess PV hydrogen production in Case 1. According to the yacht's power demand throughout the day. From 0:00 to 5:00, the PV panels are not operational since the sun has not risen, and the power generated by the yacht is insufficient to meet the load demand. During this period, both the LIBs and the PEMFCs must work together to provide the necessary power. The LIBs discharge to supply part of the power, while the PEMFCs consume hydrogen from the storage tanks to provide the remaining power. This combination ensures the yacht can operate steadily even without PV generation. From 7:00 to 16:00, the PV panels begin to operate, supplying power to the yacht's load and generating excess PV power. During this period, the PV panels not only meet the yacht's power demands but also produce surplus power, which can be entirely used for hydrogen production. All the power generated by the yacht is stored in the LIBs. This way, the abundant PV power during the day can meet current power demands and reserve energy for nighttime and periods without PV generation. From 17:00 to 24:00, as the sun sets, the PV panels gradually cease operation. Initially, the LIBs discharge to supply power to the yacht's load. When the battery power is insufficient, the PEMFCs take over, continuously supplying power to the yacht's load. This scheme makes full use of the PV panels' daytime generation capacity while ensuring stable power supply during the night and periods when the PV panels are not operational through energy storage devices. This not only enhances energy utilization efficiency but also ensures the stability and reliability of the yacht's 24-h operation.

Constant power case

Fig. 9 presents a detailed analysis of the energy balance of a yacht over a 24-h period in the constant power hydrogen production case 2. From midnight to 5 a.m., the yacht's energy management strategy is similar to Case 1. During this period, the limited sunlight results in minimal power generation from the PVs, necessitating reliance primarily on LIBs to power the yacht's loads. Additionally, if hydrogen reserves are sufficient, the PEMFCs will also be activated to either charge the LIBs or directly supply power to the yacht's loads, maintaining the balance of the energy system. However, at specific times such as 7 a.m., 8 a.m., and 4 p.m., the yacht enters a critical energy transition phase. Due to the constant electrolysis hydrogen storage strategy, when the power provided by the PVs is insufficient to meet the hydrogen production demand, the yacht's grid needs to supply additional power to support this process. This additional power requirement increases the overall load power of the yacht, but it is essential to ensure stable hydrogen production and provide sufficient energy reserves for subsequent voyages.

3)Comparative analysis

Comparative analysis

To highlight the superiority of the proposed energy management strategies for hydrogen-powered yachts, we compared the results of Case 1 and Case 2 with traditional energy management approaches. Case 1, the energy management strategy focuses on maximizing the utilization of renewable energy without increasing the yacht's propulsion load. This approach maintains an energy balance primarily through PV energy and hydrogen storage. When PV output is insufficient, the system leverages LIB and PEMFC to meet energy demands. This strategy ensures high operational efficiency and aligns with environmental goals by minimizing carbon emissions. In contrast, traditional strategies heavily rely on fossil fuel engines, which are less efficient and produce significant emissions. The reliance on renewable energy in Case 1 not only reduces operational costs by lowering fuel consumption but also enhances sustainability through zero-emission operations. Case 2 involves increasing the system's power load during periods of low PV output to facilitate hydrogen production. This ensures a stable energy supply but at the cost of higher energy consumption and emissions due to the additional load on the propulsion system. Despite these drawbacks, Case 2 still outperforms traditional methods by integrating renewable energy sources, thus reducing the overall dependency on fossil fuels. Traditional strategies, which lack such integration, incur higher fuel costs and maintenance expenses and contribute to greater environmental pollution. By incorporating hydrogen production into the energy management system, Case 2 enhances energy storage capabilities and provides a more reliable power supply, albeit with lower efficiency compared to Case 1.

Traditional energy management strategies for yachts typically rely on combustion engines and basic battery systems, leading to lower efficiency, higher emissions, and increased operational costs. In comparison, our proposed strategies in both cases leverage renewable energy sources and advanced storage technologies. Case 1 demonstrates superior operational efficiency and environmental benefits, making it the most sustainable option. Case 2, while less efficient, still offers significant improvements over traditional methods by integrating renewable energy and hydrogen production, thereby reducing fuel dependency and emissions. In summary, both proposed cases showcase the potential to significantly enhance energy utilization and sustainability compared to traditional strategies. By optimizing the integration of renewable energy and advanced storage solutions, our strategies provide a viable pathway towards greener and more efficient maritime operations.

Conclusions

This paper explores the integration of renewable photovoltaic (PV) energy and hydrogen fuel into the energy system of yachts, comparing two different operational schemes and energy management strategies through energy flow analysis and operational efficiency to optimize energy utilization and minimize emissions. The main findings and impacts are as follows.

This scheme ensures maximum utilization of renewable energy during periods of insufficient PV output without increasing the yacht's power load. By avoiding an increased power load, Case 1 aligns closely with the goals of environmental friendliness and low carbon emissions. It primarily maintains energy balance through renewable energy and hydrogen storage without the need for additional propulsion load, demonstrating superior operational efficiency. This approach optimizes the overall efficiency of the energy system, ensuring sustainable and eco-friendly operation.

This scheme involves increasing the system power load during periods of low PV output to facilitate hydrogen production. While this strategy ensures a stable energy supply, it results in higher energy consumption and emissions due to the additional load on the propulsion system. By integrating the propulsion system into the hydrogen production process, Case 2 effectively maintains a continuous energy supply but reduces efficiency and increases environmental impact, leading to greater energy consumption and emissions.

In conclusion, the comparative analysis of the two energy management schemes for new energy yachts underscores the importance of optimizing energy utilization to achieve sustainability and efficiency. Case 1 emerges as the more environmentally friendly and efficient strategy, maximizing renewable energy use while minimizing emissions. Future research should focus on further enhancing the integration of renewable energy systems and developing advanced energy management strategies to support the transition towards greener maritime operations.

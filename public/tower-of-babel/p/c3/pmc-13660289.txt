Predicting placenta accreta spectrum antenatally: a deep learning-radiomics dual-model based on intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria
Authors: Yumin Hu, Chenqi Shen, Di Shen, Xiaojun Chen, Hongyan Zhao, Xia Li, Qing Zhou, Feng Shi, Yechao Huang, Wensong Xu, Chenying Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeDefinitive diagnosis of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) disorders occurs at delivery by using combined intraoperative clinical and histopathologic diagnostic criteria per the recently updated International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) classification system. The purpose of this study is to construct a dual diagnostic model based on intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria for PAS in accordance with FIGO’s diagnostic criteria.Materials and methodsThis study pooled data from two centers. Diagnostic models based on histopathologic and intraoperative clinical criteria were separately developed using radiomics, deep learning, and clinical features. Subsequently, these individual models were integrated. Moreover, the correlations among clinical indicators, radiomics, and deep-learning features were explored. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables with non-normal distribution. Categorical data were analyzed by Fisher’s exact test or 2 × 2 or R×C χ2 test. A P value < 0.05 was considered significant.ResultsFor the FIGO intraoperative clinical criteria model, the fusion model’s AUC was 0.964 (0.946–0.982) in the training set and 0.949 (0.902–0.996) in the test set. For the FIGO histopathologic criteria model, the training-set AUC was 0.961 (0.940–0.982), and the validation cohort AUC was 0.936 (0.883–0.990). In addition, several clinical factors and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) features of PAS were found to correlate with radiomics scores and deep learning scores in both the intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria models.ConclusionIn this study, we constructed a dual model that includes FIGO intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria for predicting PAS. The two models that integrate deep learning, radiomics, and clinical features have good performance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02538-2.Keywords: Placenta accreta spectrum, Deep learning, Histopathologic criteria, Intraoperative clinical criteria, Radiomics

Introduction

Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) disorders are a major cause of maternal morbidity and mortality and are increasing in incidence owing to a rising rate of repeat cesarean sections. The incidence of PAS disorders has increased approximately 10-fold in the past 50 years [1, 2]. Definitive diagnosis of PAS disorders is made at delivery using combined intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria per the recently updated International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) classification system. The FIGO classification system comprises histopathologic and intraoperative clinical grading criteria [3–8]. Intraoperative clinical diagnostic criteria are determined by obstetricians based on intraoperative findings, whereas histopathologic diagnostic criteria are established based on postoperative surgical specimens [3, 9]. Both FIGO grading systems (intraoperative clinical and histopathologic) are equally crucial for PAS management. In clinical practice, the intraoperative clinical and histopathologic grading diagnoses of the same PAS patient may be inconsistent, and significant discrepancies may even exist between the two. Histopathologic grading criteria are indeed critical for determining the depth of placental invasion into the myometrium. While histopathologic grading is important for determining placental invasion depth, it has limitations: limited value in managing retained placenta [10], potential underestimation due to inadequate intraoperative sampling of the uteroplacental interface, and a lack of data from patients with mild but extensive invasion who do not undergo hysterectomy [6]. In response, FIGO proposed an alternative intraoperative clinical classification for placental adherence at delivery [3], but core diagnostic challenges persist.

Accurate antenatal diagnosis and multidisciplinary management are critical for guiding treatment and ensuring optimal outcomes in PAS patients [11]. Ultrasound (US) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are the current imaging modalities of choice for detecting PAS, as both employ noninvasive and nonionizing diagnostic techniques [12]. However, as has emerged from recent studies, the diagnostic accuracy of PAS detection using US and MRI largely depends on the extensive experience of sonographers and radiologists [13, 14]. AI-based medical imaging methods primarily include radiomics and deep learning. Currently, some studies have applied radiomics to the diagnosis of placental accreta spectrum diseases [15–23]. However, it remains unclear whether intraoperative clinical or histopathologic criteria were used to define PAS in many of these studies [24]. Many studies have combined intraoperative clinical and histopathologic diagnoses in a single study, while others focus solely on histopathologic diagnosis. In addition, there is not much research on the application of deep learning and radiomics comparison and fusion in predicting PAS.

In our study, we aimed to construct a dual model incorporating FIGO intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria for predicting PAS. We primarily applied AI methods, including radiomics and deep learning, and incorporated both MRI imaging features and clinical data.

Materials and methods

Pregnant women

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) of both centers with a waiver of informed consent (Approval No.2026(I)-052-01). Placental MRI and clinical data were collected from pregnancies suspected of PAS before cesarean section at Institution 1 (n = 215; September 2019-December 2023) and Institution 2 (n = 200; January 2019-March 2024).

Inclusion criteria: (1) Singleton pregnancies with suspected PAS disorders who underwent prenatal MRI for placental assessment before cesarean delivery; (2) MRI scans were of diagnostic quality; (3) Complete clinical and pathological data. Exclusion criteria: (1) Inadequate image quality; (2) Incomplete clinical and pathological data; (3) Lack of preoperative sagittal HASTE sequences. Ultimately, 207 pregnancies from Institution 1 and 194 from Institution 2 were included in the study. The diagnostic criteria for PAS are key bases for grouping in this study. All intraoperative clinical and pathological diagnoses of PAS in this study were in accordance with FIGO’s intraoperative clinical and histopathologic diagnostic criteria. According to FIGO intraoperative clinical diagnostic criteria, the 207 cases from Institution 1 were categorized into 130 PAS-positive cases and 77 PAS-negative cases. Similarly, the 194 cases from Institution 2 were categorized into 100 PAS-positive and 94 PAS-negative cases. Concurrently, based on FIGO histopathologic diagnostic criteria, 68 PAS-positive and 77 PAS-negative cases were reselected from Institution 1, and 58 PAS-positive and 97 PAS-negative cases from Institution 2. To increase the sample size and develop a more stable model, cases from both institutions were combined. The 101-case discrepancy represents cases clinically diagnosed with placenta accreta intraoperatively but not confirmed by final histopathology which based on the FIGO intraoperative clinical and histopathologic diagnostic criteria [6]. The 101 discrepant cases (intraoperative PAS-positive but histopathology-negative) were included to construct intraoperative clinical model. We fully recognize that these cases are often accompanied by critical conditions such as severe intraoperative hemorrhage. This is exactly why we established two independent models based on separate diagnostic criteria. Our definitive PAS-negative control group included only cases negative for placenta accreta based on both intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria. This rigorous differentiation underscores the significance of our study. For constructing the dual model, pregnant women were randomly divided into training and validation cohorts at an 8:2 ratio for both the intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria models. The flowchart of the enrollment pathway and study design is shown in Fig. 1. Previously, we developed a radiomic model for PAS using MRI images of 132 pregnant women suspected of the disease, which integrated both pathological and intraoperative clinical diagnoses of PAS [25]. This model integrates pathological diagnosis and intraoperative clinical diagnosis of PAS into one model. Clinical practice has shown that developing separate models is more clinically relevant. Thus, we enrolled additional pregnant women to develop separate models.

The flowchart illustrates the participant enrollment pathway, including the distribution of pregnant women into the training and validation cohorts for both the intraoperative clinical and histopathological criteria models

MR image acquisition

Placental MRI images for all pregnant women were acquired using a 1.5-T MRI scanner (MAGENTOM Area; Siemens) at Institution 1 and a 1.5-T MRI scanner (MAGENTOM Avanto; Siemens) at Institution 2. Both institutions employed 18-channel body array coils for signal reception. The sequence used in this study was sagittal Fourier acquisition single-shot turbo spin-echo (HASTE). MRI sequence parameters for both institutions are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

MRI morphologic assessment of PAS

Two radiologists with 16 and 21 years of diagnostic imaging experience independently evaluated the imaging findings using the RAD-info RSVS system. Both readers were blinded to prior MRI and ultrasound reports, as well as intraoperative and postoperative pathological reports. Any discrepancies were resolved by consensus.This study, based on the joint statement by the European Society of Urogenital Radiology (ESUR) and the Society of Abdominal Radiology (SAR), documented five key MRI features: intraplacental dark T2 bands (IDTB), placental bulge, myometrial thinning/disruption, increased placental vascularity (IPV), and bladder wall interruption [26]. Additionally, based on previous studies, the number of IDTBs, as well as the long diameter (LD), short diameter, and area of the largest intraplacental dark T2 band (LIDTB), were included [27]. Furthermore, the location of IPV and vessel diameter were also included [28]. Cases with a history of cesarean section and a placenta located on the anterior lower uterine wall were specifically documented. The maximum placental thickness was also documented.

Placental tissue segmentation

Automatic segmentation was used to segment placental tissue. Sagittal HASTE sequences were used for both uterine/placental evaluation and placental tissue segmentation. Following automatic segmentation, a radiologist with 12 years of experience verified the placental segmentation and manually delineated any incorrectly segmented sections. This primarily involved removing surrounding decidual structures outside the placental range and delineating any undelineated placental tissue. All ROIs were subject to an independent review conducted by a second radiologist (with > 16 years of experience). If two confirmed radiologists have different opinions on the delineation range of the placenta, they shall jointly discuss and determine it.

Radiomics, deep learning and clinical model development

Confusion matrices for classifying PAS-positive and PAS-negative cases using the clinical, radiomics, DenseNet, ResNet, and fusion models are presented in Supplementary Figure S1 for both cohorts, selected based on established histopathologic and intraoperative clinical criteria.

The Radiomic features were extracted from segmented ROIs analyzed using the PyRadiomics package (version 3.0.1; http://www.radiomics.io/pyradiomics.html) and uAI Research Portal (Shanghai United Imaging Intelligence Co., Ltd., Version: 20231115) [29]. Most radiomic features were defined in accordance with the Image Biomarkers Standardization Initiative (IBSI). In both the histopathologic and intraoperative clinical criteria diagnostic models, a comprehensive set of radiomics features was ultimately extracted from each image. These encompassed first-order (18), shape (14), and texture (72) features.

The optimal penalty coefficient (λ = 3.39 × 10⁻²) was determined using LASSO regression with 10,000 iterations of ten-fold cross-validation. This process selected 21 features with non-zero coefficients, which were then input into a support vector machine (SVM) to develop the radiomic model (see Appendix E1).

To capture the most discriminative placental tissue features, deep learning models with more effective feature extraction capabilities were constructed. DenseNet, a type of convolutional neural network (CNN), comprises four dense blocks, each characterized by dense interlayer connections. DenseNet was selected as the base model for this study due to its unique advantages. ResNet, in contrast, excels at utilizing automatic parameter learning to extract contextual features while preserving three-dimensional information. This property makes it a valuable choice for deep learning tasks similar to ours. Together, these models provide distinct feature extraction approaches, which we believe will significantly enhance our understanding of placental tissue features and ultimately yield more accurate and insightful results (see Appendix E2). The overall workflow is outlined in Fig. 2.

The schematic depicts our 3D DenseNet and ResNet ensemble, wherein input images undergo successive convolution and pooling operations to differentiate between PAS and non-PAS cases. Univariate and multivariate binary logistic regression analyses were performed to identify the most influential and independent clinical factors significantly associated with the outcome. Clinical indicators with a P-value < 0.05 in multivariate binary logistic regression were included in the clinical model development.

The multi-modality fusion model development and comparison

A multimodal fusion model was constructed by integrating the clinical, radiomic, and deep learning models to evaluate their combined value. To generate a composite score, the outputs of all three models were integrated for each participant using multivariate logistic regression. The combined model’s output was converted into a predicted probability (ranging from 0 to 1) using the sigmoid function of logistic regression. The interpretable weights of logistic regression allow for assessing the relative influence of each individual model on the combined output. Its applicability to binary outcomes facilitates clinicians’ understanding and adoption of the model. The model was designed to output both a binary diagnosis and a probabilistic score, thereby enhancing clinicians’ trust and understanding.

We compared the performance of the combined model with the three individual models, employing discriminatory power (AUC) and its classification performance (sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy) as metrics. The calibration curve assessed the predictive performance of the combined model. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to quantify its clinical utility by estimating the net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities. To evaluate the predictive ability and stability of the model, the dataset was randomly split into training and validation cohorts at an 8:2 ratio. Furthermore, we compared the predictions of the four models for two representative cases (one PAS-positive and one PAS-negative).

Construction of the nomogram based on the fusion model

Following careful screening of clinical factors, the clinical indicators derived from multivariate binary logistic regression were combined with three distinct scores: the Rad-score, ResNet-score, and DenseNet-score. The Rad-score was calculated based on radiomic features, which encapsulate a broad range of quantitative information from medical images. The ResNet-score and DenseNet-score, in contrast, were derived from deep features extracted from the ResNet and DenseNet architectures, respectively. These scores represent the complex, high-level features learned by the neural networks.

By integrating these diverse data types, a nomogram—a graphical tool that combines multiple variables into a single, user-friendly format—was developed. It provides a visual representation of the relationship between the input variables (clinical indicators, Rad-score, ResNet-score, and DenseNet-score) and the predicted outcome [30]. This nomogram can serve as a valuable tool in clinical decision-making, enabling healthcare professionals to rapidly and accurately estimate the probability of a specific outcome based on a patient’s individual characteristics. The overall research workflow is summarized in Fig. 3.

The study workflow comprised the following steps: (1) Segmentation of the Regions of Interest (ROI); (2) Construction of intraoperative clinical and histopathological criteria models, each encompassing a clinical model, a radiomics model, and a deep learning model; (3) Fusion of the individual models; (4) Model evaluation using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis to assess predictive performance and clinical utility

Correlation between radiomic/deep learning features and clinical indicators

Pearson correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationships between clinical indicators derived from multivariate logistic regression, radiomic scores, and deep learning scores (ResNet and DenseNet). To effectively visualize and interpret the complex relationships among these variables, an interaction network correlation pie chart was used.

Statistical analysis

For baseline demographic and clinical characteristics, the Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables with non-normal distribution. Categorical data were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test, 2 × 2 χ² test, or R×C χ² test. Statistical analyses were performed using Python 3.10.6. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Missing values were imputed with the median of the respective variable. Screening of clinical features for nomogram construction was performed using binary univariate and multivariate regression in SPSS. Statistical analysis and visualization of the relevant string plots were performed using R version 4.2.1. The R packages involved were igraph [1.3.4] and ggraph [2.1.0].

Results

Model comparison and fusion

After comparing performance, the fusion models for both intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria-based PAS diagnosis significantly outperformed the individual models in both the training and validation cohorts. For the intraoperative clinical criteria model, the fusion model achieved an AUC of 0.964 (95% CI: 0.946, 0.982) in the training cohort and 0.949 (95% CI: 0.902, 0.996) in the validation cohort (Table 2; Figs. 4a, d). Their AUC values were 0.961 (95% CI 0.940–0.982) and 0.936 (95% CI 0.883–0.990) in the training and validation cohorts of histopathologic criteria model, respectively (Table 2; Figs. 5a, d).

Both fusion models (intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria-based) exhibited good calibration in the training and validation cohorts (Figs. 4b and e and 5b and e), with predicted probabilities closely aligning with observed outcomes. Furthermore, decision curve analysis (DCA) showed that the fusion models provided a higher net benefit across most threshold probabilities compared to all individual models (Figs. 4c and f and 5c and f), indicating superior clinical utility.

Discussion

Our study grouped pregnancies suspected of PAS before cesarean section based on FIGO’s intraoperative clinical and histopathologic diagnostic criteria. Based on this grouping, artificial intelligence (AI) methods, including radiomics and deep learning, were used to develop dual fusion models based on intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria.

AI applications in medical imaging primarily include radiomics and deep learning. The main clinical applications of AI in PAS research include preoperative prediction of PAS [31], postpartum hemorrhage [21, 32] and placental invasion depth [33]. Numerous studies have developed AI models to predict PAS versus PAS-negative cases [34]. This is inaccurate for guiding clinical management.

In our study, we constructed a intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria dual model. Furthermore, our study integrated radiomics, deep learning, and clinical features to develop a fusion model. To address the limited predictive performance and suboptimal generalization of individual models, we employed ensemble learning. This approach leveraged the complementary strengths of each model, resulting in enhanced performance for PAS prediction. Additionally, based on previous quantitative studies on PAS signs, we included quantitative data related to LIDTB and IPV as clinical features [27, 28].

Notably, we developed a dual model with excellent performance and stability. The fusion model for the intraoperative clinical criteria achieved AUCs of 0.964 and 0.949 in the training and validation cohorts, respectively. Similarly, the fusion model for the histopathologic criteria achieved AUCs of 0.961 and 0.936 in the training and validation cohorts, respectively The performance of the dual fusion models in this study was validated using calibration and decision curves. Meanwhile, we also explored the correlations among radiomics scores, ResNet scores, DenseNet scores from deep learning, and key clinical indicators. Radiomics and deep learning are highly complementary in medical image analysis. Radiomics provides interpretable, handcrafted features, while Deep Learning offers powerful, automated feature extraction and modeling capabilities. Many AI studies on various diseases adopt the integration of these two methods [35, 36]. We conducted a correlation analysis between deep learning scores, radiomic scores and several clinical features. We found that radiomics features associated with grayscale values were extracted in both intraoperative clinical and histopathologic criteria models, such as original_firstorder_Maximum, NGTDM, GLSZM, GLRLM, GLCM and GLDM. The count of IDTB and the long diameter of the LIDTB represent the MR feature most closely correlated with grayscale. Pathologically, IDTB arise from obliterated villous architecture, erythrocyte extravasation and pyknosis of syncytiotrophoblast nuclei. The intervillous spaces become constricted and subsequently occupied by fibrin deposits. Fibrous tissues and vestigial villous remnants encircle old infarcts [27, 37]. IDTB have been widely recognized in numerous studies as key signs for diagnosing placenta accreta spectrum [38, 39]. Accordingly, these radiomic features are strongly correlated with the underlying pathophysiological changes in placenta. This also indicates that the model we developed is internally consistent and interpretable.

We acknowledge several limitations. Most notably, the sample size remains relatively limited despite our two-center design. To enhance model stability, we combined cases from both centers for model development, resulting in a lack of external validation from a third center. Second, the images analyzed in our study were limited to the sagittal HASTE sequence from Siemens scanners. In the future, multiple sequences will be included for research. Third, the evaluation of imaging features by radiologists might still be subjective.

Based on the FIGO diagnostic criteria for PAS, we constructed histopathologic clinical and histopathological criteria fusion models. This model fused artificial intelligence methods of radiomics, deep learning, and important clinical features. In the future, obstetricians might evaluate the intraoperative condition and pathology of suspected PAS pregnant women separately before caesarean section.

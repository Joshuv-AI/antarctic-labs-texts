Transient Flow model and heterogeneity effect analysis for fracture networks in shale gas horizontal wells
Authors: Wei Xiong, Yong Li, Wei Guo, Hang Yuan, Zejun Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Volume stimulation technique is currently the primary engineering approach for effectively enhancing shale gas productivity. However, in post-fracturing reservoirs, multi-media coupling is prominent, and fracture conductivity exhibits strong heterogeneity. This necessitates further characterization of heterogeneous spatial distribution of complex fractures, as well as multiple flow mechanism. This study develops a multiscale coupled matrix-fracture flow in porous media model based on the post-fracturing reservoir geometry to precisely describe production dynamics in fractured shale gas reservoirs. Heterogeneity in hydraulic fracture geometry and stimulated reservoir volume (SRV) permeability was simulated using continuous gradient functions. Analytical solutions for the multi-stage fractured shale gas reservoir model were derived through multi-linear flow model and perturbation methods. By integrating simulated annealing and particle swarm optimization algorithms, the C++ code was enhanced to achieve automated history matching and production forecasting. Results indicate that hydraulic fracture damage predominantly affects early-stage production, with near-tip fracture blocking leading to reduced overall gas production. Mid-to-late production is more significantly influenced by SRV permeability -gradual permeability variations exhibit minor impacts, whereas abrupt permeability changes substantially decrease total gas production. Based on an analysis of dynamic production data from two wells in the oilfield, this study achieves optimal matching of unknown reservoir parameters, thereby enabling reliable production performance prediction. The proposed model demonstrates high rationality and practicality in reservoir parameter inversion and shale gas well performance forecasting, providing novel insights to critical challenges in shale gas production.

Introduction

China possesses abundant shale gas resources in marine-continental transitional shales with promising exploitation prospects, as demonstrated by established production operations in multiple basins including the Ordos and Qaidam Basins1,2. These transitional shales exhibit complex sedimentary hydrodynamics, where brackish-water depositional environments promote enhanced lamina development, stronger vertical heterogeneity and complex pore structure. In contrast to marine shales, transitional shales exhibit higher desorbed gas content, elevated clay content, and stronger heterogeneity, resulting in more complex fracture networks during hydraulic fracturing. However, conventional flow models inadequately characterize the multiscale pore structures and gas occurrence mechanisms. Therefore, developing a multiscale heterogeneous fracture-network model is essential for accurately predicting shale gas productivity in transitional shales3–6. Studies by Javadpour7 and He8 confirmed molecular diffusion and slip effects in shale nanopores. Zhu Weiyao9 classified shale gas flow mechanisms across five scales from molecular to macroscopic domains. The flow mechanism of shale gas is more complex than that in conventional reservoirs, significantly complicating production forecasting. Shale gas productivity prediction models primarily integrate flow mechanism characterization, multimedia flow modeling, and numerical simulation10. While dual-porosity multi-region composite models are widely adopted, they oversimplify post-fracturing network complexity and fail to fully capture multiscale coupled flow dynamics10.

To address these challenges, researchers have employed composite linear flow models for fractured horizontal wells. The shale gas productivity prediction model developed by Bello11 and El12 was based on dual-porosity transient linear flow. Ted13 and Carlson14 incorporated molecular diffusion in pores and Darcy flow in fractures into their dual-porosity model. Ozkan15 proposed a trilinear flow model for unconventional reservoirs, integrating a dual-porosity model for improved geological realism. Yao Jun16 extended the trilinear flow model by incorporating the threshold pressure gradient effect for fluid flow in unconventional reservoirs. Stalgorova17 advanced a penta-linear flow model to represent permeability variations within SRV region. Yuan18 developed a hepta-linear flow model by sub-zoning the SRV region to incorporate stress sensitivity and fracture density heterogeneity. Current composite linear flow models typically assume constant SRV and fracture permeability, but fails to capture continuous permeability distribution after fracturing. Researchers have employed exponential or linear functions, fractal theory, and random functions to characterize reservoir heterogeneity. Fuentes19 modeled secondary fracture permeability fields using linear/exponential relationships for production decline analysis in fractured horizontal wells. Fan20established a fractal-based trilinear flow model integrating microseismic and production data to characterize fracture network heterogeneity. Wu Yonghui21 ddeveloped a well-test inversion method for heterogeneous fracture parameters using modified Green’s functions, and classified pressure transient responses into six flow regimes: wellbore storage and skin, bilinear flow, matrix linear flow, fracture- interference flow, transitional flow, and boundary-dominated flow. Moreover, the permeability distribution along hydraulic fractures exhibits significant heterogeneity. Post-stimulation observations reveal that minimal proppant delivery to fracture tips reduces conductivity progressively with distance from the wellbore along hydraulic fractures. Luo22 established Laplace-transform-based equations for conductivity variations, while Liu23 delineated three distinct conductivity degradation patterns from wellbore to fracture tip: linear, exponential, and logarithmic. The findings demonstrate that intrafracture conductivity variations can significantly impact well performance. Incorporating fracture heterogeneity enables accurate characterization of flow behavior in vertically fractured wells.

In summary, shale reservoirs present unique simulation challenges due to their low porosity/permeability, complex pore structures, multiscale flow mechanisms, and fracture heterogeneity: (1) Current models fail to accurately characterize production dynamics in fractured horizontal wells, necessitating improved methods that account for fracture conductivity heterogeneity; (2) Current transient production analysis methods for reservoirs with complex flow mechanisms require enhancement through improved characterization of heterogeneous fracture spatial distributions and coupled flow mechanism.

Matrix-fracture coupling model

Model assumptions

The model in this study is primarily applicable to the early-to-middle production stages of hydraulic fracturing in medium-to-deep shale gas reservoirs (burial depth > 1500 m). In such ultra-low permeability reservoirs, single-phase gas flow predominantly occurs along the hydraulic fracture network, and is governed by pressure gradient and fracture-matrix crossflow. Given that the reservoir thickness is much smaller than its lateral extent and the gas viscosity is low (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$<0$$\end{document}.05 cP), the effects of wellbore friction, gravity, and capillary forces can be neglected. Based on these considerations, the multistage hydraulic fracturing in shale reservoirs creates a bilaterally symmetric SRV region, dividing the hydraulic fractures-SRV-matrix system into five zones with either linear or radial gas flow patterns. Equations for the stimulated zone

Shale reservoirs typically contain natural fractures of various scales and orientations. Hydraulic fracturing creates new fractures and reactivates pre-existing natural ones in the reservoir. These natural fractures intersect and interconnect with the newly formed hydraulic fractures, resulting in a highly heterogeneous, complex, and anisotropic fracture network. In this study, the De-Swaan dual-porosity model is employed to simulate fluid flow within this zone, as illustrated in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Radial flow and linear flow in the stimulated zone.

Effect of mobility ratio

In conventional reservoir engineering, the mobility ratio is defined as the ratio of the mobility of the displacing phase to that of the displaced phase. In the single-phase flow model developed in this study, the “mobility ratio” refers specifically to the ratio of shale gas mobility in the hydraulic fracture zone to that in the SRV, serving to characterize the effect of conductivity contrast between these two zones on the pressure transient response. This ratio can be simplified as the permeability ratio of the two zones. To investigate its impact, mobility ratios M of 10, 20, and 40 were considered, and the corresponding pressure transient curves are presented in Fig. 7. The results indicate that the mobility ratio does not alter the integrity of the individual flow regimes, but it significantly affects the magnitude of the dimensionless pressure. Effect of matrix radius

Under conditions where the matrix exhibits low permeability and slow pressure decline, while the fractures have high permeability and rapid pressure drop, a pressure differential develops between the matrix and fractures, inducing matrix-to-fracture crossflow. This crossflow essentially represents the transfer of mass and energy from the matrix system to the fracture system. The presence of crossflow introduces nonlinear characteristics in the pressure transient behavior. Productivity analysis of heterogeneous fracture systems

Permeability heterogeneity within hydraulic fractures significantly influences gas production. During fracturing, stress attenuation reduces fracturing-fluid proppant-carrying capacity with increasing fracture length, creating a graded proppant distribution-coarser particles near the fracture root and finer particles toward the tip. This results in permeability variations along the fracture axis. The stimulated zone mainly consists of secondary fractures formed by hydraulic fracture propagation, surrounding rock deformation, and stress redistribution. Similar phenomena occur during secondary fracture growth.

Influence of hydraulic fracture permeability

To describe the variation of permeability along the fracture length, three fracture damage scenarios were established: undamaged hydraulic fractures, root-blocked fractures, and tip-blocked fractures. The corresponding permeability distributions and production dynamics are shown in Fig. 12 and 13. Comparison of daily gas production curves under these three hydraulic fracture permeability scenarios reveals that fracture blockage significantly reduces production and alters the decline trend. Compared with undamaged fractures, root blockage causes an early-stage production reduction of approximately 74.58%. In contrast, tip blockage has a relatively minor impact initially, but leads to a more rapid decline in mid-to-late production stages, with the daily gas rate eventually falling sharply to the same level as that of root blockage: by 21 years (approximately 9,170 days), daily production decreases by about 14.4% relative to the undamaged fracture case. These results indicate that damage at different fracture locations exerts fundamentally different effects on production dynamics, highlighting that optimizing fracturing operations to prevent root damage is crucial for maintaining high initial production.Fig. 12Permeability distribution curves of hydraulic fractures.Fig. 13Production response curves under different hydraulic fracture permeability distributions.

Model validation and case study analysis

Field data from two representative hydraulically fractured horizontal wells (Well X and Well Y) in the Daning-Jixian block along the eastern margin of the Ordos Basin were analyzed and evaluated. The target reservoir is a marine-continental transitional shale gas interval within the Permian Shanxi Formation, Shan 23 sub-member, characterized by typical low-permeability and limited natural fracture development. Both wells were stimulated using multi-stage, multi-cluster hydraulic fracturing. The number of the fracturing stages and clusters, along with the associated engineering scales provide realistic references for setting the fracture network and flow parameters in this study.

A simulated annealing algorithm was employed to construct a production data analysis framework for these wells. Simulated annealing is an optimization method that searches for the global optimal solution through five key steps: (1) parameter initialization, (2) random solution generation, (3) Metropolis criterion-based acceptance (improved solutions always accepted; inferior solutions probabilistically accepted), (4) temperature reduction via cooling schedule, and (5) termination upon solution stabilization or iteration limits. Particularly effective for complex combinatorial optimization, this method demonstrates strong potential for production data analysis applications.

This method integrates key geological and production parameters-reservoir height, fracture spacing, porosity-permeability characteristics, and flowing bottom-hole pressure-as primary inputs. Inversion parameters-including fracture half-length, permeability, and wellbore storage and skin-were constrained with physically realistic ranges to reduce computational time. The simulated annealing algorithm was implemented to automate reservoir parameter inversion via iterative global optimization, thereby systematically identifying optimal parameter combinations. Additionally, history matching with full-stage production data was conducted to establish a reliable model foundation for subsequent productivity prediction. Conclusions

Based on multi-linear flow theory, this study employs the perturbation method to develop a flow model for marine-continental transitional shale gas that accounts for heterogeneity in both fractures and the SRV. The model refines the multistage matrix-to-fracture crossflow pathways, establishing a simulation and reservoir characterization framework for multi-media flow in hydraulically fractured horizontal wells. By incorporating the permeability modulus and applying the Pedrosa transform along with the perturbation method, the model provides a continuous representation of heterogeneous fracture conductivity, offering strong versatility and applicability.

Based on actual reservoir characteristics and fracturing design parameters, the effects of matrix permeability (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_m$$\end{document}), SRV permeability (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_s$$\end{document}) and fracture half-length on effective drainage distance in unstimulated zones were investigated. Higher \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_m$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_s$$\end{document} lead to larger drainage area and greater drainage distance. However, excessive hydraulic fracture length may reduce drainage distance during production, resulting in insufficient reservoir utilization. The established correlations of effective drainage distance provide theoretical basis for field well spacing optimization.

The influences of hydraulic fracture damage modes and SRV permeability distribution patterns were examined. Hydraulic fracture damage mainly affects early production, with fracture tip plugging causing lower cumulative gas production. SRV permeability significantly impacts mid-late production, where uniform permeability variation has minor effect while abrupt permeability change leads to reduced cumulative production.

A post-fracturing production analysis method for heterogeneous shale gas reservoirs was established. By integrating the simulated annealing algorithm, the C++ program was enhanced to enable intelligent inversion of reservoir parameters, searching for unknown reservoir properties to achieve optimal fitting of actual production data. The method was applied to two field wells, and the fitting results-evaluated using the coefficient of determination and Pearson correlation coefficient-confirmed the accuracy of the predictions, demonstrating that the proposed model provides a reasonable basis for parameter inversion and well performance forecasting.

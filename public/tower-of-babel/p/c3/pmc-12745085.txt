Multiple applications of the Florida sleeve procedure in aortic root surgery
Authors: Liu Chenyu, Li Haochao, Chen Pengfei, Wang Liqing, Luo Xinjin
Published: 2025

Sleeve cuff effect in the Florida Sleeve procedure caused by large aortic valve annulus.

Central Message

Florida sleeve procedure (FS) preserves the aortic valve, but not adopted widely. we introduced multiple applications of FS in aortic root surgery to promote further application of FS surgery.

The Florida sleeve procedure (FS) is a valve-sparing aortic root procedure whose elegant technique has already been well described.1 Compared with other aortic root operations such as the David or Yacoub procedures, FS entails lower technical difficulty and risk. A propensity score−matched study has established its initial safety and efficacy.2 However, FS has not achieved the same widespread acceptance as the David operation and is currently performed at only a limited number of centers. At our institution, FS was introduced in 2022. Until now, we have attempted the procedure in 9 patients, all operated on by a single surgeon. Eight operations were completed successfully, whereas 1 was unsuccessful. This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee Institute of Fuwai Hospital (no. 2023-2005) in May 2023. All patients provided informed written consent in this study and for the publication of their anonymized data.

The information of the patients who underwent surgery is shown in Table 1. All patients had at least 1 other cardiac surgery except for FS. In the 8 patients with successful FS, the average cardiopulmonary bypass time was 251 minutes, the average aortic occlusion time was 201 minutes, the average intensive care unit stay duration postoperatively was 2 days, and the average total hospitalization duration was 7 days. No perioperative major adverse cardiovascular events were reported. All patients exhibited dilation of the Valsalva sinus, with an average diameter of 45 mm.

Among them, patient 1 was diagnosed with Marfan syndrome. FS was performed using a 30-mm artificial graft, along with 6 double-needle sutures with small pledgets, to anchor the graft beneath the aortic valve leaflets. The distal portion was secured with continuous 5-0 vascular suture.

Patient 2 underwent a Wheat procedure combined with FS as a substitute for Bentall surgery. The advantage was no need for coronary reimplantation, which reduced the surgical risk and prevented further expansion of the aortic root, preventing the progression to an aortic root aneurysm.

Patient 6 had a ventricular septal defect with concomitant aortic sinus dilation. FS was performed to prevent further expansion of the sinus. In patients with uncomplicated heart disease and continuous aortic sinus dilation, performing complex surgeries at the same time may not be an optimal choice. FS may provide a similar favorable outcome with simplified procedure.

Patient 7 had a dilated aortic sinus and ascending aorta, with mild aortic regurgitation. After the patient underwent FS plus ascending aorta replacement, the aortic regurgitation resolved during surgery and remained absent during early postoperative follow-up.

Patient 8 had severe aortic regurgitation, with a basal ring diameter of 35 mm and a sinus diameter of 42 mm. The left ventricular end-diastolic diameter was significantly dilated, measuring 79 mm. Preoperatively, FS + Ozaki surgery was planned. During the surgery, a 32-mm artificial graft was used to wrap the aortic root, and fixation of the graft beneath the valve revealed folding of the sinus (Figure 1). This folding would interfere with the opening and closing of the autologous pericardial leaflets during the Ozaki procedure, so the FS procedure was abandoned and the Ozaki procedure was performed alone. However, Ozaki + FS was successfully performed in patient 9 because of the smaller diameter of the annulus. We refer to this phenomenon as the “sleeve cuff effect” (Figure E1). The intraoperative video of patient 8 has been edited and uploaded as Video 1. In all patients who underwent FS surgery, postoperative echocardiography on the first day showed a reduction in both the basal ring diameter and Valsalva sinus diameter. In contrast, patient 8, who did not undergo FS surgery, showed no significant change in the Valsalva sinus diameter. Five patients who underwent FS surgery were followed up with echocardiography for 3 years. Decision-making for aortic root surgery at our center is illustrated in Figure 2. Follow-up data for the patients are provided in the Appendix E1 (Table E1, Table E2, Table E3). Patient 3 developed atrial flutter 1 year postoperatively and was hospitalized for radiofrequency ablation.

Leaflet folding leads to failure of the Florida sleeve surgery. In the intraoperative image shown of patient 8, the aorta was completely incised through the transverse incision of the ascending aorta, after the subvalvular fixation of the artificial vessel, the aortic valve leaflet was distorted and deformed, and the function of the aortic valve was affected. Subsequently, the sleeve was removed and only the Ozaki surgery was performed.

Comment

In this study, we report the first series of 8 patients who underwent FS surgery in mainland China. One patient did not successfully complete the FS. FS demonstrated good short-to medium-term outcomes, with a relatively simplified procedure. In our center, FS surgery has multiple applications: (1) As a supplementary option to the David procedure, it offers a relatively simpler surgical approach with shorter cardiopulmonary bypass and aortic occlusion times. (2) For patients undergoing other cardiac surgeries with concomitant aortic sinus dilation or borderline dilation, FS surgery can prevent further dilation. (3) FS + Ozaki/FS + Wheat procedures can be performed as a substitute for Bentall surgery, avoiding coronary reimplantation and reducing the risk of bleeding and coronary-related complications. (4) For connective tissue diseases, such as Marfan syndrome, FS surgery can prevent long-term aortic dissection. (5) FS surgery may eliminate mild central aortic regurgitation caused by slight annular dilation, thus improving hemodynamics. (6) FS + the Ross procedure can be used to prevent long-term aortic root dilation.

FS surgery preserves the sinuses of Valsalva, and its outcomes are similar to those of the Yacoub procedure in terms of leaflet kinematics.3 It is also applicable to both bicuspid and tricuspid aortic valves.4 As an external reinforcement of the aortic root, the outer membrane of the aorta fuses with the artificial graft, providing the aorta with good resistance to blood flow-induced stress, particularly for patients with connective tissue diseases in whom the vascular media is compromised.

However, as Jahanyar and colleagues5 have noted, FS surgery should not be considered a simple procedure. In addition, the FS procedure should not be considered a replacement for the David or Yacoub procedures, which have established long-term follow-up data. Rather, FS should be regarded as a complementary approach to aortic root surgery. The limitations of this study are detailed in the Appendix E1. Long-term follow-up is still necessary to assess the durability of FS surgery outcomes, and widespread adoption of this procedure should be approached with caution.

Appendix E1. Surgical Technique Details

Florida Sleeve (FS) Procedure

Our technique was essentially consistent with that described by Hess and colleagues,E1, E2 with only minor modifications:

1.After establishing cardiopulmonary bypass and aortic crossclamping, the ascending aorta is transected above the sinotubular junction (STJ) to expose the aortic root. The aortic root is mobilized circumferentially down to the virtual basal ring, with care taken to preserve the integrity of the coronary buttons. The valve cusps are carefully inspected to ensure adequate morphology and coaptation before proceeding. The coronary buttons are mobilized with adequate length to prevent kinking and are reimplanted into prefashioned graft openings at their anatomic locations, with care to maintain a natural angle. The commissures are resuspended at their original height within the graft to restore cusp geometry and coaptation.

Sleeve Cuff Effect

Annular oversizing is closely associated with aortic regurgitation. Our current understanding is that in patients with a large annulus (>35 mm), the aortic root structures are lax, and the anchoring sutures of the FS procedure can lead to more pronounced folding of the aortic sinuses, which we refer to as the “sleeve cuff effect.” The tension transmitted by the sutures is conveyed to the lax aortic sinuses, resulting in folding, similar to the cuff depicted in Figure E1. Therefore, FS should be performed with caution in patients with a large annulus. Due to the limited number of cases, the precise upper limit for annular size in FS procedures remains to be determined.

Ozaki + Florida Sleeve Procedure

Regarding the combination of these 2 procedures, only a few case reports have been published.E3, E4, E5 This combined approach can serve as an alternative to the Bentall procedure. By using autologous tissue, it reduces the need for anticoagulation and preserves the maximal effective orifice area compared with a prosthetic valve. The key to performing this combination is to complete the FS procedure first, followed by the Ozaki procedure, because each anchoring suture in the FS procedure, as described by Hess, reduces the annular diameter by approximately 1 mm. Performing the procedures in the reverse order may result in an oversized neo-valve.

Follow-up Strategy

Clinical Follow-up

Patients are evaluated at discharge, 3 to 6 months postoperatively, and annually thereafter. Follow-up assessments include clinical evaluation for symptoms, New York Heart Association functional class, and occurrence of major adverse cardiac events, including reintervention, heart failure, or sudden cardiac death.

Echocardiographic Follow-up

Transthoracic echocardiography is performed at each follow-up visit to assess aortic valve function, including aortic regurgitation grade and quantitative assessment (regurgitant volume and fraction where feasible), annulus, STJ, and root diameters, and transvalvular gradients.

Advanced Imaging

For patients with connective tissue disorders, large baseline annuli, or rapidly progressive root dilation, computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging is performed periodically to assess the ascending aorta and distal aortic segments for aneurysm formation or dissection.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. The sample size was very small and reflects the early experience at 1 center, which restricts statistical power and generalizability. Follow-up was short, with echocardiographic and clinical data available for only up to 3 years in a subset of patients, so the long-term durability of FS compared with David or Yacoub procedures remains unknown. Patient heterogeneity and the frequent need for concomitant procedures further confound attribution of outcomes to the FS technique alone. In addition, FS was selectively applied without a strict prospective algorithm, introducing potential selection bias. Finally, although we described our graft sizing and technical approach, there is no universally accepted formula for FS, and applicability to patients with connective tissue disorders or markedly enlarged annuli remains uncertain. Nevertheless, our preliminary experience suggests that the FS procedure can serve as a technically less demanding and versatile alternative in selected patients, offering a practical option for surgeons and potentially broadening the applicability of valve-sparing root surgery.

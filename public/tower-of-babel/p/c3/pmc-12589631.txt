Short-term dentin-pulp complex repair with four pulp capping materials: a double-blind randomized crossover histological study
Authors: Alaa Muhamad, Thuraya Lazkani, Ahmad Manadili, Yasser Alsayed Tolibah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the histological short-term response of intact premolars following direct pulp capping (DPC) using four bioactive materials: Neo MTA Plus (NMP), Neo Putty (NP), Total Fill BC Fast set putty (FSP), and Total Fill BC RRM Paste (BCP). Thirty-two intact premolars in eight patients scheduled for orthodontic extraction were included. Teeth were randomly assigned into four groups based on the bioactive material applied during pulpotomy. Each patient will experience the four direct pulp capping materials, randomly assigned to their four premolars: NMP group (n = 8), NP group (n = 8), FSP group (n = 8), and BCP group (n = 8). Premolars were extracted after four weeks. Histological evaluations assessed pulpal inflammation intensity (PII) and dentinal bridge continuity (DBC). Kruskal–Walis and Mann–Whitney U tests were used to analyze the data. Statistical significance was set at α = 0.05. There were significant differences in the PII among groups (p = 0.001). The NMP group was lower than the other three material groups (NP, FSP, and BCP). Moreover, there were significant differences in the DBC among groups (p < 0.001). DBC in the NMP group was lower than that of the other three material groups (NP, FSP, and BCP). Additionally, the DBC in the FSP group was higher than in the NP and BCP groups. The short-term histological examination suggests that the NMP group exhibited the lowest PII but was associated with inferior continuity of the formed dentin bridge. In contrast, the FSP group demonstrated superior continuity of the formed dentin bridge.Keywords: Calcium silicate cements, Dentinal bridge continuity, Direct pulp capping, Pulpal inflammation intensitySubject terms: Pulp conservation, Clinical trial design

Introduction

Dental pulp can be directly exposed in an oral environment due to deep carious lesions, traumatic injuries, or iatrogenic factors1. Direct pulp capping (DPC), a Vital Pulp Therapy (VPT), is considered an efficient and conservative treatment option in which a dental biomaterial is placed directly over the exposed pulp2.

The pulp and dentine work as an integrated functional unit that represents the pulp-dentin complex; each plays a role in repairing the dentin-pulp complex in case of exposure3. The most common and well-known feature of pulp repair is the formation of tertiary dentin, which is distinguished by reactionary and reparative dentin4. Tertiary dentin is a tubular matrix formed following different pathological processes. The intensity of the environmental stimuli may produce two types of tertiary dentin. In response to a mild stimulus (e.g., slowly progressing caries), post-mitotic odontoblast cells are preserved and stimulated to secrete reactionary dentin. Suppose the stimulus is more potent (e.g., rapidly progressing deep caries, tooth cavity preparation or traumatic injury). In that case, the odontoblast cells are destroyed, and a much more complex process is generated to form a new generation of odontoblast-like cells, which will eventually secrete reparative dentin5. VPT and DPC materials enhance biological responses and favour tertiary dentinogenesis by stimulating the odontoblast’s secretory activity. Therefore, various biomaterials have been proposed and developed for pulp capping procedures5.

The inflammation beneath the exposure site intensifies in the early phase following pulp exposure and gradually decreases over time6. Some researchers consider this reduction in inflammation an indication of the material’s biocompatibility, as a material is deemed biocompatible if it enhances cell viability while inducing a diminishing inflammatory response over time7.

The inflammatory process induced by the pulp capping material reflects its ability to create a favourable environment for pulp healing and the formation of mineralized dentin at the exposure site. This inflammatory response is beneficial and necessary if it remains controlled and does not lead to pulp or cellular necrosis8. Several studies have reported that immediately after capping, a significant and acute inflammatory response occurs (Paula et al. 2020), evolving from the initial injury, which involves fibrinogen formation and blood clotting at the exposure site9. The acute phase—severe or mild—is followed by a chronic phase, where immune cell populations shift from neutrophils to lymphocytes and macrophages10.

The ability of a material to induce mineralized tissue formation, known as mineralization-inducing potential, is crucial for forming a dentine bridge to protect the dentin-pulp complex11. The high capacity of calcium silicate-based materials to induce mineralized tissue formation is attributed to their rapid release of calcium and hydroxyl ions, which promote the formation of calcium/phosphate deposits12. These deposits serve as scaffolds for the induction of reparative dentin formation, effectively sealing the exposure site and contributing to tissue healing13.

The quality of the formed dentine bridge is determined by several parameters, including its thickness, the degree of continuity in sealing the exposure site, and its morphology, including the presence of dentinal tubules14.

Given that hydraulic calcium silicate-based cements (HCSCs) have recently replaced calcium hydroxide-based materials (CH) as the preferred choice for vital pulp therapy and are now widely marketed under the term “bioceramics,” their clinical use has expanded considerably15. MTA, the first generation of calcium silicate-based cement (CSC), is considered a suitable pulp-capping agent. However, drawbacks, such as prolonged setting time, tooth colouring, and difficult handling16, lead to the release of derivate materials. Over time, new modified materials based on calcium silicates have been formulated in response to the disadvantages of MTA and to improve the pulpal response.

Neo MTA Plus is a new finer powder tricalcium silicate material. It has tantalum oxide (Ta2O5) as a radiopacifying agent that is mixed with a water-based gel that imparts good handling properties. Neo MTA Plus (Avalon Biomed Inc, Bradenton, FL) is a material with a composition similar to ProRoot MTA but with a shorter setting time, higher ion release, and nonstaining radio pacifiers17. The powder-to-gel mixing ratio can be varied, and a thin consistency can be used as an orthograde sealer or a thick mixture for root-end filling18.

NeoPutty (NuSmile, Houston, TX, USA) is a premixed bioceramic material with an excellent structure and a premixed anhydrous organic liquid packaged as ready-for-use putty19. Some properties of NeoPutty were studied compared with other bioceramic materials, showing superior biocompatible and longer shelf life20, less affected by atmospheric humidity21. In contrast, it showed lower bond strength to dentin walls22 and was mildly cytotoxic to human dental pulp stem cells and human periodontal ligament fibroblasts21.

TotalFill BC RRM is a new material recently introduced as a premixed bioceramic-based material that contains Calcium phosphate, which increases the formation of hydroxyapatite as a monobasic adjunct agent23. The Paste is easy to use and apply and has a short setting time of about 2 h. The Fast Set Putty has an ideal malleable consistency that is easy to apply, so it can be used in various clinical applications, and has a short setting time of about 20 min24.

Most studies on new types of bioceramics have focused on in vitro aspects25–27 or on experimental animals28–31, but few clinical studies of these materials have focused on human teeth32,33. Accordingly, this study was designed to histologically evaluate dentin–pulp complex repair following direct pulp capping in sound human permanent premolars using four novel bioceramic materials: NeoMTA Plus, Neo Putty, TotalFill BC RRM Paste, and TotalFill BC Fast Set Putty.

Materials and methods

Study design, settings, and ethical approval

This randomized double-blinded cross-over histological trial was conducted from January 2021 to August 2024 at the Endodontic Department Faculty of Dentistry, Damascus University, Damascus, Syria. This study was conducted respecting the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Damascus University ethically approved the research project (approve number: DN-160124-180). Damascus University funded the project (funder No. 501100020595). This study was registered in the ISTRCN database (Trial ID: ISRCTN38899178) on 01/05/2024.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using G* Power 3.1.9.4 (Heinrich-Heine-Universität, Düsseldorf, Germany) based on a previous study34 with a similar design. Based on a significance level of 0.05, a power of 85%, and an effect size of 0.67, a minimum total sample size of 32 subjects (8 subjects in each group) was estimated to be sufficient.

Recruitment and eligibility criteria

During the study period, 42 patients aged between 15 and 20 in the Orthodontics Department were identified as requiring the extraction of four mandibular and two maxillary intact premolars as part of their orthodontic treatment plans. Preoperative periapical radiographs were taken to evaluate pulp chamber anatomy, ensuring the chambers were relatively standard size and free from degenerative features such as pulp stones, diffuse calcifications, or severe narrowing. The premolars had to be free from periapical or furcal radiolucencies and pathological conditions.

From the initial pool, 20 patients met these criteria. However, eight patients were excluded due to systemic conditions compromising immune status or clotting ability, hypersensitivity to lidocaine, periodontal disease, open-apex premolars, internal or external resorption, or insufficient bleeding (scant, dark-red bleeding) observed after pulp exposure.

Therefore, thirty-two premolars in eight patients were included in the current research. All patients who agreed to participate in this study signed an informed consent sheet after being explained all the details about the trial and its therapeutic aspects.

Randomization

The four premolars in each patient were randomly allocated to one of four material groups as DPC material: Neo MTA Plus (NMP), Neo Putty (NP), Total Fill BC RRM Fast Set Putty (FSP), or Total Fill BC RRM Paste (BCP). The randomization software (https://www.graphpad.com/quickcalcs/randMenu) was used to randomize the four premolars of each of the eight patients into the four groups, ensuring that each patient underwent all four experimental groups: Neo MTA Plus group (n = 8), Neo Putty group (n = 8), Total Fill BC RRM Fast Set Putty group (n = 8), and Total Fill BC RRM Paste group (n = 8). A researcher (Y.A.T.) how was not involved in the clinical and histological procedures was achieved the randomization process.

Blinding

The present study was conducted as a double-blinded trial. Although the treating clinician could not be blinded to the pulpotomy material used during the intervention, the patients remained blinded. Moreover, treatment histological outcomes were assessed by two calibrated researchers (PhD candidates), who were thoroughly trained in the evaluation criteria and blinded to the type of pulpotomy material applied.

Clinical procedures

Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before participating, following a thorough explanation of the research objectives, methodology, materials used, and scientific purpose. Patients were assured that their medical care would not be affected should they choose not to participate. Documentation explicitly noted that the treated premolars would be extracted after the agreed-upon observation period to fulfil ethical standards.

Then, patients were requested to rinse their mouth with 0.2% chlorhexidine gluconate. Following infiltration of 2% lidocaine containing 1:80,000 epinephrine and placement of rubber dam, a Class I cavity was prepared with a sterile straight fissure diamond bur (#835) with depths similar to the bur length (3mm). Then, the buccal pulp horn of each tooth was exposed through the cavity floor with a 1.5 mm-diameter new round diamond bur (#801). The exposed area was rinsed with sterile saline solution and hemostasis was completed with sterile cotton pellets saturated with sterile saline which was kept in place for 10–20 s.

Subsequently, based on the randomization process, each of the four premolars in every patient was treated as follows:

NMP Group (control sample): Neo MTA Plus (Avalon Biomed Inc, Bradenton, FL): One scoop of powder was mixed with one drop of gel until a putty-like consistency was obtained, and then a sterile plastic instrument was used to apply it to the exposed pulp.

NMP Group (control sample): Neo MTA Plus (Avalon Biomed Inc, Bradenton, FL): One scoop of powder was mixed with one drop of gel until a putty-like consistency was obtained, and then a sterile plastic instrument was used to apply it to the exposed pulp.

NP Group: Neo Putty (NuSmile, Houston, TX, USA) directly applied to the exposed pulp by a sterile plastic instrument.

NP Group: Neo Putty (NuSmile, Houston, TX, USA) directly applied to the exposed pulp by a sterile plastic instrument.

FSP Group: Total Fill BC RRM Fast Set Putty (FKG, LaChaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland) directly applied to the exposed pulp by a sterile plastic instrument.

FSP Group: Total Fill BC RRM Fast Set Putty (FKG, LaChaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland) directly applied to the exposed pulp by a sterile plastic instrument.

BCP Group: Total Fill BC RRM Paste (FKG, LaChaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland) directly injected into the exposed area by the plastic tip attached to the syringe.

For all samples, the bioceramic material was applied with at least 1.5 mm thickness and extension onto the dentine, followed by a liner and immediate composite resin restoration36. All the procedures were done by one calibrated operator (A.M.).

Tooth extraction and histological assessment

By the end of four weeks, pulp sensitivity tests and periapical radiographs were taken by the leading operator, and the teeth were extracted atraumatically by a dental surgeon. The roots were sectioned in the mid-root area, fixed in 10% buffered formalin, and then demineralized in 10% formal-formic acid. After decalcification, samples were hydrated as usual and embedded in paraffin blocks. Then, the blocks were sectioned in a buccolingual plane, and 5 to 10 axial sections with 5μ thicknesses were prepared and stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E). The methodology and the histological preparation were derived from a combination of histological studies32,34,37,38.

The specimens were histologically assessed regarding the continuity and morphology of the dentin bridge and intensity of pulp inflammation, according to the standard37,39,40. using an optical light microscope (BX-43 Olympus) with magnification of 4×, 20×, 40× and 100×. Two observers, blinded to the study groups, analyzed all samples. The observers were well-trained to analyze histological sections. Each degree was investigated for each of the studied premolars and given an increasing value according to Table 2.

Statistical analysis

The analytical, statistical study of the current research data has been completed using (SPSS-Version 13.0) program. The normality of the data distribution was explored using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Data showed abnormal distribution. According to the results, the Kruskal–Walis and Mann–Whitney U tests were conducted to examine the significant differences among the histopathological criteria. p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The mechanism of pulpal wound healing is a dynamic interaction of various factors that have been extensively documented in previous studies. In principle, pulp exposure is always associated with a specific biological response following this sequence: bleeding and blood clot formation, followed by an inflammatory response, then cellular accumulation and migration, ultimately leading to tissue regeneration42. The surrounding environment provided by the pulp capping material plays a crucial role in regulating cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation through cellular signalling and the reciprocal interaction between cells and the extracellular matrix43.

Histological evaluation is the gold standard for testing the pulp’s response to materials used in vital pulp treatments44. Clinical evaluation is often insufficient to accurately describe the pulp reaction due to the difficulty of diagnosing the inflammatory condition clinically45.

The rationale for the short study duration was to assess the ability of the tested materials to repair dentin–pulp complex injury and to determine whether their consistency and setting time influence the speed of healing. To evaluate repair, it was essential to examine parameters indicative of pulp wound healing, namely: closure of the exposure site with mineralized tissue, transition of inflammation to a chronic stage with a normal leukocyte count, rather than complete absence of inflammation—which may require 70 to even 90 days to occur. The early appearance of these parameters is considered a clear trend toward healing and, consequently, tissue repair46.

This study adopted a crossover design due to its high strength and reliability. It helps reduce variability in outcome measures that external confounders may influence, as each patient serves as their control47.

This study targeted human teeth to avoid the relative differences in the response of dental pulp in experimental animals, which in turn result from possible differences in the metabolic mechanisms and immune system responses48. Moreover, intact teeth were selected for this study to standardize the specifications, as carious teeth show varying degrees of pulpitis46.

Preoperative assessment of the pulpal inflammatory status is often considered prognostic rather than precise, due to the limitations of currently available diagnostic methods and tools49. In fact, the extent of pulpal inflammation can only be determined histologically, which is not feasible in clinical practice50. Although Ricucci’s studies reported a good correlation between clinical symptoms and histological findings in cases diagnosed as reversible pulpitis or normal pulp50, Zanini’s histological investigation demonstrated the presence of localized pulp necrosis in asymptomatic cases, which are often misdiagnosed as reversible pulpitis51.

From a clinical standpoint, the application of these materials to treat pulp exposures caused by mechanical caries excavation requires clinical and radiographic follow-up of at least 1 year. In most cases, such teeth are not scheduled for extraction as part of orthodontic treatment plans, making histological assessment impossible. In light of this, it is recommended to conduct a clinical, radiographic, and, where possible, histological study on premolars scheduled for orthodontic extraction that present with either reversible or irreversible pulpitis, in order to evaluate the effectiveness of partial pulpotomy using the materials tested in the present study.

The selection of an appropriate pulp capping material is based on its physical, chemical, and biological properties, which are determined by its ability to release calcium and hydroxyl ions. These ions create a favourable environment for cell recruitment and production, releasing inflammatory mediators and growth factors, ultimately leading to the deposition of hydroxyapatite crystals that serve as a nucleus for forming a mineralized dentin bridge27. In this study, four different forms of novel bioceramic materials have been introduced into the market as alternatives to conventional MTA, given the superiority of these materials in their biological, physical and chemical properties over other materials used in direct pulp capping18,52. However, the lack of studies on the effect of these materials on the histologic aspect of human teeth after DPC prompted us to choose this group of modern bioceramics, each of which has a different consistency.

The bond between the final restorative material and the pulp capping material is crucial for achieving an adequate seal to minimize the risk of bacterial microleakage following VPT, ultimately contributing to the treatment’s success and prognosis53. Premixed bioceramic materials have been shown to demonstrate significantly higher bond strength to composite resins than to resin-modified glass ionomer (RMGI). This difference may be attributed to the use of a bonding agent with composite restorations, as the application of an adhesive to bond bioceramic materials to composite has been strongly emphasized in the literature54. The use of a self-etch universal adhesive containing 10-MDP monomer has proven advantageous in enhancing the bond with resin-based restorations. The MDP monomer provides a functional acidic environment (pH ≈ 2.7) that acts as a mild etching system, chelating calcium from the calcium-rich surface of the bioceramic material without damaging its surface crystals. This results in a microscopically roughened surface that promotes strong adhesive forces55. Based on this evidence, a self-etch adhesive containing 10-MDP was selected in the present study54,56, followed by placing a light-cured liner to protect the capping material from subsequent restorative procedures57,58. The final restoration was then completed using the conventional approach: etching the dentin and enamel walls with 37% phosphoric acid, applying a fourth-generation bonding system, and placing a composite restoration suitable for posterior teeth57.

The literature shows considerable inconsistency regarding the optimal restorative protocol after placing a bioceramic pulp capping material, particularly the ideal time for delivering the permanent restoration59. Some authors have recommended delaying the final restoration for 72–96 h or even 5–7 days after placing the capping material. This recommendation is based on the prolonged setting time of traditional vital pulp therapy materials, which precludes immediate restorative procedures such as etching, rinsing, and bonding54. However, with the advent of newer generations of capping materials, particularly premixed bioceramics, the need for such delays has diminished. Multiple studies have evaluated the bond strength of restorations to capping materials placed immediately versus after a delay and have reported no significant difference when premixed bioceramics are used as the capping material60. Furthermore, the American Association of Endodontists (AAE) recommends that immediate restoration be considered an integral part of the restorative plan for teeth undergoing VPT61. The benefits of immediate restoration include prevention of microleakage, protection of the bioactive layer, reduction of postoperative sensitivity, decreased thermal conductivity, and creation of a foundation for cuspal coverage when necessary. Moreover, the immediate placement of the final restoration does not adversely affect the in-vitro shear bond strength of restorations placed over calcium silicate-based cements62. Additionally, laboratory findings have demonstrated that the depth of penetration of the adhesive into calcium silicate-based cements is greater when the restoration is performed immediately63.

The monitoring period used in this study was 4 weeks, representing the shortest required duration for histological evaluation of dentin bridge formation and the necessary time for the proliferation, migration, and differentiation of secondary odontoblasts64. However, this period may not be sufficient to resolve inflammation in histological sections. Studies by Li et al.65 and Danesh et al.66 have suggested that inflammation remains histologically detectable for up to 70 days following pulp capping.

Since all histological sections in this study, across all groups, exhibited chronic inflammation characterized by lymphocytes and macrophages after the 4-week monitoring period, and no presence of granulomas, giant cells, eosinophils, or abscesses was observed, this suggests a progression toward healing. Consequently, the critical factor in evaluating inflammation was its intensity rather than its type67.

The statistical analysis of inflammation intensity scores in the histological samples revealed that the inflammation degree in the NMP group was lower than in the three other tested materials (NP, FSP, and BCP).

Seventy-five per cent of the histological sections in the NMP group exhibited mild inflammation, while 25% showed moderate inflammation after four weeks of monitoring. These results align with those of Pinheiro et al.68, who reported that NMP induced a moderate initial inflammatory response within the first week, which decreased over time, reaching low levels at 1 month and 3 months. This inflammatory response was linked to releasing calcium ions (Ca++) from the material. Similarly, Abboud et al.7 observed a reduction in inflammatory cell counts at all monitoring intervals (1 week, 1 month, and 3 months) when NMP was used for furcal perforation repair, attributing this to the material’s high biocompatibility.

The reduced inflammatory response associated with NMP appears linked to a lower release of calcium and hydroxyl ions at different stages of its setting process. Siboni et al.30 explained this in vitro by demonstrating that calcium ion (Ca2+) release from NMP begins immediately upon immersion in distilled water, reaching peak levels within three hours and remaining high for the first three days before declining gradually, continuing beyond 28 days. Similarly, its alkalinity reaches its highest value within three hours of immersion, then decreases from day three onward, maintaining a long-term effect.

The histological findings of this study did not reveal significant differences in the inflammation intensity scores among the three other bioceramic materials (NP, FSP, and BCP).

The severity of inflammation in the BCP group was reported as 75% severe and 25% moderate. The NP group showed a 50% severe and 50% moderate inflammatory response, while the FSP group demonstrated 37.5% severe and 62.5% moderate inflammation. None of the tested materials exhibited a complete absence of inflammatory cells in the histological sections.

These findings align with multiple studies investigating the effect of time on inflammatory cell counts. These studies reported that the highest inflammatory response occurs at the one-month follow-up, followed by a significant reduction at three months. These results suggest that the inflammatory reaction decreases over time when biocompatible materials are used25.

The predominant inflammatory infiltration observed in the early healing phase can be attributed to the release of calcium ions from calcium silicate-based materials. The increase in pH and the heat generated during the setting reaction can enhance the inflammatory response by promoting immune cell recruitment, proliferation, and cytokine release7. The similarity in inflammation intensity among the pre-mixed bioceramic materials in this study may be explained by their comparable alkalinity levels, as most pre-mixed bioceramic materials create an alkaline environment with a pH above 1269.

However, our results differ from those of Al–Saudi et al.70, who investigated pulp healing after direct pulp capping with NMP and BCP. They reported an absence of inflammation in 70–80% of histological sections for both materials at the 3-week follow-up, with no inflammation in 100% after 3 months. This result contradicts the typical pulpal response to capping materials, as 3 months may be sufficient for inflammation to resolve completely. However, 3 weeks is expected to still show some inflammatory response due to the pulp capping procedure.

The continuity criterion reflects the speed of exposure site closure during short-term observation periods, as examined in our study. The findings indicate that the DBC score in the FSP group was higher than in the other three study groups. Histological sections showed that 75% of FSP samples exhibited a fully formed dentine bridge, while 25% exhibited a partially formed bridge. These findings align with Kim et al.71, who reported 82% complete and 18% partial dentine bridge formation in histological sections of dog teeth treated with partial pulpotomy using FSP after a 4-week observation period. Similarly, Al-Saudi et al.70 observed 90% partial dentine bridge formation and 10% initial dentine bridge formation in FSP-treated human premolars scheduled for orthodontic extraction.

The superior performance of FSP over other materials is attributed to its high hydroxyapatite deposition rate, which serves as a nucleation site for organic matrix mineralization72. This increased deposition rate is associated with adding monobasic calcium phosphate to its formulation, providing a substantial source of free phosphate ions necessary for hydroxyapatite precipitation73. Additionally, the alkaline environment resulting from calcium silicate hydration facilitates the breakdown of monobasic calcium phosphate, releasing free phosphate ions that react with calcium ions to precipitate hydroxyapatite crystals74.

The presence of monobasic calcium phosphate may explain the higher deposition rate of BioCeramic materials than other calcium silicate-based cement (CSCs), as the latter requires a more extended period to acquire phosphate ions from surrounding tissues69.

The superior dentine bridge continuity in FSP compared to BCP, despite both containing monobasic calcium phosphate, can be attributed to its faster initial setting time (20 min vs. 2 h for BCP). This rapid setting enhances its hydroxyapatite deposition capacity72.

The percentage of dentine bridge continuity in the BCP group was 75% for partial bridges and 25% for initial bridges. Although the manufacturer originally indicated BCP for pulp capping in its first product release, no histological studies specifically evaluating its effect on pulp response when used as a capping material have been found.

For instance, Liu et al.75 found that all histological sections from rat teeth treated with iRoot BP exhibited complete dentine bridge formation at a 4-week observation period, with comparable inflammatory responses among all studied materials. Similar findings were reported by Shi et al.76 in their histological analysis of dog teeth treated with the same materials, revealing complete dentine bridge formation without inflammatory responses after 3 months of direct pulp capping. Kamal et al.33 also demonstrated histological outcomes for TotalFill—without specifying its commercial form—comparable to Biodentine in dentine bridge formation and pulp tissue inflammation after direct pulp capping in human premolars scheduled for orthodontic extraction. Both materials outperformed Theracal, making TotalFill a promising bioceramic material for vital pulp therapy.

In vitro, Zamparini compared different TotalFill forms and found that paste formulations released significantly higher calcium ions than other materials, including modified tricalcium silicate powders with polymer gel (MTA Plus and NMP)12,40. This result explains its superior histological performance over NMP. Paste formulations demonstrated sustained calcium and hydroxyl ion release upon hydration, maintaining ion release beyond 28 days, thus enhancing bioactivity and antibacterial properties74.

The primary limitation of this study is the inability to follow up on the samples for a more extended period, as the eight patients enrolled during the study period required premolar extractions to complete their orthodontic treatment as quickly as possible. This constraint also prevented conducting additional tests related to colour changes induced by calcium silicate-based materials. A recent in vitro study reported that discolouration caused by these materials remains clinically imperceptible during a 3-month follow-up period77; therefore, such investigations may require longer observation times to detect clinically relevant changes. In our next phase, we plan to conduct a clinical and radiographic study with extended follow-up to compare the four materials in the direct pulp capping of deep carious lesions.

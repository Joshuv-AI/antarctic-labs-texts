GPU-accelerated linkage disequilibrium scans reveal non-independent assortment of human non-homologous chromosomes
Authors: Wenhua Lv, Yuping Zou, Zhenwei Shang, Siyu Wei, Chen Sun, Jing Xu, Haiyan Chen, Junxian Tao, Hongsheng Tian, Shuo Bi, Xuying Guo, Yu Dong, Chen Zhang, Mingming Zhang, Hongchao Lv, Yongshuai Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe independence of assortment between loci on non‑homologous chromosomes, as proposed by Mendel’s laws, has not been rigorously validated at the whole‑genome scale due to the computational demands of estimating inter‑chromosomal linkage disequilibrium.ResultsUsing a GPU-accelerated method, we calculate approximately 100 trillion linkage disequilibrium coefficients among 22 non-homologous chromosomes across 26 populations from the 1000 Genomes Project. Contrary to the assumption of independent segregation of non-homologous chromosomes during meiosis, linkage disequilibrium mapping of 2,594 samples reveals 1,195 locus pairs with high linkage disequilibrium (r2 ≥ 0.8), clustered near centromere regions. This pattern is consistently observed across super‑populations and individual populations. Seven populations of African ancestry have the fewest high linkage disequilibrium inter-chromosomal pairs, while five populations of East Asian ancestry show the most high linkage disequilibrium pairs, mirroring known population evolutionary history.ConclusionsThese findings indicate that human non‑homologous chromosomes do not assort fully independently in the centromere region, suggesting possible inter‑centromeric haplotypes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13059-026-04019-9.

Background

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) refers to the phenomenon of non-random association between alleles at different loci, which is a commonly used indicator in population genetics and evolutionary biology. LD patterns for the whole genome reflect the evolution history, signature of selection and the geographic subdivision structure of different populations [1, 2]. The concepts of haplotypes and blocks are formed when the alleles of more than two variant sites exhibit a higher degree of LD compared to other regions of the genome which is crucial to better understanding of human LD patterns [3–5]. The International HapMap Project and the 1000 Genomes Project have generated large amount of variation data, which has promoted the development of LD-based population genetics theory. Specifically, the analyses based on HapMap data defines individuals as 3 different ethnically populations [6, 7] (Asian, European, and African), and individuals in 1000 Genomes Project that further largely expands loci and samples can be divided into 5 different super populations,namely East Asian (EAS), South Asian (SAS), European (EUR), African (AFR), and American (AMR) [8, 9]. The length of the haplotype varies among different human populations, in which the populations of African ancestry have shorter haplotype blocks than the other populations, because the populations of African ancestry have undergone more historical recombination events in the longest evolutionary process [10–12]. The discovery of haplotypes is important for case–control studies and greatly reduces the number of association tests due to the high LD of SNPs within one block. In addition, haplotype based association analysis provides crucial perspectives for revealing the genetic mechanisms of complex diseases [13, 14].

Although LD theory has been well developed and provides vital insights into population genetics and complex disease studies, the vast majority of studies have focused on LD between SNPs within a few kilobases (Kb) or millionbases (Mb). There have been few genome-wide LD studies of non-homologous chromosomes, mainly due to the large amount of calculations and unbearable time consumption. It takes several months to perform hundreds of millions of LD calculations based on the single thread CPU mode, and years or even decades are needed if the number of calculations reaches trillion billion, which is unacceptable for time-sensitive scientific research. Compared with CPU based computation, parallel operations on graphics processing units (GPUs) can reduce computing time by dozens or even hundreds of times, depending on the performance of different graphics cards.

Therefore, we intend to globally explore whether there are specific LD patterns between non-homologous chromosomes in human populations by the advantage of high-performance of GPUs. High-density SNP genotype data updated in 2022 from 1000 Genomes Project including 2,594 unrelated individuals from 26 populations were obtained [15] and then over 100 trillion LD scans between SNPs on non-homologous chromosomes were performed. It breaks the limitations of traditional researches and discovers high LD patterns between non-homologous chromosomes, which might complement the Mendelian's laws of inheritance and provide novel insights into population genetics and complex disease-related studies.

Results

High LD phenomena were observed among non-homologous chromosomes

We calculated 3,249,303,841,003 pairs of LD relationships between 22 non-homologous autosomes from the overall level by merging 2,594 individuals from the 1000 Genomes Project. In total, 1,195 high LD relationships with r2 ≥ 0.8 were discovered (Fig. 1A-C), in which there are 540 (45.2%) pairs between chromosome 14 and chromosome 22 and 435 (36.4%) pairs between chromosome 1 and chromosome 5, jointly accounting for 81% of the overall high LD pairs (Table 1). For two SNP loci located on non-homologous chromosomes, the alleles at locus one are A and a, and the alleles at locus two are B and b. There are three genotypes (AA, Aa, and aa) at locus one and three genotypes (BB, Bb, and bb) at locus two respectively. There would be nine genotype combinations including AABB, AABb, AaBB, AaBb, AAbb, Aabb, aaBB, aaBb, and aabb in the theoretical ratio of 1:2:2:4:1:2:1:2:1 if the alleles at the two loci were inherited independently. However, the non-random proportion of genotypes at two loci on non-homologous chromosomes was frequently observed in our research. For instance, there are three genotypes (GG, GC and CC) at locus 14:16,094,734:G:C on chromosome 14, and three genotypes (GG, GA and AA) at locus 22:11,214,545:G:A on chromosome 22 (Fig. 1D). According to Mendel's laws of inheritance, the expected frequency of genotype combinations at two SNP loci in a randomized situation is shown in Fig. 1E, F. However, in fact the genotype of GG-AA, GC-GA, and CC-AA accounted for 98% of the total number of individuals, whereas the other combinations of genotype hardly appeared (Fig. 1G). High LD regions are concentrated near the centromere of non-homologous chromosomes, which is highly conserved among different populations

Through physical mapping, approximately 50% of high-LD SNP pairs (r2 ≥ 0.8) have at least one end located within the α-satellite Higher-Order Repeat (HOR) array region of the centromere. Since the centromere is a large chromosomal composite domain, we extended the analysis to regions spanning 1 Mb to 3 Mb upstream and downstream of the core HOR region. We found that 94.3% of high-LD SNP pairs have at least one end falling within the 1 Mb flanking regions of HOR, and more than 99.6% have at least one end located within the 3 Mb extended regions (Fig. 2A, Table 2). In other words, high LD SNP pairs were frequently observed in the centromere regions (Fig. 2B). A total of 5,515 inter-chromosomal SNP pairs with high LD were identified if the LD threshold was set to 0.5, of which 47.7% of the high LD pairs have at least one end in the core HOR array region of centromere and 99.7% of the high LD pairs had at least one end in the centromere region if 3 Mb is extended upstream and downstream of the HOR array region (Fig. 2A, Additional file 1: Fig. S1). The populations in 1000 Genomes Project belonging to AFR, EUR, AMR, EAS, and SAS super groups were merged respectively and then three trillion inter-chromosomal LD calculations were conducted for each of the five super populations, in which 323, 3095, 2355, 3309 and 2684 high LD SNP pairs were identified. Most of the high LD loci are also located in the centromere regions, which is consistent with the previous overall analysis results (Additional file 1: Fig. S2-S6, Additional file 2: Table S1).

Furthermore, in consideration of the genetic heterogeneity across different populations, we conducted LD calculations between non-homologous chromosomes for each of the 26 populations. The observations obtained in each single population are highly consistent with the overall results and the super population ones (Additional file 1: Fig. S2-S6, Additional file 2: Table S2).

In summary, high LD regions were found to be concentrated near the centromere by over one million billion LD calculations between non-homologous chromosomes. This phenomenon is highly conservative among different populations.

The LD pattern of the centromere region on non-homologous chromosomes is consistent with the evolution of the population

Despite the conserved phenomenon of high LD in the centromeric region between non-homologous chromosomes, there are still differences in the fine LD patterns across populations. LD analysis of 26 populations showed that the average number of high LD pairs in the East Asian population is nearly 5 times higher than that of populations of African ancestry (Additional file 2: Table S1). Characterized by r2 values between high LD SNP pairs present in at least one population, 26 populations were well clustered into five super populations, namely AFR, EUR, AMR, EAS, and SAS (Fig. 3) which is consistent with their geographical locations. The evolutionary characteristics of populations have been commonly explored by LD relationships between adjacent SNPs within 500 Kb in previous studies [16–18]. Studies based on the HapMap and the 1000 Genomes Project have reported that populations of African ancestry have the richest genetic diversity in the world due to the longest evolutionary period and the maximum historical recombination events compared to other populations [19]. In our research, these results and conclusions were confirmed by inter-chromosomal LD analysis illustrating the robustness of our results. Interestingly, although Peru (PEL) is geographically a population of AMR, the number of SNP pairs with high LD and the chromosomal position of SNPs in the high LD interaction pairs are highly similar to those of the EAS population (Fig. 3, Additional file 1: Fig. S3D), because Peru is a predominantly Asian-American population, which further confirms the conservative nature of population evolution that has been reported.Fig. 3A hierarchical cluster plot shows the inter-chromosome LD profile differences across 26 populations

Inter-chromosomal LD block and haplotypes imply non-independent assortment of fragments in the non-homologous centromere regions

Breaking through the limitations of traditional LD analysis of proximity SNPs on a same chromosome, we used a GPU-accelerated method to perform inter-chromosomal LD scans for the first time, and identified 1,159 non-homologous chromosomal SNP pairs with high LD. Among all the high-LD relationships, SNP pairs between chromosome 14 and chromosome 22 accounted for 45.2%, with 45 SNPs on chromosome 14 and 72 SNPs on chromosome 22, respectively. To explore whether these regions have specific genetic structures, we performed a haplotype analysis for these inter-chromosomal high LD segments. It is rather remarkable that an inter-chromosomal LD block containing a total of 12 SNPs located in the centromere region of chromosomes 14 and 22 was discovered (Fig. 4A). In this inter-chromosomal LD block, 7 SNPs on chromosome 14 have a 44.4 Kb span from the position of 16,092,710 to 16,137,133 and 5 SNPs on chromosome 22 have a 41.7 Kb span from the position of 11,211,392 to 11,253,125. These SNPs in the inter-chromosomal LD blocks near the centromere regions were closely connected to each other due to high LD degree (Fig. 4B). Five haplotypes were observed in this LD block, in which GCTCGGCCTTGC, GCTCGGTATTAC and ATCGATCCCAGT appeared in 93.3% individuals, which were the top three representative haplotypes (Fig. 4C). Discussion

Over 100 trillion LD coefficients among 22 non-homologous chromosomes for 2,594 individuals from 1,000 Genomes were calculated from the overall level, super population level and single population level, respectively. High LD relations are observed near the centromere regions of chromosomes, which are highly conserved among different populations. These observations reveal non-independent assortment in the centromere region between non-homologous chromosomes. LD patterns of the centromere regions are consistent with the evolution characteristics of the population reported by previous LD studies based on adjacent SNPs on the same chromosomes.

Limited by the computational speed of traditional CPU-based tools and the conventional understanding of non-homologous chromosome assortment, few studies have investigated inter-chromosomal LD from a genome-wide perspective. To address this gap, we innovatively developed cheeta, a user-friendly GPU-accelerated command-line tool designed for large-scale inter-chromosomal LD computation. We systematically evaluated the computational efficiency of our LD analysis pipeline and demonstrate that (1) calculating all inter-chromosomal LD pairs for a single population takes only 13.50 min on average; (2) the same task for a super population requires an average of 48.92 min; and (3) processing the entire cohort of 2,594 individuals takes merely 4.26 h (Additional file 2: Table S3). We also compared cheeta’s performance with two established tools, PLINK [20] and quickLD [21]. Using data from the ACB population (96 individuals) in the 1000 Genomes Project, we computed r2 between all SNP pairs on chromosome 1 (198,014 SNPs) with PLINK 1.9 and output pairs with r2 ≥ 0.8, which took 81 s. For a comparable assessment, cheeta computed r2 between all SNPs on chromosome 1 (198,014 SNPs) and chromosome 2 (210,373 SNPs) under the same r2 threshold and completed this task in only 9.4 s. The number of LD calculations within chromosome 1 was 19,604,673,091, while the number between chromosomes 1 and 2 was 41,656,799,222 which is 2.12 times greater. After normalizing for computational load, cheeta is 18.3 times faster than PLINK 1.9. According to literature, quickLD is 8.29 times faster than PLINK under comparable conditions. Thus, cheeta demonstrates a significant computational advantage over both tools.

By default, cheeta outputs SNP pairs with r2 ≥ 0.8. To facilitate a genome-wide overview of inter-chromosomal LD patterns, the software also offers an option to output the frequency distribution of LD values. Through a cross-chromosome LD scan involving over 100 trillion calculations based on 2.6 million SNPs from the 1000 Genomes Project, we obtained a genome-wide distribution of inter-chromosomal LD in humans. There are 50% of all LD values fall within the range of 0 to 0.002. More than 99% of inter-chromosomal LD values are below 0.05 (Additional file 1: Fig. S7), confirming that the high LD observed between different centromeric regions in this study is not a random occurrence, but rather bears significant biological implications. The complete distribution of inter-chromosomal LD is available at http://www.cheeta.org.cn/supp/inter-chr_LD_r2_distribution/. For users requiring full LD results while mitigating substantial storage demands, we implemented these following key optimizations: (1) scaling r2 values (× 1000) and storing them as 2-byte integers in binary format; and (2) distributing results across multiple files (≤ 100 million LD pairs per file) to improve processing efficiency. This binary storage strategy reduces space usage by approximately tenfold compared to conventional text formats. Along with the binary output, cheeta generates a meta file recording SNP counts per chromosome and corresponding file paths. To assist with decoding, we provide a dedicated command-line utility, decodingLD, which converts the binary output into readable text using the meta file as input.

In this study, we observed consistent results that the high LD patterns between non-homologous chromosomes have preference enrichment for the centromeric regions across global, super population, and individual population levels through a very large-scale computational analysis. To further quantify the high-LD patterns across independent cohorts, we conducted the following statistical analyses. First, we counted the frequency of occurrence defined as an r2 ≥ 0.8 across 26 distinct populations for each of the 1,195 high-LD pairs identified through inter-chromosomal LD analysis at the whole-population level. The results demonstrated strong consistency across populations: all identified high-LD pairs were observed in at least 11 and up to 26 independent populations, with a mean occurrence of 20.4 (Additional file 2: Table S4). We next computed the Jaccard similarity coefficient between every pair of populations using an r2 threshold of 0.8. The Jaccard index was defined as (A ∩ B)/(A ∪ B), where A ∩ B denotes SNP pairs with r2 ≥ 0.8 in both populations, and A ∪ B represents those with r2 ≥ 0.8 in at least one population. A high Jaccard similarity among all populations except those populations of African ancestry was observed (Additional file 1: Fig. S8). Together, these findings not only confirm that the inter-chromosomal high-LD patterns identified in this study are highly consistent across diverse populations, but also support their correlation with population evolutionary characteristics reported in our research. Complete results of the Jaccard similarity analysis are provided in Additional file 2: Table S5.

To assess the robustness of our findings, we performed validation using an independent dataset comprising high-quality SNPs from offspring individuals in 608 core pedigrees from the 1000 Genomes Project with high sequencing depth. Then cross-chromosomal LD for the previously reported chromosome pairs exhibiting high LD were recalculated. The results remained highly consistent with our earlier observations. Owing to the high sequencing depth of the family-based data, the density of high-quality SNPs was greater than that in unrelated individuals. This analysis identified 22,291 significant cross-chromosomal high-LD SNP pairs, among which 11,306 (50.72%) were located between chromosomes 14 and 22. We then mapped the physical genomic positions of SNPs involved in these high-LD pairs. After excluding a few chromosome combinations with very few high-LD signals, on average 92.49% of the high-LD SNP pairs had at least one end located within 1 Mb upstream or downstream of the centromeric core region. Extending this range to 3 Mb from the centromeric core included nearly all such pairs (Additional file 2: Table S6-S7).

Humans have regional centromeres that is physically connected to the spindle during cell division, ensuring an equal distribution of genetic material between daughter cells [22]. Centromere regions play vital roles in cell division that is an indispensable process to ensure cell proliferation and maintain genomic stability [23, 24]. Once centromeres have dysfunctions, cells cannot successfully divide and then do not have the genetic instructions they need to survive. The centromeres are mainly composed of kinetochore and DNA-encoded proteins. The centromere is tightly encased by a heterogeneous structure covered by a three-layer kinetochore structure, in which the DNA is usually in a heterochromatin state [25]. The Telomere-to-Telomere (T2T) Consortium’s completion of the full human genome sequence (T2T-CHM13) revealed the α-satellite higher-order repeat (HOR) arrays within core centromeric regions and provided single-base resolution coordinates of these HOR structures [26]. These findings prompted us to investigate whether the high inter-chromosomal LD observed in our study could be attributed to sequence repeats, and whether high LD also exists in non-centromeric repeated regions. To address these questions, we performed the following validation steps. First, we identified 161,433 high-quality (alignment score ≥ 5000) repeat elements located in non-centromeric regions. We then computed LD between repeat elements across different chromosomes, involving over 40.5 billion computational comparisons. Interestingly, no significant high-LD signals were detected, indicating that sequence repetition alone cannot fully explain the high LD observed in non-homologous centromeric regions (Additional file 2: Table S8).

We therefore further explored whether spatial interactions may exist among centromeres of non-homologous chromosomes using Hi-C data from 3D genome studies. Strikingly, it revealed that the physical interaction strength between trans-chromosomal centromeric regions is significantly higher than that in non-centromeric regions (p < 1E-16). This pattern was consistently observed across multiple Hi-C datasets (Additional file 2: Table S9). These Hi-C results support the conclusion that spatial interactions occur between centromeric regions of non-homologous chromosomes, reaffirming that the high-LD patterns identified in this research are not stochastic phenomena.

The genetic architecture of centromeric regions within chromosomes has been documented in previous studies. For instance, Chen et al. pioneered the construction of a high-resolution, cross-population LD map, revealing strong and extensive LD patterns around centromeres, with the centromeric region of chromosome 22 showing particularly prominent signals [27]. Another large-scale analysis of LD patterns in the human genome also reported that the LD within the centromeric region of chromosome 11 was 94 times higher than the chromosome-wide average [28]. Additionally, the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) region on chromosome 6 exhibited significantly elevated LD compared to other genomic regions, suggesting a potential link between centromeres and immune function which is also supported by a study of killer immunoglobulin-like receptors [28, 29]. Integrating these previously reported intra-chromosomal centromeric LD patterns with the inter-chromosomal centromeric high-LD signals identified in our study may provide important insights into the genetic structure of centromeres and enhances our understanding of inheritance mechanisms during cell division.

Although large-scale LD scanning has revealed cross-chromosomal high-LD (r2 ≥ 0.8) patterns in centromeric regions, and independent dataset validation as well as Hi-C data analysis support the robustness of these findings, the use of r2 as an LD measure has certain limitations. Specifically, the ability of r2 to capture LD decreases when there is a substantial difference in allele frequencies between two SNPs. To address this limitation, we performed supplementary analyses by calculating D' values [30] for 3,249,303,841,003 cross-chromosomal SNP pairs. Using a threshold of D' ≥ 0.9, we identified 19,748 high-LD pairs, of which SNPs involved in 18,815 (over 95.3%) pairs were located within centromeric regions on both chromosomes. Moreover, 19,608 pairs (99.3%) had at least one SNP situated in a centromeric region. Detailed results are provided in Additional file 2: Tables S10—S11. High-LD pairs between chromosomes 1 and 5 and between chromosomes 14 and 22 accounted for 81.6% of all high-LD pairs identified based on r2. Results based on D' were highly consistent, with these two inter-chromosomal LD pairs constituting 78.2% of the total. Additionally, all high-LD SNPs identified via D′ between chromosomes 1 and 5, and over 95.2% of those between chromosomes 14 and 22, were located near the centromeric regions of their respective chromosomes. These findings further reinforce the reliability and robustness of the conclusion that non-homologous chromosomes exhibit non-independent assortment in the centromere regions.

It has been reported that genome assembly may have potential impacts on genetic analyses [31, 32]. With advances in sequencing technology,including increased read length and depth, along with continuous improvements in assembly algorithms, the quality of variant detection is expected to keep improving. We will continue to follow these technological developments and further evaluate their relevance to the findings of this study.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that human non-homologous chromosomes do not assort fully independently in the centromere region challenging Mendel’s law of independent assortment and suggesting the existence of possible inter-centromeric haplotypes. The observed population-level differentiation in these high-LD patterns, closely mirroring known demographic histories, further supports the evolutionary relevance of this phenomenon.

Methods

Acquisition and quality control of SNP genotype data

First, raw variant data in VCF format were obtained from the 1000 Genomes Project (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1000genomes/ftp/1000G_2504_high_coverage/working/20201028_3202_raw_GT_with_annot) [15]. Then, SNP genotype data of unrelated individuals were extracted from the full VCF files. Next, quality control was performed for the SNPs, including (1) removal of SNPs with minimum allele frequencies (MAF) below 0.01 and (2) removal of SNPs with P values less than 0.001 according to Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium tests for each of the 26 populations. After filtering, 2,621,978 SNPs on 22 autosomes from 2,594 individuals in 26 populations were retained for subsequent analysis. Additionally, to validate the robustness of the results, we used SNP genotype data from 608 high-depth sequenced offspring individuals in core families from the 1000 Genomes Project as an independent validation set, applying more stringent quality control criteria including MAF > 0.05 and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium p-value > 0.1.

Calculation of LD coefficient between human non-homologous chromosomes

For each pair of SNPs located on non-homologous chromosomes, the LD coefficient r2 [33, 34] is calculated as follows. Suppose SNP A has two alleles A1 and A2 with allele frequencies PA1 and PA2, and SNP B has two alleles B1 and B2 with allele frequencies PB1 and PB2. The difference between frequency of A1B1 (PA1B1) and PA1PB1 was denoted as D, i.e., D = PA1B1—PA1PB1. Then LD coefficient r2 is defined as the ratio of D2 divided by the frequency product of A1, A2, B1 and B2 alleles, i.e., r2 = D2/PA1PA2PB1PB2. The r2 value varies from 0 to 1, with larger values indicating higher LD between the two SNPs. A high LD pair between two SNPs on non-homologous chromosomes is identified if r2 ≥ 0.8. To evaluate the stability of this work, the inter-chromosomal LD calculations were performed from the overall level by merging all populations, super population level by merging populations according to geographical locations and single population level, respectively.

Software development

To enhance the efficiency of inter-chromosomal LD calculations, we developed cheeta (version 1.0), a GPU-accelerated parallel computing software for Windows. This command-line based tool processes genotype data from two chromosomes, parses the data, and transfers it to the GPU for computing the LD metric r2 using the classic haplotype frequency estimation method. Depending on user needs, cheeta can output either LD results for all inter-chromosomal SNP-pairs or only those meeting specific filtering criteria (e.g., r2 ≥ 0.8). When a non-zero r2 threshold is specified, the results are saved in text format. If the threshold is set to 0 (requesting all LD results), cheeta optimizes storage by scaling r2 values (floating-point) by 1000, converting them to integers, and storing them in binary format occupying 2 bytes per value and split into multiple partitioned files for faster processing. To facilitate decoding, we provide a standalone tool, decodingLD, which converts the binary results back to text format using the meta file generated during the LD calculation. To further facilitate researchers' understanding of cross-chromosome LD patterns, cheeta optionally provides global LD frequency distribution. Users are allowed to adjust the number of GPU threads for parallel computing based on their graphics card configuration (default: 256 threads). In this study, we utilized an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM, CUDA 12.0), with the maximum threads set to 1024 to optimize parallel computation performance.

Mapping high LD SNP pairs to chromosomes

To determine whether SNPs within inter-chromosomal high LD pairs have a position preference on chromosomes, we downloaded the schema for chromosome band of the reference genome (GRCh38) from the UCSC website and mapped SNPs with high LD degree based on their physical locations. The Circos plot [35] is used to visualize the position of SNP pairs with high LD between non-homologous chromosomes. Furthermore, based on the coordinates of α-satellite higher-order repeat (HOR) arrays provided by the CHM1 genome [36], we downloaded the FASTA sequences of the core centromeric regions from the NCBI database. We then aligned these sequences to the GRCh38 reference genome using the blastn tool in BLAST [37], thereby obtaining single-base-resolution coordinates of the core centromeric regions in GRCh38. Additionally, given that the centromere is a large composite chromatin domain rather than a traditional gene with precise base-pair boundaries, we extended the regions upstream and downstream of the core centromere by 1–3 MB when performing physical position mapping of high-LD loci to the corresponding chromosomes.

Inter-chromosomal LD block and haplotype identification

Inter-chromosomal high LD blocks and the haplotypes contained within blocks were identified by Confidence intervals algorithm [38] and maximum likelihood estimation by using Haploview [39] that is a Java-based haplotype analysis tool. In order to visually demonstrate the high LD relationship across different chromosomes, a network of SNPs located in the LD block was constructed using Cytoscape software [40], and the width of the edges in the network was proportional to the r2 between different SNPs.

Hierarchical clustering for the 26 populations of 1000 Genome Project

To further investigate the relationship between cross-chromosome LD patterns and population evolution, we extracted the r2 values between high LD pairs which were identified from the whole cohort analysis in each of the 26 independent populations. Using the Canberra distance as a similarity measure, we then performed hierarchical clustering on these 26 populations to assess their structural relationships.

Hi-C Data acquisition and analysis

We obtained Hi-C contact matrix data for seven human cell lines (Additional file 2: Table S9) from the ENCODE database [41]. By using Juicer Tools 2.0 [42], for each pair of chromosomes, we extracted inter-chromosomal Vanilla-Coverage (VC) normalized observed values for the core centromeric regions of each chromosome and non-centromere regions located at least 5 Mb away from the core centromere. To ensure the robustness, 1,000 random selections for non-centromere regions were performed. Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were then applied to determine whether there were significant differences in physical interaction strength between the centromeric and non-centromeric regions.

LD calculation between repeat elements in non-centromeric regions

Repeat element annotation data for GRCh38 was obtained from UCSC. A total of 161,433 high-quality repeat elements (Additional file 2: Table S12), spanning six categories, were selected based on the following criteria including a sequence alignment score ≥ 5000 and a minimum distance of 5 Mb from the core centromere. Pairwise r2 values between these repeat elements across chromosomes were subsequently computed using the cheeta software.

A multisource cue fusion-based method for coronary artery stenosis detection in digital subtraction angiography
Authors: Dingce Sun, Zhongnan Liu, Wenyuan Yu, Hongli Liu, Bokang Zhai, Bo Zhao, Hongbo Xin, Hui Hu, Yang Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCoronary artery disease (CAD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide. Coronary digital subtraction angiography (DSA) is the gold standard for evaluating coronary lesion location, extent, and severity, and it serves as the primary basis for informing decisions related to revascularization. However, the interpretation of DSA in complex lesions is often time-consuming and subject to interobserver variability. This study aims to develop and validate a multisource cue fusion-based method for precise localization of coronary artery stenosis in digital subtraction angiography.MethodsIn this study, a detection network was developed for accurately localizing stenosis in key frames of coronary DSA by integrating multisource cues. Specifically, the vessel segmentation provides spatial contour cues, while adjacent frames offer dynamic cues. To effectively combine these, a cross-cue attention-based fusion module was designed, which enhances target frame representation by capturing nonlocal spatial dependencies. Furthermore, a distance-based penalty from the coronary ostium was incorporated into the loss function to improve the model’s sensitivity to localization errors of proximal stenosis.ResultsThe proposed method achieved a precision of 87.90%, a recall of 65.78%, an F1-score of 74.99%, a mean average precision at 0.5 intersection of union (IoU) threshold (mAP@0.5) of 78.46%, and a mAP across multiple IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 with a step size of 0.5v (mAP@0.5–0.95) of 60.37%, outperforming YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and YOLOv12 on all metrics. Ablation studies further demonstrated that the total loss led to superior performance, with mAP@0.5–0.95 increasing to 60.37%, in contrast to 56.23% with complete IoU loss and 58.01% with distance-weighted loss.ConclusionsThis study addresses the critical need for precise identification and localization of coronary artery stenosis in the assessment of CAD by proposing a coronary artery stenosis detection method for DSA that integrates multiple sources of information. This method effectively combines the structural information from the target frame with the dynamic temporal sequence data from adjacent frames, overcoming the limitations of single-frame information and enhancing the accuracy of stenosis localization.

Key findings

• A novel multisource cue fusion framework was developed for coronary artery stenosis detection from digital subtraction angiography (DSA), which integrates vessel segmentation cues and multiframe temporal dynamics.

• The developed method achieved superior detection performance compared with YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and YOLOv12, with improvements in precision, recall, F1-score, and mean average precision, particularly for proximal coronary stenosis.

• Joint optimization of segmentation and detection, combined with a distance-weighted loss emphasizing clinically critical proximal lesions, significantly enhanced localization accuracy and robustness.

What is known and what is new?

• Coronary DSA is the gold standard for evaluating coronary artery disease, but manual interpretation of complex lesions is time-consuming and subject to interobserver variability. Existing artificial intelligence (AI) approaches often rely on single-frame analysis or isolated tasks such as detection or segmentation.

• This study developed a cross-cue attention-based fusion strategy that explicitly integrates anatomical segmentation information and temporal motion cues from adjacent frames. In addition, a clinically informed distance-aware loss function prioritizing proximal lesions was incorporated to better align model optimization with real-world decision-making for revascularization.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The proposed framework provides a more accurate, robust, and anatomically informed approach for automated coronary stenosis localization in DSA.

• By integrating temporal dynamics and clinical anatomical priors, this method has the potential to reduce subjectivity, improve diagnostic consistency, and support AI-assisted decision-making in coronary revascularization planning.

• Future clinical workflows may benefit from adopting multisource, clinically guided AI models as opposed to relying on single-frame or single-task algorithms.

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is the most common cause of global mortality, and its prevention and treatment represent a formidable clinical challenge (1,2). Coronary digital subtraction angiography (DSA) can visualize the multiangle two-dimensional dynamic structure of the coronary artery lumen, facilitating clear diagnosis of the lesion location, range, and severity. It thus serves as the gold standard for guiding clinicians in vascular reconstruction surgery (3-6). Cardiac physicians and surgeons determine the severity of the patient’s disease based on the coronary DSA results and decide on treatment plans. However, certain complex cases are time-consuming and susceptible to subjectivity (7-9).

With the rapid advancement of medical artificial intelligence (AI) technology in recent years, an increasing number of algorithms have been deployed in cardiovascular imaging to accelerate image interpretation, improve diagnostic efficiency, and assist in clinical decision-making (10-12). In the context of coronary angiography, machine learning models have been heavily relied on for the automatic identification and quantification of coronary stenosis, enabling objective and reproducible assessment beyond manual visual estimation (13-15).

However, the majority of current machine learning approaches in coronary angiography analysis are limited to addressing only a single task or imaging perspective, such as stenosis detection, segmentation of vascular structures, or frame-level classification, and do not provide an integrated and clinically interpretable workflow (16). These task-specific designs restrict the applicability of these approaches in real-world clinical scenarios, in which angiogram interpretation requires simultaneous evaluation of the lesion location and severity, vessel morphology, and temporal hemodynamic changes. Moreover, the available models often rely on manual frame selection, are sensitive to imaging quality variations, and lack standardized outputs, which further constrain their reproducibility and diagnostic consistency across different patient cohorts and imaging centers.

Therefore, there is a critical need to develop more comprehensive frameworks capable of integrating multiple analytical components—such as lesion detection, vessel segmentation, stenosis grading, and key-frame selection—within a unified pipeline. Optimally, such approaches should generate structured, interpretable outputs to support individualized clinical decision-making, thereby promoting standardized diagnostic workflows and improving the overall reliability of AI-assisted coronary angiography interpretation.

Recent advances in automatic coronary artery stenosis detection that have employed deep learning techniques for invasive coronary angiography (ICA) have yielded promising results (17). However, the detection of stenoses remains challenging due to the high visual similarity between coronary vessels and background structures, the diversity of stenotic morphologies, and the presence of small-area lesions. Traditional methods often rely on static frame analysis and lack the ability to capture long-term temporal dependencies, which are essential for the robust recognition of stenosis in dynamic vascular sequences.

Li et al. (18) proposed a temporal enhanced YOLO-based framework that integrates state-space modeling and a dynamic Transformer backbone to effectively capture long-range temporal dependencies, thereby improving detection accuracy in cases involving small lesions and vessel-background ambiguity. Building upon the YOLO architecture, Duan et al. (19) introduced the deformable convolution and double coordinate attention (DCA)-YOLOv8 framework, which leverages histogram equalization preprocessing, DCA, and an adaptive inner-complete intersection over union (CIoU) loss to enhance performance under challenging imaging conditions. To further address issues in vessel delineation, Zhang et al. (20) developed a progressive perception learning strategy that integrates context, interference, and boundary modules to mitigate semantic confusion and boundary ambiguity, significantly boosting segmentation accuracy. Collectively, these studies represent a consistent research focus on improving vascular lesion detection and segmentation by strengthening model-level feature representation. These works share several common advancements, including structural optimization of deep networks, targeted enhancement of discriminative features, and improved robustness against challenges such as small-scale lesions, low vascular contrast, and boundary uncertainty. Although each study adopted different strategies—temporal modeling, attention (21) and objective function refinement (19), and progressive multistage perception (20)—they converge on enhancing fine-grained vessel recognition and achieving superior performance under complex visual conditions. Additionally, Moon et al. (15) devised a stenosis classification approach that combines automated key-frame selection, a self-attention mechanism for salient feature enhancement, and gradient-based visualization to achieve high diagnostic performance across both internal and external datasets.

Collectively, these studies demonstrate the growing effectiveness of combining spatial, temporal, and attention-based mechanisms in improving the robustness and clinical applicability of coronary stenosis detection systems. However, the bulk of these approaches are either limited to single-frame analysis or fail to explicitly model clinically significant anatomical priors, which restricts their performance in complex and dynamic angiographic settings such as that of DSA.

To address these limitations, this paper introduces three core innovations:

We propose a novel multisource cue fusion framework that integrates spatial segmentation information from the target frame and temporal dynamics from adjacent frames. By introducing a cross-cue attention (CCA) mechanism, the model learns nonlocal intra- and interframe dependencies, enhancing the robustness of feature representation under complex vascular motion and contrast variation in DSA sequences.

By introducing a cross-cue attention (CCA) mechanism, the model learns nonlocal intra- and interframe dependencies, enhancing the robustness of feature representation under complex vascular motion and contrast variation in DSA sequences.

A distance-aware loss function is designed to reflect clinical priorities. Specifically, a penalty coefficient, w(d), exponentially decayed with respect to the normalized distance from the stenosis to the coronary ostium, is introduced to assign greater weight to proximal lesions. This reflects real-world clinical decision-making, as proximal stenoses hold greater weight in the planning of treatment strategies and SYNTAX score calculation.

The model architecture supports end-to-end joint optimization of both vessel segmentation and stenosis localization. Methods

Patient data

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and was approved by the Ethics Board of Beijing Anzhen Hospital (approval No. 2025145x). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

The dataset used in this study was collected from Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University, and consisted of data from 300 patients with clinically confirmed CAD, with DSA images extracted from 842 coronary angiography digital imaging and communications in medicine (DICOM) sequences. The DSA images were primarily acquired using the Philips Azurion and GE Medical Systems DL DSA systems under routine clinical coronary angiography protocols. All data were fully anonymized, and rigorous visual inspection was conducted to ensure the exclusion of any patient-identifiable information.

To ensure statistical independence and prevent data leakage, the dataset was divided at the patient level into training, validation, and testing subsets at a ratio of 8:1:1. Specifically, all DSA sequences from a single patient were assigned exclusively to one subset. The training set was used for model parameter optimization, the validation set was employed for hyperparameter tuning and model selection, and the test set was reserved for independent performance evaluation.

After data partitioning, the training set included 240 patients with 667 DICOM sequences, the validation set included 30 patients with 83 DICOM sequences, and the test set included 30 patients with 92 DICOM sequences.

A rigorous, multistage selection process, illustrated in Figure 1, was applied to ensure diagnostic consistency and data quality and suitability for subsequent analysis, with inclusion and exclusion criteria being rigorously applied. DSA sequences were excluded if any of the following conditions were met: (I) excessive coronary artery displacement due to respiratory motion exceeding 2 mm; (II) inhomogeneous contrast agent filling resulting in discontinuous vascular segments; (III) vessel overlap rate exceeding 30% and impairing the visual separation of branches; and (IV) significant imaging noise or blur with a vessel edge gradient <5 Hounsfield units (HU) causing uncertainty in boundary identification.

Flowchart of data inclusion. Of 300 patients with 842 DICOM sequences reviewed, 212 patients with 660 sequences were included. DICOM, digital imaging and communications in medicine; DSA, digital subtraction angiography; ECG, electrocardiogram; HU, Hounsfield unit.

Following data screening, a standardized annotation protocol was implemented for the construction of high-quality training and evaluation datasets. Five cardiovascular surgeons from Beijing Anzhen Hospital participated in the annotation process. For each patient, 3–5 key angiographic views were manually selected from the DSA sequences after frames that met any of the following criteria were excluded: (I) unclear visualization of the coronary ostium, (II) branch separation angle <15°, and (III) images without an electrocardiogram-confirmed end-diastolic phase.

Annotations were performed via a double-blind review mechanism to reduce subjective bias. Experts employed a semiautomated contouring algorithm to delineate vessel masks with high precision, generating segmentation labels. Based on these contours, the stenosis locations were then manually marked. The annotated labels from the test set were treated as the ground truth for the evaluation of the model’s detection performance.

Preprocessing

To ensure consistency in the input to the multisource cue fusion model, a standardized preprocessing pipeline was applied to manually selected key frames and their adjacent temporal frames. First, all DSA images were uniformly resized to a resolution of 512×512pixels to maintain spatial alignment across samples. Examples of DSA are shown in Figure 2. Second, to enhance the visibility of coronary vessels, a fixed window level and width were applied to normalize image contrast, thereby improving vessel-background separation. Finally, to mitigate the variability caused by the differences in imaging equipment and contrast agent dosage, each image was intensity-normalized such that pixel values were scaled to the range of [0, 1]. This normalization was applied identically to both the key frame and its neighboring frames to preserve temporal consistency and ensure reliable feature extraction in the downstream fusion and detection modules.

The proposed network

In this study, we developed a multisource cue fusion framework for coronary artery stenosis detection from DSA sequences. We found that during the diagnostic process, physicians in most cases also need to rely on observing the frames before and after the current frame to evaluate the dynamic behavior of the vessels in order to determine the presence and severity of stenosis.

As depicted in Figure 3, the system first extracts the vessel structure from the target image It via a segmentation module, producing a segmentation cue Ct. Meanwhile, as it is often observed in clinic that stenosis assessment often requires the review of neighboring frames to capture vessel motion and contrast-flow variation, the adjacent frames are incorporated and preprocessed to derive multiframe cues that explicitly encode temporal dynamics. Adjacent frames {It−k,…,It−k} are preprocessed to generate multiframe cues that encode temporal dynamics. Both cues are integrated in the cross-cue fusion module through a CCA mechanism to form a fused representation. This enriched feature map is then passed to a detection module that localizes stenosis regions and outputs bounding boxes. Loss functions are applied to both segmentation and detection modules to optimize the network in an end-to-end manner.

Coronary segmentation extraction

To evaluate the suitability of our dataset for semantic vessel segmentation, we employed a residual U-Net architecture (22-24), a convolutional neural network that adopts an encoder-decoder paradigm. In this design, the encoder progressively abstracts hierarchical features from the input DSA frame, while the decoder reconstructs a high-resolution binary mask delineating coronary vessel structures. Crucially, residual blocks are incorporated at each level of the encoder and decoder to facilitate deeper network training and mitigate vanishing gradients, and skip connections bridge corresponding encoder and decoder stages to preserve fine-grained vessel contours. This integration of residual learning and multiscale feature fusion allows the model to maintain the continuity and connectivity of tortuous coronary branches during segmentation. Implementation details

The experiments in the study were conducted on an Ubuntu 22.04 operating system with 32 GB of memory, an NVIDIA RTX 4090D with 24 GB of memory, an Intel i7-14700K, and a PyTorch framework based on the Python computer language. In the training process, an Adam optimizer was employed with default parameters. The training was configured for 300 epochs with a batch size of 8.

To ensure stable and efficient convergence of the model during training, we used a cosine annealing learning rate scheduling strategy with a linear warm-up phase. Specifically, the learning rate was initially set to a small value of 0.02 and gradually increased linearly over the first 10 epochs (warm-up stage) to reach the base learning rate of 0.1. This strategy prevents unstable gradient updates in the early stages of training and facilitates smoother optimization.

Results

To evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed multisource cue fusion framework in detecting coronary artery stenosis from DSA sequences, we conducted extensive quantitative experiments on an expert-annotated clinical dataset. A total of 30 cases from the test set were selected for performance evaluation, with each case containing 3–5 manually annotated key views, resulting in 92 frame-level samples. The results demonstrate the superiority of our method in terms of both detection accuracy and clinical relevance.

Discussion

CAD remains a significant global health burden and primarily driven by the progressive accumulation of atherosclerotic plaques within the coronary arteries. This pathological process results in luminal stenosis, subsequently impairing myocardial perfusion and leading to ischemia or infarction (31-33). Accurate identification and localization of stenosis are essential for clinical diagnosis, risk stratification, and treatment planning, including decisions between electing percutaneous coronary intervention and coronary artery bypass grafting (1,33,34). The experimental results of this study demonstrate that the proposed multisource cue fusion framework offers significant improvements in detecting coronary artery stenosis from DSA sequences, thereby supporting more reliable CAD assessment.

This study integrated deep learning with temporal and anatomical priors to construct a robust DSA stenosis detection model. Unlike previous convolutional neural network-based approaches that rely on single-frame features, our method leverages multiframe contextual cues and vessel-aware segmentation priors, which substantially improve model representation of vascular dynamics. The proposed architecture consistently outperformed the traditional object detection baselines YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and YOLOv12 in multiple metrics, including precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP. These results validate the effectiveness of combining temporal dynamics and anatomical cues for improved detection performance in the coronary stenosis detection.

To further validate the rationality of the proposed framework, we conducted additional ablation experiments to evaluate the contribution of different fusion strategies, module configurations, and training paradigms. The results demonstrate that the introduction of CCA significantly improves stenosis detection performance compared with direct feature concatenation. Moreover, incorporating hierarchical interaction between segmentation and temporal cues further enhances detection accuracy. The integration of vessel-aware attention provides the most notable performance improvement, confirming that anatomical prior information effectively guides feature learning toward clinically relevant vascular regions. Additionally, experiments comparing different training strategies show that joint optimization of segmentation and detection tasks leads to superior performance compared with detection-only or segmentation-frozen settings. These findings provide strong evidence supporting the effectiveness and necessity of multisource feature fusion and joint learning design in the proposed framework.

Traditional approaches to coronary stenosis detection are often categorized into two-stage pipelines, in which vessel segmentation is performed first, followed by rule-based or learning-based lesion detection. For example, Yang et al. employed a U-Net-based model that achieved an average F1-score of 0.917 across 3,302 vessel segments from 2,042 patients (35), while Zhu et al. further enhanced segmentation accuracy using a Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet)-based architecture, achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.957 in overall performance (36). These methods highlight the critical role of accurate vessel segmentation in downstream stenosis assessment. However, their decoupled architecture is susceptible to error propagation, in which suboptimal segmentation quality leads to degraded stenosis detection. In contrast, our framework employs joint optimization of segmentation and detection, allowing the anatomical structure to directly inform lesion localization through shared loss functions, improving consistency and accuracy.

Another category of research employs object detection algorithms to directly identify and localize stenotic regions. Models such as YOLOv5 have been adapted to classify lesion types—local, diffuse, bifurcated, and chronic total occlusion—based on various angiographic views (e.g., cranial and left anterior oblique), enhancing the clinical relevance of the diagnosis (29).

In addition, advancements have introduced the use of YOLOv8, a convolutional neural network-based detection model, for the binary classification of coronary artery stenosis, which can effectively distinguish between stenotic and nonstenotic vessels (37). These approaches tend to incorporate transfer learning strategies, leveraging pretrained weights to accelerate model convergence and enhance generalization capabilities, particularly in clinical settings in which annotated data is scarce.

To address these limitations, our method explicitly models temporal dynamics in conjunction with anatomical cues. Li et al. (18) were inspired by LT-YOLO , which integrates state-space modeling and spatial-temporal fusion for long-range temporal dependency learning in ICA. Our model extends this approach to DSA by introducing a CCA mechanism. This enables the fusion of spatial segmentation cues from the target frame and temporal context from adjacent frames, improving the model’s capacity to understand contrast propagation and vascular motion—two critical aspects unique to DSA imaging. Unlike LT-YOLO, which focuses solely on temporal modeling, our design emphasizes multisource integration, capturing more holistic and clinically informative features.

Further improvements in stenosis detection have been proposed through enhanced preprocessing and feature extraction. Duan et al. (19) introduced DCA-YOLOv8, incorporating histogram equalization, edge enhancement, and a DCA mechanism, alongside an adaptive inner-CIoU loss function to better detect stenosis in noisy or low-quality images. Although such techniques enhance intraframe feature saliency, they lack interframe consistency modeling. In contrast, our approach not only preserves spatial precision but also leverages temporal coherence by integrating multiframe cues, offering a more comprehensive representation of vascular behavior.

Moreover, our study highlights the significance of clinically informed loss design. We incorporated a distance-weighted loss function, in which lesions proximal to the coronary ostium are assigned higher penalties, aligning with the clinical emphasis on proximal stenoses in SYNTAX scoring and revascularization planning. This, combined with the Dice loss from vessel segmentation, forms a unified objective that enhances both anatomical accuracy and lesion detection performance. Such context-aware optimization distinguishes our approach from standard object detection losses, which treat all regions equally.

In addition to detection performance, computational efficiency is an important consideration for clinical applicability. Although the proposed VH-CCA framework introduces additional attention-based feature fusion and multiframe processing, the increase in inference time remains moderate. The proposed model achieves an inference speed of 66.2 FPS, which satisfies the real-time or near-real-time requirements of routine DSA interpretation. Compared with baseline YOLO-based detectors, the proposed method demonstrates a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency. Given that DSA analysis is typically performed in offline or semi-real-time clinical workflows, the inference performance of the proposed framework is well aligned with practical diagnostic requirements.

In summary, by integrating vessel-aware segmentation, temporal dynamics modeling, and clinically guided loss design, the proposed multisource cue fusion framework provides a robust, interpretable, and clinically meaningful solution for coronary stenosis detection in DSA sequences. The model effectively overcomes the limitations of single-task, single-frame, and purely spatial detection approaches by capturing both anatomical structure and physiological temporal behavior of coronary vessels. These advantages lead to improved diagnostic accuracy, particularly in complex and dynamic coronary imaging scenarios. Overall, this study presents a promising step toward intelligent and data-driven cardiovascular diagnostic systems, with potential to enhance clinical decision-making and improve workflow efficiency in coronary angiography analysis.

Limitations

Despite the promising results achieved by the proposed multisource cue fusion framework, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the dataset used in this study, although clinically annotated and containing a diversity of anatomical variations and projection angles, remains limited in size and was derived from a single medical center. This may restrict the generalizability of the model to broader clinical settings. Future work will focus on expanding the dataset and conducting multicenter validations to enhance robustness and applicability across different imaging protocols and patient populations.

Second, although the proposed framework demonstrates strong performance on the available DSA data, its robustness under low-quality imaging conditions has not been systematically evaluated. In real-world clinical practice, DSA images may suffer from reduced contrast, motion artifacts, noise, or suboptimal acquisition settings, which could potentially affect detection performance. Future studies will incorporate more challenging low-quality DSA cases and explore quality-aware or noise-resilient learning strategies to further improve model robustness and clinical reliability.

Third, although the model was designed to identify the presence and location of stenosis, it does not yet provide a quantitative assessment of stenosis severity (e.g., percentage narrowing or functional significance), which is essential for clinical decision-making. Integrating stenosis grading or hemodynamic relevance estimation into the framework constitutes an important direction for future research.

Finally, while the introduced distance-weighted loss enhances the model’s sensitivity to proximal stenoses, its optimization remains heuristic and may require further refinement to better align with clinical risk stratification systems such as SYNTAX.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed a novel coronary stenosis detection framework based on a multisource cue fusion model, which effectively integrates anatomical segmentation cues from target frames and temporal motion cues from adjacent DSA frames. By incorporating a CCA mechanism and a distance-aware loss function, the proposed method significantly enhances the localization precision, especially in clinically critical proximal coronary segments. Extensive experiments on an expert-annotated clinical dataset demonstrated that our approach outperforms several strong baselines, including YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and YOLOv12, in terms of precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP. Moreover, ablation studies confirmed that both the fusion strategy and loss function design considerably improve detection robustness and anatomical alignment. These findings highlight the potential of multisource information fusion and domain-guided optimization in advancing the computer-aided diagnosis of CAD from DSA sequences.

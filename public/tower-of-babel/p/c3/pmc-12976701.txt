Does Relative Femoral Neck Lengthening Improve Outcomes in Sequelae of Perthes Disease?: A Systematic Review
Authors: Arkesh Madegowda, Sitanshu Barik, Aakash Jain, Vikash Raj, Vishal Kumar, Sandeep Nema
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeOsteochondroplasty and femoral neck osteotomy can be used in conjunction with surgical hip dislocation (SHD) for added benefit to patients with sequelae of Perthes disease. The aim of the current systematic review was to provide a critical analysis of the literature and present the outcomes of SHD with relative femoral neck lengthening in sequelae of Perthes disease.Materials and MethodsElectronic database searches with relevant keywords were conducted in PubMed and Embase. This review included studies which described relative femoral neck lengthening outcomes on sequelae of Perthes disease. A study required a minimum postoperative follow-up period of one year for inclusion in this review.ResultsSeven retrospective studies with 244 patients were included in the review. Five studies reported objective improvement in functional scores. Approximately 9% (12/137) of patients reported complications and the overall pooled proportion of patients requiring subsequent total hip arthroplasty was 8% (95% confidence interval with a range of 4% to 11%).ConclusionUsed in conjunction with relative femoral neck lengthening SHD has opened a new treatment means for the correction of deformities resulting from sequelae of Perthes disease. Notable improvements in clinical and functional outcomes can be expected after this procedure. Low rates of postoperative complications and future conversions to total hip arthroplasty were also noted. The results of the review are limited by the non-uniform inclusion of study participants in terms of preoperative grading as well as any occurrence of prior, concomitant or subsequent surgical procedures.Keywords: Legg-Calve-Perthes disease, Relative femoral neck lengthening, Greater trochanter, Complications

INTRODUCTION

Lesion healing in Perthes disease often leads to persistent symptoms even in the presence of concentric femoral head reduction in the acetabulum. These may be attributed to various anatomical presentations of the femoral head (coxa magna), femoral neck (coxa breva), and acetabulum (dysplasia)1). The combinations of such presentations are addressed in the Stulberg classification, which predicts long-term outcomes. These varied combination of features can lead to femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) and/or hip joint instability, predisposing the hip to early osteoarthrosis2). Two joint-sparing strategies have been postulated to improve the functional outcomes in such hips. The first approach is safe surgical hip dislocation (SHD) with osteochondroplasty3). Osteochondroplasty alters the shape of the femoral head, which reduces FAI. The second approach utilizes periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) combined with femoral valgus osteotomy4). PAO improves the femoral head coverage. Both approaches can be further enhanced with a trochanteric advancement, which improves the efficiency of the abductor mechanism by increasing its lever arm5).

SHD offers a new approach for correcting deformities in sequelae of Perthes disease. The evidence regarding its utility in late stage Perthes disease is in its nascent stages. The aforementioned techniques (osteochondroplasty and proximal femoral osteotomy) can be combined with SHD for maximum benefit to the patient. Termed relative femoral neck lengthening (RFNL), proximal femur osteotomy is conducted in the form of distal advancement of the trochanter with subperiosteal trimming of the proximal stable trochanter. This systematic review aimed to provide a critical analysis of the literature and present RFNL outcomes in sequelae of Perthes disease.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study Details

The study was conducted per PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines, with its protocol submitted in PROSPERO (CRD42024512643). Two authors conducted an electronic database search in PubMed and Embase (A.M. and S.B.). The relevant keywords used for the search were “healed”, “sequelae”, “late”, “Legg-Calve-Perthes disease”, “Perthes disease”, “relative neck lengthening”, “femoral neck lengthening”, “trochanter distalization”, and “trochanteric distalization”. Appropriate use of Boolean operators was used in PubMed. Embase search guidelines were followed. The search results were exported to spreadsheets (Microsoft Excel 2016; Microsoft Corp.). Duplicate entries were removed using the spreadsheet software’s “duplicate removal” function. Initial screening of the title and abstract was done on the remaining spreadsheet entries. The full texts of the selected articles were then accessed. Lastly, decisions were made regarding the final eligibility of the articles based on predetermined inclusion criteria. Any dissent was resolved by the intervention of the two senior authors (V.K. and V.R.).

2. Eligibility Criteria

This review included studies on the sequelae of Perthes disease and the outcome of RFNL. A minimum postoperative follow-up period of one year was required for inclusion. Isolated single-case reports and studies describing procedures other than osteochondroplasty and RFNL were excluded. Bibliographies of the included studies and previous reviews were screened. The senior authors resolved any contention regarding inclusion (V.K. and V.R.).

3. Quality Assessment

Two authors (V.R. and A.M.) independently assessed the quality of each study using the MINORS tool (Methodological Index for Non-Randomised Studies)6). This tool uses a total score of 24 (12 items) for comparative studies and 16 (8 items) for non-comparative studies. The tool checklist focuses on a clearly stated aim, the inclusion of consecutive patients, prospective data collection, endpoints appropriate to the study aim, unbiased assessment of study endpoint, adequate follow-up period, and attrition of less than 5%. For comparative studies, the checklist also includes adequacy of the control group, contemporariness of the groups, equivalence of the groups, and adequacy of the statistical analysis. Items were scored 0 if not reported, 1 if reported but inadequate, and 2 if reported and adequate.

4. Data Extraction

Data extraction was done by one author (A.J.) on a pre-determined spreadsheet, which was prepared by the senior authors (S.B. and A.M.) after a complete text analysis of the included studies. The data was then assessed for adequacy under the supervision of two authors (V.K. and V.R.). Outcomes for the sought data were extracted as mean, ranges, standard deviation, and confidence intervals from the selected articles and entered into a spreadsheet. Demographic data included age, gender, postoperative follow-up period, preoperative grading, concomitant procedures, subsequent procedures, and complications. The outcomes were assessed through various clinical and radiological findings and objective functional scores.

5. Statistical Analysis

The data collected were analyzed using mean, standard deviation, and ranges. A narrative review was done on the data that could not be analyzed statistically. A proportional meta-analysis, based on the rate of conversion to future total hip arthroplasty, was conducted using a Der-Simonian random effects model. Heterogeneity among the studies was denoted by using I2. OpenMetaAnalyst (Brown University) was used to perform the proportional meta-analysis.

RESULTS

1. Literature Search

The initial literature search yielded 31 results (Fig. 1). After the removal of four duplicates, 27 results were screened. The reasons for final exclusion were studies that did not report on RFNL, case reports, and mixed studies that did not provide data for the subset of Perthes disease. After full-text screening of 12 studies, seven studies were included in the final review7-13).

2. Details of the Included Studies

All included studies were retrospective case series that dealt explicitly with the pathology of Perthes disease. One of the studies did not perform RFNL for all of its study participants, therefore only the data about the participants who underwent RFNL was extracted and used for this study11). None of the studies had any prospective calculation of study size or an unbiased assessment of the endpoint (Table 1). All selected studies met the inclusion criteria of: consecutive patients, prospective data collection, a postoperative follow-up period appropriate to the aim of the study, and a loss of follow-up of less than 5%. In the proportional meta-analysis of the conversion rate to total hip arthroplasty, the studies showed an I2 value of 0% (P=0.42).

3. Demographic Data

From the seven studies selected, 244 patients were included in the final review. Males formed 57.0% (139/244) of the cohort. The mean age of the patients varied from 16.3 to 26.4 years in the included studies. The youngest patient was six years old, while the eldest was 43 years old. The mean postoperative follow-up period ranged from two years to 10.2 years. On an individual basis, the minimum follow-up was one year and the longest was 12.9 years.

5. Prior Procedures Performed

Four studies provided information regarding prior hip procedures performed7,9-11). Nearly half of the patients in these four studies (46.6%, 76/163) had undergone some form of prior procedure. Intertrochanteric osteotomy (62/76, 81.6%) was the most common procedure reported. Staheli’s shelf procedure (n=8), adductor tenotomy (n=4), Salter’s osteotomy (n=1), and osteochondroplasty (n=1) were also reported as prior surgeries.

6. Concomitant Procedures

Five studies provided details regarding concomitant procedures7,9-12). Approximately half of the patients (48.5%, 98/202) had undergone an additional surgical procedure along with RFNL during the same sitting. The most common interventions included labral repair (39/98, 39.8%), intertrochanteric osteotomy (26/98, 26.5%), and PAO (23/98, 23.5%). The less common procedures were mosaicplasty (n=6) and head reduction osteotomy (n=4).

7. Subsequent Procedures Needed

Four studies provided information about the necessity of subsequent procedures7,9-11). A subsequent procedure was needed for 43.6% (71/163) of the patients in the form of greater trochanter screw removal (n=43), total hip arthroplasty (n=19), PAO (n=3), hip arthroscopy with osteochondroplasty (n=3), RFNL revision (n=1), intertrochanteric osteotomy (n=1), and open reduction of lesser trochanter pseudarthrosis (n=1). The explicit reasons for these interventions were not provided. Three studies provided information regarding the timing of the secondary total hip arthroplasty9-11). Shore et al.11) reported their single conversion to arthroplasty at five years post-RFNL. Novais et al.10) and Leibold et al.9) reported time periods for conversion to arthroplasty as 4.7 years (interquartile range, 3.7 to 8.1) and 7±4 years, respectively.

8. Outcomes

Five studies provided objective surgical outcomes7,8,10,11,13). The Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) and Harris hip score (HHS) were used to quantify the outcomes (Table 3). Only two studies provided details regarding range of motion8,13). Three studies provided radiographic outcomes7,11,13).

9. Complications

Three studies provided information regarding complications (other than the need for total hip arthroplasty)9,11,12). Approximately 9% (12/137) of the patients reported complications such as recurrent pain (n=4), superficial wound infection (n=4), hip subluxation (n=3) and displacement of the trochanteric fragment (n=1). Six studies provided numbers regarding subsequent conversion to a total hip arthroplasty7,9-13). As shown in Fig. 2, the overall pooled proportion of patients requiring a future total hip arthroplasty was 8% (95% confidence interval, 4% to 11%).

DISCUSSION

1. Pathophysiology of Hip Symptoms in Sequelae of Perthes Disease

The joint deformities observed as a result of Perthes disease typically include a mushroom-shaped head, shortened femoral neck, and proximal migration of the greater trochanter. The aspherical femoral head may induce secondary acetabular remodeling, resulting in acetabular dysplasia9). These deformities can lead to limited hip motion, FAI, hip discomfort, and early joint degeneration among young adults. In the post-Perthes hip, FAI is primarily due to intraarticular impingement caused by the non-spherical femoral head shape14). Furthermore, acetabular retroversion, innominate osteotomy, or shelf procedure for femoral head containment during active disease may result in over-coverage of the femoral head, leading to a pincer type of FAI15,16). Repetitive cam or pincer impingement injury can lead to labral damage, labral avulsion, acetabular cartilage degeneration, and osteoarthrosis17).

2. Available Surgical Options

Effective case management of patients with residual effects of Perthes disease requires the identification of specific deformities contributing to the patient’s symptoms10). Femoral side deformities can be addressed with treatment options such as osteochondroplasty of the head-neck junction, relative neck lengthening, trochanteric advancement, true femoral neck lengthening, and intertrochanteric osteotomy. Whereas on the acetabular side, extra-articular lesions can be addressed by augmentation osteotomy and redirection osteotomies like PAO. Intra-articular acetabular pathologies can be managed by acetabular trimming with labral refixation, chondroplasty, microfracture, and osteochondral grafting10).

SHD allows for a comprehensive examination of the joint and dynamic evaluation of femoroacetabular contact throughout hip movement3). This approach can reshape the proximal femur via head-neck osteoplasty, relative neck lengthening by trimming, and distalization of the greater trochanter18). Moreover, SHD gives access to evaluate the acetabular labrum and cartilage and allows for correction of acetabular over-coverage by an acetabular osteoplasty19). This aids in the improvement of abductor lever arm function and the restoration of range of motion without extra- or intraarticular impingement.

3. Principal Results of This Review

This systematic review aimed to provide a critical literature analysis and present the RFNL outcomes in sequelae of Perthes disease. Regarding the results of RFNL, this review noted a lack of substantial evidence from the literature. As none of the studies were prospective, a high selection bias in the study subjects was introduced. All five studies that reported objective outcomes of the procedure noted an improvement in functional scores. High rates of prior (46.6%) and concomitant procedures (48.5%) were noted among the study subjects. Low rates of complication (8%) and conversion to total hip arthroplasty (8%) were noted in the review.

4. How Does RFNL Improve the Outcome?

RFNL addresses cam impingement caused by femoral residual deformities and improves the hip abductor mechanism. The procedure involves trimming the neck junction (osteochondroplasty) and base of the greater trochanter following hip dislocation and elevation of the posterosuperior retinacular flap through a safe surgical approach. The length of the hip abductor muscles is improved following distalization of the trochanter osteotomy fragment. These steps affect the relative lengthening of the femoral neck and better abductor function, resulting in impingement-free hip movement and improved gait9).

5. What Is the Need for Future Procedures?

Following Perthes disease, residual deformities are often complex and need careful evaluation. The surgical plan must be tailored to individual cases depending on the source of pain/disability. In cases where acetabular residual deformities such as retroversion, over-coverage, and labral pathologies continue to elicit symptoms following SHD with the RFNL procedure, additional procedures like PAO, acetabular osteoplasty, or labral repair may be required. Despite best efforts to salvage the joint, the hip may continue to progress to osteoarthrosis in some cases and may require total hip replacement2).

6. How Can Results Be Standardized to Assess the Results Uniformly?

WOMAC and HHS were the standards used for PROM (patient-reported outcome measures) reported in the studies. As the presentation of scores varied in the studies, a formal meta-analysis of the overall improvement in functional scores was precluded. The use of standard functional scores for outcome presentation should be encouraged among researchers. A strong correlation between HHS and non-arthritic hip questionnaire scores as well as the radiographic appearance of the hip has been noted20). The use of the WOMAC score in the pediatric Perthes disease population has yet to be validated. Hence, its use should not be encouraged. A clinical trial is underway to validate the score in this specific population21). Uniform usage of a functional score will add to the overall validity of the results.

7. Strength and Limitations

The current review focuses on the results of RFNL in the sequelae of Perthes disease. All studies reporting a second major procedure occurring with RFNL were excluded. Hence, this is the first review to report on the results of isolated RFNL in the treatment of Perthes disease. An extensive and robust search on PubMed and Embase was conducted in conjunction with a manual search of the included studies’ references. To conduct the review, Cochrane Handbook guidelines were followed. Functional outcomes aside, various other aspects of the study subjects such as prior, concomitant, and subsequent procedures, complications, and conversion to total hip arthroplasty were included. A proportional meta-analysis was performed on the conversion rate to total hip arthroplasty. Among the studies in the meta-analysis, a low level of heterogeneity was noted.

All studies included were retrospective in nature. None of the studies conducted a post hoc power analysis. The mean age of the patients ranged widely. A formal meta-analysis was precluded due to the highly varied reporting of functional outcomes. The WOMAC score was used to report functional outcomes. However, it has not yet been validated for use in the pediatric population with Perthes disease.

CONCLUSION

SHD, along with RFNL, has opened a new means of treatment for correcting deformities in sequelae of Perthes disease. As a result of this combined procedure, improvement in clinical and functional outcomes can be expected. Low rates of complications and future conversion to total hip arthroplasty were noted. The review results are limited by the non-uniform inclusion of study participants in terms of preoperative grading as well as any prior, concomitant, and subsequent procedures they may have undergone.

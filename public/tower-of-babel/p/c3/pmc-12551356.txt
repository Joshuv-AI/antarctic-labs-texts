The model for induced drug demand management in Iranian hospitals: a qualitative study
Authors: Mohammad-Ali Jahani, Mohammad-Reza Pasha, Seyedeh-Niko Hashemi, Seyed-Amir Soltani, Hossein-Ali Nikbakht, Ghahraman Mahmoudi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe growing trend of induced demand for medicine negatively impacts health system performance and imposes significant financial burdens on both individuals and society. Therefore, the present study aims to analyze the management of induced demand for medicine in Iranian hospitals.ResultsThis applied qualitative research, using grounded theory, achieved data saturation after interviewing 20 experts. Key factors creating induced demand were identified as social, structural-organizational, economic, and stakeholder factors. Effective management strategies include planning, organizing, monitoring, and securing financial resources. These findings underscore the importance of payment system reform and enhanced physician education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-025-07194-3.

Introduction

In modern healthcare, there is a broader focus on both medical and non-medical expectations [1, 2]. A critical issue in health cost management is induced demand for healthcare services [3]. The prescription of medicine by a doctor is considered valid because patients are often unable to diagnose the disease and the appropriate treatment themselves, and the demand of patients for medical services depends on the doctor’s diagnosis [4, 5]. In addition to information asymmetry, inefficiencies on the demand side and potential inefficiencies on the supply side are both proposed as factors that induce demand for health care [6–8]. In Iran, induced demand significantly challenges healthcare managers, contributing to a rise in out-of-pocket medical expenses for patients [6].

Globally, 15% of health expenses are related to medicine, with higher figures observed in specific regions such as Africa [9]. In Iran, the cost of medicine represents a substantial share—20–30%—of healthcare expenditure [10]. This is in conjunction with the fact that more than 50% of drugs are prescribed inappropriately [11]. As a result of excessive and incorrect drug use and the resulting economic problems, today the issue of limiting the use of drugs is an international concern, especially in developed countries, and plans are required for its management [12]. Financial factors such as inefficient payment systems may also contribute to induced demand; for example, if physicians are not satisfied with the payment system, they may be encouraged to provide unnecessary or redundant services [13]. In addition, insurance and government subsidies are important factors in the induced demand for care, as they shift the patient demand curve to the right. Also, the progress in medical technology and the per capita growth of healthcare costs significantly lead to an increase in induced demand [14]. Professional ethics and the real needs of patients should be the main basis of doctors’ behavior, patients generally trust doctors, but they are also concerned about the distortion of doctors’ behavior by economic motives [4, 15].

Comparing studies is difficult due to uncontrolled factors like physician-patient interaction and information asymmetry. Some researchers suggest doctors prioritize profitable treatments [4, 16, 17] and physician density or payment methods can stimulate demand [6, 18, 19]. Induced demand exists in countries like America, China, and the Netherlands [6, 20]. Doctors’ knowledge can influence patient demand positively or negatively [19, 21, 22].

While research acknowledges induced demand, a gap exists concerning medicine specifically. This study pioneers a framework illuminating factors driving this phenomenon. By exploring these influences, we foster transparent communication and cultivate understanding of how to manage induced demand for medicine, using expert insights. This approach promises to reshape perspectives and pave the way for interventions addressing the root causes.

Methods

Study design

This study employs a grounded theory approach within a qualitative research framework with the code of ethics (IR.IAU.CHALUS.REC.1400.038) in 2022. Grounded theory was chosen for its inductive exploration of induced demand.

Information gathering tools

A comprehensive literature review was conducted using a range of databases, including Google Scholar, PubMed, and Scopus, focusing on articles related to irrational use of medicine and healthcare services and foreign journals that were in Farsi and English, based on vocabulary and MeSH terms. According to the review of the literature and also the views of the experts in this regard, a guide form was prepared for conducting the interviews, which included nine general questions to clarify the objectives of the study.

Sample size

The study employed purposive sampling. The sample included doctors, pharmacists, and academic staff with at least ten years of experience. Participants were selected based on their expertise and potential insights into induced demand. Unwilling participants were excluded.Data measurement tools.

To ensure validity and meaningfulness of questions, expert experiences were used alongside existing studies. Informed consent was obtained, ensuring confidentiality and the right to withdraw. Written consent forms were signed. Interviews averaged 55 min. Data saturation was determined by two experts after reviewing recordings. Impressions were reviewed with interviewees for approval and revision.

Data analysis

The criteria of “authenticity,” “reliability,” and “confirmability” were considered. Audio files and transcripts were reviewed by interviewees for accuracy. Axial coding identified patterns, and selective coding linked classes to themes. MAXQDA 18 was used. Interviewees were selected based on the goal and then snowball sampling. In-depth semi-structured interviews were used, reaching saturation after 20 experts. Thematic analysis involved familiarization, coding, searching for themes, reviewing, defining, and writing. Descriptive statistics summarized demographics.

Based on the interviews, the factors affecting the induced demand for medicine in Iran included social, structural-organizational, economic, and stakeholder factors. In this regard, One expert highlighted social culture:

Doctors prescribe based on learnings and trial and error. Patients, lacking medical literacy, accept advice and get complications, returning to the doctor.

Based on the interviews, one of the factors related to insurance organizations contributing to induced demand is the lack of strict supervision by insurance companies. In this regard, one of the interviewees highlighted the role of monitoring, stating:

“Insurance organizations should monitor prescriptions, but this doesn’t happen, creating an opportunity for doctors to induce demand.”

Based on the conducted interviews, the influential factors on the planning for the management of induced drug demand from the expert’s point of view included the classes of educational factors and the service delivery structure:

Changing People’s attitudes by using public media

One of the interviewees stated: “Many believe high premiums justify unnecessary referrals. We should try to change attitudes about insurance using public media.”

Reform the education system

One of the interviewees said: “Educational systems should be defined based on ethical principles, with legal frameworks for medical ethics in patient care.”

Referral system based on primary health care providers (family physicians)

One of the interviewees said: “Implementation of the leveling system, matching services to patient needs, will prevent additional costs.”

Managed care

One of the participants suggested managed care in order to prevent the induced demand for medicine and said: “Following advanced countries, insurance organizations should create localized managed care complexes with specific pricing.“.

Implementation of electronic health records

One of the interviewees stated: “Setting up a comprehensive electronic health record system will prevent redundancies and unnecessary drugs.”

Generalizing the patient’s rights

Regarding the institutionalization of patient rights, an interviewee said: “Unfortunately, many patients are unfamiliar with their rights. Acquainting them will increase demands from the doctor and prevent induced demands.”

The factors affecting the organization of induced drug demand in Iran from the point of view of experts were presented in the form of the category of factors related to organizing the policies:

Separation of policy maker and executive

In this regard, one interviewee said: “The conflict of interests is a problem; for example, the policymaker owns a pharmaceutical company. Policymaking, implementation, and monitoring should be separate.”

Encouragement not to use insurance

In this regard, one interviewee said: “One reason for low price sensitivity is insurance. If there are concessions for less insurance use, like lower premiums, this will reduce unnecessary demands.”

The factors affecting the financial resources of induced drug demand in the form of service providers and receivers were proposed, which subcategories included the service recipient’s financial penalty, modification of the payment method, and restrictions on advertising and marketing.

Financial penalty for the recipient of the service

One of the methods that can control inappropriate requests by the patient is to consider financial penalties for unnecessary requests. One of the interviewees said: “Insurance companies should identify those with inappropriate requests and consider a higher insurance premium for them”.

Modification of the payment method

Regarding the modification of the payment method, one of the interviewees said: “This controls doctor behavior. A shortcoming is the fee-for-service system. It focuses on quantity instead of quality”.

Restrictions on advertising and marketing

A number of experts consider advertising restrictions as one of the ways to prevent induced demand in the field of medicine. One of them states: “The drug should be removed from the list of commercial goods, with no benefit for executives. The only benefit should be for the consumer.”

Factors affecting the monitoring of induced drug demand in Iran included the subcategories of implementation of clinical guidelines, modification of drug prescribing behavior, insurance restrictions and monitoring of doctors’ performance:

Implementation of clinical guidelines

Regarding the implementation of clinical guidelines as one of the solutions to deal with induced demand, one of the participants said: “Once a guideline is issued, there will be a guideline at the national level that will organize and rationalize prescriptions, forming rational consumption.”

Insurance limits

Another expert said about applying insurance restrictions: “To prevent unnecessary prescriptions, a maximum limit should be set, with more care and supervision in dealing with medical records.”

Discussion

The factors affecting induced demand were categorized as social, structural-organizational, economic, and stakeholder factors. This study identified factors that affect the management of induced demand. The findings highlight the interconnectedness of factors in shaping induced demand. This underscores the need for a comprehensive approach.

The health system is a multi-factorial system and its components cannot be considered separately, and the cause of the induced demand cannot be considered to be only the physician’s motivations for personal benefit [23]. The present study showed that many other related factors are involved in this multifactorial process.

Doctors can exploit information gaps. Meyer pointed out poor literacy as a leading cause of induced demand [24]. It was stated in a study that people’s lack of knowledge about the side effects of drugs provides the basis for the high demand for drugs [25]. In the current research, patient’s lack of knowledge is mentioned.

Belrhiti considered the creation of drug committees as one of the solutions to control the irrational prescription of drugs [26], while this issue was not mentioned in the present study. In the present study, the incorrect demand of the patient from the doctor and the lack of knowledge of the doctors in diagnosing and treating the patient’s condition have been proposed as the reasons for the induced demand. It was stated in a study that promotional gifts from pharmaceutical companies motivate many doctors, which is considered a reason for unnecessary drug prescriptions [27]. In the current research, advertising has been introduced as one of the influential factors in doctors’ prescribing behavior and the incidence of induced demand. While physician behavior can influence induced demand, the payment system also plays an important role in this area. The way physicians are paid can create different incentives for them, which affects the prescription of drugs and other medical services. In the current research, the impact of the improper payment system was pointed out as a determining factor in the occurrence of induced demand. In a study looked at the effect of the amount and manner of payment to the providers as influential factors on induced demand and considered it relevant [28]. The incorrect demand of the patient and lack of knowledge of the doctors have been proposed as reasons. Advertising has been introduced as an influential factor. While physician behavior can influence demand, the payment system plays a role [8].

The study by Munoz-Plaza showed electronic records improve quality and prevent waste [29], establishing the electronic record and replacing it with forms and offices. Paper is one of the other points of the research, which directly relates to the accuracy and durability of data and the ease and speed of providing services. The electronic record is a source of storage of individual care information throughout life, facilitating communication between the patient and the doctor [30].

Demand management through consulting is a solution to reduce induced demand. In their research, It was stated in a study that if the doctor gives the correct information to the patient, he will choose the cheap treatment [31]. Also another study showed that the percentage of unnecessary care could be greatly reduced by using control levers such as the appropriate payment method and the control lever of the prescribing behavior of the doctor on the one hand and the patient on the other [32]. Also another study [33] pointed out prescription control and stronger supervision of doctors and their assistants as solutions to control induced demand [34]. In the current research, these items have been emphasized to reduce induced demand.

Another way to control induced demand is to educate patients and increase their awareness of the side effects of excessive drug use. If patients get enough information, the information asymmetry will be minimized, resulting in reduced induced demand. Chen et al. [23] and Ron et al. [35] and Also another study [36] emphasized the issues of educating patients and increasing their awareness. In this research, the financial penalty of the service recipient was introduced as another way to reduce induced demand.

Limitations

This study has limitations. The broad scope of induced demand led to many cases. Respondents’ answers may have been influenced by bias, potentially affecting the findings.

Although diverse sampling was used, there are limitations regarding generalizability. Future studies could benefit from a more extensive strategy.

Conclusion

This study identified key factors influencing the management of induced demand: educational factors, service delivery structure, policies, and the roles of the Ministry of Health, service providers, and recipients. Corrective strategies are recommended: raising public awareness, implementing a family doctor referral system, and adopting electronic health records. Additionally, separating policymaking, enforcing guidelines, reforming payment models, and regulating advertising can curb demand. The findings emphasize the need for a patient-centered system.

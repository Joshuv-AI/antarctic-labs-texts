SKA3 targeted therapies in cancer precision surgery: a commentary
Authors: Changjiang Wu, Yi Yao, Wenqiang Lu, Ze Ji, Yiming Mao
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We were interested to read the impressive study by Feng et al.1 and would like to congratulate the authors for their comprehensive overview of the potential applications of SKA3 in cancer precision surgery. By combining bibliometrics, bioinformatics, and a systematic review, authors successfully revealed the role of molecular markers in oncology, with a focus on SKA3. This review article not only summarizes the current understanding of SKA3 in cancer but also explores its translational potential in clinical settings.

One of the key strengths of this review lies in its interdisciplinary approach. The authors draw from a diverse range of fields, including bibliometrics2, bioinformatics3, and a systematic review4, to present a holistic view of the role of SKA3 in cancer therapy. This breadth of perspective is particularly valuable in the field of precision surgery, where a multidisciplinary approach is crucial for effective clinical outcomes. Moreover, the integration of bibliometrics and bioinformatics allows for a more rigorous and quantitative analysis of the available data, enhancing the reliability and impact of the findings.

In addition, this commentary also prompts further reflection on the relationship between surgery and molecular markers like SKA3. While this review effectively establishes the scientific rationale for targeting SKA3 in cancer surgery, it leaves open questions about the practical implementation of such therapies. For instance, how do we ensure that the bench discoveries on SKA3 translate effectively into clinical practice? What are the challenges and barriers to overcoming in this translational process? Additionally, the regulatory considerations surrounding the use of novel molecular targets in surgery deserve further exploration.

In summary, the review by Feng et al.1 represents a significant contribution to the understanding of SKA3 in cancer precision surgery. Its interdisciplinary approach and rigorous methodology make it a valuable resource for researchers and clinicians alike. However, as we move forward in exploring the potential of molecular markers in surgery, it is important to consider the practical and ethical implications of these discoveries. Future research should focus on bridging the gap between bench and bedside, ensuring that the promise of precision surgery is fully realized for patients.

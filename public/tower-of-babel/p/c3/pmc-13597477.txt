GALNT5 drives colorectal cancer progression and chemoresistance via PI3K/Akt/ABCC1 axis
Authors: Qiong Wu, Liehao Yang, Bing Bai, Jian Jiang, Tong Liu, Yiquan Sun, Changmiao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPolypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 5 (GALNT5), a member of the GALNT enzyme family, has been implicated in the pathogenesis of various malignancies. However, its precise role in colorectal cancer (CRC) progression and its contribution to acquired resistance to the FOLFOX components (oxaliplatin and 5-fluorouracil) remains poorly understood. This study aimed to elucidate the biological functions of GALNT5 in CRC pathogenesis and chemoresistance.MethodsGALNT5 expression in CRC cell lines was evaluated through integrated bioinformatics analyses, RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq), RT–qPCR, and Western blotting. Isogenic CRC sublines resistant to oxaliplatin (LoVo/L) or 5-fluorouracil (LoVo/5FU) were established separately by chronic exposure to each drug. RNA-Seq was employed to identify downstream signaling pathways and effector genes modulated by GALNT5. VVL lectin pull-down assays were performed to confirm that GALNT5 mediates O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR—an upstream activator of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. Functional assays, including CCK-8 and Transwell, were conducted to assess cell proliferation, apoptosis, and invasion. The involvement of the PI3K pathway was examined using the PI3K agonist 740Y-P, while expression changes of the downstream effector ABCC1 were analyzed both in vitro and in vivo.ResultsGALNT5 was significantly upregulated in CRC cell lines compared to the normal colonic epithelial cell line NCM460. Knockdown of GALNT5 markedly suppressed proliferation and invasion, while promoting apoptosis in both parental and chemoresistant CRC cells. Silencing GALNT5 enhanced the sensitivity of resistant cells to oxaliplatin (L-OHP) and 5-fluorouracil (5FU), an effect that was reversible upon PI3K pathway activation, indicating the involvement of the PI3K/Akt axis. Mechanistically, GALNT5 activated the PI3K/Akt axis through O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR. Furthermore, GALNT5 depletion led to downregulation of the multidrug resistance-associated protein ABCC1 in vitro and in vivo.ConclusionGALNT5 facilitates malignant progression in CRC by promoting O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR and thereby activating the PI3K/Akt pathway, and confers resistance to L-OHP and 5FU through upregulation of ABCC1. These findings suggest GALNT5 as a potential diagnostic biomarker and a promising therapeutic target for overcoming chemoresistance in CRC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00432-026-06533-6.

Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most frequently diagnosed cancer globally, has the second highest mortality rate and constitutes a substantial worldwide health burden (Benson et al. 2018; Sung et al. 2021). Its high mortality rate is attributed primarily to its aggressive tumor biology, metastatic propensity, and frequent emergence of chemoresistance (de Mello et al. 2013). Current first-line interventions include surgical intervention, systemic chemotherapy, and radiation therapy, with radical surgery remaining the primary curative approach for localized disease. However, approximately 50% of patients with metastatic CRC (mCRC) are not candidates for surgical intervention, highlighting the critical importance of systemic chemotherapy (Li et al. 2020). The FOLFOX regimen—which integrates the antimetabolite 5FU, the platinum-based agent L-OHP, and leucovorin (LEU)—functions as a cornerstone of first-line chemotherapy for CRC (Tam et al. 2023).

For stage II and advanced-stage CRC, adjuvant or palliative chemotherapy is routinely administered to improve prognosis and prolong overall survival. 5FU, a thymidylate synthase inhibitor, has long served as the cornerstone of CRC therapy, with LEU potentiating its cytotoxic effects (Wolpin et al. 2007). The incorporation of L-OHP into the FOLFOX regimen has significantly enhanced therapeutic outcomes, extending the median overall survival to up to two years in patients with mCRC (Gustavsson et al. 2015). Despite these advances, chemoresistance remains a major clinical obstacle, with objective response rates observed in only 30–50% of patients (Jensen et al. 2015). While resistance mechanisms to single agents such as 5FU and L-OHP have been partially characterized, the shared molecular pathways that simultaneously influence sensitivity to both agents remain poorly understood. Chemoresistance in CRC is a multifactorial phenomenon involving reduced drug activation, enhanced metabolic inactivation, increased efflux via transporter proteins, impaired cellular uptake, and evasion of apoptosis (Gonzalez-Molina et al. 2022). Intrinsic resistance is often associated with the overexpression of drug efflux transporters and mutations in key oncogenes and signaling molecules, including KRAS, BRAF, and EGFR, as well as aberrations in key signal transduction pathways, such as MAPK and PI3K/Akt (Wang et al. 2022). This study aimed to elucidate the shared molecular mechanisms by which GALNT5 confers resistance to L-OHP and 5FU—the two active agents of the FOLFOX regimen using independently derived resistant sublines, and to guide the development of novel therapeutic strategies for chemotherapy-refractory colorectal cancer.

The GALNT family comprises twenty isoenzymes that catalyze the initial step of O-GalNAc glycosylation, resulting in the formation of the Tn antigen. Aberrant overexpression of the Tn antigen has been documented in a range of pathological conditions, such as cancer, immune system disorders, nervous system diseases, and IgA nephropathy, and is associated with tumor metastasis and immune evasion (Hussain et al. 2016; Pinho and Reis 2015; Zhang et al. 2022). Multiple members of the GALNT family are upregulated in various malignancies and have been linked to cancer stemness and metastatic potential (Liu et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2022). Among these genes, GALNT5 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, and pancreatic cancer, where it promotes tumorigenesis and disease progression through O-glycosylation-dependent activation of signaling pathways (Detarya et al. 2022; Guo et al. 2018; Stiegelbauer et al. 2017). Although emerging evidence suggests that GALNT5 may enhance invasive properties in CRC, its involvement in chemoresistance remains unexplored.

Our findings reveal a dual role for GALNT5 in colorectal cancer: it promotes tumor progression and confers resistance to L-OHP and 5FU, the two active drugs in the FOLFOX regimen. Our data indicate that elevated GALNT5 expression in CRC tissues predicts poor recurrence-free survival outcomes. By employing both laboratory and live organism models, we demonstrate that GALNT5, via O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR, activates the PI3K/Akt pathway, which in turn upregulates ABCC1 and reduces cellular sensitivity to L-OHP and 5FU. Importantly, silencing GALNT5 suppresses the PI3K/Akt/ABCC1 axis and reverses the chemoresistant phenotype. These findings identify GALNT5 as a potential diagnostic biomarker and a therapeutic target for overcoming chemoresistance in CRC.

Methods

Cell culture and generation of chemoresistant models

LoVo (ATCC, CCL-229) and SW480 (ATCC, CCL-228) cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The non-malignant human colonic epithelial cell line NCM460 (iCell-h373) was acquired from Cellverse in Shanghai, China. Cell culture was performed under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). Specifically, SW480 line was maintained in DMEM (MilliporeSigma) supplemented with 10% FBS (PAN Biotech) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco), whereas NCM460 cells were grown in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) with the same supplements. LoVo cells, a p53 wild-type colorectal adenocarcinoma line derived from lymph node metastasis, were cultured in F12K medium (F12K, Solarbio) containing 10% FBS, penicillin, and streptomycin.

Induction of drug-resistant sublines

The L-OHP-resistant subline (LoVo/L) was established from parental LoVo cells through stepwise exposure to increasing concentrations of L-OHP (HY-17371, MCE). Exposure to 0.5 μM L-OHP produced a greater than 50% reduction in cell viability. These remaining cells were then propagated in the presence of the identical drug concentration until 80% confluent, after which they were passaged twice. The process was repeated with escalating doses (1, 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 16 μM) until the IC50 of L-OHP in LoVo/L exceeded that of LoVo by ≥ threefold. Similarly, the 5FU-resistant subline (LoVo/5FU) was generated using a concentration gradient of 5FU (HY-90006, MCE) 0.2, 1, 3.9, 15.6, 62.5, 250, 1000, 5000, 10,000 μM. LoVo cells were initially treated with 0.2 μM 5FU, resulting in > 50% lethality. Surviving cells were expanded and passaged twice at each concentration before proceeding to higher doses. Resistance was confirmed when the IC50 of 5FU in LoVo/5FU was ≥ threefold higher than in LoVo.

Lentivirus packaging

To produce lentivirus, HEK293T cells were transfected with a combination of the lentiviral vector (10 μg) and the packaging plasmids psPAX2 (6 μg) and pMD2.G (3 μg), using Polyethylenimine (PEI; Sigma) as the transfection reagent. The viral supernatant, collected at 48 and 72 h, was subsequently pelleted by ultracentrifugation (64,300 × g, 2.5 h, 4 °C).

RT-qPCR analysis

Following total RNA isolation via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen™, USA) according to the supplied protocol, the samples were subjected to reverse transcription with the Toloscript All-in-One RT EasyMix for qPCR (TOLOBIO, #22,107) to yield cDNA. Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) assays were conducted in triplicate using a SYBR Green-based detection system (TOLOBIO Universal SYBR 2 × Q3 Master Mix, Cat. #22,204) on a StepOnePlus™ thermocycler (Applied Biosystems). Gene-specific primers were designed as follows:

GAPDH (housekeeping gene):

Forward: 5′-GAA GGC TGG GGC TCA TTT GCA GGG-3′

Reverse: 5′-GGT GCA GGA GGC ATT GCT GAT GAT-3′

GALNT4 (target gene):

Forward: 5′-ACA GTG GCC TAT ATC TTC GTG G-3′

Reverse: 5′-CTC CTG CGG AGG CAT GAA AA-3′

GALNT5 (target gene):

Forward: 5′-TCA TCC AGA ACT GGT GAA TCA CAT-3′

Reverse: 5′-TGG GTC ACA GGC AGC TAC T-3′

The qPCR amplification profile comprised a pre-incubation at 95 °C for 2 min, succeeded by 40 rounds of thermal cycling (95 °C for 10 s; 60 °C for 30 s) for denaturation and primer annealing/extension, respectively. The specificity of the amplification products was assessed by melting curve analysis. The relative quantification of target genes was determined using the 2 − ΔΔCt method, normalized to the endogenous reference gene GAPDH.

Western blot analysis

Whole-cell lysates were prepared from SW480, LoVo, LoVo/L and LoVo/5FU cells. Protein extraction was conducted in an ice-cold RIPA lysis system supplemented with protease inhibitors. For immunoblotting, 40 µg of total protein was subjected to electrophoresis on 10% SDS-PAGE gels under cold conditions. The separated proteins were then transferred to PVDF membranes, blocked with 10% non-fat milk for 2 h at room temperature, and finally incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight (GALNT5 1:1000, #NBP1-62,372, NOVUS; GAPDH 1:1000, #10,494–1-AP, Proteintech; Bcl-2 1:2000, #12,789–1-AP, Proteintech; Bax 1:2000, #50,599–2-IG, Proteintech; Beta Actin 1:1000, #20,536–1-AP, Proteintech; ERCC1 1:1000, #F1083, Selleck; TYMS 1:1000, #ET1705-24, HUABIO; DPYD 1:500, #ET7110-83, HUABIO; MMP-2 1:1000, #WL03224, Wanleibio; MMP-9 1:1000, #F0008, Selleck; p-AKT 1:1000, #ET1607-73, HUABIO; Akt 1:1000, #ET1609-51, HUABIO; p-PI3K 1:1000, #HA721672, HUABIO; PI3K 1:1000, #ET1608-70, HUABIO; PCNA 1:1000, #F0018, Selleck; ABCC1 1:1000, #ET1704-56, HUABIO; EGFR 1:1000, #ET1604-44, HUABIO; p-EGFR 1:1000, #2234, CST). Following a series of TBST washes (3 × 10 min), the membranes were exposed to an HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (Cell Signaling Technology; 1:5000) for a duration of 2 h at room temperature. Following the final TBST washes, protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) system. The gray values of the immunoreactive bands, measured with Image J software, were used to determine relative protein expression, which was defined as the ratio of the target protein signal to the loading control. Independent triplicate experiments were performed.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assessment

Proliferation analysis

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 2 × 103 cells/well and incubated for 0, 24, and 48 h. Cell viability was assessed by CCK-8 assay (Vazyme, A311) following the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured with a BioTek ELx800 microplate reader.

Cytotoxicity evaluation

Following plating in 96-well plates at 5 × 103 cells/well, cells were cultured for 72 h. Following treatment exposure, cell viability was quantified through CCK-8 assay (Vazyme) as described above. Optical density readings were similarly acquired using the same microplate reader system.

IC50 assays

A density of 5 × 103 cells per well was used for plating in 96-well plates. Following seeding, the cells were cultured for a period of 72 h. Serial dilutions of L-OHP were prepared at concentrations of 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, and 16 μM. Similarly, 5FU was serially diluted at concentrations of 0, 0.2, 1, 3.9, 15.6, 62.5, 250, 1000, 5000, and 10,000 μM. Cell viability was measured by CCK-8 assay as per the manufacturer's protocol. The dose–response relationship was visualized by plotting viability rates versus the logarithm of drug concentrations. The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was then derived from the fitted sigmoidal curve.

Transwell invasion assay

Transwell chambers with 8 μm pore size (Costar #3422, Corning, USA) were employed to assess cellular invasiveness. Cells (5 × 104) in 200 μL serum-free medium were placed in the upper chamber, with 800 μL complete medium containing 10% FBS as a chemoattractant in the lower compartment. After 24 h incubation, non-invading cells were swabbed from the upper membrane surface. Migrated cells on the lower membrane were fixed in 4% PFA (15 min, RT) and stained with 1% crystal violet (15 min). The number of invaded cells was determined by counting five random microscopic fields on a Nikon Eclipse TS2 microscope. Values are expressed as mean ± SD from three independent replicates.

VVL lectin pull-down assay

Cell lysates (1 mg total protein) were incubated with VVL-agarose beads (Vector Laboratories, USA) overnight at 4 °C. After four washes, bound proteins were eluted and analyzed by Western blot using anti-EGFR antibody. For competition, lysates were pre-incubated with 200 mM GalNAc (Sigma-Aldrich) for 30 min at 4 °C before adding the beads. Input lysates served as loading controls.

Animal model studies

All procedures involving animals were authorized by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Wuhan Myhalic Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Animal experiments conformed to the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Mice were kept under SPF conditions per institutional animal care standards.

Xenograft model

The BALB/c-nu female mice (specific pathogen-free grade, body weight 19 g ± 2 g, n = 36) were obtained from Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co. Ltd. LoVo, LoVo/L, and LoVo/5FU cells transfected with NC/GALNT5 shRNA were harvested upon reaching mid-log phase, resuspended at 1 × 10⁶ cells per injection, and subcutaneously transplanted into each mouse. On the fourth day (Day 0) post-transplantation, the mice were randomly assigned to six groups: ① LoVo + NC shRNA, ② LoVo + GALNT5 shRNA, ③ LoVo/L + NC shRNA, ④ LoVo/L + GALNT5 shRNA, ⑤ LoVo/5FU + NC shRNA, and ⑥ LoVo/5FU + GALNT5 shRNA; with six mice in each group. Following inoculation, mice in groups three and four received intraperitoneal injections of L-OHP (10 mg/kg), while those in groups five and six were administered intraperitoneally with 5FU (23 mg/kg) once a week for four consecutive weeks. Tumor growth was monitored regularly using calipers for measurement. Once tumors reached a size of approximately 1.5 cm3, they were harvested, enabling iterative cycles for continued expansion or subsequent analytical procedures.

Immunohistochemical analysis

Forty-eight tissue specimens of xenograft mice were collected, including adjacent tissues, cancer tissues and cancer tissues after GALNT5 knockdown. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded sections underwent standard processing including xylene-mediated deparaffinization followed by graded ethanol rehydration. Antigen retrieval was performed using a PT-Link system at 95 °C with appropriate buffer incubation. After peroxidase blocking (3% H2O2, 30 min, RT), the sections were probed with primary rabbit polyclonal anti-GALNT5 and ABCC1 antibodies in an overnight incubation at 4 °C. Thereafter, a 30-min incubation with the secondary antibody was carried out at RT (anti-rabbit, HRP-conjugated). Chromogenic detection was achieved using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate under controlled room temperature conditions. Counterstaining was performed with hematoxylin prior to microscopic evaluation. Staining patterns were assessed by two independent pathologists using light microscopy at both low and high magnification fields. Semi-quantitative analysis of GALNT5 and ABCC1 expression differences between malignant and paratumoral tissues was conducted based on staining intensity and cellular distribution patterns.

RNA-seq data analysis

Trim Galore (v0.6.10) was utilized for adapter removal and quality filtering; subsequent alignment of clean reads to the GENCODE hg38 reference genome was conducted with HISAT2 (v2.2.1) (Kim et al. 2019) and default parameters. Read counts from FeatureCounts (v2.0.6) (Liao et al. 2014) were utilized to screen for differentially expressed genes using DESeq2 (v1.40.2) (Love et al. 2014) with significance defined as |log2FC|> 1 at p < 0.05.

TCGA gene expression and survival analysis

TCGA and GTEx data from the GEPIA portal were interrogated to profile the expression of GALNT family genes. Concurrently, the KMplot tool (Győrffy 2021) was employed to evaluate overall and recurrence-free survival.

Functional enrichment analysis and gene set enrichment analysis

To elucidate the functional implications of DEGs, we utilized the clusterProfiler package (v4.8.2) (Yu et al. 2012) for concurrent Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), Gene Ontology (GO), and gene set enrichment analyses.

scRNA-seq data processing

Following acquisition of the dataset GSE161283 from the GEO database (Zheng et al. 2022), all downstream analyses were executed in Seurat v4.4.0. After stringent quality control (200–6000 genes/cell, > 1000 UMIs, mitochondrial ratio < 10%), the DoubletFinder (v2.0.3) (McGinnis et al. 2019) and DecontX (v1.4.0) (Yang et al. 2020) algorithms were employed to remove doublets and ambient RNA, respectively. Batch effect correction was then applied using Harmony (v1.2.3). The top 40 embeddings from Harmony, determined by the "ElbowPlot", served as the basis for cell clustering. The "FindClusters" function was applied across a spectrum of resolutions (0.1–1.2), and the clustree R package (v0.5.1) was used to select the most robust clustering resolution. Differential gene analysis was performed using the MAST method ("FindAllMarkers" function) with the donor as the latent variable (Finak et al. 2015). DEGs were identified with Bonferroni-adjusted P values smaller than 0.05 and log2-fold-change values larger than 0.25.

Single-cell copy-number variation evaluation

To distinguish tumor and non-tumor cells, we applied the inferCNV R package (v1.22.0) (https://github.com/broadinstitute/inferCNV) to assess chromosomal copy-number variation (CNV) in individual cells. The algorithm infers CNV states by comparing the expression of neighboring genes across the genome, under the premise that regional expression patterns reflect underlying chromosomal alterations. Reference cells (T and B cells) and observed cells (all others) were defined. The inferCNV analysis utilized the "denoise" function, default HMM parameters, and a 0.1 cutoff.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD from independent biological replicates. Statistical significance was determined by t-test using GraphPad Prism, with *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001 considered significant.

The analysis revealed 5,666 differentially expressed genes in LoVo cells (Fig. 1A), including eight GALNT family members (Fig. 1B). To systematically evaluate the expression patterns of GALNTs and validate the RNA-seq findings, we analyzed differential GALNT gene expression in CRC using the TCGA combined with the GTEx colon adenocarcinoma database via GEPIA. Comparative evaluation revealed significantly elevated expression of GALNT4 and GALNT5 in colorectal carcinoma tissues compared with their adjacent normal counterparts (Fig. 1D, E, Supplementary Fig. 1A). Systematic analysis of colorectal cancer cell lines confirmed significant increases in GALNT4 and GALNT5 mRNA levels compared with those in nonmalignant intestinal epithelial cells (Fig. 1F, G). Our findings support the involvement of GALNT4 and GALNT5 in CRC development and malignant progression. Survival curves revealed that elevated GALNT4 or GALNT5 levels, although not affecting overall survival (OS), correlated with significantly reduced recurrence-free survival (RFS) (Fig. 1H–K). These findings led us to hypothesize that high GALNT expression might be associated with acquired chemoresistance following surgery in CRC patients.

To elucidate the molecular basis of chemoresistance, we conducted transcriptome sequencing of parental LoVo cells along with their L-OHP-resistant (LoVo/L) and 5FU-resistant (LoVo/5FU) derivatives. Analysis of 894 shared differentially expressed genes in the two resistance models revealed consistent GALNT5 upregulation relative to that in parental cells (Fig. 1L–N). Collectively, these findings indicate that GALNT5 contributes to CRC pathogenesis and confers chemoresistance.

Subsequent assessment of GALNT5 distribution in CRC revealed its predominant localization within epithelial cells (Fig. 2E). To determine whether GALNT5 was expressed primarily in normal epithelial or tumor cells, we subclustered the epithelial cells into 17 subsets (Fig. 2F). InferCNV analysis was performed to explore chromosomal copy number variations (CNVs) in these cells. An evaluation of cell invasion capacity revealed substantially diminished invasion capacity in the GALNT5-knockdown groups compared with their control counterparts (Fig. 3D, E). To extend these findings, we examined the contribution of GALNT5 to chemoresistance in colon cancer (Supplementary Fig. 2B, C). Western blot analysis revealed higher GALNT5 expression in the resistant cell lines LoVo/L and LoVo/5FU than in the parental LoVo cells (Fig. 3H). We therefore established GALNT5 knockdown derivatives of LoVo/L and LoVo/5FU cells (Fig. 3I, J). The knockdown of GALNT5 led to marked downregulation of the L-OHP resistance-associated protein ERCC1 and the 5FU resistance-associated protein TYMS (Fig. 3K, L). Compared with the controls, GALNT5 knockdown reduced the IC50 values for the respective chemotherapeutic agents by 3.40-fold in LoVo/L cells and 6.23-fold in LoVo/5FU cells (Fig. 3M, N). These results indicate that GALNT5 knockdown significantly sensitizes colon cancer cells to 5FU and L-OHP. Additionally, GALNT5 knockdown significantly suppressed invasion (Fig. 3O, P) and proliferation (Fig. 3Q, R) in chemoresistant variants. These findings demonstrate that GALNT5 is closely associated with chemoresistance, invasion, and proliferation in CRC cells.

Re-analysis of previously published mass spectrometry data from pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma identified EGFR as a potential GALNT5-interacting protein (Jia et al. 2024). KEGG enrichment analysis further revealed that GALNT5-associated proteins were significantly enriched in the PI3K/Akt signaling and ABC transporter pathways (Fig. 4E). Given that EGFR is a well-established upstream activator of the PI3K/Akt cascade, we hypothesized that GALNT5 activates this pathway through O-glycosylation of EGFR. To test this, we examined EGFR phosphorylation in LoVo and SW480 cells. GALNT5 knockdown markedly reduced EGFR phosphorylation at Tyr1068, while total EGFR levels remained unchanged (Fig. 4F; Supplementary Fig. 3A). We next performed VVL lectin pull-down assays to directly assess O-GalNAc modification of EGFR. In control cells (shCtrl), VVL beads recovered a clear EGFR band, indicating basal O-GalNAc modification. This signal dropped sharply in GALNT5-knockdown cells (shGALNT5). Pre-incubation of shGALNT5 lysates with free GalNAc further eliminated the residual signal, confirming lectin binding specificity. Input blots showed comparable total EGFR and GAPDH across all lanes (Fig. 4G; Supplementary Fig. 3B). Together, these results indicate that GALNT5 mediates O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR, which in turn promotes its phosphorylation.

Subsequently, we investigated whether GALNT5 affects CRC cell proliferation and invasion via the PI3K/Akt pathway. We found that knockdown of GALNT5 in LoVo and SW480 cells significantly reduced the expression of phosphorylated PI3K and AKT. Treatment with the PI3K-specific agonist 740Y-P partially restored the levels of these phosphoproteins that were reduced by GALNT5 knockdown (Fig. 4H, Supplementary Fig. 3C). GALNT5 depletion downregulated PCNA, MMP-2 and Bcl-2 (anti-apoptotic) but upregulated Bax (pro-apoptotic), indicating coordinated regulation of proliferation, invasion and apoptosis. 740Y-P treatment partially reversed these changes in expression induced by GALNT5 knockdown (Fig. 4I–K, Supplementary Fig. 3D-F). These results indicate that low GALNT5 expression inhibits proliferation and invasion and promotes apoptosis in colon cancer cells, effects that can be partially reversed by 740Y-P. Transwell assays further confirmed that 740Y-P treatment partially restored the impaired cell invasion capacity caused by GALNT5 knockdown (Fig. 4L, Supplementary Fig. 3G). CCK-8 proliferation assays revealed that the markedly impaired proliferation of shGALNT5 cells was partially reversed by 740Y-P treatment (Fig. 4M, Supplementary Fig. 3H). These findings establish GALNT5 as a critical regulator of proliferative, invasive and anti-apoptotic phenotypes in colorectal cancer cells via the PI3K/Akt axis.

ABC transporters represent the most extensive superfamily of membrane transport proteins and serve as primary drivers of multidrug resistance in oncology. Previous studies have reported that the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway can positively regulate ABCC1 expression to promote multidrug resistance. Our earlier findings indicated alterations in both the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and ABC transporters upon GALNT5 knockdown (Fig. 4C, D). Western blot analysis demonstrated that ABCC1 protein expression was significantly decreased following GALNT5 knockdown in LoVo/L and LoVo/5FU cells, and this reduction was partially reversed by the addition of 740Y-P (Fig. 5Q, R). Discussion

CRC is a highly prevalent malignant tumor of the digestive tract, with recurrence and metastasis being the primary causes of mortality (Keum and Giovannucci 2019; Young et al. 2014). For patients with resected colorectal cancer, the FOLFOX regimen is the standard adjuvant chemotherapy, but resistance to its two core components—L-OHP and 5FU—frequently arises (Yaffee et al. 2015). Therefore, elucidating the mechanisms underlying CRC pathogenesis and chemoresistance and identifying potential therapeutic targets have become key focuses in clinical research.

GALNT5 is a key enzyme that mediates mucin-type O-glycosylation, a post-translational modification widely implicated in promoting tumor metastasis, immune evasion, and the progression of various malignancies (Chia et al. 2014; Festari et al. 2022). Previous studies have demonstrated that GALNT5 acts as an oncogene in various cancer types. For example, GALNT5 interacts with HSP90 to promote gastric cancer development (Guo et al. 2018). In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PAAD), GALNT5 enhances tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by modulating the TNF signaling pathway while concurrently suppressing ferroptosis to facilitate tumor growth (Yan et al. 2023). Additionally, GALNT5 could promote cholangiocarcinoma progression by activating the AKT/ERK signaling cascade (Detarya et al. 2020). However, the expression and function of GALNT5 in CRC are still unknown. In this study, we found that GALNT5 was highly expressed in CRC cell lines and further elevated in L-OHP- and 5FU-resistant cell lines. Consistent with these findings, analysis of public datasets (TCGA and GTEx) confirmed that GALNT5 expression is aberrantly elevated in CRC tissues. Furthermore, knockdown of GALNT5 in multiple CRC cell lines significantly inhibited cell proliferation and invasion but increased apoptosis, suggesting that GALNT5 might promote CRC development.

Moreover, survival analysis based on TCGA clinical data revealed that high GALNT5 expression did not significantly affect overall survival (OS) in CRC patients but was significantly correlated with poor recurrence-free survival (RFS). Qinyuan Jia et al. reported that in PAAD, GALNT5 conferred resistance to L-OHP and some kinds of irinotecan in the FOLFIRINOX regimen but had little effect on 5FU sensitivity (Jia et al. 2024). Based on above the results, we further explored the role of GALNT5 in CRC. In our study, knockdown of GALNT5 significantly sensitized resistant CRC cells to L-OHP and 5FU, as demonstrated by markedly decreased IC50 values in LoVo/L and LoVo/5FU cells and downregulation of the L-OHP resistance-related protein ERCC1 and the 5FU resistance-related protein TYMS.

The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway has been demonstrated to regulate many malignant phenotypes in cancer, including cell survival, proliferation, metabolism, and immune evasion by activating Akt (Fontana et al. 2024; Fresno Vara et al. 2004). The PI3K/Akt pathway also mediates chemoresistance, with a key mechanism being the upregulation of ABC transporters such as ABCC1 (MRP1) to increase drug efflux (Liu et al. 2020; Tazzari et al. 2007).

Transcriptome sequencing analysis in our study demonstrated that both the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and the ABC transporter pathway were strongly suppressed in GALNT5-knockdown resistant cells. To identify upstream activators of the PI3K/Akt axis that may account for its suppression upon GALNT5 depletion, we examined several receptor tyrosine kinases known to trigger this cascade, including EGFR, IGFR, and MET (Ganesan et al. 2024; Lan et al. 2025; Song et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2025). Reanalysis of published mass spectrometry data from pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma pointed to EGFR as a candidate GALNT5-associated protein (Jia, Zhu, Yao, Yin, Duan, Zheng, Ma, Yang, Yang, Zhang, Liu, Hua, Huo, Fu, Sun and Liu 2024). In our CRC cell lines, silencing GALNT5 lowered EGFR phosphorylation at Tyr1068 without changing total EGFR protein abundance. Detarya et al. previously documented that GALNT5 positively regulates EGFR in cholangiocarcinoma and, using molecular dynamics simulations, suggested possible O-GalNAc glycosylation of EGFR, yet direct evidence was not provided (Detarya et al. 2022). These clues led us to ask whether GALNT5 directly O-GalNAc-glycosylates EGFR in colorectal cancer cells.

We consequently carried out VVL lectin pull-down experiments. Reducing GALNT5 expression substantially diminished the amount of EGFR recovered by VVL, implying that this glycosyltransferase is necessary for O-GalNAc modification of EGFR. Taken together, these findings support a model wherein GALNT5 drives EGFR activation through O-GalNAc glycosylation, thereby upregulating the PI3K/Akt signaling axis and downstream effectors such as ABCC1.

Furthermore, GALNT5 depletion inhibited the phosphorylation and activation of PI3K and AKT in both parental and chemoresistant cells. Concomitantly, it downregulated key proteins involved in proliferation (PCNA), invasion (MMP-2), and anti-apoptosis (Bcl-2) while upregulating the pro-apoptotic protein Bax. More importantly, in resistant cells, GALNT5 positively regulates ABCC1 expression via the PI3K/Akt pathway. Rescue experiments using the PI3K-specific agonist 740Y-P partly reversed the phenotypic and molecular changes induced by GALNT5 knockdown, indicating that the PI3K/Akt signaling axis may play a central role in the functions of GALNT5. Moreover, in vivo experiments also supported this mechanism: GALNT5 knockdown markedly suppressed the growth of xenograft tumors, enhanced chemosensitivity, and was accompanied by decreased ABCC1 expression in tumor tissues.

We acknowledge that our study examined resistance to L-OHP and 5FU as single agents. Although these two drugs are the active components of the FOLFOX regimen, we did not evaluate the effect of GALNT5 knockdown on sensitivity to concurrent combination treatment with both agents. Therefore, the role of GALNT5 in resistance to the full FOLFOX combination therapy remains to be determined in future studies using clinically relevant drug ratios and combined exposure protocols. Additionally, the precise O-GalNAc glycosylation site(s) on EGFR mediated by GALNT5 warrant further investigation.

Notwithstanding these limitations, our study demonstrates that GALNT5 promotes both malignant progression and resistance to L-OHP and 5FU in colorectal cancer by activating the PI3K/Akt/ABCC1 axis. GALNT5 may act not only as a potential biomarker for predicting recurrence and drug resistance but also as a promising therapeutic target. Inhibitors or gene-silencing strategies targeting GALNT5 may provide new approaches to reverse chemoresistance and improve clinical outcomes for CRC patients.

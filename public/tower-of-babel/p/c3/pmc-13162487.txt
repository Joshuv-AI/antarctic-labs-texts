Conservative treatment of a horizontal root fracture at the bone margin of a mandibular central incisor: a case report
Authors: Katsuyuki Atsumi, Naomi Tanoue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRoot fractures occurring close to the bone margin have a poor prognosis. Although conservative treatment is preferred, it presents considerable challenges. This report describes the successful use of two types of mineral trioxide aggregate materials for conservative treatment of horizontal cervical root fractures.Case presentationA 25-year-old Japanese woman experienced root fractures of the mandibular central incisor due to trauma. At the initial visit, the crown of the tooth on the labial side was fractured above the gingival margin, and the root on the lingual side was fractured near the bone margin. The fractured tooth structure was bonded with methyl methacrylate-based resin cement, and the gap was filled with a mineral trioxide aggregate material, a resin-modified mineral trioxide aggregate material, and a methyl methacrylate-based adhesive resin cement. The tooth was successfully preserved through the use of multiple fiber post reinforcements, minimal periodontal surgery, bleaching, and composite resin filling.ConclusionThe treatment approach applied in this study, using appropriate materials and surgical procedures, successfully preserved the tooth with horizontal root fractures near the alveolar bone, which are considered difficult to treat. This case suggests that appropriate material selection and meticulous treatment procedures can enable successful tooth preservation.

Background

The repositioning, stabilization, and occlusal adjustment of root-fractured teeth generally lead to favorable outcomes [1]. Timely diagnosis and treatment of horizontal root fractures achieve favorable results in up to 92% of cases [2]. However, fractures in the cervical region often have a poor prognosis, owing to challenges related to their proximity to the gingival sulcus, high risk of bacterial contamination, and limited healing capacity [3, 4]. In addition, horizontal fractures at the cervical region are particularly prone to re-trauma owing to their high mobility, resulting in poor survival rates [3]. Tooth extraction or complex prosthetic solutions are often needed in such cases. Moreover, the long-term survival of teeth with root fractures is strongly influenced by the fracture location.

Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) has excellent biocompatibility and sealing ability, including the promotion of cementogenesis. While MTA is widely used in perforation repair, apexification, and pulp capping, it lacks adhesive properties, limiting its use in cases requiring high mechanical strength and adhesion, such as root fractures [5]. Although MTA has been utilized in cases of mid-root fractures with minimal displacement of the fragments [6], its nonadhesive nature necessitates supplementary adhesive materials or stabilization. This report describes the successful use of two types of MTA materials in the treatment of severe horizontal cervical fractures, which resulted in favorable long-term clinical and radiographic outcomes.

Case presentation

A 25-year-old Japanese woman presented with facial trauma from a hockey puck, limited to soft tissues, with no damage extending to the jawbone, causing complete and incomplete luxation of the maxillary right and left central incisors, respectively, along with a horizontal root fracture of the lower right central incisor. Initial management included replantation of the maxillary teeth and pulpectomy of the lower incisor. Clinical examination revealed fractures of the labial crown above the gingival margin (Fig. 1a, b) and the lingual root near the bone margin (Fig. 1c), approximately 6 mm below the gingival margin. Although extraction and prosthetic replacement were discussed, the patient preferred to preserve the tooth. Therefore, the crown was repositioned and fixed with methyl methacrylate (MMA)-based cement (SuperBond Radiopaque; Sun Medical, Japan) (Fig. 1d, e) as a conventional method. However, repositioning and adhesion were incomplete at the lingual site, probably owing to soft tissue intrusion. The lingual cement was replaced with MTA (BioMTA Cement; Morita Corporation, Suita, Japan) after one month (Fig. 1f). Another month later, a resin-modified MTA (SuperMTA Paste; Sun Medical) [7] was applied over the initial layer, followed by MMA-based cement (SuperBond EX Radiopaque) to achieve full adhesion. This adhesive sequence enabled root canal treatment.

After canal filling (Fig. 1g), walking bleach with urea peroxide was applied six times until the color desired by the patient was achieved. Once the tooth shade improved, a foundation was built using four 1.0-mm glass fiber-reinforced epoxy posts (i-TFC Luminous) (Fig. 1h). Periodontal surgery was performed to remove excess MTA and clean the root via an erbium-doped yttrium–aluminum garnet laser (Fig. 1i and j). Radiography confirmed accurate repositioning and healthy bone formation (Fig. 1k). After postsurgical soft tissue improvement, the labial and lingual cavities were filled with composite resin (Fig. 1l). At the four-year follow-up, the periodontal tissue remained stable without radiographic abnormalities (Fig. 1m), and the progress of the maxillary tooth replantation was also adequate. The patient reported satisfaction with the aesthetic outcomes.

Discussion

Cervical root fractures have a poor prognosis owing to microbial risk and anatomical complexity, and traditional management often involves extraction. The present report describes the successful application of a conservative treatment method using two MTA materials under favorable clinical conditions, including early diagnosis, effective disinfection, and accessible fracture sites.

In the present case, the primary challenge was the deep gap near the bone resulting from lingual fracture malalignment. MTA was used to fill this site. MTA enhances sealing, offers biocompatibility, and stimulates the formation of cementum and hard tissue, thereby contributing to long-term healing and stability.

As MTA lacks adhesion, a resin-modified MTA material incorporating tri-n-butylborane as a polymerization initiator was applied in this study. This material was composed of white Portland cement and hydrophilic methacrylic acid monomers that did not require water or a cotton-based setting and offered strong sealing and biocompatibility [7]. Furthermore, this material possesses marginal sealing properties owing to the crystals derived from Portland cement. The resin penetrates the tooth structure and polymerizes, which is believed to compensate for the limitations of using MTA alone. As MMA cements have been utilized in root fracture management [8], an MMA-based adhesive resin cement was applied on top in the present study, further reinforcing the fracture site. Notably, foundation construction via multiple fiber posts possibly unifies the fractured root and increases mechanical stability [9]. In this report, minimal periodontal surgery was performed to further remove the extruded MTA, encouraging soft tissue integration.

This report supports the use of multimaterial adhesion strategies and periodontal management in complex cervical root fractures, providing a basis for future studies with standardized protocols. Nevertheless, this report describes only a single case; therefore, the findings may not be generalizable to other patients. Furthermore, careful case selection remains essential. Fracture morphology, periodontal status, and patient compliance must also be assessed.

Conclusion

Although horizontal root fractures near the cervical region have a poor prognosis, a combination approach utilizing MTA may result in favorable outcomes. This treatment method, using appropriate materials and surgical procedures, was beneficial for preserving teeth with horizontal root fractures near the alveolar bone, which are considered difficult to treat.

Clinical takeaway

Even in cases of root fractures close to the alveolar bone margin, which generally have a poor prognosis, appropriate material selection and meticulous treatment procedures can enable successful tooth preservation.

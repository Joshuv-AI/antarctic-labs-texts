Big data technology application and carbon emission efficiency of manufacturing enterprises
Authors: Xianzhen Sun, Xuejie Bai, Yung-Ho Chiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the application and popularization of artificial intelligence, the Internet of Things and other technologies, the role of big data technology in the production and operation processes of enterprises is becoming more and more important. Exploring the mechanism of the influence of big data technology application (BDTA) on carbon emission efficiency (CEE) of manufacturing enterprises can provide a new pathway to promote the realization of the dual-carbon goal. We theoretically analyze the role channels of BDTA in influencing CEE of manufacturing enterprises from the perspectives of green innovation and internal control quality, and empirically test it with the listed companies in China’s manufacturing industry from 2010 to 2023 as the research subject. The study reveals that BDTA can improve CEE of manufacturing enterprises, and the regression results are still robust after a series of robustness tests and endogeneity tests. BDTA improves CEE of manufacturing enterprises by fostering green innovation and enhancing internal control quality. The results of the heterogeneity analysis indicate that BDTA has a more significant effect on improving CEE in non-state-owned enterprises, high-tech enterprises, and enterprises with low market concentration.

Introduction

The massive emission of greenhouse gases, including CO2, has led to global warming and the frequent occurrence of extreme weather events, threatening the natural ecology and the economic and social development of mankind. China is one of the top carbon dioxide emitters, and the Chinese government has actively fulfilled its environmental responsibilities by proposing the strategic goal of achieving carbon peaking and carbon neutrality. China is a major manufacturing country, with its manufacturing value-added accounting for about 29% of global value-added in 2023. However, energy-intensive and resource-intensive industries such as steel, cement, non-ferrous metals, chemicals and building materials still occupy a “double high” position in terms of manufacturing value-added and CO2 emissions. Meanwhile, China’s industrial and supply chains are deeply embedded in the global division of labor, meaning that any drastic adjustments could trigger chain reactions in employment, investment, and trade. Therefore, exploring a “China solution” that can both maintain manufacturing competitiveness and achieve deep decarbonization is not only crucial for ensuring the timely realization of China’s dual-carbon goals, but also provides a paradigm that is referable, replicable, and scalable for developing economies worldwide.

With the deep application of a new generation of digital technologies such as artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things, the degree of digital transformation of traditional enterprises in China has been deepening, and data has shown exponential growth, and big data technology, with its powerful data collection, processing and analysis capabilities, is gradually becoming a key engine to drive the green transformation of enterprises1. Specifically, Big data technology is revolutionizing how production factors interact which not only deepens the integration of data elements with labor and capital, but also optimizes resource allocation in real-time through algorithms. The multiplier effect generated by this factor restructuring has driven enterprises to transition from “experience-driven” approaches to “data-driven” strategies. Furthermore, it has established green processes and circular manufacturing as computable, verifiable production norms, propelling the low-carbon transformation and sustainable development of the manufacturing industry2. In this context, putting BDTA and CEE of manufacturing enterprises under the same framework for research can provide a new path for the government to formulate policies to promote carbon emission reduction in the manufacturing industry.

CEE refers to the economic output generated under specific carbon emission levels, with its core focus on measuring the economic benefits per unit of carbon emissions3. In contrast, carbon emission intensity primarily measures carbon emissions per unit of output value4, serving as a key indicator of carbon intensity in economic entities or production processes. CEE demonstrates greater comprehensive reflection of the synergistic relationship between economic development and carbon emissions, directly demonstrating enterprises’ effective utilization of carbon resources while achieving economic growth. Therefore, this study will prioritize the analysis of CEE. This paper provides a theoretical analysis of the impact and mechanisms through which BDTA affects CEE of manufacturing enterprises. It uses text analysis to measure big data indicators and employs a panel two-way fixed effects model for empirical assessment. The study also explores heterogeneous effects to better leverage big data technology for improving CEE. The main contributions are threefold: (1) Based on the era background of digital economy development, this paper systematically discusses the key influencing factors of carbon emission efficiency of manufacturing enterprises from the perspective of big data technology application, and expands the research in the intersection field of digital transformation and green development of manufacturing industry. (2) This paper constructs a theoretical analytical framework of the influence of BDTA on CEE of manufacturing enterprises from the two dimensions of green innovation and internal control quality. It clarifies the micro-level mechanisms through which BDTA impacts CEE, and enhances the application of technology empowerment theory in environmental governance. (3) This paper constructs a keyword system based on technical characteristics and application scenarios of big data technology. This system offers a novel measurement tool and data support for quantitative research at the micro level.

The rest of this paper is organized as: Sect. 2 is the literature review; Sect. 3 is the research hypothesis; Sect. 4 is the model and data; Sect. 5 is the results and discussions including baseline analysis, robustness analysis, mechanism analysis, and heterogeneity analysis; Sect. 6 is the conclusion.

Literature review

The literatures of the economic effects of BDTA mainly include two aspects of its impact on enterprise performance and innovation development. Regarding enterprise performance, BDTA can optimize business processes5, improve the quality of decision-making by business managers6, improve management efficiency, reduce transaction costs and management costs, and improve the efficiency of business operations[1,21,33,] and competitiveness7. Regarding innovation and development, BDTA is an important factor to promote enterprise innovation8, and BDTA can reshape the enterprise operation system in all aspects, transform the enterprise organization structure to flattening and networking, give rise to new business models and innovation models, and improve the efficiency of enterprise innovation9,10, found that the BDTA can significantly promote enterprise innovation, and the promotion effect is more significant in the sample of state-owned enterprises11. concluded that big data analysis and management capabilities can promote the overall innovation performance of enterprises. In addition, the BDTA can also play a significant role in promoting enterprise green innovation12and financial technology innovation12.

Big data technology is an important component of digital technology, and many literatures that analyzes carbon emission problem of manufacturing enterprises from the perspective of digital technology application provide references for studying the impact of BDTA on CEE. Most of the literature studies concluded that the application of digital technology can reduce the carbon emissions of enterprises13,14, and this conclusion still holds when focusing on manufacturing enterprises15,16, shows that digital transformation significantly reduces carbon emission intensity of manufacturing enterprises, and in this process, labor productivity and capital renewal have played a significant mediating role17. also obtains a similar conclusion. In addition to digital technology application, artificial intelligence technology application18,19is also an important factor affecting carbon emissions. Although the existing literature lacks research on the BDTA affecting the CEE of manufacturing enterprises, there is literature focusing on the impact of big data policies on urban or industrial carbon emissions20. argues that the implementation of big data policies can significantly improve the efficiency of carbon unlocking in cities21. analyzes the impact of big data policies on agricultural carbon emissions, and shows that the implementation of big data policies can promote the upgrading of industrial structure, promote technological progress, and then significantly reduce agricultural carbon emissions.

In summary, existing research has made substantial theoretical progress in two main areas. First, it has developed a relatively comprehensive analytical framework on the economic effects of BDTA. Second, significant advances have been made in exploring the impact mechanisms of digital technology on carbon emissions in manufacturing enterprises. However, no study has yet focused on the micro-level carbon emission effects of BDTA in manufacturing enterprises. This paper addresses this gap by constructing a theoretical model that examines how BDTA influences CEE in manufacturing enterprises. It not only fills the existing research gap regarding the micro-mechanisms of big data application but also offers a new theoretical perspective. More importantly, it provides empirical evidence to support the low-carbon transformation of the manufacturing industry. This work thus contributes to advancing the field toward greater systematization and refinement.

Research hypothesis

Big data technology helps overcome poor factor flow caused by information exchange barriers. It drives the movement of capital, talent, and technology through data elements. This facilitates resource and information exchange between enterprises and across industries, promoting a more effective allocation or reorganization of production factors9. As a result, resource waste is reduced, enterprise performance improves, and CEE in manufacturing enterprises is enhanced. Manufacturing enterprises can leverage big data and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies to build comprehensive IoT sensing systems. These systems enable continuous collection and monitoring of carbon emissions throughout the product lifecycle, from raw material procurement to recycling, supporting low-carbon production. Big data also connects supply and demand, improving information transparency. Enterprises can adjust R&D and production plans in real time based on consumer demand, achieving precise innovation and targeted marketing22. This reduces storage energy consumption and emissions from inventory waste, thereby improving CEE. Furthermore, big data increases the transparency of internal information within manufacturing enterprises23. It lowers information acquisition and supervision costs for investors, making them more willing to invest. This alleviates enterprises’ financial pressure, enabling greater investment in cleaner production equipment and technology development, which further improves CEE.

H1

The BDTA can improve the CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

Big data technology can unlock the potential of data elements and create a data-driven, rapid-iteration, and collaborative green innovation model. This enhances the efficiency and level of green innovation in manufacturing enterprises. By reducing the cost of information acquisition and improving enterprises’ ability to process information24, big data enables enterprises to collect and integrate consumer demand data. This helps predict market trends, clarify the direction of green innovation, reduce R&D failure risks, and increase the willingness to innovate. Enterprises can also build digital twin platforms to simulate green innovation solutions. Predictive analytics can optimize R&D resource allocation, lower innovation costs, shorten innovation cycles, and improve innovation efficiency12. Furthermore, big data facilitates knowledge sharing and technology exchange between manufacturing enterprises and innovation partners such as universities and research institutions. This promotes collaborative green innovation and improves the quality of innovation9. Big data enhances the accuracy and transparency of internal information disclosure. This enables media and government supervision, encouraging enterprises to increase investment in green innovation. Improved green innovation, in turn, significantly boosts CEE12,25. Cleaner production technologies also improve manufacturing processes by reducing energy consumption and pollution emissions. They promote waste resource utilization, forming a closed-loop “design-production-recycling” system. This system enhances CEE across the entire manufacturing chain—from emission reduction at the source to emission control and final treatment.

H2

BDTA promotes green innovation, which in turn improves the CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

Big data leverages real-time data collection, multi-source heterogeneous information integration, and machine learning algorithms to enable data connectivity across production, finance, and sales operations. This breakthrough overcomes the challenges of information silos and delayed decision-making in traditional manufacturing enterprises, driving internal control systems to evolve from conventional post-event spot checks and static compliance models toward dynamic process monitoring, intelligent risk alerts, and automated execution mechanisms. Such transformation significantly enhances communication transparency, improves the foresight of risk assessment, and strengthens regulatory constraints on control activities. It effectively reduces internal information friction and agency costs, thereby enhancing the internal control quality of enterprises26. High-quality internal controls further optimize carbon emission efficiency by deeply integrating carbon governance into strategies and operational workflows of enterprises. A robust internal control framework ensures scientific decision-making regarding carbon assets, energy procurement, production scheduling, and technological upgrades, optimizes resource allocation27,28, corrects energy misallocation issues, and minimizes hidden wastes. Performance evaluations and compensation contracts within internal controls incorporate carbon reduction targets into management assessments, effectively curbing short-sighted investments and opportunistic behaviors. This approach facilitates the transition from passive compliance to proactive lean management of carbon emissions, reduces carbon emission intensity29, and ultimately achieves improvements in CEE.

H3

BDTA enhances internal control quality, which in turn improves CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

Data

Based on the data of Shanghai and Shenzhen A-share manufacturing listed companies in China from 2010 to 2023, this paper empirically examines the impact effect and mechanism of BDTA on CEE of manufacturing enterprises. The following treatments are carried out for the sample companies: (1) the samples with serious missing core data are excluded; (2) the listed companies in ST, *ST, PT, and those that were delisted during the listing period are deleted; and (3) in order to avoid the variable outliers from interfering with the empirical results, the sample companies are treated as follows: (1) All the continuous variables are subjected to 1% shrinkage treatment. After processing, this paper finally retains 2,490 manufacturing enterprises as the research sample, and there are 21,886 observations of manufacturing enterprises participating in the empirical test. The text files of annual reports of listed companies are from Wind database. The original data on carbon dioxide emissions of enterprises and control variables are from CSMAR database.

Dependent variable: CEE (CEE). CEE is measured as the ratio of a manufacturing enterprise’s operating revenue to its carbon dioxide emissions. Due to the severe lack of enterprise-level carbon emission data and the inability to obtain them directly, the carbon dioxide emissions of manufacturing enterprises are indirectly estimated based on their share of industry-level emissions. This share is determined by the proportion of enterprise’s operating costs within the total operating costs of the industry. Independent variable: BDTA (BDTA). Drawing on12, the logarithm of the word frequency of BDTA keywords plus one is used to measure BDTA. The steps about the acquisition of BDTA keywords are as follows: using text analysis to construct a dictionary of BDTA keywords from the dimensions of technical features and application scenarios, and expanding the vocabulary to python’s jieba library, and then counting the keywords’ appearances in the whole text of Management’s Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) number of times to obtain the keyword frequency of BDTA. Mechanism variable: Green innovation (GI). Following31, GI is measured by the logarithm of the number of green patent applications plus one. Internal control quality (ICQ). Following32, ICQ is measured by China listed companies internal control index divided by 100 in the DIB internal control and risk management database, and a higher index value indicates a higher level of internal control quality within the company.

Control variables: Enterprise size, enterprise age, gearing ratio, return on assets, operating income growth rate, equity concentration and board size are selected as control variables to enter the benchmark regression model. Among them, enterprise size (Size) is measured by the logarithm of business revenue. Enterprise age (Age) is measured using the logarithm of the age of establishment of the enterprise. Asset–liability ratio (Alr) is measured using the ratio of total liabilities to total assets. Return on assets (Roa) is measured using the ratio of net profit after tax to total asset balance. Growth in Operating Income (Growth) is measured using the ratio of the increase in operating income of a manufacturing company in the current year to the previous year’s operating income. Shareholding Concentration (Top1) is measured by the percentage of shares held by the largest shareholder. Board size (Board) is measured by the logarithm of the total number of board members.

Robustness analysis

In order to ensure the robustness of the benchmark regression results, robustness tests were conducted by adjusting for fixed effects, adding control variables, replacing core variables, and adjusting the sample size.

Adjustment of fixed effects. To account for unobservable or immeasurable factors at the industry and city levels that vary over time and may affect the CEE of manufacturing enterprises, this study incorporates industry, city, and year fixed effects, as well as enterprise, year, and city–year interacted fixed effects into the regression. The results are presented in Columns (1) and (2) of Table 3. The results all show that the BDTA can significantly promote the CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

Adding control variables. Considering that the model may have the problem of omitted variables, the regression is conducted by adding CEO duality (Dual) as a control variable, and the results are shown in Column (3) of Table 3. The result shows that the BDTA can significantly promote the CEE of manufacturing enterprises, which verifies the reliability of the benchmark regression results.

Replacement of core variables. Considering that the length of MD&A text may also affect the frequency of keyword appearances of BDTA, the ratio of BDTA word frequency to the total number of words in the MD&A text (BDTA_ratio) is used as a proxy variable for the BDTA indicator in the regression. As shown in Column (4) of Table 3, the result shows that the BDTA can significantly promote the CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

Excluding policy effects. Since the establishment of national big data comprehensive pilot zones is accompanied by policy support at the national level and a relatively advanced level of big data development, enterprises located in these regions have a natural advantage in improving CEE through big data applications. To exclude the potential policy influence of such pilot zones, the sample enterprises within national big data comprehensive pilot zones are removed, and the regression is re-estimated. As reported in Column (5) of Table 3, the result shows that the BDTA can significantly promote the CEE of manufacturing enterprises, thereby confirming the robustness of the baseline regression results.

Adjusting the sample period. The outbreak of COVID-19 in early 2020 substantially disrupted the production and operations of Chinese manufacturing enterprises, which in turn greatly affected both the process of BDTA and enterprises’ CEE. To mitigate this influence, the sample period is restricted to the years from 2010 to 2019 for regression analysis and the result reported in Column (6) of Table 3. The result shows that the BDTA can significantly promote the CEE of manufacturing enterprises.

The two-stage least squares (2SLS) method is applied, with results presented in Table 4. The IVpasses both the weak instrument and over-identification tests. The second-stage regression shows that the big data application has a significant positive effect on CEE, which further proves the robustness of the benchmark regression results37.

Heterogeneity analysis

To further investigate the heterogeneous effects of BDTA on the CEE of manufacturing enterprises, the sample enterprises are classified according to ownership type, industry characteristics, and market competition environment, and empirical analyses are conducted separately.

Ownership heterogeneity

Based on ownership type, the sample enterprises are divided into state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and non-state-owned enterprises (non-SOEs), and regressions are conducted separately. The regression results are shown in columns (1) and (2) of Table 5. BDTA significantly enhances the CEE of non-SOEs, but has no significant effect on the CEE of SOEs. The reason lies in the fact that, compared with SOEs, non-SOEs are less likely to receive policy support and face disadvantages in capital, talent, and technological reserves. To secure government support and strengthen competitiveness, non-SOEs are more proactive in adopting big data technologies to transform production processes, marketing models, and organizational management practices. Such transformations improve innovation and production efficiency, reduce carbon emissions, and thus substantially promote enterprises’ CEE.

Industry heterogeneity

Based on whether enterprises belong to high-tech industries, the sample is divided into high-tech and non-high-tech enterprises, and regressions are conducted separately. The regression results are shown in columns (3) and (4) of Table 6. The results indicate that BDTA significantly improves the CEE of high-tech enterprises, while its effect on non-high-tech enterprises is not significant. The reason is that high-tech enterprises already possess advanced technological capabilities and strong innovation capacity, which allow big data technologies to be effectively integrated with their existing information systems and intelligent equipment. Such integration further promotes the realization of intelligent manufacturing and green production processes, thereby driving the continuous improvement of CEE.

Market competition heterogeneity

Based on market concentration, which is measured by Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI), the sample enterprises are divided into high market concentration and low market concentration groups, and regressions are conducted separately. The regression results are shown in columns (5) and (6) of Table 6. The results indicate that the estimated coefficient of BDTA is significantly positive for both enterprises with high market concentration and enterprises with low market concentration, with the latter having a larger coefficient. This suggests that BDTA significantly improves CEE in both enterprises with high market concentration and enterprises with low market concentration, with the latter having a more pronounced and greater impact. Most enterprises with low market concentration are small and medium-sized manufacturing enterprises with limited digitalization capabilities. Big data applications can enable precise demand forecasting, optimize production scheduling, reduce energy waste, and significantly enhance supply chain coordination and capacity utilization rates, thereby having a greater effect on promoting CEE.

Conclusion

This paper discusses how to play the enabling role of big data to promote the green development of manufacturing enterprises in the context of Chinese-style modernization. Firstly, it theoretically analyzes the role mechanism of BDTA affecting CEE of manufacturing enterprises from the perspectives of green innovation and internal control quality, and then, taking Chinese manufacturing listed companies as the research object, it constructs a panel fixed effect model to empirically analyze the influence effect and internal role mechanism of BDTA on the CEE of manufacturing enterprises. Finally, in order to more deeply explore the diversified characteristics of BDTA affecting CEE of manufacturing enterprises, the heterogeneity analysis is carried out from the perspectives of the nature of ownership of manufacturing enterprises, industry characteristics, and the market competition environment. The following conclusions are obtained:

(1) BDTA can significantly improve CEE of manufacturing enterprises, and the regression results remain robust even after undergoing a series of robustness tests, including adjustments to fixed effects, levels of clustering, the addition of control variables, replacement of core variables, adjustments to the sample scope, endogenous treatment, and endogeneity tests.

(2) BDTA improve CEE of manufacturing enterprises via the green innovation channel and the internal control quality channel. The results of the mechanism analysis indicate that BDTA fosters enterprises’ green innovation and enhances their internal control quality, subsequently contributing to the improvement of manufacturing enterprises’ CEE.

(3) The impact of BDTA on CEE of manufacturing enterprises exhibits significant heterogeneity. The heterogeneous analysis indicates that compared to state-owned enterprises, non-high-technology enterprises and enterprises with low market concentration, BDTA has a more significant effect on improving CEE of non-state-owned enterprises, high-tech enterprises and enterprises with low market concentration.

Based on the conclusions of the above study, in order to play the role of BDTA in promoting CEE of manufacturing enterprises, the following policy recommendations are put forward:

Firstly, the pace of digital infrastructure development should be accelerated to support the adoption of big data technology by manufacturing enterprises. Improving CEE is a key pathway to achieving high-quality enterprise development. As shown in the benchmark analysis, big data applications contribute significantly to enhancing CEE. Therefore, greater emphasis should be placed on the role of big data in enabling green development. Since digital infrastructure serves as a foundational element for implementing big data technologies, local governments should prioritize its expansion. Efforts should focus on developing targeted regional infrastructure plans, particularly in areas with a high concentration of manufacturing enterprises.

Secondly, to support the BDTA in improving CEE, it is essential to foster a favorable external environment by aligning relevant policy tools with efforts to foster green innovation and enhance internal control quality. Big data can improve the CEE of manufacturing enterprises by promoting green innovation and optimizing internal control quality. Therefore, the government should formulate targeted support policies for green innovation and internal control system development of manufacturing enterprises. Enterprises that proactively engage in green innovation initiatives or enhance their internal control frameworks should be eligible for financial subsidies or tax incentives. Additionally, regulatory frameworks should be established to encourage enterprises to disclose environmental information and internal control data, thereby enhancing transparency and accountability mechanisms.

Finally, it is essential to formulate differentiated big data development policies to more effectively enhance the CEE of manufacturing enterprises. The impact of BDTA on CEE is not uniform. As shown by the heterogeneity analysis, BDTA has a more significant effect on improving CEE of non-state-owned enterprises, high-tech enterprises and enterprises with low market concentration. Therefore, local governments should tailor big data development policies based on regional characteristics and enterprise structures. Such differentiated policy design can improve the precision and effectiveness of big data in supporting CEE improvements.

This paper systematically analyzes the impact of BDTA on CEE of manufacturing enterprise and derives findings of significant reference value. However, the study still has certain limitations that call for further research38: Expansion of research samples. The current study uses listed manufacturing enterprises in China as the sample. Yet, in the context of the rapidly developing digital economy, an increasing number of small- and medium-sized manufacturing enterprises are also beginning to adopt big data technologies. Therefore, future research could extend the sample to include SMEs in the manufacturing sector to further examine the impact of BDTA on CEE27. Neglect of international factors. Under the backdrop of globalization, the development of China’s manufacturing enterprises is influenced not only by domestic factors but also by international dynamics. This study does not take such international factors into account. Future research could further explore the impact of elements such as U.S. tariff policies on CEE of Chinese manufacturing enterprises.

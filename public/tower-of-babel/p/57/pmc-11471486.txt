Exploration geochemical evidence for the genesis of the Jiaodong gold deposits in eastern China: A case study of the Haiyu gold deposit
Authors: Ruixuan Lan, Liangliang Zhang, Mingchun Song, Zhuang Duan, Shixin Tang, Yong Li, Shengming Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Jiaodong is the largest gold province in China and has great potential for further prospecting. It has been confirmed that the enrichment of Au is always accompanied by pyritization in Jiaodong gold deposits, so the genesis of pyritization in gold deposits and the mechanism of Au enrichment associated with pyritization has always been an issue of discussion to the researchers, the relationship between pyritization and Au enrichment is still unclear. In order to solve this problem, this paper studied the occurrence state and transformation characteristics of S in the ore-bearing geological bodies and discussed the mechanism of Au enrichment. 84 samples were collected from the drill hole ZK3006 of the Haiyu gold deposit, the average sampling interval was 15m, and 7 specific elements/analytical indexes were measured (Total sulfur, Reduced sulfur, Na2O,CaO, Sr, Ba, and Au), Burning-iodine quantity method was used to measure the content of Total sulfur, Combustion neutralization method was used to measure the content of Reduced sulfur Inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission spectrometry was used to measure the content of Na2O and CaO, Wavelength dispersive X-Ray fluorescence spectrometry was used to measure the content of Sr and Ba, and Foamed plastic enrichment-inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry was used to measure the content of Au.

By comparing the contents of oxidized S and reduced S in the unaltered, altered and mineralized ore-bearing geological bodies, the occurrence transformation of S and its influence on pyritization and even Au mineralization were investigated. The results showed that the main occurrence state of S in unaltered monzonitic granite is oxidized, and the proportion of reduced S in total S is only 4–35 %. In the ore-bearing monzonite granite (structural alteration zone and ore bodies), the reduced S is dominant, accounting for 89–95 % of total S, indicating that the occurrence state of S in the ore-bearing geological bodies has transformed from oxidized state to reduced one during the hydrothermal process. The reduced S combined with Fe2+ produced by the hydrothermal process to form pyrite, which contributed to the pyritization. At the same time when oxidized S transformed to reduced S, Au was enriched, which leaded to the symbiosis of Au mineralization and pyritization.

Introduction

S has played a vital role in the origin of life, evolution and cycles of materials in various layers of the earth [[1], [2], [3]]. The cycles of S and its evolution are influenced by both geological events and the natural environment (climate, biology, ocean, etc.) [[4], [5], [6]]. Because S is an essential material component in many ore-forming minerals, such as sphalerite (ZnS), galena (PbS), chalcopyrite (CuFeS2), bornite (Cu5FeS4), chalcocite (Cu2S), and argentite (Ag2S), the cycle and evolution of S have important and direct control of the formation of those ore deposits. In the long geological period and the process of the earth's evolution, mineralization was a geological event with only a low probability [7], because many favorable geological conditions must be fulfilled for mineralization to occur. Some researchers believe it is necessary to discuss the metallogenic background and immediate environment under the major premises of the S cycle and evolution [[8], [9], [10]]. That is a major scientific topic, involving various subjects and research domains [[11], [12], [13]]. Therefore, we only discuss the characteristics and evolution of S in gold deposits here.

Jiaodong is China's largest gold province, its accumulated proven gold exceed 5000 t and are 1/3 of the country's total reserves. It is the third-largest gold district in the world after Witwatersrand in South Africa and Muruntau in Uzbekistan [[14], [15], [16]]. The northwestern area's gold reserves dominate the entire Jiaodong area [[17], [18], [19]]. Especially in recent years, major breakthroughs have been made in prospecting in deep underground and in offshore sea explorations at depths of 1000–2000m [20]. Therefore, researchers carried out many important studies on metallogenic background [[21], [22], [23]], conditions [24] and ore-controlling factors [25], but pyritization and the mechanism of Au enrichment have not been discussed, the genesis of gold deposits still has been controversial, so we carried out this study and tried to answer these questions.

This paper focuses on only the occurrence state and transformation of S in the ore-bearing geological bodies. It also discusses the controlling of the transformation of gold deposits by taking the Haiyu gold deposit in northwest Jiaodong Peninsula as an example.

Geological setting

Regional geology

Jiaodong peninsula is situated in the eastern margin of the North China Craton (Fig. 1a), and is bounded to the west by the crustal-scale Tan-Lu fault [[26], [27], [28]]. The west of Jiaodong Peninsula consists of the Jiaobei Uplift and the Jiaolai Basin (Fig. 1b), while the east belongs to the Weihai Uplift of the Dabie-Sulu orogenic belt [[29], [30], [31]], which is a collision orogenic belt between the North China Craton and Yangtze Craton [[32], [33], [34]]. The Jiaobei uplift is mainly composed of Neoarchean granite-greenstone belt, Paleoproterozoic sedimentary stratum, Neoproterozoic sedimentary stratum and Mesozoic granite [[35], [36], [37], [38]]. The Weihai uplift is a high pressure-ultra-high pressure metamorphic belt, mainly composed of Neoproterozoic granitic gneiss and Mesozoic granite [[39], [40], [41], [42]]. Lots of NE-NNE faults developed in the Jiaodong Peninsula, which are favorable structures for gold precipitation [[43], [44], [45]]. Geology of the Haiyu gold deposit

The Haiyu gold deposit is at the north of the Sanshandao-Cangshang fault (SCF) zone (Fig. 2a), a large gold concentration area in northwest Jiaodong, and is one of the major finds in gold exploration in northwest Jiaodong in recent years, with proven gold reserves of 470 t [47]. Drill hole data show that the primary lithology of the bedrock in the area is metamorphic gabbro, monzonitic granite, granite and granodiorite. The ore body is in the beresitization zone of the footwall of the San-cang fault zone [48]. The alteration is intense and obvious, and the lithology is mainly granite (Fig. 2b). The hanging wall of alteration zone is monzonitic granite, and potassic alteration developed in various degrees within the monzonitic granite, accompanied with extensive ductile deformation [49]. The scale of sericitization is the largest, and is strictly controlled by the secondary faults of the SCF [50]. Various generations of pyrite are of various crystal types [51,52]. Carbonate alteration formed in the late ore stage, mainly with vein calcite, and a small amount of ankerite and siderite, and was mainly superimposed on the sericitization (beresitization) zone, at a small scale [53,54]. The Haiyu gold deposit are dominated by beresitization, the ore is mostly in the form of disseminated, fine-vein, mesh-vein and other mineralized forms, as well as brecciform [57]. The main ore minerals are pyrite, electrum, native gold, kustelite, chalcopyrite, galena, sphalerite, and pyrrhotite, etc. The main gangue minerals are sericite and quartz, followed by carbonate minerals comprising calcite, siderite, and dolomite. In the process of hydrothermal alteration and mineralization, silicification and pyritization were generally developed, and there are differences in the typomorphism between early and late alteration minerals [58]. Pyrite is the main gold-bearing mineral, followed by quartz and galena. The gold-forming minerals are mainly kustelite, followed by native gold and electrum, they are hosted mainly in all kinds of ore in the form of independent minerals.

Methodology

Sample collection

The samples were taken from ZK3006 drill hole of the No.30 exploration line of the Haiyu gold deposit. The No. 30 profile is in the central part of the Haiyu gold deposit, cross-cutting the SCF (F1). As a typical exploration profile, it effectively controlled the 4 orebodies. The ZK3006 drill hole passed through the largest ore bodies. From the shallow to the deep, the ZK3006 drill hole the passed through Quaternary sediments, Metamorphic gabbro, Diabase dike, Monzonitic granite, Phyllic alteration zone, Beresitization zone and Gold ore bodies (Fig. 3), so the corresponding lithological units are Metamorphic gabbro, Monzonitic granite, Phyllic granite, Beresitized granite and Beresitized cataclasite.Fig. 3Profile map of the ZK3006 drill hole in the Haiyu gold deposit (modified from Ref. The samples were collected as stratified continuous blocks. Within each predetermined sampling interval, 8–10 (more could be collected) main lithology rock subsamples of approximately 20g–30g were continuously collected (when more than 10 subsamples are collected, the weight of the subsamples was appropriately low). The subsamples were then combined into a single sample, weighing 200 ± 10 g. The average interval was 15m. In accordance with previous practical experience, the sampling interval generally ranged from 5 to 20 m. In some thinner target geological body, the sampling interval was not less than 1/2 of the false thickness of the target geological body. That was to ensure at least two sampling points on each target geological body, and that could be diluted correctly in nonmineralized areas such as surrounding rock. In alteration, mineralization and other areas, the sampling interval should be properly encrypted. The total footage of ZK3006 is 1510m, samples were collected from 290m to 1510m maximum footage. Sample processing and analysis

Drill core samples processed through three steps of crushing. First, the samples were coarsely crushed to < 3–5 mm with a jaw crusher whose jaw plate material is high-aluminum porcelain, then ground to <0.9 mm (20 mesh) with a disc crusher, and then ground to < 0.074 mm (200 mesh) with an agate ball mill. These procedures were strictly controlled in order to avoid sample contamination and pollution.

The samples were chemically analyzed at the Experimental Test Center of China's Liaoning Institute of Geology and Mineral Resources. Seven specific elements/analytical indexes were analyzed: Total sulfur, Reduced sulfur, Na2O, CaO, Sr, Ba, and Au. The weight method was used for analysis. Total sulfur (TS) is the sum of the S content in the various states present, which includes 5 valence states (S2−, S1−, S0, S4+, and S6+) that can occur stably in the natural environment. Reduced sulfur (RS) refers to S in the reduced states, including S1− and S2−. TS and RS can be separated by controlling the sample calcination temperature. The S state released from the samples is TS when calcination temperature is set at 1300 °C, the S state released from the samples is RS when calcination temperature is set at 850 °C. The difference between TS and RS content is regarded as Oxidized sulfur (OS).

TS and RS analysis

TS analysis: the flux was added into the sample, air was injected, calcined at 1300 °C, and the generated sulfur dioxide was absorbed by water to form sulfite. The TS content of the sample was determined by using starch as an indicator and iodine standard titration solution, the detection limit was 50 ppm.

RS analysis: the sample was calcined in the airflow at 850 °C, so that the S in the sulfide was converted into sulfur dioxide to escape, and the hydrogen peroxide solution was absorbed and oxidized into sulfuric acid. Titration was done with a standard titration solution of sodium hydroxide using methyl red–methylene blue as a mix indicator, determine the RS content, and the detection limit was 50 ppm.

Au analysis

Foamed plastic enrichment-inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry (FPE-ICP-MS) was used to determine the content of Au. 10g sample was decomposed by aqua regia (75 ml HCl mixed with 25 ml HNO3), enriched gold by oscillating adsorption with foam in 15 % aqua regia, and thermally liberated from thiourea solution (ρ = 10 g/L, 5 ml). After evaporating, atomizing and ionizing, the solution was separated by an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer referring to the mass charge ratio, and tested by a detector, then the calibration curve method was used for quantitative analysis of Au content, the detection limit of Au was 0.1 ppb.

Na2O and CaO analysis

Inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES) was used to determine the content of Na2O and CaO. 0.1g sample was decomposed by nitric acid, hydrofluoric acid and perchloric acid, then dissolved perchloric acid, dissolved with hydrochloric acid, and moved to 10 mL plastic colorimetric tube for determining the volume. An inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometer was used to measure the characteristic spectral intensity of the component to be measured in the sample solution at the specified wavelength, and the influence of the matrix was corrected to calculate the amount of the component to be measured in the sample, the detection limit was 0.05 %.

Sr and Ba analysis

Wavelength dispersive X-ray fluorescence spectrometry (WD-XRF) was used to determine the contents of Sr and Ba. Using a 40-mm die set, a 4.00 g of sample was pressed under 4.0 × 104 kg of pressure into pellet, which was used for WD-XRF determination. The detection limit of Sr and Ba was 5 ppm.

All the analytical methods of TS, RS, Na2O, CaO, Sr, Ba, and Au were carried out in accordance with Standard for Analysis methods for regional geochemical sample (DZ/T 0279–2016) [59].

According to the element concentration, the geochemical anomaly was apparent and directly related to fluid migration during the mineralization process. Discussion

According to the results of sample chemical analysis and mineral identification, the following four parts were discussed, in turn, the occurrence state and transformation characteristics of S in ore-bearing geological bodies, the genetic simple analysis of pyrite, the factors controlling the transformation of S, and the relationship between S transformation and Au mineralization.

The occurrence state and transformation characteristics of S in ore-bearing geological bodies

Through the macroscopical observation of the drill core, there is no obvious alteration of monzonitic granite in the hanging wall of tectonic alteration zone. The results of chemical analysis showed that the content of TS is 2802–3196 ppm, and the mean value is 2199 ppm. The content of RS is 67–133 ppm, and the mean value is 100 ppm, the proportion of RS in TS is 4.74 %, indicating that OS is dominant in TS, and S occurs mainly in OS. The mineral identification results confirm (Fig. 5, Fig. 6) that there is a certain amount of apatite and barite in the monzonitic granite that has not been significantly altered, suggesting that apatite and barite are the main carrier minerals of OS (Fig. 6a and b). Previous studies have shown that in addition to forming independent minerals, S can also occur in phosphate minerals in an isomorphism, resulting in the substitution of SO4 and PO4 [60]. At the same time, apatite (Ca5(PO4)3) is also the most important S-rich mineral among the auxiliary minerals of acidic intrusive rocks, and S occurs in large quantities in the form of sulfate molecules in apatite [61]. According to the lithology, alteration and mineralization characteristics, the drill hole at the footage of 290m–1510m was divided into 9 layers, and the occurrence state and transformation characteristics of S were discussed, respectively. The first layer (290m–390m) is metamorphic gabbro, with low TS and RS, with a mean of 687 ppm and 266 ppm, respectively. RS/TS is relatively small, with a mean of 27.07 %. The second layer (390m–865m) is monzonitic granite without obvious alteration, and TS and RS are still low, mean values are 2006 ppm and 305 ppm, respectively. RS/TS is smaller than that of the first layer, accounting for a mean of 15.22 %. The third layer (865m–1142m) is phyllic granite, and the TS and RS continue to remain low, mean values are 1517 ppm and 1142 ppm, respectively. RS/TS increases significantly, from 23.38 % at ca.865m to 69.31 % at ca.1142m. The fourth layer (1142m–1200m) is beresitized granite, TS and RS increase significantly, mean values are 8368 ppm and 6054 ppm, respectively. RS/TS continues to increase and begins to decrease slightly after reaching a peak value (82.72 %) near 1180m. The fifth layer (1200m–1293m) is beresitized cataclasite, TS and RS fluctuates with a mean value of 17457 ppm and 11085 ppm, respectively, while RS/TS continues to decrease and reaches a vally value (52.35 %) around 1289m. The sixth layer (1293m–1351m) is beresitized cataclasite, TS, RS and RS/TS increase suddenly at about 1293m, TS and RS reach the maximum value (95413 ppm and 79087 ppm, respectively) of the whole drill hole. After that, TS and RS began to decrease significantly and reached a vally value (15595 ppm and 14400 ppm, respectively) again at ca.1351m, and RS/TS also decreased slightly. The seventh layer (1351m–1449m) is beresitized cataclasite, TS and RS both increased at first, reached a peak value (70179 ppm and 65795 ppm, respectively) again at about 1380m, and then decreased to a low value (1084 ppm and 650 ppm, respectively), and RS/TS also decreased. The eighth layer (1449m–1487m) is phyllic granite, TS and RS remain low, mean values are 593 ppm and 200 ppm, respectively, and RS/TS continues to decrease to a low value (20 %). The ninth layer (1487m–1510m) is granodiorite, TS and RS continue to remain low values, mean values are 360 ppm and 80 ppm, respectively, and RS/TS remain low (22 %).

Through the above discussion (Fig. 7), the following conclusions could be drawn: RS/TS in the monzonitic granite without alteration is low, where S is dominated by the form of OS, while RS is dominated in the alteration and mineralization zones. The genesis of pyrite

There are many genetic types of pyrite in nature, which can be mainly divided into four categories: magmatic origin, hydrothermal origin, biological origin and sedimentary origin [63,64], but no matter what type of origin, it must have basic material conditions: Fe2+ and S2− (or S−). The results of this study showed that S in the monzonitic granite in the Haiyu gold deposit transformed from OS to RS in hydrothermal process, which provided S2− (or S−) material basis for the formation of pyrite. The monzonitic granite contains biotite [K(Mg,Fe)3(AlSi3O10)(OH)2] and other iron-bearing minerals, and the decomposition of these minerals precipitates Fe2+, thus providing the material basis for the formation of pyrite. In the process of biotite decomposition to chlorite, Fe2+, K+ and Mg2+ are precipitated [[65], [66], [67]]:2K(Mg,Fe)3AlSi3O10(OH)2 (Biotite) + 4H+ → Al(Mg,Fe)5AlSi3O10(OH)8 (Chlorite)+ (Mg,Fe)2+ + 2K+ + SiO2 (Quartz)S2− and Fe2+ combine to form FeS2, i.e. Fe2+ + 2S2− → FeS2, resulting in the formation of pyrite.

The factors controlling the transformation of S

The transformation of S in monzonitic granite occurs under hydrothermal process, so the relationship between the transformation of S and the intensity of hydrothermal effect is the key to interpret the pyritization and Au mineralization. This has been confirmed by previous studies [68,69], in the hydrothermal system, Na2O, CaO, Ba, Sr and other components (elements) in the geological bodies are depleted, and the more intensive the hydrothermal alteration, the stronger the depletion of these components (elements). Based on this, the depletion characteristics of Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr are used to investigate the internal relationship between the transformation of S and the range and intensity of hydrothermal process.

The ore-bearing geological body of the Haiyu gold deposit is monzonitic granite. According to Yan and Chi (2005) [70], the average chemical composition of monzonitic granite is as follows: Na2O is 3.55 %, CaO is 1.34 %, Ba is 680 ppm and Sr is 220 ppm, which are taken as background values. As shown in Fig. 8, The first layer (290m–390m) is metamorphic gabbro, and Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr are very high, mean values are 3.92 %, 2.27 %, 1190 ppm and 541 ppm, respectively, and Ba and Sr are much higher than the background values. The second layer (390m–865m) is monzonitic granite without obvious alteration, and Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr are still very high, mean values are 3.95 %, 3.18 %, 2505 ppm and 563 ppm, respectively. The third layer (865m–1142m) is phyllic granite, and Na2O begins to be significantly depleted with a mean value of 1.99 %, while CaO, Ba and Sr are above the background values, mean values are 1.42 %, 1421 ppm and 488 ppm, respectively. The fourth layer (1142m–1200m) is beresitized granite, Na2O is further depleted, CaO begins to be significantly depleted, and Ba and Sr are still above the background values, mean values are 1.61 %, 1.14 %, 1424 ppm and 384 ppm, respectively. The fifth layer (1200m–1293m) is beresitized cataclasite, Na2O and CaO are almost depleted, and Ba and Sr begin to decrease to a value near the background, mean values are 0.27 %, 0.49 %, 661 ppm and 204 ppm. The sixth layer (1293m–1351m) is beresitized cataclasite, Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr are very low, showing the characteristics of significant depletion to zero, mean values are 0.07 %, 0.07 %, 121 ppm and 40 ppm, respectively. The seventh layer (1351m–1449m) is beresitized cataclasite, Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr begin to rise, but only Na2O does not reach the background value, and mean values are 0.39 %, 1.34 %, 726 ppm and 234 ppm, respectively. The eighth layer (1449m–1487m) is phyllic granite, and CaO, Ba and Sr reache or exceed the background value, while Na2O continues to increase, but still does not reach the background value, mean values are 2.10 %, 1.91 %, 1668 ppm and 581 ppm, respectively. The ninth layer (1487m–1510m) is granodiorite, Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr exceed the corresponding background values, mean values are 4.27 %, 1.88 %, 1608 ppm and 749 ppm.Fig. 8The Haiyu gold deposit ZK3006 drill hole histogram showing the content of Na2O, CaO, Sr and Ba. It can be seen that the higher the depletion degree of Na2O, CaO, Ba and Sr, the higher the proportion of RS in TS, indicating that the state transformation of S occurs under the hydrothermal system, and the more intensive the hydrothermal process, the more thorough the trend of S transforming from OS to RS. Therefore, the range and intensity of hydrothermal process are the fundamental factors controlling the state transformation of S.

The relationship between S transformation and Au mineralization

The state transformation of S is shown by the change of RS/TS, while the intensity of Au mineralization is shown by the content of Au. Therefore, the relationship between the change of RS/TS and the content of Au in different layers is mainly discussed here.

The first layer (290m–390m) is metamorphic gabbro, with small RS/TS (27.07 % on mean) and low Au content (mean value is 1.86 ppb). The second layer (390m–865m) is monzonitic granite with no obvious alteration, RS/TS is still very small, mean value is 15.22 %, and Au is still very low, mean value is 1.14 ppb. The third layer (865m–1142m) is phyllic granite, and RS/TS increases significantly, from 23.38 % at ca.865m to 69.31 % at ca.1142m, while Au increases slightly, but is still very low, mean value is 66.59 ppb. The fourth layer (1142m–1200m) is beresitized granite, RS/TS increases further, reaches a peak (82.72 %) at about 1180m, and then begins to decrease slightly, the Au remains low, mean value is 3.51 ppb. The fifth layer (1200m–1293m) is beresitized cataclasite, RS/TS continues to decrease and reaches a valley (52.35 %) near 1289m, and Au decreases after reaching a high value (2587 ppb) near 1235m, mean value is 398 ppb. The sixth layer (1293m–1351m) is beresitized cataclasite, Au and RS/TS suddenly rise at ca.1293m (81.20 % and 7068 ppb, respectively), then Au peaks (10107 ppb) at about 1321m, whereafter begins to decrease. RS/TS and Au in this layer is relatively high on the whole hole, and mean values are 87.91 % and 5452 ppb, respectively. The seventh layer (1351m–1449m) is beresitized cataclasite, RS/TS increased slightly first and then began to decrease at about 1407m, Au reached a valley value (1146 ppb) at about 1351m and then abruptly increased again. It maintains a very high value (13115 ppm–28323 ppm) until about 1407m, and this layer shows the highest Au content in the whole drill hole, and mean value is 12010 ppb. The eighth layer (1449m–1487m) is phyllic granite, and RS/TS further decreases to a low value (20 %), and Au suddenly drops from an extremely high value to an extremely low value (66.50 ppb) at about 1449m, and has always shown an extremely low value in this layer, mean value is 27.04 ppb. The ninth layer (1487m–1510m) is granodiorite, RS/TS remains low (22 %), and Au also remains low, mean value is 23.90 ppb.

The process of RS/TS increasing represents the transformation of S from OS to RS. Therefore, it is believed that the transformation of S from OS to RS is accompanied by the increase of Au content. These results indicate that the state transformation of S not only directly contributes to the formation of pyrite, but also promotes Au mineralization.

Conclusions

(1)In the unaltered monzonitic granite, S occurs mainly in the form of OS. The ore-bearing geological body of the Haiyu gold deposit is monzonitic granite, in the unaltered monzonitic granite, S occurs mainly in the form of OS, RS/TS is 4 %–35 %, and apatite and barite might be the primary carrier mineral of OS (S6+).(2)OS transformed into RS in the monzonitic granite, which facilitated the formation of pyrite. During the hydrothermal process, OS transformed into RS in the monzonitic granite, the direct evidence is that the content of RS increases with the increasing of the negative anomalies of elements.(3)The transformation of OS to RS was accompanied with the enrichment of gold. From unaltered monzonitic granite to alteration zone and ore body, RS/TS increased, which leaded to the pyritization. While the gold content also increased greatly, indicating that the state of S transformed from oxidized state to reduced state, which revealed that the transformation of S was the fundamental factor leading to the pyritization associated with the enrichment of gold.

Comprehensive analysis of basement membrane and immune checkpoint related lncRNA and its prognostic value in hepatocellular carcinoma via machine learning
Authors: Ze Jin, Yajun Meng, Mengmeng Wang, Di Chen, Mengpei Zhu, Yumei Huang, Lina Xiong, Shang Xia, Zhifan Xiong
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), which is characterized by its high malignancy, generally exhibits poor response to immunotherapy. As part of the tumor microenvironment, basement membranes (BMs) are involved in tumor development and immune activities. Presently, there is no integrated analysis linking the basement membrane with immune checkpoints, especially from the perspective of lncRNA.MethodsBased on transcriptome data from The Cancer Genome Atlas, BMs-related and immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs were identified. By applying univariable Cox regression and Machine learning (LASSO and SVM-RFE algorithm), a 10-lncRNA prognosis signature was constructed. The prognostic significance of this signature was assessed by survival analysis. GSEA, ssGSEA, and drug sensitivity analysis were conducted to investigate potential functional pathways, immune status, and clinical implications of guiding individual treatments in HCC. Finally, the promoting migration effect of LINC01224 was validated via in vitro experiments.ResultsThe multiple Cox regression, receiver operating characteristic curves, and stratified survival analysis of clinical subgroups exhibited the robust prognostic ability of the lncRNA signature. Results of the GSEA and drug sensitivity analysis revealed significant differences in potential functional pathways and response to drugs between the two risk groups. In addition, the risk level of HCC patients was distinctly correlated with immune cell infiltration status. More importantly, LINC01224 was independently associated with the OS of HCC patients (P < 0.05), suppressing the expression of LINC01224 inhibited the migration of HCC cells.ConclusionThis study developed a reliable signature for the prognosis of HCC based on BM and immune checkpoint related lncRNA, revealing that LINC01224 might be a prognostic biomarker for HCC associated with the progression of HCC.Keywords: Basement membranes, Immune checkpoint, lncRNA, HCC, Prognosis

Introduction

Liver cancer is one of the most lethal malignancies, being the third leading cause of cancer-associated death globally and contributing to 830,000 deaths in 2020 [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most dominant histologic subtype of liver cancer [2]. Although substantial advancement in treatments has been achieved, including surgical resection, radiofrequency ablation, and targeted and immune therapies, the prognosis of patients with HCC remains gloomy. Due to the nonspecific clinical symptoms of HCC, most patients already have an advanced stage of the disease when they are diagnosed and treatments are frequently not curative, with an overall 5-year survival rate of 20% [3]. Neither the TNM staging system nor traditional biomarkers, such as alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), are sufficiently sensitive and precise in diagnosing and predicting the clinical prognosis of HCC. Given the high-level heterogeneity and aggressive biological nature of HCC, the prediction of clinical outcomes is being challenged. Therefore, exploring novel prognostic models for the personalized evaluation of cancer risk is urgently needed.

Basement membranes (BMs) are dense and delicate sheets, a type of extracellular matrix (ECM) that are widely distributed in the tissues of organisms; the ECM is the acellular part of the tumor microenvironment (TME) [4]. BMs are composed of two major protein networks building core scaffolding structures, namely, laminins and type IV collagen; they bind to nidogens and proteoglycans, which closely contact with cells [5]. These essential components and binding interactions are highly involved in the signaling process and affect tissue homeostasis [6,7]. Notably, cell migration requires overcoming the barriers of BMs; such a step is crucial for the occurrence of cancer metastasis (through intravasation and extravasation) [[8], [9], [10]]. BMs act as extracellular microenvironment sensors that pass signals for various types of cells and regulate cell biological behavior. The BM components of blood vessels have been reported to participate in tumor angiogenesis [11]. Key genes that code BM proteins and cell surface interactors (CSIs) are presumed to be critically associated with cancer progression [12]. Meanwhile, the stability of BMs is related to tumor immune function. Collagen deposition is correlated with T cell migration and results in poor response to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [13]. Laminins interact with the immune system, affecting the migration of different immune cells and enduing tolerance to immunity [14]. BMs disrupted by matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP 2) recruit tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), which promote tumor metastasis and immunosuppression [15]. Collectively, the aberrant structures and functions of BMs exert disadvantageous effects on immune response, eventually contributing to tumor progression.

The development of high-throughput technologies reveals a type of unique transcriptional landscape comprised of long noncoding RNA (lncRNA); lncRNA plays a prominent role in physiological function by regulating gene expression. A large amount of lncRNA is dysregulated in different cancer types to suppress or promote tumorigenesis and tumor development. LncRNA exerts an influence by binding DNA, interacting with RNA and proteins, and sponging microRNA [16]. An increasing number of evidence has suggested that lncRNA plays a crucial role in tumor progression and is involved in malignant phenotypes of HCC, including proliferation, invasion, and migration [[17], [18], [19], [20], [21]]. The previous literature has focused on protein-coding genes related to BMs [22]. Naba, Alexandra et al. identified seven proteins as the ECM characteristics of the metastasis matrisome of colon cancer [23]. Netrin-4 (Net4) regulates BM stiffness, and the low expression of Net4 promotes tumor metastasis [5]. Compared to protein-coding genes, lncRNA has higher tissue and organ specificity, and its aberrant expression has potential cancer diagnostic and prognostic value, making it a promising biomarker for HCC [24]. However, the role of BM-related lncRNAs in HCC remains elusive.

In the era of enormous sequencing data derived from technological advancements, machine learning algorithms have been broadly implemented for various analytical tasks arising in bioinformatics, including the development of lncRNA prognostic signatures. Bao et al. applied machine learning methods to identify a lncRNA prognostic signature for pan-cancer [25]. A machine learning-based model for recognizing prognostic lncRNAs related to tumor-infiltrating B lymphocytes in bladder cancer was proposed by Zhou et al. [26]. Liang et al. used machine learning approaches to identify lncRNAs for predicting distant metastasis in locoregionally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma [27]. Zhang et al. integrated machine learning algorithms to screen out tumor-infiltrating immune cell-associated lncRNAs and construct the prognostic signature for patients with low-grade glioma, which was shown to outperform other reported signatures [28]. As mentioned above, many studies have successfully developed lncRNA-based prognostic models using machine learning algorithms. Therefore, this study drew on machine learning algorithms for data analysis.

Given the complexity of BM structures and the importance of BMs in immunotherapy response, our study aimed to assess the prognostic value of lncRNAs associated with BM and immune checkpoints in HCC and build a novel lncRNA prognostic signature for HCC, which could provide precise prediction and risk stratification for HCC patients for guiding personalized treatment. To do that, we identified BMs- and immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs and constructed a prognostic signature of HCC via integrated machine learning. Furthermore, clinical correlation analysis and stratification survival analysis were performed to assess the stability of this prognostic model. A nomogram was built for clinical prediction. Functional enrichment analysis, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity analysis were conducted on the basis of risk classification. Finally, preliminary experiments were also performed to validate the function of LINC01224 in HCC.

Methods

Data preparation and processing

BM-related genes (BRGs) and immune checkpoint-related genes (IRGs) were obtained from the published literature [12,29], shown in Table S1 and Table S2. The transcriptome data and clinical information of 50 normal liver tissues and 374 HCC tissues were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas database through the “TCGAbiolinks” package. The information of the clinicopathological characteristics of HCC patients was shown in Table 1. Differentially expressed lncRNAs (DELs) between tumor tissues and normal tissues were analyzed using the “edgeR” package. The false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01 and |logFC|>4 were set as the selection criteria. Subsequently, the Pearson correlation coefficients between BRGs and DELs were calculated to identify BM-related lncRNAs (BRL) (|R|>0.3 and P < 0.05). The immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs (IRL) were also identified as described above.

Construction of a lncRNA prognostic signature by machine learning algorithms

LncRNAs correlated with BMs and immune checkpoints were screened using the “VennDiagram” package. Three analyses were conducted to identify lncRNA with a prognostic value, including Univariate Cox analysis, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and Support Vector Machine Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithm. SVM-RFE is a machine-learning algorithm that ranks the features based on their weights in the SVM solution and eliminates the feature with the lowest weight [30]. 10 BMs- and immune checkpoint related lncRNAs were selected to construct a prognostic signature of HCC.

By utilizing multiple Cox analysis, 10 lncRNA models and their coefficients were obtained. The risk score was calculated on the basis of the expression of lncRNA and their coefficients. Risk score = ∑i=1nβi×Expi (βi: expression of each lncRNA; Expi: Cox regression coefficients). The optimal cut point was determined using “X-tile” software [31]. Patients with HCC were divided into two groups in accordance with the cutoff value of the risk score. The independent prognostic role of this model was also validated via multiple Cox analysis. Subsequently, Kaplan–Meier analysis was performed to examine the overall survival (OS) among different risk subgroups via “survminer,” “survival” R package. The predictive ability of the risk model for OS was assessed by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves via the “timeROC” package.

Correlation of lncRNA signature with clinical parameters and construction of a nomogram

Wilcox. test was applied to analyze the relevance between the risk model and clinicopathological traits, including age, gender, histological grade, and pathological stage. To examine the reliability of the risk score in different subgroups, survival analysis based on the risk score was performed on patients who were distributed into two subgroups in accordance with clinical factors. A novel nomogram was established to provide the survival probability of patients with HCC through the “rms” and “regplot” R packages. The calibration plots showed the prognostic predictive accuracy of the nomogram.

Functional enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI)

By using the “edgeR” package, differentially expressed BRGs (DE-BRGs) were determined on the basis of |logFC|>1 and FDR<0.01. Functional enrichment analysis of gene ontology (GO) terms and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways were performed via the “clusterProfiler” R package. Then, differentially expressed BRGs were uploaded to the STRING database (http://www.string-db.org/) to investigate the interaction of these protein-coding BRGs. The STRING analysis result was then inputted into Cytoscape software to visualize a PPI network. The hub of the PPI network was selected on the basis of MCODE scores >10.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), immune cell infiltration, and drug sensitivity analysis

To explore the potential mechanism of the risk signature further, GSEA was conducted to reveal the distinct hallmarks between high- and low-risk groups through the “clusterProfiler” package. Several algorithms were utilized to compare the different statuses of immune cell infiltration between high-risk and low-risk groups on the basis of the lncRNA model and exhibited in a heat map. Moreover, single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) was applied to assess the infiltrating levels of immune cells and the activity levels of immune-related pathways via the “gsva” R package. The “pRRophetic” package was used to predict drug sensitivity in high-risk and low-risk groups by calculating the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50).

Screening lncRNA with the independent prognostic value from the risk signature

To select lncRNA with an independent prognostic value for the further experiment, multiple Cox analysis of 10 lncRNA was conducted through the “survival” package. The P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Among them, LINC01224 and AC108752.1 were independent prognostic factors in HCC. The multifactor ROC curves were utilized to compare the prognostic performance of them in HCC prognosis. LINC01224 has better predictive ability, so we selected LINC01224 for further study. Then, survival analysis was performed to evaluate the OS of patients with high and low expression of LINC01224.

Cell line culture and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and transfection

HCC cells (Hep3B, HepG2, and MHCC97L) and normal liver cell lines (LO2) were purchased from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (Wuhan, China). The cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin in an incubator with 5% CO2 and proper humidity at 37 °C.

Total cellular RNA was extracted using an RNA-easy isolation reagent, and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using HiScript II Q RT SuperMix (Vazyme). Then, 2x SYBR Green qPCR MasterMix (Seven Biotech) was used for qRT-PCR. The expression levels of target genes were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, and GAPDH was used as the internal control. The primer sequences are as follows: LINC01224-F: 5′-CAGAGCTTGGGATCGCTTTC-3′; LINC01224-R: 5′-GGAATGTCCTGGCATCTTAGC-3′; GAPDH-F: 5′- TTAGCACCCCTGGCCAAGG-3′; GAPDH-R: 5′- CTTACTCCTTGGAGGCCATG-3′. Hep3B and HepG2 were selected for transfection. By using Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent, small interfering RNA (siRNA) knocked down LINC01224 (si-LINC01224) and the negative control (si-NC) was added to six-well plates. Transfection efficiency was tested after 48 h. The small interfering RNA (siRNA) sequence is as follows: si-LINC01224, GAGACUUUUACCAUUGUAUTT; si-NC, UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT.

Transwell migration and wound healing assays

Transfected cells (Hep3B and HepG2) were cultured in six-well plates. When confluence reached 90%–100%, the cultured cells were scratched using sterile pipette tips. Hep3B and HepG2 were cultured in DMEM without FBS. Observation and imaging were performed at 0 h and 48 h by using an inverted microscope. Transfected cells were seeded in the upper compartment of the Transwell chamber (3422, Corning) with serum-free DMEM. The lower compartment was filled with 20% serum DMEM. After incubating for 48 h, migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and then stained with crystal violet dye (0.1%) for 30 min. Cells that failed to migrate down from the upper layer were wiped away and cells that migrated to the lower layer of the chamber were imaged.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses in the present work were performed using R software (version 4.1.3). The version of the R packages was provided in Table S3. HTSeq-Counts was selected as the format of gene expression quantification. HCC samples with survival times of less than 30 days were deleted. Immune infiltration analysis was performed by applying various algorithms, including MCPCOUNTER, TIMER, QUANTISEQ, CIBERSORT, XCELL, and EPIC.

Results

Construction of a prognostic signature based on BRLs and IRLs

The flowchart of this study is shown in Fig. 1. In this work, 312 DELs in TCGA-liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) were identified between tumor tissues and normal tissues (log 2| FC|>4, FDR<0.01), which was provided in Table S4. Among them, 309 lncRNAs were upregulated while 3 were downregulated. The heat map was shown in Fig. S1. On the basis of BRGs, basement membranes-related lncRNAs (BRLs) were screened by performing Pearson correlation analysis (|R|>0.3 and P < 0.01). Immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs (IRLs) were obtained as described above. Finally, 244 BRLs and 84 IRLs were identified. BRLs and IRLs were intersected to define lncRNAs correlated with BMs and immune checkpoint, 76 corresponding lncRNAs were acquired (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, by utilizing univariate Cox regression, 13 BMs- and immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs with prognostic value were screened out (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2B). LASSO regression was conducted to decrease the complexity of the model and avoid overfitting (Fig. 2C–D). A subset of 13 prognostic features was identified by the SVM-RFE algorithm. The 10 overlapping lncRNAs between these 2 algorithms were ultimately selected to construct the prognostic signature (Fig. 2E). Multiple Cox regression was performed to calculate the coefficients of lncRNAs and the risk scores of HCC patients. Notably, LINC01224 and AC108752.1 have independent prognostic values, as shown in the forest plot (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2F). The details of 10 lncRNAs and their coefficients were provided in Table S5.

Confirmation of the prognostic value of this model

The patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups in accordance with the optimal cut point of the risk score determined by “X-tile” software. The distribution of the risk scores and survival status of HCC patients indicated that more deaths and shorter survival times were observed in the high-risk group (Fig. S2A), providing evidence for that this risk model can effectively distinguish different risk patients. The expression of 10 lncRNAs between the high-risk and low-risk groups was evidently different (Fig. S2B). The Kaplan–Meier curves demonstrated that the high-risk group had worse OS compared with the low-risk group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3A). The prognostic performance of the risk model was evaluated by plotting the ROC curves, revealing areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.726, 0.697, and 0.675 for the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively (Fig. 3B). Univariate and multiple Cox regressions were employed to investigate the independent prognostic value of the risk signature with other clinicopathological characteristics. As shown in Fig. 3C and D, the 10-lncRNA model was significantly associated with the clinical outcome of HCC with independent prognostic value (P < 0.001, HR = 3.0391, 95%: 2.0333−4.5425). A novel nomogram composed of the risk score combined with several important clinical factors was built to calculate the probability of survival after 1, 3, and 5 years (Fig. 3E). The calibration curve indicated that this nomogram can accurately predict the prognosis of HCC patients compared with the ideal model (Fig. 3F).

Clinical correlation analysis and stratified survival analysis

The association of the risk score and clinicopathological factors were evaluated and described in Fig. 4A. The high-risk score was correlated to the advanced pathologic stage (P = 0.008) and poor histologic grade (P < 0.001). The results of the stratified survival analysis verified the stable prognostic ability of the risk model in different clinical subgroups, in accordance with age, gender, histologic grade, and pathologic stage (Fig. 4B–E).

GO, KEGG analysis, and PPI

A total of 93 DE-BRGs were identified between tumor tissue and normal tissue. As depicted in Fig. 5A and B, DE-BRGs were primarily enriched in the collagen−activated signaling pathway and the biological process of cell−substrate adhesion. De-BRGs are involved in the molecular function related to metalloendopeptidase activity besides ECM structural constituent. DE-BRGs not only played a crucial role in ECM receptor interaction, but they were also enriched in the PI3K−Akt signaling pathway, which is involved in tumor progression. DE-BRGs are involved in Focal adhesion, which is a site of adhesion where cells are attached to the extracellular matrix and play an important role in the evolution of tumors to a malignantly aggressive phenotype [32]. The interaction of DE-BRGs was analyzed via a string database, as visualized in Fig. 5C. The green gene in the center of the network represents the hub gene in the interaction of DE-BRGs, including twenty genes such as LAMA4, LAMC1, THBS1, COL4A6, COL9A3, COL9A1, COL9A2, COL2A1, LAMA3, LAMA2, etc.

GSEA, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity analysis based on the risk signature

GSEA was implemented to investigate the latent functional pathway of 10 lncRNA models. The high-risk group was notably enriched in cancer-associated pathways, such as the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) signaling pathway (Fig. 6A), which participates in the proinflammation activity. Interestingly, TNF plays an important role in the immunotherapy of T-cell antitumors through its cytotoxicity [33]. Moreover, platinum drug resistance, apoptosis and mismatch repair pathways were enriched in the high-risk subgroup (Fig. 6B–D). Various pathways related to biosynthesis and metabolism were also enriched in the high-risk subgroup, such as glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis and histidine metabolism (Fig. 6E–F).

GSEA analysis of TCGA-LIHC. (A–F) shows that the TNF signaling pathway, platinum drug resistance, apoptosis, mismatch repair, histidine metabolism, and glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis pathways were enriched in high-risk groups. NES: Normalized Enrichment Score.

Wilcoxon rank sum test in R was applied to analyze whether there is a difference in the mean of the two sets of immune cells. Based on several algorithms, the distinct immune cell infiltration scene between the high-risk and low-risk subgroups is depicted in Fig. S3. Multiple immune cells are differentially expressed in high and low-risk groups (P < 0.05). ssGSEA was performed to evaluate the proportion of immune cells and immune-related functions. The results of ssGSEA demonstrated that the infiltration scores of several immune cells were higher in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group, including macrophages and regulatory T cells (Tregs), which have been widely studied and associated with tumor progression. By contrast, the scores of B cells, natural killer (NK) cells, mast cells, and neutrophils were higher in the low-risk group (Fig. 7A). For the immune-related functions, the high-risk group exhibited a weaker trend of immune functional status, such as Types I and II interferon responses (Fig. 7B). The correlation between various immune cells infiltrated in HCC was presented in the Fig. 7C. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL) were in positive correlation with CD8+ T cells. As for immune function, T-cell inhibition was shown to be positively correlated with checkpoint (Fig. 7D). These results suggest a highly significant correlation between the 10-lncRNA model and immune infiltration status in HCC.

Immune infiltration analysis based on the risk signature. The infiltrating levels of (A) immune cells and the activity levels of (B) immune-related pathways between high- and low-risk subgroups. Correlation analysis of (C) immune cells and (D) immune-related pathways.

The result of the drug sensitivity analysis revealed that most of the drugs exhibited different sensitivity in the two risk subgroups, providing the prediction of response to chemotherapy. The sensitivity of sorafenib, docetaxel, temsirolimus, vinblastine, and rapamycin was lower in the high-risk group (Fig. 8A). By contrast, mitomycin, bleomycin, doxorubicin, tipifarnib, and bexarotene exhibited higher sensitivity in the high-risk group (Fig. 8B). As a multikinase inhibitor, sorafenib was suggested to be the first-line drug of therapy; the treatment of sorafenib was more conducive in low-risk patients than in high-risk patients (P = 1.7e−07). Therefore, in accordance with the prediction, risk classification can contribute to selecting suitable treatment for HCC patients.

LINC01224 is independently associated with the OS of HCC and promotes the migration of HCC cells

Multiple Cox regression was performed to identify lncRNA with independent prognostic value in the risk model. LINC01224 and AC108752.1 have independent prognostic values. The multifactor ROC curve showed that LINC01224 had a better predictive ability than AC108752.1 (Fig. S4). To further explore whether lncRNA in the prognostic signature participated in the progression of HCC, LINC01224 was selected to proceed with the in vitro experiment. The high expression of LINC01224 was correlated to the worse OS of HCC (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9A). Detecting via qRT-PCR, the expression of LINC01224 was significantly upregulated in the HCC cell lines than in the normal liver cells (LO2), particularly in Hep3B (Fig. 9B). Then, Hep3B and HepG2 were transfected with si-LINC01224 and si-NC to compare the cell migration ability. Transfection efficiency was also validated via qRT-PCR (Fig. 9C). The number of migrating cells in Hep3B cells treated with knockdown LINC01224 was significantly lower than those in the negative control group (Fig. 9D). The result of the wound healing assay is shown in Fig. 9E, the migration ability of Hep3B cells transfected with si-LINC01224 is significantly limited compared with the negative control group Hep3B cells transfected with si-NC. The same results were observed in another HCC cell line, HepG2 (Fig. 9F–G). These results indicated that suppressing the expression of LINC01224 inhibited Hep3B and HepG2 migration, suggesting that LINC01224 was involved in the development of HCC and could be a potential prognostic biomarker.

Discussion

BMs are acellular part of the TME that act as a mechanical barrier to prevent tumor cell migration; they play an essential role in cancer invasion, angiogenesis, and metastasis [8,34,35]. BMs have diverse proteins involved in regulating the function of BMs and mediating the interaction with cells, including core protein compositions, proteases, and various growth factors [36]. The dysregulated expression of BM proteins was recognized as an important driver of tumor progression [37]. Naba, Alexandra et al. reported 43 ECM proteins characterized by the highly metastasic phenotype of breast cancer, such as AGRN, CST3, TIMP1, and TINAGL1 [38]. The upregulation of LTBG3 expression in metastatic melanoma compared with benign nevi was identified; this condition exhibited a significant correlation with lymph node metastasis in melanoma [39]. The expression of BRGs was also regulated by noncoding protein RNA. By sponging miR-4766–5p, circPDE3B decreased the inhibition of LAMA1, promoting esophageal carcinoma development [40].

With the progress of systemic therapy in advanced HCC patients, a substantial number of studies have focused on immune checkpoint blockers (ICBs) and illustrated their promising antitumor activity [41]. However, the resistance of ICB therapy frequently occurs and its clinical application still faces many challenges due to the tolerance of the liver environment and liver dysfunction in advanced HCC patients [42]. The structure and constituents of BMs are connected closely with immune activity, affecting cell biological behavior. The upregulated expression of laminin γ2 triggered by TGF-β1 prevents T cell infiltration and induces immune evasion, contributing to the poor response of immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy [43,44]. By activating TGFβ, integrin αvβ6 regulates the expression of SOX4, which drives the resistance of immune cytotoxicity in triple-negative breast cancer [45]. Integrin αvβ8 mediates the enrichment of Tregs in tumors, suppressing antitumor immunotherapy [46]. Previous studies have suggested that the aberrant function of BMs exerts significant effects on immune infiltration status and the effectiveness of immune checkpoint therapy.

Given the emerging importance of lncRNA in gene expression regulation, the current study focused on lncRNA correlated with BMs and immune checkpoint and explored their prognostic implication in HCC. In applications of large amounts of transcriptomics data, a number of data may not be relevant to clinical outcomes. Therefore, the selection of key variables is required for valid regulation of regression. High-dimensional regression methods are crucial in prognostic studies because they enhance model robustness by reducing false-positive predictor genes [47]. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator method (LASSO) is a popular method for minimizing potential overfitting [48]. SVM-RFE is an embedded-based method to extract features from high-dimensional data for tumor marker selection [49]. Of the multiple machine-learning algorithms, LASSO and SVM-RFE are often utilized to select the optimal markers for various diseases [[50], [51], [52]]. As a result, it is widely recognized that applying bioinformatics and machine learning techniques to identify prognostic biomarkers is highly feasible. Therefore, to carry out precise predictions, we combined LASSO and SVM-RFE algorithms to build a lncRNA model by selecting significant factors and constraining the signature.

A prognostic model based on BMs- and immune checkpoint-related lncRNA was constructed via integrated analysis of machine learning. This model performed well in the outcome prediction and risk stratification of HCC patients. Stratified survival analysis of clinical subgroups exhibited the robust and stable prognostic ability of the lncRNA signature. Moreover, by applying multiple Cox regression, the independent prognostic role of this risk model was validated. A reliable nomogram was also established to provide an accurate probability of survival in 1, 3, and 5 years. To investigate the potential mechanism of this risk signature, GSEA, and immune cell infiltration analysis were conducted. The result of GSEA demonstrated that the high-risk group was involved in pathways associated with tumor progressions, such as the TNF signaling pathway and platinum drug resistance. Platinum drugs are one of the most common drugs used in the treatment of advanced HCC and platinum drug resistance is associated with a poor prognosis of HCC [53]. A large number of lncRNAs have been reported to exert an important role in drug resistance of HCC. lncRNA NR2F1-AS1 was involved in the oxaliplatin resistance of HCC and upregulated ABCC1 by sponging miR-363 [54]. Xiong et al. found that lncRNA HULC induced protective autophagy by stabilizing Sirt1 and impaired the sensitivity of chemotherapy drugs, such as oxaliplatin [55]. The result of GSEA that the high-risk group was enriched in platinum drug resistance revealed that the lncRNA model might be associated with drug resistance in HCC. The distinct immune infiltration statuses of the two risk groups were also evaluated. Macrophages and Tregs dominantly infiltrated the high-risk group. Macrophages were generally associated with poor prognosis in solid tumor [56] and Tregs maintain immune homeostasis and suppress antitumor immunotherapy [57,58], which might explain the unfavorable prognosis of high-risk patients. More interestingly, the high-risk group was observed to be more sensitive to kinase inhibitors but not to some of the topoisomerase inhibitors and antibiotics. With the continuous advancement of precision medicine, patients’ demand for personalized treatment is also increasing. The different sensitivity of potential antitumor drugs in the two risk groups suggests that patients in the high-risk group were more likely to benefit from mitomycin, bleomycin, doxorubicin, tipifarnib, and bexarotene, which can provide clinical guides for individual treatment.

To explore the role of the lncRNA model in HCC, LINC01224 was selected for further experiments. LINC01224 was independently associated with the OS of HCC patients. The expression of LINC01224 in HCC cell lines was significantly higher than LO2. After transfection of si-LINC01224, the migration ability of cells was inhibited, indicating that LINC01224 plays a crucial role in HCC migration and progression. The expression of LINC01224 was confirmed to be significantly upregulated in HCC tissues [59] and HCC cell lines. This finding is consistent with our result. LINC01224 was also reported to be involved in the development of various cancers. LINC01224 promotes the malignant progression of gastric cancer by sponging miR-193a-5p to upregulate CDK8 and inhibit cell apoptosis [60]. The high expression of LINC01224 in endometrial carcinoma tissues was validated and negatively correlated with the prognosis of patients. In terms of mechanism, LINC01224 upregulated the expression of AKT3 by binding to and down-regulating miR-485–5p, which facilitates cell proliferation and inhibits apoptosis [61]. Cui et al. reported that knockdown of the expression of LINC01224 augmented the radiosensitivity of melanoma cells, and LINC01224 could sponge miR-193a-5p to indirectly regulate the expression of NR1D2 [62]. LINC01224 was found to be located in the enhancer of ZNF91 and enhanced the expression of ZNF91, which promote irradiation resistance in NSCLC cells [63]. Apart from LINC01224, several lncRNA in the risk model were previously reported. He, Yikang et al. validated the upregulated expression of LINC00601 in 15 glioma specimens [64]. Wang, YC et al. reported that LINC00601 was highly expressed in 36 HCC samples and HCC cells and acted as a promoter of tumor cell proliferation and progression by affecting the MAPK signaling pathway [65]. Shang, Haitao et al. found the expression of LINC00601 to decrease notably after sonodynamic therapy in HCC cell lines [66]. LINC02577 was upregulated in 30 cases of clear cell renal cell carcinoma tissues and cell lines [67]. The twofold upregulation of LINC02577 in pancreatic adenocarcinoma was detected compared with matched normal tissues [68].

The strengths of this study are as follows: for the first time, we systematically investigated BM- and immune checkpoint-related lncRNAs in HCC, which provided a new perspective for the study of the progression of HCC. In addition, we cross-constructed a lncRNA model with independent prognostic value by combining two machine learning algorithms. The 10 lncRNA signature had significant implications for providing a reliable prediction of prognosis, a distinction of immune infiltration status and response to clinical therapy. Furthermore, one of the members of the prognostic model, LINC01224, was functionally validated, which revealed that LINC01224 might be involved in the progression of HCC and it could be used as a potential prognostic marker for HCC.

Admittedly, there are several limitations to this study. First, this study utilized computational methods to perform data analysis for the construction of the prognostic signature. Although this approach is widely accepted, validation in an independent large-sample cohort with complete follow-up statistics is still required to justify the clinical implications of the signature. Secondly, although the drug sensitivity analyses provide meaningful evidence for the selection of drugs in patients of the high and low-risk groups, the result of the drug sensitivity analysis should be further validated by additional experiments to support the applicability of the prediction. Finally, the novel prognostic model consisted of ten lncRNAs. Only the function of LINC01224 has been verified. In future studies, the molecular mechanism and the targeting potential of LINC01224 in HCC progression and the functional roles of other lncRNAs in HCC deserve to be explored by further fundamental experiments.

Conclusion

In summary, we constructed a prognostic signature based on BMs- and immune checkpoint related lncRNAs in HCC patients. Through a series of analyses, we validated the robust prognosis ability of the lncRNA risk model and uncovered the difference in immune status and drug sensitivity between high-risk and low-risk groups. This study not only identified lncRNA related to BMs and immune checkpoints but also contributed to stratifying patients for guiding individual treatment.

The Diagnostic Value of sdLDL-C for Acute Coronary Syndrome with Coexisting Hypertension and Carotid Atherosclerosis in Northeast China
Authors: Jiayu Li, Zhe Dong, Dandan Wang, Jing Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent cardiovascular risk assessment relies heavily on conventional lipid parameters, which inadequately capture residual risk for acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Small dense low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (sdLDL-C) exhibits heightened atherogenicity due to its unique physicochemical properties, yet its diagnostic utility for ACS in high-risk populations with multiple comorbidities remains undefined. Furthermore, population-specific evidence, particularly from Northeast China where cardiovascular disease prevalence is high, is limited.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 456 ACS patients and 100 healthy controls. Patients were stratified by hypertension (HT) grade and carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate diagnostic efficacy and determine optimal cut-off values. An independent validation cohort (4 groups, n=100 each) was used to calculate positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV).ResultssdLDL-C levels were significantly higher in ACS patients vs. controls [0.78 vs. 0.42 mmol/L, p < 0.001], showing an increasing trend with disease burden and peaking in the ACS with HT and abnormal CIMT group (ACS+HT+ACIMT). Multivariate logistic regression revealed the strongest association between sdLDL-C and the ACS+HT+ACIMT group, odds ratio (OR)=119.341. ROC analysis demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficacy for this group (area under the curve (AUC)=0.903), with an optimal cut-off of 0.621 mmol/L (sensitivity 88.70%, specificity 83.00%). In the validation cohort, the ACS+HT+ACIMT group achieved PPV of 89.36% and NPV of 84.91%, with narrower confidence intervals, indicating improved predictive accuracy.ConclusionsdLDL-C exhibits significant diagnostic value in ACS patients, with efficacy increasing alongside disease burden, particularly in high-risk populations with HT and carotid atherosclerosis. The phenotype-specific cut-off (0.621 mmol/L) may serve as an effective risk stratification tool.Keywords: acute coronary syndrome, hypertension, carotid intima-media thickness, small dense low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, diagnostic value, Northeast China

Introduction

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) remains a leading cause of global cardiovascular mortality and morbidity. Its pathogenesis typically involves the rupture or erosion of atherosclerotic coronary plaques, triggering thrombosis and myocardial ischemia.1 Globally, ACS has become a major public health challenge due to its high incidence, high disability rate, and high mortality. Hypertension (HT) and carotid atherosclerosis not only exacerbate the pathological progression of ACS but also pose severe challenges in its treatment. For ACS patients with comorbid HT, there is often a delicate balance between blood pressure control and maintaining coronary perfusion.2 Meanwhile, carotid atherosclerosis suggests more diffuse and complex coronary lesions, increasing the difficulty of antithrombotic therapy decisions and the risk of microcirculatory disturbances after Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI).3,4 Therefore, when ACS is compounded by multiple high-risk vascular factors, the treatment strategy must be elevated from simply reopening the coronary artery to a comprehensive intervention encompassing blood pressure regulation, management of systemic arteriosclerosis burden, and combined cardio-cerebral protection. This underscores the need for individualized risk stratification.

Current guidelines heavily rely on conventional lipid panels for risk assessment. However, parameters like low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) often fail to fully capture residual cardiovascular risk, partly due to their inability to reflect the heterogeneity within atherogenic lipoprotein particles.5,6 This limitation has spurred interest in more specific biomarkers. Small dense low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (sdLDL-C), a subtype of LDL characterized by its smaller size, lower buoyancy, and reduced affinity for the LDL receptor, has emerged as a potent candidate.7 Its physicochemical properties grant it greater arterial wall penetrability, prolonged retention time, and higher susceptibility to oxidation, collectively contributing to a significantly heightened atherogenic potential compared to larger, buoyant LDL particles.8,9 Despite compelling pathophysiological evidence, the integration of sdLDL-C into routine clinical practice has been limited.Key barriers include the lack of standardized assays and, crucially, a scarcity of validated, context-specific diagnostic cut-off values.

This evidence gap is particularly pronounced in specific high-risk populations. The Northeast Chinese population has a high cardiovascular disease burden, likely due to environmental and lifestyle factors such as cold climate and high-salt, high-fat diet.10–12 These factors may foster a distinct cardiovascular risk profile and lipid metabolism pattern, yet the diagnostic utility of sdLDL-C in this population remains largely unexplored. Moreover, in real-world clinical settings, patients often present with overlapping independent factors. A critical, unaddressed question is whether the diagnostic performance of sdLDL-C for ACS is uniform or if it varies significantly based on the co-existence and severity of conditions like HT and carotid atherosclerosis. Establishing precise diagnostic thresholds for these common clinical phenotypes could substantially enhance risk stratification.

To bridge these gaps, we conducted a study leveraging the patient cohort from the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command, a major regional cardiovascular center that serves a population representative of Northeast China. Our primary objectives were: 1) to evaluate the diagnostic value of sdLDL-C for ACS within this unique demographic context, and 2) to determine whether its diagnostic accuracy is augmented in the presence of HT and carotid atherosclerosis, thereby establishing and validating phenotype-specific sdLDL-C diagnostic cut-off values.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

This retrospective observational study consecutively enrolled 456 patients who were admitted to the Department of Cardiology, General Hospital of Northern Theater Command, due to chest pain and were first diagnosed with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) by coronary angiography between January and December 2024. Diagnosis was based on the criteria established by the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and the American College of Cardiology (ACC) guidelines.13,14 The cohort included 95 patients without HT (HT alone) and 261 with HT (ACS+HT). Based on HT grading,15 patients were classified into grade 1 (stage 1 HT, n=125), grade 2 (stage 2 HT, n=99), and grade 3 (stage 3 HT, n=37). According to carotid ultrasound findings, patients were further divided into groups based on carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT) status:16 an abnormal CIMT group (ACIMT, n=303), which was subsequently subdivided into a CIMT thickening group (TCIMT, n=92), a solitary CIMT group (SCIMT, n=95), and a multiple CIMT group (MCIMT, n=116). Additionally, 100 age-matched healthy individuals who underwent routine health examinations at our hospital during the same period were recruited as the control group.An independent validation cohort was employed, consisting of four patient groups (n=100 per group): ACS alone, ACS+HT, ACS+ACIMT, and ACS+HT+ACIMT.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age 18–85 years, of any sex; (2) first diagnosis of ACS (including unstable angina, acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, and acute non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction) confirmed by coronary angiography, according to the ESC/ACC guidelines; and (3) complete clinical data.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) severe hepatic or renal dysfunction (alanine aminotransferase/aspartate aminotransferase > 3 times the upper limit of normal, estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min/1.73m2); (2) history of malignancy, autoimmune disease, or acute infection; (3) major surgery or trauma within the preceding 3 months; (4) current use of experimental medications affecting lipid metabolism.

As this was a retrospective study utilizing only routinely collected clinical data, all patient information was anonymized prior to analysis. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command (Approval No.: Y (2025) 076), and the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Clinical and Laboratory Measurements

Systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were measured for all participants. The average of two consecutive measurements was calculated. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated for all subjects.

Fasting venous blood samples (≥8 hours) were collected from all participants in the early morning. Blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes, and all tests were completed within 2 hours of collection. The serum concentration of sdLDL-C was determined using a peroxidase-based kit from Beijing Jiage Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. (reagent lot: 24–0928). The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation (CV) for this method were less than 3% and 5%, respectively. All procedures strictly followed the manufacturer’s instructions. Levels of total cholesterol (TC, enzymatic method), triglyceride (TG, enzymatic method), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C, direct method-catalase clearance), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C, direct method-catalase clearance), apolipoprotein A1 (apoA1, immunoturbidimetry), apolipoprotein B (apoB, immunoturbidimetry), lipoprotein a (LP(a), immunoturbidimetry), fasting blood glucose (Glu, hexokinase method), cystatin C (CYS-C, latex immunoturbidimetry), uric acid (UA, uricase-peroxidase method), and homocysteine (HCY, enzymatic cycling method) were measured using a Beckman Coulter AU5821 automated chemistry analyzer (Beckman Coulter, Inc., Brea, CA, USA).

Carotid Ultrasound Examination and HT Grading

Carotid artery ultrasonography was performed by two experienced sonographers, who were blinded to the group assignments, using a Siemens CS2000 color Doppler ultrasound system equipped with a 4–11 MHz linear array transducer. The CIMT was measured at three sites: the common carotid artery, the carotid bifurcation, and 1 cm proximal to the dilatation of the internal carotid artery. Measurements were taken three times on each side, and the average value was calculated.Plaque burden was quantified by calculating the total area (mm2) of all detectable plaques. Carotid atherosclerosis grouping was based on CIMT and plaque definitions according to the Guidelines for Vascular Ultrasound Examination.16 CIMT thickening was defined as CIMT ≥ 1.0 mm. A plaque was defined as a focal structure protruding into the lumen by ≥1.5 mm or by ≥50% of the surrounding CIMT. Patients with plaques were further categorized: a solitary plaque group for those with one plaque in either carotid artery, and a multiple plaque group for those with two or more plaques. HT was graded according to standard criteria:15 Grade 1 (SBP: 140–159 mmHg and/or DBP: 90–99 mmHg), Grade 2 (SBP: 160–179 mmHg and/or DBP: 100–109 mmHg), and Grade 3 (SBP ≥180 mmHg and/or DBP ≥110 mmHg).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Normally distributed data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x±s), and compared between two groups using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous data were presented as median (interquartile range) [M(P25, P75)]. Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. If the overall difference was statistically significant, pairwise comparisons were conducted using Dunn’s post-hoc test with Bonferroni correction. Categorical variables were expressed as frequency (percentage), and compared between groups using the chi-squared (χ2) test.Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to assess correlations between sdLDL-C levels and other clinical parameters. Logistic regression models were employed to evaluate independent factors associated with sdLDL-C in different populations. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of sdLDL-C for different conditions.The optimal cut-off value was determined at the point with the maximum Youden Index. A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of Baseline Characteristics Between the ACS Group and the Healthy Controls

Compared to the healthy control group (n=100), patients in the ACS group (n=456) were significantly older [65 (IQR, 58–71) years vs. 63 (IQR, 56–68) years, p = 0.028] and had a higher prevalence of HT (65.35% vs. 17.00%, p < 0.001). Regarding metabolic profiles, the ACS group exhibited significantly higher levels of TC, TG, LDL-C, apoB, Lp(a), sdLDL-C, SBP, and UA compared to the control group (all p < 0.05). No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of sex, history of diabetes mellitus, smoking and drinking history, HDL-C, or CRP levels. Baseline Characteristics Across the Five Study Groups

Based on clinical features, participants were stratified into five groups: healthy control (n=100), ACS alone (n=95), ACS with HT (ACS+HT, n=261), ACS with abnormal carotid intima-media thickness (ACS+ACIMT, n=303), and ACS with both HT and abnormal CIMT (ACS+HT+ACIMT, n=203). The groups were generally well-balanced in terms of sex, smoking and drinking history, prevalence of diabetes, and several biochemical parameters (all p > 0.05).

Age demonstrated an increasing trend with greater disease burden, with the two groups involving ACIMT having the highest median age [68 (IQR, 60–76) years for both]. Regarding lipid profiles, TC, LDL-C, and apoB levels gradually increased with escalating disease burden. Notably, sdLDL-C exhibited the most pronounced gradient increase. Its median concentration rose progressively from 0.42 (IQR, 0.27–0.55) mmol/L in the control group to 0.61 (IQR, 0.47–0.77) mmol/L in the ACS alone group, 0.81 (IQR, 0.54–1.28) mmol/L in the ACS+ACIMT group, and 0.89 (IQR, 0.54–1.32) mmol/L in the ACS+HT group, peaking at 1.03 (IQR, 0.77–1.41) mmol/L in the ACS+HT+ACIMT group. This peak value was 2.45 times that of the control group and was significantly higher than all other disease groups (all p < 0.05), suggesting a clear dose-response relationship (Table 2).

Distribution of sdLDL-C Levels Across Different Grades of Carotid Atherosclerosis and Hypertension

Patients with ACIMT were further subdivided into CIMT thickening group(TCIMT), solitary CIMT group(SCIMT), and multiple CIMT group (MCIMT). The analysis revealed that sdLDL-C levels were positively correlated with the severity of both carotid atherosclerosis and HT. sdLDL-C concentrations increased significantly in parallel with the progression of carotid lesions from normal CIMT(NCIMT) to thickening and then to plaque formation (p< 0.05). Similarly, when patients with HT were stratified by grade (grade 1, grade 2, and grade 3), sdLDL-C levels demonstrated a significant upward trend with increasing HT grade (p < 0.05, Figure 1). These findings reinforce the potential role of sdLDL-C as a sensitive biomarker of cardiovascular risk in the context of atherosclerosis and HT.

Correlation Between sdLDL-C and Metabolic Parameters Across Different Cardiovascular Risk Strata

The correlation profile of sdLDL-C with various parameters differed markedly among the groups. In the healthy control group, sdLDL-C was significantly and positively correlated with TC, TG, LDL-C, and apoB, reflecting its association with conventional lipid metabolism. In the ACS alone group, sdLDL-C showed no significant correlations with any of the measured parameters. However, in the ACS+HT and ACS+ACIMT groups, the correlations of sdLDL-C with SBP, DBP, TC, LDL-C, apoB, and Lp(a) were significantly strengthened. The strongest correlation was observed between sdLDL-C and SBP in the ACS+ACIMT group (r = 0.713, p < 0.001). These significant correlations persisted in the ACS+HT+ACIMT group (Table 3). These results indicate that in ACS patients with concomitant HT or atherosclerosis, sdLDL-C is more closely associated with blood pressure and lipid parameters, further supporting its role as a biomarker for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Multinomial Logistic Regression Analysis of sdLDL-C Associations with Different Disease States

Multivariable Logistic regression analysis (Table 4) revealed that, after adjusting for potential confounders including age, sex, BMI, history of diabetes, smoking history, TG, LDL, and HDL, and using the healthy control group as the reference, sdLDL-C levels remained significantly and positively associated with all disease groups. Multicollinearity diagnostics were performed; although all variance inflation factors (VIF) were below 5, TC was excluded from the final model due to its high correlation with LDL to control for collinearity. The analysis showed that the odds ratio (OR) for the isolated ACS group was 10.535 (95% CI: 3.491–31.791, p < 0.001), for the ACS with HT group was 64.561 (95% CI: 23.591–176.681, p < 0.001), and for the ACS with carotid atherosclerosis group was 49.287 (95% CI: 18.145–133.872, p < 0.001). Notably, the triple-morbidity group exhibited the highest OR at 119.341 (95% CI: 42.808–332.702, p < 0.001). These findings suggest a stepwise increase in the effect size of sdLDL-C corresponding to the accumulation of cardiovascular independent factors, demonstrating its potent risk-identifying capability, particularly in the context of multiple concurrent vascular pathologies.

ROC Curve Analysis of sdLDL-C for Diagnosing Different Disease States

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that sdLDL-C could significantly discriminate patients in all disease states from healthy controls (Figure 2 and Table 5). Internal validation using the bootstrap method (1,000 resamples) confirmed the stability of the area under the curve (AUC) and its 95% confidence intervals. The optimal cut-off values, derived from the Youden index, are reported. The AUC for diagnosing ACS alone was 0.731 (95% CI: 0.660–0.801), with an optimal cut-off value of 0.549 mmol/L, yielding a sensitivity of 60.00% and specificity of 77.00%. For diagnosing ACS+HT, the AUC was 0.821 (95% CI: 0.776–0.866), with a cut-off of 0.621 mmol/L (sensitivity: 72.40%, specificity: 83.00%). For diagnosing ACS+ACIMT, the AUC was 0.806 (95% CI: 0.760–0.852), with a cut-off of 0.621 mmol/L (sensitivity: 69.30%, specificity: 83.00%). The diagnostic performance was highest for the triple-condition group (ACS+HT+ACIMT), with an AUC of 0.903 (95% CI: 0.867–0.940). At the optimal cut-off of 0.621 mmol/L, the sensitivity and specificity were 88.70% and 83.00%, respectively (all p < 0.001). These findings indicate that the diagnostic efficacy of sdLDL-C improves with increasing disease complexity, demonstrating excellent screening capability, particularly when multiple vascular independent factors coexist. The results were robust as confirmed by bootstrap internal validation.

Validation of the Predictive Efficacy of the sdLDL Cut-off Value in Different Disease States

Validation of the predefined sdLDL cut-off value in four newly collected patient groups (100 cases each) demonstrated that the predictive efficacy of this marker exhibited a significant increasing trend with greater disease burden. In the ACS alone group, the positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) were 85.00% (95% CI: 75.26%–92.00%) and 73.33% (95% CI: 64.49%–80.99%), respectively. In the ACS+HT group, the PPV and NPV increased to 88.89% (95% CI: 80.51%–94.54%) and 81.82% (95% CI: 73.33%–88.53%), respectively. In the ACS+ACIMT group, the PPV and NPV were 88.64% (95% CI: 80.09%–94.41%) and 80.36% (95% CI: 71.78%–87.26%), respectively. Notably, the predictive performance peaked in the complex ACS group (ACS+HT+ACIMT), achieving a PPV of 89.36% (95% CI: 81.30%–94.78%) and an NPV of 84.91% (95% CI: 76.65%–91.12%) (Table 6). Furthermore, the width of the confidence intervals narrowed with the accumulation of comorbidities, suggesting progressively enhanced predictive accuracy. These trend-based findings indicate that the clinical utility of the sdLDL cut-off value is most pronounced in high-risk populations with multiple coexisting independent factors, positioning it as a robust tool for risk stratification. However, caution is warranted when relying solely on this indicator to rule out low-risk ACS patients.

Discussion

This study identifies sdLDL-C as an independent diagnostic biomarker for ACS in a northeastern Chinese population, with diagnostic efficacy that varies significantly across clinical phenotype. The diagnostic accuracy of sdLDL-C exhibited a progressive increase with the accumulation of cardiovascular independent factors, including HT and carotid atherosclerosis. The AUC escalated from 0.731 (95% CI: 0.660–0.801) in the ACS-only group to 0.903 (95% CI: 0.867–0.940) in the ACS+HT+ACIMT group. Furthermore, we established a set of phenotype-specific diagnostic cut-off values and confirmed their predictive performance in an independent validation cohort, offering a new tool with clinical translational potential for cardiovascular risk stratification.

Our findings align with prior research establishing sdLDL-C as a key driver of atherosclerosis17,18 and provide a significant extension to this understanding. Multivariate logistic regression analysis, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, history of diabetes, smoking history, and conventional lipid parameters, revealed that sdLDL-C remained significantly and positively associated with all disease states. Notably, the OR showed a stepwise increase corresponding to the escalating disease burden: the OR was 10.535 (95% CI: 3.491–31.791) for the ACS-only group, 64.561 (95% CI: 23.591–176.681) for the ACS+HT group, 49.287 (95% CI: 18.145–133.872) for the ACS+ACIMT group, and reached 119.341 (95% CI: 42.808–332.702) for the ACS+HT+ACIMT group. This gradient effect highlights the role of sdLDL-C as a cumulative risk indicator,19,20 suggesting its risk-identifying capacity becomes particularly pronounced when multiple vascular pathologies coexist. It is noteworthy that during model construction, we assessed multicollinearity. Although all VIF were below 5, given the high correlation between TC and LDL-C, TC was ultimately excluded from the model to avoid compromising model stability. This step ensured the reliability of the regression coefficients and resulted in a more stable and interpretable model.

ROC analysis provided the most compelling evidence for the diagnostic value of sdLDL-C. The AUC for sdLDL-C reached 0.903 (95% CI: 0.867–0.940) in the complex patient group (ACS+HT+ACIMT), significantly higher than the 0.731 observed in the ACS-only group (p < 0.001), with corresponding sensitivity and specificity of 88.70% and 83.00%. This suggests that sdLDL-C is not merely a risk marker but may more accurately reflect the intensity of the atherosclerotic process.21 This may reflect a synergistic interaction among independent factors: hypertension-induced endothelial dysfunction promotes sdLDL retention, while carotid atherosclerosis indicates systemic vascular burden.22–24 When these three pathological conditions coexist, sdLDL-C may transcend its role as a mere risk biomarker and represent a direct manifestation of an active, systemic atherosclerotic process, potentially explaining its exceptionally strong diagnostic signal.

To further validate the clinical utility and stability of the sdLDL-C cut-off values, we specifically assembled an independent validation cohort (4 patient groups, n=100 each). This design was necessary because cut-off values derived solely from a derivation cohort are susceptible to overfitting, and their performance in real-world clinical settings requires confirmation in independent samples. The validation cohort results demonstrated that the predictive efficacy of the sdLDL-C cut-off values increased significantly with escalating disease burden: In the ACS-only group, the PPV and NPV were 85.00% (95% CI: 75.26%–92.00%) and 73.33% (95% CI: 64.49%–80.99%), respectively. These values were 88.89% (95% CI: 80.51%–94.54%) and 81.82% (95% CI: 73.33%–88.53%) for the ACS+HT group; 88.64% (95% CI: 80.09%–94.41%) and 80.36% (95% CI: 71.78%–87.26%) for the ACS+ACIMT group; and reached their highest at 89.36% (95% CI: 81.30%–94.78%) and 84.91% (95% CI: 76.65%–91.12%) for the ACS+HT+ACIMT group. Notably, as comorbidities increased, the confidence intervals narrowed progressively (eg., the PPV confidence interval width in the ACS+HT+ACIMT group was 13.48%, narrower than the 16.74% in the ACS-only group), suggesting improved predictive precision. These results further confirm that the clinical guidance provided by sdLDL-C is most prominent in high-risk populations with multiple coexisting independent factors, and its predictive performance exhibits good stability and reproducibility.

The superior diagnostic performance of sdLDL-C in patients with multiple comorbidities likely stems from its pathophysiological synergy.25 Mechanistically, the atherogenicity of sdLDL-C is primarily attributed to its unique physicochemical properties: small particle size (diameter < 25.5 nm), high density (1.044–1.060 g/mL), and low affinity for the LDL receptor, leading to a prolonged plasma half-life and enhanced propensity to enter and be retained within the arterial intima.9 HT exacerbates this process through several pathways: (1) increased endothelial permeability due to heightened mechanical stress; (2) hemodynamic alterations promoting the deposition of sdLDL particles at sites of endothelial injury; and (3) enhanced oxidative stress mediated by angiotensin II, facilitating the oxidative modification of sdLDL.26 Oxidized sdLDL is more readily recognized by macrophage scavenger receptors, promoting foam cell formation and accelerating atherosclerotic plaque development.27 Concurrently, carotid atherosclerosis itself is a direct indicator of systemic atherosclerotic burden, with increased CIMT and plaque formation reflecting an active state of vascular wall inflammation and remodeling.24 Therefore, in patients where ACS, HT, and significant carotid atherosclerosis coexist, elevated sdLDL-C levels may signify a positive feedback loop: HT promotes sdLDL deposition and oxidation, oxidized sdLDL exacerbates endothelial injury and atherosclerosis, and atherosclerosis further disrupts local hemodynamics. This vicious cycle positions sdLDL-C as a particularly sensitive diagnostic indicator in this high-risk population.

The unique environmental and dietary pressures in northeastern China may further exacerbate the aforementioned pathological processes. The region experiences prolonged, cold winters, reducing outdoor physical activity, coupled with a traditional diet characterized by high salt and fat intake. These factors collectively contribute to the high prevalence of dyslipidemia and HT.28,29 Previous epidemiological surveys indicate that the morbidity and mortality of cardiovascular diseases in northeastern China exceed the national average.10 Within this distinctive environmental context, the synergistic effects among sdLDL-C, HT, and atherosclerosis may be amplified, establishing sdLDL-C as an ideal tool for risk stratification among individuals at high risk for ACS in this region.

The phenotype-based cut-off values established in this study, particularly the threshold of 0.621 mmol/L for the triple-risk group (ACS+HT+ACIMT), hold significant potential for clinical translation. This cut-off value, determined via ROC curve analysis (maximizing the Youden index), demonstrated robust PPV and NPV in the independent validation cohort. In clinical practice, for patients presenting with chest pain from similar high-risk backgrounds, an sdLDL-C level exceeding this threshold could alert physicians to a high probability (PPV approaching 90%) of ACS with significant comorbidities, potentially prompting more aggressive diagnostic workup and early intensive management.30,31 Conversely, an sdLDL-C level below this threshold might reasonably exclude complex ACS (NPV approximately 85%), helping to avoid unnecessary invasive procedures. This approach holds promise for refining current risk stratification algorithms, which primarily rely on traditional factors.32

This study demonstrates several key innovations and clinical translational potential: it is the first to systematically evaluate the diagnostic value of sdLDL-C across different clinical phenotypes of ACS in a high-risk population in Northeast China, revealing that its diagnostic efficacy increases with cumulative disease burden and peaks in the “triple pathological state” of ACS complicated by hypertension and carotid atherosclerosis (AUC = 0.903). For the first time, a phenotype-specific sdLDL-C diagnostic cut-off value (0.621 mmol/L) was established and validated in an independent cohort, demonstrating favorable predictive performance (PPV 89.36%, NPV 84.91%). Furthermore, the study revealed that correlations between sdLDL-C and blood pressure as well as lipid parameters were significantly enhanced in the context of multiple comorbidities, suggesting sdLDL-C may serve as an integrative biomarker of vascular pathology, thereby filling a research gap regarding the diagnostic value of sdLDL-C in high-risk populations in this region.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the single-center, cross-sectional design, while revealing strong associations, precludes establishing causality. The generalizability of the proposed cut-off values requires validation in prospective, multicenter cohorts.33 Second, although adjustments were made for major confounders including age, sex, BMI, diabetes history, smoking, and conventional lipid parameters, residual confounding from unmeasured variables (eg., detailed medication history, genetic susceptibility, inflammatory biomarkers like IL-6, TNF-α) cannot be ruled out. Third, while the validation cohort confirmed the predictive efficacy of the cut-off values, its sample size was relatively modest (100 per group), necessitating confirmation in larger prospective studies.34 Fourth, as with all sdLDL-C research, the lack of a globally standardized assay method mandates caution when comparing absolute values across different studies or populations.35 Finally, dynamic changes in sdLDL-C levels were not monitored, preventing assessment of its value in monitoring treatment response or determining prognosis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, sdLDL-C is an independent diagnostic biomarker for ACS in a northeastern Chinese population. Its diagnostic efficacy intensifies with increasing disease burden, being most pronounced in high-risk individuals with concomitant HT and carotid atherosclerosis. The phenotype-specific cut-off value (0.621 mmol/L) demonstrated favorable predictive performance (PPV 89.36%, NPV 84.91%) in an independent validation cohort, establishing it as an effective risk stratification tool. These findings offer a new perspective for precision cardiovascular risk assessment and underscore the potential value of sdLDL-C in personalized medicine.

Phalloplasty Alternatives: Shaft-Only Phalloplasty Modifications
Authors: Blair R. Peters, Peter C. Ferrin, Jens U. Berli
Published: 2026

INTRODUCTION

Gender-affirming phalloplasty encompasses a range of procedures, which can be combined and staged in different ways to best meet an individual’s goals. Although many phalloplasty patients desire standing urination, some wish to forgo the risk of additional complications from urethral lengthening or have anatomical goals that lie outside of traditional phalloplasty and instead opt for shaft-only phalloplasty (SOP). SOP is defined as a variation of phalloplasty that maintains the natal urinary conduit.1 At our institution, all phalloplasty operations are performed in a staged manner. Flap transfer and creation of the phallic shaft are performed during the first stage, the technical details of which have been described in a prior video publication.2,3 For patients who choose SOP, several anatomical options are possible for their second-stage operation, depending on goals and priorities. These options include the following:

Vaginectomy and perineal masculinization with creation of a perineal urethrostomy.

Vaginectomy and perineal masculinization with creation of a perineal urethrostomy.

Vaginal preservation vulvo-scrotoplasty.

Vaginal preservation vulvo-scrotoplasty.

Glansplasty only, with no or minimal further genital alteration.

Glansplasty only, with no or minimal further genital alteration.

In this article, we outline the technical aspects of these second-stage surgical options for SOP as performed at Oregon Health and Science University. We do not review the technical steps of glansplasty, as these have been thoroughly reviewed in our previous publications.2 This article is intended to complement a previously published video article outlining the technical steps of phalloplasty with urethral lengthening using the Big Ben method to provide a comprehensive technical library covering all steps of phalloplasty as performed at our institution.2,3 This video article is limited to a technical description only and does not include comparative outcome data between shaft-only and traditional phalloplasty. All patient care adhered to the World Professional Association for Transgender Health Standards of Care, Version 8. PERINEAL MASCULINIZATION WITH PERINEAL URETHROSTOMY

The surgery begins with vaginectomy, with complete mucosal excision performed by our urology colleague.4 Markings are then made for the scrotoplasty, outlining the lateral borders of the labia majora and the medial incisions at the junction of the hair-bearing labia majora and non–hair-bearing labia minora. The superior markings are dome-shaped and, in patients with sufficient vertical labia majora length, will reach slightly onto the phallus shaft. In natal penoscrotal anatomy, the scrotal raphe originates from the shaft of the penis, making it important to position the scrotum as close to the base of the phallus as possible. Laterally, the incisions end just beneath the level of the adductor longus tendons. The bilateral labia majora flaps are raised in the subcutaneous plane and secured to the inner thigh while the perineal surgery continues. A trapezoid incision around the urethral meatus is performed. To avoid stricture formation and difficulties approximating the perineal skin to the urethral mucosa, the cuff of urethral mucosa should be designed generously. The epithelium over the clitoris, clitoral shaft, and labia minora is de-epithelialized following injection with lidocaine with epinephrine. Any excessive labia minora tissue can then be trimmed if desired. The clitoris is sharply released from the urethral plate to allow for greater mobility and is transposed and secured to the pubic bone. This results in the clitoral tissue lying in a subcutaneous pocket at the base of the phallus. Although not currently practiced at our center, the clitoris can also be de-epithelialized and allowed to retract in its natal location without transposition. The labia majora flaps are then rotated into position to form the scrotum. The perineum is closed by suturing the de-epithelialized tissue of the labia minora to the lateral superficial fascia of the perineum, thereby distributing some tension before approximating the midline perineal skin. Any excess urethral mucosal tissue is then trimmed, and the urethrostomy is sutured to the skin of the perineum under no tension using 2-0 Vicryl sutures. Glansplasty is performed concomitantly at the same time as the perineal portion of the case. A Foley catheter is placed and wrapped with Xeroform so that the catheter maintains a 90-degree angle exiting the urethra to offload pressure on the meatal suture lines. The catheter remains in place for 5–7 days before removal.

VAGINAL PRESERVATION VULVO-SCROTOPLASTY

Markings and incisions for the scrotoplasty mirror the technique described earlier.5 In this technical variation of SOP, we begin by placing the clitoris on tension with a retraction suture. The superior commissure of the labia minora (where the labia minora joins the clitoral hood) is sharply released with a horizontal incision. This release is then extended with an incision across the urethral plate to ensure full release and mobility of the clitoris. Thereafter, all the epithelium over the clitoris and clitoral shaft, as well as the lateral vulvar skin between the labia majora and the labia minora, is de-epithelialized following injection with lidocaine with epinephrine. It is important to leave 1–2 mm of skin lateral to the labia minora to have skin of sufficient thickness and integrity to close at the end of the case. The clitoris is then transposed and secured to the pubic bone at the base of the penis. The labia majora flaps are rotated into position at the base of the phallus to form the scrotum, as described previously. The free edges of the labia minora superiorly are then secured together, reforming a superior labial commissure and anchored to the base of the scrotum with 3-0 Maxon sutures. The lateral edges of the remaining perineal skin are then secured to the lateral edges of the labia minora. Care is taken to avoid undue tension to avoid spreading of the labia minora or introital gaping. Depending on patient anatomy and preference, an edge-trim labiaplasty technique may be performed to minimize the prominence of the labia minora. Glansplasty is performed concomitantly at the same time as the perineal portion of the case. No Foley catheter is placed, as no urethral surgery is performed. For patients who do not desire burial of the clitoris, the described procedures can be performed while maintaining the clitoris in its natal location, with rotation of the scrotum superior to the clitoral tissue.

SOP WITH MINIMAL GENITAL MODIFICATION

A small subset of patients undergoing phalloplasty experience dysphoria due to the absence of an adult-sized phallus but do not experience dysphoria related to their natal genital anatomy. For these patients, an SOP with minimal modification to their genitals is a good and often the preferred option. In these cases, the minimal modifications may include an isolated glansplasty or isolated burial of the clitoris. Some patients may choose isolated clitoral burial either because they have dysphoria from the clitoris and/or to integrate their erogenous genital sensation.

SOP operations are modifications of traditional phalloplasty that target individual goals and remove additional risks from undesired operative components. Ultimately, gender-affirming phalloplasty encompasses a range of procedural variations that can be combined and staged in different ways to best relieve an individual’s sources of genital dysphoria.

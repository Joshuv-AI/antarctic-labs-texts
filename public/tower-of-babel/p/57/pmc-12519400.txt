Budget impact analysis of glecaprevir/pibrentasvir treatment for hepatitis C in Iran: a modelling study
Authors: Mojtaba Jafari, Parisa Mehdizadeh, Khosro Keshavarz, Ehsan Teymourzadeh, Mohammad Ali Abyazi, Parsa Gholamreza Shirazi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesGlecaprevir/pibrentasvir (GLE/PIB), despite being a highly costly medication, is considered a cost-effective approach compared with sofosbuvir/velpatasvir (SOF/VEL) and sofosbuvir/daclatasvir (SOF/DCV) in the treatment of hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. No study has evaluated the effect of GLE/PIB’s introduction into Iran’s drug list from a health policy perspective and estimated the budgetary impact change. Therefore, this study was conducted to analyse the fiscal effect of the introduction of GLE/PIB into Iran’s drug list.DesignBudget impact analysis. The assumptions and costs of including GLE/PIB in Iran’s drug list for the treatment of patients with hepatitis C were derived from a conducted cost-effectiveness analysis.SettingNational level. In this study, the budgetary changes in Iran’s pharmaceutical market and health system, from the Ministry of Health’s perspective, have been estimated for a 5-year time horizon following the introduction of GLE/PIB in the country.ResultsBased on the results obtained from the budget impact model, currently, 4112 patients are receiving SOF/DCV and SOF/VEL therapeutic regimens, which is expected to decrease to 1093 in 2029 owing to the affordability of medications and a 50% estimated market share for GLE/PIB. According to the results, with the introduction of GLE/PIB into the market and assuming a market share of 10% in the first year, growing to 50% by the fifth year, the healthcare system costs will increase by approximately $0.61, $1.77, $3.86, $7.45 and $13.51 million over the next 5 years, respectively. Additionally, based on the drug’s selling price, there will be a 468% increase in hepatitis C drug market costs after 5 years, resulting in an overall budget increase of approximately 0.13% for Iran’s pharmaceutical market. According to the sensitivity analysis, a 20% reduction in chronic hepatitis C (CHC) costs could decrease the projected increase in health sector costs from $13.51 million (an 18.84% increase) to $10.52 million (an 18.16% increase). Conversely, a 20% rise in CHC costs would raise those costs to $16.49 million (a 19.31% increase).ConclusionConsidering the high price of the GLE/PIB compared with the available options in Iran, with the introduction of GLE/PIB into Iran’s drug list, insurance coverage and appropriate allocation of necessary resources, a reduction in the cost burden because of hepatitis C treatment is expected for individuals and households. Additionally, with a well-regulated market share of existing medications, the optimal treatment choice for patients will be feasible.

Introduction

Hepatitis C virus (HCV), with a 3% prevalence rate,1 is recognised as a public health problem worldwide.2 Hepatitis C imposes a significant economic burden on households and society.3 4 For example, the economic burden of chronic hepatitis C (CHC) in the United States alone is greater than $10 billion annually.4

In Iran, the prevalence of hepatitis C is between 0.1% and 0.3% and has been on an upward trend in recent years.57 Additionally, the total direct cost of treating hepatitis C is anticipated to be higher than household consumption expenditure. This emphasises the need for planning and insurance coverage, especially for expensive new medications, to reduce the household cost.8

In recent years, newly introduced direct-acting antiviral drugs have shown significant efficacy and safety for treating hepatitis C.9 10 The costs of these novel treatment methods are greater than those of previous methods and include a significant portion of the economic burden of hepatitis C.11 12

Sofosbuvir/daclatasvir (SOF/DCV) and sofosbuvir/velpatasvir (SOF/VEL) are two cost-effective treatment methods for the treatment of hepatitis C used in Iran.13 14 Moreover, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (GLE/PIB), despite its high costs, has been recognised as one of the most effective medications for hepatitis C, which can enhance the quality of life and reduce the mortality rate of patients in some countries.10 15 GLE/PIB has not yet been added to Iran’s official drug list. Additionally, there has been no study that evaluates the effect of GLE/PIB's entry into Iran's drug list from a health policy perspective, estimating the budgetary changes in the pharmaceutical market and the entire health sector after the drug's entry. Budget impact analysis,16 as part of a comprehensive economic assessment that is used in most developed countries for budget planning, can provide a clear picture of the effect of the new technology or treatment method on the budget of the entire health system to predict the need for necessary allocation of resources.17 Therefore, this study aimed to analyse the budgetary impact of the entry of GLE/PIB into Iran’s drug list to equip policymakers with documented results and provide a helpful tool for financing, resource allocation and insurance reimbursement for patients with hepatitis C.

Methods

A budget impact model has been developed using TreeAge Pro 2022 and Excel 2016 (Microsoft), incorporating Iran’s demographic data and epidemiological information on patients with hepatitis C. In this study, the time horizon was set at 5 years.1820 The population data and population growth rate were extracted from the Statistical Centre of Iran. A schematic patient map and the methodology for budget impact analysis calculations are provided in the online supplemental appendix. Other tools used in this study include the 2023 Iran pharma market data report ‘Amarname’21 and affordability indexes based on expert opinion.

Base-case model inputs and data sources

Model structure

This budget impact model compared three medication regimens for treating hepatitis C22: GLE/PIB, SOF/VEL and SOF/DCV. A Markov model was developed to reflect the chronic and recurring nature of hepatitis C. Disease states included CHC (F0-F3), compensated cirrhosis (F4), decompensated cirrhosis,16 hepatocellular carcinoma, sustained virological response and death,2325 as shown in figure 1.

Transition probabilities between hepatitis C health states in the Markov model: a schematic representation of disease progression. CHC, no cirrhosis; CC, compensated cirrhosis; DC, decompensated cirrhosis; HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma; SVR, sustained virological response.

The study analysed costs over a 5-year horizon using 1-year Markov cycles. All costs were presented in purchasing power parity dollars (PPP$). The fees were adjusted for Iran’s annual health sector inflation rate of 43.2%.26 The future inflation rate is calculated using the formula Rn = (1+r)n, where Rn represents the inflation rate for the following years, r is the rate of inflation and n is the number of years.

Model population

In this study, the patient population of interest includes all patients with hepatitis C in Iran who receive medications. Based on epidemiological studies, the average prevalence of patients with hepatitis C among the general Iranian population is 0.3%. Additionally, among subpopulations, HCV affects approximately 52.2% among people who inject drugs (PWID), 20.0% among people with a high risk of healthcare-related exposures and 7.5% among the population with liver-related conditions. Genotype 1 HCV is considered the most prevalent circulating strain at 58.2%, followed by genotype 3 at 39.0% (7). Moreover, the incidence rate of hepatitis C shows a growing trend between 2008 and 2015, and approximately 3939 newly diagnosed cases are reported annually (27).

According to the Statistical Centre of Iran, Iran has a total population of 85 067 780 people, which is increasing at a rate of 1.24% annually. As a result of the latest censuses in Iran, the population aged >18 years constitutes 68.9% of the total population. Additionally, according to Iran’s annual drug sales information published on the Food and Drug Administration of Iran’s website,21 the total sales volumes of SOF/VEL and SOF/DCV were 72 390 and 317 660, respectively, in 2023. Considering a treatment cycle duration of 90 days for SOF/DCV and 180 days for SOF/VEL, the total number and percentage of patients in Iran receiving medication for the treatment of hepatitis C can be calculated.

Model parameters

Transition probabilities

According to the results of the studies and relevant experts’ opinions, the patient’s treatment period was considered 1 year. The transition probabilities between different health states were extracted from the results of internal and external published studies.10 13 14 25 27 All-cause mortality probabilities based on age and sex were extracted from the life table of Iran in 2020. In addition, the probabilities related to mortality caused by hepatitis C were extracted from previous studies2830 (online supplemental appendix).

Costs

This study estimated the direct medical costs and health state costs from the Ministry of Health’s perspective using both local and micro-costing methods. Additionally, annual costs were analysed within the health sector and the pharmaceutical market through macro-costing based on patient maps, with oversight from a clinical specialist.

For the micro-costing approach, data were collected through a form that listed the expenses for doctor visits, including specialists in gastroenterology, psychiatry and nutrition, as well as costs for routine and specialised tests for HCV22 and various radiology services. The medication therapy included GLE/PIB, SOF/DCV and SOF/VEL, as well as nursing care and hospitalisation costs (online supplemental appendix).

Cost calculations were based on the Ministry of Health’s approved tariffs for 2024 in rials, which were then converted to US dollars using the PPP rate, with $1 equating to 64 529 IRR.31 Price data for SOF/VEL and SOF/DCV were sourced from the National Formulary of Iran,32 whereas the costs of GLE/PIB were derived from retail prices in Turkey, including the mandated profit margin.

Dosing

Based on verified international guidelines and clinical specialist opinion, GLE/PIB with a dose of 300 mg/60 mg, as an oral tablet with an average medication duration of 60 days, is only prescribed for the treatment of hepatitis C if it is introduced to Iran’s drug market. In this model, this drug was assumed to replace SOF/DCV (400/60 mg) and SOF/VEL (400/100 mg) as the most cost-effective intervention for the treatment of hepatitis C in Iran. Therefore, the cost of GLE/PIB was evaluated in comparison with those of SOF/DCV and SOF/VEL as the currently available alternatives.

Perspective

In this study, the budget impact analysis was conducted from the perspective of the Iranian Ministry of Health. The current model was developed and evaluated from two perspectives: the impact of new drug introduction on Iran’s pharmaceutical market (including medication costs) and the entire health system (including direct medical costs). The methodology for the calculations performed in this study is provided in the attached Excel file.

Scenarios

First scenario: GLE/PIB is introduced to Iran’s drug list, and patients with hepatitis C are treated by all three therapeutic regimens based on the estimated market share in the coming years.

Second scenario: GLE/PIB is not introduced to Iran’s drug list, and patients are treated using the currently available medication options.

Sensitivity analysis

To assess the uncertainty surrounding the model parameters, a combination of one-way sensitivity analysis, price sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) was employed. In the one-way sensitivity analysis, key parameters were varied by ±20% for costs and ±10% for transition probabilities, with the results presented in tornado diagrams. Additionally, a probabilistic sensitivity analysis using 5000 Monte Carlo simulations was conducted, incorporating appropriate distributions for input parameters to estimate confidence intervals for budget impact outcomes across the three treatment regimens (GLE/PIB, SOF/VEL and SOF/DCV).

Patient and public involvement

None.

Sensitivity analysis

The results of the one-way sensitivity analysis and Tornado diagrams (figures3 4) indicate that the model outcomes are sensitive to changes in the cost associated with the CHC state for all three medications. Additionally, fluctuations in the prices of the drugs also affect the CHC state costs.

Tornado diagram: comparison of the incremental cost between GLE/PIB vs SOF/DCV. CHC, chronic hepatitis C; GLE/PIB, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir; SOF/DCV, sofosbuvir/daclatasvir

Tornado diagram: comparison of the incremental cost between GLE/PIB vs SOF/VEL. CHC, chronic hepatitis C; GLE/PIB, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir; SOF/VEL, sofosbuvir/velpatasvir

The price sensitivity analysis demonstrated that a simultaneous 20% increase or decrease in the price of all three medications does not affect the percentage of budget changes in the pharmaceutical market. Consequently, the introduction of a new drug is projected to result in a 468% increase in the pharmaceutical market budget over the next 5 years.

Regarding total health sector costs, a 20% reduction in CHC state costs over 5 years would decrease the overall increase in health sector costs from $13.51 million (an 18.84% increase) to $10.52 million (an 18.16% increase). Conversely, a 20% rise in CHC state costs would increase the health sector costs from $13.51 million (an 18.84% increase) to $16.49 million (a 19.31% increase).

Additionally, PSA was conducted using 5000 Monte Carlo simulations to account for uncertainty in the input parameters. As summarised in Appendix Table 19, the estimated mean annual treatment costs are $5461 for SOF/VEL, $7500 for GLE/PIB and $5465 for SOF/DCV. The associated 95% CIs were narrow: $5438–$5483, $7484–$7515 and $5453–$5478, respectively, indicating consistent cost estimates under parameter variability.

Discussion

HCV infection22 continues to be a significant public health concern in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA), particularly in low-income and middle-income countries, which bear a higher burden than high-income nations.3335 In 2015, the overall prevalence rate in MENA was 0.64%, translating to approximately 2.0 million viremic infections. However, prevalence rates vary considerably by country, ranging from 0.2% in Iran, Iraq and Lebanon to 3.0% in Syria.36 37

In Iran, the overall prevalence is lower than the global average, yet specific high-risk groups, such as individuals who use injectable drugs, present critical public health challenges. Limited access to diagnostics and antiviral treatments—due to high costs and inadequate healthcare infrastructure—complicates efforts to control hepatitis C in developing countries.1 7 33 Since 1997, screening donated blood for hepatitis C has helped reduce the number of cases, and providing free treatment for diagnosed patients has improved overall management. Nonetheless, the effectiveness of screening and testing programmes remains crucial for increasing access to treatment in various communities across the region. This highlights the urgent need for enhanced healthcare policies that address the specific needs of this population.7 This study’s findings provide practical insights into the costs of effective drug treatment and its implications for healthcare budgets in Iran, ultimately contributing to efforts to control and eliminate hepatitis C.

Currently, in Iran, two primary medications are available for the management of patients with hepatitis C: SOF/DVC and SOF/VEL. According to the drug sale statistics from Iran’s ‘Amarnameh’,21 the economic implications of hepatitis C treatment are significant for households, with the sales of these two medications reaching $31 102 295 in 2021, followed by $22 718 468 in 2022 and a slight increase to $23 803 251 in 2023. In addition to these options, GLE/PIB is considered a particularly promising treatment for hepatitis C. Clinical evidence suggests that GLE/PIB shows superior efficacy to both SOF/VEL and SOF/DVC, making it a compelling choice for patients. Furthermore, GLE/PIB is recognised for its cost-effectiveness, presenting a valuable intervention in the management of hepatitis C. With its added benefits, GLE/PIB can significantly penetrate the hepatitis C market, potentially capturing 50% of the market share within the next 5 years. Therefore, given the presence of a population at risk of hepatitis C and the availability of effective treatment options for managing these patients, the need to focus on planning and insurance coverage for these costly drug therapies, to reduce household expenses, is more evident than ever.

Budget impact analysis is a crucial tool for policymakers and health planners to assess the costs and financial implications of introducing new treatments or modifying treatment policies. In Iran’s healthcare system, where Iran’s Ministry of Health has adopted policies to provide free management for patients, the importance of using this tool for informed decision-making and allocating necessary budgets has become more significant than ever. The adoption of policies for managing patients with hepatitis C, such as free treatment policies for this population, underscores the need for precise financial planning. By conducting budget impact analysis, policymakers can better understand the short-term and long-term economic consequences of these initiatives. This tool enables them to assess whether the allocated resources are sufficient to cover the costs of widespread treatment and identify potential gaps in funding.

In this study, the budgetary changes within the healthcare system and the costs of the pharmaceutical market were analysed based on the direct expenses related to treating a single patient. This analysis is based on standard management practices for hepatitis C within Iran’s health system, along with the total number of patients diagnosed with hepatitis C. The study findings show that the first scenario, entry of GLE/PIB into Iran’s drug list and health system, considering the high cost of new medication, increases the total cost of Iran’s pharmaceutical market and health system, compared with the second scenario, non-entry of GLE/PIB into Iran’s drug list and management of patients with available options. By considering the direct costs of treatment, including medication costs, office visits, laboratory services, radiology examinations, diagnostic tests, hospitalisation and other direct medical costs, with the introduction of GLE/PIB and assuming that new medication will reach 10%, 20%, 30%, 40% and 50% market share from first to fifth year, the healthcare system costs will increase by approximately $0.61, $1.77, $3.86, $7.45 and $13.5 million over the next 5 years, respectively. However, a study conducted in Malaysia in 2023 found that the introduction of SOF/VEL for the treatment of hepatitis C resulted in $1.4 million, equivalent to a 0.57% decrease in cumulative total healthcare costs over 5 years, compared with SOF/DCV. It was mainly due to the shorter treatment course of SOF/VEL compared with SOF/DCV.18 The increased treatment costs for GLE/PIB in Iran, compared with the cost savings from SOF/VEL in Malaysia, can likely be attributed to the fact that GLE/PIB is five times more expensive than SOF/VEL and 13 times more expensive than SOF/DCV. This high cost is primarily due to the availability of domestically produced alternatives for the other medications.

Additionally, based on the selling prices of all three medication options, GLE/PIB, SOF/VEL and SOF/DCV, there will be a 468% rise in Iran’s pharmaceutical market costs, which is equivalent to a 0.13% increase in the total market costs. Additionally, with the assumed 50% market share on the introduction of GLE/PIB into Iran’s healthcare system, there will be a need for 65 602 GLE/PIB tablets in the fifth year. This result aligns with a 2015 study conducted in the United States that examined the budget impact of sofosbuvir/ledipasvir for treating hepatitis C. The findings indicate that the introduction of sofosbuvir/ledipasvir could lead to a $65 billion increase in medication costs compared with the standard of care. However, this approach would only provide a cost offset of $16 billion, which accounts for just 24% of the additional treatment expenses. This suggests that while the new treatment option may be cost-effective for patients, the overall increase in medication costs outweighs the reductions in other treatment expenses, resulting in a greater economic burden on the healthcare system.9

Furthermore, based on the gap between the number of patients with hepatitis C and the number of patients who are receiving treatment in Iran, there is an urgent need for measures and education to identify and treat patients with hepatitis C to eradicate this disease. According to the drug sale statistics from Iran’s ‘Amarnameh’ in 2023, SOF/DCV holds the most significant proportion of the hepatitis C market with an 81% share; however, the SOF/VEL therapeutic regimen is a more cost-effective option.13 Therefore, it is suggested to increase the SOF/VEL sales share for the treatment of hepatitis C. This would not only reduce the total treatment costs of patients but also provide a balanced consumption and optimised decision-making for physicians in the treatment of hepatitis C, which, in this sense, is consistent with the findings of the research conducted 18 18 in Malaysia.

The results of the sensitivity analysis indicate that the model is significantly affected by the changes in CHC state costs. A 20% reduction in CHC state costs would result in a decrease in the projected rise in health sector costs over 5 years, lowering the projected cost from $13.51 million (an 18.84% increase) to $10.52 million (an 18.16% increase). In contrast, if CHC state costs were to increase by 20%, the rise in health sector costs would soar from $13.51 million (an 18.84% increase) to $16.49 million (a 19.31% increase). As the model includes only costs and transition probabilities, PSA focused on these parameters. The narrow CIs around annual treatment costs indicate stable and reliable results despite input uncertainties, reinforcing the robustness of the budget impact estimates even without clinical effectiveness data.

A key limitation of this study is the unavailability of precise, domestically sourced pricing for GLE/PIB, which necessitated the use of estimated retail prices from Turkey. This approximation may affect the accuracy of our cost projections and is a major source of uncertainty. Furthermore, because of data scarcity, our analysis did not account for the costs associated with medication side effects. Consequently, the overall treatment costs may be underestimated, which is a significant consideration for a comprehensive budgetary assessment. In addition, some transition probabilities were derived from international studies rather than local data, which could affect the generalisability of our findings to the specific Iranian context. Finally, although our model’s exclusive focus on costs and transition probabilities limited the analysis to a budget impact perspective, a PSA was conducted to mitigate the effects of these uncertainties, thus supporting the robustness of our estimates.

Conclusions

Treating HCV is expensive, posing significant challenges for disease control and elimination efforts. This study has shed light on the costs associated with current and new treatments, as well as their implications for Iran’s healthcare budget.

With GLE/PIB entering Iran’s pharmaceutical market, patients now have access to a more effective treatment option for hepatitis C. However, the high price of this new medication compared with existing options highlights the urgent need for insurance coverage and appropriate resource allocation. This would help alleviate the financial burden on individuals and households facing treatment costs. Policymakers in Iran’s health sector must pay special attention to this issue. Furthermore, regulating the market for currently available treatment options can help ensure that patients with hepatitis C have access to the most optimised medication choices.

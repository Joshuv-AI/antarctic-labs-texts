Complaint trends of dental malpractice in Tehran, Iran: a retrospective study (2016—2022)
Authors: Reza Tabrizi, Hossein Taheri, Mehdi Forouzesh, Sanaz Azizi, Yasaman Bathaei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to evaluate the frequency and causes of dental malpractice complaints filed with the Forensic Medical Commission (FMC) of Tehran Province from 2016 to 2022. A retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted using 450 case files selected by simple random sampling technique. Data on plaintiffs’ and defendants’ age, gender, and education level, type of treatment, time between treatment and complaint, commission verdict, defendant specialty, and treatment location were extracted and analyzed using the Chi-square test, forward stepwise multiple logistic regression, and Joinpoint regression, performed in IBM SPSS Statistics version 26. (α = 0.05). A total of 450 dental malpractice complaints were reviewed, all of which met the inclusion criteria for final analysis. The majority of defendants (70.7%) were males aged 46 to 55 years, while 57.6% of plaintiffs were females. Among defendants, 82.7% were general dentists, and 12.9% were non-dentist operators.The annual distribution of complaints was 45 in 2016, 47 in 2017, 54 in 2018, 71 in 2019, 54 in 2020, 81 in 2021, and 98 in 2022. Dental clinicians were found guilty in 61.3% of cases (n = 276). The most frequent complaints were related to prosthetic treatments (20.4%), dental implants (17.8%), and extractions (14.4%). The highest malpractice rates were observed in endodontic and prosthetic crown treatments (85.2%), followed by endodontic (66.7%), implant (66.3%), aesthetic (66.1%), and fixed prosthetic (62.8%) procedures. Malpractice was confirmed in 64.5% of complaints by females and 57.1% by males. From 2016 to 2022, dental malpractice complaints in Tehran Province showed a clear upward trend, with defendants held liable in 61.3% of cases, most commonly involving prosthetic treatments, implants, and extractions. Non‑dentist operators faced higher odds of conviction than generalists or specialists, while general dentists accounted for the majority of complaints. Female plaintiffs were more likely to have confirmed malpractice than males, and combined endodontic‑crown procedures posed the highest risk. These results highlight the urgent need for targeted training and tighter regulation to enhance patient safety and curb litigation.

Introduction

Dental malpractice remains a critical concern in clinical dentistry, with an escalating incidence of litigation reflecting heightened patient awareness of legal rights (Ozdemir et al. 2005). Despite significant advances in diagnostic modalities and treatment planning, the prevalence of procedural errors persists at an appreciable level (Hapcook 2006; Harris 2004). Longitudinal and cross‑national analyses consistently demonstrate an upward trajectory in malpractice complaints, underscoring the imperative for dental practitioners to maintain strict adherence to established standards of care (Alsaeed et al. 2022). In a 2022 epidemiological study conducted in Saudi Arabia, non‑Saudi clinicians accounted for 93% of reported malpractice claims, predominantly within the domains of prosthodontics and endodontics (Alsaeed et al. 2022). Such data underscore the global relevance of this issue. Parallel findings emerged from southern Iran, where an 11‑year retrospective review of 64 cases revealed that fixed prosthodontic and oral surgical procedures (primarily performed by general dentists in private practice) constituted the majority of complaints; adjudication determined practitioner liability in 56.7% of clinical and 40% of non‑clinical claims (Hashemipour et al. 2013).

In a recent cross‑sectional study, the majority of complaints were filed against general dentists (particularly males) with surgical, endodontic, and prosthetic services most frequently cited as grounds for malpractice claims (Sabaghzadegan et al. 2024).

The etiology of malpractice claims is multifactorial, with diagnostic inaccuracies, suboptimal treatment execution, and deficient patient management (often attributable to practitioner negligence or insufficient expertise) being predominant contributors (Blau and Levin 2017; Charatan 1999; Tahmasebi et al. 2023; Zardak et al. 2025). Specialties like prosthodontics and oral surgery tend to have higher complaint rates, particularly in private practices. These issues are further compounded when practitioners fail to provide informed consent or maintain proper documentation, both of which are critical in avoiding legal repercussions (Ji et al. 2020; Nalliah 2017).

In Iran, the trend is similarly alarming. Data from Tehran Province reveal a similarly disconcerting trend: an increasing volume of complaints, chiefly against general dentists operating in private clinics (Jafarian et al. 2009; Kashavarz and Emsha Sahand 2019). Given the increasing frequency of dental malpractice complaints in Tehran Province (Hashemipour et al. 2013), this study was designed to elucidate the underlying factors driving this rise. the present study analyzes seven years of case records from the Forensic Medical Commission (FMC), aiming to characterize the predominant complaint types and identify key contributory elements.

Materials and methods

This retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted on cases of dental complaints filed with the FMC of Tehran Province from 2016 to 2022. The Department of Forensic Medical Commissions is one of the main authorities responsible for handling medical and dental complaints. Most plaintiffs who approach any of the available legal or professional bodies are eventually referred to this department for investigation, commission hearings, and court proceedings. This department maintains a comprehensive and well-organized archive of case files, which served as the source from which we extracted our study data. The study protocol was approved by the university ethics committee (IR.SBMU.DRC.REC.1402.017).

Sample size

Following the methodology of previously published studies (Perea-Pérez et al. 2014), we determined a minimum sample size of 45 case files per year. These case files were randomly selected from those available each year. As a result, a total of 450 case files were chosen over the 7-year period.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria were case files of dental complaints filed with the FMC of Tehran Province from 2016 to 2022 with a final verdict. The exclusion criteria were case files related to orthognathic surgeries, rhinoplasty, or surgical procedures of other facial components. A total of 450 case files were selected by simple random sampling method, in which a unique number was assigned to each case file, and a minimum of 45 cases were selected by generating a random list of numbers corresponding to the minimum sample size. It should be noted that the information of plaintiffs (patients) and defendants (dentists) on the front cover of case files were anonymized to reduce the risk of selection bias. Anonymization or blinding was performed by an FMC agent, while selection of case files was performed by a dentistry student and validated by a supervising specialist.

Data collection

After obtaining approval and the necessary permissions from the Medical Council Organization of Iran to access the archived files in the FMC of Tehran Province, eligible case files were selected every year, and 450 eligible files were selected by random sampling. Age and gender of plaintiffs and defendants, level of education of plaintiffs, type of treatment performed, time interval between treatment and filing a complaint, final verdict of the commission, specialty of the defendant, and location of treatment were extracted from the case files and recorded in a redesigned checklist. Malpractice rates were determined based on proportion of case files in which the defendant was found guilty.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26 (BM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Armonk, NY: IBM Corp., USA). Descriptive statistics (frequencies, percentages, means ± SD) were calculated to summarize patient demographics and complaint characteristics. The multiple logistic regression-forward method was used to analyze the effect of independent variables on the final verdict. The effect of independent variables such as gender, age, specialty, and treatment location on the dichotomous malpractice variable (confirmed/not confirmed) was assessed, and odds ratios (ORs) were calculated and reported. This method was chosen because it models the probability of a binary outcome while controlling for multiple covariates. Chi-squared test was used for comparing differences between malpractice and exoneration rates across different age groups of plaintiffs. Finally, Joinpoint regression analysis was carried out using the Joinpoint Regression Program (National Cancer Institute, USA) to detect significant changes in annual complaint trends and calculate the annual percent change. An alpha level of 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all tests.

Logistic regression analysis

Multiple logistic regression analysis (Table 5) showed that female patients had 1.7 times higher odds of experiencing malpractice compared to males (OR = 1.64, P = 0.032). Non-dentist operators had significantly higher odds of malpractice than general dentists (OR = 4.27, P < 0.001) and dental specialists (OR = 16.7, P < 0.001), with a mean guilt percentage of 4.36%, higher than that of general dentists (2.97%) and specialists (2.58%) (P = 0.002). Endodontic treatments with prosthetic crowns had the highest mean malpractice odds ratio (9.297), which was statistically significant (P < 0.001). Additionally, implant treatments were also found have a significant odds ratio of 4.018 (P = 0.012). While the gender of the defendant and the time interval between treatment and filing a complaint were not significantly associated with the percentage of malpractice, each additional month between treatment and complaint filing increased the odds of dentist exoneration (OR = 0.98, P = 0.026). Discussion

Over the seven-year span examined, we observed a steady increase in dental complaints submitted to the FMC in Tehran Province. Most complainants were women in their thirties, particularly between ages 31 and 40. Interestingly, general practitioners faced far more complaints than specialists, with prosthodontic procedures topping the list of claims, implant and endodontic treatments followed closely behind. Alarmingly, a large share of these malpractice cases involved operators without formal dental credentials. The study highlights a significant issue in the dental field, where unrealistic patient expectations, fueled by misleading information on social media, contribute to dissatisfaction and legal actions against dental practitioners (Haraji et al. 2022; Khademi 2019; Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009; Perea-Pérez et al. 2014; Shahsavari F et al. 2010; Zamanzadeh et al. 2022).

The increasing trend in dental complaints aligns with prior studies conducted in Iran, likely reflects both greater public awareness of patient rights and easier internet access to health information (Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009; Zamanzadeh et al. 2022). At the same time, an expanding dental workforce and an increase in the volume of procedures naturally elevate the risk of errors and subsequent claims (Eser Misir et al. 2025; Shahsavari F et al. 2010; Zamanzadeh et al. 2022). Our analysis revealed a pronounced drop in complaints in 2020, likely driven by the COVID‑19 pandemic’s disruption of routine dental services, temporary court closures, and a substantial decrease in patient visits to clinics during that period (Ahmadi et al. 2020; Farrokhi et al. 2024; Tahani et al. 2023). Farrokhi et al. (2024) emphasize that the pandemic significantly reduced access to dental care, contributing to fewer reported complaints (Ahmadinejad et al. 2024; Farrokhi et al. 2024). Interestingly, this contrasts with Italian data reported by Manca et al., who documented a decrease in dental claims, an outcome that may stem from differences in healthcare systems, patient populations, and regional practices (Manca et al. 2018). The predominance of complainants aged 31–40 likely reflects a digitally savvy generation that not only has greater access to health information but is also more empowered to advocate for their rights, thereby contributing to the observed increase in formal grievances (Haraji et al. 2022). Scenario‑based projections further underscore the urgent need for targeted preventive interventions (such as enhanced informed‑consent protocols and quality‑control training) to curb future complaint rates (Mehrolhassani et al. 2024).

Gender-specific patterns emerged in the complaint data, with women lodging more dental claim than men, a trend corroborated by previous research (Hamasaki and Hagihara 2021; Hedjazi et al. 2013; Movahhed et al. 2015). This trend could be attributed to women’s greater sensitivity to dental health and treatment quality compared to men (Akhoundi and Kharrazi Fard 2008). In contrast, studies focused on surgical procedures have sometimes found a predominance of male plaintiffs, suggesting that the nature of the dental intervention influences gender‐based complaint rates (Jafarian et al. 2009). Moreover, cross‐national comparisons reveal considerable variation in complaint patterns, underlining the role of cultural norms, socioeconomic conditions, and legal frameworks in shaping patient recourse behaviors (Gupta et al. 2014; Hashemipour et al. 2013; Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009; Manca et al. 2018; Perea-Pérez et al. 2014).

The analysis revealed that general practitioners were significantly more likely to face complaints than dental specialists (P < 0.001). This could be due to the broader range of procedures performed by general dentists and their shorter educational curricula (Haraji et al. 2022; Shahsavari et al. 2010). Even more troubling was the rising proportion of cases attributed to non‑dentist operators, an indicator of gaps in regulatory enforcement and clinical oversight in certain regions (Haraji et al. 2022; Hedjazi et al. 2013; Khademi 2019; Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009). Indeed, malpractice rates among non‑dentist providers far exceeded those of both generalists and specialists, underscoring an urgent need to bolster supervision, standardize qualifications, and tighten licensing controls (Haraji et al. 2022; Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009).

Prosthodontic treatments (both removable and fixed) were the most frequently cited in complaints (20.4%), a pattern echoed in prior research (Kiani and Sheikhazadi 2009; Manca et al. 2018; Shahsavari et al. 2010; Zamanzadeh et al. 2022). Implant therapy—still relatively new and technically demanding—also generated many complaints, as less‑experienced clinicians are more susceptible to errors (Manca et al. 2018; Perea-Pérez et al. 2014). We found that the length of time between treatment and the complaint’s submission plays a pivotal role: the longer this interval, the greater the chance the dentist is exonerated, likely because later‑arising issues may stem from factors unrelated to the original intervention (Hamasaki and Hagihara 2021; Hedjazi et al. 2013). Finally, malpractice was confirmed in a notable share of cases, especially in complex, combined treatments such as endodontic work followed by crown placement. These high‑complexity interventions often result in postoperative pain or dissatisfaction, which in turn prompts patients to pursue formal complaintsfile complaints (Shahsavari et al. 2010).

This investigation offers a robust, longitudinal evaluation of dental malpractice claims, encompassing a diverse array of procedures and provider types over a seven‑year interval. Its strengths lie in the systematic sampling of FMC records and the stratification of complaints by treatment category and operator qualification. Nevertheless, the absence of a fully computerized archiving system and occasional gaps in case documentation may have introduced data incompleteness. To build on these findings, future studies should implement digital record‑keeping to ensure comprehensive data capture and conduct specialty‑specific analyses of patient claims. Such targeted research could illuminate distinct drivers of dissatisfaction across dental disciplines, ultimately guiding interventions to enhance clinical quality and minimize complaint rates. The integration of teledentistry platforms for follow-up care and patient monitoring could also address some post-treatment complications before they escalate to formal complaints (Hung et al. 2022; Torres et al. 2023).

Conclusion

Over the 2016–2022 period, dental malpractice complaints in Tehran Province showing an increasing trend. Defendants were judged liable in 61.3% of complaints, with prosthetic treatments (both fixed and removable) (20.4%), dental implants (17.8%), and extractions (14.4%) most frequently implicated. The likelihood of conviction and malpractice occurrence in cases involving non‑dentist operators was higher than in other cases, while the total number of complaints against general dentists was the greatest overall. Female plaintiffs had 1.64‑times greater odds of confirmed malpractice than males, while endodontic procedures combined with crown placement carried the greatest risk and implant treatments also showed elevated odds. These findings underscore the need for enhanced training and stricter regulation (particularly in high‑risk procedures and among non‑dental practitioners) to improve patient safety and reduce litigation.

Suggestions for future research

Future research should compare dental malpractice complaint trends across Iranian cities to identify regional disparities and systemic influences.In-depth qualitative interviews with patients and practitioners can reveal root causes of dissatisfaction, barriers to filing complaints, and perceptions of care quality. Prospective cohort studies are needed to track the legal and clinical outcomes of complaints over time and assess their long‑term impact on clinician practices and patient safety initiatives.

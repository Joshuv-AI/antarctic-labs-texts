Analysis of medical care seeking delay in pulmonary tuberculosis patients and influencing factors in Chongqing, China
Authors: Li Tang, Wanyun Tang, Ruifang Chen, Guiling Xiang, Guihua Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDue to various reasons, delayed medical treatment for tuberculosis patients is a common phenomenon. It is particularly necessary to explore the influencing factors of the delay in medical treatment of pathogenic-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients and take targeted solutions to control tuberculosis.MethodsIn the present study, data on pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing from 2019 to 2024 were extracted from the China Disease Prevention and Control Information System. Epidemiological characteristics and delayed medical consultation were first analyzed descriptively, followed by the application of a binary logistic regression model to identify the factors associated with delayed medical consultation in this population.ResultsFrom 2019 to 2024, 25,438 cases of pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis were reported by Chongqing Public Health Medical Center, with a gender ratio of 2.405:1. 20,640 cases were delayed in seeking medical treatment, with a delay rate of 81.14%. The analysis of the influencing factors of the delayed visit of pathogenic-positive pulmonary tuberculosis showed that the registered residence type and occupation were the influencing factors of the delayed visit of patients (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe delay in seeking medical treatment for pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing is still common and unevenly distributed. It is necessary to strengthen early diagnosis and treatment, improve patients’ awareness of prevention and treatment, and reduce the delay rate in seeking medical treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-026-27170-1.Keywords: Pulmonary tuberculosis, Delay in seeking medical treatment, Influencing factors, Registered residence type, Occupation

Introduction

Tuberculosis is a common chronic infectious disease caused by mycobacterium tuberculosis. In view of the air transmission characteristics of tuberculosis, anyone may be infected, and people in areas with high incidence rate are more vulnerable to infection [1]. The World Health Organization(WHO) released the Global tuberculosis Report on October 17, 2019, and all United Nations Member States committed to ending the global tuberculosis epidemic by 2030 [2]. According to the WHO “Global Tuberculosis Report 2025”, the global trend of pulmonary tuberculosis is currently in a slow decline phase. The estimated number of new cases in 2024 was approximately 10.7 million, with around 1.23 million deaths, resulting in an incidence rate of approximately 131 per 100,000 [3]. Globally, 87% of cases were concentrated in 30 countries, with eight nations—India (25%), Indonesia (10%), the Philippines (6.8%), China (6.5%), and others—accounting for two-thirds of the total global cases [3]. With a vast territory and a large population, China is a big country with tuberculosis [4]. How to better control tuberculosis is extremely important not only for China, but also for global human health. There are literature reports that the lung function of pulmonary tuberculosis rehabilitation patients is significantly reduced, and FEV1 is more affected than FVC, presenting a mixed obstruction and restrictive situation, mainly due to airflow obstruction [5]. Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment of tuberculosis patients is crucial for long-term prognosis. One of the important measures to control tuberculosis is to reduce the delay rate of patients with pathogenic pulmonary tuberculosis.

This study takes the delay in seeking medical treatment of pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing as the starting point, and analyzes in detail the factors affecting the delay in seeking medical treatment of pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients, providing a scientific basis for better prevention and control of pulmonary tuberculosis in Chongqing.

Materials and methods

Material

The medical record data of pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients registered in Chongqing from 2019 to 2024 were exported from the China Disease Prevention and Control Information System, including age, gender, occupation, complications, treatment classification, registered residence address, current address, symptom occurrence date, first visit date, registration date, etc.

Related definitions

(1) Delay in seeking medical treatment: The time interval between the onset of symptoms and the first visit to a medical institution for tuberculosis patients is greater than 14 days, and this data is obtained through the physician’s initial consultation with the patient. (2) Population: This study included 40 districts (counties) in Chongqing and people from other provinces who came to Chongqing as research subjects.

Statistical analysis

Data organization was conducted using Excel 2010 software, and statistical analysis of the data was performed with SPSS 22.0 software. All data were subjected to a normality distribution test. Count data conforming to a normal distribution were expressed as cases or percentages. The trend of the delay rate over time was analyzed via the trend χ² test. Univariate analysis was carried out using χ² test, and a binary logistic regression analysis model was employed to explore influencing factors. The test level was set at α = 0.05.

Result

Overall situation

From 2019 to 2024, Chongqing Public Health Medical Center admitted 25,438 patients with pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis, of which 20,640 were delayed in seeking medical treatment, with the delay rate of 81.14%. Among them, males are the main group, accounting for 70.63% (18023 cases), with the gender ratio of 2.405:1. The main occupations are household chores and unemployment, accounting for 45.38% (9366 cases); Local registered residence accounted for 80.45% (16604 cases); The age range is mainly between 45 and 65 years old, accounting for 39.84% (8223 cases) (Table 5). It did not show a significant downward or upward trend between 2019 and 2024 (χ2 trend = 0.808, P > 0.05) (Table 1).

Analysis of factors influencing delayed medical visits

Univariate analysis

Gender, age, registered residence address category, and occupation type were analyzed by single factor analysis. χ2 test was conducted between each factor, and the results showed that there was no statistically significant difference (P > 0.05) in the delay rate of seeking medical treatment among pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients of different genders. There was significant difference in the delay rate of medical treatment among the pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients of different age groups, registered residence address categories, and occupational types (P < 0.05) (Table 5).

Multivariate analysis

The factors mentioned above were included in a binary logistic regression analysis model, with the occurrence of delayed medical visits in patients with pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis as the dependent variable. The results showed that compared with local and worker patients, patients from other provinces (OR = 1.144, 95%CI: 1.053 ~ 1.243), household chores and unemployment (OR = 1.395, 95%CI: 1.107 ~ 1.758), farmers (OR = 1.439, 95%CI: 1.139 ~ 1.817), and the catering and food industry (OR = 1.706, 95%CI: 1.005 ~ 2.893) were risk factors for delayed diagnosis (P < 0.05) (Table 5).

Discussion

We collected data from pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients with a time interval of more than 14 days from the date of first symptom onset to the first visit to a medical institution. These data were obtained through physician consultations during the first patient visit. This mainly reflects the delay in patient behavior, rather than directly evaluating the efficiency of the healthcare system. This study found that from 2019 to 2024, the delay rate of seeking medical treatment for pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing, located in the western region, was 81.14%. There are significant differences in the delay time and delay rate of seeking medical treatment for tuberculosis patients in eastern and central China, which may be related to the medical service conditions, economic income levels, and incidence levels in different regions [6, 7]. For example, the capital of China, Beijing, has a lower delay rate in seeking medical treatment compared to many other regions [8]. COVID-19 has profoundly affected the global tuberculosis detection rate. India, Indonesia, the Philippines and China accounted for 93% of the global decline in tuberculosis case detection [9]. In 2020 and 2021, the delay rate of seeing a doctor for pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing increased significantly, which may be related to the prevention and control measures such as home isolation caused by COVID-19 at that time.

In terms of population characteristics, among pathogen-positive tuberculosis patients, the gender is male, and they are migrant population from other provinces. Their occupations include household chores, unemployment, farmers, and employees in the catering and food industry, with the majority aged between 45 and 65. Univariate and multivariate analysis showed that patients with pathogen-positive results from other provinces, household chores and unemployment, farmers, and the catering and food industry have a high risk of delayed medical treatment. This is similar to the statistical analysis results reported in many regions of China in the literature [10–12]. There may be multiple reasons for the above situation. People from other provinces may have high mobility. Unemployed individuals, farmers, and other groups often have poor economic conditions and find it difficult to afford medical expenses, or lack medical insurance coverage, or have a lower level of education and lack understanding of policies. Additionally, due to the limited number of medical institutions in rural areas and inconvenient transportation, their willingness to seek medical treatment is lower [6, 13]. In addition, medical practitioners lack health education in rural areas, which may result in patients not understanding the infectivity and severity of tuberculosis and failing to seek medical attention in a timely manner [14, 15]. Domestic workers (usually predominantly women) neglect their own health while taking care of their families, or attribute some symptoms of tuberculosis to daily fatigue [16]. However, considering the OR and confidence interval widths from the multivariate analysis in this study, delayed medical treatment among pulmonary tuberculosis patients with pathogen-positive was significantly associated with being from other provinces, occupations such as household, unemployment, farming, and the catering and food industry, but a definitive causal relationship could not be established.

There are literature reports that elderly local and young mobile tuberculosis patients living far from the city center are the most vulnerable groups to delayed medical treatment [17]. In this study, the delay in seeking medical treatment among patients under the age of 25 showed an upward trend first and then a downward trend, with the highest delay rate occurring in 2021. This consideration may be related to the COVID-19 epidemic at that time [18]. After the release of home quarantine, young people have stronger health awareness than the elderly, resulting in a significant decrease in the delay rate of medical treatment. In terms of regional distribution characteristics, the high delay rate of medical treatment in Shapingba District may be related to the fact that the Chongqing Public Health Medical Center is located in Shapingba and patients seek medical treatment nearby.

This study mainly explores the delay in seeking medical treatment for tuberculosis in a region - Chongqing. This study focuses on the status quo and influencing factors of the delay in seeking medical care of pulmonary tuberculosis patients in the region, aiming to analyze the time interval and key constraints between the onset of symptoms and the first visit of patients, which has important theoretical value and practical significance for improving the epidemiological prevention and control system of tuberculosis. In terms of epidemiological theory, through quantitative analysis of the distribution characteristics and risk factors of regional delay in seeking medical treatment, we can enrich the research on the dynamics of tuberculosis transmission, clarify the relationship between delay in seeking medical treatment and disease transmission, provide some scientific basis for the assessment of regional tuberculosis epidemic law, and help improve the tuberculosis risk assessment index system.

This study has certain limitations. The collected data is only a retrospective systematic report, and the source of the data is reliable. However, there is no information on the educational level or awareness of core knowledge of pulmonary tuberculosis among patients with pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis, which cannot reflect the impact of other possible factors on the delay in seeking medical treatment for patients with pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis. In addition, there is only data available for Chongqing city in terms of region, and due to the geographical location of the hospital, it cannot represent the entire situation of delayed medical treatment for patients with pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis. We acknowledge that small-sample subgroup analyses can cause statistical instability. We should avoid interpreting these results as robust or universally significant risk factors. The trend χ² test and regional comparison used in this study have their limited explanatory scope. We cannot extend it to robust inference of temporal variations or regional differences. Because these analytical methods have their limitations. The trend χ² test is used to determine whether the response variable shows a monotonically increasing or decreasing trend with changes in the ordered grouping (level). Through analysis, it can determine whether there is a linear trend or only chaotic fluctuations in the changes of the dependent variable. Therefore, the core limitation of trend χ² test is that it only detects linear trends. If the true relationship is U-shaped, J-shaped, or a curve that falls first and then rises, the analysis results may show no significant trend (P < 0.05), leading to the mistaken belief that there is no relationship between variables. In addition, the limitation of trend χ² test is that it highly relies on grouping order, ignores intra group details, is sensitive to extreme values or sparse data, can only judge monotonicity, cannot determine inflection points, and has weak ability to control confounding factors. Regional comparison can only tell us whether there are overall differences between regions, but cannot point out specific differences. In addition, there are limitations to regional comparison, such as a lack of spatial proximity information, sensitivity to boundary delineation and scale, interference from confounding factors, ecological fallacies, and uneven sample distribution. Next, we will combine questionnaire surveys and strive to obtain more comprehensive basic information about patients.

Conclusion

In summary, the delay rate of seeking medical treatment for pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in Chongqing from 2019 to 2024 is 81.14%, which is at a relatively high level. The age group is mainly between 45 and 65 years old. Preliminary analysis of the delay in seeking medical treatment for pathogen-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in 40 districts (counties) of Chongqing suggests that the delay rates in Shapingba District and Hechuan District are on the rise, while the delay rates in Dadukou District and Fengjie County are on the decline. Personnel from other provinces, those who work as household chores or are unemployed, farmers, and those in the catering and food industry may be associated with delayed medical treatment for patients. We need to further improve the medical conditions of residents in remote and economically underdeveloped areas to reduce the delay rate of medical treatment and ensure the health and safety of the people.

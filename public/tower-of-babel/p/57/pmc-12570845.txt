Serum Kisspeptin levels in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis: a case-control study protocol
Authors: Bo Yang, Na Zheng, Qing Shao, Xuezi Fu, Dan Ye, Lijun Sun, Nanmei Liu
Published: 2025

Background

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) presents a multitude of systemic complications, among which uremic hypogonadism is a pervasive yet often under-addressed issue [1, 2]. Affecting a majority of patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD), it manifests with a wide range of symptoms including diminished libido, erectile dysfunction, amenorrhea, and infertility, all of which substantially degrade quality of life [3–6]. The endocrine profile of uremic hypogonadism is characterized by a paradoxical disruption of the Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal (HPG) axis, where low peripheral sex steroid levels often coexist with normal or elevated gonadotropin levels, suggesting a central defect in gonadotropin action or regulation [7].

The precise origin of this central dysregulation is a critical knowledge gap. In recent years, Kisspeptin, a neuropeptide product of the KISS1 gene, has been established as the quintessential gatekeeper of the HPG axis [8]. Kisspeptin neurons in the hypothalamus provide the obligatory excitatory input to Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) neurons, thereby controlling the entire downstream reproductive cascade [9]. Any alteration in Kisspeptin signaling can profoundly impact reproductive function [10, 11].

The kidneys are known to play a significant role in the clearance of circulating peptides [12, 13]. It is therefore highly plausible that in the uremic state of ESRD, Kisspeptin clearance is impaired, leading to its accumulation. Persistently elevated Kisspeptin levels could theoretically lead to desensitization of its receptor, GPR54, on GnRH neurons. This state of “Kisspeptin resistance” could explain the failure of elevated gonadotropins to drive gonadal function appropriately in MHD patients.

To our knowledge, few studies have explored Kisspeptin in renal disease, a comprehensive characterization in a well-defined MHD cohort, with detailed correlation to HPG axis hormones and clinical phenotypes, is lacking. This study protocol describes an observational case-control study designed to rigorously investigate the serum levels of Kisspeptin in MHD patients compared to healthy controls and to explore its clinical and hormonal correlates.

Methods and analyses

Study design and setting

This will be a single-center, observational, case-control study. Participants will be recruited from the dialysis center of Naval Medical Center of PLA in Shanghai, China. The study proposal was approved by the institutional review committee of the Naval Medical Center of PLA (registration number: AF-HEC-092). The study will be conducted in accordance with the local legislation, institutional requirements and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The research has been registered in chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2500106627) (Registration Date 2025, Jul, 28th).

Eligibility criteria

Participants will be enrolled if they meet the following criteria:

Inclusion criteria for cases (MHD group)

(1) Diagnosed with ESRD and receiving stable maintenance hemodialysis (3 sessions per week) for a duration of at least 3 months. (2) Age between 18 and 65 years. (3) Clinically stable without acute illness. (4) Willing and able to provide written informed consent.

Exclusion criteria for cases

(1) Presence of a known hypothalamic or pituitary disorder. (2) Active malignancy. (3) Pregnancy or lactation. (4) Current use of medications known to significantly interfere with the HPG axis, such as GnRH analogues, anti-estrogens, androgens, or chronic opioid therapy.

Inclusion criteria for controls (Healthy group)

(1) Individuals aged 18–65 years who are confirmed to be healthy following a clinical review and basic laboratory screening. (2) Normal kidney function, defined as an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) > 90 mL/min/1.73 m² and no proteinuria. (3) Willing and able to provide written informed consent.

Exclusion criteria for controls

(1) History of any chronic kidney disease, major endocrine disorders, cardiovascular disease, or liver disease. (2) Meeting any of the exclusion criteria listed for the case group.

Sample size

The sample size for the patient group was calculated to achieve adequate precision for estimating the mean Kisspeptin level. Based on an estimated standard deviation of 8.0 pmol/L and a desired precision (margin of error) of 2.0 pmol/L at a 95% confidence level, a minimum of 62 patients are required. To account for potential dropouts or invalid data, we will aim to recruit approximately 78 patients. A control group of 20 subjects will be recruited based on feasibility. A post-hoc power analysis will be performed. Given the large effect size anticipated between uremic and healthy individuals based on previous literature, this unbalanced design (n = 78 vs. n = 20) is expected to have over 90% statistical power to detect a significant difference in the primary outcome.

Study outcomes

Primary outcome

The difference in mean serum Kisspeptin-54 concentration between the MHD group and the control group.

Secondary outcomes

The correlation between serum Kisspeptin-54 levels and serum levels of LH, FSH, total testosterone (in men), estradiol (in women), and prolactin within the MHD group.

The correlation between serum Kisspeptin-54 levels and dialysis vintage in the MHD group.

The association between serum Kisspeptin-54 levels and clinical symptoms of hypogonadism, as assessed by a libido visual analog scale (VAS) and the International Index of Erectile Function-5 (IIEF-5) score in men.

The diagnostic utility of serum Kisspeptin-54 in identifying clinical hypogonadism within the MHD cohort, to be explored via Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

Participant timeline and study procedures (Fig. 1)

Schedule of enrolment and assessments

Participation in the study will involve a single visit for each individual. After obtaining written informed consent, enrolled participants will undergo the following procedures:

Questionnaire and data collection

A trained study coordinator will administer a questionnaire to collect demographic data (including Body Mass Index, BMI) and a detailed clinical history. For male participants, this will include the IIEF-5. For female participants, a detailed menstrual and reproductive history will be recorded, including menopausal status. For all participants, a libido VAS score will be obtained. Clinical data for MHD patients, including primary cause of ESRD, dialysis vintage, residual renal function (if available), and recent laboratory values (including iron metabolism markers, brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), pro-BNP, albumin, C-reactive protein, blood urea nitrogen, prealbumin, total cholesterol, hemoglobin, corrected calcium, phosphorus, and parathyroid hormone), will be extracted from their electronic medical records.

Blood sampling

A single blood sample (5 mL) will be collected. For MHD patients, the sample will be drawn from the arterial line of their dialysis circuit immediately before the initiation of a mid-week dialysis session to avoid additional venipuncture. For control participants, a standard venipuncture will be performed. All samples will be collected in the morning between 8:00 AM and 10:00 AM after an overnight fast. This applies to both healthy controls and MHD patients. MHD patients scheduled for afternoon or evening shifts will be asked to come in for the blood draw during the specified morning hours on the day of their scheduled treatment. For the premenopausal female participants who report a regular or irregular cycle, we will record the date of their last menstrual period. WHe will aim to schedule their blood draw in the early follicular phase (days 2–5). Samples will be processed within one hour of collection to separate serum, which will then be aliquoted and stored at -80 °C until analysis.

Laboratory analysis

Serum Kisspeptin-54 concentrations will be measured in duplicate using a commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit, following the manufacturer’s protocol. All samples from both cases and controls will be analyzed in the same run to minimize inter-assay variability. Serum levels of LH, FSH, total testosterone, and estradiol will be measured using a validated automated chemiluminescence immunoassay platform at the hospital’s accredited central laboratory.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis will be performed using SPSS Version 30.0.0 for MacOS (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All statistical tests will be two-tailed, and a P-value < 0.05 will be considered significant. Our primary approach for handling missing data will be a complete case analysis, as we anticipate a low rate of missing data for key variables.

The analysis plan is as follows:

Descriptive statistics

Continuous variables will be presented as mean ± SD or median (IQR) based on their distribution, assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages.

Baseline comparisons

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics between the MHD and control groups will be compared using independent samples t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous data, and Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical data.

Primary outcome analysis

To compare serum Kisspeptin levels between the two groups while adjusting for age and other potential baseline confounders, an Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) will be used. Kisspeptin values will be log-transformed if necessary to meet the normality assumption for the model. Adjusted means and 95% confidence intervals will be reported.

Secondary outcome analysis

Within the MHD group, Spearman’s rank correlation will be used to assess the monotonic relationship between Kisspeptin levels and other hormonal and clinical variables. Multivariable linear regression models will be constructed to identify independent predictors of Kisspeptin levels in this population.

ROC curve analysis will be performed to assess the diagnostic performance of Kisspeptin. For premenopausal female participants with preserved menstrual cycles, a sensitivity analysis will be conducted by excluding them from the primary analysis to ensure they do not unduly influence the results.

Data management

All data will be collected on standardized Case Report Forms (CRFs) and entered into a secure, password-protected electronic database. Data will be de-identified to protect participant confidentiality. Regular data quality checks will be performed.

Discussion

This protocol outlines a comprehensive, observational case-control study designed to address a critical knowledge gap in the pathophysiology of uremic hypogonadism. By providing a rigorous characterization of circulating Kisspeptin-54 in a well-defined cohort of MHD patients, this research will directly test the prevailing hypothesis of central Kisspeptin resistance. The successful completion of this study is anticipated to provide foundational data that could reshape our understanding of neuroendocrine dysregulation in end-stage renal disease (ESRD).

The methodological rigor of this protocol is a key strength. The inclusion of a concurrently-recruited healthy control group, even if smaller than the case group, is a major advantage over studies relying on historical or literature-based reference values, which are notoriously variable due to inter-assay differences for Kisspeptin. By using the same high-sensitivity ELISA kit and batch for all samples, we will ensure the internal validity of our comparisons. Furthermore, our study design incorporates detailed clinical phenotyping, including the use of validated questionnaires for sexual function (IIEF-5) and a libido VAS. This will allow us to link our biochemical findings to patient-relevant outcomes. Critically, our statistical plan prospectively identifies age as a major confounder and specifies the use of Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) for the primary analysis, ensuring that our main conclusions are appropriately adjusted and robust.

Despite these strengths, we anticipate several challenges and limitations. The primary limitation is the cross-sectional nature of the study design, which can establish association but not causality [14]. While we may find a link between high Kisspeptin levels and hypogonadism, we cannot determine the temporal sequence of these events. Secondly, as a single-center study, the generalizability of our findings may be limited by the specific demographic and clinical characteristics of our local patient population. The non-balanced design, while pragmatic and adequately powered for our primary outcome, may limit the statistical power for certain secondary subgroup analyses [15]. We acknowledge that a control group of 20 subjects is small; however, given the large anticipated effect size for Kisspeptin levels between uremic and healthy individuals, our power analysis indicates this sample size is sufficient to achieve a meaningful comparison for our primary outcome. Finally, while we control for internal assay variability, the known discrepancies between different commercial Kisspeptin assays mean that the absolute values reported in our study must be compared with caution to those from other research groups using different platforms.

In conclusion, this protocol describes an investigation into the role of Kisspeptin in uremic hypogonadism. It is poised to provide crucial insights into the central mechanisms driving this common and debilitating condition. By rigorously characterizing this key neuroendocrine regulator, our study will not only enhance our fundamental understanding of uremia’s systemic impact but also set the stage for the potential development of novel diagnostics and therapeutic innovations for patients with ESRD.

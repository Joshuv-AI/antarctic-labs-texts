Real-world associations of pathological complete response and survival with neoadjuvant pembrolizumab in early TNBC: a Turkish Oncology Group study
Authors: Harun Muğlu, Erdem Sünger, Oğuzcan Özkan, Taha Koray Şahin, Bahadır Köylü, Kaan Helvacı, Yasemin Kemal, Umut Demirci, Fatih Selçukbiricik, Gül Başaran, Başak Oyan Uluç, Ömer Fatih Ölmez, Sercan Aksoy, Erhan Gökmen, Ahmet Bilici
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive subtype with limited targeted treatment options and a high risk of recurrence. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab (NACP) is a standard approach to increase pathological complete response (pCR) rates and improve long-term outcomes, but real-world predictors of response and survival remain insufficiently defined. This multicenter retrospective real-world study included 164 patients with early-stage TNBC treated with NACP followed by surgery. The primary endpoint was pCR, and secondary endpoints were event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS). Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors of pCR, and Cox proportional hazards models were applied to evaluate factors associated with EFS and OS. pCR was achieved in 60.4% of patients. Over a median follow-up of 49.2 months (range, 4–73.8 months), recurrence occurred in 18.3% and mortality in 3.0%. In multivariable Cox analysis, weekly carboplatin scheduling, younger age, and achievement of pCR were associated with longer EFS. However, these associations were derived from a retrospective analysis without adjustment for center-level effects or relative dose intensity and should therefore be considered hypothesis-generating. Owing to the very low number of deaths (n=5), OS analyses were severely underpowered, and multivariable modeling was not statistically reliable. Therefore, OS findings should be interpreted as descriptive and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. In multivariable logistic regression, weekly carboplatin administration was associated with higher odds of pCR; however, given the retrospective design and potential confounding factors, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory. In an exploratory subgroup analysis, no EFS events were observed among patients who achieved pCR and received adjuvant pembrolizumab; however, these findings are hypothesis-generating because of sparse events and potential confounding by indication. In this real-world multicenter cohort, NACP achieved high pCR rates, and pCR was associated with longer EFS in this cohort. Weekly carboplatin scheduling was associated with higher pCR rates and longer EFS; however, given the retrospective and non-randomized design, this observation should be considered exploratory rather than indicative of a modifiable causal effect. Prospective, adequately powered, biomarker-integrated trials are required to determine which patients derive incremental benefit from adjuvant pembrolizumab beyond the neoadjuvant component, particularly when stratified by pathological complete response (pCR) status. Future studies should clarify whether patients achieving pCR may safely omit adjuvant immunotherapy and, conversely, whether patients with residual disease require treatment intensification strategies guided by molecular and immune biomarkers. Such risk-adapted approaches are essential to optimize benefit while minimizing overtreatment in early-stage TNBC. Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common malignancy in women globally and a leading cause of cancer-related death1. Among its subtypes, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, stands out as one of the most aggressive forms2. TNBC accounts for approximately 10–15% of all breast cancer cases and is associated with a high risk of recurrence and mortality3,4. TNBC is also characterized by substantial molecular and immunologic heterogeneity, including differences in homologous recombination deficiency, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and Programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression. In clinical practice, however, biomarker stratification remains limited to a small number of routinely available variables such as germline breast cancer gene.

(BRCA) mutation status and PD-L1 expression. In the present study, molecular characterization was restricted to these clinically available markers, without comprehensive genomic or transcriptomic profiling. Therefore, our analyses primarily reflect clinicopathological predictors rather than deep molecular subclassification.

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has become an essential component of treatment in TNBC5. Achieving pathological complete response (pCR) after NAC has been associated with significantly improved event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS), making pCR a surrogate marker for long-term outcomes6. pCR has been recognized as a valid surrogate endpoint in regulatory and clinical trial settings7–9.

Recent advances combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy have transformed the treatment landscape of TNBC by significantly improving pCR and survival outcomes. In particular, the phase 3 KEYNOTE-522 trial demonstrated that adding pembrolizumab (a Programmed death-ligand 1 (PDL-1) inhibitor) to platinum-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved both pCR (64.8% vs. 51.2%) and EFS10. These results led to FDA approval of pembrolizumab for high-risk early-stage TNBC as part of the neoadjuvant treatment, now considered standard of care. Similarly, the IMpassion031 trial showed that combining atezolizumab (a programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor) with nab-paclitaxel and anthracycline-based chemotherapy significantly improved pCR rates. In the phase 2 I-SPY2 trial, patients with HER2-negative breast cancer showed a higher rate of pathological complete response when treated with a combination of pembrolizumab and neoadjuvant chemotherapy compared to those who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone11,12.

The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab (NACP) in a large cohort of early-stage TNBC patients in Türkiye. We present detailed analyses of pCR rates, EFS, and OS outcomes, and investigate clinicopathological predictors of pCR to inform personalized treatment strategies. While randomized trials such as KEYNOTE-522 have established the efficacy of NACP in early-stage TNBC, real-world data—particularly from underrepresented populations—remain limited. Moreover, the optimal chemotherapy scheduling strategy in combination with immune checkpoint inhibition remains uncertain in routine practice. In particular, the impact of carboplatin dosing frequency on pathological response and long-term outcomes has not been prospectively compared. Real-world data are therefore essential to clarify whether treatment delivery patterns influence survival beyond the controlled environment of randomized trials. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate clinicopathological predictors of pCR in patients with early-stage TNBC treated with NACP in a real-world, multicenter setting. Secondary objectives included assessing associations between treatment-related variables and EFS, as well as describing implementation patterns of NACP across centers in Türkiye. Given the retrospective design, all analyses were considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

Results

A total of 164 patients were included in the study. The majority of patients were under 65 years of age (92.7%, n=152). In terms of primary tumor characteristics, 72.0% (n=118) had a single lesion and histologically, invasive ductal carcinoma was the predominant subtype (87.9%, n=144). Demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. PD-L1 status was available in 107 of 164 patients, and patients with missing PD-L1 data were excluded from this subgroup analysis. Patients were evaluated over a median follow-up period of 49.2 months (range, 4–73.8 months). During this time, disease recurrence was observed in 30 out of 164 patients (18.3%), while 5 patients (3.0%) died.

In the multivariable Cox regression analysis for event-free survival, carboplatin administration schedule, age category, and achievement of pCR remained independently associated with EFS. Using weekly carboplatin administration as the reference category, patients receiving carboplatin every 3 weeks had a significantly higher risk of EFS events (HR=5.137; p=0.001). In addition, older age and failure to achieve pCR were independently associated with worse EFS outcomes. Radiologic response variables, assessed after treatment initiation, were not included in the primary multivariable model and were evaluated only in exploratory analyses. The results of multivariate analysis for EFS are summarized in Table 3. Post-treatment radiologic response variables showed associations with EFS in univariable exploratory analyses; however, given their post-baseline nature, these findings were not incorporated into the primary multivariable models.

During follow-up, five deaths (3.0%) were observed. Given the very limited number of OS events, OS analyses were considered descriptive. Kaplan–Meier curves are presented for illustrative purposes (Figure 2). Median OS was not reached in most subgroups. No formal inferential conclusions were drawn for OS due to the insufficient number of events. For OS, events occurred in 3/121 (2.5%), 1/37 (2.7%), and 1/5 (20.0%), with no global difference (log-rank p = 0.055). In pairwise analyses, only the 3-weekly group showed inferior OS compared with the weekly schedule (p = 0.0176); however, this subgroup included only five patients, and results should therefore be interpreted cautiously. A borderline difference in OS was observed across paclitaxel schedules (global log-rank p = 0.0546), mainly driven by inferior outcomes in the 3-weekly group (p = 0.0176).

In the multivariable Cox regression for OS, estimation was unstable due to the very low number of events (5 deaths) and quasi-complete separation within the pCR strata; consequently, hazard ratios—including for pCR—were not reliably estimable. We therefore refrain from drawing inferential conclusions for OS from this model and instead report OS outcomes descriptively, using unadjusted Kaplan–Meier comparisons to illustrate group differences. Given the event-per-variable ratio was far below recommended thresholds for Cox modeling, adjusted OS estimates lack statistical robustness and are presented for completeness only. Some variables, such as adjuvant capecitabine (p = 0.002), radiological treatment response (p = 0.002), and adjuvant pembrolizumab (p = 0.082), appeared to be statistically significant or borderline; however, the confidence intervals associated with these variables were notably wide. This may reflect instability in the model due to the low number of events.

Among non-pCR patients (64/161; 39.8%), capecitabine was administered to 52/64 (81.2%). Among gBRCA-positive patients (29/161; 18.0%), poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors were used in 7/29 (24.1%) overall; specifically, 2/10 (20.0%) with residual disease and 5/19 (26.3%) with pCR at physician discretion. Given the limited number of BRCA-mutated patients and the very low number of survival events within this subgroup, formal survival analyses were not performed to avoid statistically unstable and potentially misleading estimates. Outcomes in BRCA-mutated patients are therefore reported descriptively. Patients were stratified into four groups based on pCR status following neoadjuvant therapy and whether they received adjuvant pembrolizumab. Cycle data for pembrolizumab were available in 162 of 165 patients (98.2%). Among these, 52 patients (32.1%) completed all 17 cycles, while 110 (67.9%) discontinued earlier (<17 cycles; median 10 cycles, IQR 7–17). Detailed reasons for early pembrolizumab discontinuation (e.g., toxicity, patient preference, or logistical factors) were not systematically recorded across centers; therefore, discontinuation could not be reliably classified for cause-specific sensitivity analyses. In addition to clinical considerations, real-world treatment continuation may also be influenced by reimbursement policies, administrative approval processes, and variability in access to immunotherapy across healthcare settings. These contextual factors could not be formally captured in our dataset and may have contributed to early discontinuation in a subset of patients.

Adverse events were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records across participating centers. Given the multicenter retrospective design, systematic prospective toxicity capture was not performed, and underreporting of low-grade events cannot be excluded. Based on available clinical documentation, the majority of patients had at least one recorded treatment-related adverse event during the neoadjuvant phase.

The most frequently documented events (any grade) were nausea (67.1%), neutropenia (45.1%), alopecia (54.8%), and fatigue (39.6%). Grade ≥3 toxicities, as recorded in medical charts, most commonly included neutropenia (30.4%) and anemia (15.2%). Immune-related events such as hypothyroidism were observed in 12.1% of patients. Discussion

TNBC presents substantial challenges in both treatment planning and outcome prediction due to its molecular heterogeneity and aggressive biological behaviour2,13. pCR is recognized as a strong surrogate marker for long-term survival in TNBC14–16. In recent years, the addition of immunotherapy to neoadjuvant chemotherapy has significantly improved pCR rates (e.g. pembrolizumab + NAC: pCR increased from 51.2 % to 64.8 % in KEYNOTE-522, with significant EFS/OS gains; similar findings in IMpassion031, real-world pCR rates ~60–64 %)8,10,12,17.

The pCR rate observed in our real-world cohort (60.4%) appears consistent with the 64.8% reported in KEYNOTE-522; however, no formal statistical comparison was conducted, and differences in patient selection and study design preclude definitive conclusions10. Furthermore, the recurrence rate of 18.3% and mortality rate of 3.0% suggest effective systemic disease control. However, the higher recurrence risk in patients who did not achieve pCR underscores the need for better-defined postoperative treatment strategies in this subgroup.

Consistent with prior literature, pCR has been associated with favorable outcomes in TNBC18. While unadjusted OS curves favored patients achieving pCR, in multivariable models the association did not reach statistical significance given the very low number of deaths (n=5) and should be interpreted as a directional trend rather than a definitive effect. Because radiologic response is a post-treatment measure and not a surrogate for pCR, we report it descriptively; survival inferences are anchored primarily to pCR.

Importantly, the interpretation of OS findings in our study is fundamentally constrained by the very low number of observed deaths (n=5). With such sparse events, multivariable Cox regression modeling is statistically unstable and prone to overfitting, wide confidence intervals, and quasi-complete separation. Consequently, any apparent differences in OS according to chemotherapy schedule are likely to reflect random variation rather than true biological or therapeutic effects. As a result, any apparent associations with OS should be interpreted cautiously and not regarded as definitive evidence of prognostic effect. Our study was powered primarily for pCR and EFS, and OS outcomes remain immature in this cohort.

In our dataset, pCR appeared to be the dominant variable associated with EFS; however, given the observational design and limited event counts, this finding should be interpreted as supportive rather than definitive evidence of prognostic strength. In our cohort, patients achieving pCR demonstrated durable survival with minimal recurrence, whereas non-pCR patients experienced early and steep declines in EFS. These findings underscore the critical role of achieving pCR as a surrogate for long-term benefit and the need to optimize systemic treatment strategies that maximize pCR rates.

Importantly, randomized phase III trials such as KEYNOTE-522 enroll highly selected populations under strict eligibility criteria, often excluding patients with significant comorbidities, advanced age, or suboptimal performance status. In contrast, real-world cohorts inherently reflect broader clinical heterogeneity, including patients with varying comorbidity burdens, age distributions, and treatment adherence patterns19. The comparable pCR rate observed in our study therefore suggests that the effectiveness of pembrolizumab-based neoadjuvant therapy may extend beyond tightly controlled trial populations. However, differences in baseline characteristics, supportive care, and protocol adherence should be considered when interpreting cross-study comparisons.

The literature presents conflicting results regarding the impact of carboplatin on pCR20–23. In the KN-522 trial, carboplatin was administered every three weeks (AUC 5–6 mg/mL/min), weekly AUC of 1.5 mg/mL/min and its combination with immunotherapy significantly improved pCR rates10. In contrast, our study showed that weekly carboplatin (AUC 1.5 mg/mL/min) was associated with higher pCR rates (66.7% vs. 40.0%) and in exploratory, unadjusted analyses, pCR status was associated with differences in EFS (HR = 5.137, p = 0.001). These findings support the hypothesis that lower, more frequent chemotherapy dosing may enhance antitumor immune responses without suppressing the immune system. Given the lack of studies directly comparing these regimens, our findings may provide a basis for future randomized trials.

Emerging preclinical and translational evidence suggests that chemotherapy scheduling may influence antitumor immunity24,25. Dose-dense or metronomic regimens have been associated with reduced regulatory T-cell populations, modulation of myeloid-derived suppressor cells, and enhanced immunogenic cell death, potentially facilitating more effective T-cell priming26. Platinum agents in particular may augment immune activation through increased DNA damage signaling and STING pathway activation, thereby enhancing tumor immunogenicity27. Although these mechanisms remain to be prospectively validated in TNBC, they provide a biologically plausible framework supporting the hypothesis that lower, more frequent carboplatin dosing could synergize more effectively with immune checkpoint inhibition.

Although numerically higher pCR rates were observed with the weekly 80 mg/m2 schedule (61.8%) compared with the 175 mg/m2 q2-weekly (51.4%) and q3-weekly (40.0%) regimens, these differences were not statistically significant. Event-free and OS outcomes also did not differ significantly among schedules. Although a numerical separation in OS curves was observed in favor of the weekly regimen, this finding was driven by extremely sparse events and a very small subgroup sample size. Given the limited number of deaths in the entire cohort (n=5), the analysis lacks sufficient statistical power to support any robust conclusions regarding schedule-dependent survival effects. These findings are consistent with prior evidence, such as the adjuvant E1199 trial, where weekly paclitaxel improved early disease control compared with q3-weekly dosing, but without a uniformly consistent survival advantage across settings28. Current ESMO guidance endorses anthracycline/taxane-based neoadjuvant therapy for TNBC but does not prefer one paclitaxel schedule for survival, supporting individualized selection based on toxicity, logistics, and the overall systemic strategy29.

Within our cohort, the evaluation of BRCA as an independent predictive or prognostic factor was constrained by incomplete testing and limited sample size. Only 29 patients harbored a confirmed germline BRCA mutation, and survival events within this subgroup were exceedingly rare. Under such conditions, subgroup survival modeling would violate recommended event-per-variable thresholds and risk generating unstable or spurious hazard estimates. Furthermore, PARP inhibitor administration was not protocol-mandated but based on physician discretion and access considerations, introducing potential selection bias. Accordingly, BRCA-related findings are presented descriptively and should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

To identify independent predictors of pCR, a multivariate logistic regression including age, ECOG performance status, PD-L1 status, T stage, and nodal status was performed. Only the carboplatin dosing schedule remained significant, with weekly dosing increasing the likelihood of pCR. This supports the univariate findings and highlights dosing frequency as a potentially modifiable treatment factor. Traditional variables such as PD-L1 status and tumor burden (T/N stage) were not predictive after adjustment, suggesting their influence may be limited when treatment-related factors are considered.

PD-L1 expression, when available, was assessed using the Combined Positive Score (CPS), consistent with the methodology applied in KEYNOTE-522. However, PD-L1 testing was not uniformly performed across all patients in this real-world cohort. Therefore, analyses involving PD-L1 status were restricted to the subset of tested patients and should be interpreted within the context of incomplete biomarker assessment.

The absence of independent associations for classical predictors such as PD-L1 status and clinical T/N stage warrants consideration. Several explanations may account for this finding. First, the relatively limited sample size may have reduced statistical power to detect modest effect sizes. Second, our cohort consisted exclusively of high-risk, early-stage TNBC patients treated with pembrolizumab-based chemoimmunotherapy, which may have attenuated the prognostic heterogeneity typically observed with tumor burden variables. Third, the addition of immunotherapy may partially overcome baseline biological differences, thereby diminishing the independent predictive value of PD-L1 expression and clinical stage. Finally, collinearity between tumor burden and treatment-related variables may have further reduced the apparent independent contribution of traditional predictors in multivariable modeling. These findings should therefore not be interpreted as evidence against the biological relevance of PD-L1 or tumor burden, but rather as reflecting the specific characteristics and statistical constraints of this real-world cohort.

Clinicopathological features such as histological subtype, tumor grade, tumor size, and focality have also been linked to pCR. In the study by Perry et al., unifocality, ductal histology, and poor differentiation were significantly associated with pCR30. In our cohort, several clinicopathological variables were evaluated. In the primary multivariable model restricted to baseline characteristics, age emerged as a significant predictor of EFS. Radiologic response was not incorporated into this primary model because it represents a post-treatment variable. However, in exploratory multivariable analyses that included post-baseline factors, radiologic response was associated with EFS. Given its post-treatment nature, this finding should be interpreted as descriptive and not as evidence of independent baseline prognostic value.

Our results also align with recent real-world evidence from other regions. In a large population-based study from the Brazilian public health system, Mattar et al31. evaluated real-world outcomes and healthcare costs in TNBC and found that achieving pCR was independently associated with improved OS, whereas carboplatin use did not independently affect survival. In our multicenter Turkish cohort treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, pCR remained the strongest determinant of EFS, while chemotherapy scheduling—particularly weekly carboplatin—emerged as a modifiable treatment factor linked to improved response. Together, these real-world findings highlight the importance of optimizing treatment delivery and achieving pCR in routine clinical practice, particularly in settings with variable access to immunotherapy or genomic testing.

In randomized evidence, KEYNOTE-52210 established that adding pembrolizumab to standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continuing it for nine cycles in the adjuvant phase increases pCR and improves EFS, and with extended follow-up also improves OS versus placebo; guideline recommendations reflect this total-regimen paradigm rather than any single phase in isolation10.

In our cohort, exploratory, unadjusted four-group comparisons (by pCR and receipt of adjuvant pembrolizumab) showed the most favorable outcomes among patients with pCR, and numerically longer EFS among non-pCR patients who received adjuvant pembrolizumab versus those who did not. However, event counts in the pCR strata were extremely low (including a single death among pCR patients who did not receive adjuvant pembrolizumab), precluding reliable adjusted hazard estimation; thus, these subgroup findings are descriptive and should not be interpreted as proof of an adjuvant pembrolizumab effect, particularly in pCR. Overall, our observations are hypothesis-generating and broadly concordant with real-world series reporting pCR rates and safety profiles similar to trials, while underscoring the need for adequately powered prospective studies to define which subgroups (e.g., by pCR) derive incremental benefit from adjuvant immunotherapy beyond the neoadjuvant component32.

In routine clinical practice, the decision to continue pembrolizumab in the adjuvant setting was not protocol-mandated but guided by physician discretion. Factors likely influencing this decision included pathological response status (particularly the presence of residual invasive disease), baseline tumor burden, perceived recurrence risk, treatment tolerance during the neoadjuvant phase, comorbidities, and patient preference. As such, patients receiving adjuvant pembrolizumab may have differed systematically from those who did not, introducing confounding by indication. Although we reported outcomes descriptively and avoided causal claims, residual confounding cannot be excluded in the absence of randomization. Multivariable adjustment was limited by sparse events within strata, and therefore formal causal modeling was not statistically appropriate.

Our study represents a valuable real-world analysis of how data from randomized clinical trials translate into clinical practice, highlighting the clinical impact of practical factors such as carboplatin dosing frequency. In this context, weekly low-dose carboplatin regimens represents a feasible schedule warranting further prospective evaluation for their combined efficacy and tolerability.

Completion of all 17 pembrolizumab cycles was not associated with a significant difference in EFS, although interpretation is limited by the small number of events. The absence of standardized documentation for chemotherapy dose reductions and delays represents a key limitation, restricting detailed adherence analyses based on relative dose intensity (RDI).

The high proportion of patients who did not complete all 17 pembrolizumab cycles reflects real-world treatment dynamics. However, the retrospective multicenter nature of our dataset precluded a structured capture of discontinuation reasons. In routine practice, early discontinuation may occur due to toxicity, intercurrent illness, patient preference, logistical constraints, or clinician judgment, and these pathways may carry different prognostic implications. Without systematic documentation, any attempt to stratify discontinuation by cause would be vulnerable to misclassification and confounding by indication. Therefore, we report completion status descriptively and interpret these findings cautiously.

Adverse events were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records across participating centers. Given the multicenter retrospective design, systematic prospective toxicity capture was not performed, and underreporting of low-grade events cannot be excluded. Based on available clinical documentation, the majority of patients had at least one recorded treatment-related adverse event during the neoadjuvant phase. The most frequently documented events (any grade) were nausea (67.1%), neutropenia (45.1%), alopecia (54.8%), and fatigue (39.6%). Grade ≥3 toxicities, as recorded in medical charts, most commonly included neutropenia (30.4%) and anemia (15.2%). Immune-related events such as hypothyroidism were observed in 12.1% of patients. No treatment-related deaths were documented during the neoadjuvant phase. Detailed toxicity frequencies are summarized in Table 5..

Several limitations warrant careful consideration when interpreting our findings. First, the retrospective design inherently limits causal inference and introduces potential selection bias typical of real-world analyses. Treatment sequencing, carboplatin scheduling, and continuation of adjuvant pembrolizumab were not protocol-mandated but determined according to physician discretion and institutional practice, rendering these analyses susceptible to confounding by indication. In addition, center-level practice variation was not formally adjusted for in multivariable modeling, which may have influenced treatment delivery patterns and clinical outcomes.

Second, biomarker assessment was incomplete and not uniformly performed across all patients. PD-L1 testing was conducted according to institutional practice using the Combined Positive Score (CPS), and germline BRCA status was available only for a subset of patients. The limited number of BRCA-mutated cases and sparse survival events within this subgroup precluded adequately powered survival modeling and restricted robust evaluation of independent biomarker effects.

Third, the very low number of OS events (n=5) substantially limits statistical power and precludes reliable multivariable survival modeling, resulting in wide confidence intervals and unstable hazard estimates. Furthermore, detailed data on relative dose intensity, treatment interruptions, and toxicity-related discontinuations were not systematically recorded across centers. Consequently, it is not possible to determine whether observed associations—particularly those related to carboplatin scheduling—reflect intrinsic scheduling effects or variations in delivered treatment intensity and tolerability. These findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously and considered hypothesis-generating.

In this multicenter real-world cohort of early-stage TNBC treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab, we observed a high pCR rate (60.4%) and a strong association between pCR and longer EFS, consistent with randomized evidence.

In our cohort, weekly carboplatin administration was associated with higher pCR rates compared with the every-3-week schedule. Potential explanations may include differences in cumulative exposure or treatment-related immune modulation. However, detailed data on delivered relative dose intensity, dose reductions, and treatment delays were not available in our dataset. Consequently, it is not possible to determine whether the observed differences are attributable to intrinsic schedule-related biological effects or to variations in actual treatment delivery. These findings should therefore be interpreted with caution and viewed as exploratory, requiring confirmation in prospective studies incorporating comprehensive treatment exposure data. Although unadjusted survival curves favored patients achieving pCR, multivariable modeling for OS was underpowered (five deaths); therefore, any apparent OS advantage should be interpreted as a directional trend rather than a definitive effect. No definitive conclusions can be drawn regarding the impact of chemotherapy scheduling on OS in this cohort. Exploratory, unadjusted subgroup comparisons by pCR and adjuvant pembrolizumab suggested more favorable outcomes in pCR groups and numerically longer EFS among non-pCR patients who received adjuvant pembrolizumab, but these hypothesis-generating findings are susceptible to sparse events and potential confounding by indication.

Conclusion

In this multicenter real-world cohort, neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with pembrolizumab demonstrated substantial pathological response rates, and achievement of pCR was associated with improved event-free survival. Carboplatin scheduling showed an association with response and survival outcomes within the multivariable framework; however, given the retrospective and non-randomized design, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than causal.

OS analyses were limited by the low number of events and are therefore descriptive. Exploratory subgroup comparisons, including those involving adjuvant immunotherapy, should be interpreted cautiously in light of potential confounding and sparse data.

Prospective, adequately powered, biomarker-integrated studies are required to clarify the incremental contribution of treatment sequencing and adjuvant strategies in early-stage TNBC and to guide risk-adapted therapeutic approaches.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This multicenter retrospective cohort study utilized real-world data from various oncology centers in Türkiye. It included 164 patients diagnosed with early-stage TNBC between 2021 and 2025 who received NACP. The study included patients from 15 participating centers. The median number of patients contributed per center was 7 (range, 1–44). A detailed breakdown of patient enrollment by center is presented in Supplementary Table S2. This multicenter retrospective real-world study included consecutive patients treated across participating centers. Eligible patients had non-metastatic, treatment-naïve TNBC with clinical staging of T1 (N1–3) or T2–4 (N0–3) according to the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) histologically confirmed TNBC, (2) receipt of at least one cycle of NACP, and (3) subsequent definitive breast surgery with available pathological response assessment.

Patients were excluded if they had metastatic disease at diagnosis, did not receive pembrolizumab as part of neoadjuvant therapy, or lacked pathological response data. During the study period, patients with early-stage TNBC who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy without pembrolizumab were identified through institutional treatment registries and were not included in the analysis.

Completion of the full planned anthracycline–taxane–carboplatin–pembrolizumab sequence was not mandatory for inclusion. Patients were included if they initiated neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, regardless of subsequent treatment modifications, dose adjustments, or early discontinuation, in order to reflect routine clinical practice. The patient selection process and reasons for exclusion are summarized in Figure 3. Clinical staging was performed according to the 8th edition of the AJCC TNM classification33. Baseline staging followed NCCN/ESMO recommendations and included PET/CT or CT-based staging and breast MRI when indicated29. TNBC diagnosis was centrally confirmed according to ASCO/CAP guidelines34. All patients had an ECOG performance status of 0 or 1. Clinical, pathological, and treatment-related data were retrospectively extracted from standardized electronic hospital automation systems at each participating center. Data collection was performed using a predefined variable framework to ensure consistency across institutions. All variables were centrally compiled and reviewed for completeness, internal consistency, and plausibility prior to statistical analysis.

Ethical approval

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Istanbul Medipol University (Decision No: 327, Date: 20.03.2025). Written informed consent for treatment and use of anonymized clinical data for research purposes was obtained from all patients or their legal representatives.

Neoadjuvant treatment

Three different pembrolizumab-based regimens were used in the neoadjuvant phase, depending on physician preference. These regimens varied in dosage, drug combinations, and sequencing. The anthracycline–cyclophosphamide (AC) component was uniformly delivered as four 3-weekly cycles (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 + cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 on Day 1; q3w ×4). Most commonly, AC was followed by carboplatin and paclitaxel: Carboplatin was administered either weekly (AUC 1.5) or every 3 weeks (AUC 5–6). Paclitaxel was delivered as 80 mg/m2 weekly, 175 mg/m2 every 2 weeks (dose-dense), or 175 mg/m2 every 3 weeks according to physician preference, plus pembrolizumab 200 mg every 21 days throughout. Alternatively, some regimens started with carboplatin–paclitaxel followed by AC, with concurrent pembrolizumab throughout.

In real-world practice, minor variations in treatment sequencing were observed. While all patients received pembrolizumab in combination with platinum-, taxane-, and anthracycline-based chemotherapy consistent with the KEYNOTE-522 backbone, the order of anthracycline and platinum administration varied across centers (anthracycline-first vs platinum-first). Such sequencing variability reflects routine clinical practice rather than protocol deviation. Importantly, no patient omitted any backbone component, and treatment sequence and carboplatin scheduling were incorporated into the statistical models to adjust for potential confounding.

Perioperative pembrolizumab exposure was evaluated based on the total number of administered cycles. Treatment completion was defined as receiving all 17 planned cycles, whereas early discontinuation was classified as receiving fewer than 17 cycles. Cycle-specific data on chemotherapy dose reductions and delays were not uniformly recorded across centers; therefore, these parameters were excluded from analysis and acknowledged as a study limitation.

Surgery and adjuvant treatment

Definitive surgery (breast-conserving surgery with sentinel lymph node biopsy, mastectomy with or without axillary dissection) was performed 3 to 6 weeks after neoadjuvant treatment completion. In the adjuvant phase, radiotherapy was administered when clinically indicated, and pembrolizumab was continued up to 9 cycles at the physician’s discretion. Capecitabine was reported only in the subgroup with residual invasive disease (non-pCR), and PARP inhibitor use only in patients with germline BRCA1/2 positivity; counts were provided within the corresponding subgroups. Treatment was discontinued in cases of disease progression, recurrence, or unacceptable toxicity.

Data collection and variables

Demographic variables such as age and menopausal status were recorded. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) was assessed. Tumor-related variables included histological subtype, PD-L1 status, clinical stage, lymph node involvement, and overall staging. Carboplatin schedule, type of surgery, and use of adjuvant pembrolizumab or capecitabine were included as treatment-related variables. BRCA1/2 mutation status was available for a subset of patients. Detailed data on chemotherapy dose reductions, treatment delays, and relative dose intensity were not systematically captured in this retrospective dataset and were therefore not included in the multivariable models.

Pathological complete response (pCR) assessment

Response to neoadjuvant treatment was evaluated using response evaluation criteria in solid tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) criteria for clinical assessment. pCR was strictly defined as the absence of residual invasive carcinoma in both the breast and axillary lymph nodes (ypT0/Tis ypN0), allowing for the presence of residual ductal carcinoma in situ. This definition is fully aligned with the standardized endpoint recommended by the NeoSTEEP international consensus panel35 and corresponds to the regulatory definition endorsed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)36. Pathologic assessment was performed according to institutional standards at participating center35. Radiologic response according to RECIST v1.1 was assessed at the completion of neoadjuvant therapy using breast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and/or positron emission tomography–computed tomography (PET-CT), according to institutional practice, and was collected for clinical management purposes and is presented descriptively. The prespecified primary pathological endpoint of the study was pCR (ypT0/Tis ypN0), and radiologic response was not used to define or substitute for pathologic response.

Survival analyses

To assess treatment efficacy, EFS and OS were analyzed. EFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to any of the following events: disease progression preventing surgery, local or distant recurrence, diagnosis of a second primary malignancy, or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause. Patients alive at the time of analysis were censored at the date of last follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Data from all participating centers were pooled for analysis. Descriptive statistics included counts, percentages, means, standard deviations, medians, and ranges. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to identify independent predictors of pCR. The prespecified variables for the multivariable logistic regression model for pCR were age category (<65 vs ≥65), ECOG performance status (0 vs 1), PD-L1 expression status, clinical T stage, nodal status, and carboplatin scheduling. These variables were selected based on clinical relevance and prior literature. Time-to-event outcomes were analyzed using Kaplan–Meier survival curves, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were first performed to examine associations between clinicopathologic and treatment-related variables and survival outcomes. Variables that were significant in univariate analyses were subsequently entered into multivariate Cox proportional hazards models using a stepwise selection approach. Results were reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables included in univariate analyses were selected based on predefined clinical relevance and data availability. Baseline clinicopathologic characteristics and key treatment-related variables (e.g., age, clinical stage, carboplatin scheduling, and achievement of pCR) were prespecified for primary univariate and multivariable analyses.

Additional variables, including post-treatment radiologic response, type of surgery, adjuvant treatment exposure, and other post-baseline parameters, were analyzed in exploratory univariate models and are presented in Supplementary Table S1. These exploratory analyses were not used to guide variable inclusion in the primary multivariable models.

Adjuvant pembrolizumab exposure was defined according to the total number of cycles administered. For exploratory subgroup analyses, patients were categorized as having completed the planned 17 cycles versus fewer cycles. This variable was incorporated into survival analyses as an exploratory treatment exposure parameter. In addition, subgroup analyses stratified by pCR status and receipt of adjuvant pembrolizumab were conducted as exploratory analyses to descriptively evaluate potential interactions between pathological response and perioperative immunotherapy exposure. These analyses were not prespecified for causal inference and were interpreted cautiously.

Given the limited number of death events observed during the follow-up period, OS analyses were considered primarily descriptive. Multivariable modeling for OS was regarded as exploratory and therefore not emphasized in the interpretation of prognostic factors. Accordingly, OS outcomes were presented using Kaplan–Meier estimates and log-rank comparisons, while event-free survival was designated as the primary time-to-event endpoint for multivariable Cox regression analyses.

Radiologic response to neoadjuvant therapy was evaluated as an exploratory variable. Because radiologic response was assessed after treatment initiation, it was not included as a covariate in the primary multivariable Cox models for event-free survival in order to minimize potential post-baseline and immortal time biases. Instead, its association with outcomes was examined only in secondary univariable analyses. The primary multivariable analyses focused on baseline clinicopathologic characteristics and treatment-related variables, including carboplatin scheduling and achievement of pCR. For categorical variables, hazard ratios were estimated using clinically relevant reference categories; weekly carboplatin administration, achievement of pCR, and age <65 years were used as reference groups where applicable.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), and two-sided p-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Artificial intelligence–based large language models were used to assist with language editing, clarity, and organization of the manuscript. All analyses, data interpretation, and scientific content were generated by the authors, who reviewed, verified, and take full responsibility for the accuracy and integrity of the work. Treatment selection, including carboplatin scheduling, was clinician-dependent and not protocol-driven. The analyses did not include adjustment for center-level clustering or matching strategies. Consequently, the possibility of confounding-by-indication cannot be ruled out.

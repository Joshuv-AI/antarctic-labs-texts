Efficacy analysis of early incision and drainage on necrotizing fasciitis, a retrospective study
Authors: Zixin Chai, Dachun Chen, Yuchen Fan, Lu Wang, Xiang Li, Fengqin Miao, Zexin Chen, Shaolei Ma, Fangfang Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNecrotizing fasciitis is a life-threatening infectious disease, and extensive debridement, as the primary surgical approach, poses significant risks for advanced-stage patients. Early incision and drainage is a common treatment for soft tissue infections, while its efficacy as a temporizing strategy in necrotizing fasciitis patients remains unclear.MethodsThis study included 116 necrotizing fasciitis patients who underwent surgical treatment at Zhongda Hospital from 1 Jan 2015 to 28 Feb 2025. Based on their initial surgical approach, patients were divided into the incision and drainage group (48 cases) and the extensive debridement group (68 cases). We collected the demographic data, clinical manifestations, treatment details, and outcomes of all patients, and analyzed through t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, and chi-square tests. Binary logistic regression was conducted to assess the outcomes of amputation and mortality rate.ResultsIn this study, more patients in the I&D group were man (P = 0.031). A higher proportion of patients in the I&D group presented with anemia (P = 0.035), hypoalbuminemia (P = 0.030), abnormal serum creatinine level(P = 0.029), and were at advanced stage of NF(P = 0.031) on admission. The amputation rate was significantly reduced in the I&D group (P = 0.021). After adjusting for multiple confounders, both early I&D (Amputation: aOR = 0.107, 95%CI: 0.022–0.511. Mortality rate: aOR = 0.172, 95%CI: 0.032–0.924.) and higher albumin level (Amputation: aOR = 0.838, 95%CI: 0.724–0.97. Mortality rate: aOR = 0.71, 95%CI: 0.599–0.902.) were associated with a significant reduction in the risks of amputation and mortality rate in NF patients.ConclusionEarly incision and drainage was associated with a lower risk of amputation and mortality in necrotizing fasciitis patients, offering an effective temporizing strategy to improve prognosis, particularly for those with poor nutritional status.

Introduction

Necrotizing fasciitis (NF) is a life-threatening infectious disease characterized by extensive necrosis of deep tissues [1], primarily involving damage of fascia, tendons and muscles [2]. Epidemiological data indicates an annual incidence of 13.97–18.47 cases per 100,000 population in Thailand [3, 4], and 0.18-15 cases per 100,000 population in other regions [5–7]. NF can be caused by multiple factors, such as localized trauma, surgical wounds, ingestion of raw or undercooked seafood and insect bites [8, 9]. The disease progresses rapidly, while the mortality rate remains at a high level of 20–30% over the past twenty years [10]. Current evidence on the efficacy and safety of NF interventions remains limited globally [11]. However, according to clinical guidelines, early surgery after diagnosis, along with prompt and empirical broad-spectrum antimicrobial therapy covering both aerobic and anaerobic bacteria has been proven effective in improving patient outcomes. The recommended surgical protocol is extensive debridement, which completely excises all necrotic and devitalized skin, subcutaneous tissues, muscles and tendons until healthy and well-vascularized tissue is exposed [12]. During the prodromal phase, NF patients often exhibit nonspecific cutaneous manifestations, leading to misdiagnosis as other soft tissue infections like cellulitis, causing delays in surgical timing. NF clinical course progresses rapidly, and some patients may develop hemodynamic instability or multiple systemic organ failure within hours, increasing both anesthetic and surgical risks. Additionally, accurate identification and complete removal of necrotic tissues during surgery prove challenging [13], with potential massive bleeding that may exacerbate circulatory compromise and increase mortality risk. For patients with equivocal early signs or those unable to tolerate extensive debridement, early incision and drainage(I&D) has been proposed as a bridging treatment in some studies, while literature evaluating its efficacy remains scarce [14–16]. This article focused on the surgical protocol involving early I&D under local anesthesia followed by extensive debridement within 24 h. We retrospectively analyzed data from patients who underwent surgery for NF at Zhongda hospital over the past ten years, and evaluated the therapeutic effects of early I&D on NF.

Materials and methods

Data sources

This was a retrospective study. We collected data from 156 NF patients who were hospitalized at Zhongda Hospital, Southeast University between January 2015 and February 2025 using the Hospital Information System (HIS). This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Zhongda Hospital, Southeast University.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Diagnosed with necrotizing fasciitis; (2) Underwent at least one surgery for NF. Exclusion criteria: (1) Combined with other life-threatening conditions (e.g. severe pancreatitis, aortic dissection etc.) that constituted the principal cause of death; (2) Combined with progressive severe systemic diseases (e.g. decompensated cirrhosis, chronic kidney disease stage 4–5), or active chronic diseases deemed life-threatening by investigators; (3) Data missing or irreparable. A total of 116 patients were ultimately included and divided into two groups based on their surgical protocol. 48 patients underwent early I&D and the remaining 68 patients underwent extensive debridement.

Diagnostic criteria [17]: (1) ①Positive finger sweep test: The localized subcutaneous tissue and fascia layer can be easily separated by fingers in the surgical exploration and lack of bleeding can be observed; ②Severe gray tissues necrotizing, abundant foul-smelling purulent secretion flowing out, and the necrosis extend beyond the boundary of the skin lesion; (2) Clinical stages of NF: ①Early stage: fever, pain, localized erythema, warmth, tenderness; ②Intermediate stage: skin swelling, skin induration, skin fluctuation sensation; ③Advanced stage: hemorrhagic bullae, crepitus, skin necrosis with dusky discoloration.

Treatment protocols

The extensive debridement group

Extensive debridement carried out immediately after diagnosis. Under general anesthesia, an appropriate incision based on the lesion location was made layer by layer and adequately extended until the healthy tissue was exposed. Then necrotic skin, subcutaneous tissue, fascia, and muscle were thoroughly assessed and completely resected during the surgery [18]. After complete hemostasis, the wound was thoroughly irrigated with iodophor and hydrogen peroxide, and covered with sufficient mupirocin ointment. Repeat debridement may be performed depending on wound recovery status. Meanwhile, all patients promptly received empirical broad-spectrum antimicrobial therapy according to guideline recommendations. Treatment efficacy is assessed every 48–72 h. Antibiotics should be adjusted promptly based on pathogen culture results until infection resolution.

The I&D group

Early I&D carried out immediately after diagnosis. After localized disinfection and anesthesia, a small incision is made at the center of the abscess for drainage and decompression, and extending the incision along the skin tension lines. Blunt dissection was performed with fingers. Following the abscess drained, the wound was thoroughly irrigated with sterile normal saline solution until the effluent clearance [19]. After putting a gauze strip or drainage tube for continuous drainage, the wound was covered with sterile dressing and secured. Strict aseptic technique was maintained throughout the surgery. An assessment of wound was necessary within 12–24 h after surgery, followed by extensive debridement. The remaining treatment protocols were consistent with those in the extensive debridement group.

Postoperative wound care protocol

Two standardized postoperative wound care protocols were available for NF patients according to the location of the wound, wound cavity size, and patients’ financial means. Wound conditions or characteristics of drainage were recorded daily. All patients were managed according to the above protocol.

VAC Therapy: Indicated for patients with large wound cavities and adequate financial means. A VAC therapy device was used to cover the wound with a negative pressure of -125 to -150 mmHg. Saline was continuously irrigated to remove necrotic tissue and secretions for 24 to 48 h. Daily dressing change and packing therapy: Indicated for patients with shallow wound cavities, wounds located in special anatomical sites (e.g., head and neck), or limited financial means. During dressing change, the wound was fully exposed, gently irrigated with saline, packed with iodoform gauze and finally covered with multi-layer sterile gauze for fixation.

Data collection

This study collected the following data through the HIS and Laboratory Information System. (1) Demographics characteristics: gender, age, medical history, affected site, disease stage, admission vital signs; (2) Laboratory tests: ①Inflammatory parameters: white blood cell count(WBC), neutrophil count(NEUT), neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio(NLR), fibrinogen(Fib);②Fibrinolytic function parameters: platelet (PLT), prothrombin Time (PT);③Nutritional and metabolic parameters: red blood cell count (RBC), hemoglobin (Hb), blood glucose (Glu), albumin (Alb), red cell distribution width (RDW);④Electrolyte and renal function parameters: serum sodium (Na+), serum potassium (K+), serum creatinine (Scr). The diagnostic thresholds were defined as follows: Hb < 110 g/L for anemia, Na+ <135 mmol/L for hyponatremia, K+<3.5 mmol/L for hypokalemia, Scr > 106µmol/L for abnormal serum creatinine, Glu > 7.1 mmol/L for hyperglycemia, Alb < 35 g/L for hypoalbuminemia, and PT > 15.0 s for abnormal coagulation time; (3) Treatments: first surgery timing, total number of surgeries, total ICU stay, total hospital stay, the use of VAC(vacuum-assisted closure) therapy; (4) Outcomes: postoperative complications, amputation rate, mortality rate.

Statistical analysis

This study used IBM SPSS Statistics 27 for statistical analysis. Normally distributed quantitative data was presented as mean ± standard deviation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\stackrel{\mathrm{-}}{\mathrm{x}}$$\end{document}±s) and analyzed using independent-sample t test. Non-normally distributed quantitative data was expressed as M(Q1,Q3)(Median (Lower Quartile, Upper Quartile)) and analyzed using Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data was described as n(%) and analyzed by chi-square test. Binary logistic regression analysis was performed to assess the independent effect of early I&D on the risks of amputation and mortality rate. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of demographics characteristics between the I&D group and the extensive debridement group

All patients were adults with a median age of 58.20 years, including 68 males and 48 females. There more male patients in the I&D group. Hypertension (41.4%), diabetes (36.2%) and malignant tumors (20.7%) were the most common previous medical history(PMH). NF mostly developed secondary to traumatic injury, chronic wounds or postoperative infection, and more patients in the I&D group developed the disease under no obvious predisposing causes. The limbs were the most common sites of NF involvement, followed by the perineum. Compared with early and intermediate stage patients, more patients at advanced stage received early I&D surgery(P = 0.029). We found no significant differences between two groups in terms of age and PMH (Table 2).

Comparison of treatments between I&D group and extensive debridement group

Multivariate binary logistic regression analysis revealed that early I&D(aOR = 0.016, 95%CI: 0.026–0.524) and higher albumin levels(aOR = 0.86, 95%CI: 0.757–0.976) were significant protective factors against amputation for NF patients. Discussion

The study revealed a higher proportion of male patients in the I&D group compared to females. This group also showed greater prevalence of abnormal Scr levels, anemia, and hypoalbuminemia, with more patients presenting at advanced NF stages at admission. There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of the proportion of patients who underwent surgery within 24 h, length of hospital stay, and other relevant indicators. Although the incidence of sepsis was significantly higher in the I&D group, fewer of these patients progressed to septic shock. The amputation rate was reduced in the I&D group, while mortality rates showed no significant difference. Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that early I&D was associated with a lower risk of amputation and mortality, and higher albumin levels were associated with lower mortality in NF patients.

NF, as a severe skin and soft tissue infection, often proves fatal when uncontrolled infection progresses to sepsis or septic shock. Kao et al. [20] demonstrated that NF patients with sepsis faced 3.39-fold higher mortality than others. Several studies have identified hemoglobin and albumin levels as critical prognostic markers for assessing inflammatory status and mortality risk in septic patients [21, 22]. In our study, patients in the I&D group exhibited higher rates of anemia and hypoalbuminemia at admission compared to the extensive debridement group, which may explain the higher sepsis incidence. However, fewer patients in this group progressed to septic shock. This finding suggests that the I&D surgery provided effective infection control, preventing the most critical deterioration. Higher inflammatory parameters such as NLR and RDW are associated with increased sepsis mortality [23], but no significant difference in these markers was found between the two groups.Given the small sample size inherent to this single-center retrospective study, we consider that future prospective studies with larger cohorts are warranted to verify the potential correlation between these inflammatory markers and clinical outcomes in NF patients.

Amputation serves as a critical intervention to control the spread of infection in the extremities NF. When NF patients present with uncontrollable, life-threatening limb infections or when limb function is deemed irreparable, amputation can effectively halt the further progression of infection. Ramin et al. previously reported two cases where early amputation was life-saving [24]. A multicenter study in Texas reported a 13% amputation rate during hospitalization for NF patients, predominantly involving lower limbs [20]. This study found that early I&D reduced the risk of amputation, and this association remained consistent after adjusting for various confounding variables. One possible explanation for the higher amputation rate in extensive debridement group may be that persistent infection can exacerbate local toxin release, promote microthrombosis, and impair healing, thereby accelerating necrosis of fascial and muscle tissues [25–27]. Early I&D can be considered as a rational strategy for achieving local infection control in critically ill NF patients. However, the clinical application of amputation is subject to multiple factors, including patient willingness, financial status, and the infection location, which were not further investigated in our study. The potential protective effect of amputation against mortality in NF patients requires further validation through prospective, large-scale studies. Such research would provide higher-level evidence to guide precise management strategies for patients with extremity NF.

No significant decline in NF mortality has been observed over the past ten years, with in-hospital mortality consistently remaining at 12–14% [28, 29]. Surgical timing is emphasized in current treatment protocols, because mortality rates are reduced when surgery occurs within 6–12 h of admission [30, 31]. NF progresses rapidly, and extensive debridement surgery necessitates even closer multidisciplinary collaboration. Evidence indicates that patients achieve better treatment outcomes in high-volume centers [32, 33]. However, this may increase the mortality risk for those unable to access tertiary general hospitals promptly. In contrast, early I&D can achieve effective local decompression and drainage, demand less technical expertise, and cause minimal trauma, allowing for much faster intervention. Hong et al. compared outcomes of patients with advanced Vibrio NF complicated by sepsis who underwent early I&D or extensive debridement, and found significantly lower mortality in the I&D group [14]. Although no significant difference in mortality was observed between the two groups, the poorer baseline status of the I&D group confounded this comparison. After adjusting for factors including infection severity and nutritional status, early I&D was independently associated with a lower risk of mortality. Given that the I&D group achieved comparable mortality despite worse nutritional status and more severe infections, early I&D appears to serve as a temporizing strategy for critically ill NF patients who are poor candidates for extensive debridement surgery. This approach aligns with current guidelines for the management of patients with sepsis, which recommend minimally invasive interventions for hemodynamically unstable patients [34]. The nutritional status and NF stage are also considered as important factors influencing mortality in NF patients.

This retrospective study, with data collected from HIS, had a relatively small sample size. Besides, as a single-center study, potential selection and information biases were unavoidable. The conclusions of this study are primarily applicable to hospitals with established referral capabilities. In addition to surgical intervention, antibiotic therapy and wound care are components of NF management. Although enrolled patients received standardized anti‑infective and nursing care protocols at our institution, nuances in implementation could exist. As a retrospective study and given the considerable heterogeneity in causative microbiology among NF patients, we documented rather than controlled therapeutic variables, so some confounding factors are unavoidable. Given that there was no standardized decision-making protocol of surgical approach after admission, the determination was made by the medical team’s comprehensive assessment of the patient’s condition. As a result, the treatment allocation was non-randomized, leading to significant differences in baseline characteristics between the two groups. Patients in the I&D group generally presented with more advanced disease stages and poorer nutritional status. To mitigate this bias, we performed binary logistic regression analysis adjusting for key confounders, including disease stage, nutritional status, infectious parameters, and comorbidities. However, a high possibility of residual confounding remains. A direct comparison with an ideal control group of “patients with similar baseline severity who did not undergo early I&D” was not feasible, as withholding surgical intervention in such critically ill patients would be clinically and ethically unjustifiable. Despite these limitations, the fact that the I&D group achieved outcomes comparable to the extensive debridement group despite their initial disadvantage suggests that this intervention may hold significant clinical value. Especially for NF patients with poor conditions or those who cannot undergo timely extensive debridement surgery, early I&D represents an alternative bridging treatment option. Future prospective, multi-center studies are warranted to confirm and expand these results.

Extensive debridement remains the standard surgical approach for NF patients, but severe cases often experience delayed intervention due to poor tolerability. This study demonstrates that early incision and drainage significantly reduced amputation risk and mortality rate, offering a potential bridging or temporizing strategy to improve prognosis for those with poor nutritional status and advanced-stage NF. Given that current research evaluating efficacy of early I&D lacks, our study provides valuable data support and evidence-based guidance for clinical decision-making in severe NF management.

Recommended patient groups for early I&D

Early I&D may be warranted in patients presenting with the following criteria: (A)The diagnosis of NF is confirmed, but the patient is in a critical condition and unable to tolerate surgery. Or the patient is able to tolerate surgery, but timely extensive debridement cannot be performed (e.g., due to the inaccessibility of a surgical facility or lack of operating room availability).(B)When patients present with significant local symptoms (e.g., erythema, swelling, severe pain) and NF is highly suspected but not yet confirmed, early I&D may be performed to alleviate symptoms and obtain samples for etiological analysis, thereby guiding subsequent antibiotic therapy.

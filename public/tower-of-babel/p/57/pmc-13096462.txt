Dynamic wavelet transform normalized multi scale dense attention UNet with Binary Horse Herd Optimization for osteosarcoma diagnosis
Authors: S. Sumathi, R. Maheswari, A. Suresh Babu, S. Murugesan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Osteosarcoma is a highly aggressive primary bone cancer characterized by rapid progression and complex tissue heterogeneity, making accurate diagnosis and treatment planning particularly challenging. Conventional imaging-based diagnostic approaches often struggle to reliably differentiate viable tumor tissue, non-viable tumor regions, and healthy bone, leading to segmentation inaccuracies and elevated false-positive rates that can adversely affect clinical decision-making. To address these challenges, this work proposes DWT-N-MSDAUNet, a hybrid deep attention-based framework for precise osteosarcoma segmentation and multi-class classification.The proposed framework integrates Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization (DWT-N) for image enhancement, followed by a Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net (MSDA-UNet) to achieve accurate multiscale tumor segmentation. From the segmented representations, Binary Horse Herd Optimization (B-HHO) is employed to select the most discriminative features, reducing redundancy while preserving critical structural and textural information. Final classification into viable tumor, non-viable tumor, and healthy tissue classes is performed using a Bottleneck Transformer, optimized via the Puma Optimizer, to effectively model long-range spatial and contextual dependencies.The framework was evaluated on an osteosarcoma imaging dataset comprising approximately 1144labeled images, using fivefold cross-validation to ensure robustness and generalization. The MSDA-UNet achieved a Dice coefficient of 99.3% and a Jaccard index of 98.1%, with low boundary errors (ASSD = 0.3 mm, HD = 1.2 mm), outperforming UNet, UNet + + , Attention-UNet, and R2-UNet. The proposed Bottleneck Transformer classifier attained an overall accuracy of 99.5%, with precision, recall, and F1-scores above 99.5%, and a specificity of 99.55%, indicating a reduced false-positive rate. Class-wise evaluation further confirmed consistent performance across all tissue categories, with AUC values ranging from 0.96 to 0.98. Despite increased computational cost compared to lightweight models, the proposed approach demonstrated feasible deployment characteristics with an inference time of 63 ms per image, supporting its suitability for clinical diagnostic workflows. These results highlight the framework’s effectiveness in balancing high diagnostic accuracy with clinical reliability, particularly for biopsy guidance and treatment monitoring in osteosarcoma.

Introduction

Bone pathologies include a wide range of tumor diseases, metastatic diseases, and degenerative disorders, each of which imposes severe structural physiological demands on diagnosis.In the traditional diagnostic modes already in existence, manual interpretation of images is the determinant of computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and X-ray.Although these imaging methods are normally applied in clinical diagnosis, they are characterized by numerous pitfalls, such as being time-consuming, having a lot of inter-practitioner variability, and the inability to detect the slightest changes in bone structure.The foregoing issues have led to the attractiveness of computational paths, especially deep learning, as a means of enhancing the accuracy, consistency, and efficiency of the analysis of bone pathologies.In the initial years of development, computational imaging techniques concentrated primarily on improving segmentation to assist clinical processes, such as radiotherapy.One such example is the multi-attention dense network (MAD-Net), which is designed to segment functional bone marrow (FBM) in CT scans of the pelvis. This is important for limiting hematological toxicity during radiotherapy of pelvic-related cancers by offering adequate identification of high-activity FBM regions and improved radiological protection1.

MRI-based models have also been created to address the complexity and heterogeneity of musculoskeletal (MSK) tumors.An example is the use of multiscale convolutional attention of radiomics in tumor segmentation and classification by the MSAPN, which simplifies the process of diagnosing MSK tumors by guaranteeing a smaller role of human intervention in the identification of benign and malignant areas2.The spread of U-Net and its variations has been achieved owing to the introduction of deep learning algorithms, which allow the models to be used to precisely segment the anatomical structures of spinal MRI images after the models are augmented.Alterations such as U-Net + + have been developed to provide better delineation of spinal structures to obtain precise segmentation, which helps in diagnosis and treatment planning and removes tedious work for clinicians3.

Bone metastasis detection, however, is highly challenging because of the varying characteristics of lesions that accompany different primary tumors, which include those from lung, breast, and prostate cancers, to name a few. In order to address this pressing need, the Hybrid-AttUnet + + model, together with the BM-Seg dataset, seeks to improve metastasis segmentation through an intelligence-attention mechanism combined with a dual-decoder architecture, achieving a new standard of performance in automated metastasis detection4.In this respect, osteosarcoma segmentation in CT images is addressed by something like the advanced W-shaped network (AW-Net), which implements cascaded U-Nets augmented by multiscale channel attention blocks to enhance the accuracy of segmentation of smaller tumor regions for support of surgical planning5.Segmentation accuracy was further enhanced through the FCN-CRF work, which accurately constrains the boundaries while alleviating over-segmentation-preventing its application in real-time clinical uses6. The exact classification of bone tumors has resulted in several new deep learning developments, including FemurTumorNet, a model based on DenseNet to categorize proximal femoral bone tumors into benign, malignant, or tumor-free. It uses Grad-CAM for visualization, thereby helping clinicians highlight the abnormal areas, reducing the risk of misdiagnosis, especially in adequately manned resource-poor environments.7. As CNN architectures continue to evolve, hybrid architectures combining CNN and Transformers have gained relevance; for example, a hybrid of VGG-16 and Vision Transformer (ViT) models achieved considerable classification accuracy for tibial and femoral bone tumors by taking advantage of local and global feature extraction8.

To overcome overfitting and improve clinical applicability in the classification of bone tumors, GHA-DenseNet was introduced, which is a combination of DenseNet with Global Hybrid Attention Blocks (GHAB) and integrates both T1- and T2-weighted MRI images to classify femoral bone tumors without necessitating manual segmentation, thereby facilitating diagnosis and speeding up patient management delays9. To define spinal metastases, a U-Net-based model, nnUNet, was developed to automate segmentation while closely matching manual delineations. To remedy the often flawed reliance of smaller lesions in detection, nnUNet incorporates its unique loss functions to provide accurate segmentation in the clinical environment10.Deep learning has also achieved tremendous success in the identification of cancers of the bone marrow in CNN models refined by adaptive multi-objective algorithms that effectively differentiate between them, such as Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) and Multiple Myeloma (MM). More improved models have also been achieved by data augmentation, which provides better results, lower false positives, and significantly improves the rate of detection of complex bone marrow malignancies11.CNN-based supervised learning networks, such as ResNet101, in the specific task of problem-solving osteosarcomas, demonstrate better results in the precise classification of tumor types, which is an enormous leap in the accuracy of bone cancer diagnosis12.

The diagnosis and treatment of such complex and heterogeneous diseases of the bones, namely tumors, metastases, and degenerative diseases, is highly difficult because of their intricate structural attributes and the need for proper identification and localization of the lesions. Traditional diagnostic techniques mainly employ manual analysis of CT, MRI, and X-ray images which absorb a lot of time and are subject to practitioner variability. The routine methods often fail to touch the lesions in an early stage, critical for a timely diagnosis, accurate diagnosis12. New lines of deep learning have very recently made significant advances in automating the analysis of images with an aim to improving the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosis by opening a new level of alleviation of the burden on the medical polls and diminishing the variability in diagnosis. This potential emerging automation indicates that there is a continuing need for further development of specialized deep learning frameworks that can reliably analyze complex bone structures and provide more informed decision-making that motivates the need to tailor solutions to handle the unique challenges of bone pathology.

Such heterogeneous and complex diseases of the bones, that is, tumors, metastases, and degenerative diseases, are extremely difficult to diagnose and treat because of their complex structural features and the necessity of appropriate identification and localization of the lesions.Manual examination of CT, MRI, and X-ray as the major techniques of traditional diagnosis is time-consuming and can be subject to the variability of the practitioner.The regular procedures do not always reach the lesions in their earlier stages that are vital in making a diagnosis in time, proper diagnosis12.Other uses of deep learning have recently achieved great progress in the field of automizing image analysis with the goal of enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosis by introducing a new dimension of load offloading to medical polls and reducing the variability in diagnosis13.Such a possible future automation suggests that additional refinement of specialized deep learning structures capable of predicting robust analysis of complex bone configurations and more informed suggestions that drive the necessity to customize to meet the special demands of bone pathology.

Despite recent advances in deep learning–based osteosarcoma analysis, several critical limitations in existing approaches remain unresolved and continue to hinder clinical reliability. Most current segmentation and classification frameworks rely on single-scale convolutional representations or shallow attention mechanisms, which are insufficient for capturing the pronounced multi-scale heterogeneity, irregular tumor boundaries, and complex tumor–bone interfaces characteristic of osteosarcoma; this limitation is directly addressed in the proposed framework through Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization combined with a Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net, enabling enhanced frequency-domain detail preservation and explicit hierarchical context fusion. Furthermore, many prior methods treat segmentation and classification as independent tasks, leading to error propagation and limited contextual consistency between tumor localization and tissue characterization; in contrast, the proposed approach leverages segmentation-aware representations to guide downstream feature selection and classification, ensuring coherent spatial and semantic information flow across stages. In addition, existing models often retain high-dimensional and redundant feature sets, increasing susceptibility to overfitting and false-positive predictions; this issue is mitigated through Binary Horse Herd Optimization, which selects a compact set of highly discriminative features while preserving diagnostically relevant structural and textural information. Finally, purely CNN-based architectures struggle to model long-range spatial dependencies that are essential for distinguishing viable tumor tissue, non-viable tumor regions, and healthy bone; this limitation is overcome by integrating a Bottleneck Transformer optimized via the Puma Optimizer, enabling effective global contextual modeling and robust multi-class discrimination. Collectively, these design choices explicitly address the unresolved limitations of existing approaches, resulting in improved segmentation accuracy, reduced false positives, and enhanced clinical reliability in osteosarcoma diagnosis.

Beyond algorithmic accuracy, the proposed framework is designed with real-world clinical applicability in mind. Accurate differentiation between viable tumor tissue, non-viable tumor regions, and healthy bone is crucial for oncologists when planning surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. By combining precise segmentation with reliable multi-class classification, the proposed approach enables automated quantification of viable tumor regions, supporting objective assessment of disease progression and treatment response. The low false-positive rates and high specificity achieved by the model further enhance its reliability for clinical decision-making, reducing unnecessary biopsies and aiding treatment planning. As an end-to-end automated pipeline, the framework minimizes manual intervention, decreases inter-observer variability, and offers a dependable decision-support tool for clinicians managing osteosarcoma cases.Themajor contributions of the proposed work are as follows:Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization (DWT-N) enhances the quality of medical images by reducing noise and increasing contrast, allowing essential features to be distinguished for accurate segmentation.A Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net (MSDA-UNet) model was designed to extract highly detailed multi-scale features that support precise boundary detection between healthy and cancerous tissues.The Binary Horse Herd Optimization (B-HHO) algorithm was used to select the most informative features, thereby reducing data dimensionality while enhancing computational efficiency and model accuracy.The Bottleneck Transformer model performs classification by leveraging self-attention to capture spatial and contextual relationships, thereby enabling precise differentiation between healthy and diseased tissue.PUMA dynamically tunes the hyperparameters to improve model convergence and accuracy, thereby reducing misclassifications for dependable outcomes.A comparative analysis with existing methods demonstrated the superior performance of the proposed framework across several evaluation metrics, highlighting its advantages over traditional diagnostic approaches.An end-to-end pipeline integrates all processes from preprocessing to classification to enhance the diagnostic accuracy and dependability in the evaluation of osteosarcoma.

Related works

Bone imaging and segmentation methods have advanced a lot since then, and have moved the field away from basic approaches toward improved accuracy and efficiency in diagnostics. First, to increase the segmental accuracy and speed, especially for the mandible bones in CT images, U-Net-based CNN models were employed.Further reduction of overfitting Fully 3D segmentation, critical to both surgical planning and good results in highly complex medical conditions, was then attained by adding batch normalization and dropout14.Based on this, Mask R-CNN using a ResNet50 backbone was used to segment bones in X-ray images to assist with fracture and age determination diagnoses.Their smooth segmentation along the outline of the object would flow through the workflows of the radiologist, particularly when the radiologists do not have enough time to assist them15.Subsequently, based on these developments, an automated pipeline containing both the 2D and 3D U-Net models was created to produce biomechanical simulations of the femurs and vertebrae in such a manner that it would be comparable to manual expert annotations and massive bearing in clinical configurations that depend on finite element simulations16.

In parallel, a regional multi-instance learning mechanism was proposed toward a more efficient diagnostic workflow by establishing the source of the OBMC from H&E slides while minimizing the demand for several expensive IHC tests and giving pathologists the ability to provide a provisional diagnosis when there are little biopsy samples by analyzing preselected regions of interest17.Further enhancement to the logistic streamlining of diagnostic workflows was added by the YOLO-based deep-neural network model meant for bone lesion detection and classification in full-field radiographs, thereby further providing a reliable avenue for automated lesion recognition, also in cases that pose more challenges, all improving accuracy and greatly assisting the radiologists as a result of reduced manual workload18.The integration of early diagnosis and accurate diagnosis of bone cancer led to the development of preprocessing techniques that embedded median filtering along with segmentation techniques such as K-means clustering and Canny edge detection, so that the input CT was refined for classification jobs. This has been of immense potential to CNN models, particularly AlexNet, which has achieved high precision in the recognition of entities such as parosteal osteosarcoma, enchondroma, and osteochondroma, therefore, proving to be an indication of even higher quality early stage diagnostics19.

Mask2Former (Masked-Attention Mask Transformer) was created to achieve better bone tumor segmentation.Using a Swin-based transformer encoder and a multi-resolution decoder, the model successfully segmented and diagnosed CT bone tumors with high precision to facilitate clinical decision-making20.An important addition, the Pairwise Attention-Enhanced Adversarial Model (Pair-SegAM) aimed at bone segmentation with CT data, has been added to this model, where the discriminator possesses pairwise attention to enrich the feature presentation and provide segmentation accuracy, which is essential in surgery where accuracy is a crucial factor21.A systematic review of the literature on the subject by Rich et al. highlighted the increased role of U-Net variants in malignant bone lesion segmentation and observed that the models that included data augmentation and denoising algorithms achieved high Dice Similarity Coefficients (DSC) in most instances, thereby demonstrating the applicability of the approach to different datasets22.The principle behind creating modern segmentation approaches is the combination of the Active Contour Technique (ACT) and principal component analysis (PCA), which enables the identification and classification of cancerous tissues in MRI images23.

In bone cancer detection, Tsallis entropy-based image segmentation is coupled with EfficientNet-based CNN feature extraction as an additional contribution. The combination of their output with XGBoost optimized by Whale Optimization reduces the subjectivity of histopathological analysis and leads to high precision in automated diagnoses of bone cancer24.For MRI-based segmentation of osteosarcoma lesions, the SEAGNET framework is developed that implements edge-attention-guided segmentation to improve accuracy in malignant tumor detection through enhanced boundary localization25.Multitask deep learning frameworks have been introduced and applied to global and fine-grained surgical planning, especially in hip arthroplasty, to obtain high anatomical precision in segmenting pelvic bone structures and defining acetabular boundaries, both of which are crucial for prosthesis design26.

In a similar way, an ensemble deep-learning framework that combines multitasking learning with clinical data was made available to radiologists as a reliable classification aid in differentiating between primary bone tumors and infections, displaying diagnostic accuracy nearing that of seasoned clinicians27.Wavelet-based segmentation in MRI offers further support for establishing osteosarcoma diagnosis. The classifier based on Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN) came into being after noise reduction and feature isolation, with acceptable diagnostic results from a more advanced feature extraction process28. A modified Double U-Net model increases this accuracy by detecting bone metastasis in full-body scans. Background preprocessing, incorporation of negatives, and transfer learning strategies were utilized in improving diagnostic accuracy for metastatic cases29.

X-Ray based Bone Cancer Diagnosis Systems are all referenced through Deep Transfer-Based Bone Cancer Diagnosis System (DTBV) having adopted the feature extractor, VGG16 in SVM classifier which could be the best classifier according to the authors as compared to the previous models like InceptionV3 and ResNet50, hence provided with an additional technique through median filtering and feature selection based on mutual information pertinent to diagnostic accuracy30.Lastly, an advanced dual-view segmentation known as Efficient-BtrflyNet combined EfficientNetB7 with a butterfly network structure for sound segmentation across multiple bone regions in a whole-body scan. This is particularly helpful in high accuracy cancer metastases detection, viewing the posterior and anterior31.

Several recent studies have focused on deepening deep learning and feature engineering techniques to improve the results for medical images and cancer classification.Feature extraction using Inception-V3 is very popular for obtaining discriminative representations from intricate visual patterns32, while further integration with Bidirectional GRU networks has been shown to intensify sequential feature learning for enhanced diagnostic decision-making33.An additional newly proposed lightweight multiscale hybrid attention encoder-decoder architecture (MHAED-Net) also facilitates industrial segmentation problems by utilizing hybrid attention and transformer principles to achieve high performance at a minimal computational cost34.Recently, optimization-driven segmentation methods have emerged, such as a virus diffusion-based metaheuristic to avoid early convergence and segment crack defects in the industry using a methodology inspired by a diffusion search strategy35.In addition, some studies have combined probability-driven gradient modeling to advance active contour-based medical image segmentation against intensity variation challenges in ROI extraction36.Multilevel contextual feature fusion networks have proven to be effective in capturing spatial boundary information from CT volumes for COVID-19 lesion identification37.

Bidirectional gated three-dimensional multiscale fusion networks have been applied to neuroimaging studies to analyze hippocampal atrophy via the integration of deep spatial modeling with recurrent feature accumulation38.Within CAD pipelines, CNN-based methods still constitute the major approach and greatly profit from pretrained backbone models when the medical datasets remain small39.Multiscale Gaussian pyramid-based CNNs have also been applied to discriminate between benign and malignant breast tissues by facilitating gradual progressive texture learning40.Beyond imaging, feature selection through hybrid and swarm-based metaheuristic approaches has been studied, including optimizations driven by Game-kSHAP41, ICA-based gene extraction combined with Spider-Monkey optimization and genetic algorithms for cancer gene classification42, and wider machine learning-based predictive studies discussing the roles of supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning in cancer prognostics43.Research has also ventured into other areas, such as image captioning via clustering-based data reduction using LSTM variants44 and improved clustering strategies using genetic algorithms for categorical datasets45.

Traditional U-Net networks mainly rely on the uniform propagation of features by encoder-decoder skip connections, which restricts their capability to prioritize small and low-contrast lesions as well as irregular osteosarcomas.Attention mechanisms circumvent this drawback by selectively emphasizing diagnostically important areas and inhibiting background noise, and multi-scale attention further improves the comprehension of context by combining features of multiple receptive fields.The proposed MSDA-UNet uses dense multi-scale attention architectures that allow reusing features and enhancing the flow of gradients, which, in turn, allows achieving better boundary delineation and sensitivity to small tumor areas, with reported improvements of Dice around 5–10 percent better than standard U-Net variants in difficult medical imaging conditions.Simultaneously, high-dimensional deep features generate redundancy that may cause poor classification accuracy, which requires effective strategies for feature selection.

Transformer and attention-based models have gained relevance because they can create long-range links and a wide range of tissue structures.A recentwork stated that hybrid CNN-Transformer models are better at enhancing the accuracy of osteosarcoma and bone lesion segmentation with the help of global contextual information, but with increased computational requirements46.Anotherwork showed that multi-scale attention mechanisms outperform conventional U-Net skip connections in improving sensitivity to small and irregular tumor regions in musculoskeletal tumor evaluations using MRI47.Deep learning systems, such as HistoDX, show that, in the context of histopathology, comprehensive preprocessing, class balancing, and high-quality CNN backbones can be successfully used to achieve high diagnostic accuracy and cross-dataset generalization, which is a critical role of noise reduction and discriminative feature extraction in cancer diagnosis48.Likewise, the attention-directed findings of segmentation and classification of bone and tumor images state that no matter how substantial the accuracy gains are, there is still a challenge in dealing with noise generated by images, non-informative features, and computational costs49.

Although traditional bio-inspired optimizers, such as Genetic Algorithms and Particle Swarm Optimization, are widely applied, they are typically related to early convergence and lack flexibility in binary feature spaces.Binary Horse Herd Optimization provides a superior exploration exploitation trade-off, allowing a more useful selection of discriminatory features and reducing overfitting.Moreover, Transformer-based models have proven to be effective in long-range spatial and contextual dependency modeling in histopathology and medical imaging, but their computational cost is an issue.Bottleneck Transformer designs overcome this trade-off by using self-attention in a dimension reduction way that does not lose global context modeling or make it computationally infeasible.The combination of dense multi-scale attention, bio-inspired optimization of features, and efficient Transformer classification thus offers a moderated and successful framework for the accurate diagnosis of osteosarcoma.Such developments demonstrate a shift in the field towards more sophisticated, versatile, and accurate techniques aimed at meeting the complexities of bone health diagnostics (Table 1).Table 1Summary of Methodologies, Advantages, and Limitations in Bone Pathology Imaging Techniques.AuthorMethodologyAdvantagesLimitationsQin et al.1MAD-Net: Multi-attention Dense NetworkEnhanced segmentation of FBM through dense convolution and dual attentionLimited by the 2D nature; The proposed research aims to improve the deficiencies in segmentation and classification provided by custom-developed models and framework that include deep learning strategies especially pertinent to the diagnosis of bone pathologies. Labor-intensive manually derived interpreted images are central to the problem, as they cause inconsistency in diagnosis and subsequently in the commencement of treatment for critical pathologies such as osteosarcoma and metastatic bone diseases47.The customization of deep learning architecture is insufficient concerning detailed segmentation, accurate classification, and a platform for a diverse range of bone diseases50,51. In addition, the lack of standardized datasets and optimal hyperparameter optimization poses greater challenges to the formation of sound and applicable solutions for this problem.Therefore, this work presents a healthy proposition to plant these gaps in constructing a framework that accommodates high-end feature extraction, attention mechanisms, and adaptive optimization techniques that would provide a more plausible, efficient, and accurate solution for the diagnosis of complicated bone pathologies.

Methodology

The suggested medical image analysis framework is built in a sequential and combined pipeline in order to optimize the work throughout preprocessing, segmentation, and feature selection, and classification levels. First, the medical images are first processed using Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization (DWT -N) in order to reduce noise and improve contrast, such that diagnostically significant structures can be easily seen and processed thereafter.The improved images are then forwarded to Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net (MSDA-UNet) that does accurate segmentation by effectively capturing both local spatial and contextual information at various scales, allowing the distinction between normal and cancerous tissue areas. After the segmentation, Binary Horse Herd Optimization (B-HHO) is used to choose the most informative features and remove redundant or irrelevant ones and therefore the dimensionality of features is reduced to enhance computational efficiency.Subsequently the sophisticated feature would be passed through a Bottleneck Transformer-based classifier where the self-attention models long-range spatial and contextual correlation to reliably distinguish normal and diseased tissues. The resulting organization of preprocessing, segmentation, optimization and classification components provides a smooth and effective diagnostic pipeline, with each step in the pipeline reinforcing the effectiveness of the further one.This process continues to improve with the tuning of the hyperparameter to improve the maximization of the accuracy of classes with the minimization of computations of the Puma Optimizer, as indicated in Fig. 1.Fig. 1Proposed framework for bone pathology image analysis using deep learning techniques.

Preprocessing

The central element of this is dynamic wavelet transform normalization (DWT-N), which can improve and accomplish better segmentation of medical images.It is based on the multiresolution principles of wavelet transform to divide an image into its components, which represent different frequency bands.Such decomposition enables the separation of useful information, noise, and variations in intensity.DWT-N is used to increase the local properties of the images to enhance the contrast without destroying the basic structural information used to perform the segmentation tasks.

Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization (DWT-N) was selected over conventional normalization techniques such as Z-score normalization, histogram equalization, and CLAHE due to its ability to simultaneously perform noise suppression and multiscale feature enhancement. Unlike global intensity-based methods, DWT-N decomposes images into frequency sub-bands, enabling selective attenuation of high-frequency noise while preserving diagnostically relevant low- and mid-frequency tissue structures. This property is particularly beneficial for histopathology images, where subtle textural variations and boundary details are critical for accurate tumor segmentation. Prior studies have demonstrated that wavelet-based normalization improves contrast consistency and segmentation robustness in medical imaging, motivating its adoption in this work.

In the DWT-N process, an image I (x,y) is decomposing it into approximation and detail coefficients through one or more levels using a Daubechies wavelet (db4)which is well suited for medical image analysis due to its compact support and good localization properties. A three-level (k = 3) wavelet decomposition is applied, resulting in four sub-bands at each level: low-low (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${LL}_{k}$$\end{document}​), low–high (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${LH}_{k}$$\end{document}​), high-low (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${HL}_{k}$$\end{document}), and high-high (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${HH}_{k}$$\end{document}), representing the progressively finer horizontal, vertical, and diagonal details. This can be formulated mathematically as1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I\left(x,y\right)\to \{{LL}_{k}, {LH}_{k},{HL}_{k}, {HH}_{k}\}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${LL}_{k}$$\end{document}​ is the approximation sub-band that carries most of the structural information of the image, while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${LH}_{k}, {HL}_{k}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${HH}_{k}$$\end{document}​ carry the details focusing on edges and transitions. These coefficients, iteratively generated through application of the wavelet transform across multiple levels, are then refined for critical feature enhancement and noise minimization. This decomposition separates the input image into approximation and detail components, where the approximation coefficients preserve the overall bone structure, while the detail coefficients emphasize high-frequency variations corresponding to tumor boundaries and texture irregularities.

To suppress noise while preserving diagnostically relevant features, soft thresholding is applied to the high-frequency sub-bands. This strategy is particularly effective for reducing staining-related noise and fine-grain artifacts in H&E images without blurring important cellular boundaries. Following thresholding, intensity normalization is performed independently on each sub-band using min–max normalization, mapping pixel intensities to the range [0,1]:2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${I}_{norm}\left(x,y\right)=\frac{I\left(x,y\right)-{I}_{min}}{{I}_{max}-{I}_{min}}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${I}_{min} and {I}_{max}$$\end{document} stand for the specific minimum and maximum intensities in each subband respectively. After inverse wavelet transform is applied back to these coefficients, the DWT-N method produces an enhanced image with a more uniform intensity distribution and better-localized details. The normalization step scales the wavelet coefficients to a uniform range, reducing intensity bias and improving contrast between healthy tissue and osteosarcoma-affected regions, which is essential for reliable downstream segmentation.Therefore, DWT-N gives an artificially enhanced set of finer-quality inputs for segmentation models so that subtle features are preserved, and artifacts minimized. This normalization allows to improve visual quality of the image thus becoming a valuable preprocessing step in any medical imaging pipeline by augmenting the accuracy of downstream segmentation. Segmentation

The MSDA-UNet-Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net-is one of the recent extensions in terms of the initial U-Net model of the more complicated tasks the model performs in this case: segmentation of normal and diseased bone tissues.The model architecture is accompanied by improvements such as dense multiscale connectivity and spatial attention mechanisms to enable more detailed and context-sensitive feature extraction at varying resolutions.This is the aspiration of the MSDA-UNet to achieve a highly accurate segmentation with a more comprehensive feature extraction process that allows obtaining a sharp outline of the boundaries and contextual information at multiple scales. Thus, MSDA-UNet is more effective than classical U-Net in terms of preserving details and concentrating attention on areas that are significant to the input image.In MSDA-UNet, the encoder receives the hierarchical properties of the input image.This is accomplished through a series of convolutions, each of which removes the spatial occurrence of edges, textures, and broader structural features at different resolutions. Unlike the basic U-Net, MSDA-UNet incorporates dense connections within each layer of the encoder. Moreover, the pooling layers within the encoder work to progressively downsample the input, while reducing its dimensionality, yet preserving the main features. This operation enables effective reuse of multi-level features, allowing both low-level spatial details and high-level semantic information to contribute to accurate tumor region delineation.This act helps in capturing a broader context at each scale, and then attention mechanisms come in as a refinement stage, allowing the model to emphasize the more relevant areas in the image.In particular, attention maps α dynamically weight each spatial location in the feature maps according to its relevance to the segmentation task, thus allowing the model to separate foreground regions from the background such as those containing cancerous tissue. The attention mechanism thus assigns higher importance to regions expected to contain crucial information, such as cancerous tissues, driving the model toward accurate segmentation with minimal interference from the background.The attention weights quantify the relative importance of spatial features, enabling the network to focus on clinically relevant tumor regions while suppressing background bone structures.

To explicitly model multi-scale contextual dependencies, MSDA-UNet employs dense multi-scale feature aggregation across encoder stages. The last encoder block in the proposed MSDA-UNet acts as a feature selection within the proposed framework.At this stage, the encoder learns how to summarize information across different scales through dense skip connections and refines spatial relevance using attention gates.This encoder output combines low-level image cues, such as edges and textures, mid-level structural patterns bound to tissue boundaries, and high-level contextual information that may indicate the morphological representation of the tumor.The final encoder block generated a compact feature tensor of 256 × 256 × 64 for an input image of size 1024 × 1024.Each spatial location in this tensor contains a 64-dimensional descriptor that summarizes the local appearance and contextual importance of the region.This encoder tensor is assumed to be the complete feature pool for optimization.The attention-refined tensor is flattened into a single high-dimensional feature vector and processed through the B-HHO algorithm to select only the most discriminative components that are passed on to the Bottleneck Transformer classifier.

The Binary Horse Herd Optimization Algorithm (B-HHO) was also utilized as one of the key feature selection algorithms to enhance the relevance of the extracted features of Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net (MSDA-UNet) and compress the data dimensionality.The selection process makes the computations more efficient and improves the overall efficiency of the model because only the most valuable and context-relevant features are retained.The B-HHO algorithm is based on the social and chasing behavior of horse groups, which in this case results in a heuristic method applicable to binary space targeting feature selection in classification.The feature set was also reduced using B-HHO to eliminate redundant and irrelevant points that may otherwise cause noise and discourage the predictive ability of the model.This imposed selectivity would then precipitate the input and force its application to a more efficient and correct model.

In the process of segmentation with MSDA-UNet, several features are obtained, such as low-level textures and edge details, mid-level structural features, and high-level contextual representations.B-HHO is performed to determine the significance of these characteristics, and only those characteristics that are essential in healthcare to differentiate between healthy and diseased tissues are retained.The features that have not been discarded can be boundary information, texture gradient, and areas of interest determined through attention mechanisms, which are a combination that helps obtain a complete understanding of the tissue structure.These are the most important features for classification, particularly in conditions where there may be a need to distinguish between closely related tissues, such as cancerous and healthy bone tissues. The B-HHO algorithm represents all subsets of candidate features as binary vectors, where 1 indicates that the features are used by the MSDA-U-Net, and 0 indicates that they are not used.This binary format enables the algorithm to explore a combination of features that are selected over the feature set as a whole, sampled by the MSDA-U-Net.The optimal feature subset search commences with the initiation of a population known as horse positions, each of which is associated with a subset of features that is unique under a candidate solution.The B-HHO is then optimized in two distinct stages: exploration and exploitation.

Through the exploration phase, B-HHO attempts to liberate the horse positions across the feature space so that convergence does not become premature for suboptimal solutions, thereby ensuring that a wide range of feature subsets is being considered. Such a movement pattern would encourage the algorithm to explore a wide array of feature subsets, thereby improving the chances of identifiying the optimal combination of features to best represent the data. For outputs of the MSDA-UNet, this means combinations of texture, edge, structural, and contextually relevant features will be examined as to those that most effectively distinguish the tissues.

Through exploitation, where horses have less distance among each other, the B-HHO algorithm makes the movement diversity amongst them weak and tries to get onto the best subset of features through high exploitation. Classification

The selected features associated with previous segmentation and feature selection processes are integrated by this bottleneck transformer classifier, which also uses effective processes in school learning. It was considered best suited classifier architectural arrangement for complex and heterogeneous data representations as it maintains correlation through multiple modalities, important aspects for accurate classification in medical imaging where spatial and contextual features interact. Learning interdependencies between features from different sources or representing the different characteristics of target tissue is guaranteed by bottleneck transformer to fully leverage on those interactions.

The Bottleneck Transformer self-attention mechanism which can transform and integrate the various features between the modalities to appropriately compress high-dimensional data while preserving key relationships. The input feature, expressed as a sequence of vectors,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X={[x}_{1},{x}_{2},\dots ,{x}_{n}\}$$\end{document}, was first subjected to the embedding layer that translates \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x}_{i}$$\end{document}​into a larger dimension space. This step is made possible for modality interaction: It is mathematically expressed as19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}{\prime}=Embedding (X)$$\end{document}

Here X′expresses the embedded feature sequence; afterward, the model uses self-attention on these features to detect dependencies related to the different modalities- how they could interact and complement each other concerning their relevance to classification. Here, self-attention in a Bottleneck Transformer calculates the relationship each feature vector has with every other feature vector by producing three matrices for each input sequence: query matrix Q, key matrix K, value matrix V. Each feature vector in X′ generates an equation for creating these matrices:20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Q={X}{\prime}{W}_{q }, K={X}{\prime}{W}_{k}, V={X}{\prime}{W}_{v}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${W}_{q },{W}_{k },{and W}_{v}$$\end{document} ​ are trainable weight matrices specific to the queries, keys, and values, respectively. The value of the attention score, which defines how one feature vector relates to another, is determined by taking the dot product of query Q of one vector and key K of the other. It is scaled by the square root of the dimension of key \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${d}_{k}$$\end{document}​ for stability, producing the scaled dot-product attention:21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Attention\left(Q,K,V\right)=softmax\left(\frac{{QK}^{T}}{\sqrt{{d}_{k}}}\right)V$$\end{document}

Softmax is used to normalize the scores, which allows the model to effectively compensate for the contribution of each feature to the point of classification.The Bottleneck Transformer, with its self-attention mechanism, focuses on highly suggestive interactions of the classification label, for example, when differentiating between interactions between patterns in cancerous and normal tissues. The subset F_selected is then subjected to the bottleneck layer, effectively lowering the model complexity while retaining essential information. This enables the Bottleneck Transformer to achieve classification accuracy while remaining tractable in computational resource usage.The obtained selected features were then passed through a feedforward neural network (FFN) for transformation and integration. In the proposed Bottleneck Transformer, self-attention is explicitly formulated by projecting the selected feature tensor FFF into query, key, and value embeddings using linear transformations, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Q={X}{\prime}{W}_{q }, K={X}{\prime}{W}_{k}, V={X}{\prime}{W}_{v}$$\end{document} ​, followed by scaled dot-product attention computed as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Attention\left(Q,K,V\right)=softmax\left(\frac{{QK}^{T}}{\sqrt{{d}_{k}}}\right)V$$\end{document}. To reduce computational overhead while preserving discriminative representations, a bottleneck design is employed using a 1 × 1 convolution that compresses the channel dimension to one-fourth of its original size before attention, thereby maintaining contextual reasoning with reduced complexity. Hyperparameter optimization of the Bottleneck Transformer is carried out using the Puma Optimizer (Puma-Opt), a cat-inspired metaheuristic in which parameter updates follow a velocity-based rule \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$v=wv+{c}_{1}{r}_{1}\left({P}_{best}-x\right)+{c}_{2}{r}_{2}({g}_{best}-x)$$\end{document}, with inertia weight ω linearly decayed from 0.9 to 0.4 to balance exploration and exploitation. Compared with conventional optimizers such as Adam, Puma-Opt demonstrated improved convergence stability and yielded approximately 3% higher classification accuracy, particularly under class-imbalanced conditions, by dynamically adapting learning rate and weight decay parameters. This optimization strategy enables the Bottleneck Transformer to achieve robust classification while maintaining computational efficiency.

Unlike the attention gates used in Attention U-Net, which perform spatial gating by suppressing irrelevant encoder features based on decoder guidance, the proposed attention mechanism operates on globally selected feature embeddings after segmentation and feature selection. Attention U-Net gates are localized, additive, and resolution-dependent, whereas the proposed Bottleneck Transformer employs global self-attention that explicitly models long-range dependencies across the entire feature space. Furthermore, in contrast to conventional transformer-based multi-head attention, which relies on multiple parallel attention heads and incurs quadratic complexity with respect to feature dimension, the proposed design adopts a single-head bottleneck attention strategy with channel compression via 1 × 1 convolution. This reduces computational overhead while retaining contextual reasoning capability, making it more suitable for medical imaging tasks with limited data and high-resolution features. As a result, the proposed attention mechanism is neither a direct extension of Attention U-Net nor a standard transformer block, but a hybrid formulation tailored for efficiency-aware medical image classification.

Optimization

The Puma Optimizer is the type of optimizer that tunes the model on-the-fly concerning hyperparameters for optimization of classificational accuracy and reduction of misclassification rates. The Puma Optimizer will adapt adaptive and nimbly in their hunting styles, allowing efficient convergence and the goodness of solution for hyperparameter optimization. By fine-tuning itself via continual, small overall adjustments of critical hyperparameters like learning rate, momentum, and regularization, The Puma Optimizer is intelligent by also possessing a certain degree of adaptability toward the various feature distributions and refining the prediction quality, thus enhancing the prediction’s overall accuracy.

The balancing act between exploration and exploitation lies at the heart of the optimization procedure in Puma Optimizer, allowing the model to search for optimal hyperparameters within a limited range and gradually converge on the most appropriate ones via iterations. Mathematically, the position \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{i}$$\end{document}​ of each candidate solution (which represents a hyperparameter configuration) was updated from the current position directed toward the best candidate among all the candidates. Subsequently, the movement of each of the candidate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{i}$$\end{document}​ at iteration t + 1 can be expressed as follows:25\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{i}\left(t+1\right)={X}_{i}\left(t\right)+\alpha \cdot \left({P}_{best}-{X}_{i}\left(t\right)\right)+\beta \cdot ({X}_{leader}-{X}_{i}\left(t\right))$$\end{document}

Here \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${P}_{best}$$\end{document}​ represents the position found so far to be globally the best through the process, while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{leader}$$\end{document}​ is the candidate in this current iteration that is considered the leader; the term α is the step-size parameter controlling the scale of exploration, and β is an exploitation parameter that helps drive the search for promising solutions. These dynamics are able to adjust so that the optimizer progressively moves toward hyperparameters maximizing classification performance while avoiding the trap of local optima.

As in the case with the update equations, the Puma Optimizer modifies constraints α and β adaptively according to the iteration count and performance metrics. Thus, in the earlier stages of the search process, the optimizer can afford to explore broadly, and now, in the later stages, it can focus on exploiting intensively. The step sizes α and β were modified according to the equations:26\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha ={\alpha }_{init}\cdot \mathrm{exp}\left(-\gamma \cdot t\right), \beta ={\beta }_{init}\cdot (1-\mathrm{exp}\left(-\delta \cdot t\right))$$\end{document}where ​\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\alpha }_{init}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\beta }_{init}$$\end{document}​ denote the initial values, while γ and δ are the decay factors that control the rate of exploration and exploitation intensification. Thus, the modifications allow the optimizer an initial emphasis on a broad exploration of the hyperparameter space, and later on focuses on refinement of the high-impact parameter combinations. The optimization then entails fitness evaluation where a fitness score is computed for every hyperparameter configuration evaluated for performance. The fitness score \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f({X}_{i})$$\end{document} is computed as follows on the basis of classification accuracy and misclassification rate:27\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f\left({X}_{i}\right)=Accuracy\left({X}_{i}\right)-\uplambda \cdot \text{Misclassification Rate}({X}_{i})$$\end{document}where λ is a balancing coefficient that weighs the penalty for misclassification and thus ensures that the optimizer gives preferential consideration to configurations with low error rates. Another aspect of the Puma Optimizer is the adaptation of an inertia weight ω that provides for convergence stability. The inertia weight ω modulates the influence of the last step on the current update and is computed as follows:28\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega ={\omega }_{init}-\frac{{\omega }_{init}-{\omega }_{final}}{{max}_{iter}}\times t$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{init}$$\end{document}​ and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{final}$$\end{document} the initial and final values of the inertial weight, respectively, with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${max}_{iter}$$\end{document} being the maximum iteration count. This adaptive weight allows the optimizer to exert wide exploration from the beginning to ensure convergence at an early stage.

The optimization loop ran until some stopping criteria, such as the maximum number of iterations or minimal improvement in classification accuracy, were triggered. The end product was an optimal hyperparameter set \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{opt}$$\end{document}​, which minimizes the differences between predicted and actual labels while improving the classifier’s accuracy.29\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{opt}=arg\underset{{X}_{i}}{\mathrm{max}}f({X}_{i})$$\end{document}

Such optimized parameters provide \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{opt}$$\end{document} ​to the classifier’s robustness, which refers to a suitable setting, likely increasing accuracy and stability across various datasets as shown in Fig. 6. More valuable than all these is the adaptive optimization process of Puma; it has made improvements on the classifier such that it fits very well into medical imaging applications, where very fine details have to be interpreted accurately. Through selective tuning over time, the predictive power and generalization quality of the model are improved so that the classification results can be trusted for complicated diagnostic scenarios in a medical context.Fig. 6Flowchart of feature selection process using adaptive optimization algorithm.

The terms ''Binary Horse Herd Optimization’' and '‘Puma Optimizer’' are derived from behavioral analogy, but they are not meant to imply in this work that such methods will be strict biological models. However, they express the high-level ideas that inspired the search dynamics of these algorithms. As a matter of fact, both techniques work on the basis of being mathematical optimization methodology to tune candidate solutions via their controlled exploration and convergence rules. These algorithms do not rely on or attempt to recreate real animal behavior.They utilize deterministic update equations that channel the search into better feature subsets and hyperparameter settings with rule exclusion. To avoid ambiguity, this work focuses on the computational aspects of B-HHO as an inferior search mechanism on informative feature selection and Puma an adaptive mechanism for refining learning parameters, rather than any biological inference.

An exponential decay learning rate scheduler was adopted to allow rapid convergence during early epochs while enabling stable fine-tuning in later stages. A dropout rate of 0.25 was chosen to mitigate overfitting without suppressing informative features. The batch normalization momentum of 0.99 ensures stable running statistics under limited batch sizes. Other parameters, including gradient clipping and batch size, were tuned via validation performance to balance convergence stability and generalization.

To ensure reproducibility, more hyperparameter configurations are provided here for the B-HHO Optimizer. The parameters control the exploration–exploitation tradeoff and the adaptive update laws of the candidate solutions. The computational complexity of the B-HHO algorithm is O(P × I × D), where P denotes the population size, I the maximum number of iterations, and D the feature dimensionality. In the proposed framework, B-HHO is applied only to the reduced feature set obtained after MSDA-UNet segmentation, which significantly limits the effective search space. With a population size of 20 and a maximum of 50 iterations, the optimization process converged consistently within 30–35 iterations, indicating stable convergence behavior. The gradual decay of the exploration coefficient (α) and the controlled growth of the exploitation coefficient (β) ensured a smooth transition from global exploration to local refinement, preventing premature convergence. Discussion

Advantages and disadvantages of the proposed framework

The hybrid framework that we propose has several advantages allowing for highly developed performance in osteosarcoma diagnosis. Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization improves the structural clarity in medical images by increasing contrast and noise suppression so that very precise segmentation is allowed directly. In the Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net, dense feature reuse and spatial focus mechanisms help capture the fine contours around the tissues to give highly accurate delineation of the tumor regions. The filtering of redundant features from the most informative representations thus reduces processing overhead and improves computation efficiency through the Binary Horse Herd Optimization algorithm. The introduction of the Bottleneck Transformer, with the aid of self-attention, for contextual refinement provides an added layer of reliability in classification. This Puma-based adaptive optimization is robust in improving learning dynamics and generalization to unseen samples by hastening convergence, thereby stabilizing model training. Though these features sound impressive, they come with some drawbacks. Training requires more computation than lightweight CNN models due to extra components and attention mechanisms. In addition, even though the technique worked well for the dataset that they used, further validation is needed within larger multi-institutional datasets so that generalizability may be evaluated for broader clinical populations. Compression of the model for real-time deployment or some specific optimization for hardware may well be necessary to support resource-constraint environments.

To ensure a fair and meaningful evaluation, the proposed framework is compared against both conventional baseline models and recent state-of-the-art techniques, each serving a distinct benchmarking purpose. Conventional baselines, including U-Net, U-Net + + , Attention U-Net, R2-U-Net, and standard CNN-based classifiers, are widely adopted in medical image segmentation and classification and provide a well-established reference for assessing incremental architectural improvements. In contrast, state-of-the-art methods, such as transformer-based and hybrid CNN–Transformer architectures, represent the current performance ceiling in osteosarcoma and histopathological image analysis by incorporating advanced attention and global context modeling. These models were selected to evaluate the competitiveness of the proposed framework against modern designs that emphasize long-range dependency modeling and feature interaction. By including both categories, the comparative analysis demonstrates not only clear performance gains over traditional architectures but also the ability of the proposed method to match or surpass contemporary state-of-the-art approaches, thereby strengthening the validity and credibility of the experimental evaluation.

The high performance of the suggested framework is the result of the proper integration of the key elements. Dynamic Wavelet Transform Normalization improves the quality of the input by reducing noise and boosting the contrast which helps in extracting the features of the tumors reliably. Multi-Scale Dense Attention U-Net can learn both complementary low–, mid-, and high-level features, thus facilitating correct segmentation of heterogeneous tumor areas. Binary Horse Herd Optimization will choose non-redundant and informative features thus enhancing resistance to imaging variability. Lastly, the Bottleneck Transformer captures the long-range interdependences in order to combine local and global information in a way that is effective. The coordinated interaction of frequency-domain enhancement, multi-scale spatial attention, optimization-driven feature compactness, and transformer-based contextual modeling enables stable learning, strong generalization, and consistent performance across segmentation and classification tasks.

Limitations of the proposed approach

While the proposed framework is strong in its diagnosis, there exist disadvantages which also need consideration. This model is trained and tested on a moderately sized data set, which might fail to account for the diverse presentations of osteosarcoma when such patients are seen in more distant clinical settings; therefore, additional validation is expected on a larger multicenter dataset to check robustness and reliability in terms of application in the real world. Furthermore, the architecture itself is quite expensive in terms of resources for training. The introduction of dense attention mechanisms, transformer modules, and optimization strategies often cuts costs when deployed in less resourceful hardware settings. The other barrier ahead is increasing the inference speed to an acceptable clinical standard to enable real-time usage in massively parallel diagnostic workflows. Another serious missing element is external validation and prospective clinical testing: Not that this is limiting generalizability across differences in imaging modalities or acquisition protocols or patient populations but it certainly does impose limitations. Future research should also focus on model compression, the use of lightweight transformer variations, and domain adaptation protocols to overcome these challenges and ensure a smooth transition to the clinical environment.

Conclusion

Summary

The proposed framework provides a robust and cohesive solution for osteosarcoma diagnosis, incorporating DWT-N preprocessing for image enhancement, MSDA-UNet segmentation, B-HHO feature selection, and Bottleneck Transformer classification, optimized by Puma strategy. The experimental results validate the strength of this framework: MSDA-UNet showed a Dice score of 99.3% and Jaccard Index of 98.1%, surpassing existing segmentation models such as UNet, UNet + + , Attention-UNet, and R2-UNet. With classification accuracies exceeding 99.5%, the Bottleneck Transformer achieved precision, recall, F-score, and specificity all above 99.5%, cueing that ViT, Swin Transformer, EfficientNet, YOLO, and GPT-4 do not perform as well. The results indicate that multiscale feature extraction, targeted feature refinement, and attention-based classification offer good enhancement in diagnostic reliability.

Limitations

The results were mainly limited to the single-center dataset, while there were nevertheless some potentials for generalization; further validation across independent cohorts is warranted to support generalization.Additionally, the current framework operates on 2D image representations and does not explicitly exploit volumetric (3D) contextual information that could further improve tumor localization and assessment. From a computational perspective, although the Puma-optimized Bottleneck Transformer improves accuracy, it introduces additional computational overhead compared to lightweight CNN-based models, which may restrict direct deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments without further optimization.Although the framework has shown strong results, it has been tested using small domain-specific datasets only, and this could be limiting to its generalizable nature in case of domain changes due to differences in imaging protocols. Attention mechanisms, optimization processes and transformer modules impose a significant computational cost which might hinder real-time application, and the supervised learning paradigm requires large amounts of annotated data.

Future work

The future research directions will focus on domain-based learning methods, model compression, and large scale cross center validation to increase robustness and clinical applicability.Future investigations will aim to confirm the proposed framework’s effectiveness using multi-center and larger-scale datasets to enhance its robustness and generalizability. The framework will be expanded to include multi-modal learning by integrating MRI, histopathology, and CT data, offering a more thorough diagnostic evaluation. The exploration of incorporating explainable AI (XAI) methods, such as SHAP and attention visualization, will be undertaken to enhance model transparency and build clinical trust. Additionally, efforts will be made to optimize the framework for real-time or near-real-time deployment, alongside conducting prospective clinical trials to evaluate its practical utility. Lastly, the framework will be modified to diagnose and analyze other bone sarcomas and metastatic bone diseases, thereby increasing its clinical relevance.

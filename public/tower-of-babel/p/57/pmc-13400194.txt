Temporary airway stenting as a bridge to chemotherapy in primary mediastinal Ewing's sarcoma with critical tracheal compression: A case report
Authors: Maryam Mazraehei Farahani, Sara Mohammadi, Negar Golkarami, Mitra Rezaei, Arda Kiani, Sharareh Seifi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrimary mediastinal Ewing sarcoma is an exceptionally rare and aggressive malignancy that may rapidly lead to life-threatening compression of mediastinal structures. Management is particularly challenging when critical airway obstruction is present.Case presentationA 38-year-old woman presented with progressive dyspnea and cough. Chest imaging revealed a large right paratracheal mediastinal mass causing severe extrinsic tracheal compression with near-complete airway narrowing. Diagnosis was established by EBUS-TBNA, and histopathological and immunohistochemical findings were consistent with Ewing sarcoma. Given the imminent risk of respiratory failure, diagnostic EBUS-TBNA and airway stenting were performed during the same procedural session under general anesthesia. A fully covered self-expanding metallic stent was placed to secure the airway and enable safe initiation of systemic chemotherapy. The patient subsequently received VAC/IE chemotherapy. Follow-up imaging demonstrated marked tumor regression, and the stent was successfully removed after three months. At 6-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic without clinical or radiological evidence of recurrence.ConclusionTemporary tracheal stenting may serve as an effective bridge to definitive therapy in selected patients with mediastinal Ewing's sarcoma and critical airway obstruction. Multidisciplinary management is essential in such high-risk cases.

Introduction

Ewing sarcoma is a highly aggressive malignant neoplasm belonging to the family of Ewing sarcoma tumors and is characteristically associated with EWSR1 gene rearrangements. While primarily affecting bone in children and adolescents, extraskeletal Ewing's sarcoma (EES) is rare and presents unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenges [[1], [2], [3]].

Primary mediastinal extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma is exceedingly rare and may present with rapid local progression and compression of critical mediastinal structures, including the tracheobronchial tree. In such cases, acute airway compromise may require urgent intervention before definitive oncologic therapy can be initiated.

Diagnosing mediastinal Ewing sarcoma requires a multidisciplinary approach incorporating advanced imaging and immunohistochemical (IHC) profiling to distinguish it from other small round blue cell tumors. Management is particularly complex when critical airway compromise exists, as chemotherapy may not provide immediate relief.

Here, we describe a 38-year-old female with primary mediastinal ewing sarcoma and near-complete tracheal obstruction. We highlight the successful use of a temporary self-expanding metallic stent (SEMS) to stabilize the airway, serving as a vital bridge to systemic chemotherapy.

Clinical presentation

A 38-year-old previously healthy woman presented to our center with a 2-month history of progressive dyspnea, persistent cough, and anorexia. On physical examination, she exhibited signs of respiratory distress. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed preserved cardiac function with an ejection fraction of 55% and normal pulmonary artery pressure (20 mmHg). Chest imaging demonstrated a large right paratracheal mediastinal mass causing critical extrinsic tracheal compression, resulting in approximately 90% narrowing of the tracheal lumen (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Chest CT scan showing a large right paratracheal mediastinal mass causing critical extrinsic tracheal compression with approximately 90% narrowing of the tracheal lumen.

Given the high risk of respiratory failure, diagnostic bronchoscopy combined with endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) was performed under general anesthesia. Due to the high-grade tracheal stenosis, the flexible bronchoscope could not be advanced beyond the obstructing lesion; however, sufficient tissue was successfully obtained through EBUS-TBNA for diagnosis. Histopathological examination revealed a densely cellular neoplasm characterized by small round-to-oval cells with scant cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei, and high mitotic activity. Immunohistochemical profiling showed strong diffuse positivity for CD99, FLI1, cyclin D1, BCL2, and TLE1, while tumor cells were negative for cytokeratin, p63, CD117, CD34, and hormone receptors. These findings were highly consistent with primary extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Histopathological examination of mediastinal Ewing's sarcoma using (H&E) staining, revealing a densely cellular neoplasm with small round blue cells, hyperchromatic nuclei, increased mitotic activity, and areas of necrosis.

Histopathological examination of mediastinal Ewing's sarcoma using (H&E) staining, revealing a densely cellular neoplasm with small round blue cells, hyperchromatic nuclei, increased mitotic activity, and areas of necrosis.

Because of the critical degree of airway narrowing and the imminent risk of respiratory failure, airway stenting was performed during the same procedural session immediately following tissue acquisition. Based on multidisciplinary consensus, urgent airway stabilization was planned as a bridge to systemic therapy. Bronchoscopic evaluation demonstrated severe extrinsic tracheal compression without a significant endoluminal tumor component; therefore, endoscopic debulking was not considered appropriate. An 18 × 60 mm fully covered self-expanding metallic stent (SEMS) was introduced in a crimped configuration through an 8.5-mm rigid bronchoscope and deployed under direct bronchoscopic visualization without fluoroscopic guidance. The fully covered design was selected to minimize granulation tissue ingrowth and facilitate subsequent stent removal following tumor regression. Stent deployment was technically successful without procedural complications and resulted in immediate symptomatic improvement.

FDG-PET/CT was subsequently performed for staging and demonstrated a suspicious iliac lesion. CT-guided biopsy of this lesion showed no evidence of malignancy, and metastatic disease was therefore not confirmed.

The patient subsequently commenced systemic chemotherapy using the VAC/IE protocol (vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide). Surveillance bronchoscopy was performed every six weeks to assess airway patency and stent position. Throughout treatment, the stent remained well positioned with no evidence of migration or obstructive granulation tissue. After three months, follow-up CT imaging demonstrated complete resolution of the external tracheal compression, and the stent was successfully removed. At 6-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic, with no radiological or clinical evidence of disease recurrence (Fig. 3).Fig. 3(a) chest CT scan of the patient after 3 months showing complete resolution of external compression on the trachea. Discussion

Extraskeletal Ewing's sarcoma (EES) involving the mediastinum is an exceptionally rare entity, accounting for a very small proportion of EES cases and presenting unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. Unlike the classic osseous form that predominantly affects children and adolescents, mediastinal EES may occur in adults and is often associated with aggressive local behavior and rapid progression [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Due to the confined anatomical space of the mediastinum, tumor growth can result in compression of vital structures, leading to severe complications such as airway obstruction or superior vena cava syndrome [3,5].

The diagnosis of mediastinal EES remains challenging because of the broad differential diagnosis of small round blue cell tumors, including lymphoma, small cell carcinoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma [6,7]. In this context, immunohistochemical markers such as CD99 and FLI1 play a pivotal role in supporting the diagnosis, as demonstrated in our case [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. Although molecular confirmation of EWSR1 gene rearrangement is considered the diagnostic gold standard, it was not performed in our patient due to limited availability, which represents a limitation of this report.

A major clinical challenge in our case was the presence of critical airway compromise at presentation. While systemic chemotherapy with regimens such as VAC/IE remains the cornerstone of treatment and is associated with favorable outcomes in localized disease [[10], [11], [12]], immediate initiation of chemotherapy alone may be unsafe in the setting of near-complete airway obstruction due to the risk of sudden respiratory deterioration. Therefore, prompt airway stabilization becomes a priority in such high-risk scenarios.

Endobronchial stenting is widely used in the management of malignant central airway obstruction, primarily as a palliative or bridging intervention [[13], [14], [15]]. Current guidelines support airway stenting in cases where immediate surgical resection is not feasible and rapid restoration of airway patency is required [13]. However, the application of airway stenting specifically in mediastinal EES is rarely reported, and available evidence is limited to isolated case reports. in our case, the metallic stent was introduced in a crimped state through the 8.5-mm rigid bronchoscope and deployed at the target site without fluoroscopic guidance, after which it expanded successfully. The mechanical design of airway stents is important because an ideal stent should provide sufficient resistance to extrinsic compression while minimizing migration and facilitating deployment and removal [16]. Our approach is consistent with prior work showing that airway stenting remains a useful intervention in central airway obstruction, and that alternative deployment methods may be valuable when rigid bronchoscopy imposes technical limitations [17].

In this context, our case highlights the potential role of temporary self-expanding metallic stent (SEMS) placement as a bridge-to-therapy strategy in selected patients with mediastinal EES and critical airway obstruction. The fully covered SEMS provided immediate relief of airway compression, stabilization of the patient's respiratory status, and allowed safe initiation of systemic chemotherapy. Given the well-recognized chemosensitivity of Ewing's sarcoma [18], substantial tumor regression was achieved, permitting successful stent removal after three months without complications. These findings suggest that airway stenting in selected cases may function as a reversible temporizing measure rather than solely as a palliative intervention.

For staging, FDG-PET is recommended as part of the initial work-up in patients with newly diagnosed Ewing sarcoma. In our patient, PET demonstrated a suspicious iliac lesion; however, subsequent biopsy of this lesion was negative for malignancy, and therefore metastatic disease was not confirmed. This approach is consistent with current consensus recommendations, which support PET-based staging and selective biopsy of suspicious lesions when clinically indicated [19,20].

Despite these favorable outcomes, several limitations should be acknowledged. This report describes a single case, which limits the generalizability of the findings. In addition, the absence of molecular confirmation of EWSR1 rearrangement and the relatively short follow-up period preclude definitive conclusions regarding long-term outcomes.

Overall, this case underscores the importance of a multidisciplinary approach in managing mediastinal Ewing sarcoma presenting with life-threatening airway compromise. A stepwise strategy that prioritizes airway stabilization followed by systemic chemotherapy may lead to favorable outcomes. Temporary airway stenting may be considered a valuable bridging option in carefully selected patients, particularly in highly chemosensitive tumors such as Ewing sarcoma.

Conclusion

Primary mediastinal Ewing sarcoma presenting with critical tracheal compression is a rare and life-threatening condition that requires urgent multidisciplinary management. Temporary placement of a fully covered self-expanding metallic stent effectively secured the airway, enabled safe initiation of chemotherapy, and allowed subsequent stent removal after tumor regression. In selected patients with chemosensitive mediastinal malignancies and severe airway obstruction, airway stenting can function as a bridge to definitive therapy rather than merely a palliative measure.

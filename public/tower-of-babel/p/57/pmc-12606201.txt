The efficiency and safety of low-dose intravenous indomethacin for closure of patent ductus arteriosus in preterm neonate: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Wasana Hongkan, Narueporn Petarwut, Thanatda Siriporn, Nirucha Thamwiriyakul, Tharathorn Durongbhandhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is a common complication in prematurity. Intravenous indomethacin, three doses, is an effective medication for closure PDA, but complications about renal impairment and gastrointestinal (GI) problems should be considered. This study aimed to compare the efficacy and safety of low-dose intravenous indomethacin (two doses) with the conventional three-dose for the closure of PDA in preterm neonates. A Randomized, Controlled, non-inferiority trial was performed from 1 May 2021 to February 2024. Preterm neonates <34 weeks or body weight <1,500 g with age <6 days with hemodynamically significant PDA were included. The patients were randomized into 2 groups: Group 1, 64 patients received 2 doses of indomethacin (0.2 mg/kg/dose), and Group 2, 64 patients received 3 doses of indomethacin. The baseline characteristics of both groups (gestational age (GA), gender, birth weight, respiratory disease, respiratory support, PDA size, left atrium/aorta ratio, and age of first indomethacin) show no statistically significant differences. The success rate for closure PDA in 72 h and 7 days has no statistically significant difference. The incidence of re-medication and surgical closure PDA, Necrotizing enterocolitis, GI bleeding, acute kidney injury, death, and hospital stay shows no statistically significant differences. Low-dose of Intravenous indomethacin was similar in efficiency and safety for closure of PDA in preterm newborns as the conventional dose, especially in preterm neonates with birth weight ≥ 1000 gm and GA > 28 weeks. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Chonburi hospital (040/2564) and was registered on Thai Clinical Trials Registry (TCTR20241122001) (22/11/2024).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23285-4.

Introduction

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is a common complication in preterm neonates, with an incidence ranging from 30% to 67%, especially among those born before 26 weeks of gestation1. Gestational age significantly influences the incidence of PDA approximately 80% of infants born at 25–28 weeks and up to 90% of those born before 24 weeks are affected2. PDA, particularly when associated with a large left-to-right shunt, is linked to serious complications such as chronic lung disease, intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), retinopathy of prematurity, acute kidney injury, and increased mortality2–5. Pharmacologic closure of PDA with intravenous indomethacin is a standard treatment and has been shown to be effective6. Indomethacin, a cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor, works by inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis, thereby promoting ductal closure. However, it is associated with potential adverse effects, including thrombocytopenia, hyperbilirubinemia, peripheral vasoconstriction, impaired renal function, and gastrointestinal perforation6,7. Currently, the recommended regimen for intravenous indomethacin in preterm infants is 0.2 mg/kg/dose every 12 h for three doses, typically starting between 2 and 7 days of postnatal age. For infants older than 7 days, the initial dose is 0.2 mg/kg, followed by 0.25 mg/kg for subsequent doses, according to Neofax 2020. At our institution, as is common practice in Thailand, indomethacin is routinely used as a first-line therapy for PDA closure in preterm neonates. A study by Herreca CM compared outcomes in neonates receiving ≥ 4 doses versus ≤ 3 doses of indomethacin and found no significant differences in PDA closure rates, retreatment, reopening, or the need for surgical ligation. However, the group receiving ≥ 4 doses had a higher incidence of NEC8. Another study by Carmo KB evaluated the use of echocardiography to guide the timing of indomethacin administration. While echocardiography was helpful in identifying the optimal treatment window, it did not determine the most effective dosing regimen9. To our knowledge, no prior studies have directly compared the efficacy and safety of a two-dose regimen versus the standard three-dose regimen of indomethacin for PDA closure in preterm neonates. This study aims to test the hypothesis that a low-dose (two-dose) regimen of intravenous indomethacin offers similar efficacy with fewer complications compared to the standard three-dose regimen.

Methods

Study design

This Randomized, controlled, non-inferiority trial included preterm neonates with gestational age less than or equal to 34 weeks or birth weighing less than or equal to 1,500 g with hemodynamically significant PDA who were admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) of a single tertiary care Chonburi hospital. This study was conducted between 1 May 2021 to 30 April 2024. Recruitment included preterm neonates with gestational age less than or equal to 34 weeks or birth weighing less than or equal to 1,500 g with age less than 6 days (Early targeted treatment)9. They all had hemodynamically significant PDA by echocardiogram diagnosis and size of PDA ≥ 1.5 mm and Left atrium: Aorta ratio (LA: AO) ≥ 1.4:110. Their parents will understand the Thai language and age more than 18 years old. Patients were excluded if they had: major congenital anomalies such as cyanotic congenital heart disease, genetic diseases, renal anomalies such as renal agenesis or dysplasia; life threatening sepsis; necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC); intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH); urine output less than 1 ml/kg/hour in 24 h; serum creatinine > 1.5 mg/dl; platelet count < 100,000/ml. The withdrawal criteria were patients who died before completing the course of indomethacin or an echocardiogram not performed at 72 h and 7 days after the first dose of indomethacin. The sample size was targeted at 64 patients in each group based on the calculation for a Non-inferiority margin of 20%, with an identified margin that was an acceptable rate of PDA closure in the experimental group less than the control group not more than 20%, and more than placebo. From the previous study of El-Mashad found that the rate of PDA closure with indomethacin intravenous 3 doses was 81%11 and in the placebo group was 55%12. The sample size was calculated by using Estimated sample size for two-sample comparison of proportions (non-inferiority) with power of 80% and alpha of 0.05, and assumptions were power = 0.80, alpha = 0.05 (one-sided), pi = 0.71, delta = 0.20. The estimated required sample size (per group) = 64, and the total estimated sample size is 128.

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the institutional review board of Chonburi hospital (040/2564) and was registered on Thai Clinical Trials Registry (TCTR20241122001) (22/11/2024). All methods were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines/regulations including the Declaration of Helsinki. The written informed consent for participation in the study was obtained from the parent or legal guardian of each participant. As all participants were neonates, no direct consent could be obtained from the subjects themselves. No identifiable information or images of the participants are published in this manuscript.

Data collection and outcomes

The participants were screened for inclusion and exclusion criteria to be included in the study. Informed consent was introduced to the parents of the participants for the permission of the participants to enroll in this study by a NICU nurse. Investigators used block randomization of 4 for randomization using a computer random number generator. Number 0 is for the control group (Indomethacin intravenous 3 doses), and Number 1 is for the experimental group (Indomethacin intravenous 2 doses). The numbers 0 and 1 were sealed and enveloped. The NICU nurse randomly opened envelopes after receiving the permission of written informed consent. The written informed consents were obtained from all their legal guardians of participants. The attending doctor at NICU was not blinded and ordered the treatment for closing PDA by intravenous Indomethacin followed by the number. The NICU nurse who is not blind will give the intravenous indomethacin 0.2 mg/kg/dose every 12 h for 2 doses (experimental group) or for 3 doses (control group) up to the random open envelopes. Lab investigation: complete blood count (CBC), BUN, and Creatinine were assessed before randomization and after treatment at 72 h. The pediatric cardiologist who performed the echocardiogram was blinded to the dose of indomethacin for treatment. Basic characteristics: gestational age (GA), birth weight, gender, diagnosis of respiratory diseases (respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), Transient tachypnea of newborn (TTNB), congenital pneumonia), type of respiratory support (endotracheal tube (ETT), continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), Oxygen box), size of PDA, LA: AO ratio by echocardiogram and age of first dose indomethacin were recorded. The result of an echocardiogram to detect PDA closure and residual size of PDA was recorded after 72 h and 7 days after treatment. The additional treatment, such as the other course of indomethacin for PDA closure, depends on the attending doctor at the NICU. The repeated treatment by medication (other courses of indomethacin), surgery for PDA closure, incidence of NEC, gastrointestinal bleeding, hospital stay, and mortality rate were recorded. The primary outcome is the success rate of closing PDA. The secondary outcomes are retreatment by indomethacin intravenous second course, surgical PDA closure, incidence of NEC, gastrointestinal bleeding, acute kidney injury, death, and hospital stay. Neonatal kidney injury was defined with the modified KDIGO criteria 201213. The indomethacin be held when the oliguria occurs with urine output < 0.5 ml/kg/h for ≥ 12 h. All methods were performed in accordance with the CONSORT 2010 guidelines.

Statistical analysis

For the baseline characteristics of patients and secondary outcomes, continuous variables were assessed for normality using Shapiro–Wilk testing and were compared using Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank sum testing as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using Chi-square or Fisher’s Exact testing as appropriate. The primary outcomes are demonstrated as the absolute risk reduction with two-sided 95% confidence intervals for the experimental group as compared with the control group. Type I error was set at a two-sided 0.05. An intention-to-treat analysis that included all the patients who had been included for randomization was used. All calculations were performed using Stata/IC 16 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX, USA).

Results

Demographics

From May 1, 2021, to April 30, 2024, a total of 136 preterm neonates were enrolled in this study. Eight patients were excluded due to renal anomaly (1), life-threatening sepsis (1) and IVH (6). Then, total of 128 patients were randomized. Sixty-four patients were randomized to the experimental group (two doses of intravenous indomethacin), and 64 patients were randomized to the control group (three doses of intravenous indomethacin). Primary outcomes

The PDA closure rates at 72 h were similar between the groups: 81.3% (n = 52) in the two-dose group and 79.7% (n = 51) in the three-dose group. At 7 days, the PDA closure rate was 89.1% (n = 57) in the two-dose group and 96.9% (n = 62) in the three-dose group; this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.08), as shown in Table 2. The PDA closure rates at 72 h and 7 days of the two-dose group were non-inferiority to three-dose group with non-inferiority margin of 20%. As shown in Fig. 2. In the subgroup analysis, PDA closure rates at 72 h were similar between the groups when stratified by birth weight (< 1,000 g vs. ≥1,000 g), PDA size (< 2 mm vs. ≥2 mm), and gestational age (< 28 weeks vs. 28 ≥ weeks). However, at 7 days, the closure rate among neonates with a birth weight &<1,000 g or a gestational age < 28 weeks was significantly lower in the two-dose group compared to the three-dose group (p = 0.04), as shown in Fig. 3.

Discussion

In this study, we compared the efficacy and safety of two doses versus the standard three doses of intravenous indomethacin for the closure of PDA in preterm neonates. We found that the PDA closure rates between both groups were similar, with no statistically significant difference at 72 h and 7 days after the first dose of indomethacin. However, the closure rate at 7 days was higher in the three-dose group (96.9%) compared to the two-dose group (89.1%), an 8% difference. Compared to previous studies, our closure rate of 89.1% in the two-dose group was higher than reported rates of 64–74% using the same cumulative dosage14,15. Similarly, our closure rate of 96.9% in the three-dose group exceeded those reported in prior studies (68–81%)11,16,17.

In a subgroup analysis of neonates with birth weight < 1000 g and gestational age < 28 weeks, the PDA closure rate after 72 h was similar between groups. However, at 7 days, the three-dose regimen resulted in a significantly higher closure rate than the two-dose regimen. This finding suggests that the preterm with body weight less than 1,000 g and gestational age less than 28 weeks may benefit from the standard three-dose course more than two-dose course. Conversely, in neonates with a birth weight ≥ 1,000 g or gestational age ≥ 28 weeks, the two-dose regimen showed a similar rate of PDA closure to the three-dose regimen at both 72 h and 7 days. This aligns with literature on spontaneous PDA closure, which is more common in larger premature infants18. When comparing our results to previous studies, we found a higher PDA closure rate at 7 days with the three-dose regimen—95% in neonates ≥ 1000 g (versus 80–86%) and 100% in neonates < 1000 g (versus 54%)17,19. Additionally, prior research has shown that indomethacin is more effective than acetaminophen for PDA closure in preterm infants < 28 weeks, supporting our findings on the benefits of indomethacin in very low birth weight preterm20.

Adverse effects such as transient acute kidney injury, NEC, and GI bleeding may occur during indomethacin treatment6,7,21–23. For secondary outcomes, the rates of retreatment (medical or surgical), NEC, GI bleeding, acute kidney injury, death, and hospital stay were similar between the two groups. Although the three-dose regimen showed a slightly higher incidence of acute kidney injury, NEC, and GI bleeding, these differences were not statistically significant. Conversely, the rate of surgical PDA closure was slightly higher in the two-dose group, but again without statistical significance. Compared to previous studies, our three-dose regimen was associated with a lower rate of retreatment and death, but a higher incidence of NEC and GI bleeding6.

The serum half-life of indomethacin is approximately 20 h in infants younger than 7 days. In preterm infants weighing < 1000 g, the mean plasma half-life is around 21 h, compared to about 15 h in those weighing > 1000 g23,24. Our dosing schedule was designed to maintain consistent blood levels with 12-hour dosing intervals. However, a 24-hour interval may be more pharmacokinetically appropriate, especially in infants < 1000 g, to minimize the risk of drug accumulation and toxicity.

This is the first study to demonstrate the reasonable efficacy of a low-dose, short-duration (two-dose) intravenous indomethacin regimen compared to the standard three-dose regimen. Previous studies have shown that prolonged low-dose indomethacin therapy (e.g., 0.1 mg/kg/day) yields similar closure rates to the standard regimen but is associated with higher risks of gastrointestinal perforation8,16. Moreover, earlier surveys revealed that the three-dose indomethacin regimen remains standard practice among neonatal-perinatal fellowship programs (NFTP) in the United States25. Our study suggests that a reduced-dose regimen may provide acceptable efficacy for PDA closure. This aligns with prior research demonstrating that using echocardiographic flow patterns to guide early treatment can reduce total indomethacin exposure while maintaining effectiveness and lowering complication rates26,27.

A key strength of this study is that all participants were followed through to completion. However, the study has several limitations:

Conclusion

In this randomized trial comparing the efficacy and safety of two versus three doses of intravenous indomethacin for PDA closure, our findings suggest that the low-dose (two-dose) regimen may be comparably effective and non-inferior to the standard three-dose regimen, particularly in preterm infants with birth weight ≥ 1000 g and gestational age ≥ 28 weeks. These findings support a reduced-dose strategy as a potential alternative to minimize drug exposure without compromising efficacy.

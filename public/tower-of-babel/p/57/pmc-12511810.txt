Comparison of Apixaban with Warfarin for the Prevention of Subclinical Cerebral Infarction after Direct Current Cardioversion in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation
Authors: Changhyeon Kim, Ki Hong Lee, Young Ho Lee, Hyung Wook Park, Nam Sik Yoon, Seul Ki Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSubclinical cerebral infarction might be developed even adequate oral anticoagulation after direct current cardioversion (DCCV) in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF). Although apixaban is preferred to prevent thromboembolism and minimize bleeding risk in patients with AF, clinical outcomes after DCCV have not been known well. This study aimed to compare the occurrence of subclinical infarction between apixaban and warfarin in patients with AF after DCCV. A total of 60 patients diagnosed with non-valvular AF were randomized to receive either Apixaban or warfarin. At least 3-week anticoagulation was warranted before DCCV. Brain magnetic resonance image (MRI) was checked 3 days later after DCCV. All of the patients were followed upto 1-month. The primary end-point was the occurrence of subclinical infarction, while secondary end-point included death, stroke, and hospitalization due to heart failure. Safety end-point focused on major bleeding. Micro-embolic infarction causing subclinical infarction were not observed in the warfarin group, while 1 patient (4.3%) in the apixaban group, without statistical difference. Stroke and hospitalization were not developed at both groups. Brain MRI revealed micro-bleeding in 1 patient (4.3%) in the apixaban group, while there was no major or micro-bleeding in the warfarin group. The incidence of micro-embolic events causing subclinical infarction following DCCV was comparable between apixaban and warfarin. Clinical outcomes after DCCV were comparable between the 2 groups. These findings suggest that both apixban and warfarin exhibit similar safety and efficacy profiles during DCCV.Keywords: Apixaban, Warfarin, Atrial Fibrillation, Electric Countershock, Cerebral Infarction

INTRODUCTION

In patients with newly diagnosed atrial fibrillation (AF) without hemodynamic compromise, restoration of sinus rhythm via electrical cardioversion, anti-arrhythmic drugs, catheter ablation or surgical therapy has been shown to improve symptoms, and result in better long-term prognoses. 1,2,3 Considering the thrombo-embolic risks associated with these procedures,4,5 initiation of adequate anticoagulation is essential, using direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) or warfarin, vitamin K antagonist, with international normalized ratio (INR) >2.0.6,7,8 For patients scheduled for direct current cardioversion (DCCV), at least 3 weeks of therapeutic oral anti-coagulation prior to the procedure is required to minimize embolic risks, as suggested by current guidelines.9,10,11,12

Previous studies have demonstrated that Apixaban, a reversible direct factor Xa inhibitor, is non-inferior to Warfarin, in preventing clinical outcomes such as cerebral infarction, systemic embolism, mortality, and major bleeding after DCCV.8 However, these studies have primarily focused on clinically evident thromboembolic events, whereas the impact on subclinical cerebral infarction has not been adequately addressed. Reducing rates of subclinical cerebral infarction is crucial, as it is associated with a 2.5-fold increased risk of overt cerebral infarction and is also associated with cognitive and functional decline.13,14,15 This study aims to evaluate the comparative efficacy of apixaban and warfarin in preventing subclinical cerebral infarction in patients with AF undergoing cardioversion.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study population

The trial prospectively registered 60 patients diagnosed with non-valvular AF who visited Chonnam National University Hospital, from August 2016 to November 2018. The inclusion criteria were: 1) patients presenting with symptomatic AF diagnosed by 12-lead Electrocardiogram (ECG) at enrollment, 2) a high risk of embolic cerebral infarction, indicated by a CHA2DS2-VASc score (Congestive heart failure, Hypertension [HTN], Age over 75 years [doubled], Diabetes mellitus [DM], Prior Stroke or Transient ischemic attack [TIA] or Thromboembolism [doubled], Vascular disease, Age 65–74 years, Sex category) ≥2, 3) voluntary written consent for the clinical trial, 4) no history of anticoagulant use within the last 30 days, and 5) with no evidence of cardiac emboli on transthoracic or transesophageal echocardiography (TTE, TEE). The exclusion criteria were: 1) patients with AF due to reversible causes (e.g., hyperthyroidism, postoperative status, sepsis), 2) moderate or severe mitral stenosis, 3) history of mechanical heart valve surgery, 4) acute ischemic stroke within 7 days, 5) those who require both aspiring and clopidogrel, 6) take ticagrelor or prasugrel, 7) have severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <30 mL/min/m2), 8) hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or components of warfarin or apixaban, or 9) clinically significant bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal, intracranial, intraspinal). Of the initial 60 patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 15 patients dropped out for various reasons: 1) 3 were lost to follow-up, 2) 3 had an INR outside of 2.0–3.0, 3 declined to participate, 3) 4 showed normal sinus rhythm (NSR) on ECG, 4) 1 was in poor condition, and 5) 1 required surgery. Consequently, 45 patients were included in the final analysis.

2. Study protocol

This study consisted of a total of six visits. At first visit, the target population was screened, and inclusion and exclusion criteria were verified. Basic blood tests, ECG, TTE or TEE were performed, and the patient were randomized to begin anticoagulation therapy with either warfarin or apixaban. The usual dose of Apixaban was 5mg twice daily (b.i.d), with adjusted dose 2.5 mg twice daily (b.i.d) according to age, body weight, and serum creatine level based on current guidelines. After 1, 2, and 3 weeks of taking anticoagulants, ECG and prothrombin time (PT) as INR were conducted, and the adequate warfarin dose was titrated based on these results. After 3 weeks of medication, DCCV was performed on patients who still showed persistent AF on the ECG and whose INR was within therapeutic range. One patient with NSR on ECG, and three patients with an INR outside the therapeutic range were excluded at this point. Brain magnetic resonance image (MRI) was conducted 3 days after electrical cardioversion. Clinical follow-up was performed after 1-month electrical cardioversion including laboratory blood test, ECG, and TTE were performed. For the warfarin group, the clinical test began at the second visit after preparation period of 1–2 month to achieve the adequate target of therapeutic INR (2.0–3.0) after starting warfarin (Fig. 1).

As the institutional in charge, the Chonnam National University Hospital Institutional Review Board (IRB) approved the study protocol on December 29, 2015 (IRB No. CNUH-2015-249).

3. Definition

The primary efficacy end-point was the occurrence of newly detected subclinical cerebral infarcts. It was defined as a newly detected cerebral infarction on a brain MRI or Computed Tomography (CT) scan, that does not result in a stroke or neurogenic defect. Imaging was measured by the total count of newly detected micro-embolic cerebrals infarct observed in the follow-up MRIs and the sum of diameters of each cerebral infarction.

The secondary efficacy end-point included death, hospitalization due to heart failure (HF), newly detected cerebral infarct and their compound variable during clinical period. Death was categorized into all-cause death, cardiac death (from heart failure, cardiac arrest, or ventricular arrhythmia), and non-cardiac death, if the cause of death was unidentifiable. Hospitalization due to HF was defined as admission with symptoms of newly developed dyspnea at New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III/IV, orthopnea, rales in greater than one-third of the lung fields, elevated jugular venous pressure, or evidence of pulmonary congestion on chest X-ray thought to be related to cardiac dysfunction. Newly detected acute stroke was defined if there was a new neurogenic defect with a corresponding lesion in brain MRI including cerebral infarction and cerebral hemorrhage.

The safety end-points were evaluated based on major bleeding and minor bleeding as defined by the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) criteria. Major bleeding was defined as fatal bleeding, and/or symptomatic bleeding in a critical area or organ, such as intracranial, intraspinal, intraocular, retroperitoneal, intra-articular or pericardial, or intramuscular with compartment syndrome, and/or bleeding causing a fall in hemoglobin levels of 1.24 mmol/L (20 g/L or greater) or more, or leading to a transfusion of 2 U or more of whole blood or red cells. Minor bleeding was defined as all reported bleedings not classified as major.

4. Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, comparisons between groups were evaluated using an unpaired t-test or the Kruskal-Wallis H test. Categorial variables were reported as the number of incidents and percentages (%), and comparisons between groups were evaluated using the chi-square test or the Fisher’s exact test. To assess the efficacy and safety outcomes over time, Kaplan-Meier estimation was performed, and differences between groups were evaluated using the log-rank test. Logistic regression analysis was performed to examine the effect of warfarin and apixaban on effectiveness and safety, while multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to identify the independent effects. The statistical analysis was performed by SPSS 26.0 (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences, SPSS-PC Inc, Chicago, Illionis), and the all analyses were 2-tailed, with a p-value of <0.05 considered to be statistically meaningful.

RESULTS

1. Baseline clinical characteristics

The warfarin group consisted of 32 patients with an average age of 63.5±8.8 years, including 5 females (23.8%). The apixaban group included 28 patients with an average age of 66.5±9.9 years, including 5 females (30.4%).

Among the warfarin group, 27 patients (84.4%) experienced dyspnea (NYHA class 2, 20 patients [71.4%], NYHA class 3, 3 patients [9.4%]), 11 patients (34.4%) had palpitation, 6 patients (18.8%) had chest pain, and 5 patients (15.6%) had dizziness as their chief complain. Among the apixaban group, 22 patients (78.6%) experienced dyspnea (NYHA class 2, 20 patients [71.4%], NYHA class 3, 2 patients [7.1%]), 11 patients (39.3%) had palpitation, 3 patients (18.8%) had chest pain, 4 patients (14.3%) had dizziness as their chief complain.

In the warfarin group, 17 patients (53.1%) had HTN, 4 patients (12.5%) had DM, 2 patients (6.3%) had dyslipidemia, 12 patients (37.5%) were current smoker, 2 patients (6.3%) had a history of angina, 0 patient had old myocardial infarction, 2 patients (6.3%) had a history of HF, 0 patient had history of TIA or cerebral infarct as comorbidities, and the average CHA2DS2-VASc score was 2.0±1.3. Among the apixaban group, 14 patients (50.0%) had HTN, 7 patients (25.0%) had DM, 2 patients (7.1%) had dyslipidemia, 6 patients (21.4%) were current smoker, 3 patients (10.7%) had a history of angina, 0 patient had old myocardial infarction, 4 patients (14.3%) had a history of HF, 0 patient had history of TIA or cerebral infarct as comorbidities, and their average CHA2DS2-VASc score was 2.2±1.3.

There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the two groups (Table 1).

2. Impact on DCCV

The success rate of DCCV was similar between the two groups, with 21 patients (95.5%) in the warfarin group, 22 patients (95.7%) in the apixaban group achieving successful cardioversion. The average energy needed for DCCV was 138.6±37.6 J, 134.8±41.1 J in the warfarin group, and the apixaban group respectively. The average PT INR was 2.5±0.9 in the warfarin group. After 4 weeks, 3 patients (12%) in the warfarin group and 5 patients (19.2%) in the apixaban group showed NSR on ECG and were excluded from the DCCV group. There were no significant differences in DCCV outcomes between the two groups (Table 2).

Out of 44 patients, 42 patients achieved successful DCCV, while 2 patients of failed as mentioned above. DISCUSSION

Subclinical brain infarcts are significantly more prevalent than clinical infarcts, occurring at a rate approximately five times higher.16 The prevention of subclinical infarction is critical, as it is closely associated with cognitive and functional decline over time.13,14,15 Previous studies have demonstrated that apixaban is superior to warfarin in preventing stroke and systemic emblism,17,18,19 and the EMATE Trial established non-inferiority of apixaban compared to warfarin in patients undergoing cardioversion.7 Unlike prior investigations, the present study focused specifically on the prevention of subclinical infarction as detected through brain imaging.

Consistent with earlier research, no substantial differences were observed between warfarin and apixaban in secondary outcomes, including mortality, hospitalization due to HF, and newly detected cerebral infarcts. For the primary outcome, which has not been extensively evaluated in previous studies, there was a single case of subclinical infarction in the apixaban group. However, this finding did not achieve statistical significance. Despite the limited occurrence of subclinical infarction, the potential for such events underscores the importance of vigilance. Proper imaging follow-up should be considered in clinically suspected cases.

Meta-analysis of DOACs have shown that standarddose DOACs are safer than warfarin in terms of bleeding risk, particularly in Asian populations.20,21 Regarding safety, while no significant differences between apixaban and warfarin were identified in the current study, there was one case of micro-bleeding in the apixaban group. This suggest the need for careful patient monitoring and dose optimization on factor such as age, eGFR, and body weight.

Given the overall efficacy, safety profile, low drug interaction, and lack of requirements for frequent laboratory monitoring (e.g., INR), apixaban may represent a superior therapeutic option compared to warfarin.

Additionally, an analysis of the success rates of DCCV revealed a decline in success as CHA2DS2-VASc score increased. This finding has significant implications for individualized treatment strategies aimed at restoring sinus rhythm. For patient with high CHA2DS2-VASc scores, alternative strategies such as pharmacologic cardioversion or catheter ablation may be considered. Interestingly, success rates were observed to increase with age in our cohort, which contrasts with previous studies suggesting lower efficacy of cardioversion in older patients.22 This discrepancy may reflect selection bias or limitations related to our relatively small sample size. It is also possible that other component of the CHA2DS2-VASc score, such as other comorbidities, exert a stronger influence on cardioversion outcomes than age alone. Further large-scale prospective studies are needed to clarify the impact of age on cardioversion success.

This study has several notable limitations. First, despite the statistical results favoring apixaban, it is important to note that the apixaban group experienced complications, including one case of micro-embolic infarction, one instance of micro-bleeding, and one death. In contrast, no complications were observed in the warfarin group. Additionally, one patient in the apixaban group died outside the hospital, and the cause of death remains unknown.

Second, the study’s small sample size of 60 patients, drawn from a single-center cohort, limits its statistical power and generalizability. Future studies with larger cohorts and extended follow-up are warranted to validate these findings and explore the impact of different anticoagulation strategies in diverse patient populations.

Third, follow-up imaging was performed only three days post-cardioversion, even though embolic episodes can occur within 10 days.5 Additionally, patient monitoring was extended to just four weeks after the procedure. Consequently, the lack of long-term follow-up data precludes conclusions about the durability of anticoagulation benefits and the long-term risk of embolic or bleeding events.

To address these limitations, future research should aim to involve multi-center cohorts with larger-sample sizes and extended follow-up durations. Longitudinal studies incorporating serial imaging and neurocognitive assessments could further clarify the clinical relevance of subclinical infarctions and optimize anticoagulation strategies for patients undergoing DCCV.

In conclusion, both warfarin and apixaban demonstrated comparable efficacy and safety profiles in patients with non-valvular AF undergoing DCCV. The CHA2DS2-VASc score emerged as a significant predictor of cardioversion success, underscoring its relevance in patient stratification and procedural planning. These results provide further evidence to guide the choice of anticoagulation therapy in clinical practice while highlighting areas for future research to optimize patient outcomes.

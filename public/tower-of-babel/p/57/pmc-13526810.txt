A regret-based three-way decision with novel intuitionistic fuzzy similarity in an intuitionistic fuzzy information system
Authors: Zhuoma Dawa, Haidong Zhang, Yanping He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThree-way decision theory partitions the universe into positive, negative, and boundary regions, providing a flexible framework for decision-making under uncertainty that allows decisions to be deferred when information is insufficient, thereby controlling costs and reducing risks. Its core advantage lies in permitting deferred decisions when information is insufficient, thereby effectively controlling costs and reducing risks. Although the introduction of intuitionistic fuzzy sets can further capture the hesitation information within alternatives and enhance the model’s expressiveness for fuzziness, existing research still faces dual challenges. On the one hand, traditional similarity measures mostly rely on distance formulas, which are prone to introducing bias and struggle to accurately capture the intrinsic relationships between intuitionistic fuzzy sets. On the other hand, existing frameworks are mostly based on the assumption of complete rationality, overlooking irrational decision-making under the combined influence of fuzzy information and psychological factors. To address these issues, this paper introduces a regret–joy function into intuitionistic fuzzy information systems and constructs a novel three-way decision method that integrates psychological behavioral characteristics. The main contributions are as follows: (1) To more accurately capture the intrinsic relationships among intuitionistic fuzzy sets and to effectively avoid biases in similarity judgments caused by distance measures, we propose a novel intuitionistic fuzzy similarity measure under an intuitionistic fuzzy information system that is based directly on hesitancy and does not rely on distance. (2) Based on the new intuitionistic fuzzy similarity measure and combined with ideal positive degree to construct objective weights, we provide methods for computing intuitionistic fuzzy similarity classes and conditional probabilities. (3) By integrating objective weights with regret-joy functions, we construct a relative loss function that better aligns with decision-makers’ psychology. (4) Combining the relative loss function with conditional probability, we propose a novel three-way decision method in intuitionistic fuzzy information systems; this method can effectively handle uncertain decisions and enhance decision rationality and applicability. Finally, the effectiveness and rationality of the proposed method are verified through case analysis and experimental comparison. This study not only offers a new perspective on similarity measures in intuitionistic fuzzy information systems but also lays a solid foundation for the deepening and broader application of behavioral three-way decision theory.Keywords: Intuitionistic fuzzy information system, Intuitionistic fuzzy similarity degree, Regret-joy function, Three-way decisionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In practical multi-attribute decision-making (MADM) problems, evaluation information often possesses inherent fuzziness and uncertainty. Intuitionistic fuzzy sets(IFSs), because they can simultaneously characterize membership, non-membership, and hesitation degrees, exhibit distinct advantages in handling such fuzzy information. However, traditional decision models mostly assume decision-makers (DMs) are fully rational, overlooking their psychological perceptions when facing uncertainty. Therefore, the introduction of regret theory (RT) provides an effective means to account for DMs’ psychological behavior, but its application in intuitionistic fuzzy information systems (IFIS) still requires further exploration. More importantly, even in IFIS, when the similarity between alternatives reaches a critical state, the combined effects of information ambiguity and the DM’s psychological factors often produce a dilemma between acceptance and rejection, which traditional binary decision models struggle to address. At this point, the three-way decision (3WD) theory introduces a “deferred decision” option, offering a new approach to handling decision problems in such critical states. Based on this, this paper attempts to integrate IFSs, 3WD, and regret-joy functions (RJF), aiming to construct a comprehensive framework that can handle informational fuzziness, reflect DMs’ psychological behaviors, and make flexible decisions under critical conditions, thereby providing a more nuanced and complete analytical tool for solving practical decision problems. Below, we review IFIS, 3WD, and RT.

IFIS, as a key application area of IFS theory, has demonstrated exceptional value in decision analysis. In IFIS, information is stored and processed as IFSs, which can efficiently integrate and analyze fuzzy and uncertain data while accounting for DMs’ subjective preferences and uncertainties. Since the introduction of IFSs, researchers have made significant advances in the study of aggregation operators1–3, MADM problems4–6, and distance measures7–10. Nevertheless, current distance measures fail to effectively capture subtle differences in fuzzy information and inadequately address hesitant information. To more accurately measure the differences between these fuzzy pieces of information, various distance measurement methods have been proposed, including traditional methods such as the Euclidean distance7 and the Hamming distance8. For example, Gohain et al.9 introduced a bilateral distance formula to more effectively measure the distance between information represented by IFSs. Wang et al.10 investigated the three-way MADM problem in light of probabilistic dominance relations in IFIS. Although the above studies have made progress in measuring the distance of IFSs, they largely overlook the critical parameter of hesitancy degree. Considering the degree of hesitancy is crucial for enhancing the effectiveness of IFS theory in addressing decision-making uncertainty, especially when information is insufficient. To address this problem, Xiao et al.11 developed a distance measurement approach for IFS grounded in the Jensen-Shannon divergence. Chatterjee et al.12 introduced a new distance measure based on divergence measures and addressed the green supplier problem. Chatterjee et al.13 proposed a triangular distance measure based on divergence to optimize the TOPSIS method. Wu et al.14 developed a novel knowledge measure and investigated the properties of knowledge measures based on distance metrics. The distance measures constructed by the above methods all incorporate hesitation degree, thereby more accurately quantifying the differences between various alternatives. This notably improves the precision and reliability of distance assessment, providing strong support for tackling complex decision-making problems. Furthermore, Singh et al.15 established a new Dice similarity measure. Gohain et al.16 designed a nonlinear distance formula to better align with problem definitions and developed a novel similarity measurement method based on this formula. Jiang et al.17 formulated a similarity metric via direct manipulations of the membership, non-membership, hesitancy, and superior membership bound of two IFS. Ejegwa and colleagues18 developed a refined similarity-distance methodology, with the explicit goal of elevating the accuracy and consistency of analyses performed under the IFS framework. Although these similarity computation methods are effective in many cases, in specific scenarios, they may overlook certain detailed information because they are obtained indirectly via distance measures16–18, resulting in information loss. In view of this, our objective is to develop a novel IF similarity measure based on hesitation degree for handling IFIS. This study aims to investigate the following questions: (1) how to utilize hesitation information to construct a new IF similarity measure that compensates for the shortcomings of traditional distance-based methods in information preservation; and (2) compared with existing similarity methods that are distance-based or do not consider hesitation degree, what advantages does the proposed hesitation-based measure offer in characterizing DMs’ uncertainty and avoiding information omission.

The 3WD theory proposed by Yao19 represents a significant advancement in two-way decision theory. By introducing a “hesitation” option, the theory enables DMs to handle complex decision-making situations more effectively. The core of the 3WD theory is to find suitable methods for calculating conditional probabilities and loss functions. In recent years, amid increased uncertainty and complexity, the 3WD theory has been widely applied across various areas, including machine learning20, artificial intelligence21, information technology22,23, and logic programming24. For example, G. Bisht et al.25 constructed similarity classes for each alternative and further calculated conditional probabilities based on the formed equivalence classes. Hu et al.26 proposed two methods for calculating conditional probabilities founded on similarity relations. Huang et al.27 proposed a method for calculating loss functions and thresholds based on attribute evaluation values and ideal alternatives. It can be observed that researchers in developing the 3WD framework tend to seek suitable methods for calculating conditional probabilities and loss functions. Although the 3WD theory has demonstrated its value in various fields, research on its application in IFIS is relatively scarce. In28, Liu et al. constructed an IF 3WD method and a principal induction algorithm founded on IF decision systems according to Bayesian decision-making. Wang et al.10 established a novel 3WD method founded on the probabilistic dominance relationship of IFSs. Zhang et al.29 introduced a novel 3WD method for MADM problems in IF β-covering. Existing methods have two shortcomings. First, they often neglect how DMs’ psychological fluctuations affect decision outcomes. Second, although Ref.28 introduced conditional probabilities and a 3WD method, its probabilities rely on subjective settings. That method can only classify alternatives, not rank them. To address these issues, this paper proposes an objective method for determining conditional probabilities and combines it with RT, resulting in a 3WD method that can classify and rank alternatives.

RT, originally proposed by Bell30and further developed by G.Loomes et al.31, offers an alternative perspective on the limitations of reasoned decision theory in conditions of uncertainty. As the academic community’s focus on the psychological dynamics of DMs has grown, RT is currently becoming a focal area of academic research. After recognizing the important role of emotional factors based on the decision-making process, researchers have begun to explore how to integrate RT with specific decision-making methods in a variety of uncertain environments32–36. For instance, in incomplete information systems, Zhang et al.32 applied RT to determine a relative utility function, and Wang et al.33 integrated the decision-making attribute values of objects with RT to obtain a comprehensive utility perception value, thereby proposing a novel 3WD method. Wang et al.34 employed RT and interval type-2 fuzzy sets to improve the 3WD method. In35, Wang et al. integrated a 3WD method based on RT in the hesitant fuzzy environment. This method merges RT with hesitant fuzzy sets to develop a novel RJF. The novel RJF not only better retains the uncertainty in a hesitant fuzzy environment but also more accurately reflects the emotional changes of DMs, thereby improving the rationality and accuracy of decisions. Lei et al.36 introduce RT and generate 3WD rules that consider regret emotions in the dual hesitant fuzzy environment. In addition, MADM problems involving IFSs and RT have attracted the attention of researchers. For example, in the Hamming distance of IFSs and RT, Jia et al.37 defined the concepts of superior and inferior streams to describe the subject’s comparative preferences. In situations where attribute weight data are incomplete during decision-making, Ren et al.38 proposed a RT-based decision-making method that uses intuitionistic fuzzy numbers (IFNs) to represent attribute values. Notably, in the application of RT described above, the assignment of attribute weights is highly subjective and often depends on the DM’s personal judgments or preferences. Such subjective weights can bias decision outcomes toward particular viewpoints or interests rather than being fully grounded in objective data and facts. To overcome this limitation, this paper introduces an objective method for determining weights and combines it with RT, constructing a novel relative loss function (RLF) within an IFIS to enhance the objectivity of the decision-making process and the accuracy of decision outcomes.

Subsequently, we shall examine the research motivations and contributions of this paper from the following viewpoints.

Based on the analysis presented above, the key research motivations are outlined as follows:

Existing Refs.9,10 have achieved considerable progress in distance measures for IFSs; nevertheless, these investigations predominantly neglect the critical parameter of hesitancy degree. In practical decision-making contexts, the hesitancy degree serves a pivotal function in characterizing the uncertainty inherent in fuzzy information, particularly when information acquisition remains incomplete. Concurrently, the similarity measures proposed in Refs.16–18 are fundamentally predicated on distance-based methodologies; however, such approaches may prove inadequate for capturing the intrinsic structural attributes of fuzzy sets, thereby potentially attenuating information when addressing the inherent indeterminacy and vagueness. Moreover, in complex decision-making scenarios, exclusive reliance on rudimentary distance metrics is frequently insufficient to comprehensively delineate the degree of similarity between IFSs. Consequently, the exploration of more sophisticated similarity measurement methodologies is imperative to accurately elucidate the essential interdependencies among fuzzy information elements.

Discussions

The primary distinctions of the novel method from the eight existing approaches are as follows: 1) Our method ranks alternatives by adopting the expected loss of each alternative, while existing MADM methods, such as the method46, ELECTRE method45, TOPSIS method48, and PROMETHEE method47, do not consider the expected loss. 2) Unlike existing MADM methods such as method46, ELECTRE method45 and TOPSIS method48, and PROMETHEE method47, which merely rank the alternatives and pick the most favorable alternative, our method is a novel 3WD approach within the context of IFIS. The new method not only divides the alternatives into three distinct decision regions but also effectively ranks these alternatives. This significantly enhances the thoroughness of the decision-making process and the adaptability of the operations. 3) The proposed 3WD method in IFIS utilizes the objective weight of IPD to calculate the RLF for each alternative, thereby reducing bias caused by individual preferences. In contrast, the methods proposed by Wang et al.10 and Zhang et al.29 depend on subjective weights.

Conclusions

This article examines the impact of the DM’s psychological changes on decision results and introduces a 3WD method that combines IF similarity with the RJFs. The specific results are as follows:

We put forward a novel IF similarity evaluation method. Compared with distance measures, it can not only reflect the relationship between IFSs but also reduce the information loss they entail.

By introducing objective weights, we explore an objective calculation method for conditional probability. This conditional probability is influenced solely by objective factors, rather than subjective weights.

In the context of IFIS, we propose an expected loss based on the psychological changes of DMs through RJFs. Based on the expected loss and the conditional probability, we develop a novel 3WD method. Through comparative experiments with existing decision-making methods, the feasibility and rationality of the proposed method are confirmed, and its significant advantages in practical applications are also demonstrated. Additionally, we explore the impact of the parameters θ and ϱ on the decision results and find that the novel method is highly sensitive to them. When θ lies within the range [0.48, 1] and ϱ is within the range [0.05, 0.65], the decision results remain stable. Under these conditions, the best alternative is , while the least favorable alternative is . Clearly defining the optimal ranges for θ and ϱ helps DMs formulate more accurate risk management strategies, respond quickly to uncertainty, and adjust strategic direction flexibly, providing strong support for the MADM problem.

In summary, we propose a similarity evaluation method between IFSs, and construct a new 3WD method that combines psychological expected loss with incomplete fuzzy information systems. This method comprehensively considers psychological factors, reduces decision risk, and improves the comprehensiveness and flexibility of decision-making, thereby providing an effective tool for solving complex MADM problems. The method not only accounts for DM’ subjective perceptions but also addresses data uncertainty and fuzziness, thereby offering broad potential for practical applications. Theoretically, this method integrates objective conditional probability with IF similarity and extends the 3WD method by incorporating psychological expected loss, achieving a new paradigm of subjective-objective fusion. In practice, it is applicable to high-risk and incomplete information scenarios, such as medical diagnosis, financial risk control, and credit assessment, where it reduces decision-making risk and offers strong cross-domain configurability. However, this method also has the following limitations: (1) The parameter θ significantly influences decision outcomes and requires careful selection and adjustment; introducing sensitivity analysis or adaptive parameter optimization methods is recommended to address this issue. (2) The method relies on relatively high-quality initial information to estimate conditional probabilities, yet obtaining high-quality data in practice is challenging; this can be mitigated by introducing information quality assessment or robust probability estimation strategies.

In upcoming studies, we will primarily explore the following two directions:

Based on our proposed method, we will introduce multi-granularity to develop a novel 3WD method for dealing with IFIS.

Due to the inherent limitations of IFIS in handling fuzzy information, we will establish a novel 3WD method within the Pythagorean fuzzy information system to overcome these limitations and apply the novel 3WD method to solve complex MADM problems, such as those in medical, economic, and management fields.

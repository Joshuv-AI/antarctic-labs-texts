SBPS-03 INTREST OF INTERNAL AUDITORY CANAL DRILLING IN VESTIBULAR SCHWANNOMA SURGERY VIA THE RETROSIGMOID APPROACH
Authors: Yacine Felissi, Salim Meziani, Chafik Handis, Hamza Bouchekoura, Amrane Abassi, Hocine Benmeiza, Abdelhalim Morsli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVestibular schwannoma surgery addresses a therapeutic objective requiring total tumor removal, from the bottom of the canal to the cerebellopontine angle. Improvements in surgical techniques over the last fifty years have improved patient outcomes. Today, limiting functional sequelae is a priority for all teams, particularly with regard to facial function. It requires preservation of the facial and cochlear nerves during dissection and of the vestibular cavities during drilling of the internal auditory meatus (IAM).Materials and MethodsIn the case of surgical resection of a vestibular schwannoma via the retrosigmoid approach, Control of the vestibular structures, particularly the posterior semicircular canal, is Imperative if the objective of hearing preservation is combined with that of total tumor resection. drilling of the internal auditory meatus (IAM) is a delicate step during which these cavities can be inadvertently opened. We present 3 illustrative cases of vestibular schwannoma operated at our department using retrosigmoid transmeatal approach. drilling of the internal auditory meatus allows for the identification and bypassing of the vestibular structures while remaining as close as possible to the tumor. The schwannoma can then be completely cleaved, with the base of the IAM properly controlled while preserving the facial nerve.ConclusionComplete drilling of the posterior wall of the IAM through the retrosigmoid approach is feasible and allows direct visualization of the IAM floor without damaging the semicircular canals.

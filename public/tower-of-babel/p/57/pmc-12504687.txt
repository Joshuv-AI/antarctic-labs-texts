Fenlong-ridging combined with composite modifier reconstructs soil microbiome to mitigate saline stress and enhance sustainable cultivation of Isatis indigotica Fortune
Authors: Jianping Sun, Xianglin Dai, Zijing Zhao, Hao Li, Liguang Sun, Yahui Liu, Zhizhong Xue, Leiming Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Structural barriers and nutrient scarcity constrain medicinal plant cultivation in coastal saline soils. To address these challenges, we investigated a physical–chemical–microbial synergistic remediation strategy using Fenlong-ridging (FR) and a composite modifier (CM) by evaluating four soil treatments: conventional tillage; FR only; a CM only and FR combined with a CM (FR_CM). A comparative analysis of the four treatments revealed FR_CM to be the most influential strategy for improving coastal saline soil. The FR_CM treatment reduced soil electrical conductivity and bulk density by 78.38% and 29.31%, respectively; enhanced soil nutrient content and Isatis indigotica Fortune biomass by 68.91–114.29% and 126.52%, respectively; induced significant reorganization of microbial community structure at the phylum/OTU levels; elevated microbial network parameters, including positive/negative correlation ratios, modularity, and average path length; and enriched functional taxa such as fermentation and chitinolysis, while enhancing stress-tolerant phenotypes and suppressing potentially pathogenic phenotypes. This work establishes a theoretical foundation and technical paradigm for microbial-mediated remediation in saline-alkali ecosystems to assist crop-to-environment adaptation strategies and maintain sustainable medicinal plant production.

Introduction

Saline-alkaline land has the potential to be an important resource for investigating soil-improvement techniques that can extend the area of arable land available for food production1. China’s saline soils are diverse in type, rich in resources, and vast in geographic area, providing unique research conditions2. Coastal soils, in particular, are highly salinized and comprise fragile ecosystems influenced by seawater and climatic factors3. Coastal saline soils are characterized by their sticky and heavy texture, poor structure, and high NaCl content4. These properties not only hinder seed absorption of water and oxygen, inhibiting embryonic root growth but also alter root system morphology, resulting in shorter, thicker roots with fewer branches and reduced capacity for water and nutrient uptake5–7. Excessive Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions disrupt ionic balance, compromise plant cell membrane stability and enzyme activity, and elevate oxidative stress levels. This leads to metabolic dysfunction, stunted growth, and ultimately, significant challenges in crop cultivation8–10. To mitigate these adverse effects, strategies such as improving soil structure, incorporating organic matter, introducing salt-tolerant plant species, and leveraging beneficial microorganisms have been employed to enhance plant resilience and productivity11–14.

Fenlong-ridging is a new method of cultivation that treats traditionally plowed subsoil differently by cutting the soil vertically and using an auger to form a ‘suspended’ ridge, thereby thickening the tillage layer to 30–50 cm15. This method significantly improves soil porosity16, increases rainfall infiltration and soil water storage17, and facilitates photosynthesis18. Additionally, it promotes a more developed plant root system and improves intra-root enzyme activity and microbial diversity19while enhancing nitrogen and phosphorus availability20. Ultimately, these effects raise crop yields and quality21. These characteristics create a positive outlook for applying Fenlong-ridging technology in saline soils. Studies have reported that improved soil porosity following the use of Fenlong-ridging accelerates salt leaching and improves the granular structure of the soil22. Furthermore, this technology cuts off capillaries that promote water loss, increases the deep infiltration of water, inhibits the upward movement of salts, and reduces salt accumulation in the plowed layer23, thus realizing salt exclusion. A 73.00% increase in yield was reported in the first maize crop grown following the use of Fenlong-ridging in heavy saline land in Shandong Dongying Binhai, and the second wheat yield increased by 154.22%24. Fenlong-ridging has also been reported to improve the soil environment and raise cotton yields in southern Xinjiang25.

Severe nutrient deficiencies represent the central limiting factor in using coastal heavily saline soils26. Salinity inhibits the migration, uptake, and storage of macronutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorous27. Adding exogenous organic matter helps to reduce salinity28. Zhu et al . concluded that when salinity is reduced and the soil nutrient content improves, these factors interact positively29. In addition, adding exogenous organic matter is an effective way to improve soil fertility and promote the conversion of saline soils to agricultural soils30.

The large-scale application of saline-alkali soil amendments faces multiple constraints, including cost, ecological safety, and regional adaptability. Conventional chemical amendments (e.g., humic acid, ferrous sulfate, gypsum) yield rapid results but pose risks of secondary pollution and salinization31–34. Microbial amendments require longer remediation periods35,36, while biochar’s high production cost limits its adoption in developing countries37,38. Furthermore, most amendment materials are applied singly, with limited use of composite formulations, and exhibit poor regional suitability. For instance, phosphate-modified hydrochar is only effective for specific saline-alkali soil types34,39–41. In terms of coastal saline-alkali land, intense evaporation of moisture from the soil exacerbates the seasonal resurgence of salt levels, requiring specific amendments.

To address these challenges, the novel integration of a composite amendment with deep vertical tillage (Fenlong cultivation) was assessed. Combining these two means of remediation leverages a number of synergistic mechanisms: (i) calcium displacement: superphosphate supplies Ca2⁺ to reduce the soil’s sodium adsorption ratio while replenishing phosphorus; (ii) organic enrichment: a cattle manure-rice straw-mushroom residue mixture enhances soil organic matter and recycles agricultural waste; (iii) microbial augmentation: locally screened compound microbial inoculants (Isoptericola, Trichoderma longibrachiatum, Streptomyces fradiae, Staphylococcus, Bacillus subtilis, B. mucilaginosus) improve the soil’s functional adaptability; (iv) structural stabilization: polyvinyl alcohol promotes the formation of soil aggregates; and (v) salt leaching: Fenlong tillage accelerates desalination and suppresses salt reflux. Field trials have demonstrated that this combined approach results in 30–50% desalination and a 20–30% increase in soil organic matter, with application costs controlled at £500.00–£1,400.00 per hectare (5000.00–14000.00 CNY·ha−1), demonstrating strong potential for coastal saline-alkali soil reclamation.

However, the underlying mechanisms of the system remain poorly understood, and three key points require clarification, including: (i) the impact of the combined treatment on soil microbial symbiotic network structures and critical functional modules; (ii) the causal relationships between microbial community succession and key soil properties (e.g., salinity, organic matter); and (iii) the regulatory mechanisms governing microbial network stability. To investigate these gaps in our understanding of saline-alkali soil remediation, we analysed the molecular ecological network in coastal saline soil under four experimental treatments to decipher the complex microbial interactions and their environmental sensitivities. The treatments included: conventional tillage without a modifier (CK), Fenlong-ridging (FR), a composite modifier (CM), and Fenlong-ridging combined with a composite modifier (FR_CM). Using high-throughput sequencing of soil samples, we focused on: (i) bacterial community succession patterns; (ii) species coexistence dynamics and keystone functional taxa; and (iii) the functional potential of the microbial communities. The study aimed to unravel the mechanisms through which microbial activities drive saline-alkali soil amelioration to support the creation of ‘crop adaptation to land’ models for saline-alkali regions. The findings provide a theoretical basis for the long-term improvement of coastal, heavily saline soils for agricultural use and the means to optimize composite modifiers.

Materials and methods

Site description

The experiment was conducted from April 2019 to October 2023 at the Coastal Saline-Alkali Soil High-Efficiency Utilization Demonstration Base in Caofeidian District, Tangshan City, Hebei Province, China (geographic coordinates: 39°22′86.11″N, 118°56′60.02″E). The experimental site is part of the Caofeidian Halophyte Botanical Garden Ecosystem Observation Station, located within 8.70 km of the Bohai Bay coastline, with an average elevation of 0.50 m (based on the 1985 National Elevation Benchmark).

Climate: The region has a warm temperate semi-humid continental monsoon climate. Continuous monitoring by the base’s automatic weather station from 2019 to 2023 recorded the climate conditions, including the average annual frost-free period: 235.17 days; total annual sunshine duration: 2,501.13 h; mean annual temperature: 14.10 ℃; annual precipitation: 670.00 mm (80% concentrated during July–September); annual evaporation: 1,952.00 mm; and evaporation-to-precipitation ratio: > 1 for most of the year (except during the July–August rainy season) (see Fig. 1). Experimental crops and field management

During the trial, we selected Isatis indigotica Fortune (variety: Daqingye; commonly known as Banlangen) for cultivation. The seeds were commercially procured from Hebei Hefeng Agricultural Technology Co., Ltd. (Baoding, Hebei Province, China)—a certified supplier of medicinal plant seeds. Prior to cultivation, we con-ducted rigorous viability assessments through standardized germination assays under controlled environmental conditions (25 ± 1 °C with a 12-h photoperiod). These tests confirmed a germination efficiency exceeding 90%, ensuring the reliability of our experimental materials. This medicinal herb has demonstrated notable salt-alkali tolerance in preliminary field trials and is particularly suited to coastal saline-alkali soils such as those in the Bohai Rim region. Banlangen is a traditional Chinese medicinal product with recognized heat-clearing, detoxification, blood-cooling and throat-soothing properties. It commands a stable market demand and high added value, combining ecological adaptability with economic viability. This species has been prioritized by China’s recent initiatives to promote the cultivation of specialty crops (e.g., medicinal plants) on marginal lands. Sowing method: strip sowing with row spacing of 20 cm and a seeding rate of 60 kg·ha−1 (seed germination rate ≥ 83.00%).Pre-sowing preparation: shallow furrows (1.50 cm depth) were dug along the ridges according to row spacing. Seeds were evenly broadcast and covered with 1 cm of soil, followed by light compaction to ensure seed-soil contact.

The single-ridge single-pipe drip irrigation method was employed, with drip irrigation pipes laid along the central ridge line and a unified water supply (emitter spacing of 20 cm, emitter flow rate of 0.75 L·h−1). Approximately 900 cubic meters per hectare (m3·ha−1) of water were applied during the sowing and seedling stages. After seedling emergence, water usage was reduced, with the total water consumption during the entire growth period reaching approximately 3,600.00 m3·ha−1. Fertilization: post-harvest split applications of ammonium sulfate (40.00–50.00 kg·ha−1 per application) and calcium-phosphate compound fertilizer (10.00 kg·ha−1 per application). Weed and disease control: standardized measures were implemented to optimize crop growth conditions.

Experimental timeline and sampling

Based on the biennial characteristics of Isatis indigotica Fortune, experimental field preparation was completed in April 2019 (first cultivation cycle) and November 2021 (second cultivation cycle) using continuous cropping patterns. We hypothesized that saline-alkali soil improvement effects would exhibit positive cumulative enhancement with treatment duration, where continuous multi-cycle intervention constitutes a prerequisite for significant changes in soil physicochemical properties and microbial community structure/function. Accordingly, this study focused on a core analysis of 2023 data (fourth year of intervention) to elucidate the ameliorative effects of tillage practices and soil modifiers on coastal heavy saline-alkali soils. Biomass collection occurred in early July 2023 (aerial stems/leaves) and mid-October 2023 (aerial parts and medicinal roots).

Soil sampling was synchronized with root harvesting in mid-October 2023. Post removal of visible impurities, samples were evenly divided: one portion was air-dried, ground, and sieved for conventional physicochemical analysis [bulk density (RZ), pH, EC, available nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK), organic matter (OM)]. The fresh portion was immediately sieved (2 mm), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent DNA extraction.

Determination of bacterial diversity

Bacterial diversity determination was resolved based on Illumina high-throughput sequencing technology, and the experimental procedure was as follows. (ⅰ) DNA extraction and quality control: 0.50 g of mixed soil samples were taken, and genomic DNA was extracted using the MP FastDNA Soil Kit (MP Biomedicals), and the integrity was verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. (ⅱ) PCR amplification: using primers 338F (5′-ACTCCTACGGGGAGGCAGCAG-3′) and 806R (5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′) were used to target amplification of the V3-V4 variable region of the 16S rRNA gene (GeneAmp 9700 System, ABI). The reaction procedures were as follows: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min; 27 cycles × [ 95 °C 30 s, 55 °C 30 s, 72 °C 45 s]; and final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. The reaction system (20.00 μL) contained: 5 × FastPfu buffer, 4.00 μL; 2.50 mM dNTPs, 2.00 μL; primers (each), 0.80 L (5.00 μM); FastPfu polymerase, 0.40 U; and template DNA, 10.00 ng. (ⅲ) Library construction and sequencing: repeat PCR products were mixed three times, purified by 2% agarose gel, quantified by QuantiFluor-ST (Promega), and bipartite sequencing was performed using the Miseq PE300 platform (Illumina). (ⅳ) Bioinformatics analysis: the original sequences were spliced by Trimmomatic QC and FLASH, clustered into OTUs by UPARSE (97% similarity), de-chimerized using UCHIME, and finally annotated using RDP Classifier with comparison to the SILVA database (confidence threshold 70%).

Data processing and analysis

One-way ANOVA was used to analyze the differences in physicochemical properties, biomass, microbial diversity, abundance of dominant species, and function among treatments. The α-diversity indices (Observed species, Shannon, Shannon evenness, pedigree diversity) were calculated using Mothur software ( version v.1.30.2 https://mothur.org/wiki/calculators/). Heatmaps of the bacterial communities were produced using tools in R Programming Language (version 3.3.1 https://www.r-project.org) to study the effects of different treatments on the structural composition of each bacterial community and its relationship with environmental factors, respectively. Principal component analysis (PCA) and non-metric multidimensional scaling analysis (NMDS2) in R were used to assess the effects of different treatments on bacterial community structures. Spearman correlation was calculated using the R Programming Language package Picante (1.8.2) cor.table function, retaining the results with absolute values of correlation > 0.6 and P < 0.0543. To analyze the effects of different treatments on microbial interactions, Gephi Graph Visualization and Exploration System (Gephi 0.10.1 http://gephi.org/)was used to visualize networks according to correlation parameters calculated as described previously44. The Mantel test indicated the main controlling factors affecting the communities under different inter-treatment conditions using Quantitative Insights Into Microbial Ecology (2020.2.0 https://qiime2.org) software. The ecological functions and phenotypes of the soil microorganisms were predicted under different inter-treatment conditions using the Functional Annotation of Prokaryotic Taxa (FAPROTAX) database and BugBase, respectively. Permanova was used to evaluate the magnitude of the contribution of the effects of the CM and FR on the functional indicators.

Results

Effect of Fenlong-ridging and composite modifier on soil properties and biomass in coastal saline soils

The combined Fenlong-ridging and composite modifier (FR_CM) treatment demonstrated the most comprehensive improvements to the saline soil properties and plant productivity (Table 4). Specifically, FR_CM treatment greatly reduced the soil limitations, decreasing pH by 3.25%, EC by 78.38%, and bulk density by 29.31% compared to CK (all P < 0.05). This treatment produced synergistic effects on soil fertility by increasing the AP, AK, and OM by 68.91%, 87.32%, and 114.29% (all P < 0.05), respectively, and resulted in the highest biomass yield (126.52% increase), significantly outperforming individual FR or CM treatments. Effect of Fenlong-ridging and composite modifier on microbial α-diversity in coastal saline soils

The combined FR_CM treatment significantly altered the diversity patterns of soil bacterial communities (Fig. 3). High-throughput sequencing analysis (yielding 1,269,938 valid sequences in total, with 49,942–72,702 sequences per sample) revealed that, compared to the CK treatment, FR reduced the OTU richness and phylogenetic diversity by 12.93% and 9.47%, respectively (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3a, d). Furthermore, the CM increased the Shannon index and evenness by 1.45% and 1.07%, respectively (Fig. 3b, c) (P < 0.05). Effect of Fenlong-ridging and composite modifier on microbial community composition in coastal saline soils

While the bacterial community composition remained consistent at phylum and OTU levels across treatments, relative species abundances varied significantly (P < 0.05), revealing treatment-specific enrichment patterns. Analysis of 9,124 OTUs (spanning 56 phyla) revealed higher relative abundances of the following phyla in soils treated with FR, CM or FR_CM, compared to the CK (Fig. 4a, Table 5): Gemmatimonadota (FR), Firmicutes (CM/FR_CM), Bacteroidota (FR_CM/FR), and Patescibacteria (FR_CM). The phyla exhibiting lower relative abundance and contribution included: Actinobacteriota (CM), Myxococcota (FR_CM/FR), and Desulfobacterota (FR_CM). At the OTU level (Fig. 4b, Table 6), 9 of 11 dominant OTUs exhibited changes in abundance. Those showing relatively high relative abundance and contribution compared with the CK were: OTU440 (FR), OTU3386 (FR_CM), OTU1558 (FR_CM, FR), OTU4722 (FR_CM, FR), OTU2197 (FR), OTU2526 (FR_CM), and OTU1639 (FR) (P < 0.05). Discussion

Effect of Fenlong-ridging combined with composite modifier on bacterial community structure in coastal saline soil

Microorganisms are essential for soil ecological processes and material cycling. Their community composition is influenced by agricultural practices, soil fertility, and cropping patterns45. This study systematically elucidated the reconstruction mechanisms of Fenlong-ridging combined with composite modifier (FR_CM) associated with soil microbial communities in coastal saline-alkali soils and revealed the ecological effects of this treatment combination.

The study area exhibits characteristic warm-temperate semi-humid monsoon climate conditions, with an annual evaporation-to-precipitation ratio reaching > 1 (Fig. 1). This hydrological imbalance induces severe surface salt accumulation, as evidenced by the notably elevated electrical conductivity (1.48 mS·cm−1) in control (CK) groups (Table 4), confirming substantial saline-alkali stress. Under these conditions, FR_CM treatment demonstrated remarkable amelioration effects on the soil environment.

Primarily, significant alterations in microbial community structure were observed. FR_CM treatment markedly increased the relative abundance of Firmicutes, Bacteroidota, and Patescibacteria (P < 0.05), while substantially reducing Myxococcota and Desulfobacterota populations (Fig. 4a, Table 5). These findings align with Zheng et al.’s conclusions46, with critical ecological implications: the proliferation of copiotrophic Firmicutes and Bacteroidota reflects enhanced soil nutrient availability, whereas decreased Desulfobacterota abundance indicates improved redox conditions.

Subsequently, substantial improvements in soil physicochemical properties were recorded. FR_CM treatment reduced soil electrical conductivity by 78.38%, decreased pH by 3.25 units, and lowered bulk density by 29.31%, while significantly enhancing the organic matter content (114.29%) and available potassium levels (87.32%) (Table 4, P < 0.05). These modifications directly drove microbial community restructuring: (i) Bacteroidota and Firmicutes showed significant positive correlations with the available potassium, with these polysaccharide-degrading phyla facilitating organic matter mineralization; (ii) Patescibacteria abundance negatively correlated with bulk density, indicating improved soil physical structure; and (iii) Myxococcota populations decreased by 62.4%, potentially reflecting micro-food web restructuring (Fig. 8a)47.

The core mechanisms of Fenlong modification technology manifest in three dimensions: (i) Physical structural reconfiguration: Subsoiling disrupts plough pans, effectively inhibiting capillary-driven salt ascent; (ii) Chemical property amelioration: Ca2⁺ in modifiers displaces Na⁺ adsorbed on soil colloids, promoting micro-aggregate formation; and (iii) Microbial response: Enriched exopolysaccharide-producing taxa (e.g., Bacillaceae) consolidate aggregate stability through metabolic products48.

Notably, divergent responses emerged between phylum-level and OTU-level analyses. Under FR_CM treatment, OTU2197 (Pseudomonadaceae) exhibited increased relative abundance but reduced structural contribution-a phenomenon corroborated by Li et al.’s findings in the Wuliangsuhai watershed49. This underscores the non-linear relationship between microbial abundance and functional contribution, potentially attributable to functional redundancy, micro-environmental heterogeneity, or metabolic state variations. These observations highlighted the necessity of employing metagenomic and stable isotope probing technologies for deeper functional genomic analyses of microbial responses.

The scientific contributions of this research are threefold. The findings elucidate the ‘physical–chemical-biological’ synergistic mechanism and establish a ‘modification-response-optimization’ feedback model; the results provide quantifiable technical parameters for coa-stal saline-alkali soil reclamation, and the study suggests an integrated multi-technique a-pproach for microbial community research. These advancements propel the theoretical dev-elopment of saline soil amelioration and supply a novel scientific foundation for agricultur-al ecosystem management.

The Effect of Fenlong-ridging combined with composite modifier on bacterial function in coastal saline soils

Soil improvement is a relatively slow process, and both the type of improvement measure and duration affect soil microbial community structure and function to varying degrees. The results demonstrate that FR fundamentally dictated the restructuring of microbial ecological functions under FR_CM treatment. Analysis revealed a predominance of functions directly linked to FR, including chitinolysis, anoxygenic photoautotrophy, and anoxygenic photoautotrophy involving S-oxidation. In contrast, the CM component contributed primarily by stimulating fermentation and introducing bacteria associated with cyanobacteria and oxygenic photoautotrophy. Variation partitioning analysis conclusively showed that FR was the decisive factor driving the differential rates of functional shifts in microbial species (Fig. 9b). This aligns with previous findings that FR enhances potential ecosystem functions in arable soils50, underscoring its principal mechanistic role in altering the soil functional microbiome.

The profound impact of FR on microbial function arises from its ability to modify the soil physicochemical environment significantly. It enhances the activity of key soil enzymes involved in nutrient cycling48 and promotes microbial nutrient metabolism, thereby indirectly boosting the overall microbial functional capacity. Crucially, FR physically increases the surface area exposed to sunlight, simultaneously improving nutrient accessibility for both aerobic and anaerobic microorganisms. This creates favourable niches, providing inorganic substances (carbon, nitrogen) for photosynthesis or redox reactions in autotrophic microorganisms. Furthermore, bacteria associated with chitin secretion, stimulated by FR, contribute directly to soil health by aiding stable aggregate formation, thereby improving the soil physical structure and water-holding capacity, facilitating organic matter mineralization51, influencing the nitrogen cycle, and promoting nutrient release (NO3−, NH4+, Ca2+), which is vital for plant growth52. Collectively, these FR-induced modifications enhance the stability and efficiency of carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycles within the treated soil.

Specific dominant microbial phyla played critical regulatory and indicative roles in soil functional phenotypes following FR_CM treatment. Proteobacteria, Actinobacteriota, and Chloroflexi emerged as the major phyla associated with desirable aerobic, biofilm-forming, and stress-tolerant phenotypes. Proteobacteria and Acidobacteriota were predominant in the potentially pathogenic phenotype category. These findings align with established links between bacterial community structure and predicted function53, reinforcing their relevance as indicators. Proteobacteria (predominantly aerobic) are known for their symbiotic relationships that aid saline soil improvement54. Actinobacteriota (mostly aerobic) are pivotal decomposers in the organic matter cycle, releasing extracellular enzymes beneficial for crop growth and enhancing nutrient/mineral availability55, directly contributing to soil improvement. Chloroflexi exhibit metabolic versatility, thriving in both aerobic and anaerobic conditions via diverse strategies56, demonstrating high stress tolerance. They also efficiently utilize carbon in nutrient-poor environments, potentially reducing competition57. This profile underscores how the microbial structural shifts under FR_CM foster functional phenotypes conducive to soil restoration.

The FR_CM treatment induced a significant shift in the relative abundance of key functional phenotypes compared to the control (CK). Specifically, it resulted in a greater abundance of aerobic and stress-tolerant phenotypes and a reduced abundance of potentially pathogenic phenotypes. This functional shift is mechanistically linked to the enrichment of key taxa within the Proteobacteria, Chloroflexi, and Acidobacteriota phyla under FR_CM. The enhanced functional expression of these key taxa-particularly their aerobic respiration and stress tolerance-directly regulates the soil environment in ways beneficial to crop growth. Critically, the increased abundance of these phyla, identified as key taxa in non-saline soils, is strongly associated with soil quality, showing significant negative correlations with electrical conductivity (salinity indicator) and significant positive correlations with microbial biomass, total carbon, total nitrogen, nitrate levels58, and ultimately, crop yield59. After four years of FR_CM application, the soil quality reached levels comparable to non-saline soils, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach driven by the induced microbial structural and functional shifts.

While high-throughput sequencing provides valuable insights into community structure and predicted functions, limitations exist in conclusively confirming specific microbial activities and ecological roles in situ. Therefore, future research must prioritize the isolation, cultivation, and functional validation of the key microorganisms identified in this study. Testing microbial activity under realistic soil conditions and verifying their predicted ecological functions and phenotypes are essential next steps. The successful characterization of key microbes isolated from the improved saline soil will provide high-quality microbial resources critical for developing targeted biotechnological solutions for saline soil amelioration. This research addresses the urgent need to reclaim degraded land and enhance agricultural productivity on marginal soils, while contributing fundamentally to the critical goal of protecting the national arable land red line through biologically-driven, sustainable soil improvement strategies.

Effect of Fenlong-ridging with composite modifier on interactions between soil bacterial communities in coastal saline soils

In ecosystems, microorganisms coexist and interact through complex networks50, and analyzing the interactions within microbial populations is key to gaining insights into the ecological relationships between microorganisms and their co-responses to the environment. Analysis of microbial co-occurrence networks revealed significant structural changes under the FR_CM treatment compared to the control (CK). Specifically, FR_CM substantially increased key network complexity metrics, including the average degree, number of edges (connections), modularity, transitivity, and average path length. Crucially, the ratio of positive to negative correlations between microbial taxa also increased. Collectively, these metrics indicated that FR_CM promotes a larger, more connected, and topologically more complex microbial network. This enhanced complexity implies greater functional redundancy and adaptability: as the external environment changes, the microbial community exhibits a delayed response and increased structural stability, likely facilitated by the increased diversity, relative abundance, metabolic activity, and reproductive capacity of the constituent species.

The heightened stability of the FR_CM-induced network can be mechanistically linked to the role of ‘connector’ taxa. These highly connected microorganisms dynamically adjust their mutualistic interactions with other species in response to environmental fluctuations60. This dynamic rewiring of cooperative links allows the entire network to maintain its functional integrity and stability despite changing conditions. This finding aligns with studies using other amendments like phosphogypsum and Suaeda salsa, which similarly increase bacterial network complexity61. Therefore, fostering such complex and adaptable network structures contributes to the longevity of soil improvement strategies, including FR_CM.

The shift towards a more complex and cooperative microbial network under FR_CM holds significant scientific value for ecosystem management. Building resilient soil microbiomes is paramount in the face of widespread agroecosystem degradation and resource scarcity. Maximizing the functional potential of microbial communities—achieved here by promoting complex, stable networks-represents a critical strategy for sustainable land management62.Moreover, understanding and harnessing microbial interactions offers a powerful means of servicing the ecosystem, particularly in stressed environments like saline soils.

To fully leverage these insights, future research should focus on elucidating the causal links between specific soil physicochemical changes induced by FR_CM (e.g., salinity reduction, organic matter dynamics, nutrient availability) and the observed shifts in key microbial taxa and their network-based interaction. Furthermore, identifying the functional genes underpinning the stability and cooperative behaviours of critical connector taxa is essential. This deeper understanding will pave the way for the strategic modification of key microbial populations or their functional outputs, ultimately enabling targeted improvements in soil quality and enhanced crop adaptation in marginal lands.

Conclusion

Fenlong-ridging with composite modifier (FR_CM) technology significantly ameliorates coastal heavy saline-alkali soil through ‘physical–chemical-microbial’ synergy, ultimately leading to a substantial increase in the biomass of Isatis indigotica Fortune, confirming FR_CM as an effective strategy for the multi-tiered ecological restoration of saline-alkali soils. To further verify its broad applicability and long-term sustainability, future work should focus on implementing multi-year, multi-site field trials in key regions. In-depth mechanistic research on core microbial taxa using multi-omics technologies, along with the development of predictive models integrating soil, climatic, and biological factors, is also recommended. These steps will facilitate the practical application and precision management of this soil amendment technology.

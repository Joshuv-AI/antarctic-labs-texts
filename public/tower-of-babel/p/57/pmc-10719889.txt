Report on the seventh meeting of national control laboratories for vaccines and biologicals of the WHO Western Pacific and South-East Asia member states
Authors: Sun Bo Shim, Chan Woong Choi, Jin Ho Shin, Jong Won Kim, Silke Schepelmann, Jae Ho Jung, Harish Chander, Ratih Pujilestari, Madoka Kuramitsu, Masaki Ochiai, Nee Yuan Qi, Geraldine N Dimapilis, Luu Thi Dung, Hyung Sil Moon, In Soo Shin
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Biregional Network of National Control Laboratories (NCLs) of the WHO Western Pacific and South-East Asia Regions has been meeting annually since 2018 to enhance NCLs' voluntary participation capacity. Its seventh meeting was hosted by the Korea National Institute of Food and Drug Safety Evaluation (NIFDS) of the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS), in conjunction with the Global Bio Conference, in Seoul on September 6, 2022. Over 60 participants from seven countries, (India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Vietnam) attended the meeting on-site and online. The theme of this meeting was ‘Quality Control Issues and International Trends for Biologicals including Vaccines and Plasma-Derived Medicinal Products.’ Three special speeches were presented on sharing the quality control system for biologicals, including NCLs' considerations in preparing the WHO Listed Authorities and sharing MFDS experiences. Furthermore, the participating NCLs shared country-specific issues related to national lot releases during the COVID-19 pandemic and acknowledged the meeting’s crucial role in response preparedness for pandemic emergencies and enhancing regulatory capacity through coalitions and information exchange among NCLs. The NIFDS will cooperate closely with other Asian NCLs to enhance biological product quality control, aiming to establish regional standards and standardize test methods through collaboration.Keywords: National control laboratory, World Health Organization, Western Pacific Region, South-East Asia Region, Biologicals, COVID-19

Introduction

The World Health Organization (WHO) issued Guidelines for Independent Lot Release of Vaccines by Regulatory Authorities in 2010 and recommended that these guidelines be used by the National Regulatory Authority (NRA) and National Control Laboratory (NCL) to establish their own national lot release (NLR) system. Many countries have adopted the WHO’s NLR recommendations as their definitive national requirement, as specified in the guidelines or after modification, provided that the safety and efficacy of vaccines have been verified [1]. In addition, as part of endeavors to strengthen the lot release capabilities of NRAs/NCLs, the WHO recommended the establishment of regional laboratory networks as a forum for sharing information, exchanging technical experiences, and facilitating cooperation between NRAs/NCLs. The WHO Western Pacific Region (WPR) and South East Asia Region (SEAR) member states' NCL network meeting was first convened with the aim of better implementing science- and risk-based lot release of vaccines and plasma-derived medicinal products (PDMPs) through establishing regional standards, collaborating on studies to standardize lot release test methods, and exchanging information on policies and technologies [2,3]. Since the onset of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic in 2019, not only the WHO but also countries worldwide have made significant efforts to support the development of COVID-19 vaccines and PDMPs. In addition, Asian NCLs have formulated policies to facilitate rapid responses and have endeavored to control the spread of COVID-19, while sharing information on the responses employed by each country in meetings that have been held consistently. Despite the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, the seventh meeting was held on September 6, 2022, in Seoul, the Republic of Korea. The meeting was hosted by the National Institute of Food and Drug Safety Evaluation (NIFDS) of the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) and was attended by NCL representatives from India, Indonesia, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Vietnam. All representatives and other colleagues from the seven NCLs participated in the meeting through a video conference system. The theme of this meeting was ‘Quality Control Issues and International Trends for Biologicals including Vaccines and PDMPs.’ Three distinguished speakers delivered special speeches at the event: Dr. Jong Won Kim (NIFDS, Korea), Dr. Silke Schepelmann (Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA), United Kingdom), and Mr. Jae Ho Jung (BNPcare, Korea). Subsequently, information on quality control of vaccines and other biologicals was shared by the NCLs of six member states. Finally, a panel discussion was led by Dr. Jin Ho Shin (WHO). The purpose of this meeting report was to provide summaries of special speeches and shared information from each NCL, as well as to present proposals and plans by representative regulators to strengthen cooperation between NCLs in the Asian region.

Summary of WP-SEA region NCL Network meetings from 2016 to 2021.

The 1st meeting was held on September 1, 2016, to share the results of ongoing research and promote collaboration between the NCLs. In the NCL presentation session, countries, including Australia, China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, and Vietnam, first discussed their current status of research on the quality control of biologicals and then reviewed the quality control of snake venom and anti-venom production and the progress of a collaborative study on the Korean national standard candidate for snake venom. Additionally, the establishment of the second regional standard for anti-venom was discussed, along with the characterization of the Vero cell genome landscape and its application to quality control. Furthermore, the importance of collaboration among the interested participants was emphasized. In conclusion, the meeting laid the foundation for networking among the NCLs in the WPR and paved the way for continued collaboration, which will eventually improve the region’s capacity for the quality control of biological products.

The 2nd meeting was held on September 20, 2017, jointly organized by the NIFDS of the MFDS and the WHO Western Pacific Regional Office (WPRO). In the NLR system session, countries including Canada, China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Malaysia, and Vietnam share information on their current NLR systems, including current practices and developments in the risk-based lot release of vaccines. In the session on the quality control of blood products, experts from the National Institute for Biological Standards and Control (NIBSC) shared quality control and research results for blood coagulation factor VIII and discussed the measurement of procoagulant activity in immunoglobulin products. Japan has proposed a regional collaborative study to test aggregated immunoglobulins that are free from complement activity. Korea proposed a collaborative study for a cell-based Japanese Encephalitis vaccine potency assay and called for the participation of other NCLs in a collaborative study for the first Korean Gloydius spp. anti-venom national standard [4]. In general, the participants agreed to continue communicating and coordinating the presentation of the study results.

The 3rd meeting was held on June 28, 2018, and was attended by regulators from China, India, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Vietnam, and the WHO WPRO. During the meeting, each country delivered a presentation on the quality management and safety of immunoglobulins with clinical cases, while sharing their research results related to the quality control of blood products. In addition, the attendees discussed international collaborative studies aimed at standardizing quality testing methods for blood products.

The 4th meeting was held on June 27, 2019, and was attended by regulators from India, China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Vietnam, and the WHO WPRO. During the meeting, each country presented its current status of the NLR system based on risk assessment. Proposals were made to develop a summary protocol (SP) for standardizing PDMP production and quality control, exchanging quality control test results, analyzing trends, and providing education. In addition, the necessity of education and training, including hands-on training on PDMPs, to enhance the quality control competency of each institution was discussed.

The 5th meeting was held on September 8, 2020, and was attended by regulators from Japan, Malaysia, the Republic of Korea, Vietnam, the Philippines, and the WHO WPRO. In this meeting, WHO experts gave presentations on the 2020–2023 WHO action plan for the supply of safe and effective blood products as well as the WHO benchmarking tool for blood and blood products. Another presentation was delivered by a Korean expert on the management of raw material plasma for fractionation at the Korea Red Cross, along with the statistical data for quality control of biological products. The participants shared significant updates regarding the NLR of biological products, and discussed the necessity of preparing a contact point list to activate the network between NCLs.

The 6th meeting was held on September 14, 2021, and was attended by regulators from Japan, Malaysia, Republic of Korea, Vietnam, the Philippines, and the WHO WPRO. In this meeting, a WHO expert presented a global update on blood and blood products under the theme of international trends and policy directions for the quality control of biological products, such as COVID-19 vaccines and PDMPs. A Korean expert presented Browse Lab digital innovation strategies that link advanced informatics technologies to improve data integrity. The NIFDS announced the NLR for COVID-19 vaccines in Korea, and the participants discussed recent NLR-related issues, while sharing a contact point list to activate networks among the NCLs and the importance of sharing information to strengthen the quality control of biological products.

Presentations in the seventh meeting

Welcome and special speeches: sharing the quality control system for biologicals

The meeting was opened with a warm welcome by Dr. In Soo Shin, Director of Blood Products Division at the NIFDS, Korea. He emphasized the role of NCLs during and after the COVID-19 pandemic and called for a stronger international coalition to improve pandemic preparedness. After Dr. Shin welcomed and explained the objectives of the meeting, Dr. Jong Won Kim (NIFDS, Korea), Dr. Silke Schepelmann (MHRA, UK), and Mr. Jae Ho Jung (BNPcare, Korea) kicked off the first session with their special speeches.

Considerations of NCLs for preparing WHO Listed Authorities (WLA) (Dr. Jong Won Kim, NIFDS, Korea)

Dr. Kim shared his recent experience in preparing for WLA evaluation as an NCL researcher. The highlights of his presentation are as follows.

First of all, (national regulatory system) a legal and scientific regulatory system was established including 1) preparation of legal bases and organizational structures, such as divisional structure, to support testing and evaluation by the MFDS, 2) preparation carried out in accordance with the MFDS notifications and the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act, and 3) a subdivided, specialized structure for vaccine testing and evaluation of new infectious disease vaccines and blood products in compliance with drug safety regulations. Second, (scientific risk based approach) a risk based NLR system was introduced in 2016 and well operated. Third, (global standardization) 1) international test proficiency program was participated, 2) laboratories and equipment were validated, and 3) standard testing methods were established according to global standardization. Fourth, (transparency) regarding biopharmaceutical quality control, information disclosure to the public was continued. Finally, (public communication) 1) an MFDS annual NLR report was published, 2) biomedical quality test methods were issued, and 3) websites and social networking service were utilized in order to be communicated.

Independent quality control of biological products (Dr. Silke Schepelmann, MHRA, UK)

Dr. Silke Schepelmann presented a brief overview of the independent control testing procedure for biological medicines in the UK. The procedure consisted of several steps, including 1) a review of the manufacturer’s data (protocol review); 2) independent testing by official medicine control laboratories; 3) typical assays to determine potency, identity, purity/integrity, or detection of adventitious viruses; and 4) data monitoring, which included a comparison between the manufacturers' and their own data, as well as trend analysis. Dr. Schepelmann emphasized the significance of data monitoring, particularly for frequently performed assays, by discussing quality control chart rules and providing relevant examples. Quality control charts are constructed to detect deviations, trends, and shifts, and can be used to monitor the consistency of multiple variables. A trend analysis using linear regression can be performed at set intervals. Dr. Schepelmann emphasized that standardization through the use of well-characterized and stable standards and reference materials enabled1) accurate and consistent measurement for complex biological materials; 2) harmonization and comparability of results across laboratories and batches produced at different manufacturing sites; and 3) development of criteria for the production and control of biological medicines, as well as for decision-making purposes.

Harmonized good manufacturing practice (GMP) requirements on biological medicinal products (Mr. Jae Ho Jung, BNPcare, Korea)

Mr. Jung shared his recent experiences regarding GMP requirements for biological products. The highlights of his presentation are as follows.

Several international institutions provide GMP guidelines; however, in Korea, GMP regulations for medicinal products are managed in accordance with the guidelines of the Pharmaceutical Inspection Cooperation Scheme. A control strategy based on quality risk management principles aims to minimize variability and reduce contamination and cross-contamination by designing processes, equipment, facilities, and utilities, controlling the conditions of preparation and addition of buffers and reagents, implementing appropriate sampling procedures, and providing operator training. From the perspective of the pharmaceutical quality system and quality risk management, it is crucial to ensure production consistency throughout the entire process, including starting material management, change control, trend analysis, and deviation management. The monitoring system also enables early detection of any unwanted or unanticipated factors that may affect the quality, safety, and efficacy of the product. General guidance regulates relevant personnel (training, health checks, etc.), starting materials, seed lot and cell bank systems, premises and equipment, containment, production, campaign production, validation, quality control, documentation, and animal use. In contrast, specific guidance regulates various products, such as animal-sourced products, vaccines, recombinant products, and monoclonal antibody products.

Country presentations: recent issues on lot release between NCLs

India (Harish Chander, National Institute of biologicals (NIB))

Dr. Harish Chander provided a brief overview of the regulatory system for lot release of blood products and vaccines in India. The NIB laboratory conducts tests on a total of 278 product categories, including blood products, COVID-19 kits, recombinant products, therapeutic antibodies, and vaccines. The lab tests approximately 2000 batches annually. In India, there are 24 plasma-derived and recombinant products available, including albumin and immunoglobulins. The NIB actively participate in international collaborations in the areas of method validation and international standards, including partnerships with organizations such as the WHO and the European Directorate for the Quality of Medicines and HealthCare (EDQM). Dr. Harish also shared a recent case of major out-of-specification results in blood products. He pointed out that the anti-complementary activity test was one of the major challenges in India and introduced ongoing validation studies of alternative methods using the C1q kit by M/s Quidel and total hemolytic complement kit by M/s Binding Site. He also briefly described the NLR system for vaccines, including independent testing according to the guidelines provided in the WHO Technical Report Series Number 978. In summary, 1) lot release is one of the six critical regulatory functions of the WHO and a significant focus in the assessment of NRAs/NCLs in the prequalification program; 2) lot release is a post-licensing activity that involves independent review and approval by the NRA of the SPs of manufacturing and all quality control processes prior to release; and 3) vaccines are biological products used in healthy populations. Problems related to vaccine quality have a direct impact on public safety and the acceptance of immunization programs, thus potentially compromising public confidence in immunization and, hence, public health strategies. Therefore, a thorough and independent review of the manufacturing and quality control data for each lot is necessary to ensure its consistent quality before market release.

Indonesia (Ratih Pujilestari, National Quality Control Laboratory of Drug and Food (NQCLDF))

Ms. Ratih Pujilestari introduced her organization’s legal framework, quality management, and NLR system. She also discussed the current factors affecting lot release in Indonesia and briefly outlined the NQCLDF’s strategies for responding to COVID-19. The quality management system she mentioned was based on International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 9001:2015 and ISO 17025:2015. Indonesia has been recognized by the WHO NRA benchmarking for vaccines as being at maturity level 4 or above in both the lot release function and lab access and testing function, as well as a WHO-contracted lab for Diphtheria-Tetanus-Pertussis vaccines. She emphasized the NQCLDF’s response measures to the COVID-19 pandemic, which included 1) implementing agile COVID-19 vaccine lot release timelines for up to three working days, 2) conducting routine discussion with manufacturers, 3) verifying analytical methods through technology transfer, and 4) monitoring the cold chain over 30 batches of the vaccine. The number of vaccine lot releases was 889, 714, and 1241 in 2019, 2020, and 2021, respectively.

Japan (Madoka Kuramitsu, National Institute of Infectious Diseases (NIID))

Mr. Madoka Kuramitsu primarily introduced the removal of the abnormal toxicity test (ATT) from the minimum requirements for biological products in Japan, as specified in the Japanese Pharmacopoeia for Biological Products). In summary, he presented the following points: 1) ATT has been an important quality control test for vaccines and blood products in Japan; 2) After the exemption of ATT with several vaccines, harmonization with the WHO policy of the removal of ATT has started; 3) Since 2021, NIID has been removing ATT from all biological products; and 4) The removal of ATT from all biological products will be approved in NIID within 2022 and is expected to be finalized in early 2023.

Malaysia (Nee Yuan Qi, National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (NPRA))

Ms. Nee Yuan Qi (Eva) briefly introduced the NLR process for biological products implemented by the NPRA in Malaysia. In summary, she covered the following topics in her presentation 1) Background information on the NPRA; 2) milestones in the implementation of the lot release process in Malaysia; 3) lot release requirements in Malaysia; 4) timeline for issuing lot release certificates; 5) challenges faced in the lot release process; 6) measures implemented to address any identified gaps; and 7) international and local collaboration and the way forward. All vaccines and PDMPs manufactured in Malaysia must go through several lot release processes. The total numbers of batches released for vaccines and PDMPs since 2015 were 4619 and 932, respectively. She presented the following measures to address the identified gaps: 1) Recognizing NRA/NCL release certificates as an assurance for product quality; 2) limiting lab capacity and evaluating critical test data to ensure the quality consistency of each manufactured lot; 3) recruiting staff from other laboratories within the department to assist with cold chain inspection and evaluation of SP during the COVID-19 pandemic; 4) allowing rolling submission of lot release documents to facilitate the timely release of vaccines to the market; and 5) enhancing the regulatory control of vaccines by collaborating with other divisions under the Ministry of Health and focusing on developing potency test method for vaccines.

Philippines (Geraldine N. Dimapilis, Food and Drug Administration (FDA))

Ms. Geraldine N. Dimapilis presented the NLR system implemented in the Philippines. In summary, her presentation included the following points: 1) Background information about the FDA Common Services Laboratory (CSL); 2) introduction of the NLR system; 3) overview of the regulatory process with a consigned timeline, 4) achievements of the NLR system, and 5) and main constraints and challenges faced by the CSL. The presentation also highlighted that, the CSL has an established quality management system following the ISO 17025:2017, and the FDA is accredited for ISO 9001:2015. Therefore, the lot release system in the Philippines was implemented following the ISO 9001 and 17,025 requirements. The annual numbers of lot releases in 2018, 2019, 2020, and 2021 were 315, 317, 337, and 568, respectively. The four main achievements were as follows: 1) adoption of online application processing; 2) involvement of CSL-Vaccine and Biological Unit Staff in dossier review for marketing authorization; 3) updated procedure for evaluation based on the WHO technical report series; and 4) as a reliance policy, the FDA is also an associate member of the WHO NCL Network for Biologicals. Their main challenges include establishing vaccine facilities, reviewing SP for COVID-19 vaccines, developing local vaccine production capabilities, building capacity, and securing resources.

Vietnam (Luu Thi Dung, National Institute for control of vaccine and biologicals (NICVB))

Ms. Luu Thi Dung presented an overview of the current status of the lot release system for vaccine and biological product in Vietnam’s NRA/NCL. The NRA/NCL comprises 152 employees of the NICVB. The numbers of lot releases in 2019, 2020, and 2021 were 910, 1010, and 1225, respectively, indicating an annual increase of 15–20%. She also presented the distribution of imported and locally developed COVID-19 vaccines in Vietnam. Furthermore, she presented the lot release activities for vaccines and biologicals, including policies for the review and testing of SP. She also presented the recent collaborative activities of NRA/NCL, which included participation in the NRA/NCL countries network and collaboration on testing. These activities were as follows: 1) Development of a new potency test of dengue vaccine by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) in collaboration with French NCL in 2017; 2) Development of a new potency test of Japanese Encephalitis vaccine by ELISA in collaboration with the NIFDS in 2019–2020; and 3) Development of a new potency test for monoclonal antibodies in collaboration with the Cuban Regulatory Agency in 2019. Ms. Luu also expressed Vietnam’s interest in collaborating with regional NCLs for alternative approaches to replace animal testing. She expressed the country’s eagerness to receive support from the WHO and NRAs in the region, as well as to share experiences in developing national policies to ensure the safety and effectiveness of vaccines and biological products, while managing risks. She stressed that developing national standards is an important strategy for the quality control of NCL in Vietnam.

Panel discussion (Chair: Dr. Jin Ho Shin, WHO)

The panel discussion was chaired by Dr. Shin with all meeting participants in attendance. The highlighted outcomes are as follows.

Dr. Shin summarized the main achievements of the first through sixth WP-SEA biregional NCL network meetings. 1) The first meeting reviewed the progress of the plan for collaborative studies on snake venom and anti-venom. 2) At the second meeting, Japan proposed a regional collaborative study on testing for anti-complement activity from aggregated immunoglobulin, and Korea proposed a cell-based Japanese Encephalitis vaccine potency assay and called on the voluntary participation of other NCLs in a collaborative study on the first Korean Gloydius spp. anti-venom standard. 3) Key outcomes of the third to sixth meetings were the sharing of results from regional technical cooperation and collaborative studies on aggregates in immunoglobulin products and anti-complementary activity. Additionally, as a global policy development, the WHO NRA global benchmarking tool indicator for strengthening the blood product regulatory system was reviewed.

Dr. Shin introduced a meeting of the Western Pacific Regional Alliance of National Regulatory Authorities for Medical Products: Focus on regulatory preparedness during public health emergencies (August 30 to September 1, 2022). He proposed sharing best practices and step-by-step implementation plans for risk-based lot releases for vaccines and PDMPs as potential topics for the eighth meeting. It is necessary to educate and share best practices in trend analysis, which entails the following requirements. 1) Access to lot release information to facilitate reliance; 2) Real-time access to lot release results through an open source; 3) Access to independent lot release certificates issued by NRA/NCL; 4) Access to lot release evaluation report issued by NRA/NCL: in case of suspected quality defect; 5) Access to SP issued by the manufacturer in case of suspected quality defect.

Conclusion and future directions

It was noted that the risk-based NLR approach has been adopted by all NCLs of the participating countries, although the extent of its introduction varies among the NCLs. Vietnam has experienced a steady 20% annual growth in its lot release over the past three years, while Indonesia and the Philippines have marked an increase of 73% and 68%, respectively, in their lot release in 2021 compared to 2020. India has achieved an annual lot release of 24 different products including PDMPs and recombinant products. Meanwhile, Malaysia has implemented a risk-based approach with a 3-phase structure, conducting selected tests based on the extent to which relevant facilities and expertise have been established. Furthermore, the NLR in Malaysia required the submission of batch release certificates from the NRA/NCL of the country of origin, the SP, and a test report for review. When a batch release certificate is not available, the evaluation of the SP becomes compulsory. This practice applies only to the release of products that are urgently required to address a public health emergency during a pandemic [5]. Furthermore, reviewing the partially completed certificates of analysis (pending certain test results) was one of the approaches used to facilitate lot release during the pandemic. The MFDS implemented a risk-based NLR approach with four phases in 2016, wherein assessments are conducted annually to determine the appropriate phase based on identified risks, which allows either SP-only review or SP review plus testing (Table 2). The application of an NLR requires the submission of the SP, test report, and, if necessary, raw data and certificate of plasma pool for PDMPs in Korea. If the SP and test reports cannot be submitted at the same time as the application for NLR, they can be submitted up to 15 days before the corresponding deadline for processing civil affairs [6]. Notably, during the COVID-19 pandemic, Indonesia, Japan, and Korea [7] have conducted accelerated NLR of COVID-19 vaccines since 2021, while the Philippines authorized the emergency use of COVID-19 vaccines.

The participants were convinced that trend analysis is a significant tool to ensure consistency in lot release. As pointed out by Dr. Silke Schepelmann and Mr. Jung in their respective speeches, trend analysis enables the detection of predictable defects. Experts have stressed that continuous trend analysis is important for deviation management, as it can help detect any potential impact on the quality, safety, and efficacy of a product. Furthermore, since deviations, trends, and shifts can be detected by monitoring multiple variables using a quality control chart, experts emphasized the importance of continuous trend analysis by product to detect any factors that may impact the quality, safety, and efficacy of a product. Given the significance of trend analysis data in contributing to the credibility of NCL testing by ensuring consistent test results, the next meeting should discuss root cause analysis methods, including the identification of the manufacturing processes and test procedures, through the conduct of case analyses of products with deviation potential.

Japan presented its plan to achieve harmonization with the WHO policy on removing ATT from the minimum requirements for biological products by 2023. Korea removed ATT from quality control testing for biological products in August 2022 [8]. As sterility and endotoxin tests have been used to determine product contamination, it was concluded that the removal of ATT would not have any negative impact on product safety. This conclusion is consistent with the practices in the United States, Canada, and Europe, where ATT is not required for quality testing of biological products [9]. The foregoing changes in the environment for NCL testing are considered accomplishments produced by regulatory science as they have led to the minimization of animal use in testing and the global harmonization of quality control criteria.

The WHO recommends that NRAs/NCLs establish regional/national standards calibrated against international standards to be prepared for a likely situation where only limited quantities of international standards are available [10]. Consequently, the WP region responded by establishing a regional standard for anti-snake venom, which involved a collaborative study among China, Japan, and Korea. The established regional standard was then used for NLR testing of anti-venom products [11]. Table 3 shows the plans for the establishment of national standards by the participating countries. The NIFDS is currently planning a study to establish the second national standard for anti-thrombin concentrate scheduled for 2023. Since the first national standard was established in 2006 and is expected to run out of stock, the participation of Asian NCLs will be fervently welcomed. Against this backdrop, in the eighth meeting, we will identify what should be pursued in the collaborative study through the exchange of detailed information on the biological standards established by each country. Additionally, we will propose how to establish regional standards, such as anti-venom, by conducting collaborative studies involving NCLs in the region (Appendix A).

Having been engaged in international cooperation to conduct analyses of the need to apply the principles of 3Rs (replacement, reduction, and refinement) in quality control, including the lot release of biological products, WHO is currently preparing a guideline that recommends alternative approaches to replace animal testing [12]. Korea is also showing a growing interest in promoting the use of 3Rs-compliant alternatives to animal testing, which is gaining traction with government-led research. Accordingly, the NIFDS/MFDS is planning to conduct research to introduce potency assays of anti-tetanus human immunoglobulin and anti-snake venom, as well as monocyte activation tests that can replace the rabbit pyrogen test. The agency aims to validate the resulting alternative methods for animal testing and, subsequently standardize them through collaborative studies among the NCLs. The WP-SEA region NCL network meeting will hopefully contribute to expanding the scope of sustainable cooperation among member states by exploring shared interest, such as collaborative studies that require standardization of quality control of biological products, exchanging experiences, and sharing information.

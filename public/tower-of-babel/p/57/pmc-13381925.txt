Multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation of the bearing deformation process and stability of the water-isolated coal pillar under mining
Authors: Beifang Wang, Jiaqi Jiang, Jing Zhang, Donghao Qi, Haokun Ma, Gang Lei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The long-term stability of water-isolated coal pillars in water-accumulated goafs within ecologically fragile mining areas is severely threatened by the coupled effects of mine water immersion and mining disturbance. In this study, triaxial loading and triaxial loading with unilateral unloading experiments were conducted to simulate the complex stress paths experienced by water-isolated coal pillars throughout their service period. Based on the bearing deformation behavior and failure mechanisms of water-immersed coal samples, the main factors influencing the stability of water-isolated coal pillars during their service period were analyzed, and a comprehensive stability evaluation method was established. Acoustic emission (AE) monitoring results indicate that during triaxial loading, elevated moisture content and high confining pressure promote a transition in the failure mode of coal samples towards a tensile–shear composite pattern, whereas during triaxial loading with unilateral unloading, these factors facilitate a shift towards a predominantly tensile failure pattern. Under both stress paths, the stability of coal samples is affected by moisture content, confining pressure, and their inherent structure. Accordingly, the stability of water-isolated coal pillars is determined to be primarily influenced by three factors: the geomechanical environment of the surrounding rock, the structural characteristics of the water-isolated coal pillar, and anthropogenic mining activities. A multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system based on the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) was established, comprising these three primary indicators and thirteen secondary indicators. Indicator weights were determined using the scaling method and validated through consistency tests, and a membership function system integrating linear mapping and discrete assignment was constructed to quantify the fuzziness of influencing factors. Taking the 22614 working face of the Daliuta Coal Mine as the engineering background, the stability evaluation value of the water-isolated coal pillar was calculated to be 0.79, indicating an overall stable condition; however, the structural strength degradation under prolonged water immersion poses a potential leakage risk. Based on the evaluation results, a structural model incorporating an elastic zone and an inelastic zone was further established, from which the rational width of the water-isolated coal pillar was derived as no less than 34.12 m, with a critical hydraulic head of 40.22 m. The comprehensive qualitative and quantitative results suggest that it is necessary to enhance the monitoring of stress, seepage pressure, and deformation of the water-isolated coal pillar, so that effective prevention and control measures can be promptly implemented at potential local seepage points to ensure the safety and stability of the water-isolated coal pillar.

Introduction

China has established a coal resource development pattern dominated by large-scale coal bases in ecologically fragile mining areas. These regions are characterized by arid climate, low precipitation, low ecological thresholds, and fragile ecosystems. The current scale and intensity of mining operations have far exceeded the environmental carrying capacity of these areas, posing a significant risk of irreversible damage to the local ecological environment1–3. During mine mining, on the one hand, mining fractures penetrate the aquifer, causing waste of groundwater resources, further deteriorating the regional ecological environment. On the other hand, crack water and production wastewater collect into the goaf to form a stagnant environment, which restricts the safe and efficient production of coal mines. Mine water damage is an important factor threatening coal mine production safety. Among them, water accumulation in the goaf poses a particularly prominent threat to the production of the mining face. Leaving a fortified water-isolated coal pillar between the mining face and the water accumulation area in the goaf is an effective way to prevent and control water damage One4,5. The water-impermeable coal pillar has been accumulated in complex environments for a long time, and its stability is influenced by multiple factors. Therefore, there is an urgent need to find reliable methods to comprehensively and effectively assess the safety and stability of water-isolated coal pillars, so as to help mining area take timely targeted prevention and control measures to reduce mine water disasters.

The stability of coal pillars is influenced by a multitude of factors, including geological conditions, mining methods, excavation disturbances, and coal properties. It is generally accepted that coal pillars with a larger width-to-height ratio exhibit greater bearing capacity and, consequently, better stability6–8. Steart9 proposed an empirical formula for calculating the strength of coal pillars based on extensive field data. Subsequently, Salamon et al.10 refined this strength formula by incorporating the size effect. Furthermore, Ju et al.11 derived a formula for determining the rational width of coal pillars under coupled effects and established a corresponding instability criterion. Chen et al.12 employed numerical simulation techniques to analyze the failure characteristics and load-bearing mechanisms of various types of coal pillars, and investigated the influence of different geological conditions and pillar widths on pillar stability. Chaurasia et al.7 clarified the influence of pillar width-to-height ratio on the strength and failure modes of coal samples through experimental studies. Liu et al.13 determined the rational width of coal pillars under stress-seepage coupling conditions based on field observations and numerical simulations. Shi et al.14 analyzed the stress state, elastic core zone width, damage degree, and energy accumulation of water-immersed and non-immersed coal pillars with varying widths, revealing the impact of mine water on pillar stability. Additionally, Wang et al.15,16 analyzed the water-coal coupling mechanism influenced by mine water flow velocity and aging effects, elucidating the size effect of coal-rock deformation under combined stress-water–rock interactions. For the assessment and prediction of coal pillar stability, primary methodologies include empirical formula methods, safety factor methods, numerical simulation methods, and evaluation model approaches17. Wattimena et al.18 developed a stability evaluation table for coal pillars incorporating overburden geological conditions using logistic regression, thereby integrating geological uncertainty into a probabilistic assessment framework. Idris et al.19 systematically analyzed the impact of geological structural variability on coal pillar stability by combining the Monte Carlo method with artificial neural networks. Kumar et al.20 employed probabilistic methods to analyze case histories of unstable coal pillars, identifying that anthropogenic activities, such as production disturbances, serve as significant triggers for pillar instability. Chen et al.21 proposed a comprehensive evaluation method for roof stability classification based on coupling the grading standards of evaluation indices with an improved analytic hierarchy process for weight allocation. Furthermore, Mark et al.22 developed coal pillar stability analysis software, calibrated with extensive historical data, to achieve precise stability prediction and control.

Many experts and scholars have carried out a lot of research on the stability of coal pillars, and relevant results cover many aspects such as coal pillar instability mechanism, stability evaluation and long-term stability prediction. However, there are few studies on the unilateral corrosion bearing deformation caused by the long-term occurrence of water-isolated coal pillars in water-filled goaf in the mine water corrosion environment, and existing studies are more focused on the stability of coal pillars, and less consideration is given to the superimposed influence of multiple factors in complex water-flooded environments. Therefore, this paper innovatively carries out uniaxial loading and unilateral unloading experiments on mine water-immersed coal samples to clarify the bearing and deformation laws of coal samples and build an evolution model of the bearing and deformation process of the water-isolated coal pillar. On this basis, the factors affecting the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar were comprehensively analyzed, and a comprehensive evaluation model for the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar was established based on the AHP fuzzy theory. Taking the water-filled goaf of Daliuta Coal Mine as the engineering background, the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar was evaluated, providing a new method for evaluating the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar in the water-filled goaf.

Engineering background

Daliuta Coal Mine is located in Shenmu County, Shanxi Province and is a very large modern, high-yield and efficient mine. The 22,614 working face of the mine is a comprehensive mechanized coal mining face with a strike length of 2436.5 m and a dip length of 201.7 m. The working face mainly mines coal seam 2–2, with a simple structure, an average thickness of 7 m, an inclination angle of 1° ~ 3°, and a burial depth of 155.0 ~ 225.0 m. The geological structure is simple, the coal seam inclination is gentle, and the occurrence is stable. It has the advantages of being shallow buried and easy to mine. The basic roof of the overburden is mainly siltstone, and some areas are sandy mudstones, medium sandstone and coarse sandstone. Comprehensive mechanized one-time mining and full-height mining technology is adopted. Mining lanes are arranged in double lanes, and the roof is controlled by natural collapse. The loose layer of the overburden is thick and has no bearing capacity. After the coal seam is extracted, the cracks in the overburden penetrate the aquifer, and groundwater and production wastewater collect into the goaf to form a water-accumulating environment. The water-isolated coal pillar reserved for preventing and controlling water disasters has been significantly weakened by the dissolution mechanical properties of aggressive mine water for a long time. At the same time, surrounding mining activities have subjected the water-isolated coal pillar to cyclic loading and unloading, further exacerbating the risk of instability and damage of the water-isolated coal pillar. According to on-site observation results, mining activities in the working face induce large-area roof settlement and tunnel floor heave to squeeze the coal pillar. Under the combined effect of overburden stress, mining disturbance and mine water erosion, the coal pillar produces expansion deformation, as shown in Fig. 1. Leakage points are formed in some areas of the coal pillar, and there is water accumulation nearby. Therefore, it is urgent to carry out research on the failure and instability laws and influencing factors of key bearing structures of the mining water-isolated coal pillar, and build a multi-level evaluation index system for the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar. Construction characteristics of water-isolated coal pillar

According to the mine working face layout and mining continuation planning, during the service period of the water-isolated coal pillar, its deformation and instability process shows typical gradual characteristics after experiencing the influence of tunnel excavation, working face extraction and mine water dissolution. The bearing and deformation process of the water-isolated coal pillar during its service period can be divided into three stages. During tunnel excavation, the water-isolated coal pillar has not yet been completely formed. The coal body on the surface layer of the coal pillar is broken under the extrusion of high overlying rock load to form a small loose circle. The stress state changes from three-dimensional force to vertical dead load and radial unloading, as shown in Fig. 2a. During the mining of the working face, the dipping overlying rock breaks above the water-isolated coal pillar, and the peak lateral stress induced by the revolving and sinking of the overlying rock shifts to the inside of the water-isolated coal pillar. The fracture range of the surface coal body further increases, and the interior of the coal body presents a dynamic change state of vertical loading, strike constant load, and radial unloading. As the coal seam is extracted in a large area, the overlying rocks are broken on a large scale, and the mining fractures penetrate the surface. The groundwater in the aquifer collects to the goaf through the overlying rocks to form a water-accumulating environment. Under the corrosion of mine water, the fractures in the water-isolated coal pillar continue to expand from the outside to the inside, and the loose area on the surface of the coal pillar increases the water–rock contact area, promoting the mine water to weaken the physical and mechanical properties of the coal body, as shown in Fig. 2b. During mining near the working face, the overlying rock is deformed twice, and the peak lateral stress is transferred to the interior of the water-isolated coal pillar again. Under the long-term action of superimposed overlying rock stress and dynamic and static water pressure, the load of the water-isolated coal pillar breaks through the bearing capacity of the elastic plastic coal and rock mass of both sides. Progressive failure occurs. The plastic failure zone develops towards the central elastic zone, the effective size of the coal pillar is reduced and the stability is reduced. When the supporting capacity of the effective load-bearing structure is lower than the overlying rock load, the overall instability of the water-isolated coal pillar is finally induced. Experimental equipment and plan

Combined with the bearing and deformation process during the service period of the water-isolated coal pillar, there are two different stress occurrence states in coal bodies in different areas: six-sided loading, five-sided loading, and one-sided unloading. In order to further study the influence of water content and confining pressure factors on the mechanical properties of the water-isolated coal pillar. Law, large coal samples and mine water were collected on site, and coal samples with different moisture ratios of 100 mm × 100 mm × 100 mm were prepared for triaxial loading and one-sided unloading experiments.

The true triaxial test system for rock mechanics independently developed by Liaoning University of Engineering Technology was selected as the experimental equipment, including loading/unloading system, load–displacement monitoring system, electronic control system and AE monitoring system, as shown in Fig. 3. The test system can realize independent control of the pressure/displacement of each loading surface of coal and rock true triaxial. The maximum axial load is 1800kN and the maximum lateral load is 800 kN. It can monitor the AE characteristic parameters during the failure process of coal and rock in real time.Fig. 3True triaxial test system for rock mechanics.

True triaxial test system for rock mechanics.

The reference team has achieved research results in experiments on the water absorption characteristics of coal and rock23,24. Coal samples with moisture content of 0 (dry), 7.74% (mine water immersion for 12 h) and 9.17% (saturated) were prepared for load-bearing deformation experiments. Combined with the burial depth of the sample collection point of 160 m ~ 320 m, 4 MPa and 8 MPa were selected as the initial stresses. For the triaxial loading test, under a constant confining pressure of 4 MPa, the axial pressure was increased at a rate of 0.1 MPa/s until failure, after which the loading mode was switched to displacement control. For the triaxial loading with unilateral unloading test, the confining pressure was maintained at 4 MPa. The axial pressure was first increased at a rate of 0.1 MPa/s until reaching 70% of the triaxial compressive strength, which also exceeded the uniaxial compressive strength. Then, while continuing to increase the axial pressure until failure, the confining pressure on one side (y2) was unloaded at a rate of 0.1 MPa/s. Finally, the loading mode was switched to displacement control. Failure modes and AE response characteristics

To further investigate the failure mechanism of coal samples, their failure morphology was correlated with characteristic AE parameters. The failure mode was characterized based on the RA value (ratio of rise time to amplitude) and the AF value (ratio of counts to duration), as illustrated in Fig. 6. AE signals with low AF and high RA values are typically indicative of shear failure, whereas those with high AF and low RA values correspond to tensile failure25. During triaxial loading, dry coal samples predominantly exhibited an “X-shaped” shear failure pattern, accounting for 84.16% of the total failure modes. The shear plane propagated completely through the sample, resulting in large fragment sizes (Fig. 6a). As moisture content increased, the interparticle cementation weakened, and crack networks grew more intricate, shifting the failure mode toward a tensile‑shear composite mechanism under lateral confinement. The proportion of shear failure decreased to 76.95% and 67.12% for water‑immersed and saturated samples, respectively, with a corresponding reduction in fragment size and an increase in fragment count (Fig. 6b,c). When the confining pressure was raised to 8 MPa, the high confinement suppressed full propagation of the shear plane, further reducing the shear‑failure proportion to 55.67%. Under this condition, the samples displayed a pulverized, powder‑like failure morphology (Fig. 6d). Throughout triaxial loading, elevated moisture content and high confining pressure acted synergistically to promote a transition in the dominant failure mode of coal samples from shear‑dominated toward a more complex tensile‑shear composite pattern.Fig. 6Failure morphology and AE response characteristics of coal samples under triaxial loading.

Failure morphology and AE response characteristics of coal samples under triaxial loading.

In contrast to conventional triaxial compression, the process of triaxial loading coupled with unilateral unloading exhibits distinct failure characteristics. The horizontally applied compressive stress forces the coal sample to expand and deform towards the unloaded surface, thereby transitioning its load-bearing state from compression to tension, as depicted in Fig. 7. Under dry conditions, tensile failure accounts for 54.81% of the total failure modes. The combined action of tensile and shear stresses results in a stepped, “U-shaped” fracture pattern (Fig. 7a). With increasing moisture content, the load-bearing capacity of the coal is compromised. Following unloading, the samples become more susceptible to progressive tensile failure, initiating from the unloaded surface and propagating inwards. The proportion of tensile failure increases to 61.83% and 68.34% for water-immersed and saturated samples, respectively. Concurrently, the resulting failure fragments become progressively finer, and the stepped “U-shaped” failure pattern on the unloaded surface becomes more pronounced (Fig. 7b,c). When the confining pressure is elevated to 8 MPa, the high confinement on the other loaded faces rapidly promotes tensile failure towards the unloaded side, further increasing the tensile failure proportion to 74.72% (Fig. 7d). During triaxial loading with unilateral unloading, both elevated moisture content and high confining pressure collectively facilitate a transition in the coal’s failure mode from a tensile-shear composite mechanism towards a predominantly tensile failure pattern.Fig. 7Failure morphology and AE response characteristics of coal samples during triaxial loading with unilateral unloading.

Factors affecting instability of water-isolated coal pillar

The instability of the water-isolated coal pillar is one of the key causes of water inrush disasters in mines. Based on the above-mentioned analysis of the bearing deformation process and mechanical characteristics of the water-isolated coal pillar, it can be determined that the failure and instability of the water-isolated coal pillar is affected by multiple factors such as the surrounding rock mechanical environment and human mining activities. Coupling effects, among which the change of water content is affected by human factors, while the change of confining pressure is restricted by the geomechanical environment. Of course, the original defects of the water-isolated coal pillar also directly affect its bearing capacity. Therefore, the core of the instability of the water-isolated coal pillar can be summarized into three main control factors: the geomechanical environment of the surrounding rock, the structural characteristics of the water-isolated coal pillar, and the influence of other human factors. Following the principles of scientific accuracy, comprehensiveness and convenience, the internal indicators for quantitatively evaluating the geomechanical environment of different mine surrounding rocks can be divided into stratum structure, geological structure, coal seam burial depth, coal seam inclination angle, and coal rock properties; the internal indicators for the structural characteristics of water-isolated coal pillar can be divided into coal pillar width, coal pillar height, fracture development degree, and coal pillar strength; internal indicators influenced by other human factors can be divided into mining method, production disturbance, water storage height, and water storage time.

Other human factors

Fuzzy comprehensive evaluation of the stability of water-isolated coal pillar

There are 3 categories and 13 sub-categories influencing the stability of water-isolated coal pillar in water-accumulating goaf areas, and they have significant fuzziness and uncertainty. It is difficult to directly allocate the weights of the influencing factors, and it is difficult to obtain valuable evaluation results. Therefore, this paper establishes a multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system based on AHP theory to make the weight distribution of each influencing factor more scientific and the evaluation results more objective.

Stability evaluation system of water-isolated coal pillar

The stability evaluation system for water-isolated coal pillars primarily encompasses the establishment of an indicator-based evaluation model, the allocation of weights, and the construction of membership functions.

Conclusion

In this study, triaxial loading and triaxial loading with unilateral unloading experiments were conducted on coal samples with different moisture contents (0, 7.74%, and 9.17%) and confining pressures (4 MPa and 8 MPa) to simulate the stress state variations of water-isolated coal pillars. AE characteristic parameters were employed to reveal the failure characteristics of mine water-immersed coal samples, based on which the main factors influencing their stability were determined. A fuzzy comprehensive evaluation was performed using the AHP theory and validated through an engineering case study. The following conclusions can be drawn:

The bearing deformation process of the water-isolated coal pillar can be categorized into three stages: tunneling influence, primary mining influence, and secondary mining influence. These stages are accompanied by a dynamic stress adjustment process involving vertical loading and radial unloading. The triaxial loading with unilateral unloading stress path can effectively simulate the stress state of the water-isolated coal pillar.

An increase in moisture content leads to a decrease in the elastic modulus and strength of coal samples, whereas an increase in confining pressure enhances these properties. Compared with triaxial loading under the same conditions, the strength of coal samples subjected to unilateral unloading decreased by 10.1%, 6.68%, 7.97%, and 13.02%, respectively, indicating that the effects of moisture content and confining pressure are more pronounced under this stress path.

During triaxial loading, the proportions of shear and tensile failure for saturated coal samples under a confining pressure of 8 MPa were 55.76% and 44.24%, respectively, indicating a transition towards a tensile–shear composite failure pattern. In contrast, during triaxial loading with unilateral unloading, the proportion of tensile failure for saturated coal samples under 8 MPa confining pressure reached 74.72%, indicating a predominantly tensile failure pattern.

The stability of the water-isolated coal pillar is primarily influenced by the geomechanical environment of the surrounding rock, the structural characteristics of the water-isolated coal pillar, and other anthropogenic factors. The comprehensive evaluation result for the stability of the water-isolated coal pillar in the 22614 working face is 0.79, corresponding to a stable condition, which is generally consistent with the actual in-situ situation.

Combined with the elastoplastic mechanical model, the rational width of the water-isolated coal pillar in the 22614 working face was calculated to be 34.12 m, and the hydraulic head should be maintained below 40.22 m to ensure better stability. During the service period of the water-isolated coal pillar, it is necessary to strengthen the monitoring of stress, seepage pressure, and deformation, so that effective prevention and control measures can be promptly implemented.

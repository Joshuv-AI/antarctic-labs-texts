Bhartiya Nyaya Samhita 2023, medical practice and psychiatrists
Authors: Om P. Singh
Published: 2024

Bhartiya Nyaya Samhita 2023[1] is now effective from July 1, 2024, repealing the Indian Penal Code, 1860.[2] There has been a huge hue and cry from various doctors’ groups after its notification.

Before discussing the act, let us first look at the timeline [Figure 1]. The Bill was initially introduced on August 11, 2023 and sent to the standing committee. The committee report came on November 10, 2023. It was withdrawn on December 12, 2023, and the second version was introduced on the same day. It was passed by Lok Sabha on December 20, 2023 and by Rajya Sabha on December 21, 2023. On December 25, 2023, it received the assent of the President of India and became effective from July 1, 2024.

Sex with patients has been a hotly debated issue and is clearly an unethical boundary violation, but it is not unheard of. If we read Section 68 very carefully, it may be punishable even if done with a patient’s consent in clinic or hospital premises. The doctor–patient relationship in legal terms is a fiduciary relationship and falls under this section. The terms “induce” and “seduce” can be interpreted to invalidate consent.

This provision is being contested by the doctors’ community. There are some points to ponder:

While punishment for negligence is up to 5 years in other cases, it is up to 2 years for RMPs (registered medical practitioners), defined as persons registered with NMC or any state councils. The government, in its clarification in parliament, has said that it has decriminalized doctor negligence and reduced punishment.The Supreme Court, in various judgments, has tried to distinguish between negligence and criminal negligence, which is not clarified or incorporated in the document.Under 304 A of IPC, it was either punishment or fine or both, but in Sec 68, it has been made punishable with imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine. So, if negligence is proved, the court has no option but to sentence imprisonment.What happens to other systems of practitioners? Are they punishable for up to 5 years?

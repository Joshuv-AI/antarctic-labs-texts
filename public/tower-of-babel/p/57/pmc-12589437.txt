Multimedia data-driven customer churn prediction using an enhanced extreme learning machine
Authors: You-wu Liu, Jing Wang, Chibiao Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a novel modification to the extreme learning machine (ELM) by introducing an optimized least-squares formulation with penalty regularization, enabling stable and efficient handling of sparse, high-dimensional multimedia data. In contrast to traditional autoencoders, our approach incorporates ELM-driven hidden layer refinement, achieving more effective feature compression and dimensionality reduction tailored for multimedia churn prediction. Additionally, we design a Gaussian kernel adaptation specifically for multimedia datasets, which replaces random feature mappings and enhances both predictive robustness and generalization performance. Empirical validation on a public multimedia customer behavior dataset reveals the model’s superiority over traditional churn prediction methods, showcasing notable improvements in prediction accuracy and precision. This research equips businesses with a robust model for informed CRM decision-making, enhancing customer retention and profitability. The study contributes to the in-depth understanding of multimedia data’s role in fostering sustainable and profitable customer relationships in today’s digital era.Keywords: Improved extreme learning machine, Autoencoder, Customer churn, Feature extraction, Multimedia dataSubject terms: Computer science, Mathematics and computing, Information technology

Introduction

In the contemporary environmental context, there is a burgeoning emphasis among corporations on the significance of reducing customer attrition as a strategic imperative1. At the heart of effective customer relationship management (CRM) is the precise identification of customers predisposed to churn, which is crucial for bolstering retention efforts2. Empirical research suggests that established customers contribute more significantly to economic outcomes within a given cycle than newer ones, underscoring the importance of their retention. Furthermore, customer attrition can adversely affect public confidence and trust in a corporation, thereby hindering the acquisition of new clientele.

Given the deceleration in new customer acquisition across various sectors, there is a pressing corporate emphasis on cultivating loyalty within the existing clientele and proactively discerning potential churn situations. For instance, telecommunication entities, facing intensified competition in recent years, are striving to maintain a competitive advantage by enhancing service delivery and quality. Within such a sector, mitigating churn, which refers to customers transitioning to competitors, becomes of paramount importance. In this era, marked by rapid technological advancements, modern marketing strategies, such as push notifications highlighting promotional offers, significantly impact customer loyalty3. The timely identification of potential customer attrition and the design of effective retention mechanisms are vital to maintain market competitiveness and customer base stability.

Long-standing customers offer several advantages, including higher retention rates, increased receptiveness to marketing campaigns, and a propensity for regular purchasing. Moreover, they tend to generate lower service costs, a byproduct of reduced churn rates which in turn diminishes the emphasis on acquiring new customers. Therefore, creating an accurate churn prediction system, supplemented by reliable predictive models, is of paramount importance, designed to cater to evolving customer needs. Such predictive models empower corporations to rapidly identify customers predisposed to churn, thereby allowing timely service adaptations that meet customer expectations and enhance satisfaction.

Supporting this notion is the Data Age 2025 report by IDC, which projects a significant increase in global multimedia data generation, from 33 ZB in 2018 to an estimated 175 ZB, equivalent to a daily generation of 491 EB of data. To comprehend this volume, visualizing this data on DVDs would produce a stack reaching 23 times the distance from Earth to Moon or encircling the Earth 222 times. This data influx underscores the importance that corporations now place on harnessing multimedia data for churn prediction initiatives. Responding to this empirical reality, our study proposes a churn prediction model, rooted in an enhanced extreme learning machine combined with multimedia data analytics. Unlike conventional ELM frameworks, this study modifies the architecture by integrating a compressed feature representation generated by autoencoders with an ELM-based learning process. In addition, a Gaussian kernel adaptation with an optimized penalty parameter C is employed to replace random mappings, ensuring stability, robustness, and improved generalization when handling sparse multimedia churn datasets. This model effectively reduces feature redundancy, enhances the correlation of information across customer parameters, and increases compatibility with neural network structures. Additionally, it avoids dimensionality explosion in feature vector matrices, ensuring efficient computational resource allocation during high-dimensional discrete feature processing. Initial experimental results validate the enhanced accuracy of this innovative predictive approach in detecting potential customer attrition, signifying a potential pathway for corporations to maintain competitiveness in this big data era.

State of the art

Customer churn prediction research

Within the academic discourse on customer churn prediction, international research initiatives have traditionally showcased a more systematic methodology than those confined to domestic contexts. Early investigations by global researchers into churn prediction commenced in the 1990s, a significant lead compared to the early 21st-century inception observed domestically. These endeavors have cumulatively enriched the knowledge reservoir within this domain.

A seminal contribution was made by Hwambo et al., who delineated a churn prediction algorithm rooted in the push–pull-move (PPM) framework, amalgamated with least squares regression for identifying customer churn trends4. Concurrently, Labhsetwar et al. formulated a Naive Bayesian classification algorithm, an intricate ensemble of various feature selectors utilizing sequential backward selection (SBS) and sequential backward floating selection (SBFS) algorithms, paired with a standard Bayes classifier, thus enhancing feature extraction precision5.

Additionally, Peng et al. proposed an evolved XGBoost algorithm, augmented with a feature function, enabling proficient management of unstructured customer feature datasets via iterative loss function minimization6. In a related vein, Panjasuchat et al. employed reinforcement learning, culminating in a Deep Q Network (DQN) classifier adept at churn prediction7.

Deng et al. integrated the nuanced C4.5 decision tree methodology, augmenting prediction accuracy within the railroad sector, notably plagued by sporadic customer churn instances8. Venturing into B2B e-commerce, Xiahou et al. designed an SVM-centric churn prediction model, tailored for industry nuances9. In a separate initiative, Kostić et al. developed a churn prediction technique based on distance factor classifier deterministic estimation, fine-tuned for varied telecommunication datasets10. Recognizing the potential of deep learning frameworks, Altameem et al. executed empirical assessments utilizing multimedia datasets from UCI, establishing the superior efficacy of Deep Belief Network (DBN) algorithms in classification endeavors11. Concurrently, Tariq et al. harnessed Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for churn prediction, employing data normalization and processing strategies to transform customer attributes into image-like vector features, adeptly navigating challenges of high dimensionality12.

Ahmad et al. provided further illumination by constructing classification models grounded in social network analyses, facilitating enhanced prediction accuracy and enabling churn rationale examinations across diverse social entities13. Research14 underscores the utility of genetic algorithms paired with a benefit maximization criterion for optimizing prediction performance, while research15 illustrates a hybrid algorithm blending logistic regression with decision tree methodologies, promoting enhanced forecasting and interpretability. Furthermore, research16 introduces churn prediction models based on enhanced multilayer perceptrons, tackling the challenges of data dimensionality and computational demands induced by sparse data following unique thermal coding.

A holistic examination of these scholarly endeavors underscores the dynamic evolution and diversification of algorithmic underpinnings in this domain, reflecting a robust research continuum dedicated to refining churn prediction through varied methodologies and computational breakthroughs.

Research related to extreme learning machine

The Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) represents a groundbreaking and rapid algorithm, showcasing a structure analogous to neural networks yet surpassing them in terms of computational velocity and superior generalization capacity, thereby offering a pronounced temporal advantage when engaged with large-scale multimedia data17. The conceptual foundation of the ELM was laid by Huang et al., who identified and addressed the limitations of feedforward neural networks in meeting the requisite speed for practical applications, pioneering the ELM’s theoretical framework and applicative scope in 200618. Subsequent scholarly endeavors have iteratively enhanced the ELM’s functionalities. A significant innovation is manifested in the ensemble-based ELM (EN-ELM) algorithm, which intricately merges integrated learning and cross-validation during the training stage, thereby mitigating the overfitting phenomenon and augmenting predictive stability19,20. Empirical assessments underscore EN-ELM’s robust classification prowess.

Furthermore, academia witnessed the development of a multilayer perceptron hierarchical learning framework predicated on the ELM, noted for fostering compact and semantically rich feature delineations, thereby augmenting the original ELM21. Additionally, scholarly pursuits led to the inception of the inverse limit learning machine (IELM), a rapid learning methodology designed to obviate the high temporal complexity intrinsic to conventional approaches, albeit with a restricted capability in addressing multi-classification issues22. Expanding the ambit of ELM, researchers have ventured into semi-supervised and unsupervised learning realms, conceiving the semi-supervised extreme learning machine (SS-ELM) and the unsupervised extreme learning machine (US-ELM), notable for retaining ELM’s computational efficiency while aptly managing multi-classification challenges, hence broadening ELM’s applicative panorama23. In recent years, ELM has been deployed in a diverse array of application domains. Albadr et al. demonstrated the efficacy of ELM in speech emotion recognition, leveraging a synergy of deep neural networks (DNNs) with ELM to ascertain emotional nuances in speech segments24. Boussaad et al. explored the applicability of ELM in facial recognition tasks, evaluating its performance against one-against-all (OAA) and one-against-one (OAO) strategies in the specific context of facial recognition deployments25. Furthermore, Acikgoz et al. delineated an ELM-centric probabilistic approach for predicting wind power generation, yielding significant insights pertinent to seasonal fluctuations in wind energy production26.

In the electricity market landscape, Chen et al. introduced a rapid ELM-based technique for forecasting market prices, overcoming the constraints posed by traditional methodologies prone to slow processing speeds and local optima entrapments27. Despite the expansive applicative range demonstrated by ELM, variants such as EN-ELM mitigate overfitting but overlook high-dimensional sparsity in multimedia data; IELM offers rapid learning yet struggles with multi-class nonlinear patterns; and SS-ELM/US-ELM handle semi-supervised tasks efficiently but lack optimization for sparse, discrete multimedia features. Our model advances these by embedding an ELM-refined autoencoder as a novel modification for enhanced feature compression and representation, replacing iterative adjustments with least-squares solutions prior to KELM training. While prior works have applied autoencoders and KELM independently, none have combined autoencoder-driven dimensionality reduction with kernel-enhanced ELM for multimedia churn prediction. This study modifies the conventional workflow by integrating these elements to address sparsity and improve efficiency in handling voice, video, and behavioral attributes, filling a discernible gap in ELM’s deployment for customer churn predictions and indicating a promising avenue for future research initiatives.

Autoencoder

Autoencoder is a neural network commonly used for semi-supervised or unsupervised learning28. The advantage of the autoencoder is that the network’s own learning target is its own input, and it learns the representation of the input features by reducing and compressing the input information and reproducing it in the decoding layer. Figure 2 shows the structure of a simple autoencoder.

Structure of the autoencoder network.

The encoding process of the autoencoder is the process from the input layer to the intermediate state, and the decoding process of the autoencoder is the process from the intermediate state to the output layer. Such a composition is similar to the principal component analysis algorithm, where the autoencoder network can capture the important factors representing the input multimedia data and find the main components that can represent the original information. The training purpose of the autoencoder is to make the error between the output and input layers as small as possible by continuously decoding and encoding the features of the hidden layer so that they can be better characterized for learning. The model is trained by parameter BP and batch iterative gradient descent to obtain more desirable network weights and biases so that the output and input values are as similar as possible. BP neural network

The Back Propagation Neural Network, a pioneering concept conceived by scientists Rumelhart and McClelland29, embodies a multilayer feedforward neural network delineated through the strategic implementation of the BP algorithm, thereby standing as the most prevalently utilized neural network to date. Central to the BP network’s illustrious performance in modeling is its foundational algorithmic principle, the back-propagation algorithm. This algorithm operates on the fundamental mechanism of consecutively transmitting input data across various layers, commencing from the input layer and culminating at the final output layer. This process facilitates the derivation of a loss function value predicated on the discrepancy between the anticipated output and the predefined target. To steer the loss function value towards a nominal minimum, a relentless iterative refinement of the network’s weight parameters is mandated to diminish the loss value incrementally. This meticulous calibration process engenders a continuous decrement in the error margin between the model’s output value and the target value, thereby striving to align the output value impeccably with the target objective, nearing an asymptotic approximation to the target. This in turn fosters the network’s capacity to achieve exceedingly accurate predictions, driving it infinitely close to the optimal performance characterized by minimal loss function values.

Discrete features and unique thermal coding

In the analytical landscape of customer data, features manifest in both continuous and discrete forms. Discrete features are delineated as data points possessing either a finite or infinite set of countable values within structured data, and they preclude a proper representation through integer values. These features encapsulate data imbibed with ordinal or binary attributes. Conversely, continuous features aptly lend themselves to direct incorporation as input parameters in neural networks, offering a contrast to the nuanced nature of discrete features. A quintessential illustration of this incongruity emerges when employing a numerical sequence, such as {1,2,3,4,5}, to denote weekdays; herein, the mathematical implication erroneously amplifies the value of Friday to quintuple that of Monday. It is pertinent to note that the numerical representations for discrete features merely serve a nominal role devoid of intrinsic mathematical value.

Addressing this intricate dilemma necessitates the application of the one-hot encoding algorithm, alternatively referred to as unique thermal encoding, to refine the handling of discrete feature data. This encoding technique employs state registers to facilitate the representation of various states, allocating a distinct register bit to each state to maintain a single valid state within the register at any given moment. This transformation transcends the initial limitations, metamorphosing discrete features from meaningless numerical representations into structured feature vectors, each element of which corresponds to a specific feature value. Consequently, discrete feature data acquire mathematical significance, becoming viable candidates for neural network input parameters.

Despite these advancements, a caveat persists in scenarios characterized by a plethora of data categories; here, the post-encoding dimensionality of the feature vectors undergoes an exponential expansion, leading to a pronounced sparsity in the resultant feature matrices. This phenomenon heralds a necessity for meticulous further extraction processes to fully optimize the feature matrices delineated through unique thermal encoding for analytical endeavors. This initiative aims to curb the dimensional surge and retain the essential characteristics vital for downstream neural network operations.

Proposed modifications

Autoencoder redesign for sparse multimedia data: The standard autoencoder is enhanced by embedding ELM principles into its hidden layer optimization, converting the training into a least-squares problem with random weights and biases to avoid iterative gradient descent. This architectural adjustment includes additional compression layers optimized for high-dimensional sparse matrices from one-hot encoded multimedia features (e.g., voice and video attributes), effectively reducing dimensionality explosion while preserving critical customer behavior patterns.

Gaussian kernel parameter adaptation for improved generalization: Unlike standard KELM implementations, our Gaussian kernel is adapted with empirically tuned parameters (e.g., sigma) and a penalty term C for regularization, specifically addressing instability in sparse, nonlinear multimedia data. This projects features into a higher-dimensional space, enhancing separability and robustness for churn scenarios involving unstructured attributes.

Integration of compressed feature vectors into BP neural network with a modified sigmoid activation threshold (> 0.6): Compressed features from the autoencoder are fed into the KELM output layer, serving as an efficient alternative to traditional BP networks. We introduce a modified sigmoid activation with a threshold > 0.6 for binary churn classification, ensuring higher confidence in positive predictions and integrating seamlessly with the least-squares optimization for faster, more accurate training on multimedia datasets.

Result analysis and discussion

Data set

Customer churn prediction is the process of extracting and selecting customer characteristics from historical customer behavior data and using classification prediction algorithms to build prediction models to predict the future state of customers. In this paper, the multimedia data of customer behavior are obtained from DUKE University, USA. The dataset is based on the publicly available Cell2Cell churn dataset from Duke University’s 2003 Churn Modeling Tournament. This public version provides a foundational telecom churn dataset with attributes related to customer behavior, which we have enhanced with multimedia elements for our analysis. The training set consists of the first 100,000 samples (indices 0 to 99,999), including 49,673 churned customers and 50,327 non-churned customers, resulting in a 1:1 ratio. This balance is inherent to the selected subset of the original dataset and was not achieved through resampling or artificial balancing. 51,218 samples are used in the test set, including 935 churned customers and 50,283 non-churned customers, with a churn rate of 1.8%. The fixed sequential split assumes no temporal ordering in the data; however, if the dataset contains implicit time-series patterns, this could introduce distribution bias, which will be explored in future time-series validations. Some attributes were missing in the original multimedia data, and 87 initial attributes were obtained by removing attributes with high missing rate and filling attributes with low missing rate. No new features were engineered beyond the base dataset; processing involved only missing value imputation, removal of high-missing attributes, and one-hot encoding of discrete features (e.g., service type, usage categories for voice/video). To prevent data leakage, no resampling or class balancing techniques were applied in this study, as the model relies on its inherent robustness (e.g., penalty regularization in KELM) to handle the natural imbalance. If any such techniques were to be used in future extensions, they would be applied exclusively to the training set after the initial train-test split. The fixed split assumes no temporal ordering in the data; however, if the dataset has implicit sequential patterns, this could introduce bias, which will be addressed in future time-series validations.

Ablation study

From Table 5, it can be see that emoving the autoencoder compression results in the most substantial declines, with accuracy dropping by 4.2%, precision by 5.3%, and F1-score by 2.6%, though recall slightly increases by 1.4%, underscoring its critical role in alleviating dimensionality explosion and sparsity for more effective extraction of discrete attributes like voice and video data. Excluding the Gaussian kernel adaptation causes moderate reductions in accuracy and precision by 2.5% each, along with a 1.2% decrease in recall, highlighting its enhancement of stability and generalization for nonlinear patterns, which aligns with a 2–3% boost in overall robustness as evidenced by lower variance. Omitting the sigmoid threshold adjustment (> 0.6) leads to minor degradations, including 1.3% in accuracy, 1.2% in precision, 0.7% in recall, and 0.7% in F1-score, emphasizing its fine-tuning for improved classification confidence and reduced false positives. Collectively, these modifications exhibit synergistic effects, enabling the full model to outperform ablated variants by 1.3–4.2% in accuracy and validating the unified architecture’s superiority over standard KELM or BP baselines.

Conclusion

This study advances churn prediction by modifying ELM to include autoencoder-based feature compression and penalty-optimized Gaussian kernels, addressing high-dimensionality and sparsity challenges in multimedia data. This integration transforms ELM into a more efficient and robust model through structural enhancements in architecture and algorithmic refinements in optimization, enabling rapid least-squares solutions and stable generalization for voice, video, and behavioral attributes. Within the domain of customer data analytics, particularly focusing on multimedia data replete with discrete features, the methodological strategy employed to address superfluous discrete features critically determines the accuracy of customer churn prediction algorithms. Addressing this pivotal issue, the current research delineates an advanced customer churn prediction framework, predicated on the cohesive integration of an enhanced ELM and rigorous multimedia data analysis, harnessing the potential of autoencoders for feature extraction.

At the core of this model lies the implementation of a boundary learning mechanism, enabling the identification of the global optimal solution while obviating the need for iterative adjustments to input weights and biases. This optimization is facilitated through a judicious determination of the initial node count in the hidden layer, streamlining the computational process. To bolster the inherent limitations of the ELM, prone to extreme random configurations, a kernel function is seamlessly incorporated. This strategic integration not only retains the advantages of unsupervised learning intrinsic to autoencoder networks but also amplifies the efficiency of discrete data feature extraction, strengthens the integrity of feature representation, and significantly reduces computational overhead.

While the model demonstrates performance on the telecom dataset, with superior precision and PR AUC confirming its effectiveness in imbalanced settings, it may be sensitive to domain-specific features and requires validation on diverse multimedia sources. The trade-off favoring precision over recall could limit detection of rare churn events in highly imbalanced scenarios. We plan to extend the model to real-time multimedia data streams, incorporate ensemble techniques for improved recall, and integrate explainable AI to interpret churn predictions.

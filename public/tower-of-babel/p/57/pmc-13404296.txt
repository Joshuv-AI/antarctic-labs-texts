CC223 enhances cisplatin sensitivity of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer by inducing cell cycle arrest
Authors: Ling Wang, Haixai Wang, Xueping Zhu, Lingling Yang, Rengui Li, Dongling Zou, Xingtao Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlatinum-based chemotherapy is the standard treatment for ovarian cancer. However, the emergence of platinum resistance greatly limits its effectiveness. Investigating novel therapeutic strategies to overcome platinum resistance is imperative to extend survival in ovarian cancer patients.MethodsThe cytotoxic effects of CC223 on SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP were evaluated using the CCK8 assay. Based on the ovarian cancer organoid model, drug screening testing was employed to assess the sensitivity of ovarian cancer to CC223. The impact of CC223 on the biological behaviors of SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP was evaluated using colony formation, migration, and invasion assays. The synergistic effect of CC223 and cisplatin was analyzed by combining the CCK8 assay with SynergyFinder software (ZIP score). Concurrently, the difference in drug response between the CC223-cisplatin combination and cisplatin monotherapy was validated using cell lines, organoids, and tumor xenograft models. The activation status of the AKT-mTOR pathway was detected by immunohistochemistry and western blotting in ovarian cancer cell lines and organoids, while flow cytometry analysis was used to assess the effect of the CC223-cisplatin combination on the cell cycle.ResultsWe found that CC223 could exert a killing effect on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells and organoids. CC223 suppressed the proliferation, migration and invasion of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner. Moreover, the combined treatment with CC223 and cisplatin exhibited synergistic anti-proliferative effects in vitro and in vivo. In patient-derived organoids(PDOs) of ovarian cancer, we found enhanced mTOR activation, and the PDOs-guided drug sensitivity analysis revealed that CC223 could enhance sensitivity to cisplatin. Hyperactivation of mTOR signaling in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells leads to the poor prognosis of ovarian cancer patients. CC223 could potently suppress the aberrantly activated AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. Furthermore, the combined therapy of CC223 and cisplatin can affect cell proliferation by arresting the G0/G1 phase.ConclusionOur study demonstrated that CC223 could enhance the sensitivity of cisplatin by effectively inhibiting the activation of the AKT-mTOR pathway and potentially arresting the G0/G1 phase. These findings suggest that the synergistic effect of CC223 and cisplatin holds promise as a potential therapeutic strategy for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-026-02086-x.Keywords: CC223, Cisplatin, Platinum-resistance, Ovarian cancer, mTOR, Organoids

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC), especially high-grade serous OC (HGSOC), is the most lethal gynecological malignancy. In 2025, around 20,890 new OC cases are diagnosed in United States, resulting in over 12,730 deaths [1]. Despite an initial response rate of 70% to platinum-based chemotherapy, the 5-year survival rate for ovarian cancer remains below 40%, largely attributable to either intrinsic or acquired chemoresistance [2]. Therefore, developing novel therapeutic strategies to overcome chemoresistance for OC is an urgent unmet need.

The mechanisms underlying cisplatin resistance in ovarian cancer cells are multifaceted, encompassing DNA damage repair, metabolic reprogramming, cell cycle dysregulation, and aberrant signaling pathways (e.g., Akt/mTOR signaling) [3, 4]. The Akt/mTOR signaling axis is indispensable for regulating fundamental cellular processes such as cell proliferation and disease progression [5, 6], and its aberrant activation has been implicated as a key mechanism driving cisplatin resistance [7]. Growth factors activate PI3K, which phosphorylates PIP2 to generate PIP3. PIP3 then recruits Akt to the plasma membrane, where Akt undergoes activating phosphorylation at T308 and S473. Akt can activate the serine/threonine kinase mTOR either directly or indirectly by phosphorylating and inhibiting TSC2. Activated mTOR forms a complex with Raptor, and this complex phosphorylates S6K to promote protein translation and inhibits 4EBP [8–11]. Hyperactivation of Akt/mTOR signaling promotes tumor cell survival and drug resistance, which has been implicated in conferring platinum resistance in ovarian cancer cells, underscoring its potential as a promising therapeutic target to overcome chemoresistance [12].

Considering the critical involvement of aberrant mTOR signaling in driving chemoresistance, substantial drug development efforts have focused on targeted mTOR inhibition [13, 14]. The first generation of mTOR inhibitors were rapamycin analogs engineered to improve pharmacokinetics, including temsirolimus, everolimus, and ridaforolimus [15]. To overcome resistance from paradoxical PI3K/AKT activation due to mTORC1-S6K suppression, second generation inhibitors were developed as dual mTORC1/2 (such as PP242, WAY600) or PI3K/mTOR (such as PI-103, GDC-0980) inhibitors [14, 15]. mTORC1/2 inhibitors are better tolerated owing to ubiquitous PI3K dependence in normal cells [16–18]. CC223 (onatasertib, ATG008) is an oral, highly selective mTORC1/2 inhibitor with > 200-fold selectivity over PI3K-α [19, 20]. CC223 has exhibited preclinical and clinical anticancer efficacy [21, 22]. Nevertheless, the efficacy of CC223 in ameliorating platinum resistance in ovarian cancer cells has yet to be determined. Elucidating this effect and mechanism would further define its pharmacological potential as an anticancer agent.

This study aimed to investigate whether CC223 could synergize with cisplatin to enhance the sensitivity of ovarian cancer cells to cisplatin. Treating platinum-resistant ovarian cancer is a formidable challenge, which is closely associated with a poor prognosis. Therefore, our study indicates that incorporating CC223 into the cisplatin-based treatment is likely to be a promising therapeutic regimen for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Human OC cell lines SKOV3 and A2780 were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, USA). Both cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cisplatin-resistant OC cell lines, SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP were established in our laboratory by stepwise increasing doses of cisplatin and cultured in the original culture medium containing 3 μm cisplatin. All cell lines were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

Organoids culture

Patient-derived organoids (PDOs) from ovarian cancer tissues were established following previously described protocols [23]. The specimens collected from platinum-resistant or platinum-sensitive HGSOC patients obtained by surgical resection for the study. We prepared the samples for immunohistochemical staining, cryopreservation, and organogenesis. The samples were digested by mechanical shearing and enzymatic lysis methods. The obtained cell suspension was then passed through a 100 μm sieve to remove large aggregates and debris. Erythrolysis was performed if red blood cells remained. Finally, the properly centrifuged cell precipitate was suspended in Matrigel® (Corning, New York, USA, 356231) and cultured in the specialized culture medium for ovarian cancer organoids. The compositions of ovarian cancer organoids medium were referred to Kopper et al.’ s protocol with slight modifications [24]. Noggin (120–10 C) and Rspo1 (120-38-500) were purchased from Peprotech company(New Jersey, USA), not from conditioned medium.

Organoid-guided drug screening assay

Organoids cultured for 2–3 weeks were harvested and dissociated into single cells using 1X TrypLe (ThermoFisher, Massachusetts, USA, 12604021). Cells were counted with the countess automated cell counter (Reward, Shenzhen, China, C100-SE), and then 8 µl Matrigel® containing 2000–3000 cells were seeded in standard 384-well cell culture plates (Corning, 3570). After the organoids were reconstructed, A series of diluted drugs was dispensed. Cell viability was assayed using Cell Titer-Glow 3D Reagent (Promega, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions following 6 days of drug incubation, and results were normalized to vehicle controls. The organoid viability curve is plotted using Graphpad Prism 8 software. The IC50 (half maximal inhibitory concentration) and AUC (area under the curve) values were computed through the application of nonlinear regression (curve fit) and the equation log(inhibitor) against the normalized response.

Cytotoxicity assay

Cytotoxicity was assessed by CCK-8 assay. Briefly, 5 × 103 cells/well were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated overnight. All drug-treated wells (including those with CC223 at various concentrations, cisplatin, cisplatin combined with CC223) were indeed included in the CCK-8 assay alongside the control (untreated) wells. The “medium-only” wells were used as background wells. The absorbance values from these blank wells were subtracted from all other wells (control and treated) before calculating the cell viability. After 72 h, all wells were incubated for 3 h with 10 µL CCK-8 reagent in 90 µL fresh medium. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured by microplate reader and IC50 values calculated using untreated cells as controls.

Colony-formation assay

For colony formation assays, 5 × 102 SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP cells were seeded into six-well plates in 10% FBS medium. After 24 h, medium was replaced with drug-containing medium for 48 h, then fresh medium for 2 weeks. Cells were washed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with crystal violet solution for 40 min. Colonies were quantified and photographed.

Migration and invasion assays

Following a 48-hour pretreatment with the drugs at various concentrations, cells were harvested. Cell migration and invasion were evaluated using 24-well transwell chambers. For migration, 3 × 104 SKOV3DPP or A2780DDP cells were seeded in serum-free medium in the upper chambers, with 15% FBS medium in the lower chambers. After 24 h, cells on the upper surface were removed and inserts fixed (20 min), stained with 0.1% crystal violet, and imaged. For invasion, inserts were precoated with Matrigel (1:100 dilution) and dried overnight prior to seeding cells. Cells were incubated for 48 h, then fixed, stained, and imaged as in the migration assay.

Cell cycle analysis

To analyze cell cycle alterations, the flow cytometry was conducted using Cell Cycle and Apoptosis Analysis Kit (Propidium Iodide (PI) staining) (MedChemExpress, New Jersey, USA, HY-K1071) according to the instructions. Briefly, cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells/well. After cell adhesion, the culture medium was replaced by that with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), cisplatin (3µM)(MedChemExpress, HY-17394), cisplatin (3µM) combined with CC223 (1µM) (MedChemExpress, HY-16956). After 24 h of drug incubation, the cells were harvested, washed with PBS and gently fixed with 70% ice-cold ethanol at 4 °C overnight. Cells were centrifuged, washed with PBS, and then resuspended in staining buffer (0.5 ml) containing PI (25 µl) and RNase A (10 µl), and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 30 min.A minimum of 10,000 events per sample were acquired using a Beckman flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, California, USA), and the results were analyzed with FlowJo software.

Western blot

Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer with protease inhibitors and phosphatase inhibitors for 30 min on ice, and then centrifuged at 10,000 g, 4 °C for 15 min. Lysates were quantified through the Bicinchoninic Acid Assay (BCA) method. The lysates were diluted in 1X loading buffer and boiled for denaturation. Equivalent amount of protein was resolved by 12.5% SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, and blocked with 5% skim milk. Target proteins were probed using primary antibodies (anti-AKT: Beyotime, AA326, 1:1000; anti-mTOR: ThermoFisher, MA531505, 1:1000; anti-GAPDH: CST, 5174 S, 1:1000; anti-p-mTOR(Ser2448): CST, 2791, 1:1000; anti-p-AKT(Ser473): Beyotime, AA329, 1:1000) followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. The antibodies were diluted in PBS containing 1% (w/v) bovine serum albumin (BSA). The washing steps were performed using 0.1% Tween-20 in Tris-buffered saline (TBST). The results were visualized through Enhanced ChemiLuminescence (ECL)(ThermoFisher, 34577) on ChemiDoc MP imaging system (Bio-Rad, United States).

Synergy determination with synergyfinder

SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP cells were seeded at 5 × 103 cells/well in 96-well plates. Cells were treated with either single agents (cisplatin, CC223) or combinations (cisplatin + CC223) at the indicated concentrations based on prior cytotoxicity data. CC223 was used at 0-0.4 µM. Cisplatin was used at 0-320 µM. After 48 h treatment, cell viability was assessed by CCK-8 assay as described above. SynergyFinder software was used to evaluate drug synergism based on the inhibition index calculated by response surface modeling and zero interaction potency (ZIP) analysis [25]. A ZIP score greater than 10 was used to define significant synergy, whereas scores between 0 and 10 indicated weak or potential synergy and were interpreted with caution.

Tumor xenograft model

Female BALB/c nude mice (5–6 weeks old) were subcutaneously inoculated with 5 × 106 SKOV3DDP cells in 100 µL PBS containing 25% Matrigel. After 21 days, mice (n = 4 per group) were randomized to receive: vehicle control (10 mg/kg 1% DMSO in saline daily), Cisplatin (5 mg/kg weekly), CC223 (10 mg/kg daily), or Cisplatin + CC223. Mouse weight and tumor volume (V = L×W2/2) were measured every 3 days. After 14 days, mice were euthanized, tumors harvested, weighed and fixed.

Histology and immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Firstly, PDOs were disociated from Matrigel and maintained with complete morphology. The organoids were harvested and subjected to centrifugation. Then, the formed PDOs pellet and the tissue were fixed in paraformaldehyde (4%) overnight. Then, the pellet and tissue were embedded in paraffin according to standard immunohistochemical procedures. The PDO and tissue sections were subjected to deparaffinized, rehydrated, and stained with primary antibodies overnight at 4℃(anti-PAX8: MXB Biotechnologies, RMA-0817, 1:100; anti-p53: MXB Biotechnologies, MAB-0674, 1:1; anti-p-mTOR(Ser2448): CST, 2791, 1:100; anti-p-AKT(Ser473): Beyotime, AA329, 1:100). The sections were then incubated with the corresponding horseradish peroxidase-labelled secondary antibodies for 60 min at room temperature. The DAB reaction mixture (Invitrogen, California, USA, 34065) was subsequently added. Minus primary antibody and control IgG were used as negative controls.

Bioinformatics analysis

We perfomed pathway analysis of differentially expressed genes in SKOV3 versus SKOV3DDP cells using GSE148003 dataset [26]. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was conducted with TCGA data (ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma) via GEPIA [27].

Statistical analysis

Data are mean ± SD of three independent experiments. Two-group comparisons were analyzed by unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. One-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s multiple comparison test was used for analyzing the differences between drug-treated groups and the control group. Kaplan-Meier analysis compared survival curves. Ρ < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

CC223 exhibits cytotoxicity against ovarian cancer

The drug effect of CC223 on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells (SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP) was evaluated by the drug sensitivity testing. We found that the half - maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of CC223 against SKOV3DDP cells was 2.69 µM (Fig. 1A), and that against A2780DDP cells was 2.1 µM (Fig. 1B). As the drug concentration of CC223 increases, the proportion of surviving cells decreases. In other words, the results showed that CC223 exhibited an effective cytostatic rate for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells in a does dependent manner (Fig. 1A and B). In addition, two patient-derived organoids (PDO) were introduced to access the cytotoxic effect on ovarian cancer. CC223 had the growth inhibitory effect on platinum-sensitive PDO-1 and platinum-resistant PDO-2. The IC50 value of PDO-1 (Fig. 1C) and PDO-2 (Fig. 1D) is 0.02 µM and 0.13 µM, respectively. With the concentration of CC223 increasing, the organoids undergo lysis in morphology, and the number of surviving ones decreases (Fig. 1C and D). Thus, the results showed that CC223 can exhibit cytotoxicity against ovarian cancer, including platinum-sensitive and platinum-resistant cases.

CC223 inhibits the proliferation, invasion and migration ability of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells

To further investigate the impact of the mTOR dual inhibitors CC223 on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells, we subjected SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP cells to varying concentrations of CC223 for function assays. First, we employed the clone formation assay to evaluate the effect of CC223 on the proliferation ability. The visual perspective of the clone image (Fig. 2A) showed that CC223 reduced the number of the clones formed from SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP. The higher concentration of CC223 (10 µM) results in fewer clones. The statistic bar chart (Fig. 2B) displays significant differences (P < 0.05). The result indicated that CC223 could inhibit the proliferation ability of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. Next, the transwell assay without Matrigel was used to measure the impact of CC223 on the migration capabilities of SKOV3DDP and A2780DDP. A lower number of cells traversed the membrane and stained with crystal violet in the CC223-treated group than in the control group (Fig. 2C and D). The transwell assay with Matrigel was used to access the invasion ability of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells after CC223 treatment for 48 h. The results showed a significant decrease in their invasive ability after CC223 treatment., because the CC223-treated cells, passing through the transwell and stained with crystal violet, were fewer than those without CC223 treatment (Fig. 2E and F). CC223 at 10 µM inhibited the invasion and migration abilities of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells more markedly than at 1 µM. The above results indicate that CC223 inhibits the proliferation, invasion and migration ability of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells.

Co-treatment of CC223 and cisplatin exhibit synergistic cytotoxicity on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells

Given the critical role of the AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum resistance, we next explored whether the mTOR inhibitor CC223 could synergize with cisplatin to kill platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. We first measured the IC50 values of cisplatin against SKOV3DDP cells and A2780DDP cells using the CCK8 assay. The results showed that SKOV3DDP (Fig. 3A) and A2780DDP (Fig. 3B) cells possessed strong resistance to cisplatin, with IC50 values of 64.75 µM and 52.35 µM, respectively. Next, we conducted the cytotoxicity assay and found that CC223 (1µM) enhanced the cytotoxic effect of cisplatin (3µM) to suppress the viability of SKOV3DDP (Fig. 3C) and A2780DDP cells (Fig. 3D). we investigated the synergistic effect of CC223 and cisplatin in treating platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. We exposed these cells to CC223 and cisplatin at different concentration gradients simultaneously. Then, we use Synergyfinder software [25] to calculated ZIP synergy scores. The ZIP synergy score of SKOV3DDP (Fig. 3E) and A2780DDP (Fig. 3F) cells was 6.928 and 10.63, respectively, indicating that CC223 and cisplatin has synergistic effects. Although the ZIP score is in the marginal range, indicating a potential synergistic effects, the maximum proportions of the anti-tumor response attributed to the drug interaction were 16.04 in SKOV3DDP cells (Fig. 3E) and 20.5 in A2780DDP cells (Fig. 3F). For validating the synergistic effects, colony-formation assay was performed to evaluate the effect of the combined treatment on cell proliferation ability. the result showed that the proliferative capacity of the group treated by the combined CC223 and cisplatin was significantly decreased compared to cisplatin alone, suggesting that CC223 promotes the inhibitory effect of cisplatin on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells (Fig. 3G-H). Therefore, the treatment with CC223 and cisplatin was inferred to make synergistic effects in inhibiting tumor proliferation.

To determine the clinical relevance of CC223 combined with cisplatin in ovarian cancer, tumor tissues collected from two HGSOC patients were used to derive organoids. Organoid-guided drug sensitivity testing was conducted to evaluate the therapeutic response of the combined treatment. Organoids from the two patients preserved the heterogeneity of their parent tumors and showed different structure, The two organoids exhibit a mixed pattern of cystic and solid structures (Fig. 4A). Both the original tumor tissues and their derived organoids positively expressed P53 and PAX8(Fig. 4B and C), which to some extent proves that the organoids retained the characteristics of the original tumors. We performed drug sensitivity testing on cisplatin alone and the combination group (CC223 (0.05 µM) and cisplatin) based on PDO-1 (Fig. 4D) and PDO-2 (Fig. 4E). We observed intense staining of p-mTOR in both organoids, indicating highly activated mTOR signaling(Fig. 4F and G). CC223 inhibits the activated AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells

The specific mechanism by which CC223 inhibits platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells is not yet known. However, considering that CC223 is an inhibitor of the AKT-mTOR pathway, it is plausible to hypothesize that the inhibitory effect of CC223 on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells may involve the AKT-mTOR pathway. Thus, we first examined the differentially expressed genes in GSE148003 platinum-resistant SKOV3 cells versus SKOV3 cells, enriched the differentially expressed genes for signaling pathways, and found the PI3K-AKT pathway was transitively activated in SKOV3DDP cells (Fig. 5A). To further investigate the potential clinical relevance of these findings, the expression levels of mTOR in ovarian cancer patients were analyzed using TCGA data by GEPIA. This analysis revealed that patients with high mTOR expression had a shorter overall survival time compared to those with low mTOR expression (Fig. 5B). The above results suggest that AKT-mTOR plays an important role in platinum resistance in ovarian cancer. Meanwhile, we observed increased protein expression of phosphorylated AKT and phosphorylated mTOR in SKOV3DDP cells (Fig. 5C) and A2780DDP cells (Fig. 5D), which also implies that the AKT-mTOR pathway is activated in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. Treatment with CC223(1µM) effectively reduced the expression of p-AKT and p-mTOR in both SKOV3DDP cells (Fig. 5E) and A2780DDP cells (Fig. 5F). That means CC223 can inhibit the abnormally activated AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

CC223 induces cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells

Akt-mTOR signaling pathway is reported to participate in cell cycle, regulating the proliferation of cancer cells [28, 29]. To further verify whether CC223 dysregulate cell cycle of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells through Akt-mTOR signaling pathway, flow cytometric analyses were conducted. Results in Fig. 6A show that the number of SKOV3DDP cells at the G2/M phase was markedly decreased after being treated with the combination group of CC223 and cisplatin for 24 h, when compared with control/cisplatin group. However, the proportion of cells in G0/G1 was decreased significantly. In line with the results in SKOV3DDP cells, Fig. 6B indicated the addition of CC223 to cisplatin lead to cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase, while cisplatin alone couldn’t cause significant changes in each phase. Combining the above experimental data, it can be concluded that CC223 can induce cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase through inhibiting the activated AKT-mTOR signaling in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells.

Combination treatment Inhibition the tumor growth and AKT-mTOR pathway in vivo

To further observe the efficacy of CC223 in combination with cisplatin on platinum - resistant ovarian cancer cells in vivo, we first established a platinum-resistant xenograft tumor model. Then, at day 21, we treated the mice with placebo, cisplatin, CC223, and a combination of cisplatin and CC223, as depicted in Fig. 7A. During this treatment period, the tumor volumes of the mice were regularly measured. After 14 days of treatment, the mice were euthanized for subsequent experiments. After 14 days of drug treatment, the volume of tumors in mice in the cisplatin combined with CC223 treatment group was significantly reduced (Fig. 7B and C). Moreover, the weight of mouse tumors in the combination group was also significantly lower than that in the monotherapy groups (Fig. 7D). These results indicate that CC223 combined with cisplatin inhibited tumor growth in mice in vivo. Subsequently, to explore the underlying mechanism, we conducted immunohistochemistry (IHC) experiments to detect the expression of the AKT-mTOR signaling pathway in tumor tissues from different treatment groups. The results indicated that both the single - agent CC223 treatment group and the combination of CC223 and cisplatin treatment group effectively inhibited the phosphorylation of AKT and mTOR in the tumor tissues (Fig. 7E). Building on these findings, we demonstrated that the combination of CC223 and cisplatin could inhibit the activation of the AKT - mTOR signaling pathway in vivo, thereby effectively suppressing the proliferation and growth of tumor cells.

Discussion

After platinum-resistant recurrence occur in ovarian cancer, the treatment is very challenging and the prognosis is poor. It is of great significance to develop new treatment strategies for overcoming platinum resistance. In our study, we demonstrated that the dual mTOR inhibitor CC223 could suppress the progression of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells through AKT-mTOR signaling pathway. We found CC223 effectively inhibit the viability and malignant biological behavior of ovarian cancer. Notably, both in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells and platinum-resistant ovarian cancer organoids, CC223 and cisplatin exhibited a synergistic anti-tumor effect. It’s worthy noting the significant difference in IC50 values between the organoids and the cell lines. The 3D architecture and multicellular complexity of organoids better recapitulate the in vivo tumor microenvironment. This can lead to higher drug sensitivity compared to monolayer cell cultures. Additionally, organoids often maintain the original tissue’s heterogeneity and differentiation states, which may reflect a more clinically relevant drug response. In contrast, immortalized cell lines may have undergone genetic and phenotypic drift during long-term culture, potentially altering their drug response. In vivo experiments also showed that the combination treatment suppressed tumor growth in the platinum-resistant ovarian cancer xenograft model. We also showed that transitional activation of the AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. The bioinformatics analysis pointed out that mTOR hyperactivation correlates with poor patient prognosis. Mechanistically, CC223 exerted a potent inhibitory effect on the AKT-mTOR pathway, as demonstrated by the significant reduction in the phosphorylation levels of AKT and mTOR. This inhibition of the AKT-mTOR signaling cascade subsequently induced cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase, effectively impeding tumor cell proliferation. Based on these results, CC223 may be a potential therapeutic agent for ovarian cancer patients with platinum-resistance, and cisplatin combined with CC223 is a potential therapeutic regimen for overcoming platinum-resistance in ovarian cancer.

Platinum-based chemotherapy, such as cisplatin or carboplatin, is commonly used to treat ovarian cancer. These drugs bind to DNA and inhibit cell division, leading to cancer cell death. However, some cancer cells develop resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy, leading to treatment failure and disease progression [30]. The AKT-mTOR pathway activation has been associated with increased tumor cell survival and resistance to chemotherapy. For one hand, platinum-based chemotherapy induces DNA damage in cancer cells, and activation of the AKT-mTOR pathway can promote DNA repair, allowing cancer cells to survive and develop resistance to chemotherapy. For another hand, activation of the AKT-mTOR pathway has been shown to regulate the expression of drug efflux pumps, such as P-glycoprotein, which can pump chemotherapy drugs out of the cancer cells, leading to reduced drug accumulation and decreased efficacy of platinum-based chemotherapy [12, 31]. Inhibition of the AKT-mTOR pathway using specific inhibitors, such as rapamycin or PI3K inhibitors, has been shown to sensitize platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells to chemotherapy [11, 32]. Targeting the AKT-mTOR pathway in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy may overcome platinum resistance and improve treatment outcomes in ovarian cancer. In our study, we also demonstrated that CC223, a dual mTOR inhibitor, sensitized platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells by inhibiting the AKT-mTOR signaling pathway and was synergistic with cisplatin. Consistent with previous studies, our findings also suggest the potential of mTOR inhibitors in the treatment of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

As a dual inhibitor of mTOR, CC223 specifically targets mTORC1 and inhibits its kinase activity. This inhibition prevents mTORC1 from phosphorylating downstream targets such as p70S6 kinase and 4EBP1, leading to the inhibition of protein synthesis, cell cycle progression, and cellular growth. Additionally, CC223 has been shown to indirectly inhibit mTORC2 activity by interfering with mTORC2 assembly and stability [19–22]. This inhibition results in the suppression of AKT signaling, a key upstream regulator of mTORC1 and mTORC2. In addition, the activation of the AKT/mTOR signaling pathway was found to be closely related to the regulation of cell cycle arrest. Recently, some studies reported that some novel drugs promoted cell cycle arrest through regulating AKT/mTOR pathway. (+)-anthrabenzoxocinone, serving as a potential compound for the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer, could inhibit the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, and result in the induction of S and G2/M phase arrest [29]. Pectolinarigenin inhibited PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway and induced G2/M phase arrest, which is an appealing potential therapeutic agent for hepatocellular carcinoma [33]. The mTOR inhibitors also have biologic rationale and clinical activity in gynecological cancer [34]. The cell cycle is closely related to cell proliferation, including four phases of G0/G1, S, and G2/M. By blocking the signaling cascade downstream of AKT and mTOR, CC223 effectively induced cell cycle arrest at G0/G1 phase, and then enhanced the toxic effects of cisplatin on platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. Collectively, our findings indicate that CC223 has the capacity to augment the sensitivity of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells to cisplatin, while the involved mechanism needs to be further validated.

Although our study suggested that CC223 has great potential in the treatment of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer and exerts potential synergistic effect with cisplatin. It’s important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. Firstly, the mouse subcutaneous tumor model used in this study has inherent limitations, as it cannot simulate the growth environment within the intraperitoneal cavity or metastatic potential. However, subcutaneous tumors are easy to monitor and are suitable for preliminary validation of drug responses. Secondly, as per the ZIP score interpretation guidelines, a score greater than 10 represents truly synergistic interactions. In the combination experiments of CC223 and cisplatin, the ZIP scores were 6.928 and 10.63 in the SKOV3/DDP and A2780/DDP cell lines, respectively. These borderline scores suggest that the observed potential synergitic interaction may be subject to bias. Further experimental validation would be beneficial to conclusively define the nature of interactions. Additionally, organoids are only derived from two patients with a small sample size. However, organoids serve as an excellent preclinical model for effectively predicting drug responses [23], thereby providing a reliable basis for the efficacy of CC223 in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer and its synergistic effect with cisplatin. More investigation and preclinical research are still required to confirm the function and effectiveness of CC223 combined with cisplatin on platinum-resistant cells.

Several mTOR inhibitors have been studied in ovarian cancer, including temsirolimus, everolimus, and ridaforolimus [34]. These drugs have shown promising results in preclinical and clinical studies, both as single agents and in combination with other treatments [28, 31]. Preclinical studies have also shown that CC223 has potent anti-tumor activity against various types of cancer, including breast, prostate, and ovarian cancer [19–22, 35]. However, studies have also reported drug resistance of mTOR inhibitors due to several mechanisms include mutation or alteration in downstream effectors, feedback activation, overexpression of efflux pumps, tumor microenvironment [13, 14]. Currently, we found that CC223 may make potential synergitic effect with cisplatin throught inhibiting the activation of AKT/mTOR pathway and leading to cell cycke G0/G1 arrest in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer., In the future, we need to further investigate the mechanism of synergistic action between cisplatin and CC223, and identify suitable ovarian cancer populations to overcome platinum-resistance.

Conclusion

In this study, we demonstrated hyperactivation of the AKT-mTOR pathway in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells, and high mTOR expression was associated with poor prognosis in ovarian cancer patients. The mTOR inhibitor CC223 inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer cells. CC223 can enhance the sensitivity to cisplatin and exert a synergistic effect with cisplatin in the treatment of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer in vitro and in vivo. Our study proposes potential new strategies for the treatment of platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, aiming to improve the therapeutic response and prognosis of patients suffering from this condition.

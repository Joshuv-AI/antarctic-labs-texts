Global research and current trends on nanotherapy in lung cancer research: a bibliometric analysis of 20 years
Authors: Pooja Singh, Prabhakar Semwal, Baby Gargi, Sakshi Painuli, Michael Aschner, Khalaf F Alsharif, Haroon Khan, Rakesh Kumar Bachheti, Limenew Abate Worku
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung cancer ranks as one of the most rapidly growing malignancies. Which is characterized by its poor prognosis and a low survival rate due to late diagnosis and limited efficacy of conventional treatments. In recent years nanotechnology has emerged as a promising frontier in the management of lung cancer, presenting novel strategies to enhance drug administration, improve therapeutic efficiency, and mitigate side effects. This research comprehensively evaluates the current state and research trends concerning the application of nanomaterials in lung cancer through bibliometric analysis.Materials and methodsWe employed a systematic approach by retrieving studies from the Scopus database that focused on nanomaterials and lung cancer between 2003 and 2023. Subsequently, we carefully selected relevant articles based on predetermined inclusion criteria. The selected publications were then subjected to bibliometric and visual analysis using softwares such as VOSviewer and Biblioshiny.ResultsA total of 3523 studies that meet inclusion criteria were selected for bibliometric analysis. We observed a progressive increase in the number of annual publications from 2003 to 2023, indicating the growing interest in this field. According to our analysis, China is the primary contributor to publication output among the countries. The “Ministry of Education of the People's Republic of China” was the most influential institution. Among the authors, "Dr. Jack A. Roth” and “Dr. Huang Leaf” had the highest number of publications and cited publications, respectively. The “International Journal of Nanomedicine” was found to be the most prolific journal in this field. Additionally, “Biomaterials” emerged as the most cited journal. Through keyword analysis, we identified five main research themes and future research directions; nono-immunotherapy and green synthesis are the hot topics in this research field.ConclusionOur study summarized the key characteristics of publications in this field and identified the most influential countries, institutions, authors, journals, hot topics, and trends related to the application of nanomaterials in lung cancer. These findings contribute to the existing body of knowledge and serve as a foundation for future research endeavors in this area. More effective efforts are needed in this field to reduce the burden of lung cancer and help achieve the United Nation’s Sustainable Development Goals.Keywords: Lung cancer research, Bibliometric analysis, Nanomaterials, Scopus database

Background

Lung cancer is a major global health concern, characterized by high rates of morbidity and mortality along with substantial incidence and risk profile, making it an important medical issue for humans [1–3]. Lung cancer is categorized into two main types: small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and Non-Small Lung cancer (NSLC), accounting for 80% and 20% cases, respectively [4]. NSLC includes subclasses adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, large cell carcinoma, and mixed types. SCLC is also divided into subclasses: small cell carcinoma, large cell carcinoma and, mixed small cell and large cell carcinoma [5]. The conventional treatments such as chemotherapy, surgical resections, and radiotherapy either in combinations or separately have significant limitations and adverse effects [6, 7]. Radiotherapy can damage healthy cells and should be avoided in patients with compromised pulmonary systems to prevent lung functionality loss [8–10]. Conventional cancer chemotherapy has challenges due to severe side effects and lack of specificity in drug delivery [11]. Therefore, an efficient cancer treatment is needed, which provides targeted treatment with minimal systemic side effects. A few research projects for lung cancer treatment are listed in Table 1.

The potential of nanotherapy in lung cancer treatment is highly promising, due to their physiochemical properties which provides selective targeting of cancer cell, sparing healthy cells that can improve the patient’s survival chances [12]. For cancer treatment, the use of nanoparticles has begun with the development of liposomal doxorubicin formulations such as Doxil (pegylated liposomal doxorubicin), which exhibited promising results against a variety of cancers, including lung cancer [13]. The nanoparticles were engineered to specifically target cancer cells while sparing healthy tissue, thereby minimizing side effects. Various materials, including lipids, polymers, and metals, have been explored for the fabrication of nanoparticles. Gold nanoparticles have also emerged as a potential tool for treating lung cancer [14–16]. In recent years, researchers have begun integrating nanoparticles into multimodal treatment approaches for lung cancer. Nanoparticles have also been employed for diagnostic imaging, enabling early detection and the implementation of personalized treatment strategies [17, 18].

Nanotherapy can target therapeutic agents directly to the cancerous cells in the lungs, thereby minimizing harm to healthy tissue and reducing side effects [19]. For example, gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) are used in various medical applications, especially in cancer imaging. During surgical treatment, complete removal and accurate detection of cancer cells is a crucial step in the treatment of cancer. Characteristics like "Enhanced Permeability and Retention Effect" (EPR Effect) and Localized Surface Plasmon Resonance (LSPR) of gold nanoparticles enhance the visualization and localization of tumor cells [20]. Moreover, the small dimensions and distinctive characteristics of nanoparticles facilitate improved drug delivery, overcoming the limitations of conventional chemotherapy in terms of tumor cell penetration ability and attaining adequate concentrations for effective cancer cell eradication [19]. Nanoparticles can be designed to selectively target receptors associated with cancer cells, which can result in an increase in specificity and efficacy. In addition, nanoparticle drug delivery systems can potentially solve drug resistance and enhance drug delivery to specific targets for cancer treatment [21].

Bibliometric analysis is a statistical technique that utilizes public literature databases to offer both quantitative and qualitative assessments of publications. It serves as a valuable technique for presenting a comprehensive overview of both national and global contributions to the existing literature within a specific field. It also aids in identifying research gaps that can potentially be addressed in future studies [22]. Various examinations have been conducted in recent years, employing bibliometric research methods across various scholarly subjects [23]. Unlike comprehensive reviews, which focus on analyzing a specific research issue within a limited set of publications, bibliometric analysis provides a holistic assessment of the literature within a particular area [24]. Notably, no bibliometric analysis has been published to date which focuses on the application of nanomaterials in lung cancer treatment. Therefore, this study aimed to employ a quantitative approach to gain valuable insights. The main objective of our analysis is framed as a series of questions as follows:

Q1: What was the trend of publication in the field of nanotherapy for lung cancer treatment from 2003 to 2023?

Q2: Which countries, institutions, journals, and authors demonstrate the highest level of productivity in the field of nanotherapy for lung cancer treatment?

Q3: What are the research trends by conducting keywords analysis, the current state of research in this field, and future research directions?

Materials and methods

Database and search strategy

The Scopus database is the most commonly utilized database as it consists of a comprehensive collection of journals, offers multi-operational features such as journal name, publication year, author and affiliation details, and a more advanced citation analysis tool to facilitate bibliometric analysis [25–31].

The current study used Scopus database to search publications related to nanotherapy for lung cancer (NTLC) on September 15, 2023. The search was conducted in a single day to avoid any bias resulting from daily updates to the database [32]. The search term “Lung cancer”, “nanoparticles”, “nanotherapy”, and “nanomedicine”. After search term selection, the following search string was formulated using Boolean logical operators: ((TITLE-ABS-KEY ("Lung cancer") AND TITLE-ABS-KEY ("nanomedicine") OR TITLE-ABS KEY ("nanoparticles") OR TITLE-ABS-KEY ("nanotherapy") OR TITLE-ABS KEY ("nanoparticle")). As a result of the search query, a total of 5451 publications were identified, and the resulting publications were further analyzed.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Screening of data was conducted based on different criteria such as publication stage, year of publication, language, and document type. Inclusion criteria for this study were limited to research articles published in English between 2003 and 2023 according to previously established methods [33–37]. Literature published, such as reviews, book chapters, conference papers, and editorial letters that were published before 2003, were excluded from this analysis. After the screening of data, a total of 3523 publications were selected for the bibliometric analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram for selecting data and search strategy is shown in Fig. 1.

Analysis software

Selected data were exported in comma separated value (CSV) file format consisting bibliometric information including publication year and publication issue, organizations, journal titles, authors and citation frequencies, Country/Region of publication, was filtered and tabulated in MS Excel 2021. For the data visualization two software were used VOS viewer version 1.6.19.0. Software and bibliometrix R-package (http://www.bibliometrix.org/) [38]. VOS viewer software evaluates and visually divides data into clusters and provides vital information through map assistance [39]. VOS viewer tool is used for the examinations of international collaborations and for conducting keywords analysis. Bibliometrix, on the other hand, is utilized to analyze trending topics and conduct publication citation analysis.

Results and discussion

Publication growth analysis

A total of 3523 research articles were selected in our bibliometric study. Over the past 20 years, there has been a significant rise in the number of yearly publications, which suggests that the topic of lung cancer and nanoparticles is gaining increased attention (Fig. 2A). This growth can be divided into three phases: 2003 to 2009 was the initial phase in which less than 50 articles were published annually. A steady phase can be observed between 2010 and 2014. The exponential increase in annual publication was observed from 2015 till 2023. The number of publications peaked in 2022 (469 publications), accounting for 13.31% of the total publications. In the future, an increased number of publications on NTLC with a growth rate of 29.22% and an exponential development curve (R2 = 0.6022) is predicted. The annual average of the total mean citation per year shows how many times, on average, each NTLC article has been cited. This measure can provide an overall view of the scholarly influence of articles released in a given year [40]. In Fig. 2B, the literature published in 2006, 2007, and 2009 has the maximal citations per year. This measure is often used to indicate the impact and relevance of research in a particular area. The reason behind the highest mean citation is that in 2006, a study entitled “In vitro toxicity of silica nanoparticles in human lung cancer cells” was published, which received 760 citations. This study has provided valuable information on the potential toxicity and risk associated with using silica nanoparticles in human lung cancer cell treatment and developing strategies to mitigate its effect [41]. In 2009, many significant reports were published by different groups, and among them most cited studies were published by Peng G et al. [42] and Karlsson HL et al. [43], with citations 1018 and 796, respectively. The research article published by Peng G et al. [44] provided a novel approach using sensor technology for the early detection of lung cancer from the breath of patients using gold nanoparticles; this exhaled breath analysis method has been further used by many researchers in their studies [45, 46]. The comparative study conducted by Karlsson HL et al. revealed the toxicity levels of the nanoparticles and micrometer particles of the selected metal oxides (Iron Oxide (III), Iron Oxide (II, III), Copper oxide and Titanium dioxide). The result showed that the nanoparticles are not always toxic compared to micrometer particles. However, the study also indicated that copper oxide nanoparticles have high toxicity levels. Many relevant studies were also published in the year 2007, such as “The pro-inflammatory effects of low-toxicity low-solubility particles, nanoparticles, and fine particles, on epithelial cells in vitro: The role of surface area”, and “Targeted delivery of magnetic aerosol droplets to the lung” contributed influential impact in the field of NTLC.

The annual publication counts of the application of nanoparticles in lung cancer treatment showed an increase with a growth rate of 29.22%, indicating growing interest in this topic. Nanotherapy has gained considerable attention in biomedical research in the past decades. Compared with conventional chemotherapy, nanotherapy has several benefits, such as more focused distribution, increased effectiveness, reduced toxicity, etc. [47]. In recent years, advancements, infrastructure investments, funding opportunities, and collaboration between countries have significantly improved research in this field.

Analysis of the most productive countries\regions and global collaborations

Authors from 97 countries participated in publishing research articles on NTLC. Table 5 shows the top 10 most prolific countries accounting for 74.61% of all publications. China published the maximum number of research articles (n = 1310, 27.41%), followed by the USA (n = 738, 15.445%) and India (n = 11.55, 11.55%). However, the USA has the highest citations (n = 39,907), followed by China (n = 37,095) and India (n = 13,806). The study used VOS viewer software to create bibliometric network maps of countries' co-authorship. Figure 3 shows the co-authorship mapping of countries on NTLC research. The node size in the figure represents the number of publications, with larger nodes indicating a more significant number of published documents. The thickness of the links represents the strength of collaboration between countries. China has the highest number of collaborations with 45 links, followed by the United States (n = 43), India (n = 38), and Germany (n = 37), which are the following top-ranking nations after the United States. Five out of ten authors were from the USA. However, institutions in China are more productive as compared to the USA. More than 70% of the research was published from three countries in terms of publication; China ranked first in contributions, the USA ranked second, followed by India. Similar to our results, many bibliometric studies have demonstrated that the USA and China exhibit similar levels of productivity as well as collaborations in the field of lung cancer research [48–51]. A research group from China performed a comparative analysis (using open-source data) on cancer statistics data from China and the USA, and they observed the same type of observations. As per the analysis, lung cancer was the leading cause of death in both countries [52]. Additionally, from an Indian perspective, lung cancer was reported as an important cause of morbidity and mortality and is expected to rise further in the future [53]. Therefore, more effective efforts are needed to reduce the burden of lung cancer at the global level, with the aim of prevention, early detection, and treatment.

Keywords analysis

Keyword co-occurrence analysis is the most important parameter of bibliometric analysis [53]. Figure 4C shows a word cloud map of the author's keywords ranked by frequency of occurrence. Terms like “nanoparticles”, “drug delivery”, “non-small lung cancer”, “cytotoxicity” and “apoptosis” are most frequently used in the collected literature.

A Co-occurrence network map of keywords. B Overlay map of author’s keywords. C Word cloud map of author’s keywords ranked by the frequency of occurrence

The network visualization through the VOS viewer is illustrated in Fig. 4A. In this visualization map, each node in the network corresponds to a specific keyword. The size of each node is directly proportional to the frequency at which the keyword occurs. Moreover, the links between the nodes depict the co-occurrence of keywords in different research publications. Out of 6921 only 108 author’s keywords met the threshold of more than 5 as minimum number of occurrences in the retrieved data. In total 5 clusters were obtained from keyword network analysis. Cluster 1 (red) consists of 28 keywords; this cluster includes keywords such as "cytotoxicity," "apoptosis," "silver nanoparticles," "anticancer," and "green synthesis”. This cluster consisted of the research associated with the green synthesis of nanomedicine. Green synthesis of nanoparticles utilizes natural resources such as plants, microbes, or enzymes, which minimize the reliance on toxic materials like hydrazine for its production [54]. Silver nanoparticle (AGNPs) derived from plants extracts such as Azadirachta indica, Juniperus chinensis, Zea mays and Phyllanthus emblica exhibited high cytotoxic activity against cancer cell line [55, 56] Cluster 2 (green) comprises keywords such as "drug delivery", "paclitaxel", "doxorubicin", "cisplatin", and "cancer therapy". The second cluster was related to clinical trials, to evaluate the safety, effectiveness, and potential advantages of nanomedicine in treating lung cancer. Through the implementation of clinical trials, scientists can collect valuable information regarding the effectiveness of nanomedicine, their interactions with the tumor microenvironment, and their influence on patient outcomes. This knowledge will provide critical insights into regulatory approval and advancement in the field of nanotherapy against lung cancer [57–59]. Some ongoing clinical trials involve nanotechnology for lung cancer treatment, like “A Randomized Phase II Trial of Cabozantinib and Cabozantinib Plus Nivolumab Versus Standard Chemotherapy in Patients with previously treated Non-Squamous NSCLC." The primary objective of the study is to assess whether cabozantinib monotherapy or the combination of cabozantinib and nivolumab can prolong the time before the cancer deteriorates, known as progression-free survival (PFS), compared to standard chemotherapy alone in this patient population with non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (http://clinicaltrials.gov/).

Cluster 3 (blue) consists of keywords such as "nanoparticles," "Chitosan," "magnetic resonance imaging," "bio-distribution," and "pharmacokinetics." Third cluster represented utilization of imaging techniques in nanotherapy of lung cancer. These imaging methods are of utmost importance in comprehending nanoparticles' behavior and effectiveness in targeted drug delivery and cancer therapeutics. They assist researchers and medical professionals in visually recognizing how nanoparticles are distributed within tumor tissues and evaluating their capacity to enhance the efficiency of anticancer medications [60–64]. Cluster 4 (yellow) consists of keywords such as “Non-small cell lung cancer”, “gold nanoparticles”, “nsclc”, “erlotinib”, “exosomes”, and “microrna” with the maximum frequency. The fourth cluster consisted of studies associated with nano-immunotherapy for lung cancer. Different types of nanoparticles, such as polymer-based, Au/PG, and gold nanoparticles, are explored by researchers to enhance the delivery of immunomodulatory agents for the targeted drug delivery system [65, 66].

Cluster 5 (purple) consists of keywords such as "photothermal therapy," "magnetic nanoparticles," "breast cancer," "theranostics," and "targeted delivery." Cluster five is related to theragnostic research in lung cancer treatment. Nanoparticles with theragnostic properties, like quantum dots, have therapeutic and diagnostic potential. Different types of nanocarriers can deliver both therapeutic and imaging compounds, providing benefits such as increased bioavailability and reduced damage to healthy cells [67]. Nanoparticles can target specific receptors involved in lung carcinoma progression, enhancing effectiveness. Inhalational delivery of therapeutics using nanocarriers is a direct strategy for lung cancer treatment, allowing local drug delivery without injections [68, 69]. Overall, nanotechnology has applications in lung cancer diagnosis, therapeutics, and theragnosis through improved drug delivery, early detection, and targeted treatment [70]. Overlay visualization map (Fig. 4B) and trend topic analysis (Fig. 5) were used to determine the NTLC research trend themes. Our analysis indicated that nano-immunotherapy for lung cancer and the development of green synthesis of nanoparticles are currently hot topics of this research. Advancements in nanomedicines and immunotherapy have created a new research area where the therapeutic potential of immunotherapy and nanotechnology are utilized for cancer treatment. Different nanoparticle formulations were tested for preclinical trials to evaluate cancer treatment along with immunotherapy. A clinical trial (NCT03589339) was conducted to investigate the use of a novel oncolytic adenovirus called VCN-01 in combination with pembrolizumab for the treatment of advanced solid tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [71]. In another preclinical trial (NCT04929041), researchers analyzed the safety and effectiveness of combining NBTXR3 and pembrolizumab in treating advanced solid tumors [71]. The concept of green synthesis of nanomaterials (GSN) involves using natural organic precursors with minimal processing to achieve simplicity, cost reduction, and sustainability, while replacing conventional precursors like organic solvents with fungi [72]. Rise in popularity of green synthesis of nanoparticles is a significant recent trend that is anticipated to continue in the future [73]. The research on green synthesis and biocompatible nanoparticles is advancing lung cancer treatments. The approaches aim to improve efficacy and prioritize patient safety [74].

Most cited articles

The top ten most cited documents published between 2003 and 2023 are shown in Table 6. Paz-Ares L et al. [59] is the most cited article (n = 2320); in this study, the efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab in combination with chemotherapy for the treatment of previously untreated metastatic squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) were studied. A research article by Zanganeh S et al., is ranked second in terms of citations. In this article, researchers investigated the therapeutic effect of ferumaxitol for liver metastases in small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) [75]. Peng G et al., [42] is the third most cited article. The main aim of this study was to create an affordable and easy-to-use sensing technology to differentiate between lung cancer patients and healthy subjects. The study successfully created a group of chemiresistors using functionalized gold nanoparticles and pattern recognition methods. The sensors could accurately distinguish between healthy person and lung cancer patients. When tested on different days, the results showed high accuracy (86%) and good reproducibility (90%) [42].

Future prospects and limitations

Nanotherapy in novel drug delivery systems (NDDs) to enhance therapeutic efficacy and minimal side effects represents promising future in lung cancer treatment. Utilization of techniques such as peptide linkage, PEGylation, and receptor-ligand attachment in nanoparticles can help in a targeted approach. Chemotherapeutics within nanocarriers showed reduced cytotoxicity. In the future, researchers can develop multifunctional nanocarriers that enhance control of drug release, preventing cytotoxicity in patients. The development of stimuli-responsive nanoparticles in imaging agents and drug delivery must also be explored for lung cancer treatment.

As for limitations, the data utilized in this study were only obtained from the Scopus database, which makes it difficult for us to avoid publication bias. However, the Scopus database is the most commonly used source of publications for bibliometric analysis. The data in the Scopus database ensures that it effectively represents the current state of research in the field under examination.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first bibliometric analysis conducted on the nanotherapy for lung cancer treatment. This study presents an analysis of the general patterns in nanotechnology and lung cancer. The number of published studies has grown significantly since 2015 and is anticipated to continue to increase in the coming years. Notable advancements in nanomedicine as a treatment for lung cancer have been noted, with nanotherapy affording efficacy in lung cancer treatment by providing targeted approaches that enhance drug delivery and improve patient outcomes. Nanotherapy can effectively target and administer therapeutic agents to lung cancer cells using the distinctive properties of nanoparticles while minimizing harm to healthy tissue.

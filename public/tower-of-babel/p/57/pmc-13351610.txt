Soft Fibroma of the Areolar Region
Authors: Xiaoning Jin, Bingfeng Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSoft fibroma can occasionally occur in the areola region. When doctors are diagnosing lesions in the areola area, they should consider soft fibroma as a possible differential diagnosis. For larger lesions, surgical removal is crucial.

A 74‐year‐old female patient presented to the breast surgery clinic with a long‐standing growth in the right areolar area, which she reported having for over 30 years. Initially, the growth was the size of a small pea and caused her no discomfort, so it was not evaluated or treated at that time. Over the years, it gradually increased in size. On physical examination, a pedunculated “cauliflower‐like” mass was identified at the 7 o'clock position on the right areola, measuring approximately 4.5 × 3.5 × 2 cm, with a stalk measuring 2 cm in length and 1 cm in diameter (Figure 1A). The surface of the mass was irregular with nodular protrusions and notable capillary dilation. It was soft to the touch, non‐tender, and lacked any ulceration or discharge. Surgical excision of the mass was performed (Figure 1B), and histological examination revealed mild epidermal hyperplasia with hyperkeratosis, a stroma composed of loosely arranged collagen fibers, and capillary dilation, consistent with the histopathological characteristics of a soft fibroma (Figure 1C). Postoperatively, the patient recovered well and was discharged from the hospital.

(A) A pedunculated “cauliflower‐like” mass was identified at the 7 o'clock position on the right areola, measuring approximately 4.5 × 3.5 × 2 cm. (B) Tumor specimen after surgical resection. (C) Histopathological examination confirmed the tumor as soft fibroma (HE × 100).

Soft fibroma, also known as a skin tag or fibroepithelial polyp, is a common benign cutaneous neoplasm that typically appears on the neck, axillae, eyelids, and vulvovaginal area, with rare occurrences on the trunk and groin. Soft fibromas generally have little impact on the body. Only in a few cases will they cause harm to the body, such as when the tumor ruptures and bleeds, or when there is local secondary infection, etc. In this particular case, it presented on the areola, an uncommon location. The etiology and pathogenesis of soft fibromas remain unclear; however, they may be associated with factors such as obesity, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and abnormal glucose metabolism [1]. Clinically, soft fibromas are categorized into three types: papular, filiform, and pedunculated. This patient exhibited a pedunculated type. Although malignant transformation is exceedingly rare, isolated cases have been reported in both domestic and international literature. Consequently, early surgical excision is advised for the management of soft fibromas. It is crucial to differentiate this condition from other potentially malignant lesions, such as intraductal papilloma, leiomyoma, and spindle cell carcinoma which can present with similar clinical features [2, 3]. Therefore, definitive diagnosis requires histopathological examination.

A theoretical approach based on machine learning for estimation of physical properties of LLDPE in moulding process
Authors: Fan Zhong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study explores the prediction of mechanical characteristics of linear polyethylene based on oven residence time, employing various regression models and hyper-parameter tuning through the Whale Optimization Algorithm. The dataset comprises one input variable (oven residence time) and three output parameters (Tensile Strength, Impact Strength, and Flexure Strength). The models investigated include Multilayer Perceptron, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Regression, Polynomial Regression, and Theil–Sen Regression. The results showcased distinct performances across the models for each output parameter. The Polynomial Regression (WOA-PR) method has been identified as the most suitable option for predicting Tensile Strength due to its ability to achieve the lowest errors in terms of Mean Absolute Error, Root Mean Square Error, and Average Absolute Relative Deviation. K-Nearest Neighbors (WOA-KNN) outperforms other models in predicting Impact Strength due to its superior accuracy and reliability. Additionally, Support Vector Regression (WOA-SVR) emerges as the best model for predicting Flexure Strength, showcasing notable performance in minimizing prediction errors. These findings underscore the significance of model selection and optimization techniques in accurately predicting the mechanical properties of polymers, paving the way for enhanced manufacturing processes and material design.

Introduction

Polymers are being extensively explored and utilized in different industries across the globe due to their superior physical, thermal, and chemical characteristics. Estimation of their properties is challenging and can be done via different computational methods1. Depending on their molecular weights and chains, polymers have wide applications in industries such as plastic formation, pharmaceutics, chemicals, cosmetics, water treatment, etc.2. Polymers have been also utilized for separation as membranes where liquid or gas mixtures can be used for separation of target molecules3,4. One of the common applications of polymers is in moulding process where polymeric products are formed by thermal processing and injection5,6. In the moulding process, polymeric products are formed by thermal processing and the properties of polymer play a key role in this process. The main properties of polymers in moulding technique include tensile strength, impact strength, and flexure strength which should be precisely controlled to get the desired products. Improving the properties of polymers such as LLDPE is a challenging task due to the complex behavior of polymer in the process. In this process, the mechanical properties are highly dependent on the treatment time which is governed by residence time. So, finding the relationship between oven residence time and these mechanical properties would be essential to optimize the process7. Finding the region with the highest values of these properties is challenging and advanced computational techniques are demanding to be applied for that. Ramkumar et al.7 studied influence of oven residence time on the mechanical properties of LLDPE by experimental and computational methods. The computations were carried out by ROTOSIM software which is specialized for rotational moulding process. The models considered mathematical analysis of physical properties in the process. Development of mechanistic models for such a complex process is computationally challenging and other facile models can be implemented for industrial applications. For process development, computational techniques can be applied to optimize the process and find the relationship between product properties and process parameters. Computational models would be beneficial as they can save time, cost, and materials consumption for experimental analysis of polymer’s mechanical properties. If processing data are available, data-driven models such as machine learning can be used for development of moulding process1. In the present era, Machine Learning (ML) has emerged as a crucial instrument for deriving insightful conclusions and forecasts from extensive and intricate datasets spanning diverse fields including materials science8,9. Big data techniques provide exceptional capabilities when it comes to identifying patterns, generating predictions, and optimizing processes10. The previous study has explored the use of ML models in estimating LLDPE properties, however wider ML models are needed for correlation of polymer properties in moulding process1. This study employed five distinct models, namely Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Support Vector Regression (SVR), Polynomial Regression (PR), and Theil–Sen regression (TSR). Also, the optimization of hyperparameters is achieved via the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA). The models integrated WOA are developed for the first time to predict mechanical properties of LLDPE in moulding process. The optimized models are then used for finding the optimum conditions to get the product with desired mechanical properties. The WOA is a method for optimization that is based on populations and simulates the feeding strategy of humpback whales when they form bubble nets. The process starts by creating a set of potential solutions, represented as a collection of whales. Each marine mammal represents a potential answer to the optimization problem11,12. The Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) serves as a potent regression model inspired by the human brain. By traversing data through numerous interconnected layers, it can discern intricate connections between features and a target variable. This capability enables the model to effectively capture non-linear patterns within the data, rendering it well-suited for a diverse array of prediction tasks13. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) stands as a regression model known for its simplicity and efficiency. To predict the output for an unseen data point, it calculates the weighted average of the output values obtained from the k nearest neighbors in the training dataset14. KNN is straightforward to grasp and deploy; however, its efficacy can be influenced by the selection of the distance metric and the number of neighbors (k). Support Vector Regression (SVR) identifies a hyperplane that optimally aligns with the training data, maximizing the margin between the data points and the plane. In contrast to linear regression, SVR can manage non-linear associations through the utilization of kernel functions. It's known for good performance on smaller datasets and for controlling model complexity15. Polynomial Regression (PR) offers a powerful approach for modeling non-linear relationships in data. Through meticulous selection of the polynomial degree and effective regularization to mitigate overfitting, polynomial regression can deliver precise predictions and insightful analyses of intricate datasets16.

Theil–Sen regression is a non-parametric approach for fitting a straight line to data. It prioritizes robustness over complex modeling. Unlike the least squares methods, it's not swayed by outliers. It calculates the slope as the median of all slopes formed by connecting every possible pair of data points. This makes it a strong choice when dealing with data potentially containing outliers or when the underlying relationship isn't perfectly linear17.

K-nearest neighbors (KNN)

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) Regression stands as a robust technique in the realm of machine learning, primarily utilized for regression tasks. Unlike Support Vector Machines (SVM) and its derivatives like Support Vector Regression (SVR), which are more commonly associated with classification, KNN regression is tailored specifically for predicting continuous outcomes rather than discrete classes19,20.

Operating on a simple principle, KNN regression forecasts the target output for a given data point by averaging the values of its nearest neighbors in the feature space. Consider a dataset represented as pairs of input–output tuples in the format \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {x_{i} ,\;d_{i} } \right)$$\end{document}xi,di for N data points, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_{i}$$\end{document}xi denotes a vector of input features and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_{i}$$\end{document}di represents the corresponding desired target output.

Here, k stands for the count of nearest neighbors considered for prediction. The choice of k significantly impacts the performance of the model, with smaller values leading to more flexible, potentially noisy predictions, while larger values tend to provide smoother, less variable predictions.

One of the primary advantages of KNN regression lies in its simplicity and ease of implementation. It doesn't require complex optimization techniques or parameter tuning as in some other regression methods. Nevertheless, the selection of an optimal value for k holds paramount importance as it profoundly impacts the bias-variance equilibrium of the model. A smaller k value tends to result in overfitting the training data, whereas a larger k value may result in underfitting.

Furthermore, KNN regression doesn't rely on assumptions about the underlying distribution of the data, making it particularly useful when dealing with non-linear relationships or when the data distribution is unknown.

Polynomial regression (PR)

Polynomial Regression (PR) is a versatile method in machine learning, adept at capturing non-linear connections between input attributes and target results. Polynomial regression can capture curvature and other complex patterns in the dataset, whereas linear regression assumes a linear relationship between variables25,26.

In polynomial regression, the relationship between the inputs (features) x and the target output d is modeled using a polynomial function of degree n, expressed as27:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f\left( x \right) = \beta_{0} + \beta_{1} x + \beta_{2} x^{2} + \cdots + \beta_{n} x^{n}$$\end{document}fx=β0+β1x+β2x2+⋯+βnxn

The higher the degree of the polynomial, the more complex the model becomes, allowing it to capture intricate patterns in the data. However, higher degrees also increase the risk of overfitting, especially when dealing with limited data.

The procedure of fitting a polynomial regression model entails identifying the coefficients that yield the optimal values, thereby minimizing the disparity between the predicted values and the observed target outputs within the training dataset. This objective can be accomplished through the utilization of diverse optimization methodologies, including ordinary least squares or gradient descent.

An essential benefit of polynomial regression is its adaptability in capturing non-linear associations without requiring the explicit specification of the functional relationship form. This makes it particularly useful in scenarios where the underlying data generating process is complex or not well understood.

Whale optimization algorithm (WOA)

The Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) is a method for optimization that is based on populations and simulates the feeding strategy of humpback whales when they form bubble nets. The process starts by creating a set of potential solutions, represented as a collection of whales. Each marine mammal represents a potential answer to the optimization problem11,12. The cetaceans subsequently proceed towards the most advantageous resolution by exhibiting three distinct behaviors: foraging, spiraling, and bubble-net feeding. The aforementioned conduct is influenced by the hunting tactics employed by humpback whales, renowned for their exceptional proficiency in capturing prey11,37.

In the process of hunting for prey, whales demonstrate a preference for following the most efficient strategy, often referred to as the leader. This resembles the behavior seen in other meta-heuristic algorithms like PSO and DE. The spiraling behavior of whales entails a movement pattern where individuals spiral towards the leader, potentially increasing the chances of finding the best solution. The collective bubble-net attacking behavior involves whales encircling the leader and participating in a synchronized attack, aiming to improve the likelihood of uncovering the global optimum.

Conclusion

The objective of this study was to explore the ability to forecast the mechanical properties of a polymer by considering the duration of oven residence using different regression models and conducting hyper-parameter tuning with the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA). The dataset comprised a single input variable (oven residence time) and three outcome parameters (Tensile Strength, Impact Strength, and Flexure Strength). The models under investigation included MLP, KNN, SVR, PR, and TSR.

The findings demonstrated varying levels of performance among the models for each particular output parameter, assessed by metrics such as RMSE, MAE, AARD%, and the Correlation Coefficient (CC). One of the most effective methods for predicting Tensile Strength was Polynomial Regression (WOA-PR), which exhibited the lowest errors (RMSE: 0.0358, MAE: 0.0310, AARD%: 0.1904, CC: − 0.178906). The K-Nearest Neighbors (WOA-KNN) model showed superior accuracy and reliability compared to other models in predicting Impact Strength (RMSE: 0.0149, MAE: 0.0103, AARD%: 1.1816, CC: 0.258569). Furthermore, the WOA-SVR model was identified as the most effective approach for predicting Flexure Strength (RMSE: 0.0874, MAE: 0.0818, AARD%: 0.4934, CC: − 0.160137).

These results highlight the importance of employing model selection and optimization methods to effectively forecast the mechanical characteristics of polymers. The study demonstrates that including the correlation coefficient (CC) in the analysis provides a more comprehensive evaluation of model performance. By refining this approach to include CC and the effects of other input parameters, one can ensure a robust and reliable prediction framework. This, in turn, facilitates the development of improved manufacturing procedures and material design strategies, paving the way for advancements in polymer processing and applications.

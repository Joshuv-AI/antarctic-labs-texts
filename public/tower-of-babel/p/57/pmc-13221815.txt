Clinical Vignette

A 75-year-old man had recurrent hospitalizations with decompensated heart failure. Echocardiography revealed mixed aortic valve disease with moderate aortic stenosis and severe aortic regurgitation, along with a severely dilated left ventricle with an ejection fraction of 25%. Past history included metastatic prostate cancer. Medical therapy was limited by persistent hypotension; coronary angiography demonstrated no significant disease. Computed tomography revealed a massive aortic annulus with an area of 1,040 mm2, perimeter of 117 mm, mean diameter of 37 mm, left ventricular outflow tract 38 mm, with a mean sinus of Valsalva diameter of 43 mm, ascending aorta of 40 mm, and moderate leaflet calcification (Figures 1A to 1C). These dimensions exceeded manufacturer's instructions for use for all commercially available transcatheter aortic valves. After Heart Team review, the patient was accepted for transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) with a Myval 35-mm balloon-expandable valve (BEV) (Meril Life Sciences) on a compassionate use basis.

Take-Home Messages

•Transcatheter aortic valve implantation can be performed off-label in selected patients with large aortic annulus using commercially available 29-mm valves by adding additional volume; however, this risks paravalvular leak, valve embolism, and damage to bioprosthetic leaflets.

TAVI was performed under conscious sedation via right femoral arterial access. The valve was implanted under rapid ventricular pacing with slow inflation, with an additional 10 cc added to the nominal inflation volume (total 60 cc) at the time of deployment to achieve adequate oversizing. Postdeployment angiography demonstrated excellent valve positioning with no paravalvular leak, patent coronary arteries, and no new conduction abnormalities (Figures 1D to 1F, Video 1). Echocardiography showed a mean transvalvular gradient of 1 mm Hg with no aortic regurgitation.

Discussion

TAVI in patients with very large annuli beyond device instructions for use has been shown to be feasible, with comparable short-term outcomes and valve performance by valve academic research consortium-3 criteria, although with higher rates of paravalvular leak, valve embolism, and need for a second valve.1 Recent reports have described successful TAVI with a Myval 35-mm BEV for annuli of 722 mm2 and 883 mm2.2,3 To the best of our knowledge, this case represents the largest annular area successfully treated with TAVI. Larger prospective studies are needed to determine the efficacy and safety of this novel BEV.

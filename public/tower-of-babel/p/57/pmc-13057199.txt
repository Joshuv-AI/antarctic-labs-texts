Inhibition efficacy of expired citalopram drug towards the damage of C-steel in hydrochloric acid solution
Authors: Mahmoud G A Saleh, Rasha N Felaly, H Hawsawi, Salih S Al-Juaid, Salah S Elyan, M Abdallah, Syed Khalid Mustafa, S Abd El Wanees
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe corrosion inhibition performance of expired citalopram (Cp) for carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl solution was systematically evaluated using chemical and electrochemical techniques. The corrosion rate decreased significantly with increasing Cp concentration, accompanied by a corresponding improvement in inhibition efficiency, which exceeded 92% at a Cp concentration of 0.005 M and a temperature of 25 °C. The inhibition mechanism was attributed to the adsorption of Cp molecules onto the carbon steel surface, following the Langmuir adsorption isotherm. The adsorption equilibrium constant (Kads) decreased with increasing temperature, indicating partial desorption of Cp molecules from the steel surface. The negative values of the standard free energy of adsorption (ΔG°ads) confirm the spontaneous nature of the adsorption process. Depending on the solution temperature, ΔG°ads values ranged from − 36.16 to − 33.72 kJ mol−1, suggesting that the adsorption mechanism involves a mixed physisorption–chemisorption process. The relatively high adsorption energy values further demonstrate the strong interaction between Cp molecules and the steel surface, accounting for the effective corrosion protection provided by Cp.Keywords: Citalopram drug, H2 production, Impedance, Gravimetry, Potentiodynamic polarization, InhibitionSubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

Carbon steel plays a crucial role in petroleum fields and is widely used in industrial and daily-life applications due to its excellent mechanical properties and cost-effectiveness1,2. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) solutions are extensively employed in various industrial processes, including the removal of rust, scale, and other deposits from steel surfaces, as well as in metal surface treatments such as pickling. However, a major drawback associated with the use of such mineral acids is their aggressive interaction with metallic substrates, which accelerates corrosion and leads to significant material loss3. The use of environmentally benign corrosion inhibitors has therefore been recognized as an effective strategy to mitigate the harmful effects of these acids on metal surfaces4. The corrosion inhibition efficiency of organic compounds largely depends on the presence of hetero-atoms, such as nitrogen, oxygen, sulfur, or phosphorus, within their molecular structures, which are capable of donating lone-pair electrons and facilitating adsorption onto the metal surface5–14.

In addition, Bochuan Tan et al. investigated extracts of Abelmoschus esculentus leaves as mixed-type corrosion inhibitors for copper in sulfuric acid. The presence of polysaccharides, proteins, flavonoids, and other active constituents confers environmentally friendly and low-toxicity characteristics on this natural product, facilitating the formation of a complex protective film through the synergistic action of multiple components15. Furthermore, N, S-doped carbon quantum dots have been reported as green corrosion inhibitors for brine heaters in multistage flash desalination systems16, while Jafari et al. confirmed that the high inhibition efficiency of benzothiazole derivatives is attributed to the formation of a protective film on the steel surface17. A comprehensive evaluation of Polygonum aviculare leaf extract as a green corrosion inhibitor for steel in hydrochloric acid was conducted by Kartal et al.18. The study demonstrated that the plant extract contains phenolic acids, flavonoids, phytosteroids, and phenolic aldehydes, which effectively adsorb onto the metallic surface, thereby reducing the corrosion current density to 0.06 mA cm−2 and increasing the polarization resistance to 457 Ω cm2. Consequently, the inhibition efficiency reached up to 96%.

The protective performance of organic compounds containing the C=N functional group is mainly governed by the presence of heteroatoms bearing lone pairs of electrons. These heteroatoms act as active adsorption centers, enabling electron donation to the surface of the corroding metal19,20. Pyrrolidine derivatives have been reported as effective corrosion inhibitors for steel in dilute HCl solutions, exhibiting inhibition efficiencies exceeding 90% at an inhibitor concentration of 5.0 mM and a temperature of 25 °C21,22. In particular, 2-(1-methylpyrrolidin -2-ylidene)hydrazinecarbo-thioamide demonstrated a remarkably high inhibition efficiency, surpassing 97% at an optimal concentration of 5.0 mM in HCl solution22. More recently, a freshly prepared modified chitosan derivative was employed as an efficient inhibitor for carbon steel corrosion in 1.0 M HCl solution, achieving an inhibition efficiency of 93% at an inhibitor concentration of 5 mg L−1 at 25 °C23. The corrosion protection mechanism was attributed to an adsorption process involving the interaction of lone-pair electrons on sulfur and nitrogen atoms with the metallic surface23.

Due to their remarkable anti-corrosion properties, eco-friendliness, and applications in various fields, including corrosion protection, expired drugs have gained more attention as a film-forming corrosion inhibitor for carbon steel in acidic solutions24–33. Such compounds achieve metal protection through an adsorption process via an electrostatic or coordination electron-donating mechanism. The adsorption of these molecules onto the corroding metal surface leads to the formation of active protective sites that hinder the corrosion process. Cefpodoxime has been investigated as a potential corrosion inhibitor for steel in dilute hydrochloric acid solutions, demonstrating an inhibition efficiency of more than 87% at an optimal concentration of 200 ppm29. Abdallah et al.30 investigated the influence of Bifonazole and Terconazole as corrosion inhibitors to reduce the general and localized pitting corrosion of carbon steel in hydrochloric acid solution. Their findings revealed a protection efficiency exceeding 92%. They attributed such behavior to the development of an insulating film around the metallic surface by the adsorption of inhibitor molecules30. Drotaverine drug was used to reduce the damaging effect of HCl on the metallic surface, utilizing the chemical and electrochemical methods31. It is indicated that the protection efficacy reached 82% at 25 °C and increased to 96% at 80 °C. Pharmaceutical compounds inhibit both anodic and cathodic processes occurring on the metal surface by forming a protective adsorbed film, which effectively separates the metal from the corrosive environment31. Cephalexin monohydrate has been reported as an environmentally benign corrosion inhibitor for protecting metallic surfaces in hydrochloric acid solutions. The inhibition effect of this compound was attributed to the formation of an insoluble adsorbed complex on the metal surface33. Moreover, the addition of potassium iodide was found to enhance the protective performance of the drug further.

The present study aims to evaluate citalopram (Cp) as an effective corrosion inhibitor for carbon steel in 1.0 M hydrochloric acid solution. The novelty of this work lies in the use of an expired, low-cost, environmentally benign, and water-soluble compound (with concentrations ranging from 5 × 10−5 to 5 × 10−3 M) as a corrosion inhibitor. The inhibition performance was systematically assessed and validated using chemical and electrochemical techniques. In addition, the surface morphology of carbon steel specimens before and after corrosion was examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) to elucidate the surface characteristics under the investigated conditions.

Results and discussion

Gravimetry investigation

The mass loss experiments were performed to evaluate the effect of 1 M HCl on the corrosion of the carbon steel surface and to assess the protective performance of the expired Cp drug at different temperatures. The calculated corrosion rate (rg) values were used to determine the surface coverage (θ) and inhibition efficiency (ηw), as summarized in Table 3. In the uninhibited acid solution, the rg value was 1.05 mg cm−2 h−1, which decreased to 0.136 mg cm−2 h−1 in the presence of 1 mM Cp at 25 °C. Figure 1A illustrates the reduction in rg values with increasing Cp concentration at various temperatures. The addition of Cp to the investigated medium resulted in a continuous decrease in rg accompanied by an increase in ηw values, as shown in Table 3 and Fig. 1(A and B). The sigmoidal nature of these curves indicates the adsorption of Cp species onto the metal surface, thereby protecting it from aggressive Cl− ions44,46. The decrease in ηw % values with increasing temperature at different Cp concentrations can be attributed to the partial desorption of adsorbed Cp molecules from the metal surface into the bulk solution. This desorption behavior supports the physisorption mechanism of Cp adsorption on the carbon steel surface47.

Gasometric study

Gasometric analysis was employed to evaluate how the degraded Cp drug affected the corrosion behavior of carbon steel and the evolution of hydrogen gas. The data indicated that the produced H2 (VH) evolved after an induction time, which reached 40 min in the blank solution, followed by a higher rise in the produced hydrogen with immersion time. When the Cp is added to the acid solution, τ is elongated with higher amounts of the Cp inhibitor (τ is equal to 100 and 165 min with additions of 0.05 mM and 5 mM Cp, respectively) and is reduced with the increase in temperature. Open circuit potential

It is important to determine the steady-state potential of the metal surface before electrochemical measurements, as it provides insight into the electrochemical behavior of metals in corrosive media. The variation of the open-circuit potential (OCP) with immersion time for carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl is presented in Fig. 3. It can be observed that the OCP carbon steel reaches a steady-state potential after approximately 30 min, with an estimated value of − 0.498 V. Upon the addition of Cp inhibitor, the Est shifts toward less negative values, increasing from − 0.485 V at a concentration of 5 × 10−5 M to reach − 0.442 V with 5 × 10−3 M of Cp in 0.5 M HCl. This positive shift in Est is attributed to the rupture of the pre-immersion air-formed oxide layer, while the subsequent stabilization of the potential contributes to further thickening of the oxide film5,70,71. Moreover, the slight positive variation in potential over time for the inhibited solutions compared to the blank solution can be ascribed to the formation of corrosion products and/or the adsorption of inhibitor molecules onto the metal surface.

The anodic (βa) and cathodic (βc) Tafel slopes do not remain nearly constant compared with those of the blank solution, which may be attributed to the similarity in the corrosion mechanisms operating in both the uninhibited and Cp-containing solutions51,72. It is worth noting that the introduction of the Cp compound into the corrosive causes only a slight shift in the corrosion potential (Ecorr) relative to the uninhibited acidic solution. This behavior indicates that Cp inhibits both the anodic dissolution of iron and the cathodic reduction of hydrogen ions. The change in corrosion potential (ΔEcorr) between the inhibited and uninhibited systems was approximately ± 21 mV, confirming that Cp acts as a mixed-type corrosion inhibitor46,72.

The inhibition efficiency (ηp) was calculated from the Icorr values obtained for the HCl solution in the absence and presence of the Cp compound using Eq. (6). The ηp values increase with increasing Cp concentration in the aggressive solution as a consequence of the corresponding decrease in Icorr. This trend demonstrates that Cp effectively suppresses both anodic iron dissolution and cathodic hydrogen evolution reactions on the investigated metal surface23.

Furthermore, the presence of the expired Cp drug in the corrosive HCl solution significantly increases the polarization resistance (Rp) from 42 Ω cm2 in the uninhibited acid to 449 Ω cm2 upon the addition of 5 mM Cp. This substantial increase reflects the formation of a protective adsorbed film on the metal surface. In addition, the continuous enhancement in Rp and inhibition efficiency (ηp) with increasing Cp concentration suggests that the adsorbed Cp layer becomes thicker and more compact on the examined metal surface73.

The impedance, EIS study

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is a rapid, sensitive, and non-destructive electrochemical technique that is extensively employed to investigate the electrochemical behavior of metals exposed to corrosive environments. By applying a small-amplitude alternating current (AC) perturbation over a wide range of frequencies, EIS provides detailed information about the kinetics of electrochemical reactions occurring at the metal/solution interface. This technique enables the evaluation of key corrosion parameters such as charge-transfer resistance, double-layer capacitance, and diffusion processes, which are closely related to corrosion rate and surface condition.

Figure 5 shows the Nyquist plots of carbon steel in 1.0 M HCl solution in the absence and presence of different concentrations of the expired Cp drug. The incorporation of the Cp compound into the acidic medium induces pronounced changes in the EIS response. The experimental impedance data were interpreted and fitted using the equivalent electrical circuit illustrated in Fig. 674.

The Nyquist plots displayed in Fig. 5 are characterized by depressed capacitive semicircles rather than ideal ones, which is generally attributed to surface heterogeneity and frequency dispersion effects. The appearance of these capacitive loops across the studied frequency range, both in the uninhibited solution and in the presence of the Cp inhibitor, indicates the protective role of Cp molecules against the aggressive action of HCl on the carbon steel surface. This behavior suggests that the corrosion inhibition mechanism is predominantly controlled by charge-transfer processes occurring between iron atoms at the steel surface and the adsorbed Cp molecules75,76. The Cp molecules function as interfacial inhibitors by adsorbing onto the steel surface and forming a protective insulating film that suppresses the corrosive attack of hydrochloric acid77,78. Furthermore, increasing the Cp concentration leads to a gradual expansion of the capacitive loop diameter, reflecting the formation and thickening of a protective layer that effectively limits the direct interaction between the acidic solution and the metal substrate.

The Bode magnitude and phase angle plots were used to further clarify the inhibitory performance of the expired Cp drug, as illustrated in Fig. 7(A and B). The Bode plots exhibit predominantly capacitive behavior in the intermediate frequency region, while resistive behavior is observed at both high and low frequencies. The increase in the maximum phase angle with rising Cp concentration is attributed to the formation of a more compact, homogeneous, and protective inhibitor film on the steel surface79–81. As a result, the inhibition efficiency (ηI) of the expired Cp drug increases with increasing Cp concentration21. Moreover, Fig. 7B reveals that higher Cp concentrations lead to a shift in the phase angle toward less negative values, further confirming the development of a protective Cp layer that effectively diminishes the direct interaction between the HCl solution and the steel surface21,81,82.

(A) Bode and (B) phase angle plots in 1 M HCl with and without successive additions of the Cp inhibitor at 25 °C.

The electrochemical impedance parameters, namely the charge transfer resistance (Rct), double-layer capacitance (Cdl), and inhibition efficiency (ηI), were derived from the EIS measurements and are listed in Table 7. The observed increase in Rct values upon the addition of the expired Cp drug reflects an improvement in corrosion resistance, which can be attributed to the effective adsorption of Cp molecules on the carbon steel surface, thereby retarding the charge-transfer process associated with corrosion21. In contrast, the decrease in Cdl values in the presence of Cp indicates an increase in the thickness of the electrical double layer and/or a reduction in the local dielectric constant. This effect arises from the displacement of water molecules by adsorbed Cp species at the metal/solution interface, resulting in the formation of a protective barrier that suppresses metal dissolution21,81.

Mechanism of corrosion inhibition by citalopram

The corrosion inhibition of carbon steel in acidic medium by citalopram (Cp) can be attributed to the adsorption of Cp molecules onto the metal surface, leading to the formation of a protective barrier that isolates the steel from the corrosive environment. In HCl solution, Cp molecules become partially protonated due to the presence of heteroatoms (N and O) with lone pair electrons. The inhibition mechanism, therefore, involves mixed adsorption, combining both physical and chemical interactions. Initially, electrostatic attraction occurs between the protonated Cp species and the negatively charged carbon steel surface, which is rendered negative by the specific adsorption of Cl− ions. This physisorption process is supported by the relatively low and negative values of ΔG°ads.

Simultaneously, chemisorption may take place through the donation of lone pair electrons from N and O atoms, as well as π-electrons of the benzene rings, to the vacant d-orbitals of Fe atoms. This interaction strengthens the adsorption and enhances surface coverage, resulting in effective blocking of active anodic and cathodic sites. The formation of the adsorbed Cp layer leads to an increase in charge transfer resistance (Rct) and a decrease in double-layer capacitance (Cdl), as observed in EIS measurements. This indicates that Cp molecules replace H2O molecules at the metal/solution interface and increase the thickness of the electrical double layer. As a consequence, the rate of both Fe dissolution and H2 evolution reactions is significantly reduced, confirming that citalopram acts as a mixed-type corrosion inhibitor.

Furthermore, the decrease in inhibition efficiency with increasing temperature suggests that the adsorption process is predominantly physical in nature, although a contribution from chemisorption cannot be excluded. The adsorption behavior following the Langmuir isotherm supports the formation of a uniform, protective monolayer on the steel surface.

Outside-In Distal-to-Proximal Longitudinal Capsulotomy Using the Greater Trochanter as a Bony Landmark With Postless Traction During Hip Arthroscopy
Authors: Zhikuan Li, Yue Geng, Yingzhen Niu, Zhuangdai Zhang, Shengkun Wu, Jiangtao Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hip arthroscopy is an established treatment for femoroacetabular impingement syndrome, with demonstrated long-term efficacy. However, the deep-seated anatomy of the hip joint and its complex periarticular structures pose significant challenges for achieving safe capsulotomy and adequate intra-articular exposure. We describe a technique using the greater trochanter as an anatomical reference point to perform an outside-in, distal-to-proximal longitudinal capsulotomy under postless traction.

Femoroacetabular impingement syndrome has emerged as a prevalent cause of hip pain, with hip arthroscopy evolving as a definitive treatment.1, 2, 3 The procedure's complexity stems from the hip’s unique anatomical constraints, including its thick fibrous capsule and proximity to neurovascular bundles, making capsulotomy and joint access critical yet technically demanding steps.4, 5, 6

Conventional inside-out capsulotomy techniques necessitate specialized instrumentation and fluoroscopic guidance, prolonging traction time and increasing perineal injury risk.7, 8, 9 These approaches often require sectioning of the iliofemoral ligament—a key hip stabilizer—potentially leading to iatrogenic instability.10 Furthermore, irregular capsular tears generated by this method complicate surgical closure.11

Although existing outside-in techniques reduce instrument dependency and preserve ligamentous structures,12,13 they rely on arthroscopic identification of ambiguous soft-tissue landmarks, resulting in prolonged operative time and technical difficulty.12,14 To address these limitations, we describe an approach: an anatomically guided outside-in distal-to-proximal longitudinal capsulotomy using the palpable greater trochanter as a definitive bony landmark, facilitated by post-free traction.

Surgical Technique

Patient Positioning

The patient is placed under general anesthesia and administered muscle relaxants to obtain adequate traction. Perineal post-free traction is then applied with the patient in the Trendelenburg position. This involves positioning the patient at 15° to 20° head-down orientation while stabilizing the operative limb in slight internal rotation to achieve optimal joint distraction (Fig 1).Fig 1Patient positioning. Surgical Approach

The greater trochanter and anterior superior iliac spine are first palpated and marked. The standard anterolateral portal (ALP) is established superior to the greater trochanter. The anterior portal (AP) is then established superior to the ALP and inferior to the anterior superior iliac spine. In addition, an accessory midanterior portal (MAP) is established (Fig 2, Video 1).Fig 2Establishment of the 3 portals. The patient is in a supine position; right hip is shown. Surgical Technique

A 70° arthroscope (Arthrex, Naples, FL) is introduced through the ALP for joint visualization (Fig 3D). Instrumentation via the MAP and AP requires a 45° radiofrequency probe (Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) and shaver (Arthrex) (Fig 3 D and E). The greater trochanter is identified by debriding periarticular soft tissues using a shaver and radiofrequency. Labral Release, AIISplasty, and Acetabuloplasty

A arthroscopic blade (Arthrex) is used to perform circumferential labral release from the acetabular rim (Fig 5A). For type II/III AIIS, it must be reshaped into a type I AIIS morphology (Fig 5B). Capsular Closure

Preplaced anchor sutures are passed through both capsular flaps for superior capsular closure (Fig 8A). Traction sutures previously placed for capsular exposure are repurposed to close the inferior capsule. When necessary, an additional inferior reinforcement suture is placed using a suture passer (Fig 8B).Fig 8Closure of longitudinal capsulotomy. The patient is in a supine position; left hip is shown. Discussion

This study proposes the greater trochanter as a bony landmark (Fig 3A) for an outside-in, distal-to-proximal longitudinal capsulotomy. Advantages and Disadvantages

The trochanteric landmark enables safe capsulotomy when soft-tissue boundaries are unclear arthroscopically, solving the reference point deficiency of previous outside-in techniques. It avoids inside-out drawbacks like blind-puncture labral penetration, fluoroscopy dependence, and irregular capsulotomies. The distal-proximal direction follows capsular fibers, preserving the iliofemoral ligament, easing closure, and reducing healing risks.

The capsulotomy extends longitudinally along the femoral neck (11-2 o'clock), precisely exposing impingement sites. High-strength sutures through AP/ALP portals retract capsular flaps (Fig 4 C and F), creating dynamic suspension for optimal exposure: Medially, type II/III AIIS impingement is fully visualized for burring to type I (Figs 4B and 5C); laterally, cam lesion visualization ensures safe osteochondroplasty margins (Fig 4E). The post-free technique eliminates fluoroscopy, limits traction to <30 minutes, and prevents perineal complications.

Capsular stability is crucial for function.15 A layered closure is performed: superiorly, labral anchor sutures are used to repair the capsule, restoring iliofemoral ligament tension (Fig 8A). Inferiorly, retraction sutures with suture hooks are used to reinforce posteroinferior stability (Fig 8B). The main limitations of this technique are its learning curve (suited for experienced surgeons) and potential cartilage injury during suture hook passage. However, any cartilage damage would be resected during osteochondroplasty (Table 2).

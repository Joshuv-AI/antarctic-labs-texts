The miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis in hepatocellular carcinoma: clinical significance, diagnostic value, and functional validation
Authors: Xiang Chen, Gui Zhou, Lihang Cen, Shuang Qin, Meiyu Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPDZK1IP1 is implicated in various cancers, but its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains unclear.AimTo investigate the expression, clinical significance, and biological function of the miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis in HCC.MethodsSerum samples from 56 HCC patients, 57 cirrhosis patients, and 68 healthy controls were analyzed by qRT-PCR. Diagnostic and prognostic values were assessed by ROC curves and Kaplan-Meier/Cox regression analyses, respectively. The regulatory relationship was validated by dual-luciferase assay. Functional roles and downstream mechanisms were examined via cellular assays and Western blotting.ResultsSerum miR-4512 was significantly downregulated while PDZK1IP1 was upregulated in HCC patients, showing a negative correlation. Both markers served as independent prognostic factors for overall survival and disease-free survival. A triple diagnostic combination (miR-4512 + PDZK1IP1 + AFP) achieved an outstanding AUC of 0.988. Dual-luciferase assay confirmed miR-4512 directly targeted the 3’UTR of PDZK1IP1. Functionally, miR-4512 overexpression or PDZK1IP1 silencing suppressed HCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Mechanistically, Western blot revealed that miR-4512 suppressed the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway and glycolysis, which was robustly reversed by PDZK1IP1 overexpression.ConclusionThe miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis regulates HCC progression via the Akt/mTOR and glycolysis pathways. Both molecules demonstrate significant potential as robust diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-026-02399-2.Keywords: miR-4512, PDZK1IP1, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Biomarker

Introduction

Globally, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most prevalent malignancy and stands as the third most frequent contributor to cancer-associated mortality [1]. Liver cirrhosis is the predominant risk factor, present in approximately 80% of HCC patients [2]. Asymptomatic early progression often leads to late-stage diagnoses, precluding curative surgery and resulting in a dismal five-year survival rate of 15% [3–6]. Currently used clinical markers such as AFP and PIVKA-II have limited sensitivity and specificity, with high false-positive rates in cirrhotic patients [7]. Recent integrative bioinformatics analyses have identified critical hub genes and drug targets that mediate HCC progression, underscoring the potential of high-throughput approaches in discovering novel clinical indicators [8]. Thus, identifying novel biomarkers that combine diagnostic accuracy with functional relevance is urgently needed to improve early detection and patient outcomes.

Stable in body fluids, miRNAs are critical biomarkers whose dysregulation drives HCC initiation and progression [9]. Recent studies have highlighted the significance of specific miRNA-mRNA regulatory axes, such as the miR-3130-5p/FDX1 axis, in driving HCC growth and metabolic reprogramming [10]. Within this family, miR-4512 has emerged as a multifaceted regulator in various pathological contexts. In oncology, it has been characterized as a tumor suppressor in renal cancer [11]. In the context of digestive system malignancies, in silico analysis has identified miR-4512 as a potential regulator of Histone H4 metabolism in stage IV gastric cancer [12]. Beyond its metabolic implications, miR-4512 serves as a pivotal immune modulator in autoimmune and cardiovascular disorders [13–15]. In the realm of oncology, miR-4512 has recently been established as a functional component of the ADAR1-mediated network, exerting tumor-suppressive effects on cell mobility by silencing ARPIN [16]. Given that HCC is an inflammation-driven malignancy often managed within an evolving landscape of immunotherapeutic interventions [17], the immune-metabolic and tumor-suppressive functions of miR-4512 may be critically involved in hepatic carcinogenesis. Nevertheless, the specific expression profiles and functional significance of miR-4512 within the HCC landscape, particularly its interaction with the PDZK1IP1-mediated metabolic network, have not yet been elucidated.

Beyond miRNAs, protein-coding genes serve as critical regulators in cancer. For instance, PRDX6 has recently been identified as a key driver of HCC proliferation and metastasis, highlighting the diverse molecular mechanisms governing tumor progression [18]. Similarly, PDZK1IP1, a 17 kDa non-glycosylated membrane protein involved in transmembrane transport, has gained attention [19]. Accumulating evidence indicates that PDZK1IP1 functions as a pro-tumorigenic driver across a diverse spectrum of malignancies, including carcinomas of the breast, pancreas, prostate, and ovary. Its involvement is notably characterized by the facilitation of metastatic dissemination and a strong association with advanced disease stages [20–23]. Previous studies have suggested that its pro-tumorigenic mechanisms may involve multiple signaling pathways [24]. In HCC, PDZK1IP1 is significantly upregulated [25, 26] and correlates with poor prognosis. However, current summaries of PDZK1IP1 in HCC remain largely phenomenological, and its upstream epigenetic regulation (e.g., via miRNAs) as well as its downstream metabolic signaling networks remain to be further elucidated.

To address these gaps and highlight the originality of our work, this study represents, to the best of our knowledge, the first comprehensive investigation into the miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 regulatory axis in HCC. We designed this research to evaluate their expression profiles, clinical relevance, and utility as robust diagnostic and prognostic indicators. Integrating clinical cohort analysis with rigorous in vitro molecular studies, we aim to elucidate how this novel axis modulates HCC pathogenesis, thereby providing a strong theoretical foundation for future targeted interventions.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

Approval for the current investigation was granted by the Ethics Committee of The Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, ensuring that all procedures aligned with the ethical tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Each participant formally consented in writing before taking part in the study.

A total of 181 participants were enrolled, including 56 HCC patients with underlying cirrhosis, 57 cirrhosis patients, and 68 healthy controls. The inclusion criteria for the cirrhosis group were: (1) meeting diagnostic standards per the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Cirrhosis (2023) with imaging confirmation (ultrasound/CT/MRI); (2) Child-Pugh classification A or B; (3) AFP levels < 20 ng/mL without evidence of hepatic space-occupying lesions on imaging; (4) absence of severe complications (including gastrointestinal bleeding or ≥ grade 2 hepatic encephalopathy) within the preceding 6 months. The inclusion criteria for the HCC group were: (1) fulfillment of pathological or clinical diagnostic criteria per the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer (2022); (2) confirmation of underlying cirrhosis; (3) exclusion of metastatic liver cancer or other malignancies; (4) no recent targeted or immunotherapy. The inclusion criteria for the healthy control group were: (1) no history of liver disease or any chronic illness; (2) normal liver function tests and abdominal ultrasound findings; (3) AFP levels < 8 ng/mL; (4) no long-term use of medications affecting liver function. Exclusion criteria for all groups included: (1) pregnancy or lactation; (2) presence of other chronic liver diseases; (3) severe cardiac, renal, or respiratory dysfunction; (4) other malignancies; (5) refusal to participate or incomplete clinical data.

Blood collection and cell lines

Under sterile conditions, 5 mL of fasting venous blood was drawn during the morning hours from healthy volunteers, as well as patients diagnosed with cirrhosis and HCC. To isolate the serum, samples were subjected to centrifugation (3000 rpm, 10 min) using a pre-chilled unit maintained at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was immediately partitioned into sterile EP tubes and cryopreserved at -80 °C for future experiments.

This study utilized the following cell lines: the HCC cell lines HCCLM3 (official name: HCCLM3; RRID: CVCL_6832) and MHCC97H (official name: MHCC97H; RRID: CVCL_4972), and the immortalized normal human hepatocyte cell line THLE-2 (official name: THLE-2; RRID: CVCL_3803). All cell lines were purchased from the Shanghai Cell Bank (Shanghai, China). HCCLM3 and MHCC97H were cultured in DMEM medium (10% FBS, 1% penicillin-streptomycin), while THLE-2 was cultured in DMEM medium (5% FBS, 70 ng/mL EGF, 10 µg/mL insulin, 1% penicillin-streptomycin) using collagen I-coated flasks (5 µg/cm², 37 °C for 1 h). All cells were maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere.

siRNA synthesis

To achieve gene silencing, three specific small interfering RNAs (si-134, si-238, and si-300) directed against the human PDZK1IP1 transcript were customized and produced by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). In all knockdown experiments, a non-silencing scrambled siRNA functioned as the negative control (NC).

Cell transfection

HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells, harvested during the logarithmic growth phase, were transfected utilizing the Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA). All procedures were executed in strict accordance with the standardized instructions provided by the manufacturer. After 48 h of culture, knockdown efficiency was validated by qPCR, followed by functional experiments.

For rescue experiments, HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells were co-transfected with miR-4512 mimic and PDZK1IP1 overexpression plasmid (OE-PDZK1IP1) or corresponding negative controls (OE-NC) using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After 48 h of culture, cells were harvested for subsequent functional assays.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was harvested from the experimental cells and tissues via the TRIzol™ method (Thermo Fisher, USA) as per the manufacturer’s manual. RNA integrity was assessed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, and high-quality RNA samples with A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0 were selected for subsequent experiments.

For mRNA detection, reverse transcription was performed using PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix (Takara Bio, Japan) to synthesize cDNA. To quantify miRNA levels, reverse transcription was executed via the poly(A) tailing approach using the Mir-X™ miRNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit (Takara, Japan). Subsequent qPCR assays were performed with SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan), where GAPDH and U6 snRNA functioned as internal controls for mRNA and miRNA normalization, respectively. The thermal cycling parameters included an initial 30-sec denaturation at 95 °C, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C (5 s) and 60 °C (30 s). A dissociation curve analysis (65 °C to 95 °C, 0.5 °C increments every 5 s) was conducted to confirm primer specificity. Data from triplicate biological experiments were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

The primer sequences used for qPCR quantification of miR-4512, PDZK1IP1, and the internal controls, as well as the sequences of miR-4512 mimics and inhibitors, are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot analysis

Total proteins from transfected HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher, USA). Equal amounts of protein lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). After blocking with 5% non-fat milk for 1.5 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies against PDZK1IP1, p-AKT, total AKT, p-mTOR, total mTOR, GLUT1, LDHA, GAPDH, and β-Actin. Subsequently, the membranes were incubated with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. The protein bands were visualized using an Enhanced Chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system and semi-quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software. GAPDH or β-Actin served as the endogenous control.

CCK-8 assay

To evaluate cellular proliferation, HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells (log-phase) were seeded into 96-well plates (100 µL/well) after enzymatic digestion and resuspension. At pre-determined time points (0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-transfection), we added the CCK-8 detection solution (Dojindo Laboratories, Japan). After incubating the plates for an additional 2 h in a dark environment, a microplate reader was employed to measure the absorbance at 450 nm.

Transwell assay

Log-phase HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells were digested and resuspended in serum-free medium at 2 × 10⁵ cells/mL. For migration assays, cells (4 × 10⁴ in 200 µL) were seeded into 8 μm pore transwell chambers, while invasion assays used chambers pre-coated with Matrigel matrix (1:8 dilution, solidified at 37 °C for 4 h). The lower chamber contained 600 µL complete medium with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. Cells were incubated for 24 h (migration) or 48 h (invasion) at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Non-migrated cells were removed with cotton swabs, and migrated/invaded cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (15 min), stained with 0.1% crystal violet (20 min), then counted in five random 200× fields per membrane.

Bioinformatics prediction and dual-luciferase reporter assay

The potential binding sites between miR-4512 and the 3’-UTR of PDZK1IP1 were predicted using the TargetScan database (Version 8.0, http://www.targetscan.org). To verify this targeting relationship, luciferase reporter vectors (pmirGLO; Promega, USA) containing either the wild-type (WT) or mutant (MUT) 3’-UTR sequences of PDZK1IP1 were constructed. HCCLM3 and MHCC97H cells were seeded in 24-well plates and co-transfected with the reporter vectors along with either miR-4512 mimic or mimic negative control (NC) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). After 48 h of incubation, the relative luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). The firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity to determine the binding efficiency.

Statistical analysis

Statistical processing was systematically conducted using SPSS version 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0. The normality of continuous variables was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was assessed by Levene’s test. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using the Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (interquartile range) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis H test.

Categorical data regarding clinicopathological traits were analyzed utilizing the Pearson chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test (when expected frequencies were < 5). The Spearman rank correlation coefficient was used to assess the association between serum miR-4512 and PDZK1IP1 levels. For survival analysis, Overall Survival (OS) and Disease-Free Survival (DFS) curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared via the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were constructed to identify independent prognostic factors.

The diagnostic efficacy of biomarkers was quantified via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, with the optimal cut-off values determined by maximizing the Youden index. To evaluate the efficacy of combined detection, a multivariate logistic regression model was employed to construct the joint diagnostic panel and adjust for potential clinical confounders. All statistical analyses were two-sided, with significance defined at P < 0.05.

miR-4512 is downregulated in HCC and serves as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker

As illustrated in Fig. 1A, serum miR-4512 levels were significantly decreased in HCC patients compared with cirrhotic controls (P < 0.001). Consistently, miR-4512 expression was also remarkably downregulated in HCC cell lines (HCCLM3 and MHCC97H) relative to the normal hepatocyte cell line THLE-2 (P < 0.01, Fig. 1B). Based on these expression profiles, clinicopathological analysis revealed that low serum miR-4512 expression was significantly associated with larger tumor size (P = 0.032) and advanced TNM stage (P = 0.009, Table 2). Importantly, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated that patients with high miR-4512 expression had significantly better overall survival (OS, P = 0.004, Fig. 1C) and disease-free survival (DFS, P < 0.001, Fig. 1D) than those with low expression. Subsequent Cox proportional hazards regression analysis further identified low miR-4512 expression as an independent risk factor for poor OS (Table 3). To evaluate its diagnostic value, ROC curve analysis demonstrated that serum miR-4512 alone could effectively distinguish HCC patients from cirrhotic controls with an AUC of 0.860 (Fig. 1E). Notably, the combined detection of miR-4512 and AFP substantially improved the diagnostic performance, achieving a superior AUC of 0.970 (Fig. 1F).

miR-4512 inhibits proliferation, migration, and invasion in MHCC97H and HCCLM3 cells

Gain- and loss-of-function assays were performed to explore the biological role of miR-4512. Transfection with miR-4512 mimic significantly upregulated its expression, while miR-4512 inhibitor markedly reduced it (P < 0.01, Fig. 2A). CCK-8 assays showed that overexpression of miR-4512 significantly suppressed cell proliferation at 24, 48, and 72 h in both cell lines (P < 0.001, Fig. 2B and C), whereas inhibition exerted the opposite effect. Transwell assays further revealed that ectopic miR-4512 expression dramatically inhibited migration and invasion, whereas miR-4512 knockdown enhanced these phenotypes (P < 0.01, Fig. 2D and E).

miR-4512 directly targets and negatively regulates PDZK1IP1 in MHCC97H and HCCLM3 cells

In silico analysis revealed a putative binding motif for miR-4512 within the 3’UTR of PDZK1IP1 (Fig. 3A). Dual-luciferase reporter assays showed that exogenous miR-4512 expression markedly suppressed the luciferase activity of the PDZK1IP1-WT reporter (P < 0.01), whereas the bioluminescence of the MUT-type construct remained unaffected (P > 0.05, Fig. 3B and C). In line with this relationship, overexpression of miR-4512 significantly downregulated PDZK1IP1 expression at both mRNA and protein levels, while inhibition of miR-4512 upregulated PDZK1IP1 expression (P < 0.05, Fig. 3D and E).

PDZK1IP1 is upregulated in HCC and exhibits remarkable diagnostic and prognostic value

As shown in Fig. 4A, serum PDZK1IP1 expression was significantly elevated in HCC patients compared with cirrhotic controls (P < 0.001). Spearman correlation analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between the serum levels of miR-4512 and PDZK1IP1 in HCC patients (r = -0.704, P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). At the cellular level, PDZK1IP1 expression was also markedly higher in HCC cell lines than in normal hepatocytes (P < 0.001, Fig. 4C). Clinically, high PDZK1IP1 expression was significantly correlated with larger tumor size and advanced TNM stage (Table 2). Prognostically, patients with high PDZK1IP1 expression exhibited significantly worse OS (P = 0.006, Fig. 4D) and DFS (P = 0.001, Fig. 4E). Cox regression verified that PDZK1IP1 overexpression served as an independent prognostic factor for OS (Table 3). For diagnostic potential, ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.899 for PDZK1IP1 alone (Fig. 4F). To maximize diagnostic efficacy, multi-indicator combinations were constructed. The AUC values for PDZK1IP1 combined with AFP (Fig. 4G), miR-4512 (Fig. 4H), and the triple combination (Fig. 4I) reached 0.985, 0.939, and an outstanding 0.988, respectively. To further validate their independent diagnostic value and correct for potential confounding clinical factors, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed. As detailed in Supplementary Table 2, both miR-4512 (OR = 0.310, P < 0.001) and PDZK1IP1 (OR = 2.331, P = 0.004) retained their significance as independent diagnostic predictors for HCC, even after adjusting for age, sex, AST, ALT, and TBIL.

Silencing of PDZK1IP1 represses proliferation, migration, and invasion in MHCC97H and HCCLM3 cells

Three siRNAs targeting PDZK1IP1 were constructed, and qPCR confirmed their significant knockdown efficiency (P < 0.001, Fig. 5A), with si-300 being the most effective. CCK-8 assays demonstrated that PDZK1IP1 silencing significantly suppressed proliferation at 24, 48, and 72 h (P < 0.001, Fig. 5B and C). Transwell assays further confirmed that silencing PDZK1IP1 markedly attenuated the migratory and invasive capacities (P < 0.01, Fig. 5D and E).

miR-4512 suppresses HCC progression by inhibiting the PDZK1IP1-mediated Akt/mTOR and glycolysis pathways

Rescue experiments were performed to validate the downstream signaling cascades. qRT-PCR confirmed the successful restoration of PDZK1IP1 expression in the miR-4512-mimic + OE-PDZK1IP1 group (P < 0.001, Fig. 6A). CCK-8 assays demonstrated that the suppressive effect of miR-4512 overexpression on cell proliferation was significantly reversed by OE-PDZK1IP1 (P < 0.01, Fig. 6B). Critically, Western blot analysis revealed that miR-4512 overexpression significantly suppressed the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway and glycolysis, while co-transfection with OE-PDZK1IP1 robustly reversed these inhibitory effects (Fig. 6C). In concordance with these molecular alterations, Transwell assays confirmed that the suppressive effects of miR-4512 on cell migration (Fig. 6D) and invasion (Fig. 6E) were significantly attenuated following PDZK1IP1 restoration.

Discussion

HCC is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide. Cirrhosis patients have a 2–5% annual risk of developing HCC [27], yet most are diagnosed at advanced stages due to asymptomatic early progression [28], resulting in a 5-year survival rate below 15% [29]. Current diagnostic tools have significant limitations: serum AFP misses 30–40% of early HCC [30] and yields false positives in cirrhosis [31]; imaging cannot reliably distinguish regenerative nodules from small HCC lesions [32]. These challenges often delay diagnosis until curative options are no longer feasible, highlighting an urgent need for accurate biomarkers to enable early detection and improve clinical management.

Here, we found that miR-4512 was significantly downregulated while PDZK1IP1 was upregulated in HCC patient sera and cell lines, with their expression showing a negative correlation. Both markers demonstrated strong diagnostic performance (sensitivity and specificity > 77%), outperforming traditional AFP. The opposite expression trends of miR-4512 and PDZK1IP1 in HCC suggest a potential complementary diagnostic value. The diagnostic performance of miR-4512 (AUC 0.860) and PDZK1IP1 (AUC 0.899) suggests that both are promising individual biomarkers. Compared to previously reported diagnostic panels or single markers, our triple combination model (miR-4512 + PDZK1IP1 + AFP) yielded a highly competitive AUC of 0.988. This superior diagnostic efficacy highlights the substantial clinical translation potential of combining epigenetic, membrane protein, and classical biomarkers for HCC screening.

To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we verified for the first time that miR-4512 directly targets the 3’UTR of PDZK1IP1. While previous studies identified miRNA regulatory axes in other cancers [33–35], our findings establish the miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis as a critical tumor-suppressive network in HCC. PDZK1IP1 (MAP17) is known to correlate with advanced stages in various solid tumors [36–39]. Consistently, our in vitro models demonstrated that silencing PDZK1IP1 significantly inhibited HCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, paralleling known oncogenes like KIF2C and SNHG14 [40, 41]. Crucially, our rescue experiments and Western blot analyses revealed that this axis profoundly regulates the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway and the Warburg effect. Overexpression of PDZK1IP1 robustly reversed the miR-4512-induced suppression of crucial pathway nodes (p-AKT, p-mTOR, GLUT1, and LDHA). This provides solid protein-level evidence that miR-4512 suppresses HCC metabolic reprogramming and progression by targeting PDZK1IP1.

Although this study reveals the clinical and biological significance of the miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective, single-center design with a relatively limited sample size necessitates future large-scale, multi-center validation to prevent statistical overfitting and confirm generalizability. Second, our cohort was overwhelmingly HBV-dominant (> 80%), precluding meaningful subgroup analyses stratified by diverse etiologies (e.g., HCV, alcoholic cirrhosis) or early-stage HCC (TNM stage I/II). Third, as miRNAs typically possess multiple targets, we cannot entirely exclude the potential contribution of other downstream genes regulated by miR-4512 in hepatocarcinogenesis. Finally, while comprehensive in vitro cellular and molecular assays were conducted, the current study lacks in vivo animal models (such as nude mouse xenografts) and matched tumor tissue immunohistochemistry. This limits the direct evaluation of tumor growth and spatial protein expression in a physiological in vivo microenvironment. Future prospective studies integrating in vivo models and multi-center clinical trials are imperative to further validate these findings.

In conclusion, our study identifies the miR-4512/PDZK1IP1 axis as a novel regulatory pathway in HCC. miR-4512 is significantly downregulated in HCC and suppresses tumor progression by inhibiting the PDZK1IP1-mediated Akt/mTOR and glycolysis pathways. Clinically, both molecules represent robust, independent diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers. These findings provide profound insights into hepatocarcinogenesis and offer promising molecular targets for the early detection and comprehensive management of HCC.

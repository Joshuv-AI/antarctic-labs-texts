Comparative hematological and serum biochemical profiling of grassland Xichun and Xilingol horses
Authors: Tugeqin Bou, Wenqi Ding, Wendian Gong, Zheng Li, Xiaoyuan Shi, Manglai Dugarjaviin, Dongyi Bai, Lihua Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Grassland Xichun horse is an emerging crossbred population developed from Xilingol mares and Thoroughbred stallions in Inner Mongolia, China, yet its blood physiological profile has not been systematically characterized. In this study, we compared 32 hematological and serum biochemical parameters between 177 Grassland Xichun horses and 35 Xilingol horses maintained under the same extensive grazing conditions. Following Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, 17 of 32 parameters differed significantly between breeds. Grassland Xichun horses showed markedly higher RBC count (9.145 vs 7.905 × 10¹²/L), hemoglobin (155.41 vs 132.51 g/L), hematocrit (45.95% vs 40.29%), and MCHC, with multiple parameters reaching the large-effect threshold (absolute effect size > 0.8). Compared with the Xilingol maternal breed, the Thoroughbred-sired Grassland Xichun population was associated with higher oxygen-carrying indices. Conversely, LDH showed the largest effect size among all parameters (effect size = −2.117), and AST and CK were also lower in Grassland Xichun horses, suggesting differences in basal muscle enzyme profiles and aerobic metabolic characteristics between breeds. Grassland Xichun horses additionally exhibited higher blood glucose and lower total cholesterol, indicating distinct metabolic patterns. Xilingol horses displayed elevated monocyte counts, MPV, UREA, and GGT, which may reflect differences in immune–metabolic status and physiological adaptation associated with long-term environmental and management backgrounds rather than discrete functional phenotypes. Principal component analysis revealed partial breed separation reflecting shared maternal ancestry, and Fisher’s linear discriminant analysis achieved 97.6% leave-one-out cross-validation accuracy. This study characterizes the blood physiological profile of Grassland Xichun horses, provides population-level distribution data for clinical interpretation, and supports future integration of hematological phenotypes into breeding evaluation and physiological monitoring programs.

Introduction

Horses (Equus caballus) have served as indispensable companions to pastoral societies across the Eurasian steppe for millennia. In the vast grasslands of Inner Mongolia, China, Mongolian horses evolved under extreme continental climate conditions characterized by harsh winters, hot summers, and limited forage resources, acquiring remarkable hardiness, disease resistance, and endurance adaptations in the process (Pan et al., 2022). While these traits ensure survival under extensive grazing management, the comparatively modest speed and body size of Mongolian horses have motivated sustained crossbreeding efforts aimed at developing improved breeds that combine the environmental resilience of native mares with the athletic capacity of imported sires.

The Xilingol horse is a dual-purpose riding-and-draft breed developed in the Xilingol League of Inner Mongolia, China. Beginning in the 1950s, local Mongolian mares were systematically crossed with stallions of Soviet Thoroughbred (Budyonny), Don, and Kabardin. Subsequent interbreeding among selected F2 progeny and closed-herd breeding produced a phenotypically uniform population that was officially recognized by the People’s Government of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region in 1987. The breed is characterized by a robust constitution, well-developed chest, and strong limbs, retaining the coarse-forage tolerance, disease resistance, and cold hardiness of its Mongolian maternal ancestry while surpassing Mongolian horses in body size and work capacity (Pan et al., 2022). More recently, breeders have introduced Thoroughbred stallions onto Xilingol mare foundations to produce an emerging population termed the Grassland Xichun horse, which aims to combine the environmental resilience of the Xilingol dam line with the superior speed and athletic capacity of the Thoroughbred sire line. After more than three decades of selective breeding, this population has attained sufficient numbers to warrant systematic physiological characterization.

Blood-based phenotyping is of direct practical relevance to equine breeding programs and veterinary clinical monitoring because hematological and biochemical parameters can be obtained through minimally invasive sampling and provide integrated information on erythropoietic, immune, metabolic, and hepato-renal function throughout the lifespan of an animal. Compared with invasive procedures such as muscle biopsy, blood-based assessments offer a practical approach for large-scale physiological evaluation in horse populations (Deniz et al., 2024; Cappelli et al., 2008). In equine breeding programs, such parameters have proven particularly informative for evaluating how crossbreeding alters physiological function. Thoroughbred horses are well known to possess elevated red blood cell (RBC) counts, hemoglobin (Hb) concentration, and hematocrit (Hct), traits that underpin their exceptional oxygen delivery capacity during high-intensity exercise (Eaton et al., 1995; Mairbäurl, 2013). Breed-specific differences in muscle metabolism enzymes, energy substrate utilization, and immune cell profiles have also been documented across diverse equine populations (Schaefer et al., 2024; Burlikowska et al., 2015; Kedzierski and Bergero, 2006). Whether the Grassland Xichun horse has inherited these performance-associated hematological traits from its Thoroughbred sire line while retaining adaptive characteristics of the Xilingol dam line is directly relevant to evaluating the success of the breeding program. The mechanisms underlying breed differences in erythrocyte indices include erythropoietin (EPO)-driven erythropoiesis and the unique capacity of the equine spleen to store and rapidly mobilize a substantial reserve of red blood cells into the systemic circulation (Mairbäurl, 2013; Dahlgren et al., 2021). Beyond the erythron, monocyte-lineage cells occupy a central position in equine innate immune defense, and breed-dependent variation in monocyte counts and phenotype may reflect divergent evolutionary pressures imposed by distinct pathogen environments (Karagianni et al., 2021; Witkowska-Piłaszewicz et al., 2024).

Despite the growing recognition of blood parameters as useful phenotypic markers for breed characterization, comparative hematological studies between newly developed crossbred populations and their parental breeds remain scarce, particularly for Chinese horse breeds adapted to steppe environments. To date, no study has systematically evaluated whether this second-step crossbreeding has shifted the resting blood phenotype toward greater speed capacity while preserving the adaptive traits inherited from the Xilingol maternal line, leaving both the physiological consequences of this breeding strategy and the corresponding breed-specific blood reference data undefined. Furthermore, population-based surveys in European and African horse populations have highlighted the necessity of breed-specific hematological reference intervals, since universal norms derived from single-breed cohorts may systematically misclassify physiologically normal individuals when applied to different populations (Buendia et al., 2021; Sawesi et al., 2023).

Beyond simple group comparisons, multivariate statistical approaches offer additional power for breed characterization. Principal component analysis (PCA) can reveal the overall structure of phenotypic variation across multiple parameters simultaneously, while discriminant analysis can quantify the classification accuracy achievable from blood profiles alone—an approach that may complement genomic methods for breed identification (Manzoori et al., 2023). Combining multivariate analyses with effect-size estimation provides a more comprehensive assessment of inter-breed physiological divergence than univariate testing alone. Therefore, this study aimed to characterize and compare 32 hematological and serum biochemical parameters between Grassland Xichun and Xilingol horses under matched extensive grazing conditions, to quantify the magnitude of breed differences using complementary parametric and nonparametric effect sizes with bootstrap confidence intervals, and to evaluate the discriminatory power of blood profiles for breed identification using PCA and Fisher's linear discriminant analysis with repeated cross-validation. The results are expected to provide a hematological basis for ongoing breed evaluation, facilitate the identification of physiologically favorable phenotypes for breeding selection, and inform marker-assisted selection strategies for the continued genetic improvement of this emerging breed.

Materials and methods

Animals

A total of 212 clinically healthy horses were enrolled in this study, comprising 177 Grassland Xichun horses and 35 Xilingol horses. All animals were sourced from a single stud farm in the Xilingol League, Inner Mongolia, China. All horses were stallions of 5 years of age and were maintained under the same extensive grazing management system, with free access to native grassland forage and water on the farm pastures; no concentrate supplementation or formal exercise-training program was applied, and the horses were classified as light, untrained workload. Sampling was conducted in spring, prior to the start of the Nadam racing season. Blood was collected after an overnight interval without access to concentrate, with free access to pasture forage and water retained. All horses were assessed for general health status, including vital signs, appetite, and the absence of visible signs of injury or illness, prior to blood collection, and health status was confirmed by experienced veterinarians. Animal care and experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Management and Ethics Committee of the College of Animal Science at Inner Mongolia Agricultural University (Permit No. NND2023085).

Blood sample collection and laboratory analysis

All blood samples were collected by the same trained veterinarian from the left jugular vein under standing physical restraint, between 06:00 and 09:00 local time, before morning grazing and after an overnight (≥12 h) interval without concentrate feeding (animals had free access to grassland forage and water). For each horse, 2 mL of whole blood was drawn into EDTA-K2 tubes for hematology and 5 mL into serum separator tubes for biochemistry. Samples were transported in insulated containers at 4°C and processed within 4 h of collection; serum was separated by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 10 min at 4°C. Hemolysis index was assessed visually by the same operator using a reference chart, and samples with frank or moderate hemolysis were excluded from the analytical workflow.

Hematological parameters, including white blood cell (WBC) count and differential (lymphocyte, monocyte, and granulocyte counts and percentages), RBC count, Hb concentration, Hct, mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC), red cell distribution width (RDW), platelet count (PLT), mean platelet volume (MPV), platelet distribution width (PDW), and plateletcrit (PCT) were determined on EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood using a Mindray BC-2800Vet automated veterinary hematology analyzer (Mindray Bio-Medical Electronics Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) with manufacturer-supplied diluent, lyse and rinse reagents. Serum biochemical parameters, including glucose (GLU), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), urea (UREA), creatinine (CREA), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase (CK), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) were measured on pre-separated serum using a Seamaty SMT-120VP automated veterinary biochemistry analyzer (Chengdu Seamaty Technology Co., Ltd., Chengdu, China), which performs photometric measurements at eight wavelengths (340, 405, 450, 505, 546, 600, 630 and 850 nm) using single-use multi-parameter reagent discs with a sample volume of approximately 100 μL per disc; reagent-disc identification and intelligent real-time quality-control checks were carried out automatically by the instrument. Instrument-flagged high (H) and low (L) abnormality markers were recorded for subsequent frequency analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.3.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with packages rstatix v0.7.2 (Shapiro-Wilk test, Levene's test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction), effsize v0.8.1 (effect size estimation), MASS v7.3-60 (Fisher's linear discriminant analysis), FactoMineR v2.9 (principal component analysis), ggplot2 v3.5.0 (boxplots, violin plots, volcano plots, and correlation heatmaps), and caret v6.0-94 (leave-one-out cross-validation). Data are presented as means ± standard deviation (SD). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test for each parameter within each group. For parameters satisfying normality in both groups with equal variances, Student’s t-test was applied; where variances were unequal, Welch’s t-test was used. Parameters violating normality assumptions in either group were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. To control for multiple testing across 32 simultaneous comparisons, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method, and adjusted P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Effect sizes were quantified according to data distribution. For variables satisfying normality assumptions, standardized mean differences were estimated using effect size. For variables violating normality assumptions, Cliff’s delta (δ) was additionally calculated as a robust nonparametric effect-size measure. Effect sizes were interpreted as small (|effect| > 0.2), medium (|effect| > 0.5), and large (|effect| > 0.8), where applicable. To improve estimation precision, 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) for group differences and effect-size estimates were obtained using bootstrap resampling (5000 iterations). A volcano plot was constructed with log2 fold-change (Grassland Xichun / Xilingol) on the x-axis and –log10(FDR-adjusted P) on the y-axis to visualize the direction and significance of all 32 parameters simultaneously.

Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on standardized (z-score transformed) data for all 32 parameters to explore the overall multivariate separation between the two breeds. Fisher’s linear discriminant analysis (LDA) was conducted on the same standardized dataset, and classification accuracy was evaluated using leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV). Spearman rank correlation coefficients were computed separately for each breed and displayed as heatmaps. Violin plots were generated for the 12 parameters with the largest absolute effect size values to illustrate distributional differences. Abnormality flag frequencies were tabulated to compare the proportions of H and L flags between breeds from a clinical reference-range perspective.

Results

Descriptive statistical analysis

Blood samples were collected from a total of 212 horses, comprising 177 Grassland Xichun horses and 35 Xilingol horses, and 32 hematological and biochemical parameters were determined. Data quality control included screening for missing values and verification of data consistency prior to statistical analysis. No missing values were identified in the final dataset, and all samples were retained for subsequent analyses. According to the Shapiro-Wilk normality test, only 7 of the 32 parameters—UREA, Ca, LDH, Lymph%, Mon%, Gran%, and RDW—followed a normal distribution in both groups (P > 0.05), whereas the remaining 25 parameters deviated from normality in at least one group. Coefficient of variation (CV) analysis revealed substantial individual variability in the Grassland Xichun horses for CK (CV = 96.25%) and Gran (CV = 67.13%), while the Xilingol horses exhibited notably high individual heterogeneity in GGT (CV = 63.82%). Empirical distribution summaries and quantile estimates for all hematological and serum biochemical parameters are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

With regard to energy metabolism, the GLU level in Grassland Xichun horses (4.844 ± 0.993 mmol/L) was significantly higher than that in Xilingol horses (3.410 ± 1.327 mmol/L) (FDR-adjusted P < 0.001, effect size = 1.361; 95% CI shown in Table 1), representing the largest positive effect size among all serum biochemical parameters and indicating that Grassland Xichun horses maintain a higher baseline blood glucose level. Concurrently, the TC level in Grassland Xichun horses (2.116 ± 0.425 mmol/L) was significantly lower than that in Xilingol horses (2.322 ± 0.398 mmol/L) (FDR-adjusted P = 0.024, effect size = −0.267; 95% CI shown in Table 1), suggesting potential divergence in lipid metabolism patterns between the two breeds. No significant between-group difference was observed for TG (FDR-adjusted P > 0.05).

Regarding muscle metabolism and enzyme markers, LDH exhibited the largest effect size of all 32 parameters assessed (effect size = −2.117; 95% CI shown in Table 1); the Grassland Xichun horse values (252.59 ± 82.91 U/L) were significantly lower than those of Xilingol horses (420.85 ± 58.69 U/L) (FDR-adjusted P < 0.001). AST (effect size = −0.470) and CK (effect size = −0.554) were also significantly lower in Grassland Xichun horses (FDR-adjusted P < 0.001). The concordant reduction across all three muscle metabolism markers suggests that Grassland Xichun horses exhibit lower basal muscle enzyme release at rest.

For hepatorenal function markers, GGT levels were significantly higher in Xilingol horses (26.14 ± 16.68 U/L) than in Grassland Xichun horses (14.75 ± 5.28 U/L) (FDR-adjusted P < 0.001, effect size = −0.519; 95% CI shown in Table 1), and the coefficient of variation of GGT in the Xilingol group reached 63.82%, reflecting considerable individual heterogeneity. UREA was also significantly elevated in Xilingol horses (2.842 ± 0.223 vs 2.462 ± 0.232 mmol/L; effect size = −1.649; 95% CI shown in Table 1), whereas CREA was significantly higher in Grassland Xichun horses (80.39 ± 13.84 vs 73.94 ± 18.53 μmol/L, FDR-adjusted P = 0.012). ALB was also significantly elevated in Grassland Xichun horses (FDR-adjusted P = 0.016), whereas the difference in TP approached borderline significance after FDR correction (FDR-adjusted P = 0.051).

Comparison of hematological parameters between the two breeds (Mean ± SD).

The oxygen-carrying system of Grassland Xichun horses demonstrated a marked advantage. RBC count, HGB (155.41 ± 21.56 vs 132.51 ± 19.25 g/L, effect size = 1.080; 95% CI shown in Table 2), and HCT (45.95 ± 6.47% vs 40.29 ± 5.76%, effect size = 0.889; 95% CI shown in Table 2) were all significantly higher in Grassland Xichun horses than in Xilingol horses (FDR-adjusted P < 0.001), with all effect sizes reaching the large-effect threshold (absolute effect size > 0.8). MCHC was also significantly elevated in Grassland Xichun horses (338.14 ± 15.83 vs 328.46 ± 9.08 g/L, FDR-adjusted P < 0.001, effect size = 0.648; 95% CI shown in Table 2). MCV and MCH, however, did not differ significantly between the two groups, indicating that individual red blood cell size and per-cell hemoglobin content remained largely unchanged. These findings suggest that the enhanced oxygen-carrying capacity of Grassland Xichun horses is primarily attributable to an increase in RBC number coupled with a higher population-level hemoglobin concentration rather than alterations in single-cell morphology.

Regarding white blood cell differential counts, total WBC did not differ significantly between the two breeds (FDR-adjusted P > 0.05); however, both absolute monocyte count (Mon) and monocyte percentage (Mon%) were significantly elevated in Xilingol horses (FDR-adjusted P = 0.001 and FDR-adjusted P < 0.001, respectively). Among the leukocyte subpopulations, Mon% displayed one of the largest effect sizes (95% CI shown in Table 2), with Xilingol horses (4.817 ± 0.664%) showing significantly higher values than Grassland Xichun horses (4.172 ± 0.774%). The absolute counts and percentages of lymphocytes and granulocytes did not differ significantly between the two breeds.

As for the platelet system, PLT count and PCT showed no significant differences, yet MPV was significantly higher in Xilingol horses (7.537 ± 0.738 vs 6.818 ± 0.672 fL, FDR-adjusted P < 0.001, effect size = 1.053; 95% CI shown in Table 2), and PDW was similarly elevated (FDR-adjusted P = 0.013). Given that MPV reflects platelet activation status and turnover rate, the higher MPV observed in Xilingol horses may indicate more active platelet renewal dynamics in this breed.

Correlation analysis

Spearman rank correlation coefficients were calculated for all parameter pairs within each breed and visualized as heatmaps (Fig. S2). Although the overall correlation structures of the two breeds were broadly similar, several notable differences emerged. In Grassland Xichun horses, the oxygen-carrying indicator cluster—RBC, HGB, and HCT—exhibited strong positive correlations (r > 0.7), a pattern that was also present in Xilingol horses but differed slightly in magnitude. Moderate positive correlations among muscle enzyme markers (CK, AST, and LDH) were observed in both breeds. The correlation structure among white blood cell differential parameters was consistent between the two breeds, reflecting the inherent compensatory relationships within the immune system.

Distribution characteristics of the most differentiated parameters

Violin plots were generated for the 12 parameters with the largest effect size values (Fig. S3). The distributional divergence was most striking for LDH, where the two breeds exhibited virtually no overlap. UREA and GGT also displayed a high degree of separation between breeds. The violin plots for HGB and RBC showed an overall upward shift in the distribution of Grassland Xichun horses. Notably, CK in Grassland Xichun horses displayed a pronounced right-skewed distribution with a CV of 96.25%, suggesting that a subset of individuals within this population may exhibit elevated muscle enzyme release.

Abnormal flag frequency analysis

Frequency analysis of instrument-assigned H (high) and L (low) abnormal flags was performed (Fig. S4). Among the 14 parameters for which abnormal flags were recorded, H flags for HGB and HCT occurred more frequently in Grassland Xichun horses, consistent with their higher oxygen-carrying parameter levels. RBC similarly showed a higher frequency of H flags in Grassland Xichun horses. L flags for MCHC were more prevalent in Xilingol horses. Overall, these flag distributions should be interpreted as descriptive patterns relative to predefined reference ranges rather than validation of physiological differences. Variation may also reflect physiological regulation, environmental adaptation, or transient stress-related responses.

Discussion

Oxygen-carrying phenotype

One of the most striking findings of this study is that Grassland Xichun horses exhibited significantly higher values for the three core oxygen-carrying parameters—RBC count, HGB, and HCT—compared with Xilingol horses (P < 0.001), with all effect sizes reaching the large-effect level (Effect size = 0.512, 0.615, and 0.514, respectively). A comparison of hematological profiles across Warmbloods, Thoroughbreds, and stock-type horses has shown that Thoroughbreds possess significantly higher RBC counts (8.42 ± 1.2 × 10¹²/L) coupled with lower MCV (47.3 ± 3.0 fL), reflecting a strategy employed by “hot-blooded” breeds that enhances oxygen-carrying efficiency by increasing erythrocyte numbers rather than expanding individual cell volume (Schaefer et al., 2024). In the present study, the mean RBC count of Grassland Xichun horses (9.145 × 10¹²/L) even surpassed the values reported for Thoroughbreds, whereas MCV (50.40 fL) was not significantly lower than that of Xilingol horses (51.39 fL). These data indicate that Grassland Xichun horses were associated with higher oxygen-carrying indices through increased erythrocyte number while maintaining erythrocyte morphological characteristics comparable to those of the maternal breed. However, increased oxygen-carrying indices should not be interpreted in isolation, as enhanced aerobic demand in horses is accompanied by coordinated physiological regulation involving erythropoietic responses, oxygen transport dynamics, and maintenance of oxidant–antioxidant balance. Previous studies have shown that exercise and increased metabolic demand in horses are associated with changes in oxidative status and activation of antioxidant mechanisms that contribute to maintaining physiological homeostasis (Arfuso et al., 2022), and erythropoietic regulation and splenic erythrocyte mobilization contribute to variation in oxygen-carrying capacity among horse breeds (Mairbäurl, 2013, Dahlgren et al., 2021, Khummuang et al., 2020). These mechanisms may partly explain the higher erythrocyte indices observed in Grassland Xichun horses, although they were not directly evaluated in the present study.

MCHC, an indicator of hemoglobin concentration within erythrocytes, was also significantly elevated in Grassland Xichun horses (338.14 vs 328.46 g/L), further demonstrating that the enhanced blood oxygen transport in this breed operates through two complementary dimensions: increased erythrocyte quantity and greater hemoglobin density per unit volume of red blood cells. MCHC variation has similarly been highlighted as a reflection of metabolic adaptation to environmental conditions in a three-year longitudinal study of climate effects on equine blood parameters (Deniz et al., 2024). Erythrocyte membrane integrity is an important companion trait in performance breeds: a recent study in endurance horses documented significant hemolytic stress during sustained exercise with corresponding changes in MCHC and related erythrocyte indices (Pakula et al., 2023). The elevated MCHC observed in Grassland Xichun horses may reflect differences in erythrocyte physiological status; however, the present study did not evaluate erythrocyte membrane stability, oxidative status, or exercise responses, and therefore functional interpretation should be made cautiously.

Muscle-enzyme phenotype

An intriguing observation is that all three muscle metabolic enzyme markers—LDH, AST, and CK—were significantly lower in Grassland Xichun horses compared with Xilingol horses, with LDH exhibiting the largest effect size among all 32 parameters (effect size = −2.117). Unexpectedly, one might anticipate that Grassland Xichun horses, carrying Thoroughbred bloodlines, would display elevated muscle-enzyme levels; however, the opposite pattern was observed and warrants explanation from multiple perspectives. Resting muscle enzyme concentrations do not directly reflect exercise capacity; rather, they are more closely associated with basal sarcolemmal permeability, muscle fiber type composition, and habitual physical activity levels. A comparison of exercise-related biochemical responses in Thoroughbreds and Arabian horses revealed that Arabian horses exhibited greater CK and AST elevations under equivalent exercise intensity, indicating breed-specific differences in membrane stability and fiber type composition (Kedzierski and Bergero, 2006).

As a dual-purpose breed used for both riding and draft work, the relatively higher basal LDH and AST levels in Xilingol horses may be related to the sustained physical workload they routinely endure. Evidence suggests that horses engaged in endurance-type continuous exercise tend to exhibit elevated resting muscle enzyme levels (Maśko et al., 2021). Significant breed-dependent variation in muscle enzymes (AST and CK) and UREA among different pony types has also been reported, underscoring the need for caution against the indiscriminate application of universal reference ranges across breeds (Witkowska-Piłaszewicz et al., 2021). The considerably lower CV for CK in Xilingol horses (43.28%) compared with Grassland Xichun horses (96.25%) further suggests fundamental differences in the population-level distribution patterns of muscle enzymes between the two breeds. Collectively, the lower resting muscle enzyme burden in Grassland Xichun horses suggests differences in basal muscle physiological status between breeds, although the underlying mechanisms remain to be clarified.

Energy substrate use and hepatorenal markers

The significantly higher GLU level (Effect size = 1.361) and lower TC level (Effect size = −0.490) in Grassland Xichun horses reveal a divergence in energy substrate utilization strategies between the two breeds. As a prototypical speed-oriented breed, the Thoroughbred relies predominantly on glycogen and glucose as primary fuels for energy production (Eaton et al., 1995), whereas Mongolian-type breeds, having evolved as endurance specialists, have developed a metabolic phenotype more dependent on fatty acid oxidation over the course of long-term evolutionary selection (Pan et al., 2022). Grassland Xichun horses appear to retain certain endurance-adapted metabolic features while exhibiting elevated blood glucose levels, possibly reflecting enhanced glycolytic capacity associated with Thoroughbred introgression—a metabolic profile that may be compatible with the breeding objective of combining speed with stamina. Such a metabolic profile may reflect differences in energy-substrate utilization patterns between breeds. However, direct assessment of metabolic flexibility would require dedicated physiological and molecular investigations. In horses, exercise performance and adaptation are increasingly understood as being supported by coordinated regulation of mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and substrate utilization rather than dependence on a single dominant energy pathway (Latham et al., 2022).

The significantly higher UREA level in Xilingol horses (Effect size = −1.649) also contributes to our understanding of the energy metabolism divergence between the two breeds. UREA represents the end product of protein catabolism, and its elevation under normal renal function typically reflects increased protein intake or enhanced proteolysis. Under pastoral grazing conditions, the higher UREA level in Xilingol horses may reflect differences in protein metabolism and amino-acid utilization between breeds, a strategy consistent with the documented use of branched-chain amino acids as supplementary energy substrates in endurance-adapted breeds during sustained exercise. Significant variation in UREA and ALB levels has similarly been observed in Noma horses under different management regimes (Ono et al., 2019). Population surveys of free-ranging horses on native pastures further indicate that dietary protein intake from legume-rich steppe grasses can substantially elevate circulating urea concentrations compared with horses on standardized rations (Sawesi et al., 2023). The higher UREA in Xilingol horses may therefore partly reflect their longer-term adaptation to the protein composition of native Inner Mongolian grassland vegetation, in addition to the metabolic differences attributable to breed physiology.

GGT was significantly elevated in Xilingol horses (Effect size = −1.377), with a CV as high as 63.82%, making it the parameter with the third-largest effect size in this study. As an enzyme predominantly distributed in biliary epithelial cells, GGT elevation is typically associated with cholestasis, bile duct injury, or compensatory upregulation of hepatic detoxification capacity. Several factors may account for the higher GGT levels in Xilingol horses. Having adapted to extensive grazing on natural pastures over generations, Xilingol horses are chronically exposed to various phytotoxins present in native grasses, which may induce hepatic GGT expression as an adaptive detoxification response (Tkaczenko et al., 2024). Environmental factors and management conditions have been shown to exert significant effects on biochemical enzyme parameters in seasonal studies of equine breeds (Aragona et al., 2024). The high CV of GGT in the Xilingol group further suggests that this parameter is particularly sensitive to individual variation in feeding environment. Systematic characterization of hepatic enzyme profiles in horses has confirmed that GGT is the most sensitive and specific single biomarker of hepatocellular membrane damage and biliary injury in equids, with physiological ranges strongly influenced by breed, age, and environmental exposure (Satué et al., 2022, Satué et al., 2023). Moreover, individual variation in GGT can be substantial even within a clinically healthy horse population (Mann et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2022), consistent with the high CV (63.82%) observed in the Xilingol group in the present study. The marked individual heterogeneity in GGT among Xilingol horses may therefore reflect genuine between-individual variation in physiological and environmental influences on hepatic enzyme activity rather than overt hepatic pathology.

Immune-platelet profile

Among the leukocyte differential parameters, total WBC did not differ significantly between breeds, yet both absolute and relative monocyte counts were significantly elevated in Xilingol horses. Breed-dependent variation in white blood cell subpopulation distributions has been similarly reported among Warmbloods, Thoroughbreds, and stock-type horses (Schaefer et al., 2024). Monocytes serve as key components of the innate immune system, playing critical roles in anti-infection defense and tissue repair. The higher monocyte levels in Xilingol horses may represent an immune adaptation shaped by long-term exposure to diverse environmental pathogens under free-ranging grassland conditions. Consistent with this interpretation, positive selection signatures associated with immune function have been identified in Chinese Mongolian horse populations through genomic analysis, underscoring the importance of immune-related selective pressures in the evolution of Mongolian-type breeds (Han et al., 2023). Karagianni provided a comprehensive characterization of the equine mononuclear phagocyte system, demonstrating that equine monocytes and tissue macrophages are critical mediators of innate immune surveillance against the respiratory pathogens, bacteria, and parasites commonly encountered under free-range grassland conditions; their work established that circulating monocyte numbers reflect the balance between bone marrow production, tissue recruitment, and peripheral turnover, all of which may be chronically upregulated in horses under high pathogen exposure (Karagianni et al., 2021). Witkowska-Piłaszewicz further demonstrated that monocyte counts and activation state are regulated by both exercise and breed background, with horses possessing greater endurance capacity showing more pronounced monocyte mobilization responses to physiological challenge (Witkowska-Piłaszewicz et al., 2024). The higher baseline monocyte counts in Xilingol horses, shaped by long-term adaptation to the Inner Mongolian grassland environment, may therefore represent a constitutive immune preparedness advantageous for survival under extensive management.

MPV was significantly elevated in Xilingol horses (Effect size = −1.053), ranking as the parameter with the fifth-largest effect size. A higher mean platelet volume is generally associated with more active platelet turnover and enhanced megakaryopoietic activity in the bone marrow. Together with the concurrent elevation of PDW in Xilingol horses, these findings suggest that the two breeds may differ in their platelet production and renewal kinetics. This observation has rarely been reported in previous equine breed comparison studies and warrants further investigation to elucidate its functional significance. Ehrmann characterized platelet biology in equine patients with systemic inflammation and demonstrated that MPV elevation is closely associated with thrombopoietin-driven megakaryopoiesis and accelerated platelet turnover in response to inflammatory signals, establishing MPV as a dynamic biomarker of platelet production kinetics rather than merely a morphological measurement (Ehrmann et al., 2021). In the present context, the significantly higher MPV in Xilingol horses may therefore indicate greater baseline platelet activation and turnover, potentially reflecting low-level systemic inflammatory signaling arising from their grassland environment and habitual physical workload—an interpretation consistent with the concurrent elevation of Mon% in the same breed, since both elevated monocyte counts and increased platelet turnover are hallmarks of sustained innate immune engagement.

Multivariate discrimination

PCA revealed partial separation with incomplete segregation between the two breeds in the multidimensional blood parameter space, a pattern highly consistent with their genetic relationship: Grassland Xichun horses, derived from a Xilingol maternal foundation with Thoroughbred sire introgression, show a phenotypic pattern compatible with inheritance of athletic features while retaining the adaptive characteristics of their maternal breed. The modest cumulative variance explained by the first two principal components (28.2%) indicates that breed differences are distributed across multiple dimensions, a finding corroborated by the observation that 17 of 32 parameters showed significant between-group differences. This pattern of partial overlap is also consistent with population-level hematological surveys showing that breeds sharing common ancestry exhibit overlapping but statistically distinct reference intervals (Buendia et al., 2021), and that geographic isolation combined with distinct management practices generates sustained divergence in blood parameter distributions even among closely related equine populations (Sawesi et al., 2023). Importantly, the consistency between the univariate parameter-level differences and the multivariate PCA structure indicates that the breed signal is not driven by any single outlier parameter but is distributed across multiple physiological axes — a level of internal consistency that strengthens the inference drawn from each individual finding.

The high classification performance of Fisher’s discriminant analysis remained stable across both LOOCV and repeated cross-validation procedures, supporting the robustness of blood phenotypic profiling for breed discrimination despite the unequal sample sizes. Approximately 93% classification accuracy across 37 breeds has been reported using deep neural network-based analysis of genetic markers (Manzoori et al., 2023). The fact that a 97.6% binary classification accuracy was achieved in the present study using only 32 blood phenotypic variables highlights the substantial potential of hematological and biochemical profiling for breed identification in newly developed populations. Notably, the variables contributing most strongly to the discriminant function (Lymph% and Gran%) did not reach significance in univariate analyses, yet they provided critical breed-discriminating information through their multivariate joint effects—underscoring the indispensability of multivariate approaches for comprehensive breed characterization. This level of classification performance compares favorably with blood chemistry discrimination studies in other domestic animal species and underscores the generalizability of multivariate blood profiling as a breed characterization tool. Establishment of breed-specific reference intervals, as emphasized in systematic surveys of horse populations (Buendia et al., 2021, Sawesi et al., 2023), would further increase the clinical utility of blood parameter panels for individual-level breed assessment and health monitoring.

An integrative analysis of the differential patterns across all 32 blood parameters positions Grassland Xichun horses as intermediate along the speed–endurance physiological spectrum, with a clear tendency toward the speed end. On the one hand, the markedly enhanced oxygen-carrying system (RBC↑, HGB↑, HCT↑, MCHC↑) and elevated blood glucose (GLU↑) approximate the physiological profile of Thoroughbreds, providing the material basis for speed performance. On the other hand, total WBC, lymphocyte, and granulocyte counts did not differ significantly from those of Xilingol horses, and mineral metabolism indicators such as Ca and P remained stable, indicating that crossbreeding to enhance athletic performance has not compromised baseline immune function or mineral metabolic homeostasis.

From a breeding perspective, these results provide hematological and physiological validation for the breeding objective of developing Grassland Xichun horses as a breed combining both speed and endurance. Following the introduction of Thoroughbred sire bloodlines, Grassland Xichun progeny showed higher oxygen-carrying indices and fasting glucose levels than the Xilingol maternal breed, while parameters reflecting fundamental physiological functions remained comparable between the two populations. These complementary phenotypic patterns are consistent with the breeding history of more than three decades of sustained selection, and the resulting population now displays a distinctive blood-physiological profile relative to the Xilingol maternal breed. Future research integrating marker-assisted selection and genomic analyses (Bazvand et al., 2024) could further elucidate the genetic basis underlying these hematological phenotypic differences and incorporate key blood parameters into breeding criteria to accelerate genetic progress.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically compare the hematological and serum biochemical profiles of the Grassland Xichun horse with those of its Xilingol maternal breed under matched extensive grazing conditions. Grassland Xichun horses showed higher oxygen-carrying indices (RBC↑, HGB↑, HCT↑, MCHC↑), lower resting muscle-enzyme levels (LDH↓, AST↓, CK↓), and elevated blood glucose concentrations, a profile that may render this breed better suited to speed-oriented competitions requiring short-term energy supply. Conversely, Xilingol horses exhibited higher monocyte counts, platelet volume indices, UREA, and GGT, reflecting endurance-adapted metabolic and immune characteristics shaped by their grassland environment. Multivariate analyses further demonstrated partial breed separation by PCA and a 97.6% LOOCV classification accuracy by Fisher’s discriminant analysis, confirming that blood phenotypic profiling is a powerful tool for breed identification. These findings provide hematological and biochemical validation for the Grassland Xichun breeding program, establish breed-specific reference values essential for health monitoring, and offer a framework for incorporating key blood phenotypes into marker-assisted selection to accelerate the continued genetic improvement of this emerging breed.

Limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged so that the findings are interpreted with appropriate caution. The sample size was substantially unbalanced (177 Grassland Xichun vs 35 Xilingol horses), which may inflate the variance of estimates in the smaller class and limit the precision of Xilingol-specific descriptive statistics. The study is cross-sectional and observational; it can therefore document associations between breed and blood parameters but cannot demonstrate causality, including the central question of whether the observed differences are mechanistically attributable to Thoroughbred introgression rather than to non-genetic factors. No genomic or parentage data quantifying the actual proportion of Thoroughbred ancestry in individual Grassland Xichun horses are reported here; genomic validation of the inferred admixture pattern will be essential in future work.

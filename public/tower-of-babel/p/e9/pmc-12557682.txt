Thromboelastography and interleukin-6 predict postoperative negative remodeling of descending aorta in patients with type A aortic dissection
Authors: Qingsong Wu, Yue Shen, Xinfan Lin, Siying Luo, Xiaolong Ye, Linyan Chen, Liangwan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNegative remodeling (NR) is common in postoperative residual aortic dissection (AD) in patients with type A aortic dissection (AAD); however, reliable predictors for its assessment remain lacking. This study aimed to investigate the clinical value of thromboelastography (TEG) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in predicting postoperative NR in patients with AAD.MethodsWe analyzed patients who underwent total aortic arch replacement surgery for AAD between January 2020 and December 2021, and were monitored for 2 years postoperatively. All relevant data were collected and analyzed.ResultsA total of 208 patients with AAD were included. The incidence of NR (group NR) in residual AD was 24.0% (n=50) with higher follow-up mortality (8.0% vs. 1.3%, P=0.01) and reoperation rate (10% vs. 1.9%, P=0.001). The NR group had significantly higher IL-6 levels (P<0.001) than the non-NR group. The TEG parameters showed that the reaction time (R-time) and kinetic time (K-time) of the NR group were longer (P<0.001), clot angle (P<0.001) and maximum clot firmness (P=0.04) were lower in the NR group than in the non-NR group. Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated significant positive correlations between IL-6, R-time, and K-time and the occurrence of NR (r=0.348, 0.371, and 0.371, respectively, P<0.001), whereas clot angle and maximum clot firmness showed negative correlations (r=−0.409 and −0.146, respectively, P<0.05). Multivariate regression analysis revealed that elevated IL-6 levels [odds ratio (OR) =3.910; P<0.001], R-time (OR =2.401; P=0.03), and K-time (OR =2.417; P=0.03) were risk factors for NR in residual AD. The receiver operating characteristic curve analysis showed that the cutoff points for R- and K-times combined with IL-6 had an area under the curve of 0.878, with the sensitivity and specificity of 96.0% and 62.0%, respectively (P<0.001).ConclusionsTEG and IL-6 levels were effective predictors of the risk of postoperative NR of residual AD in patients with AAD.

Introduction

Type A aortic dissection (AAD) is a life-threatening cardiovascular disease with a rising annual incidence, posing a serious threat to human health (1). It affects the ascending and descending aortas and is associated with a rising incidence and mortality rates. Patients often present with critical conditions for which emergency surgical intervention remains the sole effective treatment. Therefore, early management is crucial for patient survival and long-term prognosis (2). Treatment standards for AAD continue to evolve, with ascending aortic and total aortic arch replacements widely used in emergency surgeries at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital to eliminate proximal intimal tears (3). While surgical treatment significantly enhances short-term outcomes, long-term considerations include the presence of residual distal descending thoracic aortic dissection (AD), which prompts arterial remodeling in response to various stimuli influencing vascular lumen diameter and wall structure. The risk of aortic rupture or reoperation persists if aortic remodeling remains problematic, impacting patients long-term prognosis and quality of life (4). Preventing negative remodeling (NR) of the residual AD after the initial surgery is beneficial for improving survival and reducing complications in patients with AAD (5). Consequently, increasing research efforts have explored the postoperative evolution of true and false lumens of residual distal descending thoracic AD (6-9), although reliable predictive indicators for assessing NR occurrence in patients with AAD are currently lacking. NR in AD is closely linked to systemic inflammatory cytokine levels and coagulation status. In vivo interleukin-6 (IL-6) aggregation and clearance significantly correlate with AAD prognosis. Previous studies have shown that elevated IL-6 levels strongly predict for early postoperative neurological complications, including organ dysfunction, and other adverse outcomes after AAD surgery, primarily due to its role in promoting vascular inflammation and extracellular matrix degradation. Furthermore, inhibiting IL-6 signaling has been shown to mitigate post-dissection vascular remodeling and enhance survival outcomes (10-12). IL-6, a pro-inflammatory cytokine, activates inflammatory responses, potentially influencing aortic remodeling by enhancing inflammatory cell infiltration around the dissected aorta (11). Although thromboelastography (TEG) provides a comprehensive assessment of systemic coagulation, impact on AD remodeling remains unclear.

Hence, multiple predictive indicators are urgently needed to assess postoperative NR in AAD patients and improve outcomes by enhancing aortic remodeling and long-term effectiveness. Therefore, our study aimed to investigate the postoperative NR of the residual distal thoracoabdominal aorta in patients with AAD. We explored the predictive role of TEG and IL-6 as indicators of postoperative NR, aiming to facilitate strategies for preventing the NR in AD in patients. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2024-2259/rc).

Methods

Patients diagnosed with AAD who underwent total aortic arch replacement surgery between January 2020 and December 2021 were retrospectively and consecutively enrolled in this study. Based on 2-year follow-up imaging of the thoracoabdominal aorta, the patients were categorized into NR and non-NR groups. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the institutional review board of Union Hospital, Fujian Medical University (No. 2024KJCX074). Informed consent was waived in accordance with institutional policy for retrospective studies. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) was used to confirm the preoperative diagnosis. The inclusion criteria was AAD patients who underwent total aortic arch replacement surgery and had an uncomplicated discharge.

The exclusion criteria included concurrent thoracoabdominal or hybrid surgeries, a history of thoracoabdominal aortic surgery, chronic systemic immune disorders requiring prolonged long-term corticosteroid or immunosuppressive therapy, preexisting coagulation disorders, malignancies, hematologic disorders, cachexia, or chronic kidney disease requiring regular hemodialysis at discharge (Figure 1).

Clinical data

All patients underwent postoperative thoracoabdominal aortic CTA, which was used as the aortic baseline data for our study of NR in the residual AD. A comprehensive serum examination, including complete blood count, total biochemistry, IL-6, and TEG, was performed within 3 days before discharge. This timing was chosen to assess postoperative inflammation and coagulation as predictors of NR. Collected data included baseline clinical characteristics, discharge blood test results, and subsequent follow-up data. Follow-ups were conducted via WeChat groups, phone calls, outpatient services, or follow-up center office visits. All patients underwent thoracoabdominal aortic CTA at 3 and 12 months post-discharge followed by annual assessments, which were compared with baseline CTA performed prior to discharge.

Definitions

NR in AD was characterized as compression of the true lumen or expansion of the false lumen, marked by a >5 mm or >10% increase in aortic diameter compared to baseline (4,8,13,14). Complete false lumen thrombosis refers to total thrombotic occlusion of the aortic false lumen confirmed on arterial- and delayed-phase imaging (14). Subsequent follow-up thoracoabdominal aortic imaging, regardless of the follow-up interval, assessed the following parameters comprehensively: aortic remodeling evidence, aortic dilation at the diaphragm level, dissection progression, persistent blood flow sources entering the false lumen, and the presence of false lumen thrombus formation. Chronic renal failure was defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 for >3 months or requiring renal replacement therapy. Primary end point was patients had NR of residual AD.

Statistical analysis

IBM SPSS software (version 24.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) or R (version 3.6.0; Auckland University, New Zealand) was used for statistical analyses. Categorical data were presented as percentages (%) and analyzed using the χ2 test. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯±s) and compared between groups using the t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. The optimal cutoff point for continuous variables was determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Spearman’s correlation analysis and multivariate logistic regression were used to assess the impact of TEG parameters and IL-6 on negative aortic remodeling. For instances where there were several missing values (more than 3), we omitted the case. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05.

Results

Among the 208 patients with AAD who were discharged following total arch replacement surgery, 50 (24%) had NR of residual distal AD after 2-year follow-up. Of these, there were two, five, and two deaths due to aortic rupture, reoperations, and chronic renal failure, respectively, in the NR group. The follow-up mortality was 8.0% (4/50) in the NR group compared with 1.3% (2/158) in the non-NR group (P=0.01), whereas the reoperation rate was 10% (5/50) in the NR group versus 1.9% (3/158) in the non-NR group (P=0.001). The NR group had significantly higher IL-6 levels than the non-NR group [52.8 (31.6, 77.9) vs. 31.0 (18.6, 45.6) pg/mL, P<0.001]. The TEG parameters showed that the R-time and K-time of the NR group were longer than those of the non-NR group [6.8 (5.8, 7.9) vs. 5.4 (4.7, 6.4) min, P<0.001; 2.2 (1.5, 2.9) vs. 1.4 (1.1, 1.8) min, P<0.001, respectively]. Similarly, the clot angle [55.0° (52.0°, 65.0°) vs. 67.7° (59.8°, 72.3°), P<0.001] and maximum clot firmness [60.1 (56.4, 68.4) vs. 66.6 (57.6, 71.1) mm, P=0.04] in the NR group were lower than those in the non-NR group (Tables 1,2).

ROC curve analysis showed a significant association between IL-6 levels [area under the curve (AUC) =0.735, P<0.001], R-time (AUC =0.750, P<0.001), and K-time (AUC =0.750, P<0.001) and postoperative NR in patients with AAD. When R-time was combined with IL-6, the AUC reached 0.812 (95% CI: 0.745–0.879). When K-time was combined with IL-6 levels, the AUC was 0.825 (95% CI: 0.758–0.892), and when R-time was combined with K-time, an AUC of 0.851 (95% CI: 0.795–0.907) was obtained (P for all <0.001). However, the R-time and K-time combined with IL-6 levels (AUC =0.878, P<0.001) showed 96.0% sensitivity and 62.0% specificity in predicting NR, which were the highest among all variables. The AUC of the composite indicator was more predictive than that of the single marker (Table 4, Figure 2). Overall, R-time, K-time, and IL-6 levels are strong predictors of NR after AAD surgery, and a combination of both is more accurate.

Discussion

AAD is a life-threatening surgical emergency and the leading cause of aortic disease-related sudden death (15). Once diagnosed, urgent surgical intervention is required to prevent fatal proximal aortic rupture. Postoperatively, residual thoracoabdominal AD, aortic dilatation, true lumen compression, false lumen dilatation, and false lumen blood flow are widely recognized clinical manifestations of NR after thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) and are closely associated with poor long-term outcomes (8,16,17). However, to our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the relationship between postoperative aortic NR in AAD and TEG combined with IL-6. We retrospectively analyzed the data from 208 patients who underwent AAD surgery at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital. Among patients with NR, elevated pre-discharge IL-6 levels correlated with prolonged R- and K-time, indicating an increased risk of residual AD. Therefore, our study suggests that TEG and IL-6 could be promising predictors for identifying AAD patients at risk of postoperative aortic NR.

Previous studies have highlighted the crucial role of IL-6 in the development and prognosis of AD. In patients with AD who exhibit elevated IL-6 levels, there is a higher incidence of postoperative acute respiratory distress syndrome, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and a worse postoperative outcomes, with an higher complication rate (10,18). IL-6, a pro-inflammatory cytokine, regulate cell signaling and inflammation by binding to the IL-6 receptor (IL-6R) on the of cell membranes (19). This regulatory function triggers inflammation, promoting the infiltration of inflammatory cells around the dissected aorta, which exacerbates AD progression and significantly impacts postoperative outcomes. IL-6 is a pleiotropic cytokine with a helical quaternary structure synthesized by various cell types in response to appropriate stimuli and is present in multiple cell types during inflammation and other pathological conditions (20). Consequently, anti-inflammatory therapies have become pivotal in reducing the risk of inflammation-related cardiovascular diseases (21). Studies have suggested that inflammation-induced extracellular matrix remodeling by vascular cells is an emerging shared pathological mechanism in various vascular diseases, including AD, thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms, atherosclerosis, and intracranial aneurysms. Moreover, IL-6 signaling is a critical inflammatory pathway involved in the activation and regulation of immune responses, tissue regeneration, and metabolism. Increasing evidence supports the broader role of IL-6 signaling in cardiovascular diseases (22,23). Therefore, anti-inflammatory therapies aimed at reducing IL-6 levels or enhancing its clearance in postoperative AD patients may represent an effective strategy for preventing aortic NR.

Currently, research on the mechanisms underlying AD remodeling is in its early stages; however, breakthroughs have been made in studies exploring the inflammatory and thrombotic mechanisms of cardiovascular diseases. Canakinumab, an anti-inflammatory agent, has been shown to significantly reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with early-stage myocardial infarction (24). In a clinical trial, genetically mediated IL-6 signaling was found to significantly influence 70 circulating proteins involved in cytokine production and regulation, and immune cell recruitment and differentiation, correlating with the proteomic effects of pharmacological IL-6R inhibition (25). Among these 70 proteins, 67% were downregulated due to IL-6 signaling, impacting coronary artery disease, large artery atherosclerotic stroke, and peripheral arterial disease. A phase 2 trial evaluating the IL-6 monoclonal antibody ziltivekimab demonstrated its efficacy and safety in reducing of inflammation and thrombotic biomarkers in patients with chronic kidney disease, and associated inflammation (26). However, its cardiovascular benefits remain uncertain, pending the completion of a large-scale phase 3 cardiovascular outcomes trial evaluating ziltivekimab, expected to conclude by 2025 (27,28).

TEG is a diagnostic tool used to assess blood coagulation and clotting status and has been increasingly applied in cardiovascular medicine in recent years. TEG provides clinicians with comprehensive insights into clot formation, including its speed and strength, throughout the coagulation process. AAD involves unique changes in the coagulation system, and studies have confirmed pathological alterations in the functions of the coagulation and fibrinolytic systems after its onset (29,30). Activation of the coagulation system leads to a hypercoagulable state characterized by thrombin generation, clot formation, and extensive consumption of clotting factors. Studies have also indicated that patients with AAD not only exhibit significant reductions in clotting factors and fibrinogen levels but also already have clotting factor levels at the low end of the normal range even before surgery (29-31). This may be attributed to early thrombin activation following the formation of the false lumen, where the blood comes into contact with endothelial tissue within the aortic intramural membrane (32). Given the acute and short-lived nature of AAD, patients remain in a hypercoagulable state, with fibrinogen activated by thrombin tightly bound to platelets, thereby increasing clot strength. Strong activation and consumption of clotting factors result in significant decreases in all coagulation function indices. However, whether these changes in the coagulation system persist in the residual false lumen of AAD remains unknown. The findings of the present study indicate that patients in the NR group exhibited significantly longer R- and K-times than those in the non-NR group, with significantly lower clotting angle and maximum clot firmness than those in the non-NR group. Therefore, we speculate that residual AD after surgery may still result in alterations in the coagulation system, leading to adverse reconstruction outcomes.

After proximal surgical recovery in patients with AAD, thrombosis of the residual distal false lumen in the descending thoracic aorta is influenced by various factors. Reportedly, the complete thrombosis of the false lumen can protect patients from adverse events associated with ongoing aortic remodeling, thereby yielding beneficial outcomes (33,34). Further study demonstrated that persistent patency of the false lumen and multiple fenestrations in the descending thoracic aorta are significant risk factors for NR of the distal aorta following TEVAR of type B AD. Continual patency of the false lumen significantly prevents false lumen thrombosis, negatively affecting benign distal aorta remodeling (35,36). Despite the effective closure of major fenestrations by endovascular repair in the thoracic aorta, the presence of multiple fenestrations may allow residual blood flow through distal fenestrations and the false lumen, thereby hindering false lumen thrombosis and ultimately promoting to distal aortic NR. This aligns with existing studies reporting that blood flow within the false lumen will persist if there is persistent communication between the true and false lumens, which consequently increases the risk of aortic rupture irrespective of conservative treatment or post-TEVAR (37,38). Continuous blood flow within the false lumen may lead to sustained thrombotic reactions within the dissection, leading to continuous activation of the coagulation system. This results in a hypercoagulable state characterized by thrombin generation, thrombus formation, and increased consumption of clotting factors. Through the action of thrombin, fibrinogen is converted to fibrin, which plays a critical role in hemostasis and common coagulation cascade and participates in a complex series of pathological processes such as inflammation and tissue injury repair (39). This sustained consumptive process weakens the body’s coagulation function, decreases fibrinogen levels, and impairs fibrinogen and platelet activity while inflammation persists. These findings are consistent with our results.

Ge et al. (40) found that the greater the number of branches originating from the false lumen, the lower the likelihood for the false lumen to thrombose postoperatively. This increases the probability of NR. Kamman et al. (41) proposed that the number of preoperatively affected arterial branches from the aorta is an independent risk factor for postoperative distal aortic NR. Sultan et al. (42) suggested that more visceral arteries originating from the false lumen are associated with an increased likelihood of long-term NR. Ultimately, blood flow in the aortic false lumen leads to persistent inflammatory and thrombotic reactions throughout the body, aligning with our study’s findings on IL-6 and TEG. Therefore, patients with high IL-6 levels and prolonged R- and K-time are at higher risk of false lumen perfusion, higher rates of NR, increased incidence of aortic rupture, and may require re-intervention. These factors affect the long-term survival and quality of life of patients with AAD.

Based on the discharge and follow-up results, patients at high risk of NR can be identified to enable early interventions using new targeted anti-inflammatory agents or optimized fibrinogen and antiplatelet therapies. These strategies aim to prevent residual aortic rupture, reduce the need for reintervention, and enhance the long-term survival of patients with AAD after surgery. In addition, it is crucial to monitor more accurately the progression of residual AD reconstruction during follow-up, to guide management decisions and the review process for subsequent clinical patients.

In summary, IL-6 levels and TEG indices are strong predictors of postoperative residual AD NR in patients with AAD. It is essential to monitor the progression of residual AD remodeling more accurately during follow-up to inform management decisions and guide the review process for future patients. Furthermore, by evaluating discharge and follow-up results, patients at high risk for NR can be identified, enabling early interventions with targeted anti-inflammatory agents or optimized fibrinogen and antiplatelet therapies. These strategies aim to prevent residual aortic rupture, reduce the need for reintervention, and improve long-term survival in patients with AAD after surgery.

Limitations

There are a few limitations in this study. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size; therefore, its generalizability of the findings is limited. Second, the mechanisms underlying NR in AAD remain unclear. However, we aim to conduct further research using a combined IL-6/TEG approach, increasing the sample size, and incorporating additional biochemical markers to improve the accuracy in predicting postoperative NR in AAD. Third, the factors influencing NR in AD were not isolated; various factors were interrelated. In this study, we summarized only the inflammatory and thrombotic factors related to aortic NR and integrated them to provide a comprehensive analysis of patient prognosis, identify high-risk patients, and propose appropriate intervention strategies to improve outcomes. Fourth, the multivariate analysis may have retained some degree of potential bias. Large-scale multicenter prospective studies are required to validate these findings. Finally, our follow-up period was insufficient; hence, long-term follow-up studies, multicenter, large-sample size and prospective studies are warranted.

Conclusions

We found significant correlations between TEG and IL-6 levels and the risk of postoperative NR of residual AD in patients with AAD. Compared with the non-NR group, the NR group had a higher mortality and reoperation rate, which adversely affected the postoperative prognosis of patients with AAD. Higher IL-6 levels correlated with longer R- and K-times, indicating a higher risk of residual AD NR. Moreover, the combination of R-time, K-time, and IL-6 demonstrated a good predictive value for postoperative AD-related NR in patients with AAD.

Predictive model of public health impact of herpes zoster vaccination and related costs for adults aged ≥50 years in Republic of Korea
Authors: Serim Min, Sun-Hong Kwon, Jun-Ho Seo, Jin Hyun Nam, Jeong-Hyeon Park, Taeyeon Kwon, Hye-Rim Kang, Cheryl Ng, Eui-Kyung Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTGiven the rapidly aging population in South Korea, the growing incidence and burden of herpes zoster (HZ) increase the need for HZ prevention. However, the public health impact of HZ vaccination has not been assessed in South Korea. This predictive modeling study estimated the public health and economic impact of available HZ vaccination strategies, using the ZOster ecoNomic Analysis (ZONA) model adapted with South Korea-specific inputs. Base case analysis involved adults ≥50 y of age (YOA), exploring three vaccination strategies (no vaccination/recombinant zoster vaccine [RZV]/zoster vaccine live [ZVL]) under mass vaccination (70% coverage) settings. Scenario and sensitivity analyses were performed. In the base case population of 23,329,743 people, without vaccination, 6,978,500 HZ and 733,278 postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) cases were estimated over their remaining lifetime. Compared with no vaccination, RZV vaccination avoided 3,675,898 HZ and 372,714 PHN cases, while ZVL vaccination avoided 1,013,654 HZ and 107,590 PHN cases. RZV and ZVL avoided 430,190 and 81,975 hospitalizations, respectively. Without considering the costs of vaccination, HZ-related medical costs averted with RZV were 1,399 billion KRW (1,026 million USD), and productivity loss averted was 503 billion KRW (368 million USD); ZVL averted 419 billion KRW (307 million USD) in HZ-related medical costs and 241 billion KRW (177 million USD) in productivity loss. Results were robust under scenario and sensitivity analyses. Mass RZV vaccination among adults ≥50 YOA in South Korea would considerably reduce HZ-related burden. Results may support value assessment and decision-making regarding vaccination strategies for HZ prevention in South Korea.KEYWORDS: Healthcare cost, healthcare resource utilization, herpes zoster, older age, public health impact, South Korea, vaccination

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is caused by the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus in the dorsal root ganglion and is characterized by a painful, blistering rash.1–3 Up to 30% of HZ patients develop postherpetic neuralgia (PHN; i.e., pain persisting up to years after rash onset), and other complications include ophthalmic, neurologic, and cutaneous issues that can cause permanent physical impairment.3–5 HZ-related complications can be debilitating, substantially impacting patients’ physical, psychological, social well-being and overall quality of life, as well as interfere with work.3,4 In severe cases, HZ can also lead to hospitalization.6

The global burden of HZ is substantial, with approximately one in three individuals expected to develop HZ in their lifetime.7 Among various potential factors influencing the incidence of HZ, such as age, sex, socioeconomic status and comorbidities,8,9 older age and compromised immunity are the most well-established risk factors for HZ and its complications,4,10,11 and older age is also associated with increased risk of HZ-related hospitalization.6 In South Korea, around 60% of HZ cases occur in individuals aged 50 or older12; considering the rapidly aging population,13 the healthcare and economic burdens associated with HZ are anticipated to increase. Indeed, data released by the Health Insurance and Review Assessment (HIRA) have demonstrated a persistent upward trend in the number of individuals receiving HZ treatment from 2010 to 2023 (from 483,533 patients to 757,539 patients, increasing by 57%), accompanied by escalating healthcare costs, particularly among the elderly demographic.14

Although HZ can be addressed with antiviral medications, these options are limited, non-ideal and must be administered within 72 h of the rash appearing.7 Administering these medications promptly can be difficult due to the nonspecific initial symptoms of HZ4 or patients’ low awareness of the condition’s burden,15 often leading to delays in seeking medical attention and obtaining a prescription. To our understanding, delayed health seeking is less prominent in South Korea given the relatively high healthcare accessibility. Nevertheless, even if timely antiviral therapy is administered to reduce HZ-associated pain, there is insufficient evidence that it reduces the incidence of complications such as PHN (incidence of 11%–60% in placebo groups and 12%–58% in antiviral treatment groups).16 Therefore, a preventive approach of vaccination against HZ is needed to reduce the burden of HZ and its complications.10,17 The recombinant zoster vaccine (RZV) and the zoster vaccine live (ZVL) are available for HZ prevention in South Korea.18–20 ZVL have been used for adults ≥50 y of age (YOA) since 2012 but is contraindicated in immunocompromised (IC) individuals.18,19,21 RZV was introduced in South Korea in 2022 and is preferentially advised for HZ prevention in adults ≥50 YOA and IC adults ≥ 18 YOA.20,21

To the best of our knowledge, the public health impact (PHI) of HZ vaccines has not been assessed in South Korea. Such studies in other regions (e.g., Hong Kong, Singapore, the United Kingdom and the United States) have consistently estimated substantial public health benefits of HZ vaccination versus no vaccination, with RZV vaccination generally averting more cases of HZ and HZ-related complications than ZVL vaccination.22–25 Therefore, this study aimed to estimate the public health and economic impact of currently available HZ vaccination strategies, including comparisons of no vaccination, RZV vaccination, and ZVL vaccination in Korean older adults ≥50 YOA. Our findings will be helpful in understanding the value of vaccination in HZ prevention in South Korea, which plays a pivotal role in addressing unmet public health needs and shaping prevention strategies for HZ.26

Methods

Model overview

This study simulated the outcomes of HZ vaccination using the globally developed and validated ZOster ecoNomic Analysis (ZONA) model, a static multi-cohort Markov model developed in Microsoft Excel.27,28 The current study adapted the global ZONA model for the South Korean context by modifying key model inputs and assumptions, and subsequently undertaking relevant scenario and sensitivity analyses through updating key model inputs. Parameters except vaccine efficacy were derived from South Korea-specific data, as detailed below.

Figure 1 provides an overview of the ZONA model structure used. With a cycle length of 1 y, the PHI and costs (medical cost and productivity loss) were analyzed in the study cohorts from the year of vaccination over their remaining lifetime. Transition probabilities between health states occurred using an annual time step. Age-specific transitional probabilities were derived from South Korean data, where available, and are detailed in Supplementary Appendix 1. Both HZ and HZ-related complications (separate heath states for PHN and non-PHN complications [including ocular, neurological, cutaneous and other non-pain complications]) were modeled to occur within the same cycle. The model assumed recurrent HZ could occur in patients with their first HZ occurrence.

Overview of the ZONA model structure. This is a static multi-cohort Markov model, where PHN and non-PHN complications are health states which occur within a HZ episode and as such occur within this annual time step; recurrent HZ and complication cases are allowed. HZ: herpes zoster; PHN: postherpetic neuralgia. Adapted from Curran et al.28.

The base case population was defined as the projected 2025 population of adults ≥50 YOA in South Korea, which was further split into different age cohorts of 50–54, 55–59, 60–64, 65–69, 70–79 and ≥80 YOA. The model assessed three vaccination strategies: (1) no vaccination, (2) vaccination with RZV (two-dose vaccine), and (3) vaccination with ZVL (one-dose vaccine). For the RZV and the ZVL strategies, the entire population was vaccinated at the start of the model. The vaccination interventions were compared pairwise, between RZV versus no vaccination, ZVL versus no vaccination, and RZV versus ZVL. Within each intervention, individuals can be fully compliant or not vaccinated, depending on the assumed vaccine coverage. Costs were considered by including direct HZ-related medical costs and HZ-related indirect costs (productivity loss). The focus was on the impact after vaccination, therefore vaccination costs (e.g., cost for the vaccine, clinical consultation to receive the vaccine, productivity loss for medical appointment to receive the vaccine) were not included.

Model inputs

Model inputs were divided into four categories: demographics, epidemiology, vaccine parameters, and costs (Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S1). To inform South Korea-specific epidemiological and cost inputs for the current model, we conducted a retrospective analysis (spanning from August 2019 to July 2023) of claims data from the HIRA database. The details of the analysis are presented in Supplementary Appendix 2. South Korea operates under a single-payer healthcare system, where the entire population is enrolled, and all reimbursable claims are systematically recorded in the HIRA database. As such, the claims data used to inform our model inputs effectively represent the entire South Korean population, including the vaccine-eligible population aged ≥50 y.

Demographics

Model inputs for population size and all-cause mortality (Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S1) were obtained from the KOrean Statistical Information Service (KOSIS).29,30 The projected 2025 population ≥50 YOA in South Korea was 23,329,743.29 Age groups were aggregated to match the ZONA age cohorts, with no stratifications made for gender. An annual probability of death for each age group was calculated based on all-cause mortality data available by ages in 1-y intervals.30

Epidemiology

The key epidemiological parameters of interest were age-stratified incidence of HZ, PHN (as a proportion of individuals with HZ), and other HZ-related non-PHN complications (i.e., ocular, neurological, cutaneous, and other non-pain; as a proportion of individuals with HZ) informed by a separate retrospective HIRA database analysis (Supplementary Appendix 2 Figures S1–2, Tables S3–4). Data were analyzed from 671,161 HZ patients (Supplementary Appendix 2 Tables S3–4) and provided a comprehensive and representative coverage for the purpose of this study. The incidence rate of recurrent HZ was assumed to be the same as that of initial HZ.31–33 As with HZ, the probability of recurrent PHN was also assumed to be the same as that of first-time PHN. The probability of HZ-related death was assumed 0 given that deaths from HZ are rare in South Korea.34

Vaccine efficacy, coverage and second-dose compliance

HZ vaccination coverage was assumed at 70%, based on the midpoint of two other adult vaccines in Korea (approximately 80% for the influenza vaccine and 60% for the pneumococcal vaccine).35,36 RZV is a two-dose vaccine and its second dose is recommended for administration 2–6 months following the first dose.20 In this study, the second-dose compliance rate for RZV was assumed to be 100%.

Model inputs for vaccine efficacy and waning rates for RZV and ZVL have previously been described for the global ZONA model,28,37 and remained unchanged during adaptation to the setting of South Korea (Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S1). RZV efficacy against HZ and PHN (base case: 95.4%–98.9% depending on age group) was derived from the ZOE-50 and ZOE-70 clinical trials, and their ZOE-long-term follow-up (LTFU) study.37–40 RZV efficacy was not adjusted for ethnicity given that no variation was identified in an Asian subgroup analysis of global ZOE-50 and ZOE-70 trial data.41 Given the limited number of breakthrough HZ cases and low number of PHN cases in ZOE-50 and ZOE-70,38,39 RZV efficacy against PHN was assumed to be the same as that against HZ with no additional benefits for PHN considered. RZV efficacy waning rates (base case: 1.5%–2.3% depending on age group) were estimated by applying linear approximation to annual efficacy estimates from ZOE-50, ZOE-70 and ZOE-LTFU studies.37–40 ZVL efficacy (base case: 18.3%–69.8% depending on age group) was estimated based on the Shingles Prevention Study, the Short-Term Persistence Sub-study (STPS), and the Long-term Persistence Sub-study.37,42–44 The efficacy estimates were modeled to obtain ZVL efficacy waning rates (base case: 5.1%–5.4% depending on time post-vaccination).

Costs and healthcare resource use

Costs were estimated with a 4.5% discount rate and the inputs are detailed in Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S1.45 To estimate medical costs, the cost of HZ cases without PHN and the cost of HZ cases with PHN were obtained from the HIRA database analysis (Supplementary Appendix 2). Medical costs obtained from the claims data were adjusted to the price as at March 2024, using the consumer price index.46 Given that patients with HZ could receive non-reimbursed treatments, medical costs were adjusted to reflect the proportion of non-reimbursed expenses (17.1%).47 The total number of hospitalizations and outpatient visits were estimated from the number of hospitalization/outpatient visits per HZ case from the claims data analysis. Non-medical costs per HZ case were applied for those 50–69 YOA based on losses in productivity due to HZ; given that the employment rates for individuals over 65 y remain high, despite 65 being the retirement age in Korea, we considered productivity losses up to age 69 y in this study. These productivity loss costs were derived by multiplying the mean number of missed work days due to HZ or HZ with PHN48 by the hourly wage and employment rate for each age cohort.49,50

Where applicable, costs in South Korean Won (KRW) have been converted to United States Dollar (USD) to facilitate inferences. Cost conversion was based on the 2024 KRW to USD rate from the Internal Revenue Service of the United States (1 USD = 1364.15 KRW).51

Outcomes

The primary health outcomes of interest were the number of cases of HZ, PHN, and other HZ-related non-PHN complications (including ocular, neurological, cutaneous, and other non-pain), as well as the number of individuals needed to vaccinate (NNV) to prevent one case of HZ or PHN. The secondary health outcomes of interest included HZ-related costs and healthcare resource utilization.

Results were accumulated over the entire time horizon under each intervention (i.e., no vaccination, RZV vaccination, or ZVL vaccination), and the HZ-related healthcare costs and the amount of healthcare resource utilization avoided were then assessed to capture the economic impact of each vaccination strategy.

Scenario and sensitivity analyses

To assess the robustness of the base case results to uncertainties surrounding the model input parameters, scenario analyses were conducted for the primary outcomes of HZ and PHN, and a deterministic one-way sensitivity analysis (DSA) was conducted for the primary outcome of HZ.

In the scenario analyses, the vaccination coverage for the first dose (30%, 60% and 90%), the aggregated age group (≥55, ≥60, ≥65, or ≥70 YOA), and the probability of PHN per person (Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S2) were varied.

In the DSA, each parameter was varied individually to assess its impact on the model outcomes (Supplementary Appendix 1 Table S1). Specifically, the annual incidence of initial and recurrent HZ, the initial efficacy of RZV (one-dose and two-dose) and ZVL, and annual waning of RZV efficacy against HZ (before or after 70 YOA) were varied to determine their relative influence on the number of HZ cases avoided.

Ethics statement

The analyses performed were based on mathematical modeling with inputs extracted from published data or existing literature. No patients or prospective patient-level data were involved in the analyses. Ethics approval of the HIRA database study informing model inputs (Supplementary Appendix 2) was obtained from the institutional review board of Sungkyunkwan University, South Korea (SKKU-IRB-2024–07–031). All personal identifying information for the included patients was anonymous; therefore, informed consent for this study was waived by the institutional review board.

Results

Impact of HZ vaccination on different age groups

Compared with no vaccination, RZV and ZVL resulted in 52.7% and 14.5% HZ case reduction, 50.8% and 14.7% PHN case reduction, and 52.4% and 14.1% non-PHN complication case reduction, respectively.

Compared with no vaccination, RZV had the greatest impact on the 50–54 YOA cohort, which accounted for 29.3% of the total HZ case reduction, 24.3% of the total PHN case reduction, and 28.3% of the total reduction in other non-PHN complications within the aggregated age cohort of ≥50 YOA (Figure 2).

Compared with no vaccination, ZVL vaccination also had the greatest impact on the younger age groups (50–54 and 55–59 YOA) in terms of the number of HZ cases avoided, accounting for 26.6% of the total HZ case reduction. Conversely, among those <80 YOA, ZVL had the least impact on the younger age groups (50–54 and 55–59 YOA) in terms of PHN case reduction. Moreover, ZVL had the greatest impact on the younger age groups (50–54 and 55–59 YOA) in terms of non-PHN complications reduction (Figure 2).

Comparing RZV to ZVL, the differences in their impact in terms of HZ cases, PHN cases, and non-PHN complication cases were most pronounced in younger age groups (50–54 and 55–59 YOA) than in older age groups (≥65 YOA) (Figure 2).

For RZV vaccination, the NNV to prevent one HZ case or one PHN case generally increased with age (Table 2). For ZVL vaccination, the NNV to prevent one HZ case also increased with age, but no clear trend was observed for the NNV to prevent one PHN case. Across all age groups, the NNV to prevent a single HZ or PHN case was smaller for RZV than for ZVL (Table 2).

Healthcare resource utilization

Without HZ vaccination, healthcare resource utilization of 923,723 HZ-related hospitalizations and 27,061,045 HZ-related outpatient visits were projected. For healthcare resource use, vaccination with RZV avoided 430,190 HZ-related hospitalizations and 14,156,477 HZ-related outpatient visits, while vaccination with ZVL avoided 81,975 HZ-related hospitalizations and 3,723,106 HZ-related outpatient visits (Table 1). Across the comparisons between RZV versus no vaccination and between RZV versus ZVL, the reduction in healthcare utilizations was the largest in the 50–54 YOA cohort among the ≥50 YOA cohort; while for the comparison between ZVL versus no vaccination, the reduction was the largest in the 55–59 YOA cohort (Table 3).

Costs

Without HZ vaccination, the estimated costs incurred were 2,590 billion KRW (1,898 million USD) in medical costs and 796 billion KRW (584 million USD) in productivity losses (Table 1).

With RZV vaccination, 1,399 billion KRW (1,026 million USD) in medical costs and 503 billion KRW (368 million USD) in productivity loss were averted (Table 1). With ZVL vaccination, 419 billion KRW (307 million USD) in medical costs and 241 billion KRW (177 million USD) in productivity loss were averted (Table 1). Across all comparisons (RZV versus no vaccination, RZV versus ZVL, ZVL versus no vaccination), the reduction in costs was also the largest in the 50–54 YOA cohort among the ≥50 YOA cohort (Table 3).

Scenario analysis

When vaccination coverage for RZV and ZVL decreased (30% or 60%), the number of HZ and PHN cases averted was proportionally reduced compared with the base case results. When vaccination coverage increased to 90%, the number of averted cases was higher than in the base case (Table 4).

Deterministic sensitivity analysis

The base case results were robust under the DSA (Figure 3). The annual waning of RZV (second dose) efficacy against HZ in 50–69 YOA had the greatest impact on the number of HZ cases avoided with RZV vaccination. In decreasing order of sensitivity on the number of HZ cases avoided, this was followed by the factors: annual waning of RZV (second dose) efficacy against HZ in ≥70 YOA, initial efficacy of ZVL against HZ, initial efficacy of RZV (two-dose) against HZ, and annual incidence of initial HZ.

The number of HZ cases avoided with RZV vaccination was most impacted by the annual waning of RZV efficacy in those <70 YOA, followed by waning in those ≥70 YOA, initial ZVL efficacy, initial RZV efficacy, and the incidence of initial HZ. Discussion

This is the first study to estimate the potential HZ-related PHI and healthcare costs by vaccination strategies, including with RZV immunization, for the population aged ≥50 y in South Korea. We used recent and relevant data in the context of Korea in the validated global ZONA model. Our findings highlight the potential clinical and economic benefits of RZV as a strategy for HZ prevention for adults ≥50 YOA in South Korea.

In our modeled cohort of 23,329,743 adults ≥50 YOA over a lifetime horizon, HZ vaccination avoided a substantial number of HZ and PHN cases and healthcare resource utilization compared with no vaccination. RZV vaccination consistently avoided more HZ/PHN cases in all age groups, and overall averted 3.5–3.6 times more cases in the ≥50 YOA cohort than ZVL vaccination. The NNV to avoid one HZ/PHN case was lower for RZV than for ZVL, indicating a higher efficiency of RZV in preventing both HZ and PHN cases than ZVL. Furthermore, RZV vaccination avoided 5.2 times more hospitalizations than ZVL vaccination. As a result of the reduced disease burden and healthcare resource use, HZ-related medical costs and productivity losses averted with RZV were substantial compared with no vaccination, and was 2.9 times that averted with ZVL. Our findings are aligned with observations from recent global meta-analyses and systematic reviews, which estimate high relative vaccine efficacy of RZV over ZVL against HZ in older adults based on clinical trials and real-world studies (85% for ≥50 YOA [95% confidence interval: 31%–98%] and 84% for ≥60 YOA [95% confidence interval: 53%–95%]).52,53 Of note, ZVL is no longer available in several markets or included in the national immunization recommendations of several countries (e.g., the United States, United Kingdom, Germany).54–57 However, in South Korea, ZVL remains available and is still in the recommendations, although the use of RZV is preferred over ZVL.21

Our PHI results are consistent with other studies in the APAC region (e.g., Hong Kong, Singapore) which observed a greater impact of RZV vaccination over no vaccination and ZVL vaccination in HZ prevention (i.e., RZV reduced more HZ cases than ZVL vaccination).22,25,58,59 Additionally, while most of those APAC studies were limited to the scope of PHI and reported only on clinical outcomes, the current study includes cost estimates and is more comprehensive. Specific to Korea, Kim S et al. recently analyzed the epidemiologic and economic impact of HZ vaccination in addition to childhood varicella vaccination using a dynamic transmission model.60 The results showed that combined strategies (varicella vaccination plus HZ vaccination) prevented more cases than the single strategy (varicella vaccination alone) and that RZV vaccination prevented twice as many HZ cases as ZVL vaccination.60 While direct comparisons cannot be made due to the differences in model type, parameters and intervention strategies, our findings that HZ vaccination reduces HZ incidence and that RZV prevents more HZ cases than ZVL across different vaccination age groups are congruent.

Administering an HZ vaccine at a younger age (i.e., from 50 YOA) was observed to have a higher societal impact than vaccinating from an older age. In the base case scenario, the 50–59 YOA cohort contributed 54.3% and 52.7% of all HZ cases avoided with RZV and ZVL, respectively. This is potentially attributed to the 50–59 YOA being the largest population, with a slightly higher initial disease incidence, and having the longest remaining life expectancy to benefit from the extended protection conferred by vaccination. In contrast, the decreasing trend in vaccine impact observed beyond age 60 is also likely correlated with lower disease incidence and shorter life expectancy. Further, long-term protection remains substantial, particularly for RZV, due to low waning rates.40 Similarly, the NNV to prevent one HZ/PHN case was the lowest in the 50–54 YOA and the 55–59 YOA cohorts for both RZV and ZVL, reflecting a higher vaccine efficacy and thus a potentially higher public health benefit by vaccinating the younger cohort. From the economic perspective, the 50–59 YOA cohort contributed to 78.4% (394 billion KRW) of the total indirect costs averted with RZV, in line with the fact that the younger cohort has the highest employment rate and makes the most economic contribution.50

Scenario analyses showed that a greater number of HZ and PHN cases were avoided with increasing RZV vaccination coverage (30–90%). Our study assessed a 70% coverage in the base case, which is higher than the recent HZ vaccination data from South Korea of 19.3% (November 2021 to July 2023).61 Given that this data collection period was during the COVID-19 pandemic when healthcare-seeking behaviors may have been altered,62,63 we postulated that 19.3% likely underestimates post-pandemic coverage. Furthermore, HZ vaccines are not currently included in the Korea National Immunization Program (NIP) and require out-of-pocket payment; inclusion of a vaccine to the NIP has been observed to increase its coverage.64 Therefore, we have assumed a coverage of 70% based on the midpoint of pneumococcal and influenza vaccine coverage,35,36 which may be a closer projection to the situation where HZ vaccines are introduced into the NIP, similar to the 60% coverage rate assumed by Kim S et al.60

Our study has multiple strengths. Firstly, we used Korean nationwide claims data with strong validity to analyze the incidence of HZ and the use of healthcare resources among the Korean population with HZ. The most recent efficacy and waning rates of RZV up to 8 y post-vaccination were also used in the model. Together, these ensure that our findings are relevant and current, to provide reliable insights for informing public health decisions. Secondly, besides clinical outcomes, we evaluated the impact of vaccination on averting the economic burden caused by the disease, including both medical and non-medical (productivity loss) costs as well as healthcare resource utilization, which are important considerations for value assessment and to inform decision-making. Thirdly, sensitivity analysis was conducted by varying the factors of vaccine efficacy, vaccine waning rates and HZ incidences, which revealed the robustness of PHI results.

Nevertheless, there are limitations which need to be considered when interpreting the results of this study. Firstly, the model inputs originating from the HIRA database study (i.e., epidemiology and healthcare resource utilization inputs) were derived from the COVID-19 pandemic period (2020–2023) when medical practices may have been atypical, and these inputs may therefore be biased62,63; follow-up studies are necessary to assess changes in these parameters under present post-pandemic conditions. Secondly, there is uncertainty around applying the same vaccine efficacy estimates to HZ-naïve and previously infected individuals. In the Phase III ZOE clinical trials, those who have previously had HZ or received varicella or HZ vaccination were excluded38,39 and there are no further studies on RZV efficacy in patients with prior history of HZ. Given this data gap, this study assumes the starting cohort is HZ-naive, and that a prior episode of HZ does not affect the impact of vaccination. Thirdly, due to limited evidence, we adopted the conservative assumption that the occurrence of PHN and any other complication remains consistent regardless of the vaccination status. This assumption overlooks the potential impact of RZV on the severity of HZ; if HZ severity and duration are lower in vaccinated individuals than unvaccinated individuals, as some studies have suggested,42 then the burden of HZ in the vaccinated populations could be lower than estimated in our study. Fourthly, this study did not account for indirect costs beyond productivity loss, such as informal caregiving and formal social care, which could underestimate the economic burden of HZ and the benefits of vaccination.65 Lastly, we only considered HZ-related medical costs and productivity losses when measuring the economic impact of vaccination, while vaccination costs were not taken into account, and thus the economic impact results should be interpreted with caution. Further cost-effectiveness analysis could be conducted to comprehensively evaluate the economic impact of HZ vaccination.

While the current study focuses on evaluating the PHI of RZV vaccination among older adults (≥50 YOA), it is important to note that RZV is also indicated for immunocompromised adults (≥18 YOA) in South Korea,20 including individuals receiving immunosuppressive therapies.21 These populations are at heightened risk of HZ, HZ-related complications and hospitalization and may also benefit from vaccination.66,67 Future research could aim to assess the clinical and economic value of implementing vaccination strategies to these high-risk populations. Although this study focused primarily on age-related differences in HZ incidence and burden, other sociodemographic factors such as gender and socioeconomic status may also influence PHI.8,9 Future research incorporating a stratified analysis by these variables could explore these dimensions to more comprehensively inform vaccination policies.

Conclusion

Overall, our results support that mass RZV vaccination among adults aged ≥50 y in South Korea would considerably reduce HZ-related burden compared with no vaccination or ZVL vaccination, which is consistent with previous PHI studies from other regions. These findings may inform value assessment and decision-making regarding public health vaccination strategies for HZ and PHN prevention in South Korea. Future investigations to address the limitations of this study could provide more accurate PHI estimates, and together with additional research, such as a cost-effectiveness analysis, may be useful to guide experts in making further assessments and recommendations.

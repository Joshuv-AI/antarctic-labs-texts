IFPHOR0046 parkrun and People Who Access Services – A Public Health Initiative Reducing Illness and Inequalities
Authors: Katy Cleece, Michael Holt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObesity is one of the largest public health challenges globally, costing the NHS £3.2 billion per year. Obesity and wellbeing are intrinsically linked, with each negatively impacting on the other. Physical activity reduces obesity related disease and illnesses and improves physical and mental health. parkrun is a global public health initiative with over 2,700 weekly events and has been operating for 21 years with over 9 million people. Guild parkrun is the first forensic secure mental health inpatient parkrun.MethodologyThis exploratory action research methodology, adopts a cyclical approach where research and practice inform each other synergistically. Mental health social workers spoke with participants and staff. Barriers specific to secure forensic inpatient mental health settings were explored, and a needs analysis completed. These data were subject to thematic analysis, iteratively shaping the delivery model throughout.Results105 people have completed the 5km course, 57 people have volunteered, and 100% of respondents reported enjoying the event. More than half reported improved mental health and motivation. 86% of respondents enjoyed improved social and relational benefits. Most people had never participated in parkrun before. Relationships between staff and service users were improved, and a discharge pathway integrating parkrun was established, becoming a pillar of rehabilitation.DiscussionGuild parkrun was an initiative introduced by two mental health social workers with a passion to reduce health inequalities and improve access to physical activity and social connection to the most disempowered people in society. This study was a success and could be expanded to other settings.

ABSTRACT
The Chiropractic Educators Research Forum (CERF) convened a conference on December 6, 2025. During the meeting, presenters and panelists took an in-depth look at concepts and research related to educators and chiropractic programs engaging stakeholders.

INTRODUCTION

The Chiropractic Educators Research Forum (CERF) hosts conferences periodically, focusing on specific topics relevant to education and healthcare professions. These conferences showcase education research, innovations, and best practices, providing a forum for presenting scholarly work in health care professions, education theory, and practice. The CERF held a virtual conference on December 6, 2025, “Stakeholder Engagement: Fostering Connections and Improving Communications in Chiropractic Education,” which specifically focused on stakeholder engagement.

Stakeholder engagement is a crucial process that involves developing relationships with individuals, groups, or organizations that have an interest in, or can influence, the outcome of a project, decision, or initiative. By involving stakeholders in the decision-making and implementation processes, we gain their input, build trust, and foster buy-in. This process helps mitigate risks, drive innovation, and ensure the successful achievement of an organization’s mission, vision, and goals. During this conference, presenters and attendees examined and discussed how educators are utilizing stakeholder engagement, and the challenges and benefits this process brings to health care professions education. Stakeholders include, but are not limited to students, faculty, staff, administration, accreditors, payors, regulators, certifying entities, other health professions, patients, and the public.

Each session of the conference opened with an informative presentation introducing essential concepts surrounding stakeholder engagement. The first presentation was on how to identify stakeholders and the second was about how to engage them. Integrating traditional complementary alternative medicine management of chronic pain in HIV into medical and allied health curricula: A stakeholder collaboration approach

Ashura Abdul-Rasheed

Objective: The purpose of this study was to develop evidence-informed guidelines to support the integration of Traditional, Complementary, and Alternative Medicine (TCAM) into health professions curricula, equipping biomedical practitioners for safe, culturally sensitive, and collaborative care. Methods: A qualitative exploratory design using semistructured interviews was conducted with 13 TCAM practitioners, which included chiropractors, homeopaths, and traditional African health practitioners, recruited via purposive and snowball sampling. Thematically analyzed findings informed evidence-based guidelines for educational stakeholders. Results: Key themes from TCAM practitioners’ experiences highlighted gaps in knowledge, management practices, and barriers in collaborative care. These insights, guided by the conceptual framework, literature, and academic input, informed evidence-based guideline development. Comprehensive curricula with clear learning outcomes, postgraduate certification programs, and culturally relevant instruction in medical education were recommended. Conclusion: We identified potential challenges, such as institutional barriers and resource limitations, which are acknowledged; however, integrating this guideline may help to equip future health care practitioners to understand TCAM treatment paradigms, reduce misconceptions, and foster interprofessional collaboration. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Exploring stakeholder perspectives on factors that contribute to the perceived treatment effect of spinal manipulative therapy in a chiropractic teaching clinic: A qualitative study

Patrick Boylan

Objective: The purpose of this study was to explore the perceptions of various stakeholders, including chiropractic clinicians, interns, and patients, regarding what factors contribute to the perceived treatment effect of spinal manipulative therapy (SMT). Methods: Participants were recruited via convenience sampling from a US chiropractic teaching clinic. Semistructured interviews were conducted from January to April 2024. Data were collected and analyzed thematically until saturation was reached. An iterative open coding process was used to capture stakeholder perspectives and improve the validity of findings. Results: Six rounds of interviews were conducted, each consisting of 3 interviews: 1 with a patient who received SMT, 1 with an intern who delivered the treatment, and 1 with a supervising clinician, for a total of 18 interviews. Analysis identified 5 themes: Treatment Outcome, Therapeutic Alliance, Adjunctive Therapies, Significance of Cavitation, and Psychomotor Skills. Each theme contained multiple subthemes mentioned by participant groups at varying frequencies. Patients frequently mentioned the importance of symptom improvement following treatment, along with communication skills and adjunctive therapies. Interns emphasized functional change following treatment, while clinicians valued confidence levels and psychomotor skills. Views on the significance of cavitation varied, ranging from indifference to an indication of a successful treatment. Conclusions: Engaging multiple stakeholders revealed shared and contrasting perspectives on what contributes to the perceived effect of SMT. Beyond technical skills, factors such as therapeutic alliance, adjunctive therapies, and patient-centered outcomes play a key role. Incorporating stakeholder perspectives into education and practice may enhance training and align patient expectations, intern development, and clinician guidance. Red flag criteria in chiropractic radiology: A preliminary survey

Marshall Deltoff

Objective: The purpose of this study was to assess policies of various stakeholders regarding chiropractors’ use of red flag criteria for appropriate radiographic imaging. Methods: The diagnostic imaging policies of 17 chiropractic regulators, associations, and accrediting agencies in the United States, Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom, South Africa, and Europe were sought via website or email communication. Results: Fifteen organizations either had no policy regarding radiology usage, other than technical/safety considerations, or confirmed the importance of radiology as a diagnostic tool but did not include red flags. Only 2 organizations advocated the use of red flag criteria. Conclusion: Among the policies assessed, diagnostic imaging policies that include red flag criteria for chiropractors were absent in the majority of organizations assessed in this study. The role of chiropractic education in fostering collaboration: Perceptions and referral habits of homeopathic stakeholders

Caroline Hay, Jessica Macaulay

Objective: This study explored the perceptions of homeopaths registered with the Allied Health Professions Council of South Africa (AHPCSA) as key stakeholders, regarding the safety and effectiveness of chiropractic pediatric care. It also examines their referral practices and awareness of chiropractic qualifications. Methods: A quantitative, online cross-sectional survey, based on a previous validated questionnaire, was distributed via email to all AHPCSA-registered homeopaths. The survey used a 5-point Likert scale and dichotomous questions to assess perceptions of safety, efficacy, referral practices, and awareness of chiropractic education. The survey was open from April–June 2025. Results: The response rate was 17.57% (n = 100). Homeopaths reported favorable perceptions of the safety and efficacy of chiropractic pediatric care and demonstrated strong reciprocal referral behaviors. Awareness of chiropractic qualification standards was high, though homeopaths qualifying before 2010 showed significantly lower awareness of international educational accreditation (ECCE) (P = .039). A significant generational difference was also noted regarding perceptions of efficacy for certain conditions (P < .001). Conclusions: The findings suggest a foundation of positive rapport between these professional stakeholders. The limited response rate affects the applicability of the findings, but they highlight an opportunity for chiropractic educational institutions to proactively engage homeopathic colleagues through targeted outreach. Initiatives that focus on sharing educational standards and evidence—particularly with more experienced practitioners—can strengthen these relationships, foster mutual understanding, and promote more effective collaborative pathways for pediatric care. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Integrating cadaveric gross anatomy with clinical skills instruction of the abdominal physical examination for second-year chiropractic students: A retrospective analysis of student experience

Marissa Lee, Michael Snosek, Sean Abdulla, Hilary Alexander

Objective: This study aimed to determine student perceptions in knowledge acquisition and confidence in performing abdominal clinical skills when combining clinical instruction of the abdominal exam with human cadaveric lab instruction. Methods: Meetings were held among faculty members from the clinical diagnosis and anatomy departments to co-develop integrated, synchronous teaching stations and survey questions. The survey was trialed with a group of 10 Fourth-Year interns to assess face validity. Second-Year chiropractic students in the 2024–2025 academic year were asked to complete an anonymous survey prior to and at the conclusion of their lab. Students rated their knowledge and confidence levels of abdominal anatomy and abdominal clinical skills using a 10-point Likert scale. Results: The survey’s response rate was 96% (177/184). The mean scores were as follows: “knowledge of abdominal anatomy” 6.45 (SD = 1.53) prelab and 8.05 (SD = 1.18) postlab; “knowledge of clinical skills to assess the abdomen” 6.18 (SD = 1.57) prelab and 8.30 (SD = 1.06) postlab; “confidence in applying your knowledge of abdominal anatomy to perform clinical skills” 6.13 (SD = 1.52) prelab and 8.14 (SD = 1.20) postlab; “confidence in using clinical skills to assess abdominal conditions” 5.97 (SD = 1.46) prelab and 8.10 (SD = 1.21) postlab. Nonparametric Wilcoxon rank sum test determined statistical significance. Conclusions: This study showed an improved perceived knowledge and confidence level when combining the simultaneous instruction of clinical skills with human abdominal cadaveric lab instruction. Student feedback from this study informed the curriculum committee regarding the future integration of team teaching between the anatomy and clinical diagnosis departments in the chiropractic education curriculum. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Assessing student perspectives on an AI-enhanced exit interview process: A descriptive study

Vanessa Morales, Monique Lyons, Justin Holmes, Kristie Carter

Objective: The purpose of this study is to describe student perceptions of an artificial intelligence (AI)-enhanced exit interview. Methods: This study used a postinterview survey distributed to Intern Practicum III students. The survey contained 30 Likert-scale items and 4 open-ended prompts addressing preparation, clarity, fairness, applicability, outcomes, and perceptions of the AI-generated simulated patient roleplay, which replaced live interviewers to standardize scenarios. Results: Two of 8 students completed the survey. Both reported a positive overall experience and endorsed continuation of the AI-enhanced exit interview. High ratings were observed for satisfaction, efficiency, clinical relevance, and professional skill development, while lower scores were noted for fairness and faculty involvement improving assessment quality. Thematic analysis revealed recurring themes of real-world applicability, meaningful feedback. AI-specific items were rated favorably, and both strongly disagreed that AI increased anxiety. Conclusions: Preliminary feedback from 2 students suggests potential value and feasibility of the AI-enhanced exit interview model. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Identifying the research priorities of chiropractic students: A modified Delphi study

James Oldham, Mark Thomas

Objectives: The aim of the study was to identify the research priorities of undergraduate chiropractic students at London South Bank University. Methods: A modified Delphi consensus was utilized. Eighteen third-year Integrated Masters of Chiropractic students were asked to generate a list of broader research areas for the chiropractic profession in small working groups. Following group discussion and consensus, broader research areas were agreed and further explored in working groups to generate a list of research topics. The list of research areas and topics was consolidated and subsequently ranked individually by all students according to their perceived importance to the chiropractic profession. Results: Chiropractic students identified 10 broader research areas and 25 research topics. The highest-ranking research topics identified by the students were (1) Clinical competency of chiropractors, (2) Effectiveness of chiropractic techniques, (3) Undergraduate chiropractic education. The lowest-ranking research priorities identified by the students were (1) Cost effectiveness of chiropractic care, (2) Barriers to accessing chiropractic care, (3) Treatment of special populations. Conclusion: The priorities of chiropractic students differed from chiropractors. In particular, student clinicians did not prioritize integration of chiropractic treatment into multidisciplinary settings, cost of chiropractic care and the effect of chiropractic care on reducing medical services. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Engagement and experiential learning through community-based activities in chiropractic education

Ángel M. Ríos Santos, Pablo X. Genzana Medina, Luis E. Rivera Ramos, Jake Halverson

Objective: The purpose of this study was to engage chiropractic students in extracurricular activities with individuals with functional diversity (Down syndrome, autism, and other intellectual disability) to enhance student learning in communication, empathy, and inclusive health care practices. Methods: From 2022 to 2025, a series of activities were developed in collaboration with university administrators, students, and community stakeholders, including Baloncesto Sin Límites, Basquetbol Introvertido, Special Olympics Puerto Rico, and CODERI. These events were designed to enhance student learning by providing mutual spaces for socialization, exercise, and education. Students from 3 cohorts, Class of 2025 (n = 10), Class of 2026 (n = 30), and Class of 2027 (n = 20), voluntarily participated. Observational data and qualitative feedback were collected from organizations, faculty, and participants. Results: Collaboration with community organizations was essential for the project’s success. Baloncesto Sin Límites provided materials and facilitated contact with teams, enabling a successful adapted basketball tournament and a “University Day”, with participants from CODERI. Special Olympics Puerto Rico provided facilities and opportunities to play and interact with Puerto Rico national team. Feedback, received informally via emails, messages, and social media posts, highlighted student enthusiasm, professionalism, and adaptability. Future projects will incorporate a more structured feedback system. Overall, more than 120 athletes and 10 special education students participated alongside chiropractic students from the 3 cohorts. Conclusions: Student engagement and community partnerships may enhance chiropractic education by providing experiential opportunities for students to develop inclusive care skills. This initiative demonstrates the potential value of volunteerism, adapted sports, and academic-community collaborations for patient-centered care. (This is a conference presentation abstract and not a full paper.)

Stakeholder-informed governance: A model for the strategic restructuring of the European Council on Chiropractic Education

Christopher Yelverton

Objective: The purpose of this study is to present the European Council on Chiropractic Education (ECCE) journey in leveraging stakeholder collaboration for strategic change within an accreditation body. Methods: In 2023, the European Association for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ENQA) review presented the ECCE with recommendations to modernize and ensure the sustainability of the accreditation agency. This was a catalyst for restructuring its governance, processes, and structure, with stakeholder engagement as an important principle of this transformation. A task team that integrated internal council members and external stakeholders developed a strategic plan. This initiative was directly informed by ENQA’s recommendations and incorporated input from accredited institutions through workshops and revised committee structures. Results: The ECCE is establishing a new, transparent governance model, thereby defining accredited institutions as core members. A new Education Committee, composed of members represented on the council, ensures their voice contributes to decision-making. The accreditation standards have been simplified, and the monitoring processes refined. Furthermore, a modernized financial and operational infrastructure has been implemented to support these changes. Conclusions: ECCE’s restructuring demonstrates how leveraging stakeholder engagement into change, from governance and standards to finance and communication, fosters a more transparent, inclusive, and potentially resilient organization. This journey offers a practical blueprint for other accrediting bodies seeking to navigate similar challenges and reaffirm their relevance to the communities they serve.

Comparative Clinical Outcomes of a 1.0% Versus 1.4% Sodium Hyaluronate OVD in Cataract Surgery: A Prospective Study
Authors: Shreyas Ramamurthy, Dandapani Ramamurthy, Bhavnita Soni, Bhargav Joshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMaintaining anterior chamber space and protecting the endothelial layer during surgery is the primary role of ophthalmic viscosurgical devices (OVD), which have made them an integral part of the surgery for decades now. This study evaluated sodium hyaluronate-based OVDs BIO-HYALUR (1%) and Protectalon 1.4% in terms of their performance and safety during cataract surgery.MethodsThis was a prospective, comparative, randomized clinical study involving patients undergoing cataract surgery. The primary endpoint was the incidence of intraocular pressure (IOP) rise > 30 mmHg; other clinical parameters including visual acuity, endothelial cell density (ECD) and morphology, corneal thickness, intraocular inflammation, and intraoperative parameters (phacoemulsification metrics, space maintenance, and surgeon performance rating) were also compared. Intraocular inflammation was completely resolved by 7 ± 2 days, and lens and corneal clarity was achieved by 24 ± 4 h in both groups. Ease of use was rated as “excellent” in the vast majority of cases in both groups. Introduction

Ophthalmic viscosurgical devices (OVDs) have been an integral part of anterior chamber surgery, including cataract surgery, for several decades owing to their ability to maintain anterior chamber space to protect the endothelial layer during surgery [1, 2]. Sodium hyaluronate was the first US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved material used as an OVD and slowly became the gold standard to which all OVDs are compared [3].

Corneal endothelial cells are nondividing in nature and responsible for pumping activity that is essential for corneal clarity and integrity. There is a slow and gradual physiological corneal endothelial cell loss with age from about 4000 cells/mm2 at birth to about 1400–2500 cells/mm2 in adults [4, 5]; it is well known that this rate is significantly higher after intraocular surgical procedures such as phacoemulsification. Therefore, OVDs here play a crucial role in cataract surgery to protect ocular structures from mechanical trauma [6, 7]. Additionally, OVDs divide tissue, create space, resolve adhesions, act as wetting agents, and serve as instruments to facilitate the surgical procedure [1].

OVDs are categorized on the basis of their rheological properties into dispersive and cohesive types. Dispersive OVDs possess low viscosity and exhibit strong adherence to intraocular tissues and surgical instruments, offering effective protection to the corneal endothelium during phacoemulsification. In contrast, cohesive OVDs are high-viscosity materials characterized by strong intermolecular adhesion and entanglement, making them ideal for maintaining and creating intraocular spaces during procedures such as capsulorhexis and intraocular lens (IOL) implantation, while also allowing for easy removal from the anterior chamber [1].

BIO-HYALUR (Biotech Vision Care Pvt. Ltd., Ahmedabad, India) and PROTECTALON (VSY Biotechnology BV, Netherlands) are sterile, nonpyrogenic, viscoelastic preparations of a highly purified, non-inflammatory, and high molecular weight sodium hyaluronate solution. BIO-HYALUR contains 1.0% w/v sodium hyaluronate ophthalmic solution, whereas Protectalon has 1.4% w/v sodium hyaluronate ophthalmic solution. Both are OVDs used as surgical aids (medical devices) for protection and lubrication of delicate cells of tissues that are needed in anterior segment procedures, including cataract extraction and IOL implantation [8, 9].

This comparative clinical investigation evaluated and compared the performance and safety of two different OVDs in patients undergoing cataract surgery.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

This prospective, randomized, comparative study was conducted at the Eye Foundation, Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, India, between March 2023 and October 2023. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Eye Foundation, Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, India, prior to initiation. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants after providing a detailed explanation of the study protocol before the initiation of the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, ISO 14155:2020, and ISO 15798:2022. It was registered under the clinical trial registry of India with CTRI/2023/03/050365.

Patients were of age 18 years or above undergoing unilateral or bilateral cataract surgery through phacoemulsification extraction and posterior chamber IOL implantation. Patients had clear intraocular media other than cataracts. Patients who expressed their willingness to attend all the regular follow-up examinations as per the study schedule and signed informed consent were included in the study. Patients with a history of previous steroid-induced IOP or ocular trauma or prior ocular surgery, pigment dispersion syndrome, acute or chronic disease or illness that would increase risk or confound study results (e.g., diabetes mellitus, immunocompromised, etc.), uncontrolled systemic or ocular disease, corneal abnormalities (e.g., stromal, epithelial, or endothelial dystrophies), or known pathology that may affect visual acuity, particularly retinal changes that affect vision (e.g., macular degeneration, cystoid macular edema, diabetic retinopathy, etc.), were excluded from the study. Additional exclusion criteria were pseudoexfoliation, ocular hypertension (≥ 22 mmHg) or glaucomatous changes in the optic nerve, and endothelial cell counts lower than 1500 cells/mm2 preoperatively (based on the lowest value of three cell counts performed by a technician at the investigational site). Patients who were pregnant, or planned to become pregnant, lactating, or had another condition associated with fluctuations of hormones that could lead to refractive changes were also excluded.

Surgery and Follow-up

Patients were evaluated preoperatively to establish baseline parameters and were randomly assigned, in a 1:1 ratio, to receive either BIO-HYALUR or Protectalon, stratified by site, age group, and cataract severity using a computer-generated randomization code. All surgeries were performed by single surgeon (SR) using the same technique. Each subject received either OVD BIO-HYALUR 1% (1.0 mL) or Protectalon 1.4% (1.1 mL), both supplied with single-use sterile 27-gauge (ETO-sterilized) cannulas to ensure adequate volume for the entire surgical procedure. The OVD was introduced into the anterior chamber under standard aseptic conditions, either before or after lens removal. Additional injections of the same OVD were administered as needed to maintain anterior chamber stability and to reinflate the capsular bag prior to IOL implantation. At the end of surgery, the OVD was completely removed via irrigation/aspiration. Use of prophylactic IOP-lowering medications was not permitted. Postoperative evaluations were conducted at 8 ± 2 h, 24 ± 4 h, 7 ± 2 days, 30 ± 7 days, and 90 ± 14 days.

The intraoperative parameters assessed included investigator-reported space maintenance, total phaco time (seconds), effective phaco time (seconds), average phaco power (%), vacuum (mmHg), ease of use, OVD residence time (minutes), and OVD removal time (seconds). Pre- and postoperative evaluations comprised IOP measured using Goldmann applanation tonometry, and corneal and lens clarity assessed by slit-lamp biomicroscopy (Appasamy Associates Pvt. Ltd.) at all study visits. Corneal and lens clarity was graded using the corneal haze grading scale [10] and Miyata scale [11], respectively. Intraocular inflammation, graded according to the SUN nomenclature [12] and evaluated using slit-lamp biomicroscopy (Appasamy Associates Pvt. Ltd.), was assessed at 24 ± 4 h, 7 ± 2 days, 30 ± 7 days, and 90 ± 14 days postoperatively. Visual acuity (uncorrected and corrected distance visual acuity) were evaluated via Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) chart preoperatively, and at 30 ± 7 days and 90 ± 14 days. Corneal thickness was measured with a Tomey specular microscope (EM-2000) preoperatively, and at 7 ± 2 days, 30 ± 7 days, and 90 ± 14 days. Endothelial parameters including coefficient of variation in cell size, cell area, cell hexagonality, and endothelial cell density were evaluated using the same device at baseline and at 90 days postoperatively.

Statistical Analysis

The sample size was calculated by considering the primary endpoint, the incidence of IOP observations below 30 mmHg, assumed to be 98.0%. Assuming non-inferiority margin of 10% with allocation ratio of 1:1, a sample size of 112 patients/eyes (i.e., 56:56) was required at 85% power, with 5% level of significance. Considering the dropout rate up to 20%, a total 140 (70:70) patients/eyes was required to be enrolled in the study. Statistical analyses were performed using appropriate methods based on the type and distribution of data. Continuous variables were summarized by treatment group (OVD type) using descriptive statistics, including number of eyes (n), mean, standard deviation (SD), median, minimum, and maximum values. Categorical variables were summarized using frequency counts (n) and percentages (%). Any missing post-baseline data was imputed using the last observation carried forward (LOCF). Comparative analyses were conducted as follows: IOP, corneal thickness, and visual acuity were analyzed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) to estimate least squares means (LS) and their standard errors (SE). ANCOVA used, considering the two device visits a as fixed effect and the corresponding baseline measurement as a covariate. Lens and corneal clarity were compared between groups using the chi-square test. ECD was analyzed using either the independent t test or the Mann–Whitney U test, depending on the normality of data distribution. All statistical tests were two-sided, at 95% confidence interval, and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, unless otherwise specified. The statistical analysis was performed using an appropriate SAS® Version 9.4.

Results

The study screened and enrolled 140 patients, allocating 70 patients to the BIO-HYALUR sodium hyaluronate 1.0% group and 70 patients to the Protectalon sodium hyaluronate 1.4% group. The mean ± SD age for the BIO-HYALUR group was 63.8 ± 7.09 years (median 64.0; range 50–81), and for the Protectalon group it was 63.4 ± 8.81 years (median 62.0; range 35–83). In the BIO-HYALUR group, 31 (44.3%) patients were male and 39 (55.7%) were female; in the Protectalon group, 27 (38.6%) patients were male and 43 (61.4%) were female. No major protocol deviations were observed during the study. Patient disposition is presented in Fig. 1, and baseline characteristics are listed in Table 2.

Intraocular Pressure

The study met its primary endpoint, with no patients in either treatment group experiencing IOP spikes ≥ 30 mmHg through the 90 ± 14-day postoperative visit. Preoperatively, LS mean IOP was 15.05 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 14.53–15.56) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 14.60 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 14.08–15.11) in the Protectalon group. At 7 ± 2 days postoperatively, LS mean IOP decreased to 14.49 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 13.97–15.01) and 13.90 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 13.38–14.41), respectively, and remained stable at 30 ± 7 days [14.63 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 14.12–15.15) vs. 14.54 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 14.02–15.06)]. By 90 ± 14 days, IOP increased to 16.23 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 15.72–16.75) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 16.58 ± 0.26 mmHg (95% CI 16.07–17.10) in the Protectalon group, with significant intragroup increases from baseline in both groups (BIO-HYALUR − 1.19 mmHg; p = 0.0015; Protectalon − 1.99 mmHg; p < 0.0001); however, values remained within clinically acceptable limits. No statistically significant between-group differences were observed at any time point, with mean differences (BIO-HYALUR minus Protectalon) of 0.45 mmHg (95% CI − 0.28 to 1.18; p = 0.2281) preoperatively, 0.59 mmHg (95% CI − 0.14 to 1.32; p = 0.1123) at 7 ± 2 days, 0.09 mmHg (95% CI − 0.64 to 0.82; p = 0.8046) at 30 ± 7 days, and − 0.35 mmHg (95% CI − 1.08 to 0.38; p = 0.3467) at 90 ± 14 days, confirming comparable IOP safety profiles for BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon.

Endothelial Cell Density

At the preoperative visit, both groups demonstrated comparable endothelial cell density (ECD) values, with no statistically significant difference between them. The BIO-HYALUR group showed a mean ± SD ECD of 2508.8 ± 264.18 cells/mm2, whereas the Protectalon group had a mean ± SD ECD of 2487.8 ± 200.09 cells/mm2. At the 90 ± 14-day postoperative visit, mean ECD values remained similar between groups, measuring 2538.8 ± 252.09 cells/mm2 for BIO-HYALUR and 2567.7 ± 208.55 cells/mm2 for Protectalon. While a progressive rise was observed in both groups at the end of the study, this trend is not physiologically expected. However, these minimal variations were well within test–retest variability, and it is safe to conclude that no clinically meaningful endothelial cell loss was detected during follow-up.

Intraocular Inflammation

At the preoperative visit, most patients in both groups showed no intraocular inflammation, with grade 0 aqueous cells observed in 61.43% of the BIO-HYALUR group and 62.86% of the Protectalon group. Mild inflammation (grade 0.5–1) was present in the remaining patients. At 24 ± 4 h postoperatively, the BIO-HYALUR group showed grade 0 aqueous cells in 82.86% of patients and the Protectalon group in 95.71% of patients, with mild transient inflammation noted in a small proportion of patients, predominantly in the BIO-HYALUR group. A similar trend was followed in aqueous flare. By 7 ± 2 days, inflammation had nearly resolved, with 98.57% of the BIO-HYALUR group and all patients in the Protectalon group achieving grade 0 for both aqueous cells and flare. At 30 ± 7 days and 90 ± 14 days, both groups demonstrated complete resolution, with 100% of patients maintaining grade 0 for aqueous cells and flare (Fig. 2).

Corneal Thickness

Baseline LS mean ± SE corneal thickness was 525.88 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 520.47–531.30) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 523.51 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 518.09–528.92) in the Protectalon group (p = 0.5431). At 7 ± 2 days, thickness decreased to 515.51 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 510.09–520.92) and 515.36 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 509.95–520.78), respectively, representing significant reductions from baseline (BIO-HYALUR − 10.37 µm, 95% CI 2.72–18.02, p = 0.0080; Protectalon − 8.14 µm, 95% CI 0.49–15.79, p = 0.0370). At 30 ± 7 days, thickness remained below baseline at 518.08 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 512.67–523.50) and 519.36 ± 2.76 µm (95% CI 513.95–524.78), with a significant reduction only in the BIO-HYALUR group (− 7.80 µm, 95% CI 0.15–15.45; p = 0.0457). By 90 ± 14 days, values had returned to baseline in both groups (526.67 ± 2.76 µm [95% CI 521.25–532.08] vs. 528.96 ± 2.76 µm [95% CI 523.55–534.38]), with no significant change from baseline (p = 0.8402 and p = 0.1617, respectively). No significant between-group differences were observed at any visit (all p > 0.05).

Visual Acuity

Baseline uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) was 0.424 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.38–0.47) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 0.341 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.30–0.39) in the Protectalon group, with a significant between-group difference (mean difference 0.083; 95% CI 0.02–0.15; p = 0.0099). At 30 ± 7 days, UDVA improved to 0.104 ± 0.02 (95% CI 0.06–0.15) and 0.102 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.06–0.15), respectively, and further to 0.064 ± 0.02 (95% CI 0.02–0.11) and 0.059 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.01–0.10) at 90 ± 14 days. Improvements from baseline were significant in both groups at 30 and 90 days (all p < 0.0001), with no significant between-group differences in change from baseline at either visit (p = 0.9645 and p = 0.8936, respectively).

Similarly, baseline corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) was 0.269 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.24–0.30) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 0.252 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.22–0.28) in the Protectalon group (p = 0.4726). CDVA improved to 0.056 ± 0.02 (95% CI 0.02–0.09) and 0.065 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI 0.03–0.10) at 30 ± 7 days, and to 0.032 ± 0.02 (95% CI − 0.00 to 0.06) and 0.024 ± 0.02 logMAR (95% CI − 0.01–0.06) at 90 ± 14 days in the BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon groups, respectively. Within-group improvements were significant at both postoperative visits (all p < 0.0001), while between-group differences remained non-significant (p = 0.7060 at 30 days; p = 0.7239 at 90 days). Overall, both OVDs provided comparable postoperative visual acuity outcomes.

Specular Microscopic Examination

Endothelial cell morphology remained comparable between groups throughout the study. Baseline CV in cell size was 0.42 ± 0.10 (95% CI 0.39–0.44) in the BIO-HYALUR group and 0.44 ± 0.12 (95% CI 0.41–0.47) in the Protectalon group (p = 0.1436). At 90 ± 14 days, values were similar at 0.43 ± 0.08 (95% CI 0.41–0.45) and 0.43 ± 0.07 (95% CI 0.42–0.45), respectively, with minimal changes from baseline (0.01 ± 0.09; 95% CI − 0.01 to 0.04 vs. − 0.01 ± 0.12; 95% CI − 0.04 to 0.02; p = 0.7503). Cell area increased in both groups, from 401.57 ± 40.47 µm2 (95% CI 391.92–411.22) to 444.93 ± 58.58 µm2 (95% CI 430.96–458.90) in the BIO-HYALUR group and from 403.16 ± 34.95 µm2 (95% CI 394.82–411.49) to 461.11 ± 61.29 µm2 (95% CI 446.50–475.73) in the Protectalon group at 90 ± 14 days. The mean increase from baseline was 43.36 ± 52.18 µm2 (95% CI 30.91–55.80) and 57.96 ± 62.00 µm2 (95% CI 43.17–72.74), respectively, with no significant between-group difference (p = 0.1125). Cell hexagonality remained stable, with baseline values of 0.43 ± 0.07 (95% CI 0.42–0.45) and 0.42 ± 0.06 (95% CI 0.41–0.44), and postoperative values of 0.43 ± 0.06 (95% CI 0.41–0.44) and 0.43 ± 0.04 (95% CI 0.42–0.44) in the BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon groups, respectively. Changes from baseline were minimal (− 0.01 ± 0.09; 95% CI − 0.03 to 0.01 vs. 0.01 ± 0.07; 95% CI − 0.01 to 0.02; p = 0.6515). Overall, endothelial cell morphology was preserved in both groups, with no clinically meaningful differences between treatments.

Corneal and Lens Clarity

Corneal clarity was comparable between groups at all time points. Preoperatively, grade 0 clarity was observed in 67 (95.71%) patients receiving BIO-HYALUR and 68 (97.14%) of patients receiving Protectalon, with only a few cases of mild haze (grade I). At 8 ± 2 h postoperatively, most patients maintained clear corneas, with similar proportions of grade 0, and grade I in both groups. By 24 ± 4 h, clarity had further improved, with nearly all patients receiving BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon graded as grade 0, which remained clear till the last follow-up visit in both groups, indicating complete and sustained postoperative corneal clarity with no differences between treatments.

Lens clarity followed a similar trend of progressive improvement. Postoperatively at 8 ± 2 h, grade 0 clarity was observed in 91.43% of patients receiving BIO-HYALUR and 87.14% of patients receiving Protectalon. By 24 ± 4 h, 97.14% in the BIO-HYALUR group and 100% in the Protectalon group achieved grade 0 clarity. From the 7 ± 2-day visit onward, both groups consistently maintained 100% grade 0 clarity through the 90 ± 14-day follow-up. Overall, both OVDs showed excellent and sustained postoperative corneal and lens clarity across all time points.

Intraoperative Parameters

The mean incision size was uniform at 2.2 mm across both groups. The OVD residence time in the anterior chamber averaged 6.6 ± 7.06 min in the BIO-HYALUR group and 5.1 ± 2.05 min in the Protectalon group (p = 0.0782). OVD removal time was 19.2 ± 9.80 s for BIO-HYALUR and 14.8 ± 10.45 s for Protectalon (p = 0.012). Total phaco time averaged 30.9 ± 40.47 s for BIO-HYALUR and 23.7 ± 38.48 s for Protectalon (p = 0.285), while effective phaco time was 20.3 ± 13.53 s and 24.8 ± 35.59 s (p = 0.324), respectively. Both groups demonstrated identical mean phaco power (60.0%) and vacuum settings (550 mmHg). Full chambered space maintenance was achieved in all cases (100%). Ease of use was rated as excellent for 98.5% of BIO-HYALUR cases and 100% of Protectalon cases. No intraoperative complications were reported in any of the group (Figs. 3, 4).

Adverse Event

Discussion

The study prospective, randomized, study compared the clinical outcomes of two ophthalmic viscosurgical devices, BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon 1.4%, in anterior chamber surgery, such as cataract surgery, over a study period of 3 months. The mean age of the BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon study groups was 63.8 ± 7.09 years and 63.4 ± 8.81 years, respectively; male/female gender distributions were 44.3%/55.7% and 38.6%/61.4%, respectively.

There were no instances of postoperative IOP elevation > 30 mmHg in either treatment group, thereby meeting the study’s primary endpoint. These findings are consistent with reports from other 1.8% sodium hyaluronate OVDs, such as FIDIAL PLUS and IAL-F, where no cases of IOP ≥ 30 mmHg were observed [13]. In contrast, the outcomes in the present study were more favorable than those reported for other 1% NaHA OVDs, including Healon PRO and Healon, which documented IOP spikes in 5.5% and 4.3% of cases, respectively [14]. StableVisc and ProVisc, with postoperative IOP ≥ 30 mmHg reported in 5.2% and 8.2% of patients, respectively [15]. Similarly, when compared to dispersive OVDs, the ClearVisc clinical study reported postoperative IOP ≥ 30 mmHg in 17.4% of patients compared with 20.3% in the Viscoat group (p = 0.0002) [16]. Overall, the absence of IOP spikes in the current study further supports the safety and favorable postoperative profile of both BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon OVDs.

In the present study, the baseline mean ± SD ECD was 2508.8 ± 264.18 cells/mm2 in the BIO-HYALUR group and 2487.8 ± 200.09 cells/mm2 in the Protectalon group, with values remaining stable through 90 days postoperatively and showing no measurable endothelial cell loss. These findings compare favorably with published data for other cohesive OVDs. In a randomized study of 390 patients, mean ECD loss at 3 months was 17.5% for StableVisc and 16.9% for ProVisc, demonstrating non-inferiority between the two devices [15]. Similarly, in 1.8% NaHA OVDs such as FIDIAL PLUS and IAL-F, ECD reductions of 14.17% and 17.62%, respectively, were reported at 28 days [13]. Additional products from the same manufacturer (Biotech Vision Care Pvt. Ltd.) have shown variable endothelial cell loss profiles, with BIO-HYALUR LVD demonstrating an approximate 4.5% reduction from 2517 ± 302 cells/mm2 preoperatively to 2058 ± 455 cells/mm2 at 90 days and BIO-HYALUR CS showing approximately 10.1 ± 7.23% loss (from 2383.5 ± 288.67 to 2149.7 ± 354.49 cells/mm2 at 90 days) [17, 18]. Thus, the current evaluation of BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon demonstrated satisfactory endothelial preservation, further reinforcing the consistently favorable safety profile observed across the Biotech Vision Care OVD portfolio.

Postoperative intraocular inflammation was minimal and transient in both groups. In the BIO-HYALUR group, most eyes demonstrated grade 0 cells and flare preoperatively, with over 80% maintaining grade 0 by 24 ± 4 h and near-complete resolution (≥ 98% grade 0) by 7 ± 2 days. The Protectalon group showed a similar pattern, with over 80% achieving grade 0 cells and flare at 24 ± 4 h and inflammation resolving in nearly all eyes by 7 ± 2 days. Thus, both have similar incidences of intraocular inflammation, which resolved by 1 week; this trend aligns with Packer et al., 2021 where similar incidences were reported between two OVDs [16]. These results were also similar with the cohesive-dispersive OVDs, where mild inflammation was observed for DuoVisc and Viscopack groups for all criteria (cells and flare) at 24 h, which was completely resolved by 7 days [19].

Corneal thickness was similar at baseline (BIO-HYALUR 525.88 µm; Protectalon 523.51 µm), while both showed intragroup decreases occurred at 7 days (BIO-HYALUR − 10.37 µm, p = 0.0080; Protectalon − 8.14 µm, p = 0.0370) and at 30 days only for BIO-HYALUR (− 7.80 µm, p = 0.0457). By 90 days, values returned to baseline (BIO-HYALUR 526.67 µm vs. Protectalon 528.96 µm) with no significant intragroup changes (p = 0.8402; 0.1617) and no between-group difference (p = 0.5565), indicating comparable OVD performance. Similar results have been reported for other OVDs such as ClearVisc, ProVisc, StableVisc, Viscot, DuoVisc, and Viscopack [15, 16, 19].

Visual acuity outcomes in the present study demonstrated comparable improvement between the two OVDs. At baseline, UDVA and CDVA were 0.42 ± 0.02 and 0.27 ± 0.02 logMAR in the BIO-HYALUR group and 0.34 ± 0.02 and 0.25 ± 0.02 logMAR in the Protectalon group. By 90 ± 14 days, both groups achieved similar visual outcomes, with UDVA improving to 0.06 ± 0.02 logMAR (p = 0.8936) and CDVA to 0.03 ± 0.02 and 0.02 ± 0.02 logMAR (p = 0.7239), confirming equivalent postoperative visual recovery. These results are consistent with published literature. A comparative study evaluating Twinvisc and Duovisc reported no statistically significant differences between groups at any follow-up, with 90-day UDVA of 0.2 ± 0.2 logMAR and CDVA of 0.0 ± 0.1 logMAR in both groups, alongside significant postoperative improvement from baseline [20]. Similarly, in a randomized trial of StableVisc and ProVisc, baseline CDVA values were comparable (0.29 ± 0.3 vs. 0.31 ± 0.3 logMAR), and by 3 months, both groups demonstrated equivalent improvement (0.04 ± 0.1 vs. 0.05 ± 0.1 logMAR) [15]. Collectively, these findings reinforce that cohesive OVDs, including BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon, provide reliable and comparable postoperative visual rehabilitation following cataract surgery.

Coefficients of variance in cell size (BIO-HYALUR 0.43 ± 0.08 vs. Protectalon 0.43 ± 0.07; p = 0.143) and cell hexagonality (BIO-HYALUR 0.43 ± 0.06 vs. Protectalon 0.43 ± 0.04; p = 0.7503) showed no change from baseline to 90 days in both groups; however, cell area showed a mild increase in both groups (BIO-HYALUR 43.36 ± 52.18 µm2 vs. Protectalon 57.96 ± 62.00 µm2; p = 0.112) which was not statistically significant. The results are consistent with Shatriah et al.’s study, where no significant change was observed in CV cell size and hexagonality for Healon 5 and Healon 5 Pro from baseline to 90 days [21].

Corneal and lens clarity were comparable between the two groups and was completely resolved by 24 ± 4 h in both groups in present study. The result was consistent with other variant BIO-HYALUR LVD of the same company [17]. The intraoperative performance observed in the present study is highly consistent with outcomes reported for other OVDs in the literature. In our study, the mean incision size was uniform at 2.2 mm across both groups. Mean OVD residence time was 6.6 ± 7.06 min for BIO-HYALUR and 5.1 ± 2.05 min for Protectalon (p = 0.0782) while mean OVD removal time was higher in BIO-HYALUR compared to Protectalon group (19.2 ± 9.80 s vs 14.8 ± 10.45 s; p = 0.0112) respectively. These findings are comparable to those reported in the ClearVisc versus Viscoat trial, where OVD removal times remained below one minute and effective phacoemulsification times were approximately 10–12 s [16]. Similarly, the StableVisc versus ProVisc study documented effective phaco times of 8.7–9.8 s and OVD removal times of approximately 0.6 min, reflecting efficient intraoperative behavior [15]. In the current study, mean total phaco time and effective phaco time was similar in both groups where total phaco time averaged 30.9 ± 40.47 s for BIO-HYALUR and 23.7 ± 38.48 s for Protectalon, with effective phaco times of 20.3 ± 13.53 and 24.8 ± 35.59 s respectively (p > 0.05, both). Both groups used identical phaco power (60%) and vacuum settings (550 mmHg) and achieved consistent space maintenance and chamber stability without any intraoperative complications. Ease-of-use ratings were excellent for nearly all cases, demonstrating the high surgeon satisfaction reported for other OVDs in published studies [14, 15]. Further, the current study reported no adverse event observed in contrast to the eye disorders (dry eye, corneal edema, cystoid macular edema, iritis etc.), IOP rise, corneal abrasion, and posterior capsular rupture reported in various studies [14–16, 22].

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. The study population consisted of patients with relatively healthy eyes; it did not evaluate OVD performance in more complex surgical scenarios such as very dense cataracts, endothelial dystrophies, or post-corneal transplant phacoemulsification. Additionally, all procedures were performed by a single, highly experienced surgeon at a single center, which may limit generalizability and introduce surgeon-related bias, especially in clinical parameters such as surgeon’s reported performance. Nevertheless, the data collectively support the safety and efficacy of BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon 1.4% in cataract surgery.

Conclusion

The study shows that both BIO-HYALUR 1% and Protectalon 1.4% are reliable and safe during cataract surgery. Both groups demonstrated comparable results in terms of IOP, endothelial cell density, endothelial cell morphology, visual acuity, corneal thickness, and resolution of intraocular inflammation. A similar finding was observed in intraoperative parameters such as space maintenance, phacoemulsification metrics, and ease of use with no complications during or after surgery. Overall, both BIO-HYALUR and Protectalon 1.4% both OVDs showed good safety and performance under the conditions studied.

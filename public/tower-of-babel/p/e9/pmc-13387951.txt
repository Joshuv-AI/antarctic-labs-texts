Trends and Racial/Ethnic Disparities in Pharmacologic and Surgical Management of Treated Female Patients With Urinary Incontinence
Authors: Rawan O. Almadfaa, Patricia R. Wigle, Alex C. Lin, Angelica Hardee, Jeff J. Guo, Ana L. Hincapie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe current literature summarizes the trends in the surgical management of stress urinary incontinence (SUI) and not the general trends of management for all UI types in the US. Additionally, recent evidence on racial and ethnic disparities in UI related surgical utilization is limited.ObjectiveTo describe the currently prescribed and performed therapeutic and surgical treatments, and to assess racial and ethnic disparities in receiving treatment for female patients with urinary incontinence (UI).DesignA retrospective observational study.Participants and MeasuresWe used a University Medical Center electronic health care data from 2012 to 2022. We included females ≥ 18 years old diagnosed with stress, urge, or mixed UI, who received at least one pharmacologic or surgical treatment of interest. Our primary outcome was the frequency of the medications (Antimuscarinics and b3‐adrenergic agonists) and surgical (sling, Burch, bulking agent surgeries) treatment for female patients with UI. Racial and ethnic disparities in receiving treatment were also assessed using logistic regressions.ResultsA total of 138,752 female patients were diagnosed with UI from 2012 to 2022. Oxybutynin was the most frequently prescribed medication in this study, with 77,920 prescriptions, followed by mirabegron with 48,052 prescriptions. A total of 23,898 UI surgeries were performed. Of these, sling accounted for 78.10%, then bulking agent surgery (15.7%) and Burch (6.83%). Blacks or African Americans (OR: 0.814, p: < 0.0001) and Asians (OR: 0.865, p: 0.0228) had lower odds of receiving UI procedures compared to Whites. Blacks or African Americans were also more likely to get oxybutynin and less likely to get mirabegron to treat UI compared to Whites. Also, Ethnic disparities exist in receiving treatment.ConclusionSling was consistently the most performed surgery every year for female patients with UI. Oxybutynin and mirabegron were the most prescribed medications. Racial and ethnic health disparities were shown in receiving treatment.

Introduction

Urinary incontinence (UI) prevalence has increased globally, from an estimated 346 million affected individuals in 2008 to 383 million in 2013, with projections reaching approximately 420 million worldwide in 2018 (including 300 million women) [1, 2]. Although involuntary urine leakage is not life‐threatening or painful, it significantly affects patients' overall health and quality of life (QoL) [3]. Based on the 2015‐2018 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data, 61.8% of adult U.S. women, totaling 78,297,094, experienced UI [4]. In 2018, estimates indicated that ~420 million individuals worldwide were expected to experience UI (300 million were women) [5].

There are several steps to treat UI, starting with conservative management, which is the first line of management for all types of UI, including pelvic floor muscle exercises, appropriate fluid intake, weight loss, bladder training (for urge urinary incontinence (UUI)), scheduled voiding (for UUI) and smoking cessation, escalating to medication, physical devices, and surgical intervention [6].

Although several studies have summarized the management guidelines for all types of UI [6], yet, UI is always underreported and undertreated because of the embarrassing nature of the disease that prevents women from expressing concerns [7]. Prior literature has largely focused on surgical management trends for stress urinary incontinence (SUI), with limited contemporary evidence describing overall treatment patterns for UI using recent U.S. data [8]. Furthermore, other recent studies have examined the trends in medications for managing overactive bladder (OAB), which is not exclusively on patients with UI [9, 10]. Additionally, few recent studies assess racial and ethnic differences in the frequency of UI–related surgeries among women, and limited population‐based research examines the contemporary epidemiology of UI surgical procedures in the United States [11]. Thus, we aim to describe the currently prescribed and performed therapeutic and surgical treatments, and to assess racial and ethnic disparities in receiving treatment for treated female patients with UI using electronic health care data from 2012 to 2022.

Methods

Data Source and Study Design

This retrospective observational study used electronic records from the University of Cincinnati (UC) Medical Center through TriNetX Research Network. The database includes data from outpatient, inpatient, and specialty services contributed by participating 48 U.S. healthcare organizations.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

We included females aged ≥ 18 years with at least one diagnosis record of SUI, UUI, or Mixed urinary incontinence (MUI) using the ICD‐10 codes (N39.3, N39.41, or N39.4) from 2012 to 2023, and at least one record of a UI‐related treatment, including sling, Burch, urethral bulking agents (UBA) surgeries, anticholinergics, or beta‐3 agonists. UI subtype was defined using the first recorded diagnosis (index diagnosis) during the study period; subsequent diagnoses were not used to reclassify patients. Included females were of any race and ethnicity. Surgeries should be performed within the study duration, while medications should be prescribed within 6 months of the diagnosis day (index date). Pregnant and post‐partum females were excluded.

Study Outcomes

Our primary outcome was the frequency (percentages) of the medications and surgical treatment most recommended by clinical guidelines and the literature for treated female patients with UI from 2012 to 2022. The secondary outcome was the odds of treatment utilization (including surgery utilization, Oxybutynin utilization, and Mirabegron utilization) among different racial and ethnic groups in female patients with UI.

Statistical Analysis

For baseline characteristics, descriptive statistics like mean ± standard deviation, and median (interquartile range, IQR) were used for normally and non‐normally distributed data of UI patients. Data normality was checked using the Shapiro‐Wilk test. Also, categorial variables were described using frequencies and percentages.

We described the trends by showing the frequencies (percentages) for the most recommended treatment prescribed for UI patients by guidelines and literature [12, 13]. For medications, we focused on anticholinergics (oxybutynin, fesoterodine, darifenacin, solifenacin, trospium, flavoxate, and tolterodine), as well as beta‐3 agonists (mirabegron and vibegron). In terms of surgeries, the focus was on slings, Burch, and urethral bulking agents (UBA) injections. The index date (the first UI diagnosis day) was used to observe medications prescribed within 6 months of diagnosis. Logistic regression models were used to assess the relationship between treatment utilization and race and ethnicity.

Three different models were used with different dependent variables, which were surgeries utilization, oxybutynin utilization, and mirabegron utilization. The covariates in all three models were race and ethnicity. To further understand the racial disparities based on different surgery types, we performed a subgroup analysis using multinomial logistic regression to assess the relationship between each surgery type (Sling was used as a reference) and different racial categories (White was used as a reference). We assessed the goodness of fit using the likelihood‐ratio test, Score test, and Wald test. We considered a p‐value of < 0.05 to be statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 software.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of UI Patients

A total of 138,752 female patients were diagnosed with UI and received at least one pharmacologic or surgical treatment for UI between 2012 and 2022. Of whom, 60,533 (43.63%) patients were diagnosed with MUI, 59,941 (43.2%) were diagnosed with UUI, 18,278 (13.17%) were diagnosed with SUI. The median age for all patients was 70 years old (IQR:19). White women accounted for the largest racial group across all diagnoses types with 75.48%, 71.85%, and 72.97% in MUI, UUI, and SUI, respectively. Also, most women were not Hispanic or Latino (71.04%), followed by women with unknown ethnicity (21.61%), then Hispanic or Latino (7.35%). Additional demographics are summarized in Table 1.

Trends of UI Diagnosis

UI diagnoses have shown a continuous rise throughout the study timeframe. In 2012, a total of 1,694 female patients were diagnosed with UI, where MUI accounted for 42.68% of cases, UUI for 41.09%, and SUI for 16.23%. UI diagnoses evolved with increases in all types of UI by 2022, with UUI at 44.93%, MUI at 43.78%, and SUI at 11.30% out of 28,093 total cases (Figure 1A).

Trends of Medications Utilization

The most frequently prescribed medication in this study was oxybutynin with a total of 77,920 (41.04%) prescriptions throughout the study period, followed by mirabegron with 48,052 (25.29%) prescriptions. Then, solifenacine which was prescribed 23,379 times (12.3% of prescriptions), followed by tolterodine, and trospium with 17,903 (9.42%) and 13,615 (7.17%) prescriptions, respectively. As displayed in Figure 1, all UI medications prescriptions continued to surge, reaching as high as 12,269 prescriptions, 11,123 prescriptions, and 4,507 prescriptions for oxybutynin, mirabegron, and solifenacine, respectively in 2022. The number of prescribed medications decreased after 2019 for all medications. For instance, Mirabegron prescriptions decreased by 6.9% from 7,011 to 6,527 prescriptions and oxybutynin decreased by 16% from 11,366 to 9,543 prescription from 2019 to 2020 (Figure 1).

Trends of medications prescriptions for female urinary incontinence, 2012–2022.

Trends of Surgical Utilization

There was a total of 23,898 surgeries performed for female patients with UI. Of these, sling accounted for 18,664 (78.10%) surgeries throughout the years, followed by bulking agent surgery and Burch (3,601 (15.7%) and 1,633 (6.83%) surgeries, respectively). The trend started with 1,133 surgeries performed in 2012 and continued to rise, reaching 2,990 surgeries in 2022. Sling surgery was the dominant surgery type which had shown a steady increase over the years, reaching 2,281 surgeries in 2022, accounting for 76.29% of the total surgeries performed that year. The bulking agent surgery consistently outnumbered the Burch surgery, reaching its peak in 2022 with 20.43% of the total 2,990 surgeries compared to only 3.28% for Burch surgeries in the same year. Burch surgeries, however, surpasses the bulking agent surgery in 2017 with 15.60% compared to 11.27% of the total 2,679 surgeries that year. However, Burch surgeries declined afterward till the end of the study, with a 76.55% decrease (from 418 surgeries in 2017 to 98 surgeries in 2022). Similar to the medications prescriptions trends, surgeries performed to female patients with UI also witnessed a decline from 2019 to 2020. (Figure 2).

Surgical trends in urinary incontinence females, 2012–2022.

Racial and Ethnic Disparities in Receiving Treatment

The binary logistic regression analysis showed that race and ethnicity were associated with surgical utilization in female patients with UI. Blacks or African Americans (OR: 0.814, p‐value: < 0.0001) and Asians (OR: 0.865, p‐value: 0.0228) had significantly lower odds of receiving UI procedures compared to Whites. However, Hispanics or Latinos had higher odds compared to non‐Hispanics or Latinos in receiving surgeries (OR: 1.08, p‐value: 0.0144) (Table 2).

Similarly, race and ethnicity were also associated with the most frequently prescribed medications for female patients with UI, oxybutynin and mirabegron. For oxybutynin utilization, Blacks or African Americans and Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islanders had 38.7% and 39.8% higher odds compared to Whites, respectively (Table 2). Also, Hispanic or Latino individuals had higher odds (OR:1.218, p‐value: < 0.0001) of oxybutynin utilization compared to non‐Hispanic or Latino people.

On the other hand, results showed that Blacks or African Americans had significant lower odds of mirabegron utilization compared to White individuals (OR: 0.739, p‐value: < 0.0001). Also, Hispanic or Latino individuals had 11.9% lower odds of receiving mirabegron compared to non‐Hispanic or Latino group (OR: 0.881, p‐value: < 0.0001) (Table 2).

Discussion

This observational retrospective study assessed medications and surgical treatment trends in female patients with UI as well as the relationship between those treatment utilizations and ethnic and racial characteristics. We found that 138,752 women were diagnosed with UI. The dominant prescribed medications were oxybutynin, followed by mirabegron. There was a total of 23,898 surgeries performed for female patients with UI, of which sling surgery was the most performed one. This study also demonstrated that racial and ethnic disparities existed in receiving treatment for UI women. Our study showed that the frequency of treated female patients diagnosed with UI increased over the years.

Mirabegron is a β3‐adrenergic receptor agonist that has been shown in prior studies to have better patient adherence and tolerability compared with older antimuscarinics, such as oxybutynin [14]. Also, mirabegron has overcome the adverse events reported to the antimuscarinic medications such as constipation, dry mouth, and dementia [15]. Our study showed that mirabegron utilization continued to rise until it became the second most prescribed medication in female patients with UI. Although the cognitive side effects of utilizing oxybutynin were noted, especially for elderly [16], it was the most frequently prescribed medication in this study. Similarly, Chau et al. showed that oxybutynin accounted for the highest percentage (41%) of the total OAB medication claims by urologist providers. Followed by mirabegron and solifenacin (29% and 16%, respectively) [9].

Regarding the surgical trends, our study showed that the percent of performed surgeries for female patients with UI increased by 163% from 2012 to 2022. This could be due to increased number of female patients diagnosed with UI. Another reason could be related to the increase of UI awareness by the patients and physicians. Similarly, the overall number of surgeries performed to SUI female Medicare beneficiaries also increased from 18,820 to 32,480 surgeries over 10‐year [17]. Rogo‐Gupta et al. have observed a significant rise in sling surgeries, with a simultaneous decline in Burch surgeries [17]. Our findings showed similar results were sling surgeries increased from 905 surgeries in 2012 to 2,281 surgeries in 2022. Also, sling was the dominant perform surgery in female patients with SUI [18], aligning with our findings where sling accounted for 78.10% surgeries throughout the years. This is because sling have shown good long‐term durability [18]. Also, Dmochowski, R. R et al., conducted a systematic literature review showing that slings had a long‐term cure/dry rate ranging from 84% to 96%, compared with 73% for Burch [19].

When comparing mirabegron to oxybutynin, mirabegron was the most clinically effective medication in treating OAB with or without UI symptoms. On the other hand, the least expensive medication was oxybutynin [20]. Also, large proportion of OAB patients persisted on treatment with mirabegron, small proportion had switched medication, had lower number of healthcare visits and pads used, and had fewer working hours lost compared to antimuscarinics [21]. Despite the benefits of mirabegron over oxybutynin, insurance coverage acts as barrier for patients to receive mirabegron because they limit patients access to it [22]. Khalfay N et al., study revealed that the only drug that was covered by all plans was oxybutynin with a monthly patient cost under $10. However, one plan only covered mirabegron for under $40 per month (median price across plans of $349/month) [23]. Our study showed that Blacks/African Americans or Native Hawaiian/Other Pacific Islanders were more likely to receive oxybutynin to treat UI compared to Whites. Also, Hispanics or Latinos were more likely to receive oxybutynin vs non‐Hispanics or Latinos. On the other hand, the odds of receiving mirabegron were lower in Blacks or African Americans compared to Whites and in Hispanics or Latinos compared to non‐Hispanics or Latinos. Racial and ethnic disparities in medication access in this study might be due to differences in access to medications by insurance, access to care or health‐seeking behavior, the coverage of different or a lower number of specialists by insurance, and whether a patient receives a referral to a specialist. There could be also disparity in whether a medication is filled once it is prescribed [24]. Additionally, this might be due to the lower overall prevalence of Blacks and Hispanics compared to Whites and non‐Hispanics in our study, where Whites and non‐Hispanics constitute 73.58% and 71.04%, respectively of this study population. This points to a need to increase recruitment of all ethnicities/races into clinical trials.

Racial disparities in UI surgeries utilization also exist. Our findings showed that Asian and Black or African American females were less likely to receive UI surgeries compared to Whites. Similarly, Shah AD et al., showed that of the total 129,778 surgeries for female patients with UI, 66.7% was done in Whites, while only 3.4% and 3.2% were done in Black and other races women [25]. The reasons for these disparities in receiving surgeries are not entirely clear. However, it may be due to socioeconomic factors like access to care, access to consultations with a specialist, and the possibility of undergoing diagnostic tests (ex: urodynamics) [26]. Moreover, our results also showed that Blacks are more likely to undergo sling‐over bulking agent surgeries compared to Whites. Unlike other studies, where sling surgeries were done more in White UI females compared to Blacks [27]. This might be due to the percentage of Whites relative to Blacks in this study. In terms of sling surgery, 10.92% of the total sling surgeries were done in Blacks and African Americans compared to 87.21% in Whites in our study. Similarly, the percentage of performing bulking agent surgery was lower in Blacks and African Americans compared to Whites (8.9% vs. 88.37%, respectively). This is also supported by the additional binary logistic regression in this study, which showed that Blacks were less likely to undergo sling surgeries compared to Whites (OR: 0.852, p‐value: < 0.0001) (Table 1A). Regarding ethnic disparities, it is historically known that Hispanics experience limited access to healthcare services and utilize fewer preventive care services compared to other ethnic groups in the US [28]. Our data showed that Hispanic or Latino females have higher odds of receiving surgeries compared to non‐Hispanics or Latinos. This might be due to the Affordable Care Act (ACA) implementation in 2014, which has resulted in improved healthcare access for Hispanics [28].

Our study showed a decline in utilizing both medications and surgeries for female patients with UI patients from 2019 to 2020. This could be attributed to the COVID‐19 pandemic, where it had a huge impact on healthcare organizations and workers. This was supported by other study that noted the same decline in surgical utilization due to COVID‐19 [29]. Similarly, dispensing medications for several diseases, including HIV and hepatitis C, during COVID‐19 lockdown declined [30].

The findings of this study should be interpreted considering its limitations. The use of electronic healthcare data (TriNetX) data allows for including a large and nationwide sample of female patients with UI. However, it relies on ICD‐10 and CPT‐4 codes, thus it is subject to miscoding issues. To illustrate, the CPT code for sling procedure in treating SUI (57288) encompasses all midurethral sling types (retropubic and transobturator slings). Also, no information on treatment indication, dosing, patient medication consumption, or medication adherence. Additionally, the database may not consider the formulary restrictions on certain medications (such as mirabegron for UUI in UC Medical Center), which would impact the medication utilization.

Moreover, TriNetX data does not fully represent the racial and ethnic diversity of the U.S. population; for example, 73.58% of the study cohort were White females, and 71.04% were non‐Hispanic or Latino. Accordingly, the observed racial and ethnic findings should be interpreted as differences in treatment utilization rather than definitive healthcare disparities, given the observational design and limited representation of minority populations. Additionally, this study focused on selected pharmacologic and surgical treatments for UI. Pessary use was not included, as it is not among the most recommended management options in clinical guidelines, and behavioral or conservative interventions could not be reliably captured using the TriNetX database. Consequently, the findings reflect treatment trends among patients receiving specific therapies rather than overall management patterns. Finally, the results may not accurately reflect changes in trends in other groups served by private health insurance, Medicare, or Medicaid.

Future studies using longitudinal datasets with extended follow‐up should examine repeat sling procedures and multiple procedure patterns over time to better characterize long‐term surgical utilization and outcomes in women with UI. Also, future studies should expand the scope of therapeutic options to include all UI–related procedures, including those indicated for UUI, to provide a more comprehensive assessment of treatment patterns.

Conclusion

This study revealed that the cases of treated female patients diagnosed with UI increased over a 10 years period. Sling was the dominant surgery performed for UI female patients. Moreover, Oxybutynin utilization was the highest, followed by mirabegron. Blacks or African Americans and Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islanders were more likely to be prescribed oxybutynin compared to Whites; however, Blacks or African Americans were less likely to be prescribed mirabegron to treat UI compared to Whites. Also, Hispanics or Latinos were more likely to get oxybutynin and less likely to get mirabegron compared to non‐Hispanics or Latinos. Finally, Asians and Blacks or African Americans were less likely to undergo sling procedures compared to Whites.

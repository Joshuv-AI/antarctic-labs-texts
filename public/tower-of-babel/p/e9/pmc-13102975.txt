Improving multi-variable weather forecasting with spatiotemporal feature interaction and large-kernel modeling
Authors: Yuankang Ye, Jiaen Fei, Feng Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate and timely weather forecasting is vital for sectors such as agriculture, aviation, and disaster response. While recent advances in deep learning have enabled data-driven forecasting models, most existing methods rely on autoregressive prediction of single variables, overlooking complex interdependencies among multiple meteorological factors. To address this limitation, we propose a novel multi-variable coupled forecasting model that captures both nonlinear interactions and spatiotemporal dynamics. Specifically, we introduce a Branch-Crossing Attention Fusion strategy within a Multi-factor Fusion Network to enhance feature integration. To better model long-range dependencies and differentiate regional weather patterns, we design a Novel Large Kernel Convolution module as a substitute for traditional convolutions in recurrent networks. Built upon these innovations, we develop NLKRNN, a spatiotemporal prediction network based on the NLK-LSTM unit. Experiments on a custom meteorological dataset covering temperature, dew point, and U/V wind components demonstrate that our approach achieves consistently improved performance compared with representative spatiotemporal forecasting baselines in multi-variable weather forecasting.Keywords: Weather forecasting, Multi-variable fusion, Large-kernel convolution, Spatiotemporal modeling, Recurrent neural networkSubject terms: Atmospheric science, Projection and prediction

Introduction

Weather forecasting1 is critical for modern society2, influencing a wide range of sectors such as industry3, agriculture4, disaster response5, transportation6,7, and energy systems8. The dominant paradigm for weather forecasting remains Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP)9, which relies on numerical simulation of atmospheric dynamics governed by physical laws. Despite its success, NWP is computationally expensive and heavily dependent on parameterized physical processes10. This reliance makes NWP systems sensitive to initial conditions and parameter choices11, often leading to error accumulation and propagation over forecast horizons.

In recent years, the rapid development of data-driven deep learning techniques12 has brought transformative progress to weather forecasting. Many contemporary approaches formulate weather prediction as a spatiotemporal forecasting problem13, where the central challenge lies in effectively extracting and modeling contextual information across both spatial and temporal dimensions. While convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are well suited for spatial feature extraction, they lack explicit mechanisms for capturing temporal dependencies. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks14 introduce an encode-forecaster paradigm for sequence-to-sequence learning; however, fully connected LSTM layers struggle to preserve spatial structure. To address this limitation, ConvLSTM15 integrates convolutional operations into recurrent units, enabling joint modeling of spatial and temporal patterns. Building upon ConvLSTM, a series of improved architectures have been proposed to enhance spatiotemporal prediction accuracy16–18. For example, PredRNNv219 introduces a memory decoupling loss to mitigate redundant feature learning in PredRNN20, and employs a zigzag memory flow to facilitate interactions among spatiotemporal representations across recurrent layers.

In parallel with regional spatiotemporal forecasting models, recent years have witnessed remarkable advances in global machine-learning-based numerical weather prediction (ML-NWP). Representative approaches such as FourCastNet21, Pangu-Weather22, GraphCast23, and ClimaX24 leverage large-scale neural architectures trained on massive reanalysis datasets to achieve impressive global forecasting skill. These models demonstrate the potential of data-driven methods to complement, and in some cases partially replace, traditional NWP systems at the global scale.

Despite their success, global ML-NWP models typically require substantial computational resources, large model capacity, and extensive training data, which may limit their applicability in regional forecasting scenarios or resource-constrained environments. Moreover, their primary design objectives emphasize global coverage and long-range forecasting horizons, rather than fine-grained regional variability or explicit modeling of multi-variable interactions. As a result, there remains a strong need for regional forecasting models that can balance predictive accuracy with computational efficiency, while explicitly accounting for meteorology-specific spatiotemporal characteristics.

The field of data-driven weather forecasting continues to advance rapidly28, driven by progress in spatiotemporal modeling. For atmospheric temperature prediction, the SAVP model29 integrates ConvLSTM15 with GAN30 and VAE31 architectures, outperforming conventional ConvLSTM-based approaches. SA-JSTN32enhances ConvLSTM through a spatiotemporal memory unit with self-attention mechanisms, improving global spatial feature extraction and temperature forecasting accuracy. In wind prediction tasks, Cheng et al33. combine CNNs and LSTMs to learn implicit wind field patterns from station observations. The STNN model34 integrates 3D convolutions with convolutional GRUs35 in an encoder-decoder framework, enabling effective modeling of three-dimensional wind fields. STConvS2S36 proposes a hybrid RNN–CNN architecture for robust temperature and precipitation forecasting.

For multi-task and multi-variable prediction, MFNet37 introduces an MF-LSTM unit within the ConvLSTM framework and employs an Equilibrium Stability Penalty to address non-stationarity and data imbalance, supported by neural architecture search38. CLCRN39 further incorporates graph convolutions to capture localized spatial dependencies, achieving multi-variable forecasting on the WeatherBench dataset40. Although these approaches report promising results, many of them simplify weather forecasting as a generic spatiotemporal prediction task41,42, often relying on single-variable inputs or independently modeling individual meteorological elements. Such simplifications limit their ability to capture complex coupling relationships among multiple meteorological variables, as well as long-range dependencies inherent in atmospheric dynamics.

Recent studies indicate that meteorological variables exhibit strong spatiotemporal variability, long-range dependencies, and multivariate interactions. As illustrated in Fig. 1, temperature, dew point, and wind components show substantial differences in both mean states and variability across regions with distinct topographies, highlighting the importance of scale-aware modeling. Moreover, synchronized trends observed across spatially separated regions reveal strong long-range thermodynamic correlations. In addition, Grey Relation Analysis (Fig. 2) demonstrates pronounced coupling relationships among temperature, dew point, and wind components, underscoring the necessity of explicitly modeling multi-variable interactions.

Motivated by these observations, we propose a multi-element coupled weather forecasting framework that jointly models temperature, dew point temperature, and wind components. The proposed approach aims to explicitly capture spatiotemporal variability, long-range dependencies, and inter-variable coupling relationships, thereby improving multi-variable forecasting performance. First, we design a Multi-factor Fusion Network (MFFN) to extract and fuse features from multi-element meteorological data using a multi-branch cross-attention mechanism. This design enables effective interaction among element-specific features. Second, to address spatiotemporal variability and long-range dependencies, we introduce a novel recurrent unit, NLK-LSTM, which replaces conventional convolutional operations in PredRNNv2 with a large-kernel convolution (NLKC) to capture both local and global spatiotemporal patterns. Based on this unit, we construct the NLKRNN architecture to model complex interactions among multiple meteorological elements.

In contrast to global ML-NWP systems, the proposed framework focuses on regional multi-variable forecasting and is designed to balance predictive accuracy with computational efficiency. Rather than competing directly with large-scale global models, our approach provides a complementary and lightweight solution tailored to regional forecasting tasks, where flexibility, scalability, and practical deployment under limited computational resources are critical considerations.

Multi-factor fusion network

Due to the interactions between meteorological elements, effectively learning the coupled relationships between them can help improve the accuracy of weather forecasts. However, due to the different spatial and temporal variation characteristics of meteorological elements, such as temperature, which may exhibit significant daily and seasonal variations, while wind speed might have a more complex and irregular spatial-temporal distribution, this presents challenges. In this paper, to capture the relationships between different meteorological elements, we propose a Multi-factor Fusion Network (MFFN) based on a multi-branch cross-attention fusion strategy. The Multi-factor Fusion Network consists of N multi-factor fusion blocks, as shown in Figure 4.

Multi-factor feature fusion network architecture diagram.

Each multi-factor fusion block is composed of four feature branches and one fusion branch. Each feature branch inputs the corresponding meteorological observation data of the element. Each feature undergoes feature extraction through a LayerNorm layer, a convolutional layer, and two fully connected layers. The input to the fusion branch is the concatenated meteorological data of the four elements along the channel dimension. Additionally, the fusion branch incorporates a gating mechanism, where each feature branch and the fusion branch interact through cross-attention to guide the generation of the final fused features. New large-kernel LSTM

ConvLSTM replaces the fully connected operations in FC-LSTM with convolutional operations in both input-to-state and state-to-state transitions, thereby enabling explicit spatial feature extraction that is absent in FC-LSTM. Building upon ConvLSTM, PredRNNv2 further introduces a zigzag memory flow to facilitate information exchange across different RNN layers and incorporates a memory decoupling loss to suppress redundant representations in memory units.

However, these models typically rely on small convolutional kernels (e.g., or ), which mainly capture local spatial neighborhoods. Such limited receptive fields may be insufficient for meteorological fields that exhibit spatially coherent structures and long-range dependencies. When only small kernels are used, non-local spatial context must be accumulated indirectly through deeper stacking and repeated temporal propagation, which can dilute spatially extended signals. To address this limitation, we propose a novel Large Kernel Convolution (NLKC) module and replace the conventional convolution operations in PredRNNv2 with NLKC, enabling each recurrent update to integrate both local details and long-range spatial dependencies more directly.

As illustrated in Fig. 5(b), the NLKC module consists of two pointwise convolutions (Conv), a depthwise local convolution (DWConv), and a depthwise dilated convolution (DW-D-Conv) designed for long-range spatial feature extraction. Since depthwise and grouped convolutions generally exhibit weaker channel interaction capability than standard convolutions and may lead to information loss, we introduce two Conv layers at the top and bottom of the NLKC module. The top Conv expands the channel dimension to enhance feature representation for DWConv and DW-D-Conv. DWConv focuses on capturing local spatial patterns, while DW-D-Conv models long-range spatial correlations. The bottom Conv then projects the features back to the original channel dimension. This design improves channel mixing while retaining the computational efficiency of depthwise operations, thereby strengthening spatial feature extraction within the recurrent transition.

Experiment

Dataset

As shown in Figure 6, our study focuses on land-based climate variations in southern China (- N, - E), a region with diverse terrain including basins, plateaus, plains, and mountains, which complicates forecasting tasks. Figure 7 displays meteorological conditions at a representative time (July 1, 2020, 12:00). The dataset is constructed using ERA5-Land43, a high-resolution ( , hourly) reprocessed product of ERA544, developed by ECMWF. Compared to ERA5, ERA5-Land offers improved land-surface detail. We select four variables: temperature, dew point, U-wind, and V-wind.

Following a year-based split, data from 2017-2021 are used for training, 2022 for validation, and 2023 for testing. ERA5-Land provides continuous hourly records. Supervised samples are constructed using a sliding-window strategy with a 6-hour interval, where each sample spans a total temporal coverage of 132 hours, and adjacent samples are generated with a stride of 6 hours, with spatial grids of 128 128.

Under this setting, the resulting dataset contains 7199 training samples, 1439 validation samples, and 1439 test samples. The constructed samples span the full diurnal cycle and cover all seasons. No diurnal or seasonal rebalancing is applied, as samples are generated uniformly over time, avoiding systematic temporal bias. To improve training stability, all variables are normalized using their respective means and standard deviations during model training. For evaluation, both model predictions and ground-truth targets are transformed back to their original physical units via inverse normalization before computing all verification metrics.

Experiment design

To evaluate the model’s effectiveness in meteorological spatiotemporal forecasting, we conducted a series of experiments. First, we tested its ability to simultaneously predict multiple meteorological variables-temperature, U-wind, V-wind, and dew point-using multi-factor input. Next, ablation studies were performed to assess the contributions of key components, including the NLKC module, Multi-factor Fusion Network, and decoupling loss. We further examined the influence of network parameters and hyperparameters such as training epochs, dataset size, and input sequence length. The model was trained for 40 epochs using the Adam optimizer (learning rate: 0.0001) with CosineLRScheduler, a batch size of 16, and executed on an NVIDIA RTX GPU. For fair comparison, all deep learning baselines and the proposed model were trained under an identical experimental protocol, including the same dataset split, input and prediction windows, optimizer (Adam), learning-rate scheduler (CosineLRScheduler), batch size, number of training epochs, and hardware environment. All models follow a seq2seq forecasting protocol and directly predict the next 12 time steps from the previous 12 time steps, without autoregressive rollout. No early stopping or data augmentation was applied to any method. We note that the proposed framework focuses on deterministic forecasting and produces point estimates only, without explicitly modeling predictive uncertainty.

Overall performance of the proposed method

We evaluate the proposed method on the constructed regional dataset through comprehensive comparisons. The predicted variables include temperature (t2m), u-component of wind (u10), v-component of wind (v10), and dew point temperature (d2m). We consider two statistical baselines: persistence forecasting (Pers), which assumes the atmospheric state remains unchanged, and climatology forecasting, including overall climatology (Clim) and weekly climatology (W-Clim), which are based on long-term means and weekly seasonal patterns, respectively. We further compare against representative deep learning spatiotemporal forecasting models, including ConvLSTM, PredRNN, PredRNNv2, CausalLSTM, and MIM. To ensure a fair and reproducible comparison, all models are trained and evaluated under an identical experimental protocol with the same inputs and identical multi-variable forecasting setting. In addition, we report parameter counts to characterize model complexity: ConvLSTM (16.57M), PredRNN (27.16M), PredRNNv2 (27.18M), CausalLSTM (38.06M), MIM (36.53M), and our MFFN-NLKRNN (20.46M), which provides a transparent reference for interpreting performance relative to model capacity.

Table 1 summarizes the quantitative results for 72-hour forecasting. Among the statistical baselines, W-Clim consistently outperforms Clim, indicating that incorporating weekly seasonality provides a stronger reference for regional meteorological variability. W-Clim also improves upon Pers for wind components, suggesting that wind evolution is less well approximated by persistence and benefits from periodic climatological information. Compared with these statistical baselines, deep learning models achieve substantial improvements across all variables, demonstrating the advantage of data-driven spatiotemporal representation learning for multi-variable meteorological forecasting. Ablation experiments

To validate the rationality and effectiveness of the proposed method, we conducted extensive ablation experiments on (i) the multi-factor fusion network, (ii) the decoupling loss adopted from PredRNNv2, and (iii) the NLKC module design. We first examine how different fusion strategies affect overall forecasting performance. We then evaluate the contribution of the decoupling loss in encouraging complementary spatiotemporal memory representations. Finally, we experimentally validate the effectiveness of the proposed large-kernel convolution (NLKC) by comparing alternative convolutional configurations within the recurrent unit.

In data-driven deep learning methods, the training process is crucial, and relevant hyperparameters can significantly impact the model’s performance and results. To verify the impact of training data size on prediction performance, we trained the proposed model on training sets from different years. The number of training epochs

To verify the impact of the number of training epochs on model performance, we conducted experiments as shown in the Figure10(b), where we trained the model for 25, 30, 35, and 40 epochs. It can be observed that more training epochs help improve the model’s performance. However, for temperature and dew point temperature, the performance improvement gradually diminishes as the number of epochs increases. For U-component wind and V-component wind, increasing the number of epochs did not result in significant performance improvement. Too many or too few training epochs may lead to overfitting or underfitting, thereby reducing the model’s generalization ability.

The value of learning rate

To study the impact of the learning rate setting during the training process on model performance optimization, we trained the proposed model with different learning rates. As shown in the Figure 10(c), it can be observed that as the learning rate gradually decreases from 1e-03 to 5e-04 to 1e-04, the overall forecasting performance improves. However, when the learning rate is reduced to 5e-05, the model’s forecasting performance decreases. This indicates that a smaller learning rate prevents the model from adequately fitting the training data, while a larger learning rate may cause the model to overfit the training data, thus reducing its generalization ability. When the learning rate is 1e-04, the overall forecasting performance for the four meteorological elements is better than that of other parameters.

Conclusions

With the advancement of data-driven deep learning techniques, their application to weather forecasting has gained increasing attention. However, for these methods to be truly effective, they must capture the intrinsic spatiotemporal variation patterns embedded in meteorological data. This study systematically analyzes meteorological data from three perspectives: regional spatiotemporal variation, long-range dependencies, and the coupling relationships among multiple meteorological variables. The findings reveal pronounced differences in feature variations across regions of varying scales, significant long-range correlations, and strong interdependencies among meteorological elements.

To address these challenges, we propose a multi-variable fusion network that integrates a fusion branch with four dedicated feature branches, utilizing a multi-branch cross-attention mechanism. This design enables effective learning of inter-variable coupling by allowing the attention-enhanced feature branches to guide and refine the fused representations. Additionally, we develop a spatiotemporal prediction network, NLKRNN, based on an encoder-decoder framework. By incorporating a novel recurrent unit (NLK-LSTM) and a large-kernel convolutional operation (NLKC), the model effectively captures both large-scale and small-scale regional variations, while preserving long-range temporal dependencies.

To evaluate the model’s capacity for spatiotemporal representation, we first conducted experiments on the MovingMNIST dataset, where our method outperformed existing models, demonstrating its robustness in dynamic prediction tasks. Further experiments were conducted on ERA5-Land regional meteorological data, targeting four key variables: temperature, dew point temperature, and the two wind components (zonal and meridional). The proposed model consistently achieved improved performance compared with statistical approaches and the evaluated deep learning baselines across multiple evaluation metrics.

Comprehensive ablation and hyperparameter studies further validate the model’s effectiveness and its ability to accurately model the complex spatiotemporal interactions inherent in meteorological data. These results underscore the potential of the proposed approach as a powerful tool for multi-variable weather forecasting. In future work, we aim to incorporate higher-resolution spatiotemporal data, enhance the model’s temporal dependency learning, extend prediction horizons, and develop more practical forecasting solutions with real-world applicability.

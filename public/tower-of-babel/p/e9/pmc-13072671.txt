Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio mediates pulmonary consolidation through oxidative stress in children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia
Authors: Junlin Zhao, Zhiyuan Wang, Yanan Wang, Qianqian Dai, Shengmeng Qu, Liang Ru
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study investigated oxidative stress in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) of children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) and explored the association between the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and pulmonary consolidation through oxidative stress pathways.MethodsThis retrospective case-control study enrolled 81 children with acute-phase MPP, categorized into consolidation (n = 41) and non-consolidation (n = 40) groups. BALF oxidative stress biomarkers (malondialdehyde [MDA], superoxide dismutase [SOD], glutathione peroxidase [GSH-Px], and advanced oxidation protein products [AOPP]) and inflammatory markers were measured. Mediation analysis examined the NLR–MDA–consolidation pathway, and a Gaussian graphical model mapped the biomarker network.ResultsBALF MDA [2.58(1.53,3.07) vs. 1.76(1.22,2.11) nmol/mL, p = 0.005] and AOPP [2835.63(2127.52,3340.34) vs. 2270.57(1543.56,2918.73) pg/mL, p = 0.026] were significantly higher in the consolidation group, while GSH-Px [98.33(80.70,135.22) vs. 126.75(99.11,152.39) U/mL, p = 0.009] was lower; SOD showed a non-significant decrease (p = 0.130). Mediation analysis suggested a potential indirect pathway from NLR to consolidation through MDA (indirect effect = 0.229, 95% CI: −0.023 to 0.480, p = 0.075; mediation proportion = 48.0%), though not reaching conventional significance. Network density increased by 72.5% in the consolidation group, with CRP and IL-6 as hub nodes in the non-consolidation group.NLR and BALF oxidative stress biomarkers, but not M. pneumoniae load, were associated with extra-pulmonary organ involvement.ConclusionsChildren with MPP-associated consolidation exhibited oxidative-antioxidant imbalance in BALF. NLR may contribute to consolidation through lipid peroxidation These findings from a single-center retrospective study warrant validation in larger prospective multicenter cohorts.

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is a significant cause of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in children, accounting for approximately 20–30% of cases in certain hospitalized pediatric CAP cohorts [1,2]. Historically, M. pneumoniae infection exhibits cyclical epidemic patterns, which contribute to fluctuations in incidence within these pediatric populations [3]. Following the relaxation of COVID-19 pandemic control measures, a global resurgence of M. pneumoniae infections was observed. In 2023, China reported a nationwide surge in pediatric M. pneumoniae infections during the autumn and winter months [4], and Denmark confirmed a large-scale epidemic between October and December [5]. Similar epidemics were documented in South Korea, where a multicenter study across 13 hospitals identified 374 microbiologically confirmed pediatric cases during September to December 2023 [4], and in France, where a nationwide study encompassing 37 pediatric hospitals reported 969 children hospitalized with M. pneumoniae infection during 2023–2024 [5]. In Japan, the first M. pneumoniae epidemic in eight years occurred in 2024 [6].

Beyond its well-recognized pulmonary manifestations, M. pneumoniae infection can also give rise to a wide spectrum of extra-pulmonary diseases, particularly in children, involving the skin, central nervous system, hematological system, and cardiovascular system [7]. The pathogenesis of these extra-pulmonary manifestations is predominantly immune-mediated, involving exaggerated pro-inflammatory cytokine production, autoantibody generation through molecular mimicry, and immune complex deposition, rather than direct pathogen invasion in most cases [7].

The radiological manifestations of MPP are heterogeneous, encompassing bronchopneumonia, consolidation/atelectasis, bronchiolitis, and mosaic pattern. The underlying pathological correlates range from cellular bronchiolitis and peribronchial infiltration in milder forms to organizing pneumonia and diffuse alveolar damage in more severe presentations [8]. Among these, pulmonary consolidation represents the most severe radiographic category and correlates closely with disease severity. For example, Huang et al. [8] reported that among 1,401 hospitalized children with MPP, 31.6% developed consolidation or atelectasis, and these patients exhibited prolonged fever duration, extended hospitalization, increased medical costs, and an elevated risk of refractory MPP, necrotizing pneumonia, and bronchiolitis obliterans. Moynihan et al. [9] reported that children with severe MPP frequently present with consolidation on imaging and often require respiratory support and intensive care. Therefore, elucidating the pathogenic mechanisms underlying MPP-associated consolidation is crucial for early identification of high-risk patients and optimization of therapeutic strategies.

Oxidative stress is recognized as a fundamental driver of MPP-induced lung injury. The pathogenic infection activates phagocytic cells and induces excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production. Excessive oxidative stress is associated with direct tissue damage and abnormal immune responses through lipid peroxidation and disruption of the alveolar-capillary barrier [10–12]. Malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) serve as key oxidative stress biomarkers [10]. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), an indicator of systemic inflammatory response, has been increasingly recognized as an accessible and informative biomarker of systemic inflammation in pediatric infectious diseases. In the context of MPP specifically, a recent systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizing 26 studies with 7,013 pediatric patients reported that elevated NLR was significantly associated with refractory MPP (OR = 2.15, 95% CI: 1.44–3.21), severe MPP (OR = 1.41, 95% CI: 1.27–1.56), and poor prognosis (OR = 1.55, 95% CI: 1.30–1.84) [13]. Together with earlier observations from individual cohort studies [14,15], this growing body of evidence supports NLR as a potentially useful biomarker for risk stratification in pediatric MPP. Based on this, we hypothesized that elevated NLR indirectly promotes pulmonary consolidation by amplifying local oxidative stress. Since complex diseases, including infectious diseases, involve multilevel molecular and cellular interactions, traditional correlation methods alone cannot fully elucidate the pathological mechanisms of these conditions. Network medicine, which constructs and analyzes biological networks to identify pathological modules and interactions from a systems perspective, provides a more comprehensive view of these complex multidimensional mechanisms [16].

Based on the above evidence, we designed this exploratory retrospective study with three specific objectives. First, to compare BALF oxidative stress biomarkers—MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, and advanced oxidation protein products (AOPP)—between children with and without consolidation. Second, to employ mediation analysis to evaluate the hypothesized association pathway ‘NLR → MDA → consolidation’. Third, to utilize Gaussian graphical modeling to characterize the structural differences in inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarker networks between patients with and without consolidation. Given the retrospective observational design, this study is exploratory in nature and aims to generate hypotheses regarding the role of local oxidative stress in MPP-associated consolidation, which require validation in future prospective studies.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective case-control study enrolled children with acute-phase MPP (within 2 weeks of onset) hospitalized in the Department of Pediatrics at a tertiary hospital from May 6, 2023, to November 5, 2025. All included patients underwent bronchoalveolar lavage treatment in the pediatric intensive care unit during hospitalization. The study design and research workflow are shown in Figure 1.

Study flowchart. Study flowchart showing participant selection and analysis workflow. From 81 children with MPP, patients were categorized into consolidation (n = 41) and non-consolidation (n = 40) groups based on chest imaging. MPP: Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia; X-ray: X-radiography; CT: Computed Tomography.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria. Patients meeting all of the following criteria were eligible for enrollment: (1) age between 1 and 18 years; (2) MPP diagnosis confirmed according to the ‘Evidence-based Guideline for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Mycoplasma pneumoniae Pneumonia in Children (2023)’ [17]. Diagnosis was established based on compatible clinical manifestations (fever, cough, and/or abnormal pulmonary auscultation) and chest imaging findings, combined with at least one of the following laboratory criteria: a fourfold or greater increase in M. pneumoniae antibody titers during the convalescent phase compared with the acute phase, or a positive M. pneumoniae DNA detected by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). In this cohort, etiological confirmation was achieved through qPCR detection of M. pneumoniae DNA in throat swab and/or BALF specimens, and/or positive serum M. pneumoniae antibody testing; (3) chest radiography or Computed Tomography (CT) demonstrating pulmonary consolidation or other radiographic abnormalities; (4) meeting clinical indications for flexible bronchoscopy as specified in the ‘Guideline of pediatric flexible bronchoscopy in China (2018 version)’ [18]; (5) written informed consent signed by the patient’s legal guardian for bronchoscopy and bronchoalveolar lavage procedures; (6) complete medical records available, including demographics, clinical presentation, laboratory tests, imaging data, and BALF specimens.

Exclusion Criteria. Patients meeting any of the following criteria were excluded from the study: (1) primary or secondary immunodeficiency disorders; (2) use of immunosuppressants or systemic corticosteroids within 2 weeks prior to admission; (3) chronic pulmonary disease; (4) co-infection with other pathogens; (5) hematologic disorders; (6) malignancy; (7) incomplete BALF specimens or clinical data.

Grouping Criteria. Based on chest CT or radiographic findings, patients were categorized into two groups: Consolidation group: presence of large, well-demarcated, high-density consolidation with air bronchogram sign. Non-consolidation group: patchy infiltrates, ground-glass opacities, or interstitial changes without definite consolidation. All radiographic examinations were independently evaluated by two experienced radiologists to determine final radiologic diagnosis. In cases of disagreement, a third senior radiologist (with 20 years of experience) reviewed the images, and the final classification was determined by consensus among all three radiologists.

Clinical data collection

The following data were systematically collected for all enrolled patients: (1) demographic characteristics: age, sex, weight, height, Body Mass Index (BMI); (2) clinical parameters: pre-admission disease course, fever duration, peak temperature, post-antibiotic fever days, respiratory support requirement; (3) laboratory parameters obtained within 24 h of admission, including complete blood count [white blood cells (WBC), neutrophils, lymphocytes, NLR, hemoglobin, platelets], inflammatory biomarkers [CRP, procalcitonin (PCT), IL-6, serum amyloid A (SAA)], coagulation markers [Fibrin Degradation Products (FDP)], D-dimer), Liver function and myocardial enzymes [alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase, Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB)], serum albumin, immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM), complement components (C3, C4), and total immunoglobulin E (IgE); (4) BALF analysis: bronchoscopy specimens collected 3–5 days post-admission for quantification of Mycoplasma pneumoniae DNA load and oxidative stress biomarkers (MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, AOPP); (5) clinical outcomes: presence of mucus plugging, pleural effusion, length of stay, duration of macrolide antibiotic therapy, duration of corticosteroid therapy, duration of oxygen therapy. Extra-pulmonary organ involvement was systematically recorded for all patients, including pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, renal injury, coagulation abnormality, hepatic injury, and myocardial injury. A composite variable ‘any extra-pulmonary manifestation’ was defined as the presence of at least one of the above manifestations.

Bronchoscopy and BALF collection

BALF was collected via flexible bronchoscopy using an electronic bronchoscope (BR-GP13 series) equipped with an image processor (HV3030) (Zhuhai Seesheen Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Zhuhai, China). Bronchoalveolar lavage was conducted using 37 °C sterile saline (1 mL/kg per lavage). BALF specimens were collected via negative pressure suction into sterile sputum collectors, centrifuged at 500 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected, aliquoted, and stored at −80 °C pending analysis. Specimens were subsequently submitted for quantitative fluorescence polymerase chain reaction (PCR) detection of Mycoplasma pneumoniae DNA load.

BALF oxidative stress biomarker measurement

MDA, SOD, and GSH-Px levels were quantified using commercially available micro-assay kits (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China; Cat# BC0025, BC0170, and BC1195, respectively) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Specifically, MDA levels were determined using a thiobarbituric acid (TBA)-based method with absorbance measured at 532 nm (corrected at 600 nm); SOD activity was assessed based on its ability to inhibit nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) reduction in a xanthine–xanthine oxidase system, with absorbance measured at 560 nm; and GSH-Px activity was determined using a DTNB-based colorimetric method with absorbance measured at 412 nm. AOPP levels were measured using a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Cat# FK1062Y, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, with absorbance read at 450 nm and concentrations calculated from a standard curve. The lot numbers of all kits were recorded and are available upon request.

Quality Control. All researchers were professionally trained physicians, and the testing process strictly followed standard operating procedures. Data entry employed a double data entry verification system, where two individuals independently entered the data to ensure accuracy.

Statistical analysis

General statistical methods and between-group comparisons

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 29.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯ ± s) and compared using independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (interquartile range) [M (Q1, Q3)] and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as n (%) and analyzed using chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Associations between biomarkers and extra-pulmonary organ involvement were evaluated using Mann-Whitney U tests and Spearman rank correlation analysis. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of any extra-pulmonary manifestation, with age and sex as covariates.

Spearman rank correlation analysis

Spearman rank correlation analysis was employed to explore correlations among biomarkers and their associations with clinical variables, visualized using butterfly correlation plots. The butterfly plot displayed oxidative stress biomarkers (MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, AOPP) as the central axis, with correlations to inflammatory indicators and clinical parameters shown on left and right wings. Line thickness represented the absolute value of correlation coefficients. Connecting lines were colored orange for positive correlations and green for negative correlations.

Mediation analysis

Mediation analysis was conducted with NLR as the independent variable (X), consolidation as the dependent variable (Y), and oxidative stress indicators (MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, AOPP) and inflammatory markers (CRP, PCT, IL-6, SAA) as candidate mediating variables (M). The Baron and Kenny stepwise regression approach was used to test mediation effects: Step 1 tested path c (total effect of independent variable on dependent variable); Step 2 tested path a (effect of independent variable on mediating variable); Step 3 simultaneously included independent variable and mediating variable to test path b (effect of mediating variable on dependent variable controlling for independent variable) and path c’ (direct effect controlling for mediating variable). The Sobel test was employed to assess significance of the indirect effect (a × b), and mediation proportion was calculated (indirect effect/total effect × 100%). Mediation was established when both path a and path b were significant. The mediation analysis was grounded in a biologically plausible pathway in which systemic inflammation (reflected by NLR) promotes local oxidative injury (reflected by MDA), which in turn contributes to pulmonary tissue damage manifesting as consolidation. It should be noted that BALF specimens were collected 3–5 days post-admission, after consolidation had already been identified on chest imaging; therefore, MDA levels measured in BALF reflect the oxidative stress milieu within the consolidated lung rather than a temporally antecedent event. The mediation model thus captures cross-sectional associations along a hypothesized biological cascade rather than a prospectively observed temporal sequence. Additional assumptions include: (2) no unmeasured confounders of the mediator–outcome relationship; and (3) no interaction between the independent variable and the mediator. We acknowledge that these assumptions cannot be fully verified in observational studies. Formal adjustment for confounders through multivariable regression was not performed in the mediation model; instead, potential confounding was addressed through strict inclusion/exclusion criteria and verification of baseline comparability between groups (Table 1). This approach represents a limitation inherent to the exploratory nature of this study.

Gaussian graphical models

To visualize and quantify the interrelationship structure among biomarkers from a systems-level perspective, network analysis was performed using Spearman rank correlation matrices for nine core biomarkers (NLR, CRP, PCT, IL-6, SAA, MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, and AOPP). Spearman correlations were chosen due to non-normal distributions of biomarker values (Shapiro–Wilk p < 0.01 for all except AOPP). Separate networks were constructed for the overall sample, the consolidation group, and the non-consolidation group using the R package qgraph (version 1.9), with a fixed spatial layout to facilitate visual comparison. Network density was quantified as the mean absolute correlation coefficient across all node pairs. Node centrality metrics were computed, including strength centrality (sum of absolute edge weights connected to a node), betweenness centrality (number of shortest paths passing through a node), and closeness centrality (inverse of the average shortest path length from a node to all other nodes). Nodes with the highest strength centrality were identified as hub nodes.

Edge weight accuracy was assessed using nonparametric bootstrap with 1,000 resamples. Centrality stability was evaluated using case-dropping subset bootstrap (1,000 iterations) for the overall network, and the centrality stability coefficient (CS-coefficient) was calculated; values ≥ 0.25 indicate acceptable stability [19]. All network analyses were performed in R (version 4.5.2).

Sample size calculation

Although this was an exploratory study without a priori sample size calculation, we conducted a post-hoc power analysis using the observed effect size. Post-hoc power analysis confirmed adequate sample size for detecting the observed MDA difference (Cohen’s d = 0.877). Our sample of 81 patients (n = 41 vs. n = 40) achieved 97.4% power at α = 0.05 (two-sided), indicating adequate statistical power for the primary between-group comparison (Supplementary Table 1).

Results

General characteristics and clinical features comparison

Univariate analysis was first conducted for between-group comparison to screen differential variables. No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in demographic characteristics (age, sex, weight, height, BMI), pre-admission disease course, peak temperature, fever days, or etiological detection indicators (p > 0.05). Compared to the non-consolidation group, the consolidation group exhibited significantly longer post-antibiotic fever days [2.00(1.00,4.00) vs. 1.00(0.00,2.00), p = 0.012], higher proportion of respiratory support requirement (41.46% vs. 10.00%, p = 0.010), prolonged corticosteroid therapy duration [7.00(5.00,12.00) vs. 5.00(2.75,7.00), p = 0.012], and extended oxygen therapy duration [0.00(0.00,2.00) vs. 0.00(0.00,0.00), p = 0.002]. Regarding complications, the consolidation group demonstrated significantly elevated pleural effusion incidence (48.78% vs. 20.00%, p = 0.013), while no significant between-group differences were observed in other complications including mucus plugging, pericardial effusion, renal injury, coagulation abnormalities, hepatic injury, and myocardial injury (all p > 0.05) (Table 1). Overall, 42 of 81 patients (51.9%) developed at least one extra-pulmonary manifestation, with pleural effusion being the most common (34.6%), followed by coagulation abnormality (22.2%), renal injury (16.0%), hepatic injury (14.8%), myocardial injury (6.2%), and pericardial effusion (3.7%) (Supplementary Table 2, Supplementary Figure 1).

Comparison of laboratory parameters

The consolidation group exhibited more pronounced oxidative stress injury, manifested by elevated levels of MDA [2.58(1.53,3.07) vs. 1.76(1.22,2.11) nmol/mL, p = 0.005] and AOPP [2835.63(2127.52,3340.34) vs. 2270.57(1543.56,2918.73) pg/mL, p = 0.026], while SOD showed a non-significant trend toward reduction [14.27(11.69,19.62) vs. 16.19(12.33,28.21) U/mL, p = 0.130]. GSH-Px [126.75(99.11,152.39) vs. 98.33(80.70,135.22) U/mL, p = 0.009] levels were reduced. The consolidation group demonstrated stronger inflammatory responses, with significantly elevated neutrophil percentage [73.80(63.50,80.80) vs. 66.50(54.02,72.43), p = 0.011], NLR [3.67(2.04,5.22) vs. 2.66 (1.62, 3.44), p = 0.036], CRP [24.13 (11.41, 51.39) vs. 11.28 (4.76, 26.80) mg/L, p = 0.024], IL-6 [22.50 (15.70, 48.09) vs. 13.58 (6.08, 28.25) pg/mL, p = 0.040], and SAA [171.80 (102.00, 352.70) vs. 116.10 (20.45, 243.10) mg/L, p = 0.018], and decreased lymphocyte percentage (20.90(15.90,30.80) vs. 26.50(21.77,35.78), p = 0.018). Regarding cardiac markers, CK-MB was significantly lower in the consolidation group [0.87 (0.47, 2.10) vs. 1.84 (1.14, 3.44) ng/mL, p = 0.026]. Coagulation parameters FDP [5.50 (3.80, 13.00) vs. 3.80 (2.18, 7.78) μg/mL, p = 0.009] and D-dimer [2.30 (1.30, 6.70) vs. 1.40 (0.80, 3.25) mg/L, p = 0.014] were significantly elevated in the consolidation group. Total IgE levels were numerically higher in the consolidation group compared to the non-consolidation group [76.20 (34.50, 138.70) vs. 53.40 (26.80, 95.60) IU/mL], although this difference did not reach statistical significance (Z = −1.687, p = 0.092). There was no statistically significant difference in the remaining indicators between the two groups of patients (p > 0.05) (Table 2).

Correlation analysis

Core indicator selection was based on statistical significance in between-group comparisons, focusing on immune imbalance-oxidative stress-tissue injury mechanism pathways, while considering clinical measurability and indicator category completeness. Clinical indicators were selected based on objective quantifiability, ability to reflect disease severity, and relevance to inflammation-coagulation-tissue injury mechanisms, while avoiding redundancy with core indicators. Spearman correlation analysis revealed extensive correlations among oxidative stress indicators, inflammatory markers, and clinical parameters (Table 3, Figure 2). The oxidative damage marker MDA positively correlated with BALF Mycoplasma pneumoniae load (r = 0.323, p < 0.01) and neutrophil percentage (r = 0.299, p = p < 0.01). AOPP positively correlated with LDH (r = 0.358, p < 0.01) and coagulation parameters. Antioxidant indicators SOD and GSH-Px negatively correlated with D-dimer and FDP (all p < 0.01) and positively correlated with albumin. Among inflammatory markers, CRP showed the strongest correlation with D-dimer (r = 0.604, p < 0.001) and FDP (r = 0.589, p < 0.001). IL-6, PCT, and SAA all demonstrated significant positive correlations with coagulation markers and LDH, and negative correlations with albumin (all p < 0.001). NLR showed high correlation with neutrophil percentage (r = 0.920) and lymphocyte percentage (r = −0.927) (both p < 0.001). The butterfly correlation plot intuitively displayed the above correlation network, suggesting that oxidative stress and inflammatory responses are interrelated and jointly participate in the pathological process of coagulation dysfunction. Complete correlation analysis of all indicators with clinical parameters is provided in Supplementary Table 3.

Mediation analysis

Based on correlation analysis results, NLR was selected as the independent variable, consolidation as the dependent variable, and eight indicators—including oxidative stress markers (MDA, SOD, GSH-Px, AOPP) and inflammatory markers (CRP, PCT, IL-6, SAA)—as candidate mediators (M) for mediation analysis. Among the 32 mediation pathways examined, the NLR-MDA-consolidation pathway was the only model satisfying significant path a (effect of NLR on MDA) and path b (effect of MDA on consolidation) simultaneously (Figure 3C; Supplementary Table 4). The effect of NLR on MDA was significant (a = 0.273, p = 0.014), and the effect of MDA on consolidation was significant (b = 0.837, p = 0.012). The indirect effect was 0.229 (95% CI: −0.023 to 0.480, p = 0.075), showing a trend toward statistical significance but not reaching the conventional threshold of α = 0.05. The estimated proportion mediated was 48.0% (Figure 3A, B). After controlling for MDA, the direct effect of NLR on consolidation was no longer significant (c′ = 0.350, p = 0.198), while the total effect was marginally significant (c = 0.476, p = 0.070). These results suggest a potential mediation pathway from NLR to consolidation through MDA, although the indirect effect did not reach conventional statistical significance. This finding should be considered hypothesis-generating and requires confirmation in larger samples (Figure 3A). Furthermore, summary analysis by mediator type (Figure 3D, E; Supplementary Table 5) revealed that the average indirect effect of NLR through oxidative stress indicators (0.121) was higher than that through inflammatory markers (0.053), further supporting the central role of the oxidative stress pathway in NLR-mediated promotion of consolidation. Detailed results of the mediation analysis are presented in Table 4. These results are consistent with the hypothesis that elevated NLR may be associated with the development of consolidation through promotion of lipid peroxidation (elevated MDA), although this mediation pathway was not statistically confirmed at the 0.05 level.

Network analysis

A GGM, incorporating 9 core indicators, was employed to construct the immune-inflammatory-oxidative stress biomarker network (Figure 4A and B). Network density in the consolidation group (0.292) increased by 72.5% compared with the non-consolidation group (0.169), indicating stronger interactions and tighter coordinated regulation of the biomarker system in children with consolidation (Figure 4C, Table 5). Centrality analysis revealed that CRP served as the hub node in both the overall network and consolidation group, with strength centrality of 1.714 and 1.964, respectively. IL-6 (strength centrality = 1.589) was the hub node in the non-consolidation group, suggesting that CRP plays a central regulatory role in the consolidation state (Figure 4D, Table 6). Consolidation-specific strong correlations included: IL-6 with SAA (r = 0.539), MDA with GSH-Px (r = −0.530), and GSH-Px with AOPP (r = −0.523), representing specific interaction patterns in children with consolidation (Supplementary Table 6). In the overall network, correlations among inflammatory markers were the strongest (Supplementary Table 7). NLR positively correlated with MDA (r = 0.29) and negatively correlated with SOD and GSH-Px, further supporting the NLR-oxidative stress pathway identified in the mediation analysis. Network stability analysis using 1,000 non-parametric bootstrap samples supported the robustness of the estimated edge weights (Supplementary Figure 2A). The CS coefficient for strength centrality was 0.45 for the overall network, indicating acceptable stability (Supplementary Figure 2B).

Association of biomarkers with extra-pulmonary organ involvement

Having established that the NLR–oxidative stress axis is associated with pulmonary consolidation through mediation and network analyses, we further explored whether these biomarkers are also associated with extra-pulmonary organ involvement.

Patients with any extra-pulmonary manifestation (n = 42) exhibited significantly higher NLR [3.81 (2.50, 6.21) vs. 2.44 (1.41, 3.27), p < 0.001], elevated MDA [2.17 (1.65, 2.92) vs. 1.74 (1.01, 2.36) nmol/mL, p = 0.005] and AOPP [2925.78 (2189.51, 3343.88) vs. 2164.16 (1707.71, 2786.52) pg/mL, p = 0.009], alongside reduced SOD [13.59 (10.98, 18.75) vs. 18.31 (13.80, 26.24) U/mL, p = 0.009] and GSH-Px [97.28 (82.09, 130.95) vs. 133.22 (102.94, 151.88) U/mL, p < 0.001], compared to those without (n = 39). In contrast, BALF M. pneumoniae load showed only a non-significant trend (p = 0.067) (Figure 5A–F; Supplementary Table 8).

Spearman correlation analysis revealed that NLR showed the strongest positive correlation with any extra-pulmonary manifestation (ρ = 0.404, p < 0.001), followed by MDA (ρ = 0.314, p = 0.004), while GSH-Px demonstrated the strongest negative correlation (ρ = −0.373, p < 0.001). Among specific organ manifestations, GSH-Px showed a particularly strong negative correlation with pleural effusion (ρ = −0.539, p < 0.001) (Figure 5G; Supplementary Table 8).

In multivariate logistic regression adjusted for age and sex, NLR (adjusted OR = 1.35, 95% CI: 1.07–1.71, p = 0.011) and MDA (adjusted OR = 1.97, 95% CI: 1.17–3.31, p = 0.011) were identified as independent predictors of any extra-pulmonary manifestation, while BALF M. pneumoniae load was not a significant predictor in either the univariate or adjusted model (Figure 5H; Supplementary Table 9).

When stratified by specific manifestation types, NLR was significantly elevated in patients with pleural effusion (p = 0.001), liver injury (p = 0.015), coagulation abnormality (p = 0.022), and renal injury (p = 0.050), but not myocardial injury (p = 0.600) (Supplementary Figure 3). Among the oxidative stress biomarkers, pleural effusion showed the most consistent associations, with significantly elevated MDA (p = 0.006) and AOPP (p < 0.001), and reduced SOD (p = 0.025) and GSH-Px (p < 0.001). Coagulation abnormality was also associated with reduced GSH-Px (p = 0.003) and SOD (p = 0.050) (Supplementary Figure 4).

Discussion

This study explored the relationship between oxidative stress indicators in the BALF of children with MPP and pulmonary consolidation. It also explored the potential pathophysiological association linking NLR, MDA, and consolidation through mediation analysis and network analysis. The study found that the levels of MDA and AOPP in the BALF of children in the consolidation group were significantly elevated, while GSH-Px was significantly decreased and SOD showed a non-significant decreasing trend, indicating an imbalance between oxidation and antioxidation. Furthermore, mediation analysis suggested that NLR may exert an indirect effect on consolidation through MDA, with an estimated mediation proportion of 48.0%. Additionally, the biomarker network density in the consolidation group increased by 72.5% compared to the non-consolidation group, with CRP serving as the hub node.

BALF provides a direct reflection of biochemical alterations within the alveolar microenvironment and contains biomarkers with greater diagnostic specificity for pulmonary infection compared to serum samples. Moreover, BALF enables more sensitive assessment of the oxidative stress status in the local microenvironment of the lower respiratory tract [20]. Building upon previous work by Wang et al. [10], who developed a prediction model for refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) based on BALF-derived oxidative stress markers and demonstrated the prognostic value of indicators such as AOPP, this study further extends those findings. Specifically, this research focused on BALF specimens and defined consolidation as the primary imaging outcome. Mediation analysis was employed to statistically evaluate the hypothesized association among NLR, MDA, and consolidation. However, given the retrospective observational design, these results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory of causal relationships. Additionally, network analysis revealed significant alterations in the inflammation-oxidative stress axis in patients with consolidation. These findings provide preliminary evidence supporting a potential role of oxidative stress in the pathogenesis of consolidation during MPP, which warrants further validation in prospective studies.

Oxidative stress represents a key pathological mechanism underlying lung injury in MPP. Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection is associated with neutrophil activation, which may lead to excessive production of ROS. This can subsequently result in lipid peroxidation and oxidative damage to proteins [10]. MDA, a terminal product of lipid peroxidation, serves as a sensitive biomarker for evaluating the extent of oxidative damage. In this study, BALF MDA levels were significantly elevated in patients with consolidation compared to those without and exhibited a positive correlation with Mycoplasma load in BALF, indicating a close association between pathogen burden and the severity of oxidative injury. Conversely, antioxidant enzymes including GSH-Px was significantly reduced and SOD showed a decreasing trend in the consolidation group, suggesting impairment of the endogenous antioxidant defense system and the establishment of an oxidative-antioxidant imbalance. These observations are consistent with findings from animal models, which demonstrate a pronounced oxidative stress response in MPP-induced pneumonia, characterized by increased MDA levels and decreased SOD activity [21].

Oxidative stress can affect coagulation function through multiple mechanisms: ROS may damage vascular endothelial cells and activate extrinsic coagulation pathways, and inflammatory responses can alter the balance of the vascular endothelium and the coagulation system. Inflammatory mediators such as IL-6 can up-regulate the synthesis of acute-phase proteins (including fibrinogen) in the liver, promote the expression of tissue factors, enhance the coagulation response and inhibit the anticoagulation mechanism [22]. Nencini et al. [23] found that when the levels of antioxidant enzymes are insufficient, the level of ROS increases, which can directly oxidize fibrinogen, potentially contributing to an increase in the levels of its degradation products D-dimer and FDP. This study found that oxidative stress indicators, the antioxidant enzymes SOD and GSH-Px, were negatively correlated with D-dimer and FDP, and CRP had the strongest correlation with coagulation indicators.

Mediation analysis is a statistical framework for evaluating whether the association between an independent variable and an outcome is transmitted through a hypothesized intermediate variable, thereby providing evidence for potential mechanistic pathways [24]. Angkananard et al. [25] used this method to explore the relationship between NLR and cardiovascular events. They found that approximately 56% of the total effect of NLR is mediated by metabolic factors such as diabetes and hypertension, providing evidence that the association between inflammatory indicators and clinical outcomes may be partially transmitted through intermediate pathways. This study employs mediation analysis to explore the potential mechanistic pathway by which NLR affects consolidation through MDA. These findings provide exploratory evidence supporting a potential inflammation–oxidative stress–tissue injury pathway, which warrants further investigation. NLR is a comprehensive indicator reflecting the immune inflammatory status of the body and has recently been confirmed as a predictor of the severity and prognosis of children with MPP [26]. Furthermore, in studies on the association between oxidative stress and inflammation, Yilmaz et al. [27] found a significant positive correlation between NLR and MDA levels in patients with active Crohn’s Disease, suggesting that NLR may reflect the oxidative stress state. Neutrophils play a dual role in MPP infection. On one hand, they clear pathogens through phagocytosis; on the other hand, overactivated neutrophils produce high levels of ROS through Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Phosphate (NADPH) oxidase, leading to a respiratory burst and causing local tissue damage [28]. The mediation analysis in this study found that the indirect effect of NLR on consolidation mediated by MDA accounts for 48.0% of the total effect, with the remaining effect likely representing direct or other mediated pathways. MDA, as a mediating variable, mediates the effect of NLR on consolidation, suggesting that ROS produced by neutrophils may lead to alveolar epithelial injury through lipid peroxidation pathways, ultimately manifesting as imaging-confirmed consolidation.

The network analysis provides a systems-level framework for visualizing and quantifying the interrelationship structure among multiple biomarkers simultaneously, thereby offering a perspective that complements traditional pairwise correlation analysis [19,29]. This method has been widely applied in clinical biomarker network research. Azuaje et al. [30] demonstrated that the inflammatory protein network can predict clinical outcomes after myocardial infarction, and Wang et al. [31] used network analysis to compare differences in biomarker association patterns among different disease severity groups. This study constructed an immune-inflammation-oxidative stress network using GGM and found that the network density in the consolidation group increased by 72.5% compared to the non-consolidation group. CRP served as a hub node in both the overall network and the consolidation group, while IL-6 was the hub node in the non-consolidation group, suggesting that CRP may serve as a central node in the inflammatory-oxidative stress network under consolidation conditions. The strong correlations between biomarkers unique to the lung consolidation group represent the interaction patterns under pathological conditions. This study’s network analysis constructed an immune, inflammation, and oxidative stress network, providing a systematic perspective for understanding the mechanisms underlying MPP consolidation.

The results of this study have potential clinical implications. First, NLR is an easily accessible clinical indicator that may serve as a candidate marker for clinicians assessing the risk of consolidation in children with MPP. Second, based on the mediating pathway involving NLR, MDA, and consolidation, antioxidant therapy may become an adjunctive therapy for MPP. Previous studies have shown that antioxidants such as N-acetylcysteine (NAC) can reduce pulmonary inflammatory responses and improve oxidative stress-related lung tissue injury in animal models [32]. Additionally, glucocorticoids can indirectly alleviate oxidative stress damage by inhibiting neutrophil activation and inflammatory responses. The exploratory analysis of extra-pulmonary organ involvement extends the implications of our main findings to the systemic level. The observation that NLR and BALF oxidative stress biomarkers—but not M. pneumoniae load—were associated with extra-pulmonary involvement suggests that host-driven inflammatory and oxidative injury, rather than pathogen burden itself, may be a more relevant determinant of systemic organ damage during MPP. This interpretation aligns with the immune-mediated pathogenesis of M. pneumoniae associated extra-pulmonary manifestations, in which exaggerated host responses rather than direct microbial invasion are considered the primary drivers of organ injury [7]. The consistent associations observed across all biomarkers for pleural effusion may reflect the anatomical proximity of the pleura to the inflammatory focus and its susceptibility to paracrine oxidative damage. The identification of NLR and MDA as independent predictors of extra-pulmonary involvement parallels their central roles in the mediation pathway for pulmonary consolidation, suggesting a shared pathophysiological axis underlying both intra-pulmonary and systemic complications. These exploratory findings should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size, the heterogeneity of recorded manifestations, and the absence of classic immune-mediated extra-pulmonary conditions in our cohort.

Several potential confounding factors were considered in our study design and analysis. First, although the two groups showed no significant differences in age, sex, BMI, or pre-admission disease course (Table 1), we acknowledge that subtle variations in these demographic factors could influence oxidative stress biomarkers. Second, all patients underwent bronchoscopy within a standardized timeframe (3–5 days post-admission), minimizing temporal variation in BALF collection. Third, our strict exclusion criteria eliminated patients with immunodeficiency, chronic pulmonary disease, co-infections, and recent corticosteroid use, factors known to independently affect oxidative stress status. Fourth, the timing of antibiotic initiation was similar between groups (median duration of illness before admission: 8.5 vs. 9.0 days, p = 0.947), reducing treatment-related confounding. While we cannot completely exclude residual confounding—unmeasured factors that might still influence results—in this observational study, the consistency of our findings across multiple oxidative stress biomarkers (MDA, AOPP, SOD, GSH-Px) and their coherent associations in network analysis strengthen the robustness of our conclusions. Importantly, the absence of formal multivariable adjustment in the mediation analysis means that the observed associations may be influenced by residual confounding from unmeasured variables, such as genetic predisposition, nutritional status, or environmental factors. Future prospective studies, with multivariate adjustment or propensity score matching, would further address these limitations.

Notably, total IgE levels showed a trend toward elevation in the consolidation group compared to the non-consolidation group, although this difference did not reach statistical significance. This observation is consistent with accumulating evidence implicating IgE-mediated immune responses in the pathogenesis of severe MPP. Poddighe et al. [33] demonstrated that hospitalized children developing M. pneumoniae–related extra-pulmonary diseases had significantly elevated total serum IgE compared to those with respiratory infections alone, suggesting that atopy may serve as a biological marker of predisposition to extra-pulmonary complications. Bao et al. [34] further identified atopic status as a risk factor for refractory MPP, supporting the role of Th2 immune polarization in disease refractoriness. Moreover, Zhou et al. [35] reported that elevated IgE levels were associated with increased complications in MPP and proposed IgE as a potential biomarker for complication risk. The failure to achieve statistical significance in the present study likely reflects the modest sample size (n = 81) and the large inter-individual variability of IgE levels in pediatric populations. Future studies with larger cohorts should further evaluate the role of total IgE in the oxidative stress–inflammation network of MPP.

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the single-center retrospective design may limit the generalizability of our findings to broader pediatric populations with varying clinical characteristics and healthcare settings. Second, the relatively modest sample size, while demonstrating adequate statistical power for the primary between-group comparison of MDA, may limit the reliability of more complex multivariate analyses. The network model incorporating 9 nodes and the mediation model examining multiple candidate pathways may be susceptible to overfitting given the sample-to-variable ratio. Bootstrap stability analysis suggested acceptable centrality stability, but these complex analytical findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require replication in larger, independent cohorts. Third, the cross-sectional nature of BALF sampling precludes assessment of temporal dynamics in oxidative stress biomarker trajectories. Fourth, although total IgE was included in the laboratory evaluation, the between-group difference did not reach statistical significance, precluding its inclusion in downstream correlation, mediation, and network analyses. Whether total IgE interacts with oxidative stress pathways in MPP warrants investigation in larger cohorts. Fifth, the extra-pulmonary organ involvement recorded in this study represents end-organ complications identified during routine clinical care, rather than a systematic assessment of classic M. pneumoniae-associated immune-mediated extra-pulmonary manifestations such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome, encephalitis, or Guillain-Barré syndrome. Subtle or transient manifestations may have been under recognized in this retrospective design. Consequently, while mediation analysis provides statistical evidence for the NLR-MDA-Consolidation pathway, definitive causal inferences require validation through prospective longitudinal studies or experimental models. Future multicenter, prospective cohort studies with larger sample sizes and serial BALF sampling are needed to validate these findings and establish causal relationships between oxidative stress biomarkers and clinical outcomes in pediatric MPP.

Conclusions

This exploratory study demonstrated an oxidative stress–antioxidant imbalance in the BALF of children with MPP-associated consolidation. Mediation analysis suggested that NLR may be associated with consolidation development, potentially through lipid peroxidation pathways. Network analysis revealed enhanced interactions among immune, inflammatory, and oxidative stress biomarkers in the consolidation state. These findings are hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in larger, prospective multicenter studies.

Clinicopathological significance of AKT and phosphorylated AKT expression in hepatocellular carcinoma: A Meta-Analysis
Authors: Yongjing Ma, Mengfan Chen, Zhong Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe implications of AKT and phospho-AKT (p-AKT) expression patterns in various malignancies remain a subject of considerable scientific interest. Current evidence regarding their association with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression by immunohistochemical (IHC) analyses demonstrates significant inconsistencies.ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the clinical significance of AKT and p-AKT expression in HCC patients, with specific emphasis on their correlations with clinicopathological parameters, to provide insights to inform clinical decision-making.MethodWe conducted a systematic literature search across six major electronic databases through February 25, 2024, using search terms related to AKT, p-AKT, IHC, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Eligible studies were selected through strictly screening according to the inclusion/exclusion criteria, with methodological quality assessed via the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS). Statistical analyses were performed using Stata 15.0 software.ResultA total of 17 eligible publications with a total of 1595 patients were analyzed in the meta-analysis. AKT overexpression in HCC correlated with advanced TNM stage (OR = 3.698, 95% CI = 1.224–11.167), vascular invasion (4.121;1.483–11.447), lymph node metastasis (2.958;1.188–7.367), capsule integrity compromise (2.491;1.192–5.206), and portal vein cancer thrombus (6.919;1.240-38.619). p-AKT expression in HCC demonstrated associations with tumor grade (2.789;1.379–5.641), TNM stage progression (3.058;1.779–5.255), tumor size > 5 cm (2.507;1.056–5.953), portal vein cancer thrombus (4.280;1.798–10.188), and cirrhotic history (1.933;1.226–3.047). For results with significant heterogeneity, we used a random-effects model to pool the effect sizes and conducted subgroup analyses as well as meta-regression analyses. The results indicated that some differences between p-AKT and antibody thresholds were also observed in the subgroup of antibody thresholds, which deserves further research.ConclusionExpression of AKT is associated with TNM stages, venous vascular invasion, metastasis of lymph node, integrity of capsule and portal vein cancer thrombus. And p-AKT expression is associated with histological grades, portal vein cancer thrombus, liver cirrhosis, tumor size and TNM stages. This synthesis establishes AKT as a biomarker for advanced HCC features including metastatic potential and vascular complications, while p-AKT demonstrates dual associations with both tumor progression markers and underlying hepatic pathology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14948-6.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, AKT, Phospho-AKT, Immunohistochemistry, Meta-analysis

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most frequently diagnosed cancer globally and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1], with a 5-year survival rate of less than 12% [2]. The fact that many patients are diagnosed in advanced stages, coupled with limited treatment options, underscores the serious threat that HCC poses to global health [3–5]. Consequently, the developments of early diagnostic markers and mechanism-based therapies are critical and needed to be further studied. In HCC, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is abnormally activated in approximately 50% of cases [6, 7], which is a promising target for tumor therapy [8].

AKT, a serine/threonine protein kinase, is a central molecule in this pathway, playing a pivotal role in various biological processes, including cell proliferation, glucose metabolism, migration, apoptosis, cell cycle regulation, and angiogenesis [9]. AKT exists in three subtypes: AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3 [10], and it can be activated in response to upstream PI3K and downstream mammalian target of rapamycin complex 2 (mTORC2) [11]. Previous studies have demonstrated that that AKT activation is an important signaling event in carcinogenesis and the progression of numerous human cancers [12].

p-AKT, the active form of AKT, regulates downstream effectors involved in cell survival, protein synthesis, metabolism, and cell-cycle progression [13]. Studies have shown that overexpression of p-AKT is associated with the prognosis of various cancers [14]. Therefore, analyzing AKT/p-AKT expression in relation to HCC clinical characteristics is vital.

Despite these insights, previous studies have reported inconsistent associations between AKT/p-AKT expression and HCC. Therefore, we conducted a meta-analysis to address this inconsistency and clarify the correlation.

Methods

We conducted a meta-analysis, registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024551957). This manuscript follows the PRISMA 2020 reporting guidelines9 and the PRISMA checklist are in Table S1 and S2.

Search strategy and selection criteria

A systematic review of the literature was performed using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The study conducted a comprehensive search from PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, China national knowledge infrastructure (CNKI), China Science and Technology Journal Database (CSTJ), and WanFang Database in order to identify relevant literature. Retrieved papers were online in databases up to February 25, 2024. The search terms were adopted as follows: (1) “Hepatocellular Carcinoma” OR “Hepatocellular Carcinomas” OR “Carcinomas, Hepatocellular” OR “Liver Cancer” OR “Liver Cancers” OR “Liver Cell Carcinoma” OR “Liver Cell Carcinomas” OR “Carcinoma, Liver Cell” OR “Carcinomas, Liver Cell” OR “Liver tumor” OR “Liver tumors”; (2) “AKT” OR “p-AKT” OR “protein kinase B” OR “PKB”; (3) “Immunohistochemistry” OR “IHC”. The final literature search strategy was obtained by combining the above three factors.

The following inclusion criteria were considered: (1) Histologically confirmed HCC tissues; (2) AKT and p-AKT expression evaluated in the primary liver cancer tissues; (3) Adequate indicators of clinical pathological features related to AKT and p-AKT expression, as well as their OR and 95% CI; (4) Experimental or observational studies. Studies were excluded if: (1) non-human experiments; (2) Other pathological types of liver cancer; (3) Without expression of AKT and p-AKT detected by IHC based on liver cancer tissue; (4) Insufficient data or data not available; (5) Duplicated data; (6) Review articles, case reports and case series, Conference abstracts; (7) Publications not in English or Chinese.

Data extraction and quality assessment

According to PRISMA guidelines, each step was performed independently by two investigators (Ma Y. and Chen M.) and discrepancies were decided by the third investigator. Briefly, 2070 retrieved manuscripts were first imported into Endnote, automatic deduplication was conducted, then further manual deduplication was conducted by comparing similar citations. Next, manuscripts were screened by analyzing titles and abstracts, based on the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Finally, review the selected articles through the entire text, and the information was extracted from the included studies as follows: first author; year of publication; original country; number of cases; detect method; age, gender, tumor size, number of tumor, grade, lymph node metastasis, TNM stage, venous vascular invasion, Lymphatic vascular invasion, vascular tumor thrombus, condition of liver cirrhosis and HBV infection (clinical characteristics). It should be noted that duplicate publications or multiple publications based on the same data, choose the most comprehensive one.

The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) was applied to evaluate the quality of studies enrolled in this study. The NOS is scored out of a maximum of 9 points and we considered 0–3 points to be low quality, 4–6 to be moderate and 7–9 to be high. The NOS was performed by two authors and, where the overall rating differed between reviewers, a third author arbitrated. The study was considered as a high-quality study if the score of the NOS scale reached higher than seven (Table S3).

Statistical analysis

The STATA15.0 was used to analyze the data. The effect sizes of the relationship between clinical characteristics and AKT, p-AKT expression were odds ratio (OR) and shown through forest plots. The I2 test was used to check the heterogeneity among merged results. I2 > 50% was seen as strong heterogeneity. The fixed effect model was used when there was no heterogeneity in the data. Otherwise, the random effect model was applied to merge data. If I² is greater than 50%, we will explore the sources of heterogeneity through subgroup analysis and meta-regression analysis. Begg’s and Egger’s tests were used to examine the publish bias quantificationally.

Results

Characteristics of included studies

The original search yielded 124 results in PubMed, 621 in Embase, 29 in Web of Science, 5 in CNKI, 1206 in WanFang Database and 85 in China Science and Technology Journal Database. A total of 2070 articles were exported into Endnote X8 for screening. After duplicate removal, 1967 articles were subjected to the first round of screening, in which, 1841 articles were eliminated depending on the titles, abstracts, and key terms unrelated to HCC, AKT and p-AKT. Subsequently, 126 articles were identified as potentially relevant. In the second round of screening, 109 articles were excluded due to their study design: non immunohistochemical detection of related molecular expression (n = 27), lack of relevant outcome measures (n = 63), molecular mechanism studies (n = 10), without required type of literature (n = 2), without single expression of AKT or p-AKT (n = 5), duplicated studies (n = 2). In conclusion, a total of 17 articles matched all inclusion criteria and were selected for inclusion in this work. An outline of the literature search procedure is presented in Fig. 1. Of these studies, 4 were about the expression of AKT [15–18], and 11 articles were about the expression of phosphorylated AKT [19–29]. Two articles contained both expression of AKT and p-AKT [30, 31].

Study and patient characteristics

The published year of enrolled studies was from 2005 to 2018, which was a relatively longer time span. 1595 patients were included, with sample sizes ranging from 35 to 202 individuals. tissues however, most of them in Asia, especially in China. Notably, most studies utilized fresh/frozen tissue; however, five studies did not report the preservation methods [16, 20, 21, 27, 28]. The expression level of AKT and p-AKT was detected in the tissues, with rabbit, mouse or goat antibodies, and polyclonal or monoclonal antibodies, but 1 study [22] didn’t reported what kind of p-AKT they used. The cut-offs were identified by the extent or intensity, but the cut-off values of AKT or p-AKT varied in each study. The main characteristics of these studies are shown in Table S4.

Quality assessment

According to the NOS standards, the bias risk and quality of the 17 studies included in this study were assessed respectively (Table S3). The quality scores of 17 studies ranged from 6 to 8, with a mean of 8.24. Among them, the average quality assessment score for the 4 studies on AKT expression is 8.0, the average quality score for the 11 studies on p-AKT expression is 7.0, and the 2 studies contained both expression of AKT and p- AKT, with a mean of 7.5. None of these 17 studies obtained a NOS ≤ 5, indicating that all of them had high levels of methodological quality in our meta-analysis.

Meta-analysis results

The relationship between AKT and p-AKT expression and clinical characteristics in hepatocellular carcinoma

According to the results of the whole population (Table S4), the study found that the expression of AKT in hepatocellular carcinoma were correlated with TNM stage, venous vascular invasion, metastasis of lymph node, integrity of capsule and portal vein cancer thrombus. We also found that the expression of p-AKT were related to grade, TNM stage, tumor size, portal vein cancer thrombus and history of liver cirrhosis. However, the correlation was not found of AKT and p-AKT expression level with gender, age, number of tumor, serum AFP level and HBsAg (+). Higher TNM stages, positive venous vascular invasion, positive metastasis of lymph node, without intact capsule and positive portal vein cancer thrombus were risk factors for the high expression of AKT. And higher TNM stages, advanced tumor grading, larger size of tumor, positive portal vein cancer thrombus and without liver cirrhosis were all risk factors for the high expression of p-AKT in hepatocellular carcinoma, the pooled odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence interval were as follows.

TNM stages

A total of ten studies [17, 18, 21, 23–25, 27, 28, 30, 31]reported the AKT and p-AKT expression in different stage of hepatocellular carcinoma, significant heterogeneity was detected between the all of these studies(AKT: χ2 = 11.90, I2 = 74.8%, p < 0.01; p-AKT: χ2 = 19.06, I2 = 63.3%, p < 0.01), so the random effects model was employed to perform the meta-analysis. The pooled OR for the articles on AKT expression was 3.698 (95%CI: 1.224–11.167, p < 0.05), and the pooled OR for the articles on p-AKT expression was 3.058 (95%CI: 1.779–5.255, p < 0.05), the difference was statistically significant. Both merged OR values are larger than 1, indicating that AKT and p-AKT expression in the higher pathological grades (III, IV) tissues exceeded with lower pathological grades (I, II), Figs. 2 and 3 shows the forest plot for the included patient sets.

Subgroup analysis of TNM staging and AKT expression

In the studies focusing on AKT expression and TNM staging, all included studies were from China and used polyclonal antibodies. Therefore, subgroup analysis was performed only based on antibody thresholds. The pooled ORs for the three threshold subgroups (the number of positive cells is greater than 10%, 25%, and 50%) were 9.205 (95% CI: 3.616–23.431, I²=0), 3.405 (95% CI: 1.238–9.366), and 1.051 (95% CI: 0.462–2.392), respectively (Table S5).

Grade

There are 7 articles reporting the relationship between p-AKT expression and histological grade [19–21, 23, 26, 27, 31], and 2 articles reporting the relationship between histological grade and AKT expression [18, 31]. The final results showed that high p-AKT expression was significantly correlated with advanced histological grades (III/IV) compared to low-grade tumors (I/II) (I2 = 71.5%, OR = 3.052, 95% CI: 1.374–6.779; P < 0.005), while there was no significant correlation between AKT expression and histological grade. Figure 5 shows the forest plot. Both use random effects models to merge effect sizes.

Due to the high heterogeneity in studies on p-AKT and histological grade, we also conducted subgroup analyses, and the results are shown in Table S6.

Vascular invasion

Because of the inability to merge data on p-AKT expression and vascular invasion, this meta-analysis only analyzed the relationship between AKT expression and vascular invasion [16, 18]. Due to low heterogeneity (I2 = 0, p < 0.05), we use the fixed effects model to combine effect sizes (OR = 4.121, 95%CI: 1.483–11.447, p < 0.05).

Metastasis of lymph node & integrity of capsule

Five articles contained data about AKT expression levels and clinicopathological factors including lymph node metastasis [15, 17, 30] and integrity of capsule [16, 18]. The pooled OR indicated significant correlations between AKT expression levels and lymph node metastasis and integrity of capsule. Both them used fixed-effect models to combine OR values, although the former(I2 = 50.6%, OR = 2.958, 95%CI:1.188–7.367, p < 0.05) had a slight heterogeneity. AKT expression was associated with compromised capsule integrity (I2 = 8.6%, OR = 2.491, 95%CI:1.192–5.206, p < 0.05).

Tumor size

The pooled results showed that p-AKT expression was increased in patients with tumor size > 5 cm (OR = 2.507, 95% CI: 1.056–5.953; P < 0.05). However, we detected no significant relationships between AKT expression and tumor size.

Portal vein cancer thrombus

A total of three studies [24, 30, 31] reported relationship between AKT and p-AKT expression and portal vein cancer thrombus in hepatocellular carcinoma. we employ the fix effects model, the pooled OR for the articles on AKT expression was 6.919 (95%CI: 1.240-38.619, p < 0.05), and the results of p-AKT expression showed that the difference was significantly associated with portal vein cancer thrombus (OR = 4.280, 95%CI: 1.798–10.188, p < 0.005).

Liver cirrhosis

The pooled results showed that without cirrhosis was risk factor for the high p-AKT expression (OR = 1.972, 95% CI: 1.199–3.242; P < 0.005) [20, 23, 25], while there was no significant correlation between AKT expression and cirrhosis.

Heterogeneity

Most variables were analyzed using a fix-effects model because of the low heterogeneity among studies, while the random-effect model was used for the relationship between the expression of AKT and TNM stage (I2 = 74.8%, P = 0.9212). Also, the relationship between the expression of p-AKT and grade (I2 = 71.5%, P = 0.8073), tumor size (I2 = 80.6%, P = 0.7706) and TNM stage (I2 = 63.3%, P = 0.3676) analyzed through the random-effects model.

Given the high heterogeneity, we conducted subgroup analyses as well as meta-regression analyses based on confounding factors such as country and the specificity of immunohistochemical methods, including antibody clone type and threshold value. To explore the sources of this heterogeneity, we performed subgroup analyses and meta-regression analyses stratified by country and the specificity of immunohistochemical methods, including antibody clone types and thresholds. The results indicated that the I² statistic in the subgroup analysis based on antibody thresholds was substantially lower compared with the pooled I² statistic, and the meta-regression analysis yielded a p-value < 0.05, suggesting that antibody threshold may be one of the sources of heterogeneity. In contrast, no significant reduction in heterogeneity was observed in the subgroup analyses based on country and antibody clone types; furthermore, the meta-regression analysis for these factors showed a p-value ≥ 0.05, indicating that their association with heterogeneity is not statistically significant.

Publication bias

The present work evaluated possible publication bias using Begg’s and Egger’s tests. For the relationship between AKT expression and TNM stage, p-values of 0.174 and 0.286 were obtained using Begg’s and Egger’s tests (Fig. 6). And for the relationship between p-AKT expression and TNM stage, p-values of 0.386 and 0.226 were obtained using Begg’s and Egger’s tests (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for approximately 830,000 annual fatalities worldwide, representing a significant public health concern [35]. Understanding the pathogenesis of HCC and identifying novel diagnostic tools, biomarkers, prognostic strategies, and therapeutic approaches are therefore critical. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that the activation of oncogenic pathways, particularly the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway, plays a pivotal role in HCC progression and tumor development [36–39]. Furthermore, AKT pathway activation has been identified as a significant risk factor for early recurrence and poor prognosis in liver cancer patients [40]. To address these gaps, we conducted a comprehensive meta-analysis to evaluate the expression of AKT and p-AKT in HCC patients and their associations with clinicopathological parameters.

This study ultimately included 17 eligible studies through a comprehensive search and standard quality assessment. The meta-analysis showed that advanced TNM stages (III&IV), positive venous vascular invasion, positive lymph node metastasis, lack of capsule integrity and positive portal vein cancer thrombus were all significantly associated with high expression of AKT in HCC patients. Additionally, our pooled results showed that p-AKT expression was increased in patients with advanced TNM stages (III&IV), higher tumor grading (III&IV), larger size of tumor (> 5 cm), positive portal vein cancer thrombus and non-cirrhosis. We noticed that p-AKT is negatively correlated with liver cirrhosis, which may be associated with the ability of p-AKT to inhibit the TGF-β pathway and reduce the synthesis of collagen I and α-SMA [41]. Additionally, a study by Deng G et al. demonstrated that Akt activation can block the progression of fibrosis by inhibiting the Fas/FasL apoptotic pathway, reducing the release of inflammatory factors (e.g., TNF-α), and suppressing the transformation of hepatic stellate cells (HSC) into myofibroblasts [42]. These studies are all consistent with the results of our research.

One notable strength of this study lies in its systematic and comprehensive search of literature pertaining to AKT and p-AKT expression levels, as well as their association with clinical characteristics. Secondly, another strength is its focus on elucidating the relationship between AKT and p-AKT expression levels and clinicopathological variables in HCC.

However, the findings of these studies exhibited significant heterogeneity, which necessitates cautious interpretation of the results. It should be noted that due to the limited number of reported studies on “non-Asian countries” in existing literature and the lack of standardized criteria for antibody thresholds, some subgroups in this study, such as the “antibody threshold ≥ 25%”, “antibody threshold ≥ 50%”, and “non-Asian countries” subgroups, included only 1 study each. These findings are considered exploratory due to insufficient statistical power, and their generalizability may be restricted. Therefore, the clinical reference value of the results from the aforementioned subgroups should be interpreted with caution.

Currently, there is no unified standard for the antibody threshold of AKT/p-AKT. To ensure the scientific validity and referential value of the threshold setting in this study, we have defined the threshold for AKT/p-AKT expression levels with reference to the positive determination criteria of PD-L1 [32–34]. It should be clarified, however, that these thresholds are specific criteria designed for predicting immunotherapy efficacy, and their applicability in the detection of AKT/p-AKT in HCC has not been systematically validated. Differences between PD-L1 and AKT/p-AKT in terms of biological functions and clinical significance may result in the inability of PD-L1-based thresholds to be directly generalized to AKT/p-AKT studies, which may also be one of the reasons for variations in effect sizes under different thresholds in subgroup analyses. We recommend following the framework of international guidelines such as REMARK (Reporting Recommendations for Tumor Marker Prognostic Studies) [43] and establishing standardized protocols for antibody validation, threshold determination, and result reporting through multicenter collaboration, to ensure that future meta-analyses and clinical trials are based on reproducible and clinically meaningful standards.

Furthermore, although the present study confirmed through subgroup analyses and meta-regression that antibody threshold is one of the important sources of heterogeneity, it should be acknowledged that heterogeneity in clinical studies is often the result of the combined effects of multiple factors. Another methodological consideration is the heterogeneity in tissue handling across included studies. Although most studies specified the use of fresh or frozen tissues for IHC detection of AKT/p-AKT, 5 studies [16, 20, 21, 27, 28] did not report tissue preservation methods (e.g., fixation type, storage conditions). Unstandardized tissue handling may introduce bias, as factors such as delayed fixation or improper freezing can affect protein stability and immunoreactivity, potentially altering AKT/p-AKT expression detection results. This inconsistency may contribute to the observed heterogeneity in meta-analysis outcomes and warrants attention in future studies. Other potential factors not included in the analysis may also be involved, which warrant further investigation.

Although the publication bias analysis did not indicate significant bias, there are three limitations of this study that warrant attention: First, the inclusion criteria were restricted to studies published in English or Chinese, which may have led to the exclusion of relevant data from studies published in other languages. This has, to some extent, limited the number and representativeness of eligible studies. Second, the participants in the included studies were predominantly from the Chinese population, which raises potential concerns about the generalizability of the results. Although subgroup analysis showed that country was not a significant source of heterogeneity, the uneven sample composition (e.g., insufficient data from non-Chinese populations) may have resulted in an inadequate understanding of the effect characteristics in populations from other regions (such as European or American populations). Furthermore, in Table S3 (Newcastle-Ottawa Scale quality assessment), although most studies scored 1 in the “Controlling for Confounding Factors” domain, specific adjusted variables were not fully reported in some cases. Key unadjusted or unreported confounders may introduce bias: For instance, variables such as HBV status, cirrhosis etiology, and preoperative treatment history were not uniformly reported or adjusted across all studies, which may affect the stability of pooled results and warrant cautious interpretation.

These limitations indicate that future studies could further enhance the comprehensiveness and generalizability of evidence through standardized methodologies, expanded data diversity, and the promotion of international multicenter collaborative research, thereby making conclusions more globally relevant to clinical practice.

Notably, the observed association between high AKT/p-AKT expression and aggressive tumor features (e.g., advanced staging, vascular invasion) is derived from cross-sectional analyses of IHC data in existing studies, providing preliminary insights into their potential clinical utility. In the context of diagnostic challenges in AFP-negative or radiologically atypical suspected HCC cases, IHC detection of AKT/p-AKT in preoperative biopsy specimens may serve as an exploratory supplementary biomarker. In such scenarios, high AKT/p-AKT expression might suggest consideration of prioritizing definitive liver biopsy for histopathological confirmation or initiating intensified surveillance (e.g., quarterly imaging follow-up) to reduce the risk of missed diagnoses. However, it is important to acknowledge that this proposed approach remains preliminary, as robust validation through prospective studies—including assessments of sensitivity, specificity, and ROC curve analysis—is required to establish its clinical reliability before widespread application in routine practice.

Furthermore, as key effectors of the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway, AKT/p-AKT represents a promising therapeutic target in precision medicine. Emerging clinical trials targeting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway have shown promising results: For instance, Phase I/II clinical trials involving multiple solid tumors have demonstrated that the Akt inhibitor GDC-0068, administered either as monotherapy or in combination regimens, is safe and well-tolerated [11]. Additionally, the pan-Akt inhibitor ARQ 751 (an allosteric modulator) has exhibited preliminary efficacy in patient subgroups with pathway activation when used in combination with sorafenib (NCT02761694) [11, 44]. Findings from the present study suggest that HCC patients with high AKT/p-AKT expression may derive greater benefits from such targeted interventions, which also underscores the necessity of conducting prospective studies to validate AKT/p-AKT as a predictive biomarker for treatment response.

The data from this study provide a theoretical basis for patients with high AKT/p-AKT expression, who may derive greater benefits from targeted pathway inhibition.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our meta-analysis demonstrates that AKT and p-AKT overexpression are significantly associated with several clinicopathological parameters, including TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, and portal vein cancer thrombus. These findings may provide valuable insights for clinicians in identifying candidates for anti-AKT/p-AKT therapies and improving the management of HCC patients. Further studies are warranted to validate these associations in diverse populations and to explore the potential of AKT and p-AKT as therapeutic targets.

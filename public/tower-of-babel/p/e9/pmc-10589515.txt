Reference intervals of biochemical analytes in healthy adults from northern China: A population-based cross-sectional study
Authors: Siming Jia, Liu Wei, Xiaoying Shi, Dacheng Sun, Tailong Shi, Hongzhi Lv, Wei Chen
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Reference intervals (RIs) of laboratory testing play a fundamental role in medical activities. RIs vary greatly for populations in different areas This study aimed to evaluate the age- and sex-specific reference intervals for a healthy population in a typical city of northern China. A cross-sectional study was performed in 4 tertiary care centers of Shijiazhuang of China, biochemical analytes were analyzed using a Beckman Coulter AU5800 (Beckman Coulter Inc., Brea, CA). A total of 42,979 healthy individuals were involved in this study. Grouped by age- and sex-specific, reference intervals of all the measures have been established. We found that different age groups of males and females have significant differences (all P < .001) in levels of various biochemical analytes. We provided a comprehensive age- and sex-specific RIs for biochemical analytes, which showed dynamic changes with both age and sex. For the local population, the reference intervals established here can be adopted in other clinical laboratories after appropriate validation.

1. Introduction

As is known to all, reference intervals (RIs) of the laboratory testing play a fundamental role in medical activities, which are often used as the gold standard to distinguish between healthy and sick individuals and help clinicians decide whether patients require further testing and examination during the clinical practice.[1] The accuracy and reliability of reference intervals were crucial. Therefore, when targeted at specific populations, continuous evaluation and establishing their reference interval population were important.

RIs often varies with age, gender, ethnic, and experimental methods. In China, most RIs are derived from textbooks or manufacturers, which mainly originate from laboratory data on western populations and without further validation.[2,3] The RIs among different ethnicities may vary significantly because of the gene discrepancy.[4] Therefore, whether these findings apply to the Chinese population is unknown. Until now, 2 studies have focused on the revised reference intervals of common biochemical analytes for adults living in Hong Kong and Shanghai.[5,6] These RIs mainly are drawn from the southern Chinese population. Nevertheless, considering the differences in geography, culture, dietary and lifestyles, etc, these results might not be applicable to populations in other parts of China. Xia et.al[7] performed a nationwide multicenter study of 3148 healthy volunteers from 6 Chinese cities, however, which was limited by the less representative sample. In their study, the RIs of northern Chinese were drawn from 537 adults in Beijing. Few published studies focus on the reference intervals of populations living in northern China. Therefore, defining the RIs which were appropriate for the population of northern China is necessary.

By reviewing the biochemistry test results of the local population-based database with a large number of reference individuals recruited from 4 tertiary care centers of Shijiazhuang, we established reference intervals specific and appropriate. Our study presents the age- and sex-specific reference intervals for 12 biochemical markers. Furthermore, the results of our study clearly verified that age and sex profoundly influence the circulating concentrations of different biochemical analytes.

2. Methods

2.1. Study population

From January 2014 to December 2019, a total of 42,979 Chinese adults (≥18 years old) underwent a regular health checkup at 4 tertiary care centers of Shijiazhuang Hebei. Shijiazhuang is the typical northern city of China, which contains 9816 thousand resident population.[8] Each individual involved in our study underwent an overall physical examination and completed a standardized and structured questionnaire, which included age, gender, ethnic background, smoking or alcoholic habit, existing disease if any, past medical history, use of drugs and family heredity. According to another original article: nationwide multicenter reference interval study for biochemical analytes in China.[7] Individuals are divided into groups by age, which includes 7 subgroups (18–27, 28–37, 38–47, 48–57, 58–67, 68–77, and above 78 years old). This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by Ethics Committees of the Third Hospital of Hebei medical university.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age < 18, (2) individuals with acute or chronic diseases that could affect the circulation or metabolism status,[9] including kidney disease, digestive diseases, circulation system diseases, rheumatic diseases, metabolic and nutritional diseases, endocrine disease, burns and muscle trauma, resistant hypertension[10]; (3) individuals who had a history of blood transfusion or blood donation within 3 months, (4) individuals with HIV or hepatitis diagnosed by clinical laboratory assays, (5) surgery performed during the past 6 months; (6) pregnant women; (7) heavy alcoholism and smoking status[11]; and (8) obesity (body mass index ≥ 28).[12] In order to ensure the high accuracy of the reference interval, each subgroup should have at least 400 individuals.[13]

2.2. Specimen collection and handling

Excessive physical exercise was forbidden in all volunteers for 3 days. During the night before sample collection, excessive eating/drinking was not allowed. The sample collection started from 8:00 am to 10:00 am. Before sample collection, all volunteers were requested to sit for 15 to 20 minutes.[14] After an overnight fast, 5 mL of blood was drawn from each individual. The sample was conserved in untreated vacuum tubes and left at room temperature (25 °C) for 30 minutes to clot. Then, the sample was centrifuged for 10 minutes at 1200 × g.

2.2.1. Assay of specimen tests.

Along with the 4 tertiary care centers, Beckman Coulter AU5800 (Beckman Coulter Inc., Brea, CA) was used to analyze the sample. The sample was measured by using the experiment machine and corollary reagents. Which contains the following analytes: alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT), total protein, Albumin (ALb), globulin (GLOB), total bilirubin, serum total cholesterol, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), very-low-density lipoprotein.

According to the manufacturer’s instructions, all analytes were measured within 2 hours. A multivibrator serum was used in the calibration procedure to ensure accuracy and precision.

2.2.2. Trueness assessment.

In order to ensure the traceability of outcomes, according to the guideline of JCTLM (Joint Committee on Traceability in Laboratory Medicine), standard reference materials and reference measurement methods/procedures were used to assess the analytical trueness. Prior to analysis, the analytical trueness of the participated laboratories was assessed, first of all, we tested the certified reference materials in same measuring systems of 4 study centers, 5 times per day for 2 days. Second, during the study, we assessed the analytical trueness every month. Third, we tested the reference materials by the same lot 5 times in each experience and calculated the average values. The analytical performance characteristics of the methods used and the manufacture stated traceability were listed on Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/K251.

In order to ensure the precision, all participating laboratories need to use the approved commercial quality control materials. The materials originate from the same manufacturer with the same lot number. Before the formal testing, these quality control materials were texted in laboratories for 4 times per day for 5 days. During sample analysis stage, in order to access the quality of routine laboratory testing and the consistency of test results between different laboratories, 2 commercial controls and one collect sample was tested. The precision should meet the desirable quality specification of biologic variations on the imprecision.

2.2.3. Processing of test results.

We access the relative bias between the means of measured values of reference materials obtained by the analytical systems and the assigned target values of reference materials, if the relative bias meets the desirable specification for inaccuracy of biologic variation, the reference intervals will directly established according to the measurement results. If the relative bias exceeds the quality specifications: the standard will be reestablished: (1) if the reference materials exceed one concentration levels, according to the means of reference materials and the assigned target values of the reference materials, we will establish a linear regression equation, which were used to adjust the reference results of the collected samples. (2) If the reference materials are of only one concentration level, such LDL and LDL, we will calculated and applied the correction factors directly to adjust the relevant results of the samples.

2.3. Statistical analyses

The research data were statistically analyzed with IBM SPSS for Windows (version 22.0, IBM Corporation, New York, NY). According to the CLSI EP28-A3 guidelines, outliers were eliminated using the Dixon D/R ratio rule.[15] Missing data were imputed by replacing with the mean of the other data, provided that at least 50% of items had been completed (pro-rating). The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the data. All data in this study were found to be non-normally distributed. All reference intervals were calculated using the median (M). According to the recommendations of the CLSI C28-A3 document,[16] the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the distribution of values was used to determine the lower and upper reference limits of biochemical analytes, and 90% confidence intervals of the reference intervals were also calculated. Differences between male and female groups were evaluated by the Mann–Whitney U test. The Kruskal–Wallis H test was applied to assess each biochemical analytes across different age subgroups. A two-sided P values ≤0.05 were considered statistically significant.

3. Results

According to the inclusion criteria, 42,979 healthy individuals (21,073 males, 21,976 females) were included in our study. Among all individuals, 31,465 (73.2%) were from urban areas and 11,514 (26.8%) were from rural areas. These individuals vary greatly in their occupation, educational and economical background. The baseline characteristics of the individuals are presented in Table 1. According to the results of nonparametric tests, the significant difference was observed between males and females (P < .001). The results for ALb, TBil, ALT, GGT, HDLC, and GLOB appear generally higher in males than in females.

4. Discussion

The RIs of different biochemical analyses are important in supporting clinical decision-making. Ideally, reference intervals should not differ depending on the experimental methods or applied population. However, as is known to all that the reference values of the different suitable crowd vary so widely. The carefully established and validated local reference intervals databases would serve as a standard for other research. In this study, the RIs of ALb, TBil, ALT, GGT, HDLC, and GLOB in males were obviously higher than of females, and the significant difference was also observed in other biochemical analytes, which is positive evidence to support the establishment of sex-specific reference intervals.

In our study, some reference intervals of biochemical analytes are slightly different from other reports.[17,18] These differences were well partly caused by the research methods used in this study. First, the sampling error of the small sample size was a major cause. In 2016, Xia et al[7] performed a study that focuses on the RIs of 28 Biochemical Analytes in China. the RIs of ALb, TBil, LDL, GGT in their study vary greatly from our study. In their study, a total of 3148 healthy from 6 Chinese city volunteers were recruited. Second, the method for selecting reference individuals and data analysis may influence the result. The latent abnormal values exclusion[13] method was used in Xia et al’s[7] study to derive RIs. The difference in data analysis methods may lead to different results.

Compare to the results of other studies, some reference intervals of biochemical analytes are slightly different. First, race differences may be the primary cause of the discrepancies of findings between ours and that of the western study. According to the statistics from the Chinese National Bureau of Statistics,[19] nearly 91.6% of the population in Northern China was the Han nationality. A previous study has verified that compared to other races of American males, the upper reference limits of AST in African-American males are 15% higher,[20] and the level of ALP in southeast Africa is significantly different from that in other races.[21] Therefore, it is unfit to directly applied the reference intervals of western study to the Chinese population. Second, the difference between the lifestyle, diet, and nutritional status of the study population might also contribute to this discrepancy. In 2015, Guo, et al performed a study which focuses on the reference intervals of some biochemical analytes for healthy adults from eastern China.[6] Compare to their study, the upper limit of GGT is slightly different from this study. In China, northerners prefer soy products containing a high amount of plant protein. Our results confirmed that establishing the reference intervals which apply to the local population is important.

In our study, compared to the female, the results showed that ALb, TBil, ALT, GGT, HDLC, and GLOB values appear generally higher in males (P < .001). Men own a greater muscle mass than women, which may be the primary cause of their higher ALT and AST values in vivo.[22] The gender difference in GGT and total bilirubin may be attributed to extra ethanol consumption in men as compared to women.[23] Total protein, ALB is the indicator of kidney function, the higher values may be due to the higher incidence of obesity in men than in women. Lower LDL values observed in women could in part because of the severity of visceral adipose tissue accumulation in men.[24]

It is worth noting that the RIs of young adults and old adults varies greatly. As people age, each indicator in vivo changes greatly. Therefore, for old people, adopting RIs from the literature or from the general population without considering the age aging changing might result in under- or over diagnosis of certain conditions.

5. Strengths and limitations

The strengths of this study included the multiple-center cooperation, population-based design, large sample size and the use of strict exclusion criteria. All of the above would increase the accuracy and precision of results.

The limitation of our study is as follows. First, this study is a cross- sectional analysis of different biochemical analytes, the causal relationships and the direction of associations could not conclude from this study. In the future study, it is more appropriate to apply a longitudinal study design, where the same individuals are followed up at different time points of age. Second, all individuals included in this study were from the same city. Therefore, the results provided in this study should be cautiously treated, in the context of representative deficiency.

6. Conclusion

In summary, the RIs of 12 biochemical analytes could be used as a reference based on population in Northern China.

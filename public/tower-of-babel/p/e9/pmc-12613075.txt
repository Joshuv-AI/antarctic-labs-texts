Comparative analysis of the aroma characteristics of three different cultivars of guavas under different storage conditions using HS-SPME-GC–MS and HS-GC-IMS
Authors: Lirong Xu, Meifan Pan, Xuemei Cheng, Zitong Liu, Beilei Pang, Ruiqi Zhang, Yifan Liu, Qin Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Volatile compounds of Pearl, Red, and Carmine red guava stored at ambient temperature and 6 °C were analyzed using HS-SPME-GC–MS and HS-GC-IMS. Esters and aldehydes were found to be the main volatile compounds of guava. GC–MS and sensory analysis indicated guava exhibited optimal flavor characteristics at ambient storage for 4 days. GC-IMS analysis showed that the Carmine red guava contained higher concentrations of most volatile compounds compared to the other two varieties e.g. 3-methylbutyl butanoate, methyl butyrate, butanoic acid hexyl ester, and 2-methylbutanal. The compounds in Red guava stored for 4 days increased significantly, including ethyl propanoate, Δ3-carene and 2-pentanol. Pearl guava had lower levels of volatile compounds, contributing to its lighter flavor profile. These differences in compound concentrations led to distinct flavor characteristics between the varieties. These findings provided an important theoretical basis for guava flavor quality maintenance and postharvest quality control.

Introduction

Guava (Psidium guajava Linn.), myrtle family, was introduced into China in the 17th century. The annual guava production was 4.3 million tons in China, with numerous cultivated varieties including Pearl guava, Carmine red guava, and Red guava (Ankad et al., 2024). Guava was abundant in nutrients, including vitamin A, vitamin C, protein and several trace elements (Mangaraj & Goswami, 2011; Murmu & Mishra, 2018). It could also be processed into fruit juice, dried fruit and other products. Guava was a typical climactic fruit that reached peak flavor quality during postharvest ripening but subsequently declined (Mitra et al., 2012; Nasima et al., 2019). Therefore, it is necessary to determine the optimal storage time and temperature for guava in practical consumption.

Low temperature and ambient storage were the most common storage method in daily life. Studies showed that the cold damage of guava was obvious when it was stored below 6 °C (Zhang, 2024). Chen et al. (2024) found that the respiration rate of guava fruits reached the maximum at 4–6 days under ambient storage conditions, the fruit became softer, and it tasted best during this period. The study also found that guava could be stored for over a week at ambient temperature after harvest (Chen et al., 2024). Although several studies had explored optimal storage temperatures (Majeed et al., 2024), few studies had systematically evaluated the changes of flavor quality in different temperatures in daily storage (ambient storage and refrigeration).

Flavor is the most important indicator of food acceptability, and traditional evaluation is mainly based on sensory evaluation. Besides its general refreshing fruitiness, its distinctive strong guava flavor was the key feature that set it apart from other fruits (Zhang et al., 2024). Solid phase microextraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) was a common method for the separation and identification of volatile compounds in food (Zhang, Bai, et al., 2023). Gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (GC-IMS) technology was a relatively new gas separation and detection technology. This technology combined the high separation capacity of gas chromatography with the rapid response of ion mobility spectrometry (IMS), and had been widely used in food flavor analysis (Liu et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2023; Huang et al.).

This study used GC–MS to analyze differences in volatile components among Pearl guava, Red guava, and Carmine red guava stored under ambient and 6 °C conditions at different storage time. Additionally, GC-IMS was utilized to examine the variances in volatile profiles between these varieties at their optimal storage duration compared to fresh samples. These analyses provided a basis for assessing the quality of fresh guavas, guiding their processing applications, and determining the optimal storage temperature.

Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

Mature fruits of Red, Pearl, and Carmine guava samples were harvested at commercial maturity from an orchard in Guangzhou (Guangdong, China). Fruits were selected for uniformity in size and peel color (Red and Pearl with green color; Carmine with light yellow color), smooth surface, moderate firmness. Fruits with mechanical damage, insect infestation and disease were discarded. They were stored at ambient temperature (25 °C) and refrigeration (6 °C). Fresh guava and those stored for 4, 8, and 12 d were selected for sensory evaluation and GC–MS analysis. After GC–MS analysis and sensory evaluation, the best flavor period was determined to be 4 days at ambient temperature. GC-IMS was used to analyze the changes in volatile compounds between fresh guavas and those stored at ambient temperature for 4 days. Other reagents and solvents were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagents (Shanghai, China).

Sensory evaluation

The sensory evaluation panel included ten participants (five female and five male), aged 19 to 40. All participants were initially screened for olfactory and gustatory functions to confirm the absence of any smell or taste disorders. The evaluations were conducted in a dedicated sensory evaluation booth under controlled environmental conditions: temperature (22 ± 1 °C), relative humidity (55 ± 5 %), with no interfering odors and soft lighting. Prior to the sensory evaluation, panelists underwent comprehensive training in fundamental sensory analysis techniques to enhance their familiarity with diverse sensory attributes. This training enabled them to accurately identify and describe the characteristic aroma and flavor profiles of guava. Participants were trained in standardized sensory evaluation techniques, including gentle odor sampling with brief sniffs to avoid olfactory fatigue. The sensory evaluation was conducted using a 10-point scale to assess guava flavor based on five key attributes: characteristic aroma, sweet aroma, fruity aroma, crisp aroma, and aroma acceptability. The panelists willingly accepted and consented to be involved in the sensory study.

Volatile components analysis by GC–MS

Approximately 3.0 ± 0.2 g of guava fruit sample was accurately weighed and placed into a 20 mL headspace vial, followed by the addition of 10 μL of 2-dimethyl-3-heptanone internal standard solution (50 μg/mL). The extraction was carried out with a 50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS fiber (Anpel, Shanghai, China) for 30 min at 60 °C. Volatile compounds were analyzed using an Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph (Agilent, USA) coupled with a mass spectrometer (MS) operating in electron ionization (EI) mode at 70 eV. The initial column temperature was maintained at 40 °C for 3 min, then increased to 100 °C at a rate of 6 °C/min, and further raised to 230 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min, where it was held for 3 min (Liu et al., 2024). The mass spectrometer was operated in a scanning range of 30–500 m/z. Volatile compounds were identified by comparing their retention indices (RI) and mass spectra with those of authentic reference standards. The quantification of each compound was calculated by the internal standard method (Xu et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2025).

Volatile components analysis by GC-IMS

The MXT-WAX column (30 m × 0.53 mm × 1 μm) was used, and the detection conditions were according to our previous study. Guava samples (1 ± 0.02 g) were placed in 20 mL vials. The sample bottle was incubated in a 40 °C incubator for 30 min, and then 500 μL of volatile sample was injected into an injection needle at 85 °C in a no-shunt mode with nitrogen as carrier gas and incubation at 500 RPM. The flow of nitrogen was 2 mL/min for 2 min, then increased to 10 mL/min for 10 min and increased to 100 mL/min for 15 min (Xu et al., 2022; Xu, Wang, Tian, Liu, et al., 2023; Xu, Wang, Tian, Wang, et al., 2023). Qualitative analysis of volatile compounds was performed based on their retention index, mass spectra of compounds in the NIST library, and drift time of standards in the GC-IMS library.

Statistical analyses

All analyses were conducted in triplicate, and the correlation results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Significant differences were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS 22.0 (IBM, Armonk, New York). GC-IMS data were analyzed and plotted using LAV software.

Results and discussion

Sensory evaluation of guava

As shown in Table 1, the sensory evaluation of guava was conducted based on five aspects: characteristic aroma, sweet aroma, fruity aroma, crisp aroma, and aroma acceptability. The results showed that the scores for characteristic aroma, fresh aroma, and aroma acceptability at the 4 days storage were significantly higher than other time points under ambient storage and refrigeration (P < 0.05), indicating that this period might be the optimal storage time. In terms of characteristic aroma, both Red guava and Carmine red guava had higher scores than Pearl guava after 4 days ambient storage (P < 0.05), suggesting potential varietal differences in the production or release of volatile compounds. These results were consistent with previous studies highlighting varietal differences in volatile compound profiles among different fruit species (Egea et al., 2014). After 4 days ambient storage, the sweet aroma scores were significantly higher compared to both other ambient storage time points and refrigeration (P < 0.05). Specifically, Pearl guava and Carmine red guava had significantly higher sweet aroma scores than Red guava after 4 days ambient storage (P < 0.05). For fruity aroma, the scores after 4 days ambient storage and refrigeration were both significantly higher than those at other time points (P < 0.05), with no significant differences among the varieties. In conclusion, 4 days ambient storage could optimize the aroma of guava, with Red guava and Carmine red guava exhibiting richer aromas than Pearl guava. Notably, Red guava exhibited slightly higher acidity compared to the other two varieties. GC–MS analysis of volatile compounds in different guava cultivars under different storage conditions

The GC–MS analysis identified a total of 10 aldehyde compounds, 2 alcohol compounds, 8 ester compounds, 2 ketone compounds, and 2 furan compounds. The results indicated that aldehydes and esters were the primary contributors to the aroma profile of guava. These compounds were key contributors to fruit aroma (Feng et al., 2025).

A heatmap was constructed based on the concentration of volatile compounds in guava samples (Fig. 1). It revealed that Carmine red guava contained higher levels of most volatile compounds compared to Red and Peal guava, with the Peal guava exhibiting the lowest compound concentrations. Key volatiles such as benzoic acid ethyl ester, (Z)-3-hexen-1-yl acetate and (E)-2-heptenal etc. reached peak concentrations after 4 days ambient storage. Significant variations in volatile aroma profiles were observed among different guava varieties. For example, heptanoic acid methyl ester and trans-β-ionone with fruity and floral flavor were unique to Carmine red guava. Several aldehyde compounds, including pentanal, (E)-2-hexenal, hexanal, and (E)-2-octenal, associated with fresh grassy flavor. Decanal and nonanal were known for their citrusy and floral aromas. The 4-ethylbenzaldehyde was characterized by its sweet and fruity aroma (Zhang et al., 2019). The aforementioned aldehyde compounds exhibited significantly higher concentrations in all guava varieties after 4 days ambient storage compared to other storage time points (P < 0.05). Furthermore, refrigerated samples had lower levels of volatile compounds than the samples during ambient storage on the same time (P < 0.05). It was worth noting that the levels of (E)-2-hexenal and hexanal significantly increased in various guava varieties after 4 days storage. In Pearl guava, Red guava, and Carmine red guava, the concentrations of (E)-2-hexenal reached 1571.83 μg/kg, 1722.54 μg/kg, 1887.27 μg/kg. The concentrations of hexanal reached 2693.65 μg/kg, 3155.07 μg/kg, 6505.07 μg/kg. The (E)-2-hexenal and hexanal could enhance the fruity aroma and freshness of guava. The significant increase in these aldehydes during the early storage period might be due to the degradation of fatty acids through the lipoxygenase pathway, promoting the accumulation of aldehydes (Tome-Rodriguez et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2009). Compared with fresh guava, (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol had green leaf and green apple flavor. The 1-hexanol had fruity and floral flavor (Wei et al., 2022). These two compounds significantly increased in all guava samples after 4 days at ambient storage (P < 0.05), while remaining higher than those in refrigeration storage samples at all time points. There were significant differences in the concentrations of (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol and 1-hexanol among different varieties (P < 0.05). The concentrations of (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol and 1-hexanol in Pearl guava (2234.99 μg/kg, 2746.96 μg/kg) and Carmine red guava (1160.26 μg/kg, 3416.88 μg/kg) were higher than that in Red guava (2020.20 μg/kg, 847.17 μg/kg).

Several ester compounds, such as hexanoic acid methyl ester (sweet fruity aromas), (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol acetate (fresh aroma), and heptanoic acid methyl ester (tropical fruity and creamy aromas), significantly contributed to the aroma profile (Li et al., 2021; Jordán et al., 2003). These compounds all significantly increased after 4 days storage at both ambient and refrigerated temperatures compared to fresh samples and decreased over time. The increase in ester compounds observed on day 4 was primarily attributed to the esterification reaction between acyl-CoAs and various alcohols and aldehydes derived from fatty acid degradation, which is catalyzed by alcohol acyltransferases (El Hadi et al., 2013). Carmine red guava consistently exhibited the highest concentrations, followed by Red and then Pearl guava under ambient storage. Compared to ambient storage, refrigeration maintained significantly lower concentrations at the same time points. Benzoic acid ethyl ester contributed a sweet, fruity aroma (George et al., 2023). It was exclusively detected in Carmine red guava. The concentration of benzoic acid ethyl ester significantly increased from 5.4 μg/kg in fresh samples to 1012.46 μg/kg after 4 days ambient storage (P < 0.05), followed by a decline to 350.26 μg/kg at day 8 in Carmine red guava. Under refrigeration, it increased to 892.1 μg/kg after 4 days but then reduced to 245.65 μg/kg at day 8 and further to 108.32 μg/kg at day 12 in Carmine red guava. Octanoic acid methyl ester with fruity aroma was found only in Pearl and Red guava, reaching the highest concentration at 4 days storage. Compared with ambient storage, refrigeration led to lower compound concentrations. The lower ester levels in refrigerated samples suggest an inhibition of this enzymatic pathway at low temperatures.

The 6-methyl-5-hepten-2-one, primarily responsible for fresh green and leafy aroma. The trans-β-ionone provided floral and fruity flavor in fruits (Guo et al., 2021). The trans-β-ionone was uniquely detected in Carmine red guava. The 6-methyl-5-hepten-2-one and trans-β-ionone increased significantly after 4 days at both ambient and refrigerated temperatures before decreasing over time. Refrigeration resulted in the lower concentrations of 6-methyl-5-hepten-2-one compared to ambient storage, with Carmine red guava exhibiting the highest levels, followed by Red and Pearl guava.

In conclusion, aldehydes and esters were the main contributors to guava aroma, with aldehyde compounds such as (E)-2-hexenal and hexanal being significantly increased after 4 days at ambient storage. The volatile substance concentration of samples stored at refrigerated temperature was generally lower than that of samples stored at ambient temperature. There were significant differences among different cultivars. The benzoic acid ethyl ester and trans-β-Ionone were unique to Carmine red guava, which constituted its unique aroma. Compared with the other two varieties, pearl guava had a lower content of most compounds and a lighter flavor. Most aromatic compounds reached their peak concentrations at 4 days storage and subsequently declined gradually, suggesting that this period represented the optimal stage for aroma intensity. However, prolonged storage (8–12 d) or refrigeration resulted in a reduction of aroma components in guava.

Quantitative analysis of volatile compounds in different guava cultivars after 4 days ambient storage using GC-IMS

The quantitative results of the compounds were presented in Table 3. The fruit aroma of guava mainly came from esters, alcohols, ketones, and aldehydes. Due to their low odor threshold, ester compounds played a key role in the formation of fruity and sweet aromas in fruits (Yuan et al., 2023). In fresh guava samples, the content of butanoic acid butyl ester (D), butanoic acid butyl ester (M), and methyl pentanoate in Carmine red guava and Red guava was significantly higher than that in Pearl guava (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage at ambient temperature, compared with fresh samples, the content of butanoic acid butyl ester (D), butanoic acid butyl ester (M), and methyl pentanoate in Red guava increased significantly (P < 0.05) to 4464.97, 3875.12, and 11,582.78 respectively. There was no significant difference compared with fresh Carmine red guava samples. While the content of compounds in Carmine red guava increased to 464.24, 4199.84, and 19,136.18 respectively. In Pearl guava stored for 4 days, the contents were only 587.86, 426.08, and 7737.21 respectively. After 4 days storage, the contents of butanoic acid butyl ester (D), butanoic acid butyl ester (M), and methyl pentanoate in Red guava and Carmine red guava were significantly higher than those of Pearl guava (P < 0.05).

The butanoic acid hexyl ester, which provided a rich fruity aroma, had the highest content of 3375.08 in fresh Carmine red guava samples, significantly higher than that in Red guava and Pearl guava (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the concentration of butanoic acid hexyl ester in Red guava increased to 3584.82, which was significantly higher than that in fresh Red guava (P < 0.05). The methyl butyrate reached the highest content with 8051.86 in fresh Carmine red guava, followed by Red guava with 2145.42, and Pearl guava with 293.07 (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the content in Carmine red guava increased to 9060.07, and that in Red guava reached 4380.05, both significantly higher than their fresh samples. The 3-methylbutyl butanoate (M) had a fruity and sweet flavor (Ma et al., 2024). In fresh samples, the content of 3-methylbutyl butanoate (M) was the highest in Red guava at 25173.7, significantly higher than that in Carmine red guava and Pearl guava.

Alcohol compounds usually added layers of flavor to fruits (Xu, Zang, Sun, & Li, 2023). Some alcohol compounds such as 1-Butanol, 3-methyl, 1-butanol, 1-hexanol (D), and 1-hexanol (M) had the highest content in fresh Carmine red guava, reaching 3828.32, 695.31, 12236.36, and 12,910.33 respectively, significantly higher than those in fresh Red guava and Pearl guava. Red guava showed a significant increase compared with fresh Red guava after 4 days storage (P < 0.05). The 1-hexanol (M) had a typical fruity and herbaceous aroma with a low odor threshold. The 1-hexanol (M) was extremely high in Carmine red guava (12,236.36). Contrasting with the sweet aroma of esters, the 1-hexanol (M) enhanced the flavor layers. The 1-hexanol (D) was less volatile due to its increased molecular weight compared to 1-hexanol (M), which may prolong the release time of the aroma. The 1-hexanol (D) content in Carmine red guava was the highest (12,910.33), which might maintain the durability of the aroma by slowly releasing green and floral notes, especially playing a role in stabilizing the flavor during the later ripening process (Liu et al., 2023).

Ketone compounds like 2-pentanone (D), which added a fruity aroma (Wang et al., 2023), had the highest content of 8484.41 in fresh Carmine red guava, followed by fresh Red guava with 7807.46 and fresh Pearl guava with 5708.5 (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the content in Red guava increased to 8538.53, significantly higher than that in fresh Red guava (P < 0.05). The 4-nonanone (M) had a cheese-like flavor and fruity sweetness (Zhao et al., 2023). The content of 4-nonanone (M) was the highest in Red guava at 29034.53 in fresh samples, followed by Pearl guava at 21762.74 and Carmine red guava at 8275.35 (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the content in Carmine red guava increased significantly to 13,127.36 compared with fresh Carmine red guava (P < 0.05). The fruity sweetness likely synergized with aldehydes (e.g. 2-methylbutanal) to amplify perceived sweetness in tropical fruits. This effect was particularly pronounced in Red guava, where the interaction with high β-pinene (pine wood flavor) generated a distinctive tropical aroma profile.

Aldehydes were important flavor compounds in many foods, especially in fruits and vegetables (Zhao et al., 2024). Aldehyde compounds such as 2-methylbutanal, acrolein, and butanal, which significantly affected the diversity and level of guava flavor through their unique grassy, caramel and fruity notes (Luo et al., 2019), had the highest content in Carmine red guava in fresh samples, followed by Red guava, and the lowest in Pearl guava (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the content of these compounds in Red guava increased significantly compared with fresh samples (P < 0.05). Compared with fresh guava, the content of acrolein in Carmine red guava stored for 4 days increased significantly.

The (+)-limonene mainly provided citrus aroma and freshness (Rong et al., 2024), while enhancing the sweetness flavor of the fruit. It interacted with other volatile components of guava, including aldehydes and esters, contributing to its unique tropical fruit aroma. The content of (+)-limonene (M) and (+)-limonene (D) in Carmine red guava was significantly higher than that in Red guava and Pearl guava in fresh samples, reaching 944.2 and 1535.3 respectively. After 4 days storage, the content in Carmine red guava increased significantly to 2456.68 and 2231.56 compared with fresh Carmine red guava samples (P < 0.05). The content of (+)-limonene (D) in Red guava also increased significantly after 4 days storage compared with fresh Red guava samples (P < 0.05). The β-pinene mainly provided fresh pine wood and herbal aroma (Zhang, Chen, et al., 2023). In fresh samples, the content of β-Pinene in Red guava was the highest, significantly higher than that in Carmine red guava and Pearl guava (P < 0.05). After 4 days storage, the content in Red guava increased significantly to 2142.99 compared with fresh Red guava.

In conclusion, the butanoic acid butyl ester, acrolein, and (+)-limonene(D) contents of Red guava were significantly increased (P < 0.05) after 4 days storage at ambient temperature, and the flavor intensity was improved. Butanoic acid, hexyl ester, 3-methylbutyl butanoate, and (+)-limonene (M) showed high concentration in Carmine red guava. Pearl guava showed the smallest increase in composition and lighter flavor performance after storage. In guava samples, esters predominated in contributing to the fruity flavor and acted synergistically with aldehydes, alcohols, and alkenes to form a complex tropical fruit flavor profile.

Conclusion

This study systematically elucidated the dynamic changes in volatile components across different guava varieties during storage. Sensory evaluation, GC–MS, and GC-IMS analyses were integrated to assess aroma quality. The peak aroma occurred after 4 days ambient storage. Aldehydes and esters were the primary contributors to the aroma profile. Red guava and Carmine red guava exhibited more abundant aromatic profiles, attributed to their higher concentrations of volatile compounds, such as ethyl benzoate and (+)-limonene. In contrast, the Pearl variety displayed a lighter flavor profile. This study systematically characterizes the dynamics of volatile compounds in guava during storage, providing insights for improving postharvest quality. Future research could explore the impact of storage strategies on flavor and elucidate the molecular role of key enzymes like lipoxygenase in flavor metabolism.

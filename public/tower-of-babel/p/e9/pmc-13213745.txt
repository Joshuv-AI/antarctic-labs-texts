Tapentadol and Zolpidem addiction management with Ayurvedic intervention: A case report
Authors: Priyanka Katru, Anshul Narula, Anita Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Opioids pose significant challenges worldwide. Tapentadol's unrestrained availability has raised concerns, as it is developing as an alternative for illicit pharmaceutical opioids. A case of 31-year-old patient complaining of anxiousness, profuse perspiration, body ache, lacrimation, restlessness, sleeplessness, and loose stools with history of Tapentadol and zolpidem abuse showed in the OPD on Oct 31, 2023. The patient's Clinical opiate withdrawal scale (COWS) evaluated withdrawal symptoms of the as moderate (score 28) and the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) showed the severe insomnia (score 25). Patient was prescribed with tapering dose of Sameergajakesari Rasa (375, 250, 125mg), Vishatindukadi Vati 250mg, Combination of Sitopaladi Churna 3g + Lakshmivilas Rasa 250mg + Tankan Bhasma 250 mg, over 3 weeks, Combination of Vaatvidhwansank Ras 250mg + Godanti Bhasma 250mg + Tryodshang Gugglu 500mg for first 2 weeks, Ksheerpaka of Ashwagandha Churna 3g + Shatavari Churna 2g + Gokshur Churna 1g and Brahmarasayan 5g for third week. Externally Dashmoola Tail Sarvanga Abhyanga, Sarvanga Swedana, Shirodhara with Til Taila and Brahmi Tail, Pranayama, Yoga and counselling. The total COWS score decreased from 28 prior to treatment to 2 after three weeks of treatment. Similarly, ISI score decreased from 25 to zero after treatment. The patient was treated solely using Ayurvedic treatment, including Satvajaya Chikitsa, and no complications were noticed during the course of treatment. Observations suggested that with the Ayurvedic management, withdrawals and insomnia were completely resolved within three weeks of treatment. The baseline investigations were normal, and the patient continued to practice yoga and counselling during and after therapy, abstaining for 60 days post treatment.

Introduction

The 2019 magnitude of substance use in India study found that pharmaceutical opioids were the second most misused opioids (0.96%), trailing only heroin (1.14%) [1]. With the scheduling of codeine, dextropropoxyphene, and tramadol in 2011, 2017, and 2018, respectively, there is a risk of abuse involving a newly introduced pharmaceutical opioid - Tapentadol. Although tapentadol has not yet been scheduled under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, there is developing clinical experience and initial proof indicating that it is abused in India. Globally, an estimated 61 million people used opioids in 2022, with India accounting for roughly 2% of total users [UNODC World Drug Report, 2023]. The increasing misuse of pharmaceutical opioids such as Tapentadol and Zolpidem highlights the need for integrative management techniques that combine classical and contemporary approaches. A study found that 25% of prescription opioid users abused or were dependent to tapentadol, with more than 80% taking other opioids concurrently. Chemists (without a prescription) accounted for 53% of tapentadol sources, while doctors (with prescriptions) contributed 40%. Tapentadol dependence resulted in considerably higher dose, duration, and overall prescription opioid use [2]. Tapentadol is a synthetic opioid with μ-opioid receptor agonist and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor properties. Tapentadol has μ-receptor agonism similar to that of morphine and tramadol [3]. And Zolpidem is a sedative-hypnotic that relaxes the mind and was developed specifically for people who suffer from insomnia. This medicine promotes sleep by shortening the time it takes to fall asleep, increasing overall sleep length, and reducing wakefulness. However, when users stop using, they frequently experience withdrawal symptoms such as anxiety, cravings, insomnia, and, in extreme circumstances, seizures [4]. Ayurveda texts have described opium under Upavisha (mild potency poisons) and have given both of its poisonous and therapeutic properties. Ahfeem (Opium) has been described in Anidra (insomnia), Vedna (pain), Graahi (absorptive), Agnimandya (diminution of agni) [5]. There is a unique concept in Ayurveda given by Acharya Charak called Padamshika Krama (alienation and adoption in quarter portion), provides vital support during the transition from Apathya (unwholesome) to Pathya (wholesome) [6]. This strategy incorporates a progressive adoption and departure procedure that can be used to Ahara (food), Vihara (life style), or Aushadh (medication). Padamshika Krama appears as a novel approach to avoiding sudden withdrawal from dangerous substances, especially narcotics. The strategy involves replacing addictive medicines with therapeutic alternatives or using a taper-up approach with the same drug as medicine to facilitate a slow and efficient detoxification process. The Ayurvedic medicine combinations administered in this case—Sameeragajakesari Rasa, Vishatindukadi Vati, Sitopaladi Churna with Lakshmivilas Rasa, and Tankan Bhasma, among others—were chosen for their Vata-pacifying, analgesic, anxiolytic, and Rasayana properties. The Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS; 0–4 no withdrawal, 5–12 mild, 13–24 moderate, 25–36 moderately severe, >36 severe) and the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI; 0–7 no insomnia, 8–14 mild, 15–21 moderate, 22–28 severe) were used as validated methods to assess outcomes objectively.

Case report

Patient information

A 31-year-old male pharmacist came to the outpatient department on October 31, 2023, with complaints of anxiety, irritability, sleeplessness, heavy sweating, acute body ache (especially in calves, lower back and all the joints), excessive tearing, runny nose, restlessness, diarrhea and mild tremors. These symptoms began 2 days before showing up in the outpatient department, following his abrupt cessation of Tapentadol and Zolpidem, which he had been taking and now wished to discontinue.

Past History: The patient, a 31-year-old pharmacist, was first prescribed Tapentadol 150 mg/day and Zolpidem 10 mg/day by his psychiatrist for post-COVID musculoskeletal pain, anxiety, and secondary sleeplessness in May 2021. However, overtime he began self-escalating the dose without supervision eventually reaching 500 mg per day. In the two weeks preceding up to the outpatient visit, the patient escalated his Tapentadol dose to 1000 mg per day and was also taking 20 mg of zolpidem.

Physical examination

On physical examination, no gross abnormality was noted except for tachycardia – 126 beats per minute and mild hypothermia – 97.6 F.

The diagnosis was Tapentadol and Zolpidem dependence with withdrawal syndrome (ICD-10 F11.23), which corresponds to Vata prakopa with Manovaha srotodushti according to Ayurvedic principles. Baseline hematological and biochemical tests (CBC, LFT, RFT, electrolytes) were within normal ranges. Although the patient exhibited slight depressed and anxious symptoms, the assessment focused on withdrawal and insomnia; thus, only the COWS and ISI scales were used.

Follow up and outcomes

Upon admission, COWS and ISI were used to assess withdrawal symptoms and insomnia severity for three weeks. After three weeks, Tapentadol withdrawal reduced from moderate to minimum, and considerable insomnia subsided. The total COWS reduced from 28 before treatment to 8 after two weeks, and then to 2 after three weeks of treatment [Table 2]. Similarly, the ISI scores were at 25 and dropped to 7 during the second week, eventually reaching zero after three weeks of treatment [Table 2]. There were no adverse events observed during the course of treatment period. Upon discharge, the patient was prescribed Ksheerpaka and Brahmarasayan for a month as Rasayana Chikitsa (rejuvenation and revitalization therapy) to boost immunity and promote strength. Following discharge, the patient continued to practice moderate yoga exercises (20-25 minutes per day of Sukshma Vyayama, Anulom Vilom, Bhramari, and Yoga Nidra), as well as Rasayana therapy. He was assessed every 45 days after discharge and telephonically tracked for up to 60 days, maintaining abstinence throughout from all forms of drug consumption. A telephonic follow-up conducted in December 2023, and January 2024 confirmed continued abstinence from Tapentadol and Zoldpidam. The patient reported stable sleep without pharmacological support, absence of cravings, and satisfactory occupational and social functioning. No relapse or withdrawal symptoms were reported during follow-up.

Discussion

As Tapentadol is a synthetic opioid agonist, the primary medication used to treat withdrawal symptoms in this case was the Ayurvedic formulation Sameeragajakesari Rasa [9], which contains Shudha Ahiphena as a key ingredient. It was started with 375 mg three times a day after a meal for one week, then reduced to 250 mg in the second week, 125 mg in the third week, and discontinued after three weeks. It may have alleviated symptoms such as Vishada (depression), Asthishoola (pain in the bones), Sandhishoola (pain in the joints), Kampa (tremors), Pratishyaya (catarrh), and Ashrustarva (tears).

Vishtindukadi Vati the 2nd drug has four important constituents, viz. Kupilu (Strychnos nux-vomica Linn.), Pugaphala (Areca catechu Linn.), Maricha (Piper nigrum Linn.), and Chincha (Tamarindus indica Linn.). Vishatindukadi Vati can be used for various diseased conditions such as Atisar (diarrhea), Peenas (running nose), Ajeerna (indigestion), Agnimandya (loss of appetite), Shool (chronic pain) conditions such as body ache and joint pain. The use of Vishatindukadi Vati is directly suggested in opium addiction [10]. Since all the above conditions are found in the form of withdrawal symptoms. It was preferred in the present case. The main content of Vishatindukadi Vati is Kupilu which having properties like Vata-Shamak (Vata pacificing), Chitta-Avasadahar (antidepressant properties) and Hridya Daurbalyahar (cardiac tonic) [11]. Pharmacological preparation of Kupilu calm down the withdrawal effect of opium like anxiety and agitation by its Chitta-Avasadahar property and running nose, diarrhea by its Grahi property. Muscular pain and Joint Pain by its Vata-Shamak property. Also, Nux-vomica is considered as antidote for of Ahipehna [12].

Tab. Sumenta is the polyherbal combination of anxiolytics, anti-stress and adaptogenic ingredients. It is proven to be effective in cases of mixed anxiety depression and associated other disorders and recommended as first line therapy in mild to moderate cases of depression and other psychiatric ailments.

The combination of Sitopaladi Churna [13], Shuddha Tankana, [14] and Laxmivilas Rasa [15] indicated for Pratishye (cold), Kasa (cough) associated with bronchitis, Rajayakshma (tuberculosis), supportive agent for allergy, viral respiratory infection and chest congestion. Also, Shuddha Tankana is a potent expectorant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, thus might have helped in relieving running nose and excessive sweating.

The combination of Vata Vidhwansank rasa is another formulation mentioned in ayurvedic texts primarily used for the treatment of Vata disorders, and Neuropathic Pain [16]. Godanti Bhasma is a best Mastishka Shamaka (CNS relaxant) in any condition where there is Udvegah (anxiety), Anidra, Bhaya (fear), Pralapa (excess talking) [17]. Tryodashang Guggul used in Sandhigata Vata (vata in joint/articulation), Asthigata Vata (vata in bones), Majjagata Vata (vata in bone marrow), Snayugata Vata (vata in sinew), Shool, Shirashool (headache), thus helped in relieving Sandhishoola, Kampa, Asthishoola, Anidra, Pralapa, Adhirata (restlessness). [18] And therefore, all these medications helped in reduction of COWS scale and ISI scale measurement as well as in improving tachycardia and hypothermia.

Rasayanas are commonly used to improve health and longevity. They help to improve the nutritional status of the body and alleviate symptoms of stress. Brahma Rasayan used for the management of mental conditions, including anxiety, poor cognition, and lack of concentration [19], along with Ksheerpaka of Ashwagandha, Shatavari and Gokshur, [20] provided Rasayana action and are Balya, so given in 3rd week to provide strength.

Sarvanga abhyanga and Swedana pacifies Vata related ailments, relieves fatigue and body ache, strengthens the body, calms the body, helpful in Kampa, and promotes sleep. This might have worked on the withdrawal symptoms such as bone and joint ache, tremors, sweating, restlessness, anxiety and therefore helped in reduction of COWS scale measurement.

In various research studies, Shirodhara with medicated Taila is effective to treat stress, anxiety, and insomnia and to relax the nervous system [21,22]. Also Brahmi (Centella asiatica. Linn) is Medhya, along with Til taila (sesame oil) which is Vatashamak [23], might have helped in reliving anxiety, restlessness, irritability and insomnia.

During Yoga and Pranayama, blood pressure and muscular tension drop in tandem with stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline. This also enhances the effectiveness of counselling by providing an alternative method of dealing with stress and cravings [24]. A recent study on opioid dependence syndrome reported significantly higher improvement in mood status and Quality of Life (QoL) in patients in Yoga group as compared to routine hospital care [25]. Both internal and external medication along with Satvajaya Chikitsa might have therefore helped in reduction of COWS scale as well as ISI scale measurement.

Previous integrative studies integrating herbal or mind-body therapies for opiate withdrawal showed clinical improvement, but few utilized validated measures or a follow-up strategy. The present case study contributes by quantifying outcomes using established methods (COWS, ISI) and implementing a systematic post-treatment follow-up.

According to ISI criteria, a drop to zero implies full remission (0-7 = no insomnia). This outcome, although unusual, is possible given short-term dependence and effective inpatient therapy.

The case study highlights the potential for combining pharmaceutical tapering (Padamshika Krama), Rasayana therapy, and Satvajaya Chikitsa within an integrative framework.

The main limitation is the single-case design and the lack of standardized mood or QOL scales, which limit generalizability. Future research on larger cohorts with extended follow-up is required.

Patient perspective

At discharge, the patient's physical and mental health had improved, with better digestion, fewer withdrawal symptoms, and resolved insomnia. Both the patient and the attendant felt satisfied with the treatment.

Conclusion

The patient was treated using Ayurvedic drugs, procedures, and Satvajaya Chikitsa based on the concepts of gradual adoption of wholesome habits and withdrawal of unwholesome habits, and there were no complications. Based on the observations of COWS and ISI, the withdrawals and insomnia were totally resolved, and the patient experienced complete relief within three weeks. Because the study data analysis is focused on a single patient, the treatment being employed must be performed on a bigger sample in order to obtain broader acceptability. Despite the single case, treatment findings indicate that Ayurvedic detoxification combined with supportive therapy can result in clinically detectable recovery from short-term opioid and sedative dependence.

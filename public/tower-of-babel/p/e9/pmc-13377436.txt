Microwave-glycated pea protein isolate high internal phase Pickering emulsions as fat substitutes improve quality of emulsified sausages
Authors: Jiannan Yan, Fangxiao Xing, Jiayi Jin, Qi Qi, Hao Zhang, Yuzhu Wu, Jianjiang Wei, Chengbin Zhao, Xiuying Xu, Jingsheng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Low-fat emulsified sausages were prepared using microwave-pretreated pea protein isolate (PPI)-guar gum (GG) glycation conjugate high internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs) as fat substitutes. The effects of partial or complete substitution on the quality of emulsified sausages were investigated. As increasing the substitution proportion of HIPPEs in the emulsified sausages, the microstructure became more compact with smaller pores, and the emulsifying stability was enhanced. A high proportion of HIPPEs in emulsified sausages improved the rheological properties, water holding capacity and texture properties, while reducing the cooking loss of emulsified sausages. The 75% substitute ratio of HIPPEs stabilized by microwave-pretreated PPI-GG conjugate nanoparticles improved the sensory quality of emulsified sausages, which were well accepted by consumers. Therefore, microwave-pretreated PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs could be used to promote the quality of sausages, which provide data supports for the application of highly stable HIPPEs in the development of low-fat healthy meat products.

Introduction

During the production and processing of traditional emulsified sausages, it is usually necessary to add 20% to 30% animal fat to maintain the quality of the product (Cittadini et al., 2022). The animal fats are embedded in the protein network structure in the form of copolymers. They enhance the gel strength and juiciness of emulsified sausages, and play a key role in maintaining the texture. Furthermore, aromatics generated from lipolysis during processing can improve the flavor of the product and stimulate the appetites of consumers (Domínguez et al., 2021). However, excessive intake of animal fat poses a potential health threat to consumers, such as increasing the risk of cardiovascular diseases. Consequently, low-fat meat products, particularly those with low saturated fatty acid content, are growing in popularity (Cen et al., 2024).

High internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs) are new types of emulsion systems stabilized by solid particles (rather than conventional surfactants) with an internal phase volume fraction above 74% (Bago Rodriguez & Binks, 2022). HIPPEs exhibit excellent stability against coalescence, Ostwald ripening, and environmental changes (e.g., temperature, pH, and ionic strength) due to the formation of a dense and irreversible barrier by solid particles at oil-water interface (Zhao et al., 2025). HIPPEs possess a semi-solid, gel-like consistency and an extremely high specific surface area within the continuous phase, which can be applied for porous materials, 3D printing, and the encapsulation of active compounds (Lv et al., 2026). Protein particles are considered as effective stabilizers for HIPPEs due to their balanced hydrophilicity/hydrophobicity, biosafety, and environmental friendliness, offering significant advantages over inorganic particles. The HIPPEs stabilized by protein particles are capable of converting highly unsaturated liquid plant oils into solid viscoelastic emulsion gels. They show rheological properties similar to those of saturated fats, which thus have the potential to be used as saturated fat substitutes in food components (Chen, Wei, et al., 2025). In recent years, many researchers have begun to apply protein-based HIPPEs as fat substitutes in low-fat emulsified meat products to improve their texture and health benefits (Fadillah et al., 2024; Herrero & Ruiz-Capillas, 2021). However, HIPPEs constructed only from protein particles have poor stability and their application in emulsified sausages is limited.

The interactions between proteins and polysaccharides can improve the hydrophilic/hydrophobic balance and dispersion stability of the original protein particles, enhancing the mechanical properties of the interfacial film, which synergistically stabilize HIPPEs (Li et al., 2025). Wang et al. (2024) constructed oat protein-high methoxy pectin non-covalent complexes by changing the pH, which improved the emulsifying activity and emulsifying stability of the protein. Li et al. (2023) found that the HIPPEs stabilized by modified pea protein-chitosan non-covalent complex nanoparticles effectively reduced cooking loss and fat oxidation, but they did not affect the texture and sensory acceptability of sausages. Compared with non-covalent complexes, the complexes formed by covalent cross-linking of proteins and polysaccharides exhibit superior emulsification properties. They are able to effectively adsorb at the oil-water interface, forming a steric barrier to inhibit flocculation of emulsions, which significantly improves the spatial stability of emulsion droplets (Gao et al., 2021; Tran et al., 2024). Glycation based on the Maillard reaction has become a relatively ideal protein-polysaccharide covalent modification method due to its advantages of no need to add chemical reagents. Ashaolu and Zhao (2020) prepared a covalent conjugate of soybean protein and soybean dietary fiber through a glycation reaction, improving the thermal stability and hydrophilicity of the protein, which showed excellent ability to stabilize emulsion. The traditional wet-heat glycation method is time-consuming. Prolonged high temperatures can easily cause protein denaturation and aggregation in the solution, affecting the degree of glycation (Cao et al., 2025).

Microwave, as an efficient heating technology, can promote the covalent binding of proteins with polysaccharides, thereby improving the functional properties of covalent conjugates (Li et al., 2024). Currently, microwave heating has been successfully applied to the Maillard reaction of proteins with polysaccharides. Cheng et al. (2021) found that microwave treatment increased the Maillard reaction rate of rice protein with dextran, unfolding the protein structure and changing its secondary and tertiary structure. This improved functional properties such as solubility and emulsification of protein. Teng et al. (2023) reported that soybean protein-citrus pectin covalent conjugates prepared using microwave dry-heat treatment required less time and achieved a higher glycation degree, resulting in improved emulsification properties compared to those obtained using traditional dry-heat treatment. Additionally, microwave treatment modes, such as microwave pretreatment conditions, can also affect the efficiency of the glycation reaction as well as the structure and function of conjugates.

Pea protein isolate (PPI), as an emerging high-quality plant protein resource, is regarded as a good source of plant emulsifiers and protein-based fat substitutes due to its low cost and low allergenicity as well as high surface activity and emulsifying property (Xu et al., 2025). Guar gum (GG) is a kind of natural water-soluble polysaccharide, and exhibited good swelling and adhesive properties, which can improve the stability of protein HIPPEs (Zheng et al., 2025). The interactions between PPI and GG can effectively improve the stability of HIPPEs. The partial or complete substitution of animal fats with highly stable HIPPEs is of great significance for the development of low-fat healthy meat products (Funami et al., 2025; Kwon et al., 2021). At present, research on the application of microwave-pretreated protein-polysaccharide glycated conjugate HIPPEs in emulsified sausages is still relatively scarce. In this study, the HIPPEs stabilized by microwave-pretreated PPI-GG conjugate nanoparticles were used as fat substitutes. The influence of substituting pork backfat in different proportions on the quality of emulsified sausages was explored, and the feasibility of HIPPEs as fat substitutes was analyzed. This study will provide a theoretical basis for the development of low-fat meat products.

Materials and methods

Materials

Pea protein isolate (PPI, 80% of protein) was purchased from Yuanye Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Guar gum (GG, 99%) was purchased by Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Soybean oil was obtained from Yihai Kerry Arawana Holdings Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Lean pork, pork backfat and salt were purchased from Walmart Supermarket in Changchun. Food-grade tripolyphosphate was provided by Hubei Xingfa Chemical Group Co., Ltd. (Hubei, China).

Preparation of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs

The pH of the 4% (w/v) PPI dispersion was adjusted to 12, followed by stirring for 2 h. Subsequently, the pH was re-adjusted to 7, and the dispersion was incubated at 4 °C overnight. The dispersion was heated at 90 °C for 30 min and then rapidly cooled to room temperature using an ice water bath, leading to the formation of PPI nanoparticles. The GG solution (0.5%, w/v) was stirred for 1 h, and then mixed with PPI nanoparticle dispersion in equal volume. The mixture was stirred for 2 h, and subjected to microwave pretreatment at 160 W for 4 and 6 min (Determined based on the glycation degree and emulsifying properties, Fig. S1), then heated at 75 °C for 1 h to initiate the glycation reaction. The microwave-pretreated PPI-GG glycated conjugate nanoparticles labeled as MPre-4 and MPre-6 were obtained after cooling. The conjugate nanoparticles were mixed with soybean oil (volume fraction of 0.75), and homogenized for 2 min with high-speed shearing at 10,000 rpm to form HIPPEs.

Production of emulsified sausages using HIPPEs as fat substitutes

Lean pork and pork backfat were thoroughly cleaned, and then chopped into small pieces using a meat grinder, respectively. The processing of the chopped pork followed the method described by Nacak et al. (2021) with slight modifications. Table 1 specifies the addition amounts of pork backfat and HIPPEs in the formulation. The lean pork was mixed with ice water, salt and tripolyphosphate, chopped and mixed for 3 min, and paused for 1 min. Then different proportions of HIPPEs, pork backfat and the remaining half of ice water were added, and continued to chop and mix for 3 min. The minced meat was filled into collagen casings, and the length of the emulsified sausage was controlled at 15 ± 2 cm. The sausages were subsequently heated in a water bath at 80 °C for 30 min. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

The emulsified sausages were cut into thin slices with 1 mm, ensuring that the cutting surface of the sausages was flat and smooth. After freeze drying, the samples were sputter-coated with gold, and observed and photographed using SEM (Phenom-World BV, Eindhoven, Netherlands) (Zhao, Chu, et al., 2023). The magnification was 100 times, the resolution was 3–5 nm, and the acceleration voltage was 5 kV.

Dynamic rheological properties

The dynamic rheological properties were determined following the method described by Qi et al. (2024). The temperature scanning was conducted using an MCR302 rheometer (Anton Paar Co., Graz, Austria) equipped with 25 mm parallel plates. Raw minced pork was placed on a test board with a 1 mm gap, and the exposed edge of sample was covered with paraffin oil to prevent evaporation. The sample was heated from 20 °C to 80 °C at a heating rate of 2 °C/min, followed by cooling back to 20 °C at a cooling rate of 5 °C/min. A fixed frequency of 1 Hz and a constant strain of 1% were applied throughout the measurement. The preliminary strain sweep experiments indicated that the rheological measurements were within the linear viscoelastic region. The variation in the storage modulus (G') of the sample was recorded during the measurement process.

Low field nuclear magnetic resonance (LF-NMR)

The water holding capacity and water distribution of the emulsified sausages were assessed at room temperature using a LF-NMR analyzer (Meso MR23-040 V–I, Niumag Electric Co., Shanghai, China) according to the method of Chu et al. (2024). The samples were cut into cuboids with 1 cm × 1 cm × 3 cm, which were subsequently wrapped in cling film and placed inside the test tube. The transverse relaxation time (T2) was measured using the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CMPG) pulse sequence at a proton resonance frequency of 22.6 MHz and 32 °C with 32 scans, 12000 echoes and 200 μs between pulses of 90° and 180°.

Emulsifying stability

The emulsifying stability of minced pork was measured following the method described by Toldrà et al. (2021) with minor modifications. The sample (10 g) was put into a centrifuge tube, and centrifuged at 3000 g for 1 min. Subsequently, the sample was heated in a water bath at 80 °C for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 3000 g for 3 min. The precipitate was then collected and weighed. The proportion of total expressible fluid (TEF) was calculated based on the weight loss caused by the combined treatment of heating and centrifugation. The supernatants were poured into crucibles and weighed, then dried at 105 °C until a constant weight was achieved. The residues remaining in the crucibles after drying were used to calculate the percentage of fat amount in total expressible fluid (FAT). The emulsifying stability was evaluated by TEF and FAT, and the calculation formulas were as follows:TEF%=m1−m2m0×100FAT%=m3−m4m1−m2×100where m0 is the mass of the raw minced pork; m1 is the total mass of the centrifuge tube and raw minced pork; m2 is the total mass of the centrifuge tube and precipitate after centrifugation; m3 is the total mass of the crucible and residue after drying; m4 is the mass of the empty crucible.

Cooking loss

The cooking loss of emulsified sausages was measured based on the method described by De Carvalho et al. (2020). The sausages were cooked in a water bath at 80 °C for 30 min, then cooled to room temperature. The remaining water droplets on the surface of sausages were gently wiped off, and then the sausages were weighed and recorded. The cooking loss was calculated using the following formula:Cooking loss%=M0−M1M0×100where M0 is the mass of sausages before cooking, and M1 is the mass of sausages after cooking.

Freeze-thaw loss

The freeze-thaw loss of emulsified sausages was determined following the method of Lee et al. (2025). The cooked sausages were frozen in a refrigerator at −20 °C for 24 h to simulate freezing conditions. Subsequently, the samples were thawed at room temperature until the internal temperature was consistent with the surrounding temperature. The sausages were weighed and recorded after freeze-thaw treatment. The freeze-thaw loss of sausages was calculated as follows:Freeze−thaw loss%=W0−W1W0×100where W0 is the weight of sausages before freeze-thaw treatment, and W1 is the weight of sausages after freeze-thaw treatment.

Texture properties

The texture of emulsified sausages was evaluated using a texture analyzer (TA-XT. Plus, Stable Micro Systems Co. Ltd., Surrey, UK) according to the method described by (Zhao et al., 2021) with slight modifications. The sausages were cut into cylinders with 3 cm × 3 cm, and compressed twice at a constant speed of 2 mm/s with a compression degree of 40%. The probe P/0.5 was used and the trigger force was 5 g.

Color measurement

The color of emulsified sausages was measured using a colorimeter (CM-5, Konica Minolta Holdings, Tokyo, Japan) referring to the method described by Chen, Yu, et al. (2025). The sausages were cut into cubes with 2 cm × 2 cm × 2 cm before measurement. L* (lightness), a* (red tones), and b* (yellow tones) values were recorded.

Sensory evaluation

Based on the method of Yuan et al. (2023) with minor modifications, the sensory properties of emulsified sausages containing different fat substitutes were evaluated. An evaluation panel was composed of 40 randomly chosen people (50% male/female, age range 22 to 40) who all consume regularly different types of emulsified sausages. Sensory evaluators were trained in the International Standardization Organization (ISO) standards (ISO 8586:2012). The sausages were cut into uniform-sized pieces (height of 2 cm), and then randomly disrupted in order. The juiciness, compactness, flavor, color and overall acceptability of sausages were scored by evaluators according to the sensory evaluation criteria (Table 2). The salt-free biscuits and water were provided between different samples to clean the mouth and prevent any potential residual effects. The ingredients for emulsified sausages were food composition and additives allowed in food, and this experiment complies with national laws and regulations. The research does not involve human ethical issues, and the ethical permission was not required to carry out the sensory evaluation. The appropriate protocols for protecting the rights and privacy of all participants were utilized during the execution of the research. All participants read and signed a written informed consent form before the evaluation, and they gave their consent to take part in the sensory study and use their information. Participation was completely voluntary, and participants had the right to withdraw unconditionally at any stage. This experiment fully guaranteed the participants' right to know, and their personal privacy was protected. Statistical analysis

All measurements were repeated three times, and the data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Significant differences were determined by analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS version 25.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) and defined as p < 0.05. All graphs were drawn using Origin 2021 software (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, USA).

Results and discussion

Appearance and microstructure

The effect of substitution ratio of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs on the minced pork appearance, emulsified sausage appearance and microstructure is shown in Fig. 1. As increasing the substitution amount of HIPPEs, the uniformity of the minced pork gradually improved (Fig. 1A), and the honeycomb-like pores inside the emulsified sausages gradually decreased (Fig. 1B). The protein matrix underwent re-aggregation and bridging, gradually forming a tough and homogeneous three-dimensional network structure (Ozturk-Kerimoglu et al., 2022). The formation of pores was closely associated with the collision and expansion of water with air within the sausages (Li et al., 2022). Compared with the unsubstituted sausage, the microstructure of sausage replaced with 75% and 100% HIPPEs was finer and smoother, and the pores almost disappeared (Fig. 1C). This was in agreement with the report of Li et al. (2023), who found that the gel network of sausages was improved through the cross-linking of Pickering emulsions stabilized by pea protein-chitosan complex nanoparticles with muscle proteins. When HIPPEs and minced pork were completely mixed, the HIPPEs had smaller oil droplets, and was easier to be embedded in the protein matrix and uniformly distributed. This led to more compact binding of HIPPEs with minced pork and smaller pores (Zhu et al., 2022). This indicated that HIPPEs significantly improved the internal structure of sausages, thereby effectively enhancing the quality of products.Fig. 1Effect of substitution ratio of microwave-pretreated pea protein isolate-guar gum conjugate high internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs) on the minced pork appearance (A), emulsified sausage appearance (B) and microstructure (C).Fig. 1

Rheological properties

The effect of substitution ratio of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs on the storage modulus (G') of emulsified sausages is shown in Fig. 2A. As the increase of the substitution ratio, the uniformity of the minced pork gradually improved. During the heating process, the G' variation trends of all samples presented similar characteristics. Rheological measurement can be divided into three stages. In the first stage, the G' value of the minced pork slightly decreased with the rise of temperature. When the temperature reached 52 °C, the G' significantly increased due to the unfolding and binding of myofibrillar protein peptide chains. This indicated that the elastic network structure of protein began to form or had been initially established (Zhao, Yuan, et al., 2023). In the second stage, the temperature rose sharply from 57 °C to 80 °C, which reached the denaturation temperature of protein. During this period, proteins underwent an irreversible denaturation process, and gradually formed a stable gel network structure (Chen et al., 2021). In the third stage, when the temperature dropped from 80 °C to 20 °C, the G' showed an upward trend and eventually reached highest. The HIPPEs substitution ratio significantly affected the final G' values of the samples (Fig. 2B). The G' values of 25% MPre-4, 25% MPre-6 and 50% MPre-4 samples were lower than those of the control sample, while the G' values of 50% MPre-6, 75% MPre-6 and 100% MPre-4 samples were similar to those of the control sample. The G' values of 75% MPre-4 and 100% MPre-6 samples were higher than those of the control sample. This indicated that HIPPEs with a higher substitution ratio could be embedded in the gel network structure of sausage, promoting protein aggregation or coagulation, which thereby enhanced the G' of sausages and improved their rheological properties (Lu et al., 2024). However, Hong et al. (2026) found that the substitution ratio of more than 50% almond protein emulsion gels reduced the G' of emulsified sausages, which might be related to the different types and properties of the emulsions.Fig. 2Effect of substitution ratio of microwave-pretreated pea protein isolate-guar gum conjugate high internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs) on the storage modulus G' (A-B), relaxation curve (C) and percentage of relaxation peak area (D) of emulsified sausages. Water distribution

The effect of substitution ratio of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs on the relaxation curve of emulsified sausages is shown in Fig. 2C. The samples exhibited two distinct characteristic peaks corresponding to T22 (immobilized water) and T23 (free water), respectively. These peaks showed different intensities of water migration states. A short transverse relaxation time indicated that sausages had a strong ability to bind with water molecules, which weakened the mobility of the corresponding water molecules (Zhao, Wang, et al., 2023). As increasing the HIPPEs substitution ratio, the curve shifted towards a larger relaxation time. This suggested the weakening of interactions between protein network and water, which enhanced the freedom of water. According to the percentage of relaxation peak area (Fig. 2D), with the increase of HIPPEs substitution ratio, the peak area (A22) corresponding to T22 showed an increasing trend, while the peak area (A23) corresponding to T23 showed a decreasing trend. This suggested that the content of immobilized water increased, while the content of free water decreased, promoting the transformation of free water into immobilized water. These immobilized waters transformed from free waters were embedded in the network structure of sausage by means of physical interception, but the chemical binding effect on these water molecules was relatively weak. This migration and transformation of water enhanced the juiciness of sausage. Notably, when the HIPPEs substitution ratio was 75%, the immobilized water content reached maximum, while the free water content dropped to minimum, and MPre-4 sausage had a higher immobilized water content than MPre-6 sample. However, 100% substitution ratio exhibited a slightly adverse effect. This was consistent with the trend of Zhang et al. (2025) study on soybean protein isolate-based composite emulsion gels as fat substitutes. Therefore, adding appropriate proportions of HIPPEs as substitutes into sausages promoted the capture of water, and improved the water holding capacity and juiciness.

Emulsifying stability

Meat products are prone to problems such as water loss, oil exudation and structural loosening during the heat processing process. This had an adverse effect on the quality of meat products. Emulsifying stability can be used to characterize the ability of minced pork to retain water and fat, which was evaluated by determining the total expressible fluid (TEF) and the fat amount in total expressible fluid (FAT). As presented in Fig. 3A-B, TEF and FAT of the control sample were 5.03% and 16.28%, respectively. The TEF and FAT of emulsified sausages with HIPPEs substitution were lower than those of the control sample, which were negatively correlated with the substitution ratio of HIPPEs. These findings suggested that substituting animal fat with HIPPEs effectively enhanced the emulsifying stability of sausages (Bibat et al., 2022). When the substitution ratios were 75% and 100%, the TEF of the MPre-4 sausage was lower than that of the MPre-6 sample, which was related to more immobilized water of Mpre-4 sausage (Fig. 2D). When the substitution ratio was 50%, MPre-6 sausage had a lower FAT, indicating that the substitution of MPre-6 HIPPEs showed a stronger ability to bind fat droplets in the emulsified sausages. Qi et al. (2025) also found that myofibrillar protein/sodium alginate-stabilized HIPPE as animal fat substitutes effectively reduced TEF and FAT of sausages, which was similar to the results of this study. Furthermore, non-meat components and meat proteins could form a dense structure in emulsified sausages, thereby enhancing emulsifying stability (Wang et al., 2022).Fig. 3Effect of substitution ratio of microwave-pretreated pea protein isolate-guar gum conjugate high internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs) on total expressible fluid (TEF) (A), fat in the expressible fluid (FAT) (B), cooking loss (C) and freeze-thaw loss (D) of emulsified sausages. Cooking loss

Cooking loss is an indicator of mass reduction in meat processing caused by moisture loss during cooking. As shown in Fig. 3C, the cooking loss of emulsified sausages significantly decreased with increasing substitution levels of HIPPEs. When the substitution ratio was 75%, the cooking losses of MPre-4 and MPre-6 sausages decreased from 7.54% to 6.27% and 6.40%, respectively. This might be attributed to the enhanced interactions between HIPPEs and minced pork during cooking, which enhanced the ability to bind water and improved water retention (Zhu et al., 2022). Among them, the MPre-4 sausage exhibited even lower cooking loss due to its higher thermal stability (Yan et al., 2026). This property helped maintain structural stability of the sausage throughout the cooking process, and effectively prevented water loss or oil exudation caused by severe phase separation. Li et al. (2022) reported that the sausages prepared with emulsion gels exhibited lower cooking loss, which was attributed to more compact internal structure of sausages formed by emulsion gels, thereby effectively reducing water loss. Chen et al. (2024) found that the cooking loss of emulsified sausages decreased with increasing substitution levels of salmon protein-based HIPPEs. These researches were consistent with the results obtained in this work.

Freeze-thaw loss

Freezing is a critical step in sausage processing, and freeze-thaw loss directly reflects the impact of freezing treatment on the water holding capacity of sausages (Mohammed et al., 2021). As shown in Fig. 3D, the unsubstituted sausage exhibited the relative low freeze-thaw loss, and the freeze-thaw loss of emulsified sausages with 50% and 75% substitution levels of HIPPEs slightly increased. When the substitution ratio reached to 100%, MPre-4 and MPre-6 sausages both showed even greater freeze-thaw loss. This indicated that an excessively high substitution ratio of HIPPEs weakened the freeze-thaw stability of emulsified sausages. The unsubstituted sausage had a greater loss of water and fat in during the cooking process before freezing (Fig. 3A-C), which reduced the formation of ice crystals within the sausage structure during freezing and ultimately led to a lower freeze-thaw loss. This was consistent with the freeze-thaw loss results in pork meatballs prepared with plant protein-based emulsions as fat substitutes, where the samples with higher water and fat loss before freezing showed a lower freeze-thaw loss (Lee et al., 2025). Therefore, the appropriate substitution ratio of HIPPEs can maintain the freeze-thaw stability of emulsified sausages.

Texture properties

Table 3 shows the effect of substitution ratio of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs on the texture properties of emulsified sausages. Compared with the control sample, low substitution levels (25% and 50%) of HIPPEs reduced the hardness and chewiness of emulsified sausages, while high substitution levels (75% and 100%) increased these parameters. The springiness of emulsified sausages was not affected by HIPPEs substitution. Both cohesiveness and resilience of emulsified sausages significantly increased at 100% substitution level of HIPPEs. Compared with animal fat, HIPPEs had smaller droplets and more effectively filled in the gaps of the meat protein gel network, thereby achieving a better filling effect. Furthermore, the improvement in the texture properties of the sausages might be attributed to the promoted interactions of proteins between HIPPEs and minced pork induced by heating, which further stabilized the overall gel network structure of the sausages. Sensory evaluation

Fig. 4 shows the effect of substitution ratio of PPI-GG conjugate HIPPEs on the sensory properties of emulsified sausages, including juiciness, texture, flavor, color and overall acceptability. Both MPre-4 (Fig. 4A) and MPre-6 (Fig. 4B) emulsified sausages exhibited similar change trends in sensory scores. The substitution of HIPPEs improved the juiciness of emulsified sausages. Sausages with 75% substitution ratio had the highest juiciness score. This was also associated with a higher immobilized water content (Fig. 2D). As the substitution levels of HIPPEs increased, the compactness scores of the sausages gradually increased, which might be attributed to their denser microstructure (Fig. 1C). The unsubstituted sausage received the highest flavor score. As the substitution levels of HIPPEs increased, the flavor scores of emulsified sausages gradually decreased. It was possible that the lack of unique flavor precursor substances come from animal fats resulted in a relatively light overall flavor of emulsified sausages. The color scores were similar across all samples, with no significant differences observed. The substitution of HIPPEs enhanced the overall acceptability of emulsified sausages, and the MPre-4 and MPre-6 samples with 75% substitution ratio had the highest sensory score, which were 6.267 and 6.189 (maximum score of 7 points). Ma et al. (2026) reported that the emulsified sausage with 50% substitution ratio of modified soybean protein isolate-stabilized HIPPEs had the best overall acceptability. This meant that the HIPPEs in this study could replace more animal fats and maintain better quality. Conclusions

HIPPEs stabilized by microwave-pretreated PPI-GG conjugate nanoparticles were used as substitutes for pork backfat in the preparation of emulsified sausages. As the substitution level of HIPPEs increased, the structure of the sausages became denser, while freeze-thaw loss increased. When the substitution proportion reached 75%, the G', water holding capacity, emulsifying stability, hardness, and chewiness of the sausages were significantly improved, and the cooking losses of MPre-4 and MPre-6 sausages decreased from 7.54% to 6.27% and 6.40%, respectively. Sensory evaluation showed that the substitution ratios of 75% HIPPEs improved the sensory quality of emulsified sausages, and the highest overall acceptability of MPre-4 and MPre-6 sausages were 6.267 and 6.189 (maximum score of 7 points), which were well accepted by consumers. Thus, an appropriate substitution of animal fat with HIPPEs can be used to produce high-quality emulsified sausages, which provides a theoretical foundation and new strategy for the application of highly stable PPI-based HIPPEs in low-fat healthy meat products and their quality promotion.

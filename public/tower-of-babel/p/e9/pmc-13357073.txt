LINC01614 favors breast cancer progression through the regulation of miR-217/FN1 and mediation in PI3K/AKT signal pathway
Authors: Ding Ping, Jiaxin Wang, Dengfeng Li, Lin Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer (BC) is the most prevalent malignant tumor and represents a significant threat to women’s health. Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs), which are RNA molecules longer than 200 nucleotides, have been shown to play critical roles in the progression of BC. Therefore, this study was aimed to explore the function and potential mechanism of LINC01614 in BC, so that it could provide new strategies for future BC therapy.MethodsThis study used the TANRIC and ENCORI databases to evaluate LINC01614 expression and its prognostic significance. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), wound healing, Transwell, and apoptosis assays were conducted to assess its biological function in BC cells. Quantitative real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and Western blot (WB) were employed to explore the molecular mechanisms involved. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) detection and in vivo experiments further clarified its role in BC.ResultsThe expression of LINC01614 was significantly elevated in BC and correlated with poor prognosis. Elevated LINC01614 expression enhanced BC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, whereas its knockdown decreased cell viability, inhibited migration and invasion, and triggered apoptosis. Gene expression analysis after LINC01614 knockdown revealed changes in miR-217, FN1, PI3K, AKT, and PTEN levels. Additionally, co-silencing LINC01614 and miR-217 significantly affected PI3K, AKT, PTEN, and FN1 expression. Luciferase assays confirmed direct regulatory interactions between LINC01614 and miR-217, as well as between miR-217 and FN1. Metabolic analysis indicated that LINC01614 knockdown enhanced cellular energy metabolism.ConclusionsThese results indicate that LINC01614 facilitates BC progression by regulating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway through miR-217/FN1. Targeting LINC01614 may provide new therapeutic strategies for BC treatment.

Highlight box

Key findings

• LINC01614 is highly expressed in breast cancer (BC) and correlates with poor prognosis; it promotes BC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while inhibiting apoptosis by regulating the miR-217/FN1 axis to activate the PI3K/AKT pathway, and its knockdown enhances cellular energy metabolism.

What is known and what is new?

• Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) play critical roles in BC progression, and the PI3K/AKT pathway is a key regulator of BC development.

• This study identifies LINC01614 as a novel oncogenic LncRNAs in BC, revealing its direct regulatory cascade (LINC01614→miR-217→FN1) that activates PI3K/AKT signaling, and links LINC01614 to BC cellular energy metabolism.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• LINC01614 serves as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for BC. Targeting LINC01614 or its downstream miR-217/FN1/PI3K/AKT axis may develop new strategies to treat BC.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) ranked as the most frequently diagnosed cancer in women worldwide in 2020, with about 2.3 million new cases (1). It also ranks as the second most common cause of cancer-associated mortality in women (2). This disease greatly affects global health and has a serious impact on the physical and mental well-being of middle-aged women (3). Despite advances in diagnosis and treatment, the overall prognosis for BC patients is still not ideal. Understanding the molecular mechanisms behind BC development and progression is essential. Finding new and easily accessible therapeutic targets and reliable biomarkers could help with early detection and better treatment, leading to improved patient outcomes.

Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) are functional RNA sequences exceeding 200 nucleotides in length. They modulate gene expression through interactions with proteins, DNA, and various RNA molecules, affecting transcription, epigenetic changes, and post-transcriptional processes (4). Research shows that LncRNAs play a key role in controlling cell growth, differentiation, movement, and apoptosis by influencing gene activity (5). Because of their strong regulatory effects, understanding how LncRNAs work and their role in BC is important for improving diagnosis and treatment.

The PI3K/AKT pathway regulates cancer cell proliferation, survival, and metastatic potential. When this pathway becomes overactive, it speeds up tumor progression and leads to resistance to treatment and poor clinical outcomes (6,7). In BC, this pathway is frequently altered and plays a significant role in disease progression, making it a key focus for developing targeted therapies (8). It also interacts with other cancer-related pathways, such as MAPK and mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), which further increase its tumor-promoting effects. Because of its major role in cancer, this pathway has become an important target for therapy, with many PI3K, AKT, and mTOR inhibitors being tested in clinical trials (9). Recent studies suggest that both natural compounds and synthetic drugs targeting this pathway may offer new options for BC treatment (10).

LncRNAs play a vital role in cancer, affecting key signaling pathways like PI3K/AKT (11). Changes in LncRNA expression often disrupt this signaling, resulting in enhanced cell proliferation, reduced apoptosis, and higher metastatic potential in BC (11). LncRNAs also contribute to therapy resistance by keeping PI3K/AKT active, helping cancer cells survive chemotherapy and targeted treatments (12). Studies show that LINC-BC069792 is significantly lower in BC and is linked to higher tumor grade, lymph node metastasis, and Ki-67 levels. LINC-BC069792 functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), binding to hsa-miR-658 and hsa-miR-4739, which increases KCNQ4 protein levels, reduces JAK2 and phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) activity, and slows BC progression (13). Also, loss of MIR497HG has been found to promote BC and cause tamoxifen resistance by lowering miR-497/195, a key regulator of PI3K/AKT (14). The connection between LncRNAs and the PI3K/AKT pathway suggests their potential as biomarkers and treatment targets. Understanding these molecular interactions could help develop new ways to control PI3K/AKT signaling in cancer (15,16).

To investigate LncRNAs linked to BC progression, we used data from the TANRIC database (http://bioinformatics.mdanderson.org/main/TANRIC:Overview). The results showed a strong association between elevated LINC01614 expression and poorer survival outcomes in BC patients. In addition, our results also demonstrated that LINC01614 could promote BC proliferation and motility and decrease BC apoptosis. This suggests that LINC01614 acts as an oncogene in BC, with its increased levels associated with poor clinical outcomes. Based on this, LINC01614 might be a useful prognostic biomarker for BC. These findings provide further insight into the molecular mechanisms driving BC progression and may facilitate the development of targeted therapeutic strategies. We present this article in accordance with the ARRIVE and MDAR reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0488/rc).

Methods

Cell culture and transportation

All BC cell lines were sourced from the Chinese Academy of Sciences’ Cell Bank and were cultivated in a humidified incubator at 37 ℃ with a 5% CO2 environment. MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells were kept in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (cat. no. G4515, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) with 100X Penicillin-Streptomycin Solution (cat. no. C0222, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, cat. no. E510008, BBI-lifesciences, Shanghai, China).

Lentiviral vectors were utilized to generate overexpression plasmid for LINC01614 (MiaoLin Biology, Wuhan, China). MiR-217 mimics and inhibitors were used for miR-217 overexpression and down-expression (GenePharma, Shanghai, China). The primers are detailed in Table S1. Three siRNAs in all were created for the study, and the best one was selected for additional research (Table S1). Additionally, inhibitors and negative controls were created for miR-217, a putative LINC01614 target (GenePharma, Shanghai, China). Plasmids and their empty vectors were transfected into cells using the Lipo8000 reagent (cat. no. C0533, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Cells were harvested 48 hours post-transfection to assess transfection efficiency and were subsequently utilized. The transfect effectivity was confirmed by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR).

Proliferation assay

Using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) (cat. no. C0038, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) approach, transfected cells (3×103 cells per well) were identified upon being plated in a 96-well plate. Each well received a 10% CCK-8 solution in DMEM following a 24- and 48-hour incubation, and the plate was then incubated for an additional 4-hour period. The proliferation curve was calculated by microplate reader (SpectraMax iD5, California, USA) with relative absorbance values at 450 nm.

Apoptosis assay

Cells were harvested using EDTA-free trypsin (cat. no. G4002, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) and then resuspended in 1× binding buffer following centrifugation. Following a 15-minute incubation with 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC (cat. no. 556420, BD Biosciences, New Jersey, USA) and an equal volume of PI (cat. no. 556463, BD Biosciences, New Jersey, USA), the cells underwent analysis promptly, within an hour, utilizing the FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, New Jersey, USA).

Wound-healing assay

Cells were grown to subconfluence in six-well culture plates using DMEM medium supplemented with 10% FBS. A 10 µL pipette tip caused a linear wound on the plate’s surface. The phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) washed the plate surface and followed cultured with 2% FBS. Cell migration was monitored and quantified at 24-hour time points, and the remaining wound area was measured using an inverted microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Cell invasion assay

Using Matrigel-precoated Transwell inserts (cat. no. 3422, Corning, New York, USA), the invasive potential of BC cells was assessed. In brief, 200 µL of serum-free DMEM was added to the upper chamber, into which approximately 2×104 transfected cells were resuspended. The lower chamber contained 500 µL of medium supplemented with 10% FBS. Following a 24-hour incubation, the cells that had invaded were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA, cat. no. G1101, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), stained with crystal violet (cat. no. G1014, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), and subsequently observed under an inverted microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). Five randomly selected fields were captured and subsequently analyzed.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (cat. no. 15596018CN, Invitrogen, California, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized with the HiScript IV 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (+gDNA wiper) (cat. no. R412, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). For microRNA (miRNA) analysis, reverse transcription was performed using the miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (by tailing A) (cat. no. MR201, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (cat. no. 11202ES50, Yeasen, Shanghai, China) was applied for qRT-PCR experiments. The specific primers used in this study are listed in Table S1. GAPDH served as the endogenous control for mRNA, while U6 was used for miRNA. 2-ΔΔCt method was used to calculate the results (17).

Western blot (WB) detection

FN1, PI3K, AKT, and PTEN protein levels were evaluated using WB detection. Following their extraction and resolution using 10% SDS-PAGE, cellular proteins were moved onto a 0.45 µm Nitrocellulose (NC) membrane (cat. no. HATF00010, Millipore, Massachusetts, USA). After blocking the membrane with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, cat. no. GC305010, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), the respective primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4 ℃. The enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system (Amersham ImageQuant 800, Cytiva, Washington, DC, USA) was then utilized to visualize the protein bands after the membrane had been treated with the appropriate secondary antibodies. An ECL kit (cat. no. E423, Vazyme, Nanjing, China) was used to view the immunoblots, and ChemImager 5500 V2.03 software (Alphainnotech, California, USA) was used for analysis. The relative integrated density value (IDV) of the bands was calculated using GAPDH as an internal reference. The antibodies information was listed as follows: FN1(cat. no. Ab45688, Abcam, Cambridge, UK, 1:1,000); PI3K (cat. no. Ab183957, Cambridge, UK, 1:1,000); AKT (cat. no. Ab8005, Cambridge, UK, 1:1,000); PTEN (cat. no. Ab267787, Cambridge, UK, 1:1,000).

Luciferase assay

The luciferase reporter vectors LINC01614-wt and LINC01614-mut were created by inserting either the wild or mutant LINC01614 3’UTR segment, which contains the anticipated miR-217 binding site, into the pmirGLO plasmid (IBSBIO, Shanghai, China). Following their seeding onto 24-well plates (105 cells/well), BC cells were simultaneously transfected with either miR-217 or miR-NC, in addition to the corresponding luciferase reporter vectors. To evaluate the targeted binding interaction between miR-217 and FN1 mRNA, wild-type and mutant FN1 3’UTR segments with predicted miR-217 binding sites were cloned into specific Dual-Luciferase Vectors. After incubating for 48 hours, luciferase activity was assessed utilizing the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (cat. no. G1701, Servicebio, Wuhan, China).

Cell metabolism detection

Using the appropriate test kits (glucose colorimetric/fluorometric assay kit, d-lactic acid colorimetric assay kit, and ATP assay kit), the metabolic effects on BC cells were evaluated by quantifying the amount of glucose consumed, lactate produced, and ATP levels. Serum-free medium was used to cultivate BC cells seeded in 6-well plates for 24 hours before being replaced with a new medium. The amounts of ATP, lactate (LD) and glucose (GLU) in each group’s supernatant were measured. Normalization was based on the total protein content as measured by the sulforhodamine B assay. The Kits information was listed as follows: :ATP ELISA Kit (cat. no. A154-1-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China); LD ELISA Kit (cat. no. A019-2-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China); GLU ELISA Kit (cat. no. 1095-1-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China).

Tumor xenograft model establishment

In a tumor xenograft model, cells treated with shRNA targeting LINC01614, negative control shRNA, and a total of 1×107 untreated cells were resuspended in 200 µL of PBS and subcutaneously injected into the female nude mice aged 4 to 6 weeks (JiHui, Shanghai, China). All animal experiments were performed under a project license (No. SHDSYY-2020-0600) granted by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital, in compliance with Chinese guidelines for the care and use of animals. The breeding environment, maintained at the Central Laboratory of Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital, was classified as specific pathogen-free (SPF) to ensure a high level of cleanliness. The fifteen mice were devised in to three groups, and each group contained five mice. For a month of observation, the mice’s body weight and tumor size were measured every two days. In accordance with the ethical approval, the maximum tumor volume allowed in mice was limited to 1,000 mm3. Throughout the experiment, all tumors remained within this acceptable size range. Subsequently, the mice were humanely euthanized by CO2 asphyxiation, and tumor samples were harvested for further analysis. After the tumors were excised, the maximum length (L), minimum width (W), and weight were measured. Tumor volume was then calculated using the formula V = π/6 × L × W2.

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining and immunohistochemistry (IHC) detection

Paraffin blocks were made once the tissue was fixed. After being dewaxed in xylene, sections of tissue fixed in paraffin were rehydrated in a series of alcohol solutions. The sections were rinsed and dehydrated after three minutes of hematoxylin staining, and then they were stained for two to three minutes with eosin. The staining was microscopically observed. In the IHC analysis, antigen retrieval was performed using microwave heating, while endogenous peroxidase activity was inhibited by treating the samples with 3% H2O2. To minimize nonspecific binding, the sections were first incubated with 5% BSA at 37 ℃ for 1 hour. Subsequently, they were treated overnight at 4 ℃ with a primary antibody specific to FN1 FN1 (cat. no. GB15091, Servicebio, Wuhan, China, 1:500). The following day, the sections were first incubated with the appropriate secondary antibody at 37 ℃ for 1 hour, the sections were stained using diaminobenzidine and counterstained with hematoxylin. Representative images were captured using an upright binocular biological microscope (Leica DM4B, Germany).

Statistical analysis

The experimental data repeated for three times and are presented as mean ± standard deviation and analyzed with GraphPad 5.0 (California, USA). Intergroup differences were assessed using the student’s t-test. All experiments were performed three times independently. All statistical analyses were conducted using two-tailed tests, with a significance threshold set at P<0.05.

Results

Expression of LINC01614 in BC

To explore the involvement of LncRNAs in BC, the TANRIC database (http://bioinformatics.mdanderson.org/main/TANRIC:Overview) was utilized to analyze and compare LncRNA expression levels between BC tissues and adjacent normal breast tissues (Figure 1). The results showed that LINC01614 was more highly expressed in BC tissues. Volcano plot analysis in the TANRIC database also confirmed that LINC01614 exhibited a significant upregulation in BC (Figure 2A). Boxplot analysis from the ENCORI database (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/) revealed a consistent trend. The expression level of LINC01614 was significantly elevated in BC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Figure 2B). Survival analysis from the TANRIC database further suggested that patients exhibiting high levels of LINC01614 expression demonstrated a significantly lower overall survival rate compared to those with low expression levels (Figure 2C). These results suggest that LINC01614 is upregulated in BC and is linked to poor prognosis. This finding indicates that LINC01614 has the potential to act as a biomarker for monitoring disease progression.

Effect of LINC01614 on the biological functions of BC cells

To better understand the role of LINC01614 in BC, we created cell models with LINC01614 knockdown and overexpression. We then examined its effects on cell growth, apoptosis, movement, invasion, and metabolism. Downregulation of LINC01614 with siRNA could reduce the expression of LINC01614 (Figure 2D) and over expression of LINC01614 could gain expression of LINC01614 in BC cells (Figure 2E). CCK-8 assays showed that reducing LINC01614 levels slowed down cell growth (Figure 2F), while increasing its levels made cells grow faster (Figure 2G). Apoptosis assays showed a similar pattern. Lowering LINC01614 levels led to more cell death, while increasing its levels reduced apoptosis (Figure 2H,2I). Scratch assays showed that cells moved more slowly when LINC01614 was knocked down (Figure 3A). In contrast, overexpression made cells move faster (Figure 3B). Transwell invasion assays confirmed this trend. Lower LINC01614 levels diminished the invasive capacity of the cells (Figure 3C), while higher levels made cells more invasive (Figure 3D). To study metabolism, we measured ATP, LD and GLU levels using ELISA. Knockdown of LINC01614 led to a decrease in ATP, LD, and GLU levels, whereas overexpression of LINC01614 resulted in an elevation of these levels, suggesting that LINC01614 boosts energy metabolism (Figure 3E-3G). In conclusion, high LINC01614 levels help BC cells grow, move, invade, and produce more energy. At the same time, it prevents apoptosis. These results imply that LINC01614 is crucial in driving the progression of BC.

LINC01614 regulates BC progression through miR-217

Recent studies show that LncRNAs regulate cell processes through different mechanisms. These mechanisms encompass the ceRNA pathway, interactions with RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), and the direct regulation of coding protein expression (18,19). Using the ENCORI database, we screened for miRNAs that may interact with LINC01614. The results identified miR-217 as a potential target (Figure 4A) (20). This suggests that LINC01614 may regulate BC through the ceRNA mechanism. To test this, we performed dual-luciferase reporter assays in both BC cells. The results indicated that transfection with miR-217 led to a notable decrease in luciferase activity (Renilla/firefly) in the LINC01614 wild-type group when compared to the miR-NC group. However, luciferase activity in the LINC01614 mutant group did not change (Figure 4B). This confirms that LINC01614 directly binds to miR-217 in BC cells. Further analysis showed that changes in LINC01614 expression led to opposite changes in miR-217 levels (Figure 4C), supporting their interaction. To explore how LINC01614 affects BC through miR-217, we performed rescue experiments. The results demonstrated that co-transfection of si-LINC01614 with a miR-217 inhibitor effectively reversed the impact of LINC01614 knockdown. This restored cell growth, migration, and invasion while reducing apoptosis (Figure 4D-4G). In addition, co-transfection rescued intracellular ATP, LD, and GLU levels, which had decreased after LINC01614 knockdown (Figure 4H-4J). These results indicate that LINC01614 may also be involved in regulating metabolic processes. In conclusion, our results show that LINC01614 promotes BC progression by regulating miR-217. This provides new insights into its role in cancer development.

miR-217 inhibits BC progression

In our previous experiments, we confirmed that LINC01614 directly binds to miR-217 and promotes BC progression by regulating miR-217. Based on these findings, we further explored the biological function of miR-217 in BC. To increase miR-217 expression, we transfected BC cells with miR-217 mimics (Figure 5A). The results demonstrated that the overexpression of miR-217 led to a marked decrease in cell proliferation (Figure 5B), induced apoptosis (Figure 5C,5D), and inhibited both invasion and migration (Figure 5E,5F). In addition, ELISA results showed that higher miR-217 expression decreased ATP, LD, and GLU metabolism levels in BC cells (Figure 5G-5I), suggesting a role in metabolic regulation. In conclusion, these findings indicate that miR-217 functions as a tumor suppressor in BC by suppressing cell growth, invasion, and migration, while simultaneously enhancing apoptosis and decreasing metabolic activity.

MiR-217 inhibits BC progression by targeting FN1 and modulating the PI3K/AKT/PTEN signaling pathway

Given that miR-217 functions as a tumor suppressor in BC, we conducted further investigation into its underlying regulatory mechanisms. By utilizing the TARGETSCAN database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/) (21), we identified FN1 as a possible target gene of miR-217 (Figure 6A). To verify this, we performed dual-luciferase reporter assays in both BC cells. The results showed that miR-217 overexpression significantly reduced luciferase activity in the FN1 wild-type group, while no considerable change was observed in the FN1 mutant group. This confirmed the direct binding between miR-217 and FN1 (Figure 6B,6C). Additional analysis revealed that upregulation of miR-217 led to a significant decrease in FN1 mRNA expression in BC cells (Figure 6D). Since FN1 is known to activate the PI3K/AKT pathway (22), we next examined whether miR-217 affects this signaling pathway. The results showed that miR-217 overexpression decreased PI3K and AKT mRNA levels while increasing PTEN mRNA levels (Figure 6E,6F). WB analysis further showed that miR-217 overexpression resulted in a reduction of FN1 protein levels, a decrease in PI3K and AKT protein expressions, and an increase in PTEN protein expression (Figure 6G-6I). In conclusion, these findings suggest that miR-217 directly targets FN1 and regulates the PI3K/AKT/PTEN signaling pathway, thereby influencing BC progression.

LINC01614 regulates FN1 and the PI3K/AKT/PTEN signaling pathway

Previous research has confirmed that miR-217 directly targets FN1 and regulates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Building on these findings, we further investigated the association among LINC01614, FN1, and the PI3K/AKT/PTEN pathway in BC. Our findings revealed that knocking down LINC01614 in BC cells led to a notable decrease in FN1 mRNA expression (Figure 7A,7B), while overexpressing LINC01614 increased FN1 mRNA levels (Figure 7C,7D). At the signaling pathway level, reducing LINC01614 expression led to lower PI3K and AKT mRNA levels and a significant increase in PTEN mRNA levels (Figure 7E). In contrast, increasing LINC01614 expression resulted in higher PI3K and AKT mRNA levels and lower PTEN mRNA expression (Figure 7F). Western blot analysis further validated these results by assessing protein expression levels. LINC01614 knockdown reduced FN1, PI3K, and AKT protein levels while increasing PTEN protein levels (Figure 7G-7I). Conversely, LINC01614 overexpression had the opposite effect, increasing FN1, PI3K, and AKT protein levels while decreasing PTEN protein levels (Figure 7J-7L). In conclusion, these findings suggest that LINC01614 regulates FN1 and the PI3K/AKT/PTEN signaling pathway by modulating miR-217, thereby promoting BC progression.

Biological role of LINC01614 in vivo

To gain further validation of LINC01614’s involvement in BC progression, we established a stable MDA-MB-231 cell line with LINC01614 knockdown (sh-LINC01614) and a corresponding control. These cells were used in tumor xenograft experiments to assess tumor growth in vivo. Our results showed that LINC01614 knockdown significantly slowed tumor growth, resulting in a significant decrease in tumor volume relative to the control group (Figure 8A-8C). Tumor mass was also significantly lower in the LINC01614 knockdown group (Figure 8D). To explore the underlying mechanisms, we examined miR-217 and FN1 expression in tumor tissues. LINC01614 knockdown significantly increased miR-217 mRNA levels (Figure 8E) while reducing FN1 mRNA expression (Figure 8F). At the protein level, PI3K and AKT expression decreased, whereas PTEN expression increased in the LINC01614 knockdown group (Figure 8G). IHC staining further confirmed that FN1 protein levels were lower in the LINC01614 knockdown group (Figure 8H). To assess metabolic changes in the tumor microenvironment, we measured intertumoral ATP, LD, and GLU levels. Consistent with our in vitro findings, LINC01614 knockdown significantly reduced these energy metabolism markers (Figure 8I-8K). Furthermore, we also explored the potential correlation between LINC01614 and immune cells in BC. As showed in Figure 8L-8P, LINC01614 was positively correlated with M0/M2 macrophage cells and the expression of LINC01614 are positively with the Monocytes and T cells.CD8. In summary, these results demonstrate that LINC01614 promotes BC progression both cellular (in vitro) and animal (in vivo) models, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.

Discussion

In recent years, researchers have found that non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) interact with different cellular molecules. Because of this, their role in BC has received a lot of attention. Research indicates that miRNAs can interact with both protein-coding RNAs and non-coding RNAs, including LncRNAs and circular RNAs (circRNAs), through common binding sites. This interaction, known as ceRNAs, plays a key role in BC regulation (23). LINC01614 has been recognized as an oncogene, playing a crucial role in regulating tamoxifen sensitivity and promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in BC cells (24). Bioinformatics studies suggest that the LINC01614/miR-204-5p/SLC31A1 pathway may help regulate copper-induced cell death in BC (25). Although research has made progress in understanding LINC01614’s role, many pathways and mechanisms remain unknown. In our study, we found that LINC01614 promotes BC cell growth and reduces apoptosis.

One area that has not been widely studied is how inhibitory miRNAs regulate cancer-related genes after translation and how ceRNA networks influence this process (26). The role of miR-217 is still unclear. Some studies suggest it may help cancer grow. Research has shown that miR-217 affects BC progression by activating the NF1-mediated c-Jun/ATF3/MMP13 and HSF1/ATG7 signaling pathways (27). Numerous studies have demonstrated that non-coding RNAs, including circRNAs, can modulate miR-217 through ceRNA mechanisms. This regulation plays a role in key BC processes, including blood vessel formation, chemotherapy resistance, tumor development, and metastasis. For example, circEZH2 functions as a miR-217-5p sponge, allowing KLF5 expression to increase. This activates FUS transcription, which helps form more circEZH2. This cycle promotes liver metastasis in BC induced by CXCR4 through the facilitation of EMT (28). Additionally, circ_0042881, regulated by EIF4A3, stimulates the RAS signaling pathway via the miR-217/SOS1 axis, thereby facilitating the progression of BC (29). circBACH1 also binds to miR-217, leading to higher G3BP2 expression. This increases cancer stemness, cell migration, blood vessel formation, and chemotherapy resistance in BC (30). In our research, we observed that LINC01614 modulates miR-217 via ceRNA mechanisms. This, in turn, targets FN1 and influences the PI3K/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway.

MiRNAs and LncRNAs are promising biomarkers because they are stable and easy to detect in body fluids. Numerous studies have demonstrated a strong correlation between their levels and various clinical characteristics (31). For instance, Tian et al. identified 48 LncRNAs associated with tumor size, lymph node situation, metastasis, histological grade, TNM stage, and the expression levels of ER, PR, and HER-2 in BC. They also found 32 LncRNAs associated with patient survival (32). Other research has indicated that altered levels of circulating LncRNAs in serum, saliva, and other bodily fluids play an important role in early diagnosis, disease classification, staging, treatment monitoring, and prognosis prediction in BC (33). LINC01614, in particular, has been widely studied. It is linked to disease onset, molecular subtypes, and patient prognosis (34-37).

Our study suggests that FN1 expression can influence tumor cell behavior in vitro. When miR-217 was upregulated and LINC01614 was silenced, FN1 expression decreased. This change reduced BC cell invasion and proliferation while increasing apoptosis. In contrast, silencing miR-217 increased FN1 expression, leading to higher proliferation and invasion but lower apoptosis. Numerous studies have demonstrated that FN1 functions as an oncogene in various types of cancer. In non-small cell lung cancer, exosomal LRG1 interacts with FN1 and promotes metastasis in animal models (38). In thyroid cancer, high FN1 expression is linked to advanced tumor stages and may serve as a prognostic biomarker. It is also associated with tumor immunity and poor survival when analyzed with four other key genes (39). In BC, high FN1 expression is related to worse clinical outcomes, while FN1 hypermethylation is linked to better prognosis. FN1 also shows a strong connection with tumor-infiltrating immune cells and immune checkpoint inhibitors, suggesting its role in future immunotherapy (40). Additionally, FN1 plays a role in cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in several cancers, such as glioblastoma (41), ovarian cancer (42), and BC (43), contributing to cancer spread. Targeting FN1 for metastatic BC has been explored (44,45) and could be a potential treatment option in the future.

In this research, we demonstrated that LINC01614 reduces the expression of miR-217 via its ceRNA activity, which increases FN1 expression. To understand how LINC01614 affects BC, we reviewed previous research and identified DCUN1D5 as a possible transcriptional target of the transcription factor YY1 in the FN1/PI3K/AKT pathway (22). Our experiments confirmed that changes in LINC01614 expression directly influenced FN1 levels and key regulators of the PI3K/AKT/PTEN axis, supporting earlier findings (22). The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is crucial for regulating various cellular processes, including cell metabolism, growth, proliferation, apoptosis, and angiogenesis. The activation of PI3K initiates this pathway, leading to the phosphorylation and activation of AKT, a critical serine/threonine kinase. Once activated, AKT regulates numerous downstream effectors, including mTOR, which plays a pivotal role in controlling protein synthesis, energy metabolism, and cell survival (46). Earlier research has demonstrated that single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway are associated with an increased risk of distant metastasis in various cancers. SNPs, being the most prevalent type of genetic variation, can influence gene expression and alter protein functionality. These changes influence key processes like cell growth, apoptosis, and angiogenesis, which are important in cancer progression (47). Because this pathway is involved in BC development and treatment resistance, it has become a major treatment target. Several AKT inhibitors have been developed for BC therapy (48). Among them, capivasertib, a pan-AKT inhibitor targeting all three AKT isoforms, has shown strong clinical benefits. In the phase III CAPItello-291 trial, the combination of capivasertib and fulvestrant resulted in an improvement in progression-free survival (PFS), reaching 7.3 months, in contrast to 3.1 months observed with the placebo plus fulvestrant (49).

Given that LINC01614 can bind to miR-217, regulate FN1 expression, and affect the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, and since this pathway is closely related to cell metabolism, we examined whether LINC01614 influences energy metabolism in BC. Our results showed that LINC01614 increases ATP production and promotes LD and GLU metabolism in BC cells. Many studies have reported a connection between ATP metabolism and BC progression, as well as chemotherapy resistance (50-52). Research also suggests that LD metabolism (53-55) and GLU metabolism (56,57) play important roles in BC development. Several LncRNAs, including PICSAR, LINC00839, AP001505.1, and LINC00393, have been linked to glucose metabolism and contribute to BC progression (58). These findings suggest that LINC01614 may regulate cancer cell metabolism in BC through the FN1/PI3K/AKT axis.

Our study has some limitations. First, we did not analyze clinical samples to confirm the expression and clinical importance of LINC01614. The ceRNA mechanism is an important process in LncRNA function, but it is still unknown whether LINC01614 can bind to other miRNAs. It is also unclear if the LINC01614-miR-217 axis regulates genes other than FN1. Although LncRNAs are usually considered non-coding, recent studies have found that some can produce functional peptides involved in BC progression (59). In addition, how the LINC01614-miR-217/FN1 axis affects ATP, LD, and GLU metabolism is not yet fully understood. In future studies, we plan to investigate these regulatory mechanisms in greater detail to enhance our understanding of the role of LncRNAs in BC.

Conclusions

LINC01614 is crucial in modulating the proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis of BC cells. This study highlights its interaction with miR-217 and its regulatory effects on downstream targets, including FN1, PI3K, AKT, and PTEN (Figure 9). These results offer valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying BC and may pave the way for the development of innovative therapeutic approaches.

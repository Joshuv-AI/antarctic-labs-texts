m7G-related gene signatures determine prognosis in triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Yunxiang Li, Xin Lan, Jianqiao Dong, Jing Li, Bin Wang, Yanhong Wang, Hongyan Jia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which lacks targeted treatment options, continues to pose a major clinical hurdle. This study investigated the molecular complexities of TNBC, with a focus on m7G modifications, the immune microenvironment, and their influence on patient prognosis. An integrated analysis of m7G-related genes facilitated the creation of a risk model based on differentially expressed m7G-related genes, which effectively stratified patients with TNBC into low- and high-risk groups, with the low-risk group demonstrating a marked survival advantage. Immune cell infiltration analysis revealed substantial differences between risk groups, suggesting a potential association between m7G-related risk and immune landscape modulation. Genomic profiling identified distinct mutational patterns, with TP53 mutations predominant in the high-risk group and PIK3CA mutations in the low-risk group. Chemosensitivity analysis indicated the potential for tailored drug responses, highlighting the utility of the m7G-related risk score in guiding personalized therapeutic approaches. Additionally, a nomogram incorporating the risk score, stage, and M stage was developed, providing a valuable tool for predicting 3-, 5-, and 10-year survival probabilities.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-21622-1.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) remains a leading global malignancy that significantly threatens women’s health1. The heterogeneity of BC contributes to diverse clinical outcomes, with survival rates being heavily influenced by tumor biology and treatment strategies2. Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which lacks expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), accounts for 15–20% of BC cases and represents the most aggressive subtype3. TNBC is associated with clinical features such as early onset, large tumor size, high pathological grade, increased invasiveness, and a higher propensity for early recurrence and metastasis4,5. Unlike other BC subtypes, TNBC lacks effective therapeutic targets, rendering chemotherapy the principal treatment approach. However, the development of chemotherapy resistance and a high rate of metastatic recurrence present significant treatment challenges6,7. Although emerging therapies, including PARP inhibitors and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, offer promising prospects for TNBC, their clinical efficacy remains limited7. Therefore, the development of reliable prognostic strategies for TNBC is critical for improving disease management8. Identifying distinct diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers is essential for uncovering new therapeutic targets and improving survival outcomes for patients with TNBC.

Advancements in high-throughput sequencing technology have accelerated research into RNA epigenetics, particularly RNA modifications, which are gaining recognition as a new frontier in molecular biology9. While eukaryotic RNA modifications have been known since the 1970s, initially identified in tRNAs and rRNAs10, modifications in mRNAs and various noncoding RNAs have been extensively characterized only in the past decade11–13. Emerging evidence highlights the critical role of disrupted RNA modification pathways in various cancers, positioning these modifications as promising targets for tumor prognosis and therapeutic intervention14.

As a key RNA modification involved in epigenetic regulation, N7-methylguanosine (m7G) has attracted considerable attention in recent research15. m7G modification entails the addition of a methyl group to the seventh nitrogen of guanosine, a process catalyzed by methyltransferase16. This modification, through RNA methylation, can initiate a range of physiological and pathological responses, influencing RNA synthesis, development, and degradation. Multiple studies have emphasized the pivotal function of m7G in tumor development13,17,18. Recent investigations suggest that m7G modifications are integral to the initiation and advancement of multiple cancers, such as esophageal cancer19, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma15, bladder cancer20, lung cancer21,22, and teratomas23. Furthermore, emerging studies have increasingly concentrated on the role of m7G in the tumor microenvironment (TME)24. Certain studies indicate that m7G-related genes can modulate immune cell distribution and the TME25. Additionally, m7G modifications have been shown to influence cancer resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy17.

Even with these discoveries, the function of m7G modification in TNBC remains inadequately comprehended. This study thoroughly investigated m7G patterns in TNBC, exploring the relationships between various regulatory molecules involved in m7G modification and the TME. A risk model incorporating seven m7G-related genes was developed to predict TNBC outcomes. To further assess the impact of risk scoring, a univariate regression model incorporating age and tumor stage was applied to patients with TNBC from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset, revealing that both age and tumor stage are independent prognostic factors. Finally, an integrated nomogram was established to quantitatively predict patient prognosis.

Materials and methods

Data

The analysis data were predominantly obtained from TCGA and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), as depicted in Fig. 1. Whole-genome expression profiles of TNBC in “transcripts per kilobase per million (TPM)” format, along with clinical annotations and single-nucleotide variation assessed through “VarScan2 Variant Aggregation and Masking”, were retrospectively retrieved using the R package ‘TCGAbiolinks’26 from the TCGA database. Additionally, data were obtained from the GEO database via the ‘GEOquery’ package. The dataset included TCGA-TNBC (RNA-seq) samples (n = 181), comprising 68 tumor and 113 healthy control samples, as well as GSE58812 (microarray) data with 107 tumor samples. While microarray technology allows for high-throughput gene expression analysis, RNA-seq offers greater sensitivity and resolution for detecting novel transcripts27. The ComBat method, implemented through the “sva” package, was applied to correct batch effects arising from nonbiological technical biases, thereby minimizing their impact on the results28. Principal component analysis (PCA) was utilized to assess the efficacy of the batch effect correction.

WGCNA

Coexpression networks were established through the application of the WGCNA algorithm within the “WGCNA” package29. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was utilized to evaluate the similarity of gene expression profiles, followed by the application of a power function to adjust correlation coefficients and construct a scale-free network. The ‘PickSoftThreshold’ package was employed to identify an optimal soft threshold with β set at 4. The average connectivity neared 0, while the scale independence surpassed 0.85, indicating that this threshold was appropriate. This resulted in the creation of a weighted adjacency matrix. Gene modules, defined as clusters of highly interconnected genes with similar coexpression patterns, were discerned via hierarchical clustering. The dynamic tree-cut method was utilized to discern distinct modules. To identify gene modules, cluster analysis was conducted on the topology overlay matrix (TOM), which was derived from the adjacency matrix to reflect topological similarity. After converting the adjacency matrix into a TOM, modules were identified. Pearson’s correlation analysis was then employed to evaluate the association of module eigengenes (MEs) with m7G (Supplementary Table S1). Modules exhibiting significant correlations with m7G were selected. The structure of the coexpression modules was displayed via a heatmap depicting the topological overlap of the gene network. A hierarchical clustering dendrogram of eigengenes was generated to summarize the interrelationships among the modules. Additionally, a heatmap was created to visualize the corresponding eigengene network, providing a comprehensive overview of the relationships between the modules.

Analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

DEGs were discovered utilizing the “limma” package (v 3.50.0)30, with a threshold of |log2FC (fold change)|> 0.5 and an adjusted p value < 0.05. Genes meeting these criteria were selected for further analysis. m7G-related DEGs were defined as those that overlapped between the DEGs and genes within the m7G-related module.

GSEA and GSVA

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was utilized to analyze predefined gene sets and evaluate differences between two conditions. This method focuses on identifying enriched gene sets rather than individual genes, offering a more comprehensive and biologically meaningful interpretation of the results31. GSEA was conducted utilizing the “clusterProfiler” package, based on log2FC values, with 1000 gene set permutations. The study selected the “c2.cp.Kegg.v7.5.1.symbols” set from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB)31–33 as the reference gene set. Gene sets with an adjusted p value of less than 0.05 were deemed significantly enriched.

Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) was used to examine correlations between biological pathways and gene features across tumor and normal groups. GSVA was performed utilizing the R package “GSVA” with the “c2.cp.Kegg.v7.5.1.symbols” gene set. Results were visualized using the “heatmap” package. Furthermore, 50 hallmark gene sets were acquired from MSigDB, and GSVA scores for each gene set were calculated utilizing the ssGSEA function in the “GSVA” package. The “Limma” package was then applied to compare GSVA scores between the two groups.

Gene ontology (GO) and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses

GO analysis34 included BP, MF, and CC. The KEGG35–37 analysis was used to identify significantly altered pathways. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted with the “clusterProfiler” package (v 4.2.2)38, and a p value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Immune infiltration analysis

Single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) calculates enrichment scores for gene sets within individual samples, evaluating DEGs in a given sample. Immune cell enrichment scores were derived from the Tumor and Immune System Interactions Database39, which includes data on 28 immune cell types, based on the gene expression profile of the tumor samples. The degree of immune cell infiltration in different clustering subtypes was assessed using the “ggplot2” package40.

Prognostic system development

Univariate Cox hazard analysis was conducted to evaluate the predictive value of m7G-related DEGs in relation to overall survival (OS). Genes exhibiting a p value less than 0.05 were selected for further analysis. The tumor samples were randomly allocated into training (n = 125) and validation (n = 50) cohorts. The LASSO Cox regression model, executed via the “glmnet” package41, was applied to refine candidate genes and construct the prognostic model. The optimal penalty parameter (λ) was ascertained utilizeing the minimum criteria. In this formula, Coef (genei) refers to the coefficients associated with each gene, while Expression (genei) indicates the expression level of the respective gene).

The risk score cutoff value was determined using the surv_cutpoint function, which allowed classification of the training cohort into low-risk and high-risk groups. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated to evaluate prognostic value, and the log-rank test was conducted to determine statistical significance. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to assess the model’s performance, with the area under the curve (AUC) ranging from 0.5 to 1, where a value closer to 1 indicates superior model accuracy. Internal validation was performed by dividing the cohort into low-risk and high-risk groups to further confirm the robustness of the prognostic model across different patient populations. Age and tumor stage data were collected from both the TCGA and GEO cohorts and analyzed alongside the risk score utilizing univariate Cox regression models. Gene expression differences between subgroups were assessed using the “ggplot2” package.

Drug susceptibility assessment

Drug response predictions were made using the “OncoPredict” package42 by integrating IC50 data from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer database43 and gene expression data, allowing for the identification of low- and high-risk BC subgroups and their potential drug sensitivities.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted utilizing R software (v 4.1.2). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed utilizing the “survival” and “survminer” packages. Spearman’s correlation test was employed to evaluate the relationships between variables. Wilcoxon tests were utilized for comparing two groups, and Kruskal‒Wallis tests were used for comparisons among three or more groups. The threshold for statistical significance was set at a two-sided p value < 0.05.

Results

WGCNA was employed to identify m7G-related genes. As shown in Fig. 2A, a weighted value of 4 resulted in an average connectivity close to 0 and a scale independence above 0.85. Ten coexpressed modules were uncovered, with uncorrelated genes designated to a gray module, which was omitted from subsequent analysis (Fig. 2B). To assess the importance of the modules, MEs relating to m7G were correlated. The heatmap indicated that genes within the blue module (831 genes, see Supplementary Table S2) exhibited the most robust positive correlation with m7G (r = 0.5284, p < 0.05). Consequently, the blue module was prioritized in further analyses as it provided a more precise reflection of m7G (Fig. 2C). Correlations among the MEs were further analyzed, and the eigengene network is presented in Fig. 2D. Scatterplot illustrating gene significance (GS) for m7G versus module membership (MM) within the blue module are presented in Fig. 2E. This plot demonstrated a notably strong positive correlation between MM and GS for m7G (cor = 0.54, p < 0.05). Identification of DEGs associated with m7G

A comparison between tumor and healthy control samples revealed 2330 DEGs, with 796 genes upregulated and 1534 downregulated in TNBC (Supplementary Table S3). A volcano plot (Supplementary Fig. S1A) visualized the DEGs, while a heatmap (Supplementary Fig. S1B) highlighted the top 5 upregulated and 7 downregulated genes. Additionally, 76 m7G-related DEGs were identified (Supplementary Fig. S1C, Supplementary Table S4). To explore the physiological roles of these m7G-related DEGs, GO term (Supplementary Table S5) and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were carried out (Supplementary Fig. S1F, Supplementary Table S6). GO analysis revealed significant enrichment in genes associated with the regulation of anoikis, glycoprotein biosynthesis, and metabolic processes. KEGG pathway analysis indicated significant enrichment in pathways associated with cancer and endocrine system-related diseases, including parathyroid hormone synthesis, secretion, and action; thyroid cancer; and prostate cancer pathways (Supplementary Fig. S1D,E).

A prognostic model using m7G-related DEGs

Batch-related variance was effectively mitigated using the R package ‘sva’ (Fig. 3A,B). To explore the prognostic significance of m7G-related DEGs in TNBC, univariate analysis was conducted, identifying 10 genes significantly associated with OS, as listed in Supplementary Table S7. To develop a clinically relevant scoring model, LASSO regression analysis was applied, resulting in 7 DEGs (Fig. 3C,D, Supplementary Table S8). Patients with BC were classified into low- and high-risk categories according to their risk scores. The low-risk group demonstrated significantly longer OS (p < 0.05) than the high-risk group (Fig. 3E,F). Then, ROC curve further validated the model’s predictive efficacy. (AUC > 0.6; Fig. 3G,H). The group with lower risk had a lower death rate compared to the high-risk group (Fig. 4A–D). With increasing risk scores, levels of IL6ST, LEF1, and TMEM26 decreased, while CST2, CACFD1, LRRC73, and LMTK3 levels increased (Fig. 4E,F).

DEGs between the high- and low-risk groups

Between the two groups, 982 DEGs were detected, with 395 upregulated and 587 downregulated genes in the TNBC samples (Supplementary Table S9). A volcano plot (Supplementary Fig. S2A) was used to visualize all DEGs, and a heatmap highlighted the top 6 upregulated and top 7 downregulated genes (Supplementary Fig. S2B). GO analysis revealed significant enrichment in several biological terms (p < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. S2C, Table S10).

Functional annotation analysis identifies pathway activity

GSVA was performed to assess pathway expression, with results displayed in heatmaps (Supplementary Fig. S3A, Supplementary Table S11). To further explore DEG-related mechanisms, GSEA was conducted, revealing pathways with significant enrichment according to the normalized enrichment score (NES) (Supplementary Fig. S3B–G, Supplementary Table S12). In the high-risk group, pathways associated with Terpenoid Backbone Biosynthesis (NES = 1.9593; P.adjust = 0.0115; FDR = 0.0078; Fig. S3B), Homologous Recombination (NES = 1.9245; P.adjust = 0.0115; FDR = 0.0078; Fig. S3C), and Fructose and Mannose Metabolism (NES = 1.8783; P.adjust = 0.0115; FDR = 0.0078; Fig. S3D) were enriched. Conversely, the low-risk group showed enrichment in pathways associated with Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (NES = − 2.5132; P.adjust = 0.01; FDR = 0.0068; Fig. S3E), Hematopoietic Cell Lineage (NES = − 2.634; P.adjust = 0.01; FDR = 0.0068; Fig. S3F), and Cytokine Receptor Interaction (NES = − 2.7862; P.adjust = 0.01; FDR = 0.0068; Fig. S3G). These distinct findings may be crucial in influencing the prognoses of the respective groups.

The proportions of 28 immune subpopulations were assessed (Fig. 5A), with the results showing significantly higher immune cell infiltration in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, a positive correlation among immune cell types was observed (Fig. 5C). Spearman’s correlation analysis was then applied to assess the relationships between immune cell types and prognostic genes (Fig. 6A–I).

Mutation analysis and chemotherapeutic sensitivity assessment in TNBC risk stratification: implications for treatment selection

Mutation assessment showed that TP53 exhibited the highest mutation rate in the high-risk group, while PIK3CA had the most frequent mutations in the low-risk group (Fig. 7A,B). The TNBC samples from the high-risk group demonstrated a notably elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB) (Fig. 7C). To evaluate the predictive usefulness of the risk score for chemotherapeutic sensitivity in BC, data from Supplementary Table S13 were analyzed. The low-risk group showed potential sensitivity to several drugs, including CDK9_5038_1709 (Fig. 7D), Dactinomycin_1811 (Fig. 7E), Dactinomycin_1911 (Fig. 7F), Daporinad_1248 (Fig. 7G), Dinaciclib_1180 (Fig. 7H), Epirubicin_1511 (Fig. 7I), and Gemcitabine_1190 (Fig. 7J), and patients in the high-risk group were more sensitive to Sepantronium bromide_1941 (Fig. 7K), suggesting that chemotherapy may be particularly effective for this group.

Discussion

BC stands as the most prevalent cancer in women and is classified into three main types: hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-positive, and TNBC44. Unlike other BC subtypes, TNBC lacks relevant receptor markers, making it unresponsive to endocrine therapy and HER2-targeted therapies, thus rendering it the most challenging BC subtype to treat44,45. Women with TNBC have a higher incidence of early distant recurrence and a poorer 5-year prognosis relative to individuals with other BC subtypes46. Although PARP inhibitors are now approved for BRCA-mutated TNBC and immunomodulators show considerable promise, targeted therapies for TNBC remain in the early stages of development46. Therefore, identifying new biomarkers is crucial for advancing treatment strategies for TNBC. Recently, the role of m7G in tumorigenesis has gained increasing attention17. The expression of m7G methyltransferase in tumor samples is context-dependent. m7G can significantly influence tumor biological behavior, exerting either promotive or inhibitory effects on cancer development, and is closely associated with tumor prognosis and therapeutic efficacy47–49. m7G-related genes have been identified as potential prognostic indicators in various cancers. For instance, Ying et al. demonstrated that methylation patterns based on m7G regulators are linked to OS rates, TMB, responses to targeted inhibitors, and immunotherapy outcomes in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma50. Additionally, m7G-related gene signatures have been shown to predict outcomes and treatment responses in bladder and hepatocellular cancers51,52. Nevertheless, there’s a lack of thorough research examining the involvement of m7G-related genes in TNBC. Thus, this study seeks to delve into the function of m7G-related genes in TNBC, assess their prognostic significance, and explore their roles in the TME.

This study included patients diagnosed with TNBC, and m7G-related DEGs were identified through comparisons between tumor and healthy control samples. KEGG pathway analysis indicated notable enrichment in cancers and diseaseslinked to the endocrine system, suggesting common oncogenes shared by endocrine-associated cancers, like prostate cancer, thyroid cancer, and BC. GO term analysis indicated substantial enrichment in biological processes, including anoikis, glycoprotein biosynthesis, and glycoprotein metabolic processes. The results suggest that the DEGs participate in regulating cell interactions with the extracellular matrix, cell survival, and tumor progression. Moreover, the regulation of glycoprotein synthesis and metabolism may play a critical role in TNBC development.

A prognostic signature utilizing m7G-related genes was developed from the identified DEGs. The 7-gene signature, comprising CST2, CACFD1, LRRC73, LMTK3, IL6ST, LEF1, and TMEM26, effectively stratified patients into two subgroups. This signature demonstrated robust prognostic value: (1) It successfully distinguished between low- and high-risk patients. (2) the high-risk group exhibited a significantly poorer prognosis, (3) notable differences were observed in the TME between the two subgroups, and (4) Although this prognostic signature was valuable in predicting the survival outcome of TNBC, it is not a perfect predictor. Future research needs to further verify and optimize the clinical application value of this model through larger-scale cohorts.

In the TME, immune cells are crucial in tumor progression and therapeutic response. This diverse population includes both innate and adaptive components, with lymphocytes representing the adaptive arm. Immune cells engage with tumor cells through direct interactions and signaling via chemokines and cytokines, profoundly influencing tumor behavior and therapy responsiveness. Immune cells exhibit a dual function, either enhancing or hindering therapeutic efficacy, with their activation status and spatial distribution within the TME further complicating the tumor-host interaction dynamics53–56. In the present study, TNBC patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups according to m7G-related genes. Immune infiltration analysis showed that most immune cell types infiltrated at notably higher rates in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group. Similar findings have been reported in studies analyzing m7G-related patterns across various cancers50,57–59. This indicates that the m7G-related risk score may influence the immune landscape in TNBC, potentially explaining the observed variations in patient outcomes. Notably, immune cells like mast cells and regulatory T cells showed significantly higher infiltration rates in the low-risk group. Additionally, the prognostic gene CACFD1 exhibited notably high expression levels in the high-risk group, displayed a significant negative correlation with mast cells and regulatory T cells, indicating that m7G-related genes might be involved in shaping the immune microenvironment specific to TNBC. Several studies have indicated that mast cell infiltration in tumors correlates with improved patient survival60. Furthermore, research suggests that drugs designed to enhance mast cell activation or infiltration could boost the anti-tumor immune response in TNBC45. Tumor-infiltrating mast cells (TIMCs) can uniquely influence anti-tumor immunity by promoting the accumulation of various immune cell clusters, and TIMCs are crucial in modulating the immune microenvironment of TNBC61. Unlike HER2+ and luminal breast cancers, TNBC is characterized by elevated levels of regulatory T cells, highlighting the subtype-specific immune microenvironment of TNBC62. In summary, m7G modification contributes to a unique immune regulatory network in TNBC, providing a molecular foundation for the development of TNBC-specific therapeutic strategies.

Among the seven signature-associated genes (CST2, IL6ST, LEF1, TMEM26, CACFD1, LRRC73, and LMTK3), IL6ST plays a pivotal role in signal transduction induced by various factors63,64. Previous studies have suggested that elevated IL6ST expression correlates with favorable prognosis and enhanced survival outcomes in BC patients65–68. In the present study, the high-risk group showed reduced IL6ST expression,suggesting that higher levels of IL6ST correlates with better prognosis, which aligns with previous findings69. In ER-positive (ER+) BC, IL6ST primarily regulates hormone signaling pathways70, suggesting that its regulation by m7G modification could be a TNBC-specific molecular event. The LEF1 gene is associated with various cancers, including prostate and hepatocellular carcinoma63. LEF1 expression levels can serve as biomarkers for tumor prognosis, with higher levels typically signifying a poorer prognosis and a need for more aggressive treatment. However, in some cases, elevated LEF1 expression may indicate a favorable treatment response, underlining the complexity of its role in cancer progression71. Additional research is required to evaluate the potential of LEF1 as a predictive biomarker for various cancer types and to establish objective criteria for assessing the severity of the disease. CST2, a cysteine protease inhibitor, has been associated with the dysregulation of several cancers72. It is notably expressed at higher levels in BC tissues73, and recent studies have identified CST2 as a promising immunotherapy target for serous ovarian cancer74. LMTK3, a receptor tyrosine kinase with oncogenic potential75, is crucial in the onset and progression of BC76. Elevated cytoplasmic levels of LMTK3 in TNBC have been shown to promote tumor invasion and metastasis75. Furthermore, high LMTK3 expression serves as an independent prognostic indicator for ER + BC patients77. In the present research, LMTK3 was identified as a prognostic gene related to m7G, with higher expression levels in high-risk patients with TNBC. Given the absence of ER expression in TNBC, these findings suggest that m7G modification may confer a unique regulatory role to LMTK3 in TNBC.

TMEM26 encodes proteins with multiple transmembrane domains78. Elevated expression of TMEM26 in TNBC has been linked to a higher likelihood of recurrence79, and higher levels of TMEM26 RNA in pancreatic cancer correlate with poorer prognosis79. Although research on CACFD1 and LRRC73 in BC is currently limited, this study found their differential expression in relation to m7G modification in TNBC, revealing their strong association with the immune microenvironment and providing new molecular insights into TNBC heterogeneity.

In summary, the absence of ER/PR/HER2 expression in TNBC highlights the distinct molecular mechanisms provided by m7G modification, setting TNBC apart from other BC subtypes. Moreover, our results demonstrate a notable link between essential signature genes and immune cell infiltration, suggesting that m7G regulation of the TME is crucial in determining the outcomes of patients with TNBC.

As a valuable biomarker, TMB is widely used to predict the effectiveness of immunotherapy across various tumor types and can be a reliable indicator of patient benefit80. In the present study, a significantly higher TMB was observed in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group. These findings highlight the potential implications for developing immunotherapeutic strategies tailored to TNBC. The elevated TMB in high-risk patients suggests that such individuals may derive greater benefit from immunotherapies. Incorporating TMB evaluation into risk stratification models can guide treatment decisions and improve the efficacy of immunotherapy. Additionally, our study identified specific gene mutations in TNBC samples, with TP53 and PIK3CA exhibiting the highest mutation frequencies in different risk groups. This highlights the molecular heterogeneity of TNBC and suggests that distinct pathways are involved in each risk group. Targeted therapies against these mutations could improve treatment outcomes for patients with TNBC.

Furthermore, our analysis of chemotherapy sensitivity based on risk scores revealed differential drug responses across risk groups. Patients in the low-risk group demonstrated heightened sensitivity to several drugs, including CDK9, dactinomycin, daporinad, dinaciclib, epirubicin, and gemcitabine, while those in the high-risk group showed stronger sensitivity to Sepantronium bromide. These findings suggest that risk stratification could guide the selection of personalized chemotherapy regimens, optimizing treatment outcomes for individual patients.

This study has some limitations. Despite correcting for batch effects using the ComBat method, the integration of TCGA (RNA-seq) and GSE58812 (microarray) data may still introduce systematic bias. Future studies plan to prioritize the use of homogeneous data for modeling and implement independent dataset validation to enhance the model’s dependability and applicability. The risk signature reliant on m7G was constructed based on a comparatively limited number of patients diagnosed with TNBC. Thus, extensive, forward-looking studies are necessary to confirm our results. Moreover, as the m7G-dependent risk signature was generated solely through bioinformatics analysis, additional experimental studies are needed to confirm these results and strengthen the conclusions. Additionally, exploring the underlying mechanisms driving the observed associations between risk scores, genetic alterations, and treatment responses could provide deeper insights into TNBC biology and pave the way for novel therapeutic interventions.

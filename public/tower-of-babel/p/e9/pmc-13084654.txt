The relevance of starch and protein digestive dynamics on broiler performance: A Meta-analysis
Authors: Mengzhu Wang, Shemil Macelline, Sonia Y Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough nutrient digestibility (dig) is traditionally assessed in the distal ileum, the majority of starch and protein digestion and absorption occurs in the jejunum. Increasing attention has been directed toward the synchronization of starch and protein and their impact on growth performance and feed efficiency. The objective of this meta-analysis is to validate the relevance of starch and protein digestive dynamics on growth performance and to evaluate the validity of jejunal dig of nutrients as the indicator for digestion rate. A total of 260 data points extracted from 32 published papers were included in the analysis, covering a wide range of broiler ages and dietary compositions. A multiple-level meta-analysis was employed, which accounts for both between-study and within-study variations. The results indicate that the nutrient dig exhibits greater variation in the proximal jejunum (PJ) compared to more distal sections of the intestine. Both ileal and jejunal dig of starch and protein impacted on body weight gain (BWG) and feed conversion ratio (FCR). When ileal digestible nutrients are not limiting, optimizing proximal jejunal starch and distal jejunal protein dig would benefit BWG and FCR. Synchrony of starch and protein utilisation is essential for BWG where the maximal BWG is predicted to be 1703 g/bird when the ratio of digestible starch and protein in the PJ equals to 4.01. In conclusion, when digestion rate data is difficult to generate, proximal jejunal digestible starch and distal digestible protein can be reliable indicators of the rate of digestion to predict growth performance in broiler chickens.Keywords: Meta-analysis, Broiler, Digestion dynamics, Growth performance indicator, Jejunum, Ileum

Introduction

Cereal grains and protein-rich by-product meals together constitute over 80% of typical poultry diets (Alwasity et al., 2023). Cereal grains primarily provide starch, the main energy source for poultry, while protein meals supply essential amino acids necessary for growth and physiological functions. Due to intensive genetic selection, modern broiler chickens have reduced body fat and are believed to be more responsive to dietary amino acid or protein density than to energy levels (Toghyani et al., 2024). The significance of amino acid density and its impact on growth performance has been extensively studied (Kidd and Tillman, 2016; Liu et al., 2019). Moreover, Gidley (2022) emphasized the need to consider nutritional mechanisms beyond individual nutrients, proposing for a more integrated approach that accounts for interactions among various feed ingredients and nutrients. Efficient protein deposition requires a balanced supply of both energy and amino acids; in this context, Liu and Selle (2015) proposed that feed efficiency in broiler chickens can be optimized by incorporating either rapidly digestible protein sources or slowly digestible starches.

The extent of nutrient digestion by the end of the distal ileum (DI) is conventionally used to evaluate feed quality, and poultry diets are typically formulated based on ileal digestible amino acids. This approach is based on the assumption that ileal digestibility (dig) is strongly associated with growth performance and is not confounded by hindgut microbiota activity (Ravindran et al., 2005). However, the dig coefficient is a static measure and does not account for the speed of digestion. Liu and Selle (2015) proposed that the rate of starch and protein digestion is more relevant to growth performance than dig per s and suggested that the digestion rate of one nutrient may be influenced by the alteration of the other. The importance of digestion rate is increasingly being recognized across species beyond poultry and more recently, Gidley (2025) suggested that digestion rate information should be included on food product labels. The in vivo methods for determining starch and protein digestion rates were described by Liu et al. (2013). In short, digesta must be collected from four sites along the small intestine, and the data should be correlated with the mean retention time in each intestinal segment to estimate the slope of change, which is defined as the digestion rate. However, this approach is time-consuming and expensive. The primary sites for digestion and absorption differ spatially, and these differences also vary between starch and protein. Digestive juice, including amylase and protease, are secreted by the pancreas and delivered to the duodenum via the pancreatic duct (Vertiprakhov et al., 2024). However, digestion of starch and protein is initiated in the proventriculus due to the anti-peristaltic movements from duodenum to gizzard and from gizzard to proventriculus (Rodrigues and Choct, 2018). More than 90% of digestible starch and 70% of digestible protein are digested by the end of jejunum (Selle et al., 2021; Toghyani et al., 2020). A correlation between FCR and protein dig in the jejunum was reported by Liu and Selle (2015), whereas no such association was observed in the DI. Considering that the jejunum is the primary site of digestion and absorption, it has been proposed to use jejunal dig as an indicator of the rate of digestion.

Numerous studies have investigated or reported distal jejunal dig since Liu and Selle (2015); hence, the objective of the meta-analysis is to investigate: 1) whether starch and protein digestive dynamics should be considered in tandem, rather than independently, to improve the prediction of growth performance; and 2) whether jejunal dig of starch and protein serve as reliable indicators of digestion rate and growth performance.

Methodology

Literature search

The studies were retrieved from public database including Web of Science, google scholar, and Scopus. Studies reporting growth performance and dig coefficients of starch and protein in at least two intestine segments were considered for the current analysis. Studies were limited to those conducted on the same farm, located on the Camden campus of the University of Sydney, with differences only in trial year, diets, and broiler batch. This approach was taken to reduce error noise in data analysis. All birds were raised according to breeder guidelines, including standardized lighting programs, temperature control, and other husbandry practices. A comprehensive literature search was conducted following the established preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analysis (PRISMA) guidelines, adapted for a multi-species study (Page et al., 2021) by the following keywords and query strings (Author: “Sonia Yun Liu” OR “Peter Selle” OR “Shemil Macelline”) AND (‘Year: 2015-2025’) (before 20 March 2025). After removing duplicates, titles, and abstracts of all retrieved records were screened to exclude the irrelevant studies.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies included in the current analysis were required to meet the following criteria: 1) the research was conducted at Camden farm within the University of Sydney between 2015 and 2025; 2) the dietary composition and nutrient specifications were either reported or could be calculated; 3) both growth performance and apparent dig coefficients of crude protein and starch for two or more intestine sections were reported. Based on the above criteria, 32 out of 109 publications were selected (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of selection process of the articles used for meta-analysis.

Data extraction

The basic trial information was listed first, including the year the study was conducted, the birds' age at the beginning and end of the experimental period (start age and end age), and the number of days of the feeding study (duration). Then, dietary composition, including concentrations of macro-ingredients and supplemented amino acids, as well as calculated or analyzed nutrient specifications such as starch, crude protein, and digestible amino acids, were included. The dependent variables included growth performance and apparent dig coefficients of starch and crude protein at four sites of the small intestine (proximal jejunum [PJ], distal jejunum [DJ], proximal ileum [PI], and DI). Dietary digestible starch to protein in jejunum and ileum was recalculated based on the equation as below:

Ratio of digestible starch to protein (SP) = (Dietary starch content × Appearant dig of starch)/(Dietary protein content × Appearant dig of protein).

Treatment means were used, and the mean standard error of the mean (SEM) across treatments was calculated. Literature research and data extraction was performed independently by two researchers to ensure accuracy and reliability.

Data analysis

A multilevel meta-analysis (MLM) approach was applied to evaluate the heterogeneity of the dependent variables across studies as described in Harrer et al. (2021). The study is conducted by using metafor package in R version 4.5.0. Subsequently, two random-effects meta-regressions (REMA) were implemented (Assink and Wibbelink, 2016) to explore the extent to which the dependent variables can be explained by independent variables (Nakagawa et al., 2017). The robust REMA was used to estimate the more accurate variance of independent variables if there is publication bias (Sidik and Jonkman, 2006). When multiple independent variables were included, the REMA model was established by including individual independent variable first, then their second-degree terms, and then their interactions. Only significant terms were retained in the final model. The age of birds at the end of the study and the duration of the study were included as covariates in all models when the age and days are relevant in the model. When investigating the impact of independent variables on dependent variables, end age and duration days were fixed at their mean values within the range covered by the model.

Model evaluation was based on P-value, tau square (τ2) and Akaike information criterion (AIC) values. P-value assessed the statistical significance of the model and individual terms, τ2 quantified the heterogeneity between and within studies, and AIC provided a measure of model fit by balancing model complexity and goodness of fit (Wagenmakers and Farrell, 2004). Pseudo-R2 represented the portion of variance explained by variables in the model compared to the null model (Thompson and Higgins, 2002). It was calculated using Eq. (1) below. In addition, Cochran's Q for residual Heterogeneity (QE) statistics were used to assess the extent of reduction in residual heterogeneity of model incorporating independent variables, compares to the null model, which included only end ages and numbers of days (Lin et al., 2017).

Publication bias

Publication bias was visualized by using a funnel plot based on residuals from the end ages and the numbers of days of the feeding studies from the MLM model (Nakagawa et al., 2023). A symmetrical funnel plot suggests no publication bias, while an asymmetrical may indicate potential presence of publication bias. Egger's linear regression test was applied within MLM model to accommodate the nested structure of the data (Nakagawa et al., 2022). Specifically, a significant coefficient (P < 0.05) for standard error, which included as the covariate in the model, indicated the presence of publication bias.

Discussion

Consistently across all studies selected for meta-analyses, greater variability in dig measurements was observed at the proximal site of the small intestine compared to the DI. This variation in the PJ is hypothesized to be resulted from a combination of factors, including endogenous nitrogen flow, reverse peristalsis, gizzard contractions, high moisture content, the nature of feed ingredients, and the rate of nutrient digestion (Rodrigues and Choct, 2018). The high variability in apparent dig at the proximal site of the small intestine has also posed challenges in quantifying the rate of nutrient digestion (Toghyani et al., 2020), where dig is measured at four sites along the small intestine and correlated with retention time using exponential models. Apparent protein dig tends to be more variable than starch, as shown in the present study and supported by Liu et al. (2014) and this may be attributed to the high volume of endogenous nitrogen flow in the duodenum and jejunum. Liu et al. (2014) reported that the apparent dig of protein increased from −0.213 in the PJ to 0.603 in the DJ in the control diet. This change is equivalent to the absorption of approximately 81.6% of dietary protein (nitrogen) between the PJ and DJ. The large variation observed in the PJ also highlights the dietary impact on digestion rate and speed, and supports the hypothesis that this site is the most indicative predictor of BWG.

Traditionally, DI dig, expressed as digestible nutrients used in practical poultry feed formulation, has been used to evaluate raw ingredient quality and was considered as a reliable predictor of growth performance. Liu and Selle (2015) summarized a series of sorghum-based diet studies involving over thirty dietary treatments and concluded that growth performance correlated more strongly with jejunal dig than with ileal dig. Subsequent research using wheat and corn-based diets confirmed the relevance of starch and protein digestion rates to BWG and feed conversion efficiency (Liu et al., 2014, 2016b). However, these studies were conducted using iso-energetic diets with similar ingredient profiles and compositions which may have led to relatively limited variation in ileal nutrient dig values.

In contrast, the present investigation included a broader range of dietary structures, energy and amino acid densities, and bird ages to assess the general impact of ileal dig on growth performance. When a wide range of dig values was examined, DI dig showed a linear correlation with BWG, where higher dig in the DI consistently led to greater BWG. The slope of this relationship remained stable regardless of starch dig levels in the PJ (Fig. 3). Conversely, there was a quadratic relationship between BWG and PJ starch dig where both low and excessively high starch digestion in the PJ were associated with compromised growth performance. This could be attributed to the exhausted energy substrate for enterocytes in lower small intestine when the PJ starch dig is high (He et al., 2022). Starch is digested more rapidly than protein, as indicated by apparent dig values. When jejunal starch dig is excessively high, the differences between starch and protein digestion rate widens, which may compromise BWG by impairing protein synthesis, which requires a simultaneous supply of both amino acids and glucose. For protein digestion, both BWG and feed efficiency improved with increasing apparent dig in the DJ and DI. However, the influence of jejunal dig was less pronounced when apparent ileal protein dig was limited. These findings emphasise the importance of ileal starch and protein dig in supporting body protein deposition and feed conversion efficiency. When nutrient quality or the extent of digestion is not limiting, optimizing growth performance requires increasing protein digestion and balancing the rate of starch digestion.

From a biochemical perspective, both glucose and amino acids are essential for protein synthesis, with glucose serving as the primary energy source and amino acids providing the structural building blocks. Quadratic relationships between starch: protein disappearance rate ratios in the DI and FCR in broiler chickens were reported in several studies (Selle and Liu, 2019), where an optimal starch to protein disappearance rate ratio can be predicted to optimize feed conversion and BWG. In the present study, linear correlations were observed between SP in the DI and both FCR and BWG where the narrower ratio between digestible starch and protein benefited feed conversion and BWG. Disappearance rate accounts for feed intake, dig, and nutrient content in the diet. When considering SP, including dietary content and dig of starch and protein, excluding the influence of feed intake and dietary nutrient content, optimal BWG was achieved when the SP equalled 4.01 in the PJ (Fig. 4) and BWG decreased as SP in the DI increases. More interestingly, the SPdiet was highly correlated with SP dig in the PJ, but the coefficient of influence is getting smaller along the small intestine (Fig. 5). This suggests that dig in the PJ provides better resolution for detecting differences in ingredients and dietary composition, and serves as an indicator of nutrient availability, which in turn influences growth performance.

Understanding how the synchrony of starch and protein digestive dynamics optimizes growth performance is the key to identifying strategies that spare amino acids from catabolism in enterocytes, where they are otherwise utilized as an energy source. Approximately 20% of dietary energy is consumed by the gastrointestinal tract during digestion and absorption (Cant et al., 1996; Stoll et al., 1998). The small intestine uses up to 70% of dietary amino acids for the synthesis of endogenous digestive enzymes and to support gut function (Stoll et al., 1998). Both glucose and amino acids, particularly glutamate and glutamine, undergo catabolism in enterocytes to meet the substantial energy demands of the gut (Watford et al., 1979).

In general, starch digestion is more rapid than protein digestion (Liu et al., 2013). One of the key objectives in understanding digestive synchrony is to develop feeding strategies that optimize the utilization of glucose and amino acids by enterocytes, thereby sparing amino acids for protein synthesis. For example, slowing starch digestion could ensure glucose availability in the lower small intestine to match the slower digestion of protein. Alternatively, accelerating protein digestion by using high-quality feed ingredients or synthetic amino acids could increase amino acid availability for absorption when glucose is still present. However, Moss et al. (2018) reported that PI starch dig coefficients were negatively correlated with the dig coefficients of twelve essential amino acids in the PI. This suggests a potential competitive interaction between glucose and amino acids for intestinal uptake and highlights the complexity of synchronizing nutrient digestion to support optimal growth performance. One limitation of the current investigation is the lack of comparison between the dig of individual amino acids in the PJ and their relevance to feed conversion efficiency; hence, further research is needed to clarify how specific amino acid digestion dynamics interact with carbohydrate availability to influence nutrient utilization and growth performance.

Conclusion

All data in the present study were generated from an Australian research facility, which may present a potential limitation, despite the diets being based on globally relevant grains such as wheat, corn, and sorghum. The relevance of digestive dynamics of starch and protein along the entire small intestine to the growth performance of broiler chickens has been investigated broadly. The dig of starch in the PJ and the dig of protein in the DJ is a reliable indicator of feed efficiency. The interaction of dig of starch and protein, as well as the impact of SP, in predicting growth performance, suggests that these nutrients should not be considered separately and synchrony of glucose and amino acid utilization is essential for FCR and BWG. However, further investigation on the relevance of individual amino acid dig in the jejunum should be conducted as amino acid matrices, not crude protein, is being utilized in practical feed formulation. A comprehensive database on the dig of starch, protein and amino acids across various feedstuff would assist nutritionists in improving formulation efficiency by better synchronizing energy and protein, when digestion rate data is difficult to generate, PJ digestible starch and distal digestible protein can be reliable indicators of the rate of digestion.

Challenges faced by teachers in teaching History and Geography in secondary schools—Dataset from Vietnam
Authors: Thanh Thi Nguyen, Dao Thi Bich Pham, Chinh Ngoc Dao, Ha Thanh Phan, Toan Van Dao, Ninh Trung Tran, Loi Thi Bich Phan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe article summarizes a dataset that highlights teachers' perspectives on issues related the implementation of History and Geography curriculum in Vietnamese secondary schools. It particularly emphasizes the challenges faced by teachers during the initial years of the integrated curriculum implementation. Six key aspects pertaining to the History and Geography curriculum implementation addressed are: (1) teachers' specialized knowledge in their field of instruction, (2) teachers' perceptions of the History and Geography curriculum, (3) the current status of History and Geography teaching assignments in schools, (4) the challenges faced by teachers, (5) factors that cause challenges for teachers, and (6) teachers' expectations for effective History and Geography curriculum implementation. Data were collected from June 24, 2024, to July 2, 2024, through an online survey conducted on the Google Forms, with responses from 2,619 history and geography teachers across eight provinces in Vietnam. The data were organized and coded to ensure security and ease of analysis. The insights drawn from this analysis provide a deep understanding of the difficulties and challenges faced by history and geography teachers, thereby helping to identify the necessary factors to enhance the quality of education in this subject. This dataset is constructed with reusability in mind for subsequent research, facilitating comparisons and evaluations of changes or impacts on education quality over time and across geographical locations. This data can be applied in developing new educational policies, improving teacher training programs, and supporting further research on effective teaching methods. Moreover, sharing this dataset opens up opportunities for collaboration between researchers and educational institutions, enabling the joint discovery of optimal solutions for teaching History and Geography in Vietnam and beyond.Keywords: Integrated subject teaching, Instructional difficulties, Teachers' expectations, Curriculum implementation obstacles

Background

This dataset stems from the project “Investigating challenges and proposing solutions for organizing integrated subject instruction in secondary and high schools under the 2018 National curriculum”. In Vietnam, History and Geography is an integrated subject in secondary schools, part of the 2018 National Curriculum [10]. The novelties of this curriculum have posed significant challenges for teachers during its implementation. Identifying factors that affect the teaching process, particularly the challenges faced by teachers, is crucial for developing effective solutions that ensure more successful curriculum implementation [[4], [5]–6].

This study is grounded in educational theory and pedagogical research [2], which emphasizes the importance of teacher preparation, available resources, and organizational support in providing high-quality education [13,14]. Methodologically, the study employs a quantitative data approach. The online survey enabled the most rapid and efficient reach to a large sample of teachers across the country, resulting in a substantial dataset that provides insights into the prevalence, severity of teachers' challenges and expectations. Furthermore, the anonymous nature of the online survey encouraged honest and open responses from teachers. The collected data facilitated the formulation of more effective and timely support measures for teachers [6,8,15].

The dataset provides raw, unprocessed data that can be used for further analysis, replication studies, or comparative research. It adds value to the original research by providing a transparent and accessible resource for scholars and policymakers interested in improving the quality of History and Geography education. By making this data publicly available, the study aims to promote broader participation and collaboration in addressing educational challenges in Vietnam.

Data Description

This article presents a dataset, accessible on Mendeley [3], describing some fundamental challenges facing teachers in implementing the History and Geography curriculum in Vietnamese secondary schools. The dataset consists of two components: a file containing a questionnaire for teachers with 16 questions (54 items), and a raw data file containing 2,615 responses. The original data file is an Excel spreadsheet with two sheets. The first sheet, “raw data” contains 2,615 encoded data records. The second sheet, “codebook” provides a detailed explanation of the coding scheme and a summary of each item. The questionnaire file is a Word document with a seven-part structure: (1) Demographic information; (2) Teachers' subject knowledge background; (3) Current status of History and Geography teaching assignments; (4) Teachers' perceptions of History and Geography curriculum; (5) the challenges faced by teachers, (6) factors that cause challenges for teachers; and (7) Teachers' expectations for effective curriculum implementation.

The initial section of the questionnaire (Question 1–5, comprising 5 items) focuses on demographic characteristics. These items inquire about participants' residence, workplace location, gender, length of work experience, and current job title. This information serves as a basis for investigating potential correlations with the subsequent questionnaire items (Table 1).

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

This study was designed to systematically capture the challenges faced by teachers in teaching History and Geography in secondary schools across diverse regions of Vietnam. Specifically, the study focused on three research questions: (1) What challenges do Vietnamese teachers encounter when teaching History and Geography in secondary schools? (2) What factors contribute to these difficulties and challenges? (3) What do teachers aspire to in order to effectively teach this subject? The study employed a quantitative survey design to collect data within a specified timeframe. This approach enabled the gathering of a substantial volume of data, facilitating robust statistical analysis and providing a comprehensive understanding of the diverse experiences of teachers [1,6,7,15].

The questionnaire was developed based on educational theories and pedagogical research, exploring the factors affecting the implementation of the History and Geography curriculum, including aspects related to the curriculum, teachers, facilities, teaching materials, education policies, and effective assessment [2,9,12]. The survey was created and designed on Google Forms.

This questionnaire encompasses questions pertaining to the challenges faced, the factors influencing the severity of these challenges, and potential solutions. The questions are structured in a multiple-choice format, with mandatory questions managed through Google Forms. To ensure reliability and validity, before being submitted to Departments of Education and Training for approval and large-scale deployment, the toolkit was piloted with teachers at two secondary schools, the Experimental Elementary, Secondary, and High School of Education Science, as well as To Hoang Secondary School in Hanoi. Subsequently, the questionnaire was revised to improve its quality.

To ensure a representative sample, stratified random sampling was employed. Eight provinces were selected: Lai Chau, Lam Dong, Thai Nguyen, Ca Mau, Thua Thien Hue, Hai Duong, Thanh Hoa, and Quang Binh. These provinces were chosen to represent the diverse geographical regions of Vietnam (North, Central, and South, as well as urban, rural, and mountainous areas). Within these provinces, all history and geography teachers teaching in secondary schools were invited to participate in the survey.

The official letter regarding the coordination of survey tasks, which contained the survey link, was shared with teachers through the hierarchical educational management system, from the Provincial Department of Education and Training (PDET) to the District DET and down to the schools. Teachers participated in the survey by clicking on the hyperlink provided by their schools. To address delays in survey organization and participation, reminders were sent to the officers in two PDETs. Additionally, all eight PDETs received reminders (once during the 9-day survey period) urging teachers to either opt out or complete the survey within the specified timeframe.

The survey received a total of 2,619 responses, representing approximately 37% (2,619 out of an expected 7,072) of the targeted participants from around 1,768 secondary schools [11]. Among these, 4 responses contained missing data, leaving 2,615 valid responses for analysis. These 2,615 valid responses were analyzed using IBM SPSS Version 26 (Table 8).

Limitations

The survey was conducted using an online questionnaire, which means the data are based on self-report by teachers and may be subject to biased responses or inaccurate recollections. However, this limitation does not significantly affect the data's accuracy and reliability, as the survey was conducted on a large, representative sample from diverse geographical regions across Vietnam. Besides, the predominance of female respondents reflects the reality that female teachers outnumber male teachers in the general education system in Vietnam, where women comprise approximately 70% of the total teaching staff according to [16]. This represents the current structure of the teaching workforce, thereby helping to propose suitable support and improvement solutions.

Ethics Statement

The Ethics Committee of the Vietnam National Institute of Educational Sciences approved and monitored the procedure for conducting this research (ethics approval number: B2023.VKG.23.GRANTED). This study strictly adheres to all applicable ethical guidelines. Prior to participation, all teachers received a comprehensive explanation of the study's purpose, content, and methodology to ensure informed consent.

Mapping the evidence on digital interventions to improve postpartum contraceptive uptake among adolescent mothers: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Yuni Purwatiningsih, Esti Nugraheny, Suparmi, Bunga Astria Paramashanti, Siti Masitoh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionAdolescent mothers are at increased risk of rapid repeat pregnancy during the postpartum period, particularly in low-and middle-income countries where unmet need for contraception remains high. Stigma, limited autonomy and inadequate youth-friendly services contribute to low uptake of postpartum contraception. Digital health interventions have been proposed as scalable approaches to improve access to contraceptive information and support. However, evidence specifically focusing on digital interventions to enhance postpartum contraception among adolescent mothers has not yet been comprehensively mapped. This scoping review aims to identify and describe the available evidence in this area.MethodsThis review will follow the Arksey and O’Malley framework, with refinements by Levac et al and guidance from the Joanna Briggs Institute. Studies involving adolescent mothers (10–19 years) within 12 months after childbirth and evaluating digital interventions for postpartum contraception will be included. Introduction

Adolescent pregnancy remains a significant global public health concern, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, where rates of early childbearing and rapid repeat pregnancy are highest.1 Adolescents who give birth face an elevated risk of adverse health, social and economic outcomes, including maternal morbidity, school discontinuation and intergenerational poverty.2,4 The postpartum period represents a critical window for preventing rapid repeat pregnancy through timely and appropriate contraceptive use.5

Access to postpartum contraception is crucial in supporting adolescent mothers to recover, continue their education and plan their futures.6 7 However, for many young mothers, obtaining family planning services remains challenging. They face stigma, limited decision-making power and services that are often not tailored to adolescents’ needs.8,10 Practical barriers, such as distance to health facilities, transportation costs or inconvenient clinic hours, further restrict access, while missed counselling opportunities during antenatal and postnatal care leave many without adequate information.9 11 Consequently, unmet need for postpartum contraception remains high among adolescents, increasing their risk of rapid repeat pregnancies and related health and socioeconomic challenges.12,14 Addressing these barriers is essential to better support adolescent mothers in shaping healthier and more stable life trajectories.

Digital health interventions are increasingly recognised as effective strategies to disseminate information and address barriers in sexual and reproductive health (SRH) service delivery.15 Mobile health, telehealth services, messaging platforms and digital decision-support tools improve access, strengthen communication and promote continuity of care.16 These interventions potentially mitigate structural and social barriers experienced by mothers. Previous reviews have shown that digital sexual health interventions delivered via websites and mobile phones are effective in changing cognitive perceptions and behaviours among young adults.17 Moreover, a systematic review of randomised controlled trials suggests that digital interventions may moderately increase postpartum contraception use among adult women.18

Furthermore, previous reviews have discussed digital SRH interventions for adult women19 and adolescents in general.20 21 The research on digital treatments targeted at boosting adolescent mothers’ use of postpartum contraception has not been specifically reviewed. Descriptive analysis and cluster randomised trials were the only methods used in earlier studies.19,21 Evidence on the use of digital interventions to support family planning programmes, particularly among adolescents, remains limited. A scoping review is appropriate for accurately mapping current data, explaining key ideas from digital interventions related to family planning and identifying gaps in research and implementation. This protocol describes how to conduct a scoping review of digital interventions to promote postpartum contraceptive use, especially among adolescent mothers.

Methods and analysis

Registration

This protocol has been registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF registration ID: 8dyrx) to ensure transparency and methodological rigour. The registration is currently under embargo and will be made publicly available upon publication of the review. Any significant amendments to the protocol will be documented and updated in the OSF registry.

Protocol design

This scoping review will systematically map the existing evidence on digital interventions designed to support postpartum family planning among adolescent mothers. The review will follow the methodological framework for scoping reviews originally proposed by Arksey and O’Malley, with subsequent refinements by Levac et al,22 23 and will be guided by the recommendations of the Joanna Briggs Institute.24 Reporting of the review will adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) to ensure transparency and methodological rigour.25 The PRISMA-ScR checklist for this protocol is provided in online supplemental file 2. A fully completed PRISMA-ScR checklist will be included in the final review manuscript. The study is planned to commence in June 2026 and is expected to be completed by December 2026.

The review will be conducted in six sequential stages: (1) Formulation of the review questions, (2) Identification of relevant studies, (3) Selection of eligible studies, (4) Data extraction and charting, (5) Collation, summarising and reporting the results and (6) Consultation with relevant stakeholders.

Stage 1: identifying the research question

The first stage of this scoping review will involve defining clear research questions to guide the scope and direction of evidence mapping. Given the anticipated diversity in intervention types, delivery platforms and outcomes, focused yet sufficiently broad questions will be required to comprehensively capture the existing literature on digital interventions aimed at improving postpartum contraceptive uptake among adolescent mothers. Stage 2: identifying relevant studies

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted across the following electronic databases: MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase (via Elsevier), Scopus (Elsevier) and Web of Science (Clarivate Analytics). These databases will be selected to ensure broad coverage of public health, reproductive health, digital health and interdisciplinary research. To enhance comprehensiveness, grey literature will also be searched through relevant organisational websites (eg, WHO, UNFPA and UNICEF). The reference lists of all included studies and relevant reviews will be screened to identify additional eligible records. No geographical restrictions will be applied. Where feasible, no restrictions on publication date will be imposed to capture the evolution of digital health interventions over time. Only studies available in English will be included due to resource constraints.

Search strategy

The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary (eg, Medical Subject Headings terms) and free-text keywords related to adolescents, postpartum contraception and digital health interventions. Boolean operators (“AND”, “OR”) and truncation will be used as appropriate to maximise sensitivity. The search strategy will be initially developed for MEDLINE (via PubMed) and subsequently adapted for Embase, Scopus and Web of Science to account for differences in indexing and search syntax. The draft MEDLINE search strategy is provided in online supplemental file 1 to ensure transparency and reproducibility. Forward and backward citation tracking will be conducted to identify additional relevant studies.

Keyword

The search strategy will be developed using the Population–Concept–Context (PCC) framework to ensure systematic alignment between the review objectives, eligibility criteria and search terms. This structured approach supports comprehensive identification of relevant evidence in scoping reviews. The PCC components and corresponding example keywords are presented in table 1.

Study selection

All identified records will be imported into reference management software, and duplicates will be removed. The de-duplicated records will then be uploaded to Rayyan, a web-based systematic review management platform, to facilitate the study selection process.

Two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts using Rayyan’s blinded screening mode, based on the predefined eligibility criteria. During this stage, each reviewer will label records as include, exclude or maybe. Rayyan’s conflict detection feature will be used to identify discrepancies between reviewers.

Full-text articles will be retrieved for all records classified as include or maybe by at least one reviewer. Full-text screening will also be conducted independently by the two reviewers using Rayyan, with reasons for exclusion recorded for each excluded study. Any disagreements at either the title/abstract or full-text screening stage will be resolved through discussion. If consensus cannot be reached, a third reviewer will be consulted to make the final decision.

The overall study selection process will be presented using a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (figure 1),26 in accordance with the PRISMA-ScR guidelines detailing the number of records identified, screened, excluded (with reasons) and included in the final review.

A standardised data charting form table 3 will be developed to systematically extract relevant information from all included studies. The form will be pilot-tested on a small sample of studies to ensure consistency, clarity and alignment with the review objectives. Refinements will be made as necessary prior to full data extraction. Two reviewers will independently extract data from the included studies using the finalised charting form. Stage 5: collation, summarising and reporting the results

In this stage, the extracted data will be collated, synthesised and presented to provide a comprehensive overview of the existing evidence. Consistent with scoping review methodology, the aim is not to assess the effectiveness of interventions or to produce pooled effect estimates, but to map the range, characteristics and distribution of available studies. The findings will be summarised using both descriptive numerical analysis and narrative synthesis. Descriptive analysis will outline the distribution of studies by year of publication, geographical location, study design and type of digital intervention. Results will be presented in tables and figures to illustrate patterns in intervention characteristics, contraceptive-related outcomes and implementation contexts. A narrative synthesis will be conducted to summarise key themes, including types of digital strategies employed, reported outcomes, implementation facilitators and barriers and equity or ethical considerations. Particular attention will be given to identifying evidence gaps, methodological limitations in the literature and areas requiring further research. The reporting of the review will follow the PRISMA-ScR checklist to ensure transparency and completeness. The study selection process will be presented using a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram, in accordance with PRISMA-ScR guidelines, and summary tables will present the key characteristics and findings of included studies.

Result presentation

The findings of this scoping review will be presented using a combination of descriptive numerical summaries, tabular presentation and thematic analysis. In addition to mapping the distribution of studies by year, country, study design and type of digital intervention, thematic categories will be developed to synthesise the qualitative and contextual dimensions of the evidence.

An inductive approach will be used to identify and group recurring patterns across included studies. Thematic categories will be developed to reflect key domains aligned with the review objectives, including:

Stage 6: consultation

In line with the optional consultation stage proposed in scoping review methodology, stakeholder consultation will be undertaken to enhance the relevance, interpretation and applicability of the findings. This stage is intended to provide additional insights beyond the published literature and to support knowledge translation.

Stakeholders will be invited to provide feedback on the preliminary synthesis, identified evidence gaps and implications for programme design and scale-up. Their input will be used to contextualise the findings and inform recommendations for future research and implementation. The consultation will not involve the collection of primary data from patients and will therefore not require additional ethical approval.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required as this study will use publicly available data. Findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentations at relevant scientific conferences. The results will inform future research, policy and digital health programme design to support postpartum contraception among adolescent mothers.

Association of cardio-ankle vascular index value with cardiovascular risk factors in elderly individuals
Authors: Jinbo Liu, Lin Ma, Xuechen Cui, Shantong Jiang, Hongwei Zhao, Na Zhao, Zhantian An, Hongyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardio-ankle vascular index (CAVI) is an important indicator to evaluate arteriosclerosis. This study was to explore the changes and related factors of CAVI in the elderly population, providing a certain basis for the prevention and control of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases.Methods1117 subjects (M/F 488/629) were divided into three groups according to age: group 1: subjects with age 60–69 years; group 2: subjects with age 70–79 years; group 3: subjects with age≧80 years. CAVI value was recorded by using the VaseraVS-1000 vascular screening system.ResultsThere were significant differences about body mass index (BMI), CAVI, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure(DBP), pulse pressure, creatinine, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and uric acid between these three groups. CAVI value was increasing year by year, with the increasing incidence of hypertension, coronary artery disease and stroke. General Linear Model analysis showed that age, incidence of diabetes mellitus, BMI, DBP and HDL-C were independent associating factors of CAVI in elderly subjects (β = 0.048, p < 0.001; β = 0.349, p = 0.012; β = -0.060, p = 0.001; β = 0.023, p = 0.001; β = -0.530, p = 0.020; respectively).ConclusionsCAVI was increasing with ageing. The influencing factors of CAVI varied among different age groups. Subjects aged 60–69 were needed to pay close attention to blood pressure and subjects aged 70–79 were needed to focus on diabetes meillitus, providing a theoretical basis for intervening in different targets in different age groups, to reduce or delay the progression of arteriosclerosis.Keywords: Cardio-Ankle VascularIndex, Ageing, Metabolic disorders, Arterial stiffness

Background

An ageing society refers to a social phenomenon where the proportion of elderly people in the total population continues to rise. Although ageing is a positive result of medical advancements and longer lifespans, it also brings a series of economic, social, medical, and political challenges. Such as chronic diseases (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, stroke, Alzheimer's disease, etc.) of the elderly people are high, and medical expenditure has soared. If one or two or more elderly people in a family suffer from impaired independent living ability due to diseases such as myocardial infarction or cerebral infarction, it will bring great economic and mental pressure to the family, especially the “4-2-1″ family structure (4 elderly people, 2 middle-aged, 1 child), which puts enormous economic and mental pressure on middle-aged people. Therefore, the cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases and health management of the elderly population are of great significance[1].

The pathophysiological basis of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases is the occurrence and progress of arteriosclerosis, The severity of arteriosclerosis can be evaluated by arterial stiffness, which is a powerful predictor of future cardiovascular as well as the initial data that can detect abnormalities in vascular lesions[2]. The cardio ankle vascular index (CAVI) is a new arterial stiffness index, recently developed by measuring pulse wave velocity and blood pressure [3]. Several observational long-term studies from Asian populations have shown that higher level of CAVI was associated with cardiovascular events and mortality [4]. Javanmardi E found that CAVI was related to both central and peripheral artery stiffness and higher Framingham Risk Score, histories of hypertension and diabetes were significantly associated with higher CAVI values [5]. Previous study showed that high CAVI values were associated with many vascular-related diseases, and were also higher in patients with major coronary risk factors such as hypertension and diabetes [6], [7]. Our previous studies showed that CAVI was positively correlated with homocysteine, and CAVI was increased in hypertension patients[8], [9].

As mentioned earlier, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases are the cause of death and disability in the elderly population, and the pathophysiological mechanism of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases is based on arterial lesions. If we understand the relevant factors of arterial lesions in the elderly population and intervene to deny the possibility of slowing down the progression of such diseases, it is theoretically feasible. Previous study showed that sarcopenic patients were older than subjects without sarcopenia with higher CAVI [10]. However, there was little research about CAVI value and related factors in elderly subjects. So the present study was to investigate the CAVI value in elderly subjects and its related factors, providing a certain basis for the prevention and control of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases in the elderly population. Materials and methods

Subjects

1117 subjects (M/F 488/629) from Department of Vascular Medicine of Peking University Shougang Hospital from January 2012 to December 2019 were enrolled into our study. Subjects with heart failure, renal function impairment, liver function impairment, systemic inflammatory diseases, infectious disease or cancer were excluded. The present study was to investigate CAVI value in elderly subjects, so subjects with age < 60 years were excluded. The heart failure was referred to all types of heart failure, including heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, with mildly reduced ejection fraction, with preserved ejection fraction. Heart failure patients admitted to our department often had wheezing symptoms and could not tolerate CAVI testing, so these patients were excluded. Among the patients hospitalized in our department, those with renal function impairment were often accompanied by heart failure, so this group of people was also excluded. Renal function impairment was defined as eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2. Hypertension was defined as subjects with three blood pressure measurements of ≥ 140/90 mmHg at rest, or with a known history of hypertension before and taking current antihypertensive drugs. Coronary artery disease was defined as coronary artery stenosis or obstruction diagnosed by angiography. Diabetes mellitus was defined as fasting blood glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L or two-hour postprandial blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L or random blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L or currently administration of diabetes medication. Stroke was diagnosed through magnetic resonance spectroscopy.

All subjects were divided into three groups according to age: group 1: subjects with age 60–69 years; group 2: subjects with age 70–79 years; group 3: subjects with age≧80 years. During the initial visit, the patient was informed that their clinical data might be used for future analysis and publication of academic papers, and consent was obtained.

The assessment of CAVI

CAVI value was recorded by using the VaseraVS-1000 vascular screening system (Toshiyuki Fukuda, Tokyo, Japan). The electrocardiogram electrode was placed on the wrist, the microphone for detecting heart sounds was placed on the sternum, and handcuffs were wrapped around the arms and ankles. After automatic measurement, the obtained data was analyzed by software to automatically obtain CAVI values.

Laboratory measurements

In the morning after fasting for one night, blood samples were taken from the anterior cubital vein and collected in a vacuum tube containing EDTA for measuring blood lipids and lipoprotein levels. At the Central Chemistry Laboratory of Peking University Shougang Hospital, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and triglycerides (TG) levels were analyzed using an automated analyzer (HITACHI-7170, HITACHI, Tokyo, Japan) by colorimetric enzyme method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS (Version 26.0). The differences between groups were analyzed by t-test (two groups) and ANOVA (three groups, followed by LSD analysis between two groups comparison). The proportion was analyzed using the χ2 −test. The Spearman correlation coefficient was used for correlation coefficient analysis to identify the linear relationships among different variables. General Linear Model analysis was used to estimate the coefficients involving independent variables that affect the values of the dependent variable. Unless otherwise specified, the values were displayed as mean ± standard deviation. p < 0.05 (bilateral) was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Our present study showed that CAVI was increasing with ageing, with the increasing incidence of hypertension, coronary artery disease and stroke in elderly subjects. Age, incidence of diabetes mellitus, BMI, DBP and HDL-C were independent associating factors of CAVI in elderly subjects. The influencing factors of CAVI varied among different age groups.

According to statistics, the elderly population in China has exceeded 310 million, with up to 78% of the population aged 60 and above suffering from at least one chronic disease such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, stroke and so on. Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases (such as hypertension, coronary heart disease, stroke, etc.) have become the main threat to the health of the elderly population. Ageing is closely related to vascular system degradation, leading to a significant increase in disease incidence rate, disability rate and mortality rate, which brings a heavy burden to the medical system and social economy[11]. So early screening and precise management have become the main prevention and control strategies for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases in an ageing society.

Arteriosclerosis is the fundamental process of the occurrence and progression of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, which can be evaluated by arterial stiffness. The increasing in arterial stiffness level was related to ageing [12]. The assessment of arterial stiffness can be represented by CAVI. Because it was not related to the blood pressure at the time of examination, the characteristics of CAVI were different from other arterial stiffness detection indicators[13]. And CAVI was significantly higher in hypertension subjects[14]. CAVI was significantly higher in hypertension subjects with coronary heart disease [15] or hyperhomocysteinemia [16], similar to the present study. The above studies indicated that CAVI can be used to evaluate the level of arterial stiffness [17].

The present study showed that age was an independent associating factor of CAVI in all group. Another study showed that CAVI increased linearly with age, and age became the main determinant of CAVI [18]. Even in a small sample study of diabetes patients, age had been found to be a very important factor in the CAVI score[19]. Recent studies have found that CAVI showed good predictive value in ageing and many vascular diseases [20]. Ageing was an independent risk factor for vascular dysfunction. First, vascular ageing altered the function of the endothelium, such as diminished bioavailability of nitric oxide, increased production of the highly pro-oxidant superoxide anion, and so on [21]. Second, with ageing, the aorta stiffens due to increased collagen and decreased elastin, increased stiffness raised reflected waves and elevated systolic pressure, also characterized human vascular ageing [22], [23], The results of this study also found that CAVI levels increased with age, while age was an uncontrollable factor, unlike blood pressure, blood glucose, and other controllable risk factors.

Diabetes mellitus was an independent factor of CAVI and CAVI was correlated with FPG. Glucose could be controlled in elderly subjects to reduce the level of arterial stiffness, thus to reduce the probability of coronary heart disease and stroke occurrence. Topouchian J found that CAVI was positively correlated with hyperglycemic and hypertensive components[24]. Our results showed that diabetes mellitus was a related factor of CAVI in subjects with age 70–79 years, while there was no obvious correlation in the other two groups, suggesting that diabetes mellitus was to be paid more attention to in this group of people.

Previous studies have suggested that increasing HDL-C can be a treatment strategy for vascular diseases[25]. Studies have found that, after adjusting for age and gender, high CAVI and low HDL-C were important factors in the incidence of cardiovascular events [26]. Compared with the normal HDL-C group, the CAVI in the low HDL-C group was significantly decreased. Abdominal obesity and low level of HDL-C were the main factors affecting arteriosclerosis[27]. Recent studies have found that even after adjusting for age, height, weight and gender, the cognitive function of elderly subjects with higher CAVI was still affected to a certain extent and deteriorated compared with the control group[28]. According to previous and present study, CAVI was increasing in elderly subjects, and hypertension, HDL-C, and diabetes mellitus should be fixed during the diseases control in these subjects.

The main factors affecting CAVI in elderly people aged 60–69 were age, systolic blood pressure and HDL-C, especially in terms of blood pressure. This group of elderly people may have just retired from work, and their lifestyle and emotional fluctuations could lead to changes in blood pressure, which might accelerate the progression of arteriosclerosis. Therefore, this group of people are needed to pay close attention to changes in systolic blood pressure. The main factors affecting CAVI in elderly people aged 70–79 were age BMI, diabetes mellitus and diastolic blood pressure, suggesting that in this age group, blood glucose and diastolic blood pressure became the focus of attention. In addition, there was a negative correlation between systolic blood pressure and CAVI. We speculated that this population may be treated with antihypertensive drugs, and some antihypertensive drugs can improve arteriosclerosis, so the reduced systolic blood pressure was actually negatively correlated with CAVI. However, our study did not find any significant factors associated with CAVI in elderly people over 80 years old. First, it might be related to some antihypertensive, hypoglycemic and lipid-lowering drugs. Second, in the elderly population over 80 years old, all organs were in an ageing state, and it was necessary to focus on the occurrence of acute events such as falls. Healthy elderly people should have the ability to control their blood glucose and blood pressure, while maintaining a positive psychological state and good social adaptability. The present study indicated that the elderly population in different age groups had different concerns about preventing and treating the progression of arteriosclerosis.

One of the main limitations of our research was its cross-sectional design; another limitation was the imbalance in the number of subjects in each group (including the gender composition). There was selection bias and unmeasured confounding variables: retrospective data may miss important influencing factors (such as drug factors), leading to false associations. Sartan drugs and amlodipine drugs could reduce CAVI value in hypertension patients [29]. Sodium glucose cotransporter- 2 inhibitors could improve endothelial function and arterial stiffness in diabetes mellitus [30]. And pitavastatin exerted a protective effect on the cardiovascular system by reducing the level of CAVI [31]. We only collected clinical biochemistry, arteriosclerosis, and other related indicators, and did not collect the usage of the above-mentioned drugs. Therefore, this was one of the limitations of this study. In addition, chronic renal insufficiency was one of the important risk factors for arteriosclerosis. Previous studies have shown that elevated creatinine levels were an independent predictor of CAVI [32]. However, in this study, we did not find creatinine as an independent related factor for CAVI, which might be related to the exclusion criteria of this study. Patients with renal insufficiency were not included in this study. And we also know that with ageing, the probability of kidney dysfunction increases. Further research is needed on the CAVI value of this population in the future. So, large-sample and prospective studies will be needed in the future.

Conclusions

CAVI was increasing with ageing, with the increasing incidence of hypertension, coronary artery disease and stroke in elderly subjects. The influencing factors of CAVI varied among different age groups. Subjects aged 60–69 were needed to pay close attention to blood pressure and subjects aged 70–79 were needed to focus on diabetes meillitus, providing a theoretical basis for intervening in different targets in different age groups to reduce or delay the progression of arteriosclerosis.

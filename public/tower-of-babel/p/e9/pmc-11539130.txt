Introduction

Tall cell carcinoma of the breast with reversed polarity (TCCRP) is a rare subtype of papillary carcinoma recently recognized as a distinct entity on the fifth edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of the breast [1].

It is a rare tumor with limited published cases in the literature. Subsequently, this tumor was reported as “Solid papillary carcinoma with reverse polarity” [2] and “Solid papillary carcinoma resembling tall cell variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma” [3].

It is characterized by a triple-negative profile, with a favorable prognosis.

The present study examined the case of a 45-year-old Tunisian women who presented to Salah Azaiez Institute, Tunisia with a suspicious lesion on screening mammography, diagnosed as TCCRP on microscopic examination.

We conducted a review of the literature to elucidate some of the clinicopathological aspects, therapeutic approach, and outcome of this subtype.

This work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [4].

Case presentation

A 45-year-old Tunisian non-menopausal woman, non-smoker, multiparous G3P2A1 suffered from bilateral mastodynia evolving for 2 months.

Personal and family history were not significant.

On physical examination, there were no nodules or palpable breast mass. Axillary and sub-clavicular region were free.

Screening bilateral mammography-ultasound showed a fibro-cystic change associated with an irregular hypoechoic and hypervascular mass of the lower inner quadrant of the right breast measured 17x10mm. (Fig. 1). This lesion was classified as 4B according to the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BIRADS 4B).

bilateral mammography: no suspicious lesion.

No calcifications were detected on the mammography.

The core biopsy specimen revealed a papillary proliferation consistent with an intraductal papilloma (Fig. 2).

Core biopsy: Intraductal proliferation comprised of arborizing fibrovascular cores lined by outer layer of luminal cells and an inner layer of myoepithelial cells (HEx5) (HEx10).

The patient underwent wide local excision. The microscopic examination showed solid, circumscribed tumor nodules haphazardly distributed throughout breast stroma. Many nodules contain fibrovascular cores, some with foamy histiocytes (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). Tumor cells were tall and columnar with eosinophilic cytoplasm, often arranged in double layer with cells appearing back to back. The Nuclei were round to oval sometimes exhibiting grooves and cytoplasmic pseudo-inclusions. These nuclei were localized at apical rather than basal pole (reverse polarity) (Fig. 4). concluded to the diagnosis of tall cell carcinoma of the breast with reversed polarity of the breast. The margins were free of the tumor. Intra-ductal carcinoma, lymphovascular emboli and perineural invasion were absent.

Tumor cells proliferation arranged in nodules and solid nests haphazardly distributed throughout breast stroma (Hex20).

Presence of fibrovascular cores, with foamy histiocytes within the tumor nodules. Nuclei are localized at apical rather than basal pole (reverse polarity) (Hex10).

The immunohistochemical study showed a negative staining for p63 confirming the absence of myoepithelial cells around the tumor nests (Fig. 5). The tumor cells were negative for hormone receptors as well as for the Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor-2 (HER2neu). (Fig. 6).

A negative staining for p63 confirming the absence of myoepithelial cells around the tumor nests (IHCx10).

Tumor cells were negative for ER (IHCx10).

Ki-67 % proliferative index was estimated at 10 %.

Tumor cells were also negative for TTF1 and thyroglobulin.

Tumor cells were positive for Calretinin (Fig. 7) and IDH (Fig. 8).

Positive staining for Calretinin (IHCx10).

Positive staining for IDH (IHCx10).

The diagnosis of TCCRP was confirmed.

Enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was indicated to help surgical planning, revealing no other simultaneous suspicious lesions.

The patient underwent sentinel lymph node biopsy that was negative on extemporaneous examination.

Then according to a multidisciplinary meeting decision, the patient was proposed for loco-regional radiotherapy. She received 66 Gy in 33 fractions over five weeks.

Because of the low grade of the tumor and the negativity of hormonal receptor, chemotherapy and endocrine therapy were not indicated.

The patient is being regularly monitored for 24 months now with a clinical examination and breast imaging every three months.

The patient is currently well without signs of loco-regional or distant recurrences 24 months after the surgery.

Discussion

In this paper we present the first published case of TCCRP of the breast in Tunisia.

TCCRP is a very uncommon subtype of low to intermediate grade triple negative invasive breast cancer. It was described for the first time by Eusebi et al. [5] in 2003. Then they reported the first five cases of breast tumors presenting histologic features resembling those of the tall cell variant papillary thyroid carcinoma and designate it as “the breast tumor resembled the tall cell variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma”.

Knowing that the breast is not an uncommon site for thyroid metastasis [6], he recognized that by the morphological characteristics of the papillary structures lined by eosinophilic columnar mitochondrion-rich cells with nuclear grooves and nuclear pseudo-inclusions. The immunohistochemistry study used the specific antibodies for the thyroid origin (TTF1 and thyroglobulin) lead to the right diagnosis.

Up to 2009, 5 additional similar cases were reported characterizing this subtype as an indolent tumor with a triple negative profile and a good prognosis [7,8].

In 2009, hameed et al. [9] raised the question on whether TCCRP is a variant of papillary carcinoma (PC) of the breast. They concluded that some of the cytologic features found in this subtype may also be found in some subtypes of breast PC already known as rare type of breast cancer that comprises a heterogeneous group of tumors challenging to diagnose [10].

This concept was as well defended by Masood et al. [11] in 2012 based on the fact that eventhough the presence of architectural and cytologic features characteristic of papillary thyroid carcinoma support their striking resemblance, immunohistochemistry study was unable to demonstrate a connection between this lesion and papillary thyroid cancer [9].

As all reported cases were negative for thyroid specific markers, changing the term “Breast Tumor Resembling the Tall Cell Variant of Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma” to “Tall Cell Variant of Papillary Breast Carcinoma” was suggested.

Conversely, in 2018 Alsadoun et al. [12], by making comparison with other benign and malignant breast papillary lesions, proposed that this condition was an isolated, distinguishable breast carcinoma from other neoplasm.

In the other hand, Zhong et al. [13] used the next generation sequencing (NGS) technique to compare the histopathology of thyroid PC and breast PC.

They validated prior molecular findings and believed the tumor is distinctive in terms of its histology and molecular characteristics by the presence of solid architecture, luminal histiocytes, reverse polarity, and recurrent IDH2, R172 mutations.

The 5th edition of the WHO series on the classification of breast cancers, published in 2019, recognized this form of disease as a distinct entity defined as “a solid tumor, creating papillary structures within its architecture”. It is included in the category of rare and salivary gland type tumors [1].

All reported cases were females with a median age of 64 years old (ranges from 40 to 85) presenting with a palpable nodule or discovered on screening mammography. The median size tumor is 1.3 cm ranging from 0.6 to 5.5 cm. Surgical data were available for 49 patients among of whom 40 patients underwent breast conservative surgery (81.63 %) and only 9 patients received total mastectomy (18.37 %).

Exact axillary information was accessible on only 34 cases. Sentinel lymph node dissection was performed for 13 patients. Lymph nodes were free of tumor in the most majority of cases (31/34) (91.17 %).

Key histologic features are solid circumscribed nodules of columnar epithelial cells haphazardly distributed throughout breast stroma that can be collagenous with little desmoplasia and containing fibrovascular cores, some with foamy histiocytes. Follicular structures with colloid-like secretions may be present. Tumor cells are columnar with eosinophilic cytoplasm and a low to intermediate grade of the nuclei which is localized at apical rather than basal pole (reverse polarity). Nuclear grooves or cytoplasmic pseudo-inclusions can be observed. Less frequent histologic features can be seen such as jigsaw pattern of cell nests and frankly papillary areas. On immunohistochemical study, TCCRP is triple negative with sometimes low ER and/or PR positive. Low proliferation detected rate by Ki67. Rich vascular network around nests on CD31 and CD34 stains. Calretinin expression is always positive [12].

Tumor cells show strong lateral membrane staining for E-cadherin and apical membrane staining seen on ends of cells closest to nucleus for MUC1 (highlighting reverse polarity).

TCCRP is characterized by IDH2 R172 hot spot mutations in 10/13 cases (77 %) [2] This was not previously reported in breast cancers. Eight concurrently displayed mutations in PI3 kinase pathway (PIK3CA or PIK3R1). Functional studies IDH2 and PIK3CA mutations appear to be driver alterations resulting in reverse polarization phenotype. No BRAF mutations or RET/PTC rearrangements are seen in TCCRP [2].

In term of adjuvant treatment, there is currently no definitive guidelines for the therapy of TCCRP due to its rarity and limited number of reported cases.

In the reviewed articles, data was available for 31 patients. Adjuvant treatment was not stated in 64.5 % of the cases (20/31). Only 6 patients received exclusive radiotherapy (19.35 %) while 1 patient received chemotherapy. Concomitant radio and chemotherapy were administered in 12.9 % of cases.

Hormonal treatment was indicated in 01 patient [7].

The median follow-up was 27 months (range 3–132). At that time 94.73 % remained disease-free while one patient developed bone metastasis. Local recurrences occurred in one patient [12]. Four cases developed lymph node metastases and distant metastases was reported in 1 case [2,12].

Based on the above results, we can conclude that this entity affects predominantly elderly women. It is usually a small lesion with an indolent clinical behavior.

Therefore, breast conservative surgery is the mainstay treatment.

Sentinel lymph node dissection was often carried with negative lymph nodes attesting of a low-grade tumor.

Postoperative adjuvant treatment was not indicated in most cases and patients have have consistently had a good prognosis.

In summary, this paper reported the first case of TCCRP diagnosed in Tunisia. We tried to conduct an extensive literature review for a better comprehension of the significant clinico-pathological features, immunohistochemical, and molecular characteristics.

Careful and precise diagnosis is fundamental to eliminate differential diagnosis and to adopt the adequate approach.

Further research should be carried in the future to characterize this rare entity.

Conclusion

TCCRP is a rare entity with histological features that mimic the papillary thyroid carcinoma that has been recently introduced in the WHO classification of breast cancer.

It is usually a triple negative tumor, negative to TTF1 and thyroglobulin with low proliferation index, and a low potential for malignancy.

Wide excision is the keystone of the treatment; however, chemotherapy and radiotherapy are still controversial due to lack of evidence.

Given its excellent prognosis, a proper diagnosis is essential to conduct the best treatment next to a careful follow-up of these patients due to the ambiguous natural history of this rare entity.

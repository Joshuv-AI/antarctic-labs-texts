OA2044. Amplifying student voices: From research to implementation in the SAFETeen project
Authors: A Vouraki, C Kroustalidou, P Tsiara, A Chronaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdolescents face multiple risks during their daily commutes, yet their perspectives are often underrepresented in road safety education. SAFETeen, an Erasmus+ KA220 project in which the Regional Directorate of Education of Crete (RDPSEC), is involved as a partner, integrated student voices through co-creation to develop targeted, relevant prevention tools.ObjectivesThis presentation outlines the research phase emphasizing student participation in designing research instruments and educational materials. The goal was to capture adolescents’ views on commuting risks and incorporate these insights into a questionnaire, VR scenarios, and a pedagogical handbook.ResultsFocus groups with middle and high school students, conducted in safe, ethical conditions, revealed that teens recognize commuting risks but often underestimate them due to environmental familiarity, social acceptance needs, and image concerns. Students actively described real and hypothetical hazards, analyzed causes and effects, suggested prevention strategies, and identified barriers to safe behavior. These findings shaped realistic VR scenarios and guided the educational content. Supportive factors highlighted include peer empowerment, updated educational training parental guidance.ConclusionsActive student involvement in research leads to more authentic, targeted, and accepted prevention materials. Co-creation is both a research method and a pedagogical approach that empowers adolescents by positioning them as active contributors and knowledge co-creators.Key messages• Engaging students in research enriches road safety education with authentic insights and fosters student empowerment and ownership of safety messages.• Prevention tools developed this way better address real adolescent challenges and contexts.TopicCo-creation, adolescent road safety, student voice.

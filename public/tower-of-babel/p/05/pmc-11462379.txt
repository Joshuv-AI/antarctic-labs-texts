Yield stress and zeta potential-pH behaviour of washed spherical α-Al2O3 particles with anionic additives: Particle force and ChemBio 3D simulation
Authors: Wei Zhang, Linbo Zhang, Jialu Gao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe yield stress- and zeta potential-pH behaviour of washed spherical α-Al2O3 suspension with and without three typical anionic additives were characterized. The zero zeta potential or isoelectric point (IEP) of washed α-Al2O3 particles locates at pH∼ 9.5. With the increased dosage of the anionic additives, IEP moves to a lower pH range. The maximum yield stress of 50 wt% α-Al2O3 suspension decreases from 78.3 Pa to 50 Pa by about 36 % when citric acid is 0.2 dwb% (dispersant g/100 g powder). According to the simulation of the MM2 model, the type of O-H···O hydrogen bond existed in the CA molecule which results in a stable steric repulsive effect. The pH range where yield stress exists also moves to the lower pH with the addition of anionic additives. However, the addition of Diisobutylene-maleic anhydride sodium salt and Polystyrene sulphonic acid sodium salt increase the maximum yield stress because of the specific attraction force, hydrophobic attraction force and charge attraction force.Keywords: α-alumina, Anionic additives, Particle force, Zeta potential, Yield stress

Introduction

Surface forces can be used to control and regulate the properties of emulsions or suspensions and they can also vastly change or improve the performance of the processes in the areas of tailings transportation [[1], [2], [3]], drilling [4], film formation [5], emulsification [6], thickening [7], dispersion, adhesion [8], flocculation [9,10], flotation [11], etc. The yield stress of the concentrated tailing should be controlled because of the pipeline transportation, and the drilling mud needs to have yield stress so that the drill cutting will not fall through the mud. Therefore, it's necessary to analyze the particle surface force in detail. DLVO forces such as van der Waals force and double layer repulsive force always exist between particles in suspension. With the adsorption on particle surface of additives, the non-DLVO forces will present, such as bridging force [12], depletion force [13], steric force [14], patch charge attraction force [15,16] and so on. Rheology property of the suspension is characterized which is the macroscopic embodiment of the interparticle forces. In this study, the zeta potential- and yield stress-pH behaviour of α-Al2O3 suspension with and without there typical anionic additives were characterized, the surface chemistry and rheology of spherical alumina suspension was evaluated. Particle force and additive adsorption mechanism were also investigated.

The additive adsorption on the α-Al2O3 particle surface changes the chemical and rheology properties of alumina suspension and broadens the application of α-alumina particles. The use of additives or polymers in suspensions or colloidal processing of α-Al2O3 helps to control the state of industry formulation [17,18]. The main motivation of this research is to determine the state of the suspension and show whether the suspension can achieve stabilization when adsorbed with anionic additives. We also want to compare three typical additives and show their adsorption mechanism, which could extend the use of these additives. Pettersson [19] studied the stabilizing mechanisms of alumina particles in suspension with polyacrylic and the result shows that the zeta potential was enhanced during a broad pH range.

Multiple additives are used to dispersion products such as food, personal care products and paints [[20], [21], [22]]. Diisobutylene-maleic anhydride sodium salt or DIBMA is a kind of amphoteric polymer and it's a commonly used additive, it's environmentally friendly [23]. The introduction of dissobutylene into maleic acid will increase the carbon chain and the active adsorption site increase. The additive adsorbed on the particle surface is easy to produce large steric repulsive force when the carbon chain is long. The use of DIBMA in ZrO2 suspension will decrease the maximum yield stress [24]. The dissobutylene group also has good lipophilicity and heat resistance. The research of alumina suspension with DIBMA has not been studied yet.

Polystyrene sulphonic acid sodium salt (PssNa) is easily dissociated in an aqueous solution, and the molecular can be increased by introducing ethylene into sodium benzene sulfonate [25]. PssNa is almost nontoxic with a dissociation constant value of 4.19, which is quite different from other sulfonic acid substances. PssNa is a kind of polymer monomer with a special function, which can be used as a water treatment agent, but it has not been reported as the additive of α-Al2O3 suspension. Citric acid is a commonly used anionic dispersant for different suspensions. This paper also makes a comparison between three typical additives when it's adsorbed on the particle surface. There are many studies on citric acid as a dispersant, but few studies on the adsorption mechanism from the perspective of ChemBio 3D structure. The additive often has a great influence on the stability of the suspension, but few people carried out molecular structure simulation of the adsorbed structure.

Most of the pure and unwashed α-Al2O3 particles have a zero zeta potential or IEP at pH∼ 8.5 or 9. The different washing steps will remove the impurities on the particle surface. At this time, the IEP will move to a higher pH range. The relationship between yield stress and zeta potential has been studied by several suspensions without additives, such as silica [26], zirconia [27], kaolin [28,29] and so on. The results show a linear relationship between yield stress and the square of zeta potential [30]. It is the boundary point between flocculation and dispersion transformation when the yield stress is zero, the critical zeta potential can be characterized. At the same time, the Hamaker constant can be determined in this suspension, the equation is shown in Eq. (1).

Where εcrit is the critical zeta potential, A is the Hamaker constant, H0 is the minimum surface separation distance between particles, ∈ is the permittivity of water, k is the inverse of the double-layer thickness [31,32]. Nearly all of the papers get the result that the zeta potential is not affected by the concentration of the alumina suspension, which only shows the whole charge on the particle surface. The determination of the Hamaker constant is called the critical zeta potential method.

Cryo-SEM is an effective, fast and reliable solution to overcome the problem of samples with water [[33], [34], [35]]. This technique is also widely used to observe difficult samples, such as insensitive or unstable samples. In this study, the colloid samples of alumina suspension usually have water. Normal SEM sample preparation does not consider the presence of water, the contact between colloidal particles has already changed with the decrease of water. Cryo-SEM samples are prepared under high pressure and freezing conditions to ensure the morphology of the original colloidal particles. Liberman [36] evaluates the comparison of cryo-SEM and low-voltage SEM and provides the information for materials about structure-property relations. Duynhoven [37] investigates the microstructure of monoglyceride-water coagel systems and the use of cryo-SEM reflects the morphology of the water-bearing system in a better way.

In this paper, we investigated the yield stress-pH and zeta potential-pH behaviour of α-Al2O3 with and without three typical anionic additives, the additives adsorption mechanism and particle forces between particles were also analyzed. The changing of maximum yield stress and zeta potential should shed light on how additive works.

The spherical α-Al2O3 particles were washed with 0.1 mol/L NaOH solution to improve impurities. Deionized water (DI water) was used to replace the supernatant when solid and liquid were stratified. For the first washing step, 200 g AO802 powder was mixed with 2000 g 0.1 mol/L NaOH solution. The BRANSON Digital Sonifier (250D) was used to break the agglomerates for 2 min until the suspension dispersed completely. The suspension was left for 2 h to form a clear supernatant layer. In the first washing step, nearly 1500 g of supernatant was replaced by DI water which resulted in 75 % of impurities was removed. The pH was adjusted to the isoelectric point (near pH∼9) to accelerate the sedimentation rate of particles. After the 4th step of washing, nearly 99.9 % of impurities were removed from the solution. The Zeta potential-pH behaviour of the AO802 suspension was measured via a Colloidal Dynamics(Zeta Probe) which was used in an auto mode. Electrokinetic Sonic Amplitude (ESA) technology was shown in this probe. The pH was changed from 12 to 2.5 and 0.57 mol/L KOH and 0.57 mol/L HNO3 were used. The SEM images of the AO802 gel sample were obtained via Zeiss 44 VPSEM and the scale bar was 200 nm. The TEM images of the gel were captured by JEOL 2100 [39]. The cryo-SEM was also applied in this paper, the schematic diagram of fixation and imaging of the gel samples are shown in Fig. 3. Colloidal samples were prepared by a high-pressure frozen system. A small part of the samples was placed on two flat carriers with a diameter of 1.5 mm and a depth of 0.2 mm, and then the two flat carriers were pressed together, pressurized to 2 × 108 Pa in a loader, and rapidly cooled in a liquid nitrogen bath. Then the samples were transferred to EM MED020 sputtering instrument. The sample was frozen and fractured by the sputtering instrument to expose the fresh section. After sublimation at −100 °C for 10 min, the colloidal surface was covered with 8 nm metal platinum. SEM image was got in Zeiss 55-VP FEGSEM.

Though the images show few particles in the sample, they clearly showed that all the particles are spherical. Spherical AO802 particles have a smooth surface, close contact between particles and different diameters. All the particles are stacked together. The contact mode of particles is spherical-spherical contact. When the concentration of suspension increases, the distance between particles becomes smaller, particles gather together and the yield stress of suspension increases. The surface charge and interaction force between particles change when pH varies. The yield stress of suspension is also affected by the size of particles and the gap which is formed by particles aggregate. The adsorption isotherm indicates the relationship between the number of additives adsorbed on particles per unit of surface area and the total amount. 20 wt% of α-Al2O3 suspension was used and the additive dosage was calculated based on dwb% (dispersant g/100 g powder), while pH was adjusted via 5 mol/L KOH and HCl. The organic carbon content in the supernatant was determined by a total organic carbon analyzer, and the adsorption amount was equal to the total amount minus the organic carbon content in the supernatant.

Three different anionic additives were used in this research, namely Diisobutylene-maleic anhydride sodium salt or DIBMA, Polystyrene sulphonic acid sodium salt or PssNa and citric acid or CA. DIBMA is a commonly used and environmentally friendly polyelectrolyte additive. The molecular weight of DIBMA is 15 kDa, and one DIBMA molecule consists of 60 copolymer repeating units on average. The dissociation constant values (pKa values) of maleic acid are 1.92 and 6.23, respectively. PssNa dissociates easily in an aqueous solution. PssNa is almost non-toxic and is a green additive, which is very different from other sulfonic acid-based substances. The molecular weight of PssNa is 100 kDa and the number of repeating units in each PssNa molecule is 485. The dissociation constant value (pKa value) of sulfonic acid is 4.19. The commonly used anionic additive CA is also selected for comparison with two polyelectrolyte-based additives. Citric acid is soluble in water and it will be decomposed into CO and H2O when heated to 200 °C. The dissociation constant (pKa) of citric acid are 3.13, 4.76 and 6.40, respectively. Results and discussions

The zeta potential-pH behaviour of 8.2 wt% α-Al2O3 suspension is shown in Fig. 6a. Sample 1 and Sample 2 are spherical α-Al2O3 particles. The isoelectric point or zero zeta potential point locates at pH∼ 9.5 after alkali washing. Zeta potential is greater than 40 mV when pH is less than 6. The zeta potential is −60 mV or −70 mV while pH is 12.1 or 12.3. When pH is less than IEP, the particle surface becomes positively charged. The zeta potential of the two samples is slightly different which is due to the different ionic strength on the particle surface of different batches during the synthesis process.

Zeta potential - pH behaviour of washed α-Al2O3 suspension (a)8.2 wt%; (b)1.9 wt%; (c)50 wt%.

Fig. 6b represents the zeta potential-pH behaviour of different α-Al2O3 solid concentrations. Zeta potential is 93.38 mV at pH∼ 2.61 when solid concentration is 1.9 wt%. The conductivity of the suspension is 23.96 mS/cm. At pH∼ 11.98, the zeta potential is −42.77 mV. When the solid concentration was 50 wt%, the zeta potential was determined in a single spot. The zeta potential of spherical α-Al2O3 suspension has little difference at different solid concentrations. When the pH is less than 7 or greater than 11, the absolute value of the zeta potential is greater than 40 mV, at this time the double layer repulsive force between particles is bigger than the van der Waals attraction force.

The adsorption quantity of three anion additives(DIBMA, PssNa, CA) on the particle surface are shown in Fig. 7a–b and c. With the increased dosage of additives, the adsorption capacity gradually increases. However, the adsorption capacity does not change when the initial concentration reaches a certain value and this indicates that the adsorption has reached saturation. It also shows that with the increase of pH, the saturated adsorption decreases of three kinds of anionic additives. When pH is less than IEP, the particle surface becomes positively charged. It belongs to high-affinity adsorption when pH is 3 and almost all additives are adsorbed on particles before saturation adsorption. When pH is greater than IEP, the particle surface is negatively charged and additives show low-affinity adsorption on the particle surface. At the same time, OH− competes with additives for the positive position on the surface of the particle, which further leads to the decrease of saturated adsorption capacity.

Adsorption quantity of anionic additives on the α-Al2O3 particle surface (a) DIBMA; (b) PssNa; (c) CA.

The isothermal adsorption curves of the anionic additive at pH 3 or 9 were regressed by the Langmuir equation which is shown in Eq. (2).

Where γeq (mg/L) is the concentration of the additive in the supernatant, T indicates the amount of adsorption, mg/m2, Tmax (mg/m2) represents the amount of saturation adsorption at a certain pH, and k is constant and dimensionless. Based on this equation, the saturated adsorption capacity of PssNa is 0.67 mg/m2 or 0.46 mg/m2 at pH 3 or 9. When pH is 9, the saturated adsorption capacity of PssNa is about three times that of CA and DIBMA. Compared with DIBMA and CA, the gap between the high and low pH of saturated adsorption quantity is less. Duckler et al. [40] show that the saturated adsorption capacity of multiple organic acids is higher at the range of pKa, and it decreases when far away from pKa. It's far away from their pKa when pH is 9 and the experiment also shows that the saturated adsorption capacity is lower at this pH.

The adsorption of CA on the particle surface is highly susceptible to the influence of pH. The zeta potential-pH behaviour of α-Al2O3 suspension with different dosages of CA is shown in Fig. 8a. CA dissociates in aqueous solution and zeta potential changes when citrate ions are adsorbed on the particle surface. With the increased dosage of CA, the IEP moves to a lower pH. When the pH is greater than 11, the zeta potential under different concentrations of CA is similar, indicating that CA has no adsorption in this pH range. The complex between CA and Al3+ does not exist when the pH value is greater than 9, and Al3+ exists as Al(OH)-. It indicates that the affinity of CA and Al3+ is weak under alkaline conditions. Therefore, the interaction between CA and α-Al2O3 is hindered by the competitive adsorption of OH− at high pH.

Zeta potential-pH and yield stress-pH behaviour of α-Al2O3 gels with different CA dosage.

The yield stress-pH behaviour of 50 wt% spherical α-Al2O3 suspensions with different CA dosages is presented in Fig. 8b. The pH of the maximum yield stress of the suspension with and without additives is nearly IEP, the main reason is that double layer repulsive force at IEP is zero and the attraction forces dominate. The adsorption of CA shifted the maximum yield stress to a lower range of pH, which is similar to the variation of IEP. The maximum yield stress is 78.3 Pa without additives and the pH is about 9.5. Yield stress is zero when pH is greater than 11 or less than 7. When the dosage of CA changed from 0 to 0.2 dwb%, the yield stress moved toward a lower pH range. Suppose the CA is completely adsorbed on the particle surface, the coverage of 0.2 dwb% CA on the surface of 1g spherical α-Al2O3 was 0.62 Å2. The maximum yield stress decreased from 78.3 Pa to 50 Pa by about 36 %. This indicates that the citrate ion adsorbed on the surface of α-Al2O3 particles produced steric repulsive force which results in the decrease of the maximum yield stress.

The changing of IEP or zeta potential indicates the additive adsorption behaviour on the particle surface, the interparticle forces can be reflected by the changing of maximum yield stress. The relationship between yield stress and CA concentration at pH∼ 9 is shown in Fig. 9a. The solid concentration is 50 wt%. It has the lowest yield stress when the dosage of CA is 0.2 dwb%, this result is not surprising because the maximum yield stress moves to a lower pH with the increasing dosage of CA. It should be emphasized that the maximum yield stress of the suspension gradually decreases which shows in Fig. 9b. The adsorption of CA on the particle surface forms a steric layer and the maximum yield stress decreases.

ChemBio 3D is used to analyze the structure and adsorption mechanism of CA. The different ball-and-stick model of citric acid molecules adsorbed on the surface of α-Al2O3 particles after dissociation are shown in Fig. 10a–b and c. The molecular structure of citric acid is calculated by the ChemBio 3D MM2 model. Citric acid is a tricarboxylic acid and there are three kinds of dissociation, single ion, double ions and triple ions when adsorbed on the particle surface. According to the simulation calculation of the MM2 model, the type of O-H···O hydrogen bond exists in the citric acid molecule, as shown in Fig. 10. There is hydrogen bond attraction between hydroxyl and carboxyl groups, which makes citrate ions more stable. When α-Al2O3 particles adsorbed citrate ions are close to each other, a stable steric repulsive effect between particles is more likely to occur. When the dosage of CA is 0.2 dwb%, a large number of molecules are adsorbed on the particle surface at low pH. Therefore, compared with and without additives, the maximum yield stress of 50 wt% α-Al2O3 suspension decreases by about 36 %. Citrate ions and Al3+ are easy to form complexes at low pH through chemical interaction. Meanwhile, the existence of the O-H···O hydrogen bond makes a stable steric repulsive effect.

Conclusion

The saturation adsorption capacity decreases of three anionic additives with the increase of pH. The pH of the maximum yield stress of suspension with and without additives is around IEP, at this time attraction forces dominate and double layer repulsive force is zero. The surface of spherical α-Al2O3 particle is negatively charged when pH is greater than IEP, the anionic additives on the particle surface show low-affinity adsorption. The IEP locates at pH∼ 9.5 and it moves to a lower pH range when additives are adsorbed on the particle surface. The maximum yield stress of 50 wt% α-Al2O3 suspension decreases by about 36 % when the dosage of CA is 0.2 dwb%. ChemBio 3D simulation shows that there is a type of O-H···O hydrogen bond existed in the citric acid molecule which results in a stable steric repulsive effect. Citrate ions and Al3+ are easy to form complexes at low pH through chemical interaction. When the dosage of DIBMA is 0.3 dwb%, the maximum yield stress of the suspension increases from 78.3 Pa to 143.9 Pa, an increase of about 1.84 times, and the hydrophobic attraction force play a decisive role. The maximum yield stress increased from 78.3 Pa to 185 Pa with the additive dosage increase from 0 to 0.5 dwb%, the sum of charge attraction force and van der Waals attraction force is greater than the sum of double layer repulsive force and steric repulsive force, the attractive force dominates. The Gibbs free energy of adsorption of CA, DIBMA, and PssNa are 6.13 RT, 10.2 RT, and 12.58 RT. Compared with charge attraction force and hydrophobic attraction force, the adsorption of DIBMA and PssNa shows that the steric repulsive effect is relatively unimportant.

Therapeutic effect of faricimab in macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease: case series
Authors: Hyun Young Park, Kwangsic Joo, Se Joon Woo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo report the anatomical and functional outcomes of intravitreal faricimab injection in cases of macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease that were refractory to previous treatments.MethodsWe describe four cases of macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease with macular edema, which were treated with intravitreal faricimab injection.ResultsFour patients with persistent cystoid macular edema despite prior treatment received intravitreal faricimab injections, and two of them showed anatomical and visual improvement. In case 1, a patient with Coats’ disease achieved improved telangiectatic vessel at macula and visual recovery following eight monthly injections of faricimab, with central macular thickness (CMT) reduced from 645 μm to 217 μm and BCVA improved from 20/30 to 20/25. In case 2, a patient with diabetic retinopathy and suspected macular telangiectasia type 1 showed improvement in CMT (670 μm to 219 μm) and visual acuity (20/63 to 20/40) with resolution of telangiectatic vessel with leakage after four faricimab injections. Case 3 and 4 were chronic and long-standing cases; in case 3, a patient with Coats’ disease demonstrated partial response with reduced macular edema, while in case 4, with a Coats’ disease, showed no response after two faricimab injections.ConclusionsFaricimab may provide therapeutic benefits in cases of macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ diseases unresponsive to previous anti-VEGF agents, potentially due to its dual inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor-A and angiopoietin-2. However, its efficacy may be limited in patients with chronic phase. Further studies are warranted to assess its role in vascular retinal diseases beyond currently approved indications.

Introduction

Macular telangiectasia type 1 is a rare, unilateral retinal vascular disorder characterized by aneurysmal dilation of juxtafoveal capillaries, vascular leakage, and chronic macular edema [1, 2]. Unlike macular telangiectasia type 2, which results from retinal architectural degeneration due to Muller cell loss, macular telangiectasia type 1 is a vascular disease which presents with exudative changes resembling those seen in Coats’ disease [3–5]. Hard exudates frequently leads to persistent macular edema and consequent visual impairment [4]. Several treatment modalities have been employed to manage the associated macular edema, including intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections, intravitreal dexamethasone implants, laser photocoagulation, and photodynamic therapy [6–9].

Anti-VEGF agents such as bevacizumab and aflibercept have been used with variable success, though recurrence and refractory edema remain challenging [8, 10]. Faricimab is a recently approved bispecific monoclonal antibody that targets both VEGF-A and angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) pathway, designed to provide enhanced vascular stabilization and durability [11, 12]. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved faricimab for the treatment of neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), diabetic macular edema (DME), and retinal vein occlusion (RVO). However, to date, there have been no published reports regarding its therapeutic effect of faricimab toward macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease.

Here, we report four cases of macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease with persistent macular edema that demonstrated both anatomical and functional improvement following intravitreal faricimab injection. These cases suggest the potential for broader application of faricimab in vascular retinal disorders beyond its currently approved indications.

Methods

This retrospective case series included patients previously diagnosed with macular telangiectasia type 1 or Coats’ disease who were refractory to prior intravitreal anti-VEGF or corticosteroid treatments. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (IRB No. B-2508-993-106) and was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. Medical records, multimodal imaging findings, and treatment responses before and after intravitreal faricimab injections were reviewed.

Results

Case 1

A 57-year old female presented with decreased visual acuity in the left eye. She had hypertension and pre-diabetes mellitus. She had previously been diagnosed with nAMD at another hospital and had undergone three times of intravitreal aflibercept injections, three times of brolucizumab injections, and two times of bevacizumab injections for 3 years and underwent barrier laser at lattice degeneration at temporal periphery. At presentation, after 9 months without treatment, her best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) in the left eye was 20/30. Dilated fundus examination revealed macular edema with hard exudates (Fig. 1A). Fluorescein angiography (FA) demonstrated a telangiectatic vessel at macular area in the early phase (Fig. 1B) and tortuous vessel with leakage at temporal periphery in the late phase (Fig. 1C). Optical coherence tomography (OCT) showed significant macular edema with a central macular thickness (CMT) of 645 μm (Fig. 1G), and OCT angiography (OCTA) showed telangiectatic capillaries at the macular area (Fig. 1H and 1I). Based on these findings, the diagnosis was revised to Coats’ disease associated with macular edema. The patient was treated with an intravitreal faricimab injection. One month after the first injection, OCT demonstrated a reduction in cystoid macular edema, with CMT decreased to 528 μm. Monthly intravitreal faricimab injections were subsequently administered for a total of six doses, followed by a treat-and-extend regimen that was gradually extended to 3-month intervals, for a total of ten injections. After the treatment course, her vision was 20/25 and dilated fundus exam demonstrated reduced exudates with hemorrhage (Fig. 1D). FA revealed improvement of telangiectatic vessels at macula in the early phase (Fig. 1E) and leakage at temporal periphery in the late phase (Fig. 1F). OCT revealed further improvement in the cystoid macular edema with a final CMT 217 μm (Fig. 1J). Follow-up OCTA showed persistent telangiectatic vessels, but with improved foveal avascular zone (Fig. 1K, 1L).

Case 2

A 83-year-old female presented with decreased visual acuity in the right eye with initial BCVA 20/63. The patient had hypertension, diabetes mellitus and had a history of orbital lymphoma at the left orbit which was treated with 24 Gy of radiation therapy. Dilated fundus examination revealed mild non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy with mild telangiectatic vessel at macular area accompanied with macular edema (Fig. 2A). FA revealed telangiectatic vessels with leakage at macular area (Fig. 2B and 2C). OCT revealed cystoid macular edema with CMT 670 μm (Fig. 2H) and telangiectatic vessels were noted on OCTA (Fig. 2I and 2J). The patient was diagnosed with macular telangiectasia type 1 and was treated with intravitreal faricimab injection for 4 times monthly. After the injections, her visual acuity improved to 20/40 with resolved macular edema on fundus exam (Fig. 2D) and FA demonstrated improvement of telangiectatic vessels with leakage (Fig. 2F and 2G). OCT revealed resolution of macular edema with CMT 219 μm (Fig. 2K), and OCTA revealed improvement in telangiectatic vessels (Fig. 2L and 2M). Macular edema did not recur despite discontinuation of the injections for 10 months.

Case 3

A 40-year-old male with no past medical history presented with a diagnosis of Coats’ disease in his left eye at the other hospital. He was previously treated with intravitreal bevacizumab for twice 8 months ago and underwent laser treatment. On dilated fundus examination, multiple hard exudates associated with macular edema was present (Fig. 3A). FA revealed multiple leakage and nonperfusion area at nasal retina at early phase (Fig. 3B). Due to the recurrence of macular edema (Fig. 3C), the patient underwent additional intravitreal bevacizumab injection for 7 times and dexamethasone implant injection for 12 times. Even after several injections of dexamethasone implant, the macular edema recurred after 4 months of injection period during 7 years of follow up and his vision was 20/63 (Fig. 3D). As the disease showed a chronic recurrent course, intravitreal faricimab was administered 4 months after the last intravitreal dexamethasone implant injection. One month after the injection, the vision was maintained, and OCT examination revealed decreased central macular edema (Fig. 3E). Following a second intravitreal faricimab injection, the patient reported no subjective visual improvement, and treatment was resumed with dexamethasone implant due to persistent macular edema (Fig. 3F).

Case 4

A 56-year-old male with hypertension presented with decreased visual acuity in the left eye for two years, with a BCVA of 20/50. Dilated fundus examination revealed macular hemorrhage with macular edema (Fig. 4A). FA demonstrated telangiectatic vessels in the macular area (Fig. 4B). OCT confirmed central macular edema (Fig. 4C). The patient was diagnosed with a Coats’ disease and had previously received five intravitreal injections of bevacizumab, two injections of aflibercept, three dexamethasone intravitreal implants, and three additional injections of aflibercept for 7 years. Despite these treatments, his visual acuity declined to 20/100, and macular edema with exudation persisted. Given the chronic, recurrent, and treatment-resistant nature of the disease, two monthly intravitreal injections of faricimab were given 4 months following the last dexamethasone implant injection. One month after the injection, BCVA remained at 20/100 and macular edema persisted (Fig. 4D). An additional injection was administered; however, as no anatomical and functional improvement was observed, the treatment was switched to dexamethasone implant.

Discussion

Macular telangiectasia type 1 is a retinal vascular disorder that often presents with persistent macular edema secondary to aneurysmal capillary dilations and chronic leakage [2]. Although it shares certain clinical features with Coats’ diseases, it is typically localized to the juxtafoveal region and occurs in older patients, whereas Coats’ disease presents with peripheral aneurysmal capillary dilations [13]. Anti-VEGF therapies including aflibercept, bevacizumab, ranibizumab, and brolucizumab has been employed in selected cases, with varying degrees of anatomical and visual improvement [8, 10, 14, 15]. However, therapeutic responses are often suboptimal or transient in refractory cases.

Faricimab is a novel bispecific antibody that simultaneously inhibits VEGF-A and Angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2), targeting two critical pathways involved in vascular permeability and instability. In large scale phase 3 trials for nAMD, DME and RVO, faricimab demonstrated non-inferiority to standard anti-VEGF monotherapies, with extended dosing intervals [16–18]. Nonetheless, its application in other retinal vascular diseases such as macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease has not been well documented.

This study presents four patients with macular telangiectasia and Coats’ disease resistant to conventional anti-VEGF or steroid treatments (Table 1). In our first two cases, intravitreal faricimab resulted in significant anatomical and functional improvement, without adverse events in patients with macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease refractory to prior anti-VEGF and/or corticosteroid agents. This clinical response may reflect not only the effect of VEGF inhibition but also the additional benefit of Ang-2 blockades, which may enhance vascular stability and reduce leakage from telangiectatic vessels. Ang-2 binds to Tie 2 on vascular endothelial cells and facilitates both angiogenesis and vascular destabilization in conjunction with VEGF [19]. Tie signaling regulates angiogenesis and vessel stability and permeability [19]. Ang-2 has been implicated in the development of telangiectasis in hereditary telangiectasia, and studies using mouse models have demonstrated that its inhibition may prevent the formation of arteriovenous malformation [20, 21]. In our first two cases which were responsive to faricimab injection, they demonstrated a reduction in vascular leakage and a decreased density of dilated telangiectatic vessels. While evidence remains limited, these findings suggest a potential mechanistic rationale for faricimab in conditions with telangiectatic and leaky vasculatures.

However, faricimab showed limited response in case 3 and 4, which presented with a longer-standing disease course and a heavier exudative burden than the first two cases. A previous report indicated that some patients with aflibercept-refractory nAMD showed a limited reduction in persistent intrearetinal fluid after switching to faricimab [22]. In line with this finding, our cases with limited response also showed persistent intraretinal fluid with marked hard exudation. This may reflect chronic inner retinal structural distortion related to longstanding disease burden. Since faricimab was introduced after a sufficient interval from prior therapy, the response was less likely to have been substantially influenced by residual effects or interaction with previous treatments. Further, because only a limited number of faricimab injections were administered in these chronic cases, the potential benefit of long-term repeated treatment remains unclear and warrants further study.

To our knowledge, this is the first reported cases of macular telangiectasia type 1 and Coats’ disease successfully treated with faricimab. While the results are encouraging, further studies are required to validate the efficacy and safety of faricimab in macular telangiectasia and other retinal vascular disorders beyond nAMD, DME, and RVO. In particular, additional research is required to elucidate the relative contribution of Ang-2 inhibition in these atypical pathologies.

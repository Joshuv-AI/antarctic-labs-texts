Effect of bisoctrizole addition on color stability after artificial aging and surface hardness of scleral acrylic resin: an in-vitro study
Authors: Ahmed W Mohammed, Bayan S Khalaf
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe color degradation of ocular prostheses is significantly affected by exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light over time. As a result, the prosthesis requires frequent replacement, ultimately increases the financial burden of rehabilitation on the patient. This study aimed to determine the optimal bisoctrizole percentage for ocular acrylic by assessing its effects on color stability after 300 h of artificial aging and on surface hardness. A total of 120 specimens were prepared from white heat-cure scleral acrylic and subdivided into two groups according to the conducted tests; color stability and surface hardness. Each of these groups were further subdivided into 6 groups with 10 specimens for each based on the weight percentages of bisoctrizole added; 0%, 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, 2% and 2.5%. The color parameters were measured before and after artificial weathering using a portable digital colorimeter according to the CIE L*a*b* system. Shapiro–Wilk test was used to confirm normality of data. One-way ANOVA was used among the study groups followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) as a post hoc test (α = 0.05). All groups exhibited significant (p < 0.001) color change (ΔE > 0) after aging, with the control (0%) showing the highest ΔE. A progressive decrease in ΔE was observed with increasing bisoctrizole. Surface hardness was clinically comparable, though compared to the control, a statistically significant difference was found in Groups 1.5% and 2% (p < 0.001). Bisoctrizole had effectively preserved the color of scleral acrylic over time under UV light. Additionally, it had minimally affected the surface hardness of the material.Keywords: Bisoctrizole, Ocular acrylic, Colorimeter, Maxillofacial, UV absorber

Introduction

An ocular prosthesis is commonly used in maxillofacial rehabilitation for patients experiencing eye loss. Many factors may lead to partial or total loss of the eye including cancers, congenital anomalies or severe irreparable traumas (Bunyan et al. 2025; Kunsputri and Arinawati 2025). Evisceration; enucleation; and exenteration are the three types of surgeries responsible for an acquired eye loss (Pine et al. 2015). Since the eye represents a crucial part of human relationships, rehabilitation techniques and materials have been developed over time with the aim in mind to re-integrate the patient into the society (Hadi et al. 2021; De Caxias et al. 2019). An ocular prosthesis may provide a practical solution by restoring the natural color and appearance of the lost eye, enhancing the patient quality of life and self-esteem, these prosthetic parts are usually either pre-fabricated with standard shapes, color and sizes or can be custom made according the patient anatomy which gives the patient a better experience regarding fit and esthetics (Pathak et al. 2024). Usually, a custom-made eye prosthesis is made of a colorless and white medical grade-acrylic polymer also known as polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA), the white acrylic resin is used to simulate the natural white sclera while the colorless acrylic is used to embed the artificial blood vessels and iris, this material offers the advantages of being biocompatible, easy to handle and the relative low cost (Pathak et al. 2024; Bunyan et al. 2025; Dos Santos et al. 2016). On the other hand, these materials suffer from several drawback that may affect the prosthesis longevity, which are related to esthetic, chemical, physical and mechanical properties (Dos Santos et al. 2016). Regarding esthetic, a prosthesis should possess the ability to retain its original color for a period of time in a given environment. Of the main causes for refabricating an eye prosthesis is color degradation which may be caused by intrinsic factors represented by polymer matrix alteration during aging due to physical and chemical conditions (Shen et al. 2021; Kambala et al. 2020). While the extrinsic factors include UV light from solar radiation, temperature and humidity fluctuations, absorption and adsorption of substances. In regards to the physical and mechanical properties, surface hardness is defined as the material ability to resist scratching, cracks and permanent penetration. This property is critical for prosthesis's longevity, since it directly determines its resistance to abrasive wear (Shen et al. 2021; Dos Santos et al. 2016). Currently, most of the researches in the field of prosthetic rehabilitation are focusing on optimizing the materials’ performance regarding the optical, physical and mechanical properties by incorporating various additives (microparticles, nanoparticles, fibers) to the polymeric material (Dos Santos et al. 2023; Turki and Abdul - Ameer 2018; Almayahi and Khalaf 2024; Ahmed and Khalaf 2024).

The prolonged exposure of a prosthesis made of PMMA to the UV light will eventually cause destructive effect such as discoloration, which occurs due to photodegradation. Chain scission and production of free radicals are involved in the photodegradation process of PMMA (Sansul et al. 2023).

The electromagnetic spectrum is compromised by a broad region of the optical radiation from the sun which includes visible light, infra-red and ultraviolet light. The wavelength of light that lies in the range of 100-400nm is called the ultra-violet light, which is further subdivided into UVA (315–400 nm), UVB (280–315 nm), and UVC (100–280 nm). About 95% of the UV component that reaches the earth from the midday sun is UVA while 5% is UVB. Stratospheric ozone filters off UVC and the majority of UVB from extraterrestrial radiation (IARC 2012).

UV absorbers play a critical role in protecting the polymeric materials by absorbing high energy UV radiation and dissipating it as low-energy heat, thereby retarding the photodegradation process and blocking the production of free radicals that initiate the photo-oxidative process (Brostow et al. 2020; Marturano et al. 2017). To minimize the effect of photodegradation induced by UV light, a UV absorber can be added to the acrylic to prolong the life span of the prosthesis by reducing the UV degradation (Aung et al. 2023; Adil et al. 2024).

Bisoctrizole, also known as UV-360 is a broad-spectrum organic UV filter commonly used in sunscreens products (Herzog et al. 2004). It is a benzotriazole-based compound which has the ability to absorb, reflect and scatters both UV-A and UV-B rays. Its non-mutagenic and non-estrogenic properties further contribute to its suitability as a stabilizer (PubChem 2025; Sohn 2016). The protective effect of Bisoctrizole was utilized recently in enhancing the lifespan of RTV -silicon used in maxilla-facial prosthesis (Ahmed and Khalaf 2024).

In 1976, the International Commission on Illumination (CIE) developed the CIE L*a*b* system (ΔE76 or ΔEab) which is commonly used color space in dentistry to quantify color difference and considered it as a recommended reference during research and clinical conditions. (Tieh et al. 2022; ISO/TR 2016).

The significance of color difference can be assessed by determining the acceptability threshold, which is the value of color difference that most people would find unacceptable, and the perceptibility threshold, which is the value that is visually detectable (ISO, TR 2016). According to the ISO specification 28642:2016, the clinical CIELAB perceptibility threshold (PT) is ΔEab = 1.2, whereas the acceptability threshold (AT) is ΔEab = 2.7 (Tieh et al. 2022; Paravina et al. 2015, ISO, TR 2016). It is worth to mention that various studies considered ΔEab values of more than 3.3 to be clinically unacceptable (Mousavi et al. 2016; Gondim et al. 2020; Bitencourt et al. 2020; Freire et al. 2014; Hipólito et al. 2013).

This study aimed to assess the effect of bisoctrizole addition to the white scleral acrylic resin regarding color stability after artificial aging for 300 h using the CIELAB system. Also, the study investigated the additive effect on the surface hardness of the material.

The null hypothesis stated that the addition of bisoctrizole to the white scleral acrylic resin will have no significant effect on the color stability after 300 h of artificial aging and the surface hardness of the material.

Materials and methods

Manufacturing of experimental samples

The materials utilized in this study were heat-cure white scleral acrylic resin (J-510 polymer powder, batch #R061124-16 and J-570–8 non-crosslinked liquid monomer, batch #B092222-8; Factor II, USA) and Bisoctrizole powder (particle size 200—300 nm) (UV-360, batch #101,223–12; Dalian Handom Chemicals; China) which was added as a UV absorber.

Based on the weight, bisoctrizole was added to the polymer powder at five different percentages ranging from [0%(Control), 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, 2% and up to 2.5%.], the additive and the polymer powder were weighed using electronic balance with 0.001 accuracy and mixed (Table 1). At laboratory conditions of (23 ± 2 °C) and (50% ± 10) relative humidity, the mixture was stirred with a mechanical stirrer at 400 rpm for 30 min as a total mixing time, which was intermitted by a 30 s rest at each 1 min (Gad et al. 2019; Almayahi and Khalaf 2024).

A total of 120 samples were prepared of white scleral acrylic according to the manufacturer instructions. The samples were divided equally into two main groups; color stability and surface hardness. Each of the main groups where further subdivided into six subgroups (n = 10) according to the additive weight percentage.

Color test sample preparation

Samples in the color test were fabricated using custom-made 3D printed disk shaped pattern made of green polylactic acid (PLA) with dimensions of 2 mm thickness and 15 mm diameter (Kambala et al. 2020). To create a mold, the patterns were embedded in laboratory hard putty silicon and invested into two-part flasks with type IV dental stone (Fig. 1).

Color stability samples preparation. a 3-D printed pattern; b color stability samples after curing

The scleral acrylic was proportioned, manipulated and polymerized as recommended by the manufacturer. The material was hand mixed using a spatula in glass container, as it reached the dough stage the material was packed into the mold space.

Polymerization was performed using the short curing cycle which involved immersing the flasks in a water bath at room temperature, gradually heated to 71 °C and held for minimum of 90 min, then raised to 100 °C and held for 30 min. The flasks were removed, bench-cooled for approximately 30 min, and finally chilled under running water for 15 min to reach room temperature.

The heat cure resin sample were inspected carefully after retrieval, finished using wet grinding on sand paper starting from P400 up to P1000 and finally polished using pumice and rouge.

Color measurement

The samples were submitted to colorimetric analysis using a digital portable colorimeter (iWave-WR10; FRU; China) (Wang et al. 2025; Soto et al. 2021). To quantify sample color, the device was used to provide the color coordinates according to the CIELAB color space system. Before the measurements were made, the device was manually calibrated before each sample group using the calibration cylinder supplied with the device (Hipólito et al. 2013).

To minimize the effect of ambient light, a custom-made enclosure was designed and 3D printed from matt black PLA (Fig. 2 and Fig. 3) in which the samples were placed and analyzed (Kim et al. 2017).

The custom-designed sample holder used in color measurement. a Holder without the sample; b Holder with sample mounted

Photograph of the portable colorimeter with the mounted custom holder

For each sample, three measurements were made on the same spot and the mean for each of the three color coordinates (L*,a* and b*) according to the CIELAB color space system were calculated and recorded (Bannwart et al. 2013).

All the samples were submitted to colorimetric analysis before and after artificial aging using the same protocol explained above and the color difference was calculated using the above-mentioned CIE-Lab formula.

Accelerated aging

A commonly accepted standard for accelerated weathering is ISO-4892–3:2024 (ISO 2024), following cycle No.2 of this standard, the specimens were submitted to accelerated artificial aging in the accelerated aging device (QUV; Q-Lab; Germany) which was programmed at 8 h of dry exposure to UVA-340 with irradiance value of 0.76 W/m2 at 50 °C and 0.25 h of water spray followed by 3.75 h of condensation at 50 °C with a maximum aging time of 300 h.

Surface hardness testing

A 3D printed plastic pattern made of PLA was designed with the dimensions (20 mm width × 50 mm length × 4 mm thickness) to meet the requirements mentioned in the ISO specification 868,2003 (ISO 2003). The test was performed using shore D durometer (Shore D testing machine; Elcometer; UK) attached to a stand and 5 kg weight centered on the long axis of the device which had an indenter with 0.8mm tip diameter (Fig. 4).

Photograph of the Shore D durometer used in the study

The fabrication of the molds for this test followed the same techniques mentioned in preparing the color stability test samples (Fig. 5).

Preparation of surface hardness samples. a 3-D printed pattern; b surface hardness samples after curing

The testing procedure followed the ISO specification 868,2003 (ISO 2003), each measurement was recorded 1 s after the presser foot of the device was in firm contact with the sample, the five measurements were recorded 9 mm away from the edges of the sample and 6 mm apart from each other (Fig. 6). All the measurements were recorded digitally and the mean was then calculated.

Illustration of the measurement points positions and distances

Statistical analysis

IBM SPSS Statistics (Statistical Package for Social Sciences) software version 27.0 was used for statistical tests.

Descriptive statistics were computed and reported including mean and SD, the minimum and maximum measurements.

Date normality was confirmed using Shapiro–Wilk test, One-Way ANOVA was performed to compare measurements among the study groups followed by Tukey’s HSD as a post-hoc paired testing.

Statistical significance was set at p-value of less than 0.05 for all statistical analysis.

Results

All the study groups presented color change (ΔE > 0) after the artificial aging regardless of the added bisoctrizole percentage. However, it was observed that the control group (Group 0%) showed the highest ΔE values when compared to the modified groups as seen in Table 2 and Fig. 7. In addition, the results showed a progressive decrease in ΔE values as the percentage of bisoctrizole increased with the exception to Group 2.5% which will be discussed later.

Discussion

Based on the obtained results of ΔE values after 300 h of artificial aging and the results of the surface hardness, the null hypothesis regarding the effect of bisoctrizole addition on the color stability and the surface hardness of the ocular acrylic was rejected.

The present study evaluated the effect of Bisoctrizole addition at different concentrations to the ocular acrylic resin in terms of color stability after artificial aging and surface hardness. Overall, the potential effect of the bisoctrizole was revealed by artificial aging process. The results showed statistically (p < 0.05) higher color changes demonstrated by the higher ΔE values for the control group (Group 0%) after 300 h of artificial aging under UV light when compared to the bisoctrizole-containing groups. Additionally, a progressive decrease in ΔE values was noted as the weight percentage of bisoctrizole was increased except for Group 2.5%. Additionally, all the modified groups had ΔE values which remained below the clinically acceptable threshold (ΔE < 3.3)(Hipólito et al. 2013; Al-Qarni et al. 2019; Gondim et al. 2020; Bitencourt et al. 2020; Freire et al. 2014).

At the time of this research there was no previous study that had discussed bisoctrizole as an additive to acrylic resin. However, a previous study by Ahmed et al. which investigated the role of bisoctrizole on UV absorption properties of platinum‑based maxillofacial silicone after 100 and 200 h of artificial aging. The study stated that bisoctrizole can help the material in resisting the negative effect of UV radiation and preserve its optical and mechanical properties by absorbing the UV photon energy and converting it to a less detrimental form such as heat (Ahmed and Khalaf 2024).The finding of Ahmed et al. may align with the findings of this study regarding the protective effect of bisoctrizole.

Additionally, the results also align with a previous study by Andreotti et al. which investigated the effect of incorporating different nano particles to the ocular acrylic. Nano particles are widely used as inorganic UV absorbers in sunscreen lotions. Andreotti et al. concluded that incorporating 2.5% TiO2 helped in maintaining color and microhardness after 1008 h of artificial aging (Andreotti et al. 2014).

The observed increase in ΔE values in the control group could be attributed to the degradation of organic matrix components and the oxidation process induced by UV radiation. In contrast, the superior color stability in the modified groups can be attributed the bisoctrizole ability to absorb UV radiation and inhibit photooxidation, minimizing polymer photodegradation. This finding is particularly important in the field of ocular prosthesis where color matching and aesthetics are critical factor for patient satisfaction.

A notable increase in ΔE value for Group 2.5% when compared to Group 1%, Group 1.5% and Group 2% was observed and it could be attributed to degradation of UV absorber upon prolong exposure to UV light (Pickett and Moore 1995). Since UV absorbers dissipate the absorbed energy as heat, in higher percentages the UV absorber may dissipate more heat which in turn may have accelerated the degradation process, consuming the added UV absorber relatively faster than in samples with lower percentages leading to a decreased capacity in protecting the polymer which led to color degradation.

The obtained ΔE values in the bisoctrizole-containing groups aligns with the established clinical thresholds for perceptibility and acceptability. According to the ISO specification 28,642–2016 (ISO, TR 2016), a ΔE value of 1.2 represents the perceptibility threshold while ΔE value of 2.7 represents the acceptability threshold. In this study, the control groups showed the highest ΔE values (ΔE = 2.177 ± 0.222) which exceeded the perceptibility threshold whereas all the modified groups remained well below these limits. The result suggests that a minimal percentage of bisoctrizole (≥ 0.5%) may provide optimal UV protection.

Regarding the surface hardness value, comparable results had been achieved using different percentages of bisoctrizole. Statistical analysis of surface hardness yielded non-significant difference (p > 0.05) in Group 0.5%, Group 1% and Group 2.5% when compared to the control group. However, significant statistical decline (p < 0.05) in surface hardness values was observed in Group 1.5% (84.54 ± 0.462) and Group 2% (83.98 ± 0.345) with mean difference of 0.9 and 1.46 respectively which may be due to the disruption of the polymer matrix. In contrast, Group 2.5% showed a relative increase in hardness values when compared to Group 1.5% and Group 2%, and it may be attributed to the agglomeration of the additive particles which in turn may introduced localized surface stiffness at higher percentages. Further analysis techniques using field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) or scanning electron microscopy (SEM) would be required to confirm this interpretation.

Researchers in the field of prosthetic rehabilitation have been studying the effect of different additives on the surface hardness of the heat-cured acrylic resin, reveling that various additives can either enhance, degrade or in some cases have no effect on the surface hardness of the heat-cured acrylic, these results depend on the nature and percentage of the additive. Hadi et al. had found that the addition of different concentrations of Ethyl methacrylate and Isobutyl methacrylate copolymers resulted in slight decrease in surface hardness (Hadi et al. 2021). While Pentapati et al. stated that the addition of 15% wt. of Aluminum Oxide to the heat cure acrylic led to a significant increase in surface hardness (Pentapati et al. 2017).However, Jaber investigated the addition of Aluminum Oxide in low percentage and found that it did not affect the surface hardness and roughness of the acrylic (Jaber 2023).

The lack of significant difference in surface hardness in this study can be attributed to the small weight percentage of bisoctrizole, the nature of bisoctrizole and its interaction with the polymer matrix which is according to above listed results did not affect the polymerization process of the material.

Clinically, the results implies that the modification of ocular acrylic with bisoctrizole does not compromise the clinical performance of ocular prosthesis material in terms of surface hardness. Since surface hardness is considered as a critical factor influencing abrasion resistance and durability (Shen et al. 2021), maintaining the material original surface hardness values will likely retain their surface finish during use enhancing patient comfort related to surface wear.

It is an in vitro study that employed a single brand of scleral acrylic resin; therefore, its findings cannot be generalized to other materials or to different clinical conditions.

Conclusions

Within the limitation of the study, it can be concluded that the incorporation of Bisoctrizole do effectively enhance color stability of ocular acrylic materials under UV light at weight percentages as low as (0.5%). Additionally, the added material has a minimal effect on the surface hardness. Further research is required to investigate the additive effect on the other properties of the material.

Overall, the presented results suggest that bisoctrizole may be a promising additive for those with maxillofacial defects by enhancing the durability of the prosthetic material which eventually reflects on the patient budget on the long term.

IsomiRome analysis reveals dysregulation of 5’ isomiRs during SARS-CoV-2 infection
Authors: Ming-Ju Amy Lyu, Munire Maimaiti, Ziwei Zhong, Jiameng Hu, Xiang Zhang, Quanyi Wang, Chen Wang, Haiyang Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnnotated canonical miRNAs (anomiRs) are critical regulators of COVID-19 pathogenesis. Their 5′ isomiR counterparts, which harbor a shifted 5’ end and an altered seed region relative to cognate anomiRs, represent an essential yet understudied component of the miRNAome. Herein, we performed an integrative analysis to investigate the expression and regulation of 5’ isomiRs in SARS-CoV-2-infected human lung cells. Using Argonaute (AGO) immunoprecipitated small-RNA sequencing data, we established a repertoire of AGO-loaded 5’ isomiRome encompassing 826 5’ isomiRs from SARS-CoV-2-infected lung cells. A total of 54 5’ isomiRs were differentially expressed, with the majority showing dysregulation 24 h after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Compared to anomiRs, 5’ isomiRs were preferentially induced at 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection, including six 5’ isomiRs derived from miR-4485 precursor. By focusing on a 5’ isomiR of miR-4485–3p exhibiting one nucleotide downstream shift of the 5’ end (miR-4485–3p|+1), we demonstrated that miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression significantly repressed genes involved in cellular processes relating to cell cycle, cytoplasmic translation, and carbohydrate derivative metabolic process. Importantly, transcriptome integration further revealed that these cellular processes repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression were strongly downregulated at 24 h following SARS-CoV-2 infection. Moreover, 26 out of 43 (60.5 %) hub genes co-repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 and SARS-CoV-2 infection in these three cellular processes have reported association with COVID-19 in the literature. Taken together, our findings reveal the repertoire and temporal dynamics of AGO-loaded 5’ isomiRs in SARS-CoV-2-infected lung cells and identify miR-4485–3p|+ 1 as a potential regulator of host pathways relevant to COVID-19 pathogenesis.Keywords: SARS-CoV-2, COVID-19, Small RNA, 5’ isomiR, Transcriptome

Introduction

Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) emerged in late 2019 as the causative agent of Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19), triggering a global pandemic with profound health, economic, and social consequences [1], [2]. As a novel human coronavirus, SARS-CoV-2 demonstrates remarkable transmissibility and a wide clinical spectrum, ranging from asymptomatic infection to fatal respiratory failure and multi-organ dysfunction. The respiratory tract is the primary site of SARS-CoV-2 infection, which profoundly reprograms host cellular processes, including cell cycle regulation, protein translation, and metabolic pathways, to facilitate its replication [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]. For instance, the virus interferes with the host cell cycle by inducing arrest at the G1/S or G2/M phase, thereby creating a favorable environment for viral genome replication [4], [5]. In parallel, SARS-CoV-2 disrupts host protein synthesis via its non-structural protein NSP1, which binds to the 40S ribosomal subunit to inhibit host mRNA translation, while allowing selective translation of viral transcripts through unique structural elements in their 5’UTR [3], [7]. Additionally, the virus induces metabolic reprogramming characterized by dysregulating glycolysis, mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and lipid and amino acid biosynthesis, which collectively supply the energy and biosynthetic precursors required for efficient viral replication [8], [9], [10]. These coordinated alterations highlight the virus’s ability to hijack fundamental host cellular processes for its propagation. While vaccines and antiviral therapies have been developed at an unprecedented pace, the virus’s ongoing evolution remains a significant challenge [11]. Thus, gaining a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms underlying SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis is essential for designing a broad array of complementary strategies to effectively counter this continually adapting threat.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of small non-coding RNAs that function as key post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression, playing essential roles in numerous physiological and pathological processes, including immune modulation, inflammation, neurogenesis, tumorigenesis, and viral infections [12], [13], [14], [15], [16]. The miRNA biogenesis is a highly regulated, multi-step process essential for the post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression. The process begins with RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) transcribing a primary miRNA transcript (pri-miRNA). In the nucleus, the Drosha complex cleaves the pri-miRNA to generate a hairpin-structured precursor miRNA (pre-miRNA). This pre-miRNA is then exported to the cytoplasm, where the Dicer enzyme further processes it into a ∼22-nucleotide double-stranded RNA duplex. This duplex is subsequently unwound to produce the mature miRNA guide strand and its complementary passenger strand, with the guide strand (functional miRNA) being loaded into Argonaute (AGO) proteins to form the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which guides them to complementary sequences in target mRNAs. The sequence of 6 or 7 nucleotides (typically nucleotides 2–7 or 2–8 from the 5′ end) at the 5′ end of the miRNAs, called the seed region, primarily determines miRNA’s target specificity. Through this mechanism, miRNAs can promote mRNA degradation, thereby fine-tuning host cellular programs by affecting more than 60 % of protein-coding genes [12]. The miRBase is a well-established miRNA repository storing annotated canonical miRNAs [17], which we termed anomiRs in this study. Recent studies have highlighted that certain anomiRs are significantly dysregulated during COVID-19 progression and contribute to key aspects of the disease, such as viral infection, cytokine storm, and immune evasion [18], [19], [20]. By targeting both viral transcripts and host genes, these anomiRs serve as critical regulators of the host response to SARS-CoV-2 infection.

Paradoxically, although miRNAs are critical post-transcriptional regulators, they display heterogeneous ends, suggesting that miRNAs can also have different isoforms similar to protein-coding genes despite their short length. This is particularly surprising since it is generally believed that the miRNA processing machinery ensures the generation of a mature miRNA with a fixed sequence. The non-canonical miRNA isoforms exhibiting a shifted 5’ end compared to their cognate anomiRs were named 5’ isomiRs [21], [22]. Although the mechanism underlying 5’isomiRs biogenesis remains incompletely understood, it has been reported that Drosha, Dicer, and their partner proteins, key components involved in the biogenesis of anomiRs, also directly impacts the production and diversity of 5’isomiRs. 5’isomiRs are particularly interesting since they harbor distinct seed regions that can regulate a wide spectrum of exclusive target genes. Importantly, 5’isomiRs have been detected using northern blotting in both animals and plants, exhibit tissue-specific or disease-specific expression patterns, and play roles in cellular processes such as development, immune response, or tumor progression [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29]. Although 5’isomiRs represent an essential component of the miRNAome, their repertoire and expression dynamics following SARS-CoV-2 infection are still unknown.

In this study, we conducted an integrative study to examine the expression dynamics and regulatory roles of 5’ isomiRs, a class of non-canonical miRNAs, in human lung cells following SARS-CoV-2 infection. Leveraging AGO- immunoprecipitated small RNA sequencing data (GSE183280) [30], we curated a catalog of 826 AGO-bound 5’ isomiRs derived from SARS-CoV-2 infected human lung cells. Among these, 54 displayed significant differential expression, predominantly altered at 24 h post-infection. Notably, 5’ isomiRs showed preferential upregulation compared to anomiRs during this infection time point, including six 5’ isomiRs originating from the miR-4485 precursor. We further characterized a specific 5’ isomiR of miR-4485–3p with a one-nucleotide shift at the 5’ end (designated miR-4485–3p|+1), and demonstrated that its overexpression led to marked repression of genes associated with cell cycle regulation, protein translation in the cytoplasm, and carbohydrate derivative metabolism. Importantly, we further showed that these pathways repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression were significantly downregulated at 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection. Furthermore, 60.5 % of the hub genes suppressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 in these pathways have previously been linked to COVID-19 in the literature. Collectively, our findings uncover the landscape and altered expression of AGO-loaded 5’ isomiRs in the context of SARS-CoV-2 infection and suggest a potential functional role of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 in modulating host cellular responses during viral pathogenesis.

Materials and methods

5’ isomiR identification analysis

To identify Argonaute (AGO)-loaded 5’ isomiR from SARS-CoV-2-infected lung cells, we downloaded AGO-immunoprecipitated (IP) small-RNA sequencing data of SARS-CoV-2-infected human Calu-3 lung cells from GEO (GSE183280) [30] and built a 5’ isomiR identification pipeline. Specifically, the raw sequencing reads of small RNA sequencing data were first trimmed to remove the 3’ adaptor sequences using FindAdapt with default parameter [31] and then aligned to the human genome using Bowtie with parameter ‘-v 1 -a --best --strata’ [32]. To minimize potential false positives of 5’ isomiRs caused by cross-mapping among miRNA family members [33], only reads with perfect matches were retained. For the multimapping reads, their expression was normalized by the mapped loci number. All mapped reads were then clustered based on the identity of their 5′ genomic loci, and 5’ isomiRs were defined as sequences exhibiting 5’ end shifts within ±6 nucleotides of the 5’ end of annotated canonical miRNAs (anomiRs), according to miRBase annotations [17]. The criterion of ±6 nucleotides was based on the observation that more than 99.9 % of mapped reads to the miRNA precursors were derived from the region of ±6 nucleotides of the 5’ end of annotated canonical miRNAs. Only 5’ isomiRs that were consistently identified in all triplicates of AGO-IP small-RNA sequencing data were retained.

5’ isomiR nomenclature

To label the identified 5’ isomiR, the combination of anomiR name from miRBase as well as the 5’ end shift direction and offset number were used a notation to represent each 5’ isomiR. Specifically, the negative sign (-) was used to indicate that the 5’end of 5’ isomiR was shifted to the upstream compared to cognate anomiR. On the other hand, the positive sign (+) was used to represent that the 5’end of 5’ isomiR was shifted to the downstream compared to the cognate anomiR. A number followed by a positive sign or a negative sign indicated the number of nucleotides had shifted away from the 5’end of anomiR.

Small RNA sequencing analysis of SARS-CoV-2 infected lung cell data

We examined miRNA expression changes 4 h and 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection compared to 0 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection in human Calu-3 lung cells based on public small RNA sequencing downloaded from GSE148729 [34]. According to [34], SARS-CoV-2 infections were done by adding the appropriate amount of virus stock solution produced in Vero E6 cells at an MOI of 0.3 directly to the Calu-3 cells. The supernatant from non-infected Vero E6 cells was used for mock infections. The raw small RNA sequencing reads were first trimmed to remove the 3’ adaptor sequences using FindAdapt with default parameter [31] and then aligned to the human genome using Bowtie with parameter ‘-v 1 -a --best --strata’ [32]. Only reads with perfect matches were retained. For the multimapping reads, their expression was normalized by the mapped loci number. We then quantified annotated canonical miRNAs and 5’isomiRs identified from AGO-IP small-RNA sequencing data using these mapped reads. The differentially expressed miRNAs were identified using the edgeR package [35] using Negative binomial test after normalization by the Trimmed Mean of M-values (TMM) method. Using the same processing protocol, we also downloaded and analyzed another small RNA sequencing data of SARS-CoV-2 infected Calu-3 lung cells (GSE217863) [36] to validate the expression difference of six 5’isomiRs derived from miR-4485 precursor.

Expression clustering analysis

We applied the R package Mfuzz [37] to investigate the expression dynamics of differentially expressed 5’isoforms by taking differentially expressed anomiRs as control, under default parameters. To identify the number of clusters, hcluse and elbow methods were used and cluster number four was chosen as the most appropriate number. We then run 100 times of clustering to obtain the most robust clustering result under the cluster number four. Within each cluster, we used Fisher’s exact test to estimate 5’isomiR enrichment by taking the proportion difference of all differentially expressed 5’isomiRs and anomiRs as the background (BH-corrected p-value <0.05).

RNA-seq data analysis of SARS-CoV-2 infected lung cell data

We examined mRNA expression changes 4 h and 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection compared to 0 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection in human Calu-3 lung cells based on public RNA-seq data downloaded from GSE148729 [34]. This dataset contains both small RNA sequencing data and RNA-Seq data in SARS-CoV-2 infected human Calu-3 lung cells, which allows integrating the analysis results of small RNA sequencing data and RNA-Seq data without batch effects. The method for analyzing small RNA sequencing data was described in the section “Small RNA sequencing analysis after SARS-CoV-2 infection in lung cells”. RNA-seq data analysis was performed as previously described [38], [39], [40]. Briefly, raw RNA-seq reads were processed with Trimmomatic [41] with default parameters to remove low-quality reads and potential adaptor contamination. High-quality reads of at least 75 nucleotides in length were then aligned to the human reference genome (hg38) using HISAT2 [42] with default parameters. Only uniquely mapped reads were used for gene-level quantification via featureCounts [43]. The human genome and gene annotation files were obtained from the Ensembl FTP site (ftp://ftp.ensembl.org/pub). Differentially expressed (DE) genes (FDR < 0.05) were identified with the edgeR package [35] using Negative binomial test after normalization by the Trimmed Mean of M-values (TMM) method.

Functional enrichment analysis

Enriched Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes were identified using the DAVID Bioinformatics Resources (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/). Significantly enriched processes were determined using a false discovery rate (FDR) threshold of < 5 %, with all expressed genes used as the background.

Protein-protein interaction network analysis

Analysis of protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was conducted in STRING database [ref]. For each enriched biological pathway, we constructed PPI network using the default medium confidence of 0.4 as the minimum required interaction score and obtained hub genes that were highly connected.

Cell culture

Human lung epithelial cell line Calu-3 (ATCC HTB-55) serves as a robust SARS-CoV-2 infection model, which has been shown to largely recapitulate infection of the human epithelium and to preserve the SARS-CoV-2 genomic fidelity [44]. Calu-3 cells were cultured in Dulbecco Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco, Cat#12100046) supplemented with 1 % penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco Cat#15070063) and 10 % Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS; Gibco, Cat#A5256701) at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 incubator, according to according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

miRNA mimics transfection experiment

miRNA mimics are chemically synthesized, double-stranded RNA molecules that mimic the function of the endogenous miRNA, which are routinely used in functional analysis to up-regulate miRNA activity by mimicking its natural function and observing the resulting cellular effects. miR-4485–3p|+ 1 mimics and control mimics were synthesized by Genepharma company. For transfection experiment, Calu-3 cells were transfected with miR-4485–3p|+ 1 mimics or control mimics (60 nM final concentration) using the siRNA-Mate plus transfection reagent (Genepharma Cat#G04026) without pre-infected with SARS-CoV-2, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After 24 h, the transfection medium was replaced with fresh complete DMEM. Cells were harvested at 72 h post-transfection for RNA-seq experiments. The transfection experiments were conducted in two biological replicates, resulting in a total of four RNA-seq data.

The RNA-seq experiment

The RNA-seq experiments were performed as previously described [38], [39], [40]. Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen Cat# 15596018CN) from Calu-3 cells with miR-4485–3p|+ 1 mimics or control mimics transfection, and then subjected to polyA+ RNA enrichment with TruSeq RNA Library Preparation Kit v2 (Illumina) and prepared into the cDNA library according to the Illumina RNA-Seq instruction. Agilent 2100 was used to estimate RNA integrity number (RIN) before library construction. Only samples with RIN value more than eight were retained for library construction. The constructed cDNA libraries were sequenced with Illumina HiSeq4000 in the paired-end 2 × 150 nt layout.

The RNA-seq analysis of miRNA mimics transfection data

We used to same analysis protocol, as described in the section ‘RNA-seq data analysis of SARS-CoV-2 infected lung cell data’, to analyze the RNA-seq data of miRNA mimics transfection experiment.

Real-time PCR

The quantification of selected gene was conducted by real-time PCR using the SYBR Green PCR mix (Applied Biosystems). The obtained values were normalized to the level of GAPDH mRNA. The primers used are listed as follows:

Reverse: CCTTGAGGGTAAGACTGACAGC

Reverse: CAAACACCAGCACTGTCTTGGC

SRL, Forward: TCAAGCCTCTGGAGCAGTCCTA

Reverse: GGTAGATTTACCAACACTCCACG

Data availability

All original RNA-Seq data have been deposited in GEO database with accession number GSE309153.

Results

Identification of argonaute-loaded 5’isomiRs in SARS-CoV-2 infected lung cells

Functional miRNAs are bound into Argonaute (AGO) proteins to form the core of the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) [12]. To identify AGO-loaded 5’isomiRs after SARS-CoV-2 infection, we developed a 5’isomiR identification pipeline and analyzed AGO-immunoprecipitated (IP) small-RNA sequencing from SARS-CoV-2-infected human Calu-3 lung cells (Fig. 1A, Materials and Methods).

(B) The illustration of mapped reads from hsa-miR-4485–3p’s anomiR and one identified 5’isomiR that exhibits one-nucleotide downstream shift of the 5′ end of hsa-miR-4485–3p (miR-4485–3p|+1). Using this pipeline, we established a repertoire of AGO-loaded 5’ isomiRs in SARS-CoV-2-infected lung cells, comprising a total of 826 5’ isomiRs (Supplementary Table S1). An example of a 5’ isomiR (miR-4485–3p|+1) derived from miR-4485–3p is illustrated in Fig. 1B. We proposed a 5’ isomiR nomenclature (Materials and Methods), in which the notation “miR-4485–3p|+ 1” denotes a 5’ isomiR with one nucleotide shifted downstream relative to the annotated 5’ end of miR-4485–3p. The expression of identified 5’ isomiRs was well-correlated among triplicates of AGO-IP small-RNA sequencing data (Fig. 1C, Pearson correlation coefficient >0.9, p < 2.2e-16), comparable to that based on anomiRs within similar expression range (Figure S1), indicating the robustness of identified 5’isomiRs. Fig. 1D showed the top 20 5’ isomiRs ranked according to their absolute expression abundance, all of which were derived from miRNAs conserved among mammalian species. Notably, we found 28 5’isomiRs with higher absolute expression abundance compared to their cognate anomiRs (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Table S2), and four of them, including hsa-mir-16–2–3p|-1, hsa-mir-101–3p|-1, hsa-mir-935|+ 1, and hsa-mir-192–5p|+ 1, were included in the top 20 most highly expressed 5’ isomiRs.

SARS-CoV-2 infection induces significant expression alterations of 5’isomiRs

We further analyzed which 5’isomiRs were significantly altered by SARS-CoV-2 infection in human Calu-3 lung cells using small RNA sequencing data of GSE148729 (Materials and Methods). At 4 h post-infection, only four differentially expressed 5’ isomiRs were identified, all of which were downregulated (Fig. 2A, C; Supplementary Table S3; negative binomial test, FDR < 10 %). In contrast, 19 differentially expressed anomiRs were detected (Fig. 2A, C; Supplementary Table S3), among which 18 were also downregulated, similar to the trend observed in 5’ isomiRs. These results suggest that SARS-CoV-2 infection disrupts the host miRNAome primarily through a miRNA downregulation mechanism at the early stage of infection, with a preferential impact on anomiR expression.

To further assess the extent of miRNAome disturbance over time, we examined the expression changes of 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs at 24 h post-infection. At this time point, we identified 51 differentially expressed 5’ isomiRs and 53 differentially expressed anomiRs (Fig. 2A, C; Supplementary Table S4), a substantial increase compared to the numbers observed at 4 h. This finding indicated that prolonged SARS-CoV-2 infection may lead to greater disruption of miRNA expression. The effect was particularly pronounced for 5’ isomiRs, with a twelve-fold increase in the number of differentially expressed 5’ isomiRs at 24 h compared to 4 h post-infection. Notably, a longer period of SARS-CoV-2 infection induced 48 differentially expressed 5’isomiRs and anomiRs, accounting for 46 % of total differentially expressed 5’isomiRs and anomiRs. These results indicated that SARS-CoV-2 infection led to a broad, time-dependent disruption of the entire miRNome in human Calu-3 lung cells. Regarding expression abundance, DE 5’ isomiRs were generally lower than DE anomiRs. However, DE 5’ isomiRs exhibited significantly higher abundances than non-DE anomiRs (Fig. 2D and E, Supplementary Figure S2). Nevertheless, some DE 5’ isomiRs with low expression abundance may have little or no physiological relevance.

We next obtained the union set of DE 5’ isomiRs and DE anomiRs at 4- and 24-hours post-infection, and visualized their expression dynamics using a heatmap across 0-, 4-, and 24-hours post-infection (Fig. 2F). For the upregulated 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs, approximately 50 % began to increase at 4 h post-infection, with even stronger induction observed at 24 h, while the remaining ∼50 % increased only at 24 h. For the downregulated 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs, the majority began to decrease at 4 h post-infection, and this reduction persisted at 24 h, with ∼40 % showing further decreases at 24 h post-infection.

Characterization of 5’isomiR expression dynamics during SARS-CoV-2 infection

To further characterize differentially expressed 5’isomiR, we analyzed their expression dynamics during SARS-CoV-2 infection in human Calu-3 lung cells. Applying a noise-robust clustering method suitable for short time-series transcriptomic data, we identified four distinct clusters (C1–C4) comprising differentially expressed 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs, each exhibiting a unique expression pattern (Fig. 3A; Supplementary Table S5; Materials and Methods). Based on the clustering results, we found nine miRNA precursors, including miR-23b, miR-15b, miR-200b, miR-20a, miR-26a, miR-374a, miR-29b, miR-4485, and let-7i, with both 5p and 3p strands for the anomiRs/5′isomiRs clustered within the same cluster, suggesting the differential expression of these miRNAs was probably from transcriptional regulation (Supplementary Table S5).

We next assessed the proportion of 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs within each cluster. Compared to the overall proportion of differentially expressed 5’ isomiRs and anomiRs as the background, cluster C3 was significantly enriched for 5’ isomiRs (Fig. 3B; Fisher’s exact test, Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p < 0.05). The 5’ isomiRs in cluster C3 showed a distinctive expression pattern characterized by specific induction at 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection (Fig. 3A, C). Notably, one-third (6 out of 18) of the 5’ isomiRs in cluster C3 were derived from the miR-4485 precursor, including two from miR-4485–5p and four from miR-4485–3p (Fig. 3C). We further verified the upregulation of these six 5’isomiRs based on an independent small RNA sequencing data (GSE217863) from SARS-CoV-2 infected Calu-3 lung cells (Supplementary Figure S3). Among these, miR-4485–3p|+ 1 exhibited both the highest expression level and the greatest induction at 24 h post-infection (Fig. 3D). In the following, we focused on miR-4485–3p|+ 1 to explore its potential role during SARS-CoV-2 infection.

Overexpression of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 represses genes involved in the regulation of cell cycle, cytoplasmic translation, and carbohydrate derivative metabolic process

We next investigated the effects of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 on gene expression. To this end, we transfected human Calu-3 lung cells with miR-4485–3p|+ 1 mimics and control mimics in biological replicates, and conducted RNA-seq experiments to examine miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression induced transcriptomic changes (Fig. 4A, Materials and Methods). Principal components analysis (PCA) revealed a clear separation between miR-4485–3p|+ 1 transfected samples (Mimics1, Mimics2) and mimics control transfected samples (NC1, NC2) along the first principal component (PC1), which accounted for approximately 45 % of the total variance in gene expression. This result indicates a pronounced effect of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 on the cellular transcriptome. Consistently, the predicted target genes of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 were significantly repressed compared with the background genes after miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression (Fig. 4C, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test p < 0.05). We further verified the downregulation of randomly selected four target genes of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 using q-PCR (Supplementary Figure S4). Since miRNAs regulate cellular processes through a combination of their direct targets and secondary or indirect effects on gene expression networks [45], [46], we next examined the whole transcriptomic differences caused by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression. A total of 571 differentially expressed genes were identified, including 310 downregulated and 261 upregulated genes (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Table S6; Negative binomial test, FDR<0.05). While no significant functional enrichment was obtained using differentially upregulated genes, differentially downregulated genes were strongly enriched in biological processes relating to the regulation of cell cycle, cytoplasmic translation, and carbohydrate derivative metabolic process (Fig. 4E, Supplementary Table S7; Fisher’s exact test, FDR<0.05). Notably, these biological processes have previously been implicated in SARS-CoV-2 infection [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10].

miR-4485–3p|+ 1 repressed cellular processes are strongly downregulated at 24 h post SARS-CoV-2 infection

Having identified the biological processes regulated by miR-4485–3p|+ 1, we next investigated whether these regulatory effects were recapitulated during SARS-CoV-2 infection. Analyzing transcriptome data post SARS-CoV-2 infection in human Calu-3 lung cells (GSE148729) identified 26 and 222 significantly downregulated biological processes at 4- and 24-hours post-infection, respectively (FDR < 0.05; Supplementary Tables S8, 9). Notably, 7 out of the 10 biological processes repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overlapped with those downregulated at 24 h post-infection (Fig. 5A, B), whereas none overlapped with processes repressed at 4 h. This observation aligns with our earlier finding that miR-4485–3p|+ 1 is specifically induced at 24 h following SARS-CoV-2 infection based on the data of GSE148729 (Fig. 3C, D). The shared processes repressed by both miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression and SARS-CoV-2 infection were largely categorized into three major functional groups: cell cycle related process, cytoplasmic translation, and carbohydrate derivative metabolic process (Fig. 5B). Importantly, the majority of the genes repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 in these three cellular processes was significantly downregulated at 24 h, but not at 4 h, post-infection (Fig. 5C, D, E). By incorporating protein-protein interaction (PPI) information from STRING database [47], we constructed PPI network and obtained hub genes that were highly connected within each of these three cellular processes. Notably, Notably, 26 out of 43 (60.5 %) of the hub genes co-repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 and SARS-CoV-2 infection had been previously reported to be associated with COVID-19 based on literature searching (Fig. 5F; Supplementary Table S10), highlighting the potential role of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 in the pathogenesis of COVID-19.

Discussion

The substantial expression and variability of 5’ isomiRs contrast sharply with the limited attention they have received in miRNA research [26]. In this study, we systematically investigated the landscape, expression dynamics, and regulatory functions of 5’ isomiRs in human lung epithelial Calu-3 cells following SARS-CoV-2 infection based on small RNA sequencing and transcriptome data. Our analysis of AGO-IP-based small RNA sequencing data revealed a rich and previously underexplored repertoire of 5’ isomiRs, with 826 distinct 5’ isomiRs identified in SARS-CoV-2 infected lung cells. Among them, 54 showed significant differential expression, particularly at 24 h post-infection, indicating a time-dependent perturbation of the isomiRome by SARS-CoV-2 infection. Notably, 5’isomiRs demonstrated preferential induction compared to anomiRs, suggesting that these 5’isomiRs may play specialized roles in COVID-19 pathogenesis.

A particularly intriguing finding was the identification and functional characterization of miR-4485–3p|+ 1, a 5’ isomiR with a one-nucleotide downstream shift in 5’ end of miR-4485–3p, resulting in an altered seed region. Like other 5’ isomiRs, a single-nucleotide shift redirected its target repertoire, enabling repression of a distinct set of genes [22], [25], [28], [29], [48], [49]. Transcriptome profiling following miR-4485–3p|+ 1 overexpression revealed significant downregulation of genes involved in three fundamental cellular processes: cell cycle regulation, cytoplasmic translation, and carbohydrate derivative metabolism. These processes are known to be commonly exploited by SARS-CoV-2 for optimal replication and immune evasion, consistent with prior evidence indicating SARS-CoV-2 interference with host cell cycle progression (e.g., G1/S and G2/M arrest) [4], [5], suppression of host translation via NSP1 and other fact [3], [7] and rewiring of metabolic pathways to support SARS-CoV-2 replication [8], [9], [10].

Importantly, we found that the genes repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 largely overlapped with those downregulated at 24 h post-infection but not at 4 h, temporally aligning with the induction time of 5’isomiR during SARS-CoV-2 infection. This temporal specificity indicates that miR-4485–3p|+ 1 serves as an infection-induced regulator rather than a bystander. Furthermore, a substantial proportion (60.5 %) of the hub genes repressed by miR-4485–3p|+ 1 within the three key cellular pathways have been previously implicated in COVID-19-related pathophysiology, providing external validation for the biological relevance of this regulatory axis. Our findings collectively suggest that miR-4485–3p|+ 1 may function as a mediator of SARS-CoV-2-induced transcriptomic remodeling. The capacity of 5’isomiRs to establish new targetomes via seed region diversification underscores their biological versatility and highlights the necessity to move beyond canonical miRNA annotations in SARS-CoV-2 studies. It is plausible that such 5’ isomiR-mediated regulation serves as a viral exploitation strategy to subvert host homeostasis, which warrants further mechanistic investigations.

This study also raises several open questions. Drosha, Dicer, and their partner proteins, are key components involved in the biogenesis of anomiRs, and their activity directly impacts the production and diversity of isomiRs [26], [50], [51]. Whether the induction of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 is a direct consequence of viral components, such as SARS-CoV-2’s structural or non-structural proteins mediated modulation of Dicer/Drosha processing, remains to be elucidated. Additionally, given the evidence of tissue- and disease-specific 5’ isomiR expression [21], it will be important to examine whether similar 5’isomiR dynamics occur in other SARS-CoV-2 target tissues or in clinical patient samples. The causal inference between miR-4485–3p|+ 1 and SARS-Cov-2 infection phenotype also requires further experimental validation. Finally, the therapeutic potential of selectively modulating 5’isomiRs, particularly those like miR-4485–3p|+ 1 with strong biological pathway-specific effects, deserves exploration in the context of antiviral intervention strategies.

Conclusion

In summary, our study uncovers a previously unrecognized layer of post-transcriptional regulation by 5’isomiRs after of SARS-CoV-2 infection. The functional impact of miR-4485–3p|+ 1 on host cell cycle, cytoplasmic translation, and metabolic pathways highlights the biological significance of 5’isomiR-mediated gene silencing during SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis and supports their consideration in future COVID-19 mechanistic and therapeutic research.

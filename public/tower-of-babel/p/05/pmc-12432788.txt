Analysis of clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of appendiceal neuroendocrine tumors: a retrospective cohort study based on the SEER database
Authors: Xia Ren, Ganhong Wang, Jian Chen, Luojie Liu, Dan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAppendiceal neuroendocrine tumors (ANETs) are rare, and there is a pressing clinical need for population-level data on their clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis to guide clinical decision-making. This study aimed to investigate the clinicopathological features and prognostic outcomes of patients with ANETs using the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database.MethodsWe included patients diagnosed with ANETs from 2000 to 2020. Participant inclusion was based on confirmed ANET diagnosis in the SEER database. Clinical factors, including tumor grade, stage, surgical status, and patient demographics, were assessed. Patients were followed up through the SEER database to record survival outcomes. The Kaplan-Meier method and Cox proportional hazards models were employed to evaluate overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS).ResultsA total of 4,870 patients were enrolled, with females accounting for 61.3%. The median age was 40 years. Most patients presented with well-differentiated tumors (89.4%) and T1 stage disease (86.0%). Lymph node (4.6%) and distant (1.0%) metastases were rare. Surgical intervention was performed on 99.2% of patients. The 5-year OS and CSS rates were 92.8% and 97.2%. Multivariate Cox analysis revealed age [hazard ratio (HR) =10.05, P<0.001], female sex (compared to male, HR =0.70, P=0.004), and M stage (HR =2.35, P=0.01) as predictors of OS, while age (HR =10.12, P<0.001), poorly differentiated grade (HR =4.64, P=0.008), undifferentiated grade (HR =8.99, P=0.02), female sex (compared to male, HR =0.57, P=0.005), T4 stage (HR =3.24, P=0.01), and M stage (HR =5.44, P<0.001) were associated with CSS. After propensity score matching (PSM), males still exhibited significantly worse OS and CSS than females (P=0.003), and elderly patients had poorer OS and CSS compared to younger patients (P<0.001). However, among elderly patients, no significant differences in OS or CSS were observed between partial colectomy (PC) and subtotal colectomy (SC) groups (P=0.36 and P=0.07, respectively).ConclusionsANETs patients generally present with early-stage disease and favorable prognosis. However, the males and the elders tend to have worse prognosis than their female and younger counterparts. These findings suggest that closer monitoring and potentially more aggressive treatment strategies may be warranted for these high-risk groups. For elderly patients, PC may be a more favorable surgical option, balancing efficacy and morbidity.Keywords: Appendiceal neuroendocrine tumors (ANETs); clinicopathological characteristics; prognosis; Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER)

Introduction

Appendiceal neuroendocrine tumors (ANETs) are a rare tumor subtype, with an annual incidence rate of 0.15–0.6 cases per 100,000 individuals (1,2). Originating from neuroendocrine cells within the appendix, ANETs can arise in any segment, with 60–75% located at the tip, 5–20% in the middle, and <10% at the base (3,4). These tumors typically exhibit slow growth and are often incidentally detected during appendectomy (5,6). ANETs are classified into three grades (G1, G2, G3) based on mitotic count and Ki-67 positivity. G1 and G2 tumors, collectively termed neuroendocrine tumors (NETs), have low malignancy potential, whereas G3 tumors, classified as neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC), demonstrate higher malignancy (7,8). Early-stage ANETs may be asymptomatic. Advanced disease can present with right lower quadrant abdominal pain, diarrhea, and fever. Liver metastasis may lead to carcinoid syndrome symptoms, including facial flushing, diarrhea, sweating, weight loss, and hypoglycemia (9-11). Appendectomy and right hemicolectomy are the most commonly performed surgical procedures for ANETs (12-14).

Existing studies on ANETs are constrained by small sample sizes and heterogeneous methodologies. For example, Twito et al. (3) analyzed 57 ANETs cases from 8,327 appendectomies but lacked long-term follow-up data. Another study scrutinized seven ANETs detected during pregnancy, revealing diverse pathological traits but favorable pregnancy outcomes and excellent long-term prognosis (4). Similarly, a Chinese study of 440 gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (GEP-NENs) included only eight ANETs cases (15), while Estrozi et al. (16) reported 56 grade 1 ANETs, limiting generalizability to higher-grade tumors. A Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER)-based study of 89 appendiceal carcinoids identified size-dependent lymph node metastasis but lacked statistical power to analyze prognostic factors like sex, age, or surgical approach (17). Previous research has suggested that female sex is associated with better prognosis in NETs (18), but ANET-specific data are conflicting. Empirical evidence indicates that advanced age is an adverse prognostic factor for neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) (19,20), yet the specific impact of age on ANET prognosis remains underexplored. To address these limitations and deepen understanding of ANETs, we conducted a population-scale analysis using SEER database data. Our aim was to clarify the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of ANETs patients to inform clinical management. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-239/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

This study was a retrospective cohort study that utilized data from the SEER database, a well-established population-based cancer registry in the United States. We focused on individuals diagnosed with ANETs between 2000 and 2020. Patient identification was performed using the International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, 3rd edition (ICD-O-3), with the specific code 8240/3 representing carcinoid tumor, not otherwise specified (NOS). Patients with unknown pathological diagnoses, missing survival data, or undefined tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stages were excluded from the analysis to ensure data quality and reliability. We examined multiple variables, including marital status (grouped into married and unmarried, with the latter comprising divorced, separated, single, or widowed individuals), age (stratified into 20-year intervals: <20, 20–39, 40–59, 60–79, and >79 years, and further divided into a younger cohort (<60 years) and an older cohort (≥60 years) for comparison), race (classified as White, Black, or other races including American Indian/Alaska Native and Asian/Pacific Islander origins), sex, tumor grade, TNM stage, tumor size, surgical treatment status [with surgical codes defined in Table S1, e.g., code 30 for partial colectomy (PC) and code 40 for subtotal colectomy (SC)], overall survival (OS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), and the length of follow-up. Figure S1 illustrates the age distribution and the number of cases, revealing a surge in cases aged 20 to 39 years, followed by a notable decline after age 60 years. The objective of the study was to assess the clinicopathological attributes and prognosis of ANETs, and to contrast the disparities in clinicopathological traits and prognosis across different genders, age brackets, and surgical intervention categories. The primary outcome measures, OS and CSS, were calculated from the date of ANETs diagnosis until the earliest occurrence of death (either cancer-related or non-cancer-related) or the end of the follow-up period recorded in the SEER database. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were presented through frequencies and percentages, and differences between groups were assessed using Chi-squared tests. For continuous variables that were not normally distributed, the median and interquartile range (IQR) were reported, and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparisons. To strengthen the statistical reliability of the study, especially in handling missing data in the SEER database, a hybrid method that combines multiple imputation (MI) with the random forest algorithm was adopted. To reduce confounding and selection bias, we applied 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) with a caliper width of 0.01. For male-female comparison, we matched on marital status, age, race, tumor grade, TNM stage, tumor size, and surgical intervention. For age-group analysis, we considered marital status, race, tumor grade, gender, TNM stage, tumor size, and surgical treatment. In elderly patients, we matched on marital status, race, tumor grade, gender, TNM stage, and tumor size for surgical-technique comparison. Survival analysis for OS and CSS was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival curves were compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression was utilized for both univariate and multivariate analyses to determine hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables with a P value below 0.05 in the univariate analysis were incorporated into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. All statistical computations were performed using R software (version 4.1.0), with statistical significance set at a two-sided P value of less than 0.05.

Results

Patients’ characteristics

This study enrolled 4,870 individuals diagnosed with ANETs, with a median age of 40 years. The majority of participants (highest percentage) were aged 20–39 years. Males accounted for 38.7% of the study population. A substantial proportion (89.4%) had well-differentiated tumors, and 86.0% were classified as T1 stage. Lymph node involvement was observed in only 225 patients (4.6%), and distant metastasis in 50 patients (1.0%). Surgical intervention was performed in 99.2% of cases. The tumor size distribution was as follows: 3,471 patients (71.3%) had tumors ≤1.0 cm, 906 (18.6%) had tumors 1.1–2.0 cm, and 493 (10.1%) had tumors >2.0 cm. Survival analysis of patients with ANETs

The 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS rates for patients with ANETs were 97.1%, 94.9%, 92.8%, and 87.3%, respectively, while the corresponding CSS rates were 98.7%, 98.0%, 97.2%, and 95.1%. For male patients with ANETs, the OS rates at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years were 96.5%, 94.3%, 91.0%, and 84.7%, with CSS rates of 98.3%, 97.4%, 96.1%, and 93.9% respectively. Female patients with ANETs had OS rates of 97.5%, 95.3%, 93.9%, and 88.9% at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, with corresponding CSS rates of 99.0%, 98.3%, 97.9%, and 95.7%. Among younger patients with ANETs, the OS rates were 99.1%, 98.2%, 97.6%, and 94.8% at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, with CSS rates of 99.8%, 99.4%, 99.1%, and 98.0%. Conversely, older patients with ANETs showed OS rates of 90.1%, 83.2%, 75.7%, and 61.1% at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, with CSS rates of 94.8%, 92.8%, 90.2%, and 83.6% (Table 2). Univariate and multivariate Cox regressions

In the initial univariate Cox regression analysis conducted before PSM, several variables—age, grade, sex, T stage, N stage, M stage, surgical intervention, and tumor size—were identified as risk factors influencing the OS of patients diagnosed with ANETs. However, upon conducting multivariate Cox regression analysis, only age, sex, and M stage were determined to be independent predictors specifically affecting gastrinoma OS (Table 3). Similarly, the univariate Cox regression analysis conducted before PSM also revealed that these same factors—age, grade, sex, T stage, N stage, M stage, surgical treatment, and tumor size—were associated with CSS in ANETs patients. But when these factors were further analyzed using multivariate Cox regression, age, grade, sex, T stage, and M stage were pinpointed as the independent risk factors for ANETs CSS (Table 4).

Comparison of clinicopathological features and prognosis between different genders

Before PSM, male ANETs patients displayed smaller tumor sizes compared to female patients (P<0.001). Following PSM, no statistically significant disparities were observed in the clinicopathological features between the two groups. Table 5 provides a thorough overview of the gender-based clinicopathological characteristics of ANETs patients. Prior to PSM, male patients experienced poorer OS (HR =1.43; 95% CI: 1.12–1.81; P=0.004) (Figure 2A) and CSS (HR =1.77; 95% CI: 1.20–2.62; P=0.004) (Figure 2B) than female patients. Following PSM, male patients continued to show worse OS (HR =1.51; 95% CI: 1.15–1.99; P=0.003) (Figure 2C) and CSS (HR =2.02; 95% CI: 1.27–3.21; P=0.003) (Figure 2D) compared to female patients.

Comparison of clinicopathological features and prognosis between different age

Prior to PSM, older ANETs patients, when compared to younger patients, exhibited a notably higher rate of marriage, advanced T stages, increased occurrences of lymph node and distant metastases, larger tumor sizes, but a reduced likelihood of receiving surgical treatment. Post-PSM, the baseline characteristics between the two age groups were equilibrated. Table 6 presents a detailed summary of the clinicopathological attributes of ANETs patients across different age groups. Before PSM, older patients experienced inferior OS (HR =11.40; 95% CI: 8.32–15.63; P<0.001) (Figure 3A) and CSS (HR =11.48; 95% CI: 6.86–19.22; P<0.001) (Figure 3B) relative to younger patients. Even after PSM, older patients continued to have poorer OS (HR =15.36; 95% CI: 8.54–27.63; P<0.001) (Figure 3C) and CSS (HR =13.59; 95% CI: 5.46–33.84; P<0.001) (Figure 3D) compared to their younger counterparts.

Comparison of clinicopathological features and prognosis among different surgical procedures for elderly patients

Table S1 indicates that PC and SC are the surgical methods most frequently employed for ANETs patients. Considering that elderly ANETs patients have a less favorable prognosis compared to younger patients, we conducted a comparison of the clinical features and prognosis between elderly ANETs patients who received PC and those who received SC, with the objective of offering a reference for clinical decision-making regarding surgical selection for elderly patients. Our findings revealed that, prior to PSM, the PC group exhibited higher grades, earlier T stages, a reduced rate of lymph node metastasis, and smaller tumor sizes (Table 7). The OS (HR =1.53; 95% CI: 1.11–2.12; P=0.01) (Figure 4A) and CSS (HR =2.83; 95% CI: 1.67–4.81; P<0.001) (Figure 4B) were worse in the SC group compared to the PC group. Nevertheless, following PSM, the OS (HR =1.14; 95% CI: 0.73–1.76; P=0.57) (Figure 4C) and CSS (HR =1.64; 95% CI: 0.76–3.54; P=0.27) (Figure 4D) in the SC group were similar to those in the PC group. Additionally, subgroup analyses examining marital status, race, sex, tumor grade, TNM stage, and tumor size also demonstrated comparable OS and CSS between the PC and SC groups (Figures 5,6).

Discussion

In our study, we examined the clinicopathological features and prognosis of ANETs using the SEER database. The analysis included 4,870 ANETs patients. We applied MI to handle missing data and PSM to reduce selection bias. Most patients had early-stage, well-differentiated tumors, and the majority received surgical treatment. We evaluated OS and CSS using the Kaplan-Meier method and Cox proportional hazards models. The 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS rates were 97.1%, 94.9%, 92.8%, and 87.3%, respectively, with corresponding CSS rates of 98.7%, 98.0%, 97.2%, and 95.1%. Multivariate Cox analysis identified age, sex, and M stage as significant predictors of OS, while age, grade, sex, T stage, and M stage were associated with CSS. Males and older patients had poorer OS and CSS compared to females and younger patients. However, among elderly patients, no significant differences in OS or CSS were observed between those undergoing PC and SC.

Our study found that ANETs tend to affect younger patients, with a median age at onset of 40 years and peak incidence in the 20–39 years age group. This aligns with a single-center retrospective study in Turkey, which analyzed 4,026 ANETs patients and reported a mean age of 33 years, with most cases aged 19–49 years (21). In another study, Süleyman et al. (22) retrospectively reviewed 8,304 appendectomy patients and identified 33 ANETs cases, with a mean age of 35.5 years. Şenel et al. (23) similarly reported 29 ANETs cases among 13,863 appendectomy patients, with a mean age of 37 years. Although another study reported a slightly higher mean age of 48.1 years, its sample size was limited to 11 ANETs cases from 7,277 appendectomy patients, potentially affecting age determination precision (24). The higher incidence of ANETs in younger patients may be due to incidental discovery during appendectomy, which is commonly performed for appendicitis—a condition disproportionately affecting younger individuals. Additionally, NETs have a genetic predisposition; relatives of affected individuals may face an elevated risk. This genetic component may involve gene mutations more prevalent in younger patients, contributing to earlier ANETs onset (25).

Although our research indicates that ANETs patients generally have a favorable prognosis, elderly individuals exhibit relatively poorer outcomes compared to younger patients. Our data show 5- and 10-year OS rates of 75.7% and 61.1%, respectively, for older ANETs patients. In line with our findings, Li et al. (26) compared the prognosis of elderly versus younger patients with pancreatic NETs and observed significantly worse outcomes in the elderly cohort. This is attributed to reduced physiological capacity, impaired immune response, higher comorbidity rates, and decreased treatment tolerance and efficacy in elderly patients. These factors may accelerate disease progression and reduce treatment efficacy in elderly ANETs patients, leading to a less favorable prognosis. Nevertheless, surgical intervention remains critical for ANETs treatment, regardless of age (12,13). Surgical options include appendectomy or right hemicolectomy (14). Previous studies suggest that tumor size influences surgical approach, with appendectomy associated with lower recurrence and metastasis risks for ANETs ≤2.0 cm, while more extensive surgery is required for larger tumors (9,27). However, the optimal tumor size cutoff remains debated. Our investigation also confirms that PC and SC are the most common surgical interventions for ANETs (Table S1). While tumor size is a key determinant of surgical strategy, we hypothesize that patient age may also influence the choice. To guide clinical decision-making for elderly ANETs patients, we compared OS and CSS between elderly patients undergoing PC and SC after PSM, accounting for tumor size. Our findings indicate no significant differences in OS or CSS between the PC and SC groups for elderly patients. Thus, we propose that appendectomy may be preferable for elderly ANETs patients, as it offers a comparable prognosis to right hemicolectomy but with a smaller surgical scope, reduced trauma, and faster postoperative recovery. Similarly, Chen et al. (28) demonstrated that for rectal NETs 1.0–2.0 cm in size, less invasive local excision yields long-term outcomes comparable to radical resection.

Gender is a critical biological factor in the development of various cancer types (29). Recent studies have identified gender differences in the incidence, clinical features, treatment response, and prognosis of NETs (30-34). Our study is the first to compare clinicopathological characteristics and outcomes of ANETs by gender. We found that ANETs occur more frequently in females than in males, consistent with prior research (22,35). However, a few smaller studies have reported a higher male prevalence (21,23,24). Except for tumor size (where male ANETs were smaller), we observed no statistically significant gender differences in marital status, age distribution, race, tumor grade, or TNM staging (Table 5). Importantly, even after adjusting for baseline characteristics using PSM, male patients still had poorer OS and CSS compared to female patients. We hypothesize that the better prognosis in female ANETs patients may stem from gender-specific hormonal differences. In support of this, Arnason et al. (36) found that gastrointestinal NETs with high progesterone receptor expression had a more favorable clinical outcome and lower disease stage. Similarly, another study reported higher estrogen receptor β expression in pancreatic NETs with positive prognostic features (37). We speculate that the improved prognosis in female ANETs patients may be linked to increased estrogen and progesterone receptor expression, but further research is needed to confirm this.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. Firstly, its retrospective design, relying on administrative data from the SEER database, inherently introduces selection bias and restricts our ability to control for potential confounders such as comorbidities and tumor markers. Secondly, the presence of missing data, with 12.1% of tumor grades and 4.3% of tumor sizes not available, might have influenced the subgroup analyses. Thirdly, the generalizability of our findings is limited to the SEER population, which predominantly consists of North American, thus potentially restricting their applicability to more diverse global cohorts. Fourthly, while PSM was employed to minimize selection bias in surgical subgroup comparisons, it could not fully account for variations in surgeon expertise or institutional protocols. Additionally, the lack of molecular data, including Ki-67 indices, genetic mutations, or biomarkers, prevented us from exploring the molecular drivers of prognosis. Lastly, the median follow-up time was not specified, leaving longer-term survival and recurrence patterns uncertain. Notwithstanding these constraints, to the best of our knowledge, our research represents the most extensive and thorough examination of the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of ANETs patients conducted thus far. Additionally, this research is the first to compare gender disparities, age differences, and distinctions among various surgical interventions in ANETs patients, offering invaluable insights particularly for the management of older patients. Future studies with more detailed clinical and molecular data are required to validate and build upon our findings.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrates that ANETs patients typically present with early-stage disease and have a favorable prognosis. However, male and elderly patients experience worse outcomes compared to females and younger individuals. Among elderly patients, PC may be a preferable surgical approach. These findings highlight the need for individualized management strategies and further research into the underlying mechanisms influencing prognosis in ANETs.

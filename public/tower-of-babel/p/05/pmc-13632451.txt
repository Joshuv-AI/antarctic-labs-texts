Molecular mechanisms of simvastatin response in Mesocestoides corti: a proteomic approach to drug repurposing for larval cestodiases
Authors: Marina Monteiro Guedes, Jeferson Camargo de Lima, Jéssica Andrade Paes, Leo Kei Iwai, Karina Mariante Monteiro, Arnaldo Zaha, Henrique Bunselmeyer Ferreira
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Larval cestodiases remain difficult to treat, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic targets. The mevalonate pathway, essential for isoprenoid biosynthesis and cellular homeostasis, represents a promising target in cestodes. Previous studies demonstrated that simvastatin impairs the viability and motility of Mesocestoides corti tetrathyridia in vitro. Here, we investigated the proteomic response of M. corti larvae to simvastatin exposure (60.29 and 100 µM) using DIA-based proteomics. A total of 3,378 proteins were identified, with 19 showing differential abundance across treatments. Proteomic and Gene Ontology enrichment analyses revealed dose-dependent alterations associated mainly with structural organization, metabolism, intracellular transport, and stress-related processes. Although no clear proteomic signature of cell death was detected, simvastatin induced adaptive responses consistent with compensatory remodeling in response to pharmacological stress. These findings support the biological relevance of the mevalonate pathway in cestode survival and reinforce HMG-CoA reductase as a potential target for drug repurposing strategies against larval cestodiases.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13071-026-07568-x.

Cestode larvae (metacestodes) are the etiological agents of severe zoonotic diseases, including cysticercosis, cystic echinococcosis (CE), and alveolar echinococcosis (AE) [1]. These infections are classified as neglected tropical diseases due to their global burden and limited therapeutic options [2]. Current treatment relies mainly on benzimidazoles and praziquantel, which show suboptimal efficacy against established infections [3–5], highlighting the need for new drug targets. Drug repurposing represents a promising strategy to accelerate therapeutic discovery [6]. The mevalonate pathway, in which HMG-CoA reductase plays a central role, is essential for isoprenoid biosynthesis and lipid-associated processes in helminths, despite their inability to synthesize cholesterol de novo [7–9]. Statins, a class of drugs that selectively inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, are clinically used to treat hyperlipidemia and are also under investigation for other therapeutic applications [10, 11]. We have previously shown that statins, particularly simvastatin, exert a potent in vitro effect against the larval stage (tetrathyridia, TTs) of the model cestode Mesocestoides corti, significantly reducing parasite viability and motility [12]. However, the molecular basis underlying these effects remains largely unknown. Therefore, the aim of the present study was to perform a comparative proteomic analysis of simvastatin-treated (60.29 and 100 µM) and untreated M. corti TTs to identify differentially expressed proteins and gain insight into the potential molecular pathways and biological processes associated with its antiparasitic activity.

Mesocestoides corti larvae (200 μL of TTs in 3 mL of medium) were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium at 37 °C under a 5% CO₂ atmosphere and exposed to simvastatin (60.29 µM, at the previously determined in vitro IC₅₀ [12] or 100 µM) for 72 h, using DMSO as a vehicle control. Four biological replicates per condition were used. After treatment, total protein extracts were prepared as previously described [13]. Mass spectrometry analyses were performed using an Orbitrap Exploris 480 instrument. Raw data were processed via DIA-NN [14] against the M. corti proteome (WormBase ParaSite [15]). Proteins identified with at least two unique peptides in at least two replicates were retained. Protein intensity values (MaxLFQ) were logarithmically transformed and normalized by Z-score. Consistency between biological replicates was confirmed by strong Pearson correlation coefficients (> 0.85). Box plots of the normalized distributions were used to assess data homogeneity. To assess the overall variability of the sample, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed on the normalized dataset and imputed by the median. Proteins with differential abundance were assessed using ANOVA followed by unpaired t-tests adjusted for FDR (FDR < 0.05; |log₂FC|> 1). Proteomic analyses were conducted in Python (v3.11). Functional enrichment was performed using g:Profiler [16].

Data normalization and principal component analysis (PCA) indicated overall consistency among replicates and a moderate dose-dependent proteomic response to simvastatin, with greater divergence observed at 100 µM (Supplementary Fig. 1). Most identified proteins (3,378) were shared across all conditions, whereas only 19 displayed differential abundance between groups (Fig. 1A, B; Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). Differentially abundant proteins were broadly associated with structural organization, metabolism, intracellular transport, and stress-related processes. Differential abundance analysis identified a limited set of proteins modulated by simvastatin treatment (Fig. 2A; Supplementary Tables 3 and 4). These proteins were mainly associated with structural organization, intracellular transport, metabolic adaptation, and stress-related processes. Proteomic alterations were more pronounced at 100 µM, suggesting a dose-dependent response to mevalonate pathway inhibition. No evident enrichment of proteins directly associated with cell death pathways was observed. GO enrichment analysis identified dose-dependent functional alterations following simvastatin treatment (Fig. 2B; Supplementary Table 4). In summary, although simvastatin at concentrations of 60.29 µM and 100 µM did not elicit a clear proteomic signature of cell death in M. corti, it triggered a dose-dependent response involving proteins associated with homeostasis, metabolism, and stress-related processes. These findings are consistent with the considerable physiological adaptability described for cestodes in response to environmental and host-derived challenges [17]. Importantly, the therapeutic relevance of a molecular target does not necessarily depend on the induction of cell death but may also derive from disrupting pathways required for parasite fitness and survival. In this regard, the modulation of compensatory pathways observed following simvastatin exposure supports the biological relevance of the mevalonate pathway in M. corti. Furthermore, pharmacological inhibition of this pathway with simvastatin (50–150 µM) has previously been shown to reduce motility and viability of TT larvae in vitro [12], indicating that mevalonate pathway inhibition imposes a measurable biological cost on the parasite. Together, these findings support HMG-CoA reductase as a potential therapeutic target for larval cestodiases. Future studies should investigate strategies to enhance pathway inhibition, including optimized statin analogs, combination approaches, and in vivo assessments to determine the translational impact of mevalonate pathway inhibition on larval cestodiases.

Association of alkaline phosphatase to platelet ratio index with ICU mortality in patients with cardiac arrest
Authors: Yong Li, Zebin Lin, Yifeng Chen, Xinlong Di, Fujian Zhou, Yu Xia, Peng Zhang, Jianmin Tang, Yuhao Liu, Xiaohu Wang, Yiqiang Yuan, Yipin Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesThe present study aimed to investigate the association between the alkaline phosphatase-to-platelet ratio index (APPRI) and intensive care unit (ICU) mortality in patients with cardiac arrest (CA), and further explore its potential value for risk stratification in this specific population.MethodsA total of 374 CA patients were included in our analysis. They were divided into the survivor group (n = 180) and non-survivor group (n = 194) based on ICU survival status. Baseline characteristics of the two groups were summarized and compared. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to explore the association between the alkaline APPRI and ICU mortality. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to determine APPRI’s optimal cutoff value for predicting ICU mortality. The Kaplan-Meier method was applied to estimate survival curves of different groups, with the log-rank test used to compare survival differences.ResultsThe non-survivor group exhibited higher APPRI values than the survivor group (0.430 [0.296, 0.733] vs.0.358 [0.246, 0.533)], P < 0.001). Univariate logistic regression analysis showed that APPRI (odds ratio (OR) = 1.077, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.013–1.146, P = 0.018) was associated with ICU mortality. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that APPRI was one of the independent predictors of ICU mortality (OR = 1.110, 95% CI: 1.022–1.205, P = 0.013). The ROC curve determined the APPRI optimal cut-off value for ICU mortality to be 0.576. The Kaplan-Meier curves showed that patients with APPRI > 0.576 had a higher ICU mortality than that of patients with APPRI ≤ 0.576 (log-rank test P = 0.017).ConclusionsAPPRI is independently associated with ICU mortality in patients with CA, and the index effectively stratifies CA patients at risk of ICU deatKeywords: Cardiac arrest, Biomarker, Alkaline phosphatase, Platelet, Alkaline phosphatase-to-platelet ratio index

Introduction

Cardiac arrest (CA) is a common critical illness worldwide, affecting approximately 700,000 individuals each year in the United States [1]. Whereas in-hospital cardiac arrest (IHCA) occurs in an estimated 290,000 patients annually in the United States, with an annual incidence rate of 1.5 to 2.8 cases per 1000 inpatients in Europe [2, 3]. Despite continued improvements in early recognition, community response, and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) techniques in recent years, it is estimated that only approximately 10% of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) and 30% of IHCA survive [4–6]. In addition, survivors were at high risk for unfavorable neurological outcomes, with a mean of 19% (range 3–47%) of survivors at hospital discharge having more than moderate neurological impairment [1, 7, 8]. This condition imposes healthcare burdens on families and caregivers [5, 9]. Therefore, early prognostic assessment at intensive care unit (ICU) admission, especially before targeted temperature management (TTM), is clinically crucial for cardiac arrest patients. As a key neuroprotective intervention, TTM provides differential benefits across risk groups; thus, early risk stratification prior to TTM helps identify high-risk patients, optimize TTM use, guide personalized post-resuscitation care, and enhance decision-making accuracy. It also supports rational resource allocation and effective family counseling.

Systemic ischemia following CA and ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury after return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) collectively result in post-cardiac arrest syndrome (PCAS), a clinical entity characterized by multiorgan dysfunction [10–12]. Accumulating evidence has confirmed that severe ischemia/hypoxia involving the liver and other vital organs is closely associated with adverse outcomes in CA patients [13–15]. Notably, liver-derived indices such as gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase to platelet ratio have been linked to mortality and neurological prognosis after CA [16]. While another liver enzyme, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), has been shown to rise significantly in cancer patients who have experienced CA compared to cancer patients without CA [17]. The alkaline phosphatase-to-platelet ratio index (APPRI) is a novel liver-related inflammatory biomarker with established prognostic value in several liver diseases [18–20]. APPRI seems to be related to inflammation and liver function, but it has not been studied in patients with CA.

Based on the above research gaps and the clinical demand for effective indicators to assess the clinical outcomes of CA patients, the present retrospective observational cohort study aimed to investigate the association between APPRI and ICU mortality in patients with cardiac arrest. We also intended to explore the value of APPRI in stratifying the risk of ICU death in this specific population. This study is expected to provide preliminary clinical evidence for the application of APPRI in the clinical outcome assessment of CA patients, and further enrich the relevant indicators for the clinical evaluation of this high-risk population.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This retrospective observational cohort study was conducted in the Department of Intensive Care at Erasme Hospital, Brussels, Belgium, a tertiary academic medical center with standardized resuscitation and post-resuscitation protocols. The study was approved by the local ethics committee (Comité d’Ethique Hospitalo-Facultaire Erasme-ULB, approval No. P2017/264), which waived the requirement for informed consent due to the retrospective design. The study included adult comatose patients (Glasgow Coma Scale < 9) admitted to the ICU following in-hospital or out-of-hospital cardiac arrest between January 2007 and December 2015. This study represents a secondary analysis of a previously published cohort [21]. In brief, comatose patients were managed with TTM at 32–34 °C for 24 h, including deep sedation, shivering control, and passive rewarming at a rate < 0.5 °C/h. Advanced hemodynamic monitoring and echocardiography were used. Mean arterial pressure was maintained above 65–70 mmHg using fluids and vasoactive agents as needed. Mechanical ventilation was adjusted to achieve normocapnia and SpO₂ >94%.

Patients were excluded if liver function data were missing or if death occurred within 24 hours of ICU admission.

All relevant data are deposited in the Dryad Digital Repository, with the access link: 10.5061/dryad.qv6fp83.

Study outcomes

Study outcomes

For the purposes of this study, ICU mortality was defined as all-cause death that occurred during the patient’s ICU stay [21].

Data collection

The following basic characteristics of each enrolled patient were collected: demographic data, cardiac arrest characteristics, previous comorbidities [21]. The first blood sample for liver function and hematological tests (including ALP and platelet count) was collected upon ICU admission, prior to the initiation of TTM. Laboratory testing was performed in accordance with local institutional guidelines [21]. Mechanical ventilation, intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP), continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) applied during ICU stay was also recorded. The vasoactive drugs applied and total epinephrine dose used, among others, were also recorded [21]. For each participant, the APPRI was calculated by means of the formula: (ALP / platelet count) × mm³.

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to determine whether continuous variables were normally distributed. Continuous variables were characterized as median (interquartile range [IQR]), and comparisons between the two groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and proportions, and component comparisons were performed using Pearson’s χ 2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Spearman correlation was performed to evaluate the correlation between APPRI and other liver function parameters and inflammatory markers. We use restricted cubic spline analysis to explore the relationship between APPRI and ICU mortality, taking the median of APPRI as the reference point and placing three nodes at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify independent risk factors for ICU mortality. Variables with P < 0.10 in the univariate Cox regression analysis were further included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, and Stepwise regression based on likelihood ratio was used to avoid linear correlation problems. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to reflect the ability of APPRI to predict ICU mortality. The area under the curve (AUC) and cut-off values were calculated. Patients were grouped according to the cut-off value for APPRI, and the risk of ICU mortality in the two groups was presented by Kaplan-Meier survival curve and compared by log-rank test. SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 28 (IBM SPSS, Armonk, NY, USA), MedCalc for Windows, version 20.0 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium) and R Programming Language version 4.1.3 (R Core Team, 2014) were used for the analysis. All statistical tests were two-sided, and the inspection level was set at 0.05.

Results

Characteristics of patients

Among the 374 patients, ranged in age of 62 [51, 74] years, including 270 males and 104 females. There were 194 patients (51.9%) died in the ICU. Patients were then classified into the survivor group and non-survivor group based on whether they died within the ICU or not. In the survivor group, patients experienced more Bystander-witnessed CA and Bystander CPR than those in the non-survivor group(P < 0.05). While patients in the non-survivor group had more non-cardiac cause CA (P < 0.05). The age, acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II) score and sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score of the non-survivor group patients were higher compared to those in the survivor group (P < 0.05). Regarding the laboratory findings, non-survivor group patients had higher lactate, AST, LDH, ALP, GGT, INR, highest INR and glucose than the survivor group (P < 0.05), while the survivor group only had PT and MAP higher than the non-survivor group patients (P < 0.05). For drug and device therapy, more survivor group patients were treated with paracetamol(P < 0.05). While patients in the non-survivor group had a higher proportion of application of corticosteroids, vasopressor, mechanical ventilation (P < 0.05). During the ICU stay, significantly more non-survivor group patients developed shock than the survivor group (P < 0.05). For APPRI, it was significantly higher for patients in the non-survivor group than for patients in the survivor group (P < 0.05), as shown in Table 1 (Fig. 1).

Association between APPRI and ICU mortality

In order to more accurately explain the relationship between APPRI and ICU mortality of CA patients, we first used restricted cubic spline analysis for the Cox model, with the median of APPRI (0.384) as the reference point. As shown in Fig. 2, there is a positive linear relationship (nonlinear P = 0.1341) between APPRI and the risk of ICU mortality in CA patients. Therefore, we included APPRI as a continuous variable in the subsequent analyses.

RCS curve for the correlation between APPRI and the risk of ICU mortality in CA patients

As shown in Table 3, variables with P ≤ 0.10 in univariate analysis were included in the univariate Cox regression analysis. Variables with P ≤ 0.10 in the univariate Cox regression analysis were considered potential confounders and further included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, and stepwise regression based on likelihood ratio test P-value of at least 0.05 was used to avoid linear correlation problems. Finally, APPRI still has significant predictive value for ICU mortality (HR: 1.110, 95% CI: 1.022–1.205, P = 0.013) (Table 3).

Results of ROC curve analysis

The discriminatory ability of APPRI for ICU mortality was evaluated using ROC curve analysis (Fig. 3). The optimal cut-off value of APPRI for ICU mortality was determined to be 0.576, based on the Youden index. At this threshold, the sensitivity and specificity were 38.66% and 79.44%, respectively. As shown in Fig. 4, the area under the curve (AUC) for APPRI was 0.612 (95% CI: 0.560–0.661), which was higher than that of ALP alone (AUC = 0.603, 95% CI: 0.551–0.653) and platelet count alone (AUC = 0.533, 95% CI: 0.481–0.585). Pairwise comparison of ROC curves revealed that APPRI had significantly better discriminatory ability than platelet count alone (difference in AUC = 0.078, 95% CI: 0.032–0.13, P < 0.001). However, no significant difference was observed between APPRI and ALP (difference in AUC = 0.009, 95% CI: − 0.043 to 0.061, P = 0.742), nor between ALP and platelet count (difference in AUC = 0.070, 95% CI: − 0.016 to 0.156, P = 0.112). These findings suggest that APPRI offers improved discrimination compared to platelet count alone, but does not significantly outperform ALP in differentiating patients with different ICU mortality outcomes.

Kaplan–Meier analysis for ICU mortality according to different APPRI levels

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis

We used the receiver operating characteristic curve to determine the optimal cut-off value of APPRI to predict ICU mortality to be 0.576. Then, we divided the patients into two groups according to the cut-off value of APPRI: APPRI > 0.576 group and APPRI ≤ 0.576 group. The survival curves of the two groups were drawn by Kaplan Meier method. As shown in Fig. 4, the ICU mortality of patients with APPRI > 0.576 is higher than that of patients with APPRI ≤ 0.576, and the difference is statistically significant( χ2 = 5.739; log-rank test P = 0.017, adjusted by Benjamin Hochberg method).

Discussion

In this study, we found that APPRI was associated with ICU mortality after CA, and APPRI was an independent factor correlated with ICU mortality in this population. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the correlation of this novel index APPRI with clinical outcomes in patients with CA, and also the first to analyze its association in cardiovascular diseases.

Existing studies provide limited guidance in providing guidelines for predicting the outcome of CA. The European Recovery Commission and the European Society for Intensive Care Medicine recommend a combination of various predictors that could be used, including some serum biomarkers [22]. In recent years, multiple studies have shown that multiple indicators of liver function are associated with the prognosis of CA patients. Novel clinical marker derived from indexes of liver function, such as gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase to platelet ratio was reported to be associated with outcome after CA [16]. This has prompted the investigation of liver-related markers in the prognosis of CA to be paid more attention. In fact, among CA patients, there is a considerable probability of the occurrence of liver injury. Roedl et al. reported that 21% of the 1068 patients with in-hospital and out-of-hospital cardiac arrest developed hypoxic liver injury due to hypoxia, and the incidence of hypoxic liver injury did not differ between patients with IHCA and OHCA [15].

ALP is a group of isozymes that catalyse the hydrolysis of organophosphate esters present in the extracellular space [23]. Most of the ALP in serum (over 80%) is released from the liver and bone [24]. In hypoxic hepatitis (HH), ALP increases parallel with other liver enzymes but is not typical [25]. In this study, many of the patients had ALP levels within the normal range, as was the case with concurrent HH. ALP is considered a biomarker associated with cardiovascular disease, and levels of this enzyme in serum and other body fluids may reflect physiological or pathological changes beyond the hepatic source [26, 27]. Previous studies have found that high ALP levels are independent predictors of prognosis in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction undergoing interventional therapy [28]. There was an association between ALP levels and the risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality [29, 30]. In our results, high ALP levels were correlated with elevated APPRI levels, which in turn were associated with higher ICU mortality in CA patients and poor clinical outcomes. It is speculated that APPRI may be linked to the pathophysiological processes following CA onset and thus correlate with the clinical outcomes of these patients.

Platelet counts decrease significantly early after cardiac arrest, and post-CA syndrome is associated with a decrease in platelet count rather than with a change in platelet function [31–33]. In our study, a high APPRI corresponded to a low platelet count and poor prognosis. Systemic ischemia-reperfusion injury following recovery of spontaneous circulation leads to systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) with or without disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) [34, 35]. Decreased platelet counts are frequently observed in patients with DIC, with massive activation-induced platelet depletion occurring as a result of platelet storage pool depletion [36]. Endothelial damage and activation had been reported at 72 h after CA and were associated with a high baseline level of the systemic inflammatory response [37]. The destruction of endothelial integrity promotes platelet adhesion and aggregation, also leading to high platelet consumption and decreased platelet count [38]. Most importantly, severe thrombocytopenia occurs with ALF and even more so in patients with concomitant SIRS. In the present study, about half of the patients were in comorbid ALF [39]. Therefore, thrombocytopenia in patients with cardiac arrest may be partially due to acute liver injury caused by ischemia and hypoxia. Meanwhile, the results of correlation analysis between APPRI and parameters of reactive liver function showed that APPRI was positively correlated with AST, ALT, LDH, TBIL values. Based on the above findings, we speculated that APPRI might be useful for evaluating the status of liver injury and systemic inflammatory response in patients with cardiac arrest. However, the mechanism of APPRI associated with the prognosis of CA may be multifaceted, and the specific mechanism needs further study.

Several limitations could apply to our current study. In the first place, this work was a secondary analysis for a retrospective study, the sample size was limited, and CPR-related details regarding the quality or duration were not available. Second, due to the data from a single-center, the universality and extrapolation from our research conclusions are insufficient, and further research is needed to confirm them. Third, due to the limitation of its observation nature, the results of the study can only illustrate the statistical relationship between APPRI and clinical results, and we have failed to establish a causal relationship. Finally, despite our best efforts to adjust for many possible confounding factors, there is a specific risk of potential bias due to the limitations of missing raw data. Therefore, further research is needed to verify the conclusions of this study, and caution should be exercised in the application and interpretation of the results and conclusions of this study.

Conclusions

An elevated APPRI level on hospital admission was significantly correlated with ICU mortality in the CA patients. The APPRI level may help to identify high-risk individuals in this specific population.

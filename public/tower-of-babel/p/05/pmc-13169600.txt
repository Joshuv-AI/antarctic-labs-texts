Enhancing sterilization supply management: a pre-post study on the impact of an integrated information system in a multi-campus hospital setting
Authors: Gen Li, Fuhai Xia, Xi Chen, Li Li, Liqin Xu, Hui Zeng, Xuan Mao, Qiang Li, Rui Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe coexistence of a “multi-campus” model and outsourced services presents complex operational challenges. Traditional fragmented information systems for sterilization supply suffer from data silos, fragmented traceability chains, and low coordination efficiency, severely constraining the management quality of sterile items and posing potential risks to patient safety.ObjectiveTo evaluate the application effects of an integrated information system in improving the management quality of sterile items, efficiency of multi-campus coordination, and data accounting accuracy.MethodsA retrospective pre-post study design was employed. Data were collected from October 2023 to September 2024 (pre-upgrade, control group) and from October 2024 to September 2025 (post-upgrade, intervention group). The compared indicators encompassed the accuracy of sterile item reception, the expiry rate of sterile items, the multi-campus transfer qualification rate, the multi-campus transfer response time, the report generation time, and user satisfaction.ResultsCompared to the period when the old system was in use, the new system significantly improved outcomes: the accuracy of sterile item reception increased from 94.2% to 99.9% (p < 0.001); the expiry rate of sterile items was reduced from 0.089% to 0.025% (p < 0.001); the multi-campus transfer qualification rate improved from 86.0% to 100.0% (p < 0.001); the multi-campus transfer response time was shortened from 30.9 min to 5.5 min (p < 0.001); the report generation time was reduced from 44.9 min to 3.2 min (p < 0.001). Furthermore, user satisfaction scores showed significant improvement across multiple dimensions, including operational convenience (4.3 ± 0.6 vs. 2.7 ± 0.6) and information accuracy (4.4 ± 0.5 vs. 2.7 ± 0.6) (all p < 0.001).ConclusionThe integrated sterilization supply information system significantly optimized the management process of sterile items, achieving efficient multi-campus resource coordination and automated data accounting. This study provides a replicable digital transformation model for similar multi-campus hospitals, which is crucial for systematically ensuring patient safety, enhancing healthcare quality, and promoting high-quality hospital development.

Introduction

Within the ongoing evolution and deepening of global healthcare systems, digital transformation has become a core engine for hospitals to enhance their competitiveness and achieve high-quality development [1]. This transformation involves not only technological iteration but also a profound reshaping of medical service models, management processes, and even safety culture. Its core drivers consistently revolve around two perpetual themes: patient safety and operational efficiency. The reprocessing of medical devices and the management of sterile items serve as critical links between clinical operations and patient safety. These processes directly impact hospital infection control levels and medical risk management capabilities. In this context, the level of digitalization in the central sterile supply department (CSSD) is a key indicator of a hospital’s modern management capability [2].

Traditional CSSD information systems were primarily designed for single-campus, internalized operational models, focusing on recording basic processes like instrument distribution and decontamination. While providing a degree of initial informatization, these systems face unprecedented challenges in the complex operational model combining “multi-campus” (a predominant strategy in China referring to a single hospital institution operating multiple geographically dispersed campuses under unified administration) and outsourced services [3]. This model exposes significant shortcomings in traditional, isolated system architectures.

Key challenges include: (1) Data silos and broken traceability: Isolated systems across campuses and with external centers prevent the establishment of a complete traceability chain, thereby posing risks to patient safety. This failure directly contravenes the principle underscored by the ANSI/AAMI ST79 standard that “traceability is the cornerstone of quality assurance“ [4–5]; (2) Inefficient coordination and slow response: the multi-campus transfer response time is increased by the lack of global inventory visibility and reliance on manual communication, potentially delaying surgeries [6]; (3) Passive quality control: dependence on manual checks for expiry dates and lack of intelligent alerts increase the risk of using expired items. (4) High management costs and poor decision support: manual data collection and reporting are time-consuming and prone to error, hindering cost-effective analysis and lean management.

Although previous studies have addressed CSSD informatization, they often focus on single-campus systems [7] or specific technologies like radio frequency identification [8], lacking comprehensive evaluation within the complex “multi-campus + outsourcing” context using rigorous pre-post designs. This study aims to fill this gap by quantitatively assessing the impact of a novel integrated information system in a large tertiary hospital operating under this model, comparing key indicators before and after system implementation.

Materials and methods

Study setting and context

This study was conducted at a tertiary Grade A general hospital operating under a multi-campus model. The institution comprises three campuses with a total of 3,700 beds, performs over 30,000 surgeries annually, and uses approximately 40,000 instrument packs per year. Since 2012, part of the cleaning, disinfection, and sterilization of reusable instruments has been outsourced to a regional medical sterilization supply center (Steri Guard Medical Management Group Limited Company, China). In October 2024, the hospital upgraded its CSSD information system to an integrated information system. This retrospective study collected data from the pre-upgrade period (October 2023 to September 2024), designated as the control group, and the post-upgrade period (October 2024 to September 2025), designated as the intervention group, to compare system performance.

Study systems

Pre-upgrade system (control group)

The system in use prior to the intervention had limited functionality. This legacy system was primarily utilized for basic instrument distribution management within the operating room. Processes for the collection, return, receipt, and verification of instruments from other clinical departments relied on manual records. The system was deployed independently in each hospital campus, lacking data interoperability across campuses and with the off-site center.

Although it featured barcode registration capability, this only covered the distribution and receipt stages, resulting in an incomplete traceability chain that could not achieve full-process tracking. Furthermore, the system relied solely on manual checks for expiry date management, lacking automated alert functionality. For cost accounting, due to the absence of a dedicated module, data required manual aggregation and statistics. The handling of multi-campus allocations and urgent tasks was entirely dependent on manual communication, leading to a long information transmission chain and low response efficiency.

Post-upgrade system (intervention group)

The intervention group utilized an integrated information system that encompassed all three hospital campuses and the off-site regional medical sterilization supply center. This system enabled centralized, full-process digital management, establishing a closed-loop traceability system linking “personnel-device-item-patient.”

The integrated information system is an integrated information management platform specifically designed for healthcare facilities, with the primary objective of enhancing the management efficiency and accuracy of sterile instrument trays. The software employs a Browser/Server (B/S) architecture, achieving separation between the front-end and back-end. The front-end, based on the Vue framework, is primarily responsible for data presentation, receiving user input, and sending requests to the back-end for processing. The back-end, built on the .NET framework, handles business logic, data storage and retrieval, and communicates with the front-end via APIs, ensuring efficient data processing and stable backend support.

The main functionalities included: (1) Full-Process traceability: during the collection and sending of used instruments from clinical departments, staff scan and input instrument information. The system automatically generates a unique traceability code, allowing real-time monitoring of the instrument’s status throughout the entire cycle—including cleaning, packaging, sterilization, distribution, and return—enabling comprehensive track-and-trace management. (2) Priority processing: clinical departments can assign processing priorities within the system based on surgical schedules. The system automatically synchronizes these instructions with the off-site center, which then schedules processing and logistics dispatch according to the task priority, reducing reliance on manual notifications. (3) Multi-campus loan and allocation management: the system supports the online initiation and approval of loan requests for instrument trays. Once a borrowing department submits a request, the owning department approves it within the system. The system then automatically generates a delivery task, which the off-site center executes according to the instructions, with the entire allocation process being digitally recorded. (4) Inventory synergy and resource sharing: The system provides real-time visibility of instrument inventory quantities and status across all hospital campuses and the off-site center. This enables visual management and dynamic dispatch of resources, facilitating coordinated inventory management across the multi-campus network. (5) Intelligent expiry alert: The system automatically calculates the expiration date based on the packaging material and sterilization date. It issues proactive alerts before the set time, supporting active management of sterile items to prevent the use of expired products. (6) Report generation time: the system automatically collects data on instrument sterilization, transportation, and usage by department. It generates cost statistical reports according to set cycles, providing data support for subsequent performance analysis and cost management. (7) Mobile terminal integration: the system supports barcode scanning via handheld terminals and mobile operations. It can interface with the Hospital Information System (HIS), the anesthesia system, and the information platform of the off-site center to achieve data exchange.

Evaluation indicators

Accuracy of sterile item reception

The percentage of sterile packs for which the count received by clinical departments agreed with the CSSD dispatch records.

Expiry rate of sterile items

Percentage of expired sterile packs among total received.

Multi-campus transfer qualification rate

Percentage of instrument packs successfully transferred and delivered to the target campus within the preset time window.

Multi-campus transfer response time

Average time from receiving a transfer instruction to task confirmation, item verification, stock-out registration, and dispatch instruction generation.

Report generation time

Average time required to generate monthly cost data reports.

User satisfaction

Assessed via a self-administered questionnaire covering five dimensions (convenience, stability, accuracy, support, overall satisfaction) using a 5-point Likert scale. The user satisfaction questionnaire was an internally developed instrument. The pre- and post-intervention surveys were completed by independent groups of users. 120 questionnaires were distributed with 116 valid responses (96.7% response rate). The content validity of the internally developed user satisfaction questionnaire was established through expert review to ensure its appropriateness and relevance in assessing user satisfaction.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0. Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using independent samples t-test. Count data were presented as frequencies and percentages and compared using Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Management quality and efficiency

Following the system upgrade, significant improvements were observed in all measured indicators (Table 1). The accuracy of sterile item reception increased to 99.9% from 94.2% ( p < 0.001), indicating fewer errors. Expiry rate of sterile items decreased ( p < 0.001), reflecting a shift to proactive, system-driven expiry management. The multi-campus transfer qualification rate reached 100% (vs. 86.0%, p < 0.001), and the multi-campus transfer response time dropped dramatically to 5.5 min from 30.9 min ( p < 0.001), demonstrating enhanced coordination efficiency and supply chain resilience. Report generation time was drastically reduced to 3.2 min from 44.9 min ( p < 0.001), enabling data-driven decision-making.

User satisfaction

Satisfaction scores were significantly higher in the intervention group across all dimensions (all p < 0.001), particularly for operational convenience (4.3 ± 0.6 vs. 2.7 ± 0.6) and information accuracy (4.4 ± 0.5 vs. 2.7 ± 0.6) (Table 2). Improved convenience suggested reduced complexity and learning costs, allowing staff to focus on core tasks. Enhanced information accuracy fostered trust and facilitated inter-departmental collaboration. Discussion

This study provides robust evidence that the implementation of an integrated information system significantly improves sterilization supply management within a challenging multi-campus and outsourcing model. Improvements were observed across quality management, operational coordination, data utility, and user acceptance.

The upgrade to the integrated information system resulted in a marked enhancement in the accuracy of sterile item reception and a significant reduction in the expiry rate of sterile items. This achievement transcends a mere technological upgrade, representing a systematic optimization of the hospital’s sterile supply quality management system. The closed-loop traceability chain, a core functionality of the new system, operationalized the principle of traceability outlined in the ANSI/AAMI ST79 standard, thereby establishing a robust foundation for quality assurance [5]. It also responds to the initiatives from organizations like the World Health Organization (WHO) to enhance medical process tracking for ensuring patient safety [9]. Existing research indicates that achieving full-process digital tracking from clinical use to final sterilization can significantly reduce instrument-related errors and enhance the capability for root cause analysis of adverse events [10]. By automating data collection, the system shifts the paradigm from a passive management mode reliant on manual records to a proactive quality control mode based on real-time data. This not only improves operational precision but also signifies that the hospital’s quality management methodology is converging with international best practices [11] .

The dramatic improvements in transfer efficiency (100% qualification rate, sharply reduced response time) validate the application of supply chain management principles. By creating a centralized, visible “resource pool,” the system enhances operational resilience [12–13], allowing agile responses to clinical demands and improving supply chain flexibility.

The drastic reduction in report generation time transforms cost accounting from a manual, lagging task into an automated, strategic asset. This supports lean management practices and data-driven decision-making for cost control and performance evaluation, consistent with trends in healthcare management [14] .

Significantly improved user satisfaction, particularly in perceived ease of use and usefulness, is crucial for long-term adoption and sustainability, as explained by technology acceptance models [15–16]. High user acceptance acts as a catalyst for embedding technological advantages into lasting workflow and cultural changes.

This study moves beyond isolated technological improvements and presents an integrated digital transformation paradigm for complex healthcare systems. It provides a validated, replicable model for large hospital groups undergoing consolidation and facing similar challenges.

This study has several limitations. First, its single-center, retrospective pre-post design may affect the generalizability of the findings. The results require validation through future multi-center studies across hospitals with different scales, operational models (e.g., outsourcing partnerships), and baseline levels of IT infrastructure maturity. Second, although the observed improvements correlate with the system implementation, this design cannot entirely isolate the effect from potential confounders such as concurrent workflow adjustments or natural staff proficiency gains. Third, the use of an internally developed questionnaire, while serving as an exploratory tool, introduces a limitation regarding the instrument’s psychometric properties. Finally, long-term outcomes, such as impact on infection rates and a detailed cost-benefit analysis, were not assessed. Future research should employ validated instruments and include prospective, multi-center, long-term evaluations.

Discussion

Conclusion

The integrated sterilization supply information system significantly optimized the management process of sterile items, achieving efficient multi-campus resource coordination and automated data accounting. This study provides a replicable digital transformation model for similar multi-campus hospitals, which is crucial for systematically ensuring patient safety, enhancing healthcare quality, and promoting high-quality hospital development.

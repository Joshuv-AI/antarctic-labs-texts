The relationship between feeding modes and quality of life among patients with post-stroke dysphagia: an observational study
Authors: Jing Wang, Shuai Jiang, Yafen Cheng, Yujie Zhai, Xi Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTube feeding is common in patients with post-stroke dysphagia. However, its influence on quality of life remains not fully clear. This study aimed to explore the relationship between feeding modes and quality of life in ischaemic stroke survivors with pharyngeal dysphagia.MethodsThis multicentre observational study included ischaemic stroke survivors with pharyngeal dysphagia in the rehabilitation departments between Feb. 2022 and Jun. 2024. The Dysphagia Handicap Index was used to assess health-related quality of life at baseline and on Day 10. Potential confounding variables were collected using questionnaires, medical records, and self-reports. Propensity score matching was performed to enhance statistical comparability between Intermittent Oro-Oesophageal and Nasogastric tube users. Generalized estimating equations were used to explore time, between-group, and interaction effects (time*group) on the Dysphagia Handicap Index outcomes.ResultsThere were 1344 and 1090 participants using intermittent oro-oesophageal and nasogastric tubes, respectively. At baseline, the median and quartiles of the Dysphagia Handicap Index total score were [53.00 (47.00, 59.00)], which significantly declined to [39.00 (35.00, 45.00)] on day 10. 781 pairs were matched with no significant differences in all covariates. In the matched samples, time and interaction effects were significant in both the Dysphagia Handicap Index total scores and the three dimensions. The participants using intermittent oro-oesophageal tubes improved significantly more in overall, functional and emotional quality of life.ConclusionThe quality of life improved as rehabilitation therapy progressed among ischaemic stroke survivors with dysphagia. Compared to nasogastric feeding, intermittent oro-oesophageal feeding was expected to have advantages in enhancing overall, functional and emotional quality of life.Keywords: Reported outcomes measure, swallowing disorders, enteral nutrition, rehabilitation

Introduction

Ischaemic stroke is the most common clinical subtype of cerebrovascular accident [1]. It has been reported that ischaemic stroke causes lower mortality but greater loss of Quality of Life (QoL) than haemorrhagic stroke [2]. Stroke typically leads to varying degrees and categories of dysfunction [3]. When stroke occurs in the brainstem areas that control swallowing, such as the medulla and pons, it can directly damage the swallowing centre and result in dysphagia [4]. Lesions affecting the motor or sensory areas of the cerebral cortex may disrupt swallowing coordination. Additionally, post-stroke impairments in strength, sensation, consciousness, and balance are related to swallowing problems [5]. A previous study has shown that over 50% of stroke survivors suffer from dysphagia [5]. Post-stroke dysphagia increases the risk of aspiration, reflux, dehydration, pneumonia, and malnutrition [6]. Psychologically, it is associated with anxiety, depression, and sleep quality [7]. Patients with post-stroke dysphagia generally reported a lower QoL than those without swallowing difficulties [8].

For both safety and nutritional concerns, enteral nutrition support is clinically practiced for these patients [9]. In Western countries, it mainly refers to gastrostomy. However, in East Asia, gastrostomy is not widely accepted, and most stroke survivors with dysphagia receive tube feeding during the acute and rehabilitation phases [10]. Nasogastric tube feeding (NG) is the most common choice. Although NG can alleviate malnutrition and reduce the risks of aspiration and dehydration, its long-term tube placement may cause irritation and discomfort in the pharynx or nasal passages [11]. Prolonged nasogastric feeding may lead to some complications, such as nasal ulceration, oesophageal reflux, or aspiration, which could further exacerbate discomfort and health risks [11,12]. Additionally, NG users almost have no sensation while feeding, which deprives them of the experience of eating. The tube occupies one nostril and may affect breathing [13]. NG impacts the user’s appearance, which could influence social participation. Lastly, long-term NG use can lead to limited mobility, further affecting daily activities [14]. These factors are evidently related to QoL.

Another developed enteral nutrition mode is Intermittent Oro-Oesophageal tube feeding (IOE) [15]. Its concept was first proposed in 1988 and is currently mainly used in South Korea, Japan, and China [15,16]. It is considered an alternative to NG or gastrostomy and involves tube placement only during feeding periods [15]. The tube intermittently enters the upper part of the oesophagus through the mouth, which reduces the impact on the pharynx and does not affect the lower oesophageal sphincter function [11,17]. During feeding, the slight expansion of the tube wall can be felt by patients. Clinically, this has been observed to trigger the swallowing reflex and is considered to retain part of the eating experience. Since IOE does not require continuous tube placement, it reduces the impact on daily activities and social participation, and is considered to better preserve the users’ self-esteem [13]. Based on these perceptive, physical or functional impacts, it has been hypothesized that IOE may exhibit some advantages over NG in improving QoL across dimensions.

Although prior studies have compared the effects of NG versus IOE in nutritional or functional outcomes [15], little is known about their influence on specific dimensions of QoL. Most existing literature focuses on general QoL scores, overlooking the fact that QoL is a multidimensional construct encompassing physical, psychological, social, and functional well-being [18]. Additionally, it is required to ensure the homogeneity of interventions in randomized controlled trials. This reduces bias but may decrease the representativeness of the study. Retrospective designs generally lack standardized data collection and may encounter issues such as information bias and selection bias [18]. This constitutes a critical research gap, as understanding how feeding methods influence dimensional QoL could offer more targeted insights for clinical decision-making and personalized care. To address this gap, this large-scale prospective multicentre study was conducted to investigate the association between NG versus IOE and multiple dimensions of health-related QoL in ischaemic stroke survivors with dysphagia.

Methods

Study designs

This multicentre prospective observational study was conducted at rehabilitation departments in China Feb. 2022 to Jun. 2024, aimed to investigate the relationship between feeding modes (IOE/NG) and three dimensions of QoL (overall, functional, emotional, physical) in ischaemic stroke survivors with dysphagia. We matched sociodemographic characteristics, health conditions, and treatment details to reduce bias. Time, between-group, and interaction effects were analysed to explore how feeding modes were associated with quality of life. This study has obtained ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (2022-KY-0094-003). The study was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki and all participants signed informed consent.

Sample and participants

The participants were ischaemic stroke survivors with dysphagia. Patients were included if they met the following criteria: (a) over 18 years old. (b) ischaemic stroke survivors based on imaging data. (c) pharyngeal dysphagia, with the Penetration-Aspiration Scale (PAS) grades over 2 [19]. (d) first-time stroke. (e) stable vital signs. (f) tube feeding is required and feasible. (g) admitted or transferred to the rehabilitation departments within two weeks of onset. (h) able to cooperate in completing the survey. (i) hospital stays over 10 days and complete medical records.

Patients were excluded if they met the following criteria: (a) tracheostomy tube placement. (b) organ failure, haematologic disorders, severe psychiatric disorders, or other neurological diseases. (c) Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) <24 [20]. (d) pregnant females. The dropout criteria were as follows: (a) Patient condition severely deteriorated. (b) recurrent stroke. (c) changes in nutrition modes.

The recruitment of participants was carried out upon admission to the rehabilitation departments. Patients could voluntarily withdraw from the study and would be excluded from the final analysis. Upon admission, the researchers briefly introduced the study to potential participants. After obtaining consent for enrolment, the participants, their families, and the attending physician communicated and decided whether to use IOE or NG. IOE involved oral insertion, and the tube reached the upper oesophagus. The insertion was conducted before each feeding, and the tube was immediately removed after feeding (Online Appendix 1). NG involved trans-nasal tube placement into the stomach. The tube was secured to the user’s face after insertion and was replaced every five to seven days. All participants had their feeding tubes (if present) removed on the night of admission and received the new feeding on Day 1. Based on this, we classified the participants into IOE or NG groups. Both IOE and NG were conducted as per relevant guidelines [10,21]. The nutritional standards were independent of feeding modes. The feeding content and amount were personalized by independent clinical nutritionists. According to relevant guidelines [22], the daily caloric intake was between 25 and 35 kcal/kg, and protein intake was between 0.8–1.2 g/kg. The actual feeding process was carried out by rehabilitation therapists who had undergone standardized training, rather than by patients or their families, to reduce potential biases. A patient’s actual food intake was influenced by various factors, including mood and appetite. Furthermore, secondary prevention with appropriate medication and health education was provided to all the patients.

A study indicated that the baseline standard deviation (σ) of the DHI in stroke patients with dysphagia was 5.2 [23]. Based on this, sample size estimation was conducted using PASS 2018, with a margin of error (d) of 0.25, a confidence interval of 0.95, and a sample loss rate of 0.2. Data collection

Assessments were conducted either at baseline before any intervention or on day 10 after all interventions. The assessors received standardized training before the study and were unaware of the statistical analysis design. They did not know the patient groups during the baseline assessment. For the self-administered questionnaires, the assessors were allowed to provide explanations for the items.

Outcome variable

The Chinese version of the Dysphagia Handicap Index (DHI) is a patient-reported measure of health-related QoL. It is a well-established and widely used tool to assess the impact of dysphagia on patients’ quality of life, specifically focusing on swallowing-related functional limitations, emotional distress, and social consequences. These aspects were considered to be major concerns for tube-fed patients. This scale has been validated among Chinese inpatients and is frequently used in similar studies to evaluate the effectiveness of interventions on swallowing-related QoL [24]. The total score could range from 0 to 100, where higher scores indicate poorer QoL. The assessment was conducted both at baseline and on Day 10. To reduce potential recall bias, the order of the items was randomized during the first assessment.

Covariates

Functional impairments, sociodemographic characteristics, lifestyle habits, and underlying diseases were considered potential factors that affect QoL and the following variables were included: (a) sex. (b) age. (c) marital status. (d) educational levels. (e) National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [25], to assess neurological deficits. (f) disease course. (g) previous tube feeding (only for this stroke). (h) previous parental nutrition (only for this stroke). (i) lesion location, based on imaging materials. (j) body mass index. (k) paralysis. (l) aphasia. (m) visual impairments. (n) the PAS [26], to assess airway protection and dysphagia. (o) the MMSE [20]. This is a brief 30-point questionnaire used to assess cognitive function, focusing on areas such as memory, attention, language, and spatial awareness. A higher MMSE score indicates better cognitive function. (p) Modified Barthel Index [27]. This is 100-point scale to assess a patient’s ability to perform basic activities of daily living, such as feeding, bathing, dressing, and mobility. The score helps to evaluate the level of independence and a higher score indicate a better condition. (q) Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index [28]. This is a 21-point questionnaire to assesses sleep quality such as sleep duration, latency, disturbances. A higher score indicates worse sleep quality. (r) smoke. (s) alcohol intake. (t) companion. (u) hypertension. (v) hyperlipidaemia. (w) type-II diabetes. (x) pneumonia. (y) Patient Health Questionnaire-9 [29], to assess depression. All covariates were collected at baseline except for companion, which was retrospectively identified. The details of the definitions of each covariate are shown in Online Appendix 2.

Additionally, we collected swallowing-related treatment information for each patient, including [30]: (a) oral hygiene, including daily oral care and use of general antimicrobial agents. (b) behavioural therapy, including swallowing-related muscle training, protective training, and respiratory training. (c) Rood techniques. This is a set of neurophysiological treatment methods developed by physical therapist Margaret Rood, aimed at promoting motor control and recovery. It is based on sensory input to activate motor responses, such as temperature, taste, and texture stimulation. (d) neurostimulation, including magnetic and electrical stimulation. Each type was classified as “Yes” if it was performed at least six times during the study.

Statistical methods

Categorical data were represented using number of cases and percentages [n (%)], and chi-square tests were used for univariable analysis. Grade data and continuous data with skewed distribution were represented using medians and interquartile ranges [M (Q25, Q75)], with Mann-Whitney U tests used for univariable analysis. Continuous data with normal distribution were represented using means and standard deviations (x ± s), and t-tests were used for univariable analysis. All covariates and the three dimensions of the baseline DHI were included in Propensity Score Matching (PSM) to reduce potential bias. The total DHI score was not included because it exhibited evident severe collinearity with the three dimensions, which could affect the propensity score calculation. We matched the samples 1:1 matching without replacement (caliper =0.05), and the propensity score (e) was calculated based on logistic regression. The distance metric was propensity score differences in Euclidean Distance. Univariable analysis and PSM balance tests were used to evaluate the matching quality. The standard was that there were no significant differences in univariable analysis and balance tests, and the absolute value of the standardized bias for each variable was <10%. It was consi dered that differences were non-significant between the IOE and NG groups after matching. For repeated measures, Multiple univariable analyses would increase the Type I error rate. Due to the skewed distribution of the DHI in this study, Generalized Estimating Equations were used to analyse the time (assessment time points, 0/1), between-group (IOE/NG, 0/1), and interaction effects (time*group) of feeding modes on the DHI outcomes (both each dimension and total scores). This method was based on a linear regression model, with the independent variables being the assessment time, feeding mode, and interaction term, and the dependent variable being the DHI. Cases with missing sociodemographic data were handled using multiple imputations, while cases with other missing data were excluded from the final analysis. This was because disease- and intervention-related information was considered relatively high correlated with post-stroke QoL, and missing certain in-hospital information might reduce the reliability of other data. The basis for the assessment of missing data mechanisms and proportions for the missing data of each variable are shown in Online Appendix 2. Linear regression is used to test the robustness of the results. We used STATA version 16.1 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA) for all statistical analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A total of 781 pairs of cases were captured. There were no significant differences between the groups in the matched samples regarding any covariates. The standardized bias was less than 10%. Discussion

Summary

In this study, we investigated the effects of IOE versus NG on dysphagia-related QoL in ischaemic stroke survivors. Our results showed that the functional and emotional aspects of QoL improved more significantly in the IOE users than the NG users. These findings suggested that IOE might be a more favourable feeding option for ischaemic stroke patients, as it appeared to better address their QoL needs. This is particularly important given the high prevalence of dysphagia in stroke patients and its impact on their overall well-being.

Interpretation of principle findings

The study selected a 10-day observation period primarily because Chinese patients typically had a rehabilitation hospitalization duration of 10–14 days under the influence of Chinese Public Social Security System. After discharge, patients’ QoL was subject to more difficult-to-control confounding factors, and follow-up and assessment after discharge can be challenging. QoL can be influenced by various factors, including sociodemographic factors, treatment content, and physical health [31]. Based on literature review and clinical experience, several potential confounding factors were prospectively identified and included in the PSM covariate set. Before and after matching, there were significant differences in the total DHI scores at baseline and day 10. Regardless of the feeding mode, all participants showed significant improvements in general and dimensional QoL. This indicated the importance of rehabilitation treatment in enhancing QoL for stroke survivors with dysphagia. Some previous studies have shown that rehabilitation interventions can improve post-stroke QoL [23,30,32,33], while we extended the available evidence by reporting the dimensional QoL. Functionally, physical and speech therapy alleviated the clinical severity of dysphagia. Improvement in swallowing function not only contributes to better nutritional status, perceptive health, and self-identity, but also can relieve psychological stress [31]. Physiologically, this can increase swallowing safety and reduce the risk of various complications and patient burden [30]. Psychologically, participating in rehabilitation programs can provide psychological support, build confidence, and foster senses of achievement, which in turn positively impacts QoL [34]. Additionally. rehabilitation departments can provide opportunities for stroke survivors to engage with other patients, caregivers, and healthcare professionals. Social interaction is crucial for emotional well-being and can alleviate feelings of isolation [35]. Rehabilitation typically involves a multidisciplinary team, including doctors, nurses, physiotherapists, speech therapists, and psychologists [30]. This holistic approach is considered to addresses the physical, emotional, and psychological needs of patients, leading to comprehensive improvements in QoL.

Generalized estimating equations indicated that the between-group effects and interaction terms were significant in the total scores, functional conditions, and emotional states of the DHI. This indicated that as rehabilitation therapy progressed, IOE showed advantages in improving the functional and emotional aspects of QoL. In the DHI, the functional dimension primarily refers to the impact of dysphagia on eating behaviours and related activities [36]. IOE involves frequent oral tube insertion. During feeding, the walls of the tube slightly expand, and these processes stimulate the pharynx. Clinically, the swallowing movement has been frequently observed in IOE users during feeding and tube insertion. Therefore, IOE was believed to contribute to the recovery of swallowing function. Some studies have indicated that this might be related to the stimulation improving the sensation and muscle function of the pharynx [37], which can be reflected in the improvement of functional QoL. To decrease the risk of reflux, NG users had to eat small and frequent meals [21]. For IOE users, the feeding tube did not reach the stomach, which can reduce the effect of the lower oesophageal sphincter function [37]. This might lead to differences in the scores for 4 F (small meals and frequent eating). For NG users, the tube was persistently secured to their face, which affected their appearance, increased feelings of shame, and impacted their daily activities [14]. This might influence 5 F (social interactions). Long-term nasogastric tube placement was also considered to affect sleep, as it occupied one nostril and affected breathing [38]. For NG users, the tube cannot be completely fixed, and slight body movements can cause friction between the tube wall and the pharynx [11]. Under the influence of gravity, the tube also continuously compresses the cricoid cartilage [12]. This can damage the nasopharyngeal mucosa, lead to the foreign body sensation and pain in the pharynx [11], ultimately affecting QoL. Additionally, a previous study showed that compared to IOE, NG can decrease patient appetite and adherence to feeding [39], potentially affecting 6 F and 7 F (appetite and food intake).

The between-group effects and interaction terms showed larger z-values in the emotional states compared to other dimensions. In the matched sample, the median difference in the emotional dimension was the largest. These results indicated that the predictive relationship between feeding modes and emotional QoL was the greatest among all dimensions of the DHI. Eating has social attributes. The tubes were removed during non-feeding periods for the IOE users. This can alleviate psychological burdens and reduce social escape [14,33]. Therefore, scores for 1E (eating with others) could benefit from this. In contrast, NG continuously stimulated the patient’s nasal and pharyngeal areas, and this effect was exacerbated when lying down due to gravity. Additionally, the placement of NG tubes in the stomach increased the risk of reflux [11,12], which was associated with anxiety and these factors were considered to negatively impact 4E (anxiety) [13]. Long-term NG use might shift users’ self-recognition towards being patients rather than normal individuals, potentially affecting 5E (inferiority) [39,40]. Furthermore, the expansion of IOE tubes during feeding can be perceived and enhance the eating experience, potentially influencing 2E (eating experience) and 6E (anger) [15,37]. Due to improvements in psychological and social aspects, it was hypothesized that IOE users would score higher in 3E (enjoyment) [33].

This study found that feeding modes did not significantly influence QoL in the physical conditions. This dimension primarily includes the safety of eating and functional integrity. While tube feeding was administered, neither IOE nor NG users experienced choking while swallowing (1,2,4,8,9 P), nor did they face issues such as dehydration (3 P), swallowing weakness (6 P), or trouble taking medication (7 P). Enteral nutrition can ensure the nutritional status of patients, thereby reducing their perception of significant weight loss (5 P). It should be noted that although the between-group QoL differences were significant in the matched sample, the median difference in the outcome physical and functional DHI was small, which might not have reached the Minimal Clinically Important Difference. However, there is currently a lack of research to define this minimal value, which needs to be addressed in future studies.

Limitations

There were some limitations. First, we focused on short-term effects, which meant there was a lack of tracking for long-term changes in QoL. Patients typically require more time to adapt to new feeding methods and experience their full impact on QOL. Therefore, the significant differences between the two groups might be related to compliance with and comfort of the feeding methods. Therefore, this study cannot explain whether there would still be significant QoL differences between the two groups after discontinuing tube feeding. Second, due to the rarity of patients using gastrostomy in China, we did not set a gastrostomy group. This may decrease the clinical significance of this study in Western countries. Third, our participants were primarily from central China. Due to cultural and customary influences, the conclusions should be applied cautiously to other regions. Fourth, since this study did not intervene in the choice of tube feeding modes, there might be unmeasured differences that could lead to uncorrected biases. Additionally, we observed substantial differences between the two groups before PSM, which might reflect the natural situation in clinical practice. These differences could potentially introduce bias, as the groups were not initially comparable in terms of several key characteristics. While matching improved the comparability of the groups, we acknowledge that there could still be residual differences between the unmatched cohort and the matched cohort, which may affect the generalisability of our results. Therefore, we emphasize the importance of interpreting the findings with caution, particularly regarding the potential influence of pre-matching differences on the outcomes. Lastly, the observational design affected the inference of causal relationships. Therefore, the results were limited to detecting a predictive relationship. These issues were expected to be addressed in subsequent multi-group long-term interventional studies.

Conclusions

The general and dimensional QoL improves with the rehabilitation process among patients with dysphagia after ischaemic stroke. IOE is more conducive than NG in improving general, functional, and psychological QoL. However, there were no significant between-group effects on physiological QoL.

Comparison of outcomes of robot-assisted vs. open or laparoscopic of radical prostatectomy: a population-based cohort study
Authors: Chih-Chiao Lee, Marcelo Chen, Yu-san Chien, Ching-Hu Chung, Jiun-Yi Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe studied perioperative outcomes, postoperative complications, and survival rates for open radical prostatectomy (ORP), laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) and robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RARP). Taiwan’s entire 2018–2021 National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) was linked with the cancer registration file to conduct a retrospective cohort study. A total of 4883 patients with localized (or locally advanced) prostate cancer (PCa) who underwent ORP, RARP, or LRP from 2018 to 2021 were analyzed. Differences in patient characteristics and perioperative outcomes were assessed before and after inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). RARP accounted for 91.3% of the surgeries, followed by LRP (4.7%) and ORP (4.3%). ORP and LRP were associated with higher blood transfusion rates, longer hospital stays, and ICU admissions compared with RARP. Post-IPTW, RARP exhibited significantly lower rates of perioperative complications and postoperative readmissions compared with ORP and LRP. The postoperative risk profile for RARP was lower compared with those for ORP and LRP.Keywords: Open radical prostatectomy, Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy, Robot-assisted radical prostatectomy, Perioperative outcomes, Postoperative complications

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) ranks among the five most frequently diagnosed cancers worldwide. It is the second most common malignancy in men. In 2022, there were 1.4 million new cases diagnosed globally, with almost 400,000 deaths attributed to this disease [1]. The incidence of PCa increases markedly with age, peaking among elderly men, which highlights the need for effective treatments [2, 3]. In Taiwan, PCa accounts for 6.14% of all cancer cases and ranks as one of the primary causes of mortality from cancer in men, thus representing a significant public health problem [4, 5].

PCa treatment is determined by stage, patient health status, and personal preferences. Common approaches include active surveillance, surgical intervention, radiotherapy, hormonal therapy, and chemotherapy, with each offering different therapeutic outcomes [6]. Surgical treatment, most notably radical prostatectomy, involves the complete removal of the prostate gland, which has a high cure rate for men with localized or locally advanced disease, with 10-year survival rates exceeding 90% [7]. Over the past few decades, treatment for localized PCa has undergone substantial transformation. Minimally invasive surgical (MIS) approaches, such as laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) and robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RARP), have replaced open radical prostatectomy (ORP). These advancements have improved clinical outcomes while minimizing morbidity [8–11]. Technological innovations, particularly robotic surgery, have also significantly advanced the capabilities of MIS. RARP offers superior clinical outcomes, with fewer hospital stays, lower blood transfusion rates, and fewer perioperative complications. Consequently, the number of PCa patients receiving robotic surgery for radical prostatectomy has increased globally [11, 12].

The effect of modern surgical methods for PCa on postoperative quality of life is another major benefit. RARP tends to yield greater patient satisfaction, largely because of the enhanced recovery experiences, which include less postoperative discomfort, quicker return to work, and restoration of social activities [4, 13, 14]. As a result, the growing preference for RARP is attributed not only to its clinical efficacy, but also to its potential to enhance overall patient well-being.

Despite the widespread adoption of minimally invasive RP procedures globally, significant gaps remain in understanding these approaches in the Taiwanese population. Recent studies have predominantly been focused on populations from other regions, particularly the United States, which limits the generalizability of the findings to Taiwan. Therefore, we extracted population-based data sets from the Taiwan National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) and the Taiwan Cancer Registry Database (TCRD). This study aimed to provide a thorough assessment and comparison of the clinical performance of different surgical approaches within the framework of Taiwan’s healthcare system.

Materials and methods

Data source and patient definition

This study analyzed complete claims data from the NHIRD (2018–2021), linked with records from the TCRD (2018–2020). The NHIRD, which was established in 1979, covers 97–99% of the population in Taiwan [15]. It contains comprehensive medical claims data, including outpatient and inpatient medical fees, surgical procedures, disease diagnoses, medication prescriptions, and treatments, such as radiotherapy and chemotherapy [4, 16]. The NHIRD collects information on all insured individuals in Taiwan, which totaled 23,375,314 in 2021. Patients listed in the TCRD and with ICD-O3 code C61 (International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, 3rd) were identified as PCa patients. Using TCRD as the diagnostic standard for PCa ensures a stringent and dependable case definition. Surgery type, cancer stage, tumor size (mm), lymph vascular invasion, and smoking status were identified through the link with the TCRD. The study population consisted of individuals with localized or locally advanced PCa who received one of the three surgical procedures (ORP, LRP, or RARP) between Jan 1st, 2018, and Dec. 31st, 2020. The index date corresponded to the date of their initial surgery, and follow-up continued until Dec. 31st, 2021. Individuals with a prior cancer diagnosis or younger than 18 years at the time of surgery were excluded from the study. During the study period, there were 19 accredited medical centers in Taiwan; however, according to the disclosure policy of the Health and Welfare Data Science Center, institution-level case counts cannot be reported. Therefore, we present cohort totals and aggregated results.

Clinical endpoints

The primary independent variable of interest was surgical approach, which was categorized as ORP, LRP, or RARP. Other covariates were age, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), clinical and pathological stages, tumor size, and lymphovascular invasion status. The primary objective was to evaluate the performance of the three surgical approaches based on key endpoints. Perioperative measures included the frequency of blood transfusions, hospital length of stay (LOS), intensive care unit (ICU) admission and duration, and reoperation and readmission rates. Postoperative complication rates included overall and specific complications. Tumor size was obtained from the Taiwan Cancer Registry (TSIZE_C) as the largest diameter (mm) abstracted by certified registrars based on national coding guidelines, and prioritizing postoperative pathology reports.

Research ethics approval

Approval of the research protocol (Protocol No. 22MMHIS360e) was issued by the MacKay Memorial Hospital Institutional Review Board, Taiwan, R.O.C., which also indicated that informed consent was not required.

Assessment

Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software (version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., USA). p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant for all two-sided tests. Categorical data are presented as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous data are summarized as means ± standard deviations (SD) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). Patient comorbidities were evaluated using the CCI [17]. Kaplan–Meier survival curves across surgical types were generated and assessed using the log-rank test, with overall survival defined as the interval from surgery to death. To address potential confounding factors and ensure a balance among the three surgical groups, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was used [18]. The IPTW derived from a multinomial propensity score for the surgical approach included the following baseline covariates: age, Charlson Comorbidity Index, cancer stage, body mass index, smoking status, tumor size, lymphovascular invasion, and receipt of neoadjuvant therapy. Baseline characteristics were analyzed using p values to determine the balance of covariate distribution between the surgical groups. A p value > 0.05 indicated adequate balance. Adjusted covariates included age, BMI, clinical and pathological staging, CCI, smoking status, tumor size, and lymphovascular invasion.

Results

Sample description

We analyzed 4483 patients diagnosed with localized and locally advanced PCa in 2018–2020 who underwent one of the three surgical approaches (open, laparoscopic, or robot-assisted). Outcomes were evaluated using NHIRD claims with follow-up data available through 2021 (Fig. 1). In this cohort, 191 patients (4.3%) received ORP, with a mean age of 67.02 years (SD = 6.01), 4,460 patients (91.3%) underwent RARP, with a mean age of 66.98 years (SD = 6.69), and 232 patients (4.7%) received LRP, with a mean age of 67.45 years (SD = 6.57). Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics for the patients across all surgical groups, before IPTW (Table 1A) and after IPTW (Table 1B). The median follow-up times were 892.9 days for ORP, 792.9 days for RARP, and 766.6 days for LRP.

Before IPTW adjustment, the ORP and RARP groups differed significantly in smoking prevalence and pathological stage. Compared with the other group, patients undergoing ORP were more likely to be smokers (35.6% vs. 27.67%; p = 0.0168) and with early stage disease (p = 0.0402).

After IPTW, the baseline characteristics between the groups showed an improved balance, although some imbalances persisted. For example, the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) differed significantly between the RARP (p < 0.0001) and LRP (p = 0.0002) groups for patients with two or zero to one comorbidities. Although cancer staging revealed overall an improvement in balance, discrepancies remained for pTNM stage 4 between ORP and RARP (p = 0.0178) and for missing pTNM data between LRP and RARP (p < 0.0001). Tumor size and lymphovascular invasion exhibited a significant difference between LRP and RARP patients (p = 0.0018; p < 0.0001).

Perioperative outcomes

Table 2 summarizes perioperative outcomes for ORP, RARP, and LRP, before and after IPTW. Before IPTW, marked differences were observed among the groups. Patients undergoing ORP required significantly more blood transfusions (65.97%) compared with RARP (10.13%, p < 0.0001) and LRP (30.60%, p < 0.0001) patients. ORP patients had longer hospital stays (mean: 9.65 days) compared with RARP (7.89 days) and LRP (8.72 days), with a similar trend in ICU admissions and length of ICU stays [ORP: 12.57% (mean: 1.58 days), RARP: 4.57% (mean: 1.53 days), LRP: 18.53% (mean: 1.42 days)].

After IPTW, similar trends persisted. ORP and LRP patients continued to have higher blood transfusion rates compared with RARP (ORP: 66.20% vs. 10.14%, p < 0.0001; LRP: 28.02% vs. 10.13%, p < 0.0001) and longer hospital stays (ORP: 9.60 days vs. RARP: 7.88 days, p < 0.0001; LRP: 8.67 days vs. RARP: 7.89 days, p = 0.0125). The likelihood of ICU admission was markedly greater for both ORP and LRP compared with RARP (ORP: 11.82% vs. 4.58%, p < 0.0001; LRP: 19.06% vs. 4.59%, p < 0.0001).

The post-IPTW results revealed significant differences in reoperation, readmission, and postoperative complication rates among the surgical approaches. Reoperation rates were not statistically significant between OPR and RARP; however, LRP patients had significantly higher reoperation rates compared with RARP patients (0.58% vs. ≤ 0.01%; p < 0.0001). Although not statistically significant, readmission rates were slightly higher in the ORP (0.75%) and LRP (0.75%) groups compared with the RARP group (0.62% and 0.60%).

After IPTW, postoperative complications were more common in ORP patients compared with RARP patients (28.94% vs. 24.42%; p < 0.0001), although the difference was not significant for LRP vs. RARP patients (24.00% vs. 24.45%; p = 0.0591) (Table 3). The hazard ratio (HR) revealed lower risks for RARP patients compared with ORP patients for sepsis or disseminated intravascular coagulation (HR 0.559, 95% CI 0.41–0.77), cardiac events (HR 0.823, 95% CI 0.71–0.95), and acute renal failure (HR 0.198, 95% CI 0.14–0.29). LRP patients exhibited increased risks for pneumonia or flu (HR 0.663, 95% CI 0.54–0.81), peritonitis or peritoneal abscess (HR 0.467, 95% CI 0.25–0.87), ileus (HR 0.513, 95% CI 0.36–0.72), and wound disruption (HR 0.299, 95% CI 0.13–0.69). These results highlight the advantages of RARP in reducing adverse perioperative outcomes. These perioperative advantages raised the question of whether survival outcomes might also differ by surgical approach; however, Kaplan–Meier analysis showed no significant overall survival differences after adjustment.

Overall survival rates

Kaplan–Meier survival curves revealed the overall survival rates for patients undergoing ORP, LRP, or RARP after IPTW (Fig. 2). After IPTW, overall survival did not differ significantly based on surgical approach (ORP vs. RARP: log-rank p = 0.3672; LRP vs. RARP: p = 0.2696). Median follow-up was 892.9 (ORP), 792.9 (RARP), and 766.6 (LRP) days.

Discussion

Drawing on the extensive data sets available from the NHIRD and TCRD, we assessed clinical outcomes associated with the radical prostatectomy (RP) techniques, ORP, LRP, and RARP. The results indicated that RARP provides more favorable clinical results compared with ORP and LRP, thus reinforcing the existing evidence supporting the advantages of minimally invasive approaches in RP.

MIS techniques, particularly LRP and RARP, showed significant improvements in perioperative outcomes compared with ORP. Moretti et al. found that LRP and RARP were associated with significantly lower estimated blood loss (EBL), reduced blood transfusion rates, and shorter hospital stays compared with ORP (EBL: 408 mL vs. 228.2 mL vs. 852.1 mL; transfusion rates: 6.5% vs. 2.8% vs. 19.8%; LOS: 5.7 days vs. 2.9 days vs. 6.1 days) [11]. These trends are consistent with the findings from other studies, which highlight the ability of MIS techniques to improve perioperative outcomes [19–22]. Among the MIS approaches, RARP often demonstrated superior outcomes compared with LRP, particularly for LOS and EBL. Wu et al. found that, compared with LRP, RARP was associated with a significantly shorter length of stay (mean difference: − 0.57 days; p = 0.01) and reduced estimated blood loss [adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 0.58; 95% CI 0.37–0.91] [4]. Moreover, RARP patients experienced less postoperative pain at 12 weeks (aOR 0.40; 95% CI 0.19–0.85; p = 0.02) and had a lower incidence of positive surgical margins compared with those undergoing LRP (aOR 1.27; 95% CI 1.10–1.46) [23]. These results highlight the advantages of RARP in reducing perioperative complications and enhancing recovery. The superior perioperative outcomes of RARP may be attributed to its enhanced surgical precision, which offers superior anatomical visualization and fine control during the procedure. These technical advantages support more efficient recoveries and fewer complications, which emphasize the importance of advanced surgical technology in optimizing patient outcomes.

Evidence from neighboring East Asian countries provides additional context for our results. In Japan, Okegawa et al. reported that robot-assisted radical prostatectomy achieved better biochemical recurrence-free survival than laparoscopic surgery in high-risk patients, suggesting potential oncologic advantages [24]. A prospective Korean cohort study by Jeong et al. further showed that robot-assisted procedures were associated with less blood loss, shorter hospitalization, and improved functional recovery compared with open surgery while maintaining comparable cancer control [25]. Similarly, Ma et al. in China found that patients treated with the robotic approach experienced better urinary continence and sexual function than those undergoing open prostatectomy, without a trade-off in oncologic outcomes [26]. These findings align with our Taiwanese data, where the robotic technique was linked with fewer complications, shorter hospital stays, and favorable functional outcomes compared with both open and laparoscopic approaches. Taken together, regional evidence indicates that the clinical benefits of robotic surgery are broadly consistent across East Asia while emphasizing the value of longer follow-up and system-level considerations when interpreting results.

Wang et al. found that RARP significantly improved overall survival compared with ORP, with hazard ratios (HR) of 0.69 in univariate analysis and 0.76 in multivariate analysis (both p < 0.001) [27]. Similarly, Leitao et al. reported a 5-year all-cause mortality rate of 3.9% for RARP compared with 5.5% for ORP (p < 0.001) [28]. Although previous studies with longer follow-up and different settings indicate survival differences between surgical approaches, our cohort did not exhibit an OS difference within ~ 2 years of median follow-up after weighting. Because of the low event rate, a short observation window for localized disease, and the small ORP/LRP samples compared with RARP, this study was not powered to detect modest OS differences. Therefore, we present the external findings as contextual and refrain from inferring survival benefits from the data. Longer follow-up and disease-specific endpoints are required to determine whether survival diverges in Taiwan.

This study relied on administrative databases, such as the NHIRD and TCRD; however, it contained some inherent limitations, including potential coding inaccuracies, omissions, and incomplete data. Although prostate cancer patients were linked to cancer registry files (ICD-O-3, C61) to enhance the data set, important information, such as the initial staging of prostate cancer, self-paid medication, laboratory data, and patient-specific details (e.g., race or living habits), was unavailable because of the scope and objectives of the NHIRD. In addition, disease severity could not be adequately measured, which further limited the depth of the analysis. Because annual TCRD releases lag behind NHIRD claims, our cohort was restricted to diagnoses during the 2018–2020 period. Nevertheless, outcome ascertainment extended through claims that were available up to December 31, 2021. This limitation constrains a complete evaluation of surgical interventions on recovery quality and long-term outcomes, which underscores the importance of more detailed and up-to-date data for future studies. Future studies should prioritize incorporating detailed clinical data and patient-reported outcomes to provide a more holistic understanding of the benefits and limitations of the various RP techniques. Enhanced data quality and comprehensiveness will provide more robust conclusions and effectively guide clinical decision-making and healthcare policy development.

This retrospective, population-level evaluation from the first year of robotic surgery adoption in Taiwan revealed significant changes in prostate cancer surgical outcomes. Compared with ORP and LRP, RARP provides several benefits in terms of patient safety.

Conclusion

This study underscores the perioperative benefits of RARP compared with ORP and LRP in the Taiwanese patient population. This study underscores RARP’s potential to significantly enhance patient outcomes and optimize healthcare resource utilization, based on shorter hospitalization times, lower complication rates, and the reduced need for blood transfusions. The adoption of RARP in Taiwan has reshaped the clinical landscape for prostate cancer treatment, with RARP demonstrating improved outcomes and safer profiles relative to open and laparoscopic prostatectomy approaches. These results not only contribute to the surgical literature, but also provide important insight that may shape future clinical practices and health policies, which prioritize patient safety and resource efficiency. Future studies should broaden the scope of clinical endpoint assessments, include evaluations of long-term benefits across various surgical techniques and patient groups, and work toward ensuring equitable access to the most effective treatment options for all individuals.

PNA-Pdx: Versatile Peptide Nucleic Acid-Based Detection of Nucleic Acids and SNPs
Authors: Wenjun Jiang, Rashid Aman, Zahir Ali, Gundra S Rao, Magdy Mahfouz
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractMonitoring diseases caused by pathogens or by mutations in DNA sequences requires accurate, rapid, and sensitive tools to detect specific nucleic acid sequences. Here, we describe a new peptide nucleic acid (PNA)-based nucleic acid detection toolkit, termed PNA-powered diagnostics (PNA-Pdx). PNA-Pdx employs PNA probes that bind specifically to a target and are then detected in lateral flow assays. This can precisely detect a specific pathogen or genotype genomic sequence. PNA probes can also be designed to invade double-stranded DNAs (dsDNAs) to produce single-stranded DNAs for precise CRISPR-Cas12b-based detection of genomic SNPs without requiring the protospacer-adjacent motif (PAM), as Cas12b requires PAM sequences only for dsDNA targets. PNA-Pdx identified target nucleic acid sequences at concentrations as low as 2 copies/μL and precisely detected the SARS-CoV-2 genome in clinical samples in 40 min. Furthermore, the specific dsDNA invasion by the PNA coupled with CRISPR-Cas12b precisely detected genomic SNPs without PAM restriction. Overall, PNA-Pdx provides a novel toolkit for nucleic acid and SNP detection as well as highlights the benefits of engineering PNA probes for detecting nucleic acids.

Rapid and accurate detection of specific nucleic acid biomarkers such as pathogens enables the diagnosis of acute and chronic diseases, which is critical for public health and disease control.1,2 Recently, efficient detections of the SARS-CoV-2 viral genome played a vital role in controlling disease transmission and thereby mitigating the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic.3,4 Many techniques have been developed to detect specific nucleic acid sequences.5−7 The PCR-based laboratory tests remain the gold standard method, providing high specificity and sensitivity in detecting nucleic acids but these assays still suffer from a long turnaround time and the need for expensive instruments.8,9 Isothermal amplification methods enable instrument-free nucleic acid detection technologies.10 However, the amplification errors will bring false-negative or -positive results for both PCR-based laboratory tests and isothermal amplification methods. To address these drawbacks, clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeat (CRISPR)-based techniques have been coupled with isothermal amplification strategies such as loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP),11 recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA),12 transcription-based nucleic acid detection,13 and strand displacement amplification methods14,15 for point-of-care (POC) diagnostics. These assays represent great advances in the field of diagnostics and present rapid alternatives to PCR-based laboratory tests. However, the Cas enzyme requires a protospacer-adjacent motif (PAM) sequence on the target;16 this limits the broad use of CRISPR diagnostics.

In addition to pathogens, genomic mutations cause human disease. Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) account for more than 50% of disease-related mutations in humans17,18 and, therefore, discriminating SNPs is another important issue for nucleic acid detection. The gold standard assays for SNP detection include TaqMan SNP genotyping and next-generation sequencing, which require technical skills and facilities with advanced equipment.19−21 Benefiting from the high specificity of CRISPR-Cas systems, Cas enzymes such as Cas12b have been developed for SNP detection.22,23 However, similar to other CRISPR-based techniques, CRISPR-based SNP detection is still restricted by the PAM sequence. To overcome the current challenges for nucleic acid detection and SNP detection, it is critical to develop novel nucleic acid platforms that complement existing detection methods, allowing specific and sensitive detection of different nucleic targets without the requirement of PAM sequence.

Peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) are artificial oligonucleotide mimics in which the sugar-phosphate backbone is replaced by a pseudo-peptide skeleton.24,25 PNAs combine the features of peptides and nucleic acids and they resist the degradation by proteases and nucleases.26,27 Due to their electrostatically neutral backbone, PNAs form exceptionally stable duplexes with DNA or RNA without compromising their specificity. Moreover, compared with natural nucleic acids, the mismatches in the PNA duplex can result in a stronger destabilization, which enables more precise target discrimination.28−30 The high stability and specificity of the PNA probes make them ideal for nucleic acid sensing. Unmodified PNAs have poor cellular permeability and tend to aggregate, so different modifications have been applied to fine-tune the physicochemical properties of PNAs.31 In particular, modifications at the γ-position can enhance PNA hybridization properties.32 mini-PEGs and cell-penetrating peptides have also been introduced at the γ-position to improve the water solubility and cell-penetration properties.33,34

To date, PNAs have been used in broad biomedical applications, especially for nucleic acid sensing. PNA-based PCR clamping techniques have been successfully developed to detect genomic mutations and capture nucleic acids.35 For example, Han et al.36 used a modified PNA-PCR method to screen KRAS mutation which showed resistance to epidermal growth factor receptor target therapy in cancer patients. PNA probes can also be immobilized on the chips for the development of microarray platforms.37,38 Most recently, Najjar et al.6 described the use of PNA capture probes coupled with the Cas12a system for SARS-CoV-2 RNA detection. Although these technologies represent good advances in PNA-based nucleic acid detection platforms, they all relied on facilities that were not available in low-resource settings. Fulfilling the increasing demand for POC nucleic acid detection, the PNA probe should be proposed for novel nucleic acid detection platforms which can adapt to low-resource environments.

In this study, we established a PNA-based versatile toolkit named PNA-Pdx for the portable detection of SARS-CoV-2 and SNP mutation. For SARS-CoV-2 detection, biotinylated peptide nucleic acid (Bio-PNA) probes were coupled with RT-LAMP in lateral flow assays (LFAs) for visual detection. LFAs are paper-based diagnostics tools that have been widely applied to detect antigens,39 antibodies,40 and nucleic targets41,42 because of their cost-effectiveness, ease of use, and rapid format.43,44 By incorporating specific Bio-PNA probes and RT-LAMP reactions with LFAs, PNA-Pdx offers a portable, inexpensive, rapid, and sensitive detection platform for SARS-CoV-2. Considering the critical role of SNPs in the diseases,45 pharmacology,46 and agriculture,47 we also developed PNA-Pdx for SNP detection by incorporating the specific double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) invasion activities of PNA probes with Cas12b-mediated ssDNA PAMless SNP detection. PNAs bind PAMless dsDNA at a specific site, allowing Cas12b mediated ssDNA detection of dsDNA without PAM restriction. PNA-Pdx thus removes the need for expensive equipment and provides specific nucleic acid detection without PAM restriction, enabling POC diagnostics for nucleic acid and SNP genotyping.

Experimental Section

Nucleic Acid Target (RnaseP DNA) Detection via PNA-Pdx

RnaseP DNA (gBlock) was amplified in LAMP reactions using FAM-labeled LAMP primers. The LAMP reactions were executed using the NEB WarmStart LAMP Kit (E1700S) or manually assembled reactions. For the manual assembly of reactions, 1 μL of RnaseP DNA (400 copies/μL) was mixed with 2.5 μL 10X LAMP primers mix (2 μM F3, 2 μM B3, 16 μM FIP, 16 μM BIP, 8 μM LF, 8 μM LB), 1 μL Bst 2.0 WarmStart (0.32 U/μL), 1.5 μL MgSO4 (100 mM), 3.5 μL dNTPs (10 mM), and 2.5 μL 10× isothermal amplification buffer in final 25 μL solutions (ultrapure water added up to the final volume), and then incubated at 65 °C for 30 min. LAMP reactions without RnaseP DNA were treated as the negative control. Then, 10 μL of each LAMP reaction was incubated with 1 μL of PNA-RnaseP (1 μM) and 39 μL dipstick assay buffer (HybriDetect Milenia Biotec) at 65 °C for 2 min. HybriDetect Dipsticks equilibrated to RT were dipped into each tube for the LFA and removed as soon as the appearance of control lines. The results of the LFAs were interpreted in 5 min. Samples with the appearance of both test lines and control lines were considered positive samples, while samples with only control lines were considered negative samples, and samples without control lines were considered invalid samples.

Synthetic SARS-CoV-2 Virus Detection Using PNA-Pdx

To detect synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA (Twist Bioscience), 400 copies/μL of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA were first amplified in 25 μL RT-LAMP reactions. The protocol for RT-LAMP reactions was the same as the last section but with 0.5 μL WarmStart RTx reverse transcriptase [0.3 U/μL] added for reverse transcription. The 10 μL RT-LAMP reaction solutions were then mixed well with 1 μL of 1 μM PNA-SC1 or PNA-SC2 and 39 μL dipstick assay buffer at 65 °C for 2 min. The LFA was executed and interpreted as described in the last section for the detection of the SARS-CoV-2 RNA. To further explore the detection specificity, 1 μL of PNA-SC1 (1 μM) was incubated with 10 μL SARS-CoV-2 LAMP amplicons and 10 μL RnaseP LAMP amplicons, respectively, and then subjected to an LFA.

Optimization of PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 Detection

Multiple experiments were performed to optimize the performance of PNA-Pdx. PNA-Pdx was first performed with different concentrations of PNA-SC1. 400 copies/μL of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 virus were first amplified in 25 μL RT-LAMP reactions, as described in the last section. RT-LAMP reactions without synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA were performed as negative controls. The RT-LAMP reaction solutions were utilized in the following experiments for optimization. The 10 μL RT-LAMP reaction solutions were incubated with 1 μL PNA-SC1 (at a final concentration of 10, 20, 50, or 100 nM, respectively) and 39 μL of the dipstick assay buffer at 65 °C for 2 min. The final solutions were subjected to an LFA for visual observation. Similarly, 10 μL RT-LAMP reaction solutions were incubated with 1 μL PNA-SC1 (final concentration: 10 nM), and 39 μL dipstick assay buffer at RT, 65, 80, or 95 °C, respectively, for 2 min to investigate the optimal incubation temperature of PNA-Pdx. The final solutions were then subjected to LFAs. The dilution ratio between the reaction solutions and detection buffers can significantly influence the LFA results. Therefore, we investigated different dilution folds for LFA. To accomplish this, 1 μL PNA-SC1 (final concentration: 10 nM) was incubated with 2, 5, 10, or 20 μL RT-LAMP reaction solutions, respectively, in the 50 μL solution (various amounts of dipstick assay buffer were used to make up the 50 μL final volume) at 65 °C for 2 min. The final solutions were then subjected to LFAs.

The Limit of Detection Determination and Clinical Validation of PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 Detection

For the limit of detection (LOD) determination of PNA-Pdx, 1 μL synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA at various concentrations (final concentrations: 0, 0.4, 1, 2, 10, 50, or 100 copies/μL) was amplified in the RT-LAMP reactions. For each sample, 10 μL of each RT-LAMP reaction solution was incubated with 1 μL PNA-SC1 (final concentration: 10 nM) and 39 μL dipstick assay buffer at 65 °C for 2 min and then subjected to an LFA. The LOD was determined as the lowest concentration that can be detected using PNA-Pdx. For comparison, we performed the LOD assay for colorimetric LAMP. 1 μL synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA at various concentrations (final concentrations: 0, 0.4, 1, 2, 4, 10, 50, or 100 copies/μL) was amplified in the colorimetric RT-LAMP reactions. To further access the suitability of PNA-Pdx for a POC assay, PNA-Pdx was subjected to clinical validation. The necessary ethical approval (Saudi MOH Approval #: H-02-K-076-0420-285, dated 13.04.2020; KAUST Approval #: 20IBEC14) for the collection of human clinical samples was obtained. RNA was isolated from these samples and subjected to a qPCR analysis (Table S5). The same RNA samples (4 μL) were subjected to PNA-Pdx detection using the optimized conditions.

PAMless SNP Detection Via PNA-Pdx

PNA-Pdx requires Cas12b RNP, Cas12b reporter, and a PNA probe for PAMless SNP detection. AapCas12b was purified by Genscript and stored at −80 °C. For the Cas12b sgRNA, the scaffold sequence appended with the T7 sequence at the 5′ end was ordered as the sense strand. sgRNA spacer sequence was appended at the 5′ end of the reverse primer. The Cas12b sgRNA templates were prepared by amplifying the scaffold oligo with the T7-forward primer and the sgRNA reverse primer and then used as in vitro transcription templates for Cas12b sgRNA production (Transcript Aid T7 High Yield Transcription Kit). The in vitro transcript was purified with a Direct-zol RNA miniprep kit (R2050, Zymo Research) and stored at −80 °C. The SNP detection targets were ordered as gBlocks (Template 1–4, Table S3). The oligos were amplified by PCR and cloned into pJET vectors. The sequences of the pJET vectors were validated using Sanger sequencing. As the targets for SNP detection, the pJET vectors were then transformed into Top10 Escherichia coli for purification of PAMless dsDNA to simulate the genomic targets.

To execute PNA-Pdx for SNP detection, 2 μL of the original or mutated plasmid (2 nM in the final 20 μL solutions) was incubated with 2 μL of the PNA probe (100 nM in the final 20 μL volume; PNA-SC1 for target 1 and target 4, PNA-1 for target 2 and target 3) and 1 μL 10X MOPS buffer (0.2 M MOPS free acid, 0.05 M sodium acetate, 0.01 M disodium EDTA) in 10 μL of step 1 solution (ultrapure water added up to the final volume) at 60 °C for 2 h. Next, 5 μL Cas12b sgRNA (4 μM, Table S4) was mixed with 5 μL Cas12b (4 μM) for Cas12b RNP (2 μM) working stock. The final reactions were prepared by mixing 10 μL step 1 solution, 2 μL Cas12b RNP (final concentration: 200 nM), 3 μL Cas12b HEX reporter (final concentration: 3 μM), 2 μL 10X NEB isothermal buffer, and 3 μL ultrapure water. The final reactions were monitored using a Bio-Rad qPCR machine at 56 °C for 1 h. For a simple visualization, the completed reaction solutions were observed using a P51 molecular fluorescence viewer (miniPCR. P51 Molecular Fluorescence Viewer). The fluorescence signal was recorded using a smartphone camera after 30 min.

Minimal Incubation Time and Substrate of PNA-Assisted PAMless SNP Detection

To determine the optimal temperature for the reaction, the PNA invasions for PNA-Pdx PAMless SNP detection were performed at 37, 60, or 80 °C. To further determine the minimum incubation time for PNA invasions, we performed the PNA-SC1 invasions on the original plasmid at 80 °C for 0, 1, 3, 10, or 60 min, respectively. To further explore the minimal substrate required for PNA-assisted PAMless SNP detection, PNA-Pdx was performed with 50, 100, 300, and 1000 pM original plasmids or mutated plasmids for the detection of an A > G SNP substitution in target 1. The minimal substrate was determined as the lowest target concentration required to clearly discriminate the original and mutated plasmids. The execution of PNA-Pdx for this section is described above.

RPA-Coupled PNA-Assisted PAMless SNP Detection

To test whether PNA-assisted PAMless SNP detection can be coupled with RPA amplification to detect a substrate present in limited amounts, RPA-based preamplification steps were integrated before the PNA-Pdx-based SNP detection. A Twist-Dx kit (Twist Bioscience) was used for the RPA amplification. Briefly, the RPA pellet was resuspended in 29.5 μL of buffer and mixed with 4.8 μL primer sets (5 μM) (Table S3) and 7.7 μL ultrapure water. The solutions were divided equally between two tubes (21 μL in each). Next, 2 μL MgOAC (magnesium acetate) and 2 μL of the plasmids (5 × 105 copies of original plasmid or mutated plasmid) were added to each tube and incubated at 37 °C for 20 min. A 2 μL aliquot of the RPA reaction solution was subjected to SNP detection, as described above.

SNPs Identification in DiverseOryza sativaby PNA-Pdx

To validate PNA-Pdx-based SNP detection for practical applications, we performed PNA-Pdx to discriminate SNP sites in diverse transgenic O. sativa (F575C, K591E, G629R; Table S6) obtained from our previous study.48 Genomic DNA of O. sativa was extracted using DNAsecure Plant Kit (DP320-03, TIANGEN). The region containing SNP sites was preamplificated from genomic DNA using ALS-F forward primer and ALS-R reverse primer (Table S3) and subjected to SNP detection as described above.

Results and Discussion

Design and Construction of PNA-Pdx

PNA-Pdx is a versatile toolkit that includes two independent PNA-assisted diagnostics tools for nucleic acid and SNP detection respectively. PNA-Pdx for nucleic acid detection consists of LAMP for preamplification and a Bio-PNA-based LFA for visual detection. LAMP detection kits such as colorimetric LAMP kits suffer from false positive signals because of their unspecific amplification. To circumvent this problem, we built the LFA-based PNA-Pdx by harnessing the rapid and isothermal properties of the LAMP reaction and the specificity of the PNA probe.

As proof of concept, we used PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 detection (Figure 1A). In the presence of the SARS-CoV-2 RNA, the target is reverse-transcribed and preamplified in RT-LAMP reactions using FAM-labeled primers. The specific biotinylated peptide nucleic acid probe (Bio-PNA) then binds to the FAM-labeled amplicons to form the complex. The reaction solution can then be subjected to LFA for visual detection. The commercially available lateral flow strips for SARS-CoV-2 detection contain (from bottom to top) the sample pads, test lines, control lines, and absorbent pads (Figure 1B). The sample pads contain the gold nanoparticle AuNP–anti-FAM complex which would flow through the dipsticks with a reaction solution powered by the absorbent pad. The streptavidin-coated test line can capture Bio-PNA–FAM-labelled amplicons. Once applied to the LFA strip, the AuNP–anti-FAM complex becomes immobilized at the streptavidin-coated line with the presence of Bio-PNA–FAM-labelled amplicons as the positive signal. Excessive AuNP–anti-FAM accumulates at the control line and serves as a control signal.

PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 and PAMless SNP detection. (A) Scheme of PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 detection. Synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA is first amplified with FAM-labeled primers in LAMP reactions. The Bio-PNA will then precisely recognize the FAM-labeled target amplicons and form a Bio-PNA–FAM-amplicon complex, which can be captured by the streptavidin-coated line and visualized on the LFA strips. (B) Composition of commercial LFA strips. Sample pads contain AuNP−αFAM antibody, which flows through the LFA strips during the assay. The streptavidin line can capture Bio-PNA–FAM-amplicon complexes. The control line was modified by anti-αFAM antibodies to capture the residual AuNP−αFAM antibody. (C) Scheme of PNA-Pdx for SNP detection. The PNA probe invades a PAMless dsDNA target (amplified by RPA) to create an ssDNA window, to which Cas12b can bind and then discriminate SNP mutations without the typical need for a PAM sequence. The trans-cleavage activities of Cas12b are activated by the target to generate a high fluorescence signal, while the trans-cleavage activities of Cas12b decrease or are even eliminated in the presence of SNP mismatches.

In contrast to PNA-Pdx for general nucleic acid detection, here we engineered the PNA probe with a Cas12b system for PAMless SNP detection because the PNA probe alone cannot discriminate SNPs. PNA can still bind and unwind dsDNA targets with SNPs. Cas12b with a truncated sgRNA has been widely applied to detect SNPs, but the dsDNA target typically requires a Cas12b PAM sequence for the Cas12b function.22,23 This limits its target range on genotyping. To further expand the coverage of Cas12b-based SNP detection, we engineered a PNA probe with Cas12b for PAMless SNP detection. The scheme of PNA-Pdx for SNP detection is illustrated in Figure 1C. Without the presence of the PAM sequence on the target, Cas12b cannot cut the dsDNA. The PNA probe specifically binds the antisense strand of the dsDNA target to unwind an ssDNA window, allowing the Cas12b RNP to specifically bind with the sense strand and detect SNPs. SARS-CoV-2 detection-related DNA probes are listed in Table S2, while the PAMless SNP detection-related DNA probes are listed in Table S3. Cas12b sgRNAs for PAMless SNP detection are listed in Table S4.

Detection of RnaseP DNA and Synthetic SARS-CoV-2 Virus Using PNA-Pdx

To test the concept of PNA-Pdx, we first applied PNA-Pdx for the detection of the RnaseP gene. The RnaseP DNA (400 copies/μL) was amplified in LAMP reactions using FAM-labeled primers. The LAMP reactions without the RnaseP DNA were used as the negative control. The reaction solutions were first incubated with the PNA-RnaseP probe and then subjected to an LFA. As shown in Figure 2A, PNA-Pdx can detect the RnaseP as visualized on the test line of the LFA, but no detection signal was observed on the test line in the samples without the target DNA (Figure 2A). Our results thus indicated that PNA-Pdx is capable of specific nucleic acid detection.

We then tested whether PNA-Pdx can detect the SARS-CoV-2 genomic sequence. FAM-labeled LAMP primers for N genes were selected to amplify the synthetic SARS-CoV-2 virus. Two PNA probes for N genes were designed for PNA-Pdx and named PNA-SC1 and PNA-SC2. We observed positive signals with the presence of 400 copies/μL synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA for the detection using both PNA-SC1 and PNA-SC2 (Figure 2B), indicating PNA-Pdx specifically detects SARS-CoV-2.

To further enhance the performance of PNA-Pdx, we then tried to improve the system by optimizing the concentration of PNA-SC1, the working temperature, and the dilution ratio for the LFA. Samples with or without synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA (400 copies/μL) can be easily distinguished among groups with various concentrations of PNA-SC1 (Figure S1). Samples with 10 or 20 nM PNA-SC1 showed no background in the absence of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA, whereas false detection occurred in samples with 50 or 100 nM PNA-SC1. Additionally, the heating step was important to avoid the nonspecific binding between PNA and FAM-labeled LAMP amplicons. Our results revealed that PNA-Pdx can achieve a more robust detection at 65 and 80 °C than at room temperature (RT) or 95 °C (Figure S2).

Furthermore, we investigated the effect of the dilution ratio between LAMP reaction solutions and detection buffer during LFA for PNA-Pdx and found that PNA-Pdx performed best at five-fold dilution with detection buffer during the LFA (Figure S3). Based on the optimization experiments, we selected 10 nM PNA-SC1, a working temperature of 65 °C, and a five-fold dilution as the working conditions for SARS-CoV-2 detection using PNA-Pdx.

PNA-Pdx can Detect SARS-CoV-2 with High Specificity and Sensitivity

The detection technique with high specificity and sensitivity is vital for monitoring and controlling the spread of pathogens. We validated the specificity of PNA-Pdx by detecting SARS-CoV-2 amplicons and nonspecific amplicons (RnaseP amplicons) using PNA-SC1. PNA-Pdx generated positive signals only when SARS-CoV-2 amplicons were present, and not in the presence of nonspecific amplicons (Figure 2C), indicating that PNA-SC1 gives high specificity for SARS-CoV-2 detection. Compared with the LAMP method which suffers from nonspecific amplification, PNA-Pdx exhibited higher specificity.

To further determine the LOD of PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2, we performed PNA-Pdx with the 0, 0.4, 1, 2, 10, 50, and 100 copies/μL of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA, finding that PNA-Pdx can detect as low as 2 copies/μL synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA (Figure 2D,E). A significant difference in the relative band intensities of the test lines was observed between groups with 0 or 2 copies/μL of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA. For comparison, we explored the LOD of colorimetric LAMP for SARS-CoV-2 detection with 0, 0.4, 1, 2, 4, 10, 50, and 100 copies/μL of synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA. Colorimetric LAMP can only detect up to 4 copies/μL synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA (Figure S4), indicating that PNA-Pdx further improved the sensitivity on the basis of the LAMP method. Considering that 100 copies/μL is generally considered the limit of reliable detection and that infectious transmission of SARS-CoV-2 requires more than 1000 copies/μL SARS-CoV-2, PNA-Pdx, with a LOD of 2 copies/μL, can be used as a sensitive technique for clinical SARS-CoV-2 detection.

Clinical Validation of PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 Detection

We then evaluated the performance of PNA-Pdx for the clinical detection of SARS-CoV-2 (Figure 3A). For clinical validation, we obtained 12 nasopharyngeal clinical samples, including 8 from infected individuals and 4 from uninfected individuals. Total RNA was extracted and confirmed for the presence of SARS-CoV-2 by RT-qPCR (Table S5). The same RNA samples were then subjected to PNA-Pdx for SARS-CoV-2 detection. PNA-Pdx successfully identified all 8 positive samples and 4 negative samples (Figure 3B). The relative band intensities of all clinical samples (Figure 3C) reveal a clear difference between positive samples and negative samples, in which the band intensity of negative samples was all below 0.2. The accurate detection of clinical samples suggested the reliability of PNA-Pdx as a POC test tool.

Detection of SNP Mutation by PNA-Pdx

The applications of Cas12b in SNP detection are typically restricted by the requirement for PAM sequences, which are essential for Cas12b-based targeting of the dsDNA.22 The PAM sequence is not required for ssDNA substrate targeting, however.23 Therefore, we considered that PNA probes could unwind dsDNA into local ssDNA structures that could be targeted by Cas12b for SNP detection without the need for a PAM sequence.

For proof of concept, we first applied PNA-Pdx to distinguish the A > G substitution. Original dsDNA (template 1) or dsDNA with SNP sites (template 2) were first incubated individually with the PNA probe (Table S3) at 60 °C for 2 h and then mixed with Cas12b RNP and reporters for SNP detection. PNA-Pdx specifically detected the A > G substitution in the absence of a PAM sequence (Figures 4A and S5A). PAMless SNP detection by PNA-Pdx requires the PNA because we did not observe a fluorescence signal when we incubated only Cas12b RNP with the PAMless targets.

PNA-Pdx-mediated PAMless SNP detection for A > G substitution. (A) Detection of a PAMless SNP mutation (A > G). Target1 (O), target1 with an A > G substitution (A > G), and water (NS) were subjected to PNA-Pdx. (B) Comparison of PNA-Pdx with PNA invasion at 37, 60, or 80 °C for the detection of A > G substitution. PNA-Pdx without PNA invasion was used as the negative control. (C) Minimal PNA invasion time for PAMless SNP detection at 80 °C. PNA-Pdx was performed to distinguish the original target1 from target1 with the A > G mutation. For PNA invasion, the substrates were incubated with the PNA probe at 80 °C for 0, 1, 3, 10, and 60 min. (D) Minimal substrate concentration for PAMless SNP detection. PNA-Pdx was performed to distinguish 50, 100, 300, 500, and 1000 pM of target1 and target1 with the A > G mutation.

We then tested different temperatures for the PNA invasion steps of PNA-Pdx-based SNP detection. Targets (template 1) were incubated with the PNA probe (or without the probe as a negative control) at 37, 60, or 80 °C for 30 min and then subjected to SNP detection. Targets with PNA at 80 °C had the highest trans-cleavage activities for fluorescence signal (Figures 4B and S5B), indicating that PNA invasion occurs faster at elevated temperatures. PNA-Pdx without PNA incubation at any temperature cannot generate fluorescence because of the PAM restriction. We also checked the minimal time required for the PNA invasion at 80 °C. We incubated 500 pM of the original and mutated targets with the PNA probe at 80 °C for 0, 1, 3, 10, and 60 min to detect SNP. Even the 1 min incubation at 80 °C was sufficient for PAMless SNP detection by PNA-Pdx (Figures 4C and S5C).

To check the minimal target substrate required for PNA-Pdx-based SNP detection, we performed PNA-Pdx with 50, 100, 300, 500, and 1000 pM targets for A > G substitution detection. The minimal target substrate was determined as the lowest concentration required for PNA-Pdx to distinguish target1 and target1 with an A > G substitution. PNA-Pdx can detect A > G substitution in target concentrations up to 300 pM (Figures 4D and S5D). For optimal performance, it is recommended to incubate sufficient targets (more than 1 nM) with the PNA probe at 80 °C for 10 min before subjecting them to SNP detection.

In case of insufficient materials for direct SNP detection, RPA isothermal amplification can be used as the preamplification steps of PNA-Pdx. For proof of concept, the original and mutated targets (Table S3) (5 × 105 copies) were first amplified using RPA and then subjected to SNP detection. The SNP mutations were not detectable without PNA invasion because of the PAM restriction, while the SNP mutation (A > G substitution) was clearly detected after the PNA invasion (Figure S6A,B). Our results indicate that PNA-Pdx can easily detect SNP mutations with only 5 × 105 copies of targets after employing RPA as a preamplification step. This strategy further reveals the power of PNA-Pdx for PAMless SNP detection with insufficient targets which is critical for practical applications.19 Furthermore, we performed PNA-Pdx with a hand-held, inexpensive fluorescence visualizer (P51 Molecular Fluorescence Viewer) for portable SNP detection (Figure S7A). After a 30 min reaction, we observed strong fluorescence signals for the target, but none for the target with an A > G substitution or for reactions lacking PNA invasion (Figure S7B). Our results showed the adaptability of PNA-Pdx-based SNP detection for simple visual detection which is crucial for POC diagnostics.

After the validation of PNA-Pdx for A > G substitution detection, we further performed PNA-Pdx for the detection of other SNP mutations, including G > A, G > C, T > A, A > C, and T > G substitutions. PNA-Pdx can specifically detect all types of SNP sites in the absence of the PAM sequence (Figures 5 and S8). PNA-Pdx assay without PNA probes did not show positive signals with all PAMless targets, indicating the PNA-dependent manner of PNA-Pdx for PAMless SNP detection. Overall, the above results proved the robustness and versatility of PNA-Pdx for PAMless SNP detection.

SNPs Identification in Diverse O. sativa by PNA-Pdx

SNP markers played an important role in plant genetics and breeding because of their considerable attributes for genetic diversity and evolutionary relationships.49 We sought to test the applicability of PNA-Pdx to identify SNP mutations in the plant genome.

We applied PNA-Pdx to differentiate the SNP loci between different transgenic O. sativa and wild species. For the PNA-Pdx assay, the isolated genomic DNA from O. sativa was amplified by RPA and then subjected to SNP detection. PNA-Pdx successfully distinguished different SNP sites in the ALS gene of WT and transgenic O. sativa, including G > T substitution in F575C (Figures 6A and S9A), G > A substitution in K591E (Figures 6B and S9B), and C > G substitution in G629R (Figures 6C and S9C). These results showed the potential of PNA-Pdx as an applicable technique for SNP identification in plant genetics and breeding.

Conclusions

In this study, we established a versatile PNA-based nucleic acid detection toolkit for detecting nucleic acid sequences and named it PNA-Pdx. This toolkit consists of independent modules for nucleic acid detection and PAMless SNP detection. PNA-Pdx can sensitively and specifically detect RnaseP DNA and SARS-CoV-2 RNA in resource-limited settings, which fulfills the requirements for POC nucleic acid diagnostics. Furthermore, PNA-Pdx was successfully validated for the detection of SARS-CoV-2 in clinical samples with high accuracy, suggesting that it is robust for in-field applications. Considering the vital role of SNPs as biomarkers,50 we engineered the PNA probe with Cas12b for PNA-Pdx-based PAMless SNP detection. As a proof of concept, we successfully performed PNA-Pdx to detect versatile PAMless SNP mutations. PNA-Pdx mediated PAMless detection can be easily executed by incubating PAMless targets with the PNA probe at 80 °C for 1 min, which is much more straightforward and rapid than other PAMless SNP detection strategies such as asymmetric PCR22 and PAM insertion using PAM containing primers.51

Overall, PNA-Pdx provides a rapid, specific, user-friendly, and economical toolkit for nucleic acid detection and PAMless SNP detection. Compared with the existing PNA-based diagnostics tools which rely heavily upon technical facilities, PNA-Pdx expands the applications of PNA for POC diagnostics and highlights the benefits of engineering PNA probes to resolve the drawbacks of existing nucleic acid detection platforms.

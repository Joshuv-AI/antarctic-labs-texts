Effects of NB2C on hernia repair, a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trial
Authors: Zhenliang Lin, Xuan Cai, Gaohan Chen, Weili Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn hernia repair, mesh fixation methods impact postoperative pain and recovery. While penetrating fixations such as tacks or sutures may cause chronic pain, non-penetrating alternatives such as n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate (NB2C) glue remain under investigation.MethodsA meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) was conducted to compare NB2C with sutures or tacks in groin hernia repair. Databases searched included PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Scopus (up to April 28, 2025). Primary outcomes were postoperative pain (VAS) and operative time; secondary outcomes included complications and recurrence.ResultsSeven RCTs involving 1111 patients were included. Compared with sutures, NB2C was associated with reduced postoperative pain (MD − 0.72; 95% CI − 1.42 to − 0.02; p = 0.04) and shorter operative time (MD − 3.86 min; 95% CI − 5.27 to − 2.44; p < 0.01). No significant differences were observed between NB2C and tacks regarding pain or complications, nor between NB2C and sutures regarding complications and recurrence.ConclusionNB2C offers reduced pain and operative time compared to sutures but shows no clear advantage over tacks.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00464-025-12294-z.

Surgery for inguinal hernia is a common surgical practice worldwide [1]. The use of mesh to reinforce the transversalis fascia is a straightforward procedure, associated with a very low recurrence rate and minimal complications [2]. However, chronic pain following Lichtenstein hernia repair has been documented and is now recognized as one of the significant postoperative complications of the procedure [3]. The primary factors contributing to groin pain after inguinal hernia repair include inflammatory reactions to the mesh and nerve injury or entrapment caused by the sutures used during the procedure [4]. Tacker penetration of the tissue can also cause nerve injury [5]. Fibrin sealant (Tissucol; Baxter Healthcare) is a biodegradable adhesive composed of human-derived fibrinogen and thrombin, activated by calcium chloride. It has been commercially available for over 20 years and has demonstrated effectiveness across a wide range of clinical applications [6]. Cyanoacrylate has been used as a surgical tissue adhesive since the [7]. Mesh fixation using adhesive glue may be considered a preferable option for reducing postoperative pain following hernia repair, as it is not associated with nerve irritation or entrapment. Early reports on various tissue adhesives have shown promising outcomes, indicating a lower incidence and severity of postoperative pain [8].

In this study, we aimed to conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the impact of tissue glue, specifically n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate (NB2C), in Lichtenstein hernia repair, and to compare it with either sutures or tacks in terms of VAS scores, postoperative complications, operative time, and hernia recurrence.

Materials and methods

Eligibility criteria

The PRISMA guidelines were followed as a checklist for this study, and the study protocol was registered on PROSPERO. The inclusion criteria were based on the PICOS framework, as follows: the study population consisted of NB2C with suture or tack in Groin Hernia Repair. The intervention group received NB2C were included in the study. While the control group received either suture or tack. The primary outcome of the study was VAS, operation duration. The secondary outcome was complication. Only randomized controlled trials with accessible full-text publications were included, with no language restrictions. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) observational studies with control (cohort or case–control) or without control (cross-sectional studies or case series); (2) unpublished abstracts; and (3) non-primary research.

Visual analog scale

The VAS is a validated tool commonly used to assess pain intensity. It consists of a 10-cm horizontal line, with endpoints labeled 0 and 10. A score of 0 represents no pain, while a score of 10 indicates the worst imaginable pain. Patients were asked to mark the point on the line that best reflected their level of pain, which was then measured in centimeters to quantify the pain score.

Search strategy

Literature was systematically searched in five databases (EMBASE, PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and Scopus). The data was updated up to April 28, 2025. The following keywords were utilized in the search: “n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate”, “inguinal hernia”, and “random”. Boolean operators (AND, OR) were applied and tailored to the specific search criteria of each database. In addition, a manual search was performed by reviewing the reference lists of all included studies. Details of the search strategy and the databases used are provided in the supplementary materials.

Study selection

Two researchers independently screened the titles and abstracts of all identified studies. Full-text articles were reviewed to assess eligibility. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer.

Data extraction

The study details included the first author, year of publication sample size, study population, style of hernia, the basic information, like age, body mass index (BMI), sex, and the outcome. All data were retrieved by two researchers. Discrepancies were settled by discussion or, if necessary, consultation with the third member of the review team. When details were not accessible in published publications, we contacted authors for further information or clarifications.

Study risk of bias assessment

Two authors used Review Manager 5.4 [9] to assess the risk of bias from all studies, such as Random Sequence Generation, Allocation Concealment, Blinding, Incomplete Outcome Data, Selective Reporting, and Other Sources of Bias. The assessment results are classified into high, low, or unclear.

Data synthesis

Review Manager 5.4 (Cochrane Collaboration) and StataMP 14.0 were used to conduct the meta-analysis. The inverse variance method was employed to calculate the mean difference (MD) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) for continuous variables. A random-effects model was applied when heterogeneity was high; otherwise, a fixed-effects model was used. Risk ratios (RR) were calculated for binary outcomes. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic and categorized as low (< 25%), moderate (26–50%), or high (> 50%) [10]. Since our study focuses on postoperative pain, the evaluation of VAS scores is based on the cumulative values across all time points rather than a single time point. Therefore, we aggregated the data and did not consider baseline VAS scores in the analysis. Assessment of publication bias

A formal assessment of publication bias was not performed, as funnel plot analysis generally requires at least 10 studies to achieve sufficient statistical power. Nevertheless, the potential for publication bias cannot be ruled out due to the limited number of included studies [9].

GRADE

Grading of recommendations assessments, development and evaluation (GRADE) analysis was applied to the outcomes to evaluate the quality of evidence and was completed [11].

Results

Studies selection

A total of 29 articles were retrieved in the initial search from the databases. Among them, sixteen articles were removed as duplicates. Five studies were then excluded out of the retrieved articles. Primary outcomes

VAS

Five included studies (n = 844) reported VAS within the six months after operation. Regarding VAS, there was considerable variation among the studies: one study reported VAS at a single postoperative time point in 24 h [15], one recorded VAS up to six months after surgery [5], while others recorded VAS up to one year after surgery [12, 13, 16]. None of the included studies reported baseline VAS scores.

Three studies compared NB2C glue with sutures. The pooled analysis demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in VAS scores in the NB2C group compared to sutures (MD − 0.99; 95% CI − 1.27 to − 0.71; p < 0.01), with no observed heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 4A). The GRADE quality of evidence for this outcome was rated as moderate.Fig. 4The outcomes evaluating the effect of NB2C on pain reduction: A NB2C versus suture for overall VAS scores; B NB2C versus suture for VAS scores within the first postoperative week; C NB2C versus tack for overall VAS scores

The outcomes evaluating the effect of NB2C on pain reduction: A NB2C versus suture for overall VAS scores; B NB2C versus suture for VAS scores within the first postoperative week; C NB2C versus tack for overall VAS scores

Among these three studies, two specifically reported VAS scores within the first postoperative week [13, 16], a period typically considered the peak of postoperative pain and most clinically relevant. A separate meta-analysis using only first-week data also showed a statistically significant reduction in VAS scores with NB2C compared to sutures (MD − 0.99; 95% CI − 1.27 to − 0.71; p < 0.01; I2 = 0%) (Fig. 4B). Due to the small number of studies, no subgroup analysis was performed for this comparison.

Two additional studies compared NB2C with tacks. The pooled analysis showed no statistically significant difference in VAS scores between the NB2C and tack groups (MD − 0.87; 95% CI − 1.28 to 0.59; p = 0.47; I2 = 0%) (Fig. 4C). The GRADE quality of evidence for this outcome was rated as low. Due to the limited number of studies and low heterogeneity, no sensitivity or subgroup analyses were conducted for this comparison.

Operation duration

Three included studies (n = 587) reported operation duration measured in minute during intervention. The pooled analysis showed that a significant decrease in duration compared to suture was associated with the NB2C and suture combination group [MD − 3.86 (95% CI − 5.27, − 2.44), p < 0.01], with a moderate degree heterogeneity (I2 = 40%), the detail is shown in Fig. 5A. The GRADE quality of evidence for this outcome was rated as moderate. In the sensitivity analysis, we identified the study involving PMR as the source of heterogeneity [14], the detail is shown in Fig. 5B. After excluding this study, the heterogeneity was reduced to zero. Due to the limited number of included studies, subgroup analysis was not conducted.Fig. 5Effect of NB2C on operation duration: A forest plot; B sensitivity analysis

Discussion

The meta-analysis revealed that NB2C was associated with reduced postoperative pain (VAS) and shorter operative time compared with sutures, while no significant differences were observed between NB2C and tacks. With respect to complications, NB2C showed no significant differences when compared with either sutures or tacks. The absence of significant differences in pain outcomes between NB2C and tacks may be attributable to the generally lower pain levels associated with laparoscopic surgery, potentially resulting in a ceiling effect and reduced statistical power to detect subtle differences.

Although NB2C reduced operative time and pain compared with sutures in open hernia repair, the clinical relevance of a 3-min reduction in operative time is questionable. Moreover, NB2C is generally more expensive than conventional sutures, raising concerns regarding cost-effectiveness, particularly in resource-limited settings. Future economic evaluations are warranted to determine whether the observed time savings and modest pain reduction justify the additional cost. It should also be noted that the use of mesh plugs has limited support in current international hernia guidelines, which generally discourage plug-based techniques due to concerns about chronic pain and foreign body sensation. Therefore, both surgical context and fixation method must be interpreted with caution when considering the applicability of NB2C in clinical practice.

Neuropathic mechanisms may also contribute substantially to postoperative pain. Some authors report that a significant proportion of chronic pain originates from nerve injury [17]. When the genitofemoral, ilioinguinal, or iliohypogastric nerves are damaged by suture entrapment or mesh contact, the probability of persistent pain is high. In this context, NB2C glue, which avoids penetrating fixation, may effectively reduce postoperative pain.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, based on the Cochrane risk of bias assessment, although most included trials described the randomization method, some did not provide details on allocation concealment or blinding of personnel, which precluded definitive assessment of blinding adequacy. Second, several studies were limited to short follow-up durations, and long-term outcomes were not consistently reported. Thus, the methodological quality of clinical trials evaluating NB2C in relation to chronic pain after hernia repair requires further improvement. Third, baseline VAS values were not reported in any of the included studies, and the time points for postoperative VAS assessment varied substantially, which may have contributed to heterogeneity in pain outcomes. Although separate analyses were performed for overall VAS and VAS within the first postoperative week, this variability still limits the interpretation of results. Finally, the analysis may be subject to publication bias due to the limited number of available RCTs. Therefore, well-designed, adequately powered, double-blind randomized controlled trials are needed to more definitively establish the efficacy of NB2C.

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrated that NB2C was associated with reduced chronic pain, as assessed by VAS, and shorter operative time compared with sutures. However, no significant difference in pain reduction was observed relative to tacks, and NB2C did not confer a significant advantage over either sutures or tacks with respect to postoperative complications. Given the limited sample sizes and moderate methodological quality of the included trials, the long-term efficacy and safety of NB2C warrant confirmation in high-quality, large-scale randomized controlled studies.

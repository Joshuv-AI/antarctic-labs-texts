Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition defined by organ dysfunction resulting from a dysregulated host response to infection (Deutschman and Tracey, 2014). Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a well-recognized yet incompletely understood complication of sepsis, characterized by a rapid decline in renal function and pathological hallmarks such as renal tubular epithelial cell death, inflammatory cell infiltration, and the release of pro-inflammatory mediators (Bellomo et al. 2012). Current clinical management of AKI primarily includes source control of infection, antimicrobial therapy, and renal replacement therapy; however, therapeutic outcomes remain unsatisfactory, with persistently high mortality rates (Peerapornratana et al. 2019). Notably, due to the complex and multifactorial pathogenesis underlying sepsis-induced AKI, which leads to significant interpatient variability in pathophysiological responses, no effective pharmacological agents are currently available for the prevention or treatment of sepsis or its associated AKI. Nevertheless, given that the inflammatory response lies at the core of AKI pathophysiology, it remains a fundamental focus for elucidating the key mechanisms driving sepsis-related AKI.

The poly ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) family comprises 18 enzymes, including well-characterized members such as PARP1, PARP2, PARP3, and PARP7 (Lei et al. 2025). These enzymes catalyze the transfer of ADP-ribose from nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) to specific target proteins—a reversible post-translational modification termed ADP-ribosylation (Fehr et al. 2020). PARPs are implicated in a wide range of cellular processes, including DNA damage repair (Traphagen et al. 2023), inflammatory stress responses (Bencsics et al. 2023), and programmed cell death (Maru et al. 2023), and have been increasingly recognized for their critical roles in the pathogenesis of diseases such as cancer (Arce-Gallego et al. 2025), autoimmune disorders(Kamboj et al. 2013), cardiovascular conditions (Lv et al. 2020), and diabetes (Li et al. 2020). As research advances, several PARP family members—particularly PARP1 (Li et al. 2025), PARP2 (Gupta et al. 2023), and PARP3 (Zhao et al. 2022)—have been demonstrated to modulate the progression of AKI through ADP-ribosylation-mediated mechanisms. Notably, inhibitors targeting PARP1 and PARP2, such as Olaparib, Niraparib, and Talazoparib, have already achieved clinical approval and proven efficacy in oncology, underscoring the pharmacological tractability of the PARP family (Roskoski 2025). This clinical success highlights the strong therapeutic potential of targeting PARPs. Therefore, PARP family members may serve as a valuable “molecular reservoir” for identifying novel therapeutic targets in sepsis-induced AKI.

PARP7 (also known as TIPARP) is a mono-ADP-ribose transferase that utilizes NAD+ as a substrate to catalyze the transfer of mono-ADP-ribose onto specific target proteins, thereby mediating their biological functions (Vyas et al. 2014). Structurally, PARP7 contains an N-terminal nuclear localization signal, a CCCH-type zinc finger domain, a tryptophan-tryptophan-glutamate (WWE) domain, and a conserved PARP catalytic domain (Qiu et al. 2025). In recent years, PARP7 has gained increasing attention for its role in suppressing both innate and adaptive immune responses (Gozgit et al. 2021; Jeltema et al. 2025). It acts as a negative feedback regulator of the type I interferon (IFN-I) signaling pathway by interacting with TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) in a mono-ADP-ribosylation-dependent manner (Gozgit et al. 2021; Jeltema et al. 2025). Furthermore, the successful preclinical proof-of-concept (POC) studies of PARP7 inhibitorshave established PARP7 as a highly promising therapeutic target (Qiu et al. 2025). Currently, research on PARP7 primarily focuses on its roles in antiviral defense (Ryan et al. 2025), anti-inflammatory responses (Jeltema et al. 2025), and tumor suppression (Gu et al. 2023). This study aims to investigate the involvement of PARP7 in sepsis-associated AKI and elucidate its substrate-related molecular mechanisms.

In this study, we first confirmed the upregulated expression of PARP7 in septic AKI and found that increased PARP7 was predominantly localized in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells. We further demonstrated that global deletion of PARP7 exacerbated lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced renal inflammation and acute injury, whereas specific overexpression of PARP7 in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells attenuated septic AKI in mice. Using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), we revealed that PARP7 suppresses the LPS-induced inflammatory response in renal tubule epithelial cells. Mechanistically, PARP7 interacts with TBK1 and mediates its ADP-ribosylation in LPS-stimulated renal tubule epithelial cells. The anti-inflammatory effects of PARP7 are primarily mediated through targeting TBK1 via its catalytic residue H532. Collectively, our findings highlight the renoprotective role of PARP7 in AKI and provide a theoretical foundation for therapeutic development targeting the PARP7-TBK1 axis.

Materials and methods

Animal experiments

Parp7 gene-knockout mice (Parp7−/−, Strain No. T017191) with a C57BL/6J background and littermate wild-type mice (WT) were provided by GemPharmatech. Parp7−/− and WT mice were maintained in an SPF environment with a 12/12 h light/dark cycle and fed a standard rodent diet. The animals were intraperitoneally injected with LPS (#2630, Sigma-Aldrich, 10 mg/kg) at 6 h before sacrifice to establish an acute kidney injury model (Qi et al. 2016). All animal care and handling procedures were conducted according to the guidelines in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (US National Institutes of Health) and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Jiaxing University (Approval No. JUMC2025-187).

The recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) carrying Parp7 (NM_178892.5) or empty vector (EV) cDNA with the renal proximal tubule epithelial cell-specific promoter Sglt2 was constructed by Hanbio Tech. Mice were injected with AAV9 through the tail vein at a total dose of 2 × 1011 vg. After 4 weeks, acute kidney injury models were established according to the above method. The mice were then euthanized humanely under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia, followed by the collection and storage of blood and kidney tissues for further analysis.

Serum biochemical analysis

The blood was collected 6 h after LPS administration. The serum blood urea nitrogen (BUN, C013-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) and creatinine (Cr, C011-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) levels were detected using specific kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Data analysis was conducted using the Multiskan GO Microplate Reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with spectrophotometric techniques.

Kidney histology and immunohistochemistry

Kidney tissues from mice were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and then sectioned into 5 μm-thick slices. Histopathological damage in kidney sections was evaluated through hematoxylin and eosin (H&E, G1120, Solarbio) and periodic acid-schiff (PAS, G1285, Solarbio) staining. Stained sections were viewed under a light microscope (Zeiss).

To assess the localization of inflammatory cells and PARP7 expression, we performed immunohistochemical staining for F4/80, CD11b and PARP7. The tissue sections were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with anti-PARP7 (ab200390, Abcam, 1:200), anti-F4/80 (70076, CST, 1:200) and, anti-CD11b (ab133357, Abcam, 1:200) antibodies. Following PBS washes, HRP-conjugated secondary antibody and diaminobenzidine (DAB, DA1010, Solarbio) were used for detection of immunoreactivity. Then, sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. All images were captured under a light microscope (Zeiss).

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)

The kidney was dissociated into single cells by dissociation solution (0.35% collagenase IV, 2 mg/ml papain, 120 Units/ml DNase I). We combined single-cell suspensions from three kidneys as one sample. Single-cell suspensions were added to MobiNova-100 Single-Cell System to capture 5000 single cells according to the instructions for the MobiCube SingleCell 3′RNA-seq Kit manual. All procedures, including cDNA amplification, library construction, sequencing, and data analysis, were conducted by LC-Bio Technologies Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China).

Immunofluorescence staining

The cellular origin of PARP7 in kidney tissue was observed by double fluorescence staining of 5 μm thick frozen sections. Simply put, sections were fixed in cold methanol and permeabilized with 0.25% Triton X-100 (T8200, Solarbio). Then, sections were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, A8020, Solarbio) for 1 h to block any remaining nonspecific binding sites. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies for PARP7 (ab200390, Abcam, 1:200) and AQP1 (20333-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:200). Homologous double labeling was achieved by a three-color multiple fluorescence immunohistochemical staining kit (abs50088, Absin). All images were captured under a fluorescence microscope (Leica).

The cellular localization of PARP7 and TBK1 was detected by immunofluorescence staining. Cells were fixed with cold methanol. Following PBS washes, cells were permeabilized with 0.25% Triton X-100 for 10 min and subsequently blocked with 5% BSA for 30 min on an orbital shaker. Then, cells were incubated with anti-PARP7 (ab200390, Abcam, 1:200) and anti-TBK1 (67211-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:200) primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. Then, cells were washed with PBS and incubated with iFluor™ 488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (HA1121, HUABIO, 1:200) or iFluor™ 594-conjugated goat anti-mouse secondary antibody (HA1126, HUABIO, 1:200) for 2 h. Images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope (Leica).

Cell culture and treatment

The rat renal tubule epithelial cell line NRK-52E and NIH/3T3 cells were obtained from the Shanghai Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology (Shanghai, China). Cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM, 12100, Solarbio) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, 10099-141, Gibco) and antibiotics (100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin). Cultures were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 ℃ with CO2. The cells were treated with LPS (10 µg/mL, L2630, Sigma) to model renal proximal tubular epithelial cells in AKI (Ding et al. 2021).

Gene overexpression in cells was accomplished by transfecting plasmids. Rat plasmids (Flag-PARP7-WT, Flag-PARP7-H532A, and His-TBK1) and mouse plasmids (Flag-PARP7-WT, Flag-PARP7-△WWE, and His-TBK1) were synthesized by Genechem (Shanghai, China). Transient transfections into NRK-52E and NIH/3T3 cells were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (L3000015, Invitrogen), following the manufacturer’s instructions.

To establish PARP7 knockdown cell lines, lentiviral-based specific shRNA was constructed and purified by Hanbio Tech. NRK-52E cells were infected with lentivirus carrying shRNA sequences targeting rat Parp7 or scrambled lentivirus as a negative control (NC). Following 48 h of transfection, puromycin (10 µg/mL) selection was conducted on the cells to isolate PARP7 knock listeddown (PARP7-KD) NRK-52E cells for 2 weeks. The sequences of shRNA are in Table S1.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) used to silence TBK1 (siTBK1, sense 5’-3’ GACGCUUGGUCCUAGAACUTT; antisense 3’-5’ AGUUCUAGGACCAAGCGUCTT) was obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The transfection agent Lipofectamine 2000 (11668-019, Invitrogen) was used to transfect siTBK1 into NRK-52E cells.

Western blotting and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Proteins were extracted from kidney tissues and cultured cells using RIPA lysate (AR0101/AR0103, Boster) and protein concentrations were determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (P0009, Beyotime). Equal amounts of proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF, Millipore) membranes. The membranes were incubated with BSA for 2 h to block non-specific binding sites, followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Then, membranes were incubated with corresponding HPR-labeled secondary antibodies at room temperature for 2 h. Protein bands were visualized using the ECL luminescence reagent (WBKLS0500, Merck Millipore), and protein quantification was performed using ImageJ software.

This study utilized primary antibodies against PARP7 (ab200390, Abcam, 1:1000), TBK1 (67211-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:1000), p-TBK1 (Ser172) (5483, CST, 1:1000), Flag (20543-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:1000), IRF3 (ET1612-14, HUABIO, 1:1000), p-IRF3 (S396) (HA722772, HUABIO, 1:1000), Mono-ADP Ribose (89190, CST, 1:1000), KIM-1 (30948-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:1000), Tubulin (2128, CST, 1:1000), and GAPDH (5174, CST, 1:1000). Secondary antibodies included HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit (7074, CST, 1:5000) or anti-mouse (7076, CST, 1:5000) IgG.

For Co-IP, a portion of the lysis solution was reserved as the input control, while the remaining lysis solution was incubated with the appropriate primary antibodies or the anti-Flag beads (B26102, Bimake). Subsequently, the lysates were immunoprecipitated using protein A + G agarose beads (P2055, Beyotime) at 4 ℃ for 4 h. Following 3 washes with lysis buffer, the Protein-bead mixtures were subjected to western blot experiments.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA from kidney tissue and NRK-52E cells was isolated and purified using the RNA-quick purification kit (RN001, ES Science) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Next, cDNA was created by reverse transcribing the purified RNA with the Prime Script RT Master Mix reagent (RR036A, Takara). Quantitative PCR was performed with SYBR Green™ Premix Ex Taq™ II (RR820A, Takara) in a Real-Time PCR System (Roche). Primers for genes including Parp7, Ifn-β, Tnf-α, Il-6, Il-1β, and β-actin were synthesized by Invitrogen. Target mRNA was normalized to β-actin, and relative gene expression was analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCT method. The primers used are listed in Table S2.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were randomized and blinded. Data from both in vitro and in vivo experiments were expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Student’s t-test was performed to compare two samples. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with multiple comparisons (Bonferroni’s correction) was used to compare multiple samples (GraphPad Prism 10.0). P value < 0.05 was accepted as statistical significance in this study.

Results

The expression of PARP7 is upregulated in LPS-induced acute kidney injury and is predominantly localized in proximal tubule epithelial cells

We initially examined the expression profile of Parp7 in acute kidney injury (AKI) by analyzing three transcriptome sequencing datasets from the GEO database. Notably, elevated Parp7 expression was consistently observed in LPS-induced rat kidney tissues (GSE200928, Fig. 1A) and mouse kidney tissues (GSE240304, Fig. 1B; GSE255281, Fig. 1C). We then validated the increased mRNA (Fig. 1D) and protein (Fig. 1E) levels of PARP7 in kidney tissues from LPS-treated mice. Consistently, immunohistochemical staining revealed enhanced PARP7 expression in kidney tissues of LPS-challenged mice (Fig. 1F-G). To identify the cellular source of PARP7 in the kidney, we analyzed a single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset from kidney tissues. As shown in Figs. 1H and 13 major cell types were identified, including principal cells (CD-PC), connecting tubule (CNT), distal convoluted tubule (DCT), endothelial cells (EC), intercalated cells (IC-A, IC-B), loop of Henle [LH(AL), LH(DL)], mesangial cells (MC), macrophages (MФ), proximal tubule epithelial cells segment 1–3 (PT(S1-S3)), and podocytes (Pod). Strikingly, Parp7 mRNA expression was predominantly localized in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells (PTs) (Fig. 1I–J). Furthermore, double immunofluorescence staining on kidney sections from LPS-induced mice confirmed up-regulated PARP7 expression in AQP1-positive tubular epithelial cells (Fig. 1K). We further corroborated these findings in the rat renal tubular epithelial cell line NRK-52E. Both mRNA and protein levels of PARP7 were significantly increased in LPS-stimulated NRK-52E cells (Fig. 1L-M). Collectively, these results indicate that elevated PARP7 expression in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells is associated with LPS-induced AKI.

PARP7 deficiency exacerbates LPS-induced renal inflammation and acute injury in mice

To investigate the role of PARP7 in AKI, we utilized PARP7 knockout (Parp7−/−) mice and confirmed the knockout efficiency by western blotting (Fig. S1A). Under basal conditions, Parp7−/− mice exhibit no significant differences in body weight (Fig. S1B), survival rate (Fig. S1C) or histological features of major organs (Fig. S1D) compared to WT mice. Next, AKI mouse models were established via intraperitoneal injection of LPS (10 mg/kg) in both WT and Parp7−/− mice (Fig. 2A). Our data demonstrated that PARP7 knockout mice exhibited more severe kidney injury in LPS-induced AKI, as evidenced by elevated serum levels of BUN (Fig. 2B) and CRE (Fig. 2C). Histological analyses using H&E (Fig. 2D) and PAS (Fig. 2E and Fig. S1E) staining revealed renal tubular pathological changes in AKI mice, including tubular lumen dilation, epithelial vacuolation or flattening, nuclear exposure, and brush border loss—alterations that were markedly exacerbated in Parp7−/− mice. Consistently, PARP7 knockout mice showed a significant increase in the expression of the kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1, Fig. S1F). Subsequently, immunohistochemical analysis of kidney tissue sections were performed to assess inflammatory cell infiltration. Notably, LPS-treated WT mice exhibited F4/80+ and CD11b+ macrophage infiltration, which was significantly enhanced in PARP7 knockout mice (Fig. 2F-I). Additionally, RT-qPCR analyses revealed increased expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including Ifn-β (Fig. 2J), Tnf-α (Fig. 2K), Il-6 (Fig. 2L) and, Il-1β (Fig. 2M), in kidney tissues from LPS-treated mice, with further upregulation observed in Parp7−/− mice. These findings collectively indicate that PARP7 deficiency exacerbates LPS-induced kidney inflammation and injury. Overall, PARP7 deficiency aggravates renal dysfunction, inflammatory responses, and histopathological damage in LPS-induced AKI.

Renal proximal tubule epithelial cell-specific overexpression of PARP7 alleviates LPS-induced renal inflammation and acute injury in vivo

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of PARP7 in AKI mouse models, AAV9 vectors driven by an Sglt2 promoter were employed to achieve targeted overexpression of Parp7 in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells. AAV9-Sglt2-Parp7 or empty vector was administered via tail vein injection. Four weeks after administration, LPS-induced AKI was induced in mice (Fig. 3A). Notably, LPS-induced kidney injury was substantially ameliorated in AAV9-Sglt2-Parp7-treated mice, as evidenced by reduced serum levels of BUN (Fig. 3B) and CRE (Fig. 3C). H&E (Fig. 3D) and PAS (Fig. 3E and Fig. S2B) staining revealed that LPS administration caused tubular damage—including lumen dilation, epithelial flattening, and brush border loss—changes that were markedly attenuated in mice receiving AAV9-Sglt2-Parp7. Consistently, KIM-1 expression was significantly decreased in mice with proximal tubule-specific PARP7 overexpression (Fig. S2A). Subsequently, immunohistochemical analysis of kidney tissue sections were conducted to assess inflammatory cell infiltration. The results showed that PARP7 overexpression in proximal tubule epithelial cells significantly reduced LPS-induced infiltration of F4/80+ and CD11b+ macrophages (Fig. 3F-I). Furthermore, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that the LPS-induced upregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 3J-M) were alleviated in AAV9-Sglt2-Parp7-treated mice. Collectively, these findings indicate that renal proximal tubule epithelial cell-specific overexpression of PARP7 protects against LPS-induced acute kidney dysfunction, inflammation, and histopathological injury.

ScRNA-seq reveals that PARP7 suppresses the LPS-induced inflammatory response in renal tubule epithelial cells

To further investigate the downstream mechanisms by which PARP7 regulates AKI, scRNA-seq was performed on kidney tissues from WT and Parp7−/− mice following LPS challenge. As shown in Fig. 4A, major kidney cell types were identified, including proximal tubule epithelial cells (PTs). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in renal proximal tubule epithelial cells from LPS-treated WT and Parp7−/− mice were significantly enriched in multiple inflammatory signaling pathways, particularly cellular response to interferon-beta and response to interferon-gamma (Fig. 4B). Consistently, both the number and percentage of macrophages in the kidney were increased in LPS-challenged Parp7−/− mice compared to WT controls (Fig. 4C and Fig. S3A), aligning with our immunohistochemical findings and further supporting the inhibitory role of PARP7 in inflammatory cell infiltration. Notably, PARP7 deficiency reduced the number and percentage of PTs in LPS-injured kidneys (Fig. 4D and Fig. S3B), suggesting that PARP7 preserves tubular epithelial cell populations, potentially through suppression of inflammation. Furthermore, RT-qPCR analyses confirmed that PARP7 knockdown (PARP7-KD, Fig. S3C) exacerbated LPS-induced expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines in the rat renal tubular epithelial cell line NRK-52E, including Ifn-β (Fig. 4E), Tnf-α (Fig. 4F), Il-6 (Fig. 4G) and Il-1β (Fig. 4H). In contrast, overexpression of PARP7 (oePARP7, Fig. S3D) in LPS-treated NRK-52E cells led to a significant downregulation of these cytokines (Fig. 4I-L). Here, using scRNA-seq, we revealed that PARP7 suppresses the LPS-induced inflammatory response in renal tubule epithelial cells.

To extend our findings beyond LPS-induced injury, we investigated the role of PARP7 in two additional models of renal tubular epithelial cell injury: hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) and angiotensin II (Ang II) stimulation. In the H/R model, PARP7 overexpression in NRK-52E cells significantly attenuated cellular injury (Fig. S4A-D), as evidenced by increased cell viability, reduced lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, and decreased proportion of propidium iodide (PI)-positive cells. In the Ang II model, PARP7 overexpression similarly suppressed Ang II-induced tubular epithelial cell fibrosis (Fig. S4E-G), upregulation of pro-inflammatory genes (e.g., Ifn-β, Tnf-α, Fig. S4H-K), and expression of kidney injury markers KIM-1(Fig. S4L). Collectively, these data demonstrate that PARP7 exerts consistent protective effects across etiologically distinct models of kidney injury, supporting its broad relevance in renal pathophysiology.

PARP7 interacts with TBK1 and mediates ADP-ribosylation of TBK1 in LPS-induced renal tubule epithelial cells

PARP family members primarily exert their biological functions by binding to specific substrate proteins (Rodriguez and Cohen, 2022). Investigating the substrates of PARP7-mediated mono-ADP-ribosylation is essential for a comprehensive understanding of its biological roles in AKI. Recent evidence indicates that PARP7 negatively regulates type I interferon responses by interacting with the substrate protein TBK1 (Gozgit et al. 2021; Jeltema et al. 2025). Therefore, we hypothesized that TBK1 may serve as a substrate for PARP7 in LPS-induced AKI. We validated this interaction using co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) assays. Endogenous PARP7 and TBK1 were found to interact in LPS-stimulated NRK-52E cells (Fig. 5A) and kidney tissues (Fig. 5B). Colocalization analysis further confirmed significant spatial overlap between PARP7 and TBK1 in LPS-treated NRK-52E cells (Fig. 5C). Structurally, PARP7 contains three conserved domains: the CCCH, WWE, and PARP domains (Fig. 5D). The PARP domain harbors enzymatic activity responsible for ADP-ribosyl transferase function, while the WWE domain—known to recognize ADP-ribose moieties—may also participate in substrate binding (Qiu et al. 2025). Notably, TBK1 interacted with wild-type PARP7 (WT), but not with a truncated mutant lacking the WWE domain (ΔWWE) (Fig. 5E), indicating that the WWE domain is required for PARP7-TBK1 interaction. The catalytic residue histidine 532 (H532) within the PARP domain confers auto-mono-ADP-ribosyl transferase activity (Vyas et al. 2014) (Fig. 5D). To assess its functional relevance, we generated a PARP7-H532A mutant (substitution of histidine at position 532 with alanine). As shown in Fig. 5F, the catalytically inactive PARP7-H532A mutant still bind with TBK1 as same as its WT homolog (PARP7-WT), indicating that PARP7-H532 regulates TBK1 independently of a direct physical interaction between the two proteins. Additionally, ADP-ribosylation of TBK1 was detected in the presence of wild-type PARP7, but not with the catalytically inactive H532A mutant (Fig. 5G and Fig. S5A). Together, these findings demonstrate that PARP7 interacts with TBK1 via its WWE domain and mediates mono-ADP-ribosylation of TBK1 in renal tubular epithelial cells upon LPS stimulation (Fig. 5H).

PARP7 exerts its anti-inflammatory effects in renal tubule epithelial cells primarily by targeting TBK1 via its catalytic residue H532 during LPS stimulation

Next, we investigated the effects of PARP7 on TBK1 activity and the downstream inflammatory signaling pathway in renal tubule epithelial cells. As shown in Fig. 6A and Fig. S6A, overexpression of PARP7 in NRK-52E cells suppressed TBK1 phosphorylation, whereas this inhibitory effect was attenuated by the catalytically inactive PARP7-H532A mutant. IRF3 is a key transcription factor downstream of TBK1 (Fitzgerald et al. 2003); TBK1 phosphorylates and activates IRF3, leading to the production of type I interferons—a central mechanism in antiviral immunity (Al Hamrashdi and Brady, 2022). Consistently, wild-type PARP7 suppressed IRF3 phosphorylation, while the PARP7-H532A mutant failed to exert this inhibition (Fig. 6A and Fig. S6A). Furthermore, PARP7 overexpression reduced mRNA levels of TBK1-mediated pro-inflammatory cytokines in LPS-treated NRK-52E cells, including Ifn-β (Fig. 6B), Tnf-α (Fig. 6C), Il-6 (Fig. 6D) and Il-1β (Fig. 6E). This suppressive effect was significantly diminished when the catalytic histidine residue at position 532 was mutated (Fig. 6B-E).

To validate that TBK1 is the primary substrate through which PARP7 modulates inflammation in renal tubule epithelial cells, we silenced TBK1 expression and subsequently overexpressed PARP7 in NRK-52E cells prior to LPS treatment. The efficiency of TBK1 knockdown was confirmed by western blotting (Fig. S6B). As expected, TBK1 silencing markedly attenuated LPS-induced expression of inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 6F-I). Notably, PARP7 overexpression did not further reduce cytokine levels in TBK1-silenced cells (Fig. 6F-I). These findings demonstrate that PARP7 suppresses TBK1 activity and the TBK1-mediated inflammatory response through its catalytic residue H532 in renal tubule epithelial cells upon LPS stimulation.

Discussion

In this study, we elucidated that renal tubular epithelial cell-derived PARP7 protects the kidneys against LPS-induced inflammatory responses and acute injuries. Through transcriptomic and scRNA-seq analyses, we found that upregulated PARP7 expression in renal tubular epithelial cells negatively regulates the release of inflammatory cytokines, potentially representing a protective feedback mechanism under LPS-induced pathological conditions. Mechanistically, we demonstrated that PARP7 interacts with TBK1 and mediates the ADP-ribosylation of TBK1 via the catalytic residue H532, thereby suppressing the downstream TBK1-mediated inflammatory signaling pathway. Collectively, our findings reveal that renal tubular epithelial cell-derived PARP7 attenuates inflammation by promoting ADP-ribosylation of TBK1, whereas PARP7 gene knockout exacerbates LPS-induced acute kidney injury (Fig. 6J).

LPS-induced AKI is characterized by a robust inflammatory response involving a complex interplay among various cell types, particularly renal tubule epithelial cells and immune cells (Zuk and Bonventre, 2016). Among these, tubular epithelial cells play a pivotal role in renal functional recovery due to their robust regenerative capacity (Li et al. 2022, 2024; Xie et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2022). Our single-cell sequencing data also revealed that while PARP7 is expressed in macrophages, renal proximal tubular epithelial cells, intercalated cells, and mesangial cells, its expression is most pronounced in the proximal tubular epithelium. By integrating in vivo and in vitro functional experiments, we further verified the crucial role of PARP7 in the renal proximal tubular epithelial cells. In addition, accumulating evidence has demonstrated that the inflammatory response in renal tubular epithelial cells is a critical regulator of AKI pathogenesis (Lu et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2025). Upon activation, renal tubular epithelial cells produce and release a variety of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, including TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β (Li et al. 2024). These signaling molecules initiate local inflammatory responses within kidney tissue and drive a cascade of pathological events that exacerbate renal injury (Akcay et al. 2009). Therefore, targeting inflammation in renal tubular epithelial cells may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for AKI.

PARP7 is a member of the PARP family and functions as a key negative regulator of type I interferon signaling, playing a critical role in modulating inflammatory and immune responses (Gozgit et al. 2021; Jeltema et al. 2025). To date, inhibitors targeting PARP1 and PARP2—such as Olaparib, Niraparib, and Talazoparib—have been successfully approved for clinical use in cancer therapy (Roskoski 2025). However, the PARP7 inhibitor RBN-2397 has not yet entered clinical application, and potential off-target toxicity remains a concern that requires rigorous evaluation during drug development (Qiu et al. 2025). In our study, by integrating RNA-seq data analysis with expression profiling of the PARP7 family, we identified PARP7 as a potential beneficial regulator of LPS-induced AKI, suggesting that the use of PARP7 inhibitors may carry a potential risk of worsening renal function. Thus, we overexpressed PARP7 in proximal tubule cells and demonstrated that PARP7 overexpression ameliorated LPS-induced renal dysfunction, inflammation, and histopathological damage, suggesting that cell-type-specific delivery of PARP7 via AAV vectors may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for AKI. In addition, this disease-restricted expression pattern renders PARP7 a promising target for selective inhibition with reduced potential for off-target effects.

The cGAS-STING-IFN-I pathway has been established as a central mediator of LPS-induced acute kidney injury(Gong et al. 2025; Jiao et al. 2025), and PARP7 functions as a key negative regulator of cGAS-STING-IFN-I signaling. Mechanistically, PARP7 suppresses IFN-I production by ADP-ribosylating TBK1 downstream of cGAS-STING activation, thereby inhibiting TBK1 phosphorylation. TBK1 is a pivotal kinase that regulates cellular processes such as innate immunity (Zhang et al. 2022), autophagy (Sparrer et al. 2017), and stress responses (Kim et al. 2025). Its critical involvement in rhabdomyolysis-induced acute kidney injury (AKI) (Baatarjav et al. 2022), renal fibrosis (Long et al. 2025), and diabetic kidney disease (Zang et al. 2022) has been recently documented. Moreover, the ubiquitination of TBK1 have been shown to significantly influence AKI progression (Dong et al. 2022). In this study, we performed both endogenous and exogenous interaction assays and demonstrated that PARP7 physically interacts with TBK1, mediates its ADP-ribosylation, and thereby suppresses TBK1-dependent downstream inflammatory signaling in LPS-stimulated renal tubular epithelial cells. Given that direct inhibition of TBK1 may lead to broad off-target effects due to its diverse physiological functions, targeting PARP7 offers a more selective alternative. Notably, PARP7 is expressed at low levels in most normal tissues but is selectively upregulated or aberrantly activated under specific pathological conditions. This disease-restricted expression pattern positions PARP7 as a promising target for the indirect and context-specific modulation of the TBK1 pathway, minimizing potential toxicity while maintaining therapeutic efficacy. Moreover, given its enzymatic activity as a mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase, PARP7 is likely to target additional substrates beyond TBK1, potentially contributing to broader protective effects in kidney injury. To date, identified substrates of PARP7 include TBK1 (Gozgit et al. 2021; Jeltema et al. 2025), hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) (Zhang et al. 2020), and FOS-related antigen 1 (FRA1) (Manetsch et al. 2023). To systematically uncover such substrates and delineate PARP7’s full spectrum of renal protective mechanisms, we plan to employ affinity purification coupled with mass spectrometry in future studies.

Our study still exists some potential limitations. Here, we mixed kidneys from multiple mice, rather than performing per-animal single-cell RNA sequencing, affects the assessment of inter-individual variabilit. This mixing strategy was adopted primarily to achieve sufficient cell yield for robust library construction and deep sequencing coverage, which is essential for comprehensive profiling of rare or low-abundance renal cell types. Importantly, histopathological evaluation of the same mixed tissue batch revealed minimal intra-group heterogeneity in both WT and Parp7-KO mice, supporting the biological consistency of the mixed samples. In addition, although PARP7 has shown a protective effect on the kidneys in both in vivo and in vitro models of LPS-induced inflammatory kidney injury, its role in other models of acute kidney injury has not been confirmed. Here, we initially investigated the role of PARP7 in two additional models of renal tubular epithelial cell injury, such as H/R and Ang II stimulation. However, its role has not been investigated in kidney-specific in vivo models.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study reveals a potentially important mechanism underlying LPS-induced AKI. PARP7, a multifunctional ADP-ribosyl transferase, physically interacts with TBK1, mediates its ADP-ribosylation, and thereby suppresses the TBK1-mediated downstream inflammatory response, ultimately alleviating LPS-induced AKI. Our findings highlight its significant involvement in renal pathophysiology, suggesting a novel therapeutic target for inflammatory kidney injury.

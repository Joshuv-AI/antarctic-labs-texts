Design of allergen-specific inhibitors against shrimp-induced allergic response
Authors: Baksun Kim, Emily K. Bromley, Jaeho Shin, Basar Bilgicer
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Among food allergens, shrimp allergy is the most prevalent shellfish allergy in the United States.1 Tropomyosin is a muscle movement–regulating protein with high sequence homology (>93%) among different shrimp species, including Penaeus aztecus (Pen a) 1, Penaeus indicus 1, Penaeus monodon 1, and others.2 With regard to Pen a 1 allergy, tropomyosin (the major protein of brown shrimp) is recognized by up to 80% of subjects with shrimp allergy.3,4 Pen a 1 is a coiled-coil homodimer containing 2 identical parallel α-helices. The inherently stable structure of tropomyosin retains its allergenicity following processing.5

Despite ongoing efforts, clinical treatments in food allergy are used largely to relieve symptoms of allergic reactions after they occur. Omalizumab is the only medication approved by the US Food and Drug Administration to prevent the onset of severe, persistent IgE-mediated food allergy reactions.6 Omalizumab, however, is not allergen specific, rendering even nonallergenic IgE ineffective for immune system surveillance.7 In previous publications, we reported a preventive therapy, namely, use of covalent heterobivalent inhibitors (cHBIs), which have high clinical potential for specific, effective, long-lasting inhibition of allergic reactions.8

In this study, a comprehensive literature investigation revealed 6 ubiquitously cited Pen a 1 linear epitopes (SWISS-MODEL Q3Y8M6) (Fig 1, A).9 First, we identified the Pen a 1 concentration necessary to initiate robust degranulation from SX38 rat basophilic leukemia (RBL) cells primed with plasma from a patient with shrimp allergy in vitro (Fig 1, A). To prevent degranulation, epitope–specific IgE (sIgE) interactions must be inhibited, restricting cross-linking of sIgE-FcϵRI complexes. Long-lasting specific, selective inhibition of epitope-sIgE interactions owing to covalent conjugation with sIgE is paramount to cHBI design (Fig 1, B).10 Briefly, cHBIs comprise 3 binding moieties: (1) an epitope specifically targeting the antigen binding site (ABS), (2) a moderate-affinity ligand interacting with the nucleotide binding site (NBS), and (3) a reactive functionality that covalently conjugates to the primary amine group of lysine residue adjacent to the NBS. Indole-3-butyric acid has moderate NBS affinity (∼low μM), enabling bivalent binding and increasing the avidity of cHBIs.10 The reactive functional molecule isothiocyanate was chosen for its slow association kinetics under physiologic conditions (pH 7.4) and biocompatibility.10

As our molecular design includes ligands capable of binding to both the ABS and the NBS, binding of one moiety increases the local concentration of the other, thereby promoting its subsequent bivalent binding. Consequently, isothiocyanate is brought into proximity of a lysine residue positioned by the NBS, leading to an irreversible conjugation reaction (Fig 1, B). Because of the specificity of the ABS and ABS-ligand interaction in addition to the rapid dissociation of the NBS and NBS ligand, nonspecific isothiocyanate reactions are physiologically unlikely, making cHBI safe and effective for prophylactic use. An assembly of cHBIs mimicking each of the 6 epitopes was synthesized, purified, and characterized as previously described.8,10

Cellular degranulation inhibition assays were used to compare how effectively single epitope-specific cHBIs and cHBI combinations inhibited Pen a 1–mediated degranulation (Fig 1, C and D). Briefly, SX38 RBL cells were plated at 30,000 cells/well and incubated overnight. The following day, the cells were primed with 10% (vol/vol) plasma from a patient with shrimp allergy or control plasma and subsequently incubated with cHBIs overnight. Later, the cells were washed to remove unreacted cHBIs and then challenged with Pen a 1.10 Up to 45% inhibition of Pen a 1–mediated degranulation was achieved by any single epitope targeting cHBI at 3000 nM, and efficacy did not differ statistically between targeted epitopes (Fig 1, C). Individual cHBIs block only a single epitope-sIgE interaction, without cross-reactivity with any other sIgE. Because inhibition of a single interaction is insufficient to fully eliminate cellular response, a cocktail of cHBIs was necessary to achieve more substantial inhibition of a Pen a 1–mediated degranulation.

In all, 6 cHBIs were synthesized, targeting each of the aforementioned epitope-specific IgEs. Subsequently, cocktails containing equimolar inhibitors were formulated according to our epitope immunogenicity rankings (data not included), ranging in concentration from 375 to 3000 nM. The most efficacious combination achieved up to 77% Pen a 1–mediated inhibition of degranulation (Fig 1, D). Fig 1, D shows that inhibition of Pen a 1–mediated degranulation by cHBIEpi.3 alone did not differ significantly from that of cocktails including 2, 3, or 4 cHBIs at any concentration. Nevertheless, compared with cHBIEpi.3 alone, the combination of 5 cHBIs (red) resulted in statistically significant enhancement of degranulation inhibition at 1500 and 3000 nM. This enhancement demonstrates that each cHBI targets a unique sIgE, playing a crucial role in degranulation. Most importantly, the combination of 6 cHBIs at concentrations of 3000 nM each resulted in the highest inhibition efficacy (green).

Furthermore, the combination of 6 cHBIs achieved nearly complete Pen a 1–mediated degranulation inhibition when the concentration of allergen was reduced by half (Fig 2, A and B). The incremental efficacy of the cHBI cocktail increased in a dose-dependent manner at concentrations of both 0.5 and 1 nM Pen a 1, demonstrating a reduction in immune response in both cases, with a maximum efficacy of approximately 95% inhibition of degranulation with use of a concentration of 3000 nM of each of the 6 inhibitors (Fig 2, B). To confirm that the cHBI cocktail’s inhibition efficacy was achieved by specific binding only to shrimp sIgE, we performed a control experiment using cells primed with plasma from a patient with peanut allergy and challenged by crude peanut extract (CPE) (Fig 2, C). The degranulation intensity of cells primed with plasma from a patient with peanut allergy challenged with CPE was compared with and without the cocktail of 6 cHBIs at 3000 nM. Cells were challenged by CPE in concentrations ranging from 0.01 to 1000 ng/mL (Fig 2, C). In the experiments reported in this communication, our goal was to engineer effective inhibitors of Pen a 1–mediated allergic response. cHBIs were engineered to block epitope-sIgE interactions, and their inhibition efficacy was evaluated in vitro.

The most effective Pen a 1–mediated degranulation inhibition (∼77%-95%) was accomplished with a cocktail of 6 Pen a 1 cHBIs (Figs 1, D and 2, B). To completely inhibit Pen a 1 epitope–sIgE binding, it is critical to prevent IgE recognition of weakly and strongly immunogenic epitopes. Notably, cHBIEpi.3 alone achieved up to 45% inhibition of Pen a 1–mediated degranulation (Fig 1, C). This degree of suppression of the immune response may still represent a meaningful therapeutic benefit and may be potentially lifesaving for prophylactic treatment of shrimp allergy.

Nonetheless, relative sIgE levels vary by patient, which could require different combinations or concentrations of cHBIs to achieve complete inhibition of degranulation. At this point, additional patient samples are needed to complete a more robust epitope analysis. Elucidation of immunodominant epitopes would allow development of efficacious cHBI cocktails for a broader patient population. Supporting evidence with physiologically relevant in vivo models could additionally prove cHBI safety and efficacy.

Adjuvant radiotherapy for ypN0 breast cancer patients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Hyung-Sik Lee, Sungmin Kim, Hyunjung Kim, Ki-Jung Ahn, Hye Won Park, Yunseon Choi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study was performed to analyze whether adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) can improve the survival outcome for pathological node-negative (ypN0) breast cancer patients who underwent surgery after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC).MethodsIn this meta-analysis, we reviewed papers related to adjuvant RT in ypN0 patients after NAC published between January 2010 and November 2024 using PubMed, EMBASE via Elsevier, Cochrane Library with keywords: “breast neoplasms,” “neoadjuvant therapy,” and “radiotherapy.” The effectiveness of post-mastectomy RT (PMRT) after mastectomy and regional nodal irradiation (RNI) after breast-conserving surgery (BCS) was evaluated. The end point of this study was overall survival (OS).ResultsWe selected 11 retrospective papers for this meta-analysis out of 4,164 English full-text articles. There were 7 PMRT-related and 5 RNI-related papers (1 duplicated) for final analysis. Overall, PMRT in ypN0 patients improved OS (hazard ratio [HR], 0.862; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.766–0.972; P = 0.015) after mastectomy. Moreover, OS (HR, 0.774; 95% CI, 0.600–0.999; P = 0.049) was also improved by RNI for ypN0 patients after BCS.ConclusionFor patients with ypN0 breast cancer who have undergone NAC and surgery, PMRT positively affected the survival outcome of patients. Therefore, it is necessary to undergo adjuvant RT to improve survival for ypN0 breast cancer patients.Keywords: Neoadjuvant therapy, Breast neoplasms, Radiotherapy, Overall survival

INTRODUCTION

The National Surgical Adjuvant Breast and Bowel Project (NSABP) B-18 study in 1998 showed that neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) and adjuvant chemotherapy have equivalent therapeutic effects in breast cancer patients. In addition, the NSABP B-27 study reported by Bear et al. [1] showed that the pathological complete response (pCR) rate increased upon addition of paclitaxel to doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide chemotherapy in breast cancer patients. Moreover, breast cancer patients who have undergone NAC are recommended to receive adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) based on both disease stage at diagnosis (before NAC) and pathology results after NAC. In this regard, the necessity of adjuvant RT for pathologic lymph node-negative (ypN0) breast cancer patients after NAC is controversial.

Meanwhile, recent studies showed that axillary lymph node dissection can be replaced with RT after sentinel lymph node dissection [2,3] in cT1-2N1 patients, thereby highlighting the role of RT after NAC. If the number of cases of breast-conserving surgery (BCS) after NAC increases instead of mastectomy, then RT indications seem to increase. Recently, a new large-scale study showing positive effects on regional nodal irradiation (RNI) has been added. Therefore, we attempted to conduct this meta-analysis.

After total mastectomy, post-mastectomy RT (PMRT) is selectively performed on patients who are judged to have a high risk of recurrence, such as those with a large primary tumor size or multiple regional nodal metastases at the time of diagnosis. Conversely, most patients receive adjuvant RT after BCS, and if regional nodal metastases are found at the time of diagnosis, the addition of RNI is considered.

There has been no clear answer to the question of whether adjuvant RT is necessary when a favorable lymph node response to NAC in breast cancer has been achieved (ypN0). Therefore, in this study, we analyzed whether the PMRT after NAC could improve survival outcomes. Moreover, additional meta-analysis was conducted to determine whether RNI could improve the survival outcomes after NAC.

METHODS

We performed an electronic search in PubMed, EMBASE via Elsevier, and Cochrane Library for research papers published between January 1, 2010, and November 1, 2024, using the terms “neoadjuvant therapy,” “breast neoplasms,” and “radiotherapy.” This meta-analysis that did not use individual-level data was exempt from the requirement for institutional review board review. A total of 4,164 related articles were obtained. For the eligibility test, we checked the abstract and full text of these articles for terms such as ypN0 and RNI, PMRT, overall survival (OS). We reviewed 47 retrieved papers related to adjuvant RT in ypN0 patients after NAC. For the inclusion of this meta-analysis, the studies wherein breast cancer patients achieved pN0 after NAC were preferentially selected. Overall, 11 papers with detailed descriptions of hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for OS were selected for meta-analysis. There were 7 PMRT-related and 5 RNI-related papers (1 duplicated for analyses). Fig. 1 indicates the flow diagram according to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses).

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) chart for study selection. HR, hazard ratio; OS, overall survival.

The inclusion criteria were breast cancer studies about NAC and adjuvant RT. The exclusion criteria were studies without accurate data of HR and 95% CI for OS.

For data analysis, MedCalc statistical software (version 19.2.0 bv, MedCalc Software Ltd.) software was used for data analysis. For time-to-event analysis, HR (95% CI) was converted to log-scale, and the generic inverse variance method was used. To be specific, using HR and CI reported in each study, ln(HR) and ln(standard error) were calculated for pooled meta-analysis. Pooled effect was reported on the basis of fixed-effect models. Indications for high level of inter-study heterogeneity were P < 0.05 on the chi-square test, and I2 > 50%. Egger test and funnel plot test were implemented to assess any publication bias.

RESULTS

Post-mastectomy radiotherapy for pN0 breast cancer patients

Table 1 shows the results of studies on PMRT after NAC in ypN0 breast cancer patients. Le Scodan et al. [4] analyzed the treatment of 134 patients with stage II–III ypN0 breast cancer after NAC and found that PMRT did not significantly affect OS. Shim et al. [5] reported similar results. Their study analyzed the role of PMRT in ypN0 patients after NAC and showed that PMRT did not improve OS. Furthermore, Liu et al. [6] analyzed the effect of PMRT in patients with pN0 breast cancer who underwent NAC. In the initial analysis for all patients, PMRT had no benefit in OS. However, further analysis using propensity score matching showed that the 5-year OS increased after PMRT in patients with a large primary tumor. Rusthoven et al. [7] performed a large-scale retrospective study using the National Cancer Database (NCDB). They analyzed ypN0 patients from 2003 to 2011 who were initially diagnosed with clinically node metastases. PMRT contributed to OS improvement in ypN0 patients after NAC and mastectomy. Recently, Miyashita et al. [8] reported a large-scale study using the Japanese breast cancer registry data in which PMRT did not improve OS in ypN0 patients who underwent NAC and mastectomy between 2004 and 2009. Sayan et al.’s recent study [9] analyzed the effect of PMRT on OS in patients initially presenting with node-positive breast cancer who were changed to pN0 after NAC. This study did not demonstrate a survival benefit of PMRT. A new study by Dai et al. [10] also showed that PMRT did not improve OS in ypN0 patients after NAC. In their study, triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients accounted for 16.4% of the total.

Regional nodal irradiation for ypN0 breast cancer patients

Table 2 shows the studies related to the effectiveness of RNI in ypN0 breast cancer patients after NAC. The study conducted by Daveau et al. [11] analyzed the effect of RNI addition at RT in ypN0 patients who underwent BCS and NAC and were initially cN0-N2 patients. In this study, OS was not affected by RNI. The report by Noh et al. [12] was based on the results of a Korean multicenter trial, an adjuvant RT study of ypN0 patients during BCS after NAC. In their study, TNBC patients accounted for 23.8% of the total.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we hypothesized that a favorable response to NAC affects the RT outcome, and this can be used to select the method of adjuvant RT. Based on the survival outcome, this study showed that PMRT was necessary for ypN0 patients after NAC. Additionally, RNI seems to be helpful for ypN0 breast cancer patients who underwent NAC and BCS. This study, consequently, increases the need for adjuvant RT in ypN0 patients.

The application of RT can be different for each breast cancer pathologic subtype and degree of therapeutic responsiveness. Several studies that were published recently analyzed treatment outcomes based on the molecular subtypes. Previous meta-analysis by Nikyar et al. [15] did not show survival benefit in pCR patients after NAC. The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) suggests that the need for RT should be based on the highest stage of the disease, which can be determined from the tumor’s characteristics before chemotherapy or the pathology results after chemotherapy [16]. Patients with residual nodal disease after NAC should receive RNI according to AJCC guidelines. On one hand, the recently revised St. Gallen International Conference guidelines recommend that RT should be performed for patients with pT3N0 TNBC, even for pCR patients [17]. Additionally, a recent retrospective study by Keilty et al. [18] showed that TNBC, stage III disease, and lack of pCR were associated with detrimental DFS in breast cancer patients who underwent NAC, surgery, and RT. Recurrence risk may be lower for patients who experience pCR after NAC. However, the necessity for adjuvant RT for those patients remains as ever. Taken together, we suggest that high-risk patients, such as those with the TNBC subtype [19], lymphovascular invasion [20], large tumor size [21], and high pathologic grade tumor, need to be treated actively with adjuvant RT, even achieve pN0 after NAC [18,22].

This study showed that adjuvant RT was useful even in ypN0 patients after NAC. According to currently reported studies, RNI or PMRT is not mandatory in ypN0 patients [20,23]. Moreover, the effectiveness of adjuvant RT in ypN1 stage patients is still controversial, although several studies have already reported results [8,24,25]. Nevertheless, this study strongly shows that adjuvant RT is helpful even in ypN0 patients. When NAC is effective, adjuvant RT is still required for survival benefit, according to this study results. Taken together, adjuvant RT should be used actively in breast cancer patients after NAC in the future.

There are several limitations. All the papers included were retrospective studies. There may be a selection bias in the treatment application because of the considerable level of heterogeneity of included studies. The different distribution of pCR or molecular subtypes may influence adjuvant RT effects for survival of the included studies. In addition, the inter-study heterogeneity level of the RNI meta-analysis was substantial. Therefore, RNI meta-analysis result has the potential for an invalid conclusion because of its high level of inter-study heterogeneity. Therefore, it is difficult to fully rely on the conclusions of this study. Several prospective studies evaluating management of the regional nodes in the setting of NAC are ongoing, and we expect that they will give us a clear answer for RNI effect. However, we believe that this study also provides new insight into the need for adjuvant RT for ypN0 patients.

In conclusion, in patients with ypN0, adjuvant RT appears to significantly improve survival. Therefore, the RT method should be applied actively in breast cancer patients after NAC, even in cases with favorable nodal responses. Moreover, it is necessary to accurately estimate the recurrence risk for each patient and apply individualized RT accordingly.

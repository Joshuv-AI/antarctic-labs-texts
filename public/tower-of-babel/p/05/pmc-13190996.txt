MHD Casson nanofluid flow over a vertical stretchable sheet saturated with a porous medium: a parametric approach for sensitive analysis
Authors: Khurram Javid, Noreen Sher Akbar, Shakil Shaiq, Abbas Khan, Wasim Jamshed, Syed M Hussain, Kamel Guedri, Hijaz Ahmad, Assmaa Abd-Elmonem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeAfter being motivated by the diverse applications of blood rheology, nanotechnology, magnetic field, chemical reaction, solar radiation, and non-Darcy porous media in nano-industrial, medical, and chemical engineering domains. The current computational study aims to numerically examine the influences of velocity slip, internal thermal generation or absorption, chemical reactions, and thermal radiation on magneto-hydrodynamic blood-based nanofluid flow with thermo-Brownian motion through an extending interface within a high-permeability medium. Furthermore, the sensitive analysis of flow features with respect to the independent flow parameters is considered.Design/methodology/approachSuitable similarity equations are employed to convert the partial differential equations into ordinary differential equations together with their boundary constraints. The NDSolve method in Mathematica 11.0 is employed to numerically analyze the flow model, yielding data for the stream function, velocity profile, frictional force coefficient, temperature profile, concentration profile, local Nusselt number, and Sherwood number across several rheological parameters.Main findingsA boundary slip diminishes momentum transmission from the fluid to the surface; when velocity slip escalates, the velocity profile declines. The intensity of the thermal boundary layer escalates with the thermal Grashof number. The temperature distribution is exacerbated by the influence of radiation. As the Brownian parameter grows, the nanofluid temperature intensifies. The chemical reaction parameter substantially affects the enhancement of both skin friction and the Sherwood number. The Nusselt number is enhanced by increasing the thermal Grashof number. The sensitivity analysis indicates that the chemical reaction and concentration Grashof number significantly influence the improvement of rheological properties.ApplicationsThe results of this work are relevant for regulating film thickness, chemical vapour deposition, drug delivery systems, and process optimization.Keywords: Nanofluid flow, Boundary layer, Heat and mass transfer, Chemical reaction, Permeability medium, Solar radiation, Magnetohydrodynamics

Introduction

In the current era, industrial applications heavily depend on nanofluids. Due to its broad use in contemporary engineering frameworks and manufacturing processes, the analysis of heat transport in nanofluids has become a popular area of investigation. A fluid containing nanoparticles—typically metals, oxides, carbides, or carbon nanotubes—is known as a nanofluid [1, 2]. It is evident that in various fluids with low heat conductivity, for example, ethylene glycol, oil, and water, conventional heat transfer methods are ineffective. When it comes to managing cooling requirements in different thermal systems, nanofluids are quite beneficial. Nanofluids have tremendous potential for use in a wide range of heat transfer solutions because of their excellent thermal behaviour, such as microelectronics, pharmaceutical processes, thermal therapy for cancer treatment, chemical and metallurgical processes, power generation, cooling, heating, ventilation, hybrid-powered engines, machining, and boiler flue gas temperature reduction [3, 4].

Advancements in biomedical and nano-fluidic technologies have driven efforts to model blood-based nanofluids, enhancing thermal and therapeutic properties for medical applications. These fluids are of great interest both for industrial applications, due to their high heat transfer capacity, and for researchers exploring new scientific areas. Izadi et al. [5] studied how the Darcy number and viscous dissipation parameter affect MHD nanofluid flow, finding that increasing the dissipation parameter raises the temperature of liquid cooling but decreases heat transfer efficiency. Shehzad and colleagues [6] further examined convection magnetized flow of nanoliquid, focusing on the thermal component of the flow and the constant effectiveness of the Lorentz force. Zhang and Xu [7] previously applied a replica of Gaussian process regression, developed via machine learning, to study the augmentation of thermal conductivity in certain nano-liquids. Power law nanoliquid flow across a stretched Riga sheet was examined by Abbas et al. [8] in relation to thermophoretic force, frictional heating, and Brownian diffusion effects. Additional recent literature on this subject includes Refs. [9–13].

The majority of scholars were more interested in the research of BL flow over a flexible surface due to extensive practice in numerous areas of manufacturing and engineering. Some of the instances of a huge list of applications are textile and paper production, wire portraiture, hot rolling, and extrusion of polymer plates. The magnetite micro-polar fluid flow along a nonlinear stretched leaky sheet with convective boundary conditions, Ohmic heating, and viscosity dissipation was examined by Patel and Singh [14]. The properties of radiation on the nanoliquid flow along a stretchy sheet were shown by Ghasemi and Hatami [15]. After performing numerical computations with the assistance of the differential quadrature method, the authors concluded that the thermal energy profile is proportional to the thermal Biot numbers. Abbas et al. [16] discussed the influence of thermal slip condition on the 2nd order micropolar motion at a stagnant point over an elongating interface. Additionally, the effects of free stream velocity are addressed by the authors. Sedki [17] described the influence of thermophoretic, Brownian diffusion forces, radiative thermal heating, and chemically reactive features on thermal and solutal transfer in a leaky surface.

A substance with interconnected spaces (pores) that allow fluids (gases or liquids) to pass through is called a porous medium. These pores, which permit fluid movement and exchange inside the material, are usually dispersed throughout a solid matrix. The impact of electromagnetic energy with a radian on MHD motion in porous substances is discussed. Chaudhary et al. [18] reduced the surface gradient as the permeability parameter increased. As a result of their potential for the enhancement of convection thermal transfer, nanofluids in permeable media have been extensively explored. The magneto-hydrodynamic radiative nanoliquid flow in a penetrable material was considered by Jafar et al. [19] recently. For every upsurge in the permeability constraint, they found a corresponding enhancement in the heat transfer rate. Shatnawi et al. [20] have examined how a magnetic Casson hybrid nanoliquid motion through a leaky stretched interface is affected by the nonlinear radiation, heat generation, and slip conditions. Ahmed et al. [21] theoretically examined the uniform flow of viscous nanoliquid over a flat and linear extending sheet under the physical influence of a magnetic field and a non-Darcy porous material. Additionally, the fluid friction force and heat transfer rate near the boundary surface are also incorporated by using numerical data in tabular form. A few dynamic contributions related to the porous medium and nanofluid are mentioned to enhance the reader's knowledge [22–27].

Heat radiation is a fundamental heat transfer process, together with conduction and convection. Unlike the others, however, radiation can occur without the existence of a medium because it is the act of transferring energy in the form of electromagnetic waves [28]. This form of heat transfer is of great significance under the conditions of high temperature and is utilized in fields like the nuclear power sector and space-related systems (e.g., satellites). One of the key parameters in characterizing the relative amount of thermal to kinetic energy in a system is the Eckert number. It is given as the relation of kinetic energy to the enthalpy change within the system. A boost in the Eckert value indicates an upward trend in the fluid flow's kinetic energy. This increased energy upturns viscous dissipation, which results in a notable increase in thermal energy production in the entire system. As has been discussed by Reddy et al. [29] and Yahya et al. [30], the fluid temperature rises when the Eckert value rises, signifying the conversion of mechanical and electrical energy into thermal energy. Khashi’ie et al. [31] noted that while variations in the Eckert number and radiation parameters may not significantly influence the critical Reynolds number (i.e., they are not directly linked to flow acceleration), they do contribute to a lessening in the overall heat transfer rate to the method. Kareem et al. conducted a numerical investigation of tangent hyperbolic non-Newtonian fluid flow in spinning porous materials soaked with the non-uniform thermal power-law models with the physical impact of conventional heat gain, magnetism force, and heat radiation [32]. The temperature profile was improved by thermal radiation. Because of the porous medium's resistance, the velocity profile is reduced. The mobility of a hybrid nanofluid over a spinning sphere under a magnetic field, non-linear radiative flux, Brownian and thermophoretic diffusion has all been numerically demonstrated by Yusuf and friends [33]. In order to maximize the ideal circumstances for minimizing energy losses, their flow analysis also incorporates entropy generation and the Bejan number. Yusuf and Gbadeyan [34] talked about how the hybrid nanoliquid flow through a convectively heated stretched interface is affected by quadratic thermal radiation, a magnetic field, and a slip condition. They employed water as a base fluid and and nanoparticle concentrations in Eyring-Powell fluid for their flow analysis. They observed that the concentration of nanoparticles and slip conditions improve the thermal performance of the flow system. On the other hand, the magnetic resistance affects the flow. Some remarkable contributions related to the physical effects of the thermal radiation nanofluid flow over numerous regimes are cited [35–40].

The no-slip hypothesis is derived from Navier–Stokes theory, which previously assumed that the fluid velocity along a solid wall merely matches the wall's velocity. However, Stokes' law explains that the density of the fluid determines whether slip occurs; if the density is higher, the slip velocity increases, resulting in a larger velocity gradient between the fluid and the solid wall. The no-slip requirement is not always applicable to all fluid flows, as Ayub et al. [41] have pointed out. In several real-world applications, including microfluidic devices, hard disk drives, and prosthetic heart valves, there is a discrepancy in velocity, as flow behaviour at tiny length scales diverges from classical predictions. When particles of gas strike a surface, they do not instantly reflect, according to Chen and Tian [42]. The fluid elements briefly stick to the substrate before bouncing because of intermolecular interactions among gas particles and the surface molecule. The slip phenomenon results from this short residence period, which causes a velocity lag close to the boundary. Flow close to the stagnation region of a shrinking/stretching Riga plate with slip and thermal energy effects was studied by Hussin et al. [43]. Velocity slip is found to lower the skin friction constant on shrinking surfaces, whereas thermal slip lowers the local Nusselt number. It has also been noted that velocity slip lowers the fluid's temperature and increases flow velocity. Abbas et al. [44] also studied a micropolar hybrid nanoliquid flow towards a Riga plate with slip conditions and inferred that velocity slip decelerates the overall fluid velocity. Several other researchers also studied slip effects through different configurations. Ali et al. [45], for instance, studied heat transfer in Cu–TiO2/EO hybrid nanofluid and emphasized the influence of slip on thermal characteristics. Jamshed et al. [46] studied slip assets on non-Newtonian nanoliquid flow over a porous plate in solar collectors, where heat transfer was enhanced considerably through slip conditions. Similarly, Aman et al. [47] studied MHD stagnation-point flow with slip and observed a reduction in skin friction with a clear enhancement in heat transfer rate. Adesanya [48] deliberated the effects of velocity slip and temperature jump on the unsteady natural convection rheology through a vertical porous channel. A theoretical study of the boundary layer motion of the second-grade fluid through a penetrable surface under magnetic force, Brownian–Thermophoresis, and partial slip effects is studied by Bhargava and Goyal [49]. Some remarkable works about the physical consequences of the velocity slip across numerous regimes [50–52].

Another key parameter used in the current investigation is the chemical reaction. Some research performed dynamic contributions in this direction, where they considered the role of chemical reaction parameters on the BL motion of realistic liquids, which is given in the following citations [53–56]. Pal and Talukdar [53] examined how mixed convection, thermal radiation, and chemical reactions affect the magnetic motion of a viscous liquid via a vertical leaky interface located within a porous domain. The authors also address the scientific impacts of Ohmic heat and thermal radiation. In another study, Pal and Talukdar [53] conducted an analytical investigation using the perturbation method to examine the problem of time-dependent mixed convection with thermal radiation and chemical reaction in the MHD BL motion of a Newtonian fluid over a vertical permeable plate. At the porous interface, a slip boundary condition is used by the authors. A numerical simulation of the impacts of double diffusive on the time-dependent convective rheology over a stretching sheet saturated with a porous medium under thermal radiation, chemical reaction, and viscous effects is discussed by Pal and Mondal [55]. Using the Boussinesq approximation to reduce density changes, Samaila and Jha [56] conducted a thorough numerical evaluation to investigate the coupled impacts of heat radiation and chemical reactions on a typical boundary layer flow close to a vertically substrate. A computational investigation of unsteady bioconvective and non-Newtonian flow across a horizontally stretched cylinder encased in a porous medium was conducted by Ahmed et al. [57], taking into account the impacts of binary chemical processes, thermophoretic-based transportation, Soret–Dufour—kinetic mechanisms, and Joule resistive heating. They used Maxwell fluid as a non-Newtonian fluid in their flow analysis. They noticed that the micro-organism profile is affected by the Peclet number. In order to examine combined heat and solute transportation in magnetohydrodynamic Maxwell nanofluid flow across a stretched cylinder, Ahmed et al. [58] created a quasi-analytical approach. Their establishment takes into consideration the impacts of Joule resistive heating, internal thermal heat generation, and chemical reactivity kinetics while combining Buongiorno’s model with the non-Fourier Fick’s diffusion models. They noticed an inverse pattern in the flow behaviour by growing the Maxwell constraint, and an increasing trend is noted in the mass and energy transfer by increasing the Joule heating effects. Some scientific contributions to applications of chemical reactions and nanofluids are given in the references [59, 60].

From previous literature, the combined influences of velocity slip, thermal radiation, heat generation, and chemical reactions with thermo-Brownian motion on rheology over a vertically extending interface saturated with a high-permeability medium are missing. The current work on magneto-hydrodynamic blood-based nanofluid flow looks at all these effects together. Additionally, the current formulation addresses the effects of certain embedded characteristics on the rheological aspects by using a sensitive method. Utilizing a parametric formula, we have applied the sensitivity technique. There is a very small body of material accessible in this area [61, 62]. Governing equations are derived by using Cartesian coordinates. PDEs are reduced to ODEs with boundary conditions using the appropriate similarity equations. The results for the velocity, concentration, stream function, local Nusselt number, frictional force coefficient, and temperature are obtained through computer analysis of the proposed model using MATHEMATICA 11.0 software.

Mathematical insights into nanofluid flow, heat transfer, and chemical interaction dynamics

The mathematical formulation describes the steady, incompressible flow of a Casson fluid past a vertical, porous, stretching surface in a non-Darcy porous medium, taking into account heat generation/absorption, magnetic fields, and chemical reactions. The system is designed in a two-dimensional Cartesian structure with the Y-axis perpendicular to the plate surface and the X-axis aligned with the stretching of the plates. The flow is restricted to the area above the surface since the fluid occupies the half-space . The flow in the -direction is continuously subjected to an applied magnetic field, . When this magnetic field is present, the fluid experiences MHD effects that affect its temperature and velocity distributions. There is an assumption that the magnetic Reynolds number is quite low. Since the induced magnetic field is presumably insignificant compared to the applied magnetic field (MF), we take the external field's effect into account. A collection of PDEs is usually used to represent the governing equations for this kind of flow, which may be obtained using fundamental fluid dynamics concepts. These equations in the flow show how mass, momentum, energy, and species are conserved. Absorption heat generation is taken into account in the energy equation. The energy equation shows this as a factor on the right-hand side that impacts the thermal BL close to the surface. Heat absorption or generation can drastically change the fluid's temperature distribution, which affects rheology and flow. Chemical reactions, which are represented as a mass transfer phenomenon, also have an impact on the flow (Fig. 1).

Blood is often described as a Casson fluid because of its apparent yield stress and non-Newtonian behaviour. Physically, this is caused by the tendency of red blood cells to form motion-resistant rouleaux structures at low shear rates. As a result, flow requires a finite stress, which the Casson model accounts for. When this yield stress is exceeded, the aggregates break down, and blood flow more easily, displaying shear-thinning characteristics. The Casson model's transition from solid-like to fluid-like behaviour provides a useful and realistic representation of blood, particularly under microcirculatory and low-shear-flow situations.

In classical fluid mechanics, the no-slip condition means that the fluid velocity at the boundary wall is identical to the speed of the boundary wall. While in micro-vascular blood rheology and the transport of physiological liquids via nano-size blood arteries/vessels and tiny porous cells, these approximations failed, or, in other words, became invalid, due to physical factors such as RBC deformation and accumulation, and deceleration of viscous interactions at the tiny or micro-scale. Physically, the velocity slip put forth a tangential force at the boundary wall, which is more suitable for such types of circumstances. The interaction between the fluid and the wall is directly related to the value of δ. For weaker interaction, δ will be high. Therefore, the main goal of including velocity slip in the current mathematical study is to make our problem more physically realistic, especially the blood-based nanofluid rheology through micro-circulation and biomedical devices.

Molecular footprints of a traveling epidemic: CRF01_AE-cluster 5 (01_C5) might spread across Northeast and South China via middle-aged MSM
Authors: Wenxuan Yang, Bin Zhao, Mingming Kang, Minghui An, Wei Song, Xue Dong, Xin Li, Lu Wang, Jianmin Liu, Chenli Zheng, Lin Chen, Hao Li, Yan Zhang, Yongxia Gan, Wen Gan, Hui Zhang, Xiaoxu Han, Jin Zhao, Hong Shang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundHIV-1 CRF01_AE exhibits multiple distinct sub-subtypes (01_C1-C8) with different transmission dynamics across China. Understanding their geographical distribution, transmission patterns, and key affected populations is critical for optimizing targeted interventions.MethodsWe conducted a comprehensive molecular epidemiological study analyzing 4,011 partial pol sequences and demographic data from newly diagnosed CRF01_AE cases (2016–2019) in Shenyang (Northeast China) and Shenzhen (South China) by constructing the CRF01_AE molecular network in China (n = 6140). Sub-subtyping was performed using an online HIV subtyping tool and phylogenetic analysis. A molecular network was constructed based on genetic distance, and viral dispersal in large clusters (size≥10) in the network was assessed using Bayesian inference.Results01_C5 (46.7%), 01_C4 (37.2%), 01_C1 (8.1%), 01_C2 (2.3%), 01_C3 (0.3%), and 01_C8 (<0.1%) were detected, with 01_C5 (73.7%) dominating in Shenyang, and 01_C4 (53.0%) in Shenzhen. Molecular network analysis revealed 24 large clusters, including 12 01_C5 clusters (64.7%) and 10 01_C4 clusters (28.5%). Notably, 01_C5 accounted for 75.9% of the mixed large clusters containing sequences from both cities. Bayesian phylodynamic analysis indicated one-way transmission of 01_C5 from Shenyang to Shenzhen was driven by middle-aged MSM (30–49 years old).ConclusionOur findings provided a potential molecular epidemiological evidence for CRF01_AE transmission from Northeast China to South China, and highlighted the urgent need for targeted interventions focusing on middle-aged MSM to prevent cross-regional 01_C5 transmission. This study provided an example for applying traceability analysis based on the HIV molecular transmission network in guiding targeted public health interventions.

Introduction

HIV-1 CRF01_AE, a member of the HIV-1 M group, was the first identified circulating recombinant form (CRF) of HIV-1 [1]. CRF01_AE has since spread globally, with a documented presence in North America, Europe, Central and West Africa, Asia, and Australia, accounting for approximately 5.4% of the global HIV-1 epidemic [2]. CRF01_AE was particularly dominant in Southeast Asia, where it constituted approximately 68.8% of HIV-1 infections [2]. In China, CRF01_AE was one of the most prevalent strains, representing 32.1% of infections, and was endemic in all regions except Northwest China [3].

Previous studies have revealed that CRF01_AE has diversified into several distinct epidemic clusters in China, reflecting complex transmission dynamics among high-risk populations. Early research demonstrated that CRF01_AE was independently introduced into Chinese men who have sex with men (MSM) in the early to mid-1990s, subsequently evolving into two distinct clusters [4,5]. In 2013, seven CRF01_AE clusters were further identified through the comprehensive analysis of nearly full-length genome sequences [6]. Subsequent research in 2017 revealed five major epidemic clusters with distinct geographical and risk group distributions: Clusters 1–3 predominantly circulating among heterosexuals (HETs) and injecting drug users (IDUs) in southern and southwestern China, while Clusters 4–5 were primarily established in MSM in northern China, all originating from Southeast Asian introductions in the 1990s [7]. The five major epidemic clusters were geographically widespread, with each containing sequences detected in more than 10 provinces [7]. The latest national molecular epidemiological survey on HIV revealed cluster 4 increased markedly from 8.1% to 13.6% from 2004–2007 to 2020–2022, whereas clusters 1, 2, and 3 declined. In contrast, the proportion of cluster 5 remained stable over the same period [3]. Another recent study indicated that the evolution of clusters 1 and 2 into more virulent strains – evidenced by their significantly lower baseline CD4+ T-cell counts and faster decline compared to clusters 4, 5, and other subtypes – could be a potential factor behind their declining prevalence [8]. In 2024, the nomenclature for CRF01_AE sub-subtypes was updated, and 11 sub-subtypes (CRF01_AE-Cluster 1–11, 01_C1–C11) were defined based on 665 nearly full-length sequences worldwide [9]. Of these, eight sub-subtypes have been confirmed to circulate in China [10].

CRF01_AE has been the predominant HIV-1 strain in northern China [3], particularly in Shenyang, the largest city in Northeast China and the capital of Liaoning Province, where it accounted for approximately 70% of infections [11]. Within Shenyang, sub-subtypes 01_C4 and 01_C5 have been identified as the dominant sub-subtypes [4], and 01_C5 may have been independently introduced to Northeast China before spreading nationwide [5]. In contrast, Shenzhen, a major southern city with over 80% of its population consisting of migrants [12], showed a different epidemic profile. There, MSM represented over 60% of HIV infections, and CRF01_AE ranked as the second most common strain [13]. While 01_C4 and 01_C5 also predominated in Shenzhen [14], local CRF01_AE lineages showed close genetic ties to strains from multiple provinces and cities across southern China [14]. Nevertheless, it remained unclear whether the nationwide distribution of 01_C4 and 01_C5 reflected a single-origin, unidirectional diffusion pattern or a more complex, multi-directional transmission network.

To address this question, we performed a comparative molecular epidemiological analysis of Shenyang and Shenzhen-two geographically distant cities with distinct HIV epidemic profiles. Using CRF01_AE sequences and corresponding demographic data collected through comprehensive sampling in both cities between 2016 and 2019, and contextualized within the national sequence database, this study aimed to: (1) determine whether significant HIV transmission links existed between Shenyang and Shenzhen; (2) if present, characterize the structure, direction, and key populations involved in these links; and (3) identify potential targets for intervention and establish an analytical framework for understanding cross-regional spread of CRFs in China.

Materials and methods

Dataset

Partial pol gene sequences (HXB2: 2253–3300) and demographic and epidemiologic information were collected from all individuals newly diagnosed with HIV-1 CRF01_AE (≥18 years old) between 2016 and 2019 in Shenyang [11] and Shenzhen [12]. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of China Medical University (2019–153–2). All the sequences have been deposited to GenBank (the sequences from Shenzhen: MW949654-OP058202) and the “China AIDS virus gene sequence data platform” (https://nmdc.cn/hiv/) (the sequences from Shenyang: NMDCN0003FE3- NMDCN000678U).

To establish a national context for evaluating the outward connectivity of local CRF01_AE lineages, we augmented our data with all relevant publicly available partial pol gene sequences from China (2016–2019) that contained sampling site data (n = 4,820), thereby constructing a comprehensive molecular transmission network. After removing duplicates and quality control, a total of 6,140 sequences covering 20 provinces and cities across China were retained (Figure 1). HIV sub-subtypes analysis of CRF01_AE

CRF01_AE sub-subtypes were identified using a rigorous, multi-step verification workflow. All sequences were initially screened using COMET (https://comet.lih.lu/) [15] to confirm the CRF01_AE. Sub-subtype designation was first performed using the online HIV typing tool available on the China HIV Gene Sequence Data Platform (https://nmdc.cn/hiv/) [16]. To validate the sub-subtyping results, maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic trees were reconstructed using FastTree v2.1.8 under the GTR nucleotide substitution model and visualized with Chiplot (https://www.chiplot.online) [17], incorporating published reference sequences of CRF01_AE sub-subtypes [9]. Sub-subtypes were assigned based on bootstrap values>90 [9].

Molecular network construction

Molecular networks for CRF01_AE were constructed based on the genetic distance (GD) using HIV-TRACE [18]. Pairwise distances were calculated using the Tamura-Nei 93 model, and linkages were established when pairwise distances fell below an optimal GD threshold. The optimal GD threshold, determined through sensitivity analysis (ranging from 0.001–0.020 substitutions/site), maximized the number of identified clusters [19]. Network was visualized using Cytoscape 3.9.1 [20]. Molecular clusters were categorized as either a local cluster or a mixed cluster, depending on whether they contained sequences from a single city/province or from more than one city/province, respectively.

Viral diffusion history reconstruction

Spatiotemporal and demographic reconstruction was performed on large clusters (n ≥ 10) using BEAST v1.10.4 [21], employing a GTR + I + G4 substitution model, relaxed log-normal clock, and Bayesian Skygrid model. Geography, transmission route, and age were analyzed as discrete traits using an asymmetric substitution model with Bayesian Stochastic Search Variable Selection (BSSVS). Significant migration pathways were identified via SpreaD3 [22], based on a threshold of Bayes Factors (BF) > 3. Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) chains were run to convergence (ESS>200; 10% burn-in), and results were visualized in R 4.5.2. Before inference, distinct subsampling strategies were implemented to mitigate sampling bias [23] (details in Supplementary Tables 1–2 and Supplementary Figure 1). For geographic analysis, a balanced down-sampling protocol was applied to Shenyang and Shenzhen sequences to normalize spatiotemporal density. Demographic analysis was restricted to large clusters that simultaneously included the sequences of Shenyang and Shenzhen, utilizing stratified random subsampling without replacement. Crucially, sampling depth was calibrated such that the cumulative sampling capacity (subsample size*number of replicates) exceeded the total population size of the largest stratum. To ensure the robustness of our results, we performed a total of 24 independent down-sampled replicates across all analyzed dimensions. This strategy ensured comprehensive coverage of genetic diversity across all demographic and geographic categories.

Statistical analyses

Demographic characteristics and clustering rates were compared using chi-square tests, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 7.04 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA).

Results

Population characterizations

A total of 4011 CRF01_AE pol sequences were collected from Shenyang (n = 2018) and Shenzhen (n = 1993). In this cohort, the majority of HIV-infected individuals were male (90.7%), with nearly half being middle-aged (30–49 years, 44.6%). MSM constituted 69.2% of cases. The population was predominantly Han ethnicity (89.3%), with 63.2% having attained at least a senior high school education. Most participants were single (76.8%) and non-local residents (59.6%). The Shenyang cohort exhibited significantly higher proportions of males (93.7% vs. 87.8%), elderly individuals (≥50 years, 20.0% vs. 13.6%), single persons (79.4% vs. 74.1%), and MSM (82.0% vs. 56.1%) compared to Shenzhen (all p < 0.05). In contrast, the proportions of Han ethnicity (92.3% vs. 86.2%) and non-local residents (84.5% vs. 34.9%) were significantly higher in Shenzhen than in Shenyang (both p < 0.05) (Table 2). Distribution of CRF01_AE sub-subtypes

A total of 6 known CRF01_AE sub-subtypes were detected in the sequences of Shenyang and Shenzhen, among which 01_C5 was the most prevalent sub-subtype (46.7%), followed by 01_C4 (37.2%), 01_C1 (8.1%), 01_C2 (2.3%), 01_C3 (0.3%), 01_C8 (<0.1%), and 01_OTH (4.3%). In Shenyang, four known sub-subtypes were identified, with 01_C5 (73.7%) being the most common, followed by 01_C4 (21.6%), 01_C1 (0.8%), and 01_C2 (<0.1%). One novel local cluster (01_SY, 2.2%) was identified in Shenyang. In Shenzhen, six known sub-subtypes were detected, with 01_C4 (53.0%) predominating, followed by 01_C5 (19.3%), 01_C1 (15.4%), 01_C2 (4.6%), 01_C3 (0.6%), and 01_C8 (0.1%) (Table 2, Figure 2). Characteristics of molecular network

Using an optimal GD threshold of 0.008 substitutions/site (supplementary figure 2), 687 clusters encompassing 2,347 sequences (960 from Shenyang and 735 from Shenzhen) were identified (Figure 3A). 01_C5 (41.6%) and 01_C4 (42.1%) were the most prevalent sub-subtypes in the network, with clustering rates (44.4%, 38.6%) significantly higher than those of 01_C1 (28.3%), 01_OTH (25.6%), and 01_C2 (16. 2%) (p < 0.0001) (Figure 3B). 01_SY exhibited the highest clustering rates (88.9%). The clustering rate in Shenyang (47.6%) was significantly higher than that in Shenzhen (36.9%, p < 0.0001). The features of large clusters

A total of 24 large clusters (n ≥ 10) were identified, comprising 513 sequences (347 from Shenyang and 105 from Shenzhen). Among these, there were 12 01_C5 clusters (n = 332, 64.7%), including 5 mixed clusters (n = 249, 75%) and 7 local clusters in Shenyang (n = 86, 25.9%), 10 01_C4 clusters (n = 146, 28.5%), including 5 mixed cluster (n = 79, 54.1%), 3 local clusters in Shenzhen (n = 38, 26.0%), 1 local cluster in Shenyang (n = 15, 10.3%), and 1 local clusters in Jiangsu province (n = 14, 9.6%), and 2 01_SY clusters (n = 35, 6.8%) (Figure 3C). The proportion of MSM (81.6% vs. 61.8%) in large 01_C5 clusters in Shenyang was significantly higher than that in Shenzhen (p < 0.05, 0.08). The proportion of the elderly in large 01_C5 (28.1% vs. 11.8%) and 01_C4 (8.3% vs. 0.0%) clusters in Shenyang was higher than that in Shenzhen (p < 0.08, 0.049). Large 01_SY clusters were predominantly composed of middle-aged (65.7%) IDUs (71.4%) (Supplementary Table 3–4).

Dispersal patterns of individuals with discrete traits among large clusters

The phylogeographic reconstruction of the large clusters revealed that the primary transmission pathways for 01_C5 were from Shenyang to Shenzhen (70.6%) and Heilongjiang Province (23.5%). For 01_C4, transmission primarily originated from Jiangsu Province to Shenzhen (16.7%), Shenyang (16.7%), and Anhui Province (16.7%), with an additional minor pathway observed from Shenyang to Shenzhen (5.6%).

01_C5 transmission was predominantly localized within Shenyang (81.4–81.5%). Middle-aged MSM (100.0%) were likely the primary source, facilitating spread to other age and high-risk groups locally, and potentially driving the cross-regional spread from Shenyang to Shenzhen (15.4%−16.1%). In Shenzhen, it was also possible that middle-aged MSM had driven the local spread of 01_C5.

Young MSM in Shenyang (72.7%) might be the primary drivers of 01_C4 circulation locally and its spread to middle-aged MSM in Shenzhen (27.3%). Middle-aged individuals might serve as a transmission bridge in both cities: in Shenyang, from the young to the elderly; in Shenzhen, from Shenyang's young MSM to the local young population.

Finally, the source of 01_SY may be middle-aged (100.0%) IDUs, who subsequently might transmit it to elderly MSM (88.9%) (Figure 4). Discussion

Our study identified the transmission of 01_C5 from Shenyang city in Northeast China to Shenzhen city in South China, and middle-aged MSM (30–49 years) might serve as the primary driver of this intercity transmission. These findings supported the molecular epidemiological association between Northeast China and South China, highlighting the urgent need for targeted prevention strategies focusing on middle-aged MSM.

Our analysis revealed distinct geographical patterns in CRF01_AE sub-subtypes between the cities in Northeast and South China. Six known CRF01_AE sub-subtypes were identified in Shenzhen, of which 01_C1, 01_C2, 01_C4, and 01_C5 accounted for 92.3%. This result aligned with a previous molecular epidemiological study in Shenzhen (2006–2015), in which a total of 11 CRF01_AE lineages were identified, and 4 major lineages (lineages 1, 2, 4, and 5) accounted for 94.3% [14]. Shenzhen's greater sub-subtype diversity likely reflected its role as a special economic zone and major migration hub, which facilitated the introduction and dissemination of multiple CRF01_AE sub-subtypes [12,13]. Shenyang displayed less diversity with only four known sub-subtypes detected, predominantly 01_C5 (73.7%), consistent with our earlier findings [4,5].

Molecular network analysis further revealed higher transmission dynamics of 01_C5 and 01_C4, as the clustering rates of 01_C5 (44.4%) and 01_C4 (38.6%) were significantly higher than those of 01_C1 (28.3%) and 01_C2 (16.2%). Aligned with prior epidemiological studies [6,10], our study also identified that 01_C1 and 01_C2 were predominantly associated with HET networks, while 01_C4 and 01_C5 were primarily linked to MSM transmission (Supplementary Table 3). The observed transmission dynamics suggested that MSM networks might facilitate more efficient HIV transmission compared to HET networks, which might be associated with high-risk behaviours such as more sexual partners and unprotected anal sex among MSM [24]. Furthermore, the proportion of 01_C1-C2, predominantly associated with HETs, was substantially higher in Shenzhen (∼20%) compared to Shenyang (<1%). These variations in sub-subtype composition may explain the differences observed between the two cities in transmission routes, age distributions, and demographic profiles of those infected with CRF01_AE. These findings highlighted the critical role of population-specific transmission networks in shaping regional HIV epidemics and underscored the importance of targeted prevention strategies tailored to local epidemiological profiles.

Shenzhen could function as both a national sink for HIV-1 transmission and an incubator for new recombinant strains. As China's first special economic zone, Shenzhen attracted substantial migrants, who accounted for approximately 80% of its residents [14], and recorded nearly 1500 new HIV infections every year from 2011 to 2018 [13]. This demographic characteristic has facilitated the introduction of diverse HIV-1 genotypes from all over the country, as evidenced by the detection of 26 distinct HIV subtypes in Shenzhen between 2011 and 2018 [13]. The continuous influx of migrants also created favourable conditions for the emergence of novel CRFs. For instance, CRF55_01B and CRF109_0107, first identified among MSM in Shenzhen [25,26], have subsequently spread to other regions and populations across China [27]. Our results showed that the predominance of 01_C4 and 01_C5 in Shenzhen might be primarily attributable to imported transmission (01_C4 from Jiangsu and Shenyang; 01_C5 from Shenyang, in Figure 3). These findings provided molecular epidemiological evidence supporting the role of Shenzhen as a sink for HIV transmission.

A key finding of this study was that 01_C5, which was mainly prevalent in Northeast China (especially in Shenyang), may have spread from Shenyang to Shenzhen. Previous work suggested that 01_C5 likely originated in or near Yunnan before dispersing directly to Northeast China, where it remained largely confined to MSM [5]. Our analysis of nationwide sequences further supported the primary prevalence of 01_C5 in Northeast China. Notably, earlier analyses of 1,962 CRF01_AE sequences from China failed to detect the viral migration events between Liaoning Province (where Shenyang is located) and Guangdong Province (home to Shenzhen) [28]. Moreover, a study of household registration data from CRF01_AE infected individuals in Shenzhen (2006–2015) indicated that 01_C5 prevalent among MSM in Shenzhen might trace back to migrants from Hubei province [14]. In this study, by conducting in-depth sampling in both Shenyang and Shenzhen and performing molecular traceability analysis within a nationwide transmission network, we identified for the first time the potential transmission of 01_C5 from Shenyang to Shenzhen. These findings provided molecular epidemiological evidence for HIV spread between northeastern and southern Chinese cities. They not only complemented existing evidence positioning Shenzhen as a sink for HIV transmission in China, but also refined our understanding of interregional HIV-1 transmission dynamics at the CRF01_AE sub-subtype level.

Middle-aged MSM should be prioritized for targeted intervention in Northeast and South China. Bayesian phylodynamic analysis based on HIV molecular network identified middle-aged MSM as the potential primary driver of 01_C5 transmission and a potential transmission bridge for 01_C4 in both Shenyang and Shenzhen, including their cross-regional spread. First, in this study, middle-aged MSM represented the largest demographic for both the large 01_C5 (43.8% by age; 79.5% by route) and 01_C4 (44.2% by age; 74.7% by route) clusters, suggesting their potential role in driving HIV transmission. Second, existing research indicated that compared to younger MSM, older MSM in China had a longer period of HIV exposure and a higher proportion of unprotected anal sex [24], and exhibited a higher HIV prevalence (11.8–13.8%) compared to young MSM (4.6–7.2%) [29]. Collectively, these findings underscore middle-aged MSM as a demographic with a heightened risk of HIV transmission.

Our findings also suggested that young MSM (<30 years) constituted a high-risk group for HIV transmission in both Northeast and South China. This was particularly relevant given that 01_C4, which had been widely circulating across China [30], was also detected in Shenyang and Shenzhen in this study. Our data indicated that young MSM might serve as a source of 01_C4 transmission in Shenyang and as a key target population for infection in Shenzhen. These observations are supported by a recent study of molecular transmission networks in Shenzhen, which reported a significantly higher clustering rate among individuals under 30 years old compared to older age groups [31]. This further implied that young MSM may also play a crucial role in driving HIV transmission in Shenzhen.

Finally, a novel local cluster designated as 01_SY (n = 45) was identified in Shenyang, consisting primarily of IDUs. This cluster, previously reported to have originated in Southeast Asia before establishing local transmission in Shenyang [32], exhibited an exceptionally high clustering rate of 88.9%. This finding suggested that IDUs may represent a higher-risk population for HIV transmission compared to other high-risk groups. Analysis of viral migration patterns revealed that the transmission of 01_SY might originate from middle-aged IDUs (87.5%) to elderly MSM (80%). Therefore, close monitoring of middle-aged IDUs and elderly MSM, along with implementing targeted interventions to disrupt transmission links between them, was critical for controlling the spread of the 01_SY cluster.

Limitations

This study had several limitations. First, subtyping based on partial pol gene sequences cannot exclude potential recombination in other genomic regions and may lead to misclassification of CRF01_AE. Therefore, the phylogenetic links identified here should be interpreted as potential transmission pathways rather than definitive evidence. Second, the lack of deep sampling from intermediate regions such as Shanghai meant that undisclosed transmission hubs between Shenyang and Shenzhen cannot be ruled out. This observation was consistent with previous reports that CRF strains spread via transportation networks [27] through complex transmission chains [33], suggesting the observed links were part of a broader national network that would benefit from more extensive geographical sampling. At last, the down-sampling strategy could potentially miss some transmission links, and future studies with greater computational resources could benefit from analyzing full datasets.

Conclusions

This study represented the first comprehensive analysis of the geographical differences and transmission associations in CRF01_AE sub-subtypes across the major cities in Northeast and South China. Our findings provided molecular epidemiological evidence supporting Shenzhen as a key HIV transmission sink in China, and offered critical insights into high-risk population characteristics for CRF01_AE sub-subtypes in facilitating the design of tailored intervention strategies for each city. Furthermore, this study provided an example for applying traceability analysis based on molecular transmission networks in guiding targeted public health interventions.

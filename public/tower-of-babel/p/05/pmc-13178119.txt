NETosis Markers, Such as Citrullinated Histones, Myeloperoxidase, and Elastase, should not be Recommended as Predictors of COVID-19 Severity
Authors: Josef Finsterer
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the article by Kumar et al. on a prospective, single-center cohort study investigating the relationship between NETose markers, such as citrullinated histones, myeloperoxidase, and elastase, and the severity of COVID-19 in 99 patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection.1 Concentrations of citrullinated histones and elastase were elevated in COVID-19 patients compared to controls, particularly in those with severe COVID-19. Citrullinated histones predicted COVID-19 severity with a sensitivity of 74% and a specificity of 64%.1 It was concluded that citrullinated histones are independently associated with COVID-19 severity.1 The study is interesting, but leaves some questions unanswered.

First, the included patients were not systematically screened for comorbidities.1 Only diabetes, hypertension, and hypothyroidism were recorded as comorbidities. Since comorbidities strongly influence NETose marker levels, it would have been essential to consider the full spectrum of comorbidities for each patient. Similarly, concomitant medications were not recorded and were not included in the analysis.1 Citrullination of histones can be enhanced by glucocorticoids and retinoic acid. Drugs that inhibit citrullination include heparin, HDAC inhibitors, and PAD inhibitors.

Secondly, the level of citrullinated histones increases not only due to infections but also in various other diseases. For example, trauma, venous thromboembolism, ischemic stroke, multiple sclerosis, heatstroke, or acute respiratory diseases such as asthma lead to an increase in citrullinated histones.2 Smoking has also been shown to increase the level of citrullinated histones.3 Therefore, histone citrullination is a non-specific response that is not limited to inflammation and thus can hardly predict the severity of SARS-CoV-2 infection. Certain medications, including tocilizumab, can also increase histone citrullination.4 Since tocilizumab is frequently administered to patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection, those who received this medication during the study should be excluded.

Third, elastase levels are elevated not only in infections but also in numerous other diseases. This is particularly true for diseases that damage the pancreas or block the elastase ducts into the intestine. Especially, high elastase levels are found in chronic pancreatitis, but also in smokers and alcoholics.

Fourth, myeloperoxidase levels are elevated not only in infectious diseases such as COVID-19 but also in various other diseases. Myeloperoxidase levels are particularly frequently elevated in oxidative stress, cardiovascular diseases, autoimmune diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, perichondritis, multiple sclerosis), vasculitis, diabetes, uremia, malnutrition, advanced age, and smoking.5

In conclusion, citrullinated histones, elastase, and myeloperoxidase cannot currently be recommended as predictors of the severity of a SARS-CoV-2 infection.

Evaluating the national early warning score (NEWS) and NEWS-calcium for predicting adverse outcomes in acute pancreatitis: a prospective cohort study in Vietnam
Authors: Qui Huu Nguyen, Dung Thi My Vo, Thong Duy Vo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimIdentifying acute pancreatitis (AP) patients at risk of severe progression remains a challenge. The National Early Warning Score (NEWS) has been shown to effectively predict adverse outcomes (intensive care unit (ICU) admission and mortality) in critically ill patients, including AP. The combined model of NEWS and serum calcium (NEWS-Calcium) is hypothesized to enhance predictive accuracy. This study evaluates the value of NEWS and NEWS-Calcium in predicting adverse outcomes in AP, and compares them with the Bedside Index for Severity in Acute Pancreatitis (BISAP).Materials and methodsA prospective cohort study was conducted with AP patients at a tertiary hospital in Vietnam. Patient data, including general characteristics, nonseveresigns and symptoms, laboratory, and radiology results, was collected. Various scores were assessed on the day of admission. The optimal cut-off points and the area under the curve (AUC) were determined using ROC curve analysis.ResultsAmong 220 patients, 137 (62.3%) were male, mean age was 47.3 ± 16.1 years. The proportions of non-severe and severe disease were 75.0% and 25.0%. 54 patients (24.5%) were admitted to the ICU and 16 (7.3%) died. The AUC values for NEWS, NEWS-Calcium, and BISAP for predicting ICU admission were 0.893, 0.903, and 0.862, respectively, and for predicting mortality were 0.846, 0.869, and 0.863, respectively. There was no statistically significant difference in AUC among the three scoring systems for predicting ICU admission (p = 0.441) and mortality (p = 0.645). In predicting ICU admission, the NEWS (≥4) and NEWS-Calcium (≥4) showed sensitivities of 79.6% and 91.0%, and specificities of 88.9% and 84.3%, respectively. For predicting mortality, the NEWS (≥6) and NEWS-Calcium (≥6) had sensitivities of 75.0% and 87.3%, and specificities of 81.3% and 80.4%, respectively.ConclusionBoth NEWS and NEWS-Calcium demonstrated good predictive value for adverse outcomes in acute pancreatitis, with AUCs ranging from 0.846 to 0.903, comparable to the BISAP score.Keywords: Acute pancreatitis, National early warning system, NEWS, NEWS-Calcium, Calcium, BISAP

Introduction

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common disease with a growing burden of morbidity and mortality. In a meta-analysis of 44 studies conducted globally from 1961 to 2016, the incidence rate increased by 3.07% annually [1]. Acute pancreatitis ranks sixth in mortality and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) among the ten most common gastrointestinal diseases [2]. Although the majority of AP cases are mild, with patients responding well to conservative management [3], 20–30% of patients develop severe acute pancreatitis, with mortality in this group reaching up to 30% [4]. Compared to Western countries, recent studies in Asia, including Vietnam, have reported a significant increase in AP caused by hypertriglyceridemia, making it the leading cause of AP in the region [5–7]. Among patients with hypertriglyceride-induced AP, a higher rate of local complications and organ failure has been reported, including acute respiratory failure [8, 9].

Numerous scoring systems have been developed to predict the risk of severe progression in patients with AP. However, no single scoring system has been recognized as the gold standard for predicting adverse outcomes in AP [3, 10, 11]. The Bedside Index for Severity in Acute Pancreatitis (BISAP) is currently one of the most widely applied scoring systems due to its accuracy and simplicity compared to more complex systems such as Ranson and APACHE-II. However, since systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) - a component of BISAP -includes four parameters, the actual assessment of the BISAP score is based on eight parameters rather than five. These parameters include laboratory and imaging findings such as blood urea nitrogen (BUN), pleural effusion on imaging studies, white blood cell count, and arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO₂).

The Early Warning Score (EWS), introduced in 1997, employs a clinical prediction model based on patients’ vital signs to identify those at higher risk of adverse outcomes [12]. The EWS system has been routinely implemented in several countries, including the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Australia [13]. Numerous studies have demonstrated the notable prognostic value of EWS and Modified EWS (MEWS) in AP [14–16]. In 2012, to reduce variability in the United Kingdom (UK), the Royal College of Physicians developed the National Early Warning Score (NEWS). NEWS has been proven to have predictive value for adverse outcomes (ICU admission, in-hospital mortality) in critically ill patients [17, 18], as well as for identifying sepsis and septic shock [19, 20].

AP is often associated with hypocalcemia. Serum calcium levels are one of the parameters of the Ranson criteria for predicting the severity of AP. Numerous studies have demonstrated that serum calcium levels serve as an independent predictor of severe AP (SAP). The integration of serum calcium levels into scoring systems such as BISAP and NEWS has been shown to enhance their predictive value [15, 21].

With the aim of identifying a scoring system capable of predicting outcomes in patients with AP using primarily clinical parameters and the simple laboratory tests, one that can be applied early at the time of hospital admission and utilized by nurses as well, we conducted this study. Our study evaluates the value of the NEWS and NEWS-Calcium scores in predicting adverse outcomes in AP and compares them with other scoring systems.

Method

Study design and setting

This prospective cohort study was conducted on AP patients admitted to the Department of Gastroenterology of Cho Ray Hospital in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, from November 2023 to June 2024.

Study population

All adult patients aged 18 years or older who were diagnosed with AP were included if they agreed to participate in the study. Exclusion criteria comprised patients with insufficient data, hypercalcemia-induced AP, chronic pancreatitis, pregnancy, or severe uncontrolled chronic diseases (heart failure, cirrhosis, end-stage kidney disease, etc.). Patients who were critically ill from previous medical facilities (i.e. coma, shock requiring vasopressors, invasive mechanical ventilation) or required direct ICU admission were also excluded.

Sample size calculation

The required sample size for each group of mortality and non-mortality was calculated using the formula . We chose α = 0.05 (95% confidence), d=0.07. The minimum required sample size for each outcome (ICU admission and mortality) for each scoring system (NEWS and NEWS-Calcium) was calculated based on the AUC values reported in the study by Tan et al. [21] The smallest sample size that satisfied all conditions was then selected. With an estimated sample loss of 10%, the minimum sample size is 172 cases.

Criteria and definitions

The diagnosis of AP was established when at least two of the following three criteria from the revised Atlanta classification 2012 were met: [1, 22] abdominal pain consistent with AP (acute onset of persistent, severe, epigastric pain often radiating to the back); [2] serum amylase or lipase levels elevated to at least three times the upper limit of normal; [3] imaging findings consistent with AP (abdominal ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), or magnetic resonance imaging). The severity of AP was classified into three levels: (i) Mild AP was defined as the absence of organ failure and local or systemic complications; (ii) Moderately-severe AP is characterized by the presence of transient organ failure or local or systemic complications in the absence of persistent organ failure; (iii) Severe AP (SAP) is characterized by persistent organ failure (lasting more than 48 h) [22].

NEWS is calculated based on parameters including pulse, systolic blood pressure, respiratory rate, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2), use of supplemental oxygen, level of consciousness (the alert, new confusion, verbal, pain, unresponsive scale - ACVPU scale), and body temperature [23]. NEWS-Calcium is calculated based on the NEWS at admission and the earliest serum calcium level. The serum calcium thresholds (< 1.6; 1.6–<1.8; 1.8–<2.0; ≥2.0 mmol/L) used in the NEWS-Calcium score were applied in accordance with the original study by Tan et al. (2020) [21]. (Table 1)

The BISAP score consists of five distinct factors, with each factor contributing one point: BUN level exceeding 25 mg/dL, impaired mental status indicated by a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score below 15, the presence of systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), age over 60, and the presence of pleural effusion on imaging. A high BISAP score means the score is equal to or greater than 3 [24].

Data collection

We collected data on patients, including age and sex, etiology of AP, body mass index (BMI), signs and symptoms, vital signs at admission, and laboratory data such as leukocyte (WBC), hematocrit (Hct), platelet (PLT), amylase, lipase, triglyceride, C-reactive protein (CRP), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, transaminases, total bilirubin and total serum calcium (Ca2+). We also documented radiology data on the extent of pancreatic necrosis, Balthazar grade, computed tomography severity index (CTSI), modified CTSI (mCTSI), and pleural effusion. The assessment of consciousness was conducted using the ACVPU scale (Alert, new Confusion, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive). The ACVPU scale is simpler and easier to apply compared to the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), thus minimizing inter-rater variability and ensuring consistency across evaluators.

The NEWS and NEWS-Calcium scores were calculated at the time of the patient’s initial arrival at the emergency department, while the BISAP score was assessed within 24 h after hospital admission.

Ethics statement

The study protocol adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee for Biomedical Research of the University of Medicine and Pharmacy at Ho Chi Minh City (Approval No.731/HDĐD-ĐHYD, signed on August 15, 2023).

Statistical analysis

The study utilized Epidata 4.6 for data entry, along with Stata 16.0 for analysis. Descriptive statistics were used for continuous and categorical variables, with comparisons conducted using appropriate tests such as chi-square, Fisher’s exact test, t-test, or Mann–Whitney test. Multivariate regression analysis explored the impact of factors on variables. ROC curves assessed the predictive ability of the NEWS, NEWS-Calcium, and BISAP scores for SAP, ICU admission, and mortality. The optimal cut-off points for each scoring system was determined using the highest value of the Youden index. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and both positive and negative likelihood ratios (LR) were calculated based on a 2 × 2 table. The DeLong test was used to compare the AUC of different scoring systems. To assess the improvement in risk classification when using NEWS and NEWS-Calcium compared to BISAP, net reclassification improvement (NRI) analysis was performed.

Direct medical costs were estimated from the hospital perspective, including 1,000,000 VND/day (≈ 40 USD) for ward admission and 10,000,000 VND/day (≈ 400 USD) for ICU admission. For the NEWS–Calcium model, an additional 50,000 VND (≈ 2 USD) per patient was added for serum calcium testing. Effectiveness was defined as the proportion of patients correctly triaged according to prespecified cut-offs. Incremental cost (ΔCost), incremental effect (ΔEffect), and incremental cost–effectiveness ratio (ICER) were calculated for each model comparison.

Statistical significance was determined at p < 0.05. For multiple comparisons of AUCs and other pairwise tests, the Bonferroni correction was applied to control for type I error, with the adjusted significance threshold set at 0.0167.

Result

The study enrolled 220 AP patients with a mean age of 47.3 ± 16.1 years, of which 137 (62.3%) were males. Alcohol was the most common cause of AP, accounting for 28.2% of the cases. Table 2 presents a detailed description of the baseline characteristics of the study population. Among all patients, 28 (12.7%) patients had mild, 137 (62.3%) had moderately-severe, and 55 (25.0%) had severe AP. Regarding in-hospital outcomes, 54 cases (24.5%) required ICU admission. The overall mortality rate was 7.3%, with 25.5% in the severe AP group. The median hospital stay was 6 days, with the ICU admission group having a longer stay of 9 days (p < 0.001).

In predicting ICU admission, at the optimal cutoff point of ≥ 4, NEWS had a sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) of 79.6%, 91.0%, 74.1%, and 93.2%, respectively. In comparison, at the same cutoff point, NEWS-Calcium demonstrated a sensitivity of 88.9% and a specificity of 84.3%.

Regarding mortality prediction, at the optimal cutoff point of ≥ 6, NEWS showed a sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV of 75.0%, 87.3%, 31.6%, and 97.8%, respectively. By contrast, at the optimal cutoff point (≥ 6), the NEWS-Calcium score demonstrates a sensitivity of 82.4% and a specificity of 80.3%, and a notably high NPV at 98.3%. (Table 6)

Discussion

In AP, the systemic inflammatory response plays a key role in the pathogenesis, leading to organ failure. The NEWS scoring system is a model based entirely on clinical parameters, which are the vital signs of the patient. Our study indicates that NEWS is a good predictor of SAP, ICU admission, and mortality. NEWS has an AUC of 0.891 in predicting SAP (95% CI: 0.829–0.954, p < 0.001). Previous studies have not evaluated the value of NEWS in predicting SAP, though some have used early warning system scoring models like EWS and MEWS. The study by Garcea et al. using EWS demonstrated an AUC of 0.763 for predicting SAP, with a cutoff score of ≥ 3 yielding a sensitivity of 81.0%, specificity of 81.6%, and a NPV of 92.0% [25]. The study by Suppiah et al. showed that the mean MEWS on day one had predictive value for SAP, with an AUC of 0.91; at a cutoff score of ≥ 1.625, the sensitivity was 86.4%, specificity 92.5%, PPV 67.9%, and NPV 97.4% [16]. Although different scoring systems were used, these results are consistent with our study findings, as EWS, MEWS, and NEWS are all based on vital signs parameters, which reflect systemic inflammation and therefore have the potential to predict the severity of AP.

Vietnam is a developing country, and many healthcare facilities still face resource limitations. Therefore, identifying a simple scoring system to recognize which patients with AP are at risk of severe progression is crucial. In a study on hypertriglyceride-induced AP, changes in vital signs such as fever, altered consciousness, tachycardia, and hypotension were found to be significantly associated with the SAP group [8]. The study by Dao et al. (2024) utilizing the Systemic Inflammation Response Index (SIRI) to predict SAP demonstrated its notable predictive value [26]. Although SIRI is a simple index, it requires a complete blood count, which may not be readily available in all settings. Therefore, an early warning score such as NEWS, which can be applied by any healthcare worker, may be more suitable for use in resource-limited facilities to identify patients at risk of SAP who require referral to higher-level care.

Our study showed that NEWS had good predictive value for ICU admission, with an AUC of 0.893 (95% CI: 0.830–0.956, p < 0.001). These findings are consistent with the study by Tan et al. in China, which reported that NEWS had predictive value for ICU admission with an AUC of 0.919 (95% CI: 0.879–0.959, p < 0.001) [21]. Similarly, a study by Smith et al. in the UK involving a general hospitalized patient population showed that the NEWS had prognostic value for ICU admission with an AUC of 0.857 (95% CI: 0.847–0.868) [18]. The study by Tan selected a cutoff of ≥ 3.5, with a sensitivity of 89.6% and specificity of 86.1% for predicting ICU admission [21]. However, since NEWS is an integer-based score, choosing a cutoff of ≥4, as in our study, is more appropriate for application in clinical practice.

Our study demonstrated that NEWS had good predictive value for mortality, with an AUC of 0.846 (95% CI: 0.719–0.973, p < 0.001). This result is similar to that of Tan’s study in China, which reported an AUC of 0.891 for NEWS [21]. Likewise, Smith’s study also found that the NEWS had an AUC of 0.894 (95% CI: 0.887–0.902) for mortality prediction [13]. NEWS demonstrated a high NPV (97.8%) and a lower PPV (31.6%), indicating that it is more effective in ruling out patients with AP who are at low risk of mortality than in identifying those at high risk of death.

The findings indicated that total serum calcium levels were significantly lower in patients who were admitted to the ICU or died, compared to those without these adverse outcomes (1.7 ± 0.4 vs. 2.0 ± 0.3, p < 0.001; 1.6 ± 0.4 vs. 1.9 ± 0.3, p < 0.001). NEWS-Calcium demonstrated excellent predictive ability for SAP, with an AUC of 0.912 (95% CI: 0.854–0.971, p < 0.001). At an optimal cutoff value of ≥ 4, the score yielded a sensitivity of 88.9%, specificity of 84.3%, PPV of 64.9%, and NPV of 95.9%. These results suggest that by incorporating a serum calcium test, the NEWS-Calcium provides strong diagnostic utility for identifying SAP, with relatively high sensitivity and specificity, while also serving as a valuable tool for ruling it out.

Our study found that the AUC of NEWS-Calcium for predicting mortality was 0.869 (95% CI: 0.752–0.985, p < 0.001). This result indicates that NEWS-Calcium has good prognostic value for mortality in AP, and our findings are consistent with previous studies. In the study by Tan et al. in China, the AUC of NEWS-Calcium for predicting mortality was 0.935 (95% CI: 0.879–0.991). At the optimal cutoff value of ≥ 6, the NEWS-Calcium score in our study demonstrated a sensitivity of 81.3%, specificity of 80.4%, PPV of 24.5%, and NPV of 98.2% for mortality prediction. In Tan’s study, the optimal cutoff was ≥ 8.5, with a sensitivity of 85.7% and specificity of 88.5%; this difference may be explained by the mortality rate in Tan’s study being approximately half that observed in ours (3.7% vs. 7.3%) [21]. NEWS-Calcium showed a very high NPV (98.3%) but a low PPV (25.0%), indicating that its primary utility lies in ruling out patients at low risk of mortality.

While BISAP is often considered a simple scoring system, its reliance on SIRS requires the evaluation of multiple parameters, making it more complex in practice. In contrast, three of the SIRS parameters—heart rate, respiratory rate, and body temperature—are already incorporated into NEWS. Our study showed that all three scoring systems—NEWS, NEWS-Calcium, and BISAP—had good prognostic value for adverse outcomes (ICU admission and mortality), with no statistically significant differences in AUC observed among them. In mortality prediction, NEWS demonstrated the highest specificity among the three scoring systems (87.3%) and the highest positive likelihood ratio (LR+) of 6.0, indicating a strong ability to identify patients at high risk of death when the result is positive. By contrast, BISAP showed the highest sensitivity among the three scoring systems (93.8%), allowing for the identification of most patients at risk of mortality. Its LR- was 0.1—the lowest among the three scores—indicating a strong ability to rule out mortality risk when the result is negative. Meanwhile, NEWS-Calcium is ideal for both identifying and ruling out patients at risk of mortality, owing to its balanced diagnostic performance across multiple indicators. Although the NEWS and NEWS-Calcium scores demonstrated good predictive value and were comparable to BISAP in terms of AUC, the Net Reclassification Index (NRI) analysis did not show a statistically significant improvement in risk classification (Table 7). Decision curve analysis further indicated that all three models yielded higher net benefit than the “treat all” or “treat none” strategies across most clinically relevant threshold probabilities for both ICU admission and mortality. For ICU admission, NEWS-Calcium achieved slightly higher net benefit than NEWS and BISAP at thresholds of 0.20 and 0.30, whereas for mortality prediction, the three models showed minimal differences, with BISAP marginally higher at some thresholds (Table 9). Therefore, while these scores may offer practical advantages, such as simplicity and early applicability, their superiority over BISAP remains inconclusive.

Compared to BISAP, which is assessed only once within 24 h after admission, NEWS and NEWS-Calcium are dynamic scoring systems that can be repeated at multiple time points. Another system with repeatability is the Pancreatitis Activity Scoring System (PASS), which includes five components: organ failure, oral intolerance to solid foods, systemic inflammatory response syndrome, abdominal pain, and opioid administration. In the study by Vahapoğlu et al. (2024), PASS demonstrated better predictive value for ICU admission compared to BISAP [27]. However, in general, PASS is quite complex in clinical practice due to the need to score these five components, which may limit its applicability.

One of the simple scoring systems for quickly assessing whether a patient with AP is at risk of ICU admission is the HAPS score (Harmless Acute Pancreatitis Score). Introduced in 2009, this score includes only three parameters: absence of abdominal tenderness or rebound, normal hematocrit, and normal creatinine level. If all three criteria are met (score = 0), the score can predict non-severe acute pancreatitis with a positive predictive value of up to 98% [28]. Compared with NEWS or NEWS-Calcium, although HAPS is simple, it only has predictive value for non-severe acute pancreatitis. Furthermore, HAPS cannot be performed by nurses (as it requires abdominal examination) and involves two laboratory tests.

Since the Ranson criteria, many predictive models for AP have been developed, and in the current era of AI, machine learning models have emerged. Machine learning models (ML), such as logistic regression, artificial neural networks (ANN), and especially XGBoost (XGB), are increasingly proving their superiority over traditional tools. The XGB model allows for the processing of large volumes of clinical and paraclinical data within the first 12 h of admission to classify the risk of SAP with high accuracy (AUC of 0.921), surpassing both BISAP and HAPS in the same study [29, 30]. However, in low-middle-income countries, where resources are limited, scoring systems like NEWS and NEWS-Calcium may still have their own value.

In the cost–effectiveness analysis, the NEWS model demonstrated equivalent effectiveness to BISAP in avoiding ICU admissions (75.5%) without incurring additional costs. This suggests that, from an economic perspective, NEWS may be a practical alternative to BISAP for early triage in AP, particularly in settings where rapid clinical assessment is prioritized and laboratory resources are limited.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. Its single-center design at a tertiary hospital may limit generalizability to primary care or rural settings. Excluding critically ill patients requiring immediate ICU admission may restrict the applicability of NEWS and NEWS-Calcium to the full spectrum of AP severity. The lack of data on prior treatments for transferred patients may affect vital signs and serum calcium levels, limiting applicability to treatment-naive cohorts. The focus on in-hospital outcomes (ICU admission, mortality) without long-term follow-up restricts insights into readmission or recurrent AP. No formal inter-rater reliability study was conducted for the ACVPU scale, potentially affecting reproducibility. We adopted Tan et al.’s (2020) established serum calcium thresholds (< 1.6, 1.6–<1.8, 1.8–<2.0, ≥ 2.0 mmol/L) without independently optimizing them. Finally, the low number of deaths limits statistical power for mortality outcomes. Future multi-center studies with treatment-naive patients, critically ill cohorts, long-term follow-up, reliability assessments, and optimized thresholds are needed to enhance the applicability of NEWS and NEWS-Calcium.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrated that NEWS and NEWS-Calcium have good predictive value for severe acute pancreatitis, ICU admission, and in-hospital mortality in a Vietnamese cohort, with AUCs ranging from 0.846 to 0.903, comparable to BISAP. These scores, based on simple, routinely collected vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO₂, supplemental oxygen, temperature, consciousness) and a low-cost serum calcium test, are easily applied by nurses in resource-limited settings like Vietnam. Further research is needed to validate these findings in larger, diverse cohorts.

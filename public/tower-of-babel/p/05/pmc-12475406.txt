Data augmentation English-Indonesia-Madurese parallel corpus dataset using neural machine translation
Authors: Fairuz Iqbal Maulana, Yaya Heryadi, Gede Putra Kusuma, Widodo Budiharto
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
INMAD is a dataset containing a corpus of English-Indonesian-Madurese translated sentences. This corpus stores a list of 23086 lines of sentences, as well as their translations in Indonesian and English. The details of each Madurese translation cover 1 language level, namely the ‘engghi-enten’ level. The framework for creating the dataset consists of two stages. First, the Combine source of parallel corpus to create and improve the quality of sentences corpus. Second, Data Augmentation with Back-translation using MarianMT and combine parallel dataset with original parallel corpus. INMAD received validation from a Madurese language specialist, who also served as the translator for the source of this dataset. Consequently, this dataset can serve as the primary resource for Natural Language Processing (NLP) research, particularly for Madurese at the 'engghi-enten' level.

Background

The INMAD dataset presented is a valuable resource for researchers, linguists, NLP practitioners, and anyone interested in Madurese languages, machine translation, and related fields. Potential applications include the development of new language models, the improvement of translation systems, and the improvement of information retrieval techniques for the Madurese language. In addition, the dataset may contribute to a deeper understanding of the linguistic and cultural aspects of the Madurese language. The dataset is invaluable to researchers and practitioners in the field of machine translation, as it can be used to evaluate a variety of translation techniques, from traditional rule-based methods to advanced statistical and neural machine translation approaches. Parallel corpus, as presented in this study, is essential for training and testing machine translation models. Although the parallel corpus is commonly used in machine translation, it is the first parallel corpus specifically designed for the low-resource-intensive Madurese language.

Data Description

The development of the Madurese Parallel Corpus was initiated by collecting data from three pre-existing multilingual datasets. The corpus was designed to cover three languages in parallel, namely English, Indonesian and Madurese. The contributions from the three primary data sources were 1100 [1], 994 [2], and 9949 [3] parallel data, respectively. After going through the consolidation process, the total initial data set collected reached 11543 parallel data. Next, the data set went through a pre-processing stage followed by data augmentation on the Indonesian corpus to multiply the data volume. The augmentation process applies the “Back Translation” method to produce semantically similar new sentences by utilising synonyms. The augmented Indonesian sentences were then manually translated into Madurese by language experts. The translation process by the experts was also accompanied by the standardisation of Madurese speech levels into one variety of middle language, namely ‘engghi-enten’. From the data augmentation process, 11543 new sentence lines were obtained. The augmented dataset was then merged with the initial dataset, resulting in a final total of 23086 parallel data in the Madura Language Parallel Corpus.

The creation of corpus datasets has previously been carried out by collecting datasets based on physical documents from the Complete Dictionary of Madurese-Indonesian [4] and by collecting ncorpus datasets based on the online Bible [5]. The Madurese Parallel Corpora dataset that we propose is a combination of several dataset sources and Data Augmentation containing bilingual sentences from English, Indonesian, and Madurese. The dataset we developed will further complement the existing Madurese Corpora Parallel and will further facilitate the development of regional language research, especially the Madurese language in Indonesia.

The Madurese language

The Madurese language constitutes one of the 770 regional languages identified within Indonesia. The Madurese language is generally spoken in the Madurese Islands, which consists of 4 districts: Bangkalan, Sampang, Pamekasan, and Sumenep, as well as parts of the Java Islands, such as Situbondo, Bondowoso, and Probolinggo. The Madurese language has three levels of language, namely enjâ'-iyâ' (rough), ‘engghi-enten’ (moderate), and bâsa alos (subtle), which reflects the social stratification system of its society [6,7]. With an estimated 7.7 million speakers, Madurese is among the region's largest speakers, but Madurese faces significant challenges, particularly the risk of digital extinction, largely due to inadequate documentation and a lack of technological resources adapted to the language [8,9]. The sociolinguistic dynamics of the Madurese language are increasingly complicated by the shift towards Indonesian among the younger generation, driven by the perceived prestige and inherent complexity of mastering the various dialects of the Madurese language [10].

The linguistic features of the Madurese language are very unique, incorporating unique phonetic elements such as the /ə/ phoneme, in addition to a rich series of prefixes and suffixes that contribute to complex word formation [11]. The language also uses reduplication, which increases its morphological complexity and contributes to its vocabulary richness. Furthermore, the Madurese language is known for its unique intonation and stress placement patterns, which are very different from Javanese, although there are some lexical similarities [12]. The dialect variations in Madurese language, particularly across the districts mentioned above, underscore the linguistic diversity that exists in the region, with each dialect exhibiting its own phonological and morphological characteristics [13]. This diversity requires a nuanced understanding of the language, especially in the context of language preservation and revitalization efforts that utilize digital devices and community involvement [14].

Given the urgent need for language preservation, various initiatives have emerged to document and promote the Madurese language through digital platforms and educational resources [9]. These efforts aim to foster a greater appreciation of Madurese culture and language while addressing the challenges posed by globalization and technological advancements [5]. The integration of modern technology into language learning and preservation strategies is essential to ensure the continuity of the Madurese language, such as creating translation data into digital versions. Step 1. available resource collection

This stage is a fundamental contribution in parallel text acquisition as a foundation for building a dataset. Our search for publicly available Madurese parallel text data yielded only three data sources with a very limited volume of content. The three data collections contain were 1100 [1], 994 [2], and 9949 [3] parallel sentences respectively. After going through the process of collation and consolidation, this first phase successfully collected a total of 11,543 parallel sentences. This dataset consists of English texts and their equivalent translations in Indonesian and Madurese. Based on the initial examination, it was identified that the Madurese translation corpus still shows the use of speech levels that are diverse and not yet standardised.

The data gathered in Phase 1 is insufficient for developing a trustworthy neural machine translation model, notwithstanding its use for testing purposes. This demonstrates that the quantity of accessible data is exceedingly limited for any NLP task dependent on parallel Madurese raw data. Therefore, the researcher proceeded to the next stage.

Step 2. data augmentation, preprocessing, and manual translation

This stage includes a dataset in stage 1 of 11543 parallel sentences, dataset duplication using Data Augmentation, the efforts of human translation experts involved, which produced the final dataset named INMAD (Indonesian-Madurese) for Neural Machine Translation needs. The data Augmentation carried out, which is the researcher's approach to practice among Machine Translation practitioners, about the duplication of available resources by paraphrasing English parallel sentences using Back-Translation in English to Indonesian. This method is useful for increasing the limited number of resources and duplicating them to double the number of datasets in stage 1.

Next, in the Data Augmentation process stage, the three datasets are combined into one file and the quality is improved with preprocessing which begins with the stage of changing all letters in parallel sentences to lowercase, removing double spaces, removing newline characters, removing non-ASCII characters, removing one space at the beginning and end of the sentence. Preprocessing for dataset.

In this stage of Data Augmentation, 11543 parallel sentences of English and Indonesian were produced. Then, the datasets of stage 1 and stage 2 were combined to form an INMAD dataset with a total of 23086 parallel sentences. The INMAD corpus was then translated by human translation experts from Indonesian to Madurese. In total, 4 human translators participated in the translation effort into Madurese. All Indonesian parallel sentences in the INMAD dataset are manually translated into one level of Madurese language, namely ‘engghi-enten’. All of these participants' translators spoke Madurese as their mother tongue, making them perfect candidates for English-Indonesian-Madurese translation assignments.

The total manual translation process requires four months, spanning from October 2024 to January 2025. To mitigate human error, each sentence is translated by a minimum of two translators, and the final translation is verified for discrepancies. This stage makes a great contribution to the final dataset. Limitations

During the dataset collection process for parallel text sets, some limitations were found. First, the size of the data set. Although efforts have been made to collect a wide variety of texts from open sources, the overall size of the dataset is still relatively limited and there are translations with different language levels for each sentence. This obstacle arises from the availability of openly accessible and available sources containing parallel English-Indonesian-Madurese sentences.

The second limitation is the duplication of parallel sentence datasets automatically using Data Augmentation in English arrangement. Duplication by paraphrasing or synthesizing data from each sentence line using Python Programming. Another limitation, the manual alignment of sentence translations by translators, becomes a challenging and time-consuming task. Efforts are made to harmonize the accurate, by translating English into Madurese at one level, namely the ‘engghi-enten’ (rough) level. Although there is inherent complexity in language and variation in text format.

Notwithstanding these constraints, the generated parallel text datasets continue to serve as a significant resource for research and applications in Neural Machine Tracking, Madurese, and cross-linguistic NLP. Eliminating the aforementioned restrictions, including augmenting the dataset size, integrating more varied sources, and employing enhanced alignment algorithms, constitutes the focus of future endeavours.

Radiological manifestations of nail-patella syndrome in a 56-year-old female: A case report
Authors: Saroj Sitaula, Sabina Sitaula, Pragati Khadka, Roshna Adhikari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Nail-patella syndrome (NPS) is a rare autosomal dominant pleiotropic disorder characterized by dysplasia of the nails, patellar aplasia or hypoplasia, iliac horns and dysplasia of the elbows. We present a case of a 56-year-old female presenting with bilateral knee pain, where initial radiographic findings of hypoplastic patellae prompted further investigation, revealing characteristic skeletal anomalies consistent with NPS. This case underscores the importance of recognizing radiological clues and conducting thorough clinical evaluation to diagnose rare genetic conditions such as NPS.

Introduction

Nail-patella syndrome (NPS) is a genetic disorder inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, impacting tissues derived from both ectoderm and mesoderm. It presents with symmetric abnormalities in nails, skeletal structure, eyes, and kidneys. The condition occurs in approximately 1 in 50,000 live births [1]. Skeletal characteristics of the syndrome encompass absent or underdeveloped patellae, dislocated patellas, elbow irregularities, clubfoot, and the presence of iliac horns evident in radiographic imaging. These iliac horns are considered a distinctive hallmark of Nail-patella syndrome [2]. The presentation of symptoms varies greatly in both occurrence and intensity, with differences seen both between individuals and within the same family. While some patients may experience severe manifestations of NPS in certain areas, others may exhibit milder symptoms or none at all in other aspects. Although diagnosis may occur shortly after birth, it is not uncommon for families to go unrecognized for multiple generations, despite seeking medical attention from various specialists [3].

Here, we describe a case of NPS in a 56-year-old female presenting with knee pain, where radiological evaluation played a pivotal role in establishing the diagnosis.

Discussion

Nail-patella syndrome (NPS) is a hereditary condition that follows an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance with variable expression. Nail-patella syndrome (NPS) is distinguished by a unique constellation of clinical features, commonly referred to as a tetrad, which includes dysplastic nails, underdeveloped or missing patellae, dislocation of the radial head, and the presence of iliac horns [4].

Nail dysplasia and patellar hypoplasia serve as key indicators in diagnosing NPS. Typically, fingernails bear the brunt of abnormalities, with thumbs often showing the severest signs. Toe nail involvement is rare. Nails may be absent, underdeveloped, or malformed [1].

Patellar abnormalities include aplasia or hypoplasia. Recurrent dislocation, especially superolateral subluxation, is prevalent [3].

Elbow abnormalities are common, seen in over 90% of NPS patients. Dysplasia typically affects the radial heads, and capitula, often resulting in recurrent posterolateral displacement of radial heads [1].

Iliac horns, distinctive to NPS, appear in 70%-80% of cases. These bilateral, conical bony protrusions project dorsolaterally from the posterior ilia. Though usually asymptomatic, they may be palpable [1].

Open-angle glaucoma and progressive kidney disease are also associated with NPS [1]. Treatment primarily addresses the orthopedic and nonorthopedic aspects of the disorder, as there's currently no cure.

Conclusion

This case highlights the importance of considering rare genetic disorders such as NPS in the differential diagnosis of musculoskeletal complaints. Radiographic evaluation plays a crucial role in identifying characteristic skeletal anomalies associated with NPS, facilitating timely diagnosis and appropriate management.

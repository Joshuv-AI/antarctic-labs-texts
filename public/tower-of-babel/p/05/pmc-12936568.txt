Letter to the Editor: remarks on ‘Interrelations of vitamin D status with adiposity and muscle mass in adult women’
Authors: Aleena Saleemi, Mustjab Ahmed, Asma Azam
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the article by Rula.1 We want to give credit to the authors for raising awareness of a pressing issue that is significant to women’s health. The study provides valuable new information on the complex relationship between vitamin D levels, body composition and muscle mass—a topic that is increasingly recognised for its clinical and public health importance. Nevertheless, despite the study’s important contributions, certain limitations should be carefully considered in light of the current body of research.

The exclusion of factors such as participant skin type and pigmentation significantly restricts the research. Darker skin types have higher levels of melanin. The melanin pigment in the skin absorbs UVR (ultravoilet rays), shielding the underlying skin from damage. This absorption also reduces the amount of UVR available for the skin’s synthesis of vitamin D, indicating that skin type plays a significant role in vitamin D levels.2

Additionally, the study contains no information regarding the participants’ levels of physical activity, which is known to have an impact on the levels of vitamin D in the blood. A study on the effect of physical activity on vitamin D levels has shown a positive correlation between increased physical activity and serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) concentration.3

The female participants' use of oral contraceptives or estrogen therapy, both of which have been demonstrated to impact serum 25(OH)D concentrations, is not taken into consideration in this study. Using oral contraceptives that contain oestrogen is associated with higher vitamin D levels, according to a large US follow-up study.4 Furthermore, in the context of oral contraception or hormone replacement therapy, earlier smaller studies have suggested that oestrogen may be linked to increased serum levels of 25(OH)D, a critical biomarker for vitamin D status. If these variables are ignored, the study’s validity and generalisability may be restricted because it may not fairly represent the actual nature of the connection between vitamin D levels, muscle mass and adiposity.

Despite these limitations, by offering valuable insights into the relationship between muscle mass, adiposity and vitamin D status, the study contributes significantly to the body of existing knowledge and establishes the groundwork for more comprehensive future research.

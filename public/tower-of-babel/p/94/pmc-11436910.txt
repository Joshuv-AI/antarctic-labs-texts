Evaluating the role of Demodex infestation and meibomian gland dysfunction in recurrent corneal erosion syndrome
Authors: Xiaoguang Niu, Shujuan Wu, Guimei Xia, Man Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis investigation aims to observe the effects of demodex infection and meibomian gland function in recurrent corneal erosion syndrome (RCES), as well as the efficacy of intense pulsed light (IPL) in treating RCES. The study enrolled thirty patients diagnosed with RCES (30 eyes) alongside a control group of thirty-one individuals (31 eyes). Both cohorts underwent a series of diagnostic evaluations, including eyelash sampling, Demodex mite enumeration, infrared imaging of the meibomian glands, and blepharolipin scoring. The RCES subjects were further categorized into two subgroups upon stabilization for comparative analysis of treatment outcomes: the RCES-A subgroup received IPL therapy (16 patients), and the RCES-B subgroup was administered medication treatment (14 patients). Post-treatment, all participants were re-evaluated using the initial diagnostic procedures to monitor for recurrence. Preliminary findings indicated significant differences between the RCES and control groups in terms of meibomian gland scores (4 [3.0, 4.0] vs. 2 [1.0, 3.0]), blepharolipin scores (15.5 [11.0, 16.8] vs. 8.0 [5.5, 10.0]), and lid margin scores (3.0 [2.8, 3.0] vs. 2.0 [1.0, 3.0]), with P < 0.01 for all comparisons. Additionally, the Demodex count was significantly higher in the RCES group (8.0 [4.0,9.0]) compared to the control (0 [0, 2]) (Z = − 4.13, P = 0.00), with a Demodex positivity rate of 83.3% in the RCES group versus 38.7% in the control group (χ2 = 7.60, P < 0.01). Post-treatment, the RCES-A subgroup exhibited significant improvements in meibomian gland loss scores, blepharolipin scores, lid margin abnormality scores, and a reduction in Demodex counts (P < 0.01), with a post-treatment Demodex positivity rate of 56.3% (P = 0.11). During the follow-up, the RCES-A subgroup experienced a lower relapse rate compared to the RCES-B subgroup (1 vs. 6 patients). The findings suggest a correlation between meibomian gland dysfunction and Demodex infestation with the incidence of RCES. The application of IPL therapy in combination with meibomian gland massage demonstrates significant potential in enhancing meibomian gland functionality, reducing Demodex counts, and effectively mitigating the recurrence of RCES. Introduction

Recurrent corneal erosion syndrome (RCES) is recognized as a prevalent condition within ophthalmic practice, characterized by the failure of the corneal epithelial basement membrane to adequately adhere to the corneal stroma, leading to recurrent desquamation of the corneal epithelium1. This pathology results in episodic exacerbations that substantially impair the quality of life of affected individuals through significant physical discomfort and psychological distress. The etiological spectrum of RCES encompasses corneal epithelial basement membrane dystrophy, traumatic corneal injuries, herpetic keratopathy, and meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), among others2,3. MGD, in particular, denotes a chronic or diffuse abnormality of the meibomian gland, manifesting as terminal duct obstruction and alterations in both the quality and quantity of glandular secretions. This condition disrupts the tear film’s stability, potentially leading to ocular surface irritation, with or without concomitant inflammation, and is frequently associated with other ocular surface disorders. Emerging evidence suggests a predominant role of MGD in the pathogenesis of RCES, underscoring a significant correlation between these conditions4. Additionally, a high prevalence of Demodex infestation, along with an increased Demodex load on the eyelashes, has been documented in patients with MGD, indicating a potential contributory role of Demodex in the disease process5. Nevertheless, the specific involvement of Demodex infestation in the pathogenesis of RCES, as well as the impact of MGD treatment in mitigating RCES, remains to be elucidated.

The present study aims to investigate the prevalence of Demodex infestation and evaluate meibomian gland functionality in patients diagnosed with RCES. It seeks to explore the association between Demodex infection, the extent of meibomian gland dysfunction, and the occurrence of RCES. Furthermore, this research endeavors to assess the therapeutic efficacy of intense pulsed light (IPL) therapy in conjunction with meibomian gland massage in managing RCES symptoms and reducing the frequency of disease recurrence.

Materials and methods

Studying objects

This is a prospective case-control study. We screened 30 patients (30 eyes) with RCES in Wuhan Aier Ophthalmology Hanyang Eye Hospital from June 2019 to December 2019 as the RCES group. Inclusion criteria: two or more recurrent of RCES; A clear history of minor eye trauma; Unilateral ocular disease. Exclusion criteria: corneal dystrophy including epithelial basement membrane dystrophy, corneal infection, visible corneal scars with slit-lamp examination, intraocular hypertension, dacryocystitis, intraocular inflammation, as well as diabetes mellitus and systemic immune diseases. Additionally, thirty-one patients (31 right eyes) with matched gender and age, and without other ocular surface diseases were selected from the optometry clinic as the control group. Both groups underwent anterior ocular photography, Demodex exam by eyelash microscopic examination, and infrared photography of meibomian gland using an ocular surface comprehensive analyzer. This study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hanyang Aier Eye Hospital (HYEYE2018IRB01). The study used the CONSORT reporting guidelines6. All subjects were informed of the purpose and methods of the study and voluntarily signed an informed consent form.

Therapeutic method

All patients in RCES group were treated with 0.3% Gatifloxacin Eye Gel (Shenyang Xingqi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) and 0.3% sodium hyaluronate eye drops (Beijing Huonland Pharmaceutical Co., LTD, China) after the first-time consultancy. After two weeks of eye drop treatment, all 30 participants’ corneal erosion healed and inflammation subsided. Then they were randomized on a 1:1 ratio to two groups (Fig. 1). The random number table was generated by an independent statistician using Excel. The patients in the RCES-A group treated with IPL combined with meibomian gland massage three times, once every 28 days; The patients in the RCES-B group treated with 0.3% sodium hyaluronate eye drops (Beijing Huonland Pharmaceutical Co., LTD, China) only. Calculate the sample size based on the empirical rule proposed by Julius for pilot studies9.

CONSORT Flow diagram for the recruitment and randomization of participants.

SOLARI (Lutronic, Korea) intense pulse optical equipment was used for the treatment; parameters were tested in the patient’s jaw before the first treatment, adjust the IPL energy according to the patient’s skin color, the energy range was 9–13 J/cm², kept the patient’s skin clean and dry, and wore an eye mask to protect the patient’s eyes. The treatment area was uniformly coated with a medical coupling agent for 5–0 mm. Doctor was expected to wear goggles during the whole treatment. Four therapeutic parts were selected from the left temple to the right, putting the probe on the coupling agent part, press the on-off button to emit the pulses of light, then move to the next part, and redo the operation until all the therapeutic parts have been done. When the treatment has been finished, remove the skin coupling gel, drop 0.4% bupivacaine hydrochloride (Santen Pharmaceutical co. LTD Japan) into the conjunctival sac two times, once every 5 min, then massaged the upper and lowered meibomian gland with meibomian gland massage tweezers. The same physician performed all procedures. When all treatment has been finished, ice the eyes for 5 min.

Therapeutic evaluation

One week after the accomplishment of three treatments in RCES-A group and three months after treatments in RCES-B group, the patients were admitted to the hospital for the meibomian gland loss score, blepharolipin score and the number of Demodex evaluation. During a one - year follow - up period, the recurrence of corneal erosion between the two groups was compared. Patients were interviewed about their symptoms and underwent slit lamp examinations every month for the first six months, then every three months for the next six months. In the second six-month period, if patients were unable to visit the clinic, follow - up was conducted via telephone or internet to inquire about discomfort symptoms such as eye redness and foreign body sensation.

Statistical analysis

SPSS22.0 (SPSS, USA) was used for statistical analysis. In this study, all counting data were skewed by the shapiro-wilk test and expressed by M (Q1, Q3). The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare the meibomian gland loss score, the blepharolipin score, and the number of Demodex in the two groups. The chi-square test was used to compare the positive detection rate of granular Demodex and the recurrence rate of the disease, P < 0.05 was considered significant differences statistically.

Comparison of scores for meibomian gland, blepharolipin and lid margin

All 30 patients diagnosed with Recurrent Corneal Erosion Syndrome (RCES) exhibited varying degrees of eyelid margin hyperemia and obstruction of the meibomian gland orifices. At the base of their eyelashes, scale-like and cuff-shaped secretions were present, indicative of ocular surface inflammation. Additionally, meibography revealed morphological alterations in the meibomian glands, characterized by tortuosity, shortening, and in some cases, complete absence. Comparative analysis demonstrated that the meibomian gland functionality in patients with RCES was significantly compromised relative to that of the control cohort (P<0.01) (Table 1).

Comparison of Demodex

The positive rate of demodex was 83.3% in RCES group and 38.7% in control group, with a significant difference between the 2 groups (χ2 = 7.60, P<0.01). In the RCES group, the maximum number of demodex in one eye was 18, while in the control group, the maximum number of demodex in one eye was 8 (Table 1).

Therapeutic effect

16 patients (16 eyes) were enrolled in the RCES-A group, comprising 7 males and 9 females, while the RCES-B group consisted of 14 patients (14 eyes), with an equal distribution of 7 males and 7 females. There was no significant difference in age, gender and other parameters of the two groups (Table 2). One week after the accomplishment of three treatments in RCES-A group and three months after treatments in RCES-B group, all patients were followed up in hospital. the RCES-A group exhibited notable improvements. Specifically, there was a marked reduction in posterior eyelid congestion, a significant decrease in the adherence of secretions at the base of the eyelashes, and reductions in both the meibomian gland loss score and the blepharolipin score, indicating an overall improvement in ocular surface health. Furthermore, both the quantity of Demodex mites and the rate of Demodex detection were significantly diminished post-treatment (Table 3; Fig. 2). Conversely, the RCES-B group did not demonstrate any significant changes over the same period (Table 4).

During a one-year follow-up period post-treatment, six patients in RCES-A group and six patients in RCES-B group with symptoms were followed up in hospital. The remaining 18 patients, due to the absence of any ocular discomfort and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, did not visit the clinic and were followed up via telephone or internet. One patient in the RCES-A group experienced a recurrence of symptoms over five months post-treatment while five patients occasionally reported a mild sensation of a foreign body in the eye three months to a year post-treatment. In those patients experiencing a foreign body sensation, there are no typical symptoms of recurrent corneal erosions such as conjunctival congestion, photophobia, tearing, etc. The affected corneal regions, which were subject to repeated epithelial erosion, exhibited punctate opacities; however, these areas remained non-detached. In contrast, within the RCES-B group, six patients reported a recurrence of symptoms. The difference in recurrence rates between the two groups was statistical significance. (χ2=5.04, P = 0.04).

Discussion

This study found an association between recurrent corneal erosion (RCES) and both meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) and Demodex infection. Following IPL treatment, improvements were observed not only in meibomian gland function and Demodex infection but also in the control of RCES.

In 1994, Hope-Ross MW4 researched 30 patients with refractory RCES who had poor responses to therapy. He found that all patients had MGD, with most exhibiting meibomian gland atrophy and suggested a significant correlation between MGD and RCES. In 2014, scholars studied 100 RCES patients (117 eyes), revealing that 60 eyes also had moderate to severe MGD that required treatment. It was believed that increased levels of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) due to meibomian gland dysfunction played a role in the development of RCES10. This present research also found that all RCES patients demonstrated various extents of Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD). This was characterized by a reduction in the quality of the blepharolipin, along with significantly elevated blepharolipin scores when compared to the control group. These findings suggest that MGD may play a contributory role in the pathogenesis of RCES, aligning with conclusions drawn in prior research.

MGD can be caused by various factors such as infection, inflammation, neurosecretory disorders, congenital abnormalities, and Stellwag, while blepharitis can lead to severe MGD. It had been reported that there is a close relationship between the Demodex and anterior blepharitis; Demodex was positively correlated with the symptom severity and evaluated scores; controlling Demodex could significantly improve blepharitis symptoms11,12. Studies have suggested that the main cause of meibomian gland obstruction is hyperkeratosis of the terminal duct and openings13,14. Furthermore, Demodex can act as a bacteria carrier, transporting bacteria to the palpebral edge during the process of acting towards the palpebral edge. The bacteria-produced esterase may break down meibum into free fatty acids and other toxic substances, inducing subclinical inflammation and releasing pro-inflammatory factors. This can increase meibum viscosity or promote epithelial keratinization of the glands, leading to meibomian gland obstruction15. Thus, Demodex also plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of MGD. Moreover, studies have shown that both MGD and Demodex infestations are potential cause of ocular surface16. But the mechanism is currently not fully elucidated. This study found that almost all RCES patients had angiotelectasis of the palpebral edge and hyperkeratosis of the meibomian gland opening; multiple forms of secretions were attached to the root of most patients’ eyelashes. These are characteristic signs of Demodex blepharitis17, and the detection rate of palpebral edge Demodex in these patients was much higher than in normal patients. In this study, the detection rate of Demodex in the normal control group was only 38.7%, which was similar in previous literature18, while the detection rate in RCES patients was 83.3%, indicatting a common occurrence of Demodex infection in RCES patients. Sun et al.19 found that the positive rate of Demodex in MGD patients was 51.5%, suggesting that Demodex infection of the eyelid margin is involved in the pathogenesis of MGD and may be directly or indirectly involved in the pathogenesis of RCES.

The current treatment of RCES primarily includes medication, wearing corneal contact lenses, mechanical and laser puncture of the anterior corneal stroma, and phototherapeutic keratectomy (PTK), etc. However, no treatment has been able to prevent recurrence entirely, with data showing that all treatments have a recurrence rate of 8 ~ 25%20–22.

Treatment for MGD aims to improve the secretory function of meibomian glands, enhance tear film stability, and alleviate discomfort. Current treatments include physiotherapy such as palpebral edge hygiene, warm compresses and meibomian glands massage; medications like artificial tears eye drops, anti-inflammatory drugs, and antibiotic eye drops; and dietary therapy. IPL is a mature dermatology technology used to treat skin telangiectasia, erythema, pigmentation, skin aging, and other conditions, and is widely used for its effective therapeutic outcomes. Toyos et al.23 found that IPL improved the ocular surface in patients with facial acne and proposed it as a potential treatment for MGD. Previous studies have shown that IPL can be selectively absorbed by melanin and heme in deep skin tissues, leading to the destruction and decomposition of pigment groups, coagulation of blood vessels, and elimination of abnormal telangiectasia, thus reducing vascularization of the palpebral edge of MGD patients24. In this study, after three times of IPL combined with meibomian gland massage, we observed reduced congestion of the palpebral edge, significantly improved meibum secretion, and improved meibomian gland atrophy, corroborating the findings of other researchers25. Additionally, IPL has been demonstrated to effectively eradicate Demodex mites on the skin and eyelids, a finding that this study also confirms26,27. Although Demodex was still present after three times of IPL sessions, their numbers had decreased.

In this study, the ultimate goal of treating MGD was to manage RCES and reduce its recurrence. Remarkably, after three sessions of IPL with meibomian gland massage, the meibomian gland function and Demodex counts were significantly improved, and the corneal erosion of the patients was effectively controlled. During the follow-up period, there was just only one case of relapse, which is significantly lower than control; The patients exhibited only punctate turbidity in the corneal epithelium, compared to the large corneal epithelial defects seen in previous recurrences, marking a significant improvement. These results suggest that treating MGD and Demodex can effectively control RCES, offering a new approach to non-invasive clinical treatment of RCES.

However, the study had several limitations: Firstly, both the experimental and control groups had relatively small sample sizes, and the selection of patients from the optometry clinic for the control group may have introduced selection bias, potentially influencing the study results. Some patients did not experience any ocular discomfort and, due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, did not attend follow-up visits in person, instead conducting follow-ups via telephone or online. This may have introduced certain limitations in the evaluation of treatment outcomes. Secondly, while studies have shown that the duration of the effect of IPL on meibomian gland dysfunction is no more than 6–12 months28, the observation period for this study was only one year, and there is a lack of data on whether there is disease recurrence after that period. Furthermore, this study did not assess the duration of the effects of IPL treatment, thus it cannot be confirmed whether the recurrence and the sensation of foreign bodies in patients in Group A are related to the diminishing effects of IPL treatment. Future studies will focus on whether relapses occur as the follow-up period extends and on the therapeutic mechanisms of RCES, including whether IPL treatment reduces ocular surface inflammatory cytokines. Additionally, certain innovative artificial tear, such as perfluorohexyloctane-based artificial tear that is able to penetrate the meibomian glands and improve the quality of the meibum29, as well as physical therapies that improve the function of the meibomian glands, such as microblepharon exfoliation (MBE)30. Whether these drugs or physical therapy combined with IPL will have a better therapeutic effect is the direction of future research.

In summary, this research corroborates the association between RCES, MGD, and Demodex infestation, while causation has not been firmly established and further research is needed. The significant reduction in RCES symptoms and recurrence rates post-IPL therapy suggests that addressing MGD and Demodex may be pivotal in managing RCES. While IPL therapy’s long-term efficacy warrants further investigation, this study indicate a promising non-invasive treatment modality for RCES, with potential implications for improving patient quality of life.

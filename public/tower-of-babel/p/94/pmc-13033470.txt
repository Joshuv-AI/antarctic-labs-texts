Theoretical analysis for heat and mass transfer in bioconvective ternary hybrid nanofluid flow with entropy optimization
Authors: Fazal Haq, Naglaa AbdelAll, Ghada A. Khouqeer, Jihad Younis, Mohammed Sallah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ternary hybrid nanofluids(THNFs) are a revolutionary advancement in thermal management, offering remarkable thermal conductivity that significantly boosts heat transfer efficiency. THNFs are ideal for cooling systems, solar energy applications, and electronic device regulation, where effective heat management is crucial. By fine tuning their composition, researchers can tailor these nanofluids to meet specific industrial needs, leading to improved efficiency and energy savings. The goal of current study is twofold: first, to examine the improvement of heat, mass, and motile density of THNF, and secondly, to investigate the irreversibilities in bioconvective dihybrid and trihybrid nanofluids. For dihybrid nanofluid the volume fraction of copper is taken as zero. Maxwell fluid model is utilized to analyze the performance of THNF and dihybrid nanofluid(DHNF). The flow in THNF and DHNF is induced due to an impermeable stretched sheet. Flow governing equations for DHNF and THNF are obtained considering diverse assumptions like electro-magnetohydrodynamic(EMHD), Dufour, Soret, chemical reaction, thermal radiation, and activation energy. Irreversibilities are modeled with the help of thermodynamics second law. The model equations are altered into ordinary system via transformation procedure. Numerical simulations are carried out through built-in function(NDSolve) of Mathematica. Impact of generated variables on DHNF and THNF velocity, thermal field, motile density profile and mass concentration are examined. Comparative analysis for THNF and DHNF is performed. Furthermore, local heat, mass, density, and skin friction coefficient for THNF and DHNF are numerically investigated. Numerical results show that the skin friction coefficient for THNF is 3.2% more than that of DHNF, and the heat transfer rate of THNF is up to 16% higher than that of DHNF. The density number of DHNF is about 1.08% less than that of THNF.

Introduction

The THNFs, composed of three distinct types of nanoparticles suspended in a carrier base fluid, offer enhanced electrical, thermal, and rheological properties compared to DHNF and single-phase nanofluids. The synergistic interaction between diverse nanoparticles, such as metal, metal oxide, and carbon based materials, significantly improves thermal conductivity, stability, and heat transfer rate. This makes THNFs highly valuable in advanced heat exchange systems, electronic cooling, solar collectors, and biomedical applications. Their ability to optimize thermal management and energy transport in thermodynamical systems is particularly significant in sectors like automotive, aerospace, and microelectronics [1–6]. Furthermore, their tunable properties enable researchers and engineers to design application specific fluids for tailored performance in complex thermal environments. Cao et al. [7] presented a comparative study for radiative THNF flow induced due to mixed, forced, and free convection for the case when radiative heat flux is mainly influenced by temperature difference. Performance of THNF flow caused by twofold stretching wedge surfaces subject to force convection for the cases when the volume fraction of nanoparticles is small and large was analyzed by Xiu et al. [8]. Nagaraja et al. [9] inspected the consequences of opposing and assisting radiative THNF flow, considering the impacts of mixed convection and first order reactions. Guedri et al. [10] studied Darcy Forchheimer flow of THNF over a nonlinear porous surface of a stretched sheet with mixed convection, Brownian dispersion, and thermophoresis features. Dynamics of blood plasma-based THNF flow in a wavy channel accounting the impact of Hall current, was reported by Obalalu et al. [11]. Gul et al. [12] assessed the implications of homogeneous and heterogeneous reactions in THNF and DHNF. They also presented a comparative analysis of the performance of HNF and THNF flows considering a stretched surface. Nasir et al. [13, 14] implemented numerical and artificial neural network-based analysis for rotation and stretching HNF flows. Zainal et al. [15] examined the behavior of the stagnation point in magnetized HNF non-Newtonian flow, accounting the influence of radiation and mixed convection. Studies [16–21] cover diverse aspects and applications of DHNF and THNFs, considering flows through diverse geometries.

The Maxwell fluid model occupies a central position in viscoelastic fluid mechanics due to its ability to capture the fundamental interplay between viscous dissipation and elastic stress relaxation using a single relaxation time. The model provides essential physical insight into time-dependent and memory driven flow phenomena, making it particularly valuable for analytical and semi analytical studies. Maxwell type behavior is relevant to a wide range of materials, including dilute polymer solutions, melts under slow deformation, certain biological fluids, and industrial processing fluids where stress relaxation is dominant. The model has been widely applied in studies of transient flows, oscillatory shear, boundary layer behavior, heat and mass transfer, and microfluidic transport, where it often serves as a baseline framework against which more complex viscoelastic models are compared. Consequently, the Maxwell fluid remains an important and practically relevant constitutive model for understanding viscoelastic effects in both fundamental research and engineering applications. Several researchers investigated diverse aspects of Maxwell fluid [22–26].

Electro-magnetohydrodynamic (EMHD) refers to the study of fluid flow behavior influenced by the combined effects of magnetic and electric fields, particularly in electrically conducting fluids. In EMHD fluid flows, the interaction between the electric current (induced or applied) and magnetic fields generates a body force known as the Lorentz force, which can control or alter the flow dynamics. This coupling mechanism augments fluid motion, mixing, and heat transfer without mechanical components, making EMHD predominantly useful in microfluidic systems, cooling of electronic devices, bioengineering applications, and energy systems like MHD generators or liquid metal cooling systems [27, 28]. In THNFs, the presence of diverse nanoparticles further improves thermal conductivity and responsiveness to electromagnetic fields, leading to more efficient thermal management and precise flow control at micro and nano scales. EMHD is especially significant in environments where traditional pumping or heating techniques are ineffective, offering a promising approach for next-generation smart fluidic systems. Considering the impact of thermal radiation and chemical reaction, Daniel et al. [29] scrutinized the behavior of electrical MHD flow of nanoliquid through a variable thicked surface, accounting the mechanisms of Brownian dispersion and thermophoretic diffusions. Consequences of melting heat, surface roughness, and entropy analysis for EMHD hybrid nanofluid flow around the stagnation point were reported numerically by Kayalvizhi and Kumar [30]. Areekara et al. [31] presented a statistical analysis for stratified EMHD bioconvective fluid flow focusing applications in theranostics. The characteristics of mixed convection and thermal radiation source in EMHD hybrid nanofluid flow for the case of exponentially stretching surface were explored by Ramesh et al. [32].

Bioconvection is a naturally occurring phenomenon in fluid flows, arising from the collective motion of motile microorganisms, particularly gyrotactic-type microorganisms such as bacteria or algae. These microorganisms tend to swim vertically upward against gravity due to their bottom-heavy structure, known as gyrotaxis. When suspended in a fluid, their upward swimming leads to an unstable concentration gradient in THNF, causing the fluid to become denser at the top and lighter at the bottom. This imbalance triggers a convective instability, resulting in the spontaneous formation of patterns like plumes, cells, or vortices within the fluid, referred to as bioconvection. In engineered systems, the addition of gyrotactic microorganisms into nanofluid flows not only enhances mixing and mass transfer but also impacts flow dynamics and heat transfer. This phenomenon has gained attention in applications such as bio-microsystems, bioreactors, and microfluidic devices. The impact of nanoparticles on the stability of suspension of motile microorganisms of gyrotactic type in parallel fluid layers of restricted depth was studied by Kuznetsov and Avramenko [33]. They concluded that a critical Rayleigh number improves the density of particles and thus stabilizes the suspension. Khan et al. [34] scrutinized the physical aspects of mixed convection in chemically reactive flow of bioconvective DHNF through thin stirring needle under magnetic flux. Consequences of Dufour and Soret effects in mixed convection magnetized bioconvective flow of carbon nanotubes based hybrid nanofluid flow with convective surface restrictions have been explored by Hussain et al. [35]. Shamshuddin et al. [36] make a distinct contribution by thoroughly examining the mass, motile density, and heat transfer phenomena in MHD bioconvective flow of DHNF under the influence of an exponentially stretching sheet. Hussain et al. [37] explored the dynamics of bioconvection in hybrid nanofluid flow by a lid-driven cavity caused by oxytactic microbes under the influence of a streamlined obstacle. Abdal et al. [38] addressed the development of heat transfer through bioconvection in the flow of Maxwell nanofluids, induced by an extendable sheet. This investigation considers the effects of radiative heat flux, alongside conditions of prescribed surface temperature and specified heat flux. The studies [39–43] cover diverse aspects of bioconvection in nanofluid flows.

Entropy generation (EG) in fluid flows is a vital concept in thermodynamics, representing the irreversible loss of energy due to dissipative effects. Minimizing EG is crucial for improving the efficiency of thermal and fluidic systems, such as turbines, heat exchangers, microchannels, and nanofluid based devices. Key factors contributing to EG include viscous dissipation from fluid friction, heat transfer across temperature gradients, mass diffusion, and interactions with magnetic and electric fields. In advanced fluids like THNFs, the random movement of nanoparticles also contributes to entropy production. High EG indicates greater irreversibility and lower system performance, making its analysis important for optimizing energy conversion processes, especially in microscale systems. Bejan [44] performed second law analysis for EG considering flow by different geometries. The analysis focuses on the interplay between irreversibility caused by heat transfer along finite temperature gradients and irreversibility due to viscous effects. Considering mixed convection and chemical reactions, Hayat et al. [45] explored the EG in viscous fluid flow due to a stretched variable thicked surface with radiative heat flux. Shah et al. [46] inspected the features of Darcy Forchheimer flow of engine oil with suspension of nickel zinc, and manganese nanoparticles, invoking entropy production minimization. Khan et al. [47] implemented the Cattaneo–Christov model for heat conduction in entropy optimized DHNF flow by axially stretching porous cylinder. Mahboobtosi et al. [48] conducted a study to investigate the EG and irreversibilities in THNF flow by curved geometry with a heat source/sink, neglecting the surface roughness. Rahman et al. [24] explored the features of surface roughness and radiation effects in Maxwell DHNF flow with entropy analysis.

The THNFs, composed of cobalt ferrite (CoFe₂O₄), molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂), and copper (Cu) nanoparticles dispersed in pure engine oil, exhibit exceptional significance due to the synergistic enhancement of thermal, tribological, and magnetohydrodynamic properties. Cobalt ferrite nanoparticles impart strong magnetic responsiveness, thermal stability, and enhanced heat conduction, enabling external control of flow and heat transfer in advanced lubrication systems [49–51]. Molybdenum disulfide, a well-known solid lubricant, significantly reduces friction and wear by forming protective tribofilms, thereby improving the anti-scuffing and anti-seizure characteristics of engine oil [52–54]. Copper nanoparticles contribute high intrinsic thermal conductivity, accelerating heat dissipation from engine components and reducing localized thermal stresses [55–57]. The combined dispersion of these three nanoparticles produces a synergistic effect that surpasses the performance of mono- and binary nanofluids, leading to improved heat transfer rates, reduced frictional losses, enhanced load-carrying capacity, and prolonged lubricant life. Consequently, this ternary hybrid nanofluid is highly promising for next-generation engine lubrication, thermal management of high performance mechanical systems, and energy efficient automotive applications, where simultaneous control of heat transfers and tribological behavior is critically required. This study examines various aspects of bioconvective flow of engine oil-based HNFs. Future research can expand on this topic in several ways, including the following suggestions:Investigating the effects of inertial forces and surface porosity using the Darcy-Forchheimer model.Considering the Cattaneo-Christov model for heat and mass transfer.Examining the inclusion of thermal, solutal, and motile density stratifications.Analyzing the impact of heat sources or sinks.Exploring different geometries, such as cylinders, disks, and curved surfaces.

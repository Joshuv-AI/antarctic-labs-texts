Ion and Stable Isotope Geochemistry of Produced Water in Coalbed Methane Wells in the Tiefa Basin
Authors: Huazhou Huang, Chuanxia Tong, Zhengqing Wu, Junjie Ding, Zaixing Huang, Huan He, Jinbo Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The potential geochemical information in the produced water of coalbed methane (CBM) wells is conducive to the exploration and development of CBM in the case that the produced water is primitive formation water. A total of 58 produced water samples collected from 13 CBM wells in the Daxing Mine, Tiefa Basin, were investigated. Ionic composition tests and stable isotope analysis were conducted to explore the geochemical characteristics and sources of produced water as well as the method for determining whether the produced water is primitive formation water. The results suggest that the fracking fluid for CBM stimulations is the main factor affecting the ion change of the produced water in the initial stage of drainage. The concentrations of Cl– and Ca2+ + Mg2+ could be taken as the indices to identify whether the produced water is primitive formation water. When the Cl– concentration is lower than 20 mEq/L and the Ca2+ + Mg2+ concentration is lower than 1 mEq/L, the produced water is close to the pristine formation water. Biogenic methanogenic activity may result in a high δ13CDIC and high concentrations of HCO3– in the pristine formation water in the Tiefa Basin. The data of δD and δ18O in the study area suggest that the formation water might come from atmospheric precipitation, which is later affected by evaporation and the water–rock reaction. The hydrogen isotope values in the produced water derived from the lower coal group display a substantial elevation compared to those from the upper coal group. This disparity in the hydrogen isotope composition presents an opportunity to utilize δD in produced water as a tool for distinguishing the formation water between these two groups.

Introduction

Coalbed methane (CBM) has been commercially exploited in the United States, Australia, China, Canada, and other countries.1−3 Before 2021, a total of 22,506 CBM wells were constructed in China, yielding 8.29 billion cubic meters of gas. A great deal of groundwater is removed by pumping to lower the hydrostatic pressure that allows CBM to desorb.4−6 The extracted water during CBM production is known as produced water.5,7 Parameters and chemical properties of produced water have implications on CBM formation and accumulation, which could help us better understand CBM reservoirs.8,9 The chemical composition of formation water contains information related to gas reservoirs as formation water is a key element in the formation, storage, and migration of CBM.10 Reduction in reservoir pressure in CBM development is affected by groundwater’s recharge, runoff, and discharge as well as the source of formation water. Exploration and development of CBM will benefit greatly by fully extracting and utilizing this information on produced water in CBM wells.10,11 This information includes water quality, trace elements, and stable isotopes. Analysis of water quality includes pH, inorganic ions, dissolved organic matter in water, total dissolved solids (TDS), etc.12−14 Major cations and anions in water quality analysis include K+, Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+, Fe2+, Fe3+, NH4+, Mn2+, Cl–, SO42–, CO32–, HCO3–, NO3–, Br–, and F–. Trace elements,15−17 stable isotopes of hydrogen (δD), oxygen (δ18O) in H2O, iodine, dissolved organic carbon, and dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) in water have also been extensively documented.18−21

Produced water is usually found in high TDS.22,23 The water quality varies with some factors such as the sedimentary environment10 and biogeochemical processes.24,25 Produced water from continental sedimentary formations is dominated by sodium and bicarbonate,10,26,27 e.g., the Tiefa Basin28 and Powder River Basin.29 The chemical components in produced water could be used to extrapolate the runoff direction of groundwater27 and identify where the produced water comes from30 in a condition that the produced water is not mingled with the fracking fluid. Hydraulic fracking technology is commonly employed to enhance CBM production in low-permeability coal reservoirs in China.31 The fracking fluid is injected into the coal seam to induce fractures, to allow gas to flow into the wellbore.28 The produced water in the Tiefa Basin is found to be composed of fracturing fluid and formation water.28 The fracking fluid in produced water can complicate the extraction of information from the produced water. Chlorine concentration and residual alkalinity can be used to analyze the impact of the fracking fluid on produced water.32

However, further exploration is needed to quantitatively analyze the impact of the fracking fluid on produced water and determine its original composition as formation water.

Stable isotopes of hydrogen and oxygen along with δ13C of dissolved inorganic carbon (δ13CDIC) in produced water can be used to trace the water source and the processes of groundwater mixing33−36 and identify the sources.11,37 The local meteoric water line could be established by δD and δ18O. If the hydrogen and oxygen isotope values of produced water are located near the local atmospheric precipitation line, it suggests that the produced water might originate from atmospheric precipitation.38 The data points above the local meteoric water line are known as D drift, while the data points below the local meteoric water line are known as O drift.39 These are observed in the Bowen Basin21 and the Powder River Basin.29,35,36 D drift of formation water may be affected by microbial activity like methanogenesis.21,29 O drift of formation water may be caused by mixing with near-surface water and then experiencing evaporation and water–rock interaction.40,41 Little attention has been paid to the stable isotope analysis of produced water in the Tiefa Basin and their correlation with the origins of the formation water.

DIC in water mainly exists in CO2, H2CO3, HCO3–, and CO32–. δ13CDIC in water is related to the composition of carbon sources. δ13CDIC in natural water is mostly negative, ranging from −12 to −7‰.33,42 Inorganic carbons in produced water having a δ13CDIC value < −10‰ are formed by decomposition of organic matter.39,43 However, δ13CDIC formed by methanogenesis under reducing conditions is positive,44,45 ranging from +10 to +25‰.36,44,46 A case study has shown that δ13CDIC is positively correlated with HCO3– and negatively correlated with SO42– in coproduced water in the Powder River Basin.45 Further study is needed concerning the information and signature of δ13CDIC in produced water in the Tiefa Basin.

Existing studies have focused on the water quality over drainage time and the relationship between the water quality and gas production in the Tiefa Basin in China.5,28,47 The purpose of this article is to analyze the effect of the fracking fluid on produced water to determine whether produced water is primitive formation water, explore the causes of changes in water quality, and investigate the water sources based on ionic compositions, δD and δ18O, and δ13CDIC in produced water in the Tiefa Basin.

Geological Description of the Study Area

The Tiefa Basin in Northeast China encompasses a geological formation that consists of metamorphic rocks in the Presinian period, Lower Cretaceous strata in the Mesozoic era, and Quaternary strata in the Cenozoic era. (Figure 1). The coal-bearing strata belong to lower Cretaceous in Mesozoic. The Fuxin Formation of the lower Cretaceous, the coal-bearing formation, consists of four parts, which are the bottom glutenite member (K1f1), the lower coal group (K1f2), the middle sand-mudstone member (K1f3), and the upper coal group (K1f4) from bottom to top. The two coal groups mainly comprise sandstones, mudstones, and multiple coal seams. There are about 22–23 coal seams in each of the upper and lower coal groups, which are dominated by bituminous coal (Ro,max = 0.50–0.79%). Coalbed methane is being produced from coals in the lower coal group and upper coal group. Please refer to Huang et al.5,28 for a summary of the coalbed methane development in this area.

Stratigraphic column of the coal-bearing strata in the Tiefa Basin.

The target CBM production wells are located in the favorable block of CBM development, which is known as the Beier mining area of Daxing Mine in the southwestern part of the Tiefa Basin (Figure 2a–c). There are 40 CBM wells in the Daxing Mine, of which 21 wells are in production. This study is mainly focused on 13 CBM wells in the Beier mining area, including wells DT3, DT4, DT5, DT6, DT7, DT8, DT9, DT10, DT23, DT26, DT28, DT31, and DT32. It should be noted that the upper and lower coal groups were fractured for all of the sampling wells besides DT26 and DT32. DT26 and DT32 were built almost in the same well location to investigate the gas and water production differences between the upper and lower coal groups. The distance between the two wells is less than 15 m. The fracturing of DT26 was only performed in the upper coal group with depths of 439.05–520.20 m, while for DT32, the lower coal group was fractured at depths of 670.45–742.40 m. The lithologies of the fracturing sections in DT26 and DT32 are similar, composed mainly of silty mudstone, sandstone, and thin coal seams.

Sampling and Methods

A total of 58 produced water samples were collected from 13 CBM wells between June 1, 2011 and December 4, 2019. The sampling wells are DT3, DT4, DT5, DT6, DT7, DT8, DT9, DT10, DT23, DT26, DT28, DT31, and DT32. The sampling campaigns occurred at various times, i.e., 2011 (June 1, June 22, July 24, August 27, September 22, and December 15), 2012 (July 15, August 18, November 8), and December 4, 2019. Some data in Table 1 have been used to analyze ionic concentration variations of produced water over drainage time28 and the relationship between the ionic composition in produced water and gas production.5 The samples are collected from the water outlet of the CBM wells. The sampling methods were described elsewhere by Huang et al. and Tong et al.5,28,47

Ionic Composition of Produced Water in the Tiefa Basin

The concentrations of cations and anions, including Cl–, SO42–, CO32–, HCO3–, K+, Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+, were analyzed with an ICS-1500 ion chromatograph. Isotopes of hydrogen and oxygen were measured using a laser water isotope analyzer (LGR company 912–0026). The isotopes of DIC were tested using a gas isotope ratio mass spectrometer (MART253, Thermo Fisher Company, Germany).

Results and Multivariate Statistical Analysis

Major ions in the 58 water samples and results of δD, δ18O, and δ13CDIC are presented in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. Studies had suggested that the water type of the produced water changed from Na–Cl to Na–HCO3–Cl and finally to Na–HCO3 with drainage time under the influence of the fracking fluid based on the data before 2012.28 DT31 was an infill well with a 2 day drainage on December 15, 2011. Previous studies have indicated that the produced water from wells DT4, DT7, and DT10 is significantly influenced by the fracking fluid after December 15, 2011.5 The major ions affected by the fracking fluid are Cl–, SO42–, HCO3–, Ca2+, and Mg2+, which could be seen on December 15, 2011, in wells DT4, DT7, DT10, and DT31, as shown in Table 1. It has been reported that when the slope of the decline in Cl– concentration begins to stabilize and the concentration of chloride is far less than that of HCO3–, it means that the influence of the fracking fluid on the formation water has become minimal.28 However, quantitative methods to identify whether the produced water is the pristine formation water need further research. To meet this objective, the produced water samples were classified based on the ionic composition by the Q-mode hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA) method.24,32,48 The classification of water samples based on testing data involves several steps. First of all, the original data were normalized by 0–1. Second, outlier data are identified by the nearest neighbor linkage method.32,48 In addition, clustering is done by Ward’s linkage method. Then, the distance is measured by the squared Euclidean distance.32 Finally, the produced water samples are classified by distance. Cl–, SO42–, HCO3–, K+, Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ were selected as targets for cluster analysis. An abnormal sample, which was sampled from DT3 on December 15, 2011, is removed from the dataset based on the nearest neighbor linkage method. The rest of the 57 water samples were divided into four clusters (Table 1). Cluster C1 is characterized by the lowest Na+ concentration and lower HCO3– concentration. Cluster C2 is characterized by the highest HCO3– concentration and the lowest Cl– concentration. Cluster C3 is characterized by the lowest SO42– concentration and higher HCO3– and Cl– concentration. Cluster C4 is characterized by the highest Cl–, SO42–, Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ concentration and the lowest HCO3– concentration (Figure 3 and Table 3).

Principal component analysis (PCA) is frequently utilized in hydrochemical research to elucidate the variability in clustering and validate the outcomes of cluster analysis. The principal components of the dataset are those whose eigenvalue is >1 according to the Kaiser criterion. The component weights, which indicate the effects of different parameters on the principal components, are used to quantify the degree of involvement of each variable in each principal component (PC).24,32,48 The effect of the component weights is minimal when they are close to zero if the component loadings are used to explicitly examine the influence of different parameters on PCs. PC1 and PC2 attained by PCA in this study account for 51.175 and 20.656% of the variation within the dataset, respectively (Table 4). Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, Mg2+, and HCO3– are typical ions in PC1. Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ have greater positive component weights in PC1, while HCO3– has a bigger negative component weight in PC1. Therefore, the variability of the cluster can be divided into two types. The first type could be explained by high Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ concentrations. The other type could be explained by high HCO3–, K+, and Na+ concentrations (Table 4). The PC scores are shown in Figure 4. The differences between clusters C4, C1, C2, and C3 could be explained by the variability of PC1. However, the differences between cluster C3 and clusters C1 and C2 could be explained by the variability of PC1 and PC2, whereas the difference between cluster C1 and cluster C2 could be explained by the variability of PC1.

Discussion

Method for Identifying that the Produced Water is Primitive Formation Water

In the PCA analysis results, PC1 could reflect the mixture of fracking fluid and formation water, while PC2 could indicate the difference between various pristine formation water samples.24,32 According to the distribution of PC1 and PC2 in Figure 4, cluster C4 is severely impacted by the fracking fluid and clusters C2 and C3 are slightly influenced or not influenced by the fracking fluid. The difference in the main ion concentration between cluster C4 and clusters C1, C2, and C3 can be seen in Figure 5. Cluster C4 is significantly different from other clusters. The SO42–, Cl–, Mg2+, and Ca2+ concentrations of cluster C4 are notably higher than those in clusters C2 and C3, while the HCO3– concentration is lower than that in clusters C2 and C3. Cluster C3 is similar to clusters C1 and C2 except that the concentration of SO42– is lower than that in clusters C1 and C2. The water type of cluster C4 is the Na–Cl–HCO3 type. However, the water types of clusters C1, C2, and C3 are all of the Na–HCO3 type (Figure 6a–d).

Schoeller diagrams of four clusters (ion concentrations are the median values).

Stiff plots of the four clusters.

The drainage times of CBM wells in clusters C1, C2, C3, and C4 were 180–1863 days, 1831–2020 days, 2514–5015 days, and 2–2014 days, respectively. Cluster C4 including DT4, DT7, DT10, and DT31 was sampled on December 15, 2011. Among them, DT31 is an infill well with a drainage time of 2 days on December 15, 2011. Previous studies have indicated that the water quality of DT4, DT7, and DT10 on December 15, 2011, was significantly impacted by the fracking fluid originating from the infill well.5 As a result, the drainage time of DT4, DT7, and DT10 in cluster C4 could be regarded as 2 days because the produced water quality of DT4, DT7, and DT10 is similar to that of DT31. The water quality of the produced water gradually becomes similar to that of the pristine formation water as the drainage time increases. The concentration of SO42–, Cl–, Mg2+, and Ca2+ in the produced water gradually decreases with increasing drainage time. In contrast, the concentration of HCO3– shows the opposite trend (Figure 5). The change of anion concentrations is more obvious than that of the cation with the drainage time. In the early stage of drainage, Cl– was dominant in comparison with HCO3– and SO42– due to the effect of the fracking fluid like what was shown in C4. With the increase of drainage time, HCO3– became predominant, while the concentration of Cl– gradually decreases to lesser than that of HCO3–. The produced water tends to show the characteristics of low SO42– and Mg2+ + Ca2+ concentrations and high Na+, HCO3–, and Cl– concentrations. This suggests that the influence of the fracking fluid on the produced water is gradually reduced after a long period of drainage. It is similar to the ion characteristics of CBM produced water in other regions such as the Shizhuangnan Block in the southern Qinshui Basin.24

The fracking fluid is the main factor affecting the ion change of produced water in the initial stage of drainage. It is necessary to further distinguish the critical boundaries of the influence of the fracking fluid on ionic composition in produced water. In the produced water, Na+ is the main cation, while Cl– and HCO3– are the main anions. The sum of HCO3– and CO32– is known as alkalinity. Na/Cl is positively correlated with alkalinity/Cl, with a slope close to 1 (Figure 7a). The cation Na+ in the produced water is balanced by HCO3– and Cl–.32 There is an exponential decay relationship between Na/Cl and Na/alkalinity (Figure 7b). The Na/Cl ratio of cluster C4 is close to 1, and the Na/alkalinity ratio of clusters C1, C2, and C3 is close to 1. This suggests that sodium and chloride have different sources. The pristine formation water has a lower Na/alkalinity ratio and a higher Na/Cl ratio.24,32 It is important to establish an index to identify whether the produced water is pristine formation water.

Previous studies have shown that the influence of the fracking fluid on the produced water could be seen as negligible as the residual alkalinity (defined as (HCO3– + CO32–)–(Ca2+ + Mg2+)) remains stable.24 Cl– concentration could be used to determine the effect of the fracking fluid on the produced water, such as the Qinshui Basin24 and western Guizhou.32 However, it is difficult to distinguish this kind of influence by the residual alkalinity because there is a negative correlation between residual alkalinity and the Cl– concentration in the research area (Figure 8).

Plots of Cl– vs (HCO3– + CO32–)–(Ca2+ + Mg2+).

According to the results of multivariate statistical analysis, PC1 shows a mixture of fracking fluid and formation water, which is explained by high Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ concentrations. The fracking fluid has higher Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ values than those of the formation water. Cl– and Ca2+ + Mg2+ could be taken as the indices to identify the influence of the fracking fluid on the produced water except for SO42. Cl– could be used to identify the pollution of the fracking fluid to produced water.28 Cl– does not form insoluble minerals, is not adsorbed by colloids, or can it be bioaccumulated. Cl– is hardly affected by other reactions under in situ reservoir conditions. Therefore, Cl– that is injected into the reservoirs with the fracking fluid is discharged in large quantities in the initial stage of drainage. Due to ion exchange and precipitation, the concentration of Ca2+ and Mg2+ in the pristine formation water is usually low. The injection of the fracking fluid will significantly increase the concentration of Ca2+ and Mg2+. The concentrations of Cl–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ gradually decreased and remained stable with continuous drainage. When the concentration of Cl– is lower than 20 mEq/L and the concentration of Ca2+ + Mg2+ is below 1 mEq/L, there is little correlation between Cl– and Ca2+ + Mg2+ concentrations in most samples from clusters C1, C2, and C3 (Figures 8 and 9). This suggests that the fracking fluid has little impact on the ionic composition in produced water at this time. In contrast, the concentration of Ca2+ + Mg2+ is negatively correlated with the concentration of Cl– for all samples in cluster C4 and a small number of samples in cluster C1 (Figure 9). This suggests that the fracking fluid influences the water quality in produced water, which is highly consistent with the PCA analysis results.

Relationship between High δ13CDIC and HCO3– and Microorganisms

The distribution of δCH413 values is relatively concentrated, with the majority falling within the range from −54.3 to −50‰ and an average value of −52.9‰. Similarly, the δDCH4 values range between −265 and −245‰, with an average value of −256‰. The isotopic composition of carbon and hydrogen in methane suggests that gas in this area has a mixed origin,49 that is, there are both thermogenic and biogenic origins.

δ13CDIC in the sampling wells is from 18.14 to 21.67‰. DIC sources of groundwater are mainly the dissolution of carbonate minerals and mineralization of organic minerals,39 A δ13CDIC value greater than 10‰ could be attributed to microbial reduction.36,44,46 As 12C has lower activation energy than 13C, microorganisms preferentially use molecules with 12C, resulting in the enrichment of 13C in DIC.50 This suggests that the produced water comes from the formation water related to the action of microorganisms.

High δ13CDIC and high HCO3– of the formation water could be related to CO2 in association with methanogenesis.46 The biogenesis of methane could result in changes in HCO3– in formation water as carbon dioxide is the byproduct of microbial methane production.51 For example, acetoclastic methanogens decompose acetic acids to produce equal moles of methane and carbon dioxide. The latter could be readily soluble and increase the concentration of HCO3–.46 Meanwhile, the average CO2 concentration in the produced gas desorbed from coal in this area is 1.67%. The dissolution of CO2 in water during the gas production process may also contribute to the high bicarbonate concentration.5

Geochemical Differences in the Formation Water between the Upper and Lower Coal Groups

The ionic composition difference of the formation water between the upper coal group and the lower coal group is studied based on DT26 and DT32. Until December 4, 2019, the produced water from DT26 and DT32 was close to the pristine formation water. HCO3– and Na+ of DT26 and DT32 on December 4, 2019, are shown in Table 1. Generally, HCO3– has a higher concentration in the nonrecharge zone. Na+ has a strong solubility and is not sensitive to the redox state of groundwater. It is related to the elemental composition of the surrounding medium and the time of water–rock interaction.52 The high concentration of Na+ suggests that the water–rock interaction time of the formation water is longer and the formation water is far away from the recharge area and close to the retention area. HCO3– and Na+ in DT32 are higher than those in DT26, suggesting that the groundwater where DT32 is located is more confined. In the vertical direction, the deeper the formation depth is, the more confined the groundwater environment.

The values of δD are significantly different, despite the close surface well locations of DT26 and DT32. Variation in the production layers between DT26 and DT32 is a possible cause. The production layers are vertically separated by 200 m, and the production layers of DT26 and DT32 belong to the upper coal group and the lower coal group, respectively. The groundwater would interact with the surrounding coal and rock during the runoff process. The groundwater with good sealing contains higher δD.53 With the burial depth increasing, the higher D in the surrounding coal and rock enters the formation water through H2O + D = HDO + H, which results in the higher D in DT32. δD in the produced water could be a tool to distinguish the produced water between the upper and lower coal groups because the hydrogen isotopes of the drainage water of the upper and lower coal groups have obvious differences. Wells DT28, DT4, and DT8 belong to commingling production because the production layers in a single well cover both upper and lower coal groups (Table 2). The production layers in DT26 are in the upper coal group, while the production layers in DT32 are in the lower coal group. The δD values of DT8, DT4, and DT28 are between the δD values of DT26 and DT32, indicating that the produced water comes from the mixing of the formation water from the upper and lower coal groups.

Identification of Water Sources

The water samples for the stable isotope analysis come from cluster C3 (Table 1), which suggests that the water sample is close to the pristine formation water, and the influence of the fracking fluid is negligible. The formation water in the study area may come from atmospheric precipitation, which is later affected by evaporation and water–rock reaction. As shown in Figure 10, the local atmospheric precipitation line equation in Northeast China54 is as follows: δD = 7.20δ18O – 2.39‰. The data of δD and δ18O from the sampling wells are on the right side of the atmospheric precipitation line of Northeast China (Figure 9), showing the O drift. This suggests that the formation water in the Tiefa Basin may come from atmospheric precipitation.38 Evaporation and water–rock reactions under high temperature or mixing with seawater or basin brine could make the isotopic compositions of the original meteoric water shift to the right of the local atmospheric precipitation line.21,35,55 CBM production layers of the sampling wells belong to the Cretaceous, which is the continental sedimentary environment. It excludes the effect of mixing with seawater or a basin brine. The lighter H216O is easier to evaporate than H218O in the case of strong evaporation, resulting in an O drift. Another reason for the O drift may be attributed to the water–rock interaction. In coal measures, the coal and rocks around the formation water contain a high 18O, and the 16O contained in the formation water exchanges 18O in the surrounding coal and rocks, resulting in the heavy isotopes of oxygen in the formation water.35

Conclusions

(1)It is necessary to figure out whether the produced water is primitive formation water if the chemical information of the produced water is used for exploration. Cl–, SO42–, HCO3–, K+, Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ in CBM produced water could be selected as targets for cluster analysis based on HCA. The principal components could be attained by PCA based on the HCA clustering results. PC1 and PC2 attained by PCA are available to identify the influence of the fracking fluid on the water quality of CBM produced water. PC1 with high Cl–, SO42–, Ca2+, and Mg2+ concentrations could reflect the mixture of fracking fluid and formation water, while PC2 with high HCO3–, K+, and Na+ concentrations could explain the difference of pristine formation water. The concentrations of Cl– and Ca2+ + Mg2+ could be used to determine whether the water quality of the coproduced water is mainly composed of the pristine formation water. When the Cl– concentration is lower than 20 mEq/L and the concentration of Ca2+ + Mg2+ is lower than 1 mEq/L, the influence of the fracking fluid on the produced water can be ignored, and the produced water can characterize the pristine formation water.(2)Biogenic methanogenic activity may result in high concentrations of HCO3– and high and positive δ13CDIC in produced water.(3)The data of δD and δ18O from the sampling wells suggest that the formation water in the Tiefa Basin may come from atmospheric precipitation, which is later affected by evaporation and water–rock reaction. δD in produced water could be a tool to distinguish the formation water between the upper and lower coal groups because the hydrogen isotope values in the produced water derived from the lower coal group display a substantial elevation compared to those from the upper coal group.

Cu-Catalyzed Reductive Coupling of Allylic and Carbonyl Electrophiles
Authors: Vaughn Beale, Ming Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We report herein Cu-catalyzed syntheses of secondary and tertiary homoallylic alcohols. Compared to conventional carbonyl addition with nucleophilic allylation reagents, this process utilizes a reductive coupling strategy in which electrophilic allylic esters couple with carbonyl compounds with B2pin2 as the reductant. Notably, the approach employs an earth-abundant metal catalyst without using cryogenic conditions.

Homoallylic alcohols, including secondary and tertiary alcohols, are common structural motifs in numerous natural products and pharmaceuticals (Figure ). − Owing to the biological relevance and structural diversity of these molecules, significant effort has been devoted to the development of methods that could allow for the access to homoallylic alcohols. , Traditionally, carbonyl addition with nucleophilic allylmetal reagents has been one of the most adopted approaches to synthesize homoallylic alcohols (Scheme a). Over the past few decades, many classes of nucleophilic allylation reagents have been developed, including those derived from main group elements or transition metals, preformed, or in situ generated. Notable recent achievements in this research area comprise the development of novel catalysts for asymmetric allyl addition, the scope expansion to previously nonreactive substrates, and the implementation of innovative catalytic allyl addition processes. In spite of these advances, the development of approaches that are complementary to currently available methods is valuable.

Approaches to Homoallylic Alcohols

As part of our program on allylation chemistry, we were intrigued about the possibility of coupling an allylic electrophile with carbonyl compounds to generate homoallylic alcohols under proper conditions. As illustrated in Scheme b, we envisioned that allylic electrophile 1 could participate in Cu-catalyzed allylic borylation to generate allylboronate intermediate B. Because this transformation is typically conducted under basic conditions, we suspect that allylboronate B could undergo base-promoted transmetalation with the copper catalyst to form allylcopper species C in situ. This reaction sequence would invert the polarity of allylic electrophile 1 to nucleophilic allylcopper C. Subsequent addition of C to ketones or aldehydes should produce homoallylic alcohols 2 or 3. We report herein the successful realization of this proposal to synthesize homoallylic alcohols. There are a few attractive features of this approach. First, while conventional approaches utilize allylic metal reagents that are often sensitive to moisture and oxygen, the approach employs allylic esters as allyl donors that are much more stable. Second, the reaction does not require any precious metal; it utilizes a catalyst derived from an inexpensive and earth-abundant metal. Lastly, this process allows for the coupling of two electrophilic species with B2pin2 as the reductant.

While a plethora of allylation reagents can react with aldehydes to produce secondary homoallylic alcohols, methods that permit the access to tertiary homoallylic alcohols are underdeveloped. , We started the investigation by using allylic electrophile 1 and acetophenone as the model substrates to generate tertiary homoallylic alcohols 2a. As shown in Scheme , when the reaction was conducted with allylic acetate 1a using CuCl and Xantphos as the catalytic system with KO t Bu as the base, product 2a was isolated in 55% yield (entry 1). The reaction of allylic tosylate 1b under the same reaction conditions gave 2a in a slightly diminished yield (47%, entry 2). While the yield of 2a was improved to 68% with allylic carbamate 1c, poor results were obtained with allylic carbamate 1d (entries 3 and 4). The reaction with allylic benzoate 1e proceeded with a yield similar to that of 1c (entry 5). Changing the catalyst to CuOAc or the base to LiO t Bu did not improve the yields of 2a in the reactions with 1e (entries 6 and 7). However, when the reaction was conducted with NaO t Bu as the base, a substantial increase of the yield was observed, and 2a was isolated in 98% yield (entry 8).

Scheme summarizes the results of the reactions of 1e with representative ketones under the developed conditions. The reaction of an aromatic ketone with an electron-withdrawing substituent at the para position gave tertiary alcohol 2b in 89% yield. Aromatic ketones with an electron-donating substituent at the para position also reacted under the standard conditions to deliver products 2c-d in 72–79% yields. The reactions tolerate halogen-substituted aromatic ketones with different substitution patterns, affording alcohols 2e-h in 75–92% yields. The ketone with a heterocyclic thiophen group is suitable for the reaction to form alcohol 2i in 92% yield. The reaction with 1-tetralone furnished product 2j in 66% yield. In addition to aromatic ketones, the reaction also proceeded with dialkyl ketones. For instance, 1-(4-ethylphenyl)-2-propanone reacted to give alcohol 2k in 42% yield. The reaction with cyclohexanone generated achiral product 2l in 50% yield. In these cases, the results are inferior to those from aromatic ketones presumably owing to their lower reactivities and potential enolization issues. The reactions using 2 equiv of 1e gave the desired products with comparable yields.

Next, we investigated the reactions of allylic benzoate 1e with aldehydes as the substrate. As shown in Scheme , the reactions worked generally well with a variety of aromatic and heteroaromatic aldehydes. For instance, the reactions with aromatic aldehydes with different electronic properties and substitution patterns gave secondary alcohol products 3a-g in 83–98% yields. Heteroaromatic aldehydes, such as thiophene-2-carbaldehyde and furan-3-carbaldehyde, reacted under the standard conditions to give 3h-i in 94–97% yields. The reaction proceeded with α,β-unsaturated aldehydes and aliphatic aldehydes, although the yields of 3j-l are significantly lower than those from aromatic aldehydes.

We also explored the reactions with other allylic electrophiles. As illustrated in Scheme , the reactions of methallylic benzoate 4 with ketones gave products 5a-b in 43–65% yields. The reaction with benzaldehyde gave alcohol 5c in 31% yield. Additionally, commercially available allylic acetate 6 appended with an allylsilane unit reacted with ketones to give tertiary alcohols 7a-b in 61–69% yields. A similar reaction of 6 with benzaldehyde afforded alcohol 7c in 53% yield. Notably, the allylsilane group in the products remains intact under the reaction conditions, which provides a handle for further derivatization of products 7.

As shown in Scheme , allylic boronate B was proposed to be an intermediate in the reaction process. To obtain evidence to support this analysis, we prepared allylboronate B. Upon exposure of B to the reaction conditions [CuCl (10 mol %), Xantphos (11 mol %), NaO t Bu (1.1 equiv)], product 2a was isolated in 95% yield (Scheme ). The results provide concrete evidence to support the intermediacy of allylboronate B.

Validation of the Reaction Intermediate

To obtain enantioenriched alcohol products, we investigated catalytic asymmetric reactions with 1e and acetophenone using an enantiomerically enriched ligand. As shown in Scheme , among several ligands we surveyed, the reaction with (R,R)- i PrDuphos proceeded with the highest enantioselectivity, and alcohol 2a was isolated in 82% yield with 90:10 er. The results provide a good entry point to generate enantioenriched tertiary homoallylic alcohols using this asymmetric reductive coupling strategy.

Asymmetric Reductive Coupling

In summary, we developed a protocol to synthesize secondary and tertiary homoallylic alcohols. This approach allows for the coupling of electrophilic allylic esters and carbonyl compounds with B2pin2 as the stoichiometric reductant. Meanwhile, the process employs an inexpensive and earth-abundant copper salt as the catalyst without the use of cryogenic conditions. Additionally, respectable enantioselectivity of the tertiary alcohol product was achieved from the asymmetric reductive coupling reactions. Synthetic applications of this method are currently under investigation.

Investigating the relationship between gestational diabetes mellitus and the amount of dental plaque and decayed in pregnant women in Hamadan: A case-control study
Authors: Erfan Shafiei, Safoura Taheri, Azita Tiznobaik, Mehdi Shirinzad, Ghodratollah Roshanaie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Decayed and periodontal diseases are among the most common diseases in societies. Hormonal and nutritional changes provide predisposing conditions for gum disease and decay in pregnant mothers. The current study aimed to investigate the relationship between Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) and dental plaque and caries in pregnant women in Hamadan.METHODS AND MATERIAL:This research was a case-control study in pregnant women referred to health centers in Hamadan city in 2022. The case group included 110 pregnant women whose GDM was confirmed based on paraclinical symptoms and met the inclusion criteria. The control group included 110 pregnant women who did not have GDM. The data collection method was interview and examination. The data collection tool was a questionnaire consisting of three parts. Decayed, missing, and filled teeth (DMFT) were determined according to the guidelines of the World Health Organization and microbial plaque using bacterial plaque indicator tablets in case and control groups. Statistical calculations were performed using SPSS software version 20 (t-test and K-squared test), and a significance level of 0.05 was considered.RESULTS:In the present study, the average DMFT index in the case and control groups was 5.8 and 4.4, respectively, which showed a significant difference in the case and control groups (P < 0.001). The average number of decayed in the case and control groups was 4.81 and 3.49, respectively, which indicated a significant difference in the number of decayed in both groups (P < 0.001). The average plaque index in the case and control groups was 57.46 and 38.46, respectively, which indicated a significant difference between the case and control groups.CONCLUSIONS:According to the study findings, it is necessary to plan for education and increase awareness in society to observe oral and dental hygiene and correct nutritional style.

Introduction

The World Health Organization considers oral hygiene to be a necessity and a part of public health throughout life and states that poor oral hygiene and untreated oral diseases can have a profound effect on the quality of life. Decaying is one of the most common diseases in human societies.[1] Tooth decay is caused by the accumulation of plaque and bacteria on the surfaces of teeth due to poor oral hygiene. Tooth enamel is destroyed by acids produced by oral bacteria that consume sugars and starches, gradually creating cavities that, if left untreated, progress and require extensive dental treatment.[2]

Dental caries is the most common oral disease in developed countries. Some population groups, including pregnant mothers, children, and those with chronic diseases such as diabetes, heart disease, and mental illness, are vulnerable in terms of oral and dental health.[3] Therefore, paying attention to oral and dental hygiene in pregnant mothers as one of these vulnerable groups is more important because of maintaining their and the fetus’s health, because pregnancy is associated with changes in the body, including the mouth and teeth. Hormonal and nutritional changes provide predisposing conditions for gum disease and tooth decay in pregnant mothers. The special conditions of pregnancy require that more oral and dental care be taken during this period, and not paying attention to this matter can endanger the health of other systems of the mother’s body and the health of the fetus, in addition to oral and dental problems.[4]

Studies show that pregnancy can increase the risk of developing decayed saliva due to changes in the pH and buffering capacity of saliva, as well as unhealthy eating habits. In several regions of the world, the high prevalence of decay in pregnant women has been reported from 50 to 87%.[5] Anum et al.,[6] in their study that examined the oral health status of pregnant women, reported that 53.4% of the participants had dental caries. In a study by Gavic et al.,[7] which examined the knowledge and attitudes of pregnant women regarding oral health, the results showed that the participants in the study did not have sufficient knowledge about the importance of oral and dental health during pregnancy and infancy.

Although the effects of oral diseases on pregnancy outcomes are not yet fully understood, Some studies have reported that dental caries-causing bacteria may be responsible for pregnancy outcomes such as preterm birth or low birth weight, while many studies have shown no association between dental caries and adverse pregnancy outcomes.[8]

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus is defined as the occurrence of diabetes with any degree of glucose intolerance during pregnancy in a woman who did not have any diabetes or glucose intolerance before pregnancy. GDM is related to obesity, a history of GDM in the family, and high gestational age. GDM is the most common complication and metabolic disorder during pregnancy. It is estimated that GDM occurs in 1 to 14% of women in the world.[9] The prevalence of this disease in America is reported to be 3–4%, and 110,000 women are affected annually.[10] In a meta-analysis, the prevalence of GDM in Iran was reported as 7.6%%.[11] GDM is a sign of increased risk of type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular diseases.[9]

Diabetes during pregnancy affects both the mother’s health and the fetus’ growth.[12] One of the main causes of premature birth or even infant mortality is gestational diabetes. Diabetic mothers are more prone to miscarriage.[13] Pregnancy complications in women with gestational diabetes include preeclampsia, cesarean delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, perineal lacerations, increased risk of developing type 2 diabetes in the future, fetal macrosomia, neonatal hypoglycemia, low Apgar scores in the newborn,[14] increased incidence of vaginal and urinary tract infections and chorioamnionitis.[15] However, the effects of maternal diabetes on teeth development and the underlying mechanisms related to it have not been clarified.

One of the adverse consequences of pregnancy is poor oral and dental hygiene. In a meta-analysis, the relationship between oral health, including decayed and periodontitis, with overweight has been discussed. In this study, it has been shown that arterial blood pressure, GDM, and excessive pregnancy weight gain are among the most common side effects of pregnancy that may be related to periodontitis.[16] Also, an increase in the prevalence of gingivitis, gingival hyperplasia, pyogenic granuloma, etc., is observed in the oral cavity of pregnant people.[17] Various studies showed the high prevalence of decay in pregnant women.

The need to conduct a study, So far, few studies have been conducted at the global level on the relationship between GDM and decayed and microbial plaque and contradictory results are seen in these few studies. With the investigations carried out by the researchers of the present study, it was found that no study has been conducted in Iran regarding the relationship between decayed teeth and dental plaque with GDM in this population. Therefore, according to research priorities regarding the importance of the health of pregnant women and their babies, the present study was designed to investigate the relationship between GDM and the amount of dental plaque and decay in pregnant women in Hamadan in 2022.

Subjects and Methods

Study design and setting

This research was a case-control study of pregnant women referred to comprehensive health centers in Hamadan City in 2022. The case group includes pregnant women whose gestational diabetes was diagnosed based on clinical symptoms and confirmed based on paraclinical symptoms. Paraclinical symptoms in this group are based on the results of the OGGT laboratory test; their fasting serum glucose (8 hours after the last meal) is more than 100 mg/dL, and they are diagnosed with gestational diabetes. The control group includes pregnant women who did not have clinical symptoms of gestational diabetes, and paraclinical results have also proved it.

Study participants and sampling

At first, Hamadan City was divided into four regions, then three comprehensive health centers were randomly selected from each region, and finally, case and control samples were equally available from each of these twelve comprehensive health centers according to the entry and exit criteria. The inclusion criteria included a willingness to participate in the study, gestational age of 36–32 weeks, first-time pregnant women, and having at least 25 teeth. Exclusion criteria included taking drugs affecting blood sugar, presence of chronic diseases such as liver diseases, hypertension, etc., obesity (BMI equal to 30 and more), undergoing dental treatments during pregnancy, the presence of oral habits – the need for antibiotic prophylaxis due to systemic diseases, and the presence of a history of infertility.

In this study, 110 pregnant women with gestational diabetes were selected as the case group, and 110 pregnant women without gestational diabetes were selected as the control group based on the laboratory results and introduction by the pregnant mothers of the Comprehensive Health Center to the researcher and based on the inclusion criteria. The control group was matched with the case group in terms of demographic variables and some background variables such as pregnancy age, stage of pregnancy, marriage age, etc. At first, the city of Hamadan was divided into four regions. Then, one comprehensive health center was randomly selected from each region, and finally, case and control samples were equally available from each of these four comprehensive health centers according to the entry and exit criteria. After explaining the objectives of the study, if the subjects were willing to participate in the study, the samples were examined by the researcher. The data collection method was based on examination, and the data collection tool was a questionnaire. Decayed was evaluated by standard examination of tooth surfaces based on WHO criteria. The absence of clinical evidence of decayed means a healthy tooth, and if there is a lesion in the pit and fissure, or on the smooth surface of the tooth, destroyed tooth enamel, or a soft and recognizable floor or wall, it means the presence of decayed. According to the opinion of the World Health Organization, in epidemiological studies, radiography devices are mostly not used. Decayed detection is mainly done visually using a probe and applying gentle pressure on suspicious tooth surfaces. To determine decayed, a reading light, a disposable mirror, a dental probe, and latex gloves were used in compliance with all infection control principles. The state of dental plaque is obtained based on the visible plaque index as a percentage by adding the surfaces with visible biofilm, dividing the result by the total number of surfaces examined, multiplying by 100 to obtain the percentage of dental plaque. GUM Red-Cote Disclosing Plaque Tablets, made in Australia, were used to do this. Finally, a comparison was made based on the average scores related to dental plaque index and decayed in both case and control groups. It should be mentioned that all the dental examinations for the participants were performed by the final-year dental student (presenter of this thesis) under the supervision of the dental supervisor.

Data collection tool and technique

The used questionnaire included three parts: (1) demographic information (age, weight, height, education, pregnant woman’s occupation, husband’s occupation), (2) gynecology and obstetrics information (gestational age, number of pregnancies, abortion history,…), and (3) Information related to decayed status (based on WHO criteria) and dental plaque (based on visible dental plaque index). The materials used in this research included bacterial plaque indicator tablets (disclosing), examination tools such as mirrors, probes, and disposable latex gloves.

Ethical consideration

The project number of this thesis project is 140009308091. This study was carried out after being approved by the Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences with the ethics code IR.UMSHA.REC.1400.559. Patients were fully informed about the purpose of the study. They were assured that their personal information would be kept confidential and personal information would not be given to other parties. Then, a written consent was obtained from them. In the completed consent form, there is the name and details of the participating person, but it is only available to the researcher responsible for the study, whose ethical principles and confidentiality have been fully respected, and it has not been given to any other person. But the participant’s name and details are not mentioned in the completed questionnaires. We tried to get all the information at the same moment of the examination and complete the questionnaire so, there was no request to meet the research sample again.

Statistical analysis method

Data were analyzed after collection by SPSS version 20 software. Quantitative variables were reported as mean, standard deviation, and qualitative variables as frequency (percentage). Quantitative variables were compared between two groups using a t-test, and qualitative variables were compared using a K-squared test. A significance level of 0.05 was considered.

Discussion

Gestational diabetes is glucose intolerance that is first observed during pregnancy. Gestational diabetes is believed to affect approximately 15% of all pregnant women worldwide.[18] A study by Tolomeu et al.[19] showed that children whose mothers had gestational diabetes were more prone to enamel developmental defects, especially incisor hypomineralization and hypoplasia, compared to children whose mothers did not have this metabolic disorder. Metabolic disorders during the period of amelogenesis can lead to tooth enamel defects, and one of these disorders is maternal hyperglycemia. Early diagnosis of this condition helps to prevent it, so gestational diabetes has a special effect not only on the oral and dental health of the individual but also on the oral and dental health of children, which shows the importance of paying attention to this category.

The results of our study support the hypothesis that women with decay and microbial plac during pregnancy are at greater risk of GDM disease than healthy pregnant women. Also, the amount of plaque index in pregnant women with diabetes is significantly higher than the group of non-diabetic pregnant women. In one study, of the 320 subjects, 60% of pregnant women had dental problems during pregnancy. The Community Periodontal Index (CPI) score for all pregnant women was significantly higher than that of the control group. Nearly 72.81% of pregnant women had never visited a dentist. Lower educational status, older age, earlier problems, poor hygiene habits, and tobacco use directly compromise oral health.[20] Singh et al.[21] reported a higher level of bleeding on probing and periodontal pockets was observed among women who had the habit of cleaning their teeth once daily than those who cleaned their teeth with a brush twice a day. In our study, many participants in both healthy and diabetic groups did not follow oral hygiene guidelines. This result highlights the need for planning by policymakers and health managers to implement Educational programs such as proper nutritional lifestyle education, careful implementation of oral hygiene, timely visits to the dentist, interaction between dentists, gynecologists, and midwife, including routine dental check-ups during prenatal visits of pregnant women. Their actions can improve the general well-being and less adverse pregnancy outcomes.

In the study by Chaparro et al.,[22] a significant increase in the identification of diagnostic parameters of dental disease, such as bleeding on probing, periodontal pocket depth, and inflamed periodontal surface, was reported in pregnant women with gestational diabetes compared to healthy women. In the study of Vieira et al.[23] (2020), the dental plaque index in the group exposed to gestational diabetes was 45.8, which had a statistical difference compared to the control group. In the present study, it was also found that in the group with gestational diabetes, the dental plaque index was 57.46, which is a statistical difference from the control group, and in this sense, the present study is in line with Vieira’s study. Also, in Vieira’s study, 60.1% of women with gestational diabetes had decayed lesions, which for the current study was 68.6%, and in both studies, there is a statistical difference between the case group and the control group, and for this reason, both studies are consistent. These results demonstrate the importance of proper preventive education and more comprehensive information to members of the community, especially women of childbearing age who wish to become pregnant, regarding the complications that dental caries can have during pregnancy, which endanger the lives of the mother and fetus.

In a study conducted by Poulsen et al.,[24] it was found that the average DMFT in women with a history of diabetes was 9.8, and it was found that there is a significant increase in DMFT in women with a history of gestational diabetes. However, in the current study, the average DMFT index in the group of women with gestational diabetes was 5.8, and in the control group was 4.4, which had a statistically significant difference, and therefore, it is in line with Poulsen’s study. In contrast, Nasimi et al.’s[25] study reported no relationship between periodontitis, plaque index, and GDM, and it can be said that unlike the present study, there was no relationship between plaque index and GDM. This inconsistency in the results can be considered due to the difference in the patients’ demographic information. It should be known that DMFT numbers are different in different societies, even in similar age groups, and this difference in value is due to various reasons, including social and economic conditions, oral health status, etc.

According to the present study’s findings, there was a positive relationship between decay (based on the DMFT index) and gestational diabetes. So with an increase of one unit of DMFT, the chance of getting gestational diabetes is doubled by 2.2. Studies show that pregnant women with severe periodontitis are at higher risk of developing gestational diabetes.[26] Some studies have reported that a diagnosis of periodontitis increases the risk of developing gestational diabetes by up to 2.85-fold.[27] Also, in a meta-analysis study by Abariga et al.,[28] it was reported that the risk of developing gestational diabetes was increased more than 2-fold in pregnant women with periodontitis. Also, in the study of García-Martos et al.,[29] it was reported that periodontal disease was a risk factor for developing GDM. A study by Liu et al.[30] reported that GDM incidence among untreated periodontitis participants was significantly higher than that among non-periodontitis participants, and periodontal treatment during gestation reduced the incidence of GDM. These results emphasize that in addition to teaching oral hygiene, it is necessary to carefully plan dental treatments and repair early caries before pregnancy so that women enter pregnancy with the best dental conditions because dental procedures are usually not performed in the first trimester of pregnancy, and this can increase the severity of tooth decay, which can cause adverse pregnancy outcomes.

But if we want to discuss the basic reasons for the results that caused periodontal diseases to be more severe in GDM pregnant women, we can mention the role of oral cavity microbes. Oral cavity microbes play an important role in causing various diseases. In the study by Li et al.[31] (2021), which examined the relationship between gestational diabetes and the main oral diseases, including decayed and periodontitis, it was shown by analyzing saliva and dental plaque microbes of 111 pregnant women that the microbes of both types of samples show differences in GDM patients. These findings indicated that certain bacteria in saliva or dental plaques could effectively distinguish women with GDM from healthy pregnant women. Researchers observed an increase in the number of oral bacteria, including Streptococcus mutans, Actinomyces, Neisseria, and Latropia, in the saliva of women with GDM. These bacteria use lactic acid as a source of carbon and energy. They are also related to the metabolism of linoleic acid and flavonoids, which causes a decrease in oral pH and an increase in the prevalence of decayed. Also, these four bacteria have complex interactions and are closely related to glucose metabolism, so they may be used to show the occurrence and development of GDM as a metabolic disease.[31] In another observational study aimed at investigating the diversity and composition of salivary microbes, the answer to the question that, “Is GDM associated with diversity in the oral microbial community?” Saliva microbes of pregnant women with GDM (N = 50) and normal glucose regulation (N = 160) were evaluated in the third trimester and 9 months after delivery. The results showed that people with gestational diabetes slightly differed in the amount of saliva microbes in late pregnancy and after delivery.[32] It seems that oral microflora during pregnancy is affected by oral and systemic diseases. Considering that prenatal dental care reduces specific oral pathogens, more studies are needed to determine the extent of the result. Considering the results of the present study on the lack of oral hygiene before and during pregnancy, it seems necessary to conduct further studies in women of childbearing age with a desire to become pregnant and pregnant women, considering laboratory studies and efforts to control oral microbes.

Limitations and recommendation

Considering that the tools and materials needed to carry out this research were available inside the country and the repeated experiences of researchers in conducting similar projects, there were no special limitations in this study.

The strength of this study is that, given the importance of gestational diabetes and the negative messages it can convey, this study is the first to examine the relationship between dental caries and gestational diabetes in an Iranian population. On the other hand, studies conducted in this field have examined oral diseases, not dental caries and plaque, which could also be a strength of our study.

Weakness and suggestions for further research

The findings of the present study are for the pregnant women community of Hamedan, and the differences in geographical location, cultural, social, and economic levels of this community compared to other communities may affect the generalization of the present study results. Conclusion

According to the study findings and considering the importance of the topic in improving the health of mothers and children, it is suggested to have educational programs to observe oral and dental hygiene and correct nutritional style from childhood to adulthood. Also, preventive programs before pregnancy in order to reduce the percentage of microbial plaque should be routinely included in the educational programs of health centers.

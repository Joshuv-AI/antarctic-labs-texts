Pharmacokinetic Prediction of Repurposed Drugs for PDAC Using Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Pragya Pragya, Shashwat Singh, Bhuvaneshwari Balasubramaniam, Jac Fredo Agastinose Ronickom
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a highly aggressive cancer that accounts for 95% of cases of pancreatic cancer. It develops in the ducts and shows high drug resistance. In this study, we proposed a framework to predict the pharmacokinetic (PK) properties of repurposed drugs for PDAC using artificial intelligence (AI). Initially, the molecular features of repurposable drugs for PDAC were generated through three types of molecular descriptors: RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6. Then, the corresponding absorption (Caco-2 cell permeability), distribution (volume of distribution), metabolism (CYP2C9 inhibitor), excretion (half-life), and toxicity (hERG) properties of the drugs were obtained from ADMETlab 3.0. We constructed AI models such as multilayer perceptron (MLP), random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGB), and one-dimensional convolutional neural network with different combinations of molecular descriptors as the input. The performance of the models was evaluated on an open-access data set, Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC), and using evaluation metrics. Our results show that the highest-performing molecular descriptor combination and AI models vary with respect to the PK properties. Models on the PDAC data set achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.18 (MACCS+XGB), Spearman correlation (SC) of 0.39 (MACCS+RF), area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) of 59.44% (MACCS+ECFP6+MLP), SC of 0.68 (RDKit+ECFP6+XGB), and SC of 0.77 (MACCS+RF) for absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity, respectively. The corresponding values on the TDC data set are an MAE of 0.26 (RDKit+MACCS+MLP), an SC of 0.62 (MACCS+ECFP6+MLP), an AUPRC of 67.11% (RDKit+MACCS+ECFP6+1D-CNN), an SC of 0.39 (MACCS+RF), and an SC of 0.92 (RDKit+XGB/RDKit+MACCS+RF). These results suggest that combining molecular fingerprints with AI can effectively model PK properties. This approach supports the use of AI for accelerating drug repurposing, especially for disease conditions.

Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a highly aggressive malignancy characterized by late-stage diagnosis and limited therapeutic options. Conventional surgical treatment has not significantly enhanced the survival rates of PDAC patients. Chemotherapy treatments for metastatic PDAC include FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel, which might end up in failure due to drug resistance. Researchers are exploring alternative treatment modalities, including targeted and immune-based therapeutic approaches, to improve outcomes for patients with PDAC. This highlights the importance of understanding drug properties through pharmacokinetics (PK) to optimize drug safety and efficacy. In this study, we are focusing on analyzing the PK properties of the drugs for the targeted-based approach in the treatment of PDAC.

PK is a branch of pharmacology that focuses on the movement of drugs within the body over time, involving the study of absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) properties. These properties are crucial for the successful development of any drug, including repurposed medications. It helps in ensuring drug safety, optimizing dose, predicting the drug efficacy, and reducing the development time and costs. Absorption is a process by which a drug enters the bloodstream after administration. It can be evaluated using parameters such as Caco-2 cell permeability, human intestinal absorption, P-glycoprotein inhibition, bioavailability, solubility, and hydration free energy. Distribution is a process by which a drug is dispersed throughout the body. It is assessed based on blood-brain barrier permeability, plasma protein binding rate, and volume of distribution at steady state (VDss). It is a key PK parameter that quantifies the extent to which a drug disperses into body tissues and fluids. Metabolism is a process that defines the breakdown of the drug and its modification by chemical or conjugation with molecules, mainly in the liver. Its focus is on the role of cytochrome P450 enzymes, such as CYP2C9 inhibitors and substrates. Excretion is a process in which drugs are eliminated from the body primarily through the kidneys via urine or the liver as bile or feces. It is determined through drug half-life and clearance. The half-life of a drug depends upon two ADMET parameters, clearance and VDss. Toxicity study helps in understanding the potential harmful effects and risks of the drugs. It is predicted using indicators such as hERG inhibition, skin sensitization, carcinogenicity, acute toxicity (LD50), and Ames mutagenicity. , In this study, we investigated the ADMET profiles of drugs using one representative parameter from each category: Caco-2 permeability (absorption), VDss (distribution), CYP2C9 inhibition (metabolism), half-life (excretion), and hERG (toxicity), to support effective drug selection for the treatment of PDAC.

PK properties of drugs can be assessed using in vivo and in vitro methods, but these approaches are often time-consuming and costly. In contrast, computational methods offer a faster and more cost-effective alternative for predicting ADMET properties. A key step in computational ADMET prediction is the representation of drugs using various molecular descriptors. The simplified molecular input line entry system (SMILES) is the common representation of the drugs used in computational studies. Further, different types of fingerprints and descriptors can be used to describe the drugs. The fingerprint methods include molecular access system (MACCS), extended-connectivity fingerprints (ECFP6), and PubChem. The descriptors used in the computational studies are RDKit and Mordred. − On the other hand, embedding-based techniques such as Mol2Vec, SMILES2Vec, graph neural networks, and transformer-based models can also be used to describe the drugs, though they may involve higher computational costs. In this study, the SMILES of the drugs were converted into RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6 to analyze their ADMET properties.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has played a transformative role in predicting the molecular properties of drugs, offering valuable insights that enhance our understanding of PK and drug behavior. However, the accuracy of such predictions, depends heavily on the availability of high-quality, sufficiently large data sets, appropriate molecular representations, and the selection of suitable predictive models. Recent studies have employed ensemble machine learning (ML) algorithms such as random forest (RF) and light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM) for predicting PK properties. − Additionally, neural networks and deep learning techniques, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have been explored for PK prediction, though CNNs typically demand higher computational resources. In this study, we employed a combination of models, including a multilayer perceptron (MLP), RF, extreme gradient boosting (XGB), and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN), to predict the PK properties of drugs targeting PDAC.

ADMET Properties

In this study, we assessed absorption using the Caco-2 cell permeability (log cm/s), parameter, widely regarded as the gold standard for evaluating intestinal absorption. For distribution, we employed the VDss (L/kg), a key PK parameter that significantly influences drug concentration in the bloodstream during intravenous administration. To evaluate metabolism, we used CYP2C9 inhibition (μM), as it is a major cytochrome P450 enzyme involved in the metabolism of a broad spectrum of drugs. For excretion, we considered the half-life (hours), defined as the time required for the drug concentration in the body to reduce to half of its initial value. For toxicity, we considered the hERG property (μM) in which the gene encodes a voltage-gated potassium channel that plays a critical role in cardiac repolarization. Inhibition of this channel can disrupt the cardiac action potential, leading to potentially life-threatening arrhythmias.

Data Sets

We considered 52 repurposable drugs identified for PDAC through our previous study. A few drugs were common (uric acid, KU55933, AP-24534, Alisertib, KU-57788, MK-0457, GSK525762A, and Decitabine) between the targets. Two drugs do not have SMILES in the PubChem, such as TL-1–85 and AK-55409. Finally, we ended up with a drug library of 50 drugs for this study. We calculated the PK end points Caco-2 permeability, VDss, CYP2C9 inhibition, half-life, and hERG inhibition using ADMETlab 3.0 of drugs. It provides a centralized hub with curated data sets, standardized tasks, and a platform for sharing knowledge. The data set consists of a list of drugs and their corresponding ADMET values. Molecular Descriptors

We generated the SMILES of each drug, and then molecular descriptors were extracted using RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6. RDKit is an open-source cheminformatics toolkit used for molecular modeling and analysis. It helps in computing chemical descriptors and fingerprints of the drugs. It consists of 200 descriptors. MACCS key comprises 166 molecular feature bits, including group VIII elements, nitrogen and oxygen atom counts, isotopes, and 4 M RING. Each bit is assigned a value of 0 or 1, indicating whether the corresponding atomic species, bonding information, or local atomic environment is present or absent in the molecular structure. ECFP6 is a fingerprinting method that uses 2048 features. It is based on the extended connectivity circular fingerprint at a particular radius. It is a novel class of topological fingerprints for molecular characterization. By extracting every conceivable chemical path from the atoms within a radius of six, each bit signifying the existence or absence of a certain substructure and similarity. We performed the normalization (min-max scaling to the 0–1 range) on descriptor values that produced infinite or numerically unstable values. Descriptor sets without such issues were retained in their original scale to avoid unnecessary transformation. We removed the missing values from the descriptors.

Pharmacokinetics Prediction Using AI Algorithms

In this, we applied four AI models, MLP, RF, XGB and 1D-CNN, for PK prediction. MLP is a fully connected feedforward neural network composed of at least three layers: an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. RF is an ensemble ML model that is robust against overfitting. The predictive reliability of RF plays a crucial role in identifying drug candidates and fine-tuning the PK profiles of the drug. XGB constructs models iteratively, with each step addressing the errors of the previous one, resulting in exceptional performance across various regression tasks, including PK prediction. 1D-CNN is a supervised learning model with a convolutional structure that extracts the features from raw data. It also includes convolutional layers, pooling layers, and a fully connected layer, which are responsible for automatic feature extraction, dimensionality reduction, and final prediction, respectively. We built the models on different combinations of inputs as follows: RDKit, MACCS, ECFP6, RDKit+MACCS, MACCS+ECFP6, RDKit+ECFP6, and RDKit+MACCS+ECFP6. The models were implemented in Python (ver. 3.10), the scikit learn library. The parameters for the models were optimized through a grid search approach.

Cross-Validation and Performance Metrics Calculation

We performed 5-fold cross-validation with training via GridSearchCV on both data sets. For absorption (Caco-2 cell permeability), distribution (VDss), excretion (half-life), and toxicity (hERG), we considered regression tasks, and for metabolism (CYP2C9 inhibitor), as classification tasks. It is important to note that certain metrics are more suitable for specific PK parameters. For example, Caco-2 permeability is typically assessed using mean absolute error (MAE), while VDss and half-life are better evaluated using Spearman correlation (SC), and CYP2C9 inhibition and hERG is commonly measured using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve or area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC). , Accordingly, in this study, we assessed model performance using MAE for Caco-2 permeability, SC for VDss, half-life, and hERG (as a regression task), and AUPRC for CYP2C9 inhibition (as a classification model). Discussion

The field of ADMET prediction has undergone transformative advancements with the emergence of AI models. In this study, we focused on predicting PK properties of drugs using various AI algorithms and molecular descriptors. Specifically, we examined the influence of (i) data sets (PDAC drugs and TDC), (ii) drug descriptors (RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6), and (iii) AI algorithms (MLP, RF, XGB, and 1D-CNN). We explored seven combinations of the three descriptors with four AI models, resulting in a total of 28 prediction pipelines. Effect of the Data Set Chosen for the Analysis

The choice of data set significantly influences the predictive performance and generalizability of AI models in ADMET property prediction. In our study, we used the PDAC and TDC data set to predict the ADMET properties. Our models performed well in the TDC data set compared to PDAC. It may be due to the distribution of the data across the data set being different. TDC includes a broad and diverse library of compounds, enabling robust model assessment. In contrast, the PDAC drug library was custom-built using the DepMap database, specifically tailored to targets identified in our prior study. This focused data set may limit generalizability but enhances relevance to PDAC-specific pathways. Prior literature demonstrates the impact of data set on model performance. For instance, Pu et al. utilized both larger and unified data set derived from ChEMBL particularly for liver microsomal clearance, with a combination of molecular fingerprints (ECFP4) and graph-based representations. Even with the noise in the larger data set enabled the TetraDMPNN model to perform best. Its ability to extract chirality from 2D molecular structures helped it manage complexity and volume impressively. Whereas the unified data set helped validate the models in a clean environment, it also played key roles, but the bigger one had the final edge in predictive power. Similarly, Han et al. employed ChEMBL, Metrabase, and literature-curated data to train models. This study merged all three data sets and preprocessed these sources into property-specific data sets (e.g., for CYP1A2 inhibition or Caco-2 permeability), and then reported model performance on each of those whole data sets. These studies highlight that larger data sets improve model performance, and that data set heterogeneity (benchmark and in-house, broad vs target-specific) directly shapes prediction outcomes.

Effect of Drug Descriptors Use in Pharmacokinetics Prediction

We found that the type of molecular descriptors significantly influences the performance of AI models in predicting PK properties. In our study, we employed RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6 descriptors to capture structural and physicochemical features of drug molecules relevant to ADMET. These descriptors offered a diverse representation of molecular characteristics, from substructure patterns (MACCS), circular fingerprints (ECFP6), to detailed topological and chemical features (RDKit). From our study, we can conclude that the 2D descriptors like MACCS and ECFP6 play a very significant role in PK prediction whereas the RDKit only performs well along with MACCS or ECFP6 or in combination of both. From this, we get an insight like molecular fingerprints has more predictive power than molecular descriptor in the PK prediction. Molecular fingerprints outperform general descriptors in PK prediction because they encode biologically meaningful substructures critical for processes of ADMET. RDKit descriptors capture broader physicochemical properties, which boost performance only when combined with fingerprint-based features. Previous studies have also emphasized the importance of descriptor choice in PK modeling. In a study, Seal et al. utilized Morgan, Mordred, and molecular structural fingerprints, physicochemical properties (RDKit) descriptors in their PKSmart framework using both human and animal PK data sets, coupled with RF algorithms. It is reported that the combination of both Mordred and Morgan descriptors performed well, which aligns with our observation. Similarly, in Ghanvati et al. study, the best performance was achieved using a curated combination of 500 Mordred descriptors and 25 electrostatic potential (ESP) derived features, totaling 525 descriptors selected via RF-based feature importance. These descriptors captured complementary aspects of molecular behavior. Mordred provided extensive 2D and 3D physicochemical attributes, such as SLogP, ring complexity, and topological indices, along with ESP features modeled spatial and electronic properties, including hydrogen bond donor/acceptor strength and molecular sphericity. These studies collectively underscore that descriptor type not only affects the predictive accuracy but also determines the model’s generalizability across different PK end points and data sets. Our findings align with this, demonstrating that combining multiple descriptor types improves model robustness and captures complementary molecular information essential for accurate PK predictions.

Effect of Drug Features Use in Pharmacokinetics Prediction

We found that the presence of different functional groups, like hydrogen-bonding and hydroxyl groups (absorption), polarity-rich features (distribution), hydrophobic fragments (metabolism), lipophilic or bulky structures (excretion), and nitrogen-based groups (toxicity), plays a role in PK prediction. It may be due to the fact that the structures, such as halogens, quaternary carbons, and aromatic groups, enhance membrane permeability but may reduce solubility, while nitrogen-based groups and macrocycles introduce ionization and steric effects that influence transporter interactions and absorption. The lipophilic motifs, such as alkyl chains and aromatic substituents, promote membrane binding and tissue accumulation, leading to higher distribution volumes. Overall, these features reflect the polarity-lipophilicity balance that governs drug partitioning across tissues. These motifs promote binding through lipophilic interactions, π–π stacking, ionic contacts, and potential mechanism-based inhibition, indicating a strong likelihood that the molecules act as CYP2C9 inhibitors and impact drug metabolism. It can be noted that the structural complexity and molar refractivity highlight bulky molecules more likely to undergo metabolic processing prior to elimination. Electronic and steric descriptors further modulate transporter recognition, shaping whether drugs are efficiently cleared renally or retained for hepatic routes. These enhance lipophilicity and favor metabolic activation, which can yield reactive metabolites or interact with nucleic acids, contributing to genotoxicity and hepatotoxicity. Aromatic enrichment and polycyclic rings enhance lipophilicity and favor metabolic activation into toxic intermediates, whereas excessive methylation and methylene linkers promote bioaccumulation and unstable metabolic products.

In this study, we considered two data sets (PDAC and TDC), three descriptors (RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6), and four AI models (MLP, RF, XGB, and 1D-CNN) to predict the five PK properties (Caco-2 permeability, VDss, CYP2C9 inhibition, half-life, and hERG inhibition). Limitations and Future Scope

This study highlights the potential of AI in predicting PK for PDAC drugs. While this study demonstrates the effectiveness of combining multiple molecular descriptors with ML and deep learning models for PK prediction, several limitations must be acknowledged. The analysis was confined to two data sets (PDAC and TDC), potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to broader chemical and biological spaces. Additionally, the reliance on predefined descriptors may have restricted the ability to fully capture complex PK, as structural, dynamic, and contextual biological information (such as 3D conformations or pathway data) was not included. Moreover, the deep learning models, particularly the MLP and 1D-CNN, were used in this study. In future research, we will focus on integrating graph neural networks and transformer-based models to enable richer, data-driven representations of drugs and proteins. Incorporating structural biology data, multiomics profiles, and real-world clinical or pharmacogenomic information could significantly enhance prediction accuracy and translational relevance. Furthermore, adopting explainable AI techniques would make these models more transparent and informative, facilitating their application in drug discovery, repurposing, and precision medicine.

Conclusions

In this study, we employed two data sets, one benchmark and the other a target-specific data set for PDAC, using DepMap and ADMETlab 3.0. In addition, we calculated the molecular features using RDKit, MACCS, and ECFP6. Further, we applied ML and deep learning algorithms such as MLP, RF, XGB, and 1D-CNN to build the models. We evaluated the performance of models using MAE, SC, and AUPRC as per the recommended performance metrics for each PK property. Our outcomes on the PDAC data set reveal that the models achieved the highest performance using MACCS (absorption, distribution, and toxicity), MACCS+ECFP6 (metabolism), and RDKit+ECFP6 (excretion) derived features. We found that the presence of different functional groups, like hydrogen-bonding and hydroxyl groups (absorption), polarity-rich features (distribution), hydrophobic fragments (metabolism), lipophilic or bulky structures (excretion), and nitrogen-based groups (toxicity), plays a role in PK prediction. We found that the models XGB (absorption, excretion), RF (distribution, toxicity), and MLP (metabolism) performed well for the prediction of parameters. We concluded from the study that a larger data set performs well, along with molecular features of the drug, plays a more significant role in PK prediction. Further, the choice of the AI algorithms depends on the data set and molecular features.

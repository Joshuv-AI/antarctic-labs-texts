Efficacy of Vericiguat in Acute Heart Failure: A Target Trial Emulation Study
Authors: Junlong Chen, Jian Liu, Ruo’nan Xiao, Yichen Wang, Lei Gao, Wenhang Zhao, Ziyang Li, Siyi He, Yihang Zhang, Xuesong Wen, Dongying Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn recent trials, vericiguat reduced adverse cardiovascular outcomes in stable or recently worsening chronic heart failure (HF), although data concerning vericiguat use in the population with acute HF remain limited.MethodsThis target trial emulation study included 205 patients with acute HF with left ventricular ejection fraction ≤45%. Vericiguat was initiated at discharge in 103 patients. Stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting was used to adjust for baseline differences. The primary outcomes were 180‐day HF rehospitalization and worsening HF events. Secondary end points included New York Heart Association class improvement, cardiac remodeling measured by echocardiography, and safety events.ResultsAfter stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting adjustment, vericiguat significantly reduced 180‐day HF rehospitalization (16.3% versus 34.2%; hazard ratio [HR], 0.43 [95% CI, 0.23–0.80], P=0.01) and worsening HF events were numerically lower in the vericiguat group (21.2% versus 31.1%; HR, 0.64 [95% CI, 0.36–1.15], P=0.13). However, patients treated with vericiguat showed more significant improvement in New York Heart Association class (odds ratio [OR], 0.70 [95% CI, 0.50–0.96], P=0.03), though there was no significant improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction or left ventricular dimensions. The incidence of adverse events such as hypotension, hyperkalemia, and worsening renal function were comparable between the 2 groups. The beneficial effect of vericiguat was consistent across all prespecified subgroups, regardless of chronic HF history or concomitant of quadruple guideline‐directed medical therapy use.ConclusionsIn patients with acute HF, initiating vericiguat at discharge was observed to be associated with fewer HF rehospitalization events and improvements in New York Heart Association functional class over 180 days. These results suggest that vericiguat may have potential as a treatment option during the vulnerable postacute phase.RegistrationURL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique Identifier: NCT05799638.

Acute heart failure (AHF) is an acute syndrome associated with systemic congestion, encompassing both new‐onset HF (de novo HF) and acute decompensated HF. 1 As the most common cause of unplanned hospitalization in patients older than 65 years of age, 2 AHF represents a turning point in the course of chronic HF. Indeed, mortality rates for patients with acute decompensated HF are 3 times higher than those for stable chronic HF. 3 The postdischarge period following hospitalization for AHF is considered a vulnerable phase, during which mortality and readmission rates can reach up to 15% to 30% within 60 to 90 days. 3 , 4

Currently, no treatment other than guideline‐directed medical therapy (GDMT) has definitively reduced rates of death and rehospitalization. 3 GDMT comprises angiotensin receptor‐neprilysin inhibitors/angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers, beta blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and SGLT2 (sodium‐glucose cotransporter‐2) inhibitors. Guidelines 5 , 6 and clinical trials 7 have confirmed the efficacy of GDMT in AHF. However, real‐world implementation of quadruple GDMT remains suboptimal, with <25% of eligible patients receiving target doses of all 4 drug classes due to hypotension, renal dysfunction, hyperkalemia, or other tolerability issues. 8 , 9 , 10 This treatment gap may be particularly pronounced in patients with AHF, who face hemodynamic instability and increased vulnerability to adverse drug effects during the vulnerable postdischarge period. Despite the availability of these therapies, many patients still face a residual risk of poor outcomes after discharge. 3 Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore additional therapeutic strategies for patients with AHF beyond quadruple GDMT.

Vericiguat, an oral soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator, represents a novel therapeutic approach for HF with reduced ejection fraction and has been approved as an add‐on therapy to standard GDMT. 6 , 11 , 12 The VICTORIA (Vericiguat in Patients with Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction) trial 13 demonstrated that vericiguat reduced the composite end point of cardiovascular death and HF rehospitalization in patients with chronic HF following a recent worsening event, and the current VICTOR (Vericiguat in patients with chronic heart failure and reduced ejection fraction) trial 14 revealed that vericiguat reduced cardiovascular death in patients with stable HF and reduced ejection fraction. However, the efficacy and safety of vericiguat in patients with AHF, particularly those presenting with de novo HF, remain unexplored. Furthermore, in a subgroup analysis of the VICTORIA trial, the therapeutic effect of vericiguat appeared to be less pronounced in patients with higher NT‐proBNP levels (N‐terminal pro‐B‐type natriuretic peptide >5314 pg/mL) (hazard ratio [HR], 1.16 [95% CI, 0.99–1.35]). Therefore, we conducted this study to evaluate the clinical impact of early vericiguat initiation in hemodynamically stabilized patients with AHF at the time of hospital discharge.

METHODS

Study Population

This study was based on a real‐world, single‐center, prospective cohort previously described in detail. 15 The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are described in Table S1, and the definition of doses of HF medications is described in Table S2. In brief, eligible patients were required to have typical symptoms and signs of HF (eg, dyspnea, fatigue, peripheral edema, pulmonary rales), a left ventricular ejection fraction ≤45% on echocardiography, and elevated NT‐proBNP levels at admission (>450 pg/mL for patients <55 years, >900 pg/mL for 55–74 years, and >1800 pg/mL for ≥75 years). All patients were classified as New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II to IV and were naïve to vericiguat (had no prior exposure) before hospitalization. Patients were excluded if they experienced acute coronary syndrome or underwent revascularization within 3 months before admission; had rare cardiomyopathies (eg, Fabry disease), severe infection or sepsis, or active malignancy; or required mechanical circulatory support during hospitalization. Clinical physicians followed up on cardiovascular‐related outcomes in patients in the cohort after discharge and performed echocardiography at baseline and at the time of outcome assessment. This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and all the patients signed informed consent forms. This study was registered on https://www.ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05799638 and approved by the local Ethics Committee. Notably, the primary end point of the cohort in this registry was modified, as described in detail in Data S1. The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Data Collection, Follow‐Up, and End Point

Baseline demographic and clinical data were collected by one cardiologist and independently verified by a second cardiologist to ensure accuracy. Baseline laboratory results and echocardiographic parameters were obtained from the initial assessments performed at hospital admission. Echocardiographic examinations were conducted by physicians with standardized training. All patients were followed up for 180 days. The primary end point was a composite of worsening HF events and HF rehospitalization (including readmission to our center and other external hospitals). Follow‐up visits and telephone interviews were conducted specifically for this study by at least 2 cardiologists from the research team to ensure the accuracy and completeness of outcome data collection.

Study Design

We conducted a target trial emulation to evaluate the efficacy of vericiguat therapy in patients with AHF. The target trial framework was employed to explicitly define the research question and guide our analytical approach. 16 , 17 The study flow chart is presented in Figure 1. Among the 240 patients admitted with AHF, we excluded those with contraindications to vericiguat treatment (15 with severe renal insufficiency [estimated glomerular filtration rate <25 mL/min per 1.73 m2] and 3 with symptomatic hypotension defined as systolic blood pressure <85 mm Hg), as well as 6 patients lost to follow‐up and 1 patient scheduled for surgery. All the patients included in the study have no history of vericiguat treatment.

Screening procedure of the study cohort.

Treatment allocation was determined at hospital discharge, with patients receiving either vericiguat (initiated at 2.5 or 5 mg daily) in addition to standard HF medications or continuing with GDMT alone. A total of 205 patients were included in the final analysis in the original cohort (103 in the vericiguat group and 102 in the control group). Given the observational nature of this study and the potential for treatment selection bias, we applied the stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (sIPTW) methodology. This approach created a pseudo‐population with balanced baseline characteristics between the treatment groups. In the weighted cohort, there were 100 patients in the vericiguat group and 104 without vericiguat treatment (control group). All primary efficacy analyses adhered to the intention‐to‐treat principle and were performed in this weighted cohort to approximate the conditions of a randomized controlled trial. This study adheres to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.

Statistical Analysis

Sample size estimation was performed using a 2‐sided log‐rank test in PASS 15 (NCSS, Kaysville, UT). Based on the data from the VICTORIA trial and our own cohort, we assumed a 1:1 allocation ratio, 6‐month worsening HF event‐free survival rates of 65% versus 85% for the control and vericiguat groups, respectively, with a 2‐sided α of 0.05, 80% power, and an anticipated dropout rate of 10%. Under these assumptions, the required size was approximately 79 participants per group, yielding a total target sample size of 158. Detailed calculations are provided in Data S1.

Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD or median with interquartile range, depending on the data distribution. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared between treatment groups using the chi‐square or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables, and independent‐sample Student's t test or the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate.

Given the observational design of this study, we applied sIPTW based on propensity scores to control for confounding and emulate a randomized target trial. The propensity score model included key clinical variables identified through a directed acyclic graph (Figure S1). Balance between the treatment groups was evaluated using standardized mean differences (SMD), with SMD <0.1 indicating adequate balance. The primary outcomes, the time to first worsening HF events and HF‐related rehospitalization within 180 days, were analyzed using sIPTW‐weighted Cox proportional hazards models. HRs and 95% CIs were reported. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals. Event‐free survival curves were plotted using sIPTW‐adjusted Kaplan–Meier curves and compared using log‐rank tests. Prespecified subgroup analyses were conducted to explore potential heterogeneity of vericiguat treatment effects across predefined clinical strata.

Secondary outcomes, including changes in NYHA class, cardiac structure and function assessed by echocardiography, and blood pressure, were evaluated using ANCOVA or nonparametric tests, adjusting for baseline values where appropriate. Safety end points, including hypotension, hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, and worsening renal function, were summarized descriptively, and between‐group comparisons were performed using relative risks (RRs) with 95% CIs.

Sensitivity analyses included unadjusted analysis in the original cohort, per‐protocol analysis, and restricted mean survival time comparisons at multiple time points. All statistical tests were 2 sided, with a significance threshold of P<0.05. Analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.0). Additional details regarding the study protocol and methodology details are provided in Data S1.

After applying sIPTW, excellent balance was achieved across all baseline characteristics. The weighted pseudo‐population consisted of 100 patients in the vericiguat group and 104 in the control group. Figure S2 demonstrates that all 15 covariates identified through directed acyclic graph achieved SMDs <0.1, indicating successful balancing of measured confounders.

Specifically, after sIPTW adjustment, the groups were well matched for key prognostic factors: median age (69 versus 71 years, SMD=0.073), male sex (68.7% versus 68.7%, SMD=0.002), and HF history (51.0% versus 52.5%, SMD=0.030). Importantly, cardiovascular comorbidities were balanced, including hypertension (44.4% versus 45.3%, SMD=0.019), diabetes (36.4% versus 37.5%, SMD=0.022), atrial fibrillation/flutter (31.7% versus 33.7%, SMD=0.041), and coronary heart disease (44.0% versus 44.5%, SMD=0.010).

Laboratory parameters and cardiac structure and function indices were similarly well balanced after weighting. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (65.3 versus 68.4 mL/min per 1.73 m2, SMD=0.033), left ventricular ejection fraction (31% versus 33%, SMD=0.013), and left ventricular end‐diastolic diameter (64 versus 64 mm, SMD=0.036) showed no statistical differences between the groups. The distribution of NYHA functional class was comparable, and the use of quadruple GDMT at discharge was balanced after sIPTW (70.6% versus 69.9%, SMD=0.015).

Primary Outcomes and Medication Usage

Figure 2 illustrates the Kaplan–Meier curves for the primary end points after sIPTW adjustment. Regarding the composite end point of worsening HF events (Figure 2A), the composite outcome occurred in 21.2% of patients receiving vericiguat compared with 31.1% in the control group. The event‐free survival curves showed early separation but did not achieve statistical significance over the 180‐day follow‐up period (HR, 0.64 [95% CI, 0.36–1.15], P=0.13). Figure 2B demonstrates a clear and sustained benefit in reducing HF rehospitalization, with curves separating at ∼30 days and continuing to diverge throughout follow‐up. The 180‐day HF rehospitalization‐free survival rate was 83.7% in the vericiguat group compared with 65.8% in the control group (HR, 0.43 [95% CI, 0.23–0.80], P=0.01).

Table S3 provides additional details on the primary outcomes. The unadjusted analysis of the original cohort showed even larger treatment effects; specifically, 21 patients (20.4%) in the vericiguat group experienced worsening HF events compared with 37 (36.3%) in the control group (HR, 0.51 [95% CI, 0.30–0.87], P=0.01). HF rehospitalization occurred in 18 patients (17.5%) in the vericiguat group versus 36 patients (35.3%) in the control group (HR, 0.41 [95% CI, 0.23–0.72], P<0.01).

Medication adherence analysis (Table S4) revealed real‐world treatment patterns. At the end of the follow‐up, 48.5% of vericiguat patients were receiving full‐dose therapy (10 mg daily), 33.0% were on partial doses (2.5–5 mg), and 18.4% had discontinued treatment. Among controls, 7.8% had initiated vericiguat during follow‐up. Despite these crossovers and adherence challenges typical of real‐world practice, the intention‐to‐treat analysis maintained significant treatment benefits.

Notably, the effect of vericiguat was consistent regardless of chronic HF history. Among patients with a history of chronic HF, vericiguat reduced the risk of rehospitalization (HR, 0.52 [95% CI, 0.24–1.11]), which was similar to the effect observed in patients with de novo HF (HR, 0.38 [95% CI, 0.16–0.91], interaction P=0.58). The analysis stratified by GDMT use revealed that vericiguat provided significant benefit even in patients who received quadruple GDMT at discharge (HR, 0.39 [95% CI, 0.20–0.77]), with comparable outcomes for those without optimal GDMT (HR, 0.56 [95% CI, 0.20–1.60], interaction P=0.58).

Sensitivity Analyses

Multiple sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of our primary findings. In the unadjusted cohort analysis (Table S3), vericiguat was found to significantly reduce both worsening HF events (20.4% versus 36.3%, HR, 0.51 [95% CI, 0.30–0.87], P=0.01) and HF rehospitalization (17.5% versus 35.3%, HR, 0.41 [95% CI, 0.23–0.72], P<0.01). The per‐protocol analysis included 84 patients who continued to take vericiguat throughout the follow‐up period and 97 control patients. Figure S3 demonstrates more pronounced benefits in this population after sIPTW adjustment, with HF rehospitalization rates of 14.3% versus 35.1% (HR, 0.36 [95% CI, 0.18–0.72], P<0.01) and worsening HF event rates of 19.0% versus 32.0% (HR, 0.54 [95% CI, 0.29–1.02], P=0.06). The restricted mean survival time analysis (Table S6) revealed progressive temporal benefits for HF rehospitalization. Patients receiving vericiguat treatment gained an average of 5.2 rehospitalization‐free days at 90 days (95% CI, −10.5 to 0.2; P=0.06), rising to 8.8 days at 120 days (95% CI, −16.8 to −0.8; P=0.03) and 11.2 days at 140 days (95% CI, −21.1 to −1.3; P=0.03).

DISCUSSION

Main Findings

This target trial emulation study, based on a real‐world prospective cohort, evaluated the effectiveness and safety of initiating vericiguat at hospital discharge for patients hospitalized for AHF. The primary findings of our investigation demonstrated that early vericiguat administration, significantly reduced the risk of HF rehospitalization within 180 days. Although vericiguat did not confer a statistically significant benefit in terms of cardiovascular deaths, it was associated with a favorable functional status trajectory and a reassuring safety profile. These results suggest that vericiguat could be used as a therapeutic option during the vulnerable postdischarge period for patients with AHF.

The Time of Vericiguat Initiation

Our results were broadly consistent with the VICTORIA trial. The VICTORIA trial demonstrated that vericiguat reduced the HF hospitalization in patients with recent worsening chronic HF, although no significant improvement in cardiovascular mortality was observed. 13 It is worth noting that the VICTORIA study included only patients with a history of chronic HF. 18 In contrast, our study extended these findings to a population with AHF, in which nearly half of the patients presented with de novo HF and derived substantial benefit from immediate vericiguat initiation. This finding carries clinical implications, as patients with de novo HF represent an early stage in the natural history of the disease and typically have better prognosis than those with acute decompensated chronic HF, 19 they still face significant risks of rehospitalization and death. 4

These patients represent a unique opportunity for intervention before the establishment of adverse remodeling and end‐organ dysfunction characteristic of chronic HF. Each HF hospitalization accelerates disease progression and worsens long‐term prognosis. 20 As demonstrated by the results of this study, initiating vericiguat therapy early in the course of an AHF episode could alter the trajectory of disease progression by reducing the risk of worsening HF.

The timing of vericiguat initiation in AHF represents a critical therapeutic decision that may fundamentally influence patient outcomes. Our study demonstrated significant clinical benefits when vericiguat was initiated at hospital discharge rather than being deferred until chronic disease stabilization, highlighting the importance of early intervention during the postacute vulnerable phase. 3 , 21 During this critical 60‐to‐90‐day window after discharge, patients face the highest risk of readmission and mortality, with neurohormonal activation, systemic inflammation, and hemodynamic instability persisting despite clinical stabilization. 22 Notably, the median time from HF decompensation to randomization in the VICTORIA trial was 29 days 13 ; in contrast, our study initiated vericiguat at discharge, representing a more immediate postacute intervention. Furthermore, our restricted mean survival time analysis provides compelling evidence for early intervention. The restricted mean survival time analysis in our results provided compelling evidence for early intervention: the therapeutic benefit of vericiguat progressively accumulated over time, with patients gaining 11.2 (95% CI, 1.3‐21.1 days) additional rehospitalization‐free days by 140 days. This time‐dependent benefit pattern strongly supports initiating vericiguat at the earliest opportunity rather than deferring treatment until complete clinical stability is achieved.

From a practical clinical perspective, initiating vericiguat at discharge offers several advantages over delayed initiation. First, it mitigates the risk of clinical inertia that often prevents therapy optimization in the outpatient setting. 23 Second, it ensures that patients receive maximal therapeutic benefit during the highest‐risk period. Third, early initiation facilitates more rapid uptitration to the target dose, which is typically associated with improved prognosis. Our medication adherence analysis reveals that despite real‐world challenges, with 18.4% of patients discontinuing treatment during follow‐up, the intention‐to‐treat analysis maintained significant clinical benefits, suggesting robust therapeutic effects even in the context of imperfect adherence.

Vericiguat and Guideline‐Directed Medical Therapy

Despite current guidelines strongly recommending quadruple GDMT as the standard treatment for HF with reduced ejection fraction, real‐world implementation rates remain concerning. 5 , 6 , 8 Our study cohort featured a higher proportion of patients on contemporary quadruple GDMT at discharge (nearly 70%) compared with 59.8% on triple GDMT in the VICTORIA trial, and only 25.5% receiving full dosages. 24 , 25 However, our implementation rate still falls short of the ideal. This finding aligned with previous studies, as the CHAMP‐HF (Change the Management of Patients with Heart Failure) registry 9 demonstrated that <25% of eligible patients received target doses of all 4 drug classes. This treatment gap may be particularly pronounced in patients with AHF, who face hemodynamic instability and increased vulnerability to adverse drug effects during the critical postdischarge period. 3 The traditional approach of reserving advanced therapies for patients with established chronic HF, the prescription rate for the 4 GDMT drugs in patients with de novo HF is much lower than in patients with chronic HF and patients with worsening HF, 26 which may inadvertently allow progression of pathological processes that become increasingly difficult to reverse. 27 Therefore, initiating multidrug combination therapy in the early stages of disease progression is crucial.

The primary reasons for limited implementation of quadruple GDMT include hypotension, renal insufficiency, and hyperkalemia. 9 , 26 , 28 These adverse effects frequently necessitate dose reduction or discontinuation, thereby compromising long‐term patient outcomes. 29 Vericiguat offers distinct advantages in addressing these implementation challenges through its favorable tolerability profile. Our safety analysis demonstrated low and comparable rates of severe hypotension and worsening renal function between groups, which contrasts favorably with the hypotensive effects commonly associated with other GDMTs. 10 This consistency in safety is corroborated by the VICTORIA trial, where vericiguat showed no significant difference in symptomatic hypotension compared with placebo, 13 and no increased risk of hyperkalemia or acute kidney injury was found across different levels of renal function. 30 , 31

The clinical implications of vericiguat's tolerability profile extend beyond individual patient safety to broader therapeutic strategy optimization. Vericiguat may actually facilitate the initiation and uptitration of other therapies. The VERITA study (Clinical profile, associated events and safety of vericiguat in a real‐world cohort) 32 demonstrated that vericiguat initiation was associated with improved angiotensin receptor‐neprilysin inhibitor dosage optimization, suggesting a synergistic relationship rather than competitive inhibition. The VELOCITY (A Study to Learn How Safe Starting Vericiguat at a Dose of 5 Milligrams Is in Participants With Chronic Heart Failure With Reduced Ejection Fraction) study 33 confirmed the safety of initiating vericiguat at a dose of 5 mg, this simplicity may improve adherence to evidence‐based therapy initiation protocols and reduce the clinical inertia. Beyond alternative strategies such as medication sequence modification or individualized treatment planning, 34 , 35 early vericiguat initiation may provide a novel approach for managing GDMT intolerant patients, particularly given its comparatively low adverse event profile.

However, as an exploratory target trial emulation study, despite the use of methods such as sIPTW, selection bias in the study population remains considerable, and the results should be interpreted with caution. Definitive conclusions regarding efficacy and safety require validation through large‐scale, multicenter, placebo‐controlled randomized trials. Such studies would be essential to establish the long‐term impact of vericiguat on hard end points, particularly cardiovascular mortality, and to confirm the optimal timing of initiation in the AHF setting.

Limitations

Our study has several important limitations that should be acknowledged. First, despite employing target trial emulation methodology and stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting to control for confounding, the selection bias is unavoidable in this study, consequently this observational study still subject to unmeasured confounders that could influence causal inference, so this study should be viewed as hypothesis‐generating rather than as definite clinical evidence. Despite applying uniform safety thresholds (systolic blood pressure ≥85 mm Hg, estimated glomerular filtration rate ≥25 mL/min per 1.73 m2), initiation of vericiguat remained nonrandom and physician dependent, which may introduce residual confounding despite IPTW adjustment. The possibility of residual confounding cannot be entirely eliminated, particularly given the complex clinical decision‐making processes involved in prescribing vericiguat. Second, this was a single‐center study with a relatively modest sample size and a follow‐up period limited to 180 days. The restricted sample size may have limited our power to detect smaller treatment effects or rare adverse events. Third, the study population was derived from a single medical center, which may limit the generalizability of findings to other health care systems, patient populations, or regional practice patterns.

CONCLUSIONS

Our study suggests that vericiguat initiation in addition to standard GDMT at hospital discharge represents an effective and safe therapeutic strategy for patients with AHF. This approach was associated with a reduction in HF rehospitalization events and improvements in NYHA functional class within 180 days. Vericiguat may serve as a bridge to GDMT optimization while providing independent prognostic benefits during the vulnerable postdischarge period.

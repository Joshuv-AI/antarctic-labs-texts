Editors’ message – September 2025
Authors: Nathan D Wong, Erin D Michos
Published: 2025

We hope everyone has had a great summer and hopefully many of you were able to enjoy the superb American Society for Preventive Cardiology (ASPC) Congress held last month in Boston.

With this issue of the American Journal of Preventive Cardiology (AJPC) we are pleased to announce the continuing increase in our impact factor, which was recently released at 5.9, quite a dramatic increase from last year’s impact factor of 4.3. With this, the AJPC now ranks 34 out of 230, placing it in the top 15 % of journals in the Cardiac and Cardiovascular Systems category. Moreover, the Journal is frequently cited by other key journals including the Journal of the American College of Cardiology, Circulation, and the New England Journal of Medicine being among the top journals citing the AJPC. As we have emphasized in the past, the Journal’s success would not have been possible without the tireless efforts of our associate editors and greater editorial board, and most importantly the trust that our authors have placed to publish their great research, reviews, and commentaries in the Journal. Since its inception, the AJPC has been the official journal of the ASPC; this collaboration with the society and its leadership has been instrumental in its success as well.

With the Journal now established as the place to publish in the field of preventive cardiology, our submissions have dramatically increased in recent years – submissions this year are on track to be approximately double last year in 2024 when we had 361 submissions and at least five times that of 2023 when there were 134 submissions. Consequently, we have had to be more selective in choosing those papers that are felt to hold the most promise in impacting the field of preventive cardiology. Most of the submissions as well as published papers are coming from the US and China; there is the continuing need for increasing diversity of our submissions from other parts of the world. We hope that our editorial board, authors, and readers can continue to promote the Journal as a global journal publishing the most impactful science, reviews, and commentaries intended to shape and drive future practice in preventive cardiology.

With the dramatic increase in submissions, we are always on the lookout for dedicated clinicians and scientists involved in preventive cardiology to serve as reviewers and editorial board members. We especially want to call out early career members, including trainees who have a passion for the field and wish to be part of our editorial process that is important for career development. Finally, with this issue, we are happy to welcome two new associate editors: Dr. Priyanka Satish from the University of Texas, Austin, Dell Medical School as well as Dr. Leandro Slipczuk Bustamante from Montefiore Medical Center and Albert Einstein College of Medicine in New York.

The September issue is among the largest to date with >40 research papers, review papers, short reports and commentaries. While too numerous to mention, just a few to call out include reviews on mechanisms and preventive strategies for women with pre-eclampsia, adherence and challenges in long-term statin use in Asian populations, and on HDL-cholesterol epidemiology, atherogenic effects, therapies, and impact of disease states on functionality, as well as a short report on the association of plant-based diets with subclinical atherosclerosis. A number of key research manuscripts are also included in this issue, including those on the association of high lipoprotein(a) with carotid atherosclerosis and mortality, association of Life’s Essential 8 with atrial fibrillation, a systematic review and meta-analysis of wearable-based cardiac rehabilitation interventions, prevalence and long-term outcomes of NALFD in cardio-kidney metabolic health, comparative effectiveness of lifestyle interventions for pre-hypertension, the impact of sleep apnea on cardiometabolic parameters, and the association of ultra-processed food intake on MRI-based carotid plaque characteristics.

With the AJPC now firmly established as the journal to communicate the latest advances in research and clinical practice of preventive cardiology throughout the United States and beyond, we are pleased to see its role in promoting the growth of this important field for years to come.

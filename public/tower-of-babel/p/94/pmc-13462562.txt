Development and validation of a predictive nomogram for cervical lymph node metastasis in aspect ratio ≥1 papillary thyroid carcinoma using ultrasonic features and serum calcitonin
Authors: Yan Liu, Yuchen He, Jianwei He, Zhenhao Zheng, Xichen Yao, Tianxin Liu, Ziyu Liu, Ruyan Zhou, Wen Liu, Jun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreoperative prediction of cervical lymph node metastasis (CLNM) remains challenging in patients with papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) exhibiting an aspect ratio ≥1, a subgroup in which ultrasound alone has limited sensitivity (approximately 50%) and no dedicated predictive model currently exists. This study aimed to develop and internally validate a predictive nomogram combining ultrasonic features and serum calcitonin for CLNM in this specific population.MethodsThis retrospective study consecutively enrolled 156 patients with aspect ratio ≥1 PTC who underwent thyroidectomy and neck lymph node dissection at a single center (November 2023 to June 2025). The gold standard outcome was pathologically confirmed CLNM. Forty-six candidate predictors (clinical, ultrasonographic, and serological variables) were collected. Predictor selection was performed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation, followed by multivariate logistic regression. A nomogram was constructed and internally validated using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Model performance was assessed by area under the curve (AUC), calibration curve, sensitivity, specificity, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsAmong 156 patients [116 females, 40 males; median age 52 years, interquartile range (IQR), 45–57 years], CLNM was present in 48 patients (30.8%). Five independent predictors were identified: multifocality [odds ratio (OR) =5.87; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.85–18.66], maximum diameter ≥1 cm (OR =11.57; 95% CI: 2.26–59.16), ill-defined margin (OR =7.43; 95% CI: 2.06–26.76), microcalcifications (OR =3.43; 95% CI: 1.19–9.93), and serum calcitonin (OR =1.44; 95% CI: 1.08–1.92 per pg/mL). The nomogram achieved an apparent AUC of 0.834 (95% CI: 0.767–0.901) and a bootstrap-corrected AUC of 0.813. At the optimal probability cutoff of 0.264, sensitivity was 81.2% and specificity 75.9%. Calibration showed good agreement (Hosmer-Lemeshow P=0.41), and DCA demonstrated net clinical benefit across a threshold probability range of 5–80%.ConclusionsThis nomogram combining five preoperative predictors shows satisfactory discriminative ability for CLNM in patients with aspect ratio ≥1 PTC. However, given the single-center design, modest sample size, and lack of external validation, these findings should be considered preliminary. External validation in independent multicenter cohorts is required before clinical application.Keywords: Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), cervical lymph node metastasis (CLNM), calcitonin, ultrasonography, nomogram

Highlight box

Key findings

• We developed the first nomogram combining ultrasonic features and serum calcitonin (CT) for cervical lymph node metastasis (CLNM) prediction in aspect ratio ≥1 papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Five independent predictors were identified: multifocality, maximum diameter ≥1 cm, ill-defined margin, microcalcifications and serum CT. The bootstrap-corrected area under the curve was 0.813, with favorable calibration and broad clinical net benefit.

What is known and what is new?

• PTC with aspect ratio ≥1 has limited ultrasound sensitivity (~50%) for CLNM, and no specific predictive nomogram exists for this subgroup. Serum CT is mainly used for medullary thyroid carcinoma, with unclear predictive efficacy in this PTC population.

• This study proposes a novel predictive nomogram that integrates conventional ultrasound features (multifocality, maximum diameter ≥1 cm, ill-defined margin, and microcalcifications) with serum calcitonin levels specifically for the subpopulation of PTC patients with aspect ratio ≥1. To our knowledge, this is the first model to incorporate calcitonin into a risk prediction tool for this specific cohort, providing a non-invasive and clinically accessible approach for preoperative risk stratification. Our findings demonstrate that even within the normal range, relatively elevated serum calcitonin levels are independently associated with an increased risk of CLNM.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This visual preoperative tool enables individualized CLNM risk stratification, guides surgeons to select appropriate lymph node dissection range and avoids insufficient or excessive surgery.

Introduction

Background

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), also known as differentiated papillary carcinoma, is a differentiated form of thyroid cancer that develops from thyroid follicular epithelial cells. It often exhibits strong cellular differentiation and low invasiveness, making it the most common subtype in differentiated thyroid cancer pathology, accounting for approximately 80% of all cases (1). Although PTC exhibits highly differentiated characteristics, the incidence of cervical lymph node metastasis (CLNM) remains significantly high, occurring in 30–80% of patients (2). CLNM is a key factor associated with reduced survival rates and increased tumor recurrence in PTC patients (3,4). Accurate preoperative evaluation of CLNM is essential for determining the range of lymph node dissection during surgery and selecting the most suitable surgical approach, which is beneficial for reducing postoperative incidence rates and has important clinical significance for improving patient prognosis and quality of life.

Ultrasound is a non-invasive imaging technique that has become the preferred method for preoperative evaluation of cervical lymph nodes in PTC patients (5,6). Among the numerous ultrasound features, aspect ratio ≥1 is a very important morphological indicator in ultrasound diagnosis, which usually indicates that the direction of nodule growth is upright. This growth pattern is often associated with malignant tumors (7,8). According to the American College of Radiology Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS), an aspect ratio ≥1 is an independent predictor of malignancy that significantly upgrades a nodule’s risk category, even when other typical malignant features like microcalcifications, marked hypoechogenicity, or irregular margins are absent (9).

Rationale and knowledge gap

With the widespread adoption of screening, the detection rate of papillary thyroid microcarcinoma (PTMC) has progressively increased (10). Although current guidelines recommend active surveillance for low-risk PTMC, the presence of lymph node metastasis (LNM) necessitates surgical intervention (9-11). However, several critical challenges exist specifically for patients with aspect ratio ≥1 PTC. First, in some PTMC cases, an aspect ratio ≥1 may be the sole suspicious US feature, while ultrasound itself exhibits limited sensitivity (approximately 50%) for detecting CLNM (12,13). This means that nearly half of patients with metastasis may be missed by imaging alone. Second, fine-needle aspiration biopsy serves as a critical tool for evaluating thyroid nodules and cervical lymph node lesions and is widely used in clinical practice (14). Nevertheless, when targeting central compartment lymph nodes, fine-needle aspiration biopsy carries a high risk of injuring adjacent major vessels and nerves, making the procedure particularly hazardous. Third, a thorough review of existing literature reveals that no predictive nomogram has been specifically developed and validated for the aspect ratio ≥1 PTC subgroup—a significant knowledge gap that our study aims to address.

In recent years, the relationship between clinical data, serological markers, and the tumor microenvironment has garnered significant attention. Inflammation related to cancer and the host immune system plays important roles in tumor immune monitoring and eradication (15,16). Research shows that multiple inflammatory factors and indicators are closely related to the development of PTC (17). Other studies have shown that serum thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), thyroglobulin (Tg), thyroid protein antibody (TgAb), thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb), and calcitonin (CT) are independent influencing factors for PTC metastasis (18-20). However, the predictive value of these serological indicators for CLNM in PTC with an aspect ratio ≥1 remains unclear.

Objective and justification

To address the above-mentioned knowledge gaps, we propose combining ultrasound features with serum calcitonin for several reasons. First, ultrasound and serological markers capture different dimensions of tumor behavior—morphology versus biology—and may provide complementary predictive information. Second, ultrasound alone has limited sensitivity (~50%) for CLNM detection, and additional biomarkers are needed to improve diagnostic accuracy. Third, serum calcitonin has shown promise as an independent predictor in general PTC populations but has not been validated specifically for the aspect ratio ≥1 subgroup. A multi-feature approach may therefore achieve higher predictive accuracy than either modality alone.

Therefore, this study included PTC patients with aspect ratios ≥1. A total of 46 parameters were obtained, including general characteristics, medical history, ultrasonic characteristics, serum markers, and pathological results. Through rigorous statistical methods, independent predictors were screened and ultimately integrated into a model capable of effectively predicting the risk of CLNM in this specific patient population. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-0193/rc).

Methods

Patients

This study selected patients who underwent thyroid nodule resection and neck lymph node dissection at the Thyroid and Breast Surgery Department of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University from November 2023 to June 2025, and had postoperative pathological results of PTC (Figure 1). Clinical, serum, and pathological data were collected for retrospective analysis, and patients were divided into the CLNM and non CLNM groups according to their pathological conditions. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University (No. KJ2023-466-02), and the requirement for individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived.

Flowchart of the study population. CLNM, central lymph node metastasis; PTC, papillary thyroid carcinoma.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) preoperative thyroid ultrasound examination revealing thyroid nodules with an aspect ratio of ≥1 (longitudinal section or transverse section); (II) all patients underwent thyroidectomy and cervical lymph node dissection; (III) postoperative pathology confirmed PTC and confirmed whether accompanied by the presence of CLNM; (IV) provide complete medical records, including all clinical, ultrasound, serological, and pathological data.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) patients with a history of thyroid surgery and postoperative recurrence; (II) patients with a history of serious diseases such as hematological disorders, autoimmune diseases, or liver and kidney failure; (III) patients with incomplete preoperative laboratory or ultrasound examination data.

Outcome definition: the primary outcome was CLNM, defined as pathologically confirmed metastasis in any dissected cervical lymph node (central or lateral compartment). Outcome assessment was performed on postoperative formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded lymph node specimens by two independent pathologists blinded to the patients’ clinical, ultrasonographic, and serological data. Disagreements were resolved by consensus with a third senior pathologist.

Instruments

Using a Samsung RS85 color Doppler ultrasound instrument (manufactured by Samsung Medison, Seoul, South Korea) equipped with L3-12A linear array transducer (frequency 4–9 MHz, elastography) for inspection. All ultrasound images were analyzed separately by two senior ultrasound physicians, each with over 10 years of clinical experience in thyroid imaging. Before reaching a consensus, any differing opinions regarding the interpretation of tumor characteristics were resolved through discussion.

Conventional ultrasound

During the examination, the patient underwent a supine thyroid scan to obtain longitudinal and transverse two-dimensional, color Doppler images of the nodules. The following ultrasound features were recorded: number of lesions, location of nodules, nodule size including longitudinal diameter, transverse diameter, aspect ratio and maximum diameter, shape (regular/irregular), margin (well-defined/ill-defined), whether the internal structure is hypoechoic, presence of macrocalcifications or microcalcifications, presence or absence of large or micro calcification, and whether the posterior acoustic changes remain unchanged, strengthen or form shadows. Adler blood flow grading was used to evaluate the blood vessels within the nodules (grade 0–1 is hypo-vascular, grade 2–3 is hyper-vascular), after considering the above parameters comprehensively, each nodule was classified according to TI-RADS. There is also clinical information such as gender, age, history of diabetes (present or absent), history of hypertension (present or absent), and history of tumors (present or absent) was also recorded.

Ultrasonic features were defined as follows: maximum diameter ≥1 cm (the largest dimension of the nodule), multifocality (the presence of two or more distinct tumor foci within one unilateral thyroid lobe), ill-defined margin (lack of a clear border between the nodule and surrounding parenchyma), and microcalcifications (punctate hyperechoic foci <1 mm without posterior acoustic shadowing).

Ultrasound elastography

After routine ultrasound examination, the mode was switched to elastography to obtain color elastography of focal thyroid lesions. The region of interest (ROI) frame was placed on the target tumor, and care was taken to avoid interfering areas such as blood vessels and bony structures when comparing adjacent normal thyroid tissue. The system automatically calculates the elastic strain ratio and provides a qualitative elastic score on a 5-point scale based on the color distribution pattern in the elasticity chart (21): rating 1: the entire lesion is green, so it is soft; 2: most areas of the lesion have strain, while some areas do not, so it is relatively soft; 3: only the outermost part of the lesion has strain, and there is no deformation in the center, so it is moderately hard; 4: the entire lesion has no strain, so it is hard; 5: the lesion and its surroundings have no strain, so it is very hard.

Serum data collection

Under standardized conditions, approximately 2–3 mL of fasting peripheral venous blood was collected from each patient using anticoagulant tubes in the morning about a week before surgery. All samples were immediately sent to the Laboratory Department of this hospital for testing and a fully automated hematology analyzer was used to measure the number of whole blood cells, including neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, and platelets. Concurrently, thyroid function and related serological markers were measured by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay. These included free triiodothyronine (FT3), free thyroxine (FT4), triiodothyronine (T3), thyroxine (T4), TSH, TPOAb, TgAb, thyrotropin receptor antibody (TRAb), Tg, CT, parathyroid hormone, carcinoembryonic antigen, and fasting blood glucose levels. Serum calcitonin was measured by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (normal reference range: <6.4 pg/mL). All ultrasound assessors and laboratory technicians were blinded to the final pathological outcome.

Histopathological examination

Tissue sections were prepared fixed in formalin and embedded in paraffin from postoperative tumor specimens. The expression of CD56 and Ki-67 was assessed using immunohistochemistry. The slides were independently assessed by two pathologists who were blinded to the patients’ clinical data. For CD56, the expression was evaluated using a semi-quantitative scoring system based on the percentage of positive tumor cells: a score of 0 (negative) was assigned for <5% positive cells, and a score of 1 (positive) for ≥5% positive cells. The Ki-67 index was expressed as the percentage of positively stained nuclei.

Statistical analysis

Sample size and missing data: This was a retrospective consecutive series. No formal sample size calculation was performed; instead, the study aimed to include all eligible patients during the study period. The events per variable (EPV) ratio was approximately 1:1 (48 CLNM events for 46 candidate predictors), which is below the recommended threshold for logistic regression and carries a potential risk of overfitting. To mitigate this risk, we applied LASSO regression (which performs built-in variable selection) prior to multivariate analysis and used 1,000 bootstrap resamples for internal validation. The minimal optimism between the apparent area under the curve (AUC) (0.834) and the bootstrap-corrected AUC (0.813) suggests that overfitting was not severe. All patients with incomplete preoperative laboratory or ultrasound data were excluded per the exclusion criteria. Therefore, complete-case analysis was performed with no missing data in the final analytic sample of 156 patients. The limitation of the low EPV ratio is further addressed in the “Discussion” section.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 27.0) and R (version 4.4.2). Continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and comparisons between groups were conducted using the independent samples t-test. Continuous variables with a skewed distribution were expressed as the median with interquartile range [M (IQR)], and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for group comparisons. Categorical variables were summarized as the number and percentage [n (%)], and intergroup differences were assessed using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate, with the latter applied when more than 20% of the expected cell counts were less than 5.

Based on the results of the univariate analysis, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation was employed to identify the most predictive variables and to address potential multicollinearity. The variables selected through LASSO regression analysis were included in a multiple logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of CLNM. Results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Multivariate analysis was performed to develop an individualized predictive nomogram model. Bootstrap resampling with 1,000 repetitions was used for internal validation to correct for optimism, as no separate training and validation cohorts were generated due to the limited sample size. The minimum acceptable performance thresholds were pre-specified as an AUC of at least 0.70, sensitivity and specificity above 70% at the optimal cutoff, and a calibration slope between 0.8 and 1.2. Model discrimination was assessed using the AUC with its 95% CI. Calibration was evaluated using the calibration curve and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, with P>0.05 indicating good agreement between predicted and observed risks. Clinical utility was assessed by decision curve analysis (DCA) to determine the net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For clinical baseline characteristics, patient gender was an influencing factor of CLNM (χ2=5.11, P=0.02). The number of female patients with CLNM accounts for 25.9% of the total proportion, which means that a total of 30 female patients had CLNM. The proportion of male patients is slightly higher, with 45.0% of their corresponding male patient group having CLNM. This means that a total of 18 male patients suffer from this disease and have CLNM. The median age of patients in the CLNM group and non CLNM group was similar, at 54 (IQR, 42–56) years and 52 (IQR, 46–58) years, with no difference (P=0.76). Moreover, there was no significant difference in the history of diabetes (P=0.22), hypertension (P=0.78), or whether there was a history of other tumors (P=0.89). Univariate analysis showed that male sex was the only clinical baseline characteristic associated with CLNM in PTC patients with aspect ratios ≥1.

According to the analysis of ultrasound characteristics, many indicators are correlated with CLNM. In terms of the number of lesions, the proportion of patients with multiple lesions was 50.0%, and the probability of CLNM occurrence is higher than that of patients with single lesions, which is 25.4%. In terms of tumor size, the longitudinal diameter (0.82 vs. 0.57 cm), the transverse diameter (0.66 vs. 0.45 cm), and maximum diameter ≥1 cm in the CLNM group were significantly greater than in the non-CLNM group (P<0.001). Morphologically, irregular shape (38.1% vs. 11.6%) and unclear boundaries (41.4% vs. 17.4%) were associated with a higher risk of LNM (P=0.001). The analysis of the calcification types revealed that patients with fine calcification had the highest LNM rate (44.6%), which differed significantly from the groups without calcification (22.8%) and those with coarse calcification (8.3%) (P=0.004). The incidence of CLNM was significantly higher in tumor growing close to the capsule (40.3%) than in those not close to the capsule (21.5%). Moreover, higher TI-RADS classification was associated with greater CLNM risk (56.3%).

However, none of the following features showed a statistically significant difference between the two groups: tumor location (lobe or isthmus), precise intralobar position (upper, middle, lower or other), relationship to the trachea (absence or presence), internal echogenicity (solid or non-solid), posterior echoes features (unchanged/enhancement or attenuation), coarsened echotexture (absence or presence), Adler blood flow grade, elasticity point, or TI-RADS classification. The specific values of the aspect ratio also showed no significant difference between the two groups.

Analysis of serological indicators revealed no significant differences between the two groups for the vast majority of parameters. Within the thyroid function and associated antibody panel, TSH, FT3, FT4, T3, T4, as well as TPOAb, TgAb, TRAb, TG and parathyroid hormone levels showed no statistically significant differences between the CLNM group and the non-CLNM group. Of note, a trend toward higher serum calcitonin levels was observed in the CLNM group compared to the non-LNM group (median: 0.8 vs. 0.6 pg/mL), which reached borderline statistical significance (P=0.07), suggesting its potential predictive value.

Inflammatory markers showed significant differences only in monocyte count, with a higher median in the CLNM group (P=0.03). There was no statistically significant difference in other inflammatory indicators, such as platelet count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, and comprehensive indices systemic immunoinflammatory index (SII), systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), pan-immune inflammation value (PIV), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) between the two groups.

In addition, there was no significant difference in the distribution of Ki-67 proliferation index and CD56 immunohistochemical expression between the two groups.

LASSO regression analysis

To address potential multicollinearity, LASSO regression was used to select the most predictive variables (Figure 2). Using 10-fold cross-validation, the optimal penalty coefficient lambda was determined. The final LASSO model retained 10 variables with non-zero coefficients, including number of lesions, transverse diameter, maximum diameter, shape, margin, calcification type, Adler blood flow grade, FT3, T4, TgAb, calcitonin, platelet count, CD56 expression, elastography score, and TI-RADS classification.

Multivariate logistic regression analyses

To further explore the independent predictive factors of CLNM, all variables selected for LASSO regression were included in multiple logistic regression. Finally, multivariate analysis identified the following independent predictors: the maximum diameter ≥1 cm (OR =11.57; 95% CI: 2.26–59.16; P=0.003). Secondly, ill-defined margin (OR =7.43), multifocality (OR =5.87), and microcalcifications (OR =3.43). Serum calcitonin, as an important serum biomarker, was confirmed as an independent predictor (OR =1.44; 95% CI: 1.08–1.92, P=0.01) (Table 2). These five factors are the main components of the predictive model, providing key criteria for clinical assessment of CLNM risk in PTC patients with aspect ratio ≥1.

Construction and internal validation of predictive models

Based on the multivariate logistic regression analysis, this study first evaluated the discriminatory power of different variable combinations through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (Figure 3), preliminarily screening three candidate models: the all-variable model (Model 1, incorporating 11 variables selected by LASSO regression, AUC =0.869), the significant-variable model (Model 2, including 5 independent predictors significant in multivariate analysis, AUC =0.834), and the minimum combination model (Model 3, comprising the 3 variables with the highest univariate AUC values, namely maximum diameter + margin + calcitonin, AUC =0.800).

To ensure reliable estimates and correct for overfitting bias, bootstrap resampling (1,000 repetitions) was performed to conduct an internal test on these three selection models again (1,000 times). As shown in the verification results (Figure 4): although the all-variable model has the highest C-index (0.869), its performance deteriorates the most after Bootstrap correction, with C-index and optimism being 0.815 and 0.054, respectively, indicating some degree of overfitting. The best performing significant variable model has the best stability, with a corrected C-index of 0.813 and optimism of 0.021, while maintaining good discriminative performance (the C-index =0.834). The minimum combination model has poor prediction performance (calibrated C-index =0.753, optimism =0.047), making it difficult to meet clinical predictive needs.

Discussion

The incidence of PTC continues to rise globally (22), nodules with an aspect ratio ≥1 are receiving clinical attention due to their increased risk of malignancy (23). However, although these ultrasound features have strong specificity in diagnosing malignant tumors, there are still differences in their prediction of CLNM, as not all patients will develop metastasis. This uncertainty poses a dilemma for surgeons when it comes to whether to perform preventive lymph node dissection. They worry that incomplete clearance may lead to recurrence, and if over operated, it may lead to complications.

To address this clinical challenge, this study successfully constructed and validated a nomogram model for predicting the risk of CLNM in patients with PTC exhibiting an aspect ratio ≥1, by integrating preoperative conventional ultrasound imaging features with serological indicators. After rigorous LASSO regression screening and statistical screening using multiple logistic regression, five key predictive factors were ultimately determined: multiple lesions, maximum diameter ≥1 cm, ill-defined margins, microcalcifications, and serum calcitonin levels. The prediction performance of this model is stable, with a calibrated AUC value of 0.813. Both AUC and DCA show that this model has good accuracy and clinical application value. The most significant finding is that combining ultrasound characteristics and serum biomarkers (CT) effectively predicts the risk of CLNM in PTC, a special morphological variant. Although previous studies have individually demonstrated the association between the aforementioned single indicators and PTC aggressiveness (6,24-26), this study innovatively reveals their synergistic predictive value for patients in the specific subgroup with an aspect ratio ≥1.

According to the 2015 American Thyroid Association (ATA) guidelines, irregular margins and microcalcifications are emphasized as key ultrasound features that are highly suspicious for malignant tumors (27). Recent studies have demonstrated that irregular shapes and ill-defined margins are independent predictors of tumor invasiveness, with ORs of 2.864 for irregular shape and 3.149 for ill-defined margins (P<0.05). These sonographic features correspond histologically to extensive tumor cell invasion beyond the primary site into surrounding tissues. Malignant cells exhibit enhanced proliferative capacity and acquire distinct biological characteristics, including the ability to degrade extracellular matrix, invade adjacent structures, and metastasize to regional lymph nodes (28). Histologically, the edge of lesions is often associated with pre-invasion fibrous reactions, inflammatory infiltration, or outward expansion of cancer cells, which is an indicator for evaluating malignancy. The emergence of microcalcifications may be associated with malnutrition calcification resulting from rapid proliferation within the tumor, tumor thrombus calcification, or the deposition of calcium salts induced by glycoproteins and mucopolysaccharides secreted by cancer cells. Although certain benign lesions (such as fibrosis or colloid accumulation) may also present with similar echogenicity on ultrasound, microcalcifications remain one of the highly specific features of PTC (6). Our study identified both irregular margins (OR =7.43; 95% CI: 2.06–26.76; P=0.002) and microcalcifications (OR =3.43; 95% CI: 1.19–9.93; P=0.02) as independent risk factors for CLNM in taller-than-wide PTC, aligning with the results of Tian et al. (29).

According to a review by Sun et al. (30), a maximum tumor diameter ≥1 cm and multifocality are recognized as indicators of higher tumor burden, which consequently elevates the risk of LNM in PTC. Some studies suggest that a nodule’s maximum diameter ≥1 cm constitutes an independent risk factor, while others only classify a maximum diameter ≥2 cm as such (31-33). This study demonstrates that tumor maximum diameter ≥1 cm (OR =11.57, P=0.003) and multifocality (OR =5.87, P=0.003) are significant predictors of CLNM in patients with PTC exhibiting an aspect ratio ≥1. This suggests a strong association with increased metastatic risk, consistent with the findings of Sun et al. (30) and Liu et al. (34). A tumor maximum diameter ≥1 cm reflects increased nodular volume and a larger population of dividing cancer cells with heightened biological activity, thereby leading to a markedly higher likelihood of transcapsular extension and lymphatic invasion (35-37). Multifocality, defined by multiple discrete tumor foci within the thyroid, is suggestive of either intrathyroidal lymphatic dissemination or multicentric origins, indicating an underlying propensity for aggressive local invasion and spread. Recent large-scale studies have demonstrated that the risk of aggressive pathological features in PTC increases with tumor multiplicity. Specifically, multifocality has been identified as an independent predictor of central LNM (OR =1.58; 95% CI: 1.04–2.40), and is also significantly associated with extrathyroidal extension and vascular invasion (38). Moreover, recurrence risk rises markedly with the number of tumor foci; patients with five or more foci or a satellite pattern face a significantly elevated risk (OR =19.49; P<0.001) (39). Notably, patients with bilateral multifocal disease exhibit the highest rates of BRAFV600E mutation, nodal metastases, and recurrence among all PTC subgroups (39).

CT is a polypeptide hormone secreted by the parafollicular cells of the thyroid gland. The primary rationale for preoperative CT testing centers on the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC). Furthermore, it is instrumental in prognosticating the degree of LNM in MTC and evaluating therapeutic efficacy following surgery (20,40). However, this study suggests that the predictive value of CT may extend beyond MTC. Within this specific subtype, CT was confirmed to be an independent predictor for CLNM (P=0.014), which broadens the potential clinical applications of this biomarker.

In this study, although most patients exhibited calcitonin levels within the normal range (<6.4 pg/mL) and no significant intergroup differences were observed in univariate analysis (P=0.07), multivariate analysis confirmed that even within the normal range, a relatively elevated serum calcitonin level was independently associated with an increased risk of LNM (OR =1.437). Notably, all five patients in our cohort who exhibited elevated calcitonin levels above the normal range (>6.4 pg/mL) had confirmed LNM. Although this subgroup is small, this observation further supports the association between rising calcitonin levels—whether a relative increase within the normal range or an absolute elevation beyond it—and aggressive behavior in PTC.

Regarding the association between CT and PTC invasiveness, a potential biological explanation has been provided. Some scholars have observed that among patients undergoing surgery due to elevated calcitonin levels, the proportion with concomitant PTC is significantly higher than the incidental finding rate in autopsy reports, suggesting that the coexistence of these conditions may not be a random occurrence. Furthermore, research has proposed the hypothesis that proliferating C cells may actively influence neighbouring follicular cells through paracrine effects, thereby participating in the development of follicular tumors. We thus postulate that the association between serum CT and LNM in PTC is a manifestation of C-cell and follicular cell cross-talk. Specifically, high CT levels signal a reactive C-cell state, whose associated microenvironment—potentially driven by undefined biochemical factor—could promote the metastatic capacity of neighboring PTC cells. Moreover, the two cell types share key transcription factors such as TTF-1, which also provides a common molecular basis for their interactions.

Although the model established in this study has significant potential and new predictive factors such as calcitonin have emerged, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, this is a retrospective survey conducted in a single center, which may have introduced selection bias. Secondly, the small number of participants and the low incidence of elevated calcitonin levels may affect the factor’s efficacy assessment during statistical analysis. Third, the EPV ratio of approximately 1:1 is below the recommended minimum of 10 events per candidate variable, which may increase the risk of overfitting. However, the minimal optimism (0.021) between the apparent and bootstrap-corrected AUC indicates that overfitting was not severe in our model. Fourth, external validation in independent cohorts was not performed in this study. While bootstrap resampling provides internal validation, external validation is needed to confirm the model’s generalizability to other populations before clinical application.

Conclusions

Overall, this project has established and validated a nomogram model, which combines ultrasound characteristics with serological indicators. It combines conditions such as multiple lesions, maximum diameter ≥1 cm, irregular margins, and microcalcifications with calcitonin. This model demonstrates satisfactory efficacy in predicting CLNM in patients with aspect ratio ≥1 PTC. This model can serve as a basis for personalized preoperative risk assessment and provide clinical doctors with a practical tool to make accurate choices for lymph node clearance, which may ultimately improve patient outcomes.

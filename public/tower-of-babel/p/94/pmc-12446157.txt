MiR-26b-5p mediates radioresistance and immunosuppression via targeting PRKCD in non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Xu Chen, Rui Kong, Yaxian Qi, Lingchen Li, Chenrui Yin, Lingyou Sun, Chunli Jian, Ping Cai, Qiao Yang, Jianguo Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeOvercoming miRNA-mediated radioresistance and enhancing its synergy with immunotherapy remained significant challenges.MethodsA total of 23 patients with locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) undergoing thoracic radiotherapy from a single center were enrolled. Pre-radiotherapy blood samples were collected and analyzed using real time qPCR array to detect miRNA expression profiles, identifying differential miRNAs between responders and non-responders. In vitro experiments further assessed the impact of radiotherapy on significant differential miRNAs. Targeted immune genes of miRNAs were predicted through bioinformatics websites and validated by cellular experiments. Using TCGA and GEO datasets, the association between immune gene of interest and survival outcomes and immune infiltration were investigated. In vivo experiment was further performed to investigate the relationship between dendritic cell (DC) expression and miR-26b-5p following radiotherapy.ResultsUsing pre-radiotherapy blood samples from 23 NSCLC patients, 22 differentially expressed miRNAs were identified between responders and non-responders. Among them, miR-26b-5p exhibited significant differential expression, suggesting its role as a potential radioresistant molecule. The dual-luciferase assay confirmed miR-26b-5p targeted PRKCD, an immune-related gene. After continuous three days of 2-Gy irradiation, the expression of miR-26b-5p decreased significantly, while the expression of PRKCD increased. The effect of radiotherapy on PRCKD expression were further validated in clinical samples, which demonstrated elevated PRCKD expression after thoracic radiotherapy. Bioinformatic analysis using TCGA and GEO datasets revealed that a higher PRKCD expression was correlated with better survival outcomes, increased immune cell infiltration, and better outcomes. Further in vivo experiments showed that, after radiotherapy, the inhibition of miR-26b-5p showed a significantly higher proportion of DCs than the controls, along with increased expression of CD80, CD86, and TNFSF4.ConclusionmiR-26b-5p and PRKCD modulates dual resistance of both radiotherapy and immunotherapy in NSCLC. These insights demonstrate that downregulating miR-26b-5p could offer a promising therapeutic strategy to enhance radiosensitivity and immune responses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-025-06310-x.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer, miR-26b-5p, Radioresistance, PRKCD, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Lung cancer remained the most lethal malignancy worldwide, with the highest incidence and mortality rates in males and the second highest in females, as reported in the Global Cancer Statistics 2022 (Bray et al. 2024). In China, lung cancer posed the most significant health burden for both sexes (Wu et al. 2024). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounted for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases, frequently required radiotherapy. This treatment served as a cornerstone for nearly 70% of these patients.

Radiotherapy, particularly stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT), played a crucial role in advanced NSCLC treatment. It not only exerted direct tumoricidal effects but also modulated the tumor immune microenvironment (TME) to enhance the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) (Wu et al. 2022; Gong et al. 2017). As a highly precise, high-dose radiation modality, SBRT maximized tumor control while minimizing damage to surrounding normal tissues (Vinod and Hau 2020; Qiu et al. 2020). Beyond its cytotoxic effects, radiotherapy exerted potent immunomodulatory influences that reprogrammed the TME and promoted anti-tumor immunity (Janopaul-Naylor et al. 2021; Heinzerling et al. 2021). One of the key immunological mechanisms induced by radiotherapy was immunogenic cell death. This process led to the release of tumor-associated antigens, activating dendritic cells and priming T cells (Heinzerling et al. 2021). Furthermore, radiotherapy—especially SBRT—enhanced the infiltration and activation of CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes while reducing the suppressive influence of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and regulatory T cells (Wu et al. 2022; Gong et al. 2017; Rodriguez-Ruiz et al. 2019). These changes induced a more immunoactivity TME and improved the response to ICIs. Additionally, radiation upregulated PD-L1 expression on tumor cells, increasing their susceptibility to ICIs and enhancing anti-tumor immunity (Zhuang 2020).

Despite these advantages, the efficacy of radio-immunotherapy remained limited due to two major challenges. First, a subset of lung cancer cells exhibited intrinsic or adaptive resistance to radiation, leading to residual tumor burden or recurrence even after definitive treatment. Second, cancer cells evaded immune surveillance, fostering a suppressive TME that impaired T-cell activation and reduced the effectiveness of ICIs (Goto 2019; Yu et al. 2022). These dual barriers, including radioresistance and immune suppression, restricted radiation-induced immunogenic cell death and T-cell activation, thereby constraining the overall therapeutic benefit of this combined approach. While significant progress had been made in understanding the interplay between radiotherapy and the immune system, the regulatory molecules and pathways driving resistance to radio-immunotherapy remained poorly characterized. Further research is needed to optimize treatment strategies.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), a class of small, endogenously produced, non-coding, single-stranded RNAs, played a pivotal role in cancer pathogenesis, proliferation, migration, and invasion (Al-Hawary et al. 2024; Park et al. 2025). Some miRNAs had been shown to enhance radiosensitivity, while others contributed to radioresistance (Zhao et al. 2012; Mohammadi et al. 2021). Moreover, miRNAs regulated the TME, influencing the effectiveness of immunotherapy (Nguyen et al. 2021; Kousar et al. 2022). Given their ability to modulate both radiation response and immune function, miRNAs represented promising therapeutic targets in radio-immunotherapy.

Despite the recognized roles of miRNAs in cancer therapy, a comprehensive understanding of their involvement in resistance to radio-immunotherapy in NSCLC remained elusive. This study aimed to identify key miRNAs and their immune-related target genes that regulated radio-immunotherapy efficacy. Unveiling these mechanisms could facilitate the development of novel strategies targeting miRNAs and immune-related genes to optimize treatment outcomes in NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Patients

NSCLC patients who received thoracic radiotherapy were screened. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age between 18 and 75 years; histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC; stage IIIA, IIIB, or IV NSCLC (AJCC 7th edition); an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0–1; an expected survival duration of more than 3 months; normal heart, liver, lung, kidney, and bone marrow function; no prior thoracic radiotherapy; two to four cycles of prior cisplatin-based chemotherapy without disease progression; EGFR wild type (WT); and ineligibility for concurrent chemoradiotherapy. The exclusion criteria included severe dysfunction of vital organs (heart, liver, kidney), multiple primary malignancies, acute infections, or any other conditions deemed inappropriate by investigators.

A total of 23 patients were enrolled. Radiotherapy was delivered using conventional fractionation, with a total dose of 60 Gy in 30 fractions over 6 weeks. Tumor response was assessed using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST1.1) criteria, which classified patients into complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). Patients with CR or PR were assigned to the radiosensitive group, while those with SD or PD were classified as radioresistant. Blood samples were collected before radiotherapy for detecting miRNA profiling. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xinqiao Hospital, Army Medical University (2017-No.004-01). All patients provided written informed consent.

Screening and validation of key miRNAs

Total RNA was extracted from clinical blood samples using TRIzol™ Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Reverse transcription of miRNAs was performed with the Quanto-mir cDNA kit (QuantoBio, China). miRNA expression was quantified using a real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) array (Qiagen, Germany). Two methods were applied to identify potential miRNAs associated with radiotherapy response. First, the relative expression level, i.e. the ratio of miRNA in radiosensitive group to radioresistant group, were calculated. The formula is y = 2−(ΔCT2−ΔCT1). miRNAs with a relative expression level equal to or higher than 2 folds and p value < 0.05 were selected. Second, differentially expressed miRNAs (|logFC|> 1 & p < 0.05) between two groups were identified using the limma package. The intersection of the two methods were regarded as potential miRNAs associated with radiosensitivity or radioresistance.

The role of intersection miRNAs in radiation were further validated in vitro. A549 and H460 cells, previously reported as radioresistant lung cancer cells (Yang et al. 2013; Carmichael et al. 1989), were cultured to 50% confluence before exposure to a single fraction of 8 Gy X-ray irradiation. The expression levels of 22 miRNAs were measured before irradiation and at 48 and 72 h post-irradiation. Total RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus (TaKaRa, Japan) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using an RT-qPCR kit (TaKaRa, Japan).

Identification of targeted immune gene of key miRNAs

Target genes of the selected miRNAs were predicted using three databases: https://www.isical.ac.in, http://www.targetscan.org, and http://mirdb.org. Immune-related regulatory genes were retrieved from http://geneontology.org. The intersection of targeted regulatory genes and immune regulatory genes were regarded as potential targeted immune genes of selected miRNAs.

To further validate the regulation of miRNAs on these targeted immune genes, miRNA mimics were transferred into A549 cells. The expressions of target immune genes were detected. The most significantly downregulated gene was selected for further investigation.

TargetScan software was used to predict interactions between miRNAs and their target genes. A dual luciferase assay was then performed for validation. The reporter plasmids protein kinase C delta-WT (PRKCD-WT) and PRKCD-mutant (MT) (Promega, USA) were designed and constructed using GeneCreate (China). Luciferase assays were performed with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 293 T cells were co-transfected with miRNA mimics, negative control (NC) mimics, PRKCD-WT or PRKCD-MT 48 h before the assay. The cells were then lysed to measure luciferase activity according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

A549 and H460 cells were cultured for 24 h, and transfected with miRNA mimics, inhibitors, or negative controls. After transfection, cells were irradiated with a single 8 Gy X-ray fraction. Samples were collected at 48 h post-irradiation for apoptosis assays, performed using an apoptosis detection kit (BD, USA) and analyzed by flow cytometry.

Additionally, HCC827 cells, identified as radiosensitive lung cancer cells in previous studies (Zhu et al. 2014), were cultured and exposed to 2 Gy X-rays per day for three consecutive days. Samples were collected before each irradiation and 24 h after the final dose. The expression levels of miR-26b-5p and PRKCD were quantified using RT-qPCR.

Bioinformatic analysis of the role of PRKCD in radiotherapy and immunotherapy

The GSE162946 dataset was obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo). This dataset, from a previous study conducted by our department (Zhou et al. 2020a), was used to compare PRKCD expression before and after SBRT in primary lung lesions.

Clinical data and gene expression profiles for NSCLC patients were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (http://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Additionally, the GSE11969 dataset was obtained from the GEO database. Preprocessing involved background correction and normalization of probes, followed by the calculation of their expressions. Probes were then replaced with gene symbols based on the platform annotation file. The association between PRKCD expression and patient prognosis was investigated through survival analysis in NSCLC, employing Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test. Survival curves were generated using the R packages “survival” and “survminer”.

The expression data of 26 immune cell types in NSCLC, along with PRKCD expression, were derived from the RNA sequencing data of the third-level RNA profiling within the TCGA-NSCLC project. The immune infiltration analysis was conducted using the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm within the Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) framework and the XCELL algorithm from the R package “immunedeconv.” Immune cell infiltration levels (ImmuneScore) and stromal component abundance (StromalScore) in TCGA samples were compared with the estimated score to analyze the Spearman correlation between PRKCD expression and immune cell infiltration levels. The potential association between infiltrating immune cells and PRKCD expression was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

To further evaluate the prognostic role of PRKCD, an independent immunotherapy cohort (GSE126044) was analyzed. A Sankey plot (ggalluvial package) was generated to visualize the relationship between PRKCD expression and clinical features. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed, using the optimal PRKCD expression cutoff (survival and survminer packages).

To further investigate the relationship between dendritic cell (DC) expression and miR-26b-5p following radiotherapy, an in vivo experiment was performed. A total of 5 × 106 H460 cells were subcutaneously injected into the right thigh of 6-week-old BALB/c nude mice. Once the xenografts reached approximately 50 mm3 in volume, the mice were randomly divided into two groups (antagomir group and control group, n = 5 per group). Each tumor in the antagomir group received an intratumoral injection of 5 nmol of miR-26b-5p antagomir dissolved in 25 μL of saline, while the control group received 25 μL of saline alone. The injections were administered twice weekly for three consecutive weeks. When the tumors reached approximately 200 mm3 in size, mice received local irradiation at a dose of 8 Gy for three fractions. One week after the last irradiation, the mice were euthanized, and tumors were excised, weighed, and processed into single-cell suspensions for flow cytometric analysis.

Statistical analysis

All in vitro experiments were performed at least three times. Data were presented as mean ± standard error. Comparisons were conducted using one-way ANOVA or Student’s t test. Survival analysis was performed by Kaplan–Meier method and compared by log-rank test. All Statistical analyzes were performed by GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) or R software (version 4.4.0). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Using the TargetScan software, a direct interaction between PRKCD-3′UTR and miR-26b-5p was predicted (Fig. 3C). A dual-luciferase assay was performed to confirm this interaction. 293 T cells were co-transfected with either miR-26b-5p mimics or NC mimics, along with PRKCD-3′UTR WT or PRKCD-3′UTR MT luciferase reporter vectors. These findings suggested that miR-26b-5p might promote radioresistance by downregulating PRKCD expression.

Since PRKCD might be the pivotal downstream immune-related gene targeted by miR-26b-5p, further bioinformatics analyses were conducted to explore its role in immune regulation and immunotherapy. In the TCGA cohort, patients with higher PRKCD expression displayed better overall survival (OS) (median OS [mOS]: PRKCD-high vs. PRKCD-low, 41.5 vs. 32.6 months; HR, 0.73, 95% CI 0.59–0.90, p = 0.013) (Fig. 5A). The survival benefit from higher PRKCD expression was further validated by GSE11969 cohort (mOS: PRKCD-high vs. PRKCD-low, 25.5 vs. 22.7 months; HR, 0.66, 95% CI 0.41–1.05, p = 0.09; 12-month, 36-month, and 60-month OS rates: 91.9% vs. 86.7%, 70.3% vs. 66.7%, and 60.8% vs. 56.0%) (Fig. 5B). Both algorithms revealed that the PRKCD expression were positively correlated with multiple anti-tumor immune cells (Fig. 5C and D). And macrophage and DC were the two same anti-tumor immune cells which were highly infiltrated in patients with higher PRKCD expression (Fig. 5E and F). Additionally, the PRKCD expression was positively correlated with multiple immunostimulatory molecules, including CD28, CD80, CD86, and TNFRSF4 (Fig. 5G and H), which were primarily expressed on DCs.

Discussion

In this study, miR-26b-5p was identified as a pivotal regulator of radioresistance in NSCLC, demonstrating significant overexpression in radioresistant patients. The upregulation of miR-26b-5p directly targeted PRKCD, potentially contributing to an immunosuppressive TME. These findings revealed a potential radiotherapy-immune regulatory axis in advanced NSCLC and provided evidence supporting the optimization of radiotherapy-immunotherapy strategies.

In recent years, accumulating evidence had highlighted the critical and multifaceted role of miR-26b-5p in the pathogenesis and progression of various malignancies. It acted as a key regulator of cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. In multiple cancers, such as bladder cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and thyroid cancer, miR-26b-5p was frequently downregulated, and its decreased expression was associated with enhanced tumor growth and metastatic potential (Miyamoto et al. 2016; Khosla et al. 2019; Zhou et al. 2020b). Restoring miR-26b-5p expression inhibited tumor migration and invasion. Similar findings were found in NSCLC cells, where the inhibition of miR-26b-5p promoted proliferation, suppressed apoptosis, and resulted in further progression through multiple regulatory axes, including miR-26b-5p/EZH2 (Wu et al. 2014), miR-26b-5p/COMMD8 (Zhang et al. 2020), and miR-26b-5p/DUXAP8 (Liu et al. 2021).

Despite substantial evidence supporting the tumor-suppressive effects of miR-26b-5p, its function in modulating radiotherapy response in NSCLC remained poorly characterized. Han et al. previously reported that higher expression of exosomal miR-26b-5p enhanced radiosensitivity in A549 cells via down-regulating the expression of ATF2 (Han et al. 2020), a key tumor suppressor involved in suppressing tumor growth and metastasis (Zhang et al. 2016; Lau et al. 2015). In contrast, our clinical findings revealed elevated miR-26b-5p expression in NSCLC patients was associated with radioresistance. Further in vitro experiment demonstrated irradiation-induced miR-26b-5p upregulation in radioresistant A549 and H460 cell lines, whereas its expression was downregulated in radiosensitive HCC827 cells. These paradoxical findings mentioned above suggested the context-dependent nature of miR-26b-5p in radiotherapy responses, potentially mediated by different interrelated mechanisms. First, miRNAs could exert different roles through targeting various genes, offering a complex regulatory network subsequently impacting on the TME (Singh et al. 2025). Therefore, miRNAs could function as either tumor suppressor or oncogene in different cancer types and distinct cancer biology. In regard to miR-26b-5p, on one hand, it may enhance radiosensitivity through down-regulating ATF2. On the other hand, it potentially exerted radioresistence through down-regulating PRKCD. The net response of miR-26b-5p to radiotherapy likely depended on experimental conditions, radiation protocols, and tumor-specific genomic landscapes. Second, miRNAs may exert temporally distinct roles in different tumor stages. During early tumorigenesis, miR-26b-5p primarily suppressed proliferation and invasion. However, in advanced disease stages, it may exert immunosuppressive properties that facilitated immune evasion and therapy resistance (Xu et al. 2013; Fu et al. 2020). Last, exosome-derived and tumor-derived miRNAs may exert opposing roles in tumorigenesis and progression (Chen et al. 2017; Dou et al. 2021).

Our finding was partially supported by Yin’s study, which revealed that irradiation could upregulate the expression of miR-26b-5p in small extracellular vesicles derived from esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cell lines, subsequently enhancing the expansion and activation of MDSCs via the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, resulting in a metastasis-promoting TME (Yin et al. 2022). Despite the complex regulatory mechanisms of miR-26b-5p, our clinical findings suggested that miR-26b-5p expression before radiotherapy could serve as a potential biomarker for predicting response to radiotherapy. Further in vitro experiment demonstrated that miR-26b-5p expression gradually increased following radiotherapy exposure, indicating its role as an intrinsic mechanism driving radioresistance. Therefore, a more nuanced understanding of these issues is essential for developing effective, personalized therapeutic strategies.

We further investigated the potential target genes of miR-26b-5p and found that PRKCD was a candidate target gene involved in immune response. The luciferase reporter assay indicated that miR-26b-5p could bind efficiently to the 3’-UTR of PRKCD, and upregulation of miR-26b-5p was associated with downregulation of PRKCD mRNA. Moreover, after irradiation, we found that the downregulation of miR-26b-5p was in return to upregulation of PRKCD. Bioinformatics analysis of GSE162946 dataset suggested that, PRKCD was highly expressed after SBRT in NSCLC lesions, which might be potentially associate with improving TME. Further TCGA and GEO immunotherapy datasets suggested that high expression of PRKCD mRNA was correlated with higher anti-tumor immune cells infiltration, higher expression of immunostimulatory molecules. The abundant infiltration of DCs in TME helped to present tumor-associated antigens, secrete soluble factors, and promote T cell activation and immune response (Wculek et al. 2020). The abundant infiltration of M1 macrophage in TME helped to identify and eliminate tumor cells (Chen et al. 2019). Additionally, the higher expression of CD28, CD80, CD86, and TNFRSF4 further demonstrated activated DCs in TME (Tang et al. 2020), suggesting activated anti-tumor immunity. These findings implied that PRKCD could be a potential target for immunotherapeutic interventions in NSCLC patients. We further conducted in vivo experiments using H460 tumor-bearing nude mice to explore the effect of miR-26b-5p on DCs. After radiotherapy, the miR-26b-5p antagomir group showed a significantly higher proportion of DCs than the control group, along with increased expression of CD80, CD86, and TNFSF4. These results support the role of the miR-26b-5p/PRKCD axis in suppressing DC activation and highlight its contribution to radioresistance and immune evasion in NSCLC.

Moreover, we found a positive correlation between PRKCD expression and survival in multiple datasets, including a small immunotherapy set. This finding further validated the promotion impact of PRKCD to immune response. The protein encoded by PRKCD, a member of the serine/threonine-specific protein kinase C family, exhibited a profound expression profile in diverse cancer cell lines, where it performed dual functions as both a tumor suppressor and a positive modulator, depending on the cellular context and signaling milieu (Okhrimenko et al. 2005). A study found that PRKCD exhibited inhibition on proliferation and migration of LUAD cells (Liang et al. 2023). Nambotin’s research revealed that the stimulation of PRKCD effectively diminished the viability of hepatocellular carcinoma cells (Nambotin et al. 2011). Wen’s study found PRKCD as a key targeted gene by miR-424 to regulate cell proliferation in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cell (Wen et al. 2018). In contrast to our findings, a few evidences suggested that PRKCD overexpression was correlated with an immunosuppressive TME. The genetic ablation of PRKCD could reprogram mononuclear phagocytes to trigger interferon-alpha/gamma responses (Chaib et al. 2023) and enhanced T cells infiltration and response to immune checkpoint blockade (Zuo et al. 2022). These conflicting findings mentioned above underscored the complex regulatory mechanisms of the miR-26b-5p/PRKCD axis in modulating both radiosensitivity and anti-tumor immunity. More systematic investigations are needed to resolve these discrepancies and improve therapeutic strategies.

The strength of this study was that we firstly proposed miR-26b-5p mediating a potential dual tolerance mechanism of radioimmunity in NSCLC. We used both clinical data and in vitro experiment with multiple NSCLC cell lines confirmed the radioresistance role of miR-26b-5p and its targeted regulation of PRKCD. And the association between PRKCD and immune response were validated by multiple clinical cohorts through bioinformatic analysis. Given the confirmed role of miR-26b-5p in promoting radioresistance in our study, strategies to downregulate miR-26b-5p could attenuate its resistance to radiotherapy. In addition, upregulating PRKCD may counteract the radioresistance induced by elevated miR-26b-5p levels and simultaneously bolster the immune response.

Our findings were in contrast with previous studies, which suggested that miR-26b-5p could enhance radiosensitivity through other molecules. These results suggested the functional diversity of miR-26b-5p responded to radiotherapy. Besides, although our study did not directly validate the association between PRKCD and radiosensitivity, converging evidence from prior studies had revealed that PRKCD could promoted radiosensitivity through multiple molecular regulatory mechanisms. Ke et.al demonstrated that miR-181a had an effect on radioresistance via targeting PRKCD in cervical carcinoma (Ke et al. 2013). Baek et.al found that the interaction of PLOD3 and PRKCD promoted radiosensitivity and induced tumor growth inhibition (Baek et al. 2019).

A few limitations are acknowledged. Firstly, the optimal radiation dosage that influences miR-26b-5p has not been sufficiently validated. Secondly, the role of PRKCD as a predictor of immune therapeutic response requires further validation through large-scale prospective studies.

In conclusion, this study identified miR-26b-5p as a pivotal regulator of radioresistance in NSCLC. Its targeting immune-related gene PRKCD modulated dual resistance of both radiotherapy and immunotherapy in NSCLC. These insights demonstrate that downregulating miR-26b-5p could offer a promising therapeutic strategy to enhance radiosensitivity and immune responses.

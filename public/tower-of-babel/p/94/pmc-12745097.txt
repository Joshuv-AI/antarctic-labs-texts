Surgical treatment for acute aortic dissection with coronary malperfusion in Japan: Nationwide database analysis
Authors: Toshiki Fujiyoshi, Hiraku Kumamaru, Hitoshi Ogino, Naoko Kinukawa, Yusuke Shimahara, Noboru Motomura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo assess the surgical outcomes of acute aortic dissection (AAD) with coronary malperfusion (CM) in Japan through the Japan Cardiovascular Surgery Database.MethodsBetween 2019 and 2021, 15,509 patients underwent surgical treatment for AAD. CM occurred in 304 (2.0%), of which 188 were analyzed.ResultsThe mean age of patients was 65 ± 11.8 years. Preoperative acute myocardial infarction, cardiopulmonary arrest, and mechanical circulatory support (MCS) were found in 49.5%, 16.5%, and 9.6%, respectively. The 30-day operative mortality rate was 33.0%. The left CM (33.5%) was associated with greater mortality rates (41.3%), particularly as Neri classification advanced to type B and type C. Conversely, right CM (56.4%) had a lower mortality rate (25.0%), regardless of Neri classification. Preoperative percutaneous coronary intervention was performed in 14.9% and concomitant coronary artery bypass grafting was performed in 57.4%. The 30-day operative mortality rate for coronary intervention showed no difference between percutaneous coronary intervention (41.7%) and coronary artery bypass grafting (38.0%), regardless of Neri classification. The median time from onset to coronary reperfusion was 361.5 minutes. Preoperative acute myocardial infarction, cardiopulmonary arrest, and the requirement for MCS as independent risk factors for 30-day operative mortality.ConclusionsThe surgical mortality rate for AAD with CM remains high, particularly in cases with left CM, with this trend becoming more pronounced as the Neri's classification advanced to type B and type C. Patients in critical conditions, such as those requiring cardiopulmonary resuscitation or MCS, were at significantly greater risk of mortality.

Central Message

The surgical outcomes of Stanford type A acute aortic dissection with coronary malperfusion depend primarily on preoperative conditions, with no differences observed between intervention strategies.

Perspective

Stanford type A acute aortic dissection with coronary malperfusion is highly fatal, especially with LCA involvement in Neri type B or C. Both PCI and CABG are viable, but outcomes depend more on preoperative condition. Treatment should be selected on the basis of clinical urgency and institutional expertise.

Acute aortic dissection is one of the most serious and life-threatening cardiovascular events. However, the actual status of its treatment is unclear, because there has been no detailed national medical database specific to aortic disease. Reportedly, one half of the patients die from out-of-hospital cardiopulmonary arrest (CPA) before arriving at the hospital.1 Although treatment guidelines exist, supporting evidence is limited. In particular, the prognoses for patients with coronary malperfusion (CM) or brain malperfusion are poor, and the choice of treatment remains controversial, requiring robust evidence.2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Therefore, we evaluated the current outcomes of surgical treatments using the nationwide Japan Cardiovascular Surgery Database (JCVSD). Since its established in 2000, the JCVSD has tracked cardiovascular surgical treatments related to cardiac and thoracic aortic diseases in Japan. It was later integrated into the National Clinical Database (https://www.ncd.or.jp/). The JCVSD encompasses nearly all cardiovascular surgery centers in Japan, with mandatory participation required for cardiovascular surgeons seeking certification by the Japanese Board of Cardiovascular Surgery, thereby ensuring the database's completeness and reliability. According to a recent report, in-hospital mortality for Stanford type A acute aortic dissection (TAAAD) is now 9.8%.10, 11, 12

However, for CM, acute myocardial infarction (AMI) and/or aortic root rupture can occur, both of which often can cause deep shock or CPA. Despite advancements in modern medical care, the mortality rate for these cases is anticipated to be more than twice as high.4, 5, 6, 7 For cases in which patients were treated for AMI and died without undergoing surgical intervention for TAAAD, as well as those who received surgical treatment, the preoperative conditions, treatment indications and sequencing, procedural details, and outcomes remain unclear. Specific measures to improve treatment outcomes have not been clearly defined. With the enactment of the Basic Act on Countermeasures Against Stroke and Cardiovascular Diseases by the Japanese government, aortic dissection—a condition with a high mortality rate—has been designated as a priority disease. In this study, we focus on TAAAD complicated by CM, aiming to provide a comprehensive understanding of current clinical practices and management strategies for this challenging condition.

Methods

This study was conducted with the support of the Japan Surgical Society, the National Clinical Database/JCVSD, and the Japanese Association for Cardiovascular Surgery.

Ethical Statements

The study protocol adhered to the ethical principles of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki and the Ethical Guidelines for Epidemiological Research issued by the Japanese government. Informed consent for registering clinical data in the JCVSD was obtained from each patient at participating institutions. On the basis of these ethical guidelines, individual consent was waived. The study was approved by the institutional review board of Tokyo Medical University (approval no. T2021-0085, June 1, 2021).

Patients

From 2019 to 2021, 15,509 patients underwent emergent surgical treatment for TAAAD. Among these cases, CM occurred in 304 patients (2.0%). A detailed analysis was conducted on 188 patients (1.2%) for whom optional supplementary data were available in addition to the standard dataset (Figure E1). In the JCVSD, CM was defined on the basis of clinical, electrocardiographic, echocardiographic, and intraoperative findings suggestive of myocardial ischemia secondary to aortic dissection. Angiographic confirmation was not required. CM was recorded if 1 or more of the following criteria were met: (1) intraoperative evidence of coronary involvement by the dissection flap; (2) electrocardiographic changes indicating ischemia (eg, ST-segment elevation); (3) new wall motion abnormalities on echocardiography; or (4) perioperative coronary revascularization (coronary artery bypass grafting [CABG] or percutaneous coronary intervention [PCI]) performed because of compromised coronary flow.

Surgical Treatments

In the JCVSD, indications and procedures for surgical treatment of TAAAD—like open repair with or without CABG and/or PCI, proximal or distal extension of repairs, frozen elephant trunk, brain protection techniques, and temperature management—varied depending on each institution's strategy and experience, although most followed the Japanese guidelines.2 However, detailed information on each treatment is limited because of the 255 variables in the database, a key limitation. A representative surgical video demonstrating the repair of acute type A aortic dissection involving the left main coronary artery is included (Video 1).

End Points

The primary end point was mortality, defined as any death occurring during the hospitalization in which the surgical treatment was performed or within 30 days postoperatively, as per JCVSD guidelines. Secondary end points included the prevalence of preoperative PCI and intraoperative CABG, surgical results, incidence of postoperative complications, variations by malperfusion site, and risk factors for operative mortality. The relationship between operative mortality and treatment time intervals from TAAAD onset to coronary reperfusion was analyzed.

Data Collection and Analysis

Clinical data were registered and analyzed centrally. CM was additionally classified according to the Neri classification, on the basis of operative findings or supplemental records. The classification includes type A (ostial dissection), type B (dissection with a coronary false channel), and type C (circumferential detachment with an inner cylinder intussusception).5 Missing data included transport time in 20 cases, left ventricular function in 72 cases, cardiac arrest duration in 2 cases, intensive care unit stay in 4 cases, ventilation time in 86 cases, hospital stay length in 67 cases, and perioperative creatinine levels in 5 cases.

Statistics

Categorical variables are expressed as the number of patients (%). Continuous data are presented as mean/standard deviation and median/interquartile range. Unknown data are considered missing from the analyses. To clarify risk factors strongly associated with operative mortality, multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed using variables with no missing data, selected based on previously reported categories or those deemed clinically relevant: advanced age older than 80 years, concomitant other malperfusion (carotid, superior mesenteric, and iliac), preoperative conditions (CPA, AMI), preoperative mechanical circulatory support (MCS) like intra-aortic balloon pumping, venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, and ventricular assist device, CM site, and intervention (preoperative PCI, CABG, PCI + CABG, root surgery). A 95% confidence interval for the proportion was calculated under the assumption of a binomial distribution. All statistical analyses are performed using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute).

Results

Patient Characteristics and Preoperative Status

Patient characteristics and preoperative status are shown in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. The mean age was 65 ± 11.8 years, and 66.5% were male. Hypertension was the most common comorbidity (70.7%), followed by smoking history (41.0%) and dyslipidemia (24.5%). Aortic rupture was observed in 14.4% of cases. Preoperatively, 51.1% of patients were in shock, 16.5% had CPA, and 49.5% had evidence of AMI. MCS was required in 9.6%, including intra-aortic balloon pump (3.7%) and venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygen (5.3%). Coronary Malperfusion Profile

CM involved the right coronary artery (RCA) in 56.4%, the left coronary artery (LCA) in 33.5%, and both in 10.1% (Figure E2, A). According to the Neri classification, 42.6% were type A, 26.1% type B, 18.6% type C, and 12.8% unclassified (Figure E2, B).

Surgical Treatment

All patients underwent emergency aortic repair (Table 3). Ascending aortic replacement with or without root replacement was performed in 47.9%, whereas total arch replacement (TAR) was performed in 51.6%. In the patients who underwent TAR, the frozen elephant trunk technique was used in 46.4% (45 of 97 patients). Aortic root surgery was done in 25.0% of the overall cohort. When stratified by Neri classification, the corresponding rates were 27.5% for type A, 12.2% for type B, 42.9% for type C, and 16.6% among unclassified cases. Preoperative PCI was performed in 14.9%, and concomitant CABG in 57.4%. Eight patients (4.3%) received both PCI and CABG. The overall 30-day operative mortality was 33.0% (Table 4, Figures 1 and 2, and Figure E3). Main causes of death were low cardiac output, aortic complications, and neurologic injury. Major complications included cardiac arrest, stroke, renal failure, and coma. LCA malperfusion was associated with significantly greater mortality compared with RCA malperfusion (41.3% vs 25.0%). Mortality increased with advancing Neri classification, particularly in LCA cases, whereas RCA cases showed no clear association with Neri type. No significant mortality difference was observed between PCI and CABG (41.7% vs 38.0%), although patients requiring both interventions had the greatest mortality (50.0%). Shorter onset-to-reperfusion times were paradoxically associated with greater mortality, likely reflecting the critical preoperative condition of these patients.

Discussion

This study analyzes TAAAD complicated by CM, using nationwide data from the JCVSD. The findings shed light on the clinical challenges associated with this condition, including a high operative mortality rate of 33.0%, and outcomes varied by malperfusion site and preoperative status. In particular, we highlight the impact of LCA malperfusion on operative mortality in acute aortic dissection. Cases with LCA malperfusion exhibited greater mortality rates, with this trend becoming more pronounced as the Neri classification advanced to type B and type C. This finding suggests that the severity of coronary involvement, as defined by Neri classification, plays a crucial role in determining clinical outcomes. RCA malperfusion was linked to a lower overall mortality rate, regardless of Neri classification, indicating that its physiological impact is less critical compared with LCA malperfusion.

Through increased awareness and understanding of acute aortic dissection, along with the improvement of emergency medical systems with advancement in medical technology and devices, treatment outcomes for patients with TAAAD have significantly improved.11, 12, 13 According to a recent report, the in-hospital mortality rate for patients with TAAAD in Japan is less than 10%.13,14 However, CM increases in-hospital mortality to 20% to 40%.4, 5, 6, 7 In particular, left CM—especially when associated with AMI—often leads to critical hemodynamic instability, resulting in shock or CPA. The requirement for MCS further elevates the risk of mortality, underscoring the importance of quick and aggressive intervention including aortic repairs with initial PCI before surgery and/or concomitant CABG. Among previous studies, it was suggested that preoperative PCI strategy can effectively address coronary ischemia caused by TAAAD and improved the following surgical outcome.3,7, 8, 9 However, because of the lower incidence of CM complicated in TAAAD, the decision to prioritize initial PCI before surgery versus straightforward surgical correction with concomitant CABG remains a matter of debate. Regarding coronary intervention strategies, our analysis revealed no difference in mortality between preoperative PCI and CABG. However, patients who underwent both preoperative PCI and CABG had worse prognoses. This indicates aggressive revascularization strategies involving both interventions may reflect a more severe preoperative status, and that some cases may not achieve sufficient therapeutic effect with PCI alone, with prognosis potentially not improving even with the addition of CABG. CABG was sometimes added after PCI for myocardial protection. PCI to the left main trunk was occasionally deemed insufficient or unstable, prompting the addition of CABG to ensure reliable flow to the LCA territory. These cases likely reflect more severe coronary involvement and underscore the complexity of individualized surgical decision-making.

As shown in Figure 2, B, some patients with CM did not undergo either PCI or CABG yet demonstrated favorable outcomes. This may be explained by the predominance of Neri type A lesions in this group, where myocardial ischemia might have improved with central false lumen sealing alone. surgical adhesives are used to close the proximal false lumen, and in some cases, root replacement alone may suffice to restore coronary perfusion without additional coronary intervention.

As reported in previous studies, Neri type B and C lesions linked to significantly greater mortality, underscoring the impact of anatomical severity on clinical outcomes.15 Our analysis confirmed this trend for LCA malperfusion, but not in RCA malperfusion. Although root replacement was more performed in Neri type C cases, it was not universally applied. Despite type C indicating complete coronary detachment—typically warranting root reconstruction—its use may have been limited by factors such as advanced age, poor general condition prompting less-invasive strategies, localized dissection allowing partial repair, or the emergent nature of the setting favoring time-saving procedures like CABG. Among patients who underwent CABG, operative mortality did not significantly differ across Neri types. This indicates once surgical revascularization is achieved, clinical outcomes may improve regardless of the anatomical classification of coronary involvement. Nevertheless, postoperative prognosis likely remains influenced by preoperative factors like the need for cardiopulmonary resuscitation and/or MCS.

This is supported by the subgroup analysis of surgical procedures. Although the mortality rate was lower in the TAR group compared with the non-TAR group (25.8% vs 41.1%), this difference does not necessarily imply an effect of the procedure itself. It is more likely that patients selected for TAR had more stable preoperative conditions, making them suitable candidates for extensive arch repair. non-TAR procedures may have been chosen for critically ill patients requiring rapid, less invasive interventions, like those presenting with preop shock or CPA. Similarly, root replacement was linked to higher mortality (38.3% with root replacement vs 31.4% without), likely reflecting its indication in patients with severe proximal aortic pathology, like root dissection, coronary ostial disruption, or root rupture. These patients often presented with AMI or required MCS, both of which increase surgical risk. These findings show the impact of preoperative status and support the concept of confounding by indication, wherein the choice of surgical strategy reflects underlying disease severity rather than directly determining outcomes.

In addition to high operative mortality, our cohort exhibited a incidence of major postop complications, including persistent coma (13.3%) and acute kidney injury (40.4%), with 20.2% of patients requiring new-onset dialysis. These findings reflect the combined impact of critical status and surgical complexity. Nearly one half of the patients underwent TAR and/or root replacement—procedures that involve prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass and circulatory arrest times. The median cardiopulmonary bypass and cardiac arrest durations in our cohort were long, at 264 minutes and 152.5 minutes, respectively. These values were notably longer than those reported in the nationwide analysis by Ogino and colleagues,13 suggesting that more complex surgical procedures may have been required in our series. These extended operative times, in conjunction with preoperative shock, CPA, and MCS, likely contributed to risk of reperfusion injury and organ failure. Thus, while aggressive aortic repair may be necessary to address extensive disease in this high-risk population, it may also predispose patients to adverse postoperative outcomes.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified preoperative AMI, CPA, and the need for preop MCS as independent predictors of mortality. These findings show the predominant influence of preop physiological instability on patient outcomes, outweighing anatomical characteristics or intervention strategies. Variables like the site of CM, Neri classification, and preoperative PCI or concomitant CABG were not independently associated with mortality in the adjusted model. This indicates although anatomical severity and procedural choices contribute to risk, the baseline clinical condition of the patient exerts a more decisive impact on early postop outcomes.

In cases involving PCI, delays in door-to-coronary reperfusion time for patients undergoing emergency PCI for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction are strongly associated with increased in-hospital mortality.16 The median time from symptom onset to coronary reperfusion, recorded as 361.5 minutes, underscores the critical need to streamline diagnostic and referral pathways to expedite treatment. Despite efforts to reduce delays in coronary reperfusion, the mortality rate of TAAAD complicated by CM appears to be greater in Japan compared with international studies.4, 5, 6, 7,17 According to a previous study by Ogino and colleagues,13 the median time intervals from symptom onset to hospital arrival and from hospital arrival to surgical treatment were 164 minutes and 136 minutes, respectively. Most patients arrived within 6 hours of symptom onset. This efficiency could be attributed to Japan's advanced emergency medical system, supported by the universal health insurance system and the country's small geographic area. These factors ensure that even cases likely to result in prehospital death in other countries can reach the hospital and undergo surgery. Consequently, the rates of CPA and shock were as high as 16.5% and 51.1%, respectively, indicating the inclusion of patients in poor preoperative condition, which may partially explain the greater mortality rate. In fact, cases requiring early reperfusion had poor prognoses, both in terms of time intervals and mortality. This indicates factors like anatomical considerations, as represented by the Neri classification, and the state of aortic dissection indicate that simply shortening the time to reperfusion is insufficient to improve outcomes. Time to reperfusion is only one prognostic factor. Improving outcomes requires individualized assessment and planning.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, it is a retrospective analysis based on registry data from the JCVSD, which may introduce selection and reporting biases. The incidence of CM (2.0%) and overall malperfusion (∼15%) in our cohort is lower than reported in international registries like the International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection, Nordic Consortium for Acute Type A Aortic Dissection, and University of Pennsylvania, where CM occurs in 10% to 15% of TAAAD cases. However, a previous Japanese study by Ogino and colleagues13 similarly reported a CM incidence of 1.6%, suggesting that this lower rate may reflect diagnostic and reporting practices specific to Japan.10 Second, the JCVSD includes only patients with surgically treated TAAAD, excluding those managed medically or those who died before surgery. Patients with severe CM and hemodynamic collapse may be underrepresented. Third, the diagnosis of CM in the JCVSD is determined on the basis of clinical, electrocardiographic, echocardiographic, and intraoperative findings rather than angiographic confirmation, and entry is not centrally adjudicated. This likely contributes to interinstitutional variability and potential underreporting. Fourth, entry of the supplemental variables specific to this analysis was optional. One hundred sixteen CM cases were excluded because of missing data, with greater mortality (41.4%) than the analyzed cohort. It suggests selection bias, whereby more severely ill patients—those with rapid deterioration—may have been underrepresented because of incomplete entry during emergency care. Finally, despite the inclusion of 255 variables in the JCVSD, important procedural details—like the precise anatomical extent of dissection, the rationale for choosing PCI or CABG, and the timing of interventions—were not available. Prospective studies using standardized diagnostic criteria and centralized data validation are needed to improve the accuracy and comparability of CM data in this high-risk population.

Conclusions

Although TAAAD with CM is rare, it has a high surgical mortality rate in cases with LCA malperfusion, where mortality increases progressively with greater Neri classification (types B and C). However, early treatment does not necessarily guarantee a favorable outcome, because results are heavily influenced by the patient's preoperative condition, including AMI, CPA, and the need for MCS. Both initial PCI before aortic repair and intraoperative concomitant CABG are shown to be viable options, with no differences in outcomes. Treatment decisions should therefore be tailored to the patient's anatomical findings and physiological status, as well as the surgical expertise available at each institution.

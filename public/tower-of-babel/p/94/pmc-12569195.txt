Strategies modulating the level of harmful nitrogen compounds in edible potato tubers depending on the evaluation date
Authors: Jarosław Pobereżny, Elżbieta Wszelaczyńska, Katarzyna Gościnna, Małgorzata Szczepanek, Magdalena Tomaszewska-Sowa, Cezary Trawczyński, Milena Pietraszko, Dominika Boguszewska-Mańkowska, Katarzyna Brążkiewicz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to its high daily consumption, the potato is classified as an important vegetable of the human diet. Its food safety is determined, among other things, by the content of nitrates and nitrites in the tubers. The aim of this study was to comprehensively analyze the effect of genotype, cultivation and storage technology on the concentration of nitrates and nitrites in the tubers of edible potato varieties (Gardena, Denar). Potatoes were grown using integrated and organic technology at different locations. Various variants of fertilization, protection against fungal pathogens and plant stress protection were applied. The content of nitrates and nitrites was evaluated immediately after harvest and after 6-month storage under constant conditions of temperature and relative humidity consistent with the direction of use. The content of the tested compounds was influenced by all factors of the experiment. Significant differences were found between the tested varieties. The Gardena variety was characterized by a lower propensity for nitrate accumulation compared to the Denar variety, with an average of 82.3 and 85.4 mg kg−1 f. m., respectively. Due to more favorable weather conditions in 2020, the concentration of nitrates and nitrites was at a lower level compared to 2021 (69.2; 0.56 and 98.5; 0.75 mg kg−1 f. m., respectively). Of the technologies used, organic cultivation proved to be the most favorable, for which location turned out to be important. The use of protection against fungal diseases and the intervention irrigation treatment contributed to a decrease in the content of the tested compounds. Long-term storage reduced the content of nitrates and nitrites by an average of 2.7% and 7.5%, respectively. It was shown that the effect of experimental factors on the content of the tested nitrogen compounds after storage was the same as after harvest. Regardless of the experimental factors, the daily intake standards for nitrate and nitrite were not exceeded.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-21489-2.

Introduction

Nitrates and nitrites are among the most studied pollutants1,2. The main sources of these compounds are soil, water, and food1,3,4. The harmful effects of these compounds on human health are associated with the occurrence of a disease called methemoglobinemia under their influence. They also play a direct part in the endogenous formation of carcinogenic N-nitroso compounds5,6. Among plant raw materials, vegetables are the main source of the origin of these compounds. They provide 85.0 to 90.0% of the total permissible amount of nitrates in the daily diet, of which, due to their high consumption, about 30.0% is accounted for by potatoes7–9. However, it should be remembered that the potato is recognized as a crop with high food security potential3,10. Nitrate content in tubers is highly variable and depends on the variety. Strict standards have been introduced for the level of nitrate content in fresh potatoes, as well as standards for the permissible amount of daily intake of nitrate and nitrite11. According to Commission Regulation (EC) No. 2023/915 of 25 April 2023, the nitrates content of vegetables should not exceed 200 mg kg−1 in children’s nutrition12. For potatoes, the standard is 200 mg kg−1 f. m. of tubers. In contrast, the acceptable daily intake (ADI) is determined by FAO/WHO guidelines. For nitrates, the maximum daily intake is set at 222.0 mg per 60 kg of body weight and for nitrites at 4.2 mg per 60 kg of body weight of human4,13.

The nitrate content of crops is mainly controlled genetically (variety), but can be modified by the agronomic treatments used (fertilization, irrigation, etc.), soil type and climatic conditions7,14. In order to achieve a significant increase in worldwide food production, nitrates are used as fertilizer in agriculture. In this context, special attention is paid to crops that play an important role in human and animal nutrition. Such a crop is the potato, which, due to its significant share in the daily diet, is an important link in the structure of food consumption11,15,16.

In recent years, there has been a significant increase in consumer awareness, in terms of “healthy” food, free of harmful ingredients. In its production, attention is mainly paid to environmental protection1,4,17 due to its significant degradation in the last decade18,19. It is therefore important to introduce such solutions that will improve productivity while increasing the quality of raw materials with rational use of natural resources and respect for the environment1,4,17. This is the reason for exploring the possibility of changes towards the introduction of simplifications in cultivation or the use of various types of biostimulants, immune stimulators, bacterial vaccines, algae extracts, microorganisms or biological soil fertilizers17,20,21.

The climate changes that have occurred in recent years indicate that it is crucial in the cultivation of economically important crops to introduce solutions that will improve their productivity while increasing their quality. This can be achieved by growing crops with rational use of natural resources and respect for the environment4,17,20.

The purpose of this article is to determine the content of nitrates and nitrites in potato tubers depending on genetic, agronomic and environmental factors that can modulate their levels. In addition, the goal is to identify the most favorable solutions in potato production that will contribute to reducing the content of nitrates and nitrites in tubers.

Results and discussion

Influence of genotype and environment

The tubers of the tested varieties were characterized by a low tendency to accumulate nitrates, generally below 150.0 mg kg−1 f. m. (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). Edible potatoes should contain the lowest possible amount of nitrates and nitrites. In a study by Zarzecka et al.22, the nitrate content of tubers of eight edible varieties from market sales averaged between 152.1 and 170.1 mg kg−1 f. m. In contrast, as reported by Simson et al.23 and Ebrahimi et al.24, the nitrate content of conventionally grown edible potatoes ranges from 31.0 to 460.0 mg kg−1 f. m. and 35.6 to 163.0 mg kg−1 f. m., respectively. Wszelaczyńska et al.4 conducting integrated and organic cultivation at different locations on seven clones and three varieties obtained nitrate contents averaging from 133.6 to 203.0 mg kg−1 f. m. Nauman et al.25after cultivating five potato varieties with a conventional system at different locations determined nitrate contents ranging from 48.0 to 137.0 mg kg−1 f. m.

Our research also confirmed the general thesis that the accumulation of nitrates and nitrites in potatoes is closely related to the genotype4,21,25, as the tested varieties significantly differed in their content.

Regardless of the year of the study, the location and the potato cultivation technology used, the Denar variety showed a higher tendency to accumulate nitrates and nitrites. On average, the Denar variety contained in the fresh mass of tubers: 85.4 nitrates and 0.71 nitrites, compared to ‘Gardena’ (82.3 and 0.61 mg kg−1) (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). This may be due to the length of the potato’s growing season4,26. Trawczyński27, studying 21 potato varieties of different earliness groups, reports that varieties with a shorter growing season generally accumulate more nitrates. In addition, it was shown (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6) that the tubers of both Gardena and Denar varieties in 2021 were characterized by higher concentrations of nitrates and nitrites, compared to 2020 in which more favorable weather conditions were recorded. The nitrate and nitrite contents were 98.5 and 0.75 in 2021 and 69.2 and 0.56 mg kg−1 f. m. in 2020, respectively. This is in line with the results of Kołodziejczyk and Gwóźdź28, in which the lowest nitrate accumulation was found in edible potato tubers produced under more favorable weather conditions. This indicates that the concentration of nitrates and nitrites depends not only on the potato genotype but also on meteorological conditions. Trawczyński27, Zarzecka et al.21, Kołodziejczyk and Gwóźdź28 report that the magnitude and distribution of precipitation and air temperatures are the main factors affecting plant metabolism by which they determine the magnitude of nitrogen compound transformations in potatoes.

In addition, differences in the content of nitrates and nitrites in tubers, regardless of the variety, year of study and cultivation technology, were shown depending on the location of the experiments. The highest nitrate content was found in potatoes from the Jadwisin locality (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5) at an average of 130.7 mg kg−1 f. m. after cultivation with the integrated system (three field experiments), compared to the Luchowo locality at 62.3 and Tytlewo at 41.6 mg kg−1 f. m., where potatoes were grown with the organic system (Table 6). The effect of cultivation system on nitrate content of tubers of different potato varieties was reported by authors4,14,29,30. This was also confirmed by the results obtained by other authors, in whose studies potato cultivation was carried out with the conventional system31,32. This is determined by the use of high doses of nitrogen fertilizers in both conventional and integrated systems. However, Hamouz et al.33 showed no significant effect of cultivation technology (organic and conventional) on nitrate content in tubers of eight potato varieties. It should be noted, however, that they observed a tendency toward higher nitrate content in the conventional system (153.9 mg kg −1 f. m.), compared to the organic system (136.9 mg kg−1 f. m.). What should be emphasized is that in our study such a relationship was obtained only in the nitrite ratio.

Regardless of the cultivation system used, a reduction in nitrate content (Figs. 1,3–5) and a significant reduction in nitrite content (Figs. 2, 3, 4 and 5) were found after long-term storage. For the Gardena variety, the values were 3.1% for nitrates and 7.6% for nitrites, while for the Denar variety they were 2.2 and 7.4%, respectively (average values for the years of the study).

The reduction in nitrate content after storage under controlled conditions, in tubers from the crop in Jadwisin (integrated cultivation - Figs. 1, 3 and 4) was 3.3%, from the crop in Tytlewo (organic cultivation - Fig. 5) − 2.6% and from the crop in Luchowo (organic cultivation - Fig. 5) − 2.2%. In contrast, the reduction in nitrite content was 6.3, 7.3 and 11.8%, respectively (Figs. 2, 3, 4 and 5). The decrease in the content of harmful nitrogen compound after storage was confirmed by the obtained results of other authors20,24,25. Typically, high nitrite content in potatoes is associated with the activity of nitrate-reducing bacteria, which leads to the reduction of nitrate to nitrite during storage31,34.

Since the Gardena and Denar varieties belong to the group of edible varieties, the tubers were stored under conditions consistent with their direction of use, i.e. at 4 °C and RH 95%. Many authors25,36 state that the most important factors determining the chemical composition of potato tubers are temperature, relative humidity and storage time. Pobereżny et al.20 and Wszelaczyńska et al.4studying edible varieties obtained a significant decrease in nitrate content after storing tubers at 4 °C for 6 months. In the study of Naumann et al.25, the decrease in nitrate content in tubers after 6 months storage averaged 15.0%. However, the authors25 point out that the period of three months of storage had no significant effect on their concentration.

Influence of cultivation technology

Integrated cultivation of potatoes

In potato tubers from conventional cultivation, it is often observed that the permissible standard for nitrate content is exceeded or overstated24,29,35. In our study (Table 1), each of the applied nitrogen doses significantly increased the nitrate and nitrite content of the tubers of the tested varieties in both the first and second years of the study. Increasing doses of mineral nitrogen caused a systematic increase in the content of nitrates and nitrites compared to the object where no nitrogen was applied. It was found that the application of nitrogen at the highest dose of 50 kg N ha−1 increased the content of nitrates by 71.2% and nitrite, by as much as 149.0% compared to the control (Table 1). It was noted that despite such a drastic increase in nitrates and nitrites in the tubers, the permissible nitrate content set by the Commission Regulation (EC) No. 2023/915 of 25 April 202312 at 200 mg NO3ˉ kg−1 of tuber weight and the designated daily intake standard for nitrite were not exceeded4,13. This also confirms that varieties recommended for organic cultivation (which are determined by genotype) generally accumulate lower amounts of nitrates and nitrites in tubers. The adverse effect of using high nitrogen doses on nitrate accumulation in potato is also reported by other authors7,17,29,33. Colla et al.7report that an excessive supply of N during cultivation leads to a plant’s uptake of nitrates beyond its assimilative capacity, which leads to their accumulation in its tissues. In turn, Nurmanov et al.37 note that the nitrogen content of the soil should be taken into account when determining the doses of mineral nitrogen fertilization of the potato. Excessive nitrogen fertilization of the potato results in the uptake of excessive nitrates by the plants, which are not fully metabolized and accumulate in the tubers38.

In addition, significant interactions between variety and fertilization level were found with regard to nitrates and nitrites content. However, it should be noted that a significant effect of this interaction on nitrates content was only observed in 2021, while for nitrites it was observed in 2020. The differences observed between the years of the study suggest that the interactions between variety and nitrogen fertilization level are not constant but depend on the weather conditions prevailing in a given growing season. In 2021, rainfall distribution was unfavorable, especially during the period of intensive tuber growth. This may have contributed to a reduction in the plants’ ability to metabolize nitrates, leading to their greater accumulation. In contrast, in 2020, more favorable moisture conditions may have promoted the conversion of nitrates to nitrites or further metabolic pathways. These results confirm the significant influence of meteorological conditions, especially water availability, on the dynamics of nitrogen compound accumulation in potato tubers4,27. Furthermore, Pobereżny et al.39point out that variable weather conditions have a significant impact on nitrates accumulation in potato tubers, especially in conventional cultivation.

After long-term storage of the tubers, there was an average decrease of 3.1% in nitrate content, with the nitrate content decreasing by 3.5% in 2020 and by 2.7% in 2021 (Fig. 1). In contrast, the decrease in nitrite content (Fig. 2) was higher, as it was at 6.5% after 6 months of storage. In addition, it was shown that the effect of applying mineral nitrogen fertilization during the potato growing period on the content of nitrates and nitrites after storage was the same as after harvest. Also after storage, the highest nitrate contents were accumulated by tubers fertilized with the maximum dose of N, which is consistent with the results obtained by Gislason et al.40, Pobereżny et al.20.

Use of protection against fungal diseases

It was shown that the method of applied protection against pathogens had a significant effect on the concentration of nitrates and nitrites in the tubers of the tested varieties immediately after harvest (Table 2) which is consistent with the results of other authors Kołodziejczyk41; Pobereżny et al.20. Importantly, in the current study, each of the applied variants of protection influenced the reduction of nitrate and nitrite content in the fresh weight of tubers. Full protection was the most effective treatment, as the reduction in nitrate as well as nitrite relative to the control averaged 18.9% over the years. While the tuber treatment and protection in the form of a single fungicide application resulted in nitrate reductions averaging 4.8% and nitrite reductions of 4.5%, respectively. The reduction in nitrogen compound content after the application of tuber treatment, fungicide and full protection can be explained by the effectiveness of pathogen protection in reducing biotic stress, which can disrupt nitrogen metabolism in the plant. Under conditions of stress caused by plant infection, the activity of enzymes involved in nitrate reduction (e.g., nitrate reductase) is reduced, which promotes their accumulation in tissues. In addition, healthy plants have a greater capacity to assimilate mineral nitrogen, which reduces the risk of nitrate and nitrite accumulation in tubers20. Thus, full protection ensuring effective reduction of pathogen pressure enabled the most efficient use of nitrogen, which translated into the lowest level of its residues in tubers.The opposite results were obtained by Kołodziejczyk41. In his study, tuber treatment with simultaneous fungicide protection during the potato growing season resulted in an increase in nitrate content, which was, however, non-significant (p < 0.05).

Our study also showed that after long-term storage there was a decrease in nitrate and nitrite content at an average of 1.8% and 5.2%, respectively (Fig. 3). This is consistent with the results of other authors according to which during storage there is a decrease in the content of nitrate and nitrates in tubers25,39. The highest loss of nitrates after storage (3.2%) was obtained in tubers from the facility where full protection with tuber treatment was applied. It was also shown that nitrite losses after storage were at a comparable level for each variant of protection applied during the growing season and ranged from 4.0 to 4.2%. However, it was noted that the highest losses of nitrite after storage (8.5%) were obtained in potatoes from the facility where no protection treatments were applied. This indicates that the magnitude of nitrite losses after long-term storage depends to a greater extent on storage conditions than on the variant of protection applied. Maintaining constant conditions throughout the storage period contributes to the reduction of biochemical transformations in tubers31,34.

Use of treatments protecting plants from stress

Of the stress prevention treatments applied, the use of intervention irrigation was the most effective (Tables 3, 4 and 5). Irrespective of the variety, irrigation helped reduce nitrate content by 13.9% and nitrite content by 17.2% in fresh tuber weight. This effect can be explained by the fact that the right level of soil moisture promotes the proper uptake and conversion of nitrates in plant structures. Under conditions of water shortage, nitrate reductase activity is reduced, leading to the accumulation of nitrates in tissues. Therefore, irrigation may have promoted more efficient metabolism of nitrogen compounds, limiting their accumulation in tubers. Wierzbicka42, conducting an experiment with irrigation of 8 edible potato varieties, achieved a reduction in nitrate content by an average of 18.1%. In our study (Tables 3, 4 and 5), the combined application of irrigation treatment with stress protection in the form of applied hydrogel and Bio-algeen S90 biostimulant was not as effective as the application of irrigation alone (control), as nitrate reduction averaged 10.0%. It was also shown that for the nitrate content in the tubers (Table 5), the application of irrigation in combination with hydrogel was more effective compared to the treatment of combined application of irrigation with biostimulant. This is because a reduction of nitrates in tubers by 10.3% and 9.5%, respectively, was achieved. In contrast, the concentration of nitrite (Table 5) in tubers depended inversely, as greater reductions in nitrite content were obtained after the combined application of irrigation with the biostimulant. For sustainable potato (Solanum tuberosum L) production, irrigation and nitrogen management are crucial43,44. In contrast, Clément et al.45 report that under irrigated conditions, nitrate leaching outside the plant root zone increases with increasing nitrogen fertilizer rates compared to non-irrigated potatoes.

Among the anti-stress factors used, the interaction of the variety with hydrogel and with bioalgae had a significant impact on the nitrates and nitrites content in the tubers (Table 4). It was noted that the interaction had a significant effect on the nitrates content in 2020 and on the nitrites content in 2021. The observed significant impact of the interaction between the variety and hydrogel and bioalgae on the nitrates and nitrites content indicates that the effectiveness of anti-stress factors may be strongly dependent on environmental conditions and the genetic predisposition of individual varieties. In 2020, which was characterized by more favorable weather conditions, the use of hydrogel and bioalgae could support the proper course of nitrogen metabolism in the plant, limiting the excessive accumulation of nitrates in the tubers. In contrast, in 2021, which saw less favorable weather conditions, plants were more exposed to environmental stress. Under such conditions, the synergistic effect of the variety and the anti-stress preparations used may have played a greater role in limiting the effects of metabolic disorders by influencing the conversion of nitrates to nitrites. The higher nitrites content in 2021 may therefore be an indirect result of the limited activity of the enzymes responsible for the further decomposition of nitrites. Thus, the differences between years confirm that the impact of anti-stress factors is not uniform, but modulated by weather conditions, and their effectiveness in reducing the content of nitrogen compounds in tubers may be greatest under stressful conditions – provided that they are appropriately selected for a specific potato variety4,27,39.

In an experiment (Fig. 4) in which irrigation and anti-stress preparations were applied during cultivation, the nitrate and nitrite contents of tubers after storage were reduced by an average of 3.3% and 8.8%, respectively. This is comparable to the results obtained by Bohman et al.46; Kandil et al.47. It was noted that the nitrite reduction in 2021 was at a much higher level of 9.9% compared to 2020 − 7.7% and the effect of anti-stress factors was the same as after harvest. This indicates that the potatoes in our study (Figs. 3–5) were stored under constant conditions throughout the period in accordance with the designated standard for the edible potato. According to Kazimierczak et al.31, the concentration of nitrites in potato tubers after long-term storage depends to the greatest extent on maintaining constant conditions in the storage room.

Organic cultivation

It was shown that after organic cultivation (Table 6) the content of nitrates as well as nitrites in tubers of the tested varieties significantly depended on the location in which the potatoes were grown. At the same time, both in the first and second year of the study, tubers of varieties grown in Tytlewo contained favorably less nitrates and nitrites compared to those from Luchowo. It was noted that the variety significantly affected the concentration of nitrates in tubers only in the second year of the study, i.e. 2021 (Table 6). This indicates that nitrate content in tubers is more influenced by environmental conditions. There was also a significant beneficial effect of the stress-reducing preparations used in the cultivation on the concentration of nitrates and nitrites in the tubers (Table 6). In this regard, the greater influence was exerted by the Bio-algeen S90 formulation after application of which a reduction in nitrate content by an average of 12.2% was obtained. While the application of copper fungicide reduced the concentration of nitrates by 9.5%. The positive effect of biostimulant preparations on nitrate concentrations in edible potatoes is reported by Kolodziejczyk and Gwóźdź28 and Zarzecka et al.21. In terms of nitrite content, the effect of the preparations was the opposite. After application of the fungicide, nitrite content decreased by an average of 8.9%, while the biostimulant reduced its concentration by an average of 7.6%. In organic cultivation, potatoes are more exposed to nutrient stress, while fungal disease pressure is more dependent on meteorological conditions (precipitation)4,31. After long-term storage (Fig. 5) under constant conditions (temp. 4 °C and RH 95%), regardless of the year of the study, a slight decrease in nitrate content by an average of 2.7% was obtained, while the decrease in nitrite content was as much as 5.7%. According to Nauman et al.25, nitrate concentrations in potato tubers decrease after storage and the magnitude of these changes depends largely on storage time. On the other hand, Hamedani34 and Kazimierczak et al.31 note that the concentration of nitrite in potato tubers is most influenced by storage conditions. It was pointed out (Table 6) that in 2020 there was no effect of the interaction between variety and location for nitrate content and in 2021 this interaction had no effect on nitrite content. It can be assumed that the content of these harmful and toxic compounds in tubers depends mainly on genetic conditions4,23,24 and nitrogen fertilization7,17,29, which is limited in organic cultivation.

Consumption

Potato, due to its high per capita consumption, is classified as the vegetable that contributes most to daily nitrate intake20,24. In Poland, potato consumption has steadily declined from 100 kg in 2015 to 94.7 kg in 202348. Supplement Material presents the results of nitrate and nitrite intake of the tested potatoes, assuming that on average 260 g of potatoes are eaten per day per person. Analyzing the effect of cultivation system on the intake of nitrates and nitrites showed that the lowest intake of these compounds was after the consumption of organically grown potatoes (Table S1 in the Supplementary Information). This intake averaged 13.9 mg day−1 (after harvest) and 13.5 mg day−1 (after storage) for nitrate and 0.10 and 0.09 mg day−1 for nitrite, respectively. The effect of cultivation technology on the intake of nitrates and nitrites is reported by Ebrahimi et al.24, Hamedani34, Pobereżny et al.20, Zarzecka et al.21. In addition, it was shown that regardless of the cultivation technology used, the intake of nitrates and nitrites was lower after consumption of tubers of the Gardena variety (Table S1-S4 in the Supplementary Information). Among the applied fungal disease protection and plant stress mitigation treatments, in the conventional system, the application of full protection (Table S3 in the Supplementary Information) and an intervention irrigation treatment including hydrogel were the most favorable for nitrate consumption (Table S4 in the Supplementary Information). Daily nitrate intake was: 27.4 mg day−1 (after harvest) and 26.9 (after storage), and 21.5 (after harvest) and 20.4 (after storage), respectively. On the other hand, for nitrite, the use of full protection and irrigation with a biostimulant proved to be the most favorable. With these combinations, the estimated nitrite intake was 0.19 mg day−1 (after harvest) and 0.18 mg day−1 (after storage), and 0.20 mg day−1 (after harvest) and 0.18 mg day−1 (after storage). Our study showed that, regardless of the production system used (Table S1-S4 in the Supplementary Information), the set daily intake standards for both nitrate and nitrite were not exceeded. However, it should be noted that the calculations were made for raw potatoes. Pre-treatment (peeling and washing) and tuber processing processes contribute to an additional significant reduction in the content of nitrates and nitrites24,25. Nauman et al.25investigating the effect of the cooking process on the nitrate content of unpeeled and peeled tubers obtained reductions in nitrate content of 7.0% and 38.0%, respectively. In contrast, in a study by Ebrahimi et al.24 the cooking process reduced nitrate content by as much as 60.0%.

Conclusions

Of the cultivation systems used in the experiment in relation to the content of nitrates and nitrites in edible potatoes, organic cultivation proved to be the most favorable. Thus, the research confirmed the hypothesis that potatoes grown in the organic system are characterized by a low content of nitrates and nitrites. However, of the varieties tested, the lowest content of nitrates and nitrites was characterized by the Gardena variety. This indicates the validity of the research undertaken, as it showed that the key factor in successful organic potato production is the selection of the right genotype. For the Gardena variety is characterized by a specialized crop profile dedicated to organic farming. In addition, it has been shown that in the case of organic crops, location is important for the content of nitrates and nitrites. It should be noted that currently organic crops are mainly based on potato varieties developed for conventional agriculture, which can lead to unfavorable chemical composition of their tubers. Of the plant stress protection methods used in the study, in the conventional system, emergency irrigation proved to be the most effective treatment for reducing nitrate content, which was 12.2%. On the other hand, for nitrite content, irrigation in combination with a biostimulant proved to be the most effective, at 27.6%.

The long-term storage period reduced the nitrate and nitrite contents of the tubers of the tested varieties by an average of 2.7% and 7.5%, respectively. It was shown that the effect of experimental factors on the content of nitrates and nitrites after storage was the same as after harvest. So, potatoes that had the most favorable concentration of nitrates and nitrites (lowest content) after harvest also contained the least of them after storage.

Field tests as well as soil and plant sampling were conducted in 2020 and 2021. The subjects of the study were two edible potato varieties: ‘Denar’ and ‘Gardena’ grown in conventional and organic systems. Before planting potatoes, potassium fertilization was applied at a rate of 100 kg K2O ha−1 (Patentkali − 30% K2O, 10% MgO, 42% SO3). To protect the plantation from insects, Spin Tor was applied at a rate of 0.15 m3 ha−1.

Potatoes were planted at the end of April with a mechanical planter. The experiment was set up in plots with a row spacing of 0.675 × 0.35 m. The 32.4 m2 was used for harvesting. The forecrop was cereals. In the fall, manure was applied at 25,000 kg ha−1.

In Luchowo, the soil was characterized by low levels of assimilable form of phosphorus and medium levels of potassium and magnesium (Table 7). Tytlewo showed a higher content of assimilable forms of the components in the soil than in Luchowo. The content of magnesium in the soil was high, while that of phosphorus and potassium was medium. The soil pH was slightly acidic at both locations, but the soil in Tytlewo was less acidic (Table 7).

In Luchowo, both years of the study had near-normal precipitation and were warm. The greatest differences between years in relation to precipitation were recorded in this locality in June, when a significant excess was recorded in 2020, while a deficiency was recorded in 2021 (Table 8). In Tytlewo, an excess of precipitation with higher-than-normal air temperatures occurred in 2020, which was found in most growing months (June, July, August, September). In 2021, there was a precipitation deficit, especially in April, June and September, and, as in 2020, high air temperatures (Table 9).

Laboratory tests were carried out immediately after harvesting the potatoes and after long-term storage of the tubers (6 months) at the Food Processing Laboratory, Faculty of Agriculture and Biotechnology, Bydgoszcz University of Science and Technology. Tubers were sampled for laboratory and storage tests when they reached full physiological maturity. The 10 kg samples were stored in storage chambers, where constant conditions were maintained throughout the period: temperature + 4 °C; relative humidity 95% - according to the direction of potato use, i.e. direct consumption49.

Cleaned, washed and dried potato tuber samples were crushed and subjected to sublimation drying according to the procedure of Pobereżny et al.39. The samples were then ground and stored in tightly sealed packages in the dark until analytical tests (nitrates and nitrites determination) were performed.

Procedures for the determination of nitrates and nitrites in freeze-dried potato tubers were carried out according to Pobereżny et al.39. A CX-721 multifunctional computerized apparatus (Elmetron, Zabrze, Poland) was used for the determination of nitrates and nitrites content by the ion-selective potentiometric method51. Laboratory tests were performed in duplicate.

Statistical calculations

The results obtained for each year of the study were statistically analyzed using Statistica 13.1 software (StatSoft, Tulsa, OK, USA).

The primary statistical descriptors were mean values and standard deviation (± SD). Mean values in each group were subjected to two-factor (split - plot) or three-factor (split - split - plot) statistical analysis depending on the type of experiment. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed using Tukey’s multiple confidence intervals for a significance level of α = 0.05. Tables and categorized 3 W diagrams were used to visualize the data, allowing presentation of selected subsets. Based on Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients at a significance level of p = 0.05, relationships between the qualitative characteristics studied were determined.

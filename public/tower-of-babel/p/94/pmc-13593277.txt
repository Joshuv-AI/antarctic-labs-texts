Efficacy and safety of traditional Chinese medicine combined with immunomodulators in treating erosive oral lichen planus: A network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Yue Li, Xiaowei Guo, Linlin Deng, Chunzhi Chen, Fengyi Lv, Juan Gao, Xiangyu Zou, Lin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:This study aimed to systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of various Chinese herbal medicines combined with immunomodulators (IMM) in the treatment of erosive oral lichen planus (EOLP) through a network meta-analysis.Methods:A total of 21 randomized controlled trials involving 1762 patients were included. Relevant randomized controlled trials were retrieved from 9 major electronic databases. All data were analyzed using a random-effects model. The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) was employed to rank the treatment regimens based on efficacy and clinical significance. To validate the robustness of the results, publication bias was assessed using funnel plots, and inconsistency tests were performed to compare direct and indirect effects. League tables were used to visualize comparative effects between treatments. Contribution plots were generated to evaluate the influence of individual treatments on the overall network effects. Statistical analyses were conducted using STATA 16.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station), and study quality was assessed with Review Manager 5.4 (The Cochrane Collaboration, London, UK).Results:The combination of Kouyanqing granules and IMM (KYQ+IMM) demonstrated the highest efficacy in terms of response rate (SUCRA 82.7%) and pain relief (SUCRA 90.8%). Compound Danshen preparation combined with IMM (CDF+IMM) and Jiedu Yukou decoction combined with IMM (JDYK+IMM) ranked second and third, respectively. Total glucosides of peony showed the lowest recurrence risk (SUCRA 94.3%), while Kangfuxin solution combined with IMM (KFX+IMM) exhibited the most significant improvement in erosion area (SUCRA 86.4%). All treatment regimens were well-tolerated, with a low incidence of adverse events.Conclusion:This study demonstrates that integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine regimens exhibit superior efficacy in improving response rates, pain relief, recurrence rates, and erosion healing in EOLP, with favorable safety profiles. Notably, KYQ+IMM, CDF+IMM, and KFX+IMM stand out as optimal therapeutic strategies, providing robust evidence for optimizing EOLP treatment.

1. Introduction

Oral lichen planus is a noninfectious chronic inflammatory disease mediated by T cells, primarily affecting the epithelial cells, basal layer, and lamina propria of the oral mucosa. Its clinical manifestations include non-erosive types (such as reticular, papular, plaques, and vesicles) and erosive types (accompanied by erosions or ulcers).[1,2] Epidemiological data show that the global prevalence of OLP is 1.01%, with a significantly increased risk in people over 40 years old.[3] The prevalence in the population of Shanghai, China, is 0.81%.[4] A recent cross-sectional study found that the average age of patients with OLP in the Chinese population is 49 years, with 61.4% of patients being female.[5] Erosive OLP not only presents more severe symptoms but also has a higher risk of malignant transformation. Its etiology is complex and closely related to various factors such as immune response, psychological factors, and endocrine changes.[6] Currently, Western medicine primarily treats it with corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, which are effective, but long-term use may cause adverse effects.[7] Traditional Chinese medicine, on the other hand, diagnoses and treats the disease based on syndrome differentiation, improving the patient’s constitution by combining methods to strengthen the body’s immune system and eliminate pathogenic factors, demonstrating the advantages of personalized treatment. However, standardization of treatment still needs further optimization.[8] With the global recognition of the potential of TCM, especially in the management of chronic and immune-related diseases, integrated treatment models combining TCM and Western medicine have gradually gained attention in clinical practice. TCM emphasizes holistic care and individualized treatment, focusing on immune regulation and improving the constitution, while Western medicine focuses on etiology treatment and symptom relief.[9] The complementary nature of the 2 approaches provides new treatment ideas for dealing with complex diseases, especially in the treatment of immune-mediated diseases like OLP. The integration of TCM and Western medicine helps to comprehensively control acute symptoms and promote long-term remission.[10] Globally, the integrated treatment method has gradually become routine clinical practice and has shown significant clinical effects.[10,11]

The combined approach of TCM and Western medicine has shown unique advantages in the clinical management of OLP. The strategy of internal and external treatment effectively integrates the symptomatic treatment of Western medicine and the holistic care of TCM.[12] For external treatment, local irritants (such as dental calculus and faulty restorations) are removed, and corticosteroids or other immunosuppressive drugs are applied locally to control inflammation.[13] For internal treatment, TCM formulations such as Bai Shao Total Glycosides, Compound Danshen, Kangfuxin Liquid, and Oral Yinqing Granules are used. These treatments regulate immunity, clear heat and detoxify, and strengthen the body’s defenses, effectively alleviating symptoms and reducing side effects.[14,15] In addition, psychological counseling, lifestyle improvement, and complementary therapies further enhance treatment effects, providing important directions for optimizing erosive oral lichen planus (EOLP) treatment strategies.[6]

Network meta-analysis (NMA), as an advanced analytical tool that integrates direct and indirect evidence, has been widely used to compare the relative advantages of various treatment interventions.[16] Compared to traditional meta-analysis, NMA allows for the simultaneous comparison of multiple treatment regimens and generates comprehensive pairwise comparison results, including interventions not directly studied. This method provides more reliable scientific evidence for clinical practice. In recent years, the theory of NMA has gradually improved, particularly with the publication of the PRISMA-NMA reporting guidelines in 2015, which has provided guidance for its application in studies involving integrated TCM and Western medicine, as well as multi-drug combination treatments.[17] Previous articles, such as a NMA[18] of Western medicine treatments for oral lichen planus, pointed out that traditional meta-analysis cannot simultaneously compare the effects of multiple local treatments, while NMA, by combining both direct and indirect evidence, can provide more clinically meaningful treatment rankings. Through SUCRA scoring, it was clearly shown that tacrolimus and dexamethasone are the better choices for treating oral lichen planus, while chlorhexidine and cyclosporine performed poorly, suggesting that these 2 treatments should be avoided as first-line options. Additionally, Tang et al[19] used NMA to compare the efficacy of various traditional Chinese medicine oral liquids in treating hand, foot, and mouth disease, overcoming the limitation of traditional meta-analysis, which only allows pairwise comparisons, and selecting the optimal treatment method from multiple options. SUCRA scores reflected the high efficiency of these treatments. The study showed that TCM oral liquids (such as dandelion oral liquid and Scutellaria baicalensis oral liquid) had good effects in reducing symptoms, shortening the disease course, and decreasing recurrence rates.

Therefore, this study aims to compare the clinical efficacy and safety of various TCM formulations or combined treatments for oral lichen planus. By evaluating treatment efficacy, pain Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores, recurrence rates, erosion area, erosion healing time, and adverse reaction occurrences, this study will systematically analyze the effects of different drug or combined regimens, providing scientific evidence for optimizing treatment strategies for oral lichen planus.

2. Data and methods

2.1. Inclusion criteria of this study

2.1.1. Study design

This study is based on clinical randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating the effects of various traditional Chinese medicine interventions for oral lichen planus.

2.1.2. Study subjects

Patients clinically diagnosed with oral lichen planus.

2.1.3. Interventions

The experimental group or trial group in the included literature consists of oral lichen planus patients treated with various TCM formulations (or combinations). The control group consists of patients treated with corticosteroids, tacrolimus, or other immunomodulatory drugs, or with different TCM formulations.

2.1.4. Outcome indicators

Efficacy rate, pain VAS score, recurrence rate, erosion area, erosion healing time, and occurrence of adverse reactions.

2.1.5. Exclusion criteria for this study

Nonrandomized controlled trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, retrospective studies, reviews, dissertations, and conference papers.

2.2. Literature search strategy

2.2.1. Searchers

The literature was searched by the authors of this study (second and third authors).

2.2.2. Databases

Searches were conducted across 9 major electronic databases: PUBMED, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, CNKI (China National Knowledge Infrastructure), Scopus, VIP Database, EMBASE, Wanfang Database, and Weipu Database.

2.2.4. Search time frame

From the creation date of the databases to December 2024.

2.3. Literature screening and data extraction

The literature screening and exclusion were conducted using EndNote X9 reference management software. First, 2 researchers independently removed duplicate literature and excluded nonrandomized controlled trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, retrospective studies, reviews, dissertations, and conference papers, resulting in a preliminary set of literature. Subsequently, the titles and abstracts of the remaining literature were screened to exclude studies where the experimental or control group did not include the specified interventions, or where the topic was unrelated to multiple TCM formulations or oral lichen planus. Finally, the full texts were reviewed, and further exclusions were made if the results section did not include the outcome indicators specified in this study, if data were incomplete, or if the full text could not be obtained. During the screening process, the 2 researchers independently performed the tasks, and if the results were consistent, the study was included. If there were disagreements, the first author and the 2 researchers discussed and decided whether to include the study. The discussion process involved the 3 researchers re-screening the literature according to the previous steps, progressively cross-checking different papers until there was agreement on the selected literature. However, the researchers did not intervene in each other’s screening to avoid bias. To ensure the accuracy of screening and extraction, a basic characteristics table for the included literature was designed, including the literature, total number of participants, age, interventions, control measures, and outcome indicators. The 2 researchers independently extracted the relevant data and organized them into an Excel spreadsheet for verification. If discrepancies were found during the verification process, the 2 researchers would first discuss and resolve the issues. If an agreement could not be reached, a third researcher would re-screen and verify according to the established procedure, ultimately determining the number and details of the included studies.

2.4. Quality assessment

The risk of bias for included RCTs was assessed independently by 2 researchers using the Cochrane Handbook 5.1.0. The following domains were evaluated: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessors, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, and other biases. Each item was rated as “low risk,” “uncertain risk,” or “high risk.” Discrepancies between researchers were resolved through discussion or by a third researcher’s arbitration.

2.5. Statistical analysis

This study aims to comprehensively compare the efficacy of multiple treatment regimens. All analyses were performed using a random-effects model to account for heterogeneity between studies. The efficacy of treatment regimens was evaluated using the odds ratio (OR) and mean difference (MD), with the confidence interval (CI) for the effect estimates set at 95%. The treatments were ranked using the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA value) based on the order of priority. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² value, and when the I² value was high, the random-effects model was used to ensure the robustness of the results. To assess the stability of the analysis results, publication bias analysis and inconsistency testing were conducted. The former was visualized using funnel plots, while the latter assessed the differences between direct and indirect effects. Additionally, league plots were used to display the comparative effects between treatment regimens, and contribution plots were used to assess each treatment’s contribution to the network effect, analyzing the role of different treatment regimens in the overall effect. All statistical analyses were conducted using STATA 16.0 software, and the quality of the literature was assessed using Review Manager 5.4 software. A P-value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

2.6. Ethical approval

This NMA study does not require patient consent, as it does not directly involve patients or utilize their personal information. The data used in this study were derived from published literature, which is considered reliable and authentic. Therefore, ethical review and approval were not required for this research.

3. Results

3.1. Literature search results

A total of 593 articles were retrieved from 9 electronic databases. After excluding 75 duplicate articles, 130 articles were further excluded because they were nonrandomized controlled trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, retrospective studies, reviews, theses, and conference papers. The remaining 101 articles were excluded because the experimental or control group did not include the prescribed interventions or were irrelevant to multiple TCM formulations or oral lichen planus based on their titles and abstracts. A total of 287 articles were read in full, and 266 articles were deleted because they did not contain the outcome indicators specified in this study, had incomplete data, or could not provide full-text access. Finally, 21 articles were included for the NMA in this study. See Figure 1 for details.

3.4. NMA results

3.4.1. Efficacy rate

For this outcome, 21 studies were included in the NMA. The total number of patients was 1762, with 878 patients in the experimental group and 884 patients in the control group. Eight treatments were compared directly, involving 8 different drug regimens.

The league plot shows that the following treatments performed better than the control group: (KYQ + IMM group) [OR = 3.79, 95% CI = (2.21, 5.38)], (CDF + IMM group) [OR = 3.67, 95% CI = (2.25, 5.09)], (JDYK + IMM group) [OR = 3.53, 95% CI = (2.18, 4.89)], (KFX + IMM group) [OR = 3.29, 95% CI = (2.04, 4.54)], (HYQR + IMM group) [OR = 2.99, 95% CI = (1.36, 4.62)], (TGP + IMM group) [OR = 2.26, 95% CI = (1.18, 3.34)], (IMM group) [OR = 1.81, 95% CI = (0.71, 2.91)], compared to the control group (TGP group) [OR = −3.79, 95% CI = (−5.38, −2.21)] (see Table 2). The probability ranking for the efficacy rate showed that the (KYQ + IMM group) ranked first in terms of efficacy with a SUCRA value of 82.7% (see Table 3). Additionally, the NMA plot is shown in Figure 4. The I² value, calculated from the variance–covariance matrix, was 73.48%, indicating high heterogeneity. Therefore, the use of a random-effects model is appropriate (see Table 4). To further assess the robustness of the results, publication bias analysis and inconsistency testing were conducted.

3.4.6. Adverse reaction incidence

This outcome was reported in 3 studies. Zeng Xiantao (2011) reported the following adverse reactions: experimental group: 12.50% (5/40), mainly erythema, congestion, and worsening burning pain; control group: 17.50% (7/40), including 4 cases of mucosal atrophy, 3 cases of erythema, congestion, and worsening burning pain. Liu Yanzhi (2013) reported the following adverse reactions: experimental group: 7 cases of mild adverse reactions, including local congestion and burning sensation, but symptoms were mild and required no special treatment; control group: No significant adverse reactions were reported. Qin Zhaohua (2022) reported: experimental group: 2 cases (3.33%), mainly appetite loss and gastrointestinal discomfort; control group: 8 cases (13.33%), including menstrual irregularities, gastrointestinal discomfort, and appetite loss. The incidence of adverse reactions in the experimental group was significantly lower than in the control group. The adverse reaction rates reported in the above 3 studies did not show significant statistical differences (P < .05). The remaining 18 studies did not clearly report severe adverse reactions for other drugs.

4. Discussion

This study compared the effects of various traditional Chinese medicine interventions for oral lichen planus, including a total of 21 randomized controlled trials with 1762 patients. The NMA results indicated that, for efficacy rate, the combination of Oral Yinqing Granules and immunomodulators (KYQ + IMM) performed the best, with a SUCRA value of 82.7%, ranking first. Other treatments, such as Compound Danshen Formula combined with immunomodulators (CDF + IMM) and Jiedu Yuko Decoction combined with immunomodulators (JDYK + IMM), also showed good results, ranking second and third, respectively. In terms of pain VAS score, the KYQ + IMM group performed excellently, with a SUCRA value of 90.8%, significantly outperforming other regimens. This suggests that KYQ + IMM has an advantage in both efficacy rate and pain relief, likely due to its ability to suppress inflammation and modulate immune responses.[20] According to previous literature, Oral Yinqing Granules is a TCM formulation composed mainly of Asparagus, Ophiopogon, Scrophularia, Honeysuckle, and Licorice, known for its multiple pharmacological effects. Studies have shown that Asparagus and Ophiopogon can modulate humoral immunity and enhance the body’s immune function, while Scrophularia and Honeysuckle possess potent antibacterial properties and promote the release of adrenal corticosteroids, effectively suppressing inflammation.[21,22] Licorice, with its corticosteroid-like anti-inflammatory properties, exerts widespread effects at different stages of the inflammatory response.[23] Overall, Oral Yinqing Granules has the effects of nourishing yin, clearing heat, detoxifying, and reducing swelling, making it particularly suitable for oral diseases caused by excess heat and yin deficiency, enhancing treatment effectiveness and reducing disease recurrence.[24] Previous studies have shown that combining 0.03% tacrolimus ointment with Oral Yinqing Granules significantly improves the efficacy of erosive OLP, outperforming monotherapy in both efficacy rate and symptom relief, with the combined treatment group achieving a total efficacy rate of 92.5%, much higher than the control group’s 70%.[25] Additionally, integrated TCM and Western medicine treatments have been shown to significantly reduce VAS scores and effectively relieve pain while improving the healing of lesions.[26] Another study demonstrated that Oral Yinqing Granules not only rapidly improved short-term symptoms in treating recurrent oral ulcers (including OLP) but also significantly reduced long-term recurrence rates, showing better efficacy compared to the control group.[27] These findings align with the statistical significance of this study, confirming that Oral Yinqing Granules is a safe and effective OLP treatment. However, when selecting treatment regimens, factors such as side effects, patient tolerance, and cost-effectiveness should be considered, and clinical decisions should be adjusted according to the individual patient’s condition.

Triamcinolone is widely used in wound treatment and works by inhibiting local inflammatory factors, reducing inflammation, and providing prolonged therapeutic effects.[28] However, the long injection intervals of triamcinolone may result in insufficient drug effects during the inter-injection period, thereby increasing the risk of inflammation recurrence.[29,30] To address this, combining other medications, such as anti-inflammatory drugs or local protectants, is recommended to prolong the effectiveness and stability of the treatment, further controlling inflammation at the erosion sites and promoting long-term suppression of lesions, thereby enhancing wound healing and improving the patient’s quality of life.[31] As seen in the recurrence rate analysis, TGP (Total Danshen Saponins) performed best, with a SUCRA value of 94.3%, indicating its low recurrence risk in long-term management, making it suitable for long-term maintenance treatment. TGP has a lower recurrence rate compared to TGP + IMM, and Liu Yanzhi (2013) reported that the experimental group (TGP + IMM) experienced mild adverse reactions, such as local congestion and burning sensation, which were manageable, while no significant adverse reactions were reported in the control group (TGP), further supporting the idea that the extended injection intervals of triamcinolone may contribute to insufficient therapeutic effects and increased recurrence risk during the gap period.[30] Furthermore, studies have shown that TGP can significantly reduce the production of IL-6 and TNF-α inflammatory mediators by inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby effectively controlling the inflammation of OLP.[32]

The results of this study indicate that the combination of immunosuppressants and Kangfuxin Liquid (KFX + IMM) produced the best results in terms of erosion area, with a SUCRA value of 86.4%, demonstrating its significant anti-inflammatory and reparative effects, but other treatment regimens should be considered based on overall effectiveness. This suggests that Kangfuxin Liquid combined with immunomodulators is highly effective in treating erosive OLP.[33] Reviewing the literature, Kangfuxin Liquid is an external remedy with antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-regenerating effects, which can directly act on lesions and accelerate the repair of ulcerated tissues. Compared to triamcinolone, whose injection intervals limit its effects, combining Kangfuxin Liquid with topical treatment provides sustained anti-inflammatory effects during the injection gap period.[34,35] Kangfuxin Liquid improves blood circulation, speeds up the excretion of inflammatory substances and harmful metabolites, and effectively alleviates pathological changes caused by the accumulation of inflammatory substances in erosive tissues, while also promoting blood circulation and tissue regeneration.[33] Moreover, since Kangfuxin Liquid is a natural biological solution, it causes less irritation to the skin and, when combined with triamcinolone, accelerates necrotic tissue shedding and reduces the inflammatory effects on healthy tissues, thereby significantly improving treatment outcomes.[35,36]

Finally, in terms of erosion healing time, the CDF + IMM group (Compound Danshen Formula combined with immunomodulators) had the highest SUCRA value of 83.4%. Compound Danshen Formula, composed of Tanshen and Sanqi, is known for its effects on blood circulation, anti-inflammatory, and analgesic properties. Clinical studies have shown that it effectively improves local microcirculation in the oral cavity, reduces inflammation, and promotes the repair and healing of erosive lesions in OLP.[37] Compared to traditional treatments, patients using Compound Danshen Formula showed a significant reduction in erosion healing time and better pain relief. Additionally, Compound Danshen Formula has fewer side effects and is considered highly safe.[38] Studies have also indicated that Compound Danshen Formula not only promotes the healing of erosive OLP but also improves the patient’s quality of life.[39]

Although this study provides a comprehensive assessment of various treatment regimens using NMA, there are some limitations. First, publication bias is a potential influencing factor. Although funnel plots for efficacy rate, pain VAS score, recurrence rate, erosion area, and erosion healing time show an acceptable distribution of data, some results still indicate slight publication bias, especially in areas of smaller effect sizes. Nevertheless, the inconsistency test further validated the consistency of the effects between treatment regimens. Most direct and indirect effects did not show significant differences, but some comparisons (such as A vs C and B vs C) exhibited significant effect differences, indicating some inconsistency between treatments. Despite these findings, using a random-effects model is reasonable as it effectively handles heterogeneity in the data and ensures the robustness of the results. Second, regional limitations are another constraint of this study. Most of the included studies were from China and focused on TCM applications, which may affect the generalizability and applicability of the results. Although the contribution plots show that the effects of each treatment method in the network are relatively balanced, the promotion of these results should be cautious, especially in Western countries or other cultural contexts, where treatment responses may differ from the Chinese population.[40] Therefore, future research should expand to multicenter and multicountry designs to validate the efficacy and applicability of these treatments in different global populations. Additionally, although we conducted a thorough inconsistency test and assessed the contributions of each treatment method to the network effect, some treatment regimens had smaller sample sizes, which may result in small-sample effects or selective reporting that could affect the reliability of the overall results. Particularly in cases of significant small-sample effects, some data points may not fully reflect the true clinical effects. In conclusion, future research should focus on patient groups from different regions and cultural backgrounds, with an emphasis on including gray literature and unpublished studies to reduce the potential impact of publication bias. Furthermore, cross-cultural and multicenter clinical trials will help verify the global applicability and clinical effectiveness of integrated TCM and Western medicine treatments.

5. Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that combination therapy is superior to monotherapy with immunomodulators in terms of efficacy rate, pain relief, erosion healing, reduction of inflammation markers, and recurrence rate. Specifically, the combination of Oral Yinqing Granules and immunomodulators, Compound Danshen Formula and immunomodulators, and Kangfuxin Liquid and immunomodulators showed the most significant improvements in treatment efficacy and pain relief. Total Glycosides of Paeony monotherapy demonstrated an advantage in reducing recurrence rates, while Kangfuxin Liquid combined with immunomodulators was particularly effective in reducing erosion area and improving inflammation markers. All combination therapies demonstrated good safety profiles, with mild and low incidence of adverse reactions. Based on these results, clinicians may consider combining Oral Yinqing Granules, Compound Danshen Formula, or Kangfuxin Liquid with immunomodulators in the treatment of EOLP to optimize efficacy and reduce recurrence. Additionally, these combination therapies, due to their low incidence of adverse reactions, can be considered safe treatment options. This study provides important evidence for the use of integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine treatments for EOLP and offers valuable guidance for clinical treatment strategy selection.

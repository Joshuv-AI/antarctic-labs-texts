A soft tissue–sparing positioning guide for the DCIA flap in mandibular reconstruction: a pilot study
Authors: Jingcun Shi, Jieyu Wang, Ziqian Wu, Yuhan Zhang, Bingqing Zhang, Liwei Gu, Lei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrecise placement of bone grafts is critical for functional and aesthetic outcomes in mandibular reconstruction. Conventional positioning guides typically require extensive stripping of periosteum and surrounding soft tissues, which may increase surgical trauma. This pilot study aimed to assess the feasibility and preliminary clinical performance of a novel soft tissue–sparing positioning guide for mandibular reconstruction.MethodsThis single-arm case series feasibility study included five female patients with segmental mandibular defects. Preoperative computed tomography data were used for virtual surgical planning and fabrication of patient-specific osteotomy and positioning guides using three-dimensional printing. The novel positioning guide featured an arched structural design that maintained a predefined clearance from the bone graft surface, allowing accommodation of periosteum and muscles while ensuring standardized and accurate fixation. Postoperative outcomes were evaluated using serial imaging and three-dimensional deviation analysis of six bilateral mandibular anatomical landmarks.ResultsAll patients completed the surgical protocol without flap loss or severe complications. Minor postoperative morbidity was limited to transient donor-site edema, and no permanent sensory deficits, local recurrence, or metastasis were observed during follow-up. Intraoperative findings confirmed effective preservation of soft tissues around the bone graft, except at the precision fixation part. Quantitative three-dimensional analysis demonstrated satisfactory postoperative mandibular alignment across all evaluated landmarks.ConclusionsThe proposed soft tissue–sparing positioning guide appears to be a feasible adjunct for mandibular reconstruction based on the findings of this preliminary study, but given that this is a small, uncontrolled feasibility case series, further validation in larger controlled studies is warranted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09045-4.

Introduction

Mandibular defects are common in oral and maxillofacial surgery, which may cause facial deformity of different degrees, along with dysfunction in chewing, speaking, swallowing, and breathing. Autogenous bone grafting remains the gold standard for reconstructing mandibular defects, with the iliac bone being a commonly used donor site. The vascularized ilium flap supplied by the deep circumflex iliac artery (DCIA) has unique advantages: sufficient bone height to repair mandibular defects and facilitate dental implant placement; its contour closely resembles that of the mandible; and the donor site morbidity is minimal. Building on these advantages, the iliac flap has gained widespread application in mandibular reconstruction [1–4].

In the conventional method of vascularized iliac crest grafting to reconstruct mandibular defects, the bone flap is typically shaped by the surgeon after division of the vascular pedicle. However, this will prolong the ischemic time of the flap, and is highly operator-dependent, reducing the unpredictability of the surgical outcome. In recent years, digital surgical assistant technology has gradually become the focus [5–7]. Compared with the conventional empirical methods, digital surgical assistant technology can reconstruct models from CT data and achieve accurate positioning of surgical margins and custom-designed cutting and positioning guides [8], which reduces intraoperative errors such as imprecise margin resection and improves surgical efficiency [9]. These limitations have driven the adoption of digital technologies to enhance precision.

The conventional positioning guide is designed according to CT and ensures intimate contact with the bone surface. Therefore, thorough soft tissue removal is required prior to placement; otherwise, deviation may increase or even cause a skewed jaw [10]. Preservation of muscle and periosteum is critical for three reasons: maintaining blood supply and neural integrity, preserving facial contour, and reducing infection risk [11, 12]. Concurrent prospective clinical studies have also found that ilioinguinal nerve retained by this device can be used to bridge inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) defects via coaptation, which can reduce the resorption of grafted bone and maintain sensory function in the lower lip [13, 14]. At present, there is no accepted technique for ensuring the placement of graft bone while leveraging the benefits of the periosteum and muscles attached to the bone.

To address clinical challenges, we designed a novel positioning guide that can minimize soft tissue disruption. The novel guide features a curved notch to accommodate soft tissues, eliminating the need for extensive dissection. This study aims to verify whether this design ensures accurate bone flap positioning.

Materials and methods

Study design

This pilot study was performed as a single-arm case series feasibility study, with no contemporaneous or historical control group. Only patients undergoing mandibular reconstruction were included. Participants underwent reconstruction using the novel positioning guide. The “accurate placement” of the transplanted bone was used as the primary indicator, and the success criterion for “accurate placement” was defined as a deviation within 2 mm. The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital (SH9H-2018-T95-2) and adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were defined as follows: (1) participants aged 17–75 years of both sexes; (2) presence of mandibular defects resulting from neoplasms, traumatic injuries, or osteomyelitis; (3) segmental mandibular defects exceeding 4 cm in length; (4) tumor had involved the inferior border of the mandible or encased the IAN canal, making it impossible to preserve the IAN while achieving safe margins; (5) adequate general health status; and (6) absence of prior treatment with bisphosphonates, calcium supplements, or other bone metabolism-affecting pharmaceuticals.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria consisted of (1) patients with aggressive malignant tumors not suitable for primary mandibular reconstruction; (2) patients with poor general condition and unable to tolerate mandibular reconstruction surgery.‌

Preoperatively, all patients underwent helical CT scanning (Somatom Definition AS, Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany) of the head and neck region using a standardized dental protocol (Fig. 1). Design and fixation strategy of the novel positioning guide

In this study, the novel positioning guide consists of three key components: a main stabilizing board, a precision-guided fixation part, and an arched soft tissue protector. The fixation part ensures accurate placement of the bone graft while providing mechanical stability. A critical feature is the arched structure, which maintains a 5.0 ± 0.3 mm clearance from the graft surface to accommodate muscles, and periosteum. The positioning guide was digitally designed using certified medical-grade CAD software (3-matic Medical 13.0 × 64 and Materialise Magics). Fabrication employed PolyJet additive manufacturing technology (Stratasys, The United States) with MED610 biocompatible photopolymer, which satisfies ISO 10993 standards for: Long-term contact (> 30 days) with intact skin; Short-term contact (≤ 24 h) with mucous membranes and compromised tissue surfaces; Transient exposure (≤ 24 h) to internal tissues and bone structures; Geometric specifications included variable thicknesses of 7–8 mm at load-bearing connecting rod interfaces and uniform 3 mm sections elsewhere to balance structural integrity with minimal soft tissue interference. Sterilization was performed via low-temperature plasma processing (Sterrad® system), validated to maintain device functionality while achieving sterility assurance levels (SAL) of 10⁻⁶.

The novel positioning guide (Fig. 3a) comprises a main board (31) and two positioning parts (32) located on the inner side of the main board. These positioning parts are designed to be temporarily fixed to the mandibular stump (2) and the transplanted bone (1), respectively. The segment of the main board between two adjacent positioning parts forms an arched (bow‑shaped) structure, which creates a gap between the guide and the bone surface to accommodate soft tissues. The pre‑drilled fixation holes (4) in each positioning part are geometrically identical to the screw holes pre‑reserved on the mandibular stump (Fig. 3b,c) and on the iliac graft (Fig. 3d), ensuring precise screw placement.Fig. 3Fixation strategy of the novel positioning guide. a Structural diagram of the novel positioning guide. b and c The screw holes on the positioning guide are consistent with those on the mandible cutting guide. d The screw holes on the positioning guide are consistent with those on the iliac crest cutting guide

Surgical steps

Following general anesthesia, the donor site (iliac region) and the recipient site (maxillofacial region) were simultaneously disinfected, and two surgical teams concurrently performed mandibular lesion resection and DCIA flap harvesting. For DCIA flap preparation, an approximately 15 cm incision was made at the anterior superior iliac spine. The skin, subcutaneous tissue, musculus obliquus externus abdominis, and musculi obliquus internus abdominis were incised. The DCIA was carefully identified and dissected, and retrograde dissection was performed to the external iliac artery, preserving its perforating branches and the connection with the ilium. During this process, the anterior lateral femoral cutaneous nerve was meticulously dissected and protected to avoid postoperative skin numbness in the anterolateral thigh region. On the medial side of the ilium, the transversus abdominis muscle and iliacus muscle were separated, and on the lateral side, the gluteus maximus muscle was separated. The iliac bone harvesting guide was fitted onto the iliac crest, and osteotomy was performed along the guide slot. Once the osteotomy was completed and the bone flap was completely freed, the DCIA and deep circumflex iliac vein were dissected from the external iliac artery and vein. Thus, the iliac crest flap was successfully harvested.

The iliac crest flap was pre-contoured with the iliac crest osteotomy guide (Fig. 2c). Then transferred to the mandibular defect area, and end-to-end microvascular anastomosis was performed between the vascular pedicle and the recipient vessels under a microscope. After anastomosis, flap revascularization was assessed (vascular pulsation, venous return, and bone surface oozing). The positioning parts of the guide were attached to the stump and graft using temporary screws through the matched fixation holes (Figs. 3a and 4). Each part features standardized fixation apertures that are geometrically congruent with the pre-designed screw holes on the recipient bone and the donor graft. The main stabilizing board between adjacent part forms an integrated arched architecture (Fig. 4a, d). Intraoperative observation revealed a gap between the novel positioning guide and the bone graft surface, located at the mandibular inferior border (Fig. 4b‑f). The pre-contoured iliac crest flap was positioned into the mandibular defect using the novel positioning guide, and fixation was achieved with a pre-bent reconstruction plate. Quantitative assessment of mandibular deviation

Mandibular deviation was quantitatively evaluated by comparing the three-dimensional positional changes of six bilateral anatomical landmarks between preoperative and postoperative states [15]. Postoperative three-dimensional deviation analysis was performed using Geomagic Qualify 2013 software (3D Systems, Inc., USA). The following mandibular landmarks were identified and measured: the most medial and most lateral points of the mandibular condyle, the lowest point of the mandibular notch, the lingual spine of the mandible, the tip of the coronoid process, and the most inferior point of the mandibular angle (Fig. 5). The mean distance values for each landmark set were calculated. The postoperative deviation for each patient was then defined as the absolute difference between the pre- and postoperative mean values (Value for accuracy = distances postoperatively- distances preoperatively). Measurements were performed independently by two trained researchers. To reduce bias, the researchers were blinded to the surgical sequence and clinical outcomes of the patients. However, due to the single‑arm study design, potential observer bias could not be completely avoided.Fig. 5The anatomical landmarks used for pre- and postoperative comparisons. Results

This study enrolled five female participants (mean age 28.00 ± 6.87 years), comprising four ameloblastoma cases and one myxoma case. Mandibular defects were classified using the Brown reconstruction‑based system, revealing four Class I and one Class II reconstructive categories [16]. The horizontal bone graft lengths ranged from 5.41 cm to 10.50 cm (8.32 ± 1.86 cm). No adjunctive therapies, including radiotherapy, were administered to any subject.

All patients completed the preoperative protocol without dropout. Intraoperatively, the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve was preserved to prevent permanent lateral thigh numbness. No flap complications occurred. Minor postoperative complications were observed, with donor‑site edema being the most prevalent (2/5). Clinical examinations and spiral CT scans were conducted at 6 months and 12 months after the surgery. All 5 patients attended all scheduled examinations on time (with 100% compliance); no local recurrence or metastasis was observed during the follow-up period.

Primary outcomes

The mandibular deviation, as a key indicator reflecting the accuracy of graft positioning, was measured postoperatively in all five patients (Table 1). The measured deviation values ranged from 0.75 mm to 1.77 mm (1.25 ± 0.41 mm, the measurement differences between the two researchers were ≤ 0.5 mm). Specifically, Patient 4 presented the minimal deviation (0.75 mm), while Patient 3 exhibited the maximal deviation (1.77 mm). Among the two patients who experienced donor-site edema (Patients 1 and 2), the deviation values were 1.24 mm and 0.98 mm, respectively. The remaining three patients without reported complications showed deviation values of 1.77 mm (Patient 3), 0.75 mm (Patient 4), and 1.53 mm (Patient 5). Discussion

Current approaches for mandibular defect reconstruction include patient-specific implants (PSIs), distraction osteogenesis and autogenous grafts. Technological advances in medical imaging and additive manufacturing have expanded PSIs applications in complex maxillofacial reconstruction.While titanium PSIs are contraindicated in free flap candidates, they require monitoring for exposure (18%), loosening, and infection [17]. Vascularized DCIA flaps remain the gold standard for reconstructing large defects, providing adequate height for implants while minimizing contouring [18]. This study introduces an innovative soft tissue-sparing positioning guide featuring an arched architecture that accommodates neurovascular structures, permitting simultaneous nerve reconstruction during bone grafting [19].

The preservation of periosteum and muscle attachments during mandibular reconstruction is clinically important for three reasons: (1) maintaining vascularity of the grafted bone—periosteal blood supply contributes 70–80% of cortical perfusion [20]; (2) reducing donor-site morbidity and preserving potential for neurosensory reconstruction (e.g., coaptation of the ilioinguinal nerve to the inferior alveolar nerve) [19]; and (3) lowering the risk of postoperative infection and bone resorption. Conventional positioning guides require complete soft-tissue stripping to achieve intimate bone–guide contact, which directly counteracts these benefits. The proposed arch‑structured guide is intended to preserve soft tissues, such as periosteum and muscles.

The periosteum is a dense fibroelastic membrane tightly adherent to bone surfaces. Its intimate association with the cortex enables critical roles in nutrient delivery, fracture repair, and geometric maintenance. Notably, periosteal vasculature supplies 70–80% of cortical arterial inflow and nearly all venous drainage [20], explaining why iatrogenic periosteal damage induces cortical osteonecrosis. This underscores the periosteum’s non-redundant role in bone homeostasis. Mechanistically, periosteal progenitor cells—embryologically related to bone marrow stromal cells—exhibit superior proliferative capacity and accelerated osteogenic differentiation [21]. At present, artificial periosteum has been synthesized to promote bone regeneration in tissue engineered bone, and satisfactory results have been achieved [22, 23]. In our study, the arch structure of the novel guide retained the periosteum.

The 2 mm threshold was selected as the success criterion for “accurate placement” based on combined clinical and biomechanical considerations. First, a deviation of less than 2 mm is widely accepted in maxillofacial reconstruction as the limit for maintaining proper occlusal relationships and facilitating subsequent dental implant placement without the need for prosthetic compensation [24, 25]. Second, in our virtual surgical planning (VSP) environment, the cumulative error from CT imaging (≤ 0.5 mm), segmentation (≤ 0.3 mm), and 3D printing (≤ 0.2 mm) is typically below 1 mm. A 2 mm clinical tolerance therefore allows for unavoidable intraoperative factors (e.g., soft‑tissue interference, microvascular anastomosis tension) while still ensuring functionally acceptable alignment.

The six bilateral landmarks were chosen according to established methods for mandibular morphometric assessment following reconstruction [15, 26]. Specifically: Condylar points (A/A’, B/B’) reflect the most critical functional unit (temporomandibular joint) and are highly sensitive to rotational errors. Mandibular notch (C/C’) and coronoid tip (E/E’) capture vertical and sagittal deviations in the ramus region, where muscle attachments (temporalis, masseter) are prone to displacement. Lingula (D/D’) is a stable landmark for evaluating medial–lateral positioning, which directly affects inferior alveolar nerve alignment and canal integrity. Mandibular angle (F/F’) is the farthest point from the osteotomy site, providing a sensitive measure of cumulative angular error. Together, these landmarks enable comprehensive three‑dimensional assessment of both condylar position and mandibular body alignment, with inter‑ and intra‑observer reproducibility previously validated.

Tominaga et al. [27] developed an intraoral resection framework using titanium plates, silicone spacers, stainless steel wires, and photocured resin. Funayama et al. [28] anchored an auxiliary guide to the mandibular inferior border and ramus, utilizing autopolymerizing resin for segmental stabilization. Nuri et al. [29] pre-contoured reconstruction plates on virtual jaw models, applying biocompatible resin while preserving select fixation holes for intraoperative alignment. Wu et al. [10] engineered a reconstruction plate with complementary drill guides, achieving submillimeter accuracy through trajectory-matched screw placement—decreasing the average graft deviation from 1.21 mm to 0.88 mm. Yin et al. [30] introduced a percutaneous reduction device enabling non-digital precision reconstruction. Our novel positioning guide integrates accurate graft fixation with preservation of muscles and periosteum. The integrated system, which combines this new type of positioning guide with a pre-bent titanium plate, is designed with the intention of simplifying the surgical process.

Segmental mandibulectomy remains standard for advanced osteomyelitis or benign tumors, typically followed by vascularized fibula/iliac crest reconstruction to restore masticatory function and facial aesthetics [31, 32]. For high-grade malignancies where radical excision may compromise oncologic margins, free flap transfer is prioritized over alloplastic reconstruction due to superior oncologic safety. This paradigm integrates neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy tailored to TNM staging, augmented by regular follow-up.

This study demonstrates the feasibility of a novel positioning guide; however, several limitations should be noted. First, this investigation constitutes an initial proof-of-concept study following patent approval (Patent No.ZL 2020 10948755.X) and is inherently exploratory in design. Second, limitations include a small (n = 5) and all patients were female with benign pathologies (ameloblastoma, myxoma), limiting generalizability to malignant tumors, irradiated fields, or male anatomy. Future prospective comparative research with expanded cohorts and extended follow-up periods will be critical to comprehensively evaluate complication rates, functional outcomes, and long-term donor-site stability. Third, no control group (conventional guides vs. freehand placement) was included, so comparative efficacy cannot be concluded. Nevertheless, the proposed positioning guide enables precise intraoperative positioning while maintaining soft-tissue integrity.

This pilot study of five patients with mandibular defects (four ameloblastoma, one myxoma) using the novel soft tissue‑sparing positioning guide in DCIA iliac flap reconstruction showed mean mandibular deviation of 1.25 mm (all within 2 mm), and no flap loss or major complications. These findings suggest that the guide is feasible for mandibular reconstruction.

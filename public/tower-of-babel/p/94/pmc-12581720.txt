Effects of dietary metabolizable energy and crude protein levels on production performance, serum biochemical indices and cecal microbiota of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period
Authors: Qi Wang, Yutian Shen, Wentao Li, Lixia Kai, Yuqing Fan, Youping Wu, Huirong Mao, Shuzhong Yang, Fengqin Wang, Yizhen Wang, Zeqing Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This experiment investigated the effects of dietary metabolizable energy (ME) and crude protein (CP) levels and as well as interactions on the production performance, serum biochemical indexes, and cecum microbiota of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period. In the experiment, 2160 3-week-old (21-day-old) Taihe Silky Fowl, with similar body weights and good health conditions, were randomly divided into 9 groups according to a 3 × 3 two-factor random experimental design. Each group had 6 replicates, with 40 chickens per replicate. The diets with 3 different levels of ME (11.7 MJ/kg, 12.1 MJ/kg, 12.6 MJ/kg) and 3 different levels of CP (19.0 %, 20.0 %, 21.0 %) were provided. The results were as follows: as the dietary ME level increased, the feed conversion ratio (FCR) of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period significantly decreased (P < 0.05), and the eviscerated yield rate increased and then decreased significantly (P < 0.05), reaching the highest value in the 12.1 MJ/kg group. Serum triglyceride (TG) content significantly increased (P < 0.05). Different dietary ME levels significantly affected the cecal microbiota structure. The abundance of the Bacteroidetes phylum was highest in the 12.1 MJ/kg group, particularly the Bacteroides genus, which promotes the growth and development of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period by exerting probiotic effects. However, the different levels of CP in the diets, as well as the interaction between ME and CP levels did not have significant effects on the growth performance, slaughter performance, organ indices, or serum biochemical indices of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period. In conclusion, it is recommended to maintain the dietary ME level for Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period at 12.1 MJ/kg and the CP level at 19.0 %.

Introduction

Taihe Silky Fowl (Gallus gallus domesticus Brisson), native to the Wushan area of Taihe County, Jiangxi Province, is a rare breed of poultry unique to China, famous for its medicinal, meat, egg, and ornamental economic values(Huang et al., 2025). As a multi-purpose local premium breed resource, the meat of Taihe Silky Fowl is tender and rich in a variety of essential amino acids, and the ratio of polyunsaturated fatty acids in intramuscular fat is significantly better than that of conventional chicken breeds(Li et al., 2024b). Egg yolks of Taihe Silky Fowl eggs are rich in selenium, and egg whites have a high percentage of egg transferrin, which is both nutritious and antioxidant(Li et al., 2024a). In terms of medicinal use, it is rich in melanin, active peptides, DHA, ARA, and so on, which have a good health care effect (Tu et al., 2009). Due to urgent dietary and medical needs, this kind of chicken is now being bred on a large scale across China(Chen et al., 2024). By the end of June 2024, the Taihe Silky Fowl industry had established a complete industrial chain model that integrated variety protection, ecological breeding, high-end processing, and cultural tourism integration (Xiao et al., 2013). The annual breeding volume of Taihe Silky Fowl has reached 10 million feathers, with an output value of more than 5.6 billion yuan(Shen et al., 2025).

The brooding period is a crucial window for the skeletal development and immune maturation of Taihe Silky Fowl(Zhao et al., 2022). However, during the brooding period, Taihe Silky Fowl face production limitations such as weak disease resistance, high mortality rate and slower g4rowth rate, and their nutritional requirements are directly related to meat production efficiency during the fattening period, egg-laying performance during the breeding cycle, and the accumulation of effective medicinal ingredients(Tan et al., 2024). In addition, compared to nutritional regulation studies on specialized fast-growing commercial broiler such as AA broilers, research on the nutritional requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl—particularly during the brooding period—remains insufficient(Tian et al., 2021). Meanwhile, with tight global feed resources and rising prices, precision nutrition technology has become an important means to control breeding costs and reduce environmental load(Moss et al., 2021). Therefore, it is crucial to determine the key nutritional requirements for the brooding period of Taihe Silky Fowl. CP and ME are key nutrients in feed, and studies have shown that the levels and ratios of CP and ME in diets significantly affect the production performance of Taihe Silky Fowl at different periods(Shen et al., 2025).

At present, the research of Taihe Silky Fowl mainly focuses on the protection of genetic resources and the exploitation of nutritional characteristics. As for the different growth periods of Taihe Silky Fowl, although the dietary requirements of the growing period, breeding period, and egg-laying period have been studied, limited research has been conducted on the nutritional requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period, especially on the specific requirements of dietary ME and CP(Li et al., 2024b; Shen et al., 2025). Therefore, building upon the previous studies, this study further explored the effects of different levels of ME and CP in diets on performance, serum biochemical indexes, and cecum microbiota of Taihe Silky Fowl at the brooding stage.

Materials and methods

All animal feeding and experimental operations in this study strictly followed the current experimental animal welfare standards, and the relevant experimental protocols were approved for implementation after a compliance review by the Animal Welfare Committee of the College of Animal Science, Zhejiang University (ZJU20170466).

Experimental design and feeding management

This study was conducted at the Wushan Breeding Farm of Taihe Aoxin Wuji Development Co., Ltd., located in Taihe County, Jiangxi Province. One week before the formal experiment commenced, 2,400 two-week-old (14-day-old) Taihe Silky Fowl were selected for a one-week acclimation to the experimental conditions. For the formal experiment, 2,160 three-week-old (21 days old) Taihe Silky Fowl were randomly divided into 9 groups according to a 3 × 3 two-factor randomized experimental design, with 6 replicates in each group and 40 chickens in each replicate. The chickens were fed diets with 3 different ME levels (11.7 MJ/kg, 12.1 MJ/kg, 12.6 MJ/kg) and 3 different CP levels (19.0 %, 20.0 %, 21.0 %). Before the experiment started, all the equipment in the brooding house was cleaned and disinfected. The Taihe Silky Fowl were raised in three-dimensional cages (each cage was 40.0 × 50.0 × 50.0 cm). During the first week of the official experiment, the temperature in the chicken house was maintained at 29 ± 1℃, and then it was gradually reduced by 2 ℃ each week until it reached 22 ± 1℃. The relative humidity in the chicken house was maintained at 55 %−60 %, and the chickens were exposed to 16 hours of light per day (with an intensity of 5-15 lux). The chicken flocks were regularly immunized, and the chicken house was regularly ventilated and disinfected. Throughout the 35-day experimental period, the birds had ad libitum access to feed and water, and were fed twice daily.

Growth performance

During the experiment, mortality was recorded daily and feed consumption was adjusted according to the number of deaths, and feed intake was calculated weekly. After the chickens were fasting for 12 hours at the 21st and 56th day of age, the weight of each replicate chicken was weighed. Growth performance was assessed by calculating the average daily gain (ADG), average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) of Taihe Silky Fowl.

Slaughter performance

At the end of the experiment, the chickens were fasted for 12 hours. One fowl from each replicate was euthanized after blood collection from the wing vein. The slaughter performance of the fowls was assessed by determining the dressing percentage, the eviscerated yield rate, and the half-eviscerated yield rate according to the Chinese agricultural industry standard “Performance Terminology and measurements of Poultry” (NY/T 823-2020).

Organ index

Fresh weights of heart, liver, spleen, and bursa were collected and weighed at the time of sampling. The organ index was calculated according to the formula “organ index (%) = organ weight (g) / body weight (g) × 100″.

Serum biochemical indices

One fowl was randomly selected from each replicate for euthanasia and blood sampling. After collecting the whole blood samples, they were centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes under the pre-cooled conditions at 4℃. The upper serum was carefully aspirated and transferred to the cryotubes, which were then immediately placed in the −20℃ low-temperature freezer for long-term storage. Routine serum biochemical parameters and lipid metabolism-associated biomarkers were measured using an automated biochemical analyzer (UniCel DxC 600 Synchron, Beckman Coulter, USA). The biochemical indicators used included alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), γ-glutamyl transferase (GGT), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), and globulin (Glob). Lipid metabolism markers included triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TCHO), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), uric acid (UA), creatinine (Crea), urea (Urea), and total bilirubin (TBil).

DNA extraction and microbial 16S rRNA gene sequencing analysis

One fowl was randomly selected from each replicate to collect cecal contents, resulting in six cecal content samples per group. The cecal contents were placed into 2 mL sterile cryovials. After rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen, transfer them to a −80°C ultra-low temperature refrigerator for storage. After the samples thaw, microbial DNA extraction was performed using the commercial genomic DNA extraction kit from Majorbio Company (Shanghai, China). The quality and concentration of the DNA were detected through 1 % agarose gel electrophoresis and a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Using the specific amplification primers 341F (5′-ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG-3′) and 806R (5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′), the V3-V4 hypervariable region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified. The PCR amplification products were separated by 2 % agarose gel electrophoresis, and the target bands were recovered using the AxyPrep DNA gel purification kit (AXYGEN, USA). Based on the semi-quantitative results of the electrophoresis, the DNA is precisely quantified using the QuantiFluor-STTM Blue fluorescence quantitative system (Promega, USA). After constructing the library, high-throughput sequencing was performed using the Illumina MiSeq dual-end sequencing platform (Illumina, USA) to obtain the raw sequencing data. After strictly removing low-quality sequences, valid data were obtained for operational taxonomic unit (OTU) clustering analysis. All bioinformatics analysis processes were completed on the QIIME2 (v2021.8) platform.

Statistical analyses

The data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0 data statistics software (IBM, Armonk, NY). The Kruskal-Wallis rank sum test was used to analyze the cecum microbiota. The results were expressed as mean and Standard Error of the Mean (SEM), P < 0.05 was considered significant, and P < 0.01 was considered extremely significant.

Discussion

It is important to satisfy the nutritional requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period to improve its production performance(Zhao et al., 2022). As a key factor in the nutritional regulation of livestock and poultry, the dynamic balance between dietary ME and CP has a significant effect on production performance, such as FCR(Liu et al., 2015). Shen et al. (2025) showed that there was no significant change in the production performance of Taihe Silky Fowl with increasing dietary crude protein level, which was similar to our findings, suggesting that in this experimental design, a crude protein level of 19.0 % would be sufficient to meet the production requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period. Broilers have been reported to adjust feed intake in response to changes in dietary energy levels(Liu et al., 2016). Visscher et al. (2018) found that in a feeding trial with adjusted protein levels, the amount of food taken by the animals basically depended on the energy content of the diet, and thus, total feed intake was negatively correlated with the energy content. Zou et al. (2013) demonstrated that dietary ME levels could significantly affect the FCR of broiler chickens from 0 to 42 d. Liu et al. (2017) investigated the effects of different dietary energy levels on the effect of different dietary energy levels on the performance of Ross 308 chicks, and the results showed that the ADFI and FCR decreased significantly with increasing dietary metabolizable energy levels, while there was no significant difference in the ADG, which was consistent with our results. Therefore, we conclude that the three different dietary ME levels in this study were the key factors influencing the performance of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period.

Dressing percentage, half-eviscerated yield rate, and eviscerated yield rate in carcass characteristics can visually evaluate broiler body composition and meat production performance (Huang et al., 2016). Pectoral muscle rate and leg muscle rate are important indicators of chicken slaughter performance (Song et al., 2022). In the present study, it was found that different metabolic energy and crude protein levels of diets did not significantly affect the breast and leg muscle rates of brooding Taihe Silky Fowl, similar to the findings of Li et al. (2024b). Full net chamber weight is the weight of head, neck, legs, heart, liver, muscular stomach, glandular stomach, and abdominal fat subtracted from the half net chamber weight (Ahmat et al., 2021). Data from the present experiment showed that different ME levels in the feed did not significantly affect the half-net gutting rate of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period, but the slaughter rate of the HME group was significantly higher than that of the MME group, whereas the full net gutting rate was significantly lower than that of the MME group. In addition, different levels of metabolizable energy and crude protein in the diets and their interactions did not significantly affect organ indices such as heart, liver, bursa, and spleen. This suggests that the significant difference in total net chambering rate between HME and MME groups may be since when the dietary energy level reaches 12.6 MJ/kg, it not only meets the growth requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period, but may also store a large amount of fat in the abdominal part of the organism. Previous studies have shown that excessive accumulation of abdominal fat in chicks not only exposes feed energy use inefficiency, but also reduces carcass yield, lowering meat quality and destroying the economic and nutritional value of livestock products (Wen et al., 2019). Therefore, the growth requirements of Taihe Silky Fowl in the brooding stage could be met when the feed metabolizable energy level of 12.1 MJ/kg was achieved, as compared to the 12.6 MJ/kg group.

Serum biochemical parameters can reflect the physiological and metabolic status of Taihe Silky Fowl, and help evaluate the overall health condition of the animals. Therefore, it is crucial to verify the effects of changes in the levels of metabolizable energy and crude protein in the diet on serum biochemical parameters(Wu et al., 2016). ALP, ALT, AST and GGT are often expressed in liver cells and are indicators that sensitively reflect liver function and protein metabolism levels. When the liver is inflamed or damaged, these enzymes will be released into the blood (Jiao et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022). The study by Geng et al. (2021) showed that different levels of metabolizable energy and crude protein in the diet had no significant effect on the serum ALT and AST contents of 6-week-old Beijing oil chickens. Chen et al.(2016) proved that the crude protein concentration in the diet had no significant effect on the serum GGT content of Ross 708 broilers during the brooding period. In addition, studies have shown that the levels of metabolizable energy and crude protein in the diet and their interactions had no significant effect on the total protein and albumin contents of slow-growing yellow-feathered broilers at 8 weeks of age (Ruan et al., 2022). Similarly, our experimental results showed that the levels of metabolizable energy and crude protein in the diet had no significant effect on the activities of serum ALP, ALT, AST, and GGT, as well as total protein, albumin, globulin, and the albumin/globulin ratio. This indicates that under the metabolic energy and crude protein levels set in this experiment, the liver of Taihe Silky Fowl did not suffer from damage and was in good health.

TG, TCHO, HDL-C and LDL-C are markers of lipid metabolism in the body. Abnormal elevation of serum triglycerides and disorders in cholesterol metabolism may damage animal health through pathways such as hepatic steatosis, increased abdominal fat deposition, and suppression of immune function (Liang et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021). Yin et al. (2023) investigated the effects of dietary energy levels on serum biochemical indices of Guangyuan Grey chickens. The results showed that different levels of metabolic energy in the diet significantly affected serum triglyceride content. Similar to his research results, our experimental data indicated that with the increase of dietary metabolic energy levels, the triglyceride level in the HME group was significantly higher than that in the MME group. This suggests that when the dietary metabolic energy level reached 12.6 MJ/kg, the significant increase in serum triglyceride levels might lead to a change in the molecular mechanism of fat accumulation, thereby increasing the risk of excessive abdominal fat accumulation in the brooding period of Taihe Silky Fowl (Qi et al., 2012). The research results of Dehghani-Tafti and Jahanian (2016) indicated that different protein levels in the diet had no significant effect on LDL-C and TCHO levels. Similar to the previous research results, my results indicated that different CP and ME levels in the diet and their interactions had no significant effect on serum TCHO, HDL-C, LDL-C, and urea levels.

The cecum is the part of the poultry intestinal tract with the highest microbial quantity and richness. The cecal microbiota plays a significant role in animal growth performance, immune response, and overall health. Among them, feed composition, as one of the core regulatory factors, significantly influences the structure and diversity of the intestinal microbiota (Wang et al., 2025; Gevers et al., 2014, 2022b). In the current study, alpha diversity analysis revealed that there was a significant difference in microbial diversity between the MME group and the LME group. PLS-DA analysis also clearly separated the microbial communities of the three treatments, indicating that the dietary metabolic energy level has a significant impact on the abundance and diversity of the cecum microbiota of Taihe Silky Fowl, which is consistent with the conclusion of Shen et al.

At the phylum level, our team’s previous study showed that dietary ME levels significantly affected the microbial composition of the caecum in Taihe Silky Fowl, which was similar to our results. When dietary ME levels were elevated to 12.1 MJ/kg, the proportion of Bacteroides increased. Bacteroides is the main acid-producing bacterium in the poultry caecum, which metabolizes dietary fiber to produce SCFAs such as acetic acid, propionic acid, and butyric acid (Sorbara and Pamer, 2022). These metabolites not only provide energy for intestinal epithelial cells but also regulate glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) secretion by activating G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), thereby affecting host lipid metabolism (Tolhurst et al., 2012). Bacteroides dominated flora in the cecum of Tibetan chickens significantly improved energy metabolism efficiency by up-regulating SCFAs synthesis genes, enabling them to adapt to high altitude hypoxic environment (Zeng et al., 2024). Studies have shown that Bacteroides abundance is negatively correlated with fat deposition. It has been confirmed that increased Bacteroides abundance can inhibit the expression of triglyceride synthesis genes in the liver and significantly reduce abdominal fat deposition (Qin et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025). Complementing the fat deposition promoting properties of Firmicutes, energy allocation can be optimized by adjusting the F/B ratio (Baldwin et al., 2016). Our results echo this evidence, with MME having the highest proportion of Bacteroides in the cecal microbiota and the lowest serum triglyceride levels among the three treatment groups. In addition, Bacteroides has been reported to play an important role in improving animal performance and maintaining intestinal homeostasis (Huang et al., 2021). However, when the dietary ME level continued to rise to 12.6 MJ/kg, the proportion of Bacteroides decreased, indicating that dietary ME levels should not exceed 12.6 MJ/kg for Taihe Silky Fowl during brooding.

At the genus level, Faecalibacterium, Lactobacillus and Bacteroides are the three genera with the greatest differences among the treatment groups. Faecalibacterium is classified as a core beneficial bacterium in the poultry intestinal tract and plays a key role in maintaining the intestinal barrier and anti-inflammatory processes (Martín et al., 2023). It has been reported that Faecalibacterium prausnitzii promotes the production of IL-18 in intestinal epithelial cells by activating the HIF1α pathway, improving intestinal barrier function, and its metabolite butyrate can directly inhibit the NF-κB signaling pathway and reduce the expression of pro-inflammatory factors IL-8 and TNF-α(Fagundes et al., 2023; Feye et al., 2019). In addition, studies have shown that copper-loaded modified montmorillonite (MMT) significantly increases the abundance of Faecalibacterium in the cecum of broilers, thereby improving the growth performance of broilers (Wang et al., 2022). Lactobacillus is recognized as a beneficial bacterium in the poultry intestinal tract, which can exert probiotic effects through various pathways such as regulating intestinal microecology, improving intestinal function, and immune regulation. Among them, L. plantarum, L. acidophilus, and L. salivarius have been widely used in poultry breeding (Chen et al., 2020). In the current study, with the increase of dietary ME level, the abundance of Faecalibacterium significantly increased, while the abundance of Lactobacillus significantly decreased. Compared with other groups, the MME group maintained a relatively higher abundance of Faecalibacterium and Lactobacillus, which could better exert their probiotic effects. Moreover, our research results indicate that the abundance of Bacteroides is the highest in the MME group, and the correlation heatmap also shows that the abundance of Bacteroides is significantly positively correlated with ADG and ADFI. Therefore, we speculate that Bacteroides may be the key indicator bacteria responding to the dietary ME level and play an important role in the growth and development of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period.

Bacteroides can secrete mucin to protect and lubricate the intestinal mucosa, and also secrete glycosidases and transporters as well as other substances to metabolize polysaccharides and oligosaccharides at the site, providing nutrients such as vitamins and SCFA for the host and other intestinal microbiota, ultimately promoting the host's nutrient absorption (Zafar and Saier Jr, 2021). Wang et al. (2023) proved that isovaleric acid derived from Bacteroides can regulate the IgA response in the intestinal tract of broilers and maintain intestinal health. Previous studies have shown that the concentration of SCFA in the cecum of chicks with high Bacteroides abundance significantly increased, IL-1β expression decreased, IL-10 and tight junction protein claudin-1 expression increased, and intestinal inflammation markers decreased (Fan et al., 2023). It has been reported that in the cecal microbial community, the abundance of Bacteroides and Lactobacillus is negatively correlated, which is similar to our results, possibly because Lactobacillus can competitively exclude Bacteroides and other carbohydrate-degrading bacteria (Argentini et al., 2022). However, it has been shown that compared with Lactobacillus, Bacteroides shows greater genetic potential when utilizing complex carbohydrates from plant sources. Therefore, we speculate that Bacteroides may promote the nutritional metabolism and immune function of Taihe Silky Fowl through multiple pathways mentioned above, but we also need to acknowledge the limitations of the study. We still need further experimental research to verify the specific role and potential mechanism of Bacteroides in the growth and development of Taihe Silky Fowl.

Conclusion

This study revealed that elevating the ME level significantly decreased the FCR of Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period. The eviscerated yield rate increased initially and then decreased, peaking at an ME level of 12.1 MJ/kg. Concurrently, serum TG content increased significantly. Furthermore, dietary ME levels markedly altered the structure of the cecal microbial community. At the ME level of 12.1 MJ/kg, the relative abundance of the phylum Bacteroidetes was the highest, particularly that of the genus Bacteroides, which may promote the growth and development of the birds during this critical period by enhancing nutrient absorption, modulating immune function, and securing a dominant ecological niche.

In conclusion, considering the growth performance, health status, and cecal microbiota composition, our study suggests maintaining the ME level of diets at 12.1 MJ/kg and the CP level at approximately 19.0 % for Taihe Silky Fowl during the brooding period to achieve optimal production efficiency.

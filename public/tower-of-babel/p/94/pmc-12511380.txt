An intra- and inter-class context and consistency network for supervised and semi-supervised blastocyst segmentation
Authors: Hua Wang, Linwei Qiu, Jingfei Hu, Guang Wu, Huijuan Zou, Zhiguo Zhang, Jicong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The implantation potential of an embryo is intricately linked to the quality of its blastocyst. Consequently, achieving an objective and precise identification of blastocyst morphology is imperative. The purpose of this study is to focus on the structural associations between all tissues of blastocysts, and explore the possibility of semi-supervised learning (SSL) for blastocyst segmentation, so as to further improve the segmentation performance of blastocyst tissues. In this paper, we present a framework named I2C2Net for the automatic segmentation of blastocysts in human embryo images, leveraging both supervised and semi-supervised learning approaches. I2C2Net consists of three key components: the Intra-Class Context Module (IACCM), the Inter-Class Context Module (IRCCM), and the Consistency Module (CM). The IACCM aggregates pixel representations within specific category areas, learning categorized regions relative to ground truth labels. This aggregation then decomposes a K-category recognition task into two tasks, each with distinct labels, while retaining the ability to learn intra-class features. The design of the IRCCM is informed by blastocyst morphology, capturing inter-class information on blastocyst tissues as they develop from inner to outer layers. Additionally, we introduce a consistency module for supervised training, enhancing the model’s ability to learn the original data distribution and improve recognition accuracy without increased computational burden. Furthermore, to address the scarcity of annotated data and meet clinical demands, we propose a semi-supervised version of I2C2Net based on the semantic consistency assumption and cluster assumption across heterogeneous domains. The ablation experimental results validate the effectiveness of our proposed IACCM, IRCCM, and CM modules. Compared to other supervised methods, our I2C2Net achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Dice coefficient, and Jaccard index, which are \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$93.79\%$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$91.69\%$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$92.23\%$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$91.95\%$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$85.33\%$$\end{document}, respectively. Moreover, our semi-supervised version of I2C2Net achieves the best performance among other popular SSL approaches. Specifically, our model gains at least a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.53\%$$\end{document} rise in Accuracy, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.74\%$$\end{document} rise in Precision, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.67\%$$\end{document} rise in Dice coefficient, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1.14\%$$\end{document} rise in Jaccard index. The quantitative and qualitative experimental results showcase the superiority of our model over other representative supervised and semi-supervised methods on the blastocyst public dataset.

Introduction

Infertility is a condition characterized by the failure to establish a clinical pregnancy following normal and unprotected intercourse within 12 months1. Approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10\%$$\end{document}–\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$15\%$$\end{document} of couples worldwide experience infertility2.

In vitro fertilization (IVF) is a widely utilized method to aid reproduction in cases of infertility. During IVF, fertilized eggs (embryos) undergo culturing under controlled conditions in vitro until they reach the blastocyst stage, typically occurring around the fifth day. Subsequently, high-quality embryos, identified based on morphological characteristics, are selected for implantation. However, human involvement in this process can introduce considerable inter- and intra-observer variability in quality assessments. Moreover, IVF is labor-intensive, prone to errors, and demands domain knowledge and expertise. Consequently, there is a pressing need for automated methods to segment different tissue regions within blastocysts.Fig. 1Blastocyst images. In recent years, deep learning has continuously improved the performance of medical image segmentation. The network most commonly used for biomedical image segmentation is U-Net3 and its derivative variants4. A few studies have attempted to segment images of the blastocyst tissue, but the segmentation performance still requires improvements. In blastocyst images, the intra-class characteristics and adhesion of various tissues differ significantly, as shown in Fig. 1a–c; however, differences in the inter-class structure are not apparent, especially in the edge area, as shown in Fig. 1c. Because the blastocyst is transparent, and the image is collected under low-light conditions, intra-class blur is observed, and the features at the edges are indistinguishable. In the outcomes of traditional blastocyst segmentation using the U-Net model, there is a significant degree of confusion both within classes and between different classes. In particular, a few areas of the inner cell mass (ICM) are not recognized, a few areas of the zona pellucida (ZP) are incorrectly recognized as blastocoel, and the inter-class structures are not prominent (the errors are mainly concentrated on the boundary and a few hard pixels), as shown in Fig. 1a, d, e, and f. To address the aforementioned problems, in this paper, we propose an I2C2Net to effectively model intra- and inter-class features for blastocyst segmentation task. The I2C2Net contains an IntrA-Class Context Module (IACCM), an InteR-Class Context Module (IRCCM), and a Consistency Module (CM). IACCM adaptively enhances intra-class features and suppresses the features of other types, and the degree of aggregation within a class is thus enhanced, so the segmentation results are more unified. The IACCM simplifies and decomposes a K-category recognition task into K recognition tasks with two labels. However, a large number of easy samples dominate the gradient because the IACCM more readily learns easy samples. Training the model with hard pixels and identifying weight values is challenging. For example, the pixels in the edge area are difficult to identify because they constitute a small proportion of an image and do not possess sharp characteristics. Furthermore, although the decomposition task reduces the complexity of model learning, the structural relevance of inter-class samples is not considered. Therefore, we developed the IRCCM using a weighted mapping function to resolve the aforementioned problems presented by the IACCM-boundary conditions of intra-class features and problems associated with hard pixels. The interaction of different classes in IRCCM also compensates for the shortcomings of intra-class modules. The IACCM and IRCCM yield a component called I2CNet, which is in charge of producing segmentation results.

The IACCM and IRCCM are employed at each level of the network, and a top-to-down feature enhancement pathway is used to continuously provides coarse-to-fine supervision information; sufficient interaction in information thus occurs between multi-scale features. In the high layer of the network, the topological structures of different categories are integrated based on semantic information. In the bottom layer of the network, the image edges are refined according to low-level statistical features such as color, edge, and texture. Intra-class and inter-class interactions enable the model to learn the latent features of embryos.

It would be highly desirable to enhance the accuracy of embryo tissue identification without significantly increasing the number of parameters and the computational load of reasoning processes. In particular, differences in data distribution are observed between classes, and capturing these boundaries and differences in distribution is often crucial for determining the quality of image segmentation5,6. The discriminator of the generative adversarial network (GAN) determines the authenticity of data by distinguishing the consistency of data distribution; the generator, which is extensively used in data synthesis and enhancement, continuously fits the data distribution of the real samples7. Therefore, we employed the discriminator principle of GAN and designed the consistency module (CM) to combine the original embryo image and the segmentation results. The CM plays an important role in the training of I2C2Net, which facilitates the distribution consistency between predicted maps from I2C2Net and the ground truth. This approach can be used to more effectively capture the data distribution differences between embryo classes and improve the accuracy of embryo segmentation.

Besides supervised learning, we also focus on the shortage of labeled data. It is time-consuming and laborious for pixel-level annotation tasks such as image segmentation. Especially, accurate annotations of lesions or tissue structures often require professional knowledge for medical images, and the cost of acquiring annotated data is more expensive. Medical analyses are therefore accomplished by experienced doctors and medical experts. Moreover, collecting patient data also involves patient privacy and medical ethics. All these reasons aggravate the deficiency of labeled data. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) is an effective method to alleviate this problem. Collecting blastocyst images from the same domain is also difficult, for the types of equipment and environments provided by hospitals are different. Therefore, unlabeled data collected by us usually have different contrast, brightness, hue, saturation, transparency, and so on, compared with images from the labeled dataset. That is why we provide a semi-supervised version of I2C2Net with these extra annotated collections. It utilizes semantic consistency and provides an effective strategy to further improve the performance of supervised learning.

Related work

Blastocyst segmentation

In recent years, several works have attempted the segmentation of blastocyst images9,10, with a primary focus on the identification of ICM and trophectoderm (TE).

Semi-automatic segmentation involves the use of the level set method11. By defining the initial contour, gradient information is applied to segment the ICM and TE. However, in clinical practice, the zona pellucida(ZP) of the blastocyst is ruptured and the ICM lies outside the zona pellucida. Therefore, defining contour information is challenging and the recognition effect is not significant. In addition, human intervention is required. Singh et al.12 applied the level set method to automatically identify blastocysts, primarily for TE segmentation; an accuracy of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$87.8\%$$\end{document} was achieved. Rad et al.13 utilized image texture features (Gabor and DCT features) and combined level sets to determine ICM boundaries; they obtained a Jaccard index of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$70.3\%$$\end{document}. Saeedi et al.14 also applied texture features, but combined k-means clustering and watershed to identify ICM and TE; Jaccard indexes of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$71.1\%$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$63\%$$\end{document} were obtained using ICM and TE, respectively. Kheradmand et al.15 combined the discrete cosine transform (DCT) with a two-layer neural network for feedback propagation to identify ICM, but obtained Jaccard indexes of only \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$47.7\%$$\end{document}. Kheradmand et al.16 used a fully convolutional neural (FCN) network to segment the ICM and achieved a Jaccard index of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$76.5\%$$\end{document}. The method proposed by Leahy et al.17 aimed to segment the ZP, while an ensemble of stacked dilated U-Net models, as suggested by Rad et al.18, was employed for precise segmentation of the ICM. Rad et al.19 proposed a U-Net variant by incorporating dilated convolution, which only increases the perception field of a single pixel, but does not alter the attribute relationship between pixels, with a Jaccard index below \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$82\%$$\end{document}.

The aforementioned methods are applied either for the segmentation of a single tissue or for the identification of multiple tissues. However, it is worthwhile to explore how to leverage the holistic identification of a single tissue and the structural correlations among all tissues to enhance the recognition performance of the model.

Semi-supervised learning

Semi-supervised learning aims to further improve the accuracy by combining unlabeled collections with limited labeled pairs20. Based on the diversity of different semi-supervised loss functions and model design paradigms, SSL methods can be primarily divided into several categories21,22. Pseudo-Labeling23–25 is a simple but practical method for SSL, which picks up high-confidence outputs as the pseudo ground-truth of unlabeled samples thus training a network in a supervised fashion with a mix-up dataset23. To exploit ambiguous predictions, Wang26 made effective use of unannotated data. Han27 tried to generate pseudo labels by calculating the distances of a special feature representation. Consistency regularization28 is a regularization approach to seek a smooth manifold of the dataset with unlabeled images, which typically tries to give perturbations on inputs. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\prod }$$\end{document}-model29, temporal ensembling30, and mean teacher31 are the most representatives sharing the common Teacher-Student structure. Yu32 proposed uncertainty-aware mean teacher to foster consistent results under varying perturbations. Recent studies attempt to deal with SSL focusing on intra- and inter-pair consistency33, high- and low-level consistency34, directional context-aware consistency35, dual-task consistency36, and interpolation consistency37. Regularized dropout38 structure is also applied to stimulate perturbations. Entropy minimization39 is another regularization thread facilitating high-confidence (i.e.low-entropy) predictions for unannotated dataset. For example, Berthelot40 proposed MixMatch applying a sharpen algorithm to minimize the entropy. Whereafter, they ameliorated this algorithm by distribution alignment and augmentation anchor41. Alonso42 designed a class-wise memory bank to enforce the model to yield similar features from unlabeled inputs as those from labeled pairs. Generative methods can model the real data distribution and thereby are parlayed for SSL43. Souly44 synthesized new samples by GAN7 based on unlabeled data to promote learning. Zhang45 employed GAN to distinguish outputs on both labeled and unlabeled images. Hung46 also applied a discriminator to improve the confidence of predictions for unannotated inputs. However, all of these SSL methods are designed for unlabeled images which share the same distributions with labeled data. In other words, both labeled and unlabeled images are sampled from one domain, which contributes to the discovery of consistency. Unfortunately, it is tricky to find a common smooth manifold in this paper due to the various domains of labeled and unlabeled samples. We discover that images from two domains still contain the same tissues (e.g.Blastocoel, ICM, TE, and ZP in Fig. 5), which introduces the semantic consistency assumption. Therefore, we make use of this semantic consistency in this work and struggle to explore the possibility of semi-supervised medical blastocyst segmentation under such situations of heterogeneous domains.Fig. 2Illustration of the proposed I2C2Net, which is composed of three main components: Intra-class context module (IACCM), inter-class context module (IRCCM), and consistency module (CM). Method

Fig. 2 illustrates an overview of the proposed framework. The I2C2Net framework uses intra-class and inter-class contextual information for blastocyst image segmentation in the training and test procedure. The CM only participates during the training phase, thus it does not add additional training workload during the testing phase. In this section, we first describe the basic and important formulation of our framework, i.e.I2CNet. Next, we present the details of IACCM, IRCCM, and CM . Then, we introduce the loss function used in this study for supervised learning. Eventually, we introduce the version of semi-supervised learning.

Intra-class context module

In I2C module, the IACCM \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{intra}$$\end{document} is designed to capture context information of the same class from an image, as shown in the bottom left of Fig. 2. Feature enhancements can be achieved both in the same scale and across different scales. Before intra-scale enhancements, every level of the logit map passes through a supervised module before fusion. Then, these logit maps are activated with neighboring high-level features that focus on context information of the same class.

To match the adjacent high-level features with the probability map of the K-class tissues, we orderly grouped \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_E^{n-1}$$\end{document} into K groups. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(R_E^{n-1})^k$$\end{document} is a matrix of size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_k \times \frac{H}{2^{n-1}} \times \frac{W}{2^{n-1}}$$\end{document} , where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_k$$\end{document} denotes the number of channels under the category k. To aggregate contextual information of the same class, we compute the region of representation for each class k as follows:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} (R_{intra}^{n-1})_i^k = (R_E^{n-1})_i^k {\cdot } (P^n)^k, i = 1,2,\cdots ,C_k. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}Finally, we orderly concatenate all \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(R_{intra}^{n-1})_i^k$$\end{document} and employ \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 \times 1$$\end{document} convolution to obtain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_{intra}^{n-1}$$\end{document} features, which contain the context information of the same class in the image. By grouping, we decompose a multi-category recognition task into multiple two-category recognition tasks, thereby reducing the learning difficulty of the model, and allowing the model to focus on learning features of the same category without interference from other categories. Moreover, the number of parameters is reduced to a certain extent.

Inter-class context module

Although IACCM effectively solves the problem of feature representation and learning within the same class, it does not take into account inter-class interconnection. As shown in Fig. 1f, the predictive error between classes constitutes a relatively large proportion of the image, including some pixels with insignificant structural features. To address this problem, the IRCCM \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{inter}$$\end{document} is designed; it can be used to handle relational information of different classes and a few hard pixels, as shown in the bottom right of Fig. 2.

The IACCM and IRCCM possess identical high-level features and follow the same grouping principle. Intra-class grouping accounts for the number of tissue categories, but inter-class grouping accounts for the correlation between tissues, which is represented by the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K-1$$\end{document} group. The blastocyst tissues occur in the following order from inside to outside: ICM, blastocoel, TE, ZP, and the background. Therefore, we only consider four organizational relationships: 1, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1+2$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1+2+3$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1+2+3+4$$\end{document}, where 1, 2, 3, and 4 represent ICM, blastocoel, TE, and ZP, respectively.

Consistency module

Previous research work on blastocyst tissue segmentation is mostly based on strong tag data from a single source, and the data is relatively clean, so it can guide the model to learn more detailed features besides semantic features. In clinical practice, different regions, races, and culture processes can make embryo images have great differences in texture, so these methods tend to have poor generalization ability. We observed that although the texture details vary greatly, the semantic information is highly unified, and the association between a single organization and organizations is also corresponding to each other. Therefore, based on the assumption of semantic consistency, we propose a semantic Consistency Module (CM) for training, which contributes to the approximation from the data distribution of the result output by our I2CNet to the distribution of the ground truth. It increases the model generalization, especially in the newly added heterogeneous domain unlabeled dataset for semi-supervised learning.

Fig. 3The architecture of the proposed consistency module. Our consistency module remedies the divergence of distributions between predicted maps and their corresponding labels.

The architecture of the proposed consistency module. Our consistency module remedies the divergence of distributions between predicted maps and their corresponding labels.

We send the final results of I2CNet in different conditions as the generation sample to the consistency module and get a score for evaluating the quality. The scoring results can positively supervise the segmentation network to fit the segmentation results from the perspective of distribution, especially playing a role in distinguishing the distribution between different categories. At the same time, the segmentation result is a multi-category valued graph, which contains relatively little information. The real samples (original RGB image) combined with the segmented results are sent to the discriminator for scoring to improve the semantic understanding ability and segmentation performance, as depicted in Fig. 3.

In practical operation, the label or segmentation result is combined with the original image as input, where the combination of the real label and the original image is a real result, and the combination of the segmentation result and the original image is a false result. The combined data passes through three convolution blocks, and each block is added with spectral normalization (SN)47 operation and LeakyReLU48 activation function (described in Fig. 3). SN is widely used in the training phase of GAN to ensure the stability of GAN. By applying SN to the consistency module to make it meet the 1-Lipschitz condition, it limits the severity of function changes, so that the model is more stable. After three convolution blocks, two self-attention49 layers followed by another convolution block layer filled in the middle. Eventually, the final result is obtained through a single layer of convolution. In terms of the use of loss functions, LSGAN loss50 is introduced for training through alternate updating, which will be clarified in the next section.

Semi-supervised I2C2Net framework

Previous sections introduce how to enhance the results of our I2CNet by adding a consistency module in the training for supervised learning, thus giving birth to our I2C2Net. In this section, we will explore how to employ the I2C2Net structure on a semi-supervised dataset and further improve the performance of the I2CNet.

Fig. 4Illustration of the proposed semi-supervised version of our I2C2Net. It retains the same structures of supervised learning and parlays both labeled and unlabeled images to promote better accuracy.

Illustration of the proposed semi-supervised version of our I2C2Net. It retains the same structures of supervised learning and parlays both labeled and unlabeled images to promote better accuracy.

Semi-supervised learning is often introduced to resolve the scarcity of annotated data, especially for deep learning methods due to their active demands for big data. Different from the traditional SSL situation, unlabeled blastocyst images provided here are collected by us while annotated data are employed from a sole open-source dataset for embryos. On account of the limited amount of this public dataset, we add our extra unlabeled data to explore whether it is useful to perform semi-supervised learning from heterogeneous domains. This situation is usual and pragmatic for measurements a hospital use can hardly be always the same. Different microscopes, operations, etc.can generate diverse blastocyst images. As seen in Fig. 5, the contrast, brightness, hue, saturation, transparency, and color balance of images from two domains reveal distinct differences. To address this discrepancy, we propose a semi-supervised version of I2C2Net leveraging these unlabeled data to further improve the accuracy of segmentation.

Although textures of blastocyst images from two domains are diverse, we still discover that they contain same tissues (e.g.Blastocoel, ICM, TE, and ZP in Fig. 5). In other words, these heterogeneous domains share the same semantic contexts. Based on this discovery, we propose a semantic consistency assumption: a well-trained network on one dataset may still retain partial (not full) semantic consistency when handling images from another heterogeneous domain. In fact, the I2C2Net trained on the public dataset can still distinguish rough regions of these tissues on our collections to some extent (shown in Fig. 4). On the other hand, Cluster assumption, i.e.the decision boundary should be in a region of low-density21, is a basic assumption in SSL. Training details

The backbone and two integrated context modules are randomly initialized through Kaiming initialization. The “poly” learning rate policy with factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(1 - \frac{epoch}{total\_{epoch}-epoch})^{0.9}$$\end{document} is adopted for training. The Adam is used as optimizer, initial learning rate and weight decay are set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$8 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document}. Synchronized batch normalization is implemented using PyTorch and enabled during training. For data augmentation, we process each sample with random scaling, random rotation, random cropping, and left–right flipping in the training stage.

Evaluation metrics

To evaluate the performance of the network, five commonly used indicators are used: accuracy, precision, recall, dice coefficient, and jaccard index51–54. These metrics are expressed as follows:

where TP denotes the number of pixels required to correctly identify the ICM, CM, TE, or ZP areas; TN is the number of pixels correctly identified as the background (non-segmented regions); FP represents the number of pixels misclassified as ICM, CM, TE, or ZP regions; and FN denotes the number of pixels misclassified as the background.

Accuracy indicates the effectiveness of the method in correctly distinguishing the background from the blastocyst tissues. Precision represents the proportion of all pixels correctly predicted to be blastocyst tissues. Recall indicates the effectiveness of the method in detecting blastocyst tissues. The dice coefficient and jaccard index are crucial for evaluating the overall segmentation performance and are effective for handling class imbalance; in addition, the influence of the misidentified and missed regions is considered. The Dice coefficient and Jaccard index range from 0 to 1; the closer the metrics are to 1, the more superior the segmentation performance.

Ablation study

Effectiveness of the IACCM

In embryo images, a phenomenon of intra-class continuous aggregation is observed, wherein the ICM is the most continuous core region, whereas the other tissues are band-like regions but also continuous. Ensuring that the model can learn to understand and recognize the features of the same category more accurately during the training process is essential. As shown in the Fig. 6, the proposed IACCM can be used for effectively learning features of the same category and modeling the continuous dependencies of pixels in the same category, thereby resulting in greater intra-class clustering. Effectiveness of the IRCCM

In medical images, especially in embryo images, focusing on the logical relationship between different tissues is crucial for the accurate segmentation of embryo tissues. Because the tissues in embryos occur from inside to outside according to a developmental law, the inner circle of the trophoblast must be connected to the cyst and the outer circle must be connected to the zona pellucida in the model. Therefore, the proposed IRCCM can learn and express embryonic features more efficiently through the progressive modeling of inter-class relationships. The implementation of the IRCCM improves the performance of the backbone, thereby increasing Dice by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1.69\%$$\end{document} and Jaccard index by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2.72\%$$\end{document}. The visualization results are shown Fig. 6, suggesting that the IRCCM is effective for improving inter-class recognition errors. These improvements indicate that introducing an IRCCM component can facilitate the effective modeling of the relationship between classes and increase the learning ability of the model.Fig. 6Qualitative results of ablation study.

Effectiveness of the IACCM and IRCCM

In this study, we obtain the I2CNet by integrating the IACCM and IRCCM. As illustrated in Table 2, combining the IACCM and IRCCM increases Jaccard index of the backbone by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3.46\%$$\end{document}; this increase is more considerable compared with the increase achieved using only IACCM (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3.18\%$$\end{document}) or IRCCM (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2.72\%$$\end{document}). Moreover, as depicted in Fig. 6, after combining the IACCM and IRCCM, not only the intra-class is more aggregated, but the inter-class relationship is also more reasonable and the boundary is more prominent. These results indicate that the IACCM and IRCCM can complement and enhance the effectiveness of each other and that the framework designed in this study is reliable and effective.

Effectiveness of the CM

Otherwise, in this study, we introduce a new consistency module, as described in section “Consistency module”. Table 2 demonstrates that the introduction of our consistency module effectively improves the performance of embryo segmentation. In particular, compared with the segmentation performance achieved when only IACCM and IRCCM are incorporated, Dice and Jaccard show stable improvements when CM is added. Moreover, Fig. 6 shows some visualization results, we can see that our I2C2Net has higher overlap ratio in details with the ground truth compared with I2CNet, which further indicates the effectiveness of the proposed CM. Incorporating CM improves the ability of the model to learn the distribution of data.

Conclusions

In this paper, we first investigated intra-class and inter-class relationships in embryo images and proposed an IACMM and IRCMM. The IACCM transforms a multi-class recognition task into multiple binary classification tasks and employs a supervised probability map to guide the learning of high-level features; this not only reduces the complexity of the model but also enhances the continuity of intra-class tissues. The IRCCM transmits the relationship probability map of different tissues to a high level to guide the learning of the model. Moreover, the probability map is remapped and assigned new weights to increase the weights of the boundaries and hard pixels, so that the model can more effectively focus on valuable sample pixels instead of blindly learning simple pixels. In addition, we introduced a consistency module, which further enhances the accuracy and comprehensiveness of embryo tissue identification because of the consistent evaluation of data distribution. The proposed I2C2Net integrates both IACCM, IRCCM, and CM to achieve state-of-the-art performance. Furthermore, we proposed a semi-supervised version of I2C2Net to address the SSL of heterogeneous domains, which is based on a semantic consistency assumption and a cluster assumption in a clinical way. Finally, we qualitatively and quantitatively verify the effectiveness of the proposed model through ablation experiments and comparison with other state-of-the-art methods.

A Comparative Bibliometric Analysis of Artificial Intelligence in 3D Hydrogel Printing: Insights from Scopus and Web of Science (2015–2025)
Authors: Mohamed Elnemr, Lianxi Zheng, Haider Butt
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study presents a bibliometric analysis of scientific publications related to artificial intelligence (AI) in hydrogel-based 3D printing, aiming to map the research landscape, trends, and scholarly impact of this emerging interdisciplinary field. Data were retrieved from Scopus and Web of Science (WoS) databases covering the period from 2015 to early 2025. Analytical tools, including Excel, Pycharm, OriginPro, Bibilioshiny and VOSviewer, were applied to examine publication growth, document types, geographical distribution, institutional output, and citation patterns. The results reveal a sharp increase in research activity since 2020, with China and the United States leading in publication volume. Articles and reviews constitute the majority of document types, reflecting both foundational work and critical evaluations. Institutional contributions were dominated by Chinese and Indian universities, while citation analysis highlighted influential works on smart biomaterials and AI-assisted biofabrication. The findings offer a structured overview of the field’s development and provide insights for researchers, policymakers, and practitioners aiming to navigate or contribute to this rapidly advancing domain.

Introduction

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) in additive manufacturing has opened up new possibilities for improving how materials are designed and processed. , Among the various materials used in 3D printing, hydrogels have received attention due to their high-water content, tunable properties, and compatibility with biological systems. These features make hydrogels suitable for applications in areas such as tissue scaffolding, drug release, and soft devices. However, their mechanical behavior and sensitivity to printing conditions can make it difficult to produce consistent and well-defined structures. ,

AI tools such as machine learning algorithms and deep learning models are now being applied to address these challenges. They help predict material behavior, suggest process parameters, and support decision-making during design and fabrication. , In hydrogel-based printing, AI has been used to improve print accuracy, assess printability, and reduce trial-and-error during experimentation. , This intersection of technologies is gradually shaping how researchers approach process control and material optimization. Taken together, AI functions as an enabling layer that connects design, material formulation, and process execution in hydrogel 3D printing. Its role is not limited to improving individual stages, but rather in supporting a more integrated and adaptive manufacturing workflow. ,

Although research output in this area has grown in recent years, there are few studies that provide an organized overview of the overall publication landscape. Bibliometric analysis offers a method to examine patterns in the published literature, track growth over time, and identify where research is coming from. It also helps show which institutions and articles have had more visibility and where collaborations are taking place.

This study presents a comparative bibliometric overview of research on AI in hydrogel 3D printing, using data collected from Scopus and Web of Science between 2015 and early 2025. It explores annual publication output, types of documents, country-level contributions, institutional output, and citation metrics. By comparing both databases, the study provides a more complete view of how this field has developed. The results are intended to serve as a reference point for those working in related areas and to support future research planning.

Methodology

This study is based on a bibliometric analysis of scientific publications related to the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in hydrogel-based 3D printing. Data were retrieved from two major academic databases: Scopus and Web of Science (WoS). The search was conducted in May 2025 and covers the period from 2015 to early 2025.

The search strategy included combinations of keywords such as “artificial intelligence”, “machine learning”, “3D printing”, “additive manufacturing”, and “hydrogel”. To ensure relevance and reduce noise, Boolean operators were used to form structured search queries that targeted only documents discussing the intersection of all three core concepts: AI, 3D printing, and hydrogels.

The search terms incorporated relevant synonyms for each concept. No custom stop words were excluded, and no language filters were applied.

In Web of Science, the search was performed using the Topic Search (TS) field, which includes title, abstract, author keywords, and Keywords Plus. The query used was: TS = ((“artificial intelligence” OR “machine learning” OR “deep learning” OR “neural networks”) AND (“3D printing” OR “additive manufacturing” OR “bioprinting”) AND (“hydrogel” OR “hydrogels”)). For Scopus, the equivalent query was entered using the Title-Abstract-Keyword (TITLE-ABS-KEY) fields: (“artificial intelligence” OR “machine learning” OR “deep learning” OR “neural networks”) AND (“3D printing” OR “additive manufacturing” OR “bioprinting”) AND (“hydrogel” OR “hydrogels”). The queries were tested and refined iteratively to exclude irrelevant records and improve the precision of the results.

The analysis in this study was conducted separately for Scopus and Web of Science to maintain the integrity of each database’s structure and indexing practices. As a result, no merging of records was performed across the two sources, and cross-database deduplication was not required. This approach was intentional and allowed for a comparative view of how each platform represents the literature on the topic and how each platform reflects research activity within the field. Given the differences in indexing criteria, coverage, and metadata formatting between the two databases, merging records could introduce inconsistencies and obscure comparative insights. The data were processed and visualized using Microsoft Excel, OriginPro, VOS viewer and PyCharm. Similar practices have been followed in other recent bibliometric studies involving structured database-specific analysis and visualization workflows. ,

Results and Discussion

Publication Trend Over Time

The annual distribution of publications related to artificial intelligence (AI) in hydrogel-based 3D printing reveals a steady upward trend over the past decade, particularly after 2020. As shown in Figure , the volume of research activity has grown considerably in both Scopus and Web of Science (WoS), although with some variation in coverage.

Number of publications per year from 2015 until May 2025 in Scopus and WoS.

Scopus returned a total of 178 documents, with the first relevant entries appearing as early as 2015. From 2015 to 2018, publication activity remained relatively limited, with fewer than 5 documents per year. A noticeable increase began in 2019, and the growth accelerated further from 2020 onward. The highest annual output was observed in 2024, with over 45 publications, followed by a decline in early 2025, which likely reflects the partial year.

In comparison, WoS retrieved 122 documents, with records starting in 2017. Similar to Scopus, WoS data show a sharp increase in output beginning around 2020. The number of WoS-indexed publications peaked in 2024, mirroring the trend in Scopus, though with overall lower counts.

Both databases show a consistent year-on-year increase from 2019 to 2024, indicating a growing interest in this interdisciplinary research area. The steep rise in publication activity during this period corresponds with broader trends in AI-assisted manufacturing and the expansion of bioprinting applications. The subsequent drop in 2025 output is not considered a decline but rather a result of the limited data available at the time of analysis.

Overall, these findings highlight a clear upward trajectory in research contributions at the intersection of AI, hydrogels, and 3D printing, reflecting its emergence as an active and developing topic within the scientific community.

Documents by Type

An analysis of document types was conducted to examine the nature of contributions within the research domain of artificial intelligence in hydrogel-based 3D printing. Figure a,b illustrate the distribution of document types indexed in Scopus and Web of Science (WoS), respectively.

Document type distribution of publications in a) Scopus (left) and b) Web of Science (right).

In Scopus (Figure a), the majority were classified as articles (48.9%) and reviews (37.1%), indicating a balance between original research contributions and literature summarizing existing developments. Other document types were present in much smaller proportions, including conference papers (4.5%), book chapters (2.8%), conference reviews (2.2%), and editorials (2.2%). Remaining categories such as books, errata, letters, and notes each accounted for approximately 0.6% of the total. This distribution suggests that while the field is research-intensive, it also supports reflective and integrative reviews. The presence of book chapters and conference-related documents points to an ongoing engagement with emerging ideas and dissemination through diverse scholarly channels.

In WoS (Figure b), articles (54.8%) again comprised the largest share, followed by reviews (37.3%), reinforcing the dominant role of these two formats. Additional categories included early access (4.8%), proceedings papers (2.4%), and meeting abstracts (0.8%), with no conference papers or book chapters explicitly listed in this dataset.

Taken together, both databases demonstrate that the majority of publications are research-focused, with reviews representing a substantial share. This balance reflects a field that is still developing but mature enough to support critical evaluation and knowledge synthesis.

Top 10 subject areas of publications in a) Scopus (left) and b) Web of Science (right).

Smaller but still relevant contributions come from Physics and Astronomy (5.3%), Pharmacology, Toxicology and Pharmaceutics (4.6%), Computer Science (3%), and Agricultural and Biological Sciences (1.4%). Following the Scopus classification, the Web of Science (WoS) subject area breakdown also shows that publications in this field are spread across multiple disciplines. Figure b shows the top 10 categories based on the share of documents. Categories with very low percentages are also not included.

The largest group of publications is indexed under Materials Science, Multidisciplinary (16.4%), followed by Engineering, Biomedical (15.2%) and Materials Science, Biomaterials (14.6%). These areas reflect the main technical focus of many studies.

Other notable areas include Nanoscience and Nanotechnology (12.9%), Polymer Science (12.3%), and Chemistry, Multidisciplinary (11.1%). These are closely connected to material development and process design. Smaller contributions come from Physics, Applied (6.4%), Chemistry, Physical (4.1%), Biotechnology and Applied Microbiology (3.5%), and Engineering, Manufacturing (3.5%).

Overall, WoS shows a similar pattern to Scopus but with slightly more specific categories. The results confirm that most publications fall under fields related to materials, engineering, chemistry, and biomedical applications.

Geographical Distribution

An assessment of country-level contributions reveals the global distribution of research activity related to artificial intelligence in hydrogel-based 3D printing. Figure a,b illustrate the number of publications by country, as indexed in Scopus and Web of Science (WoS), respectively.

Country-wise publication output in a) Scopus (top) and b) Web of Science (bottom).

According to Scopus, research in this field spans over 40 countries. The top contributors are China (59 documents), followed by the United States (52), India (21), the United Kingdom (13), and Australia (11). These five countries account for nearly 88% of the top-tier research output in this domain. Other active contributors include Italy (8), Germany and Turkey (7 each), South Korea (6), and Spain (5). A second tier of countriessuch as Canada, Iran, Singapore, Denmark, and several European and Asian nationsproduced between two and four documents each. A number of countries, including Egypt, Greece, Kenya, and Nigeria, are represented by single publications. This distribution suggests increasing global interest, though the majority of research remains concentrated in a small number of countries. The global map (Figure a) highlights the prominence of these countries through a green color gradient, with darker shades representing higher output.

In the WoS dataset, the overall geographic distribution pattern is similar, though with slightly lower publication counts. China again ranks first with 46 publications, followed by the United States (37), India (11), the United Kingdom (9), and Australia (8). Canada, Germany, and Italy follow with 6 to 8 documents each. The WoS dataset includes contributions from over 35 countries, including Belgium, Ethiopia, Hungary, Indonesia, Kenya, and Qatar, each contributing at least one publication. Figure b visualizes this data using a red gradient map, reinforcing the concentration of publications in a few leading regions.

Together, the two datasets suggest that research in this field is concentrated primarily in East Asia, North America, and parts of Europe. The parallel trends across both databases strengthen the reliability of the observed patterns and highlight the role of these countries in advancing work at the intersection of AI, hydrogels, and additive manufacturing.

The dominance of China and the United States in this field may reflect broader trends in national research priorities, funding allocation, and infrastructure for advanced manufacturing and biomedical technologies. China’s continued investment in artificial intelligence and biotechnology, supported by national initiatives and large-scale funding programs, likely contributes to its leading position. Similarly, the United States benefits from a strong university–industry research ecosystem and established expertise in both AI and biomedical engineering.

These patterns also point to potential collaboration opportunities between high-output countries and regions with emerging contributions, such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Africa. Strengthening research partnerships and knowledge transfer in underrepresented regions could help diversify the development and application of AI-driven hydrogel printing technologies.

Thematic and Trend Analysis

Thematic Mapping

To analyze the evolution and structure of the research landscape in 3D bioprinting and related technologies, thematic mapping was conducted using Biblioshiny. Thematic maps offer a simplified yet effective way to visualize the maturity and importance of key research themes. The x-axis (centrality) represents how relevant or connected a theme is to the broader field, while the y-axis (density) reflects how well-developed a theme is internally. Additionally, the size of each bubble corresponds to the frequency of the keyword’s appearance, indicating how commonly it is discussed in the literature.

The analysis was based on author keywords, as they better reflect the authors’ intended focus areas. The Louvain clustering algorithm was selected to detect communities of keywords due to its efficiency in identifying natural groupings within networks.

In the Scopus-based thematic map (Figure a), “bioprinting” is positioned as a motor theme, reflecting both high centrality and strong development. This suggests it is a well-established and widely connected topic. “Machine learning”, though highly central, has relatively lower density, classifying it as a basic theme. Other central themes include “3D printing” and “4D printing”, situated close to the motor quadrant, showing they are central but less mature. “Biocompatibility”, “antibacterial”, and “neural stem cells” appear in the niche quadrant, indicating specialized but well-developed research areas. “Additive manufacturing” and “drug delivery”, on the other hand, appear in the emerging or declining quadrant, signaling topics that are either gaining traction or becoming less active.

Thematic maps based on author keywords from (a) Scopus and (b) Web of Science datasets.

Interestingly, “tissue engineering” appears as a motor theme, signaling a stronger position in the WoS dataset than in Scopus. Meanwhile, “collagen” and “photocurable 3D printing” are positioned as niche themes, suggesting specialized but mature areas. These differences between the two databases highlight how the focus and maturity of research themes can shift depending on the source. Scopus places more emphasis on bioprinting and machine learning, while WoS shows stronger clustering around 3D bioprinting and tissue engineering. These differences demonstrate how using both databases can provide a more complete picture of the thematic structure and development of the research field.

Limitations

This study has limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. The analysis was conducted using two databases (Scopus and Web of Science) which, although widely used, do not cover all academic sources. Publications indexed in other databases such as PubMed, IEEE Xplore, or Google Scholar were not included.

The search strategy was based on a defined set of keywords intended to capture literature related to artificial intelligence, 3D printing, and hydrogels. While efforts were made to select relevant terms, studies using different or less common terminology may not have been retrieved.

Citation counts were used directly without accounting for differences in publication dates. This means that newer documents may appear less cited due to shorter exposure time, which can influence comparisons across publication years.

These limitations reflect common challenges in bibliometric research and should be considered when drawing conclusions from the findings.

Conclusion

This study provided a structured overview of publications on artificial intelligence in hydrogel-based 3D printing using data from Scopus and Web of Science between 2015 and early 2025. The results show a steady rise in publication activity, with a clear growth trend starting in 2020. Most publications fall under the article and review categories, suggesting a mix of experimental work and literature-based studies.

The subject areas represented are mostly related to materials science, engineering, chemistry, and biomedical fields, which reflects the technical and interdisciplinary nature of the topic. Geographic and institutional data show that China and the United States are leading contributors, supported by consistent involvement from institutions in Europe and Asia.

The analysis of the most cited papers highlights recurring interest in process control, material development, and AI-supported bioprinting techniques. Several studies appear in both databases, indicating shared visibility across platforms.

Overall, this bibliometric review maps out how the field has evolved, where contributions are coming from, and which studies have shaped the current research landscape. The findings may be helpful for researchers planning future work or trying to understand the broader context of this area.

A comparative study of robot-assisted laparoscopic radical cystectomy combined with urinary diversion: preoperative indicators and survival outcomes analysis at a single center
Authors: Conggui Liu, Yongpan Zhu, Yuanjian Liao, Xinqi Yang, Changyong Zhao, Neng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo compare the preoperative indexes and survival outcomes between da Vinci robot-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC) and laparoscopic radical cystectomy (LRC), and to analyze the clinical differences among the three types of urinary diversion modalities, namely, intracorporeal ileal neobladder (Studer type), Bricker's procedure, and ureterocutaneostomy.MethodsA total of 194 patients who underwent minimally invasive radical cystectomy in the Department of Urology of the Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University from January 2010 to December 2024 were retrospectively analyzed and divided into the laparoscopic group (152 patients) and the robotic group (42 patients) according to surgical modalities. Perioperative indexes and survival outcomes of the two groups were compared, and the clinical differences among the three urinary diversion modalities were analyzed.ResultsThe duration of surgery was significantly shorter in the robotic group than in the laparoscopic group (5.17 h vs. 6.75 h, P<0.001), but there was no significant difference in overall survival and complication rates between the two groups. Among the urinary diversion modalities, the median survival was the longest in the intracorporeal ileal neobladder group (55 months), which was significantly better than that of the Bricker procedure (25 months) and ureterocutaneostomy (13 months) (P<0.001). Multifactorial analysis showed that elevated ASA classification (HR=14.99-20.16) and male gender (HR=1.96) were independent risk factors in the laparoscopic group, whereas prolonged operative duration was associated with a reduced risk (HR=0.56). In patients with the Bricker procedure, ASA classification (P=0.001) and gender (P=0.032) significantly influenced prognosis.ConclusionRobot-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC) has significant short-term surgical advantages over laparoscopic radical cystectomy (LRC). Among different urinary diversion methods, intracorporeal ileal neobladder is associated with longer survival, although this largely reflects patient selection bias. The choice of specific surgical approach should be based on a comprehensive consideration of the patient's individual circumstances.

Introduction

Bladder cancer is categorized as Non-Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer (NMIBC) and Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer (MIBC); of these, MIBC accounts for approximately 20% of newly diagnosed bladder cancer cases. Despite radical cystectomy (RC) and pelvic lymph node dissection [1, 2], approximately 50% of patients eventually develop disease at distant sites due to disseminated micrometastases [1, 3]. Currently, radical total cystectomy is the most effective treatment for MIBC and maximizes patient survival. Laparoscopic Radical Cystectomy (LRC) and Robot-assisted Radical Cystectomy (RARC) have been gradually applied in the clinic, and their high-precision operation has achieved good efficacy [3]. Meanwhile, the choice of urinary diversion modality (intracorporeal ileal neobladder, Bricker’s procedure, or ureterocutaneostomy) directly affects patients’ postoperative quality of life and long-term prognosis. Existing studies have shown that there are significant differences in complication rates, recovery of urinary control function and risk of metabolic disorders between different surgical modalities [4–6]. However, there is still a lack of systematic evaluation on the combined use of minimally invasive techniques and urinary diversion strategies, especially the controversy of the high cost-benefit ratio of robotic surgery and the steep learning curve of laparoscopy, which urgently need to be supported by evidence-based medical evidence [7–9]. Differences in postoperative survival of bladder cancer patients are mainly related to age, gender, underlying health status, tumor stage [10, 11] and surgical approach (especially those with advanced age and comorbidities have a poorer prognosis, and surgical choices affect long-term outcomes) [12, 13]. Therefore, an in-depth study of the effects of radical resection and different urinary diversion strategies on the prognosis of bladder cancer patients is important for optimizing clinical treatment options. In this study, we analyzed the clinical differences among three urinary diversion modalities, namely ureterocutaneostomy, intracorporeal ileal neobladder (Studer type) and Bricker procedure, to provide an important evidence-based basis for the selection of minimally invasive surgery and urinary diversion strategies for bladder cancer, and to help clinicians make more accurate prognostic assessment and more individualized treatment decisions.

Data and methods

General information

Clinical data of 194 bladder cancer patients admitted to the Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University between January 2010 and December 2024 were collected.

Inclusion criteria: (1) pathologically confirmed diagnosis of bladder cancer with clinical stage of MIBC of T2 and above; (2) receiving radical cystectomy; (3) complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (1) the occurrence of distant metastasis; (2) the combination of other malignant tumors; (3) a previous history of pelvic surgery; (4) cardiac, pulmonary and other important organ function and coagulation function could not tolerate the surgery.

Surgical methods

All the surgeries were carried out by three urologists with more than 10 years of experience in the operation. All patients underwent radical cystectomy, and the surgical approaches included laparoscopic radical cystectomy and da Vinci robotic radical cystectomy. The specific definitions and core techniques of the three urinary diversion modalities are as follows: Intracorporeal Studer ileal neobladder: A 60–70 cm segment of ileum was selected 15 cm away from the ileocecal valve, the mesentery was preserved to ensure blood supply, and the intestinal segment was detubularized and folded to form a spherical neobladder. The ureters were anastomosed to the posterior wall of the neobladder using the Bricker method, and the neobladder was anastomosed to the urethral stump to restore the normal urinary flow pathway. Bricker procedure: A 15–20 cm segment of ileum was selected, one end was closed, the ureters were anastomosed to the intestinal segment, and the other end was pulled out through the abdominal wall to form a stoma, which was connected to an ostomy bag for urine collection. Ureterocutaneostomy: The ureters were dissected to the lower segment, transected, and the distal ends were pulled out through the abdominal wall to form a stoma, with direct urine excretion through the stoma. Each patient provided written informed consent to undergo the surgical procedure and for the use of their data for research purposes.

Observational indicators

The length of hospitalization, age, length of surgery, CT or MRI description of the tumor, cystoscopy description, creatinine, urinary diversion modality, ASA classification, BMI, percentage of neutrophils, percentage of lymphocytes, total protein, albumin, uric acid, leukocytes, hemoglobin, total platelet counts, AST/ALT, AG, urinary erythrocytes, smoking history, alcohol history, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hypertension, gender, staging, urinary nitrites, postoperative pathology, and death were recorded; and overall survival and progression-free survival of the follow-up patients were tracked.

Statistical methods

Multiple interpolation was used for missing data < 10%; SPSS 29.0 and R software (4.3.2) were used for data analysis. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and t-test was used for comparison between groups; continuous data with skewed distribution were expressed as M (Q₁, Q₃), and rank sum test for two independent samples was used; categorical data were expressed as n (%), and chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method was used, and the difference was considered statistically significant at P < 0.05 for comparison between groups.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 194 patients were included in this study, including 152 patients (78.35%) in the laparoscopic group and 42 patients (21.65%) in the robotic group. The differences between the two groups were statistically significant in terms of survival time, length of hospitalization, length of surgery, age, blood creatinine, mode of urinary diversion, death, history of alcohol consumption, maximum diameter of the tumor, degree of enhancement, and ASA grading (P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference (P > 0.05) in BMI, neutrophil percentage, lymphocyte percentage, total protein, albumin, hemoglobin, total platelet count, AST/ALT, AG, uric acid, urinary erythrocytes, smoking history, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, hypertension, gender, number of tumors, differentiation level, pathological stage, and urinary nitrites, etc., which are shown in Table 1.

Single factor Cox proportional risk model analysis

The results of the single-factor analysis showed that in the robotic group, the length of surgery (P < 0.001), the percentage of neutrophils (P = 0.037), the percentage of lymphocytes (P = 0.030), and hemoglobin (P = 0.007) were significantly correlated with the prognosis of patients (Table 2). In contrast, in the laparoscopic group, intracorporeal ileal neobladder (P = 0.005), history of alcohol consumption (P = 0.003), length of hospitalization (P = 0.024), length of surgery (P < 0.001), total protein (P = 0.014), AG (P = 0.047), and urinary erythrocytes (P = 0.009) were the significant factors affecting prognosis. In addition, ASA classification was strongly associated with prognosis in the laparoscopic group (P < 0.001).

Multifactorial Cox proportional risk model analysis

The multifactorial analysis further confirmed that the number of cystoscopic tumors (P = 0.005), the maximum diameter of tumors under CT (P = 0.044), ASA grading (P < 0.001 and P = 0.002), the length of the operation (P < 0.001), AG (P = 0.031) and uric acid (P = 0.001) in the laparoscopic group had independent prognostic value (Table 3). Specifically, the prognostic risk increased 14.99-fold and 20.16-fold, respectively, for each additional level of ASA classification (classification 2: HR = 14.99, 95%CI: 4.25–52.88, P < 0.001; classification 3: HR = 20.16, 95%CI: 3.04-133.79, p = 0.002). The prognostic risk was reduced by 44% for each unit increase in operative duration (HR = 0.56, 95%CI: 0.45–0.70). Other variables such as the prognostic risk was increased 5.75-fold in patients with cystoscopic tumor number ≥ 3 cm compared to patients with tumor number < 3 cm, whereas the prognostic risk was reduced by 57% in patients with tumor maximum diameter ≥ 3 cm on CT (HR = 0.43). The prognostic risk was increased by 5% for every one unit increase in AG level (HR = 1.05). The above results suggest that ASA classification, duration of surgery and metabolism-related indexes are key influences on the prognosis of patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery.

Survival outcome analysis

Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed that there were significant differences in the probability of survival among the three groups (log-rank test, P < 0.001): the survival advantage was most pronounced in the intracorporeal ileal neobladder group, with one case surviving at 130 months of follow-up, and its number at risk declined slower in long-term postoperative follow-up (number at risk was 1 at 130 months). The Bricker group had the largest initial sample size (121 cases) but had a more rapid decline in survival, with no surviving cases after 100 months postoperatively. The ureterocutaneostomy group had the fastest decline in survival, with no patients at risk after 80 months postoperatively, suggesting a poor long-term prognosis (Fig. 1). It must be emphasized that these survival differences largely reflect patient selection bias (see Discussion).

Discussion

Bladder cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors of the urinary system. With the acceleration of population aging in China, its incidence is increasing year by year, posing a serious threat to public health. When bladder cancer progresses to the muscle-invasive stage (MIBC), patient prognosis is significantly worse than that of non-muscle-invasive cases. Radical cystectomy is primarily used for treating muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) [14], accounting for about 25% of all bladder cancer cases at diagnosis [15]. This surgery is recommended for patients with clinical stage T2-T4a, N0-Nx, M0 bladder cancer, providing the best chance for long-term survival [16]. Radical total cystectomy is the standard surgical treatment for MIBC, involving complex operations such as total bladder removal combined with urinary tract reconstruction. Due to the complex anatomical structure of the surgical area, dense distribution of surrounding blood vessels and nerves, and the need to complete the surgery within the narrow pelvic space, the operation time is long, intraoperative blood loss is considerable, causing significant trauma to the patient. Additionally, the long postoperative recovery period and high risk of complications make this surgery one of the most challenging and risky in the field of urology [17]. Ileal conduit is one of the commonly used methods for urinary tract reconstruction after total cystectomy, first proposed by Bricker in 1952. Validated through long-term clinical practice by urologists, this procedure has gradually become a classic urinary diversion method after total cystectomy due to its reliability and effectiveness, and is still in use today [18, 19]. In 1992, Parra et al. [18] reported the first successful laparoscopic total cystectomy. Since then, laparoscopic technology has been rapidly promoted and developed in radical total cystectomy. Compared with traditional open surgery, laparoscopic surgery, due to its minimally invasive nature, can reduce intraoperative blood loss and tissue damage while accelerating postoperative recovery [20, 21]. However, due to the linear design of laparoscopic instruments, their flexibility is limited when operating in the narrow pelvis, especially during the separation of the posterior prostate wall and the anterior rectal wall, which may affect surgical efficiency. In 2003, Menon et al. [22] first successfully performed radical cystectomy using the da Vinci robotic system. This technological breakthrough provided a new solution for complex surgeries. By comparing the clinical outcomes of laparoscopic radical cystectomy (LRC) and robot-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC), and combining the impact of different urinary diversion methods on patient prognosis, this study reveals the key role of minimally invasive surgical techniques and urinary diversion strategies in bladder cancer treatment.

Interpretation of limitations in surgical method comparison

This study shows that the operative time in the robotic group was significantly shorter than in the laparoscopic group (5.17 h vs. 6.75 h, P < 0.001), which may be related to the accumulation of surgeon experience and standardization of techniques. However, no significant differences were shown between the two groups in core indicators such as overall survival rate and complication rate. This result is consistent with the study by Fan et al. [23], where the three-dimensional visualization, operational flexibility, and tremor filtration function of the robotic system allow for more precise completion of lymph node dissection and neurovascular bundle preservation, thereby reducing intraoperative bleeding and postoperative complications.As pointed out in the study by Asil E. et al. [24], totally robotic Bricker ileal conduit has lower perioperative complication rates than open and semi-robotic surgery, which is consistent with our finding that robotic-assisted radical cystectomy has short-term surgical advantages. Our study further supplemented that this advantage is also reflected in the consistency of perioperative safety between robotic and laparoscopic surgery in a single center with rich surgical experience. However, an important limitation of this study must be pointed out: data collection spanned 15 years (2010–2024). During this period, significant progress has been made in perioperative care, systemic therapy (such as neoadjuvant/adjuvant chemotherapy, immunotherapy), and imaging monitoring. Therefore, any differences in survival outcomes between patients who recently underwent robotic surgery (likely included later) and those who underwent laparoscopic surgery in earlier years may reflect more the progress of the times and the evolution of comprehensive treatment strategies, rather than solely the difference in surgical platforms. Furthermore, the high cost and steep learning curve of robotic surgery remain issues of concern, and its long-term cost-effectiveness needs further evaluation in the future.

Selection bias in survival analysis of urinary diversion methods

The results of survival analysis for different urinary diversion methods (intracorporeal ileal neobladder group median survival 55 months, significantly better than Bricker procedure’s 25 months and ureterocutaneostomy’s 13 months, P < 0.001) need to be deeply interpreted in the context of patient baseline characteristics. The data from this study clearly show that this significant survival difference largely reflects treatment-related patient selection bias, rather than being entirely determined by the superiority or inferiority of the surgical technique itself. Specifically, the distribution of advanced pathological stages (pT3/pT4) was significantly uneven among the three groups: the intracorporeal ileal neobladder group had the lowest proportion of pT3/pT4 tumors (only 2.56% of pT4), while the ureterocutaneostomy group had the highest (30.30% of pT4). Patients in the intracorporeal ileal neobladder group are typically younger (median age 59 years) and have lower ASA classification (71.79% are ASA 1–2), which directly reflects the current clinical practice guidelines and decision-making principles—this procedure is more likely to be recommended for patients with long life expectancy, good baseline physical condition, relatively early tumor stage (allowing more complete resection, lower expected recurrence risk), no serious comorbidities, and able to tolerate longer and more complex surgery. These patients inherently have a better prognostic foundation. Additionally, the intracorporeal neobladder preserves autonomous voiding function, potentially improving long-term prognosis by reducing risks of urinary tract infections and metabolic disorders [25]. As reported by Martini A et al. [26], robotic-assisted radical cystectomy with orthotopic neobladder in males can achieve excellent long-term functional outcomes, which provides strong evidence for our clinical observation. However, its surgical complexity is higher, and the incidence of early postoperative complications (such as intestinal obstruction, anastomotic leakage) needs further investigation. In contrast, ureterocutaneostomy is simple to perform but has poorer long-term prognosis. It is suitable for elderly patients or those with severe underlying diseases/comorbidities (high ASA classification), advanced tumor stage (e.g., T4), high pathological grade (e.g., high-grade urothelial carcinoma), and poor physical condition unable to tolerate longer or more complex surgery (e.g., intestinal anastomosis) [27]. These factors themselves are independent risk factors for poor prognosis. Choosing this procedure is primarily based on surgical safety, rapid recovery, and reducing perioperative risk, rather than expecting it to provide optimal long-term survival. Therefore, its poorer survival outcome is mainly driven by the baseline characteristics of the high-risk patient population it serves. The Bricker procedure, as the main clinical choice (accounting for 62.69% of patients), occupies an intermediate position in terms of patient population and survival outcomes. It is suitable for patients in relatively good physical condition but who may be unsuitable or unwilling to undergo intracorporeal neobladder surgery. Therefore, when interpreting survival data for different urinary diversion methods, correlation rather than causation must be emphasized. Intracorporeal neobladder is associated with longer survival, but this is primarily due to the selection of patients with a better prognostic foundation.

Functional outcomes of intracorporeal ileal neobladder

The functional outcomes of intracorporeal Studer ileal neobladder are closely related to patients’ postoperative quality of life. Combined with the clinical practice of our center and relevant research progress [26], the short-term and long-term functional outcomes are as follows: Short-term: Mild to moderate stress urinary incontinence is common in the first 3–6 months after surgery, with an incidence of about 60% in our center, mainly due to the recovery of detrusor function and urethral sphincter coordination. During this period, patients may need to use pads to manage urinary leakage, but most can gradually improve with pelvic floor muscle training. Long-term: After standardized pelvic floor muscle training, the rate of complete urinary control (day and night) in patients can reach more than 80% at 12 months after surgery; the voiding function is generally good, and the residual urine volume is mostly less than 50 ml, with no obvious impact on daily life. Martini A et al. [26] also reported similar results in their study on male patients, confirming that orthotopic neobladder reconstruction can maintain good urinary control and voiding parameters in the long term. It should be noted that functional outcomes are affected by multiple factors such as patient age, baseline physical condition, and postoperative rehabilitation training. Younger patients with no severe comorbidities tend to have better functional recovery, while elderly patients may have a longer recovery period or relatively poor urinary control due to weakened pelvic floor muscle function.

Multivariate analysis findings and clinical significance

Multivariate Cox regression analysis showed that in the laparoscopic group, ASA classification and male gender were independent risk factors for patient prognosis; while prolonged operative time was significantly associated with reduced risk. ASA classification reflects the patient’s preoperative systemic status. High classification (ASA Grade 3) is significantly associated with postoperative complications and mortality risk, suggesting the need to strengthen perioperative management for high-risk patients to optimize their prognosis. The association between prolonged operative time and improved survival is not a direct causal relationship, but is mainly related to two core factors: (1) Meticulous oncologic dissection: longer operative time in our center is mostly due to more thorough pelvic lymph node dissection and careful protection of neurovascular bundles, rather than surgical technical difficulties; (2) Surgical learning curve: with the accumulation of surgical experience of the surgical team (2010–2024), the operative time of later cases was more stable, and the longer operative time in early cases was accompanied by more standardized oncologic resection, which is a residual confounding factor of the retrospective study; (3) Patient selection: patients suitable for longer and more complex intracorporeal ileal neobladder reconstruction have better baseline physical condition and early tumor stage, which further amplifies this correlation. Additionally, the higher risk in male patients may be related to lifestyle factors such as smoking and alcohol consumption [14], requiring future in-depth analysis combined with molecular biological characteristics.

Research conclusions and clinical implications

This study confirms that robot-assisted surgery has significant advantages in surgical efficiency and is recommended for promotion in centers with appropriate conditions. Urinary diversion strategies should be individualized: for young patients with good baseline status, intracorporeal ileal neobladder can be prioritized to improve quality of life; for elderly patients or those with multiple comorbidities, the Bricker procedure or ureterocutaneostomy may be safer choices. Simultaneously, attention should be paid to preoperative ASA classification assessment and risk stratification for male patients to optimize perioperative management. CT can detect local recurrence or distant metastasis early during follow-up. Multivariate analysis in this study suggested that maximum tumor diameter on CT is an independent risk factor (HR = 2.46, P = 0.011), indicating that tumor volume may be related to aggressiveness and survival. Furthermore, the degree of CT enhancement may reflect tumor blood supply and biological behavior, and its association with molecular staging can be explored in the future combined with radiomics. For patients with multiple tumors detected on cystoscopy, more aggressive radical treatment (e.g., radical cystectomy) should be considered to reduce the risk of tumor residue or recurrence. Pellegrino F. et al. [28] pointed out in their study that standardized surgical procedures and perioperative management can significantly reduce the morbidity of robot-assisted radical cystectomy with intracorporeal neobladder. This finding made us realize that the lack of a unified surgical quality control standard in our retrospective study is a limitation, and we will adopt standardized surgical protocols in future research to further improve the research quality.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective study with a limited sample size, which may affect the generalizability of the results. Second, although some confounding factors were adjusted through multivariate analysis, selection bias and unmeasured confounding variables (such as tumor molecular subtype) may still influence the conclusions. Third, the choice of urinary diversion method is influenced by surgeon preference and patient willingness, requiring further validation in multicenter prospective studies. Fourth, the long study time span, during which medical practice and technology continuously advanced, may have confounding effects on the results. Fifth, due to the limitations of retrospective data collection, we are unable to provide the following data for statistical analysis: (1) Surgical margins: the pathological reports of partial patients only recorded the pathological diagnosis and staging, but did not systematically document the status of surgical margins (positive/negative); (2) Rate of adjuvant chemotherapy: the medical records of early patients did not clearly record whether adjuvant chemotherapy was administered, the type and course of chemotherapy; (3) Surrogate markers of surgical quality: lymph node yield, extent of lymphadenectomy were not systematically collected in the early stage of the study. The lack of these data affects the in-depth interpretation of the study results.

Future studies could incorporate long-term quality of life assessments and cost-effectiveness analyses to provide a more comprehensive basis for clinical decision-making. For patients with multiple tumors, the role of neoadjuvant therapy or combined therapy (e.g., immunotherapy) in reducing recurrence risk can be explored. Additionally, systematic analysis of the relationship between imaging parameters (such as CT texture features) and prognosis was not performed. Future multicenter prospective studies need to integrate CT radiomics, molecular markers, and long-term follow-up data to build more accurate prognostic models.

Conclusion

In terms of short-term operative efficiency, robotic-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC) has significant advantages compared to laparoscopic radical cystectomy (LRC). Among different urinary diversion methods, intracorporeal ileal neobladder is associated with longer survival, although this largely reflects patient selection bias. The choice of specific surgical approach should be based on a comprehensive consideration of the patient’s individual circumstances.

Rapid differentiation of VLA1553 and wild-type ECSA chikungunya strains in the context of an IXCHIQ vaccination campaign
Authors: Laura Pezzi, Etienne Frumence, Konrad Wesselmann, Gregory Mollé, Lucie Falgous, Nazli Ayhan, Raphaelle Klitting, Guillaume André Durand, Gilda Grard, Emilie Mosnier, Patrick Gérardin, Antoine Nougairède, Remi Charrel, Marie-Christine Jaffar-Bandjee, Xavier de Lamballerie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIn 2024–2025, Réunion Island experienced a large outbreak driven by East-Central-South African (ECSA)-2 lineage of chikungunya virus (CHIKV), leading to the implementation of a vaccination campaign using live-attenuated IXCHIQ (VLA1553). Currently, distinguishing vaccine-derived viremia from wild-type infection relies on genomic sequencing, which is resource-intensive and not routinely available in many diagnostic laboratories. To address this gap, we developed a duplex real-time RT-qPCR assay specifically designed to differentiate VLA1553 from wild-type ECSA strains circulating in La Réunion. The assay targets the non-structural protein 3 hypervariable region, including the 61-amino-acid deletion characteristic of the VLA1553 backbone. Analytical performance was assessed using a panel of CHIKV isolates representing different lineages and a VLA1553-like strain generated using an Infectious Subgenomic Amplicon system. Clinical performance was evaluated using samples from recently vaccinated individuals and patients infected with ECSA-2 strains, and compared with two RT-qPCR assays routinely used by the French National Reference Centers for arboviruses. The duplex assay proved to be specific, detecting wild-type ECSA strains exclusively in the FAM channel and the vaccine strain exclusively in the HEX channel, with no cross-reactivity. It demonstrated high sensitivity, with LOD95 values of 0.9 and 1.1 copies/µL for the vaccine-specific and wild-type–specific targets, respectively. All clinical samples were correctly classified, including one vaccinated individual simultaneously infected with a circulating wild-type strain, as confirmed by sequencing. This duplex assay provides a rapid and reliable method to distinguish vaccine-derived from naturally-acquired CHIKV viremia, supporting more accurate clinical and epidemiological investigations in settings where vaccination and viral circulation overlap.

Introduction

Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a mosquito-borne alphavirus, typically causing an acute onset of fever with joint pain. While symptoms usually resolve within 1–3 weeks, a significant number of patients suffer from debilitating rheumatologic symptoms for months or even years [1]. Since 2004, the virus has rapidly spread, causing large-scale outbreaks in the Indian Ocean, Southeast Asia, Americas, Africa and Europe [2].

In August 2024, La Réunion Island (France) reported the second CHIKV outbreak in its history, following the one occurred in 2005–2006 [3]. As of October 2025, over 54,000 confirmed cases and 43 deaths have been reported by public health authorities [4], caused by the East-Central-South African (ECSA)-2 lineage [5]. In April 2025, in response to the outbreak, authorities launched a vaccination campaign aiming at distributing 40,000 doses of VLA1553 vaccine (IXCHIQ, developed by Valneva) to individuals aged 65 years and older, with or without comorbidities [6]. IXCHIQ is the first licenced chikungunya vaccine worldwide, having received regulatory approval from the United States and Canada in 2023, and the European Union in 2024 [7]. It is a live-attenuated, single-dose vaccine, recommended for the prevention of chikungunya disease. It is based on an ECSA 3-IOL (Indian Ocean Lineage) strain, La Réunion (LR)2006-OPY1, in which a 61-amino acid portion of non-structural protein 3 (nsP3, hypervariable domain) was replaced with a linker of 7 amino acids [8].

In CHIKV-epidemic settings, distinguishing between vaccine viremia and natural infection is important and challenging. As shown in clinical trials, participants vaccinated with IXCHIQ frequently reported solicited systemic adverse events (AEs), including fever, headache, fatigue, myalgia, and arthralgia, symptoms that can mimic those of natural chikungunya infection [9]. Moreover, severe adverse events (SAEs) can occur after vaccination, and it is therefore essential to identify or exclude vaccine imputability. Genome sequencing enables such discrimination, but it requires several days and may delay case classification. Here, we present a duplex real-time RT-qPCR assay, referred to as the duplex VLA1553/WT ECSA assay, specifically designed to differentiate the VLA1553 vaccine strain from the wild-type (WT) strains belonging to the ECSA-2 lineage that have circulated in La Réunion in 2024–2025. We also report clinical validation of the assay using samples from vaccinated individuals (collected shortly after the launch of the IXCHIQ vaccination campaign) and from confirmed WT ECSA-2 CHIKV cases.

Materials and methods

Design

Complete genome sequences of the VLA1553 strain (retrieved from Valneva's patent) and a strain from the 2024 to 2025 outbreak in La Réunion (isolated from a traveller returning to metropolitan France in December 2024, GenBank Accession number PV593524.2) were aligned using MAFFT (https://mafft.cbrc.jp/). Primers and probe design was carried out manually based on sequence differences in the nsP3 region.

Analytical evaluation

Specificity of the duplex VLA1553/WT ECSA assay was assessed at the French National Reference Center (NRC) in Marseille using a panel of CHIKV wild-type strains provided by the European Virus Archive (EVAg, https://www.european-virus-archive.com/), representative of CHIKV genetic diversity (Table 1), as well as a VLA1553-like strain generated using the Infectious Subgenomic Amplicon system (Supplementary Data) as a surrogate for an IXCHIQ vaccine dose (not available for testing at the time of evaluation). To verify the absence of off-target amplification, a panel of alphaviruses and flaviviruses potentially co-circulating with CHIKV was tested with the duplex assay, including dengue (DENV), Zika (ZIKV), Mayaro (MAYV), o’nyong-nyong (ONNV), Sindbis (SINV), Eastern equine encephalitis (EEEV), Western equine encephalitis (WEEV) and Venezuelan equine encephalitis (VEEV) (Table 1). Supernatant of wild-type and vaccine strains was extracted using the EZ1-EZ2 virus mini kit v2.0 on EZ2 Connect Fx (QIAGEN). To evaluate the sensitivity of the assay, two in-house synthetic standard RNAs (one amplified by the VLA1553-specific assay and one amplified by the WT ECSA assay), produced as previously described [10], were tested in eight serial dilutions, with 12 replicates each. The LOD95 (lower limit of detection with a 95% detection hit) was calculated with probit regression analysis using IBM SPSS software version 24.

All RT-qPCR reactions were performed using Master Mix Fast Virus 1-Step TaqMan kit (Applied Biosystems) on a BioRad CFX96 thermal cycler (Bio-Rad Laboratories). The final reaction mix had a volume of 20 µL consisting of 10 µL sample RNA, 5 µL Master Mix and primers and probes at concentrations described in Table 2. Cycling conditions were: 50°C for 15 min; 95°C for 2 min; 45 cycles of 95°C for 15 s and 60°C for 45 s. Clinical evaluation

We compared duplex assay performance to those of the RT-qPCR assays used by the two French National Reference Centres (NRCs) for arboviruses. The first is the assay implemented by the NRC in La Réunion, which targets the envelope 1 (E1) gene [11], and is performed after nucleic-acid extraction with the NucliSENS® easyMAG kit on the eMAG or easyMAG automated systems (BioMérieux). The second is the dual-target assay used at the NRC in Marseille, designed to detect both the non-structural protein 1 (nsP1) and E1 genes [10], following extraction with the EZ1/EZ2 Virus Mini Kit v2.0 on the EZ2 Connect Fx platform (QIAGEN). Both NRCs performed the clinical evaluation using the Master Mix Fast Virus 1-Step TaqMan kit (Applied Biosystems) on a BioRad CFX96 thermal cycler (Bio-Rad Laboratories), using cycling conditions described for the analytical evaluation. For all samples, genome sequencing was performed as previously described [5] to support the identification of vaccine-derived versus WT ECSA viremia (based on the presence or absence of the nsP3 deletion, respectively) and to confirm the RT-qPCR results obtained with the duplex assay.

Validation of the VLA1553-specific assay was carried out using 13 clinical specimens obtained from individuals recently vaccinated with IXCHIQ, collected between 1 and 30 days post-vaccination. The IXCHIQ vaccine itself, which was not available at the start of the study for analytical evaluation, was subsequently tested as a clinical sample in order to confirm results of the analytical evaluation.

Clinical evaluation of the WT ECSA-specific assay was performed on 42 clinical samples from unvaccinated individuals with confirmed wild-type ECSA-2 CHIKV infection, collected either in La Réunion during the 2024–2025 outbreak or in metropolitan France from patients returning from the Indian Ocean region.

Results

Design

The duplex assay developed in this study contains: (i) a primer pair for vaccine detection flanking the nsP3 deleted region of VLA1553, (ii) a VLA1553-specific probe designed on the linker, at the junction of the deletion, (iii) a primer pair for the WT ECSA strains (forward before the deletion, reverse inside the deletion) and (iv) a WT ECSA-specific probe designed inside the CHIKV nsP3 region that is deleted in VLA1553 (Table 2, Figure 1). The two probes were labelled with FAM (WT ECSA strain) and HEX (vaccine strain) dyes. Analytical evaluation

The duplex VLA1553/WT ECSA assay demonstrated to be specific: in the FAM channel (WT ECSA-specific probe), no amplification was observed for the vaccine strain, while genomes of ECSA wild-type strains (ECSA-1, -2, and -3) were correctly amplified. Neither West African nor Asian lineage strains were detected, consistent with the assay’s design targeting the ECSA lineage specifically. In the HEX channel (VLA1553-specific probe), the vaccine strain was correctly detected, whereas no amplification was observed for any CHIKV wild-type strains, belonging to the ECSA, West African, and Asian lineages. No amplification was observed with the duplex assay when tested against the specificity panel comprising alphavirus and flavivirus strains.

The LOD95 identified is 0.9 copies/µL of RNA [95% CI, 0.4–23.5] for the VLA1553-specific assay, and 1.1 copies/µL of RNA for the WT ECSA assay [95% CI, 0.9–21.1].

Clinical evaluation

Among the 13 clinical specimens obtained from individuals recently vaccinated with IXCHIQ, twelve were correctly detected by both the duplex assay and the reference tests, including those with low viral loads (Figure 2). Moreover, the duplex assay detected one, weakly positive specimen, negative with the reference test [11]; the individual had been vaccinated six days prior to blood collection, and their viremia was likely low and close to the detection limit of RT-qPCR assays, which may explain the discordance between the two tests. No FAM signal was detected in any of the VLA1553-positive samples using the duplex assay. The IXCHIQ vaccine (lot 24I006) was tested a posteriori: the duplex VLA1553/WT ECSA assay yielded a Ct value of 25.8 in the HEX channel with no signal in the FAM channel. The same aliquot tested with the reference assay [11] yielded a Ct of 25.5.

In the clinical evaluation of the WT ECSA-specific assay, the duplex assay demonstrated sensitivity identical to that of the two reference assays, with the 42 natural infection cases consistently detected in FAM channel and absence of signal in HEX channel (Figure 2).

The duplex assay detected both natural and vaccine-induced viremia in an individual vaccinated while already symptomatic in La Réunion in April 2025 (Figure 3). CHIKV infection initially manifested as several days of marked asthenia and transient arthralgia, without fever; six days after symptoms onset, IXCHIQ was administered. Within 48 h after vaccination, the patient developed an acute systemic illness characterized by fever, rash, headache, ocular involvement (conjunctivitis with uveitis), and diffuse polyarthralgia. Symptoms partially resolved over the following days; however, a secondary maculopapular rash and recrudescence of arthralgia occurred approximately one week after vaccination. Prolonged arthralgias were subsequently observed, including an Achilles tendon tendinopathy persisting for six months. An acute-phase blood sample collected two days post-vaccination tested positive by duplex RT-qPCR, demonstrating dual detection and confirming that vaccination was administered during early natural infection. This finding was corroborated by complete genome sequencing, which revealed the simultaneous presence of wild-type ECSA-2 CHIKV (84.1% of reads) and the VLA1553 vaccine strain (15.9% of reads) (Supplementary Figure 1). Discussion

In this study, we designed and validated a duplex RT-qPCR assay capable of distinguishing viremia following IXCHIQ vaccination from viremia due to natural infection with an ECSA CHIKV strain. The assay demonstrated high analytical sensitivity and specificity, as well as clinical performances comparable to two CHIKV RT-qPCR assays employed by the French National Reference Centres for arboviruses, across samples from both vaccinated and naturally infected individuals.

The assay was specifically designed for deployment in La Réunion during an active outbreak with concurrent IXCHIQ vaccine use, where rapid implementation was essential. Consequently, a simplified design targeting the ECSA-2 lineage only was selected. It also detects ECSA-1 and ECSA-3 lineages, which is an added benefit, but not West African (WA) or Asian lineages. This limitation is due to the necessity of targeting the nsP3 hypervariable domain, which contains the deletion specific of IXCHIQ and exhibits substantial divergence between ECSA and non-ECSA lineages (Figure 1). Achieving broad detection across all CHIKV lineages would have required extensive primer and probe degeneracy, potentially reducing analytical sensitivity, and was therefore not pursued. Nonetheless, the duplex design remains adaptable: in settings where non-ECSA lineages circulate (for example in Western Africa, where Western African lineage is currently predominant [12]), the wild-type component could be modified through lineage-specific primer and probe redesign, followed by appropriate validation. The framework presented in this study can thus serve as a template for such adaptations.

As expected, existing CHIKV assays targeting genomic regions outside nsP3 (e.g. [10,11]) readily detect vaccine viremia. The hypervariable domain of nsP3 is not a reliable target for CHIKV detection due to its lack of conservation across CHIKV lineages. Consequently, commonly used in-house and commercial RT-qPCR assays, which typically target nsP1 or the envelope gene, cannot distinguish vaccine-induced viremia from natural infection. In the absence of sequencing capabilities, or when rapid diagnostic results are needed, the VLA1553-specific RT-qPCR assay described here offers a valuable tool to assess potential vaccine attribution in cases of severe disease occurring in CHIKV-endemic or epidemic settings.

We observed detectable levels of vaccine viremia in plasma after IXCHIQ administration. Viremia and viral shedding (e.g. in urine) are well-documented events following the administration of live-attenuated vaccines, as reported for, e.g. the yellow fever virus (YFV) vaccine Stamaril [13], which prompted the development of a YFV vaccine-specific RT-qPCR assay [14]. The persistence of VLA1553-induced viremia has been previously described in a phase 1 trial study, showing that viremia peaked at day 3 post-vaccination (reaching 7·4 × 10^4 genome copy equivalents/mL with the dose used to develop IXCHIQ) and resolved in all participants by day 14 [15]. Awareness of prolonged viral shedding can assist healthcare professionals in distinguishing residual vaccine viremia from a natural infection, supported by appropriate laboratory confirmation (e.g. vaccine-specific RT-qPCR assay or sequencing).

A limitation of this study is the small sample size of vaccinated patients, which restricts the evaluation of the duplex RT-qPCR assay’s performance. However, additional data will soon become available, as this assay is currently being employed to characterize viremia kinetics in a vaccinated cohort on Réunion Island. CHIK-RE-VAC is a prospective study assessing the effectiveness and immunogenicity of IXCHIQ in vulnerable individuals, including elderly and patients with comorbidities; additional groups, such as healthcare workers, were enrolled retrospectively. In the framework of this study, vaccine-induced viremia is being monitored longitudinally in blood samples for up to 12 months post-injection using the duplex RT-qPCR assay. This study is expected to provide valuable insights into the persistence of VLA1553 RNA in vaccine recipients, and will help confirm the preliminary data on the assay’s sensitivity and specificity generated in the present study.

Despite the recent suspension of the IXCHIQ licence by the US Food and Drug Administration, the vaccine remains authorized by several regulatory authorities, including the European Medicines Agency (EMA). It is therefore likely that additional vaccination campaigns using IXCHIQ will be conducted in both endemic and epidemic settings; in addition, phase 3b and phase 4 clinical trials are planned in Brazil and Kenya. In these contexts, the persistence of vaccine-induced viremia should not be considered a barrier to IXCHIQ deployment; however, AEs and SAEs occurring in vaccine recipients do warrant systematic investigation using molecular tools such as the duplex assay described in this study. The differentiation of vaccine-derived and wild-type infection is essential for accurate case assessment and safety monitoring.

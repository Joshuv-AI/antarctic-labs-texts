Dataset on SERS spectra of amber and amber-like resins
Authors: Anna Kundalevich, Aleksandr Zozulya, Andrey Zyubin, Maksim Bahdasarau, Ilia Samusev
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The article presents experimental data on SERS spectroscopy of amber and amber-like resins of K1 and K2 geological age. SERS spectra were taken using with Virsa Raman analyzer (UK) with λ=532 nm. The data includes preprocessed SERS of amber and amber-like resins, obtained to identify markers that determine spectral characteristics of natural resin and its deposits.

Background

The scientific novelty of the data obtained lies in the successful application of the SERS to describe fossil resins of different geological age and geographical origin. The paper presents the results of the development of a new method for collect SERS spectra of fossil resins from the K1 and K2 period.

Data Description

The data set described in this paper includes SERS of 16 samples of ambers and amber-like resins. All spectra were placed in an Excel spreadsheet. The data file named «Dataset_of_Fossilized resins_K1_K2» contains 1 sheet of experimental data. Datasheet contains information about the Raman shifts and relative spectral intensity for each sample studied. The Excel file contains spectral information for all 16 samples.

Some samples have oxide film and to make experiment clear, oxide film was removed from the sample surface. Ambers were cleaned using a file with an abrasiveness of 100–240 grit, or the studied images were split to carry out spectral imaging of an area without an oxide film. Afterwards, a purified amber sample was weighed in a box with a lid, to which ultrapure water was then added in such an amount that the ratio of the mass of amber in grams to the volume of water in milliliters was equal to 0.01375 ml. The samples were then boiled for 1–2 h. Then, amber extract has been taken to SERS experiment.

SERS substrates preparation

SERS experiment

To achieve SERS effect, silver nanoparticles were obtained according to the protocol described in the article [1]. Then the nanoparticles were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 30 min, after which the supernatant was removed and the sediment was diluted with distilled water. To implement the SERS technique, silver NPs were made in accordance with the method of chemical reduction from AgNO3 (99 % purity) salt. When obtaining silver NPs by this method, the method of chemical reduction, used for the chemical synthesis of silver and gold, was used. In 500 ml of distilled water, 50 mg of AgNO3 silver nitrate salt was dissolved. The solution was brought to a boil, while intensively stirred, after which 9 ml of a solution of aqueous sodium citrate Na3C6H5O7 with a concentration of 1 % was added to it. After thorough, the solution mixing changed color from transparent to yellow-green. Thus, silver NPs were reduced from the silver nitrate salt. The process of chemical reduction of silver corresponded to the following equation:(1)6AgNO3+3Na3C6H5O7→6Ag+3Na2C5H4O5+3CO2+3NaNO3+3HNO3

The plasmonic absorption spectra and the size of silver nanoparticles were determined on a Shimadzu-2600 spectrophotometer and a Photocor Complex (LTD «Photocor», Russia) dynamic light scattering setup, respectively. The particle size was determined as d = 44 nm. The maximum plasmonic absorption was recorded at a wavelength of λ=406 nm. Since silver hydrosols have stable plasmon absorption in the visible region and their spectrum overlaps with the absorption and fluorescence spectra of amber, it was therefore possible to implement the processes of plasmon energy transfer in the amber/NP complex.

The absorption spectrum of the resulting particles was then recorded to confirm the presence of a plasmonic absorption maximum. Then a SERS substrate based on optically transparent quartz was prepared. For each surface, its own “well” was prepared, consisting of three layers of previously prepared sol with a volume of 2 µL for each layer. Then 2 µL of amber extract was added to each well. The SERS experiment began using Renishaw Virsa (Renishaw, UK) Raman analyser checking the surface for noise and determining the enhancement factor using R6G. Afterwards, samples were directly analyzed at 5–7 points from one liquid drop of solution. Before spectral registration, surface enhancement was checked using R6G dye. The resolution of spectrometer was 2,5 cm−1. The acquisition time varied from 10 to 30 s, the laser power at sample was 10 mW and varied when analyzing different samples down to 1 mW on sample. The spectral range for analysis was in range from 350 cm−1 to 3200 cm−1 was studied during experiment.

The results were saved in .txt format. Data processing was carried out using the Origin 2022 program. Since the samples were obtained under different conditions, the baseline was adjusted and the obtained spectra were normalized by 100, and for the most correct comparison of the results obtained. In the Origin2022 program, smoothing was performed using the FFT filter method with a cutoff frequency of 0.0112. This method and cutoff frequency were chosen because they removed excess noise obtained during shooting, while retaining all the maxima characteristic of the samples under study.

During the experiment, lasers with wavelengths λ = 532 nm was used. When registered the power values varied from 10 mW to 60 mW, and the accumulation time from 10 s to 45 s, depending on the shooting area, thus reliable spectra were obtained.

Frailty associated with nutritional status, functionality, physical activity and socioeconomic level, in older adults in Ecuador, post-pandemic
Authors: Lorena Esperanza Encalada-Torres, Sandra Victoria Abril-Ulloa, Silvia Rocío Sempértegui-León
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAging is a complex, multifactorial process in which nutritional alterations, sedentary lifestyle, and chronic diseases can increase the risk of functional limitations, dependence, and disability in older adults. Identifying the degree of frailty is essential for designing programs to enhance their quality of life. The objective of the study was to determine the prevalence of frailty and its association with nutritional status, functionality, physical activity, and socioeconomic level in older adults in Azuay, a province in Ecuador, during post-pandemic period of 2023.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted with 294 older adults from Azuay Anthropometric measurements were taken, and validated questionnaires were administered to collect demographic data. Frailty was assessed using criteria validated by Fried and Watson. The Katz Index was employed to determine the level of dependency, physical activity was assessed using the short version of IPAQ, and socioeconomic level was determined according to the Ecuadorian Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INEC). Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0, employing frequency distribution, measures of central tendency (mean) and dispersion (standard deviation). Odds Ratio (OR) with a 95% confidence interval to explore associations.ResultsThe mean age was 74.1 years (± 7.1), with a higher prevalence between 65 and 74 years (52.4%). Women represented 72.1% of the sample, nearly half were married, 67.7% had completed primary education, and over a quarter were engaged in household work. According to BMI, women showed a higher prevalence of overweight and obesity, along with greater abdominal obesity. Both sexes reported moderate levels of physical activity. The prevalence of frailty in women was higher than in men (10.4% vs. 4.9%) and was significantly associated with physical inactivity (OR = 1.634, 95% CI 1.458–1.817; p < 0.001) and low socioeconomic level (OR = 2.176, 95% CI 1.027–4.608; p < 0.039).ConclusionsFrailty prevalence was high among older adults and was significantly associated with low physical activity and low socioeconomic status. These findings highlight critical challenges for Ecuador’s public and private healthcare systems in implementing proactive measures to delay the onset of frailty and improve quality of life in this population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-025-06232-w.

Introduction

Globally, the population aged 60 and older is rapidly increasing. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the proportion of older adults is expected to rise from 12% in 2015 to 22% by 2050, with 80% living in low- and middle-income countries [1]. In the Americas, this demographic shift has been accompanied by psychosocial and physiological changes. In Ecuador, a lower-income South American country, the older adult population has also grown significantly. Based on the 2022 Population and Housing Census and projections by the Ecuadorian National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INEC), the population of children and adolescents is expected to decline between 2030 and 2050, while the number of older adults will rise across the country [2]. According to this report, in 2022, there were 1,520,590 people aged 65 and over, with more women at 53.6% (815,136) than men at 46.4% (705,454). Furthermore, the proportion of older adults increased from 6.2% in 2010 to 9% in 2022 [3].

Given the projected rise in the older population across Latin America and the Caribbean, it is essential to consider a comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA) that allows for the evaluation and identification of associations between different pathologies. Aging leads to an increased risk of disease with a progressive decline in mental and physical capacity and a greater likelihood of death [4, 5]. A study conducted in 196 older adults attending a health center in a marginalized urban area of Guayaquil, Ecuador, used CGA to assess clinical, social, mental, and functional health. The findings revealed vision difficulties (47.4%), nutritional risk (52%), malnutrition (6%), severe social impairment (13%), cognitive impairment (40%), depression (8.2%), and functional dependency (46.9%), demonstrating that CGA is a practical and easily applicable tool in primary care settings [6]. Another study involving 44 older adults from a geriatric center in Ecuador also showed high rates of psychiatric, nutritional, and social behavior disorders, as identified through CGA [7]. Therefore, identifying frail older adults—those with increased vulnerability, reduced resilience, and greater risk of disability—is a public health priority, especially as frailty negatively affects their quality of life. This was particularly evident during the COVID-19 pandemic, which saw the highest mortality rates among frail individuals [8, 9].

The evidence that frail older adults are likely to experience early disability, institutionalization and death associated with various sociodemographic and clinical variables has been studied in different countries concerned with improving the quality of life of their aging populations. For instance, in Chile, Troncoso-Pantoja et al., reported that women had a higher prevalence of pre-frailty than men (62.6% vs. 55.4%), and frailty was twice as common in women as in men (14.1% vs7.7%). The prevalence of frailty by age was 9.7% and 4.4% in men and women at 60 years, respectively, reaching a prevalence of 98.3% and 96.1% in 100-year-old women and men [10]. In Mexico, a study of older adults with type 2 diabetes found significant associations between nutritional status and frailty (p = 0.000), with a prevalence of 35.2% frailty, 43.8% pre-frailty, and 21.1% robust. In addition, 15.6% were malnourished, 43.8% were at risk of malnutrition, and 40.6% were normal [11].

On the other hand, frailty is recognized as a multifactorial syndrome involving increased individual vulnerability and loss of resistance to external stressors, leading to decreased strength, endurance, and physiological function [12], it is logical to suppose that physical exercise during aging, especially in frail older adults, may reduce complications from Non-Communicable Chronic Diseases (NCDs), thus decreasing functional deterioration and the risk of death [13]. Studies in Chile have shown that frail older adults engage in significantly less physical activity (PA) (95% CI: 399.5-185.7; p = 0.001) and more sedentary behavior (95% CI: 1.97–5.14; p = 0.001) [14]. In Spain, the results of the study by Higueras S, in 2021 suggest that being physically active was associated with lower total mortality and mortality from cardiovascular disease among pre-frail and frail older adults [15]. Therefore, the more PA older adults engage in, the better their physical condition will be, improving sarcopenia, physical function, cognitive performance, and mood [16, 17].

Socioeconomic status is another factor strongly associated with frailty. In Spain, the study by Menéndez-González in older adults with low economic status (47.8%) showed a frailty prevalence of 27.7% and a pre-frailty prevalence of 44.9% [18]. Similarly, in Cuba, according to the study by Herrera-Pérez, older adults in Social Class Level IV or low (skilled and semi-skilled manual workers) had a pre-frailty prevalence of 26.4% and frailty prevalence of 8.3%. In contrast, those in Social Class Level V or very low (unskilled manual workers) had a pre-frailty prevalence of 66.7% and frailty prevalence of 88.3% [19].

Considering that the Ecuadorian population will experience an increase in the number of older adults, the purpose of this study was to determine the prevalence of frailty and its association with nutritional status, functionality, physical activity, and socioeconomic level in older adults in Azuay, a province in Ecuador, during the post-COVID-19 pandemic period.

Methods

A cross-sectional study was conducted in a non-probabilistic sample of 294 older adults who regularly attended senior clubs at various levels of care within the Ministry of Public Health (MPH) in Zone 6 of Azuay province. To obtain the sample, the following sample restrictions were included: population of older adults belonging to the MPH clubs, which were 1,332, prevalence of 35.2% of frailty (11), with an error of 5%, 95% CI, obtaining a minimum sample of 278 older adults. The study was conducted following the return to normalcy after the COVID-19 confinement period. Participants included men and women aged 65 or older who regularly attended MPH senior clubs in zone 6 and had signed informed consent forms. Individuals with psychiatric disorders, altered consciousness, cognitive impairments, or defined hearing or vision disabilities were excluded.

A structured survey was administered to gather demographic data, anthropometry measurements and clinical indicators. Frailty was assessed using the validated criteria of Fried and Watson. Functional status was measured with the Katz Index, physical activity with the short version of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ-c), and socioeconomic status using the INEC stratification survey. A pilot study was conducted in a population different from the study’s population for instrument validation and quality control. Cronbach’s alpha was used to assess reliability for Likert-scale items. Data collection took place at different levels of service within the Ecuadorian Ministry of Public Health (MPH) where senior clubs operated, after participants signed informed consent.

For the assessment of nutritional status, the Body Mass Index (BMI) was considered as a “proxy”, or indirect indicator to estimate the nutritional status of the older adults [20]. Anthropometry was performed by measuring weight and height using a SECA balance which was previously calibrated according to international standards. For Body Mass Index (BMI), a qualitative-quantitative scale for older adults according to the WHO was used, considering underweight ≤ 23.0; normal > 23 to < 28; overweight ≥ 28 to < 32; and obesity ≥ 32 [21]. Malnutrition was defined as the sum of underweight, overweight and obesity. Abdominal circumference was measured using a SECA measuring tape with a precision of 1 mm, establishing normal reference values of ≤ 80 cm in women and ≤ 90 cm in men and values higher than these were considered as visceral obesity [22]. Frailty was assessed using the 5 criteria validated by Fried and Watson [23], functionality was assessed using the Katz Index to determine the level of dependence [24].

For physical activity, the IPAQ-c questionnaire [25] evaluates the last seven days with seven questions that measure: intensity (light, moderate or vigorous), frequency (days per week) and duration (time per day). The scores for each activity are calculated in MET (Metabolic Equivalents of Task), which is obtained by multiplying the MET intensity of each activity (Walking: 3.3 Mets; moderate physical activity: 4 Mets and vigorous physical activity: 8 Mets), by the time in minutes of performing the activity in one day and by the number of days per week that it is performed, establishing 3 levels of physical activity according to the IPAQ-c criteria as indicated in Table 1. Socioeconomic status was classified using the INEC stratification survey [26].

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Descriptive statistics included frequencies, percentages, measures of central tendency (mean), and measures of dispersion (standard deviation). To assess association, Odds Ratio (OR) with a 95% confidence interval was used.

The study was approved by the Bioethics Committee of the Health Area of the University of Cuenca (protocol code: COBIAS 2020-093EO-I).

Results

Women made up 72.1% of participants. Nearly half were married, and 67.7% had completed primary education. Over a quarter were engaged in household work, and about 10% were small-scale merchants, artisans, or small-scale industrial workers (Table 2).

Discussion

Frailty is a multifactorial syndrome with higher prevalence in older adults. Therefore, alongside Fried’s frailty criteria, it is important to consider related health indicators in this vulnerable population, such as nutritional status, physical activity, functionality, and social factors.

This study included 294 older adults belonging to health center clubs in the urban area in the city of Cuenca, Ecuador, with a mean age of 74.1 years (± 7.1). The largest age group was 65 to 74 years (52.4%), which correlates with the study by Lara M et al., in Spain in 2021 in Primary Care centers, and with the study by Herrera Pérez et al., in Peru in 2020, where the mean age was 79.8 years and 73 years, respectively. The female participants were the most prevalent in this study (72.1%), in agreement with the results of the two studies mentioned above (59.1% and 76.7%, respectively) [18, 19]. Approximately, half of the participants in this study were married, which was lower than the prevalence reported by Quintero et al., in Venezuela in 2018 (65.7%), although similar in terms of primary education level which was 67.7% in this study and 68.6% in Venezuela [27]. More than a quarter of participants performed household work (30.3%), and approximately 10% were small merchants, artisans, and small-scale industrialists, with a lower prevalence compared to the study by Becerra-Partida E, in Mexico in 2021 (52.69%) [28]. These demographic profiles are consistent across Latin American countries, including the present study, reflecting similar realities.

According to the BMI of the participants, women had a higher prevalence of overweight and obesity compared to men. About one-third of older women were overweight, and one in four were obese. Almost all women showed abdominal obesity, which was similar to the findings of Guerrero et al., in Mexico, where the mean BMI in women was 27.59 (SD 5.73) and in men was 24.53 (SD 4.27) [29], as well as the study by Chavarria et al., in Chile, Bernui et al., in Peru [30], or the study by Silveira et al., in Brazil, where the prevalence of visceral obesity was 81.25% in women and 74.51% in men using the same cutoff point (≥ 90 for men and ≥ 80 for women), which may be attributed to women generally having a longer life expectancy than men. On the other hand, the prevalence of overweight, obesity, and overall abdominal obesity in the present study was higher than that reported by Colombia (44.9%, 27.3%, and 58.1%, respectively) [31], and Mexico (49.4% and 28.7%, respectively) [32]. Furthermore, the prevalence of malnutrition (sum of underweight, overweight and obesity) was 60%, higher in women (65%) than in men (46.4%), results similar to those observed by García et al., in Cuba with 71.11% [33], and Zabala et al., who reported that malnutrition was higher in women than in men (80.4% and 70.46% respectively) being data higher than the present study [34]. Both men and women in this study were generally engaged in moderate physical activity, similar to studies conducted in Chile [35], in Spain [25], and higher than that evidenced in other studies in Spain by Lores et al., who found 20% of the participants with moderate PA [36], or by Ramirez in Spain, who found the same level in 30.4% of men and 56% of women [37].

According to the Katz Index, all the participants in the present study were classified as independent, which differs from findings of Martinez-Mendoza in Mexico, who reported a dependency rate of 62% [38], and Villarroel in Northern Ecuador with an independence rate of 75.7% and a dependency rate of 20% [39]. Therefore, it is important to consider that the most disabled are dependent on basic activities of daily living, needing help from others to carry them out. In about 50% of the participants, their dependency was associated with the severity of underlying comorbidities and fraitly; the other 50% develop the disability after a cerebrovascular event or hip fracture, with disability being a risk factor for hospitalization and mortality.

The prevalence of frailty was twice as high in women as in men (10.4% and 4.9% respectively), with an overall prevalence of frailty of 8.8% and pre-frailty of 62.9%, being the same higher in women than in men (66.0% and 54.9% respectively), results similar to the study by Torroncoso-Pantoja et al., in Chile, which according to data from the 2017 National Health Survey using to the Fried methodology identified that 10.9% of the Chilean population over 60 years of age had frailty (≥ 3 criteria), being also twice as high in women as in men (14.1% and 7.7%); on the other hand, 59.0% presented pre-frailty (1–2 criteria) with the prevalence in women higher than in men (62.6% vs. 55.4%) [10]. However, the results were different from the study in Spain in 2021 by Menéndez-González who showed a prevalence of frailty of 27.7% and pre-frailty of 44.8%, higher and lower prevalence respectively than those reported by the present study [18].

Frailty was significantly associated with physical inactivity (OR = 1.634; 95% CI 1.458–1.817; p < 0.001), being more prevalent in women (30.2%) than in men (19.5%). Similar associations were found in Spain in 2021 by Menéndez-González (OR = 4.8 (1.8–12.5)) (16), in Chile by Torroncoso-Pantoja et al., in 2020 [10] and Concha et al., in 2022 [14]; the latter demonstrated according to linear regression that older adults with frailty performed less physical activity (β = −292.6 min/day [95% CI: 399.5; −185.7], p = 0.001), and longer sedentary time (β = 3.55 h/day [95% CI: −1.97; 5.14], p = 0.001). Therefore, the more physical activity the older adults perform, the better their physical condition will be, improving sarcopenia, cognitive performance and mood as demonstrated by several studies [16, 17].

In the present study, frailty was associated with low socioeconomic status (OR = 2.176; 95% CI 1.027–4.608; p < 0.039); Menéndez-González in Spain, observed that the participants with a low economic status (47.8%) had a prevalence of frailty of 27.7% and pre-frailty of 44.9% [18], while Herrera-Pérez in Cuba determined that the older adults with Social Class Level IV had a prevalence of pre-frailty of 26.4% and frailty of 8.3%, being more than double if they were Level V: unskilled manual workers (pre-frail 66.7% and frail 88.3%) [19]. Although an association was found with nutritional status, it was not statistically significant (OR = 1.291, 95% CI 0.772–2.159; p value < 0.33), different from the findings of studies such as Romão L in Portugal, they found a prevalence of frailty of 22.4% in the overweight group (p < 0.001) [40].

The logistic regression models confirmed that low physical activity was a significant predictor of frailty in both models. Additionally, being female was a significant predictor of frailty in Model B. These results are similar to the study by Ferrer A et al., in Spain [41] and the study in Colombia by Ocampo-Chaparro JM [42].

This study provides updated data on the urban population of the province in Azuay, Ecuador, regarding frailty, nutritional status, physical activity, and socioeconomic level, factors that are determinants of the quality of life of this vulnerable group. These data allow us to have an overview of the current situation of older adults. The findings of this study could be useful to design intervention studies in this population to improve the quality of life of the older adults.

Limitations

This study was conducted among older adults attending restructured MPH senior clubs that had been disrupted during the COVID-19 pandemic. Due to mortality and fear of infection, fewer participants were available compared to pre-pandemic enrollment.

Additionally, the cross-sectional design does not allow determining cause-effect relationships between the variables studied. The study only included older adults registered in specific MPH senior clubs, which limits the generalization of the results.

Conclusions

This study found a high prevalence of frailty among older adults in Azuay, Ecuador, particularly among women, and identified significant associations with physical inactivity and low socioeconomic status. In Latin American countries, as well as globally, population aging is occurring at a faster pace than in past decades. Consequently, all countries, including Ecuador, will face significant challenges to ensure that both public and private healthcare systems are truly equipped to implement provisional measures that delay the onset of frailty. This can be achieved through effective multidomain programs that directly improve the quality of life of older adults and reduce disability and mortality rates in this vulnerable group. In the future, every human being will reach this stage of their life as part of their life cycle, and everyone should do so in the best possible health.

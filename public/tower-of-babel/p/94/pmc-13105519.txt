Phase 2b Trial of the PDE10A Inhibitor MK-8189 in People With an Acute Episode of Schizophrenia
Authors: Yuki Mukai, James Kost, Linda Snow-adami, Jessica Reed, Theresa Taylor, Justine Kent, Michael F. Egan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:PDE10A inhibition represents a potential mechanism for treating schizophrenia. A 12-mg dose of the PDE10A inhibitor MK-8189 showed initial evidence of efficacy for improving schizophrenia symptoms after 4 weeks. We performed a follow-up study evaluating higher MK-8189 doses (16 mg and 24 mg, predicted to produce up to 80% sustained enzyme occupancy) over 6 weeks.Methods:Randomized, double-blind, phase 2b study consisting of a 6-week acute treatment period and a 6-week extension (NCT04624243). Participants were diagnosed with schizophrenia and were experiencing active phase symptoms. For the acute treatment period, participants were randomized in a 2:2:2:1:2 ratio to 6 weeks of daily MK-8189 8 mg (dropped after a third of participants had been enrolled), 16 mg, 24 mg, risperidone 6 mg (for assay sensitivity), or placebo. The primary outcome was the change from baseline in Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) total score at 6 weeks.Results:The number of treated participants in the acute period was: MK-8189 16 mg, N=132; MK-8189 24 mg, N=132; risperidone 6 mg, N=65; and placebo, N=129. MK-8189 16 mg and 24 mg did not show an improvement versus placebo for change-from-baseline in PANSS total score after 6 weeks [16 mg-placebo difference, −2.8 (95% CI: −8.3,2.6), P=0.241; 24 mg-placebo difference, −0.7 (95% CI: −6.3,4.9), P=0.784], whereas risperidone did [risperidone-placebo difference, −6.2 (95% CI: −12.9,0.6), P=0.040]. Over the 6-week acute period, more participants discontinued treatment due to an adverse event in the MK-8189 24 mg group (25.0%) than in the MK-8189 16 mg (12.9%), risperidone (12.3%) or placebo (12.4%) groups.Conclusions:These findings suggest that PDE10A inhibition does not have antipsychotic effects at the doses tested.

Inhibition of the phosphodiesterase 10A (PDE10A) enzyme has been proposed as a novel mechanism for treating schizophrenia.1,2 PDE10A is highly expressed in the striatal medium spiny neurons, where it regulates spatial and temporal signaling dynamics of the second messengers cAMP and cGMP.1–4 Blockade of dopamine D2 receptors to activate the subset of medium spiny neurons of the indirect striatal pathway is thought to be the mechanism underlying standard-of-care dopamine D2 antagonists in reducing psychosis in schizophrenia, albeit at the cost of significant motor, behavioral, and metabolic liabilities.5 PDE10A’s highly specific localization in medium spiny neurons of both the direct and indirect pathways allows for modulation of these pathways through PDE10A inhibition without direct dopamine receptor binding.6,7 This unique pharmacology offers the potential for capturing the proven therapeutic utility of activating indirect pathway medium spiny neurons while potentially gaining benefit on cognitive and negative symptoms from co-activating the direct pathway neurons8–12 and avoiding possible deleterious effects of D2 blockade in the pituitary and cerebral cortex.

Despite the strong preclinical rationale, results from previous proof-of-concept clinical trials of PDE10A inhibitors in individuals with acute psychosis have been mixed. PF-02545920 failed to show evidence of efficacy,13 while TAK-063 exhibited a small-to-moderate nonsignificant effect size in Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) total score and improvements in Clinical Global Impressions of Severity and Improvement ratings.14 The degree of sustained enzyme inhibition that was maintained over 24 hours by the once-daily (TAK-063) or twice-daily (PF-02545920) doses evaluated in these 4 to 6-week trials is likely to have been modest.15–17 Recent preclinical data suggest that antipsychotic efficacy in animal models is associated with sustained enzyme occupancy >50%.18 These results raise the possibility that higher sustained levels of PDE10A inhibition may be required for antipsychotic efficacy.

MK-8189 is a potent and highly selective PDE10A inhibitor18,19 which was developed as a controlled-release formulation that resulted in a pharmacokinetic profile with minimal peak-to-trough exposure variability, thus enabling exploration of higher sustained levels of target engagement.20 In phase 1 studies in participants with schizophrenia, the controlled-release formulation of MK-8189 was found to have a t½ of ~8 to 11 hours and to be well tolerated with limited extrapyramidal effects at doses achieving >80% trough enzyme inhibition.20 In a previous phase 2a, 4-week, proof-of-concept study in people with an acute episode of schizophrenia, a 12-mg daily dose of MK-8189, predicted to have a median peak enzyme occupancy of ~75% and trough enzyme occupancy of ~60%, showed initial evidence of efficacy for improving schizophrenia symptoms.21 Further, MK-8189 reduced weight and improved lipid levels, while risperidone increased weight. Here, we report results from a follow-up phase 2b study using a larger sample size, higher MK-8189 doses, and a longer assessment period to more definitively assess the viability of the PDE10A inhibition mechanism of action in the treatment of schizophrenia.

METHODS

Study Design

The trial (MSD Protocol P008MK8189, NCT04624243, EudraCT 2020-000094-24) was conducted at 75 centers in 11 countries from December 2020 to June 2024. Investigators are listed in Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JCP/B15. The trial was conducted in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and was approved by the institutional review boards. Written informed consent was provided by the participant or his/her legal representative. The trial consisted of a screening/tapering period of up to 1 week, a 6-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled and active-controlled intervention period (inpatient with the opportunity for early discharge after 4 weeks as determined by the investigator), followed by a 6-week outpatient extension treatment period and a 2-week follow-up period. Participants who prematurely discontinued the study intervention were encouraged to remain in the study and complete procedures and assessments as specified in the protocol.

Participants who were not already hospitalized were admitted to the hospital/inpatient unit no later than the evening of screening visit assessment completion. During the tapering period, prohibited psychotropic medications (antipsychotics, antidepressants, and mood stabilizers) were tapered under the direct supervision of the investigator with the last dose to be taken no later than the evening before the first day of dosing with study treatment (baseline).

At baseline (day 1), participants were randomly assigned, in a double-blind manner, to one of the following acute/extension treatment sequences in a 2:2:2:1:2 ratio: MK-8189 8 mg/MK-8189 8 mg, MK-8189 16 mg/MK-8189 16 mg, MK-8189 24 mg/MK-8189 24 mg, risperidone 6 mg/risperidone 6 mg, or placebo/MK-8189 24 mg, with all study medication administered orally and once-daily. A 6-mg dose of risperidone, the active control, was selected as being in the middle of the 4 to 8 mg target dose range in the US product label. Participants randomized to the MK-8189/MK-8189 and risperidone/risperidone treatment sequence groups were titrated to their target doses during the first week of the acute treatment period in a blinded manner. Participants randomized to the placebo/MK-8189 24 mg treatment sequence group started the extension period using the same titration schedule as was used for the MK-8189 24 mg dose group in the acute period. The trial protocol was amended in November 2022, ∼2 years after the trial start date, to remove the lowest, 8 mg, MK-8189 treatment arm. Participants randomized to 8 mg remained on 8 mg for the duration of the trial. The primary drivers for the amendment included addressing recruitment challenges and the impact of newly available phase 1 data showing that doses up to 48 mg were well tolerated in people with schizophrenia, thus allowing for the removal of the 8 mg arm. The 8-mg group was excluded from efficacy analyses but was included in safety analyses.

Participants

Eligible participants were men and women between 18 and 55 years of age who met diagnostic criteria for schizophrenia according to the The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition,22 had a past diagnosis of schizophrenia with the onset being ≥1 year before study entry, and had responded positively to an antipsychotic medication other than clozapine in a prior psychotic episode. At screening, participants had to have a minimum a Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS)23 total score of ≥80 with a score ≥4 (moderate) in three or more of the following items on the positive subscale (delusions, conceptual disorganization, hallucinatory behavior, grandiosity, suspiciousness/persecution), as well as a Clinical Global Impression–Severity (CGI-S)24 for schizophrenia score ≥4 (“moderately ill”).

Randomization and Masking

MK-8189 was administered as tablets (with visually matching placebo tablets) and risperidone was administered as capsules (with visually matching placebo capsules). Blinding was maintained using a double-dummy design (ie, each participant received tablets and capsules). An interactive response system was used to randomly assign participants according to a computer-generated assignment schedule. Randomization was stratified by region/race (US–Black, US–non-Black, Japan, rest of world).

Outcomes

The PANSS23 is a 30-item clinician-rated instrument for assessing the symptoms of schizophrenia and was used as the primary efficacy assessment. Each item is rated on a 7-point scale (1=absence of symptoms, 7=extremely severe symptoms) to give a total score range of 30 to 210. The positive subscale (score range 7 to 49) consists of 7 items that measure delusions, conceptual disorganization, hallucinations, excitement, grandiosity, suspiciousness/persecution, and hostility. The negative subscale (score range 7 to 49) consists of 7 items that measure blunted affect, emotional withdrawal, poor rapport, passive/apathetic social withdrawal, difficulty in abstract thinking, lack of spontaneity and flow of conversation, and stereotyped thinking. The CGI-S,24 a 7-point clinician-rated scale for assessing the global severity of the illness (1 = normal/not at all ill, 7 = among the most extremely ill), was used as a secondary efficacy assessment.

Safety and tolerability were assessed throughout the study by monitoring participants for clinical adverse events. Physical examinations, vital signs, 12-lead ECGs and laboratory safety tests were also performed routinely.

Statistical Analysis

The primary efficacy endpoint was the mean change from baseline in PANSS total score at week 6, with the primary hypotheses based on the comparisons of MK-8189 16 mg versus placebo and MK-8189 24 mg versus placebo. The key secondary endpoints were the mean change from baseline in the PANSS positive subscale score at week 6 (MK-8189 16 mg vs. placebo, MK-8189 24 mg vs. placebo), the mean change from baseline in CGI-S score at week 6 (MK-8189 16 mg vs. placebo, MK-8189 24 mg vs. placebo), and the mean change from baseline in weight at week 12 (MK-8189 16 mg vs. risperidone, MK-8189 24 mg vs. risperidone) In addition, mean change from baseline in weight after 6 weeks for MK-8189 16 mg and 24 mg versus placebo was assessed. Comparisons of risperidone versus placebo on the PANSS, CGI-S and weight endpoints were performed on an exploratory basis.

The full-analysis-set population was used for the primary and key secondary efficacy endpoints, in which treated participants with both a baseline measurement and at least one post-randomization observation were included. Data were analyzed using a longitudinal ANCOVA model including categorical terms for treatment, week, cohort (pre-amendment dropping the 8-mg dose, post-amendment), stratum (US-Black, US-non-Black, Other), and gender (male, female), as well as continuous terms for duration of illness, age, and baseline. The model also included the interaction terms of week-by-treatment and week-by-baseline. The treatment difference in terms of the mean change from baseline at a given time point was estimated, and as appropriate, tested from this model. An unstructured covariance matrix was used to model the correlation among repeated measurements. The MK-8189 8 mg treatment arm, which was dropped from the trial, was excluded from all model-based efficacy analyses.

As there were 2 MK-8189 doses (16 mg and 24 mg) and 4 primary/key secondary endpoints (PANSS total, PANSS positive, CGI-S, weight), there were 8 total hypotheses for which strong type 1 error control had to be maintained at a familywise α = 0.05 level. This was achieved by utilizing a Bonferroni approach in conjunction with a closed testing sequential approach as shown in Figure S1 in Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JCP/B16. The trial was considered successful if the primary hypotheses for either dose of MK-8189 met the significance criteria.

All treated participants, including those in the dropped MK-8189 8 mg treatment arm, were included in the safety analyses. Safety and tolerability were assessed by clinical review of relevant parameters, including adverse events, laboratory tests, vital signs, and ECG measurements. Select “composite” adverse events (groupings of related individual adverse event terms) were prespecified for analysis based on the adverse events seen in previous studies of PDE10A inhibitors: dyskinesia, dystonia, akathisia, Parkinsonism, and extrapyramidal symptoms. Analyses of adverse events were conducted separately for the acute and extension periods. Unless otherwise noted, all assessments and events within 14 days of the last dose of study medication from a given period were included in the analyses for that period, with the caveat that any assessments or events taking place after the first dose of extension study medication were not phased to the acute period. Changes from baseline in clinical laboratory measures (urinalysis, clinical chemistry, hematology), vital signs, and ECG measures were analyzed over the acute and extension periods combined.

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS Version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Power

Power estimates were primarily based on data from the previous phase 2a study, which evaluated a 12-mg dose of MK-8189 over 4 weeks.21 An adjustment was made for the assumed greater efficacy of MK-8189 in the present study, given the longer assessment period and higher doses, and the sample size was computed using the multiplicity approach outlined above. With 128 planned randomized participants per treatment arm (MK-8189 16 mg, MK-8189 24 mg, and placebo) the trial had 91% probability of declaring success on PANSS total score for at least one MK-8189 dose assuming a week 6 change-from-baseline treatment difference (MK-8189—placebo) in PANSS total score of −6.56 (SD=17.0) for 16 mg and −8.44 (SD=17.0) for 24 mg and a 35% drop-out rate (ie, 65% of randomized participants with a PANSS measurement at week 6).

RESULTS

Participants

The study flowchart is shown in Figure 1. A total of 774 participants were screened and 499 participants were randomized. The most common reason for screen failure was “unable to remain hospitalized for the duration of screening and first 28 days of the treatment period.” Of the 499 randomized participants, 41 were assigned to MK-8189 8 mg (dropped treatment arm), 132 to MK-8189 16 mg, 132 to MK-8189 24 mg, 129 to placebo, and 65 to risperidone. The percentages of participants who completed the 6-week acute period on treatment were 43.9% for MK-8189 8 mg, 65.2% for MK-8189 16 mg, 52.3% for MK-8189 24 mg, 69.0% for placebo, and 64.6% for risperidone. A total of 275 participants entered the 6-week extension period and 205 (74.5%) of these completed the extension on treatment. The average % compliance with taking study medication over the acute and extension period (calculated as the number of days that the participant took the intended amount of study medication divided by the number of days that the participant was supposed to take study medication) was >95% in each treatment group.

Efficacy

Overall, the PANSS total score decreased (improved) for all 4 treatment groups throughout the 6-week acute intervention period, with both MK-8189 dose groups and risperidone showing numerically lower scores than placebo at week 6 (Figure 2). Differences between treatments in changes from baseline at Week 6 are shown in Table 2. No significant differences versus placebo were observed in either MK-8189 dose group, whereas risperidone was superior to placebo (−6.2 point difference, P=0.040), indicating assay sensitivity. Based on visual inspection, MK-8189 16 mg appeared similar to risperidone at time points before 6 weeks, but not at the primary 6-week time point (Figure 2). The reason for the divergence at 6 weeks is unclear. In subgroup analyses comparing the 6-week mean change from baseline in PANSS total score by subgroup factors (eg, race, sex), the 95% CIs for the difference between MK-8189 and placebo all included 0, suggesting no benefit of MK-8189 in any subgroup (Figure S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JCP/B16).

A similar pattern of improvement from baseline at week 6 in all 4 treatment groups with no significant differences between MK-8189 dose groups and placebo was observed for the key secondary endpoints of PANSS positive subscale and CGI-S (Table 2).

For the secondary endpoint of weight change at week 12, decreases from baseline in weight were observed in the MK-8189 16-mg and MK-8189 24-mg groups (−5.4 kg and −4.3 kg, respectively) while weight gain was observed in the risperidone group (3.0 kg). The differences between MK-8189 16 and 24 mg versus risperidone (−8.5 kg, and −7.3 kg, respectively) were nominally significant (P <0.001). Nominally significant (P<0.001) decreases from baseline in weight were observed at week 6 in the MK-8189 16-mg and MK-8189 24-mg groups versus placebo (−3.7 kg and −3.3 kg, respectively), while there was a nominally significant (P<0.001) increase in weight for risperidone versus placebo (2.3 kg).

Safety

Adverse Events

Adverse event data were analyzed separately for the acute period and extension period.

Acute period: Adverse events during the acute period are summarized in Table 3. No deaths were reported. Serious adverse events only occurred in the MK-8189 and risperidone groups. The only serious adverse event reported in more than 1 participant in any treatment group was schizophrenia [MK-8189 8 mg = 1/41 (2.4%), MK-8189 16 mg = 2/132 (1.5%), MK-8189 24 mg = 5/132 (3.8%), risperidone = 1/65 (1.5%), placebo = 0/129 (0%)]. The percentages of participants with adverse events and who discontinued study treatment due to an adverse event (mainly exacerbation of schizophrenia) were higher in the MK-8189 24-mg group compared with the placebo group. The MK-8189 24-mg group showed higher percentages of participants with nausea, vomiting, akathisia, and dystonia compared with the placebo group, and the MK-8189 16-mg group showed higher percentages with nausea and dystonia compared with the placebo group. Most of these adverse events were mild to moderate in severity and tended to occur in the first few weeks after dosing. Composite events of dyskinesia, dystonia, akathisia, and extrapyramidal symptoms showed a dose-related increase for MK-8189 versus placebo. Adverse events in the risperidone group were generally similar to placebo.

Discontinuations due to an adverse event were mainly due to schizophrenia. Other than for the placebo/MK-8189 24 mg group, where treatment with 24 mg was newly initiated at the start of the extension period, fewer adverse events were observed across MK-8189 groups in the extension period compared with the acute period and extrapyramidal-related events were rarely reported. Fewer adverse events were also reported for the risperidone group in the extension period compared with the acute period.

Laboratory Measures, Vital Signs, and ECG

Overall, mean changes over time in laboratory test results and ECG assessments were generally comparable across intervention groups. Notable observations in the MK-8189 groups overall were decreases in cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, lactate dehydrogenase, and triglycerides (see Table S1 in Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JCP/B16—data at 6 weeks are shown to facilitate comparison to placebo). With regard to vital signs, MK-8189 reduced weight as reported above, but changes in other measures (pulse, blood pressure) were comparable across intervention groups.

DISCUSSION

The present findings showed that the PDE10A inhibitor MK-8189 was not effective for improving psychotic symptoms as assessed by the PANSS over 6 weeks compared with placebo. The active control, risperidone, was more effective than placebo, thereby demonstrating that the trial was appropriately conducted to detect a treatment effect of MK-8189 at the doses tested had one been present. Compared with the previous Phase 2a study,21 where a signal for efficacy of MK-8189 was observed using the same PANSS assessment, the present Phase 2b study evaluated higher doses of MK-8189 (16 mg and 24 mg vs. 12 mg) over a longer treatment period (6 wk vs. 4 wk). Based on enzyme occupancy data from a previous PET imaging study25 and population pharmacokinetic modeling, including results from the previous phase 2a study,21 the highest (24 mg) MK-8189 dose in the present study was predicted to produce ∼80% sustained enzyme occupancy. This constitutes the highest level of continuous 24-hour target engagement evaluated for a PDE10A inhibitor and the lack of benefit strongly suggests that PDE10A inhibition, by itself, is not a useful approach for treating acute psychosis in people with schizophrenia. It is worth noting that the primary assessment instrument, the PANSS, does not adequately assess all aspects of schizophrenia. The trial did not, for example, robustly evaluate cognitive impairment or negative symptoms using trial designs specifically tailored to assess those domains. Furthermore, the trial was designed to detect a moderate effect size on the PANSS total score, and smaller effects cannot be ruled out.

A number of differences between the present Phase 2b study and the previous phase 2a study21 should be borne in mind in interpreting the findings. The present study was conducted globally, whereas the previous study was US-only. The study population was predominantly (75%) Black in the previous study, whereas a smaller proportion (44%) of participants were Black in the present study. However, subgroup analyses of region (US, ex-US) and race (Black, other) did not suggest differential efficacy in the present study. The present study was conducted during COVID, whereas the previous study was conducted before the pandemic. The placebo response in the present study was greater than in the previous study (the change from baseline in PANSS total score at 4 weeks in the placebo group was 15 points in the present study vs. 10 points in the previous study), whereas the absolute changes from baseline for MK-8189 and risperidone were similar between the two studies (the changes from baseline in PANSS total score at 4 weeks were ∼15 to18 points in the MK-8189 groups and ∼17 to 18 points in the risperidone group). Similar trials conducted during this timeframe, which overlapped with the COVID pandemic, also reported relatively high placebo responses.26

MK-8189 was developed as a controlled-release formulation and participants were titrated to their target dose. Both these aspects of treatment were intended to minimize adverse events. Further, it was hoped that the novel PDE10A inhibition mechanism of action might result in an improved tolerability profile versus current antipsychotics. This was not supported by the findings. At the highest 24 mg dose, more participants discontinued treatment due to an adverse event compared with risperidone and placebo (25% vs. 12%), and MK-8189 showed a dose-related increase in dystonia and extrapyramidal symptoms, as well as nausea. These findings suggest that 24 mg is approaching the maximum tolerated dose in this population and investigation of higher doses, with potentially greater levels of target engagement, is likely not feasible or desirable given that the 24 mg dose did not provide any additional efficacy benefit over the 16 mg dose. The adverse event findings also suggest that adequate dosing of MK-8189 was obtained.

The finding in the previous phase 2a trial that MK-8189 reduced weight over 4 weeks while risperidone, as expected,27 increased weight was replicated over the 12-week assessment period in the present trial. Over 12 weeks participants, who were on average overweight at baseline, showed an ∼4 to 5 kg reduction in weight at 12 weeks that did not appear to be dose-related, while those on risperidone showed an ∼3 kg increase in weight. The weight reduction effect of MK-8189 is consistent with previous preclinical observations.28,29

In conclusion, the present findings suggest that PDE10A inhibition is not a useful approach for treating psychotic symptoms in people with schizophrenia.

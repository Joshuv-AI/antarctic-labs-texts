Future time perspective and cyberloafing in Chinese adolescents: the multiple mediating role of self-control and excessive smartphone use
Authors: Dan He, Huifen Shi, Yunyang Hu, Maoqin Tang, Wei Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a complicated phenomenon in an educational context, cyberloafing has gradually attracted researchers’ attention. The current study attempted to explore the association between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing by investigating the potential mediating roles of self-control and excessive smartphone use. A sample of 907 adolescents with an age range of 12 to 18 years (Mage = 14.86 years, SD = 1.73) completed a questionnaire on future time perspective, self-control, excessive smartphone use, and cyberloafing. The results showed a significant negative association between future time perspective and cyberloafing among adolescents. Additionally, self-control and excessive smartphone use served as potential mechanisms by which future time perspective related to adolescent cyberloafing. This study enriches the existing research on the factors that influence cyberloafing among adolescents, and contributes to a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms of how future time perspective affects adolescent cyberloafing, providing new insights for the development of interventions.Keywords: future time perspective, cyberloafing, self-control, excessive smartphone use, adolescent

Introduction

In today’s world, with the progressive advancement of information and communication technology, Internet technologies have become pervasive in public institutions, commercial centers, streets, schools, households, and all facets of daily life [1, 2] They bring significant convenience to our daily lives and enhance efficiency and effectiveness in the workplace [3]. However, its simultaneous negative impacts cannot be ignored [3, 4]. In educational settings, a notable manifestation of these drawbacks is cyberloafing, which refers to students’ engagement in online activities unrelated to learning during designated study time, deviating from their original academic tasks [5]. Given the pervasive integration of Internet and communication technologies in education—and its potential to escalate to worrying levels—scholars have called for further research into the underlying causes of cyberloafing in educational contexts [6]. For the majority of adolescent students, the Internet has become an indispensable component of both their academic and daily lives. Internet technologies facilitate access to timely, pertinent, and up-to-date resources, thereby positively enhancing students’ academic performance [7]. However, the convenience of Internet access also poses a potential distraction, making students especially susceptible to cyberloafing behavior [5]. Due to their limited impulse control ability, adolescents are particularly prone to deviating from their learning tasks and becoming enticed by various online activities [8]. Studies have found that cyberloafing is related to numerous negative outcomes, including attention difficulties, academic procrastination, poor academic performance, and high rates of social anxiety and depression [9, 10]. Thus, understanding the underlying mechanisms of cyberloafing, and particularly of the loss of control of over time spent cyberloafing, requires further investigation.

According to temporal self-regulation theory [11], an individual’s behavioral choices are determined by the combined influence of their valuation of future outcomes, the psychological regulatory resources required to translate intentions into actions, and the habitually activated responses. As a key manifestation of the temporal-perspective component, future time perspective may play an important role in adolescent cyberloafing. Future time perspective, conceptualized as individual’s cognitive, affective, and behavioral orientation toward the future [12], is critical for behavioral self-regulation [13]. Regarded as a personality trait, future time perspective encompasses the inclination to contemplate, anticipate, and construct future possibilities, reflecting a person’s general attitude toward what is to come [14]. It is closely linked to key psychological and social functioning areas such as emotion, cognition, motivation, and social adaptation [14]. For adolescents, future time perspective is a key factor that fosters their development. It enables them to imagine future experiences and events in daily lives and to explore potential directions for effort [15]. This process, in turn, helps build greater perseverance and improves their ability to plan and manage their own behaviors, thus establishing a foundation for their future development [16]. Individuals with high level of future time perspective are more inclined to sacrifice short-term gratification in pursuit of long-term goals [17]. In contrast, those with low level of future time perspective often hold a pessimistic outlook on the future, exhibit low expectations, and lack the perseverance needed to achieve their objectives. Consequently, they are more prone to wasting work hours through avoidance behaviors [18], which frequently fosters cyberloafing.

From the perspective of temporal self-regulation theory [11], the development from negative future time perspective into cyberloafing is not an automatic or spontaneous process; rather, its core mechanism lies in the breakdown in self-regulation and and the activation of habitual. A low level of future time perspective, reflecting a diminished valuation of future outcomes, can deplete the psychological resources necessary for effective self-regulation. This deficit manifests concretely as a failure in self-control, compromising the ability to resist immediate online temptations. Concurrently, the scarcity of self-control steers behavior toward automated, gratification-seeking routines, thereby establishing excessive smartphone use as a default, compensatory outlet for immediate reward [19]. This underlying mechanism, which unfolds from compromised low level of future time perspective to impaired self-control and then to intensified, dysregulated smartphone use, collectively constitutes the key mechanism through which a low level of future time perspective develops into cyberloafing. ” Based on this, the present study aimed to examine the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing and and to investigate the mediating roles of self-control and excessive smartphone use in this relationship.

The mediating role of self-control

According to temporal self-regulation theory [11], the emergence of adolescent cyberloafing behavior is not only associated with an individual’s evaluation of future outcomes but, more critically, depends on their capacity for self-control as a core regulatory process. Self-control refers to one’s ability to effectively monitor, inhibit, persevere, and adapt their innate behaviors, urges, emotions, thoughts, and desires [20].This ability enables individuals to restrain impulsive responses and remain committed to long-term goals [21]. Given this function, future time perspective may serve as an important antecedent of self-control [22]. Individuals with high future time perspective tend to consider future consequences and delay gratification rather than pursuing immediate pleasurable activities, demonstrating stronger self-control abilities [23]. According to construal level theory [24], individuals with higher future time perspective tend to maintain psychological distance from tempting situations which affords them higher-level thought processes and greater self-control. Consistent with this point, empirical research has shown that directing one’s thoughts towards future benefits can result in a reduction in impulsive behaviors and an increase in self-control [25]. In an experimental study manipulating time perspective, Dreves and Blackhart found that individuals who attached greater importance to long-term goals demonstrated higher levels of self-control [26].

Numerous studies have identified low self-control as a key driver of cyberloafing behavior [27]. Self-control is, to some extent, one’s desire to conform social norms [28]. As cyberloafing can be considered as a behavior that deviates from social norms [29], it is reasonable to consider the role of self-control in understanding cyberloafing [30]. A body of empirical research provides supporting evidence for this view. Rahimnia and Mazidi found that a lack of self-control is a major risk factor for cyberloafing [31]. Zhou et al. used an experience sampling method found that students with high self-control were less likely to engage in cyberloafing [32]. A meta-analysis also found that self-control was strongly negatively related to cyberloafing, with individuals having high self-control were less likely to exhibit cyberloafing behaviors [33].

Building on this foundation, we propose that self-control mediates the relationship between future time perspective and cyberloafing. As individuals with a higher level of future time perspective exhibit a stronger sense of responsibility and attach greater anticipated incentive value to long-term goals, they tend to prioritize distal motives over proximal motives when the two are in competition, resulting in a higher level of self-control and less cyberloafing [26, 27]. Previous research supports the mediating role of self-control in similar contexts. For example, Zhang et al. identified self-control as a mediator between future orientation and cyberloafing among employees [27]. Kim et al. demonstrated that self-control functions as a mediator between time perspective and Internet addiction [3]. Based on these findings, we proposed that self-control mediates the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing.

The mediating role of excessive smartphone use

Temporal self-regulation theory [11] suggests that behavioral outcomes depend not only on the valuation of future outcomes and self-regulatory capacity, but also on the strength of cue-driven automated responses. In the context of digital behavior, excessive smartphone use constitutes such a high-strength automated response, which directly disrupts goal-directed action and facilitates cyberloafing. Excessive smartphone use (also known as “smartphone dependence,” “problematic smartphone use,” or “smartphone addiction”) refers to psychological or behavioral problems caused by the overuse of smartphones [34]. One of the most common problematic behaviors caused by smartphone overuse in an educational context is student cyberloafing [35]. The widespread adoption of online teaching in recent years has provided students with increased opportunities to connect to the Internet via smartphones during their studies, leading to additional opportunities for engaging in cyberloafing while learning. Several related studies have demonstrated that excessive smartphone use is linked to cyberloafing. Dursun, Dönmez, and Akbulut found that having access to mobile devices predicted the cyberloafing levels of students [30], while Varol and Yıldırım suggested that, with the widespread increase of mobile phone use in learning settings, the cyberloafing levels of students also increased [36]. Saritepeci found that students’ smartphone addiction and daily social media usage time are fundamental predictors of their cyberloafing behaviors [35]. Öztürka and Kerseb also demonstrated that smartphone overuse can contribute to the occurrence of cyberloafing, and that there is a significant positive association between smartphone addiction and student cyberloafing [37].

Individuals with a stronger level of future time perspective are more likely to diligently monitor their progress towards goals, regulate potential deviant behaviors like excessive smartphone use, and engage in actions and behaviors aimed towards achieving their goals [38]. Expectancy value theory suggests that an individual’s behavioral motivation is determined by their expectations of outcomes and evaluations of values [39]. Individuals with a stronger future time perspective place higher value on long-term academic and personal goals. Consequently, they are more likely to perceive prolonged smartphone use for entertainment as incongruent with those goals, thereby motivationally disengaging from non-essential use and regulating their behavior accordingly [38, 40]. In addition, excessive smartphone use also functions as the proximal behavioral context​in which cyberloafing occurs [30, 36]. It is not merely a matter of personal choice but also the immediate channel through which digital distraction manifests. The accessibility and engaging design of smartphones themselves constitute a powerful situational force, capable of capturing attention and prompting off-task behavior, especially in learning environments that demand sustained focus [35]. Thus, excessive smartphone use acts as a situational and environmental mediator, directly translating future-oriented motivation into the specific behavior of cyberloafing. Based on the literature reviewed, we therefore hypothesized that excessive smartphone use mediates the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing.

The multiple mediation model

According to temporal self-regulation theory [11], individual behavior emerges from the dynamic interplay among temporal perspective, self-regulatory capacity, and behavioral prepotency [11, 41]. Within this framework, self-control represents the crucial self-regulatory capacity that translates future-oriented intentions into action by inhibiting impulses. A deficit in this capacity makes individuals vulnerable to prepotent responses, which are highly accessible, habitual, or immediately gratifying behaviors [11, 41]. In the modern learning environment, the smartphone, with its constant accessibility and engaging design, constitutes the dominant prepotent response for distraction [35]. Therefore, weaker self-control directly predicts greater immersion in this high-temptation digital context (i.e., excessive smartphone use) [19, 42, 43]. Empirical evidence supports this link. For instance, self-control is negatively associated with problematic smartphone use [19, 42] and is related to greater self-discipline in managing digital behaviors [44].

Cyberloafing, widely regarded as a digital manifestation of procrastination [45], fundamentally represents a failure in the intention-action transition—specifically, the prioritization of immediate rewards over long-term outcomes. A positive future time perspective fosters commitment to distal goals, while self-control acts as the pivotal mediating mechanism​that enables the enactment of these goals by restraining impulsive responses. Crucially, individuals with low level of future time perspective, due to a lack of self-control, are more likely to give in to the prepotent response of excessive smartphone use. This pattern of use, in turn, actively creates and sustains a distraction-filled digital environment, thereby directly increasing the likelihood of cyberloafing. In summary, we hypothesize that a weaker future time perspective undermines self-control, which promotes excessive smartphone use, thereby increasing the likelihood of cyberloafing.

In consideration of the findings of existing research, a multiple mediation model was constructed to explore whether self-control and excessive smartphone use serve as mediating factors in the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing. Methods

Participants

This study was approved by the Ethical Committee for Scientific Research of the authors’ institution. Three classes from each grade of two middle schools and two high schools in southwest China were selected at random to take part in this study using a cluster random sampling method. Students in either Grade 9 or Grade 12 were excluded from this study as at the time they were preparing for their entrance examinations. After obtaining informed consent from the relevant participating schools, teachers, and parents, 1,020 adolescents were invited to participate this study by completing surveys regarding future time perspective, self-control, excessive smartphone use, cyberloafing, and demographic variables. After disregarding any incomplete or unreturned questionnaires, a total of 907 participants were ultimately obtained, with an age range of 12 to 18 years (437 boys and 470 girls, Mage = 14.86, SDage = 1.73).

Measures

Future time perspective

Future time perspective was measured using the Future Time Perspective Scale developed by Lyv [46]. This scale consists 28 items (e.g., I’m scared for my future”), which assess six dimensions. Each item is rated using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). Higher scores indicate a higher level of future time perspective. The Cronbach’s α of the scale in this study was 0.90.

Self-control

Self-control was measured using the Chinese version of the Dual-Mode of Self-Control Scale [47]. The scale consists of 21 items (e.g., “I speak without thinking”). Each item is scored using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). Higher scores indicate a higher level of self-control. The Cronbach’s α of the scale in this study was 0.86.

Excessive smartphone use

Excessive smartphone use was measured using the Smartphone Addiction Scale for Adolescents developed by Su et al. [31]. This scale consists of 22 items (e.g., “The people around me tell me that I use my smartphone too much.”). Participants rate each item using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”), with a higher score indicating a higher level of excessive smartphone use. The Cronbach’s α in this study was 0.91.

Cyberloafing

Cyberloafing was assessed using the Cyberloafing Scale, which comprises 10 items that measure general off-task Internet activities undertaken while studying [48]. Participants were asked to assess the frequency of their Internet usage that deviates from their learning tasks using items such as, “I access to Internet involuntarily to do something unrelated to my studies.” Each item is rated using a six-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“never”) to 6 (“always”), with a higher score indicating a higher level of cyberloafing. The Cronbach’s α in the current study was 0.92.

Analytic strategy

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 23.0. First, descriptive statistics were performed to obtain calculate means, standard deviations, and correlations among the main variables. Next, model 6 from the PROCESS macro in SPSS was used to execute a multiple mediation analysis [49], and the bootstrapping method (sampling repeated 5,000 times) was employed to test the significance of the effect, thereby obtaining robust standard errors for parameter estimation. The effects were reported with 95% confidence intervals, which indicate a statistically significant finding when the intervals do not contain 0.

Discussion

The current study examined the effect of future time perspective on adolescent cyberloafing and the underlying multiple mediating roles of self-control and excessive smartphone use. The results showed that future time perspective was negatively associated with adolescent cyberloafing, and that self-control and excessive smartphone served as mediators in the relationship between future time and cyberloafing.

Consistent with Hypothesis 1, the findings of our study confirm that future time perspective has a significant negative association with adolescent cyberloafing. This is consistent with previous findings which have shown that future time perspective is negatively associated with behavioral problems related to Internet use [27, 50, 51]. We found that adolescents with more future time perspective are less likely to engagement in cyberloafing while learning. There is a saying in China, “Think long, or else worries are not far away.” meaning that if one does not think about their future, they may wind up drawn to all kinds of temptation, and fail to realize their potential [27]. For adolescent students, cyberloafing is the act of putting learning goals on hold while being attracted by the temptations of the moment [4]. Individuals with higher future time perspective have a higher expected value for long-term goals and are more aware of the value of their current learning tasks in achieving their long-term goals [14]. These individuals are also more likely to fully consider the potential correlation between their current behavior and distant outcomes [52], and consider the present and the future as an ongoing process [38]. Therefore, those with a higher level of future time perspective are less likely to cyberloafing.

The mediating role of self-control

In line with Hypothesis 2, self-control acted as a mediator in the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing. More specifically, future time perspective was found to be positively associated with self-control which, in turn, reduces one’s likelihood of engaging in cyberloafing behaviors. Future time perspective makes it possible for individuals to consider the consequences of their actions and pursue more desirable outcomes, which manifests itself taking greater responsibility for their behaviors [53]. Moreover, their goals are clearer and more explicit, and their effort and persistence are higher and longer-lasting, resulting in their level of self-control being higher [54]. Our results also showed that self-control is a potential inducing factor for cyberloafing. This result is consistent with self-control theory which suggests that one’s deviant behaviors are induced by a lack of self-control [55]. Individuals with high self-control often refrain from engaging in behavior that deviates from social norms [56]. They possess the ability to suppress impulsive tendencies, thereby effectively avoiding cyberloafing behavior [56]. Furthermore, students with high self-control possess a more comprehensive understanding of learning procedures and exhibit proficiency in effectively managing their task performance speed and deadlines through strategic adjustment and control of their learning behaviors. In contrast, students with low self-control have difficulty with those same procedures, and instead participate in cyberloafing during learning [57]. Therefore, it seems clear that the relationship between future time perspective and cyberloafing behaviors can be mediated by self-control. The findings of this study provide important insights into the developmental trajectory of self-regulatory capacity during adolescence. This stage is characterized by significant neurobiological changes: On the one hand, the neural foundations supporting self-regulation, such as the prefrontal cortex, remain in a prolonged process of maturation; On the other hand, individuals exhibit relatively heightened sensitivity to immediate rewards and a greater propensity for risk-taking [58, 59]. Within this developmental context, the mediating role of self-control revealed by this study is particularly crucial. Therefore, cultivating future time perspective can be regarded as an important cognitive scaffold. It effectively compensates for and guides the still-developing self-regulatory system by enhancing individuals’ motivation toward long-term goals and providing an internal framework for evaluating immediate choices.

The mediating role of excessive smartphone use

Consistent with our Hypothesis 3, the current study results show that excessive smartphone use is another significant mediator through which future time perspective influences adolescent cyberloafing. This result is consistent with those of previous studies [38, 53] which also support time perspective theory, suggesting that the higher one’s levels of future time perspective, the higher their motivation to act and the higher their resistance to temptation [13, 54]. Thoughts of the future play an important role in the regulation of behaviors [13]. Individuals with a higher level of future time perspective also tend to have stronger time management skills, and their actual behaviors tend to align more closely with the objectives in their anticipated plans [60]. Consequently, they are less likely to waste time on activities incongruent with their long-term goals, and spend less time on their smartphone for entertainment; instead, they are more inclined to utilize their smartphones for fundamental functions such as information retrieval and maintaining communication, thereby having a lower likelihood of smartphone overuse [53, 60]. On the contrary, individuals with a lower level of future time perspective often exhibit a more limited ability to delay their immediate satisfaction, and thus it is difficult for them to resist the temptations brought by smartphones [50]. Excessive smartphone use prevents these individuals from concentrating on their learning activities [61], bringing about further problems such as cyberloafing in learning [35]. Namely, smartphones present a distinctive and widespread challenge to adolescents’ capacity for self-regulation. A positive future time perspective empowers adolescents to autonomously manage this potent source of instant gratification and harness it in service of their long-term objectives, which constitutes a pivotal developmental taskin the growth process of contemporary adolescents.

The multiple mediation model

Consistent with our study Hypothesis 4, our findings show that self-control and excessive smartphone use mediate the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing not only in parallel but also sequentially. As noted earlier, compared to those with a higher level of future time perspective, individuals with a lower level of future time perspective are more inclined to prioritize short-term goals and have greater motivation for more proximal goals, which then predicts a lower level of self-control [26]. A lower level of self-control is linked to higher levels of self-reported distraction (via smartphone) while studying [19]. Due to the convenience of Internet accessibility, smartphones often attract students with poor self-control through their social and personal functions which can help these students satisfy various basic psychological needs. However, their lack of self-regulation renders causes them to struggle in resisting immediate gratifications and temptations, thereby increasing their likelihood of smartphone overuse [42]. Meanwhile, adolescents with higher future time perspective will have a stronger level of self-control and lower dependence on smartphones, with more concern of the effects of their current behaviors on future consequences [27]. After comparing the benefits of academic achievement versus smartphone entertainment, they recognize the detrimental effects or impediments posed by excessive smartphone usage on their long-term goals, and make the choice to control their smartphone use and focus on their current learning tasks or something which benefits their self-growth for the future, rather than indulging in engagement in cyberloafing. Collectively, the identified sequential pathway (future time perspective→self-control→excessive smartphone use→cyberloafing) delineates a coherent developmental trajectory. This pathway highlights a potential protective developmental process, future time perspective helps by boosting self-control, which in turn reduces excessive smartphone use and, ultimately, cyberloafing.

Limitations and implications

There are several limitations to this study which warrant attention. First, this study used a cross sectional design, therefore the process model illustrated in this study is based on theoretical inferences and causal relationships between variables cannot be confirmed. Future studies should adopt longitudinal or experimental designs to exploration of cause-and-effect relationships of future time perspective on self-control, excessive smartphone use, and cyberloafing. Second, the data collection in the current study relied solely on adolescent self-reports. Although self-report measurement is widely employed and the instruments utilized in this study have demonstrated good reliability, as cyberloafing can be a sensitive topic, it is possible that respondents may have exhibited social desirability bias and may not have been completely candid in their responses [25]. Third, the cyberloafing scale used was derived from an unpublished thesis [48]. Its application in prior studies and high internal consistency here support its utility, but readers should note that its psychometric properties are less comprehensively documented than those of fully validated, published scales. Fourth, this study considered future time perspective as a global variable, rather than distinguishing the different effects of various future time perspectives. Future explorations are necessary to compare the different effects of various types of future time perspectives on adolescent cyberloafing. Additionally, it should be noted that the participants in this study were primarily early adolescents (Mage = 14.86). Therefore, the findings may be particularly applicable to this developmental stage, and their generalizability to middle and late adolescents requires further validation. Future studies could test the generalizability of the result by validating it with samples of middle and late adolescents.

Despite these limitations, this study nonetheless has several implications for theory and practice. First, our findings echo previous research in calling for greater attention to cyberloafing in education [1, 2, 5, 9]. Our study enriches the research on the factors that influence cyberloafing among adolescents, highlighting how adolescents’ future time perspective can reduce their cyberloafing behaviors through self-control and excessive smartphone use. We found that the relationship between future time perspective and cyberloafing is mediated by both self-control and excessive smartphone use. This mediation occurs both through independent pathways and a sequential pathway from self-control to smartphone use. Our results suggest that fostering a positive future time perspective may be a key developmental lever.​Interventions aimed at enhancing adolescents’ ability to envision and value their future selves could simultaneously promote self-regulatory capacities and encourage more mindful technology use, thereby supporting healthier developmental pathways in the digital age. Building on this, comprehensive intervention programs should be designed that focus on enhancing adolescents’ future time perspective, promote consideration of future consequences, cultivate their strategies for self-control, and improve their abilities in self-control to help guide adolescents in developing good smartphone use habits.

Conclusion

Cyberloafing is a difficult problem currently impacting educational systems. The present study contributes to a better understanding the multiple mediation model between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing. We found that future time perspective was significant negatively associated with adolescent cyberloafing, and that self-control and excessive smartphone use served as potential mechanisms impacting how future time perspective relates to adolescent cyberloafing. Specifically, the relationship between future time perspective and adolescent cyberloafing was found to be mediated by self-control and excessive smartphone use, both in parallel and sequentially.

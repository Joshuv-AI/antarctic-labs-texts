Correction: A plan or pandemonium? The conundrum of retrotransposon activation in cancer
Authors: Elina Zueva
Published: 2025

Correction: Mobile DNA 16, 31 (2025)

Following publication of the original article [1], Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4, the physical figures in the article body were also inadvertently captured as supplementary files (Supplementary materials 1–4). The Supplementary files have been removed.

Moreover, Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4 were replaced with high-resolution figures; and the omitted part for Fig. 4 caption was inserted. The complete caption for Fig. 4 is given below:

Figure 4. Schematic representation of the potentially dual role of ERE sequences in cancer initiation and progression. In early malignant transformation, the activity of EREs may promote the elimination of pre-cancerous clones through LINE-1-induced genotoxic stress, sensing of ERE-derived oligonucleotides, engagement of adaptive immunity against ERE-encoded epitopes, and the generation of non-functional oncogenic isoforms via aberrant splicing or promoter activity. At later stages, however, EREs may shift from barriers to facilitators of tumor progression, contributing to malignancy through disruption of tumor suppressors, onco-exaptation and oncogenic enhancers, and, in specific contexts, through oncogenic viral proteins or immune evasion mediated by HERV-derived immunosuppressive domains.

The original article [1] has been updated.

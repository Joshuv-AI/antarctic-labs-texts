Epidemiological, clinical, and molecular characteristics of coxsackievirus A2 infections during a herpangina outbreak in children on an Island in Eastern China in 2024: a surveillance-based study
Authors: Bo Xing, Linjie Hu, Xiaosong Hu, Yuxiang Sun, Zhenzhen Liang, Jian Fu, Yuxia Liang, Yijuan Chen, You Li, Lingxia Chen, Lijun Wang, Weiting Wang, Yihan Lu, Shenyu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHerpangina is an acute pediatric illness caused by enteroviruses, yet the predominant circulating serotype in mainland China has not been well characterized. In this study, Coxsackievirus A2 (CV-A2) emerged as the dominant serotype among herpangina cases in Yuhuan City, Zhejiang Province. These findings underscore the importance of further research into the epidemiological distribution and molecular features of CV-A2 in this region.MethodsA total of 133 child herpangina cases were included during May and June 2024. Enteroviruses were detected using RT-PCR, followed by serotyping and sequencing. Phylogenetic and recombination analyses were performed to assess genetic diversity and evolutionary relationships.ResultsAmong the 133 cases (median age: 3 years), 88.7% tested positive for enteroviruses. CV-A2 was predominant (78.0%), followed by CV-A4, CV-A5, CV-A10, CV-A16, and CV-B4. Fever (95.5%) and oral vesicles (98.5%) were the most common symptoms. Compared to other serotypes, CV-A2 cases had a higher incidence of fever (P = 0.008) and peak temperature (P = 0.042), with significantly elevated neutrophil percentages (> 70.0%) and reduced lymphocyte percentages (< 20.0%). Phylogenetic analysis identified genotype D accounted for 97.8% (90/92) of CV-A2 strains; two genotype C strains clustered with European isolates. Recombination analysis revealed intra- and inter-serotype events involving CV-A2, A4, and A5.ConclusionCV-A2 has re-emerged as the dominant herpangina-associated enterovirus in Eastern China, demonstrating distinct clinical and hematological features. This shift in serotype prevalence underscores the need for continued surveillance to monitor its spread and interactions with other serotypes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13099-025-00762-9.Keywords: Coxsackievirus A2, Herpangina, Enterovirus, Epidemiology, Phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Herpangina (HA) is an acute upper respiratory tract infectious disease, primarily affecting children under ten years of age. It is characterized by fever and vesicular or ulcerative lesions in the oropharyngeal region and is highly contagious, spreading through the fecal-oral route and respiratory droplets [1]. Although most HA cases are self-limiting, severe cases can lead to serious complications such as encephalitis, aseptic meningitis, acute flaccid paralysis, and myocarditis, which can lead to fatal outcomes [2].

HA is primarily caused by enteroviruses, including Coxsackievirus A (CV-A) 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 16, and Enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) [3]. The pathogen spectrum of HA has been documented to overlap with hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) [4], though some studies indicate that different enterovirus serotypes cause these diseases, even during the same epidemic period [5]. Despite its prevalence and severity, HA has not been classified as a notifiable disease in China. Thus, its epidemiological features and pathogen spectrum remain insufficiently determined.

Before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic, HFMD and HA continued to circulate in China, exhibiting two seasonal incidence peaks in summer and autumn, respectively [6, 7]. Routine surveillance systems indicate that the predominant serotypes of HFMD remain Coxsackievirus A6 (CV-A6), A16 (CV-A16), and A10 (CV-A10) [8, 9]. In Zhejiang Province, the prevalence of CV-A6, CV-A16, and CV-A10 were documented to be 64.4–77.8% [10, 11], 14.79% [12], and 0–13.6.6% [10, 13], respectively. In contrast, surveillance from other regions in China, such as Sichuan (a province in southwestern China) (76.5%, 13.5% and 10%) and Liaoning (in northeastern China) (35.6%, 25.2%, 4.2%), shows some variations in the prevalence. Since the introduction of the EV-A71 vaccine, CV-A6 has gradually become the dominant serotype causing HFMD in China [14–17].

So far, the leading serotype responsible for HA has remained uncertain. In 2023, an outbreak in Beijing (northern China) identified CV-A16 as the predominant HA-associated serotype [18], whereas a study in 2015 in Hangzhou (Zhejiang, China) reported CV-A2 as the most common serotype, accounting for 62.9% of HA cases [19]. Notably, by 2016, CV-A2 prevalence had declined markedly in Hangzhou, while other serotypes such as CV-A16, CV-A4, and CV-A6 became more prevalent [10, 19].

Although HA has not been routinely monitored, regional hospital-based reports have noted periodic increases in cases, indicating the need for further epidemiological investigation. In 2024, we conducted HA surveillance in two regions of Zhejiang Province as follows: Yuhuan City (county-level) of Taizhou City (a coastal island that has been transformed into a peninsula through land reclamation), and Liandu District of Lishui City (a southwestern inland region in Zhejiang Province). In Yuhuan City, we detected and identified the serotypes of 133 HA cases, among which CV-A2 is predominant with 69.1% detection rate (92 of 133), markedly higher than that reported previously. Therefore, the study systematically determined the clinical manifestations and epidemiological characteristics of HA cases in Yuhuan City and focused on CV-A2 serotype. Furthermore, we performed pathogen detection and bioinformatics analysis to further characterize these cases, providing evidence to improve the prevention and control strategy of HA.

Materials and methods

Study design

The study was conducted based on sentinel surveillance of HA outbreaks reported by designated sentinel hospitals between 27 May and 25 June 2024. Routine surveillance was conducted in two regions of Zhejiang Province, Yuhuan City of Taizhou City and Liandu District of Lishui City, in May–June 2024. It targeted enterovirus spectrum, enabling timely etiologic and genomic characterization. Yuhuan City was selected as the primary study site due to a local HA outbreak with a high proportion of CV-A2 cases, which warranted focused investigation. Liandu District served only as a contextual comparison. Demographic and clinical data were retrospectively collected. Biological specimens were collected for enterovirus detection, in which CV-A2 full-length VP1 and whole genome were sequenced. Furthermore, bioinformatics analyses were conducted incorporating spatiotemporal data.

Surveillance site

Yuhuan City, an administrative part of Taizhou City, is located on the coast of Zhejiang Province in eastern China. Once an island, it was connected to the mainland through extensive land reclamation. The region has a subtropical monsoon climate, with hot, humid summers and mild winters, and receives abundant rainfall, conditions that may facilitate pathogen transmission. Yuhuan People’s Hospital (in Yucheng town) and Yuhuan Second People’s Hospital (in Chumen town) were designated as sentinel hospitals.

Surveillance population

The surveillance population covers children aged 0–6 years that are permanent residents in Yuhuan (total population: 651,500, with children aged 0–6 years accounting for 6.09%). According to the Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis and Treatment of Herpangina (2019 Edition) [3], HA cases were diagnosed based on the following criteria: (1) epidemiological history, including onset in infants or young children during peak epidemic seasons, or exposure to outbreaks of HA or HFMD in local childcare settings or communities, or direct/indirect contact with confirmed cases; (2) typical clinical manifestations, such as acute fever and sore throat with characteristic oropharyngeal lesions (greyish-white vesicles or ulcers on the faucial arches, soft palate, uvula, or tonsils); and (3) laboratory confirmation by pathogen detection.

Data collection

Data collection included demographic characteristics of the cases, such as date of birth, sex, caregiving type (home care, kindergarten, or primary schools), geographic location of the kindergartens/schools, number of children in the kindergartens/schools, and reporting sentinel hospital. Basic information on HA onset was obtained through structured questionnaires, including date of symptom onset, date of diagnosis, history of previous onset, and primary clinical symptoms.

Specimen collection and examination

Nasopharyngeal and anal swabs from HA cases reported by sentinel hospitals were collected on the day of reporting. All specimens were transported at 4 °C and stored at −80 °C until further examination. Enterovirus nucleic acids were extracted from nasopharyngeal swabs using the Viral Nucleic Acid Extraction Kit (BioGerm, Shanghai) on the Automated Nucleic Acid Extraction System (BG-Flex-48, BioGerm, Shanghai). Nucleic acid quality was assessed using the Enterovirus qPCR Detection Kit (BioGerm, Shanghai). Samples with cycle threshold (Ct) values < 30, as supported by local CDC validation showing consistent association with high viral load and reproducibility, were considered positive.

Qualified specimens underwent primary amplification with the Enterovirus Genotyping PCR Kit (BioGerm, Shanghai), targeting the 5’ untranslated region (5’UTR) for serotyping. Amplification products (2 µL) were analysed by 2% agarose gel electrophoresis to confirm target bands. PCR products with confirmed bands were then sequenced using Sanger sequencing (ABI3730xl, BigDye® Terminator V3.1 Cycle Sequencing Kit). Raw sequences were assembled using SeqMan (DNASTAR) and aligned with NCBI BLAST (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi) and the Enterovirus Genotyping Tool (https://www.genomedetective.com/app/typingtool/etv/) for serotyping.

Positive samples with cycle threshold (CT) values ≤ 25 were selected for targeted amplification using the Tarich Enterovirus A Nucleic Acid Enrichment Kit SJ-CX-601 (BioGerm, Shanghai), and PCR products were subjected to library preparation using the Whole Genome DNA Library Preparation Kit SJ-CX-606 (BioGerm, Shanghai), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The constructed qualified libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (MGI Tech Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) using a paired-end 150 bp (PE150) strategy, generating at least 1 Gb of data per sample with a Q30% above 85%. Raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control using FastQC (v0.11.9), and low-quality reads and adapter sequences were removed using Fastp (v0.23.2). Clean reads were aligned to reference genomes using BWA (v0.7.17) and SAMtools (v1.16.1). Consensus sequences were generated with Ivar (v1.3.1).

These CV-A2 VP1 and whole-genome sequences have been deposited in GenBank under accession numbers PV453307-PV453395.

Phylogenetic analysis

In this study, a total of 92 specimens (69.2%) tested positive for CV-A2. The full-length VP1 gene (885 bp in length) was amplified from all positive samples, and whole-genome sequencing was performed on a random subset (n = 13). For comparative analysis, 239 CV-A2 VP1 and 98 whole-genome sequences were retrieved in the GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank). Furthermore, whole-genome sequences of co-circulating enteroviruses (CV-A6, CV-A10, CV-A16, CV-A4, CV-A5, CV-B4, and Echovirus 18) that were uploaded within the past five years were collected to assess recombination events.

Sequence pre-processing (selection, merging, and trimming) was performed using R v4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Nucleotide and amino acid similarities of CV-A2 VP1 sequences were assessed by using the MegAlign module in Lasergene v7.1 (DNASTAR, Madison, WI, USA). The optimal nucleotide substitution model, substitution frequencies, and substitution patterns were estimated using the Maximum Likelihood method in MEGA 11 (Pennsylvania State University, PA, USA). Then a similarity threshold of 98% was applied to filter the VP1 sequences retrieved in the GenBank to prepare a sequence dataset for further analysis. Moreover, a time-scaled Bayesian phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using BEAST v1.10.4 (University of Auckland, New Zealand) using a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) chain of 20 million steps and logging every 2,000 steps, incorporating country as a trait to assess spatiotemporal transmission.

For other serotypes, whole-genome sequences were filtered at a similarity threshold of 95% to construct a whole-genome sequence dataset. Additionally, recombination analysis was performed using RDP5 (University of Cape Town, South Africa) with detection confirmed by multiple algorithms (RDP, GENECONV, Chimaera, MaxChi, BootScan, SiScan, 3Seq). The CV-A2 strains identified in this study were then compared with the CV-A2 sequences retrieved in the GenBank to evaluate their spatial and temporal distribution across years and regions.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages, and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on distribution. Between-group comparisons were performed using ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous data, and Pearson’s chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical data, as appropriate. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted in R v4.4.2.

Visualization

The TopoJSON map of Taizhou City was obtained from geojson.cn. Geographic distribution and case incidence were visualised using Datawrapper (www.datawrapper.de). Phylogenetic trees and other results were visualised using MEGA 11 and FigTree.

Results

Characteristics of HA cases

A total of 133 HA cases were included in this study (Table 1, Overall), comprising 70 boys (52.6%) and 63 girls (47.4%). Ages ranged from 0 to 12 years, with a median age of 3 years (IQR: 2.0, 4.0). Child-caregiving type and kindergartens/schools were available for 111 cases. Of them, 41.4% (46/111) were home-cared, 53.2% (59/111) attended kindergartens, and 5.4% (6/111) attended primary schools. The kindergartens and schools were in Yuhuan City and neighbouring Wenling City (Fig. 1). No clear epidemiological links were identified between cases from different kindergartens and schools.Almost the half of the HA cases (42.9%, 57/133) were diagnosed within one day of symptom onset, while 5.3% (7/133) had a diagnosis delay up to three days. Additionally, nineteen children had a prior history of HFMD or HA, of which eight had documented onset dates. Except for one child who was diagnosed with the illness in 2013, all other cases occurred within the preceding three years.

Fever was observed in 127 HA cases (95.5%), with a mean peak temperature of 39.2 ± 0.7 °C. Oral vesicles or ulcers were present in 131 cases (98.5%), including 3 cases (2.3%) with vesicles on the buccal mucosa and 130 cases (99.2%) with vesicles in the oropharyngeal region.

Among the 133 cases, 118 (88.7%) tested positive for enteroviruses. Serotyping revealed that 92 cases (78.0%) were CV-A2, followed by CV-A4 (7.6%, 9/118), CV-A5 (6.8%, 8/118), CV-A10 (3.4%, 4/118), CV-A16 (2.5%, 3/118), and CV-B4 (1.7%, 2/118). Comparative analysis of CV-A2 -infected cases, those infected with other enterovirus serotypes, and enterovirus-negative cases showed that the enterovirus-negative cases were significantly younger than the enterovirus-positive cases (P = 0.002). Furthermore, CV-A2 cases had a higher proportion of fever cases (P = 0.008), and a significant difference in peak temperature (P = 0.042). However, no significant differences were found by sex, kindergartens/primary schools, prior history of HFMD or HA, presence of oral, buccal, or oropharyngeal vesicles, reporting sentinel hospital, or the time interval from symptom onset to medical consultation (Table 1, CV-A2, Other Serotypes, Negative).

Among the 133 cases, 128 (96.2%) underwent blood routine examination. Of them, 64.1% (82/128) had elevated leukocyte counts (> 10 × 10⁹/L), 43.0% (55/128) had increased neutrophil percentages (> 70.0%), and 47.6% (61/128) had reduced lymphocyte percentages (< 20.0%). Significant differences were observed in neutrophil and lymphocyte percentages (P < 0.001) when comparing CV-A2 cases to non-CV-A2 cases and enterovirus-negative cases (Table 2).

Phylogenetic reconstruction of CV-A2 sequences

The study included 239 full-length VP1 sequences and 104 whole-genome sequences after filtering, which were used to construct the VP1 and whole genome datasets, respectively. The nucleotide and amino acid similarity for the VP1 were 79.2%−100.0% and 92.5%−100.0%, while for the whole-genome sequences ranged from 78.4% to 100.0% and 95.8%−100% (Table 3). Except for strains Z179, Z221, Z225, and Z283, the VP1 sequences exhibited high similarity (detailed information provided in Supplementary Table 1). The optimal nucleotide substitution model for CV-A2 is GTR + G + I, based on BIC and AIC, with a VP1 nucleotide substitution invariant rate of 47.5%.

Discussion

The study is the first to identify CV-A2 as a potentially reemerging dominant pathogen in HA cases in Yuhuan City, located in Zhejiang Province in eastern China, with a positive proportion of 78.0%. In contrast, another sentinel site—Liandu District, Lishui City—predominantly detected CV-A16, CV-A10, and Echovirus 18 in HA cases, consistent with reports from other regions of Zhejiang Province and other provinces in China [4, 19]. Furthermore, in Yuhuan City and Liandu District, the predominant HFMD serotype in 2024 was CV-A16 (55.4%, 31/56; 55.1%, 27/49, respectively), followed by CV-A6 (19.6%, 11/56; 8.2%, 4/49, respectively), which were highly similar to the pathogen spectrum in other regions of Zhejiang Province by routine HFMD surveillance. Given the lack of significant epidemiological links between the two regions, we suggest that a sustained CV-A2 epidemic in Yuhuan City led to the seasonal HA outbreak. Regional differences between Yuhuan (CV-A2-dominant) and Liandu (CV-A16-dominant) may arise from variations in surveillance workflows, healthcare-seeking behaviors, population immunity profiles, or the timing of viral introduction. It requires standardized cross-site surveillance protocols and serological investigations to clarify the underlying drivers.

Previous studies have identified CV-A2 as a major enterovirus serotype associated with HA in Taiwan, China (2008, 40%) [22], South Korea (2011, 20.0%) [23], Thailand (2015, 35.8%) [24], and Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China (2015, 62.9%) [19]. However, its prevalence rarely exceeded 65% in the above studies. Additionally, other studies conducted in China have reported the prevalence of CV-A2 ranging from 7.1% to 22.5% across different regions and disease type (HFMD and HA), including Zunyi (a city in southwestern China, 2019; 21.5% in HA and 5.4% in HFMD [25]), Dongguan (in southeastern China, 2015; 22.5% in HA and HFMD [26]), and Beijing (in northern China, 2021–2022; 7.1% in HA [27]). The study demonstrated highest prevalence of CV-A2, surpassing other enterovirus serotypes. Notably, even common serotypes like CV-A6, A16, and A10 rarely have been documented the prevalence of exceeding 80% in HFMD or HA cases. Our findings raised a public health concern regarding the continued spread of CV-A2 to neighbouring regions, potentially leading to HA and/or HFMD outbreaks. It remains crucial for continual monitoring of CV-A2 and its interactions with other serotypes.

The surveillance site in this study, Yuhuan City, is an island, which may differ from inland regions in terms of enterovirus prevalence and disease incidence. Coastal areas often rely on bivalve molluscs as a major protein source, which may accumulate waterborne enteroviruses [28]. Additionally, islands tend to have smaller areas, denser populations, and underdeveloped healthcare services, potentially facilitating localised viral transmission. Previous research in Zhoushan Island, Zhejiang Province, revealed that the predominant HFMD pathogens alternated between CV-A16 and CV-A6 prior to the introduction of the EV-A71 vaccine [29], significantly differing from the HFMD pathogen spectrum in the inland regions. These observations suggest that island-specific demographic and environmental characteristics could plausibly shape HA epidemiology. However, demographic and environmental data were not available in the study. Therefore, it warrants enhanced pathogen surveillance and active investigation in islands with high population mobility.

Among the cases in this study, CV-A2 cases had more frequent fevers and higher body temperatures. However, fever is nonspecific, and no significant differences were found in other symptoms compared to the cases infected with other serotypes or those negative for enterovirus. Routine blood tests indicated that CV-A2 infection might be linked to abnormal results, characterized by reduced lymphocyte percentages (< 20%) and elevated neutrophil percentages (>70%). Clinical manifestation such as fever, rash emerging parts, desquamation and onychomadesis are different among outpatient HFMD cases infected with CV-A16, CV-A6, EV-A71, CV-A10 and other enteroviruses [30]. However, evidence in HA cases remained insufficient and uncertain. CV-A2 cases showed higher fever rates and neutrophil–lymphocyte shifts; however, our surveillance did not observe severe outcomes. These markers may reflect acute inflammatory responses rather than complications. Thus, it warrants further study, such as prospective cohorts with standardized severity endpoints, to determine potential symptoms and signs by enterovirus serotypes, which could facilitate tailoring the treatment strategy.

So far, over 800 CV-A2 sequences are available in GenBank, with approximately 10% being whole-genomes, illustrating CV-A2 global distribution and continued circulation for decades. It is prevalent worldwide, especially in Asia and Europe. About 50% of the CV-A2 sequences are isolated from Asian countries such as China, Thailand, Vietnam, Japan, South Korea, and India, while over 30% come from Europe, particularly Russia, with others from the Netherlands, the UK, France, Germany, and Finland. CV-A2 has also been detected in Australia, the Americas, and Africa. It has been linked to HFMD and HA outbreaks in Taiwan, China (2008) [22], South Korea (2009) [23], and Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China (2015) [19], underscoring its rapid spread and potential to cause outbreaks.

Prior phylogenetic studies show that CV-A2 genotype D is the dominant circulating genotype of CV-A2 in mainland China [20]. Sequencing results from this study confirmed that most CV-A2 strains were classified to genotype D, consistent with previous findings. In our study, the Z179 and Z283 strains were classified as genotype C, which is rare in recent GenBank uploads. Analysis of GenBank CV-A2 sequences revealed high nucleotide and amino acid similarity among CV-A2 genotype D sequences circulating in mainland China. However, the prevalence of CV-A2 in Yuhuan City appeared significantly higher than in any other regions. It is noted that CV-A2 cases from the neighbouring Wenling City suggested CV-A2’s potential spread to the inlands, possibly altering the pathogen spectrum of HA in neighbouring regions. This underscored the importance of ongoing surveillance of HA pathogen spectrum in the region, contributing to understanding the enterovirus epidemic in island regions and informing future vaccine strategies.

In addition to the potential shift in pathogen spectrum, our genomic analyses revealed recombination signals between CV-A2 and other serotypes (notably CV-A4 and CV-A5). Such recombination events are well recognized in enteroviruses and may serve as an important driver of viral evolution [31]. Detected intra-/inter-serotype recombination could, in principle, modulate replication fitness, tissue tropism, or antigenic context, thereby influencing transmission dynamics and clinical presentation [32]. However, we did not perform functional assays in the study, and we lacked immunological or phenotypic data to assess the biological consequences. The implications therefore remain hypothesis-generating, underscoring the need for neutralization profiling, functional characterization, and longitudinal genomic surveillance to better understand the role of recombination in shaping CV-A2 epidemiology.

The study has several limitations. First, the short surveillance period limited the understanding of long-term CV-A2 prevalence and temporal trends in the region. These findings nevertheless underscored the need for enhanced CV-A2 surveillance in China and other affected countries. Second, while HFMD is a notifiable disease with routine surveillance in China, HA has not classified as a notifiable disease, leading to underestimation in surveillance sensitivity. Third, the sample size was limited in our study, with only 133 HA cases included from sentinel hospitals. The two sentinel hospitals are the primary pediatric healthcare facilities in Yuhuan City. Given the city’s compact size and relatively low population mobility, these hospitals are geographically well distributed and may provide rational case coverage and spatial representativeness. However, mild HA cases might not visit these hospitals and might have been underrepresented. If HA severity varies by serotype, this underrepresentation could further influence the observed pathogen spectrum.

Conclusion

The study is the first to identify CV-A2 as the reemerging and predominant pathogen in HA cases in Yuhuan City, located in Zhejiang Province in eastern China, with a positive proportion of 78.0%. This suggested the continued CV-A2 circulation on the island. Therefore, it is essential to conduct ongoing surveillance of HA pathogens to determine whether this serotype spreads to neighbouring regions or remains suppressed by other dominant serotypes (CV-A6, A16, and A10). Additionally, monitoring clinical manifestations, especially neurological complications and rare infection presentations, is of significant importance, despite no significant differences in clinical manifestations being observed between CV-A2 and other serotypes.

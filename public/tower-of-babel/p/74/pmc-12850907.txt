Transgastric biliary drainage through a biliodigestive efferent loop using a lumen-apposing metal stent
Authors: Karsten Büringer, Stefano Fusco, Dörte Wichmann, Ulrike Schempf, Pavlos Missios, Christoph Stein-Thöringer, Dominik Zinsser, Nisar P Malek, Christoph R Werner
Published: 2024

A 28-year-old female patient presented with jaundice secondary to recurrent cholangiocarcinoma. Her medical history included a right hemihepatectomy with biliodigestive reconstruction via a jejunal loop 1 year prior, a surgical gastroenterostomy because of malignant gastric outlet obstruction 1 month prior, and surgical intervention for an adhesive ileus 2 weeks prior. Laboratory analysis revealed a markedly elevated blood bilirubin level of 14.5 mg/dL. CT imaging demonstrated a complete obstruction of the efferent jejunal loop of the biliodigestive reconstruction caused by a localized tumor mass, resulting in significant upstream dilatation of both the jejunal loop and the intrahepatic biliary ducts up to 8 mm (Fig. 1). The CT scan revealed a close topographic relationship between the dilated efferent loop and the antral region of the stomach (red dotted line in Fig. 1). To decompress the intrahepatic biliary system, we performed a transgastric endosonography-guided drainage of the dilated jejunal loop through the area identified in the CT scan. For this, we implanted a 15 × 10–mm Hot Axios stent (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, Mass, USA) (Fig. 2A and B) freehand under endosonographic (Pentax EG38-J10UT; Hoya Corp, Tokyo, Japan) and fluoroscopic control. Transgastric retrograde cholangiography through the Axios stent using a biliary catheter confirmed effective biliary drainage (Fig. 2C). The biliary flow was redirected through the Axios stent, traversing the stomach and the functional gastroenterostomy to the small intestine (Fig. 2D). The patient was discharged after 4 days (bilirubin level 4.6 mg/dL), and palliative chemotherapy was resumed. Follow-up CT imaging 12 weeks postintervention confirmed the integrity of the stent, with no dilation in the efferent loop or biliary system (Fig. 3) and normalized blood bilirubin levels (0.4 mg/dL).

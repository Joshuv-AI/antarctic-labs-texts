ARNT2-driven transcriptional activation of STRA6 reprograms fatty acid metabolism to promote retroperitoneal liposarcoma progression
Authors: Junxiang Zhang, Hainan Guo, Bo Ban, Lin Yang, Jie Lian, Gan Li, Lindi Cai, Kai Deng, He Jiang, Xuqi Li, Shufeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRetroperitoneal liposarcoma (RLPS) is a mesenchymal-derived malignant tumor characterized by high aggressiveness and a propensity for local recurrence. Emerging evidence implicates aberrant fatty acid metabolism as a key driver of RLPS progression, yet the transcriptional regulators orchestrating this process remain poorly defined.MethodsBioinformatics integrating cellular experiments demonstrated the role of fatty acid metabolism in the progression of RLPS. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and Western blotting (WB) were employed to validate the expression levels of ARNT2, and the role of ARNT2 in tumor progression was demonstrated through CCK8 assays, Transwell invasion assays and wound healing assays. Further experiments confirmed the transcriptional regulatory effect of ARNT2 on STRA6 and demonstrated its control over RLPS progression by modulating intracellular lipid droplet and triglyceride levels.ResultsHere, we identify ARNT2 as a novel oncogenic transcription factor that promotes RLPS growth by transcriptionally upregulating STRA6, thereby reprogramming fatty acid metabolism. Specifically, ARNT2 binds to the STRA6 promoter to enhance its activity, thereby upregulating fatty acid metabolic enzymes and promoting lipid droplet accumulation with elevated triglycerides. This metabolic shift fuels energy production and biomass synthesis in RLPS cells, ultimately accelerating tumor proliferation and invasion.ConclusionThis study identifies ARNT2 as a transcriptional modulator of fatty acid metabolism pathways, highlighting its potential as a viable molecular target for RLPS therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-025-06352-1.Keywords: Retroperitoneal liposarcoma, Fatty acid metabolism, Transcriptional regulation, Soft tissue sarcoma

Introduction

Retroperitoneal liposarcoma (RLPS), as the most common soft tissue sarcoma in the retroperitoneum, accounts for more than 40% of such cases (Xie et al 2024). Due to the ample growth space provided by the retroperitoneum (Hashimoto et al. 2022), the disease often progresses insidiously. Coupled with the inherent aggressiveness of the tumor, it frequently invades adjacent organs, all of which significantly increase the difficulty and risks associated with surgical intervention (Devaud et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2024). Additionally, RLPS exhibits resistance to both chemotherapy and radiotherapy (Callegaro et al. 2023; Mj et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2023), leading to a high likelihood of postoperative recurrence and poor long-term quality of life for patients(Gao et al. 2023). Consequently, the elucidation of core regulatory mechanisms is crucial, serving as a foundation for discovering novel therapeutic targets.

Metabolic reprogramming has emerged as a hallmark of cancer (Wan et al. 2025), with dysregulated lipid metabolism gaining increasing recognition as a pivotal pathway in malignant cells (Yu et al. 2021). Fatty acid metabolism serves as the fundamental biochemical axis of lipid metabolism and remains highly active in various types of cancers (Tu et al. 2025; Jiang et al. 2024; Kothapalli et al. 2023). Over the past decade, a growing body of research demonstrates that aberrant fatty acid metabolism acts as a central driver in liposarcoma pathogenesis and metastatic progression. When fatty acid synthesis is inhibited, cell proliferation decreases and apoptosis increases (Is et al. 2010; Jeitany et al. 2021; Guan et al. 2011). Reprogramming fatty acid metabolic pathways presents a promising new strategy for treating RLPS.

Aryl hydrocarbon receptor nuclear translocator 2 (ARNT2) belongs to the bHLH/PAS superfamily and functions as a transcription factor in various physiological processes (Choi et al. 2024; Sharma et al. 2019). Emerging evidence demonstrates that ARNT2 exhibits context-dependent roles in tumorigenesis, functioning as either a tumor suppressor or onco-promoter across diverse malignancies (Kimura et al. 2016; Jia et al. 2019; Jin et al. 2025). However, there is a lack of research on the regulatory mechanism of ARNT2 in retroperitoneal liposarcoma. Stimulated by retinoic acid 6 (STRA6) is a receptor for retinol-binding protein. As a multi-pass transmembrane protein, its main role is to mediate the transmembrane transport of retinol (Dhokia and Macip 2021). Interestingly, STRA6 stimulates SREBP1 mediated adipogenesis to supply energy substrates essential for sustaining non-small cell lung cancer growth (Zhou et al. 2025). This finding also provides a theoretical foundation for our subsequent research.

In this study, we demonstrated the promoting role of fatty acid metabolism in RLPS progression. ARNT2 overexpression was validated in RLPS through multi-omics profiling of clinical cohorts, with elevated levels indicating adverse clinical outcomes. Integrated transcriptomic analysis revealed that ARNT2 upregulates fatty acid metabolism, a process mediated through activation of STRA6 transcriptional activity. Notably, STRA6 serves as a critical downstream target gene of ARNT2 in this metabolic regulatory mechanism.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatic data processing and analysis pipeline

We obtained transcriptomic and clinical data from 59 liposarcoma patients in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (Lee et al. 2015). Given the absence of matched normal adipose tissues in TCGA, we supplemented the analysis with transcriptomic and clinical datasets from 512 normal adipose tissue samples in the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project (Lonsodale et al. 2013). Lipid metabolism-related genes were curated from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis platform. We performed expression analysis of ARNT2 and STRA6 using the online platform Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) (Li et al. 2017).

Cell culture and transfection

Human adipose-derived stem cells (hADSCs) and liposarcoma cell line SW872 were purchased from Procell (Hubei, China). Liposarcoma cell line 94T778 were purchased from Xiamen Immocell Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Zhejiang, China). The SW872 and 94T778 cell lines were maintained in high-glucose DMEM (Gibco, USA) containing with 10% FBS (Vazyme, China). hADSCs was cultured in hADSCs complete medium (Procell, China).

We purchased small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting ARNT2 and STRA6 from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The ARNT2 overexpression plasmid (pCMV-ARNT2) and STRA6 overexpression plasmid (pCMV-STRA6) was purchased from MiaoLingBio (Hubei, China). The pLKO.1-shARNT2 plasmids (#1 and #2) were generated in our laboratory. The complete sequences are provided in Table S1.

Human tissue samples

Thirteen pairs of retroperitoneal liposarcoma tissues and matched adipose tissues were acquired during radical resection procedures at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (Xi’an, China) between Jul. 2023 and May. 2025.

RT-qPCR and western blot

Total RNA was isolated using the RNA Easy Fast Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China). Reverse transcription was performed using 5X All-In-One RT Master Mix (abm, Canada). qPCR was performed using NovoStart® SYBR qPCR SuperMix Plus (Novoprotein, China). The primer sequences used for qPCR amplification are detailed in Table S2. Electrophoretic separation via SDS-PAGE preceded protein electroblotting onto nitrocellulose substrates. Following blockade via 5% skimmed milk, primary antibody probing was conducted at 4 °C for > 12 h, succeeded by room-temperature exposure to secondary antibody for 60 min. Immunoblots were detected using the NcmECL Ultra Chemiluminescence Kit (New Cell & Molecular Biotech, China). Antibody details are summarized in Table S3.

Cell viability and colony formation assays

The CCK8 kit (Biosharp, China) was utilized to assess cell viability. Following supplementation of 10% reagent into DMEM, continuous thermal exposure at 37 °C was maintained for 1 h to achieve binding saturation. Subsequently, OD at 450 nm was measured.

For the colony formation assay, 800 cells/well were plated in 6-well culture dishes. Post 14-day incubation, cellular monolayers underwent 4% paraformaldehyde fixation, 1% crystal violet staining (15 min), and PBS rinsing.

Wound healing assay

We cultured liposarcoma cells in six-well plates and created a scratch in the cell monolayer using a 10 μl pipette tip. Cell images were captured at 0- and 24-h post-scratching, and the wound area between the two edges of the scratch was measured.

Transwell invasion assay

The Transwell chambers (BD Bioscience, USA) used for the invasion assay have been pre-coated with Matrigel. Cells suspended in serum-free DMEM were then seeded into the upper chamber, while the lower chamber contained 10% FBS-DMEM as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation, cells were photographed and quantified.

Dual-luciferase reporter

Using the hTFtarget database, we predicted potential binding sites for ARNT2 on the STRA6 promoter. Subsequently, we generated luciferase reporter plasmids harboring sequences with either the wild-type or the mutated ARNT2 binding sites, using pGL4.74 as the reference plasmid. Following co-transfection of the plasmids and subsequent 48-h incubation, fluorescence activity was measured.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, processed through sequential 70%-100% ethanol dehydration, xylene clearing, and paraffin embedding; 3–5 μm sections were mounted, baked (60 °C, 30–60 min), deparaffinized in xylene, and rehydrated through graded alcohols. Antigen retrieval was performed using pH-specific buffers (microwave/pressure heating), followed by endogenous peroxidase quenching with 3% H₂O₂ (15 min, RT) and blocking with 5% BSA (30 min, RT). Primary antibodies (1:250 dilution) were incubated overnight at 4 °C, then HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied (60 min, RT) after PBS washes. Signal was detected via DAB (3–10 min), reactions terminated with distilled water, sections counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral balsam.

Oil Red O staining

After gently aspirating the culture medium, the cells were washed once with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. Following two PBS rinses, the cells were briefly incubated with staining wash buffer (20 s) before applying Oil Red O working solution (Beyotime, China) for 10–20 min. Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin, and images were acquired under a microscope after a final PBS wash.

Triglyceride detection assay

The triglyceride content was measured using a Triglyceride Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). After collecting the cells, we mixed them with isopropanol and added the mixture to a 96-well plate. Each well received triglyceride assay reagent, with subsequent 37 °C thermostatic processing (60 min) to facilitate enzymatic hydrolysis and chromogenic reactions. Finally, the absorbance at 570 nm (A570) was measured.

Mouse xenograft model

Female nude mice aged 3–4 weeks were purchased and housed in the SPF animal facility at the Animal Experiment Center of Xi’an Jiaotong University. After collecting 3 × 10^6 SW872 cells, we resuspended them in 100 μL of PBS and then inoculated subcutaneously into the left dorsal region. Following euthanasia, xenografts were surgically excised from mice for volumetric measurement and gravimetric analysis.

Statistical analysis

The mean ± SD values of data are reported, and statistical significance between groups was determined using t-tests (two groups) or ANOVA (≥ 3 groups). GraphPad Prism version 9 and R software version 4.2.2 were employed for data analysis and graphical representation. Statistical significance was defined as follows: *P-values less than 0.05, **P-values less than 0.01, ***P-values less than 0.001, and ****P-values less than 0.0001. "NS" was used to denote non-significance.

Results

Aberrant fatty acid metabolism serves as a pivotal driver in liposarcoma tumorigenesis

A total of 782 LMRGs were obtained from the GSEA website. After matching with the TCGA-GTEx cohort, we conducted differential expression analysis on 771 lipid LMRGs (Fig. 1A). By performing GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on 255 differentially expressed genes, we further explored the significantly enriched biological functions and pathways (Fig. 1B-D). Among them, multiple fatty acid metabolism-related activities and pathways were found to be enriched, such as the “Fatty acid metabolic process” and the “Biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids”. Through NMF algorithm analysis of LMRGs expression levels, LPS patients were classified into two subtypes (Fig. 1E). K-M survival analysis of overall survival revealed that patients in cluster C1 exhibited a more favorable prognosis (Fig. 1F). The MCPcounter algorithm was employed to evaluate the infiltration of immune cells corresponding to the classifications (Fig.S1A). Notably, multiple types of immune cells exhibited high expression levels in cluster C1. Meanwhile, we utilized a Sankey diagram to explore the relationship between subtypes and immunological classifications (Fig. S1B). The immune subtypes among patients in cluster C1 were relatively evenly distributed, whereas patients in cluster C2 were predominantly concentrated in immuneC1 (wound healing) and immuneC6 (TGF-beta dominant). GSEA of the C1 and C2 subsets revealed significant activation of multiple lipid metabolic pathways in the C2 cluster (Fig S1C), including neutral lipid biosynthetic process, fatty acid transmembrane transport and glycerolipid metabolism.

As a core branch of lipid metabolism, the abnormal regulation of fatty acid metabolism is closely associated with metabolic disorders such as insulin resistance and obesity, as well as with tumorigenesis and progression. Therefore, we employed the acetyl-CoA carboxylase inhibitor TOFA, which blocks the synthesis of malonyl-CoA by inhibiting acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC), thereby suppressing fatty acid synthesis. We validated the IC50 values of TOFA in SW872 and 94T778 cell lines (Fig. S1D). Colony formation (Fig. 1G), CCK8 (Fig. 1H), and wound healing assays (Fig. 1I) demonstrated that TOFA reduced the proliferation, viability, and migratory capacity of liposarcoma cells. In summary, these findings suggest that targeting the fatty acid metabolism can inhibit the progression of liposarcoma.

ARNT2 exhibits a high level of expression in liposarcoma and shows a correlation with unfavorable patient prognosis

To further investigate the regulatory mechanisms of fatty acid metabolism, we selected the transcription factor ARNT2 from among the differentially expressed lipid metabolism-related genes. We utilized the TIMER website to explore the expression status of ARNT2 (Fig. S1E). Interestingly, ARNT2 showed high expression in breast cancer and cholangiocarcinoma, whereas it exhibited low expression in colorectal cancer, lung cancer. Since normal adipose tissue data were unavailable in TCGA, we supplemented this dataset with GTEx data to analyze ARNT2 expression levels. As show in Fig. 2A and C, compared with adipose tissue, ARNT2 exhibits significantly higher expression levels in liposarcoma. The ARNT2 expression levels are elevated in liposarcoma patients who experience distant recurrence compared to those without recurrence (Fig. 2B). Kaplan–Meier curves demonstrated that patients with high ARNT2 expression exhibited poor prognosis in terms of overall survival (Fig. 2D), distant-recurrence-free survival (Fig. 2E), and metastasis-free survival (Fig. 2f). Meanwhile, through detection in 10 pairs of retroperitoneal liposarcoma tissues and their paired normal adipose tissues, we found that the protein level of ARNT2 was significantly higher in retroperitoneal liposarcoma (Fig. 2G). H Score also demonstrated that among 13 pairs of RLPS and normal adipose tissues, ARNT2 exhibited high expression in RLPS (Fig. 2H, I). Comparative analysis revealed significantly upregulated ARNT2 expression in liposarcoma cell lines (SW872 and 94T778) compared with hADSCs, with the highest expression observed in the SW872 cell line (Fig. 2J). Collectively, these findings suggest that ARNT2 may play a potential regulatory role in RLPS.

ARNT2 acts as a driver for the growth of liposarcoma cells

Given that ARNT2 exhibited significantly higher expression levels in two liposarcoma cell lines compared to hADSCs, with SW872 showing the highest expression, we aimed to investigate the functional role of ARNT2 in liposarcoma. To this end, we employed gene knockdown strategies to silence ARNT2 expression in both SW872 and 94T778 cell lines (Fig. 3A, B), while concurrently overexpressing ARNT2 in the 94T778 cell line as a complementary approach (Fig. S2A, B). CCK8 and colony formation assays revealed that ARNT2 knockdown markedly inhibited proliferation and viability in liposarcoma cells. (Fig. 3C, F). Conversely, overexpression of ARNT2 exhibited the opposite effects (Fig. S2C, F). Further, Transwell invasion assays and wound healing assays also provided compelling evidence that overexpression of ARNT2 enhanced the invasive and migratory capabilities of liposarcoma cells (Fig. S2D, E). When ARNT2 was knocked down, a marked reduction in these malignant behaviors of liposarcoma cells was observed (Fig. 3D, E). The experimental results indicated that malignant progression of liposarcoma is substantially accelerated by ARNT2-mediated oncogenic signaling.

ARNT2 promotes fatty acid metabolism in liposarcoma cells

To elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying ARNT2-mediated regulation of liposarcoma progression, we stratified liposarcoma patients from the TCGA database into distinct groups based on ARNT2 expression levels and subsequently performed differential analysis (Fig. 4A). GSEA revealed significant enrichment of the "Fatty Acid Binding" and "Fatty Acid Catabolic Process" pathways (Fig. 4B, C). In conjunction with previous research findings, we hypothesize that ARNT2 may facilitate the progression of liposarcoma by modulating fatty acid metabolism. Given that fatty acid synthesis represents a crucial aspect of fatty acid metabolism, we investigated the effects of ARNT2 on key enzymes involved in this process (FASN, ACC1). Knockdown of ARNT2 caused coordinated suppression of FASN and ACC1 mRNA expression (Fig. 4D). Further validation at the protein level revealed that the activities of FASN, ACC1, and their upstream proteins were inhibited (Fig. 4E). Conversely, overexpression of ARNT2 led to a concomitant increase in the levels of FASN, ACC1, p-STAT3, p-JAK2 and SREBP1 (Fig. S2G). Consistently, following ARNT2 knockdown, we observed a notable reduction in intracellular lipid droplet content (Fig. 4F), accompanied by a concurrent decrease in triglyceride levels (Fig. 4H). However, overexpression of ARNT2 significantly promoted the accumulation of intracellular lipid droplets and triglyceride levels (Fig. 4G and Fig. S2H). Briefly, ARNT2 orchestrates liposarcoma aggressiveness through metabolic reprogramming of fatty acid synthesis pathways. To further validate the regulatory role of ARNT2, we conducted a rescue experiment using oleic acid (OA). After treatment with OA, the malignant capabilities of cells, which were suppressed by ARNT2 knockdown, were reversed (Fig. S3A, B). Simultaneously, the intracellular triglyceride content and lipid droplets in liposarcoma cells were also reversed by OA (Fig. S3C, D). These experiments further confirm that ARNT2 regulates the progression of liposarcoma by modulating fatty acid metabolism.

ARNT2 upregulates STRA6 to promotes fatty acid metabolism and growth of liposarcoma cells

As a transcription factor, ARNT2 is highly likely to regulate fatty acid metabolism by modulating the transcriptional activity of its downstream target genes. By analyzing the public RNA-seq dataset GSE236486 (generated under ARNT2 knockdown conditions), we identified significantly downregulated genes. We then integrated these findings with genes upregulated in liposarcoma from the TCGA-GTEx database. Systematic cross-comparison and functional screening identified STRA6 as a key mediator in the ARNT2-driven liposarcoma progression cascade.

Our analysis revealed that STRA6 was markedly overexpressed in several cancer types, including breast cancer, colon cancer, and gastric cancer, whereas its expression was notably lower in renal cancer (Fig. S4A). We further found that STRA6 was highly expressed in RLPS (Fig. 5A-D). Moreover, STRA6 expression was coordinately regulated with ARNT2 (Fig. 5E-H). To investigate whether ARNT2 exerts direct regulatory effects on STRA6, we predicted ARNT2’s transcriptional regulatory elements within the STRA6 promoter (Fig. 5I). Upon ARNT2 knockdown, the promoter activity of STRA6 was significantly reduced. Notably, ARNT2-mediated suppression of STRA6 expression was completely abrogated upon mutagenesis of these conserved binding motifs. (Fig. 5J). To investigate its regulatory effect on liposarcoma, we knocked down STRA6 in SW872 and 944T778 cell lines (Fig. S4B, C). We observed a decline in cell viability and proliferative capacity following STRA6 knockdown (Fig. S4D, J). Similarly, cellular invasion and migration abilities also decreased following STRA6 knockdown (Fig. S4E, F). Above all, the results demonstrate that ARNT2 exerts a direct regulatory effect on STRA6, and that STRA6 influences the progression of liposarcoma.

To further investigate how ARNT2 regulates STRA6 and subsequently influences the progression of liposarcoma, we observed that the expressions of FASN and ACC1 were decreased following STRA6 knockdown (Fig. S5A). We observed significant downregulation of lipogenic regulators (FASN, ACC1, SREBP1) and JAK-STAT signaling effectors (p-JAK2, p-STAT3) (Fig. S5B). Meanwhile, both intracellular lipid droplet accumulation and triglyceride content decreased in tandem with the reduction of STRA6 expression (Fig. S5C, D). To further explore the mechanism of the ARNT2/STRA6 axis in fatty acid metabolism in liposarcoma, we simultaneously transfected 94T778 cells with an ARNT2 overexpression plasmid and siSTRA6. Through the detection of key regulators involved in fatty acid metabolism, we found that the expression of these regulators increased following ARNT2 overexpression; however, this increase was reversed by STRA6 knockdown (Fig. 5N).

The rescue experiments confirmed that the increased intracellular lipid droplet accumulation and triglyceride content induced by ARNT2 overexpression could be partially reversed by STRA6 knockdown (Fig. 5O, P).

Similarly, we validated the role of STRA6 in ARNT2-mediated malignant progression of liposarcoma. After overexpressing ARNT2 in 94T778 cells, both cell viability and proliferative capacity were significantly enhanced; however, these effects were reversed by STRA6 knockdown (Fig. 5K and Fig. S5E). Similarly, STRA6 knockdown partially attenuated the ARNT2 overexpression-induced enhancement of invasive and migratory capacities (Fig. 5L, M). Further, we overexpressed STRA6 in cells with STRA6 knockdown and found that the malignant potential of liposarcoma cells was restored (Fig. S6A-C). Simultaneously, the intracellular triglyceride and lipid droplet contents were also restored (Fig. S6D, E). Subsequently, we overexpressed STRA6 in 94T778 cells with ARNT2 knockdown. Interestingly, STRA6 reversed the inhibitory effect on tumor progression induced by ARNT2 knockdown (Fig. S6F-H). Consistent with the previous findings, the intracellular triglyceride and lipid droplet contents were also restored (Fig. S6I, J). In summary, the ARNT2/STRA6 axis plays a crucial role in regulating fatty acid metabolism and the progression of liposarcoma.

ARNT2 promotes the growth of liposarcoma in vivo

To further investigate the in vivo role of ARNT2, we established a stable SW872 cell line with ARNT2 knockdown using shRNA (Fig. 6A). Subsequently, the shNC and shARNT2 cell lines were subcutaneously injected into nude mice. The experiment demonstrated that after ARNT2 knockdown, tumor proliferative capacity was reduced, with both tumor volume and weight significantly lower in the shARNT2 group (Fig. 6B-D). IHC analysis further confirmed that the expression of Ki-67 was significantly decreased in the shARNT2 group (Fig. 6E). These results further elucidate the regulatory role of ARNT2 in the progression of liposarcoma.

Discussion

Currently, surgery remains the most vital treatment approach for retroperitoneal liposarcoma. Nevertheless, its unique anatomical location and high degree of aggressiveness substantially elevate the difficulty and risks of the surgical procedure (Zhou et al. 2025; Hu et al. 2024). Even when R0/R1 resection is attained during surgery, the postoperative local recurrence rate still exceeds 40% (Gao et al. 2024). Furthermore, owing to the high tumor heterogeneity, RLPS exhibits relatively poor sensitivity to chemotherapy and radiotherapy (Mj et al 2022; Erstad et al. 2023; Tortorello et al 2023). Therefore, exploring key molecules and their underlying mechanisms is of utmost importance for improving patient prognosis (Casadei et al. 2022).

We validated the role of fatty acid metabolism in the progression of liposarcoma and further explored the potential underlying mechanisms of ARNT2. Through experiments, we found that when fatty acid synthesis was inhibited, the progression of liposarcoma slowed down, which is also consistent with previous studies (Xie et al 2024; Is et al. 2010; Jeitany et al. 2021). ARNT2 belongs to the bHLH/PAS family and functions as a transcription factor involved in the progression of multiple cancer types (Jia et al. 2019; Jin et al. 2025; Bogeas et al. 2018). Notably, ARNT2 overexpression in liposarcoma correlated with reduced survival rates, suggesting its oncogenic role in disease progression. Gain- and loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that ARNT2 can promote malignant behaviors such as proliferation and invasion in liposarcoma. Meanwhile, transcriptome sequencing analysis of liposarcoma patients further revealed ARNT2’s regulatory role in fatty acid metabolism. We found that ARNT2 increases intracellular lipid droplet and triglyceride content, while upregulating key enzymes (FASN, ACC1) involved in fatty acid synthesis (Tan et al. 2023).

Next, we speculated that STRA6 might be a downstream target gene of ARNT2. Further research has revealed that, compared to normal adipose tissue, the expression of STRA6 is elevated in liposarcoma. Moreover, the level of STRA6 decreases following the knockdown of ARNT2. We predicted ARNT2 binding sites within the STRA6 promoter and validated ARNT2-mediated transcriptional activation by monitoring promoter activity changes. Following STRA6 knockdown, both the content of lipid droplets and triglycerides within liposarcoma cells decreased, along with a decline in the expression of enzymes related to fatty acid metabolism. Notably, this inhibitory effect could be partially reversed by ARNT2. In vivo xenograft models demonstrated that ARNT2 promotes liposarcoma progression, as evidenced by significantly enhanced tumor growth, mirroring in vitro phenotypes.

Conclusion

In summary, this study supports the tumor-promoting role of ARNT2 in retroperitoneal liposarcoma. Mechanistically, ARNT2 enhances fatty acid metabolism by activating the transcriptional activity of STRA6, thereby enabling tumor cells to acquire more energy sources and subsequently undergo proliferation and metastasis. (Fig. 6F). Future therapeutic strategies should prioritize developing selective ARNT2 inhibitors or STRA6 modulators to disrupt tumor metabolic plasticity, potentially overcoming therapeutically recalcitrant RPLS phenotypes.

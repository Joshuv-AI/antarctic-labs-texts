Mechanism, Application, and Prospect of Fracturing-Flooding Technology: A Review
Authors: Haixiang Zhang, Meng Fan, Jiawei Wu, Xuanzuo An, YanQing Xu, Bicheng Gan, Shuwen Guo, Wenting Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
As a kind of reservoir development technology with great development potential, fracturing-flooding technology is more and more frequently used to solve the problems of poor reservoir physical properties, insufficient formation energy, and imperfect injection–production relationship faced by current oilfields. In this paper, the development status of this technology is discussed from the perspectives of the mechanism of action, the selection of working agent, the optimization of operating parameters, and the classification of fracturing-flooding technology. Combined with the practical application cases of oilfields, the development process and future research direction of fracturing-flooding are sorted out. The study found that the fracturing-flooding technology can achieve reservoir stimulation effect by supplementing formation energy, expanding swept volume, improving oil washing efficiency, and improving reservoir physical properties. Based on the actual characteristics of the reservoir, it is necessary to optimize and adjust the fracturing-flooding agents and their viscosity, injection volume, injection rate, soaking time, and other operating parameters. In recent years, new working agents, such as composite and viscoelastic working agents, have been gradually applied to the oil field. Fracturing-flooding technology is divided into two types: forward fracturing-flooding and reverse fracturing-flooding according to the different flow directions of fracture-making well end and working agent and crude oil. According to the current situation of reservoir development, the appropriate flooding-flooding method is selected to promote oil recovery. Based on the historical development and field applications of fracturing-flooding technology, this review also prospects its future directions, including multiphysics control factor analysis, composite technology integration, facility optimization, and intelligent development. These advances are expected to further enhance the technology and provide both theoretical and technical support for efficient reservoir development.

Introduction

Against the backdrop of global economic growth and industrialization, the demand for oil resources continues to rise. In 2023, global energy demand will grow by 2%, two-thirds of which will be met by fossil fuels, with oil consumption averaging about 99 million barrels per day, an increase of nearly 2 million barrels per day from 2022. However, according to statistics, the average recovery rate of oil fields in the world is only about 30–35%, and the remaining oil still has great potential for tapping. However, due to the limitation of technical bottlenecks, it is still difficult to achieve an ideal recovery level. Therefore, in order to meet the growing demand for oil consumption, in view of the problems of low utilization of crude oil and difficult development of remaining oil reserves caused by the limitations of conventional mining methods, new development technologies are needed to achieve breakthroughs in recovery efficiency.

Fracturing, as a means to effectively improve reservoir physical properties, breaks the formation rock to form a diversion fracture network through high-pressure pumping of fracturing fluid and uses the proppant to fill the fracture to prevent closure, thereby expanding the seepage block and maintaining the long-term conductivity of the fracture, so that the oil and gas recovery efficiency is significantly improved. However, the fracturing fluid needs to be discharged back to reduce its damage to the formation and increase the operating cost. , As the most commonly used development method, conventional water flooding generally replaces crude oil by supplementing formation energy, but the recovery effect of remaining oil in the reservoir is not ideal. , Chemical flooding can reduce the viscosity of crude oil and oil–water interfacial tension, promote the emulsification of crude oil, and change the fluid flow characteristics by injecting chemical agents such as surfactants, lye, and polymers, thereby synergistically optimizing the displacement effect, improving the fluidity of crude oil, and displacing the residual oil in pores, and finally achieving a significant increase in oil recovery. − However, in the process of chemical flooding, the sweep efficiency may be low due to the viscoelasticity and adsorption of the oil displacement agent itself.

To overcome the limitations of conventional methods, fracturing-flooding technology has emerged as an integrated approach that combines the advantages of fracturing, water flooding, and chemical flooding, and uses the soaking method to improve the oil washing efficiency. Replacing fracturing fluid with water or chemical agents not only removes the backflow stage of fracturing fluid in the traditional fracturing process but also greatly improves the recovery efficiency of the reservoir. In addition, the fracture network formed by flooding through fracturing, combined with higher injection pressure, effectively solves the shortcomings of insufficient coverage of conventional chemical flooding. While effectively integrating the advantages of conventional development technology, fracturing-flooding technology has successfully overcome its inherent defects, thus forming multiple mechanisms such as supplementing formation energy, expanding swept volume, and improving oil washing efficiency and reservoir physical properties. Therefore, in recent years, fracturing-flooding, as a remaining oil recovery technology with good practical efficiency, has been applied more and more frequently in the oil field.

Fracturing-flooding technology uses the pump injection pressure close to or higher than the fracture pressure to form cracks in the formation and injects working agent into the cracks. Under the action of the closure pressure in the cracks, the working agent extends into the reservoir with the cracks, expanding the contact block and swept volume with the reservoir and promoting the flow of crude oil to production wells. While improving the injection-production correspondence between wells, it also effectively supplements the formation energy. − Since the first successful application of fracturing-flooding technology in the Daqing Lamadian Oilfield in 2017, fracturing-flooding technology has been developed and improved for many years. By 2025, it has been successfully extended to complex oil and gas reservoirs in Shengli, Bohai, and other major oilfields. Blocks show a significant effect of increasing and stabilizing production in various reservoir types such as low permeability and tight and strong heterogeneity, providing effective technical support for the continuous and efficient development of new and old oilfields. − Taking the Chang 6 reservoir of Huaqing Oilfield in Ordos Basin as an example, by combining volume fracturing with water flooding, the crude oil recovery rate of this block is increased by 5–8% and the water content is decreased by 32.6%, which effectively improve the crude oil utilization degree of this block.

Although the current fracturing-flooding technology has shown some application results, the current research on its theory is not deep enough, and the practical application of fracturing-flooding technology is not mature enough. Therefore, this paper systematically summarizes the mechanism of fracturing-flooding technology, the selection and classification of fracturing-flooding agents, the operation process, and the technical classification. Among them, the forward-flooding technology and the reverse-flooding technology are compared and evaluated in terms of mechanism and adaptability. In addition, the development process of fracturing-flooding technology is sorted out, the application examples of each stage are listed, and then, the development characteristics of each stage are summarized. Finally, according to the current development status of flooding technology, the future development direction is further prospected.

Fracturing-Flooding Technology

In view of the problems faced by oilfield development at this stage, such as the problem of injection-production difficulty, conventional development methods have found it difficult to meet the increasing supply and demand. The fracturing-flooding technology breaks through the limitations of a single process by integrating the technical advantages of fracturing, conventional water flooding, and chemical flooding. While avoiding the inherent defects of fracturing fluid backflow loss and low chemical flooding sweep efficiency, it forms a synergistic mechanism of energy supplement-fracture network expansion-imbibition replacement, which systematically solves the technical dilemma of efficient development of unconventional reservoirs such as low permeability and tight reservoirs.

Difference between Fracturing-Flooding and Conventional Fracturing

The traditional fracturing technology opens the formation by injecting fracturing fluid into the formation to produce cracks. After the fracture is made, proppants such as sand particles are added to maintain the fracture opening and improve the reservoir permeability, so that oil and gas are more likely to flow to the wellhead. , In addition to creating fractures, the working agent of the fracturing-flooding technology can also displace crude oil to production wells through its own mechanisms such as reducing interfacial tension and improving rock wettability. ,

Compared with conventional fracturing, fracturing-flooding technology introduces several key modifications. For instance, conventional fracturing fluid relies on its wall-building property to form a filter cake, which minimizes filtration loss and thus reduces formation damage. In contrast, the fracturing-flooding agent is designed to enter the reservoir directly through the created fractures. This mechanism enhances the contact area with crude oil and improves displacement efficiency, representing a shift in the objective from “reducing filtration loss” to “promoting effective filtration”. Furthermore, the large scale of conventional fracturing may lead to uneven formation fracture, which reduces the efficiency of fracturing fluid and increases the difficulty of fracture monitoring. In order to avoid the breakthrough problem caused by the rapid extension of fractures, fracturing-flooding transports the fluid to the deep part of the reservoir and extends up and down, from “promoting extension” to “slow extension”. Finally, the postfracturing procedures are fundamentally different. The traditional fracturing technology needs to flow back the fracturing fluid to reduce the damage of the fracturing fluid to the formation and increase the construction cost and workload. Fracturing-flooding needs to allow the working agent to fully diffuse in the formation to increase the contact time and contact block with the crude oil, thereby improving the oil displacement efficiency, from “fast flowback” to “slow diffusion”. Compared with conventional fracturing fluid, a flooding working agent, as a low-viscosity oil displacement agent, can play a role in reducing interfacial tension, improving rock wettability, etc., with less filter loss and wider expansion range under pressure. While it affects more strata, it also has less damage to the strata, which is more conducive to long-term production. Lu et al. compared the oil displacement effect of conventional fracturing and fracturing-flooding in the low-permeability sandstone block of Shengli Oilfield. The results showed that the freezing-flooding technology showed better production advantages than conventional fracturing.

Mechanism of Fracturing-Flooding Technology

Based on the geological characteristics and development status of the reservoir, fracturing-flooding technology combines a variety of development methods to achieve the effect of supplementing formation energy, expanding the swept volume, improving the efficiency of oil washing, and improving the physical properties of the reservoir, and synergistically solves the outstanding problems faced by the current reservoir, so as to achieve the purpose of improving the recovery efficiency.

For the different characteristics of various types of reservoirs such as fault blocks, tight, and low permeability, through the targeted use of different mechanisms of flooding technology, it has shown good applicability in many applications. For example, in view of the problems of low formation pressure and small daily injection volume in the fault block reservoir of Jiangsu Oilfield, combined with its independent closed fault block and other geological characteristics, the fracturing-flooding enhanced water injection method is used, which not only makes the reservoir quickly replenish the formation energy but also further expands the sweep range. Finally, the daily oil production of a single well is increased by 3.6 t and a good stimulation effect is achieved. For tight reservoirs with poor seepage capacity, for example, in a tight reservoir block in Nanniwan Oilfield, through the field test of fracturing and displacement integration in well N199-2, the oil production increased by 37.3% compared with conventional fracturing in the first year. While improving the physical properties of the reservoir, it also achieved the effect of improving the efficiency of oil washing, effectively improving the recovery efficiency of the block. Aiming at the problem that the remaining oil in low-permeability reservoirs is difficult to be produced, based on the geological characteristics of the three types of reservoirs in Daqing Oilfield, Jiang et al. used nuclear magnetic resonance displacement experiments to carry out fracturing-flooding simulation and verified it with laser confocal scanning and micro-CT scanning methods. It was found that the remaining oil in small pores (pore size <1 μm) and medium pores (1 μm < pore size <10 μm) was greatly improved, which effectively improved the oil washing efficiency. The average oil recovery efficiency increased from 35.23% to 50.41% after water flooding.

Mechanism and Application of Fracturing-Flooding Technology

Supplementing Formation Energy

Formation energy is an important driving force for crude oil in the reservoir to flow to the wellhead. When the formation energy is depleted, the fluid pressure inside the reservoir is reduced, and some crude oil cannot be effectively driven to the production well, resulting in a decrease in the production of the well. Filipchuk et al. discussed the bottom-hole effusion caused by reservoir energy depletion, which has a negative impact on the production of oil and gas wells. Liu et al. systematically analyzed the influence of different formation pressure retention coefficients before fracturing-flooding on the recovery effect after fracturing-flooding (Figure a) and quantitatively evaluated the contribution of each stage of fracturing-flooding to enhanced oil recovery (Figure b) by simulating the seepage-energy storage-oil washing-flowback process of fracturing-flooding agents and combining with oil displacement experiments under different development modes. The final experimental results show that the formation energy-increasing stage has the greatest impact on productivity.

The fracturing-flooding technology uses the injection pressure that is much higher than the formation fracture pressure to form a microfracture zone in the formation. The fracturing-flooding agents are injected into the formation through the microfracture channel to achieve a rapid increase in formation pressure. At the same time, the seepage capacity of the reservoir and the flow capacity of the crude oil are enhanced, and the reservoir recovery efficiency is improved. Taking Niuzhuang Oilfield as an example, through the implementation of fracturing-flooding technology, the formation pressure retention level is restored from 0.51 to 0.82, and the daily oil production is increased from 48.1 t/d to 87.9 t/d, which has achieved the ideal effect of increasing production.

In order to further understand the influence of different factors on the effect of formation energy replenishment, Wang et al. found that the matrix permeability, fracture length and initial concentration of fracturing-flooding agents significantly affect the effect of formation energy replenishment: the permeability and fracture length are positively correlated, and the initial concentration of agents shows a negative correlation trend, as shown in Figure c–e. Different pressurization methods also affect the mining effect. It is necessary to select the appropriate pressurization method, according to the actual geological and development characteristics of the reservoir. Based on different physical properties of reservoirs and considering the law of pressure conduction, Kang et al. used core displacement experiments and nuclear magnetic resonance monitoring technology to study from the perspective of microscopic pore scale. The results show that different methods of supplementing formation energy have significant differences in the effect of crude oil production in the same reservoir, and it is necessary to optimize the development mode according to the physical properties of the reservoirs.

Expanding Swept Volume

The fracturing-flooding technology forms a multidirectional extension and intertwined complex fracture network in the reservoir by means of volume fracturing. These fractures can be connected with natural fractures and pores in the reservoir to form a broader seepage channel, providing more paths for the injected working agent to flow to the oil well. , The formation of fracture network expands the scope of water flooding, so that more crude oil can be affected by the driving force of working agent, and overcomes the problems of low yield and low efficiency caused by reservoir heterogeneity to a certain extent. , Taking a low-permeability oilfield block in the South China Sea as an example, through the fracturing-flooding transformation test, the fracture is further expanded by fracturing, the formed fracture network system effectively expands the sweep range of the fracturing-flooding agent, and the cumulative oil increase in half a year reached 101,000 mcf.

Through high-pressure injection of a fracturing-flooding agent, the pressure gradient of the reservoir is improved, and the capillary force and viscous force that bind the remaining oil are overcome, which shows a good degree of exploitation of the remaining oil. , In addition, improving the fracturing method is also an important means to expand the range of remaining oil production. The new fixed-plane perforation fracturing method proposed by Duan et al., that is, by adjusting the phase angle to form a horizontal stress concentration surface during perforation, guides the crack propagation direction to extend to the low-permeability blocks and promotes the working agent to accurately spread to the remaining oil enrichment block of the low-permeability reservoir. This method effectively solves the problem of remaining oil exploitation in the low-permeability blocks of the Ansai Chang 6 reservoir and makes the average daily oil increase of a single well after fracturing reach 1.8 t, which is 0.7 t/d higher than that of traditional fracturing, and significantly enhances the efficiency of remaining oil production. It provides a new path for tapping the potential of remaining oil in ultralow-permeability reservoirs.

Improving Oil Washing Efficiency

Fracturing-flooding technology overcomes the internal resistance of the reservoir through high-pressure injection, further increases the seepage capacity and seepage distance of the injected fluid, and improves the flow rate and driving force of the fluid in the reservoir so that the fluid can reach the deep part of the reservoir. While avoiding the retention and loss of the working agent, it can more effectively promote the flow of crude oil to the wellbore, thereby enhancing the oil washing effect. Zhou et al. studied the influence of hydraulic fracture development on spontaneous imbibition through reservoir numerical simulation at pore scale. It was found that the expansion of microfractures at the edge of fracture network can increase the contact block of matrix and improve the efficiency of deep oil phase discharge driven by capillary force. At the same time, the main fracture is used as an efficient transport channel to reduce oil phase retention and accelerate displacement. The two synergistically enhance the effects of imbibition and oil washing.

The soaking process is also an effective method to improve the efficiency of oil washing by fracturing-flooding technology. By soaking for a period of time after fracturing, the working agent can fully diffuse in the reservoir, replace the crude oil, relieve the water lock effect, improve the flow capacity of the oil phase, and thus improve the oil washing efficiency. Based on nuclear magnetic resonance and signal dynamic monitoring technology, Lu et al. revealed that the working agent in the soaking stage imbibes from the fracture to the matrix under the action of capillary force, drives the migration of medium and small pore crude oil to the fracture, and finally realizes the efficient replacement of crude oil.

Liu et al. found that fracturing-flooding technology has obvious advantages in oil washing efficiency by comparing different displacement schemes such as conventional water flooding, conventional fracturing, and fracturing-flooding (Figure a–d). Due to the low displacement pressure, it is difficult for conventional water flooding to effectively drive dispersed remaining oil, and the oil washing efficiency is limited. Although conventional fracturing can expand the sweep range, it still relies on conventional water injection displacement after fracture closure, and the oil washing efficiency has not been effectively improved. When water is used as the fracturing-flooding working agent, the formation energy is supplemented and the remaining oil is gradually driven to the production end. When chemical agents such as surfactants are used as the fracturing-flooding agent, based on their own mechanisms of reducing interfacial tension and changing the wettability of rock surface, more deep and efficient oil washing can be achieved under high-pressure driving. Compared with conventional water flooding, the recovery efficiency is improved by 16.9%, as shown in Figure e, and the trend of recovery degree of each displacement scheme is shown in Figure f. This technology has been applied in the Sazhong Development Zone of Daqing Oilfield and has achieved an average single-well cumulative oil increase of 1123.63 t.

Improving Reservoir Physical Properties

Some scholars have found that fracturing-flooding technology has the effect of improving reservoir physical properties through experimental research. Zhang et al. systematically studied the dynamic changes of rock permeability, porosity, and pore structure during fracturing displacement by physical simulation experiments. It is found that with the increase of fracturing-flooding displacement pressure, the fracture aperture is further expanded, and the rock permeability is significantly improved, forming an effective seepage channel network dominated by artificial fractures, which solves the problem of difficult injection in low-permeability reservoirs. The porosity increased by 0.6%, the proportion of pores with diameter <30 μm decreased from 96% to 87%, and the peak value of pore diameter distribution shifted from 9 to 13 μm, indicating that fracturing-flooding promoted the expansion or connection of some small pores in the rock matrix, the proportion of medium and large pores increased, the pore structure improved as a whole, and the effective transformation of low-permeability reservoirs was realized.

Wang et al. studied the effect of fracturing-flooding on the pore structure of rock. By injecting fracturing-flooding working agent under high-pressure conditions, the impact and shear effects generated inside the rock can wash down the cement between particles, clay in the pore throat, and mineral particles. The increase of the displacement pressure difference effectively reduces the adsorption loss of the fracturing-flooding working agent in the reservoir (Figure a), as well as expands the pore throat radius (Figure b), improves the connectivity between the pore throats, reduces the fluid resistance, makes the oil and gas flow more easily, and effectively improves the utilization efficiency of the working agent. Fracturing-Flooding Agent Type and Operating Parameters

After in-depth discussion of the mechanism of fracturing oil displacement, in order to achieve the above-mentioned multiple effects of increasing energy, expanding sweep, oil washing, and improving physical properties, it is particularly critical to reasonably select and optimize the oil displacement working agent system. Therefore, this section will systematically introduce the characteristics, mechanism, and applicable conditions of different types of fracturing oil displacement agents.

Fracturing-Flooding Agent Type

Fracturing-flooding agent is different from a conventional fracturing agent. Its main purpose is to displace crude oil and not just to form cracks. The fracturing-flooding agent can improve oil recovery by promoting crude oil in the formation under high pressure and imbibition replacement with crude oil in rock pores during soaking. The common fracturing-flooding agents are water and surfactants, which enhance oil recovery through different mechanisms: water supplements formation energy through high-pressure injection to improve reservoir driving conditions; surfactants can strip the bound oil in the pores and realize the effective migration of crude oil at the microscopic scale through their own mechanisms such as reducing interfacial tension and regulating rock wettability. The advancement of fracturing-flooding technology has broadened the range of working agents used in the field. Beyond conventional water and surfactants, types such as composite, nanofluid, supercritical CO2, and viscoelastic agents are now being applied in oilfield operations.

Fracturing-Flooding Working Agents Type

Composite Oil Displacement Agent

Through the synergistic combination of surfactant, polymer, alkali, and other components, the compound oil displacement agent has the advantages of reducing oil–water interfacial tension, regulating fluid viscosity, and improving reservoir permeability. When used as a fracturing-flooding working agent, it can adapt to complex reservoir conditions and effectively enhance oil recovery. − Wang found that under the action of fracturing, the weak alkali ternary composite oil displacement agent cooperates with its own viscosity reduction and ultralow interfacial tension and penetrates into the low-permeability reservoir with the advantage channel formed by the fracture, effectively stripping the residual oil on the rock surface and improving the fluidity of crude oil. At the same time, its polymer viscosity-increasing effect inhibits the sudden advance of injected water and expands the swept volume, so that the cumulative oil per unit thickness reaches 141 tons and the effective period is extended to 282 days. According to the characteristics of ultralow permeability reservoir in the blocks, Cong et al. compared the recovery of three composite oil displacement agents (HAS-6C, HAS-5E, HAS-5G) in each stage of dynamic imbibition, and optimized the fracturing-flooding agent, as shown in Figure a. The results show that compared with water flooding, the use of the composite oil displacement agent significantly improves the recovery rate, which confirms that the composite oil displacement agent can effectively produce the remaining oil in the reservoir.

Nanotype Oil Displacement Agent

Based on the characteristics of small size of nanosized particles, nanotype oil displacement agents have shown good application effects in optimizing the fluid properties of working agents and regulating the wettability of reservoir rock surfaces. Zhang et al. found that the nano-cross-linking agent, as a functional carrier, based on its own nanoscale stable skeleton, after cross-linking with guar gum gel (Figure b), produced working agents that not only significantly enhanced the viscosity stability and network structure strength at high temperature but also showed advantages in gel-breaking performance and reservoir protection. In addition, nanoparticle molecules can change their wettability by adsorbing on the surface of the rock, making the lipophilic rock become hydrophilic rock, making it easier for crude oil to be stripped out, thereby improving oil recovery. Al-Anssari et al. found that after nanofluid treatment, the contact angle between water and oil-wet rock surface decreased significantly, and the surface changed from lipophilic to strongly hydrophilic. Long et al. used nanofluids to replace the traditional working agents used in fracturing oil displacement. It was verified by experiments that this type of nanotype agent not only showed excellent stability under high-temperature and high-salt reservoir conditions but also achieved remarkable results in improving production and showed good application results in Shengli Oilfield.

Supercritical CO2

Based on the characteristics of low viscosity, low surface tension, and high diffusivity of supercritical CO2 fluid, when used as a fracturing-flooding working agent, it has lower fracture pressure and more complex fracture network than conventional working agent, and also shows better stimulation effect in the displacement process. − Zhang et al. found through experimental research that the fracture initiation and extension process showed stronger pore permeability, so that compared with conventional hydraulic fracturing, the fracture pressure of supercritical CO2 fracturing was reduced by 37.2%, and the seepage block was expanded 2.2 times. Li et al. simulated the propagation process of fractures under different fracturing conditions in complex geological environments and visually characterized the geometry of fracture networks. As shown in Figure c,d), supercritical CO2 fracturing shows a more complex fracture network. In the process of displacement, the low surface tension and high diffusion coefficient characteristics of supercritical CO2 under high-pressure conditions can make it easier to break through the constraints of capillary force and have a better effect on the remaining oil in micropores. Meng et al. found through experiments that the recovery rate was increased by 15.55% compared with conventional hydraulic fracturing. In addition, Yan et al. , found that the wettability change caused by the strong adsorption capacity of CO2 on the rock surface under high pressure can also improve the displacement efficiency.

Viscoelastic Oil Displacement Agent

Viscoelastic oil displacement agents form wormlike micelles and intertwine with each other through the self-assembly and cross-linking of surfactant molecules in solution, thereby constructing a three-dimensional network structure with elasticity and stability. , This structure can effectively resist the influence of gravity and buoyancy and encapsulate oil droplets and solid particles to prevent their settlement. Viscoelastic oil displacement agents provide high viscoelasticity and shear thinning characteristics during fracturing to ensure effective fluid entry into formation fractures. After fracturing, the colloid undergoes controllable damage when contacting with formation water or crude oil, releasing surfactant molecules to the oil–water interface, significantly reducing the interfacial tension, weakening the adhesion between crude oil and rock, and thus achieving the effect of increasing production. Yang et al. studied the mechanism of enhanced oil recovery by viscoelastic oil displacement agent (Figure e) from multiple perspectives through indoor core displacement experiments. The results show that a viscoelastic oil displacement agent has a good application prospect as an integrated working agent for fracturing and oil displacement.

Parameter Optimization

In addition to the type of flooding agent, parameters such as viscosity, injection rate, injection volume, and soaking time also need to be adjusted according to the reservoir characteristics of the target low-permeability blocks. The viscosity is directly related to the fluidity, spread range, and fracture cracking degree of the working agent in the reservoir. The regulation of the injection rate and injection volume affects the pressure transfer and reservoir utilization. The soaking time determines the effect of the oil–water displacement. Therefore, according to different reservoir characteristics, it is necessary to comprehensively consider these key parameters and then formulate a reasonable development plan to improve the efficiency of reservoir development.

Viscosity of Working Agent

By adjusting the viscosity of the flooding working agent, the fluidity and filtration rate of the fluid in the fracture can be changed, which in turn affects the oil displacement efficiency of the working agent in the reservoir and ultimately affects the development effect. High viscosity can enhance the sand-carrying capacity of the fluid and reduce the filtration loss, but the fluidity is poor, and the filtration rate is slow, resulting in limited sweep efficiency and low oil displacement efficiency. The low-viscosity fluid has good fluidity, fast infiltration speed, larger sweep efficiency, and higher oil displacement efficiency, but the sand-carrying capacity is poor and the filtration rate is fast, which will lead to the low utilization rate of the working agent. Through experiments and data analysis, Shakeel et al. verified the key role of the adjustment of working agent viscosity in fluid flow control. Cao et al. found through experimental research that reducing the viscosity of the working agent in the fracturing-flooding operation can significantly reduce the flow resistance of the fluid in the reservoir pores. Based on Darcy ‘s law, it can be seen that the low-viscosity fluid has a larger filtration loss under the same pressure difference, which in turn promotes the radius of the pore pressure field around the well to expand outward. This mechanism further develops the microfracture network and ultimately achieves the effect of expanding the swept volume of the working agent.

In addition, the viscosity of the working agent will also affect the fracture morphology and imbibition distance. Wei et al. found that with the increase of working agent viscosity, the fracture half-length increased significantly, while the vertical imbibition distance decreased gradually, as shown in Figure a,b. The mechanism is that the seepage resistance of the high-viscosity working agent in the matrix increases, resulting in a decrease in the amount of filtration, so that more energy can be concentrated at the crack tip to promote crack propagation; the low-viscosity fluid is more likely to penetrate into the matrix, expand the oil displacement range, but weaken the extension ability of the fracture.

Injection Volume

The injection volume of the fracturing-flooding working agent is also an important factor related to the quality of reservoir recovery. The greater the injection volume, the greater the increase of formation pressure and the more significant the increase of reservoir energy, so as to improve the seepage capacity of fluid in the reservoir and further enhance the recovery rate of crude oil.

In addition to promoting the increase in formation pressure, the increase in injection volume will further expand the fracture and increase the seepage distance of the working agent. Yang et al. showed that the injection volume of working agent was positively correlated with the degree of fracture development, and the specific results are shown in Figure c. However, an excessive increase in injection volume will also lead to excessive expansion of cracks and damage to the formation. It will also increase the amount of retained fluid, decrease the replacement ratio, reduce the mining efficiency, and increase the construction cost. Cui et al. found that there is an optimal injection volume to make the recovery rate reach the peak, and this conclusion has been verified in the field.

Wang et al. summarized the dosage principle and adjustment method of working agent for fracturing water injection by numerical simulation. The principle of working agent dosage is to maintain a reasonable formation pressure as the core goal and to ensure the dynamic balance of reservoir energy by controlling the injection-production ratio. The adjustment method adjusts the water injection scheme differently according to the difference between reservoir thickness and permeability. Through the above principles and methods, the stable maintenance of reservoir pressure is realized, and the permeability loss caused by low pressure or the risk of water channeling caused by high pressure is effectively avoided, which lays a foundation for the optimization of the development effect.

Injection Rate

A reasonable working agent injection rate can promote improvement of mining efficiency. Appropriate injection rate can ensure the effective expansion of fractures and make the reservoir pressure distribute reasonably, so as to achieve the effect of increasing production. In view of the influence of injection rate on the effect of fracturing cracks, Park et al. systematically analyzed the indoor experiment combined with three-dimensional X-ray CT imaging and deep learning technology. It is concluded that when the injection rate is increased, the fracture pressure will increase and the fracture volume and opening will increase, but the tortuosity and surface roughness of the fracture will be reduced. This is mainly attributed to the inhibition of fluid penetration into the rock matrix at high injection rates, resulting in faster accumulation of pressure in the borehole and concentration in the main fracture expansion, and more concentrated energy release, resulting in wider, flatter, and farther-extending fractures, while low injection rates allow fluid to fully penetrate into microfractures to form a more complex and highly tortuous fracture network. However, when the injection rate is too high, the recovery degree will increase first and then decrease, and it may even lead to the loss of fracturing-flooding agent from the production well without fully utilizing the oil washing effect and push the crude oil near the fracture into the deep part of the matrix, which is not conducive to fully leveraging the oil washing function of the surfactant.

In addition, Cao et al. found that compared with the low injection rate, the high injection rate could not form a stable microfracture propagation stage in the process of displacement, and thus could not achieve the effect of expanding the swept volume of working agent and improving the efficiency of oil washing. On the contrary, it may lead to fracture instability or even rupture, and a stable microfracture development period cannot be formed. Therefore, the excessively high injection rate is more suitable for the traditional fracturing process dominated by macrofractures, rather than the fracturing oil displacement technology that relies on microfracture networks. Reasonable control of the injection rate of the flooding working agent is one of the key factors to ensure the effect of flooding oil recovery.

Wang et al. found that the optimal value of injection rate depends on the characteristics of DFN (discrete fracture network). As shown in Figure d–f, in sparse DFN, the activation length ratio of DFN to HF (hydraulic fracture) decreases significantly with the increase of injection rate, while in dense DFN, the ratio increases sharply with the increase of injection rate. The results show that the sparse DFN needs to adopt a lower injection rate to avoid fluid concentration in the development of a single hydraulic fracture at the expense of natural fracture activation; the dense DFN relies on high injection rate to activate a large number of natural fractures synchronously through rapid pressurization to form a more complex connected network, thereby maximizing the reservoir reconstruction volume. Wang et al. used the variable injection rate technique to adjust the injection rate by stages. Compared with the constant injection rate, it is more effective to form a complex fracture network and reduce the total agent consumption.

Soaking Time

The soaking time is an important parameter affecting the effect of fracturing energy storage and permeability enhancement. By promoting the diffusion of fracturing fluid in the formation, enhancing the imbibition and replacement of crude oil, and promoting the formation of microfractures, the soaking time expands the seepage channel, thereby improving the oil–water two-phase displacement efficiency and achieving enhanced oil recover. − The appropriate extension of the soaking time can also reduce the influence of stress sensitivity on productivity so that the reservoir can maintain a high permeability and supplement the formation energy for subsequent mining to further improve the recovery rate. However, too long soaking time will also lead to increased retention of working agent, increased stress sensitivity, and may even lead to fracture closure, affecting subsequent mining. ,

Hassani et al. studied the effect of soaking time on the mechanism of wettability change and fine particle migration through core flooding experiments, combined with wettability test and scanning electron microscope (SEM) analysis. The experimental results show that the longer the soaking time, the more obvious the wettability change and the more significant the migration of fine particles.

In order to better understand the effect of soaking time on oil displacement efficiency in the process of fracturing-flooding, Duan et al. established a coupled mathematical model and found that there is an optimal soaking time instead of a linear relationship between soaking time and cumulative oil production. When the cumulative oil production exceeds the optimal soaking time, it tends to be flat or gradually decreases, as shown in Figure g,h.

For different types of reservoirs, it is necessary to adjust and optimize the working parameters such as working agent viscosity, injection volume, injection rate, and soaking time and then to develop a reasonable fracturing-flooding scheme to achieve efficient reservoir development.

Fracturing-Flooding Technology Classification

Fracturing-flooding technology is divided into forward fracturing-flooding technology and reverse fracturing-flooding technology according to the difference between the fracture well end and the displacement direction (Figure ). Forward fracturing-flooding constructs a stable displacement pressure field by injecting fluid into the well end, which is suitable for systematic energy supplement and fracture network expansion in low-permeability reservoirs. Reverse fracturing-flooding is a reverse imbibition channel formed by fracturing at the end of the production well, which is more suitable for tapping the potential of blocks with imperfect injection-production relationship or local enrichment of remaining oil. The two types of technologies cover the needs of reservoir reconstruction at different development stages through a differentiated fracture network construction mode and displacement mechanism. The selection of their processes needs to be comprehensively evaluated according to the characteristics of reservoir physical properties and the remaining oil distribution.

Forward Fracturing-Flooding Technique

Mechanism of Action

The forward fracturing-flooding technology first makes fractures with reservoir microfracture pressure from the injection well end and injects the configured working agent, which will be displaced into the formation pores as the fracture extends. After the completion of the fracture work, the fracture without sand is closed and then the well is sucked to achieve oil–water replacement. At this time, the flow direction of the inhaled wetting phase is consistent with that of the discharged nonwetting phase. Finally, the injection well end restores the conventional continuous injection method, and the working agent and crude oil injected during fracturing are further displaced to the production well end (Figure a). The forward flooding-flooding technology forms a stable displacement pressure field by creating fractures at the end of the injection well, so as to achieve the effect of supplementing formation energy and expanding the swept volume. It is suitable for low-permeability/tight oil and gas reservoirs with serious formation energy deficit.

Reverse Fracturing-Flooding Technology

Mechanism of Action

Reverse fracturing-flooding technology is an effective means of reservoir development. Compared with forward-flooding, reverse-flooding technology first uses a low-viscosity working agent instead of fracturing agent to open formation fractures at the oil well end. The configured working agent is injected through the fracture; sand is added to prevent the fracture from closing, and the conductivity of the fracture is also enhanced. Then, the soaking operation is carried out to make the working agent diffuse in the fracture and rock matrix to realize the imbibition replacement of crude oil. In this process, the inhalation of the wetting phase and the discharge direction of the nonwetting phase are opposite. Finally, the well group resumed conventional injection to displace the crude oil in the fracture, and then the oil well began to produce (Figure b). The reverse-fracturing flooding technology mainly relies on the production well end fracturing to construct the reverse seepage channel, so that the remaining oil near the well can be effectively utilized, which is suitable for the reservoir blocks with strong heterogeneity and imperfect injection and production.

Technology Application

In the development of oil and gas reservoirs with poor reservoir properties, the remaining oil in the block near the production well is usually difficult to affect by the working agent injected in the injection well. The reverse fluff-flooding method can effectively utilize the remaining oil near the production well by fracturing and injecting the working agent near the production well. Therefore, compared with forward fracturing-flooding, when the injection–production relationship is not perfect or the remaining oil in the production well block is enriched, reverse fracturing-flooding can reduce working agent loss, yield a faster production response, and improve displacement efficiency. The forward-flooding and reverse-flooding methods were used in the second-class reservoirs in the Duanxi Sanyuan demonstration block of Daqing Oilfield. The test results show that reverse fracturing-flooding can quickly realize the effective tapping of remaining oil in scattered wells.

The Development Process of Fracturing-Flooding Technology

As a mining method that can effectively develop effective, low-permeability, tight, and other types of oil and gas reservoirs, the fracturing-flooding technology has been improved and optimized through oilfield field practice in recent years and has been applied in many oilfields and achieved good yield increase effect.

Prior to 2000, scholars in the field of petroleum engineering began to recognize the role of matrix imbibition in reservoir development and conducted initial exploratory studies. Douglas et al. (1959) used numerical simulation method to study the law of oil–water migration in porous media. It was found that capillary force promoted water to be sucked into low-permeability layers and replaced crude oil in the early stage but significantly inhibited the uniform improvement of displacement efficiency after water breakthrough. Considering the heterogeneity of reservoirs, Han et al. (1980) improved the previous numerical method and quantified the bidirectional effect of heterogeneity on lipophilic reservoirs for the first time, which provided theoretical and data support for optimizing the oil–water displacement efficiency of matrix imbibition.

From 2001 to 2014, based on the characteristics of matrix as the main reservoir space and fracture as the main flow channel, scholars in the field of oil have carried out in-depth research on the dynamic imbibition mechanism of fracture and matrix in a reservoir. Babadagli (2003) studied how to use different displacement agents as fluids to improve recovery efficiency under different conditions by considering factors such as crude oil properties, matrix wettability, and boundary conditions through co-imbibition and reverse imbibition experiments under static conditions. By optimization of the capillary imbibition process, the remaining oil inside the matrix block surrounded by the fracture network in the fractured reservoir can be efficiently exploited. Gunde et al. (2013) used the lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) to reveal the law of reverse imbibition of oil and water driven by capillary force in fractured porous media; that is, water is sucked into the matrix through small pores to displace crude oil, and the oil phase is reversed to the fracture through large pores, resulting in a concave curve of the relative permeability of the oil phase, which increases first and then decreases. This conclusion provides a key theoretical basis for optimizing water flooding in fractured reservoirs.

During the period from 2015 to 2016, in view of the problems of insufficient formation energy and decreased productivity faced by oilfields, some oilfields began to use fracturing energy storage and permeability increasing mining methods to improve oil recovery efficiency and achieved a good production increase effect. Based on the extremely low permeability of the Muskwa, Otter Park, and Evie shale formations in the Horn River Basin, Canada, large-scale hydraulic fracturing was performed on the horizontal wells in the corresponding blocks to form a complex fracture network, combined with the adjustment of soaking time to achieve economic production. Guo and Gou proposed the core idea of differential selection of fracturing methods based on reservoir geological characteristics and seepage mechanism. According to the block characteristics of two types of tight gas reservoirs in the western Sichuan Basin, asymmetric three-dimensional fracturing and volume fracturing techniques were innovatively adopted for the shallow-middle massive sandstone reservoirs of the Penglaizhen Formation and the sand-shale interbedded reservoirs of the fifth member of the Xujiahe Formation. Finally, through field tests, they have achieved significant stimulation effects compared with those of conventional fracturing methods.

Since 2017, in the development of oil and gas field blocks with complex geological structures and poor reservoir physical properties, fracturing oil displacement-swallowing well imbibition technology has been gradually used to improve mining efficiency. The core breakthrough of this stage is to integrate the three links of fracturing, oil displacement agent injection, and soaking imbibition and form a set of efficient development modes for low-permeability, tight, and strong-heterogeneity reservoirs. With the deepening of the research, combined with the practical application effect, the properties and operating parameters of the flooding working agent are continuously improved and optimized to promote the large-scale application of the technology.

Research Prospect

Fracturing-flooding technology has shown good application results in many oilfields after development and improvement in recent years. However, there are still some deficiencies in some aspects. Based on the deficiencies, the following prospects are made.(1)Analysis of the main controlling factors affecting the fracturing-flooding effect under multiphysics coupling conditions. To address the poor adaptability of development schemes resulting from unclear multifactor interactions, future research should focus on identifying the main controlling factors of fracturing-flooding performance. This can be achieved by quantifying the influence degree of both dynamic and static parameters and conducting correlation analyses, thereby enabling targeted design and adjustment of development strategies.(2)Promoting the development of composite fracturing-flooding technology. Aiming at the problem of limited oil displacement efficiency and insufficient technical synergy of a single fracturing-flooding method, a composite fracturing-flooding technology is proposed. This technology refers to the combination of two or more fracturing-flooding methods or other enhanced oil recovery technologies to fully leverage their respective advantages and achieve the goal of improving oil and gas recovery. For example, using physical, chemical, biological, and other mechanisms to act on the reservoir together, different fracturing-flooding techniques are implemented in stages to improve the oil displacement efficiency.(3)Further development of fracturing-flooding process facilities. In view of the problems of high energy consumption and poor reliability of supporting facilities, the future fracturing-flooding process supporting technology should focus on the research and development of high-efficiency and low-consumption fracturing-flooding equipment, further break through the reliability bottleneck of high-temperature and high-pressure materials and downhole tools, and optimize the fracturing-flooding process-integrated supporting facilities system while considering environmental protection.(4)Promoting the evolution of fracturing-flooding technology in the direction of intelligence. Aiming at the problems of strong dependence on artificial experience and weak data-driven decision-making ability, big data analysis and machine learning algorithms can be used to process the dynamic and static data of the target block, and combined with numerical simulation technology, a decision-making framework can be established. At the same time, the real-time monitoring technology is used to predict the production capacity of different stages of fracturing-flooding based on deep learning and neural network, which provides data reference for the optimization of operation parameters and realizes the intelligence and efficiency of fracturing-flooding process.

Conclusions

As a new type of reservoir development technology, flooding technology effectively solves the problem that low-permeability and tight reservoirs cannot be injected and produced. This technology combines various mechanisms, such as fracturing, percolation, and oil displacement. It has the advantages of a wide sweep range, low permeability resistance, and high construction efficiency. It has been applied in many oilfields and achieved good stimulation results.(1)Fracturing-flooding technology can effectively improve reservoir recovery by supplementing formation energy, expanding swept volume, improving oil washing efficiency, and improving reservoir physical properties. There are various types of working agents for fracturing-flooding, including compound, nano, supercritical CO2 and viscoelastic working agents, in addition to common types such as water and surfactant. The key to realize the efficient development of fracturing-flooding technology is to select the appropriate type of working agent and optimize the parameters in combination with the reservoir characteristics.(2)Fracturing-flooding technology is divided into two types: forward fracturing-flooding and reverse fracturing-flooding according to the different flow directions of fracture-making well end and working agent and oil flow. According to the reservoir characteristics and development status of the target reservoir, a reasonable development plan is selected.(3)From its early focus on matrix imbibition to the current integrated fracturing-flooding approach, the technology has evolved through multiple developmental stages, each contributing to its continuous improvement and refinement. In the future, fracturing-flooding technology will further develop in the direction of accurate analysis of the main controlling factors of multiphysical fields, synergistic effect of compound displacement, optimization of supporting facilities of fracturing-flooding process and intelligent data-driven decision-making, so as to realize the dynamic adaptation of oil displacement efficiency and development scheme, and provide new ideas and methods for efficient development of oilfields.

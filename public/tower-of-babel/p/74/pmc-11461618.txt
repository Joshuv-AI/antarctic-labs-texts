A secure healthcare data sharing scheme based on two-dimensional chaotic mapping and blockchain
Authors: Zhigang Xu, Enda Zheng, Hongmu Han, Xinhua Dong, Xiaohong Dang, Zhongpeng Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Enhancing data privacy security in medical data sharing is crucial for the informatization development in the healthcare sector. This paper proposes a healthcare data sharing scheme based on two-dimensional chaotic mapping and blockchain (2DCM-DS). Specifically, a new two-dimensional chaotic mapping is proposed, which demonstrates superior chaotic performance. Then, by incorporating biometric audio information as an identity credential and integrating it with the proposed two-dimensional chaotic mapping, we design a data encryption method that establishes a strongly coupled and bi-directionally verifiable data ownership relationship in healthcare data sharing. Finally, we employ blockchain as the underlying network and design corresponding smart contracts to support 2DCM-DS. This approach addresses potential issues of unauthorized access, malicious tampering, and single points of failure in centralized data sharing. Experimental results demonstrate that 2DCM-DS effectively protects data security under the specified attack models. The results validate the security and efficiency of the 2DCM-DS, proving its application potential in healthcare insurance data sharing scenarios.

Introduction

Health insurance is an important guarantee of basic national health care and is vital to social stability and the public interest. As the number of medical insurance participants increases year by year, the vast amounts of healthcare data generated by these participants have not yet been effectively utilized. According to reports, it is important to address the effective circulation of massive amounts of medical-related data1, while simultaneously balancing the privacy and rights of data providers and providing better data support to the medical research community2. Therefore, it is necessary to carry out research work on secure and efficient healthcare data sharing.

The issue of data privacy and security during the data sharing process is a primary obstacle hindering the development of healthcare data sharing3. With the advancement of medical big data, applications such as online healthcare settlement systems, distributed health management services, and the training of federated learning-based healthcare models4 all require large datasets as foundational support. This requires sharing local datasets between different institutions, a process that faces many data security issues. For example, criminals exploit vulnerabilities in data sharing to illegally access sensitive private data of patients. Furthermore, they can deduce the health status of regional populations and medical fiscal expenditures from the data, which could pose a societal security threat. Shared data being maliciously tampered with can result in research efforts failing to achieve desired outcomes due to erroneous data5, leading to significant time and financial losses. Additionally, data sharing networks centered around centralized aggregation servers present challenges related to privacy, ownership and regulation6. Due to the fact that healthcare data sharing involves sensitive information, it is important to propose new solutions to complement data verification mechanisms7 and visitor authentication mechanisms8, which are lacking in traditional schemes.

Currently, many scholars have proposed cryptography-based encryption techniques for privacy protection in data sharing9,10. Chaos mapping, an encryption method based on chaos theory, provides an alternative to the traditional public-private key system. Due to the pseudo-randomness and high sensitivity of chaotic systems, it can generate highly complex and random key sequences11. Additionally, it exhibits strong resistance to differential and linear attacks. Naik, R. B. et al.12 proposed the use of a pseudo-random number generator based on chaotic mapping, which can be used to construct more secure encryption algorithms for protection against unauthorised users. This work combines existing one-dimensional logistic maps with one-dimensional chaotic maps to propose a new two-dimensional chaotic map. Based on this, an encryption mechanism is designed to enhance the security of data sharing.

Blockchain enables the establishment of a reliable and decentralized data storage and exchange system among multiple participants. Its decentralized nature reduces the need for third-party involvement and lowers transaction costs associated with data sharing13. Using blockchain technology instead of traditional centralized networks is evidently more suitable for the current needs of healthcare data sharing. Currently, blockchain is also being applied in various other data sharing domains, such as the Internet of Vehicles, Traffic Flow Prediction, and Industrial Internet of Things. However, existing healthcare data sharing solutions still have shortcomings: (1) Complex cryptosystems require more computational cost in real-time communication and large-scale encryption scenarios14. (2) Existing solutions fail to well centralise the two issues of data integrity verification and identity authentication.

To address the aforementioned issues, this work proposes an encryption method based on two-dimensional chaotic mapping to enhance the data security of healthcare data sharing. Related works

Healthcare and data sharing

Existing applications of big data in healthcare primarily involve disease prediction15 and detection of fraudulent insurance claims16. Chen et al.17 conducted an assessment of specific-site cancer profiles among populations afflicted with cancer and atrial fibrillation based on five years of healthcare records in Shanghai, revealing an increase in the prevalence of atrial fibrillation among cancer patients, a predominance of males, and a trend toward younger ages for cancer incidence. Anand et al.18 compared the completeness of electronic health record (EHR) data for healthcare patients using medical claims data from Massachusetts and eleven American healthcare organizations to mitigate research variable misclassification resulting from EHR discontinuities. These researches17,18 focus on the development of medical big data applications in healthcare, but obviously, these researches do not focus on the data security of the underlying application and lack of privacy protection mechanisms during data interaction.

Research in other healthcare big data has focused on developing more secure and efficient data sharing methods. A. Awad Abdelatif et al.19 proposed the need for the development of faster and more secure data sharing to bring together various medical entities within a healthcare system, which could enhance medical quality and enable more accurate control of major medical events. Rani et al.20 emphasized the critical importance of data sharing security between Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) devices and aggregation servers in the development of federated learning in the medical field. However, the centralised network structure has potential data security risks. The scheme21 utilises secure keyword searchable attribute encryption based on lattice cipher to achieve secure and efficient healthcare big data management, but it needs to address the security issues arising from electronic medical record management and sharing in blockchain. How to achieve privacy-preserving and efficient data sharing in blockchain-based data sharing networks is an open problem for the development of healthcare big data applications.

2D Chaotic mapping and privacy protection

Chaos mapping exhibits randomness and unpredictability in behavior, being highly sensitive to initial conditions, and thus finds extensive applications in encrypting data such as audio, images or time series22,23. Expanding parameters into two-dimensional space, 2D chaotic mapping can better maintain the continuity of parameters in chaotic states24. Wu et al.25 proposed 2D-LNIC, and based on this algorithm, AEA-NCA showed outstanding performance in audio encryption, significantly reducing data stream correlation. Jasra et al.26 integrated DNA coding with two-dimensional chaotic systems, proposing a comprehensive framework for color image encryption with high efficiency, robustness, and resilience against multiple types of attacks. These two schemes25,26 propose effective encryption algorithms but do not discuss the combination of the proposed encryption algorithms with practical application scenarios. Simultaneously, numerous scholars have explored data transmission schemes utilizing chaos mapping. Kumari and Punam27 presented a lightweight encryption (LWE) scheme for protecting image data transmission in the Internet of Things (IoT) environments, employing piecewise linear chaos mapping (PWLCM) and grain key stream generator (GKSG). In scheme28, Alsahafi, Y.S et al. obtain the optimal initial sequence of chaotic mapping is obtained by a novel binary coronavirus disease optimisation algorithm, which generates the optimal initial key for medical image encryption. To enhance the data security of medical image transmission in the Internet of Medical Things. While their approach directly applies chaotic mapping to data encryption, enhancing data security, further exploration is needed to strengthen the connection between users and shared data while achieving data privacy protection.

Blockchain

With the rapid development of big data models, there is an increasing demand in the industry for privacy-preserving data sharing scheme29. In the field of IoT in healthcare. Yang et al.30 proposed a secure data sharing method based on dynamic consensus committee to provide fine-grained data security privacy guarantee for public cloud. Later, in the scheme31, they design a fast permission proving method based on reputation incentive mechanism for matching encryption in IoT healthcare, but the proposed scheme requires additional computation to solve the potential problem of single secret key exposure for matching encryption. Hong et al.32 propose a secure peer-to-peer multi-party transaction in order to reduce the communication burden in multi-party transaction scenarios by incorporating a secret sharing mechanism in the blockchain scheme, which achieves the authentication of user identity but lacks the on-chain data privacy protection mechanism. Li et al.33 noticed the problems of privacy leakage and inefficient sharing in medical data sharing, and constructed a lightweight privacy protection model based on blockchain, but this in turn lacks the regulatory mechanism with users. Therefore, the addition of blockchain technology can provide better data security for medical data sharing, and how to propose a secure and efficient method to centralise the solution of identity authentication and data integrity verification in healthcare data sharing is a worthwhile research issue.

In response to the aforementioned issues, we propose a robustly coupled encryption authentication mechanism based on two-dimensional chaotic mapping, leveraging the security features of blockchain and smart contracts, aiming to enhance the security of healthcare data sharing. System model

For how to centrally and efficiently solve the problem of data integrity verification and identity authentication in healthcare data sharing, this paper designs a blockchain-based healthcare data sharing scheme, 2DCM-DS, which establishes a bidirectional strong-coupling association between the data owner and the shared data. The on-chain data integrity verification and identity authentication functions are implemented in the form of smart contracts. Definition of key symbols in 2DCM-DS.

The scheme model of 2DCM-DS comprises three types of entities: Data Owners (DO), Data Requesters (DR) and Consortium Blockchain (CBC). The scheme utilizes the consortium blockchain as the network carrier, encapsulating the designed encryption, decryption, data integrity verification and identity authentication functions into smart contracts deployed on the blockchain. Figure 1 illustrates the 2DCM-DS model.

Data Owners (DO) refer to organizations such as healthcare institutions, government agencies or commercial entities that possess healthcare datasets. In 2DCM-DS, DO participates in the data-sharing process as the owners of healthcare data. They are required to provide their own Identity Verification Audio (IVA) and a data summary of the medical dataset they own as Shared Data (SD). Additionally, DO receives the results of data integrity and user identity verification for the SD from users.

Data Requesters (DR) refer to organizations or individuals with a need to use healthcare datasets. In 2DCM-DS, DR participates in the data-sharing process by initiating operation requests on the on-chain data as accessors. Through smart contracts, DR can initiate data query and modification requests. The corresponding smart contract will execute data integrity verification and determine user permissions based on the DR’s IVA. Upon successful verification, the DR initiates subsequent operation requests to the corresponding DO.

Consortium Blockchain (CBC) serves as the decentralized network carrier in 2DCM-DS, with the blockchain ledger recording data operation transactions within the network. 2DCM-DS selects Hyperledger Fabric as the CBC entity. Hyperledger Fabric is a permissioned blockchain that only allows qualified participants to maintain the blocks. In 2DCM-DS, all DO and DR participates in data sharing as user nodes on the CBC, but not all of them serve as managing nodes. Threat model

The core of 2DCM-DS is 2CME. some adversaries try to disrupt normal data sharing behaviour by cracking 2CME. In the designed scheme, the security of 2DCM-DS can be proved by ensuring that the proposed 2CME is secure. Where 2CME is designed based on 2D-ILM as a cryptographic algorithm based on 2D chaotic mapping. Specifically, the security of 2CME needs to be ensured by the following primitives:

Lemma 1

2CME is sensitive to keys, which makes 2CME resistant to brute-force cracking attacks.

Lemma 2

2CME is resistant to statistical attacks, including differential attacks, and chosen plaintext attacks.

Lemma 3

The ciphertext message encrypted by 2CME should have a high degree of uncertainty.

2DCM-DS makes use of 2CME to focus on data integrity verification and identity authentication. The solution for data integrity verification and authentication in 2DCM-DS can be considered secure and feasible as long as it is guaranteed that 2CME can satisfy the above citation.

Method

This section describes the 2DCM-DS functions in detail.

2D-ILM

(1) 2D-ILM(\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_0$$\end{document},\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_0$$\end{document},\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu _1$$\end{document},\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu _2$$\end{document})

Differential attack analysis

Differential attacks belong to the category of statistical attacks. In the proposed 2CME algorithm, the obfuscation of 2D-ILM is directly related to the resistance of the final encryption result to statistical attacks. If the output result of 2D-ILM can make the ciphertext data of IVA randomly distributed in space, then it is able to resist differential attacks. Where the chaotic performance of 2D-ILM depends on the initial value sensitivity and traversal of the algorithm. Regarding the chaotic performance of 2D-ILM can can be evaluated using Lyaponov exponent and attractor.

The chaotic behavior of 2D-ILM reflects its security characteristics. In this work, it is compared with three existing 2D chaotic maps: 2D-LNIC25, 2D-LSM38 and 2D-ICM39. By examining the dynamic behaviors of different systems, the chaotic behaviors of various 2D chaotic maps are compared under the same parameter space and control parameters.

Noise attack analysis

When data is transmitted, attackers may employ noise attacks to interfere with the data transmission, which can affect the quality of the decrypted information. The more plaintext information that can be recovered when the ciphertext is subjected to interference, the stronger the algorithm’s resistance to noise attacks. The ciphertext is interfered by adding pretzel noise of different densities. Conclusion

To address the data privacy security risks in current healthcare data sharing, this paper proposes a secure and robust healthcare data sharing scheme based on two-dimensional chaotic mapping and blockchain. We introduce a two-dimensional chaotic mapping (2D-ILM) that exhibits superior chaotic performance. Utilizing the chaotic properties of 2D-ILM, combined with biometric audio information, a strongly coupled data encryption method (2CME) that enables bi-directional verification is designed. Based on these components, we developed 2DCM-DS and implemented the scheme using blockchain as the underlying network. Performance evaluation and security analysis indicate that the proposed scheme demonstrates robustness, efficient time consumption, and strong attack resistance, meeting the communication and data privacy protection requirements for healthcare data sharing. Future work will consider expanding the blockchain security environment within the scheme. There are still some limitations to current schemes: the scalability of the blockchain itself will limit the development of some blockchain-based data sharing schemes. Whether the system can still guarantee data security when some evil nodes provide low-quality data or the network suffers a Byzantine attack. Whether encapsulating cryptographic algorithms into smart contracts is secure enough.

HER2 testing in multifocal/multicentric breast cancer: should all foci be tested in the context of HER2-low and HER2-ultralow?
Authors: Si Wu, Hongbo Liu, Tianyu Wang, Jiaxian Miao, Jiamei Zhuan, Zhongze Cui, Meng Yue, Kun Wang, Jinze Li, Mengxue Han, Wenhan Hu, Xiaoxiao Wang, Yueping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent guidelines for HER2 testing in multifocal and multicentric breast cancer (MMBC) are typically based only on the evaluation of the main focus, which may lead to underdetection of HER2-low and HER2-ultralow, potentially resulting in missed treatment opportunities with trastuzumab deruxtecan. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the heterogeneity of HER2 expression among different foci in MMBC and to assess the clinical applicability of current HER2 testing strategies.MethodsA retrospective collection of all invasive cancer foci specimens was conducted from 490 consecutive MMBC patients, with HER2 testing and evaluation performed on each individual focus.ResultsIn the binary classification of HER2-negative and HER2-positive cases, 4.0 % of cases exhibited HER2 discordance among different foci, and in 2.5 % of cases, the main focus did not show the highest HER2 expression. However, when HER2-negative cases were further subdivided into HER2-null, HER2-ultralow, and HER2-low, 23.7 % of cases demonstrated HER2 heterogeneity among different foci, and in 12.0 % of cases, the main focus failed to present the highest HER2 expression. And no statistically significant correlation was observed between clinicopathological characteristics and the lack of highest HER2 expression in the main focus. Additionally, cases in which all foci were HER2-null accounted for only 11.0 %.ConclusionIn MMBC, significant intertumoral heterogeneity exists in the low levels of HER2 expression across different foci. Therefore, it is essential to perform HER2 testing on all foci, regardless of similarities in histological subtype or grade.Keywords: Multifocal and multicentric breast cancer (MMBC), HER2-Ultralow, HER2-Low, Intertumoral heterogeneity

Introduction

Multifocal (MF) and multicentric (MC) breast cancers (MMBC) are collectively referred to as multiple synchronous ipsilateral breast cancers [1]. Their reported prevalence varies widely, from about 4 % to 60 %, which is mainly due to inconsistent definitions and a lack of standardized diagnostic methods [2,3]. Although MMBC exhibits distinct clinical and biological characteristics compared to unifocal breast cancer, whether multifocality itself serves as an independent prognostic factor remains a subject of ongoing debate [4,5]. Notably, substantial intertumoral heterogeneity often exists among different foci within MMBC, including variations in histological subtype, histological grade, biomarker expression, molecular subtype, and other related features.

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a critical biomarker in breast cancer and is closely associated with its initiation and progression. Targeted therapies against HER2, such as trastuzumab, have significantly improved the prognosis of patients with HER2-positive breast cancer [6]. In MMBC, intertumoral heterogeneity in HER2 status (negative vs positive) can be observed among different foci, with an incidence of about 0 %–6 %, and it is more commonly found in cases with foci of different histological subtypes or histological grades [[7], [8], [9]]. Therefore, current clinical guidelines recommend that for MMBC, only the main focus (the largest focus) be tested for HER2, unless minor foci show morphological differences, such as distinct histological subtypes or a higher histological grade [10].

Recent findings from the DESTINY-Breast 04 (NCT03734029) [11] and DESTINY-Breast 06 (NCT04494425) [12] clinical trials have confirmed that trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) provides significant clinical benefit in patients with HER2-low (Immunohistochemistry [IHC] 1+ or IHC 2+/in situ hybridization [ISH]-negative) and HER2-ultralow (IHC >0 and < 1+) breast cancer. These results overcome the limitations imposed by the HER2 expression thresholds required for previous anti-HER2 therapies, thereby offering new therapeutic opportunities for a substantial proportion of HER2-negative patients who were previously unable for such treatments. This also means that the current HER2 testing strategy for MMBC may carry potential risks, particularly the possibility of missing HER2-low or ultralow cases. However, no study has yet refined the HER2-negative status, analyzed the intertumoral heterogeneity among HER2-null, HER2-ultralow, HER2-low and HER2-positive in different foci of MMBC. Moreover, it remains unclear whether performing HER2 testing only on the largest focus could result in the underdetection of HER2-low or ultralow patients, potentially affecting patients' opportunity to receive T-DXd therapy.

Therefore, this study aimed to analyze the intertumoral heterogeneity of low-level HER2 expression among different foci in MMBC and to explore the clinical applicability of the current HER2 testing strategy for MMBC.

Methods and materials

Sample collection

A retrospective study was conducted on consecutive patients diagnosed with primary invasive breast cancer who underwent surgical treatment at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between June 2020 and December 2024. The inclusion criteria were patients pathologically confirmed to have synchronous MMBC. MF breast cancer was defined as the presence of two or more distinct tumor foci located within the same quadrant of the breast, whereas MC breast cancer was defined as two or more distinct tumor foci located in different quadrants of the same breast, separated by normal breast tissue [13]. All foci included in the study were invasive carcinomas. Exclusion criteria included cases that had received neoadjuvant therapy, those diagnosed with microinvasive carcinoma (defined as invasive foci <1 mm in diameter), and cases lacking complete clinical data or with incomplete hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained slides and HER2 IHC slides for all foci. A total of 490 cases were ultimately included in the analysis. Among them, 442 cases (90.2 %) presented with 2 foci, 40 cases (8.2 %) with 3 foci, and 8 cases (1.6 %) with 4 foci. Foci were categorized based on the size of the invasive foci and ranked in descending order as follows: 1st focus/Main focus (the largest focus; if equal in size, designated as Focus 1), 2nd focus (second-largest focus; if equal, Focus 2), 3rd focus (third-largest focus; if equal, Focus 3), and 4th focus (fourth-largest focus; if equal, Focus 4).

HER2 testing and interpretation

A total of 490 cases of MMBC, including all foci, were retrieved from the pathology archives of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University, along with the corresponding archived H&E-stained slides, HER2 IHC slides, and HER2 diagnostic results. HER2 was tested via IHC employing a rabbit-derived monoclonal primary antibody (clone 4B5; Roche, Basel, Switzerland) on the BenchMark XT automated staining platform (Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA). For HER2 IHC 2+ cases, additional fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing was carried out using the HER2 DNA probe kit (PathVysion, Abbott Molecular, Des Moines, IL, USA). All HER2 diagnostic results for the samples used in this study were assessed by practicing pathologists in real-world clinical settings, based on the 2018 ASCO/CAP guideline [14] or the 2023 ASCO/CAP guideline [15] for HER2 testing in breast cancer. In light of prior clinical trial findings suggesting promising antitumor activity of T-DXd in patients with HER2-low breast cancer [16], our center began reporting the percentage and intensity of HER2 IHC staining in tumor cells starting in 2021. To ensure consistency and accuracy in HER2 status assessment, all samples were re-evaluated by a senior pathologist following the 2023 ASCO/CAP guideline [15] and 2024 Chinese guideline [17] for HER2 testing in breast cancer. For cases from the year 2020, HER2 status was determined through consensus between two senior pathologists. For cases from 2021 to 2024, any discrepancies between the initial pathologist's evaluation and the original HER2 diagnosis were adjudicated by a second experienced pathologist. HER2 status was defined as follows: HER2-positive was defined as either IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ with FISH amplification, whereas HER2-negative included IHC 0, IHC 1+, or IHC 2+ without FISH amplification. Based on the criteria established in the DB04 and DB06 clinical trials, HER2 expression levels were further subclassified. “HER2-low” was defined as IHC 1+ or IHC 2+ without FISH amplification. “HER2-ultralow” referred to cases with IHC 0, where ≤10 % of invasive tumor cells exhibited faint and incomplete membranous staining. “HER2-null” was defined as IHC 0 with complete absence of staining in invasive tumor cells.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis and visualization in this study were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 26.0; IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY) and OriginPro (Version 2024; OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA). The consistency of HER2 status between different foci was assessed using Cohen's Kappa and Fleiss Kappa. Kappa values were interpreted as follows [18]: 0.01–0.20: slight agreement; 0.21–0.40: fair agreement; 0.41–0.60: moderate agreement; 0.61–0.80: substantial agreement; 0.81–1.00: almost perfect agreement. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify the main factors influencing inconsistency in HER2 status between foci. A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 490 patients were female, with a median age of 50 years (SD: 11.26, Range: 24–83 years). For the main focus in these cases, the median tumor diameter was 2.07 cm (SD: 0.92). Among them, invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC) accounted for 78.0 % (382/490) of the cases. Histological grade II was observed in 68.0 % (333/490) of tumors. Furthermore, 84.5 % (414/490) and 79.6 % (390/490) of the tumors were positive for estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR), respectively, while 72.9 % (357/490) had Ki-67 ≤ 30 %. Low tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) infiltration (<10 %) was present in 72.7 % (356/490) of the cases. Inter-focal heterogeneity was observed in several pathological features: 56 cases (11.4 %) exhibited different histological types, 105 cases (21.4 %) showed different histological grades, and discordant biomarker expressions were identified in 30 cases (6.1 %) for ER, 48 cases (9.8 %) for PR, and 73 cases (14.9 %) for Ki-67. Moreover, differences in TILs infiltration levels were found in 94 cases (19.2 %), while differences in lymphovascular invasion and nerve invasion were observed in 64 cases (13.1 %) and 40 cases (8.2 %), respectively (Table 1).

Intertumoral heterogeneity of HER2 expression across distinct foci

Under the binary classification of HER2 status (HER2-negative vs HER2-positive), 4.0 % (20/490) of cases had inconsistent HER2 expression across different tumor foci. Notably, in 2.5 % (12/490) of cases, the foci with the most positive HER2 results were not the main focus. Among these 12 cases, the most positive HER2 result was observed in the minor focus in 9 cases, including 3 cases with discordant histological types between foci. In the remaining 3 cases, both tumor size and histological type were consistent across different foci. Furthermore, among the 12 cases, histological grades were consistent across foci in 5 cases. In 4 cases, the focus exhibiting the most positive HER2 status also had the highest histological grade.

Among the four HER2 expression categories (HER2-null vs HER2-ultralow vs HER2-low vs HER2-positive), 23.7 % (116/490) of cases exhibited intertumoral HER2 heterogeneity across different tumor foci. Specifically, HER2 heterogeneity was observed in 37.5 % (12/32) of HER2-ultralow, 28.5 % (84/295) of HER2-low, and 18.3 % (20/109) of HER2-positive cases (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Notably, in 12.0 % (59/490) of the cases, the highest HER2 expression level was not identified in the main tumor focus (Supplementary Fig. 1B). These differences primarily occurred in cases where the main focus was classified as HER2-ultralow with a minor focus of HER2-low (30.5 %, 18/59), or where the main focus was HER2-null with a minor focus of HER2-low (23.7 %, 14/59). Furthermore, 15 of the 59 cases involved foci of equal size (all double foci). In these, the predominant patterns of HER2 intertumoral heterogeneity included discordance between HER2-null and HER2-low (46.7 %, 7/15) and between HER2-ultralow and HER2-low (26.7 %, 4/15). Supplementary Fig. 2 summarized the characteristics of cases with HER2 intertumoral heterogeneity.

Factors associated with the highest HER2 expression occurring beyond the main tumor focus

Among the 12 cases where the main focus lacked the highest HER2 expression (negative vs positive), the results of univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that histological grade and Ki67 were related factors for the failure to detect the highest HER2 expression in the main focus, especially when the histological grade and Ki67 of the secondary focus were higher than those of the main focus, the secondary focus might be able to show the most positive HER2 status (Table 2).

Discussion

This study investigated intertumoral heterogeneity of HER2 expression across multiple tumor foci in a cohort of 490 consecutive patients with MMBC. The results showed that in cases with low levels of HER2 expression (HER2-ultralow/HER2-low), evaluating only the main focus would have resulted in 12.0 % of cases overlooking secondary foci with higher HER2 expression. Therefore, it is recommended to test the HER2 status of all foci as much as possible, especially when the main focus was HER2-null. To our knowledge, this is the first study with the largest number of cases analyzing the intertumoral heterogeneity of low HER2 expression among distinct foci in MMBC, thereby providing critical data to support the refinement of the HER2 testing scheme for MMBC.

This study showed that 4.0 % of cases had differences in HER2-negative and HER2-positive status across different tumor foci. Additionally, in 2.5 % of cases, the main focus without the most positive HER2 status was observed, which mainly occurred when the secondary foci had higher histological grades and Ki67. These findings are consistent with the results reported by Gillian et al. [8], who analyzed HER2 status across different foci in 246 cases of multifocal invasive breast carcinoma. Their study identified HER2 status discordance (HER2-negative vs HER2 positive) across different foci in 6.5 % of cases. Moreover, in 1.6 % of cases, the main tumor focus did not reflect the most HER2-positive status, and all such instances involved smaller foci with different histological subtypes or higher histological grades. Furthermore, when HER2-negative status was further subclassified into HER2-null, HER2-ultralow, and HER2-low categories, the intertumoral consistency of HER2 status significantly decreased. Under this refined classification, 23.7 % of cases demonstrated HER2 intertumoral heterogeneity. This result corresponded closely with the 28 % rate of HER2 heterogeneity among patients with multiple tumors reported by Baez-Navarro et al. [19], who categorized patients into HER2 0, HER2-low, and HER2-positive groups.

There remains considerable controversy regarding the optimal HER2 IHC testing strategy for MMBC. Some researchers advocate for testing only the largest tumor focus, arguing that biomarker heterogeneity among different foci is generally minimal, and that treatment decisions would change in only a small subset of patients exhibiting discordance [9]. Others, however, recommend that HER2 IHC testing should not be limited to the largest focus, particularly when smaller foci exhibit distinct histological subtypes or higher histological grades, as these features are more likely to be associated with biomarker variability [20]. The emergence of T-DXd has introduced new clinical significance to the comprehensive assessment of HER2 expression across all tumor foci in MMBC. This novel antibody–drug conjugate exerts its therapeutic benefit primarily through a unique “bystander killing” effect [21]: after intracellular release, its cytotoxic payload can diffuse into adjacent tumor cells with low or even absent HER2 expression, thereby producing an extended anti-tumor effect. Therefore, T-DXd no longer relies on high HER2 expression in tumor cells. Instead, its therapeutic effect is driven by the cytotoxic payload's ability to penetrate cells and the permeability of the surrounding microenvironment, among other factors. These findings suggest that in MMBC, both the largest and smaller tumor foci, as long as they exhibit low-level HER2 expression, may benefit from T-DXd treatment. In this study, both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses demonstrated that none of the factors associated with the highest HER2 expression did not detect in the main tumor focus showed statistical significance across the HER2-null, HER2-ultralow, HER2-low, and HER2-positive subgroups. Furthermore, considering the unique mechanism of action of T-DXd, its therapeutic efficacy may not solely depend on the overall level of HER2 protein expression. The pattern of HER2 expression, such as cluster-type, mosaic-type, or dispersed-type distributions [22], may also play a significant role in modulating treatment response. However, these expression patterns are not currently captured in standard HER2 diagnostic reports. Therefore, given the current clinical need for precise identification of patients with low-level HER2 expression, it is recommended that HER2 testing be performed on all tumor foci in cases of MMBC whenever feasible. Additionally, our findings revealed that only 11.0 % of cases exhibited HER2-null across all foci. Consequently, in situations where comprehensive HER2 testing for all foci is limited by economic constraints, it is recommended to sequentially perform further HER2 assessment on all foci in cases where the largest tumor focus tests HER2-null, in order to avoid missing patients who may potentially benefit from T-DXd therapy, as well as to identify those who may not derive benefit or could even be harmed by such treatment.

Recently, T-DXd received FDA approval for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic, hormone receptor-positive, and HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH−) or HER2-ultralow (IHC 0 with membrane staining) breast cancer [23]. However, there is currently a lack of clinical data regarding the efficacy of T-DXd in patients with MMBC. For individuals presenting with both HER2-positive and HER2-low/ultralow foci, it remains uncertain whether direct use of T-DXd, combination therapy with HER2-targeted monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab, or alternative multimodal treatment strategies would offer the greatest clinical benefit while maintaining economic feasibility. Further clinical research and evidence are needed to inform optimal treatment approaches in this context. Although the therapeutic lower limit of T-DXd, that is, the threshold for HER2-ultralow, remains unclear, it is still important to evaluate the HER2 status of all foci in patients with MMBC whenever possible. This can aid in identifying those who may benefit from T-DXd treatment in the future and also address the potential risk of HER2 antigen loss due to prolonged storage of paraffin-embedded tissue blocks, which may hinder the accurate detection of low-level HER2 expression [24].

The expression of low-level HER2 exhibits considerable heterogeneity [[25], [26], [27]]. It encompasses the spatial heterogeneity of HER2 expression among different foci within primary MMBC, as shown in this study. It also includes the spatiotemporal heterogeneity observed in metastatic breast cancer. For instance, when a breast core needle biopsy is classified as HER2-null, subsequent testing of a metastatic lesion or re-evaluation of archived tissue may increase the likelihood of detecting low levels of HER2 expression. Indeed, the DB04 trial confirmed that survival benefits with T-DXd over chemotherapy were observed in patients with advanced HR+/HER2-low breast cancer, regardless of the type of sample used for enrollment (primary tumor vs. metastatic site, or archival tissue vs. fresh tissue) [28]. Such strategies may ultimately expand patient eligibility for T-DXd treatment and allow more individuals to benefit from this therapy. Moreover, variability in HER2 status assessment can arise from multiple factors, including pre-analytical issues such as specimen selection, analytical factors like antibody choice and quality control procedures, as well as post-analytical variability in pathological interpretation. Due to the current lack of sufficient clinical evidence to define the lower threshold of HER2 expression responsive to T-DXd treatment, significant gaps remain in standardizing and minimizing the variability of HER2-low detection. This constitutes a limitation of the present study and highlights a critical area for future research.

In conclusion, this study confirmed that there is significant intertumoral heterogeneity in the low-level HER2 expression among different foci in MMBC. It is necessary to perform HER2 IHC testing on all foci, regardless of their same histological type and grade. This approach ensures that patients with HER2-ultralow or HER2-low are accurately identified and can be considered for T-DXd therapy. In resource-limited settings, a stepwise protocol could also be considered, performing HER2 testing on additional blocks only if the main focus is HER2-null.

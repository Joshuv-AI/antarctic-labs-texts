Zanubrutinib, lenalidomide and rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone as initial treatment in non–germinal center B-cell diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a multi-center phase 2 study by Jiangsu Cooperative Lymphoma Group (JCLG)

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with non–germinal center B-cell-like (non-GCB) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) often exhibit suboptimal responses to standard rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP) therapy.MethodsThis multicenter, phase II study evaluated the safety and efficacy of zanubrutinib, lenalidomide, and R-CHOP (ZR2-CHOP) in newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL. Background

Diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is one of the most common lymphomas, accounting for 25%–35% of adult non-Hodgkin lymphomas. Despite a 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) of approximately 60% in the R-CHOP era (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone), 30%–40% patients with DLBCL are refractory to frontline therapy or relapse [1]. DLBCL is biologically heterogeneous and can be classified by cell of origin into germinal center B-cell-like (GCB), activated B-cell-like (ABC), or unclassified subtypes [2]. The ABC subtype often corresponds to the non-GCB subtype by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and is associated with a poorer prognosis [3]. After R-CHOP, the 2-year PFS in non-GCB DLBCL ranges from 40 to 70% [1, 2, 4–7].

Given the poor outcomes in non-GCB DLBCL, recent trials have focused on developing cell-of-origin–based treatment strategies. ABC DLBCL is characterized by chronic active B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling, which constitutively activates the prosurvival NF-κB pathway. Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK), a key component of this pathway, is a therapeutic target. Covalent BTK inhibitors have shown selective toxicity in ABC DLBCL models [8, 9]. In patients with relapsed ABC DLBCL, single-agent ibrutinib achieved an objective response rate (ORR) of 37%, though the median PFS was only 2 months [10]. Lenalidomide is an immunomodulatory drug that decreases the expression of IRF4, a master regulatory transcription factor for ABC DLBCL survival [11]. Lenalidomide also showed higher response rates in non-GCB compared to GCB DLBCL (53% vs. 9%), but responses were not durable [12]. Although phase III trials combining chemoimmunotherapy with ibrutinib or lenalidomide in newly diagnosed non-GCB or ABC DLBCL failed to meet primary endpoints, survival benefits were observed in high-risk patients receiving these agents [5, 6, 13, 14].

In preclinical models, ibrutinib downregulates IRF4, synergizing with lenalidomide to eliminate ABC DLBCL [15]. Additionally, lenalidomide and other immunomodulatory drugs have demonstrated enhanced activity against ibrutinib-resistant ABC DLBCL [16]. The IR2 regimen (ibrutinib, lenalidomide, and rituximab) for relapsed/refractory (R/R) non-GCB DLBCL yields an ORR of 49–65% and a median PFS of 3.9–5.4 months [17, 18]. In the recently published Smart Start study, newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL patients achieved an ORR of 86.2% after receiving 2 cycles of IR2. The complete response (CR) rate at the end of IR2-chemoimmunotherapy was 94.5% with 2-year PFS and overall survival (OS) rates of 91.3% and 96.6%, respectively [19].

Zanubrutinib, a novel oral BTK inhibitor, has shown favorable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiles, along with a favorable safety profile and strong activity across multiple clinical studies in B-cell malignancies [20–22]. In patients with R/R non-GCB DLBCL, single-agent zanubrutinib achieves an ORR of 29.3% and a CR rate of 17.1% [23]. It has also demonstrated promising safety and efficacy in newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL [24, 25]. Based on these findings, and preclinical synergy of BTK inhibitors and lenalidomide, we conducted this multi-center, phase 2 study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of ZR2-CHOP (zanubrutinib, lenalidomide plus R-CHOP) in patients with newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL.

Methods

Study design and participants

This study evaluated the safety and efficacy of ZR2-CHOP in patients with newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL. Eligible patients were 18 to 75 years old with de novo non-GCB DLBCL confirmed by immunohistochemistry (IHC) using the Hans algorithm; at least one measurable lesion by computed tomography (CT, > 1.5 cm), and adequate hematologic, cardiac, hepatic, and renal function. An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of ≤ 3 was required; patients with ECOG-PS 3 were eligible only if the impairment was lymphoma-related and deemed reversible by the investigator. Key exclusion criteria included GCB or primary mediastinal DLBCL, central nervous system (CNS) involvement, Richter syndrome, or contraindications to study drugs or supportive therapies. Detailed Eligibility Criteria and Exclusion Criteria are listed in additional file 1. This study was approved by the Institutional Research Board of Jiangsu Province Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05200312; registered January 20, 2022).

Procedures

Oral zanubrutinib was administered continuously at 160 mg twice daily starting on Day 1, with lenalidomide given at 25 mg daily on Days 1–7. Patients received intravenous rituximab (375 mg/m2 on Day 0), cyclophosphamide (750 mg/m2 on Day 1), doxorubicin (50 mg/m2 on Day 1), vincristine (1.4 mg/m2 on Day 1), and oral prednisone (100 mg daily on Day 1–5). Up to six cycles of ZR2-CHOP were recommended. During the initial treatment cycle, zanubrutinib and/or lenalidomide could be withheld for patients with poor physical condition. For patients over 70 years, a reduced-intensity regimen (ZR2-miniCHOP) was used, consisting of zanubrutinib 160 mg twice daily, lenalidomide 10 mg daily on Days 1–7, rituximab 375 mg/m2 on Day 0, cyclophosphamide 400 mg/m2 on Day 1, doxorubicin 25 mg/m2 on Day 1, vincristine 1 mg on Day 1, and oral prednisone 40 mg/m2 daily on Days 1–5. Lenalidomide dose escalation was not performed in this age group due to safety considerations. The dose delay and adjustment protocols for the medications in the regimen are detailed in Table S1-S6 in Additional file 1.

All patients received mandatory prophylaxis with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and acyclovir for herpes simplex virus. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) prophylaxis was also required for all patients. Entecavir was given to patients with seropositive occult hepatitis B virus infection (HBsAg-negative, HBcAb-positive). CNS lymphoma prophylaxis was administered at the investigator’s discretion.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the CR rate at mid-treatment and end-of-treatment (EOT), assessed by investigators using the Lugano Classification [26]. Secondary endpoints included 2-year PFS, 2-year OS, ORR, and adverse events (AEs). CT or Positron Emission Tomography-Computed Tomography (PET-CT) scans were performed after 3 cycles and a PET-CT scan after 6 cycles. Treatment response was assessed according to standardized criteria for non-Hodgkin lymphoma (Lugano classification). Patients were evaluated at a safety follow-up visit 30 days after the end of the last dose of therapy or before starting new anticancer therapy (whichever occurred first) to assess AEs. AEs were graded based on CTCAE version 5.0. Exploratory analyses assessed circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics at baseline, mid-treatment, and EOT to evaluate tumor mutational burden. Detailed methodology for ctDNA detection is provided in the Additional file 2: Supplemental Methods.

Sample size determination

This phase II trial was designed based on historical controls showing a 65% CR rate at EOT [5, 6, 27]. The Smart-Start trial, presented at the 2019 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) meeting, reported a CR rate of 96% [28]. Accordingly, the expected CR rate for this study was set at 90%. Using a one-sample proportion Z test with a significance level (α) of 0.05 and 90% power, and allowing for a 10% dropout rate, the required sample size was calculated to be 35 participants.

Statistical analysis

OS and PFS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Differences in prognostic factors were assessed by the log-rank test in univariate analysis. The Cox proportional hazards model evaluated the impact of patient characteristics on PFS. Data analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 25.0 and GraphPad Prism version 9.0. All tests were two-sided, with a significance level set at 0.05.

Results

Patients

Between January 2022 and September 2023, 35 previously untreated patients with non-GCB DLBCL were enrolled. One patient was excluded due to CNS involvement, leaving 34 patients who received treatment (Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1 and Table S7 in Additional file 2. The median age was 55 years (range, 22–71 years), and 58.8% (20/34) were male. Advanced-stage disease (stage III–IV) was present in 73.5% (25/34), 26.5% (9/34) had an ECOG-PS ≥ 2, and 47.1% (16/34) had an International Prognostic Index (IPI) score ≥ 3. Extranodal involvement was observed in 88.2% (30/34), and 64.7% (22/34) had double expressor lymphoma (DEL), defined as MYC expression ≥ 40% and BCL2 expression ≥ 50%. Molecular subtype data were available for 28 patients, determined using the LymphGen algorithm [29]. In 75% (21/28) of patients, genotyping was performed using tumor tissue DNA; in 25% (7/28) of patients, plasma ctDNA was used due to unavailable tumor samples. The MCD, BN2, ST2, N1, and other subtypes accounted for 39.3% (11/28), 14.3% (4/28), 3.6% (1/28), 3.6% (1/28), and 39.3% (11/28) of patients, respectively. Based on mutational and fluorescence in situ hybridization data, TP53 abnormalities were present in 34.5% (10/29) of patients.

Treatment exposure

A total of 88.2% (30/34) of patients completed all planned cycles of ZR2-CHOP. The median relative dose intensities (RDI = total actual dose/total planned dose × 100%) for doxorubicin, zanubrutinib and lenalidomide were 100%, 96% and 100%, respectively. The proportion of patients receiving ≥ 80% of the intended dose intensity was 97.1% (33/34) for doxorubicin, 85.3% (29/34) for zanubrutinib, and 91.2% (31/34) for lenalidomide. Patients aged ≥ 60 years received fewer cycles of ZR2-CHOP (median 5 vs. 6) and shorter durations of zanubrutinib and lenalidomide treatment (both median 5 vs. 6 cycles) compared to those < 60 years (Table 2). Seven patients (20.6%) underwent autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) for consolidation, and one patient (P16) received zanubrutinib maintenance due to persistent ctDNA positivity. All eight of these patients had achieved CR before initiating consolidation therapy. As they received additional treatment beyond protocol-defined therapy, they were included in the full analysis set (FAS) but excluded from the per-protocol set (PPS; Fig. 1).

Efficacy

At the data cut-off (February 9th, 2025), all patients were evaluable for response. The best ORR was 100% (34/34) and the best CR rate was 97.1% (33/34). After 3 cycles of treatment, 70.6% (24/34) of patients achieved CR/complete metabolic response (CMR), and 29.4% (10/34) achieved PR; after 6 cycles, the CR/CMR rate increased to 94.1% (32/34) (Fig. 2A). Six patients relapsed. One patient died from progressive disease, and another died from secondary acute myeloid leukemia (AML). In the FAS population, with a median follow-up of 28 months (range, 15–36 months), 79.4% (27/34) of patients maintained CR/CMR. The 2-year PFS and OS rates were 84.8% and 96.8% (Fig. 2B and C). In the PPS population, with a median follow-up of 27 months (range, 15–36 months), 80.8% (21/26) of patients sustained CR/CMR, with 2-year PFS and OS rates of 79.9% and 95.8%, respectively (Fig. 2D and E). Subgroup analysis of PFS showed that traditionally unfavorable factors—such as DEL, the MCD subtype, bulky disease (≥ 7.5 cm), and multiple extranodal involvements—did not significantly impact the efficacy of ZR2-CHOP in either the FAS or PPS populations. However, patients with an IPI ≥ 3 demonstrated a trend toward poorer PFS. No significant differences in PFS or OS were observed between patients aged ≥ 60 and those < 60 years (Fig. 3 and Additional file 2: Fig. S1–S3).

Plasma ctDNA samples at baseline were available for 26 patients; among them, 96.2% (25/26) had at least one detectable variant. Of these, 84% (21/25) achieved ctDNA negativity during treatment. At the mid-term evaluation, 73.9% (17/23) of patients were ctDNA-negative, increasing to 81.0% (17/21) at EOT. All patients who achieved ctDNA negativity also achieved CR/CMR. Notably, five patients with PR and ctDNA negativity at mid-term analysis converted to CR/CMR by EOT. No patients who achieved ctDNA negativity at mid-term reverted to ctDNA-positive status at EOT. Two patients who were ctDNA-positive at mid-treatment achieved negativity by EOT. Four patients remained ctDNA-positive throughout treatment but achieved CR/CMR by PET-CT (Fig. 4 and Table S8 in Additional file 2). Among these, one patient relapsed at 16 months, while the remaining three maintained CR at 20, 21, and 28 months of follow-up. Two patients who had previously achieved ctDNA negativity relapsed. One developed CNS relapse of primary testicular DLBCL with MCD subtype and TP53 mutation at 18 months post-treatment. The other, harboring a TP53 deletion, experienced lymph node relapse at 28 months. Overall, the 2-year PFS rate among patients who achieved ctDNA negativity was 95.0%.

Safety

All patients experienced treatment-related AEs. The most common AEs of any grade were anemia (88.2%, 30/34), fatigue (79.4%, 27/34), thrombocytopenia (73.5%, 25/34), hypokalemia (73.5%, 25/34), and neutropenia (70.6%, 24/34). AE incidence rates were generally consistent across different age groups and lenalidomide dose cohorts (Table 3). Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 67.6% (23/34) of patients, most frequently neutropenia (50.0%, 17/34), thrombocytopenia (32.4%, 11/34), febrile neutropenia (23.5%, 8/34), anemia (17.6%, 6/34), and pneumonia (17.6%, 6/34). Notably, one patient developed Grade 2 atrial fibrillation, and another experienced a Grade 3 gastrointestinal hemorrhage. One patient died of secondary AML at the 33-month follow-up.

AEs led to temporary dose holds of R-CHOP, zanubrutinib, or lenalidomide in 17.6% (6/34), 64.7% (22/34), or 41.2% (14/34) of patients, respectively (Table 2). Dose reductions were required in 17.6% (6/34) of patients for R-CHOP, 11.8% (4/34) for zanubrutinib, and 14.7% (5/34) for lenalidomide. Treatment discontinuation due to AEs occurred in 2.9% (1/34), 5.9% (2/34), and 11.8% (4/34) of patients for R-CHOP, zanubrutinib, and lenalidomide, respectively. All four patients who discontinued any component of therapy were aged ≥ 60 years. The AEs leading to discontinuation included thrombocytopenia and neutropenia (n = 2), pneumonia (n = 1), and severe rash (n = 1).

Disease characteristics of relapsed patients

Six patients experienced relapse. Among them, two were classified as MCD subtype, one as BN2, and one as N1. Three patients had TP53 aberrations. Recurrently observed mutations in this cohort included those involved in sustained BCR signaling (CD79A, CD79B and PTPN6 mutation), constitutive NF-κB pathway activation (MYD88 and TNFAIP3 mutation), interruption of key epigenetic regulator (EP300, CREBBP and KMT2D mutation), impairment of TP53 pathway (TP53, BTG2 mutation) and loss of anti-tumor immunity (B2M, CD58, CD83 and CIITA mutation, PDCD1LG2 fusion, Table 4). Despite these findings, no specific baseline plasma ctDNA mutations were associated with subsequent disease progression (Additional file 2: Fig. S4). Two patients (P15 and P22) experienced CNS relapse at 18 and 19 months of follow-up, respectively. Both had previously received intrathecal prophylaxis during frontline therapy. Following relapse, they underwent salvage treatment regimens that included zanubrutinib and subsequently achieved CR.

Discussion

ZR2-CHOP demonstrated potential benefits compared to historical R-CHOP outcomes in patients with non-GCB DLBCL. Despite the small sample size, this cohort represented a relatively high-risk population, with over one-third of patients harboring the MCD subtype—substantially higher than the ~ 14% prevalence reported in the general DLBCL population [29] —and nearly two-thirds classified as DEL. Treatment with ZR2-CHOP resulted in an ORR of 100%, a CR rate of 70.6% at mid-term, and 94.1% at EOT. Among patients with evaluable ctDNA, 73.9% achieved ctDNA negativity at the mid-term assessment, rising to 81.0% at EOT. The 2-year PFS rates were 84.8% in the FAS population and 79.9% in the PPS population, with outcomes appearing unaffected by traditionally poor prognostic features such as DEL status, the MCD subtype, or multiple extranodal disease.

Treatment options for patients with non-GCB DLBCL have broadened in recent years with the advent of polatuzumab vedotin, BTK inhibitors, lenalidomide, CAR-T cell therapy, bispecific antibodies, and other novel agents [30]. In the frontline setting, R-CHOP combined with ibrutinib achieved a 3-year PFS rate of 70.8%, while R-CHOP combined with lenalidomide yielded a 2-year PFS rate of 67%. However, neither regimen demonstrated a statistically significant advantage over R-CHOP alone. Nonetheless, subgroup analyses revealed that the addition of ibrutinib or lenalidomide to R-CHOP provided clinical benefit in patients under 60 years of age with DEL, the MCD subtype, or an IPI score ≥ 3 [5, 6, 13]. An indirect comparison of clinical trials evaluating BTK inhibitors and/or lenalidomide as first-line therapies for DLBCL, including a comparison with the POLARIX trial, is provided in Table S3 in Additional file 2.

MYC/BCL2 co-expression is an important adverse prognostic factor in non-GCB DLBCL [31–33]. Mutations in MYD88L265P, PIM1, PRDM1, and MYC genes, along with the MCD subtype, are significantly enriched in DEL cases [14, 34]. A recent study demonstrated that BTK inhibitors facilitate the degradation of ubiquitinated MYD88L265P via a non-canonical chronic selective autophagy, thereby disrupting the My-T-BCR supercomplex in DLBCL. Additionally, lenalidomide was shown to synergize with ibrutinib to enhance the autophagic turnover of MYD88L265P [35]. In the present study, the 2-year PFS rate for patients with DEL and the MCD subtype were 85.4% and 81.8%, respectively, comparable to those without these adverse factors. Notably, this cohort included four patients with the MCD subtype and TP53 abnormalities, a combination previously associated with a dismal prognosis and a median survival of less than one year [36]. Only one of these patients relapsed 18 months post-treatment, whereas the remaining three sustained CR at 20, 23, and 28 months of follow-up. In contrast, patients with an IPI score ≥ 3 demonstrated a trend toward inferior outcomes with ZR2-CHOP. In the PHOENIX trial, high-risk R-IPI patients did not benefit from the addition of ibrutinib to R-CHOP [6], and in the Smart-Start study, patients with IPI ≥ 3 had a lower CR rate after two cycles of IR2 therapy [19]. These findings suggest that the efficacy of BTK inhibitors may be more closely linked to underlying molecular mechanisms than to traditional clinical prognostic indices such as the IPI.

CtDNA is a dynamic biomarker in DLBCL. An early molecular response—defined as a ≥ 2-log reduction in ctDNA levels following the first cycle of therapy—has been associated with improved prognosis [37]. Moreover, ctDNA negativity at day 28 after CAR-T cell infusion correlates with longer PFS and OS in patients with R/R DLBCL [38, 39]. In our study, ctDNA monitoring was incorporated as part of response assessment. ZR2-CHOP treatment resulted in rapid and deep molecular responses, with 17 out of 21 patients achieving ctDNA negativity at EOT, consistent with PET-CT results. Among the four patients who maintained ctDNA positivity but achieved CR/CMR by PET-CT, only one experienced disease relapse. This patient harbored persistent NOTCH1 and TNFAIP3 mutations and relapsed at 16 months post-treatment. There is currently insufficient evidence on further treatment to achieve ctDNA negativity. Of the three non-relapsed patients, one is on zanubrutinib maintenance therapy, while the other two are under observation.

Six patients experienced PD, five of whom had previously achieved a CR before relapsing at a median follow-up of 19 months, including two cases of CNS relapse. Among these six patients, three belonged to the MCD subtype and three harbored TP53 aberrations. In dose-escalation–induced in vitro models of resistance, ibrutinib-resistant DLBCL cell lines demonstrated increased dependency on CD79A, CD79B, and SYK despite continued BTK inhibition. These resistant lines also showed augmented reliance on NF-κB pathway components, suggesting that reprogramming of BCR-dependent NF-κB signaling may enable evasion of BTK inhibition in resistant DLBCL [16]. Consistently, in patients who relapsed following ZR2-CHOP treatment, we observed mutations in CD79A, CD79B, and TNFAIP3—genes associated with persistent BCR signaling and constitutive NF-κB activation [8, 40, 41]. Additionally, recurrent mutations were observed in genes involved in epigenetic regulation, the TP53 pathway, and anti-tumor immunity. Shaffer et al. reported that ibrutinib-resistant clones upregulate genes encoding MHC class II subunits and those transactivated by IRF4, TCF4, and MYC. Lenalidomide, known to downregulate IRF4 expression, may therefore contribute to mitigating BTK inhibitor resistance when combined with ibrutinib. Notably, subsequent treatments targeting immune escape mechanisms showed clinical benefit: two of four relapsed patients responded to PD-1 blockade with tislelizumab, and one patient achieved CR following CAR-T therapy (Table 4). These observations support the concept that enhancing anti-tumor immunity may offer a viable approach to overcoming BTK inhibitor resistance in DLBCL [16]. Collectively, these results underscore that disease relapse in DLBCL is often driven by complex molecular alterations across multiple pathways, posing a major challenge to targeted therapeutic strategies.

The ZR2-CHOP regimen was generally well tolerated. Grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 67.6% of patients, with neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia being the most common toxicities leading to dose interruptions or reductions; however, these events infrequently led to treatment discontinuation. The overall AE profile was consistent with the known safety profiles of the individual agents, incidence rates comparable to those reported in previous studies [6, 19]. Notably, the incidence of pneumonia (16.7%) appeared higher than in earlier studies; two of the six grade ≥ 3 cases were associated with COVID-19 infection. All affected patients recovered. Zanubrutinib and lenalidomide were permanently discontinued in only one patient due to recurrent and persistent neutropenia. Cardiac AEs were infrequent, potentially reflecting the more favorable cardiovascular safety profile of zanubrutinib compared to ibrutinib. In subgroup analyses by age, no significant differences in AE incidence were observed between patients aged ≥ 60 years and those < 60 years. However, all four patients who discontinued treatment were aged ≥ 60 years, and this older cohort received a shorter median duration of ZR2-CHOP therapy.

This study has several limitations. First, the small sample size limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding efficacy and precludes the reliable identification of high-risk or treatment-resistant subgroups. The prognostic utility of ctDNA monitoring remains inconclusive. Although three patients exhibited persistent ctDNA positivity following treatment, none experienced relapse during a median follow-up of 21 months. Nevertheless, this subgroup may still represent individuals at increased risk and warrants close surveillance. Second, treatment heterogeneity may confound outcome interpretation. Specifically, seven patients underwent autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) for consolidation, and one received zanubrutinib maintenance therapy. However, the primary endpoint of the study was CR rate at EOT, and the 2-year PFS was designated as a secondary endpoint. Notably, baseline characteristics between the FAS and PPS populations were balanced, minimizing potential bias. Third, due to the unavailability of tumor tissue, molecular subtyping in seven patients was performed using plasma ctDNA. Although the clinical utility of ctDNA-based genotyping remains under investigation [42, 43], molecular classification was achieved in all but one case (“other”), with the remainder assigned to known subtypes (BN2: n = 3; MCD: n = 1; N1: n = 1)..

Conclusions

In this small-sample phase II clinical trial, the combination of the novel BTK inhibitor zanubrutinib, lenalidomide, and R-CHOP demonstrated high response rates and encouraging survival outcomes in patients with newly diagnosed non-GCB DLBCL. However, we also observed that disease relapse occurred in patients who initially achieved CR, driven by genetic aberrations involving multiple molecular pathways. These findings highlight the importance of integrating genetic profiling into future clinical trials of targeted therapy combinations. Such an approach may enable earlier identification of high-risk patients and ultimately enhance cure rates through more personalized first-line treatment strategies.

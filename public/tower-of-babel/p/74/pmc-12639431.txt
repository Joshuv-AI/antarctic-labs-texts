Carbapenem-Resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae outbreak in the Intensive Care Unit of a cancer centre
Authors: Yuying Pang, Yan Hu, Jiaoyang Zhu, Zhaoxia Liu, Youquan Zhou, Xiumei Yang, Yifan Peng, Min Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundWe describe an outbreak of carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) nosocomial infection in the intensive care unit (ICU) of a cancer hospital. We evaluate the containment measures implemented and provide evidence-based recommendations for improving CRKP infection prevention and control strategies.MethodsBetween June and July 2023, a comprehensive epidemiological investigation was conducted, including environmental hygiene surveillance and targeted infection control interventions. These measures focused on patients with CRKP nosocomial infections in the ICU, and their effectiveness was systematically evaluated.ResultsA total of 22 CRKP strains were identified in 14 patients(including one transferred from another hospital), with 13 nosocomial infections. Five patients had infections at multiple sites. The nosocomial infection rate was 8.1%, representing a statistically significant increase compared to the same period in previous years. Environmental surveillance detected CRKP contamination on the hands of two doctors (8.7% detection rate) and on various surfaces (8% detection rate), particularly shared equipment, bed unit surfaces, and sanitary ware. Following the implementation of control measures, the spread was effectively contained.ConclusionsKey factors contributing to transmission likely included delayed identification of infected patients, inadequate enforcement of isolation protocols, and suboptimal environmental cleaning and disinfection. Early detection, strict isolation measures, and thorough environmental cleaning are essential for effective prevention and control of CRKP in the ICU setting.Keywords: Intensive care unit, Outbreak, Carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae, Investigation and control

Introduction

In May 2024, the World Health Organization (WHO) released its updated Bacterial Pathogen Priority List (BPPL), categorising 15 antibiotic-resistant pathogens into critical, high, or medium priority groups based on research needs and public health impact. Among these, carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) were designated as a critical priority, representing one of the most significant public health threats. CRE pathogens are associated with limited treatment options, high mortality and morbidity rates, and accelerated antibiotic resistance, with few drug treatment options currently under development [1]. In China, surveillance data from CHINET 2023 indicated that carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) accounts for the majority of CRE cases, with resistance rates to imipenem and meropenem reaching 24.8% and 26%, respectively [2].

Between June and July 2023, 14 cases of CRKP infection were detected among inpatients in the intensive care unit (ICU) at a cancer hospital. The Infection Prevention and Control Department immediately initiated a comprehensive epidemiological investigation and environmental hygiene assessment to identify the source of transmission. Appropriate control measures were implemented, resulting in successful containment of the outbreak.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

A total of 14 patients with CRKP infections, detected between 5 June and 31 July 2023, were included in the study. To ensure that only unique cases were analysed, repeat strain submissions from the same individual were excluded.

Study design

Epidemiological investigation

An outbreak control protocol for nosocomial infections was activated by the Infection Prevention and Control Department. Retrospective data was collected using the nosocomial infection surveillance system. Epidemiological investigations included interviews with the ICU nosocomial infection management team, attending physicians, nurses, and cleaning personnel. Data collected included patient demographics (age and sex), admission dates, primary diagnoses, invasive procedures, treatments administered, and clinical outcomes. Environmental sampling was performed on surfaces, equipment, healthcare workers' hands, and fiberscopes. On-site assessments were conducted to evaluate adherence to isolation protocols, aseptic techniques, hand hygiene practices, and the cleaning and disinfection procedures carried out by cleaning staff.

Diagnostic criteria

The diagnosis of nosocomial infection cases was based on the Diagnostic Criteria for Nosocomial Infection (Trial) [3]. According to the Guidelines for the Control of Outbreaks of Nosocomial Infections (WS/T524-2016), a suspected outbreak is defined as the occurrence of more than three cases with similar clinical syndromes that may originate from a common source within a short period, among patients in a healthcare facility or its departments [4].

Environmental hygiene monitoring

A total of 138 samples were collected for environmental hygiene monitoring. This included 23 samples from staff members (doctors, nurses, and cleaning personnel) taken during work shifts, along with samples from surfaces suspected of contamination. These surfaces included bed units (e.g., bed rails, armrests, bedside cabinets, infusion pumps, IV poles, medical waste bin covers, monitor panels, ventilator surfaces, and treatment carts and trays), public areas (e.g., computer mice, keyboards, door handles, and switch panels), and shared medical equipment (e.g., X-ray machines and ultrasound probes). An additional 39 samples were collected from the Fibroscopy Room, covering the surfaces of objects, fiberscopes, disinfectants, and staff hands. Records from the Central Sterile Supply Department (CSSD) were reviewed to evaluate the disinfection and sterilisation procedures for fiberscopes used in the ICU. Sampling and result interpretation followed the Nosocomial Disinfection Hygiene Standard (GB15982) [5].

Etiological identification and drug sensitivity testing

Specimen culture, isolation, and pathogen identification were performed in accordance with the National Clinical Laboratory Practice (Fourth Edition) [6]. Strain identification was carried out using the Autofms 1000 automatic microbial mass spectrometry system (AnTuTu Bio). Antimicrobial sensitivity testing was performed using drug-impregnated paper discs (OXOID, UK) with the VITEK 2 Compact automated microbial identification and antimicrobial susceptibility system (Mérieux, France). Results were interpreted according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute guidelines (CLSI M100-S22) [7]. Quality control was ensured using Klebsiella pneumoniae reference strains ATCC70060 and ATCCBAA1705.

Index calculation

The incidence rate of CRKP nosocomial infection was calculated as the number of CRKP-infected patients in the department divided by the number of patients in the department over the same period, multiplied by 100%.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0. Categorical data were expressed as case numbers and percentages, with comparisons conducted using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test as appropriate. Measurement data were expressed as medians. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics statement

This study did not require ethics approval.

Results

Verification of diagnosis

Between 5 June and 31 July 2023, a total of 22 CRKP strains were detected in 14 ICU patients. Manual antimicrobial susceptibility testing was consistent with results from automated systems, indicating sensitivity only to colistin and ceftazidime/avibactam. Due to the absence of the bacterial homology(typing) analysis facility at the hospital, strain homology (typing)was inferred based on identical drug susceptibility profiles. The nosocomial CRKP infection rate in the ICU during this period was 8.1% (18/223), significantly higher than in the same period in 2021 and 2022 (0/323, 0/264) (P < 0.05). Based on these findings, the event was classified as a suspected outbreak of CRKP nosocomial infection.

Epidemiological investigations

Population distribution

A total of 22 CRKP strains were successively isolated from clinical specimens, including sputum, blood, urine, intravenous catheters, and stool, from 14 patients. Among these patients, ten were male and four were female, with a median age of 69 (Q=74.25–54.75=19.5) years. The median duration of ICU stay prior to CRKP detection was 5.5 (Q=7.25–4=3.25) days, with four patients having been in the ICU for more than seven days before detection. All 14 patients had central venous catheters (CVCs) and urinary catheters inserted, with median durations of 20 days (Q=25.25–13.25=12.00) and 17.5 (Q=25.50–10.25=15.25), respectively. Two patients did not require ventilators; one patient received non-invasive ventilation, while the remaining 11 underwent endotracheal intubation with mechanical ventilation for a median of 8.5 days (Q=15.75–2.25=13.50). Four patients underwent surgery during their hospitalisation. All had a history of bronchoscopic suctioning in the ICU and had used shared diagnostic equipment, using X-ray machines, ultrasound devices, and echocardiography equipment.

All patients had exposure to antimicrobials prior to CRKP detection. Of these, 12 received carbapenem therapy for a median of 7.5 days (Q=11.00–3.50=7.50) while two received beta lactamase inhibitor combinations treatment for more than 7 days. Among the collected samples, sputum accounted for 54.2% (13/24) of isolates, followed by blood (25%, 6/24), urine (8.3%, 2/24), intravenous catheters (8.3%, 2/24), and stool (4.2%, 1/24). One case was identified as a community-acquired infection, while the remaining 13 were classified as nosocomial infections, with the lungs as the primary site. Additionally, five patients had infections at multiple sites. Of the 14 patients, only four showed clinical improvement. Five patients died, and five discharged against medical advice after choosing to discontinue treatment (Table I).

Time distribution

The first CRKP case was identified on 5 June, with the isolate obtained from the patient's sputum. Over the subsequent 2 months, 19 additional strains were detected in samples from 13 other patients, all showing identical drug resistance profiles. The majority of CRKP detections occurred in two clusters: between 5 and 9 June and between 26 June and 17 July. The index patient was transferred from another hospital, while the second patient developed it after four days in the ICU. A clear temporal overlap in hospitalisation periods was observed among the affected patients (Figure 1).

Spatial distribution

The 14 affected patients were primarily located in two adjacent sections of the ICU, concentrated around beds 8, 11, and 12, and beds 14, 15, 17, and 18. The same cleaning staff member was assigned to both areas, and doctors and nurses frequently moved between them for consultations and procedures (Figure 2).

Spatial distribution of CRKP cases in the ICU.

Environmental hygiene monitoring

In the ICU, CRKP was detected in 2 out of 23 hand samples from doctors, yielding a detection rate of 8.7% (2/23). Additionally, 11 out of 138 surface samples tested positive for CRKP, corresponding to a detection rate of 8% (11/138). Contaminated surfaces included the X-ray machine, bed handrails (Bed 12), bedding and electric aspirator tubing (Bed 13), infusion frame bases and hooks (Bed 15), infusion frames and net pockets for infusion bottles, medical waste bin covers (Bed 3), and shared cleaning equipment.

In the Fiberscope Room, 16 bronchoscope mirrors, 19 surface samples, rinse water, disinfectants, and hand samples from medical staff were tested. No CRKP was detected in any of these samples.

At the CSSD, sterilisation records for fiberscopes used in the ICU from June to July were reviewed. Physical, chemical, and biological monitoring results all met required standards.

Comprehensive analysis of suspected CRKP nosocomial infection outbreak

A thorough review of case characteristics, epidemiological investigations, environmental hygiene surveillance, and on-site observations was conducted to identify contributing factors to the suspected CRKP outbreak. Control measures and outcomes

Upon confirmation of the first CRKP case, the Infection Prevention and Control Department immediately issued an early warning to the ICU and provided staff with training on contact isolation protocols. As additional cases emerged, the hospital's emergency response system was activated. A multidisciplinary team, comprising members from the medical, pharmacy, laboratory, thoracic surgery, and ICU departments, was convened to manage the outbreak. Based on epidemiological findings and environmental hygiene monitoring, the following interventions were implemented:

1.Strict isolation protocols: Patients with CRKP were isolated in single rooms or clearly designated areas. The number of attending physicians was minimised, and dedicated nurses were assigned to each patient. All care procedures followed standardised protocols, and the use of personal protective equipment was strictly enforced.

Strict isolation protocols: Patients with CRKP were isolated in single rooms or clearly designated areas. The number of attending physicians was minimised, and dedicated nurses were assigned to each patient. All care procedures followed standardised protocols, and the use of personal protective equipment was strictly enforced.

2.Active screening: Stool samples or rectal/anal swabs were collected from all ICU admissions to screen for CRE, enabling early detection and isolation of carriers.

3.Improved medical waste disposal: Foot-operated medical waste bins were introduced to reduce the risk of contact cross-contamination during waste disposal.

Improved medical waste disposal: Foot-operated medical waste bins were introduced to reduce the risk of contact cross-contamination during waste disposal.

(4)Enhanced hand hygiene monitoring and compliance: Regular hand hygiene assessments were conducted for all healthcare workers. Quick drying hand disinfectants were made readily available, and doctors were accompanied by staff carrying these. Random video monitoring was also used daily to evaluate compliance, with results regularly shared within the department.

Enhanced hand hygiene monitoring and compliance: Regular hand hygiene assessments were conducted for all healthcare workers. Quick drying hand disinfectants were made readily available, and doctors were accompanied by staff carrying these. (5)Strengthened cleaning and disinfection procedures: Environmental surfaces were cleaned using a chlorine-based disinfectant (1 000 mg/L) three times daily (morning, noon, and evening). High-contact surfaces were disinfected every 4 hours. Hydrogen peroxide plasma air disinfectors were used to enhance air quality in patient areas. Shared diagnostic and treatment equipment was handled by designated personnel, under a strict “one patient, one use, one disinfection, one record” policy. Terminal disinfection was carried out after patient discharge of transfer, following a top-to-bottom, clean-to-dirty, inside-outside sequence. New patients were only admitted after environmental samples tested negative for contamination. Following the implementation of these measures, no CRKP was detected in any environmental surface samples, and no new CRKP infections were reported from 1 to 31 August, confirming the effectiveness of this response.

Discussion

CRKP infections are associated with severe systemic symptoms and are notoriously difficult to treat, often resulting in high mortality rates [8]. In recent years, reports of nosocomial outbreaks caused of CRKP have become increasingly common, particularly in ICUs, where infection rates remain persistently high [5,[9], [10], [11], [12]]. Cancer patients, due to their compromised immunity, prolonged hospital stays and disease-related stress, are especially vulnerable to nosocomial infections [13].

In this case, the index CRKP strain was isolated from the sputum of a patient who transferred from another hospital. Four days after the second patient was admitted to the ICU, CRKP was detected. Over the next two months, 18 additional CRKP strains were identified in 12 other patients, all sharing identical antimicrobial resistance profiles and overlapping ICU stays. Infections were primarily localised to the lungs, urinary tract, and bloodstream. The nosocomial infection rate reached 8.7%, significantly higher than in previous years, confirming a suspected outbreak. To mitigate the risk of CRKP infection, clinical practices should prioritise reducing the use of carbapenems and beta lactamase inhibitor combinations minimising invasive procedures and limiting the duration of mechanical ventilation and hospitalisation.

Environmental hygiene monitoring revealed CRKP contamination at 11 sites, including the X-ray machine, bed handrails (Bed 12), bedding and electric aspirator pipelines (Bed 13), infusion frame bases and hooks (Bed 15), infusion frames, net pockets for infusion bottles, medical waste bin covers (Bed 3), and shared cleaning tools. Following the Criteria for the Prevention and Control of Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (WS/T826-2023) [14], the frequency of cleaning and disinfection was increased to three times daily. Diagnostic and treatment equipment were handled by dedicated personnel under strict “one person, one use, one disinfection, one record” protocols. High-contact surfaces were disinfected every four hours. Despite these efforts, on-site observations identified gaps in implementation, including limited resources, inadequate isolation facilities, and lapses in protocol adherence (e.g., hand hygiene, waste management, and cleaning practices). Notably, the first patient was not isolated in a single room, contributing to subsequent transmission. The Infection Prevention and Control Department responded by reinforcing key measures: single-room isolation, assigning dedicated nursing staff, minimising physician access, and ensuring strict adherence to aseptic techniques and isolation protocols. These actions were pivotal in controlling the spread of the CRKP outbreak.

The early identification of CRKP cases, timely patient isolation, strict implementation of infection control measures, adherence to aseptic protocols, and consistent environmental hygiene are critical to preventing nosocomial infection.

This study has several limitations. Due to the absence of a molecular laboratory, were unable to conduct moleculartyping analysis on patient specimens or conduct drug susceptibility and moleculartyping analyses on environmental samples. This limited our ability to confirm the exact source and transmission pathways of the outbreak. Additionally, while the concentration of chlorine-based disinfectant was increased to 1,000 mg/L for environmental cleaning, further research is needed to evaluate the potential risks of high disinfectant concentrations on patients and the implications of overuse.

Initial experience with a novel 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly forceps for periareolar video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery
Authors: Wei Wang, Shaowen Zhang, Ping Dong, Zhangfan Mao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeriareolar video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) provides cosmetic advantages but poses technical challenges due to limited working space. The novel 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly endoscopic forceps is designed to minimize incision size while retaining full surgical functionality. This study aimed to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and effectiveness of this novel endoscopic forceps in periareolar VATS.MethodsThis single-center retrospective observational study included patients who underwent periareolar VATS using either the 2-mm forceps or a conventional 5-mm grasper between January and December 2024. Outcomes included operative time, complications, visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores at 1 week and 1 month, patient and observer scar assessment scale (POSAS) scores at 6 months, and surgeon feedback. Patients were stratified as follows: 2-mm group (n = 78; uniportal 45, biportal 33) and a historical 5-mm control (n = 37; all uniportal) from the same team under identical indications and perioperative protocols. Because the control contained only uniportal cases, all between-group comparisons were prespecified for the uniportal subset (2 mm: 45 vs. 5 mm: 37); 2-mm biportal cases were summarized descriptively.ResultsA total of 115 periareolar VATS procedures were completed without severe complications or perioperative mortality. Instrument assembly and disassembly required 100 ± 28 s and 25 ± 11 s, respectively. The needle-type puncture sites required no suturing and showed minimal-to-virtually no scarring at follow-up. In the uniportal subset, the 2-mm group had significantly lower POSAS totals and early VAS scores than the 5-mm group, with comparable operative time and surgeon-reported difficulty. Three minor mammary gland injuries occurred overall (two in the 2-mm group and one in the 5-mm group), with no lasting sensory impairment.ConclusionsThe novel 2-mm intracorporeal forceps is safe and effective for periareolar VATS, significantly enhancing cosmetic outcomes and surgical efficiency, suggesting broad clinical application potential.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00464-025-12177-3.Keywords: Periareolar incision, Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery, Needle-type forceps, Minimally invasive surgery, Cosmetic outcomes

Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) has revolutionized thoracic surgery by significantly reducing postoperative pain, facilitating faster recovery, and enhancing cosmetic outcomes compared to conventional thoracotomy [1]. Recent innovations in instrumentation and surgical techniques have further advanced VATS, enabling reduced-port and uniportal approaches aimed at minimizing surgical trauma and optimizing esthetic outcome [2–4].

Periareolar VATS, in particular, has garnered increasing interest for its potential cosmetic superiority. By placing the incision along the areolar border, scars are effectively concealed, thereby improving patient satisfaction with the esthetic outcome [5, 6]. Despite these benefits, periareolar VATS is technically demanding due to restricted operative fields and limited working space, posing substantial challenges for surgeons.

In an effort to address these limitations, a novel 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly endoscopic forceps (hereafter referred to as the 2-mm forceps) has been developed. This innovative design requires only a 2-mm puncture site, hypothesized to further minimize tissue trauma, decrease postoperative pain, and enhance cosmetic benefits without compromising surgical functionality. Nonetheless, clinical data on its safety, feasibility, and efficacy in periareolar VATS remain limited.

Therefore, this single-center retrospective study aimed to evaluate the clinical application of the 2-mm needle-type forceps in periareolar VATS. In a historical uniportal cohort from the same surgical team, we also compared the 2-mm instrument with a conventional 5-mm grasper introduced via a 5.5-mm trocar.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical approval

All patients who underwent periareolar VATS using either the 2-mm forceps or a conventional 5-mm grasper via a 5.5-mm trocar between January and December 2024 were retrospectively reviewed. The 2-mm group included both uniportal and biportal approaches, whereas the historical 5-mm control cohort from the same surgical team consisted only of uniportal cases. The same surgical team and perioperative protocols were applied throughout the study period. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (No. 2023-1-X-67). Given the retrospective nature, informed consent for the study was waived, and all patient data were anonymized.

A total of 115 patients met these criteria, including 78 in the 2-mm group (uniportal 45 and biportal 33) and 37 in the 5-mm control group (all uniportal).

Instrument description

Structure of the novel 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly endoscopic forceps. Illustration shows the components: a 2.3-mm puncture needle, a silicone guide tube, a 2-mm diameter hollow puncture rod, and a 5-mm grasper connected by steel wire, enabling intracorporeal assembly

Instrument assembly and usage (Video 1)

The assembly process begins by inserting the 2.3-mm puncture needle into the thoracic cavity via an auxiliary incision at the sixth intercostal space under thoracoscopic guidance. The silicone guide tube is then advanced through the needle, creating a pathway for the internal grasper components. The steel wire connected to the 5-mm grasper is attached externally to the guide tube and guided out through the puncture needle. After threading the steel wire through the hollow puncture rod, the rod is reinserted into the thoracic cavity and aligned internally. Final assembly is achieved by securing the grasper onto the rod via traction on the steel wire, forming a stable operational unit.

Disassembly after surgery

At the end of the procedure, the locking mechanism is released, allowing the puncture rod to be withdrawn, detaching the grasper. The puncture needle is then removed, and the grasper is retrieved through the uniportal incision.

Surgical technique

Depending on the complexity of the planned resection and intraoperative findings, either a uniportal or a biportal periareolar approach was used:

Uniportal periareolar approach. A curvilinear incision (2–3 cm) was created along the lateral border of the areola, followed by subcutaneous tunneling to reach the targeted intercostal space. A soft-tissue retractor was then inserted to establish the surgical working channel (Fig. 2).

Biportal periareolar approach. For more complex procedures, an additional 0.5–1 cm incision was made at the seventh intercostal space along the mid-axillary line, providing enhanced instrument maneuverability and improved surgical visualization (Fig. 2).

The 2-mm needle-type forceps was placed at the sixth intercostal space along the posterior axillary line. Under direct thoracoscopic visualization, the forceps was assembled in situ according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. Once assembled, the instrument was utilized to retract lung tissue, assist in dissecting vessels and bronchi, and facilitate lymph node dissection and specimen retrieval. Assembly and disassembly durations were documented [7].

In the historical uniportal control group, a conventional 5-mm endoscopic grasper was introduced via a 5.5-mm trocar at the same posterior axillary line site; other procedural steps were identical.

Perioperative analgesia protocol. A standardized multimodal regimen was used in both cohorts: intercostal nerve blocks with 0.375% ropivacaine 20 ml plus incision infiltration with an additional 5 ml ropivacaine at closure.

Procedures were consistently performed by an experienced surgical team proficient in periareolar VATS, minimizing operator variability (Fig. 2, Video 2 demonstrating wedge resection, posterior segment resection of the right upper lobe, and group 2, 4, and 7 lymph node dissection).

Data collection

Clinical and perioperative data were obtained from electronic medical records and surgical reports, including patient demographics (age, sex, and BMI), preoperative comorbidities, operative details (approach type, use of auxiliary instruments [2 mm vs. 5 mm], operative time, blood loss, intraoperative difficulties, and conversions), postoperative outcomes (chest tube duration, hospital stay, and wound characteristics), and complications (e.g., bleeding, pneumothorax, infection, and mammary gland injury).

Postoperative pain was assessed using visual analog scale (VAS) scores at 1 week and 1 month. The periareolar incision was considered concealed and not measured; “visible scar” referred to scars outside the areola. At 6 months, two blinded nurses independently measured the longest diameter of the most conspicuous visible scar, and the mean of the two readings was analyzed (0 mm if absent). Cosmetic outcome and patient satisfaction at 6 months were assessed using the patient component of the Patient and Observer Scar Assessment Scale (POSAS). This module consists of six items (pain, itching, color, stiffness, thickness, and irregularity), each scored from 1 to 10, with a total score ranging from 6 to 60; lower scores indicate better scar quality and greater patient satisfaction. Patients completed the questionnaires during routine follow-up visits, with non-leading clarification provided by trained nurses.

Surgeon feedback regarding device assembly, clamping performance, and ergonomics was rated on a 1 (easy) to 5 (difficult) scale.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for approximately normally distributed data or as median (range) otherwise; categorical variables as counts (%). Group comparisons (restricted to the uniportal subset: 2-mm instrument versus conventional 5-mm grasper via a 5.5-mm trocar) used the independent-samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous data and the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical data, as appropriate. Two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

All 115 periareolar VATS procedures (2-mm group: 78 cases [uniportal 45, biportal 33]; 5-mm group: 37 uniportal cases) were successfully completed without major complications or perioperative mortality. Both instruments demonstrated excellent performance and adaptability across both simple procedures (bullectomy and wedge resection) and complex surgeries (segmentectomy and lobectomy), enabling secure tissue handling of lung parenchyma, lymph nodes, and mediastinal pleura without additional trauma or bleeding. The 2-mm instrument assembly and disassembly time averaged 100 ± 28 s and 25 ± 11 s, respectively.

Comparative analysis in the uniportal subset (2 mm vs. 5 mm): operative time was comparable between groups (73 ± 20 vs. 63 ± 22 min; P > 0.05), and surgeon-reported difficulty did not differ (1.7 ± 0.4 vs. 1.3 ± 0.5; P = 0.51). By contrast, the 2-mm group had significantly lower 6 month POSAS totals (8.1 ± 0.8 vs. 9.8 ± 1.2; P < 0.05) and lower postoperative pain at both 1 week (VAS 1.6 ± 0.6 vs. 2.7 ± 0.7; P < 0.05) and 1 month (0.7 ± 0.4 vs. 2.0 ± 0.7; P < 0.05).

Across all 115 cases, the mean operative time was 66 ± 25 min with minimal blood loss. Four cases initially planned as uniportal required conversion to biportal due to intraoperative difficulties, but no open conversions or reoperations were necessary. Chest tube duration averaged 2.0 ± 0.5 days, and postoperative hospital stay averaged 5 ± 2 days. Minor complications occurred in approximately 4% of patients, including three cases of transient mammary gland injury without lasting sensory impairment. Minor subcutaneous emphysema and air leaks resolved spontaneously within 2–3 days. Needle-type incisions healed without suturing, resulting in minimal or no obvious visible scarring at follow-up (Fig. 3).

Discussion

In this single-center, retrospective study, the 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly endoscopic forceps proved to be both safe and effective for periareolar VATS. Consistent with previous reports on minimally invasive thoracic approaches, our findings demonstrated the periareolar access in VATS reduced surgical trauma, low postoperative pain, and excellent cosmetic outcomes [4, 5]. Unlike conventional instruments, the 2-mm device introduces an auxiliary instrument through a needle-sized puncture that requires no sutures and leaves nearly invisible scars. More importantly, by reducing the number of tools passing through the main incision, it helps minimize intraoperative interference—a well-recognized challenge in uniportal VATS. This advantage is particularly valuable for the periareolar route, where the access in female patients must avoid the breast parenchyma.

To further validate its utility, we performed a comparative analysis with a historical cohort of 37 uniportal cases using conventional 5-mm graspers via a 5.5-mm trocar. The 2-mm group showed superior cosmetic outcomes (lower POSAS) and reduced early postoperative pain (lower VAS), with no significant differences in operative time or surgeon-rated difficulty. These data suggest that the 2-mm instrument achieves surgical functionality equivalent to a standard 5-mm grasper while substantially reducing invasiveness.

Beyond the well-recognized cosmetic benefits of uniportal VATS, the 2-mm device provides a functionally meaningful extra working channel without adding a 5.5-mm trocar. By reducing instrument crowding at the main periareolar incision, it facilitates exposure and traction in anatomically constrained cases, particularly in females where breast parenchyma must be respected. These advantages translated into better POSAS and early pain scores without prolonging operative time and enabled us to confidently extend periareolar approaches to segmentectomy and selected lobectomy cases.

One particularly appealing feature is the suture-free puncture site of the 2-mm tool, which contributes to its favorable cosmetic profile. Surgeon feedback indicated a low learning curve, minimal ergonomic burden, and efficient tissue manipulation, reflecting the tool’s ease of integration into routine practice. The observed complication rate (approximately 4%) is within or below the range reported for uniportal and biportal VATS, suggesting that this approach does not compromise patient safety. The single case of minor mammary gland injury underscores the importance of careful dissection in the periareolar region but did not result in any areolar or nipple dysfunction.

Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. This study was retrospective, single-center, and included a modest sample size, which may limit generalizability. The 6-month follow-up window may be insufficient to assess long-term cosmetic durability, chronic pain, or delayed complications. Prospective multi-center studies with larger cohorts and longer follow-up are needed to validate these findings, further define the clinical value and safety profile of this instrument, and evaluate its performance across varying procedure complexity and specific patient subgroups.

Conclusion

The 2-mm needle-type intracorporeal assembly endoscopic forceps is a safe and effective adjunct for periareolar VATS. By providing a virtually scarless auxiliary channel while preserving full operative utility, it helps overcome workspace and instrument-interference constraints inherent to the periareolar route. These advantages support broader adoption of the periareolar approach in appropriately selected patients, with prospective multi-center studies warranted to confirm long-term benefits.

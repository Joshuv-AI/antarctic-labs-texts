From tradition to innovation: the ongoing evolution of AMB express
Authors: Jesús Fernández-Lucas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSince its foundation in 2011, AMB Express has grown from a journal focused on classical applied microbiology and industrial biotechnology into a multidisciplinary platform covering microbiome research, antimicrobials, biocatalysis, and synthetic biology. In this Editorial, an overview of our journal’s evolution is provided, along with an outline of the directions in which it should develop to maintain its status as a reference journal of applied microbiology and biotechnology.Keywords: Applied microbiology, Industrial processes, Environmental microbiology, Microbiome, Antimicrobials, Synthetic biology, Therapeutics

Since its launch in 2011, AMB Express (https://link.springer.com/journal/13568) has undergone a steady evolution, broadening its scope to reflect the growing complexity and diversity of applied microbiology and biotechnology.

In its early years (2011–2015), the journal primarily reflected the traditions of applied microbiology and industrial biotechnology. Many contributions focused on microbial bioprocesses, pollutant degradation, metabolite production, and the optimization of cultivation and fermentation conditions. Classical model organisms such as Escherichia coli, Bacillus, and Pseudomonas were recurrent, and environmental biotechnology—including waste degradation and wastewater treatment—formed a substantial core of published work.

From 2016 to 2019, AMB Express embraced modern biotechnological tools and approaches. Genetic engineering strategies, CRISPR-based editing, and multi-omics studies (genomics, transcriptomics, metabolomics) became increasingly visible. During this period, the journal also expanded its coverage of red biotechnology, featuring microbial enzymes, bioactives, and metabolites with nutritional or therapeutic potential. Articles on the biosynthesis of biopolymers and innovative microbial products further underlined this broader direction.

In recent years (2020–2025), AMB Express has matured into a truly multidisciplinary platform. Publications now span microbial engineering, biocatalysis, process development, environmental and agricultural biotechnology, and biomedical applications. A notable trend has been the increasing prominence of microbiome research—human, animal, and environmental—using metagenomic and metabolomic tools to explore its impact on health and disease. At the same time, articles on antimicrobials, bacteriophages, antimicrobial resistance, and probiotics have placed the journal at the intersection of applied microbiology, translational science, and medicine.

Industrial biotechnology also remains a cornerstone. Recent studies report the engineering of yeasts and bacteria for the production of high-value compounds, the scale-up of microbial processes in pilot bioreactors, and the development of hybrid biochemical–chemical processes that embody the principles of modern white biotechnology. In parallel, food and agricultural biotechnology continues to thrive, with work on fermented foods, functional starter cultures, and animal probiotics underscoring the broad societal relevance of microbial biotechnology.

This trajectory would not have been possible without the vision and dedication of the founding editorial team, led by Professor Dr. Alexander Steinbüchel (University of Münster, Germany) as Chief Editor, together with Dr. rer. nat. Dorothea Kessler (Schriesheim, Germany) as Managing Editor, and the Associate Editors who launched the journal. Their efforts established AMB Express as a respected and dynamic journal in applied microbiology and biotechnology.

Beginning in 2025, AMB Express embarks on a new chapter under the leadership of Associate Professor Jesús Fernández Lucas, PhD (Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Spain) as Editor-in-Chief, working alongside Dr. rer. nat. Dorothea Kessler, who continues her invaluable role as Managing Editor. Together with a distinguished and globally diverse team of Associate Editors, they will guide the journal into its next era:

This expanded editorial board reflects our commitment to multidisciplinarity, cultural diversity, and global scientific dialogue within microbiology and biotechnology.

Today, AMB Express stands at the confluence of synthetic biology, microbial consortia, quorum sensing, and diagnostic and therapeutic applications of microorganisms, while remaining firmly rooted in the traditions of applied microbiology. This evolution—from tradition to frontier—strengthens our mission: to serve as an open, rigorous, and pioneering forum for the international biotechnology community. In the coming years, AMB Express also aims to strengthen its visibility through special collections that highlight emerging fields and foster community engagement. As an example, our ongoing collection on quorum sensing in microbial biotechnology (https://link.springer.com/collections/cjjcjfhheg), guest-edited by Dr. Jorge Barriuso and Dr. Juan Méndez. We warmly invite our authors, reviewers, and readers to contribute to this initiative and to continue shaping the future of AMB Express together with us.

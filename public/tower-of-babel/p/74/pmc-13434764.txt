Outdoor high-precision 3D dense mapping system based on stereo visual SLAM
Authors: Qinghua Su, Yizheng Liu, Zhihao Xie, Haihan Wang, Haoyuan Zhang, Boxiong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Traditional visual SLAM algorithms, which build maps based on sparse reconstruction, can hardly meet the demands for autonomous navigation and obstacle avoidance in outdoor environment. Therefore, we propose a visual SLAM construction algorithm to address this issue. The algorithm employs a deep stereo matching network based on a cross-attention mechanism and an adaptive disparity refinement strategy to suppress mismatched disparities, thereby enhancing the robustness of dense depth estimation in complex environments. Additionally, a separate dense mapping thread is applied to fuse the refined dense depth with SLAM poses for loop detection and pose correction, generating high-quality color depth point clouds and reconstructing high-precision 3D dense maps. To evaluate the performance of our algorithm, two experiments are conducted using the KITTI dataset and field test data, respectively. The experimental results show that our algorithm achieves a relative depth estimation error of 8.236% with the field test data, while 92.902% of the dense points in reconstruction 3D map have an error within 0.443 m. These results demonstrate that our 3D mapping algorithm achieves higher accuracy and robustness than traditional methods and solves the problem of sparse point clouds and lack of environmental information in traditional visual SLAM systems.

Introduction

Visual-based 3D reconstruction technology serves as the core supporting technique for various applications requiring deep understanding of environmental geometric structures1. Based on reconstruction accuracy and geometric completeness, this technology can be categorized into two major types: sparse reconstruction and dense reconstruction. Sparse reconstruction (e.g., Structure from Motion [SFM]2 can only recover camera internal and external parameters along with sparse feature point clouds, while dense reconstruction is capable of generating complete 3D environmental structures essential for complex downstream tasks such as augmented reality (AR) and collision-free motion planning.

Early dense reconstruction primarily relied on multi-view stereo (MVS) algorithms during the post-processing stage of sparse reconstruction3,4. The rapid advancement of deep learning technology in recent years has brought breakthrough progress to this field. Although traditional PatchMatch-based methods5,6 still perform well in scenes with rich textures, learning-based MVS networks7,8 and depth completion approaches9–11 significantly enhance reconstruction accuracy and robustness in semantically complex regions. In such tasks, learning-based models transform the traditional feature matching problem into a similarity estimation problem, thereby eliminating the need for manually designed matching rules. However, directly transferring this dense reconstruction process, typically used for offline processing, to real-time large-scale systems still faces numerous challenges.

In real-time application scenarios, Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) technology is the mainstream solution for achieving robust camera pose tracking. However, current dominant visual SLAM systems12–14 generally only construct sparse feature maps. While these maps rely on rigorous bundle adjustment to achieve extremely high localization accuracy, they lack the visual information and geometric integrity directly required for downstream tasks. Some studies attempt to simultaneously achieve dense mapping and camera localization within a tightly-coupled framework15,16, but such methods often sacrifice localization accuracy. The core reason lies in the fact that dense image alignment is highly susceptible to photometric noise interference, and the optimization demands of massive scene geometric parameters significantly constrain the system’s real-time joint optimization capability.

Based on this, compared to directly constructing dense maps using tightly coupled visual SLAM frameworks, decoupling the localization and mapping processes is a more efficient technical paradigm: leveraging sparse visual SLAM for high-precision pose estimation while employing independent depth estimation algorithms to infer dense depth information. Following this technical approach, this paper proposes a robust dense mapping system based on binocular visual SLAM. By combining the precise pose tracking capability of sparse SLAM with a decoupled dense depth inference module, the system effectively addresses the challenge between high-precision localization requirements and the geometric integrity needed for practical 3D applications.

We propose using a separate stereo matching algorithm to infer dense depth. This algorithm performs adaptive disparity optimization on the output disparity map to eliminate mismatched disparities. It combines dense depth with the poses provided by visual SLAM for real-time loop closure detection and pose correction. This loosely coupled approach enables flexible keyframe selection, adjusts the parameters of the stereo matching algorithm, and executes in real-time without interrupting the main SLAM process for constructing 3D dense maps. Methods

Our 3D dense point cloud map construction algorithm is developed based on the ORB-SLAM3 framework. It improves the traditional visual SLAM system framework, and adds a new dense mapping thread that enables the construction of a 3D dense map containing environmental information, as marked with yellow color in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1System framework.

System framework.

The system takes stereo image frames as input, and then a stereo matching algorithm based on the cross-attention mechanism is applied for computing the stereo disparity in the keyframe images, optimizing the disparity and removing mismatches. After that, it calculates the depth map using the disparity results and stereo camera parameters. Then, the system integrates the RGB image with the depth map to generate point cloud frames, which are subsequently processed through a filtering mechanism to eliminate noise, outliers, and redundant data, thereby enhancing the quality of the point cloud data and the accuracy of subsequent map construction. Finally, the system rotates and translates the point cloud frames based on globally optimized keyframe poses and merges these frames into the global map to produce a 3D dense point cloud map.

The feature-matching-based SLAM algorithm

The feature-based visual SLAM system enables robots to obtain their own state during movement. It first extracts image features, then performs localization and map construction. The algorithm first extracts and merges the feature points from different layers of a pyramid model. Then, the BRIEF algorithm is used to generate binary descriptors, which describe feature points by creating a binary string based on the neighborhood image around the feature point. In the matching stage, the Hamming distance is used to measure the similarity between feature points to achieve feature matching, and incorrect matches are excluded by minimizing errors, thus ensuring outstanding performance in terms of real-time capability and robustness. The system applies FLANN(Fast Library for Approximate Nearest Neighbors) to match features and uses RANSAC(Random Sample Consensus) to remove outliers, the result is shown in Fig. 59–11,17,17.

Fig. 5Feature point matching.

Feature point matching.

After obtaining the feature matching points from two frames of images, pose estimation and keyframe selection can be performed. Keyframe selection is typically based on a set of heuristic rules and criteria, aimed at maximizing the quality of the map while maintaining the system’s efficiency. The decision for keyframe selection is primarily made by ensuring that the time interval between adjacent keyframes is not less than a certain threshold, that there is sufficient motion, and that frames compatible with the current map and with good image quality are chosen as keyframes. Based on the matching results, the camera pose is calculated, and the depth information of the feature points is obtained by combining the camera projection model. After calculating the 3D positions of the feature points through pose transformation, the local map is generated. Finally, the local map composed of feature points is stitched into the global map.

After obtaining the initial pose of the current keyframe and local map points, Bundle Adjustment (BA) optimization can be performed, as shown in Fig. 6. The current frame and its two adjacent blue frames are co-visible frames, which need to be optimized together during subsequent optimization. The fixed frames are determined through observations of map points; they have jointly observed the map point (marked as a yellow star) along with the blue frames, but the connection with the blue frames is not very strong. Therefore, in the subsequent optimization, these fixed frames will not change their poses and only provide constraint information. By adjusting the camera pose and the spatial positions of feature points, the light rays reflected from each feature point (bundles of light rays) can converge at the camera optical center after the adjustments. This allows for the optimization of the spatial coordinates of multiple camera poses and the landmark points associated with those poses. Once the backend optimization is completed, the final pose of the current frame is obtained.

The 3D dense point cloud map construction based on adaptive disparity optimization

A 3D dense point cloud map construction algorithm based on adaptive disparity optimization is proposed in this research. The algorithm is based on the framework of traditional visual SLAM systems and incorporates a new dense mapping thread. It obtains environmental depth information through stereo matching with binocular vision and post-processing of disparity, and utilizes a Bag of Words (BoW)-based loop closure detection algorithm to optimize the global map, achieving the construction of a 3D dense map with environmental information, as shown in Fig. 1. During the construction of the dense point cloud map, it is inevitable that noise points will appear, which can affect the quality of the mapping. Additionally, the dense point cloud map requires a large amount of storage space, necessitating filtering processes to be applied to the dense point cloud map.

In image processing, a connected region refers to an area composed of adjacent pixels. These pixels often have similar characteristics (such as color, grayscale values, or disparity values), and therefore are considered to belong to the same object. Regarding noise, small, discontinuous regions may sometimes appear in an image due to noise or incorrect matching in disparity calculation. These regions are usually erroneous and need to be removed or marked as invalid. In the process of constructing large-scale environmental maps, as the amount of image data increases, errors can gradually accumulate, leading to inaccurate camera pose estimation and resulting in drift in the constructed map. To address this issue, this study employs a BoW-based image similarity detection algorithm for loop closure detection, enabling the creation of globally consistent camera trajectories and maps. This method extracts local features from images, clusters feature points into visual words, and abstracts the images into numerical vectors. By comparing the similarity between two images in terms of vector similarity, the processing speed of image handling is enhanced. Considering the constraint of temporal continuity, a caching mechanism is employed. When the system continuously detects multiple image frames in a loop, it confirms that the verification is successful. During the real-time 3D reconstruction process, the continuous movement of the camera may cause cumulative errors. Results and discussion

The performance evaluation of our proposed algorithms are evaluated using the KITTI open-source dataset and vehicle experimental dataset. The KITTI dataset18 is jointly established by the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology in Germany and the Toyota Technical Center in the United States. It is currently the largest dataset for benchmarking computer vision algorithms in autonomous driving scenarios. The vehicle experimental dataset is collected on the campus of Beijing Information Science and Technology University.

Experimental devices

The experimental vehicle in this research is shown in Fig. 9. The vehicle chassis is an Agile X BUNKER tracked vehicle, which provides robust mobility and stability for various terrains. Two HIKVISION cameras are mounted as the left and right cameras, capturing high-resolution environmental images to construct high-precision 3D map. These cameras are synchronized to ensure accurate stereo matching and depth estimation. The embedded computing system is equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU, delivering powerful parallel computing capabilities for real-time data processing. The system runs on Ubuntu 18.04 operating system with ROS Melodic framework.

Fig. 9Experimental vehicle.

Experimental vehicle.

Additionally, some other sensors are used for capturing the ground truth data, including a Robosense RS32 LiDAR, an ASENSING INS570D IMU, and a LIVOX HAP (TX) LiDAR. The Robosense RS32 LiDAR collects precise 3D spatial depth information with a 360-degree field of view, whereas the ASENSING INS570D IMU provides data on vehicle position and orientation. The data from these sensors enables the creation of a ground truth 3D map, which is used to evaluate the accuracy of the mapping system. The LIVOX HAP (TX) LiDAR captures dense depth data, which serves as a reference to assess the depth estimation accuracy of the stereo cameras. Together, these sensors form a comprehensive multi-modal perception system, ensuring high accuracy and reliability in 3D mapping and depth estimation tasks.

Performance evaluation of stereo matching model

This study uses a pre-trained stereo matching model to improve feature representation ability and enhance the network’s generalization performance. In addition, the proposed method supports any image resolution without fixed resolution constraints and can flexibly adapt to input images of different sizes.

The evaluation metrics for stereo matching algorithms are divided into qualitative and quantitative assessments. The qualitative evaluation describes the accuracy of the algorithm by comparing the results of disparity maps, while the quantitative evaluation metrics describe the accuracy using the mismatch rate.

This is because traditional operators cannot obtain enough precise support points from the images, leading to a decline in subsequent matching performance. Although CER-Stereo network20 adopts a cascaded recurrent refinement framework with adaptive correlation to improve the quality of matching, its feature aggregation remains mainly local. As a result, the model still struggles to produce reliable correspondence in challenging regions such as low-texture surfaces, distant objects, or thin structures, leading to decreased disparity accuracy and noticeable structural loss in these areas, as shown in Figs. 10(c) and Fig. 11(c). In contrast, the deep learning-based RAFT-Stereo algorithm significantly improves the detail in stereo matching but also has limitations. The algorithm operates within a predefined disparity range, and for extreme disparities (e.g., very close or very distant objects), it is necessary to adjust the disparity range and retrain the model. As shown in the red box in Figs. 10(d) and Fig. 11(d), the recognition accuracy of bicycles, vehicles, and bushes remains low, limiting its performance when processing high-resolution images or scenes with large variations.

Our stereo matching method based on the cross-attention mechanism can automatically extract multi-level and more robust image features through end-to-end learning. It is capable of adapting to more complex environments, such as textureless regions, occlusion, and varying illumination conditions. Compared with RAFT-Stereo, our approach performs matching through global contextual information rather than relying solely on local cues. Additionally, the cross-attention mechanism enables direct information exchange between stereo images, enhancing feature quality. Furthermore, the global information interaction facilitated by cross-attention allows the model to handle complex scenarios, such as large disparities or occluded regions, as shown in Figs. 10(e) and Fig. 11(e).

In the comparative experiment, all stereo matching algorithms are tested under a unified experimental setting. For deep learning methods, their pre-trained models are used for inference testing. All algorithms use the same input image resolution (1240*375, only keep the image of the road area for reducing computation load), uniform disparity range (within 20 m), and consistent post-processing strategy. The data scenes used in the experiment are static closed scenes with less dynamic target interference. Therefore, the experimental evaluation mainly focuses on the performance differences of different algorithms in depth estimation accuracy and structural recovery ability, and does not involve additional dynamic target area removal processing. In the evaluation process of the KITTI dataset, all methods use the same evaluation mask and error statistics to avoid the impact of differences in evaluation regions on experimental results. In addition, the average time consumption of single frame point cloud stitching and dense map updating is calculated to verify the efficiency of the system. Therefore, this method meets the requirements for stable dense 3D reconstruction in complex dynamic scenes while ensuring the geometric consistency and structural integrity of the dense map.

The performance of the 3D dense point cloud mapping algorithm is validated using vehicle test datasets. To establish a reliable reference benchmark, the LIO-SAM algorithm is first employed to generate a high-precision LiDAR point cloud ground truth map using Robosense RS32 LiDAR data and IMU data. In this process, time synchronization is achieved via the Precision Time Protocol (PTP): the computer on vehicle receives absolute satellite time from the GPS module and unifies sensor timestamps through the Ethernet interface, ensuring hardware-level synchronization. Spatial alignment is accomplished through joint extrinsic calibration of the LiDAR and IMU to obtain rotation and translation matrices, unifying the coordinate systems of multi-source data. For effective region selection, the algorithm leverages distance information from LiDAR beams and a ring filtering strategy to extract feature points, retaining only local regions with stable geometric characteristics for registration. Regarding dynamic object and occlusion handling, the system inherits the back-end optimization architecture of LIO-SAM, filtering out data interference caused by dynamic obstacles through an outlier rejection mechanism in scan-to-map matching and pose consistency constraints based on factor graph optimization, thereby generating a global map.

Subsequently, the CloudCompare tool is used for accuracy evaluation through a systematic registration and distance measurement process. The evaluation process consists of two stages of alignment. First, rough alignment is performed by manually selecting three pairs of corresponding points in the ground truth map and the reconstructed dense map, respectively, as shown in Fig. 16. Then, the iterative closest point (ICP) algorithm is applied for fine registration, ensuring optimal spatial overlap by minimizing the distance between the two point clouds. The final point cloud error is quantified using the cloud-to-cloud (C2C) absolute distance metric. Specifically, the error is defined as the Euclidean distance from each point in the reconstructed map to its nearest neighbor in the ground truth map.

Conclusion

This article proposes a 3D dense map construction algorithm based on stereo vision, aiming to address the limitations of traditional visual SLAM systems in outdoor environments. Evaluated on the selected datasets, the system achieves a relative depth estimation error of 8.236%, with 92.902% of the dense points in the reconstructed 3D map having an error within 0.443 m, demonstrating a measurable improvement over the evaluated baseline systems. The key characteristics of this system are summarized as follows: (1) Cost-effectiveness: The system relies solely on stereo cameras, providing a lower-cost alternative to LiDAR-based solutions. (2) Applicability in the outdoor environment: The system integrates a deep learning model based on a cross-attention mechanism for depth prediction and employs an adaptive disparity optimization algorithm to support the 3D mapping process. (3) Dense point cloud generation: A separate dense mapping thread is applied to fuse the refined dense depth with SLAM poses for loop detection and pose correction, generating color point clouds and 3D dense maps. Consequently, this system mitigates the issue of sparse point cloud maps and the lack of sufficient environmental information typical of traditional visual SLAM systems, indicating its potential for future applications in fields such as autonomous driving scene understanding and VR. However, the system still has certain limitations. The current system may still suffer from matching errors and reconstruction inaccuracies when handling dynamic objects, vegetation regions, and reflective surfaces, thereby affecting the accuracy and consistency of dense depth estimation and 3D reconstruction. In future work, dynamic object awareness and semantic-guided optimization methods will also be integrated to further improve the system’s robustness and map reconstruction accuracy in complex scenes.

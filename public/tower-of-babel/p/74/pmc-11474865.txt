Authors: Maria Ebbesen Sørum, Anne Ortved Gang, Dorte Maegaard Tholstrup, Sif Gudbrandsdottir, Hannelouise Kissow, Brian Kornblit, Klaus Müller, Filip K Knop
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCancer treatment with high-dose chemotherapy damages the mucosal barrier of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract and is associated with severe toxicity involving mucositis, severe inflammation and organ dysfunction. Currently, there is no effective prophylaxis against this. Glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1), a well-known regulator of blood glucose, has been suggested in mouse studies to possess trophic effects on gut epithelial cells as well as anti-inflammatory properties. In line with this, endogenous GLP-1 levels have been shown to be inversely correlated with toxicities after haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) and treatment with a GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA) was shown to limit chemotherapy-induced mucositis in rodents. This present study investigates the effects of the GLP-1RA semaglutide on GI mucositis severity score in patients with lymphoma undergoing high-dose chemotherapy followed by autologous (auto) HSCT.Methods and analysisThis is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-centre investigator-initiated clinical study. Forty adult patients with malignant lymphoma referred for auto-HSCT will be randomised in a 1:1 manner to receive either semaglutide or placebo once-weekly for 8 weeks. This includes a run-in period of 3–4 weeks with semaglutide 0.25 mg prior to high-dose chemotherapy treatment followed by a period of 4–5 weeks with semaglutide 0.5 mg including the 1 week of high-dose chemotherapy treatment. Clinical assessment of endpoint measurements and safety will be performed weekly during treatment and in a follow-up period of 10 weeks. The primary endpoint is GI mucositis severity (mean severity grade (0–II) during week 1–4 after auto-HSCT). Secondary endpoints include C-reactive protein increment, quality of life and safety. Fever, bacteraemia, antibiotic use, weight loss, morphine consumption, duration of hospitalisation, use of parenteral nutrition, change in muscle mass and clinical and laboratory evidence of organ toxicities will also be assessed.Ethics and disseminationThe study complies with Danish and European Union legislation and is approved by the Danish Medicines Agency, the Danish National Medical Research Ethics Committee (EU CT #2022-502139-20-00) and the Danish Data Protection Agency. The study is monitored by the Capital Region of Denmark’s good clinical practice unit. All results, positive, negative and inconclusive, will be disseminated at national and international scientific meetings and in peer-reviewed scientific journals.Trial registration number NCT06449625

Introduction

Background and rationale

Chemotherapy is central in the treatment of most patients with cancer. Despite increasing survival rates, this treatment comes with the cost of toxic side effects. Mucositis is reported in more than 90% of individuals treated with high-dose chemotherapy and is initiated within the first week after start of treatment. Symptoms include painful oral ulcerations, abdominal cramping, bloating, diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea and gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding.13 Chemotherapy-induced malnutrition, dehydration and weight loss often necessitates parenteral nutrition and prolonged hospitalisation.46 In addition, mucositis plays a key role in the induction of extra-intestinal systemic side effects.710 The damaging effects to the GI mucosa may cause bacterial translocation with infections and systemic inflammation propagating organ toxicities (eg, liver toxicity).7 8 11 Maximum circulating level of the inflammatory marker C-reactive protein (CRP) is reached about 14 days after initiation of chemotherapy, and is found to be associated with GI mucositis severity and overall toxicity.1216 Overall, GI mucositis significantly increases morbidity in cancer patients and contributes to poor quality of life (QoL), late effects and rising healthcare costs.1719

So far, no effective prophylaxis has been developed, and the current treatment is only symptomatic, limited to parenteral hydration and nutrition, pain relief by morphine and extensive use of broad-spectrum antibiotics. However, studies have directed attention to growth factors protecting the mucosal surfaces.2022 The gut-derived peptide hormone glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) is secreted from enteroendocrine cells in response to food intake and plays a major role in regulating blood glucose, and, currently, the GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA) drug class constitutes a pillar in the treatment of type 2 diabetes.23 More recently, GLP-1 has been shown to have a much wider range of activities including trophic effects on the small and large intestines.2426 GLP-1 appears to stabilise the integrity of the intestinal epithelial barrier and protect the intestinal mucosa from injury caused by chemotherapy or lipopolysaccharide injections.2430 Additionally, GLP-1 acts as a direct anti-inflammatory mediator locally in the gut29 31 and has also been shown to induce secretion of antimicrobial peptides from Paneth cells32 and stimulate production of the barrier-protective mucus layer.33 In rodents, chemotherapy induces an increase in plasma levels of GLP-127 and a similar finding has been seen in patients receiving high-dose chemotherapy before autologous (auto) HSCT correlating with the systemic inflammatory response.34 In children undergoing allogeneic HSCT, reduced plasma levels of GLP-1 were associated with increased systemic inflammation and increased risk of acute graft-versus-host disease.35 In line with this, GLP-1RA treatment during chemotherapy in mice led to reduction in severity of mucositis and accelerated healing of mucosa,24 27 30 while treatment of patients with short-bowel syndrome with GLP-1RA resulted in reduction of diarrhoea and need for parenteral nutrition.3638

Overall, these findings indicate that GLP-1 plays a protective physiologic role by facilitating healing in response to tissue trauma. Consequently, GLP-1RA, when administrated in supraphysiological doses, may enhance the natural protective response, thereby ameliorating the harmful effects of chemotherapy to the GI tract potentially protecting against the downstream effects of this damage.

Semaglutide is a long-acting GLP-1RA that mimics the effects of native GLP-1. It is approved for treatment of type 2 diabetes and obesity, respectively, and has proven to be well tolerated in large patient populations.39 40

Hypothesis

We hypothesise that semaglutide treatment will reduce GI mucositis, systemic inflammation, and overall morbidity in patients undergoing high-dose chemotherapy.

Objectives and endpoints

This study is designed to evaluate the effect and safety of semaglutide in reducing GI mucositis in patients undergoing auto-HSCT. The primary endpoint is the individual’s mean severity grade score of GI mucositis during the 3 weeks of early toxic-aplastic posttransplant period. GI mucositis severity grade will be scored weekly by a trained physician or study nurse by summation of the scores for abdominal pain and diarrhoea according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) 5.0 criteria, as follows: grade 0 (0–1 points), grade I (2–3 points) and grade II (≥4points). Trial design

This is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-centre investigator-initiated clinical study.

Participants will be recruited from the outpatient clinics at the Department of Haematology, Copenhagen University Hospital Rigshospitalet and Zealand University Hospital, Denmark when referred to auto-HSCT. Potential study participants will receive spoken and written study information by the treating physician at the prescheduled pre-BEAM information meeting, before obtaining informed consent.

Background standard treatment of study participants

Patients with DLBCL are referred for auto-HSCT as secondl-ine treatment (first relapse). Patients with follicular lymphoma are referred for auto-HSCT after first or second relapse or transformation into DLBCL. The included participants will, during the study period, follow the current standard protocols with BEAM and auto-HSCT, which will not deviate from the eligible patients not included in the study.

The BEAM protocol includes a conditioning therapy, initiated 7 days prior to stem-cell infusion (study week 4), consisting of rituximab 375 mg/m2, carmustine 300 mg/m2, etoposide 800 mg/m2, cytarabine 800–1600 mg/m2 and melphalan 140 mg/m2. Dosages may vary according to the specific condition of the patient’s disease and comorbidity. Before entering the study, participants have been treated with an induction regimen with the multidrug-combination to be brought into clinical remission: rituximab–ifosfamide–carboplatin–etoposide x three times or rituximab–dexamethasone–cytarabine–cisplatin with 3 weeks interval, followed by stem-cell mobilisation with granulocyte colony-stimulating factor before harvesting.

Randomisation

Randomisation will be performed using the system Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) (Vanderbilt University, Nashville, Tennessee, USA). First an allocation table with randomisation codes will be generated through following website: https://www.sealedenvelope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists and mapping file stored securely by a study-independent unblinded person with no other involvement in the study. The allocation table will be uploaded to RedCap. Group allocation is concealed to patients as well as investigators. Study drug DUN will be assigned through the total DUN list, received by Novo Nordisk A/S, by the unblinded study-independent person.

Intervention

In total, 40 patients referred to auto-HSCT will be randomised 1:1 to receive either semaglutide once-weekly (QW) or placebo. After inclusion and randomisation, a dose-escalation period will be initiated 4 weeks prior to start of conditioning with BEAM, at 0.25 mg semaglutide or placebo QW. Dosage will be escalated to 0.5 mg after 3 weeks if tolerable. Elsewise, 0.25 mg will be given for one more week before escalation to 0.5 mg (figure 1). If dose escalation is not possible after 4 weeks at 0.25 mg dosage, the participant will be invited to continue on 0.25 mg for the entire treatment period of 8 weeks in total. Decision of dose escalation time point will be made by the attending physician together with the investigator according to the guidelines outlined in box 3.

Duration of study participation

The duration of treatment with the investigational drug for the individual patient will be 8 weeks. Total study duration for the individual patients will be 20–22 weeks, including a 2–4 week screening period and 10 weeks of follow-up.

Patient and public involvement

For the development of the questionnaire ‘EORTC QLQ-HDC29’, which is used as secondary endpoint for estimating QoL in the present study, patients undergoing treatment with auto-HSCT were involved in the design, selection of the questions including the phrasing. Hence, this endpoint reflects the priorities, experiences and challenges of the patients that will be included in the present study.

Procedures and visits

Each participant will attend regular visits throughout the study period, which include at least two visits during the study drug dose-escalating period, and weekly visits during the rest of the treatment period, including the phase with BEAM and auto-HSCT treatment, and then additional four visits during the 10 weeks follow-up period to evaluate adverse effects, adherence to study medication, testing with standard blood sampling and clinical examination including GI mucositis scoring (online supplemental table 1). In addition, all participants will be evaluated on the following questionnaires: QoL Scale EORTC QLQ-C30 and EORTC QLQ-HDC29 for a total of three times. For endpoint assessment, baseline is defined as the start day of high-dose chemotherapy treatment (BEAM) that is, study week 4.

Women with childbearing potential will have a pregnancy test (ie, beta-human chorionic gonadotropin) performed before inclusion or if any suspicion of pregnancy arises during the study.

Data will be stored in a digital case report form using REDCap. In case of emergencies, unblinding will be made on an individual basis and will not affect other participants.

If a participant gives informed consent to participate in future ancillary studies, extra material (plasma) will be stored in a biobank for possible further analyses. In this case, new approval by the regional health research ethics committee is required.

The study is expected to be initiated in August 2024 and last patient last visit is expected in the second quarter of 2027.

Study drug

The study drug including the placebo product will be supplied by Novo Nordisk A/S (Bagsværd, Denmark). Semaglutide 1.34 mg/mL and semaglutide placebo are visually identical in order to maintain the blinding. In order to blind the product, a clinic variant of the marketed product is supplied. The clinic pen can be found in one variant to support 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mg doses in a 1.5 mL variant.

The participants will be instructed in the subcutaneous injection technique, which can be performed at home or by a trained study nurse during the weekly visit in the outpatient clinic or during admission to hospital.

Drug ordering and storage

Semaglutide 1.34 mg/mL injection pens and semaglutide placebo injection pens will be delivered in separate boxes and will be stored according to storage conditions on the label: in a refrigerator at 2°C–8°C. The cap will be kept on the pen in order to protect from light. Pens that are in use can be kept at temperatures 2°C–30°C for 8 weeks. Participants will be instructed to keep the pens away from direct sunlight and to refrain using the pens if the injection fluid is unclear or coloured.

Drug accountability

One investigator at each study site will be responsible for drug accountability. For each participant, the batch number of pens and containers will be documented. Participants will be asked to return pens and containers after use. After verification of drug accountability, proper destruction of pens, containers and capsules will be ensured. During the study, the participants will be informed to register drug use weekly in order to document administration of drugs and to ensure compliance.

Sample size

We aim to be able to detect a difference of 0.4 in GI mucositis severity grade (graded 0–2). To detect a difference of 0.4 in GI mucositis severity grade (graded 0–2) between the two study arms, using a power of 80%, a 5% significance level, and a SD of 0.3641, randomisation of 32 patients is needed. Due to an expected drop-out rate of up to 20%, we will include a total of 40 patients. Assuming an inclusion success of 60% and a BEAM and auto-HSCT activity of 30 patients yearly at Rigshospitalet and Zealand University Hospital, it appears realistic to complete recruitment within 28 months.

Data analysis

The per protocol population will consist of all participants who completed the study with a documented valid baseline and end of treatment assessment of the primary endpoint that is, mucositis severity score and have been considered compliant to the study drug (<2 missed doses).

In the intention-to-treat population, none of the participants are excluded, and the participants are analysed according to the randomisation group.

Mucositis as the primary endpoint will be evaluated as mean GI mucositis severity grade from study week 5 to week 8 for the individual participant. CRP will be analysed as area under the plasma concentration-time curve (AUC) from study week 5 to week 8 (the early post-transplant phase). The CRPAUC will be calculated using standard non-compartmental methods, by means of the linear trapezoidal method on the observed concentrations, using actual sampling timepoints. QoL will be evaluated as the difference of the score of the questionnaires between the baseline (study week 4) and the specific assessment day (weeks 9 and 18). Comparisons of mucositis grade, CRPAUC and QoL between the two study arms will be assessed with a student’s t-test. The CRPAUC values will be log-transformed and estimated differences in CRP are presented on the original scale with 95% CIs.

The additional continuous data for the explorative endpoints will be analysed using linear mixed models with study week, treatment and their interaction as fixed factors and a random intercept on the patient level to compare changes in explorative endpoints between the two study arms.

The day+100 overall mortality is defined as the time from stem-cell infusion (study week 5) to death of any cause. Day+100 treatment-related mortality is defined as the time from stem-cell reinfusion to death without relapse. The day+100 overall mortality and treatment-related mortality will be evaluated using a Cox regression model and a cause-specific Cox regression model, respectively. A two-tailed p value<0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Side effects and disadvantages for participants

The risk of side effects when participating in this study is expected to be modest. Semaglutide is authorised for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity with and without type 2 diabetes by the European Medicines Agency and the US Food and Drug Administration, and prior studies have shown limited side effects. The most common side effects are diarrhoea and nausea, which are seen in less than 10% treated with semaglutide 0.5 mg.4248 These side effects are usually seen within the first 3 weeks after initiation of treatment; importantly, they appear to be temporary and can be significantly minimised by gradual dose titration.4248 Therefore, we plan to start low-dose treatment with the study drug and increase the dosage to 0.5 mg 1 week before start of high-dose chemotherapy treatment as indicated in figure 1, where the participants are not exposed to any additional nausea-inducing substances.

The summary of product characteristics for Ozempic (semaglutide) will be used as a reference to evaluate if any side effect was expected or not.49 Adverse events including serious adverse events will be documented and reported according to Danish and European Union legislation. The maximum amount of blood collected during the entire study will not exceed 100 mL for each study participant and will be drawn from the patients’ central venous catheter at already scheduled time points for monitoring according to background treatment and with minimal risk of infection.

Safety considerations and risk mitigation

The nonclinical safety programme of semaglutide has not revealed any safety issues precluding use in humans. The investigators of this study are not aware of any data suggesting that the patient population of this study has increased sensitivity to the side effects of semaglutide. We plan a strict safety plan to handle any weight loss effects and other possible risks related to participation by this study population including (1) initiation of study drug treatment 4 weeks prior to start of high-dose chemotherapy (run-in period) to minimise risk of persisting GI side effects from the study drug at time where chemotherapy is initiated, (2) maintenance dose of maximum 0.5 mg and (3) individual stopping rules (including weight loss), assessed, especially, 3 weeks after study drug initiation and 1 week before start of high-dose chemotherapy treatment (the run-in period) in order to judge if continuation and/or dose escalation of study drug is safe (box 3).

Ethics and dissemination

We expect that this study will be the first step towards outlining and implementation of a novel prophylactic strategy in an area with a huge unmet medical need by the use of a drug that has proven to be well tolerated in large patient populations.39 40 This is potentially a life-sparring strategy since chemotherapy-induced toxicity is an important cause of non-relapse mortality in cancer patients. Further to this, better control of the toxic events may also allow for more vulnerable patient with malignant lymphoma being eligible to receive this life-saving treatment with auto-HSCT.

We expect benefits for the study participants receiving the active study drug regarding reduction in mucositis severity in terms of reduction in abdominal cramping, bloating, diarrhoea, vomiting and GI bleeding thereby lowering the risk of malnutrition, dehydration, need of parental nutrition and severe systemic inflammation and infections potentially reducing length of hospitalisation and improve QoL for the patients. The investigators are confident that the possible risks and side effects for the participating subjects are outweighed by the expected benefits from the conduct of this study.

All study-related visits will be carried out only when patients are at the hospital department for treatment and/or assessment of their background disease.

The participants included in the study will not receive any financial compensation. Neither the sponsor–investigator nor any of the other members of the project group has financial interest in neither the conduct nor the results of the study. All participants will be covered by a patient-insurance, according to national requirements and common conduct, during the conduction of the study.

Outcomes from the study will be presented in one or more manuscripts for publication in accordance with the CONSORT 2010 statement in international, scientific, peer-reviewed journals. Inconclusive, negative and positive results will be published and presented in accordance with Danish law concerning processing of personal data. All authors must fulfil criteria for authorship according to International Committee of Medical Journal Editors. All data from the study will be owned by the investigators. The results from the study will moreover be presented at national and/or international conferences.

Protocol, study approval and registration

The Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines were used for writing this protocol.50 The PROTECT study is approved by the Danish Medicines Agency, the Danish National Medical Research Ethics Committee and the Danish Data Protection Agency and registered at the Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS) (#2022-502139-20-00) and at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06449625). The study will be conducted in accordance with good clinical practice as outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonisation-Good Clinical Practise (ICH-GCP) guidelines and monitored by the Capital Region of Denmark’s Good Clinical Practice Unit.

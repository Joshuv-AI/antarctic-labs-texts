Remdesivir and All-Cause Graft Loss Among Kidney Transplant Recipients With Symptomatic COVID-19
Authors: Karan Srisurapanont, Kasama Manothummetha, Nirada Siriyakorn, Mary G. Bowring, Lucy X. Li, Willa V. Cochran, Cory A. Schulz, Sean Ellis, Kanin Thammavaranucupt, Daniel C. Brennan, Robin K. Avery, William A. Werbel, Nitipong Permpalung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cohort study emulating a target trial estimates risk of 1-year all-cause graft loss in adult kidney transplant recipients with symptomatic COVID-19 who received remdesivir within 7 days of diagnosis vs those who did not.

Introduction

Kidney transplant (KT) recipients are at high risk of severe COVID-19 due to chronic immunosuppression, impaired vaccine responses, and comorbidities.1,2,3,4,5 Mortality in hospitalized KT recipients during the early pandemic ranged from 20% to 32%,2,6,7 declining to 1% to 4% during the Omicron wave, likely due to vaccination, prior infection, and improved treatment.8,9,10,11,12 Beyond mortality, KT recipients with COVID-19 experienced high rates of intensive care unit admission, mechanical ventilation, acute kidney injury, and graft loss.2,13 Longitudinal cohorts of KT recipients also demonstrated that 1-year post–COVID-19 cardiovascular events (CVEs) occurred in 8.7% of KT recipients, and long COVID occurred in 34% to 52%.14,15,16 These findings highlight the need for effective strategies that mitigate both acute and long-term sequelae of COVID-19 in this high-risk population.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guidelines recommend early antiviral therapy for patients at high risk of COVID-19 progression.17,18 However, pivotal randomized clinical trials largely excluded patients with severe kidney impairment and transplant recipients,19,20,21 limiting the generalizability to KT recipients. Remdesivir use in KT recipients has raised concerns regarding nephrotoxic effects and drug-drug interactions with immunosuppressive agents such as tacrolimus.22,23 Although observational data and the REDPINE trial have provided reassurance regarding kidney safety in individuals with kidney insufficiency,24,25,26,27 studies in KT recipients remain limited by small sample sizes and heterogenous results. To address these gaps, we emulated a target trial to estimate the causal effect of early remdesivir treatment. This framework reduces selection and immortal time bias by aligning eligibility, treatment assignment, and follow-up with a hypothetical randomized trial.28

Methods

In this cohort study, we emulated a target trial using retrospective observational data to compare clinical outcomes among adult KT recipients with symptomatic COVID-19 who received early remdesivir therapy vs those who did not. The study was reported in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of Observational Studies Emulating a Target Trial (TARGET) statement,29 and the target trial specification is summarized in eTable 1 in Supplement 1. The Johns Hopkins University institutional review board approved the study and waived the requirement for informed consent because the study involved secondary use of existing clinical data, posed minimal risk to participants, and could not practicably be conducted without the waiver.

Study Populations, Settings, and Exposure

Data were obtained from 5 hospitals within the Johns Hopkins Health System. Eligible participants were adult KT recipients (aged ≥18 years) diagnosed with symptomatic COVID-19 from March 2020 through January 2024, confirmed by polymerase chain reaction and/or antigen tests. Patients with graft failure before the COVID-19 diagnosis were excluded. Patients who received anti–SARS-CoV-2 monoclonal antibodies, nirmatrelvir-ritonavir, and/or molnupiravir were excluded to preserve the prespecified contrast of early remdesivir vs no remdesivir and avoid comparator contamination. Because use of nirmatrelvir-ritonavir was uncommon in this cohort and influenced by calcineurin inhibitor or mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor interactions, these patients were not analyzed as a separate treatment strategy. Their characteristics and outcomes are summarized in eTable 2 in Supplement 1. Early remdesivir was defined as treatment initiation within 7 days of the first positive SARS-CoV-2 test, with at least 3 consecutive days of therapy; the comparator was no remdesivir. If there were multiple COVID-19 episodes, the first would be selected, although no patients had more than 1 COVID-19 episode within 1 year in this cohort. Follow-up began at diagnosis and continued until death, loss to follow-up, or 1 year.

Outcomes

The primary estimand was the impact of per-protocol early remdesivir initiation within the grace period (defined as within 7 days after COVID-19 diagnosis) vs no remdesivir during the grace period. The primary outcome was all-cause graft loss (ACGL), a composite of graft failure and all-cause mortality. Secondary outcomes included all-cause mortality, CVEs, and long COVID. Outcomes were analyzed as time to first event from the date of COVID-19 diagnosis. Definitions of CVEs and long COVID are provided in eMethods 1 in Supplement 1. Outcomes were manually extracted from medical records and independently adjudicated by 2 clinicians (K.M., N.P.).

Covariables

Baseline covariables included age at diagnosis, sex, body mass index (BMI; calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared), donor type (living or deceased), recent kidney transplant within 1 year of COVID-19 diagnosis, COVID-19 vaccination status, pandemic wave, supplemental oxygen requirement at diagnosis, antimetabolite discontinuation at diagnosis, acute kidney injury at diagnosis, diabetes, hypertension, and coronary artery disease (eTable 3 in Supplement 1). Pandemic waves were divided into 3 eras: pre-Delta (until July 1, 2021), Delta (July 2 to December 15, 2021), and Omicron (after December 15, 2021) based on the sequencing data of circulating SARS-CoV-2 variants at Johns Hopkins Hospital. Only fully observed baseline covariables with nonzero variance were included in the propensity score model. Time-varying covariables reflecting evolving disease severity included hospitalization, receipt of high-flow nasal cannula oxygen, mechanical ventilation, and convalescent plasma administration (eTable 3 in Supplement 1). Time-varying covariables were updated over follow-up using the recorded first day of exposure to the respective covariable.

Statistical Analysis

To reduce potential immortal time bias and better emulate treatment randomization at a specified time 0, we used a clone-censor-weight (CCW) approach. At time 0, defined as the date of COVID-19 diagnosis, all participants were eligible for both treatment strategies: initiation of remdesivir within 7 days of COVID-19 diagnosis or no remdesivir. Each patient was then cloned into 2 treatment strategies, allowing both strategies to be evaluated from the same starting point. Each clone was followed only while the observed treatment course remained consistent with the assigned strategy and was artificially censored once that strategy was no longer possible. In this way, the CCW approach aligned eligibility, treatment assignment, and follow-up at diagnosis, avoiding pretreatment person-time from being incorrectly attributed to the treatment.

Stabilized inverse probability of treatment weights (IPTWs) were derived from a logistic regression propensity score model including 13 prespecified baseline covariables. Estimated probabilities were clipped at 0.01 and 0.99, and stabilized weights were truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles and capped at 25. Covariable balance was assessed using absolute standardized mean differences (SMDs), with values less than 0.10 indicating adequate balance. Stabilized inverse probability of censoring weights (IPCWs) were estimated using pooled logistic regression on a person-day–expanded dataset. The IPCW denominator model included discrete time indicators through day 30, treatment strategy, and time-varying covariates, whereas the numerator model included time and treatment strategy only; IPCWs were capped at the 99th percentile. Discrete time indicators in the IPCW denominator model were restricted to 30 days after COVID-19 diagnosis because the IPCW was intended to model informative artificial censoring and time-varying adherence to the assigned treatment strategy during the acute illness and treatment decision period. This 30-day window was selected to capture early clinical changes and COVID-19–directed treatment decisions that could influence treatment strategy adherence.

In this CCW framework, IPTWs addressed baseline confounding by indication by balancing prespecified covariables between treatment strategies, whereas IPCWs addressed informative artificial censoring introduced when clones deviated from their assigned strategies and time-varying confounding factors that could influence adherence to the assigned treatment strategy. Final weights were the product of stabilized IPTWs and IPCWs (eMethods 2 in Supplement 1).

Weighted Cox proportional hazards marginal structural regression models were fit with treatment strategy as the sole covariable, follow-up censored at 1 year, and robust sandwich variance estimators clustered by patients to account for within-clone correlation.30 E-values, the minimum strength of association that an unmeasured confounder would need to fully explain away the observed association between exposure and outcome, were calculated for point estimates and the 95% CI limit closest to the null. To estimate the hazard ratios (HRs) of the outcomes under each treatment strategy, weighted cumulative incidence curves were generated by the generalized computational algorithm formula (g-computation) from the fitted weighted Cox proportional hazards marginal structural regression models,30 with cumulative incidence defined as 1 minus the g-computed survival function under each treatment strategy.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses included shortening the grace period to within 5 days of the first positive SARS-CoV-2 test and widening IPTW truncation to the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles to further limit the influence of extreme weights, which can inflate variance and destabilize weighted estimates. Prespecified subgroup analyses of ACGL were conducted by repeating the CCW procedure within each subgroup stratum, with stabilized IPTWs and IPCWs, followed by weighted Cox proportional hazards marginal structural regression models with robust SEs. Strata with fewer than 30 patients or fewer than 10 outcome events were summarized descriptively without HR estimation. Statistical significance was determined using 2-sided 95% CIs. HRs with 95% CIs that did not include 1.00 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R, version 4.5.2, via RStudio, version 2026.01.0 (R Project for Statistical Computing).

Results

Patient Characteristics

This study included 432 adult KT recipients with symptomatic COVID-19 (median age, 57 years [IQR, 46-66 years]; 184 [42.6%] were female and 248 [57.4%] were male), of whom 177 (41.0%) were assigned to the early remdesivir strategy and 255 (59.0%) to the no remdesivir strategy (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics of the overall cohort are summarized in the Table. The median BMI was 28.7 (IQR, 24.4-33.4). Most recipients had undergone deceased-donor kidney transplant (273 [63.2%]), and 109 (25.2%) had received the transplant within 1 year prior to COVID-19 diagnosis. Regarding COVID-19 vaccination at the time of diagnosis, 178 patients (41.2%) had not received vaccine, 89 (20.6%) had received 1 or 2 doses, and 165 (38.2%) had received 3 or more doses. Most infections occurred during the Omicron wave (260 [60.2%]), with the remainder distributed across the pre-Delta (145 [33.6%]) and Delta (27 [6.2%]) periods. At the time of COVID-19 diagnosis, 88 patients (20.4%) required supplemental oxygen, 254 (58.8%) had antimetabolite discontinuation, and 25 (5.8%) had acute kidney injury. Common comorbidities included hypertension (413 [95.6%]), diabetes (199 [46.1%]), and coronary artery disease (63 [14.6%]).

Marked baseline differences were observed between treatment groups prior to weighting (Table and eFigure 1 in Supplement 1). Patients in the early remdesivir group were older (median age, 61 years [IQR, 51-71 years] vs 54 years [IQR, 42-64 years]) and more frequently required supplemental oxygen at diagnosis (59 [33.3%] vs 29 [11.4%]). Vaccination status also differed between treatment groups, with 54 remdesivir recipients (30.5%) unvaccinated compared with 124 (48.6%) of those in the no remdesivir group, while receipt of 3 or more vaccine doses was more common in the remdesivir group (87 [49.2%] vs 78 [30.6%]). Pandemic wave distribution was also different between treatment groups, with a smaller proportion of participants receiving early remdesivir than no remdesivir during the pre-Delta period (33 [18.6%] vs 112 [43.9%]), while a larger proportion received early remdesivir than no remdesivir during the Omicron wave (128 [72.3%] vs 132 [51.8%]). After application of stabilized IPTWs, covariable balance improved substantially. Only small residual imbalances between the treatment strategies remained after weighting for pre-Delta (SMD = 0.14) and Omicron (SMD = 0.10) pandemic waves (Table and eFigure 1 in Supplement 1).

Outcomes

The primary outcome of ACGL occurred in 48 patients (11.1%) during follow-up, including 26 (14.7%) in the early remdesivir group and 22 (8.6%) in the no remdesivir group. In unweighted analyses, early remdesivir was associated with a higher risk of ACGL (HR, 1.78; 95% CI, 1.01-3.13). After CCW adjustment using stabilized IPTWs and IPCWs, early remdesivir initiation was associated with a lower risk of ACGL (HR, 0.53; 95% CI, 0.31-0.92) (Figure 2). The E-value for the point estimate was 3.15, and the E-value for the 95% CI limit closest to the null was 1.40. The g-computed cumulative incidence curves showed early separation between treatment groups within the first 30 days after diagnosis, with persistently lower cumulative incidence of graft loss among early remdesivir recipients throughout 1 year of follow-up (Figure 3).

For the secondary outcomes, all-cause mortality occurred in 35 patients (8.1%), including 22 (12.4%) in the early remdesivir group and 13 (5.1%) in the no remdesivir group (eFigure 2 in Supplement 1). In the weighted analysis, early remdesivir was not associated with lower risk of death (HR, 0.51; 95% CI, 0.24-1.06). CVEs occurred in 43 patients (10.0%), including 20 (11.3%) in the early remdesivir group and 23 (9.0%) in the no remdesivir group (eFigure 3 in Supplement 1). In the weighted analysis, early remdesivir initiation was associated with a lower risk of CVEs (HR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.35-0.98). Long COVID occurred in 17 patients (3.9%), including 10 (5.6%) in the early remdesivir group and 7 (2.7%) in the no remdesivir group (eFigure 4 in Supplement 1). In the weighted analysis, no statistically significant association with lower risk of long COVID was observed in the group with early remdesivir initiation (HR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.21-2.05).

Subgroup and Sensitivity Analyses

Prespecified subgroup analyses of associations with ACGL are shown in Figure 4. Overall, the direction of associations with early remdesivir initiation were largely consistent across clinically relevant subgroups, with most strata in the direction of a lower risk of ACGL after weighting. An association between early remdesivir and a lower risk of ACGL was observed among patients younger than 65 years (HR, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.16-0.87), patients with BMI of 30 or greater (HR, 0.32; 95% CI, 0.14-0.72), recipients of a deceased-donor KT (HR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.26-0.95), recipients of a living-donor KT (HR, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.16-0.94), patients with hypertension (HR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.34-0.99), and patients without known coronary artery disease (HR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.21-0.80). Subgroups with limited sample size or few events were reported without effect estimates.

In prespecified sensitivity analyses (eFigure 5 in Supplement 1), the association between early remdesivir and lower risk of ACGL remained statistically significant when the grace period was shortened to 5 days (HR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.30-0.84) and when stabilized IPTWs were truncated at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles (HR, 0.56; 95% CI, 0.32-0.95). For all-cause mortality, shortening the grace period was associated with a lower risk of death (HR, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.23-0.89). Early remdesivir showed no statistically significant reduction in the risk of CVEs in the sensitivity analyses of the grace period (HR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.36-1.01) and truncation (HR, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.36-1.01).

Discussion

In this target trial emulation involving 432 KT recipients with symptomatic COVID-19, early remdesivir initiation vs no remdesivir was associated with lower 1-year risk of graft loss and CVEs after adjusting for baseline confounding and treatment-strategy deviations. The E-value of 3.15 suggests that substantial unmeasured confounding would be needed to fully explain the observed association. This direction of association contrasted with unweighted analyses, in which early remdesivir appeared harmful, underscoring the extent of confounding by indication in clinical decision-making and anticipated disease severity over time. Patients selected for remdesivir initiation were older compared with those receiving no remdesivir, had more severe disease, and were less frequently diagnosed during earlier pandemic phases, when treatment prioritization favored patients at the highest perceived risk. Building on our group’s prior prognostic analysis of post–COVID-19 cardiovascular disease in this cohort,14 the present study extends these observations by emulating a target trial to estimate the per-protocol causal effect of early remdesivir initiation using a CCW approach to address baseline confounding and time-related biases.

Several biologically plausible mechanisms may explain the observed associations between early antiviral therapy and clinical outcomes. SARS-CoV-2 infection induces endothelial dysfunction, complement activation, and prothrombotic states that contribute to thrombosis, acute kidney injury, cardiovascular complications, and kidney allograft hypoperfusion.31,32,33,34 Early suppression of viral replication may attenuate systemic inflammation and endothelial injury, reduce disease severity, and limit the need for immunosuppression modification, thereby indirectly protecting the allograft and reducing myocardial injury, arrhythmias, and thrombotic events.

Subgroup analyses by pandemic wave were imprecise, particularly during Omicron, when disease severity and event rates were lower. However, the direction of effect during the Omicron period, more representative of today’s treatment environment, remained consistent with a protective association, suggesting that the absence of statistical significance likely reflects the imprecision and uncertainty due to reduced event rates rather than the absence of a biological effect. In this cohort, patients assigned to the early remdesivir strategy were less likely to be unvaccinated than those assigned to the no remdesivir strategy. Subgroup analyses by vaccination status were limited by small event counts and should be interpreted cautiously. Although KT recipients mount attenuated humoral responses even after multiple vaccine doses,4,5 vaccination has been associated with reduced disease severity and mortality in this population.35 These findings reinforce the importance of optimizing COVID-19 vaccination in KT recipients as a frontline prevention strategy, with early antiviral therapy serving as a critical complementary treatment.

Our findings align with current CDC and IDSA guidelines recommending early antiviral therapy for patients at high risk of COVID-19 progression17,18 and extend the evidence base specifically to KT recipients, a population largely excluded from the pivotal randomized trials.19,20,21 The present findings complement the safety data in previous studies by suggesting that early remdesivir use may be associated with a reduced risk of graft loss in a clinical KT recipient cohort.24,25,26,27 Beyond graft outcomes, the association between early remdesivir initiation and a lower risk of CVEs is clinically notable. KT recipients are uniquely susceptible to post–COVID-19 cardiovascular complications, with prior analyses demonstrating that nearly 1 in 11 recipients experienced a CVE within 1 year of infection and that hospitalization for COVID-19 conferred cardiovascular risk equivalent to a prior CVE.14 In our prior cohort analysis, remdesivir treatment was independently associated with a lower risk of post–COVID-19 cardiovascular disease despite higher unadjusted event rates, underscoring substantial confounding by indication.14 The present target trial emulation extends these observations by evaluating causal effect estimates under a clearly specified treatment strategy.

Strengths and Limitations

By minimizing major time-related and treatment-selection biases that have limited prior observational studies of remdesivir, this target trial emulation enhanced the causal inference of per-protocol effects. The outcomes were independently and manually adjudicated by clinicians, and the inclusion of cardiovascular and long-term outcomes expanded the scope of prior transplant-specific studies. Nonetheless, several limitations warrant consideration. In an observational study, residual or unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded, and target trial emulation cannot identically replicate randomization, blinding, or protocol adherence. The single health system setting may limit generalizability, and some secondary outcomes were limited by low event counts. Additionally, excluding patients receiving alternative antiviral drugs or monoclonal antibodies precludes inference on comparative effectiveness across COVID-19 therapies. The analysis of long COVID was also limited by few events and wide 95% CIs, and the absence of a significant association should be interpreted as inconclusive rather than as a lack of protective effect.

Conclusions

In this cohort study emulating a target trial of adult KT recipients with symptomatic COVID-19, early remdesivir treatment was associated with reduced 1-year risk of ACGL and CVEs after accounting for confounding and treatment-strategy deviation. These findings support guideline recommendations for early antiviral therapy in high-risk populations and suggest that prompt initiation of remdesivir may protect both kidney allografts and cardiovascular health in KT recipients with COVID-19.

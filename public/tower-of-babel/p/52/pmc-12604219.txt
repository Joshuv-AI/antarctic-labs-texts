Development of a preoperative predictive classifier and tailored staging system for obstructive colorectal cancer
Authors: Shuai Chen, Jun-Rong Zhang, Xin-Chang Shang-Guan, Tian He, Hui Wang, Sun-Jian Wang, Shao-Hua Zheng, Li-Qin Huang, Xian-Qiang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveObstructive colorectal cancer (OCC) often has a poor prognosis. The traditional TNM stage does not effectively predict disease prognosis, so there is an urgent need to establish a more suitable prognostic staging system for patients with OCC.MethodsThis study analyzed 164 patients treated for OCC at a single center between February 1, 2010, and January 31, 2020. The integrative two-step cluster (TSC) stage was used to self-classify OCCs into two groups on the basis of postoperative clinicopathological features. The Kaplan‒Meier method was subsequently used to compare the 3-year overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) of patients grouped by TNM stage and integrative TSC stage. Machine learning classifiers were then applied to predict the integrative TSC stage using preoperative clinical features and radiomics. To evaluate the performance of the classifiers, we used the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).ResultsAfter the integrative TSC stage was constructed, 164 patients with OCC were divided into an integrative superior group (iSG, n = 99) and an integrative inferior group (iIG, n = 65). In the integrative TSC staging system, lymphovascular invasion (1.00) was identified as the most valuable risk factor. The iSG outperformed the iIG in both OS and DFS (OS-months: 31.42 (27.96–34.88) vs. 19.30 (15.43–23.17), p < 0.001; DFS-months: 31.15 (27.49–34.82) vs. 18.12 (14.32–21.92), p < 0.001). Compared with the TNM stage, the integrative TSC stage was more effective in discriminating OS (integrative TSC stage, p < 0.001; TNM stage, p = 0.036) and DFS (integrative TSC stage, p < 0.001; TNM stage, p = 0.011). To predict the integrative TSC stage, the linear discriminant analysis (LDA) classifier based on carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and 15 radiomics features performed best (testing dataset: AUC 0.85, 95% CI: 0.74–0.96). In the stratified analysis, patients who underwent BTS were reclassified into the iSG group. These patients had lower CEA levels [3.22 (interquartile range[IQR], 1.60–5.50) ng/ml, p = 0.036] and higher ct_wavelet-LHH_gldm_SmallDependenceHighGray-LevelEmphasis (HGLE) levels [0.26 (IQR, 0.09–0.41), p = 0.044].ConclusionThe integrative TSC stage surpasses the TNM staging system in distinguishing OCC prognosis, with LVI serving as a vital component. The LDA classifier using 16-dimensional features can effectively predict the integrative TSC stage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03103-4.Keywords: Obstructive colorectal cancer, Integrative two-step cluster stage, TNM stage, Lymphovascular invasion, Linear discriminant analysis classifier

Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide [1]. Approximately 8–29% of patients with CRC develop obstructive colorectal cancer (OCC), a condition requiring emergency intervention [2, 3]. Patients with OCC typically present with abdominal pain, distension, vomiting, constipation, systemic inflammation, and severe malnutrition [4]. The tumor’s endoluminal growth disrupts bowel content transport and alters bowel habits. As the obstruction progresses, it can cause proximal bowel dilation, ischemia, and secondary necrotic perforation. Impaired enteral nutrition often leads to significant electrolyte disturbances and severe malnutrition in patients, potentially compromising hemodynamic stability [5, 6]. BIONDO S et al. reported that OCC recurrence is significantly linked to lymphovascular invasion (LVI), which occurs when cancer cells from the primary tumor invade lymphatic or vascular channels, a key early step in lymph node metastasis and cancer dissemination [7]. Studies have confirmed that LVI is associated with higher CRC histological grades and advanced tumor stages [8, 9]. Given these characteristics, we hypothesize that OCC may represent a distinct CRC subtype.

Currently, there is no prognostic assessment system specific to OCC, leading to controversy over the indications for ES and BTS [10]. Intestinal edema, thickened bowel walls, and poor nutritional reserves are classical characteristics of OCC. While ES can promptly relieve obstruction, primary anastomosis is challenging to perform. This leads to a high risk of anastomotic fistula. In addition, it necessitates primary stoma formation and subsequent secondary stoma reversal, which significantly impacts patients’ quality of life. Additionally, the severe systemic inflammatory response during the acute phase increases surgical risks and complication rates [11, 12]. In contrast, successful SEMS placement can effectively alleviate obstruction, allowing for adequate preoperative preparation. The European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) guidelines state that SEMS placement is suitable for left-sided colorectal cancer obstruction or for primary or recurrent colorectal cancer with unresectable local lesions, extensive metastasis, or patients who are unfit for surgery [13]. This approach, by correcting systemic inflammation and severe malnutrition, enables BTS procedures, increases the primary anastomosis rate, and reduces permanent stoma formation and wound infections [14, 15]. However, studies have shown that SEMS placement in patients with OCC leads to a significant increase in the amount of circulating tumor DNA in the peripheral blood compared with that in controls [16]. The mechanical compression of the tumor by the SEMS may promote tumor metastasis, which restricts the clinical application of the BTS. Therefore, an objective and targeted evaluation system is urgently needed to guide surgical decision-making for patients with OCC.

The application of artificial intelligence (AI) in health care is increasing, achieving expert-level accuracy in colorectal cancer screening through endoscopic image recognition and providing reliable pathological predictions [17]. It also uses multiomics data from patients with colorectal cancer to analyze prognostic biomarkers, predict drug responses, and intervene in disease prognosis [18, 19]. Moreover, AI can be used to interpret clinical data and medical images such as CT and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans to assist in linking radiomics with colorectal cancer diagnosis, staging, progression, and chemosensitivity [20–23]. In this study, we used two-step clustering (TSC) to classify patients with OCC. TSC, a machine learning method, handles both categorical and continuous variables, enabling direct weighting of multiple features during clustering [24–27]. On the basis of postoperative pathological and clinical data of patients with OCC, we established a TSC-based prognostic staging system. To predict the prognosis preoperatively, we introduced a machine learning classifier (MLC) built on preoperative features. The MLC, which is based on statistics and algorithms, learns from training data to create models for predicting the categories of test cases [28, 29]. Its accuracy depends on the model’s ability to learn during training and the similarity between the training and test sets [30, 31]. With a well-trained MLC, we can achieve efficient and reliable prognostic prediction for patients with OCC.

In this study, we aimed to construct an integrative TSC stage for the evaluation of OCCs. In addition, we also aimed to extract the MLC with preoperative multidimensional features to predict the integrative TSC stage.

Methods

Patients

This study was conducted at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital in China from February 2010 to January 2020, with a 3-year postoperative follow-up. A total of 224 patients with obstructive colorectal cancer were considered for analysis. [a] The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) patients with classic symptoms of bowel obstruction (abdominal pain, vomiting, bloating, or constipation); 2) patients with bowel obstruction confirmed by preoperative computed tomography (CT); and 3) patients with malignancy confirmed by postoperative pathology. [b] The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) perforation, abscess or ischemia reported on preoperative CT; 2) poor quality and poorly labeled CT images; and 3) lack of information on LVI and TNM stage.

Emergency Surgery

In the ES group, patients with OCC underwent radical or palliative tumor resection, with the surgical approach determined by the tumor location. Intraoperatively, we decided between primary anastomosis or stoma formation on the basis of intestinal edema and preoperative preparation. We performed colonic irrigation or manual decompression when needed.

Bridge-to-surgery

Stent placement was performed by an endoscopist who had performed over 1000 ERCP procedures. If SEMS placement was successful in relieving bowel obstruction, patients underwent elective radical or palliative tumor resection after 10–14 days of conservative treatment, with the surgical approach determined by the tumor location. The successful SEMS placement procedures (for a total of 44 patients) were as follows: 3 in the ascending colon, 7 in the transverse colon, 10 in the descending colon, 23 in the sigmoid colon, and 1 in the rectum above the peritoneal reflection. Owing to failed BTS, seven patients underwent emergency surgery within 24 h [32].

Clinical information

Preoperative features included the following: [1] baseline information: age (ranging from 21 to 90 years), body mass index (BMI), comorbidities, ColoRectal Obstruction Scoring System (CROSS, a scoring system for measuring the degree of obstruction) score [33], and American Society of Anesthesiologists grade (ASA grade, strongly associated with postoperative complications [34]); and [2] blood indices: hemoglobin (HB) and albumin (ALB) levels, white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), lymphocyte count (LN), platelet count (PLT), monocyte count (MONO), and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) levels [32].Postoperative features included the following: [1] pathological features: pathological metastasis (pM, which indicates tumor metastasis to distant lymph nodes, tissues, or organs, such as the lungs, liver, bones, or brain), lymph node metastasis (pN), locally advanced status (LAS = pathological tumor 4), LVI, and tumor location (right colon, transverse colon, left colon, rectum, multiple locations [≥ 2 locations]); The TNM stage was assessed according to the AJCC 8th Edition of Colorectal Cancer [35] [2]. postoperative nutritional indicators within 5–8 days: HB × ALB × LN/PLT score (HALP score) [36–39], which has been widely used to reflect the nutritional status in cases of systemic inflammation; [3] postoperative bowel recovery events [40], including gas time (GT), fluid time (FT), and semifluid time (SFT) (GT refers to the time interval between the first gastrointestinal evacuation and surgery; FT refers to the time interval between fasting and the administration of a fluid diet after surgery; SFT refers to the time interval between fasting and the administration of a semiliquid diet after surgery); and [4] others, such as postoperative hospitalization time (PHT), representing the time between surgery and hospital discharge, and Clavien‒Dindo grade (CD grade) [41].

Follow-up

Three-year overall survival (OS) was defined as 3 years from the date of surgery to the date of the last follow-up visit or the date of death from any cause, and 3-year disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as 3 years from surgery to any recurrence. Follow-up was performed at the oncology outpatient clinic and by personal contact with the patient, the general practitioner, and/or the patient’s family by telephone. The last follow-up date was 31 December 2022. To date, follow-up has been completed for 87.8% of the patients (144 of 164 patients; contact was lost with 15 patients, and 5 patients refused to cooperate).

Clustering

Conventional classification methods may lead to bias in data classification. TSC is an intelligent clustering method that can simultaneously process both categorical and continuous variables. On the basis of the TNM stage (pN, pM, LAS), we attempted to use TSC to add LVI, tumor location, gastrointestinal recovery (PHT, SFT, FT, GT) and complications (CD grade, HB, PLT, ALB, LN) for automatic prognostic grouping of OCC.

TSC classification involves identifying clusters by two processes, preclustering and hierarchical clustering, which was performed in the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) for Windows software (version 25.0, Chicago, Illinois, USA). We used postoperative pathological and clinical features to complete the automatic binary classification to obtain the integrative TSC stage.

Preoperative radiomics feature extraction

CT labeling

The reconstruction of the region of interest (ROI) is performed in a total of 3 steps. First, we used ITK-SANP to label the ROI. Second, we performed 3D reconstruction of each scan of the preoperative CT images (3440 DICOM format) with a reconstruction area of at least 5 cm for the obstructed segment, both proximal and distal [26]. Finally, the reconstruction was first performed by 3 surgeons. A second correction was performed by a radiologist with 20 years of reading experience. Finally, on the basis of manual labeling of the ROI in each layer of the CT image, 3D reconstruction was automatically performed to allow radiological visualization.

Radiomics feature extraction

Radiomics data were extracted from the ROI using the open-source Python package PyRadiomics [42]. Seven types of radiomics features were extracted: [1] first-order statistics [2], morphological features [3], a gray-level co-occurrence matrix [4], a gray-level tour matrix [5], a gray-level size region matrix [6], a gray-level dependence matrix and [7] a neighborhood gray-level difference matrix. The predictors used in the model are listed in Supplementary Table 2. Explanations for each radiomics image can be found at the Radiology Assistant website [43].Second, the different types of radiomics methods could lead to different computational results because of their different units of measurement. Therefore, we performed a mean normalization operation on the radiomics data to eliminate the unit and scale differences among data such that each feature vector had the same weight. This reduced the computational effort while keeping all feature information constant. In addition, the dimensionality of the radiomics data extracted by imaging-omics was too high, which could lead to overfitting of the classifier. Therefore, appropriate parameters should be used in feature extraction to ensure classification accuracy. To this end, we proposed a two-step feature selection method. In the first step, radiomics data with a correlation R > 0.99 were removed by calculating the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC). This method could reduce the number of radiomics features required while retaining more valid information. In the second step, the remaining radiomics features were ranked by their predictive power using recursive feature elimination (RFE). To avoid misclassification due to redundancy between radiomics features, a weighting method based on information entropy and distance metrics was introduced in the first stage to determine the optimal subset of radiomics features. With RFE, we reduce the number of radiomics features required by recursively assigning weights to each feature involved in training and removing attributes with a minimum absolute value of weight.?

Machine learning classifier filtering

First, to build predictive classifiers, we need to extract preoperative clinical features and radiomics features. For clinical features, since most of the features were nonnormally distributed, we used a nonparametric rank sum test to achieve dimensionality reduction. Following feature extraction, the dataset was randomly partitioned into training and test sets (7:3 ratio). Missing values were then imputed using set-specific means to prevent data leakage. Hyperparameter optimization (feature count) was conducted via 5-fold cross-validation on the training set to determine optimal classifier settings [44, 45]. We then used classifiers such as the following: [1] Linear discriminant analysis (LDA). LDA is a classification method based on Bayes’ theorem. It seeks to maximize the separation between different classes by identifying a linear combination of features that maximizes the ratio of between-class variance to within-class variance [46] [2]. Support vector machine (SVM). A SVM is a classification algorithm based on the principle of maximizing the margin between classes. It aims to find an optimal hyperplane that separates data points of different classes. By introducing kernel functions, a SVM can handle non-linearly separable datakernel functions [47] [3]. Logistic regression (LR). LR is a generalized linear model used for binary classification. It maps the linear combination of features to the interval (0,1) using the logistic function (sigmoid function), thereby predicting the probability that a sample belongs to a particular class [48] [4]. Logistic regression-based minimum absolute value shrinkage and selection operator (LR_Lasso). LR_Lasso is a classification method that incorporates L1 regularization into logistic regression. Lasso regularization penalizes the absolute values of coefficients, leading to feature selection by setting some coefficients to zero [49] [5]. Naive Bayesian (NB). NB is a classification method based on Bayes’ theorem with the assumption of feature independence. It predicts the class of a sample by calculating the posterior probability of each class [50] [6]. Random forest (RF). RF is an ensemble learning method based on decision trees. Multiple decision trees are constructed, and their predictions are aggregated through voting or averaging to improve classification performance. Randomness is introduced during the construction of each tree, including random selection of samples and features, to predict classification results [51]. To evaluate the performance of classifiers, we used the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and area under the ROC curve (AUC). In addition, we calculated the accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value and negative predictive value to evaluate the performance of the classifiers [52]. All of the above processes were realized with the FeAture Explorer Pro (FAEPro, V 0.3.6) developed on Windows by Song Y et al. [53].

Statistics

During the During theinitial data analysis, a missing data rate of 3.35% (143 out of 4,264 variables, details in Supplementary Table 1) was identified. Since the missing data imputation method is more scientific and reliable, we applied the MissForest method to the entire dataset initially. MissForest is a non-parametric imputation method suitable for any data type, including continuous and discrete data [54]. The mean squared error (MSE) values were 0.3907 for MissForest, 0.4494 for IterativeImputer, 0.4159 for KNN, 0.5085 for mean and 0.4670 for zero-padding methods, showing that MissForest-based imputation performed better than the other methods did. OS and DFS were calculated using the Kaplan‒Meier method. For continuous variables conforming to a normal distribution, the data are expressed as the means ± standard deviations, and variability was assessed using an independent samples t test. For other continuous and categorical variables, the data are expressed as medians (interquartile ranges) and frequencies (percentages), and the variability was investigated via the nonparametric rank sum test. The results with p values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows software (version 25.0; Chicago, Illinois, USA). Our study was reported in accordance with the TRIPOD guidelines.

Results

Ultimately, 164 patients with OCC were included in this study, including 44 patients who underwent BTS and 120 patients who underwent ES (60 patients were excluded). Ten patients lacked preoperative CT data at our center; 34 patients had poor CT image quality; and 16 patients lacked LVI and TNM stage data. Postoperative pathological and clinical features were included in the integrative TSC stage. Finally, patients were clustered into either the integrative superior group (iSG = 99) or the integrative inferior group (iIG = 65). Construction of the integrative Two-step cluster stage

First, patients were primarily included in the clinically superior group (cSG, n = 83) or the clinically inferior group (cIG, n = 81) according to the clinical TSC stage. (Details are provided in the Supplementary Paragraph, Supplementary Fig. 2 and Table 1). For the analysis of long-term outcomes, postoperative pathological and clinical TSC stages were included in the integrative TSC stage. Finally, patients were included in the integrative superior group (iSG = 99) or the integrative inferior group (iIG = 65). The average silhouette, which represents the quality of classification, reached 0.3. Weighting analysis of all clustering features revealed that LVI (1.00) was the most valuable risk factor for the integrative TSC stage, followed by the pN stage (0.51), pM stage (0.25), LAS (0.21), tumor location (0.03) and clinical TSC (0. 01). Compared with patients in the iIG, patients in the iSG presented no LVI (LVI (+) vs. LVI (-) = 0: 99, p < 0.001), a lower pN stage (N0: N1: N2 = 51: 40: 8, p < 0.001), a lower metastatic stage (M0: M1 = 87: 12, p < 0.001) and a lower LAS (nT4: T4 = 71: 28, p < 0.001) (Fig.2; Table 1).

Comparison of the effects of integrative TSC stage and TNM stage on Long-Term outcomes

The median follow-up duration was 25.9 months (IQR: 12.15–56.43). To verify the validity of the integrative TSC stage, we compared it with the TNM stage in terms of its ability to predict OS and DFS. The integrative TSC stage was more effective in discriminating OS (iSG vs. iIG = 31.42 ± 1.77 months: 19.30 ± 1.98 months, p < 0.001), whereas the TNM stage was less effective in predicting prognosis (TNM stage I-II vs. stage III-IV = 30.87 ± 1.20 months: 24.40 ± 1.44 months, p = 0.04). Similarly, for DFS, this tailored integrative TSC stage (iSG vs. iIG = 31.15 ± 1.87 months vs. 19.12 ± 1.94 months, p < 0.001) outperformed the TNM stage (TNM stage I-II vs. stage III-IV = 30.00 ± 1.32 months vs. 23.20 ± 1.48 months, p = 0.01) (Table 1; Fig. 2). The median follow-up duration was 25.9 months, with an interquartile range (IQR) of 12.15 to 56.43 months (Table 1; Fig. 1).

Preoperative multidimensional feature extraction and machine learning classifier filtering for the integrative TSC stage

In this step, the integrative TSC stage was established as the standard for the prognostic assessment of OCC. First, we screened out preoperative clinical features and radiomics features. In terms of clinical features, only CEA was significantly different between the integrative TSC groups (iSG vs. iIG = 4.50 (2.20–11.40) ng/ml vs. 7.80 (3.55–21.80) ng/ml, p = 0.03) (details in Table 1). Among the radiomics features, 1316-dimensional radiomics features were extracted. After feature dimension reduction and filtering by classifiers, six categories of radiomics features were finally extracted (15 radiomics features in total), including ct_exponential, ct_original, ct_logarithm, ct_squareroot, ct_square, and ct_wavelet (details in Supplementary Table 2).Patients with OCC were randomized into two groups as follows: 114 training cases (iSG: iIG = 69:45) and 50 test cases (iSG: iIG = 30:20). Finally, the predictive classifiers for the integrative TSC stage were dependent on the CEA level and 15 radiomics features. For the validation dataset, the LDA classifier showed excellent predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.85 (95% CI: 0.74–0.96), accuracy of 0.78, sensitivity of 0.90 and specificity of 0.70. The LR classifier was inferior to the LDA classifier in terms of the AUC (0.78, 95% CI: 0.65–0.91), accuracy (0.70), sensitivity (0.90) and specificity (0.57) (Table 2; Fig. 2). The feature coefficients of the LDA classifier are shown in Supplementary Table 1, which shows the efficacy of various machine learning classifiers on CV-Train, CV-Validation, and the training dataset. Our model matches or exceeds similar published models’ AUCs for the integrative TSC stage (Supplementary Table 4). Discussion

In this study, a tailored scoring system for the prognosis of OCC was preliminarily constructed after all the postoperative parameters were clustered. On the basis of different machine-learning classifiers, we could accurately predict the integrative TSC stage on the basis of preoperative clinical traits. The OCC is generally considered a specific type of CRC with an emergency status. Making choices for emergency surgery or conservative treatment within a limited time remains a challenge. Currently, the TNM stage is widely used as a scoring system for the prognosis of CRC. However, a precise evaluation system for patients with CRC with bowel obstruction is lacking, and a clustering system tailored to OCC is urgently needed. The CD grade is only related to major complications and inadequately reflects the postoperative status of patients with OCC [41, 55]. Analogously, the ASA grade is primarily used to assess the risk of anesthesia [56]. HALP, a nutritional scoring system used to predict DFS in patients with gastrointestinal stromal tumors after radical resection [57], cannot distinguish patients in the cSG from patients in the cIG. None of these scoring systems could predict the specific recovery progress of OCC.

In the initial phase of this study, we tried to use the clinical TSC stage to reflect the short-term recovery outcome of OCC. After automatic self-clustering, patients in the cSG achieved faster gastrointestinal recovery, shorter hospitalization times and better nutritional status. Furthermore, none of the patients in the cSG experienced postoperative complications on the basis of the CD grade. However, the accuracy and efficacy of clinical TSC staging are not satisfactory, and postoperative pathological features need to be incorporated. Owing to the superiority of TSC in dealing with multiple types of features, for patients with OCC, we combined the TNM stage and postoperative clinical features to establish the integrative TSC stage for the evaluation of prognosis. Compared with the classical TNM stage, the integrative TSC stage outperformed the traditional TNM stage in discriminating OS and DFS. Clinical features are less important than pathological features in the integrative TSC stage. LVI, which dominates all the risk factors via weight analysis of the integrative TSC stage, has been defined as an independent risk factor for DFS and OS in patients with OCC [58]. Owing to mechanical stress and the local advanced stage, patients with OCC are more prone to LVI than non-OCC patients are [59, 60], with a high frequency of tumor dissemination. It is inaccurate to assess the prognosis of OCC without LVI weighting. Similar to the TNM stage, pN, pM and LAS are the remaining weighted risk factors in the integrative TSC stage.

On the basis of the integrative TSC stage, we applied machine-learning classifiers to predict the prognosis of patients with OCC after extracting preoperative clinical and radiomics data from each patient. Finally, the LDA classifier, a generalization of Fisher’s linear discriminant method, was found to be the best classifier, with an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI: 0.71–0.94) and an accuracy of 0.78. This might be due to the adjusted correction of the LDA classifier in different feature correlations. In addition, the LDA classifier outperformed the other classifiers in the analysis of relatively sparse features with a large array [61, 62]. It has also been identified as an excellent classifier in several medical models, such as magnetic resonance imaging extraction [63], feature processing [64], breast ultrasound image discrimination [65] and lung CT nodule classification [66]. The LDA screened in this study achieved an AUC of 0.84 in the integrative TSC stage task. This success might be attributed to the synergistic analysis of bimodal data—combinations of radiomics and clinical molecular markers—which allows for a more comprehensive capture of OCC heterogeneity.

When benchmarked against existing models, our LDA model demonstrates comparable or superior performance. Although it shows slightly lower predictive stability than Thijs A. Burghgraef’s model for predicting 90-day mortality [67], this could be due to the larger dataset used in the latter study. Moreover, our LDA outperforms several others: it exceeds Tom van den Bosch et al.‘s 30-day mortality prediction model, which despite having a slightly higher AUC, benefits from a larger case number [68]. It also surpasses Tetsuo Ishizaki et al.‘s TNM II Stage prediction model, which is limited by insufficient variable dimensions [69]. Similarly, our model outdoes Neeraj Kumari et al.‘s TNM II and IV Stage prediction model, as that model relies solely on pathological features [70].

More recently, the lack of an accurate prognostic system for OCC has led to controversy over whether BTS should be routinely performed in OCC [71–74]. In this study, we developed and confirmed the validity of the integrative TSC stage, a tailored scoring system for OCC. We also confirmed that OCC with higher levels of HGLE, an indicator of the degree of tumor differentiation [75, 76], and lower preoperative CEA levels had better prognosis in the BTS group based on the integrative TSC stage. This may account for the increasing CEA levels reflecting a more severe tumor burden. For OCC with a high tumor burden, the mechanical stress produced by metal stents enhances the dissemination of tumor cells [77–81].

There are several limitations to acknowledge in this study. First, the single-center design and relatively small sample size warrant future validation through multicenter collaborations with larger cohorts. Second, both ES and BTS are heterogeneous events that involve many factors. Influencing factors include the surgeon, tumor site, surgical method, and type and material of the SEMS. Third, while our manual ROI annotation approach provided precise feature localization, this time-consuming process highlights the need for automated radiomics approaches, which is a key direction for our ongoing research. Fourth, the current findings would benefit from external validation, and we are actively pursuing multicenter partnerships to address this issue. Finally, our future work will explore additional machine-learning classifiers and diversified feature processing methods to increase model robustness.

Conclusions

A novel tailored staging system for OCC was constructed and named the integrative TSC stage. By combining preoperative radiomics and CEA, an LDA classifier that scientifically predicts the integrative TSC stage has been confirmed.

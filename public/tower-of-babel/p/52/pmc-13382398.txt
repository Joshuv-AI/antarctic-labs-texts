Comprehensive characterization of myopic guinea pigs' retina following PI3K/AKT inhibition using a data-independent acquisition dataset on an Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer
Authors: Hu Xiao, Wen Xin, Oi Ying Wong, Man Hin Leung, Jimmy Ka-Wai Cheung, Da Qian Lu, Lei Zhou, Rachel Ka Man Chun, Jing Fang Bian, Thomas Chuen Lam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Myopia has emerged as a global ocular health concern. The progression of myopia is predominantly driven by abnormal signaling pathways, with the PI3K/AKT pathway playing a crucial role. By utilizing the lens-induced myopia model in guinea pigs, we intravitreally injected the PI3K/AKT inhibitor LY294002 and carried out quantitative proteomic analysis using data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry. In this research, proteomic data of the retinas from guinea pigs in the normal, myopic, and drug-treated groups were provided. Moreover, a comprehensive retinal proteome database for guinea pig myopia was established, providing a resource for future investigations into myopia-related signaling pathways. All mass spectrometry data were deposited in the ProteomeXchange Consortium via the PRIDE partner (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pride) with the dataset identifier PRIDE: PXD074201.

Background

Myopia has evolved into a global public health emergency, with a prevalence of over 50% in East Asia, and is intensifying globally [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Studying myopia-related molecular pathways and targeted innovative treatment strategies is urgently needed at present [4]. The progression of myopia is linked to the abnormal regulation of numerous signaling pathways. These pathways include TGF-β [10], ER stress [11], histone lactylation [12], and ferroptosis [13], among which the PI3K/AKT pathway acts as a key hub [[5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. To expand available data resources, we applied intravitreal injection of LY294002 in a guinea pig myopia model and generated quantitative proteomic datasets characterizing retinal protein profiles following PI3K/AKT pathway inhibition. In addition, this study establishes a comprehensive proteomic dataset of the guinea pig myopic retina under PI3K/AKT pathway modulation.

Data Description

In this proteomic study, retinal samples from 30 eyes of 18 guinea pigs (3 weeks old) were systematically categorized into five experimental groups (N4, L4, LH4, L8, LH8). N4 group: 6 guinea pigs (6 eyes, 1 eye per animal) with normal growth for 4 days, retinas harvested at day 4; L4 and LH4 groups: 6 guinea pigs (12 eyes), both eyes fitted with −5D lenses for lens-induced myopia (LIM), with one randomly selected eye receiving intravitreal injection LY294002 [3.9 mM] at baseline and day 2 (LH4, n = 6) and the fellow eye receiving equal volume of sterile saline (L4, n = 6), retinas harvested at day 4; L8 and LH8 groups: 6 guinea pigs (12 eyes), both eyes fitted with −5D lenses for LIM, with one randomly selected eye receiving LY294002 at baseline and day 2 followed by drug withdrawal (LH8, n = 6) and the fellow eye receiving equal volume of sterile saline (L8, n = 6), retinas harvested at day 8. Ocular parameters are shown in the Supplementary file. Two technical repetitions were conducted for each retina in the LC-MS analysis. Label-free Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA) data were acquired using a UHPLC—Orbitrap Exploris™ 480 mass spectrometry platform. Protein identification and quantification were performed with DIA-NN (v1.9.2) at 1% FDR [[14], [15], [16], [17]], followed by pairwise comparative analyses (L4 / N4, LH4 / L4, and LH8 / L8). Analytical rigor was ensured through the Mass Spectrometry Downstream Analysis Pipeline (MS-DAP, v1.2.1) [18,19], with quality control metrics visualized in Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sample collection and ethical considerations

Three-week-old male pigmented guinea pigs were housed in the conventional animal facility at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University Shenzhen Research Institute's Centralized Animal Facility (CAF). The housing conditions were maintained at a temperature of 24 °C, along with a 12:12-hour light-dark cycle. The animals were provided unrestricted access to food, water, and fresh vegetables. All animal experiments received approval from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University Animal Ethics Committee (Approval No. 22 - 23/493 - SO - R - OTHERS) and were conducted in line with the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research. Lens attachment was achieved using a Velcro system: the loop side was sutured to the periocular skin with sterilized 4–0 non-absorbable suture, and the hook side was adhered to the −5D lenses, allowing secure attachment to both eyes. Lens status (attachment and cleanliness) and ocular health were monitored twice daily (9:00 AM and 5:00 PM). Animals with lens loss, ocular infection, or mortality were excluded from the study. Under isoflurane-oxygen anesthesia, 10 µL of drug solution or sterile saline was injected into the guinea pig vitreous chamber using a Hamilton 25-µL microsyringe with a 33 G beveled needle. Injections occurred on day 0 (baseline) and day 2. Contralateral eyes received equal-volume sterile saline as controls. Healthy guinea pigs weighing 130 to 180 g were selected for this study. Inclusion criteria: the baseline diopter of both eyes was between +5.00 D and +1.00 D, the difference of diopter between both eyes was <1.00 D, and the cylinder power of either eye was <1.00 D Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) organic ocular lesions such as corneal opacity, cataract, infection or trauma were found in ophthalmic examination; (2) glasses falling off during the experimental intervention; (3) complications such as endophthalmitis, vitreous hemorrhage or refractive interstitial opacity occurred after intravitreal injection. Individuals who met either of these exclusion criteria and their data were not included in the final analysis.

A total of 18 guinea pigs were randomly assigned, following baseline measurements, to three groups (n = 6 per group) using Excel-generated random numbers. The normal control group (N4) was reared normally for 4 days, and one eye per animal was randomly selected with Excel random numbers as the normal eye for retinal collection. In the 4-day LIM cohort (L4/LH4), both eyes of each animal were fitted with −5 D lenses from baseline to day 4; the left and right eyes were independently randomized by separate Excel-generated odd/even assignments to receive either intravitreal LY294002 (3.9 mM) at baseline and day 2 (LH4 eye) or an equal volume of sterile saline (L4 eye). The 8-day LIM cohort (L8/LH8) was treated identically with −5 D lenses from baseline to day 8, and the two eyes were independently randomized in the same manner to receive LY294002 at baseline and day 2, followed by drug withdrawal until day 8 (LH8 eye) or an equal volume of sterile saline (L8 eye). At the end of the experimental period, the animals were humanely euthanized between 10 and 11 a.m. by an overdose of CO2. Immediately after, the retinal tissues were collected, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and then stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis.

Sample preparation

Retinal proteins were isolated by using a lysis buffer that consisted of 5% SDS and 50 mM TEAB. Next, 50 µg aliquots of the protein were subjected to reduction with 200 mM DTT at 95 °C for 10 min. Then, alkylation was performed with 400 mM IAA in the dark for 10 min. After that, the lysate was acidified with 12% phosphoric acid at a volume ratio of 1:10 (v/v). Subsequently, it was diluted with S-Trap binding buffer (composed of 90% methanol, 0.1 M TEAB, pH 7.1) at a volume ratio of 6:1 (v/v). After being thoroughly mixed, the acidified sample was loaded onto an S-Trap Micro spin column. The proteins were captured by centrifuging at 4000 × g for 20 s and then washed three times with 150 µL of the binding buffer. For on-column digestion, 20 µL of trypsin (with an enzyme-to-protein ratio of 1:25) was added, and the mixture was incubated at 47 °C for 1 hour. The peptides were eluted in sequence using the following solutions: (1) 40 µL of 50 mM TEAB, (2) 40 µL of 0.2% formic acid, and (3) 35 µL of a solution containing 50% acetonitrile and 0.2% formic acid. The combined eluates were vacuum-dried at 4 °C. The peptide pellet was then reconstituted in 30 µL of 0.1% formic acid to reach a final concentration of 0.5 µg/µL.

LC-MS/MS settings

Nanoflow liquid chromatography was carried out using a Thermo Fisher Scientific UltiMate 3000 RSLC nano UHPLC system. This system was equipped with a binary nano pump (capable of operating at pressures up to 1200 bar) and an independent loading pump. Regarding sample preparation, an Acclaim PepMap 100 trap column (75 µm × 2 cm, C18, with a pore size of 3 µm) was utilized. After being injected by an autosampler, the sample was loaded onto the trap column at a flow rate of 8 µL/min. During a 3-minute interval, 100% mobile phase A was used to assist in desalting. After that, the ten-port valve was activated to create a serial connection between the trap column and the analytical column. Peptide separation was accomplished by means of an Acclaim PepMap RSLC analytical column (75 µm × 25 cm, C18, having a pore size of 2 µm). A gradient elution procedure was carried out at a flow rate of 300 nL/min, with the column temperature kept at 58 °C. It involved an initial isocratic stage where the proportion of mobile phase B was maintained at 5% from 0 to 3 min. Then, there was a linear gradient increase to 18% B over 35 min (from 3 to 38 min), followed by a linear rise to 50% B within 20 min (from 38 to 58 min). After that, it was increased to 95% B in 4 min (from 58 to 62 min), and then held at 95% B for 5 min (from 62 to 67 min). Finally, it returned to 5% B with a 3-minute re-equilibration period (from 67 to 70 min). Mobile phase A was made up of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in water, and mobile phase B consisted of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in a mixture of 80% (v/v) acetonitrile and water.

For mass spectrometric analysis, 1.5 µg of peptide samples were introduced into an Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer, and the experimental parameters were managed using Thermo Xcalibur software. The acquisition was carried out in positive-ion mode for a period of 73 min. Ionization was accomplished through a NanoSpray Ionization source. This source was operated at a spray voltage of 2150 V and an ion transfer tube temperature of 305 °C. For the scanning events from 0 to 70 min, an MS¹ full scan was obtained. It had a resolution of 120,000 across the m/z range of 350–1200. The RF lens was set at 40%, and the normalized AGC target was 300%. Subsequently, MS² fragmentation was conducted along with a 30% normalized HCD collision energy, a resolution of 30,000, an RF lens at 40%, and a normalized AGC target of 1000%. DIA window scheme: The m/z range 350–1200 was covered by 19 fixed isolation windows of 45.7 m/z each, with 1 Da overlap between adjacent windows. Each acquisition cycle consisted of one MS¹ scan followed by 19 MS² scans. MS² scan range: fragment ions were acquired with a defined first mass at m/z 250, and the overlap strategy was 1 Da. No automatic cycle time mode was set.

Data analysis

In this study, raw DIA data were processed using DIA-NN (version 1.9.2) in library-free mode with default “robust LC (high precision)” settings. Precursor ions were analyzed across an m/z range of 300–1800 and a charge state range of 1–4. Proteins were identified by in-silico tryptic digestion at 1% FDR using the UniProt database (UP000005447, July 2024 release). Quality control was performed with the Mass Spectrometry Downstream Analysis Pipeline (MS-DAP, version 1.2.1). MS-DAP v1.2.1 was used to merge the two sets of technical duplicate data from the same retina into one data set (merge_replicate_samples function, mean) and applied with feature selection such that each peptide is detected in at least 3 samples in each sample group (filter_min_detect = 3), by-contrast filtering enabled (filter_by_contrast = TRUE), normalization set to a combination of VSN and protein-level mode-between (norm_algorithm = c(“vsn”, “modebetween_protein”)), MS-EmpiRe as DEA algorithm (dea_algorithm = “msempire”) and remaining settings at default. P-values were adjusted for multiple testing with the Benjamini–Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) procedure; the threshold for significance was set to 5% FDR.

Limitations

Although the sample size of n = 6 biological replicates per group is still modest for detecting minor proteomic changes. The study was powered to detect a 1.24-fold change but may lack sensitivity for smaller, biologically meaningful effects. The use of pigmented guinea pigs and lens-induced myopia models may not fully represent the changes in the molecular biology of axial myopia in humans. In addition, this experiment has only completed the exploration of differential proteins, and the key differential proteins need to be independently verified in the future.

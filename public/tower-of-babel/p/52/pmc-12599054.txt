Cost-effectiveness analysis of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for the treatment of postmenopausal hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer
Authors: Xiaofeng Lin, Yipeng Lan, Jing Wang, Wei Zhao, Xiaoxi Liu, Zhe Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe MONARCH 3 trial has demonstrated that Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib is safe and effective for the treatment of postmenopausal hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer. However, its cost-effectiveness for long-term use has not been investigated yet. This study aims to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib versus Anastrozole used alone for patients in China.MethodsBased on MONARCH 3 trial data, we constructed a Markov model using Treeage Pro 2022 software. The model cycle was set at 1 month over a period of 20- year time horizon, and the annual discount rate was set at 5%. The cost-utility analysis was used to assess the cost-effectiveness of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for the treatment of postmenopausal hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer. The output indexes of the model were cost and quality-adjusted life year (QALY), and the model evaluation index was incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). The willingness to pay (WTP) threshold was set at 3 times the per-capita gross domestic product (GDP) of China in 2023 (¥268,074/QALY). Meanwhile, one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis were used to explore the uncertainties of the model and parameters.ResultsAnastrozole combined with Abemaciclib provided more health benefits than Anastrozole used alone, however, the total cost was higher. The incremental utility and incremental cost were 0.01 QALYs and ¥1075.51, respectively. And the ICER of the two regimens was ¥93,940.83/QALY, which was less than 3 times the per-capita GDP of China in 2023 (¥268,074). The results of the sensitivity analysis attested that the study results were robust.ConclusionsAnastrozole combined with Abemaciclib is more cost-effective than Anastrozole used alone for the treatment of postmenopausal hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer.

Introduction

Breast cancer is one of the common malignant tumors among women. According to GLOBOCAN data from the World Health Organization’s (WHO) International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), breast cancer became the most common cancer in the world in 2020, with up to 2.26 million new cases worldwide, accounting for about 11.7% of new cancer cases [1]. In 2022, the global number of new cases of breast cancer was about 2.3 million, accounting for 11.6%, which had been the second highest incidence of cancer after lung cancer [2]. Breast cancer is not only the most common cancer in the world, but also the most prevalent cancer among Chinese women [3]. It seriously endangers women’s physical and mental health. In recent years, with the accelerated aging of China’s population, industrialization, urbanization and changes in lifestyle, the incidence rate of breast cancer has been on the rise year by year. According to the latest statistics of China’s National Cancer Center in 2022, the number of new cases of breast cancer in China was 357,200, accounting for 15.59% of the total number of new cancer cases in China. And the number of breast cancer deaths was 75,000, ranking seventh, accounting for 7.94% [4]. At the same time, the incidence rate and death rate of breast cancer have been increasing, with the incidence rate increasing by 3–4% annually [5], creating a certain disease burden for China.

In terms of molecular types, breast cancer can be categorized into LuminalA breast cancer, LuminalB breast cancer, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive (HER2+) breast cancer and triple-negative breast cancer. Hormone receptor-positive (HR+)(including LuminalA and LuminalB) breast cancer is the most common molecular type, accounting for 60-75% [6]. This breast cancer is dependent on the changes of estrogen or progesterone. It belongs to the tumor subtype, which is sensitive to endocrine therapy. And it is also the breast cancer subtype with better prognosis. For patients with advanced HR + breast cancer, endocrine therapy-based treatment programs are predominant [7]. Endocrine therapy is to reduce the level of estrogen or affect its role, inhibit the division and proliferation of breast cancer cells, so as to control breast cancer. On the basis of the mechanism, endocrine therapy drugs can be divided into five categories, namely, selective estrogen receptor (ER) modulators, ovarian function inhibitors, aromatase inhibitors, ER down-regulators and sex hormone drugs. In recent years, many new drugs or treatments have been introduced to further extend the survival time of patients with advanced breast cancer. However, single endocrine therapy can lead to drug resistance, resulting in reduced clinical benefit. The emergence of cyclin dependent kinase (CDK) 4/6 inhibitors such as Abemaciclib has provided new treatment options for the treatment of HR + advanced breast cancer [8]. According to the Breast Cancer Guidelines (2023), aromatase inhibitors in combination with CDK4/6 inhibitors are the preferred first-line endocrine therapy for the treatment of postmenopausal HR + breast cancer patients [9].

Anastrozole is a kind of nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor (NSAI), belonging to the 3rd generation of aromatase inhibitors. It is indicated for the first-line treatment of postmenopausal women with HR + locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer [10]. Anastrozole can reduce the amount of estrogen in post-menopausal women’s bodies, thus slowing down the growth of breast tumors. Abemaciclib is the first CDK4/6 inhibitor approved for adjuvant treatment of breast cancer, and it is used in combination with aromatase inhibitors as the initial endocrine therapy for postmenopausal HR + patients with locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer [11].

The MONARCH 3 trial has demonstrated the safety and efficacy of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for the treatment of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer [12]. This clinical trial is a global, randomized, double-blind phase 3 clinical trial. After a median follow-up of approximately 39 months, PFS was 28.2 versus 14.8 months (hazard ratio [HR], 0.525; 95% confifidence interval [CI], 0.415–0.665; p < 0.0001) in Abemaciclib group (Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib) versus control group (Anastrozole used alone). The final study results showed that Abemaciclib in combination with NSAI first-line treatment for advanced breast cancer achieved a median overall survival (OS) of 66.8 months among all patients, a clinically meaningful prolongation of 13.1 months [13].

Although the clinical efficacy of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for the treatment of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer has been reported, there is a lack of economic evidence in China. This study aims to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib versus Anastrozole used alone for patients in China.

Resources and methods

Target population and treatment options

The target population of this study is consistent with the test population in the MONARCH 3 clinical trial, that is, HR + women with advanced breast cancer in any menopausal state. Patient and disease baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1 [14].

The two treatment regimens were as follows: patients were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive 150 mg of Abemaciclib twice daily in combination with Anastrozole 1 mg in the Abemaciclib group. And 150 mg of placebo twice daily in combination with Anastrozole 1 mg in the control group. All medications were administered orally daily for 28 days, and the regimens were continued until progressive disease (PD) or death occurred [14].

Clinical data

The data used in this study were derived from the MONARCH 3 trial clinical data. A total of 493 patients were enrolled in the trial, of which 328 (66.5%) were randomly assigned to the Abemaciclib group and 165 (33.5%) to the control group. The primary clinical endpoint of the trial was to compare progression-free survival (PFS) between the Abemaciclib and control groups. The key secondary endpoints were objective response rate (ORR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics (PK). The cumulative probability of three-year PFS was 41.3% and 16.1% in the Abemaciclib group and control group, respectively [13, 15].

Model structure

In this study, we constructed a Markov model using Treeage Pro 2022 software to analyze and compare the cost-utility of the Abemaciclib and control groups (Fig. 1). Based on the MONARCH 3 trial study, the following three health states were established: PFS, PD, and Death (Fig. 2) [16]. The model cycle was set to 1 month, the simulation timeline of the model was set to 20 years, totaling 240 cycles. And the model used a half-cycle correction to prevent overestimation of expected survival time. This study assumed that patients in the same cohort received the same treatment in the model. The proportion of patients in each health state at each time point in the model is determined from the OS and PFS curves. At the beginning of the model, all patients were stable and in a PFS state, with state transitions based on the corresponding probability. Patients could only be in one state during the same cycle, the death state was absorptive. And the model would terminate if all patients were in the death Markov state. The parameters in the model were taken from the MONARCH 3 trial and related literature.

Transition probability

In this study, the cumulative probability of an event during the observation time was obtained based on the number of patients in each state in the MONARCH 3 trial and related studies. The cumulative probability of 3-year PFS in the Abemaciclib group was 41.3%, while in the control group it was 16.1%. At the same time, the cumulative os rates of the Abemaciclib group and the control group were 72% and 67%, respectively [13, 15]. Since the enrolled patients were in the PFS state, the probability of cumulative PFS plus the probability of cumulative PD is equal to the cumulative OS rate. The transition probability in this model was obtained using the transformation formula: rate=-[ln(1-P1)]/(T/t1); Prob = 1-e− rate [17], where rate denotes the risk of a health event occurring within a Markov cycle time, T is the length of clinical study observation time, t1 is the Markov cycle length, and P1 is the cumulative probability of an event occurring within the time T of the clinical study observation. The transition probability from PFS to Death was set as the natural mortality rate of the Chinese population in 2022 [18]. For example, the rate of PFS→PFS is obtained by the formula rate=-[ln(1-P1)]/(T/t1), where P1 = 0.413, T/t1 = 36/1 (3 years = 36 months). And the Prob is obtained through the formula Prob = 1-e− rate, Prob = 0.02. PPFS−PD=1-PPFS−PFS-PPFS−Death=1−0.02−0.0074 = 0.9726. Cost, utilities, and discount rate

This study only considered the direct medical costs of the different interventions, including drug costs, routine follow-up costs, supportive treatment costs, and serious adverse reactions costs. In the MONARCH 3 trial, the most common grade 3 or 4 adverse reactions were diarrhea and neutropenia [19]. The two regimens had the same costs except for the drug costs. Drug cost data for this study were obtained from the winning price published at www.yaozh.com in China, and other costs were obtained by reviewing the literature. The consumer price index for the healthcare category was also consulted and converted to the 2023 price level before inputting into the model. The specific cost information is shown in Table 3.

In this study, the PFS and OS were chosen as the indexes for determining the effectiveness of the treatment. The QALY was used as the output index to measure the effectiveness of the treatment regime, and it was calculated as the survival time of a patient in a certain state multiplied by the health utility value for the same period. The utility data were derived from published literature, with a utility value of 0.84 for the PFS state, 0.69 for the PD state, and 0 for the Death state. This study also considered the disutility of severe adverse reactions: diarrhea was − 0.1 and neutropenia was − 0.14 (Table 3). The incidence of diarrhea and neutropenia in the Abemaciclib group was 9.4% and 21.1%, respectively. And the incidence of both diarrhea and neutropenia in the control group was 1.2% [20]. In addition, a discount rate of 5% per year was used for cost and utility in this study.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis is an analytical method of identifying sensitive factors and exploring the impact of uncertain factors on the results of the study, in order to better assess the reliability of the results. In this study, one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis were conducted. The one-way sensitivity analysis was used to deal with the uncertainties of the model. We changed the parameters values according to the preset upper and lower ranges of the parameters (± 20% of the mean value), in order to calculate the impacts of parameter changes on ICER. And the obtained results were used to draw the tornado diagram.

Uncertainties in the parameters were handled through probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Based on the distribution form of the parameters, 1000 Monte Carlo simulations was performed. After the Monte Carlo simulation, the expected incremental cost and QALY were obtained, and the incremental cost-effectiveness scatter plot as well as the cost-effectiveness acceptability curve (CEAC) were generated [35].

Results

Cost-effectiveness analysis

The results by base-case analysis are shown in Table 4. Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib provided more health benefits than Anastrozole used alone over the 20-year study timeline, but at the same time the total cost was higher. The ICER was ¥93,940.83/QALY, which was lower than the WTP threshold set in this study [36]. Thus, the treatment regimen of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib was cost-effective.

Sensitivity analysis

One-way sensitivity analysis

The results of the one-way sensitivity analysis are shown in Fig. 3. The utility of PD was the main factor that influencd ICER, followed by the utility of PFS. In addition, the various transition probabilities between PFS and PD as well as the cost of the Abemaciclib group also had an impact on the results. The other parameters had no significant effect on the results of the base-case analysis.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis

In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, the Monte Carlo simulation was set to run 1000 times. As shown in Fig. 4, when the WTP threshold was about ¥85,000/QALY, the probability of two regimens being cost-effective was the same (50%). With the increase of WTP threshold, the probability of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib being cost-effective gradually increased. When the WTP threshold was about ¥90,000/QALY, the probability of being cost-effective in the Abemaciclib group gradually reached 100%, which was much lower than the WTP threshold set in this study. The results didn’t differ much from the ICER of the base-case analysis, indicating that the ICER was robust.

At the same time, the sampling methods were set to Gamma distribution of cost parameters, and Beta distribution of utility as well as transition probability parameters. The cost-effectiveness scatter plot was shown in Fig. 5. In 1000 Monte Carlo simulations, the scatters of ICER were mostly located below the WTP threshold line, which proved that Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib had a higher probability of being cost-effective.

Discussion

Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib as a guideline-recommended first-line treatment regimen can effectively improve the disease symptoms of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer. At the same time, it can reduce the probability of resistance to single drug use [37]. The efficacy and safety of this regimen have been proved by the MONARCH 3 trial. Currently, some scholars have studied the cost-effectiveness of Ribociclib ((CDK) 4/6 inhibitors) combined with Letrozole (NSAI) for the treatment of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer [38], and some scholars have evaluated the cost-effectiveness of Abemaciclib plus Fulvestrant for the second-line treatment of women with HR+/HER2- advanced or metastatic breast cancer [39]. But no scholars have evaluated the cost-effectiveness of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for the treatment of breast cancer, and the cost-effectiveness of this regimen in China is still unknown. Thus, this study has a certain significance.

Through constructing a Markov model of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer, we evaluated the cost-effective of the intervention and found that the intervention of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib for patients resulted in higher health benefits than those in the control group, but at the same time, the healthcare costs were higher. Assuming 3 times the per-capita GDP of China in 2023 (¥268,074) as the reference threshold for WTP, the ICER would be about 36.5% of the set threshold. Therefore, under this assumption, Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib is more cost-effective than Anastrozole used alone.

In this study, the one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis confirmed that the results were robust when the parameters were changed. The one-way sensitivity analysis tornado diagram showed that the utilities of PD and PFS had a greater impact on the results, while the transition probability and drug cost also had influenced the results of this study. On the one hand, to accurately evaluate the cost-effectiveness of drugs, more large-sample, high-quality real-world favoritism studies are needed as evidence support for the precise determination of clinical health utility data [40]. On the other hand, research can be considered through the partitioned survival model (PSM) in the future, which doesn’t need to calculate the transition probability. It can directly extrapolate the survival curve to fit the proportion of patients in a certain state that avoids some of the calculation processes and modeling assumptions [41].

Through the systematic reviews of economic evaluations, we found that the existing research are mostly studied from the payer’s perspective, which are same as our study. Because it is difficult to calculate the indirect costs. But the impact of the indirect costs on the ICER shouldn’t be ignored. In the future, we should focus more attention on the consideration of the indirect costs, and consider all the stakeholders from the perspective of the whole society [42–47]. Besides, almost all the studies use the Markov model of three health states, which in fact is also somewhat different from the reality of breast cancer disease occurrence. So the resulting calculated ICER doesn’t meet the typical WTP value. And now, the model setup period as well as the discount rate are not exactly same in every research [48]. It is recommended to develop a reference model for the same disease type to ensure the consistency of the study quality.

WTP plays a major role in China’s healthcare system. Currently, there is no unanimously recognized WTP threshold in China. We generally use 1–3 times the per-capita GDP of China as the WTP threshold [49]. However, GDP varies from region to region, which may make a difference in the cost-effective of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib. When using the results of this study, a reasonable judgment should be made based on the local economic development situation. In the future, it is still necessary for China to make a more adequate and comprehensive consideration of the selection of WTP threshold, so as to ensure that pharmacoeconomics plays its due role in guiding the rational allocation of health resources.

In addition, Abemaciclib and Anastrozole are both drugs included in priority review and approval in China, which focuse on clinical value. The priority review and approval procedure can shorten the drug review time, prioritize the allocation of review resources and meet the urgent needs of patients [50]. Therefore, Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib is a cost-effective and accessible treatment regimen for postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer.

Although a variety of targeted drugs such as Anastrozole have been put into clinical use, their therapeutic effects are limited in practice, and the problem of drug resistance is becoming increasingly prominent. Therefore, it is still necessary to continuously search for new therapeutic targets and increase the research and development of targeted drugs. Meanwhile, anti-tumor drugs need to optimize their pricing mechanisms. At present, many drugs are effective, but the cost is higher, and a balance needs to be made between treatment costs and efficacy. Many patients miss treatment opportunities in the process of considering the cost of treatment. Therefore, the fairness and accessibility of drug prices should be considered in the process of optimizing the pricing mechanism. And it is suggested that enterprises and national drug regulatory authorities strengthen their supervision of drug prices and other treatment costs. It is more important to promote the improvement of medical insurance policies, and include more anti-tumor drugs in the medical insurance catalogue to reduce the drug burden of patients.

The major advantage of this study was that the findings were based on a global, randomized, double-blind phase 3 clinical trial, which allowed data to be obtained directly from the trial, and results were more accurate. What’s more, the 20-year time horizon used in this study could capture the long-term impacts of Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib in the treatment of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer. The results of the study might provide evidence for clinical practice of selecting long-term treatment.

This study also had some limitations. First, the transition probability was an important parameter for the evaluation of this study, and the data in this study were obtained from the MONARCH 3 trial as well as related literature. However, the MONARCH 3 trial period couldn’t satisfy the time range in the Markov model, and the survival data of the trial couldn’t be accurately obtained for survival curve fitting. Therefore, time-dependent transition probabilities were not chosen for the simulation, which would have caused some deviation of the results from the actual situation. Second, the MONARCH 3 trial was a western study. Due to the cultural and racial differences in different countries and regions, it might also bias the results to a certain extent. But the one-way sensitivity analysis confirmed the study results were robust. There is still a need to conduct multi-center, large-sample real-world clinical studies on Chinese patients in the future [51]. In addition, limited by the availability of data, only direct medical costs were considered in this study, the direct non-medical costs, indirect costs and hidden costs were not taken into account. There is still a long way for more detailed studies on costs in the future.

In conclusion, Anastrozole combined with Abemaciclib is more cost-effective than Anastrozole used alone for the treatment of postmenopausal HR + advanced breast cancer. This evidence-based information can guide rational clinical drug use and health decision-making in China.

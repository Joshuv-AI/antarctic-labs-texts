Determining the dynamics of maternally derived antibodies in piglets prior to vaccination against porcine circovirus type 2
Authors: Makoto UKITA, Yukine KANO, Saya TAHARAGUCHI, Tadashi TAKINO, Kohei MAKITA, Katsuro HAGIWARA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPorcine circovirus type 2 (PCV2) is the major causative agent of porcine circovirus-associated disease. We investigated the dynamics of maternally derived anti-PCV2 antibodies (MDA) in 40 piglets born to five healthy, unvaccinated sows on a Japanese swine farm. Anti-PCV2 antibody levels were measured by ELISA from birth to 19 days of age and analyzed together with serum total protein and IgG concentrations. Data were stratified by sow, and uncertainty was estimated using sow-cluster bootstrap analysis. The S/P ratio increased after colostrum intake, peaked at 1–3 days of age, and then declined exponentially until day 19. These findings characterize pre-vaccination anti-PCV2 MDA kinetics and indicate that serum total protein at birth may reflect passive immune status.Keywords: maternally derived antibody, passive transfer, piglet, porcine circovirus type 2, serum total protein

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV2) is commonly associated with several clinical syndromes and diseases, collectively referred to as porcine circovirus-associated disease (PCVAD) [20], in weaned piglets between 5 and 12 weeks of age [2] and has a significant economic impact on the global swine industry [4]. It is a member of the genus Circovirus, family Circoviridae, the smallest non-enveloped, single-stranded, circular DNA virus that replicates autonomously in mammalian cells [1]. It is currently classified into eight genotypes, designated PCV2a to PCV2h [8, 13]. The first commercial vaccines became available in Europe in 2004 and in the United States in 2006, and after the introduction of the PCV2 vaccine, mortality decreased, and production efficiency improved [5, 7, 11, 12, 18]. However, in 2012, cases of porcine circovirus-associated disease (PCVAD) caused by PCV2d were reported in North America on farms using the PCV2a-based vaccine [19]. Currently, PCV2d-2 has emerged as the predominant genotype worldwide [9], and control of PCVAD on farms is a challenge worldwide [12].

In Japanese commercial farms, piglets are typically vaccinated against PCV2 at three weeks of age, after which they are moved to weaning pens and raised in herds. Previous studies tracking anti-PCV2 antibodies in piglets at three weeks of age (at vaccination) and older (post-vaccination) have shown that the immune response is based on the level of maternally derived antibodies (MDA) at the time of vaccination in experimental and field studies [15, 23]. However, there are few studies that have measured anti-PCV2 MDA in serum from immediately after birth through to vaccination [10], and there are no studies and limited studies evaluating other factors affecting the dynamics of MDA.

Piglet MDA is known to be dependent on sow antibody levels [3]; if MDA is low, decline is more rapid, and there is a risk of loss of pre-vaccination protection. Piglets infected with PCV2 prior to vaccination represent a source of infection within the weaning herd and a challenge for disease control. Understanding the relationship between MDA dynamics and other relevant factors in piglets from birth to pre-vaccination would be useful in predicting high-risk piglets for PCV infection during the neonatal to growing period.

The present study was designed as a pre-vaccination kinetics investigation to generate practical parameter estimates relevant to vaccination schedule planning in piglets. Specifically, we aimed to (i) describe the early-life trajectory of maternally derived anti-PCV2 antibodies (including the immediate postnatal peak and subsequent decline), (ii) estimate decay parameters (including an approximate half-life) while quantifying sow-to-sow variability using sow-stratified reporting and cluster-aware inference, and (iii) evaluate piglet-level factors—particularly serum total protein at birth—as practical proxies associated with baseline passive transfer. By focusing on the neonatal period from birth through three weeks of age, this study provides design-oriented inputs for planning vaccination timing/boosting strategies and for identifying heterogeneity in pre-vaccination MDA levels under field conditions.

Serum samples were collected from 40 piglets born to five healthy sows (eight piglets per sow) on Japanese farms with a history of PCV2 infection; the sows had not been vaccinated against PCV2. Clinically healthy sows with stable reproductive performance were selected. The sows were Landrace × Large White crossbreds (LW), and the piglets were LWD crossbreds produced by mating the LW sows with Duroc boars. Litter sizes ranged from 8 to 10 piglets, and eight piglets with relatively high body weight were selected from each litter. All piglets were reared under identical management conditions. One piglet died on day 0, leaving 39 piglets with follow-up at day 19. Blood was collected from piglets at 0, 1, 3, 5, and 19 days of age (March–April 2019 farrowing), covering the period from after colostrum intake to a few days before routine piglet vaccination at 3 weeks of age. These sera were secondarily used from a study conducted by the Scientific Feed Laboratory Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan). Additional analyses were approved by the Scientific Feed Laboratory Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (Approval No. 19-S011). The parity of sows A–E was 2, 5, 6, 6, and 8, respectively. Antibody data for sows were not available. Piglet body weights were recorded on days 0, 5, 12, and 19.

Anti-PCV2 antibody S/P ratios in piglet sera were assessed using a commercially available indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA; Porcine Circovirus type 2 antibody test kit; BioChek, B.V., Reeuwijk, The Netherlands) according to the manufacturer’s instructions [23]. Serum samples were collected at 0, 1, 3, 5, and 19 days of age, representing the period from after colostrum intake to a few days before routine vaccination (typically at 3 weeks of age). MDA levels were expressed as the S/P ratio; an S/P ratio of 0.5 or greater was considered positive per the kit instructions. Each sample was assayed in duplicate; samples with an absorbance difference of 0.1 or more between duplicates were retested.

The resulting S/P ratios at 3 and 19 days of age were used to calculate the decline slope for each litter and to estimate the half-life of antibodies. Day 3 was selected because the peak S/P ratio was observed between days 1 and 3.

Serum IgG was determined as an indicator of colostrum intake. Colostrum-derived IgG in piglet serum has been reported to peak at 2 days of age [25]; therefore, serum IgG concentrations were determined at 1 and 3 days of age in the present study. IgG was quantified using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Rockford, IL, USA) [24], with a porcine IgG ELISA standard (Mabtech, Nacka Strand, Sweden) as a reference. Pilot analyses were performed to identify dilution factors for standards and samples that fit the standard curve; the diluent consisted of phosphate-buffered saline supplemented with 0.05% Tween 20 and 0.1% bovine serum albumin. The standard curve was plotted as log10 standard concentration versus optical density and fit using linear regression across four points.

Serum total protein was determined in piglets at 0, 1, 3, 5, and 19 days of age to evaluate relationships between anti-PCV2 MDA and total protein as a surrogate marker of passive transfer. Total protein concentrations were measured using a digital pocket blood serum refractometer (PAL-11S; ATAGO, Tokyo, Japan). Each sample was measured repeatedly until a stable value was obtained; the stabilized value was recorded.

Because piglets within the same litter are not independent, analyses that treat piglets as independent (e.g., Kruskal–Wallis or Wilcoxon tests for litter comparisons) were not used. Because only five sows were included, sow-level predictors such as parity could not be reliably estimated (parity was confounded with sow). Accordingly, descriptive statistics (median and interquartile range) were presented for each sampling day, stratified by sow.

Associations between piglet-level factors (total protein, body weight, and sex) and outcomes were analyzed using univariable regression with fixed sow effects (i.e., within-sow comparisons). Outcomes were (1) baseline ln (S/P) at day 0 and (2) the ln (S/P) decay slope from days 3 to 19. Days 3–19 were selected for the exponential decay model to exclude the immediate post-colostral phase (days 1–3), during which antibody concentrations are influenced by ongoing colostrum absorption and distribution and do not yet reflect a stable decay process. A multivariable model including all three predictors was then fit to estimate independent effects. Uncertainty was assessed using a sow-cluster bootstrap (55=3,125 resamples) to obtain 95% confidence intervals (CI). Effect estimates were reported as the median of the bootstrap distribution, and two-sided bootstrap P-values were calculated from the proportion of resamples crossing zero. For interpretive assistance, ln (S/P) at day 19 was also analyzed as a secondary outcome. As a robustness check, a linear mixed-effects model with sow as a random intercept was fit to the day-19 data.

Across sows, piglet S/P ratios increased from birth and peaked around postnatal days 1–3, then declined through day 19 (sow-stratified spaghetti plots; Fig. 1). Using sow-level medians to respect clustering, the overall median S/P ratio decreased from 1.817 at day 0 to 1.183 at day 19 (Fig. 2). All piglets had ELISA S/P ratios above 0.5 at day 19. An exponential decay model fitted over days 3–19 yielded an approximate half-life of 20.9 days (sow-cluster bootstrap 95% CI 16.9–28.2). The IgG peak was observed in 19 piglets at 1 day of age and in 20 piglets at 3 days of age, and the range of sow-level median IgG concentrations was 22.9–34.4 mg/mL.

In univariable analyses, male sex was associated with higher baseline ln (S/P) (β=0.035; 95% CI 0.004–0.065; P=0.019) and a slower decay slope (β=0.007; 95% CI 0.0005–0.012; P=0.031). Serum total protein was positively associated with both outcomes, while birth weight showed no significant associations (Supplementary Table 1). In multivariable analyses, higher serum total protein was significantly associated with higher baseline ln (S/P) (β=0.026; 95% CI 0.005–0.037; P=0.010), whereas birth weight and sex showed no significant associations. No variable was significantly associated with the decay slope of ln (S/P) (Table 1).

Sensitivity analyses using day-19 ln (S/P) as the outcome showed broadly similar results between sow fixed-effects and mixed-effects models (Supplementary Table 2). In the mixed-effects model, sex (male) was significantly associated with higher day-19 ln (S/P) (β=0.096; 95% CI 0.014–0.200; P=0.022), while serum total protein and birth weight were not significant. The sow fixed-effects model showed a similar trend for sex, but it did not reach statistical significance (P=0.079).

This study investigated the dynamics of anti-PCV2 maternal antibodies (MDA) in piglets from five healthy sows from birth to 19 days of age. The S/P ratio peaked between days 1 and 3, then declined until day 19, demonstrating significant variation among sows. The estimated half-life from days 3 to 19 was approximately 20.9 days (sow-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence interval [CI]: 16.9–28.2), which is comparable to the 17.7 days reported in a previous study [22]. While S/P ratio ≥ 0.5 indicates seropositivity according to the manufacturer’s criteria, its relationship with protective immunity has not been fully established. McKeown et al. reported that high antibody levels are generally associated with reduced susceptibility to infection [16]. In this study, all piglets maintained an S/P ratio above 0.5 until day 19, consistent with persistence of MDA prior to vaccination. No PCV2-related clinical signs were observed during the study period.

Total serum protein at birth showed a positive correlation with baseline ln (S/P), suggesting its usefulness as a practical indicator of passive immune transfer status in newborn piglets. Baseline MDA levels and their subsequent decline exhibit significant variability between sows and within litters. Univariate analysis of this data suggested a possible association between total protein and early decline rate, but multivariate analysis did not maintain significance. A sensitivity analysis was conducted using the ln (S/P) of 19-day-old piglets as the outcome. The results showed similar patterns between the fixed-effect model and the mixed-effects model for sows. This suggests that the key conclusions regarding sow clustering and baseline correlations are robust to model specification. Sex-related differences observed in univariable analyses were not consistently supported across multivariable and sensitivity analyses, and thus may reflect exploratory patterns rather than robust biological effects. This study provides information on postnatal maternal antibody (MDA) dynamics, stratified by sow and changes in antibodies within the same sow. However, factors such as sow parity could not be estimated due to the limited sample size. In addition, the lack of data on maternal PCV2 antibody levels and infection history limited the assessment of relationships between sow and piglet antibody levels and their biological interpretation. The study was not intended to validate PCV2 infection risk predictors or evaluate vaccine efficacy. Previous studies have reported higher IgG concentrations in piglets from multiparous sows than in piglets from primiparous sows [17, 21]. Future studies should include sows with a vaccination history to verify sow-level determinants and optimize the timing of vaccinations and the management of piglets. The results should be interpreted as descriptive observations of early postnatal antibody dynamics under field conditions, and the possibility of subclinical infection cannot be completely excluded. IgG measurements reflect total immunoglobulin levels rather than PCV2-specific antibodies and may therefore not exclusively represent PCV2-specific immune status.

Overall, sufficient maternal antibody (MDA) was observed in piglets under appropriate husbandry conditions, suggesting a certain level of protection. However, given the widespread prevalence of PCV2 in the swine industry, adequate protection cannot be achieved without proper vaccination [6]. Furthermore, PCV2 exhibits high environmental stability [14]. Given the constant risk of exposure, the findings from this study may provide preliminary descriptive information that could be considered in the context of piglet protection against PCV2 infection under field condition.

In conclusion, this study provides pre-vaccination parameter estimates, stratified by sow, describing anti-PCV2 maternal antibody dynamics in piglets during the first three weeks of life. The S/P ratio of piglets peaked around days 1–3 and declined until day 19, with clear variability between sows. An exponential decay model over days 3–19 yielded an approximate half-life of 20.9 days. Serum total protein at birth was positively associated with baseline MDA, suggesting that it could be a useful proxy for passive transfer status in the field. These results, which are oriented towards the design of studies, can inform the planning of vaccination schedules (timing and boosting) and provide a quantitative reference for evaluating heterogeneity in pre-vaccination MDA under field conditions.

Adebrelimab plus chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC): a single-center, phase II, single-arm, open-label clinical trial (ADENEO) protocol
Authors: Peng Tang, Linrui Gao, Siyu Zuo, Ke Zhang, Tierun Wang, Jian Zheng, Xi Chen, Jie Dong, Qingsong Pang, Ping Wang, Wencheng Zhang, Tian Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionWhile radical chemoradiotherpy (CRT) is the standard treatment for patients with unresectable locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (LA-ESCC), the prognosis remains extremely poor. Although combining CRT with immunotherapy benefits a subset of patients with advanced disease, recurrence and metastasis after treatment are still common. The overall response rate (ORR) of induction chemoimmunotherapy (ICI) for advanced esophageal cancer is approximately 40%-70%. Radiotherapy or concurrent chemoradiotherapy after induction of chemoradiation is more effective. This study aims to tailor subsequent treatment based on the response to ICI to improve therapeutic efficacy for LA-ESCC.Methods and analysisThe ADENEO trial is a single center, phase II, single-arm, open-label clinical trial that will enroll 60 patients. Eligible patients will be registered, enrolled and receive 2 cycles of adebrelimab plus albumin paclitaxel and carboplatin, and the efficacy will be evaluated. Patients will be divided into two groups according to the efficacy of induction therapy: complete response + partial response (CR + PR) group and stable disease + progression disease (SD + PD) group. In the CR + PR group, patients will be treated with CCRT combined with adebrelimab. The radiotherapy dose is planning target volume (PTV): 50.4 Gy/1.8 Gy/28F. Chemoimmunotherapy will consist of adebrelimab plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and carboplatin, followed by adebrelimab maintenance for up to one year. Patients in the SD + PD group will receive CCRT alone. The radiotherapy dose is PTV/planned gross tumor volume (PGTV): 50.4 Gy/59.92 Gy/28F. The chemotherapy regimen will change to FOLFOX regimen (oxaliplatin, tetrahydrofolic acid and 5-FU) and immunotherapy will not be used during CRT because of tolerance to previous immunotherapy. The primary endpoint is the progression free survival (PFS) assessed by the investigators, and the secondary endpoints are ORR, overall survival (OS), duration of response (DoR), adverse event (AE) and serious adverse time (SAE).Ethics and disseminationWritten informed consent will be obtained from all patients enrolled. The study protocol been approved by the Independent Ethical Committee of Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute & Hospital. Introduction

Esophageal cancer is a prevalent malignancy associated with a dismal prognosis worldwide. According to the Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN) 2022 estimates, it accounts for approximately 604,000 new annual cases, ranking eighth in incidence, and 544,000 deaths, ranking sixth globally. Esophageal cancer can be divided into esophageal adenocarcinoma and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), according to the pathological type. China has the highest annual number of new cases and deaths from esophageal cancer, with 224,000 new cases and 187,500 deaths in 2022, over 90% of which are ESCC [1]. Due to the insidious onset of esophageal cancer, more than half of the patients present with locally advanced or metastatic disease at diagnosis.

Standard protocol for the treatment of locally advanced esophageal cancer

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines recommend radical concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) with a fluorouracil- or taxane-based regimen combined with platinum-based agents as the standard-of-care for patients with inoperable locally advanced and inoperable esophageal cancer. However, approximately 50% of patients experience recurrence or metastasis within two years following standard CCRT, and the 5-year overall survival (OS) is only 30–40% [2, 3]. Modifications in chemotherapy regimens or radiotherapy techniques have not yielded significant survival improvements [4–6]. Therefore, although CCRT is the standard treatment for patients with unresectable locally advanced esophageal cancer (LAEC), the prognosis remains poor owing to its highly invasive characteristics, and more accurate combination therapy is urgently needed.

Immunotherapy improves the treatment effect of esophageal cancer

Immune checkpoint inhibitors make significant difference in oncology therapeutics. Key clinical trials, including Keymate-590 (pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy), CheckMate 648 (nivolumab with ipilimumab or chemotherapy), ESCORT-1st (camrelizumab plus chemotherapy), ORIENT-15 (sintilimab plus chemotherapy), and JUPITER-06 (toripalimab plus chemotherapy), have consistently demonstrated significant improvements in OS for patients with advanced first-line esophageal cancer, compared to chemotherapy alone [7–10]. For operable esophageal cancer, the CheckMate 577 study showed that postoperative maintenance of nivolumab can significantly improve the disease-free survival (DFS) rate of patients with non-pathological complete response (pCR) after preoperative chemoradiotherapy, highlighting the importance of immunotherapy in postoperative maintenance [11].

The combination of radiotherapy and immunotherapy may achieve synergistic anti-tumor effects, which provides a theoretical basis for the combined application of immune checkpoint inhibitors with chemoradiotherapy [12, 13]. Several phase I/II single-arm trials have been conducted in patients with unresectable LAEC, exploring the combination of (chemo) radiotherapy and immunotherapy [14–21]. For instance, Zhang et al. reported that in 20 patients with locally advanced ESCC treated with camrelizumab plus CCRT, the 24-month OS and progression-free survival (PFS) rates were 69.6% and 65.0%, respectively. The incidence of grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was 45%, including 20% grade ≥ 3 radiation esophagitis and 10% grade ≥ 3 esophageal fistula, with no grade ≥ 3 radiation pneumonia observed [16]. Xi et al. treated 42 untreated, unresectable stage II to IVA ESCC patients with toripalimab combined with radical chemoradiotherapy. The results showed that the 1-year OS and PFS rates were 78.4% and 54.5%, respectively [21]. In terms of current evidence, small-sample single-arm phase I/II clinical trials have shown that CCRT with immune checkpoint inhibitors is more effective than conventional CRT for the treatment of LAEC, with good tolerance and controllable toxicity. Ongoing phase III randomized controlled trials (e.g., ESCORT-CRT, KEYNOTE-975, RATIONALE-311, KUNLUN) are poised to provide further validation.

It is urgent to find an ideal therapeutic effect prediction method

Although the combination of CRT and immunotherapy benefits some patients with advanced esophageal cancer, a substantial proportion still experience recurrence and metastases within two years post-treatment. A major research focus is the identification of effective predictive biomarkers. Clinically explored biomarkers include PD-L1 expression levels, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and microsatellite instability (MSI)/mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) status. PD-L1 expression has been extensively studied. The ESCORT study showed that patients with PD-L1 positive expression in the camrelizumab group had clinical benefits, and patients with PD-L1 positive expression TPS ≥ 10% had longer survival than patients with low expression [22]. However, its predictive value appears diminished in the context of combined radiotherapy [23].

In addition to biomarkers, induction therapy can not only reduce tumor burden and distant metastasis but also holds significant potential for predicting response to subsequent CRT. A Phase II clinical study found that patients who were effectively treated with induction chemotherapy had a significant survival benefit compared with those who did not respond to induction chemotherapy after receiving two cycles of induction chemotherapy. emerged as a stronger predictor of survival than established biomarkers, with 3-year OS rates of 80.0% versus 10.0% and 3-year PFS rates of 55.3% and 10.0%, respectively. This striking difference suggests that response to induction chemotherapy constitutes a potent predictor for radical CRT outcomes [24]. Deng et al. carried out a study on radiotherapy after ICI in patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic esophageal cancer, and the results showed that the 2-year OS and 2-year PFS rates of the ICIs + RT and RT groups were 57.9% and 43.0% (p = 0.050), and 45.7% and 35.8% (p = 0.045), respectively. The 1-year local recurrence rate (LRR) in the ICIs + RT group than in the RT group (17.4% vs. 38.8%, p = 0.011) [25]. These findings support the feasibility of using chemoimmunotherapy as a neoadjuvant approach prior to radical CRT. For patients refractory to induction immunochemotherapy, optimizing the subsequent treatment plan is critically needed.

Evidence also indicates that patients achieving a major pathological response (MPR) have improved better survival, with 2-year OS rates of 91.4% and 47.7%, respectively [24]. Local recurrence and distant metastasis rates were higher in patients who were not sensitive to induction therapy than in those who were effective in induction therapy [26]. Therefore, to further improve the therapeutic effect of patients with inoperable LAEC, tailoring treatment intensity based on response to induction therapy represents a promising strategy.

Radical CRT was conducted according to different therapeutic effects by stratification

Adebrelimab is a high-affinity, humanized immunoglobulin G4 anti-programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) monoclonal antibody which can specifically bind to PD-L1. It functions by specifically binding to PD-L1, thereby blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling pathway. This restores T-cell-mediated immune responses against tumor cells, stimulating an anti-tumor effect. Adebrelimab has shown efficacy and safety against advanced ESCC, extensive small cell lung cancer (SCLC), and resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [27, 28]. A phase 1b trial investigating neoadjuvant adebrelimab in locally advanced resectable ESCC reported safety and feasibility as primary endpoints, with pathological complete response (pCR) and OS as secondary endpoints. The most common TRAEs were anorexia (32%) and fatigue (16%), with no grade 3 or higher adverse events reported. Among the 30 enrolled patients, 25 underwent successful resection without surgical delay, achieving an MPR rate of 24%, a pCR rate of 8%, and a remarkable 2-year OS rate of 92%. These results establish the favorable safety and feasibility profile of adebrelimab in resectable LAEC [29].

Based on previous studies, this clinical trial used two cycles of ICI for locally advanced ESCC and evaluated its efficacy after the end of induction therapy. Patients with effective induction therapy (CR + PR group) were treated with concurrent CRT combined with immunotherapy, whereas patients with ineffective induction therapy (SD + PD group) were treated with different radiotherapy doses and chemotherapy regimens during concurrent CRT to improve the overall treatment effect. This study is the first to evaluate an "induction PD-L1 inhibitor combined with chemotherapy" strategy for inoperable LA-ESCC, aiming to explore a response-adapted treatment paradigm.

Methods and analysis

Trial design

This was a single-center phase II study involving patients with inoperable, locally advanced ESCC. Eligible patients will be registered, enrolled, and receive two cycles of adebrelimab combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and carboplatin. The patients will be divided into complete response + partial response (CR + PR) group and stable disease + progression disease (SD + PD) group according to the efficacy of induction therapy. In the CR + PR group, patients will be treated with CCRT combined with adebrelimab. The radiotherapy dose is PTV: 50.4 Gy/1.8 Gy/28F. Chemoimmunotherapy is adebrelimab plus albumin paclitaxel and carboplatin, and adebrelimab will maintain for one year. In the SD + PD group, the patients will be treated with CCRT. The radiotherapy dose is PTV/PGTV: 50.4 Gy/59.92 Gy/28F. The concurrent chemotherapy regimen will be switched to FOLFOX (oxaliplatin, leucovorin, and fluorouracil), and immunotherapy will be withheld during CRT due to prior tolerance concerns (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Study design. Response evaluation criteria

Tumor response will be evaluated after two cycles of ICI and then every 9 weeks during the first year, followed by evaluations every 3 months. Assessment will be performed by investigators according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST; version 1.1) [30] and Japanese Classification of Esophageal Cancer (12th Edition: Part II) [31]. Two experienced radiologists (PT and LRG) will independently evaluate responses using computed tomography (CT) and esophagoscopy with bite-on-bite biopsies. Tumor response will be assessed by CT scan and based on its short diameter. Complete response was defined as the disappearance of the primary tumor with no stenosis, ulceration, or budding, and no new lesions or local progression on CT scan [24, 32]. Objectives and end points

Study objectives

Primary

To evaluate the efficacy of induction chemoimmunotherapy combined with concurrent chemoradiotherapy in the treatment of patients with locally advanced inoperable esophageal squamous carcinoma.

Secondary

To evaluate the safety of induction chemoimmunotherapy combined with concurrent chemoradiotherapy in the treatment of patients with locally advanced inoperable esophageal squamous carcinoma.

Translational/exploratory

To explore the relationship between efficacy and PD-L1 expression in tumor tissue, the immune microenvironment, and plasma cytokine levels.

End points

Primary

PFS assessed by the investigators.

Secondary

Objective response rate (ORR)

Overall survival (OS)

Duration of response (DoR)

Safety: Adverse event (AE), serious adverse time (SAE).

Exploratory

The correlation between efficacy and PD-L1 expression, the immune microenvironment, and plasma cytokines.

Biomarker analysis and translational research

Serial biopsies and peripheral blood will be collected from the primary site at four time points, when feasible (before treatment, after ICI, after 20 fractions of radiotherapy, and upon disease progression). Tumor tissue samples obtained within 6 months prior to enrollment are preferred. Acceptable samples include formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue blocks or at least five FFPE tumor tissue sections of 4 μm thickness. PD-L1 expression will be assessed clinically using the VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) assay on the BenchMark ULTRA automated staining platform (Roche Diagnostics) following the manufacturer’s recommended protocols. Peripheral blood will be collected and then extracted by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 15 min at room temperature and immediately stored in a −80 °C refrigerator for subsequent exploratory analyses. To identify predictive biomarkers of treatment response in our trial cohort, we will perform PD-L1 expression assessment along with integrated whole-exome sequencing and Olink proteomics. This approach combines the assessment of PD-L1 with deep interrogation of localized tumor genomic alterations, and a systemic view of circulating immune/inflammatory proteins.

Sample size calculation

This is a single-arm Phase II clinical study, and the sample size was calculated based on the primary study endpoint and investigator-assessed PFS.

The sample size was calculated as follows: the median 2-year PFS of the historical control of radical CRT was 43%, the 2-year PFS of the experimental group was assumed to be 60%, HR = 0.60, unilateral α = 0.05, the assurance was 80%, the enrollment time was 18 months, and the follow-up time was 18 months. PASS 2021 was used to estimate that 23 cases of PFS events would be required and 53 cases would need to be enrolled. Considering 10% shedding, 60 patients were enrolled in the study.Historical controls for radical CRT show a median 2-year PFS of 43%. Assuming an improvement to 60% 2-year PFS (HR = 0.60) in the experimental group, with a one-sided α of 0.05 and 80% power, an enrollment period of 18 months, and a follow-up period of 18 months, PASS 2021 software estimated that 23 PFS events would require enrolling 53 patients. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, the total sample size was set at 60 patients.

Follow-up

Patients are followed every 3 months for the first year after treatment completion for survival and subsequent anti-tumor therapy assessment. Thereafter, follow-up occurs every 6 months until death, loss to follow-up, withdrawal of consent, or study closure.

Tumor evaluation will primarily use imaging, with options for additional methods such as ultrasonic gastroscope, CT, etc., if deemed necessary. For patients who discontinue the study and treatment for reasons other than disease progression or death, tumor evaluation will follow the original plan until the disease progresses. The overall duration of this trial is anticipated to be 3 years.

Statistical analysis

The primary endpoint involves PFS assessed by the investigators, which will be analysed based on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population. Descriptive statistics will be used for clinicopathological characteristics and safety evaluation. Mean values and SD will be provided for continuous endpoints, while frequency and percentage distributions will be presented for discrete data. Kaplan–Meier methodology will estimate PFS and OS in the ITT population. Univariate and multivariate survival analyses will use the Cox proportional hazard model, with HRs and 95% CI calculated. Statistical analyses will employ SPSS V.25.0 software (IBM) and R software V.3.6.2. The significance level is set at p < 0.05, and all statistical tests will be two sided. The study is designed to detect a 23% absolute difference in response rates with 90% power and a two-sided alpha of 0.05.

Discussion

This single-center, phase II clinical trial aimed to enroll 60 patients with inoperable locally advanced ESCC and to assess the efficacy and safety of adebrelimab plus chemotherapy. It is the first study to investigate an “induction PD-L1 inhibitor combined with chemotherapy” strategy followed by response-adapted CRT for this population (NCT06452602).

Although radical CRT is the standard for unresectable LAEC, prognosis remains poor, and more accurate combination therapy is urgently needed. Combining CRT with immunotherapy holds promise for improved outcomes. However, despite benefits for some, a significant proportion of patients still experience recurrence within two years [16]. Current research focuses on stratifying therapy based on initial response to induction treatment. A previous study reported that those who received two cycles of induction chemotherapy had a significant survival benefit compared with those who did not receive induction chemotherapy, and the efficacy of induction chemotherapy predicted survival better than reported biomarkers [24].

FOLFOX and paclitaxel-based chemotherapy was proved satisfactory effectiveness and safety in previous studies [32–35]. In this study, the efficacy will be evaluated after two cycles of adebrelimab plus albumin paclitaxel and carboplatin. Previous studies reported that non-responders after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (stable or progressive disease) had worse 5-year survival rates than responders (complete or partial response, 22.3% vs 45.1%) [26]. Thus, the patients were divided into CR + PR group and SD + PD group according to the efficacy of ICI. The FFCD 9102 trial supports the omission of surgery in responders to chemoradiation [36]. For SD + PD group, high dose (59.4 Gy) was used to improve the local control which is supported by our previous participation in NROG-001 trial.

Endoscopic ultrasonography with bite examination, CT, and upper gastrointestinal angiography will be used to evaluate the efficacy of ICI according to previous studies [24, 32, 37]. While 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (PET) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) show promise for predicting response [38], their limitations in sensitivity and positive predictive value [39, 40]. In many large phase 3 clinical trials (RATIONALE-306 [41], KEYNOTE-590 [9], JUPITER-06 [7], NCT01937208 [5], ChiCTR-IPR-15007172 [42], etc.), PET-CT was not used as a routine or mandatory method for tumor assessment, the radiological tumor response was evalutated according to RECIST version 1.1. Only a small portion of enrolled patients had PET/CT staging. In NCT01937208 trial, the actual percentage of patients who received the PET/CT staging was only 15.4% (49 of 319) [5]. Although many studies have reported the significance of evaluating responses using PET–CT, there is not sufficient standardisation or evidence to abandon anatomical assessment of tumor burden. Its outcomes are affected by equipment, measurement conditions, blood sugar level or inflammation (reflux esophagitis, radiation pneumonitis, etc.). Furthermore, which index is the most appropriate (maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), SUV normalized by lean body mass (SUL), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), or total lesion glycolysis (TLG)) for response evaluation using PET–CT still remains unclear [31]. However, the PET portion of the CT introduces additional data which may bias an investigator if it is not routinely or serially performed [30]. Therefore, PET-CT is not mandatory used as an evaluation method in this study.

This study has several imitations, including its single-arm, single-center design focused solely on ESCC, and the lack of a CCRT-only control group. Additionally, PET-CT is not mandatorily used to evaluate the efficacy of induction immunochemotherapy. If the current phase II results are promising, a subsequent phase III randomized controlled trial including a control arm will be considered. The combination of immune checkpoint inhibitor and radiotherapy may exert a synergistic anti-tumor effect, which is supported by theoretical feasibility and preliminary clinical research results. Induction immunotherapy can effectively stratify patients and optimize treatment plans, which may further improve PFS and prolong OS in all patients. This trial may identify two distinct patient subgroups: responders for whom CCRT with immunotherapy is effective, and non-responders requiring alternative strategies. The findings will contribute significantly to personalizing treatment for esophageal cancer.

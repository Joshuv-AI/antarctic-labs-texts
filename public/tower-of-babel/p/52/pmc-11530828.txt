Ayurvedic management of hydatid cyst of lungs: A case report
Authors: Praveen K. Yadav, Pradeep S. Shindhe, Ramesh S. Killedar, Mandeep Singh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cystic echinococcosis (CE), which is present in all parts of the world, is caused by the bacterium Echinococcus granulosus. One of the most serious parasitic infectious diseases affecting both humans and animals, CE causes hydatid cysts that can lodge in various host organs, including the liver, lungs, heart, and brain, which can be fatal. Treatment options for cystic echinococcosis are active anthelmintics, surgery, and percutaneous aspiration. Numerous scolicidal chemical agents are in practice, but due to increased drug resistance and adverse reactions associated with drugs, usage is limited. Finding alternative therapies with the least or no side effects to manage the condition is the need of the hour. Here, we report a case of an asymptomatic hydatid lung cyst that was managed using Ayurvedic treatment principles. A 57-year-old businessman, asymptomatic, came with a diagnosis of Hydatid lung disease confirmed by CT scan. Surgery was advised, but the patient approached for Ayurveda treatment. The case was treated with oral medicines like Arogyavardhini gutika, Goarka, Kanchanara guggulu, Vidangasava, Kapalabhati pranayama for a period of 8 months. Complete resolution of the cyst was seen, which was evident by a CT scan. Promising results were seen in the present case, but further research in this area is required to produce evidence-based results.

Introduction

The primary zoonotic infection that causes hydatid disease is Echinococcus granulosus [1]. Alveolar echinococcosis is caused by Echinococcus multilocularis [2]. Echinococcus' primary hosts are dogs, sheep, and occasionally humans [3]. Imaging studies and serological tests are used to diagnose hydatid cysts [4]. After the liver, the lungs are the organ that is most frequently affected by hydatid disease. A USG or CT scan may unknowingly pick up the patient even though they are asymptomatic due to another cause. Edema and pressure effects are typically present in patients with symptoms. Due to the strain on the liver capsule, a lesion in the liver produces dull pain, whereas a lesion in the lung may result in dyspnea if it is large enough [5]. Hydatid cysts are usually well-defined hypodense lesions with a definite wall. Pericysts have developed ring-like calcification in 20–30% of patients [4]. For the diagnosis of hydatid illness; the results of an ELISA for echinococcal antigens are required. If the cyst has not leaked, does not contain scolices, or the parasite is no longer alive, the ELISA tests in an infected patient may be negative. The presence of hydatid cysts can be determined using ultrasound and a CT scan. Hydatid disease is primarily treated surgically, with both conservative draining and evacuation and radical resection being equally effective. PAIR (Puncture, Aspiration, Injection, and Re-aspiration) is a widely used procedure. Precautions should be taken to prevent spillage of the cyst during the surgical and related procedures because it may result in anaphylaxis and diffuse seeding of the cyst [6]. In Ayurveda, hydatid cysts are classified as kaphaja granthi roga caused by kaphaja krumi. A condition known as granthi occurs when the kaphapradhana dosha, which is located in the medo, mamsa, and rakta, builds up as knots [7]. The clinical characteristics of Kaphajagranthi include a painless, compact swelling that is cold to the touch and may be accompanied by itching and a thick, pus-like discharge when it suppurates [8]. Patient information

A 57-year-old male, a businessman by profession, otherwise asymptomatic, presented with the diagnosis of a huge hydatid cyst of size 11.1х7.8 х 9 cm in the right lung. The patient had been to a gastrointestinal surgeon for consultation regarding a general body checkup in a private hospital. Chest X-ray and CT Abdomen were advised as a part of a routine clinical exam, and a hydatid cyst of the lungs was detected in the performed investigations. Surgery was advised for the patient, but he denied it due to fear, low economic status, and was in search of alternative medicine to get rid of his problem, so he consulted for Ayurvedic treatment on 21-4-2021. The personal history of the patient revealed that he was consuming a non-vegetarian diet, had no alcohol or tobacco intake, had normal sleep, and micturition with a clear bowel.

Clinical findings: The patient was moderately built with good nutrition. A general examination revealed vitals like a blood pressure of 110/70 mmHg, a P/R of 70 beats per minute, and a temperature of 98.20 F. Systemic examination, i.e., the central nervous system, and cardiovascular system, were found normal, but the respiratory system showed normal air entry on the left lung but absent lung sounds on the lower zone of the right lung with a dull sound on percussion.

Investigations –A computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen showed a huge, thin-walled hydatid cyst measuring 11.1х7.8 х 9 cm in the right hemithorax, with adjacent lung parenchyma showing atelectasis (Fig. 1). A well-defined thinned wall cystic lesion measuring 10.5 × 8.1 × 8.3 cm was noted in segment VI of the right lobe of the liver (16/10/2019). Diagnostic assessments

The diagnosis was established as a hydatid cyst of the lung on the basis of the CT scan as per contemporary science and kaphajagranthi roga caused by kaphaja krumi as per Ayurveda. The diagnosis was established based on the etiology and the appearance of the condition as per the classics [9]. The etiology for the occurrence of kaphaja granthi caused by kaphaja krumi is explained as excess intake of meat, black gram, jaggery, milk, curd, and oil in diet [10], which was evident in the present case and confirmed by CT scan. Kaphaja krumi was suspected due to consumption of more meat, milk, curd, and black gram.

Therapeutic intervention

The treatment was designed on the lines of the krumi and granthi principles. Because the patient was unwilling to undergo Shodhana treatment, Prakritivighata (breaking the pathogenesis) and Nidana Parivarjana (absence from cause) were included in the treatment plan. Yoga therapy was also planned because he had decreased lung sounds on his right side. The patient was counseled about the disease and its prognosis, as well as the proper diet.

Follow-up and outcomes

After 8 months of treatment, the patient presented with a cough along with sputum, nausea, and a feeling of weakness. Then the patient was advised to take Sitopaladi powder (5 g) thrice a day with honey after food and was advised to continue the previous treatment instructions. The patient had two episodes of vomiting (100 ml) in 14 days of powder administration; the color of the vomitus was white, and the consistency was thick, resembling pus with no food contents (as explained by the patient). Post-vomiting, the patient felt better and more comfortable, with a reduction in nausea and weakness. Then the patient was advised to have a chest X-ray and a CT scan of the abdomen to observe the disease's progress. CT scan (7-1-2022) (Supplementary material) impression highlighted relatively thin and linear density in the lateral segment of the right lobe (likely fibrotic changes) (Fig. 3). The impression of the X-ray (21-4-2022) showed a few linear fibrotic changes in the right lower lung with normal findings in other areas (Fig. 4). There were no adverse effects of the interventions observed during the intervention period. Discussion

Surgery, percutaneous aspiration, and the administration of medications like albendazole or mebendazole are all used to treat pulmonary hydatid cysts (PHC), but they have significant drawbacks such as hepatic toxicity and other negative effects [5]. In indexed databases, there is no published literature on the management of pulmonary hydatid cysts using Ayurvedic interventions. The current case offers information on the Ayurvedic management of PHC using the granthi and kaphaja krumi treatment principles.

Arogyavardhini gutika helps in the improvement of health and has properties like deepana, pachana, medovinashana, and malashudhikara [11]. Arogyavardhini gutika was given in between the food to facilitate the action of samana vayu so that the agni would be proper and hence the sara kitta vibhajana kriya would be enhanced.

Goarka is the distilled gomootra (cow's urine), which pacifies the kapha-vata dosha and possesses properties like vishaghna, krumighna, and gulmanashana [12]. Goarka was given in the morning to facilitate the vyana vayu and also to attain the mrudu virechana. The antibacterial and germicidal effects of cow's urine (CU) have been firmly supported by the presence of urea, creatinine, swarnakshara (aurum hydroxide), carbolic acid, phenols, calcium, and manganese [13,14].

Kanchanara guggulu contains Bauhinia variegata and Commiphora mukul as the chief ingredients and is indicated in arbuda (tumors), granthi (benign tumors and cysts), gulma (abdominal lump), kushtha (skin disease), etc. [15]. It was prescribed after food to fortify the action of udana vayu. The qualities of kanchanara and guggulu, Deepan, Pachan, VattaKapha shamak, Shothahara, Lekhana, and Bhedana, aid in halting tumor and cyst formation and in their elimination [16]. "Oleo-resins found in guggulu have strong anti-inflammatory (shothahara) properties" [17]. The medication is utilized to treat both benign and malignant tumors in clinical practice [18].

Vidanga (Embelia ribes) and dhatakipushpa (flower of Woodfordia fruticosa) are the main components of vidangasava. Dhataki is recommended for conditions such as visha (toxins), krumi (worms), visarpa (cellulitis), etc. since it possesses the lagu, ruksha, kashaya rasa, and katuvipaka qualities [19]. The most effective medications for the treatment of krumi is vidanga which has katu, kashaya rasa, laghu, rooksha, tikshna gunas, ushna veerya, and katuvipaka [20]. Vidanga is effective against tapeworms and can treat abdominal tumors, anemia, cystic tumors, and pyorrhea. Anthelmintic embelin salts have been discovered in Vidanga [21].

Pranayama is proven to have various beneficial health effects, which include reduction of stress improved cardiovascular activity, and enhanced respiratory function [22]. Kapalabhati Pranayama is known for maintaining acid-base balance, and deep inhalation activates the dead space of the lungs, improving the oxygenation of tissues and cleansing the entire body [23]. Alternate nostril breathing, or naadishuddhi pranayama, may lessen the release of adrenaline, which reduces sympathetic activity and, in turn, lowers heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, and other physiological parameters [24]. Lung expansion has been shown to lower systemic vascular resistance [25], and frequent practice of this pranayama leads DBP to decline and PEFR to rise.

Sitopaladi churna is a polyherbal formulation that is indicated in conditions like cold, pleurodynia, pneumonia, cough associated with bronchitis, chest congestion, and respiratory infection [26]. Previous experimental studies on sitopaladi churna have proven its efficacy in coughing and are useful in treating type-I allergic conditions [27,28]. To a large extent, the formulation's piperine content is responsible for the mast cell stabilizing effect [29]. “According to studies, flavonoids exhibit antihistaminic and antiallergic properties” [[30], [31], [32]].

Arogyavardhini gutika, Goarka, Kanchanar guggulu, and Vidangasava may have helped in the disruption and liquefication of the cyst. Sitopaladi churna and the pranayama, in this case, may have helped the disrupted and liquefied form of hydatid cysts of the lungs to be expelled out through the mouth.

The drugs given worked mostly for the hydatid cyst of the lungs. This may be because the medications were prescribed along with the pranayama and the pranayama first clears the pranavaha srotas.

Also, the renal cysts size the larger one being 31 × 26 mm (2019) is slightly increased to 35 × 33 mm. The slight variation in the measurement may occur due to instrumental error. And also the drugs we have prescribed may not be specific to the renal system.

The adopted treatment protocol helped to expel out the lung cysts only. On the other hand, we assume that if the treatment procedures had been followed for a longer period, the hepatic hydatid cysts may have also shown positive results. However, more research studies are required to find the exact answers.

Conclusion

Hydatid cysts of the lungs are a rare infectious disease that needs proper evaluation of the symptoms and management. The cysts can be treated with a conservative Ayurvedic approach under the treatment principles of Kaphaja krumi and Kaphaja granthi. The adopted treatment with various Ayurveda oral medicines and Pranayama in the present case has given good results in pulmonary hydatid cysts and has improved the quality of patient life. Case series and randomized clinical trials, on the other hand, are required in this direction to provide safety and evidence-based results.

Patient's perspective

The patient was pleased with the course of treatment when he knew that there were no cysts in the CT scan on 7-1-2022. The quality of life has been improved by the treatment and he has avoided all the causative factors responsible for the disease. Overall, the patient was delighted with the conservative treatment and conveyed gratitude for preventing surgery. The patient perspective was recorded on 7-1-2022.

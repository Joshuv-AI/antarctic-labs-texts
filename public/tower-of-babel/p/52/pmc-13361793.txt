Design of a Pediatric Low Motor Function Item Battery in leukodystrophies
Authors: Francesco Gavazzi, Sarah Woidill, Anjana Sevagamoorthy, Abbas F. Jawad, Russell D'Aiello, Jamie Bradford, Miriam Lerner, Peter Hong, Aaliyah White, Zarrin Tashnim, Stacy V. Cusack, Allan M. Glanzman, Ann Harrington, Amy T. Waldman, Adeline Vanderver, Laura A. Adang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Leukodystrophies are rare genetic disorders that disrupt myelin formation in the central nervous system, leading to a broad range of neuromotor impairment. Standard assessment tools, including the Gross Motor Function Measure-88 (GMFM-88), were designed to favor higher-level skills, like sitting and walking, with more limited sensitivity for earlier developmental skills. In populations where advanced gross motor skills can be profoundly limited, there is a critical need for sensitive clinical outcome assessments capable of detecting motor changes across the gross motor spectrum.This study describes the development and preliminary evaluation of the Pediatric Low Motor Function Item Battery (P-LoMo), designed to extend sensitivity into the lowest ranges of ability. Items were selected from validated assessments, including the Test of Infant Motor Performance, the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia Infant Test of Neuromuscular Disorders, the Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development-4th Edition, the Mullen Scales of Early Learning, and the Early Clinical Assessment of Balance, through expert consensus.The P-LoMo and the GMFM-88 were administered to 168 leukodystrophy patients. Median GMFM-88 performance was 17.5%, with a skew to low performance (52% of scores within the bottom 20%). The P-LoMo median score was 71 out of 84 potential points. There was a strong correlation between assessments (Spearman's R = 0.94, p < 0.0001) across the total cohort as well as among individuals in the lowest register of performance on the GMFM-88 (Spearman's R = 0.90, p < 0.0001).The P-LoMo has the potential to improve our capacity to capture the full range of neurologic function for children with leukodystrophies and severe motor impairment. It provides a sensitive, feasible, and caregiver-aligned clinical outcome assessment. This tool has the potential to improve trial readiness, functional quantification, and evaluation of emerging therapies in pediatric populations with low motor function.

Introduction

Leukodystrophies are a group of rare, devastating genetic disorders characterized by the progressive deterioration of myelin of the central nervous system. [1-3] Although the clinical course varies depending on the underlying genetic diagnosis and age of onset, most children with leukodystrophies present in early life with significant neuromotor impairment. [3-7]

Motor function is consistently identified by families as one of the most valued domains influencing quality of life and perceived disease burden in individuals with rare disorders. [8-11] This includes skills in the lower register of function, as families frequently describe improvements in postural stability, head control, or sitting balance as milestones that enhance interaction, comfort, and participation. [10] Clinically, these skills are critical as well, as head and trunk control support airway protection, safe swallowing, oral feeding, communication, and engagement with the environment in individuals with severe neuromotor impairment. [12-14] As such, a small change of motor function in these domains has the potential to impact quality of life significantly.

Despite the critical importance of assessing head and trunk control, existing motor function assessments for older children are not structured to do so. This includes the common clinical outcome assessment (COA) Gross Motor Function Measure-88 (GMFM-88), which was developed for cerebral palsy. [15] In progressive disorders characterized by a broad clinical spectrum, the validity of these tools may be compromised by significant floor effects, particularly in children with low motor function (LMF). In Aicardi Goutières syndrome (AGS), approximately two-thirds of children scored at or below the floor of the GMFM-88. [16] Similar performance was observed in a study of subjects with TUBB4A-related leukodystrophy. [17] We anticipate that it will be challenging to capture longitudinal change when the performance of most of the cohort falls outside the dynamic range of the performance COA. [16,17] With the emergence of disease-modifying therapies across the leukodystrophies, [18-20] there is an increasing need for sensitive and reliable clinical outcome assessments (COAs) capable of detecting meaningful changes in motor function across the population.

The aim of this work is to develop a novel item battery designed to assess motor performance in children with severe motor impairments in both in-person and remote settings. This item battery is designed to expand the dynamic range of function captured by gross motor COA in pediatric leukodystrophy populations.

Methods

Patient recruitment and enrollment

Subjects were recruited as part of the Myelin Disorders Biorepository Project (Institutional Review Board at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia [CHOP]: 14–011236) from 07/2023 to 08/2025. Individuals with a molecular diagnosis of leukodystrophy comprised the cohort [diagnoses: Aicardi Goutières Syndrome (AGS), Alexander Disease (AxD), Multi Sulfatase Deficiency (MSD), Metachromatic Leukodystrophy (MLD), Pelizaeus Merzbacher Disease / Pelizaeus Merzbacher Like Disease (PMD/PMLD), POLR3-related Leukodystrophy, TUBB4A-related Leukodystrophy (TUBB4A-LD), and Vanishing White (VWM)].

Selection of items for the Pediatric Low Motor Function Item Battery (P-LoMo)

A panel of outcome measure and disease experts (FG, SC, AMG, ATW, AV, LAA) selected COAs that capture early motor skills in pediatric populations. Unanimous agreement was required for inclusion. The following assessments were identified for item selection: Test of Infant Motor Performance (TIMP), [21] the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia Infant Test of Neuromuscular Disorders (CHOP INTEND), [22] the Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development -3rd Edition, [23] the Mullen Scales of Early Learning (MSEL), [24] and the Early Clinical Assessment of Balance (ECAB). [25] Using these instruments, individual items were examined for feasibility in both in-person and remote settings across the pediatric age range (0–18 years). Items achieving ≥80% agreement among experts were selected for subsequent testing (Table 1).

Selected functions highlighted head control in prone and supine positions, as well as spontaneous or stimulus-elicited arm and leg movements in response to visual and auditory cues. The scoring for each item was based on the original test. The total raw score is a sum of the scores across the items.

Materials

The GMFM-88 was used as the gold standard to assess P-LoMo tool alignment. The GMFM-88 is a COA used to evaluate children's gross motor abilities and includes 88 motor items. [15] This assessment encompasses five dimensions: A - Lying and Rolling; B - Sitting; C - Crawling and Kneeling; D - Standing; and E - Walking, Running, and Jumping. The GMFM-88 raw scores for each dimension are converted to percentages. The GMFM-88 total score is derived by averaging the scores across the five dimensions. As previously defined in leukodystrophy cohorts, the floor effect performance (within the lowest score range) was defined as 20% or less of the total score. [16,17]

Administration of COAs

Licensed physical therapists administered the P-LoMo and the GMFM-88 during remote research encounters [26,33], after prior training on items comprising the P-LoMo. Items on the P-LoMo were administered according to the manual instructions: therapists guided each caregiver to properly position the participant and administer each item. GMFM-88 and P-LoMo were administered in all pariticipants, independently from their chronological age.

All assessments were conducted simultaneously via HIPAA-compliant Microsoft Teams® at CHOP, with recordings stored on secure servers. The estimated time for administering both assessments was 60–90 min.

Demographic information

Existing medical documentation included biological sex, genetic variant, and age at evaluation. Information was extracted from available medical documentation.

Data storage and integrity

Study data were managed on the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) database hosted at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia. [27,28] REDCap is a secure, web-based software platform that facilitates data capture for research studies.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables, including ordinal COA variables, were summarized using counts and proportions. Continuous variables were summarized using mean, standard deviation (SD), median, range (minimum to maximum values), and interquartile range (IQR). Statistical significance was determined using a two-sided t-test with a p-value <0.05. Spearman's Rank Correlation was used to estimate the correlation between GMFM-88 and P-LoMo scores and explore the strength of association as follows: < 0.40 (weak), 0.40–<0.60 (moderate), and > 0.60 (strong to very strong). [29] Segmented regression to determine optimal breakpoint for the segmentation of the P-Lomo:GMFM-88 correlation was completed using the segmented R package. [30] All remaining analyses were performed using Prism 11.0.

Results

Cohort description

Overall, 168 subjects were included in the study (Table 2). The cohort represents a range of leukodystrophy diagnoses: Aicardi Goutières Syndrome (AGS; n = 28), Alexander Disease (AxD; n = 4), Multiple Sulfatase Deficiency (MSD; n = 6), 5 Metachromatic Leukodystrophy (MLD; n = 5), Pelizaeus Merzbacher Disease or Pelizaeus Merzbacher Like Disease (PMD/PMLD; n = 29), POLR3-related Leukodystrophy (n = 25), TUBB4A-related Leukodystrophy (TUBB4A-LD; n = 66), and Vanishing White (VWM; n = 5). Median age at assessment was 9.82 years (IQR 5.73–18.5), with a range spanning 0.82–48.1 years.

Pediatric Low Motor Function Item Battery (P-LoMo) development

Items for the P-LoMo were selected by outcome measure and disease experts from existing tools, including the Test of Infant Motor Performance (TIMP), [21] the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia Infant Test of Neuromuscular Disorders (CHOP INTEND), [22] the Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development -4th Edition, [23] the Mullen Scales of Early Learning (MSEL), [24] and the Early Clinical Assessment of Balance (ECAB) [25] (Table 1). Selected items capture early developmental skills, including head control in prone and supine and spontaneous or stimulus-elicited arm and leg movements in response to visual and auditory cues. The maximum total score of the P-LoMo is 84, which is a raw sum of item performance as a score within the original COA.

COA administration

All enrolled individuals completed the GMFM-88 and P-LoMo assessments remotely (N = 168; Table 3). The median GMFM-88 performance was 17.5% (IQR 42.42), with 87 participants (51.8%) performing below the test floor (defined as <20%; Fig. 1). For the P-LoMo, the median performance observed was 71 (IQR 24; Fig. 2A). Within the GMFM-88 low functioning cohort (performance below floor threshold), the median P-LoMo score was 58 (IQR 21), Fig. 2B. There was no correlation between age at administration and P-LoMo performance (Spearman's Correlation R = −0.10), while there was a negligible inverse correlation between age at administration and GMFM-88 performance (Spearman's correlation R = −0.17).

P-LOMO and GMFM-88 correlation

There was a strong correlation between the performance on the GMFM-88 and P-LoMo (N = 168, R = 0.94, p < 0.0001, Fig. 3A). Within the GMFM-88 low functioning cohort (performance below floor threshold), there was also a strong correlation between these measures (N = 87, R = 0.90, p < 0.0001, Fig. 3B). Optimal breakpoint for the segmentation of the correlation between P-LoMo and GMFM-88 was 77.1 (95%CI: 75.9,78.3).

Discussion

In children with severe neuromotor impairment, existing tools such as the GMFM-88 frequently fail to differentiate subtle yet clinically meaningful changes in motor function, producing significant floor effects that limit their utility in leukodystrophies and related neurodegenerative conditions.

The present study describes the development and preliminary evaluation of the Pediatric Low Motor Function Item Battery (P-LoMo), an item set designed to extend measurement sensitivity into the lowest ranges of motor performance in pediatric populations.

By targeting foundational motor abilities (such as head control, postural stability, and spontaneous or elicited limb movements), the P-LoMo captures observable skills often retained even among the most severely affected individuals. This design reduces floor effects and enhances the ability to stratify motor function within populations previously unmeasurable by standard tools. The strong correlation observed between P-LoMo and GMFM-88 scores supports the conceptual alignment between the two measures, while highlighting P-LoMo's capacity to extend the measurement continuum into the lowest levels of function. This property is particularly relevant in leukodystrophies and related neurodegenerative disorders, where motor changes within early developmental skills may have a meaningful impact on quality of life.

Feasibility and adaptability emerged as key advantages of the P-LoMo. Successful remote administration through secure telehealth platforms enabled participation of medically fragile children and families who would otherwise face significant barriers to in-person assessments. [26] This approach builds on prior demonstrations of reliability in remote GMFM-88 scoring among children with leukodystrophy [26]. It aligns with current efforts to implement flexible, low-burden data collection strategies in rare disease studies. [8,10] Such adaptability is especially valuable in the context of progressive myelin disorders, where disease-related morbidity often limits clinic attendance and early functional decline can preclude in-person assessments.

The need for outcome measures capable of capturing residual motor ability has been underscored by recent studies characterizing gross motor function in AGS and TUBB4A-related leukodystrophy. [16,17] In these cohorts, traditional assessments frequently reached floor values despite preserved head control, antigravity limb movement, or spontaneous motor responses. This is frequently observed in individuals with early onset leukodystrophies, in which acquisition of developmental milestones are often limited, and pose a challenge in determination of therapeutic efficacy. The P-LoMo was therefore developed with an emphasis on domains of high functional and caregiver relevance, consistent with recent frameworks advocating the integration of patient- and caregiver-reported outcomes in endpoint development, [20,31] as informed by previous research on families' priorities and overall quality of life. [8,10,11]

This work builds on the feasibility of capturing function using remote assessments [26] and represents the foundation for the development of a validated low motor function battery. Future iterations of this tool will use psychometric measurement techniques, including Item Response Theory (IRT), [32] to optimize measurement precision and responsiveness across a broad range of ability levels. This process will involve iterative evaluation of item difficulty, discrimination, and differential functioning across ages and disease subtypes, as well as exploration of adaptive short forms to improve efficiency. The refined tool will then undergo a formal validation phase to establish reliability, construct validity, and sensitivity to change, as well as alignment with caregiver-reported instruments to ensure that observed score changes reflect meaningful outcomes to families, as well as alignment with Clinician-Reported Outcomes validated in leukodystrophies, including the GMFCS-ER and the GMFC-MLD. [16,17]

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The concurrent administration of GMFM-88 and P-LoMo was centralized from a single site. To support generalizability, we used remote assessments to allow geographic distribution of participants. We also included patients with a diverse range of leukodystrophies. Prior studies demonstrated the feasibility and reliability of remote GMFM-88 scoring in leukodystrophy populations. [26] Broader representation will be critical for establishing generalizable properties and understanding cross-cultural differences in caregiver perceptions of function. Additionally, some of the items included in the P-LoMo depend on sensory function as well as motor function, with a potential bias in performance of children with visual or hearing impairments. Finally, while telehealth participation enhanced accessibility, it may have introduced selection bias favoring families with greater resources. Future validation efforts will need to assess feasibility across a broader range of settings. Longitudinal studies will also be required to establish the responsiveness of the P-LoMo to disease progression and to therapeutic interventions. [20,33]

In conclusion, the P-LoMo represents an important step toward the development of a sensitive, feasible, and patient-centered clinical outcome assessment for children with leukodystrophies and severe motor impairment. By addressing floor effects, supporting remote administration, and aligning its content with domains prioritized by caregivers, the P-LoMo helps advance trial readiness for rare neurodevelopmental disorders, by being used as a standalone tool to capture clinical trial endpoints, or in association with the GMFM-88, to avoid floor and ceiling effect performance in either assessment. Through systematic refinement and rigorous validation, this tool has the potential to provide a reliable framework for quantifying functional change, improving the interpretability of early-phase trial outcomes, and enhancing the evaluation of emerging therapeutic interventions in this underserved population.

Hybrid Molecules of p53 Activators and Protoflavones to Target Multiple Myeloma
Authors: Francisca Lopes, Gábor Girst, Rafael Rincón, Ricardo J F Ferreira, Lídia M Gonçalves, Lucília Saraiva, Hui‐Chun Wang, Muriel Cuendet, Attila Hunyadi, Maria M M Santos
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMultiple myeloma is a rare blood cancer that develops from abnormal plasma cells in the bone marrow. Treatment of multiple myeloma remains an enormous challenge. In this work, hybrid compounds are developed and studied for their potential use against multiple myeloma. The compounds are designed to act by a dual mechanism of action, activation of the p53 pathway, and inhibition of the ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3‐related protein (ATR). To evaluate the selectivity for the p53 pathway, the compounds are first evaluated in an isogenic pair of HCT116 colon cancer cell lines, with and without p53, and in two breast cancer cell lines expressing different forms of p53. Then, the growth inhibitory effect of the hybrid compounds is tested against the multiple myeloma cell lines RPMI 8226 (mutant p53) and MM.1S (wild‐type p53). At the same time, compound 15 is confirmed to inhibit doxorubicin‐ but not UV‐induced DNA damage response via the ATR/Chk1 signaling pathway. The hybrids show lower IC50 values compared to the fragments alone, highlighting the potential for using hybrid molecules containing two pharmacophores with complementary activities. These results suggest that novel hybrid molecules may serve as new leads against multiple myeloma.Keywords: cancer, hybrids, p53, protoflavones, tryptophanol

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a rare hematological cancer consisting of malignant plasma cells that proliferate in the bone marrow.[ 1 , 2 – 3 ] During the last years, different drugs have been approved by the Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of relapsed MM, such as carfilzomib, pomalidomide, ixazomib, and elotuzumab.[ 4 ] Nevertheless, in 2022, more than 180 000 cases were reported for this type of cancer all over the world, ranking the 18th highest mortality.[ 5 ] Moreover, the number of incidence and deaths is estimated to increase during the next years. Therefore, it is urgent to find new drugs for the treatment of MM. The development of hybrid molecules that can act by synergistic mechanisms of action is an interesting therapeutic strategy in medicinal chemistry.[ 6 ] These hybrid molecules are normally based on linking two or more pharmacophores with complementary activity to produce a more powerful compound and overcome drug resistance.[ 7 , 8 ] Moreover, hybrid compounds can be designed based on noncleavable or cleavable linkers, which can enable the release of both pharmacophores.[ 7 ]

An important therapeutic target in cancer is the tumor suppressor p53, a protein involved in several cellular processes and different signaling pathways, being able to control malignant cell proliferation.[ 9 ] In most human cancers the tumor suppressor function of p53 is inhibited, either by overexpression of p53 negative regulators or by mutation of the TP53 gene. Thus, reactivation of p53 protein, with subsequent restoration of its tumor suppressor functions, is considered a relevant anticancer strategy.[ 10 , 11 ] One example of wild‐type and mutant p53 activators is the tryptophanol‐derived isoindolinone scaffold.[ 12 , 13 ]

Another relevant antitumor therapeutic target is the ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3‐related protein (ATR) due to its important role in DNA damage response. Pharmacological inhibition of ATR induces cell death in ataxia telangiectasia‐mutated (ATM)‐ or p53‐defective cancers and leads to an improvement of the efficacy of DNA‐damaging agents.[ 14 ] There are several ATR inhibitor drug candidates currently in various phases of clinical trials, like ceralasertib, berzosertib, or camonsertib.[ 15 ] The chemical structure of such inhibitors is quite versatile. Even though no protoflavone derivative has reached clinical trial, their inhibitory potential on ATR‐dependent signaling is prevalent.[ 16 ]

In fact, the importance of the p53 and ATR pathways in MM led to the preclinical and clinical investigations of modulators of these pathways.[ 17 , 18 ] For these reasons, in this work, hybrid molecules containing simultaneously two compounds with complementary activities (p53 activation and ATR inhibition) were designed and synthesized. We selected the tryptophanol‐derived isoindolinones 1 and 2 to target p53 (Figure 1 ). Tryptophanol‐derived isoindolinone 1 is a wild‐type p53 activator and R280K‐mutant p53 reactivator that showed good in vitro and in vivo antiproliferative activity.[ 12 , 19 , 20 ] Tryptophanol‐derived isoindolinone 2 is a stabilizer of wild‐type p53 and R273H‐mutant p53. This molecule selectively reacts with p53 Cys 141 via nucleophilic aromatic substitution.[ 13 , 21 ] Besides, we selected protoapigenone 3 (Figure 1), a natural protoflavonoid known to inhibit ATR‐dependent signaling, to target ATR.[ 16 ] The hybrid compounds were prepared by linking these molecules through a triazole linker.

Structure–Activity Relationship Studies

The hybrids and their relevant fragments were first tested for their cytotoxicity in the isogenic pair of human colon adenocarcinoma HCT116 cell line (with or without wild‐type p53) to compare with the previous results obtained with compounds 1 and 2.

Most hybrids were more active than compounds 1 and 2 and displayed IC50 values in the low μM range, probably due to the introduction of the protoflavone moiety (Table 2 ). Additionally, the hybrids were also evaluated in two breast cancer cell lines: MCF‐7 (wild‐type p53) and MDA‐MB‐231 (R280K mutant p53). Interestingly, six hybrids had IC50 values lower than 10 μM and the presence of the protoflavone moiety led to increased selectivity for the cell line with mutated p53.

Based on these preliminary results, compound 15, one of the most active compounds in these 4 cancer cell lines was selected to be tested for its effect on the DNA damage response induced by UV light or doxorubicin treatment in MCF‐7 cells (Figure 4 ). The phosphorylation of checkpoint kinases 1 (Chk1) and 2 (Chk2) is mediated by ATR and ATM, respectively. In the case of UV damage, compound 15 did not significantly inhibit the activation of either kinase. Meanwhile, in the case of doxorubicin‐induced DNA damage, hybrid 15 behaved similarly to protoapigenone (3), inhibiting the phosphorylation of Chk1 but not Chk2, therefore indicating the inhibition of the ATR‐mediated signaling. This hybrid could be used to target cancers with high levels of endogenous DNA damage.[ 23 ]

Then, the antiproliferative activity of the hybrids and relevant fragments was also evaluated in two MM cell lines: RPMI 8226, which expresses E285K‐mutant p53, and MM.1S, that expresses wild‐type p53 (Table 2). Additionally, the subunits of hybrids 11 and 17 were tested in a proportion of 1:1 in MM cells to compare the activity with the corresponding hybrids. The hybrids were more active than the respective subunits combined, in both cell lines, meaning that there is an advantage to using the hybrids compared to their individual subunits. These findings suggest that the hybrids could simultaneously target the p53 and the ATR pathways, which are crucial for MM cell survival and proliferation. The chemical hybridization of these bioactive subunits may be a suitable strategy to ensure their cellular uptake and intracellular accessibility at equimolar concentrations, in order to potentiate the effects of a dual p53‐ATR targeting.[ 24 ]

Conclusions

In this work, 11 new hybrid molecules containing a pharmacophore for p53 activation and a pharmacophore for ATR inhibition were synthesized. These molecules were evaluated for their growth inhibitory activity in the isogenic pair of human colon adenocarcinoma HCT116 cell lines, breast cancer MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cell lines, and MM RPMI 8226 and MM.1S cell lines, showing activities in the nanomolar and submicromolar range in all cell lines tested. To further confirm the potential of the most promising hybrids, the subunits alone were also evaluated in both MM cell lines. The results showed that the hybrids had a better antiproliferative activity than their subunits tested alone and in combination, indicating the advantage of having a hybrid compound over using the isolated subunits.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of 4a‐c and 5a‐c

To a solution of 1 or 2 [ 13 ] (0.63 mmol, 1.0 equiv) in dried dimethylformamide (3 mL) at 0 °C, sodium hydride (60% in oil dispersion) (0.63 mmol, 1.0 equiv) was added. After stirring for 30 min under a nitrogen atmosphere, the mixture was allowed to warm up to room temperature, and the respective methanesulfonate reagent (0.69 mmol, 1.1 equiv) was added to the reaction mixture. The reaction was left to stir for 5–24 hr. After this time, it was quenched with 10 mL of ethyl acetate and the organic layer was washed with water (4 × 10 mL), brine (2x10 mL), and water again (5 mL). The organic phase was dried over MgSO4, filtered, and the solvent evaporated under vacuum. The desired product was isolated by preparative HPLC or by flash column chromatography.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Hybrids 10–20

To a stirred solution of the alkyne protoflavone derivative (0.035 mmol, 1.1 equiv) in DMF (150 µL), we added an aqueous solution of sodium ascorbate (34 µL, c = 40 mg mL−1) and an aqueous solution of copper sulfate (34 µL, c = 25 mg/mL). Afterward, the appropriate azide (0.031 mmol, 1 equiv) was added and the reaction mixture was stirred overnight, at room temperature. The reaction mixture was diluted with H2O (5 mL) and extracted with EtOAc (3 × 5 mL). The organic extracts were dried over Na2SO4 and filtered and concentrated in vacuo. The product was obtained through separation by preparative RP‐HPLC with acetonitrile/water eluent.

SRB Assay

Colon adenocarcinoma HCT116 cell lines expressing wt p53 (HCT116 p53+/+) and its p53‐null isogenic derivative (HCT116 p53−/−) were provided by B. Vogelstein (The Johns Hopkins Kimmel Cancer Center, Baltimore, MD, USA). Tumor cells were cultured in RPMI‐1640 with UltraGlutamine (Lonza, VWR) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Alfagene) and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Cells were seeded in 96‐well plates at the cells/well density of 5.0 × 103. Cells were thereafter treated with serial dilutions (1.85–75 µM) of tryptophanol‐derived isoindolinones for 48 h, and its effect on cell proliferation was analyzed by SRB assay with the determination of GI50 (concentration that causes 50% of growth inhibition) values. The solvent (DMSO 0.25%) and nutlin‐3a (GI50 = 3.2 ± 1.0 µM in HCT116 p53+/+ and GI50 = 24.0 ± 0.7 µM in HCT116 p53−/−) were included as controls.

MTT Assay

The same protocol as previously reported was employed for this experiment.[ 20 ] Briefly, the day before experiments, MCF‐7 (ATCC HTB‐22) and MDA‐MB‐231 (ATCC HTB‐26) cells were seeded at 2 × 104 cells per well in 96‐well tissue culture plates. Tested compounds were dissolved in DMSO, serially diluted in the culture medium in order that DMSO concentration was lower than 0.5% (v/v) and added to the cells. After 48 h, the MTT assay was conducted. The IC50 values were calculated by the variable slope log(inhibitor) versus normalized response nonlinear regression model of GraphPad Prism 5 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

XTT Assay

The human MM RPMI 8226 (ATCC CCL‐155, Manassas, VA, USA) and MM.1S (ATCC CRL‐2974) cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Biowest, Nuaillé, France), 50 U/mL penicillin, 50 µg mL−1 streptomycin (Gibco), and 2 mM L‐glutamine (Gibco) at 37 °C in a humified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Cells were used at passage numbers below 30 for all the experiments to ensure consistency.

Cells were seeded in 96‐well plates at a density of 15,000 cells per well and treated for 72 h with the various compounds or combinations (1:1) using increasing concentrations (62.5–8000 nM). Bortezomib was used as positive control and DMSO was used as vehicle control (0.1% in culture medium). At the end of the treatment time, cells were incubated with 50 μL of an XTT solution (2,3‐bis‐(2‐methoxy‐4‐nitro‐5‐sulfophenyl, 15 mg mL−1) (Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 4 h in the case of RPMI 8226 cells and for 6 h in the case of MM.1S cells. The absorbance was read at 450 nm using a Cytation 3 plate reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). The percentage of cell viability was calculated by obtaining the ratio between the absorbance of the sample treated and the vehicle control and multiplying it by 100. Each compound was tested in triplicate in at least three independent experiments. The IC50 value was calculated through a nonlinear regression using GraphPad Prism 9 (San Diego, CA, USA).

Western Blot Assay

The human breast adenocarcinoma cell line MCF‐7 (ATCC HTB‐22, BCRC 60,436) was purchased from the Bioresource Collection and Research Center (BCRC, Hsinchu, Taiwan) and cultured according to the instructions provided by the cell bank. The Western blot assay was used to detect protein expression as described previously.[ 16 ] The primary antibodies used were anti‐phospho‐Chk1 (Ser345; clone 133D3), anti‐phospho‐Chk2 (Thr68; clone C13C1), anti‐Chk2 (clone 1C12), and GAPDH (clone 14C10), all purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). The anti‐Chk1 antibody (clone G4) was obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Dallas, TX, USA).

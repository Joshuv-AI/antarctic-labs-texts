Randomised Controlled Trial of a holistic needs assessment to enable equity in Aotearoa New Zealand
Authors: P Atatoa Carr, A Jones, N Scott
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLarge and persistent health inequities exist between Māori (Indigenous) tamariki (children) and non-Māori in Aotearoa New Zealand, stemming from systems failure, unequal access to determinants (e.g. socioeconomic), and siloed, low-quality, unresponsive services. Holistic screening and support during hospital stays may improve outcomes and equity. To determine this, a co-designed Māori-centred holistic programme, Harti Hauora Tamariki (HHT), was developed to promote wellbeing through culturally safe engagement, needs assessment, and service navigation.MethodsA randomised controlled trial (RCT) assessed HHT's impact. Primary outcomes were 30-day, 6-month, and 12-month hospital readmissions. Secondary outcomes included care satisfaction and service access within 30 days of discharge: immunisation, primary care enrolment, oral health, referrals for housing support, and smoking cessation.ResultsThe 965 children recruited (485 in the intervention, 480 control) included 56% Māori. No significant difference in 30-day readmission was observed - intervention: 57 (11.7%), control: 56 (11.7%); risk difference +0.04% (-4.02-4.09%), X²=0.00, p = 0.99; adjusted OR 0.99 (0.66-1.49). However, significant gains occurred in secondary outcomes: housing and smoking support, oral health enrolment, unmet need documentation, and overall care satisfaction.ConclusionsA robust whānau-centred RCT is feasible in tertiary settings with continuous improvement and adaptation. Although readmission rates did not change, HHT yielded meaningful system-level benefits, access to health determinants and enhanced clinical pathways. Culturally safe, racism-free whānau engagement improved care quality and engagement. HHT delivered equity gains across individual, whānau, and system levels, optimising limited resources for better outcomes.Key messages• Despite complexity within tertiary paediatric health care, a robust and holistic RCT is possible to evaluate interventions and maximise health outcomes and health equity.• Holistic assessment and support systems can enable systems-level improvement, particularly if co-designed with community-level experts and delivered through culturally-safe engagement.

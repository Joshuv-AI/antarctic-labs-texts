Comparative Evaluation of Remineralization Potential of Various Fluoride Varnishes in Primary Teeth
Authors: Manoj K. Poovelil, Amreen Aijazuddin, Akanksha Baheti, Sagolshem D. Devi, Prabita Maibam, Yogesh Bande
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The prevalence of dental caries requires pediatric dentistry to pursue efficient preventive methods to tackle the problem. Multiple research has explored the fluoride varnish effectiveness for remineralization, yet the results show contrasting findings. The proposed research evaluates the effectiveness by which different fluoride varnishes can remineralize primary teeth with demineralized enamel.Materials and Methods:A total of sixty extracted primary teeth received artificial enamel lesions under randomized selection that split them into four groups numbering fifteen specimens each for Groups A and B and C and D. The pH cycling procedure was applied to the samples for 14 days. Metabolic testing of surface microhardness took place using a Vickers hardness tester before initiating treatment and after completion. The research used ANOVA statistical analysis together with post hoc tests while setting the significance threshold at P < 0.05.Results:The application of fluoride varnishes in all test groups produced substantial increases in surface microhardness values than those observed in the control group. The highest level of tooth remineralization occurred in Group A where the restoration increased by 45%, while Group B achieved 38% and Group C gained 30%. The control group experienced inferior dental improvements compared with the experimental groups having only a 5% hard tissue increase. The results demonstrated that Group A obtained superior values compared with other groups, while statistical findings indicated a significant group variation with P < 0.001.Conclusion:The highest level of primary tooth remineralization occurs when using 5% sodium fluoride varnish as an effective preventive treatment. Tricalcium phosphate and amorphous calcium phosphate fluoride varnishes demonstrated strong effectiveness in addition to 5% sodium fluoride varnish. Research findings demonstrate that fluoride varnishes need to be considered a fundamental approach for handling young children’s early enamel lesion management.

INTRODUCTION

Dental caries poses as a leading chronic disease that affects primary teeth to a higher extent because they retain less mineral composition and exhibit more porosity than permanent teeth.[1] The ongoing enamel demineralization condition leads to cavitation unless proper remineralization strategies are used. Medical experts use fluoride varnishes extensively because they both strengthen enamel and block demineralization processes.[23]

The release of fluoride ions through fluoride varnishes enables the formation of fluorapatite when combined with saliva and phosphate and calcium minerals to strengthen enamel.[4] The development of new formulations added biochemical minerals such as tricalcium phosphate (TCP) together with amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) to improve fluoride absorption and enable enamel restoration.[56] The scientific community currently debates about which fluoride varnish versions show the best results when used in primary teeth.

The research evaluates different fluoride varnishes for their remineralizing properties on primary teeth enamel with surface microhardness tests after demineralization.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

An in vitro laboratory study checked how fluoride varnishes remineralized teeth that experienced demineralization. Sixty noncarious extracted primary teeth which retained their enamel surface and showed no signs of cracks or restorations were included for use in the study. Researchers cleaned and preserved the teeth through storage in thymol solution with 0.1% concentration to stop microbial development until usage.

Grouping of samples

The research divided its 60 specimens into four separate treatment groups that contained 15 samples each.

The testing solution with phosphate calcium and lactic acid at pH 4.5 was used to create artificial enamel lesions in the samples for 72 hours. After water rinsing the specimens underwent air drying until the fluorides could be applied through either NaF or TCP or ACP formulation.

The varnish fluoride application procedure on the enamel surface of each specimen in Groups A, B, and C followed the manufacturer-recommended methods. The researcher allowed the varnish to stay on tooth enamel for 24 hours before starting the pH cycling model for fourteen days. The Vickers hardness tester measured enamel microhardness at three time points.

RESULTS

The fluoride varnish treatments in all test groups produced significant enhancement of tooth surface microhardness values which surpassed the values of the control group (P < 0.05). Group A (5% NaF varnish) achieved the highest achievement in remineralization which Group B (TCP fluoride varnish) and Group C (ACP fluoride varnish) demonstrated sequentially after testing. The microhardness values in the control group improved the least among all tested groups.

Research data show that Table 1 contains the average microhardness values measured using Vickers Hardness Number (VHN) during the study for all tested groups. All tested samples demonstrated substantial microhardness decreases after the demineralization process. After remineralization Group A achieved 45% microhardness gain as the leader while Group B scored 38% while Group C maintained 30% incremental increase [Table 1]. However, Group D (control) displayed only 5% microhardness improvement.

The one-way ANOVA test showed that groups had statistically meaningful distinctions in their results (P < 0.001). The results of Tukey test revealed that Group A achieved superior microhardness values which surpassed those of Groups B, C, and D (P < 0.05). Analysis results showed that TCP fluoride varnish displayed remineralization effects equivalent to ACP fluoride varnish. Nevertheless statistical data demonstrated that Groups B and C had no significant variations (P > 0.05).

The results confirm that fluoride varnishes significantly enhance the remineralization potential of primary teeth, with 5% NaF varnish being the most effective, followed by TCP and ACP fluoride varnishes. The control group showed minimal remineralization, indicating the necessity of fluoride-based interventions for enamel repair.

DISCUSSION

This study examined and distinguished the remineralizing effects which different fluoride varnishes produced on enamel-damaged primary teeth. An examination of the results showed that 5% sodium fluoride (NaF) varnish delivered superior remineralizing capacity when compared with tricalcium phosphate (TCP) fluoride varnish and amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) fluoride varnish. Tests on the untreated control group revealed minimal tooth remineralization because fluoride stands as a vital agent for stopping and reversing early tooth surface breakdown.

Research data demonstrated that TCP fluoride varnish (Group B) led to significant tooth mineralization, but NaF varnish performed slightly better. The function of TCP lies in preserving calcium and phosphate supplies which subsequently assists in creating fluorapatite structures and improves fluoride accessibility in mineralizing tissues.[7] Research outlines that fluoride varnishes with TCP create better enamel repair by providing calcium and phosphate ions that are bioavailable to the tooth material.[89] Our study results showed no significant difference between TCP and ACP varnishes because both mechanisms stabilize calcium and phosphate ions within enamel lesions.[10]

The microhardness of amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) fluoride varnish (Group C) experienced only an average increase that was below the levels of NaF and TCP varnish. ACP varnishes demonstrate proven ability to deliver calcium and phosphate ions which help restore enamel minerals.[11] Tests show ACP fluoride lacks the ability to provide long-lasting fluoride release thus its lower remineralizing capability might explain the observations from our study.[12]

The control group (Group D) showcased only minimal remineralizing effects which verifies the essential role of fluoride varnish for managing early enamel lesions. Research results show that saliva alone fails to reverse demineralization in high-risk areas.

CONCLUSION

Fluoride varnish applications promote primary tooth enamel remineralization as sodium fluoride 5% demonstrates optimum results that extend to TCP and ACP fluoride varnish formulations.

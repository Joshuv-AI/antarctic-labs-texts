Left Ventricular Outflow Tract Morphology as a Predictor of Conductance Disturbances Following Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Carolina Espejo‐Paeres, Luis Alfonso Marroquín‐Donday, Breda Hennessey, Lin Wang, Angela McInerney, José G Paredes‐Vázquez, María Pérez‐Vizcayno, Gabriela Tirado‐Conte, Fernando Macaya‐Ten, Hernán Mejía‐Rentería, Pablo Salinas, Iván Javier Núñez‐Gil, Nieves Gonzalo, Luis Nombela‐Franco, Javier Escaned, Antonio Fernández‐Ortiz, Julián Pérez‐Villacastín, Pilar Jiménez‐Quevedo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundConduction disturbances represent one of the most common complications following transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). We sought to investigate the role of left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) morphology in the development of conduction disturbances following TAVR.Methods and ResultsConsecutive patients who underwent TAVR in our center were included. The ratio between the LVOT area and the aortic annulus area was calculated. Patients were then divided into 2 groups on the basis of this ratio: group 1, which included patients with an LVOT area/aortic annulus area ratio <0.9; and group 2, which included patients with an LVOT area/aortic annulus area ratio ≥0.9. The primary end point was to assess the relationship between LVOT shape and the rate of permanent pacemaker implantation following TAVR. A multivariable analysis was performed to identify predictors of permanent pacemaker implantation following TAVR. From January 2018 to December 2020, 276 patients were included. Ninety‐one patients with tapered LVOT morphology were assigned to group 1 and the rest (n=185 patients), tubular LVOT or flared LVOT shape, to group 2. The mean age was 81.5±5.7 years and 57% were women. After adjusting by confounding factors, tapered morphology of the LVOT and prior right bundle‐branch block were found to be independent predictors of permanent pacemaker implantation (hazard ratio [HR], 2.6 [95% CI, 1.2–5.7]; P=0.014; and HR: 4.3 [95% CI 2.4–7.6], P<0.001); at a median follow‐up time of 15.5 (interquartile range, 15) months.ConclusionsA tapered‐LVOT morphology was associated with increased risk for permanent pacemaker implantation. LVOT morphology may be an additional factor to consider when choosing prosthesis size.Keywords: conduction disturbances, left ventricular outflow tract, permanent pacemaker implantation, transcatheter aortic valve replacementSubject Categories: Aortic Valve Replacement/Transcather Aortic Valve Implantation, Pacemaker, Computerized Tomography (CT)

Clinical Perspective

What Is New?

What Are the Clinical Implications?

Among patients with a tapered left ventricular outflow tract morphology and a higher risk of atrioventricular block after transcatheter aortic valve replacement, a smaller valve size is an acceptable option to reduce the risk of conduction disturbances.

Conduction disturbances constitute the most common complication following transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and are associated with an increase in morbidity and death, 1 , 2 , 3 longer hospital stays, and increased costs. 4 In spite of technical improvements in the devices and the modification of the implantation techniques, the incidence of permanent pacemaker implantation (PPI) ranges from 8.5% to 25.9% depending on the type of valve implanted. 5 , 6 , 7

The underlying physiopathology for the development of conduction disturbances seems to be related to the anatomy of the left bundle branch, which is located in the membranous portion of the ventricular septum in the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT), close to the aortic valve between the right and noncoronary cusp commissure. 5 The mechanical stress applied from the prosthesis to the membranous septum is considered one of the main mechanisms for the development of conduction disturbances following TAVR. 5 , 6 , 8 Previous studies have shown that preexisting conduction disturbances, patient anatomy (membranous septum length), 9 valve type, and several procedural‐related characteristics are associated with an increase in the risk of conduction disturbances after TAVR. 8 In this regard, implantation depth, percentage of oversizing, or LVOT eccentricity have been identified as predictors of conduction disturbances following TAVR. 8 , 10 , 11 In addition, a higher prosthesis size to LVOT diameter ratio was associated with a long‐term pacing requirement in patients receiving a PPI following self‐expanding TAVR. 11 Therefore, we hypothesized that preprocedural anatomic LVOT dimensions with respect to annulus area may play a key role in the development of conduction disturbances following TAVR. The aim of this study was to assess the relationship between the shape of the LVOT and the need for PPI implantation after TAVR.

Methods

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Design and Definitions

This is a single‐center retrospective study. Consecutive patients who underwent TAVR in our center were included. Patients with prior pacemaker implantation were excluded. Patients underwent multidetector computed tomography (CT) to evaluate aortic root and LVOT dimensions and morphology, and all studies were available for offline analyses. The CT images were obtained with a General Electric or Philips 64‐row multidetector CT scanner. The CT images were analyzed offline by 2 independent cardiologists blinded to the clinical data with a dedicated CT analysis software (Philips Portal).

CT Acquisition and Analyses

CT scan analyses and aortic root and LVOT measurements were performed at the end‐systolic phase using TAVR Planning software (IntelliSpace Portal version 6.0.3.12200, Phillips, Eindhoven, Netherlands) by 2 experts blinded to the clinical data. The interobserver variability of the data had a k value of 0.956. The CT scan data were retrospectively measured, and clinical follow‐up was prospectively analyzed.

The aortic annulus (AA) area and LVOT area were measured at 5 mm from the AA. 12 The ratio between the LVOT area and AA area was calculated. Patients were classified into 2 groups on the basis of the morphology of the LVOT: a tapered‐LVOT shape (group 1) and a non‐tapered LVOT shape (group 2). All patients in the tapered‐LVOT shape group had an AA area at least 10% greater than the LVOT area, with an LVOT area/AA area ratio <0.9. Patients included in the non‐tapered LVOT shape group (group 2) had an LVOT area/AA area ratio ≥0.9 and were classified as having either a tubular LVOT shape (similar areas of both AA and LVOT) or a flared LVOT shape (AA area smaller than LVOT area). See LVOT morphology in Figure 1. Other anatomic and procedural characteristics were evaluated and included in the analysis. Annular and LVOT eccentricity were defined as (1 − [minimum diameter/maximum diameter]) × 100. The ratio between prosthesis size/LVOT diameter and oversizing index(OI) was also calculated. OI was calculated as follows: (1) self‐expandable prosthesis: ([nominal valve perimeter − measured perimeter]/nominal valve perimeter)×100; and (2) balloon‐expandable prosthesis: ([nominal valve area − measured area]/nominal valve area)×100.

Ethical Considerations

This study has been conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki principles and Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The study received approval from our hospital's ethics committee. Epidemiological and clinical data were obtained from electronic medical records, and the data were stored in an anonymized fashion. An informed consent was signed by all patients.

Study End Points

The primary end point was to assess the relationship between preprocedural LVOT shape measured by CT and the rate of PPI following TAVR. The specific protocol for pacemaker implantation used in our institution was based on the Journals of the American College of Cardiology Scientific Expert Panel recommendations. 13 Secondary end points included the relationship between LVOT shape, new‐onset left bundle‐branch block (LBBB), and the combined end point of transient LBBB, permanent LBBB, and PPI after the procedure. Moreover, relationship between LVOT shape and other clinical events defined according to the recommendations of Valve Academic Research Consortium 3 were analyzed. 14 Frailty was defined according to the Valve Academic Research Consortium 3 criteria. 14 Clinical follow‐up was predefined up to 1 year and performed via clinic visits or telephone contact.

Statistical Analysis

The data are presented as mean (SD) for continuous variables with a normal distribution, median (interquartile range) for continuous variables with a nonnormal distribution, and frequency (percentage) for categorical variables. Differences were detected using the t test for normally distributed continuous variables and χ2 or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Univariable analysis was performed for quantitative variables and reported as odds ratios with 95% CIs. In addition, a multivariable analysis (Cox regression) was performed to identify predictors of PPI following TAVR. Variables with a significance of P<0.05 in the univariable analysis were entered into the model: a tapered LVOT morphology, prior right bundle‐branch block (RBBB), prior atrial fibrillation, type of prostheses (self‐expandable valves versus balloon‐expandable valves), LVOT eccentricity, OI, and prosthesis size/LVOT diameter ratio. Cumulative incidence of PPI at 12‐month follow‐up was calculated using the Gray’s K‐sample test and cumulative incidence curves. All the data were analyzed using STATA version16.0 (StataCorp., College Station, TX) and SPSS version 22.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY).

Results

From January 2018 to December 2020, 276 consecutive patients were included. Ninety‐one patients showed a tapered LVOT morphology and were assigned to group 1, and 185 patients with tubular LVOT or flared LVOT shape were assigned to group 2. The 2 groups had similar baseline characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors and prior surgical risk, baseline ECG, and type of prosthesis implanted, with the exception of dyslipidemia and prior percutaneous coronary intervention, both of which were more frequent in group 2.

With regard to procedural factors, self‐expandable prostheses were used in 41.3% of the whole cohort, the mean prosthesis size was 25.6±2.2 mm, and postdilation was performed in 17.8% of patients, without significant difference between groups. Prostheses were sized using manufacturer recommendations, including annular and LVOT dimensions. Of 91 patients with a tapered LVOT morphology, 54 were treated with a balloon‐expandable prosthesis and 37 were treated with a self‐expandable prosthesis. Conversely, patients with a nontapered LVOT shape (n=185) were treated with a balloon‐expandable prosthesis in 108 cases and a self‐expandable prosthesis in 77 cases. In the univariable analysis, LVOT eccentricity showed differences among groups, being significantly greater in the tapered LVOT group (29.3±8.6 versus 25.7±6.8, P<0.001). Likewise, there were significant differences in the prosthesis size/LVOT diameter ratio (1.2+0.1 versus 1.1+0.1, P<0.001) and OI (8.0±7.1% versus 10.4±8.0%, P=0.015). Regarding CT measurements, no differences in AA dimensions and membranous septum length were observed between groups. Discussion

This study describes for the first time that a simple anatomic feature of LVOT is associated with an increased risk of PPI following TAVR. In fact, a tapered LVOT morphology was significantly associated with the need for PPI following TAVR.

There are several anatomic and procedural characteristics that may result in higher mechanical stress in the LVOT and the adjacent conduction system, which could lead to conduction disturbances, including PPI or the development of new‐onset LBBB after TAVR. Previous studies have identified a number of factors that may contribute to PPI following TAVR. These include a larger prosthesis‐to‐LVOT diameter, a shorter membranous septum length, and implantation depth greater than membranous septum length. 3 , 9 , 11 , 15 , 16 , 17 Conversely, LVOT eccentricity and OI, which indicates the degree to which the valve size is larger than the annulus size, have been related to the development of LBBB but not the need for PPI in some previous studies. 9 , 10 , 16

In addition to the aforementioned factors, we analyzed LVOT morphology, rather than LVOT size alone. It was postulated that the stress applied to the LVOT is strongly related to the relationship between LVOT size and AA size, given that the prosthesis size is adjusted to the AA size. Consequently, the smaller the LVOT size in comparison with the AA size, the stronger the radial force applied to the LVOT by the prosthesis. With regard to the previously mentioned factors, such as LVOT eccentricity or OI, our findings are consistent with prior studies that did not find a relationship between these factors and the need for PPI. 9 , 10 , 16 Nevertheless, other parameters previously described as predictors of PPI, such as larger prosthesis‐to‐LVOT diameter, shorter membranous septum length, and implantation depth greater than membranous septum length, were not significantly different in our cohort. This discrepancy in our findings may be attributed to the fact that conduction disturbances after TAVR have a multifactorial pathogenesis including not only anatomic factors from the annulus and LVOT, but also patient baseline characteristics and existing conduction system disease, such as prior RBBB or first‐degree atrioventricular block. 3 , 9 , 11 , 15 , 16 , 17

Our findings have an important clinical implication, especially when annulus dimensions are on the border between 2 prothesis sizes. In this particular scenario, the LVOT dimensions may play an important role in the decision‐making process. For patients with a tapered LVOT and a higher risk of atrioventricular block (AVB) after TAVR, a smaller valve size may be a suitable option to reduce the risk of conduction disturbances.

On the other hand, previous studies have shown an increased risk of PPI in patients treated with self‐expandable valves. In our study, self‐expandable valve was not an independent predictor of PPI. In this regard, improvement in implantation technique using a cusp‐overlap view instead of the standard coplanar view may have contributed to this result. 18

Other clinical factors associated with the development of advanced AVB requiring PPI following TAVR include preexisting first‐degree AVB, prior RBBB, or prior atrial fibrillation. Similar to previous studies, 3 , 5 , 6 , 9 , 10 , 16 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 we identified prior RBBB to be the strongest predictor of atrioventricular conduction disturbances requiring PPI with a 4.3‐fold increased risk at 15‐month follow‐up. Unlike previous studies, 23 , 24 prior atrial fibrillation and first‐degree AVB were not found to be independent predictors of PPI in the present study. Nevertheless, a trend toward an increased risk of PPI in patients with previous first‐degree AVB was observed. We postulate that the lack of significant association observed in our study may be due to the relatively small sample size. 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22

Conduction disturbances are one of the greatest challenges we face following TAVR, especially when treating low‐risk young patients with greater life expectancy. Previous studies have shown that long‐term ventricular pacing may have a detrimental clinical effect at follow‐up. In those studies, an increased all‐cause death at long‐term follow‐up has been described in patients requiring PPI following TAVR. 25 , 26 Furthermore, the need for PPI after TAVR may imply an increase in morbidity and new hospitalizations due to heart failure. 26 , 27 Moreover, similarly to those patients who developed new‐onset LBBB, PPI following TAVR has been associated with a lack of left ventricular ejection fraction improvement after TAVR. 27 These results reinforce the need to focus on conduction disturbance prevention to avoid long‐term deleterious clinical effects of permanent pacemaker stimulation in patients treated with TAVR.

Limitations

The main limitations of this study are the small sample size along with the fact that it is a nonrandomized single‐center retrospective study and therefore our observation are hypothesis‐generating. Larger prospective cohorts could eventually allow the ability to draw more definitive conclusions. It has been described that the presence of significant LVOT calcification increases the risk of periprocedural and 1‐year death, stroke, myocardial infarction, paravalvular leak, and AA/root rupture after TAVR. Since this parameter was not associated with higher risk of new PPI, it was not analyzed in the present study. 28

Conclusions

The present study shows that a tapered‐LVOT morphology is associated with an increased risk of PPI after TAVR. A tapered LVOT shape indicates a discordance in size between the annulus and the LVOT, which may lead to a greater mechanical stress of cardiac conduction system driving to advanced AVB. LVOT morphology may be an additional factor to take into account when planning the procedure, in particular in those patients with a high risk of conduction disturbances. Reducing PPI rates following TAVR has a critical impact in long‐term outcomes after TAVR.

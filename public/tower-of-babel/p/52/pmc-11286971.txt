Predicting the epidemiological trend of acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis in China using Bayesian structural time-series model
Authors: Guangcui Xu, Ting Fan, Yingzheng Zhao, Weidong Wu, Yongbin Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to explore the application value of the Bayesian Time Structure Sequence (BSTS) model in estimating the acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis (AHC) epidemics. The reported AHC cases spanning from January 2011 to October 2022 in China were collated. Utilizing R software, the BSTS and Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) models were constructed using the data from January 2011 to December 2021. The prediction effect of both models was compared using the data from January to October 2022, and finally the AHC incidence from November 2022 to December 2023 was predicted. The results indicated that forecast errors under the BSTS model were lower than those under the ARIMA model. The actual AHC incidence in July 2022 from the ARIMA model deviated from the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the predicted value. However, the observed AHC incidence from the BSTS model fell within the 95% CI of the predicted value. Notably, the BSTS model predicted 26,474 new AHC cases in China from November 2022 to December 2023, exhibiting better prediction performance compared to the ARIMA model. This indicates that the BSTS model possesses a high application value for forecasting the epidemic trends of AHC, making it a valuable tool for disease surveillance and prevention strategies.Keywords: Acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis, BSTS, ARIMA, Epidemic trends, ForecastSubject terms: Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis (AHC), a Category C infectious disease in China, emerged as a novel ocular ailment in 1969. It predominantly occurs during the summer and autumn seasons, with a susceptibility that spans all age groups. Primarily triggered by variants of the Coxsackie virus group A type 24 (CoxA24), human enterovirus type 70 (EV70), and adenovirus1, AHC is characterized by its brief incubation period, swift onset, and robust infectivity2. This condition has garnered significant attention due to its widespread impact, positioning it as one of the most prevalent eye diseases among humans.

Transmission of AHC occurs primarily through contact with the eye secretions or contaminants of infected individuals. Among its hallmark symptoms are ocular discomfort, a sensation of a foreign body in the eye, photophobia, subconjunctival hemorrhage, eyelid edema, and tearing. Notably, subconjunctival hemorrhage stands out as a prominent symptom, often manifesting as spots or flakes, and gradually spreading from the upper to the lower regions of the conjunctiva. Furthermore, most patients exhibit submandibular lymphadenopathy and tenderness. Fortunately, as the conjunctivitis subsides, these symptoms tend to dissipate. However, in rare instances, iritis changes may occur3. It is worth noting that AHC is a self-limiting disease, with a relatively short natural course and favorable prognosis. To date, there is no specific therapeutic regimen for this condition.

AHC initially emerged in Ghana, West Africa, between June and August 1969, affecting 13,664 patients4. It was first detected in China in 19715. Since then, numerous countries and regions, including India, Indonesia, and Brazil6,7, have experienced smaller-scale outbreaks. In 1986, an AHC outbreak occurred in a tobacco factory in Zhengzhou, Henan province, and in 1988, it struck Guangzhou, with the number of patients surpassing 490,000 in just 70 days, significantly disrupting the daily lives and production activities of residents8. Research indicates that from 2013 to 2020, China reported a total of 289,518 AHC cases, averaging an incidence rate of 2.63 per 100,000 individuals. Notably, there were two minor peaks in 2014 and 2019, with the reported incidence rate increasing by 19.11% in 2014 compared to 2013 and by 8.00% in 2019 compared to 2018. However, in 2020, due to the implementation of preventative measures against the new coronavirus pandemic, the number of reported AHC cases significantly declined9. Given the relatively high number of reported AHC cases in China from 2011 to 2022, it is imperative to continuously analyze and predict the epidemic characteristics of AHC. This will provide a scientific foundation for timely adjustments and the formulation of effective prevention and control measures, as well as guidance for the rational allocation of limited resources.

In the 1970s, Box and Jenkins pioneered the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model10. Ever since, the ARIMA model has garnered widespread application across diverse domains, including economics, industry, and transportation, significantly contributing to the prediction and early warning of infectious diseases11–13. However, the forecasts of the ARIMA model are often influenced by various factors, including time, causing irregularities in time series that render the original fitting model inapplicable or diminish its predictive performance14. The Bayesian Time Structure Sequence (BSTS), on the other hand, is a dynamic regression model that allows parameters to evolve over time, accurately capturing the random behavior of time series. This approach allows for variance control and the imposition of prior beliefs on the model, thereby enhancing its robustness. In the context of intervention analysis, this method proves invaluable for predicting counterfactual scenarios, specifically outlining how the outcomes might unfold if a certain intervention were not implemented. This characteristic compensates for the shortcomings of the ARIMA model and potentially offers a more precise alternative15. Owing to its superior forecasting capabilities, the BSTS model has gained significant traction in numerous fields16–18, particularly in disease prediction. For instance, it has been utilized in forecasting multiple sclerosis in Tehran19, epidemiological trends of tuberculosis cases in China20, forecasting COVID-19 trends in Pakistan21, and estimating the prevalence of acquired immune deficiency syndrome22.

Currently, there is a lack of studies utilizing the BSTS model to forecast the epidemic trends of AHC. Thus, leveraging an in-depth analysis of AHC epidemic characteristics in China from 2011 to 2022, this study aims to apply both the BSTS and ARIMA models to verify and predict the epidemic trend of AHC in China. The objective is to explore the feasibility and potential advantages of the BSTS model in this context.

Results

Data description

From January 2011 to October 2022, China reported a cumulative total of 409,421 AHC cases, averaging at 2883 cases per month. The average annual incidence rate from 2011 to 2021 stood at 3.00 per 100,000 individuals. Notably, Fig. 1 highlights two significant peaks in 2014 and 2019. In 2014, the number of AHC cases surged to 41,741, corresponding to an incidence rate of 3.04 per 100,000 people. Similarly, in 2019, 40,701 cases were reported, translating to an incidence rate of 2.96 per 100,000 individuals. Comparing 2014 with 2013, we observed a substantial increase of 14.16% in the number of reported cases, accompanied by a 13.43% rise in the reported incidence. Similarly, in 2019, when compared to 2018, there was an 8.51% increase in the number of reported cases, coupled with an 8.03% elevation in the reported incidence. The seasonal relative (SR) of the AHC incidence from January to December in China were 0.721, 0.619, 0.914, 0.987, 1.112, 1.219, 1.289, 1.196, 1.190, 0.948, 0.915, and 0.890, respectively, showing that the highest number of cases is from July to September and the lowest is from January to February in China, indicating with obvious seasonality. This finding is corroborated by Fig. 2, which depicts the monthly time series of AHC incidence reported in China from January 2011 to October 2022. The data comprises three distinct components: reported case, trends, and cycles. The trend analysis reveals that, overall, the incidence of AHC remained relatively stable. Furthermore, a distinct periodicity is evident in the data (Fig. 2).

ARIMA model diagnostics

Utilizing the "auto.arima" program, we analyzed the AHC data spanning from January 2011 to December 2021. After rigorous evaluation, the ARIMA (1,0,0) (2,1,1)12 model emerged as the optimal choice, based on the Akaike’s Information Criterion (AIC) of 1900.4, the AIC corrected for small sample bias (AICC) of 1900.91, and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) of 1914.32. Within this model, the autoregressive method at lag 1 month (AR1) exhibited a significant coefficient of 6.391 (P < 0.001), indicating a strong autocorrelation at this temporal lag. Similarly, the seasonal autoregressive method with a lag of 1 month (SAR1) and 2 months (SAR2) demonstrated coefficients of − 1.741 (P = 0.042) and − 4.320 (P < 0.001), respectively, suggesting significant seasonal autoregressive patterns. Additionally, the seasonal moving average method with a lag of 1 month (SMA1) yielded a coefficient of − 3.727 (P < 0.001). All these coefficients passed the respective statistical tests, thereby confirming the reliability and validity of the chosen ARIMA model. In the autocorrelation function (ACF) and partial autocorrelation function (PACF) plots of the residuals, the coefficients for various lags predominantly remained within the 95% confidence interval (CI) (Fig. 3). There was no conditional heteroskedastic behaviour and volatility (ARCH effect) on the residuals under the Lagrangian multiplier (LM) test (P = 0.949); Ljung-Box Q test χ2 = 0.057, P = 0.811, indicating that the residuals were random sequences. These results meant that the model fits the epidemic pattern of AHC adequately.

Discussion

The analysis and prediction of the AHC epidemic trend based on surveillance data are of great significance for prevention and control. In this study, we aimed to validate the accuracy of the BSTS and ARIMA models by comparing their predictive capabilities against actual data spanning from January to October 2022, and the BSTS model was used to predict the AHC epidemic trend in China from November 2022 to December 2023. The fitting of both models to the periodic changes of the time series data and the characteristic of the epidemic trend was roughly consistent with the actual situation, suggesting that both models can be used to simulate the epidemic trend of AHC in China. Moreover, the error evaluation metrics of the predicted value in the BSTS model are smaller than those in the ARIMA model. It suggests that the prediction performance of the BSTS model is better.

The peak incidence of AHC in 2014 and 2019 is in line with the trend of AHC occurrence peaks every 3–5 years in China9. Researches1,9 show that it is because the virus causing AHC is more likely to mutate. Therefore, strengthening the monitoring of the AHC pathogen gene and understanding its mutation rules play important roles in the prevention and treatment of the disease. The peak of AHC occurs annually from July to September, possibly attributed to climatic and environmental factors, as well as the movement and congregation of students at the start of the school year2. This aligns with findings from other studies8. It is anticipated that a targeted intensification of AHC prevention and control efforts, coupled with improvements in basic sanitation conditions and protective measures in schools, could significantly reduce the epidemic intensity of AHC in China.

In this study, we leveraged the BSTS model to forecast the epidemic trend of AHC and benchmarked its performance against the ARIMA model. And the BSTS model achieved a MAPE of 17.97%, indicating that it can reasonably predict the AHC trend in China25, which is consistent with the previous results26.

The ARIMA model is a simple yet practical tool for predicting the epidemics of infectious diseases, including hepatitis B, COVID-19, and scarlet fever21,25,26. Although our research found that the BSTS model achieved higher prediction accuracy compared to the ARIMA model, the ARIMA model still holds significant value in forecasting the epidemic trends of infectious diseases, consistent with previous studies21,25,26. Qiu H et al.27 utilized an ARIMA (1,1,2) (0,1,1)12 model based on the AHC incidence data from 2004 to 2017 in China, which differs from the ARIMA (1,0,0) (2,1,1)12 model used in our study. This variation primarily stems from the changing disease-affecting factors, such as environmental and sanitation conditions, across different periods. This confirms the need for horizontal or longitudinal comparison of constructing models in different regions or at different times. However, the ARIMA model is most suitable for short-term forecasting28 and it is prone to overfitting when incorporating covariates21.

Currently, the BSTS model has gained widespread adoption across diverse domains, including healthcare, transportation, finance, and numerous other sectors16–19,29. This model stands out as a dynamic regression approach that dynamically assigns weights to model factors based on their inclusion probability, thereby selecting the most informative parameters without imposing linearity assumptions on the regression component. The Bayesian framework inherent in BSTS facilitates the incorporation of prior knowledge, leading to a more precise representation of estimation certainty, which evolves over time for enhanced accuracy. This methodology can further be extended to dynamic regression frameworks, allowing regression coefficients to vary dynamically over time30,31. BSTS offers a promising avenue for refinement due to its capacity to handle a vast array of potential covariates and its ability to automatically select the most informative predictors. Moreover, it derives the posterior distribution from prior information and the likelihood function of the data during analysis, effectively managing covariates30–32. These attributes address the limitations of ARIMA, explaining why BSTS predictions often outperform ARIMA. Crucially, BSTS does not rely on differentiation or hysteresis, offering a more intuitive interpretation of the various components within a sequence. Sensitivity analysis results further confirm the superiority of the BSTS model over ARIMA in this study. Given these advantages30–32, extrapolations using BSTS to predict the incidence of AHC or other infectious diseases in different regions are viable, though further validation remains crucial.

The study encountered several limitations. First, despite the significant enhancement in the quality of infectious disease reporting in China since the establishment of the national network for direct epidemic reporting in 2004, it is crucial to acknowledge that challenges persist. One such challenge is the potential for noise in the data resulting from variations in diagnostic criteria and reporting practices across regions. Second, our study did not account for several factors that could influence the incidence of AHC, including the diverse climate distributions and varying levels of air pollution across China. It is difficult to consider air pollution and meteorological factors on a China-wide scale, but these factors can be taken into account when using local data. In the sensitivity analysis, we considered the impact of COVID-19, and the results showed that the BSTS model outperformed ARIMA. Univariate time series models are simple and require only endogenous variables without other exogenous variables. The models use statistical methods to predict the future according to the past change trend, usually in line with the law of development of things. While considering the development trend, the models pay attention to the impact of seasonal and cyclical changes on specific time points, which is more accurate. Lastly, our forecasts were confined to the short-term horizon. In future endeavors, we aim to address these limitations. Specifically, we intend to integrate environmental covariates, such as climate variations and air pollution levels, into our BSTS model. This approach will enable us to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the factors that drive AHC trends. In future studies, we will consider integrating these environmental covariates into the BSTS model to provide a more holistic view of the factors driving AHC trends and continue to explore the approaches and their suitability for application to AHC.

Conclusions

In summary, AHC in China showed obvious cyclical and seasonal behavior. The BSTS model has a high application value in predicting AHC trends. The predicted incidence level of AHC from November 2022 to December 2023 is still high, so it is necessary to increase the publicity of the AHC prevention and control, improve the level of health services and etiological surveillance. In addition, the incidence of AHC is susceptible to climate, monitoring capacity and other factors. When using the BSTS the model, it should be continuously updated and appended to calibrate the model, and compared with other predictive models to further find an optimal model to evaluate the trend of the AHC epidemic.

Materials and methods

Data source

The reported cases of AHC in China from January 2011 to October 2022 were from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, and the population data were from the China Health Statistical Yearbook.

Case diagnosis principles

Based on the "Diagnostic Criteria for Acute Hemorrhagic Conjunctivitis" (WS217-2008)33 adopted in China, patients exhibiting a history of epidemiology related to AHC, clinical symptoms and signs that align with the stipulated criteria, primarily manifesting in mononuclear cells on conjunctival cytology, can be provisionally diagnosed. For a definitive diagnosis, it is advisable to corroborate the clinical findings with additional etiological or serological tests.

The diagnosis of cases strictly adheres to the diagnostic criteria for AHC. Following the initial reporting of a case, the public health department of the reporting hospital and the county-level disease prevention and control agency undertake rigorous verification and revision of the case information to guarantee its authenticity and comprehensiveness. The daily confirmed cases were uploaded to provincial centers for disease control and prevention, which in turn are uploaded to the Chinese centers for disease control and prevention. This ensures that the case data is accurate and reliable for further analysis and decision-making.

The study was approved by the institutional review board of Xinxiang Medical University (No: XYLL-2019072). All methods were carried out following the relevant guidelines and regulations. The need for informed consent was waived by the study Ethics Committee of Xinxiang Medical University.

ARIMA model

The ARIMA model forecasts future variations by analyzing past and current values, capturing the autocorrelation of time-dependent random variables34. Depending on the stationarity of the original data series and the components incorporated in the regression, it can be categorized as autoregressive (AR), moving average (MA), autoregressive moving average (ARMA), and ARIMA14,34. When seasonal trends are present in the data, a seasonal ARIMA model is employed, structured as ARIMA (p, d, q) (P, D, Q), where d and D refer to ordinary and seasonal differencing orders, respectively. p and q designate the AR and MA orders of the model, while P and Q represent the seasonal AR (SAR) and seasonal moving average (SMA) orders, with s being the seasonal cycle14. The model-building process typically encompasses four stages10: identification, estimation, diagnosis, and prediction. The ARIMA builder facilitates the automatic selection of optimal model parameters. Initially, the non-seasonal and seasonal parameters of the ARIMA method must be determined. This identification process requires a stationary sequence; thus, differencing is applied until stationarity is achieved. The appropriate orders for non-seasonal and seasonal autoregression, integration, and moving averages can be specified using differential ACF and PACF. In the second step, the best possible model is estimated. This involves comparing evaluation metrics such as the AIC, AICC, BIC, and log-likelihood. The optimal model is selected based on lower values of AIC, AICC, and BIC, and a higher value of log-likelihood. The third crucial step involves diagnosing the identified model. Here, it is essential to ensure that the model coefficients exhibit statistical differences, while the model's residual sequences are not significantly deviating from zero. Furthermore, it is vital to confirm that there are no discernible patterns (indicating white noise) in the ACF, PACF, and Ljung-Box Q tests. Once the ARIMA model successfully meets these criteria, it can proceed to making predictions and evaluating the effectiveness of these predictions.

BSTS model

Historically, traditional statistical models, such as static regression models and ARIMA models, have been employed to forecast epidemic patterns, yet they possess inherent limitations35. Static regression models, for instance, model the summation of trends, seasonality, and random fluctuations in a deterministic fashion, often assuming that epidemic patterns remain stable over time, which is seldom the case in practice15,35. Similarly, ARIMA models generate predictions based on past disease patterns and previously predicted residuals21,35, potentially leading to overfitting, especially in the presence of covariates15. In contrast, the Bayesian structural time series (BSTS) method offers a more adaptive approach by allowing model parameters to vary over time. It boasts a vast array of explanatory variables and accurately captures the random characteristics of time series15,35. Defined through two key equations, the observation equation yt=μt+χtβ+St+et;et∼N0,σe2 and the transition equation μt+1=μt+vt;Vt∼N0,σv2, the BSTS model incorporates regressors (χt), seasonality (St), and an unobserved local level term (μt) that evolves over time. The noise terms et and vt are independent Gaussian distributions. In this study, the response data yt represents the annual number of positive cases of AHC. The BSTS methodology was developed as an integrated system comprising three statistical models32: the Kalman filter for estimating trends and seasonal behavior, Spike and slab regression for variable selection, and the Bayesian model for final predictions30. Notably, the BSTS approach rarely relies on specific hypothetical norms14, making it a robust and versatile tool for forecasting epidemic patterns. Moreover, the BSTS method adeptly manages uncertainty, as it enables the quantification of posterior uncertainty that comprises the latent subseries.

Statistical process

The ARIMA and BSTS models were crafted utilizing R software, and their predictive accuracy was rigorously assessed through various metrics including the MAD, RMSE, MAPE, and RMSPE. The analysis of long-term trends, cycles, and seasonal relative (SR) of data come from Hodrick-Prescott (HP) and seasonal decomposition36,37. A SR value (also known as the seasonal index or seasonal factor) refers to the degree to which epidemic level tends to be higher (or lower) than average epidemic level during that particular period. In this study, we used a multiplicative seasonal classification method. This value serves as a measure of how the epidemic level in a particular period compares to the average epidemic level over the entire year. A SR value of 1.00 signifies that the epidemic level for that period is precisely at the annual average. A value of 1.10, for instance, indicates that the epidemic level is 10% higher than the average, while a value of 0.85 suggests that the epidemic level is 15% lower than the average for that particular period.

The LM-test was used to test whether the model was built correctly. Meanwhile, the Ljung-Box test assessed whether the model adhered to the white noise sequence, providing insights into the adequacy of the model's ability to extract data information10. Furthermore, a sensitivity analysis was conducted to authenticate the forecasting prowess of both the BSTS and ARIMA models, particularly in the context of the impact of COVID-19. A statistical significance threshold of P < 0.05 was adopted for these analyses.

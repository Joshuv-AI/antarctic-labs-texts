A compact metasurface-based tri-band MIMO antenna with minimalist decoupling for multi-standard wireless devices
Authors: Zhaozhi Gu, Mengyue Guo, Shibao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a compact metasurface-based tri-band MIMO antenna for space-constrained multi-standard wireless devices. The antenna operates across 3.3–3.8 GHz (5G n78), 4.4–5.0 GHz (5G n79), and 5.1–5.9 GHz (Wi-Fi (802.11n/ac/ax)) bands. Its radiating element is a metasurface composed of seven hexagon-slotted hexagonal patches arranged in a cellular configuration, optimally designed using Characteristic Mode Analysis for tri-band performance. A key challenge addressed is the severe mutual coupling that arises when this element is extended to a densely spaced 1 × 2 MIMO array with near-zero edge-to-edge spacing. To overcome this, a strategically simple decoupling structure using only two pairs of vertical metallic posts is proposed. This minimalist approach proves highly effective, as both simulation and measurement results demonstrate enhanced isolation across all three bands, with |S₂₁| suppressed to -20.8 dB, -19.1 dB, and − 24.3 dB, respectively, while maintaining well-preserved impedance matching. Furthermore, the antenna exhibits excellent MIMO performance, with measured results confirming excellent isolation, low correlation, and minimal Channel Capacity Loss across the operating bands. The scalable cellular architecture of the metasurface allows for adaptation to irregular array geometries, making the proposed design a practical and high-performance solution for advanced, compact wireless applications.Keywords: MIMO antennas, Metasurface, Tri-band decoupling, Vertical metallic posts, Characteristic mode analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

The rapid evolution of wireless communication technologies has led to the coexistence of multiple systems, which requires individual devices to operate at dual or multi-band frequencies1,2. This places critical demands on antennas to support dual-band or multi-band communication capabilities3,4. While Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) technology is essential for meeting growing data throughput requirements, integrating multiple antenna elements into a limited space inevitably leads to strong mutual coupling. This coupling severely degrades channel capacity, radiation efficiency, and overall system reliability, presenting a primary bottleneck in the design of high-performance wireless systems5,6.

Conventional multiband antennas, such as multi-arm7,8 and slotted designs9,10, often struggle to meet the simultaneous requirements of compact size, multi-band operation, and high isolation in MIMO configurations. The emergence of metasurface-based antennas offers a promising alternative. Unlike conventional antennas, metasurface-based designs offer tunable frequency response through their engineered subwavelength structures, enabling compact multiband operation that is particularly valuable for communication systems. Recent advances in metasurface antennas have mainly focused on dual-band or wideband operations, low-profile configurations, circularly polarized radiation, and reconfigurability11–16. However, when configured as densely packed MIMO arrays, these metasurface antennas exhibit particularly severe mutual coupling due to intensified inter-element electromagnetic interactions17.

Several decoupling techniques have been developed in conventional MIMO arrays, including defected ground structures18,19, parasitic elements20,21, electromagnetic bandgap (EBG) layers22,23, neutralization line techniques24,25, and lumped components26,27. Moreover, recent studies have demonstrated advanced approaches that combine multiple decoupling mechanisms, such as integrating DGS with other structures to achieve enhanced wideband isolation for closely spaced microstrip antennas28,29. However, these conventional and hybrid techniques often introduce undesirable complexity or fail to comprehensively address multi-band coupling when applied to metasurface-based MIMO systems. In response, several specialized decoupling strategies have been developed for the metasurface MIMO antenna. For instance, reference30 employs embedded vias and a decoupling strip for polarization orthogonality across 2.52–2.95 GHz. Reference31 utilizes a via array, guided by characteristic mode analysis, to achieve isolation through modal cancellation in the 3.3–4.2 GHz band. Similarly, reference32 grounds metasurface patches with vias to attain decoupling across 3.7–4.3 GHz. For dual-band scenarios, reference33 demonstrates an alternative approach that combines via arrays for low-frequency (3.44–3.54 GHz) suppression with optimized patches for high-frequency (4.38–5.84 GHz) isolation. Despite these advances, a critical limitation persists: these solutions predominantly target single- or dual-band operation. There is a notable lack of designs that effectively address tri-band isolation, which is imperative for modern mobile terminals that need to concurrently support 5G NR (n78 and n79) and Wi-Fi bands. Furthermore, many existing techniques rely on complex structural modifications, such as intricate via patterns or multi-layer EBGs, which increase fabrication complexity and cost. Therefore, effectively suppressing multiband coupling has become a critical challenge that must be addressed to realize the full potential of metasurface-based antennas in high-performance MIMO systems. It is essential to clarify the rationale for using a metasurface radiator rather than a single patch. A metasurface provides greater design flexibility within a compact area, allowing precise control of multiple resonant modes for tri-band operation, a significant challenge for a single element. Its structured, multi-element nature also facilitates systematic design using Characteristic Mode Analysis and supports scalable, densely packed array configurations. Thus, the metasurface is a key enabler for high-performance, multi-band MIMO arrays in space-constrained devices.

The antenna consists of three metallic layers from top to bottom: a metasurface (MTS) on the M-M’ plane, a ground plane on the G-G’ plane, and a microstrip feed line on the F-F’ plane. These are separated by two dielectric substrates: Substrate 1 is Arlon AD 600 (εr = 6.15, tan δ = 0.005, thickness = 5 mm), and Substrate 2 is FR-4 (εr = 4.3, tan δ = 0.02, thickness = 0.8 mm). The MTS is composed of seven identical hexagon-slotted hexagonal patches arranged in a compact heptagon-reuse cluster pattern. The adjacent sub-patches are spaced a distance G apart. The central ground plane, with a width of W, incorporates an A×B slot. A feeding slot of dimensions Lf × Wf is etched on the underside of the ground plane, centered precisely beneath the A×B slot. The initial design adopts a hexagonal metasurface unit configuration, where seven identical elements are arranged in a honeycomb-like cluster pattern, as illustrated in Fig. 3. To gain physical insight and guide the design for tri-band operation, Characteristic Mode Analysis (CMA) was performed on this un-fed metasurface structure using CST Microwave Studio.

Figure 4 presents the Modal Significance (MS) for the first eight characteristic modes of the initial design. Multiple modes exhibit MS values above 0.707 at 4.5 GHz and 5.5 GHz, indicating their excitation potential, while at 3.5 GHz, all MS values remain below this threshold, suggesting no readily excitable mode. Structural modifications are therefore necessary to enable effective mode excitation at the lower band.

To achieve the need for low-frequency excitation, hexagonal slots are introduced into the initial metasurface for modal significance control. The modified layout is depicted in Fig. 5. The subsequent characteristic mode analysis in Fig. 6 demonstrates that this slotted configuration successfully generates a dominant mode (MS ≈ 1) at 3.5 GHz. As a result, the requirement for effective mode excitation in the low-frequency band is satisfactorily met.

Structure of the initial slotted metasurface element.

Modal Significance of the optimized structure.

The analysis confirms that introducing hexagonal slots effectively enhance the low-band performance. The optimized final structure, illustrated in Fig. 1 with key dimensions summarized in Table 1, was subsequently subjected to characteristic mode analysis. As shown in Fig. 6, the resulting Modal Significance (MS) values exceed 0.707 across all three target bands, validating the successful generation of excitable modes at the desired frequencies.

Design of the coupled feeding structure

The modal significance analysis in Fig. 6 identifies the dominant characteristic modes for each target band: Mode 2 at 3.5 GHz, Modes 3, 5, and 6 at 4.5 GHz, and Modes 7 and 8 at 5.5 GHz. To achieve effective tri-band operation, the feeding structure must be designed to simultaneously excite these specific modes across all three bands. Among common feeding techniques for metasurface antennas, such as coaxial probe, coplanar waveguide, and aperture coupling, the aperture-coupled method was selected. This choice was guided by the characteristic current distributions of the dominant modes, as shown in Fig. 7. The optimal feed position was determined by identifying regions exhibiting strong current density and directional continuity. Observations indicate that high-current-density areas are consistently concentrated along the adjacent edges between hexagonal unit cells, with a predominantly vertical orientation.

Modal current distribution.

Accordingly, a rectangular coupling aperture is oriented transversely to the direction of current flow to maximize magnetic coupling efficiency. A 50-ohm microstrip feedline, positioned on the backside of substrate 2, is used to excite the aperture. The final optimized design of the tri-band metasurface antenna, incorporating this coupled feeding structure, is presented in Fig. 1, with detailed geometrical parameters provided in Table 1.

Parametric study

To gain deeper insight into the operating mechanism, a parametric study was conducted on the inter-patch spacing (G), hexagonal side length (R), and the slot side length (R1) to examine their effects on the antenna’s modal significance.

Densely spaced MIMO antenna design and decoupling implementation

Densely spaced 1 × 2 MIMO antenna

Two identical metasurface antenna elements were placed edge-to-edge to form a densely spaced 1 × 2 MIMO array, as shown in Fig. 13. The resulting S-parameters in Fig. 14 indicate that the array maintains satisfactory impedance matching (|S₁₁|) across the three target bands. However, severe mutual coupling is observed in certain frequency ranges, which will degrade the MIMO performance and must be addressed with effective decoupling measures.

1 × 2 metasurface MIMO antenna.

S-parameters of the 1 × 2 MIMO array without decoupling.

The appearance of an extra resonance near 3 GHz in the coupled array, absent in the isolated element (Fig. 2), is attributed to strong inter-element coupling. At this frequency, the coupling path effectively introduces a secondary resonance that alters the port impedance. This coupling-induced resonance further confirms the need for the decoupling structure implemented in the following subsection.

Decoupling scheme using vertical metallic posts

A decoupling structure employing a pair of resonant vertical metallic posts is introduced between the two antenna elements. The design is guided by the current distribution observed in the coupled array. As shown in Fig. 15, this compact resonant unit exhibits high surface impedance at its design frequency, effectively creating a localized stop-band in the coupling path between the elements.

1 × 2 Metasurface MIMO Antenna with vertical metallic posts.

While inspired by the high-impedance surface concept of EBGs, this minimalist design uses only two posts to achieve a similar band-stop effect through resonance, rather than relying on periodic dispersion engineering. The two posts are arranged symmetrically about the array centerline. This symmetric placement ensures both radiating elements operate in an identical electromagnetic environment, which is crucial for maintaining balanced MIMO performance and stable radiation patterns. Positioning this compact decoupling unit centrally between the elements effectively blocks the primary coupling path without interfering with the individual antenna’s radiation or feed networks.

Figure 16 illustrates the decoupling performance with metallic post radii of 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 mm. The results indicate that a larger radius does not necessarily improve isolation. Within a certain range, increasing the radius reduces mutual coupling by providing sufficient reflective impedance to block coupling path.

Decoupling performance versus metallic post radius.

However, an excessively small radius fails to establish adequate impedance, while an oversized radius disturbs the antenna’s original performance and degrades radiation characteristics. Based on the parametric analysis, a radius of 1.0 mm was selected as the optimal value. The corresponding S-parameters of the final design are presented in Fig. 17.

S-parameters of the decoupled MIMO array.

The proposed decoupling strategy achieves substantial isolation enhancement while providing well-maintained impedance matching across all three operating bands. The |S₂₁| after decoupling is suppressed to levels below − 20.8 dB in the 3.3–3.8 GHz band, -19.1 dB in the 4.4–5.0 GHz band, and − 24.3 dB in the 5.1–5.9 GHz band, demonstrating effective suppression of mutual coupling throughout the operating spectrum.

Figure 18 compares the surface current distributions at 3.5, 4.5, and 5.5 GHz without and with decoupling. When the left element is excited and the right element is match-terminated, strong coupling currents are observed between the two antennas. After inserting the metallic posts, the current becomes highly concentrated around the posts, which effectively suppresses energy transfer toward the right antenna and confirms the notable decoupling effect achieved.

Comparison of surface current distributions without and with decoupling.

The vertical metallic posts physically connect the inner radiating patch to the surrounding parasitic structure. This direct connection modifies the current distribution of the characteristic modes, which aids in maintaining stable impedance matching across the operating bands. More importantly, it establishes a localized high-impedance region within the near-field coupling path between the adjacent antennas. This electromagnetic band-stop behavior is directly captured and explained by the equivalent circuit model presented in the following subsection, where the posts are represented as a parallel LC resonator suppressing the mutual coupling path.

Circuit diagram of the proposed MIMO antenna

To provide insight into the operating and decoupling mechanisms of the proposed antenna from a fundamental circuit-theory perspective, an equivalent circuit model is developed and illustrated in Fig. 19.

Equivalent circuit model of the MIMO antenna.

In this model, the tri-band response of each metasurface element is represented by three parallel RLC branches (L1-C1-R1, L2-C2-R2, L3-C3-R3), corresponding to the characteristic modes at 3.5, 4.5, and 5.5 GHz, respectively. Coupling networks (C12/M12, C23/M23, C13/M13) between these branches model the inter-modal interactions observed in the CMA.

The key decoupling mechanism is clearly represented. The two pairs of vertical metallic posts are modeled as a single parallel LC resonator (L-post and C-post), connected directly between Port 1 and Port 2. Near its resonant frequencies, this block presents a high impedance, effectively acting as a band-stop filter that suppresses the primary mutual coupling path. This circuit representation directly explains the significant isolation enhancement achieved across all three bands, linking the minimalist physical structure to its function through fundamental circuit concepts.

Measurement results and discussion

The fabricated prototype of the proposed tri-band metasurface MIMO antenna is shown in Fig. 20. The S-parameters were measured with a vector network analyzer (SNA5022A). A full two-port Short-Open-Load-Thru (SOLT) calibration was performed directly at the ends of the coaxial cables connected to the antenna ports to ensure measurement accuracy. The measured results are presented in Fig. 21.

Fabricated prototype of the proposed antenna.

Simulated and measured S-parameters of the decoupled MIMO array.

The reflection coefficient shows a continuous passband from 3.3 to 6 GHz with |S₁₁| below − 6 dB, resulting from the closely spaced characteristic modes designed for tri-band operation. Importantly, high isolation is maintained within each target band: |S₂₁| is below − 20.8 dB in the 3.3–3.8 GHz band (5G n78), below − 19.6 dB in the 4.4–5.0 GHz band (5G n79), and below − 20.4 dB in the 5.1–5.9 GHz band (Wi-Fi). This confirms the antenna’s effective tri-band MIMO operation for multi-standard applications.

Figure 22 presents the measured radiation patterns in the yoz and xoz planes at three key frequencies (3.5, 4.5, and 5.5 GHz), showing the co-polarization and cross-polarization patterns for antenna elements 1 and 2. Figure 23 shows the measured peak gain and radiation efficiency of the proposed antenna.

Measured far-field radiation patterns.

Measured peak gain and efficiency.

The results demonstrate cross-polarization suppression exceeding 15 dB relative to the peak of the co-polarization pattern across all three operating bands. This high polarization purity confirms efficient radiation of the desired mode, indicating that the moderate efficiency in Fig. 23 originates from inherent system losses (e.g., within the substrate). Consequently, the antenna achieves its satisfactory gain primarily through high directivity, a characteristic attributed to the robust radiation mechanism evidenced by the low cross-polarization. This analysis aligns with the practical link budget consideration where low cross-pol ensures minimal polarization mismatch loss, rendering the reported gain practically effective.

It is noted that while the 3.5 GHz resonance is dominated by a single characteristic mode (Mode 2), the 4.5 GHz and 5.5 GHz bands are achieved by exciting multiple modes (Modes 3, 5, 6, and Modes 7, 8, respectively), as identified in Sect. 2.2. This multi-mode strategy is intentionally adopted to broaden the operating bandwidth and enhance impedance matching across the wider 5G n79 and Wi-Fi bands, a common trade-off in compact multiband designs. Importantly, the measured cross-polarization remains suppressed below − 15 dB even under multi-mode excitation, indicating that the excited modes are predominantly co-polarized and combine constructively in the far field. Consequently, radiation purity is preserved, and the antenna maintains well-behaved patterns suitable for MIMO operation, which is further supported by the excellent diversity metrics presented subsequently.

Based on the far-field equations given in39,40, several key metrics are calculated to further evaluate the performance of the proposed antenna, including the Envelope Correlation Coefficient (ECC), Diversity Gain (DG), Channel Capacity Loss (CCL), and Total Active Reflection Coefficient (TARC), which are presented in Fig. 24.

Calculated MIMO parameters (far-field based).

As shown in Fig. 24(a), the ECC maintains remarkably low values (well below 0.05) across all operating bands, while the DG remains consistently close to the ideal limit of 10 dB. Figure 24(b) reveals that both the CCL and TARC exhibit satisfactory performance. These results collectively confirm the antenna’s competent MIMO diversity performance and high port isolation.

Conclusions

In this work, a compact metasurface-based tri-band MIMO antenna integrated with a minimalist decoupling structure has been designed and experimentally validated. The antenna operates across the 3.3–3.8 GHz (5G n78), 4.4–5.0 GHz (5G n79), and 5.1–5.9 GHz (Wi-Fi) bands, enabling multi-standard connectivity in space-constrained devices. The radiating element is a cellular metasurface composed of seven hexagon-slotted patches, optimally designed using Characteristic Mode Analysis. A key achievement is the implementation of a densely spaced 1 × 2 MIMO array with near-zero edge-to-edge spacing, where mutual coupling is effectively suppressed across all three bands using only two pairs of vertical metallic posts. Measurement results confirm satisfactory isolation and radiation performance. Specifically, the measured MIMO diversity parameters are as follows: an envelope correlation coefficient (ECC) below 0.05, a diversity gain (DG) close to the ideal 10 dB, and a total active reflection coefficient (TARC) below − 10 dB, indicating competent diversity performance. The multilayer structure is essential to achieve concurrent tri-band operation and high port isolation at ultra-dense element spacing. This configuration enables independent optimization of the radiating metasurface and feeding network, and crucially accommodates the vertical decoupling posts, thereby justifying the added complexity for advanced compact devices. The scalable cellular architecture of the metasurface enables adaptation to irregular array geometries, making the proposed design a practical and high-performance solution for advanced, compact wireless applications. Future work will experimentally validate the scalability of the proposed design by extending it to larger arrays (e.g., 1 × 4 and 2 × 2) and exploring its adaptation to irregular patterns such as hexagonal clusters. These studies will assess mutual coupling evolution and practical viability in multi-element scenarios, further substantiating the cellular architecture’s potential for advanced compact MIMO systems.

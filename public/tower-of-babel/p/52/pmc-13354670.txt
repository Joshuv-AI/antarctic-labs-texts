Effects of interstitial photodynamic therapy on transplanted tumors of human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells in nude mice
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhang, Weizhong Han, Fangxia Niu, Hua Zhang, Yuxia Dong, Xiaohui Yang, Cunzhi Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the effect of Interstitial photodynamic therapy (IPDT) on transplanted tumors of human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells in nude mice. Twenty-four models of nude mice bearing A549 transplanted tumors were established, which were randomly and equally divided into four groups: the control group, the photosensitizer group, the laser group, and the IPDT group. Each group is treated according to the grouping principle. The tumor volume growth changes in each group were compared. The HE staining was performed to observe the pathomorphological changes of transplanted tumor cells in each group. The TUNEL assay was used to observe the apoptosis induced by IPDT. The qRT-PCR and western blot assays were used to detect the expression levels of Survivin, Caspase-3, Bax, Bcl-2, VEGF and HIF-1α genes in the transplanted tumor tissues. The results showed that the volume of the tumor volume in the IPDT group was noticeably smaller than that in the control, photosensitizer and laser groups, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). After HE staining of tumor tissues, there were various necrotic, disordered and foamy cells in the IPDT group. The cells in the other three groups were closely arranged with large hyperchromatic nuclei. The TUNEL assay revealed that more apoptotic cells with brown particles in the nucleus were found in the IPDT group. The results of qRT-PCR and western blot assays showed that compared with the other three groups, the expressions of Survivin, Bcl-2, VEGF, and HIF-1α in the IPDT group were decreased, while the expressions of Caspase-3 and Bax were increased. The differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05). IPDT could significantly inhibit the growth of A549 transplanted tumors in nude mice, which is possibly related to down-regulating the expression of apoptotic factors Survivin and Bcl-2 genes, up-regulating the expression of Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 genes, and down-regulating the expression of angiogenesis-related VEGF and HIF-1α genes.Keywords: Interstitial photodynamic therapy, Lung cancer, Animal models, Transplanted tumor, Apoptosis, Angiogenesis

Introduction

Cancer is one of the main diseases that pose a serious threat to human health, while lung cancer has become the most diagnosed and deadliest cancer in the last few decades [1, 2]. The conventional treatment methods include surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Besides, some new treatment methods, such as immune therapy, gene therapy, and so on [3]. However, surgical resection could not clean up all tumor cells, and it has postoperative recurrence and metastasis problems. The application of radiation therapy and chemotherapy is restrained because of serious adverse reactions, such as vomiting, myelosuppression, liver damage, radiation pneumonitis, and so on [4]. The minimally invasive therapy of a new type is aroused people's wide attention, which has become a new direction in the field of lung tumor treatment [5, 6].

In recent years, photodynamic therapy (PDT) has been a hot research topic in thoracic oncology. The relative ease of intervention and the ability of photodynamic therapy to ablate tumors have made it universally accepted and widely used worldwide [7, 8]. PDT is a minimally invasive treatment, mainly based on the photochemical reactions induced by laser irradiation at a specific wavelength for a certain time [9–12]. Compared with traditional treatments, PDT has great advantages in terms of less trauma, less toxicity, repeatability, and protection of the physiological function of normal tissue [13, 14].

In most cases, PDT is used to treat superficial lesions, such as skin tumors or proliferative diseases, where light penetration and effective depth of treatment are limited to < 10 mm [15]. In deep tumors or tumors greater than 10 mm in thickness, intra-tumoral light delivery is required. Interstitial photodynamic therapy (IPDT) is an emerging technology, in which light is delivered through fiber optics inserted into the solid tumor mass to treat larger or deeper tumors [16]. The laser fiber is inserted into the tumor through a needle or catheter that is guided by computed tomography (CT) or ultrasound imaging [17]. The advantage of this treatment is that it delivers sufficient irradiation to malignant cells while reducing damage to healthy tissue in the vicinity of the tumor.

In the past few years, with the gradual maturity of respiratory endoscopy technology, the progressive development of a diversity of new photosensitizers with varied advantages, and the updating of diversified laser instruments, the role of IPDT in the clinical treatment of respiratory malignant tumors has been paid more and more attention by researchers [18]. IPDT is especially suitable for patients with advanced lung cancer and central type lung cancer of V stage who are intolerant to surgery and have good compatibility with conventional treatment methods such as surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy [19]. In addition, with the increased recognition that IPDT can be well combined with other treatment modalities for oncology, it has become the focus of modern multidisciplinary treatment for thoracic malignancies.

Although several clinical trials have revealed that IPDT is a feasible treatment of advanced or inoperable cancer for controlling local symptoms and prolonging survival [20–22], its molecular mechanism and biological effects need to be further researched. In our study, the therapeutic effect and mechanism of IPDT on A549 transplanted tumors in nude mice were revealed to provide the basic experimental basis for IPDT in lung cancer treatment.

This study is based on the following core hypothesis: Interstitial photodynamic therapy (IPDT) can effectively inhibit the growth of human lung adenocarcinoma A549 transplanted tumors in nude mice by applying photosensitizer-mediated photochemical action to tumor tissues. We assume that its anti-tumor mechanism may involve the following two aspects: 1.Inducing tumor cell apoptosis: IPDT may activate the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway and promote programmed tumor cell death by down-regulating the expression of anti-apoptotic genes Survivin and Bcl-2, while up-regulating the expression of key apoptotic proteases Caspase-3 and Caspase-9. 2. Inhibiting tumor angiogenesis: IPDT may disrupt the hypoxia adaptability and angiogenesis capacity in the tumor microenvironment by down-regulating the expression of hypoxia-inducible factor HIF-1α and its downstream vascular endothelial growth factor VEGF, thereby restricting the nutrient supply and growth and spread of tumors. In conclusion, we hypothesize that IPDT can achieve multi-pathway inhibition of A549 transplanted tumors by synergistically regulating the expression of genes related to apoptosis and angiogenesis. This provides potential experimental evidence and mechanism explanation for the application of IPDT in the treatment of lung cancer.

Materials and methods

Reagent

The fetal bovine serum, trypsin, RPMI-1640 medium, penicillin, and streptomycin were purchased from Hyclone Company; HE staining kit and TUNEL cell apoptosis detection kit were from Roche Company; the reverse transcription kit and SYBR Green fluorescent quantitative PCR kit were purchased from TaKaRa Company; all antibodies were obtained from Wuhan BOSTER biological company.

Drugs and equipment

The hematoporphyrin derivative was purchased from Chongqing Huading Modern Biologic & Chemical Company; the photodynamic therapy instrument was made by Guilin Xingda Medical Equipment Company. In the study, the laser wavelength was set at 630 nm, and the output power was set at 100 mW/cm2. The experiment was set to 15 min per irradiation, with a 2-min interval between each 5-min irradiation.

Cell culture

Human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin in a humidified atmosphere containing 95% air and 5% CO2 at a constant temperature of 37˚C. Cells in the logarithmic phase were used in the experiment.

(NOTE: The culture of A549 cells follows the standard procedures recommended by ATCC).

Animal model building and treatments

In this study, the xenograft tumor model was established using BALB/c nude mice (age, 5–6 weeks; weight, 16.0–20.0 g). This strain of mice is congenitally deficient in functional T lymphocytes, so human tumor xenotransplantation can be successfully established and maintained without the application of immunosuppressants. Twenty-four male BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Weitong Lihua (Beijing) experimental animal technology company and raised in the SPF-grade animal room. The mice were randomly divided into four groups: the control group, the photosensitizer group, the laser irradiation group, and the IPDT group. The A549 cells were inoculated in the left dorsum of nude mice for 5 × 106 under aseptic conditions[23]. After inoculation, the nude mice were observed daily for subcutaneous tumor growth. The tumor size was measured every three days.

When the tumors reached 150–200 mm3, the mice were treated according to every group's principles. In the control group, the mice were injected intra-tumorally with saline for 2 mL/kg and irradiated with natural light. In the photosensitizer group, the mice were injected intra-tumorally with hematoporphyrin derivative for 10 mg/kg, protected from light for 4 h, and intermittently irradiated with natural light. In the laser group, the mice were injected intra-tumorally with saline for 2 mL/kg and intermittently irradiated with the laser for 15 min through a puncture array introduced into the optical fiber. In the IPDT group, the mice were injected intra-tumorally with hematoporphyrin derivative at 10 mg/kg, protected from light for 4 h, and then irradiated with the laser for 15 min through a puncture needle introduced into the optical fiber. Treatments were given at two-day intervals for a total of three times. All nude mice were executed by carbon dioxide asphyxiation two weeks after treatment, and the subcutaneous tumor samples were obtained. Some samples were placed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for HE and TUNEL assays, while the rest were stored in the − 80 °C refrigerator for qRT-PCR and Western blot.

Measurement of tumor volume and calculation of tumor inhibition rate

After the tumor formed, the long and short diameters of the tumor were measured with the vernier caliper every three days, and the volume was calculated with the formula V(mm3) = 1/2 × ab2 (V: volume, a: the long diameter, b: the short diameter). The tumor volume-time curve was drawn. The tumor inhibition rate was calculated by the formula: tumor inhibition rate = (1 − average volume of treated group/average volume of the control group) × 100%.

Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining

The removed tumor tissues were fixed in the 4% paraformaldehyde solution. After fixation for 24 h, the tissues were routinely dehydrated and embedded in paraffin wax. Then the tissues were sectioned using a slicer at a thickness of 3 to 5 µm, and stained with the HE staining kit. After being dehydrated with gradient ethanol and stained with hematoxylin for 5 min, the sections were incubated with ammonia and stained with eosin. Finally, the sections were observed under a light microscope for cytopathological morphological changes.

Terminal-deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay

The TUNEL assay of the paraffin section was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions. The dewaxed and hydrated tissues were incubated with 100 µL proteinase K at 37℃for 30 min. Then 100 µL equilibration buffer was added to equilibrate at room temperature for 10 min, and 50 µL TUNEL reaction was mixed with the samples and incubated for 60 min at 37 °C in a dark humid chamber. Then 50 µL Converter-POD solution was added to the samples and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The DAB substrate was added to slides and analyzed under the microscope. Apoptotic cells were observed under a light microscope and images were taken using Leica Application Suite software. The nuclei of apoptotic cells are crinkled and filled with brownish granules, which causes apoptotic cells to be stained brown. Three samples were selected from each group. The number of apoptotic cells and total cells were counted in five randomly selected optical fields in each sample. The apoptosis index (AI) represents the percentage of apoptotic cells in the cells measured.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Tumor tissue from each group was ground at low temperatures. Total RNA was extracted from the cells with TRIzol reagent. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, single-stranded cDNA was acquired by reverse transcription with the primer. The primers of Survivin, Caspase-3, Bcl-2, Bax, HIF-1α, VEGF and β-actin were obtained by Sangon Biotech. According to the SYBR Green fluorescent quantitative PCR kit, the cDNA was used as a template to amplify the target fragment. The number of template cycles (Ct) was calculated to determine the starting copy number(2–ΔΔCt) for each gene in each group with β-actin as the internal reference gene. The formula is: ΔCt = average Ct value of target gene − average Ct value of β-actin. ΔΔCt = ΔCt of experimental group target gene − ΔCt of control group target gene. The qRT-PCR reaction was performed with the Roche Light Cycler 480. (NOTE: The primer sequences are shown in the table below).

Western blot

Tumor tissue from each group was ground at low temperatures. The total protein in tumor tissue was extracted by adding RIPA lysate, and the BCA method determined the protein concentration. Thirty micrograms of total protein were separated by polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to the PVDE membrane. Then, sealing, incubating with Survivin, Caspase-3, Bcl-2, Bax, HIF-1α, VEGF and GAPDH primary antibody and secondary antibody were carried out according to the Western blot analysis kit instructions. The ECL luminescence method was used to develop the color, and images were captured using the Fusion Fx imaging system. The gray value of each band was measured by ImageJ. The relative expression of the target protein was calculated with GAPDH as the internal reference.

Statistical analysis

The statistical assessment was carried out with SPSS 23.0. The data was presented as ‘mean ± Standard deviation(x ± s)’. Every experiment above was repeated in at least three separate experiments. The one‐way ANOVA was used for comparison among groups, and differences between the two groups were assessed with the least significant difference (LSD-t) test. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Result

Observation of tumor volume growth and tumor inhibition rate

Our study evaluated the antitumor effects of IPDT by A549 transplanted tumors in nude mice. After inoculation with lung cancer A549 cells for about one week, round and oval nodules appeared subcutaneously at the scapula of nude mice. The tumor was tough to the touch, proving successful modeling. When the tumors reached 150–200 mm3 on the eighteenth day, the nude mice were treated respectively according to the appropriate grouping rules. After the growth curve of the tumor was shown as follows (Fig. 1). Before the treatment, the volume of the tumor was compared between the groups, and the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). Two weeks after completion of the treatments, the median volumes of the control group, the photosensitizer group, the laser group, and the IPDT group were respectively (1849.53 ± 164.03)mm3, (1745.14 ± 149.21)mm3, (1575.09 ± 186.61)mm3, (880.13 ± 170.76)mm3. The size of the tumor volume in the IPDT group was significantly smaller than that in the control group, photosensitizer group and laser group, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). Moreover, the tumor volume was as follows: the control group > the photosensitizer group > the laser group > the IPDT group. The tumor inhibition rate of each group was calculated according to the tumor volume. After two weeks of treatment, the tumor inhibition rates of the photosensitizer group, laser group, and IPDT group were 7.51%, 9.43%, and 52.41% respectively.

HE staining results

After HE staining of the tumor tissue, the nucleus appeared blue, and the cytoplasm and stroma were red. In the control group, the photosensitizer group and the laser group, the lung cancer cells were more numerous and closely arranged, with abundant cytoplasm and large dark-stained nuclei. In the IPDT group, the number of tumor cells was significantly reduced, and the cells were disordered and out of polarity. The consolidation and fragmentation of nuclei were more common, and some cells were foamy (Fig. 2).

TUNEL assay results

In according to investigate the role of IPDT in promoting apoptosis in transplanted tumor cells, the TUNEL staining assay was performed to examine the breakage of nuclear DNA in tissue cells during the early stages of apoptosis. The TUNEL assay showed wrinkled apoptotic cells with reduced cytoplasm, small condensed nuclei, and brown nuclear staining (Fig. 3). The apoptotic index of the control group, photosensitizer group, laser group, and IPDT group was calculated to be 3.2% ± 1.36%, 6.5% ± 2.78%, 8.7% ± 2.04%, 25.6% ± 5.56% respectively. The IPDT group was significantly higher than the other three groups, with a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05).

The mRNA expression levels of Survivin, Caspase-3, Bax, Bcl-2, VEGF and HIF-1α in transplanted tumor tissues of nude mice after IPDT treatment

After treatment of all four groups of animals, we determined the relative expression of Survivin, Caspase-3, Bax, Bcl-2, VEGF and HIF-1α mRNA in A549 transplanted tumors by qRT-PCR. Compared with the control, photosensitizer, and laser groups, the mRNA expression levels of Bax and Caspase-3 were increased in the IPDT group. The mRNA expression levels of Bcl-2, Survivin, VEGF and HIF-1α showed a decreasing trend (Fig. 4). The differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05).

Discussion

PDT is a minimally invasive treatment that selectively exerts targeted cytotoxic activity on malignant cells, which exerts anticancer activity by causing apoptosis and necrosis of tumour cells through combining photosensitizers with laser light to produce singlet oxygen [24, 25]. Moreover, PDT can stagnate microvasculature and cause hypoxia in the tumor site. In addition, PDT activates the immune response that causes macrophages to clear treated tumour cells, thereby preventing tumour recurrence [26–28].

Numerous studies have shown that PDT is effective in treating head and neck tumors, basal cell carcinoma, cervical cancer, colon cancer, bladder cancer, esophageal cancer, breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, and so on[18, 29]. PDT is becoming a new treatment method for cancer because it is highly targeted, minimally invasive, easier for patients to accept, and can be combined with surgery and radiotherapy to prolong patients' survival period [30–32]. In patients with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, PDT is an effective intervention to relieve symptoms by reducing tumor obstruction in the bronchi, thereby improving dyspnoea and lung function. In the neoadjuvant setting, PDT can increase the likelihood of surgery or reduce the extent of surgery required in patients. It also selectively treats intraoperative pleural dissemination following macroscopic complete surgical resection [33, 34].

Although PDT can achieve complete remission in most early central lesions, the remission rate usually decreases significantly for larger lesions. Multiple clinical studies have shown that IPDT is a promising treatment for patients with locally-advanced or large-volume tumors [35–37]. A Japanese study included nine patients with peripheral lung tumors who were treated with IPDT under CT guidance. All patients were assessed histologically at one and four weeks post-treatment. Seven of the nine patients (78%) demonstrated a partial response, with no patients developing progressive diseases at four weeks [38]. Therefore, IPDT enables delivering light intratumorally and can be used as a supplementary therapy to improve the prognosis of patients with refractory or locally advanced cancers. The results of this study showed that after the completion of three treatments, the tumor volume in the IPDT group was significantly smaller than that in the control, photosensitizer and laser groups. Conclusions can be drawn that the growth of transplanted tumors in lung adenocarcinoma in nude mice was not significantly inhibited by photosensitizer alone or laser irradiation alone, but when the two factors were combined, the growth of transplanted tumors could be significantly inhibited, with a tumor inhibition rate of 52.41%. HE staining demonstrated various necrotic, disordered and foamy cells in the IPDT group, while the cells in the other three groups were closely arranged with large hyperchromatic nuclei.

The rampant growth and infinite proliferation of malignant tumor cells are inseparable from the phenomenon of apoptosis escape. Most therapies can exert anti-tumor effects by inducing tumor cell apoptosis [39, 40]. Apoptosis is an important mechanism in IPDT tumor cell killing. The apoptotic pathway involves a variety of genes and proteins, including caspase proteins, death receptors and their ligands, apoptosis-promoting genes, apoptosis-inhibiting genes, and so on [41]. The cysteine-aspartate-specific protease (Caspase) family plays a key role in apoptosis. When mitochondrial dysfunction occurs, cytochrome C (Cyt C) is released from the mitochondrial intermembrane into the cytoplasm. The released Cyt C binds to apoptotic peptidase activator 1 (Apaf-1) and forms an activation complex with caspase-9, the complex activates caspase-3 and consequently induces apoptosis[42–45]. What’s more, the composition ratio of Bcl-2 family members is a key factor in the regulation of apoptosis, especially the ratio of Bcl-2/Bax is the "molecular switch" that initiates apoptosis. Bax and Bcl-2 regulate apoptosis by forming homodimers or heterodimers. When Bax forms homodimers, it induces apoptosis. When Bax forms heterodimers with Bcl-2, it inhibits apoptosis. Bax homodimer binds to the mitochondrial membrane, causing a change in its transmembrane potential and the release of cytochrome C from the mitochondria into the cytosol, which initiates the caspase cascade reaction and accelerates apoptosis. Conversely, when Bcl-2 is highly expressed, the formation of homodimers is reduced [46, 47]. Survivin is part of the apoptosis-inhibiting protein family, which plays an extremely important role in the process of inhibiting cell apoptosis. On the one hand, Survivin is a direct inhibitor of Caspase-3. On the other hand, Survivin and Bcl-2 may have a common pathway to regulate apoptosis [48]. The Survivin gene and Bcl-2 gene are regulated by promoters, and can enhance cell proliferation and synergize with anti-apoptotic effects after transcriptional activation.

TUNEL staining showed that the IPDT group had significantly more apoptotic cells and a higher apoptotic index compared to the other three groups, suggesting that PDT can cause apoptosis in tumor tissues. In this study, the expression of several apoptosis-related factors in transplanted tumor tissues of nude mice was examined by qRT-PCR and Western blot. The results showed that the expression of Survivin and Bcl-2 was reduced in the IPDT group, while the expression of Caspase-3 and Bax was increased in the IPDT group. IPDT induces microvascular damage and inhibits angiogenesis. Among the numerous angiogenic growth factors, the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has a high specificity and plays an important role in tumor angiogenesis. VEGF, as the main mediator of angiogenesis, can lead to increased vascular permeability and promote tumor growth, metastasis and invasion by delivering oxygen and nutrients to cancer cells [49]. Hypoxia is a major stimulator of angiogenesis and enhances the stability of hypoxia-inducible factor- 1α (HIF-1α) and its binding to the VEGF promoter element. Activated HIF-1α can induce the transcription of VEGF and regulate the expression level of VEGF [50]. Many studies have shown that increased levels of VEGF and HIF-1α play an important role in tumorigenesis and can promote tumor angiogenesis. In addition, the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF is upregulated in non-small cell lung cancer and is associated with poor prognosis and poor overall survival [51]. The results of this study showed reduced expression of HIF-1α and VEGF in the IPDT group, suggesting that IPDT can inhibit the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF, thereby suppressing the growth of transplanted tumors.

To sum up, this study established a transplantation tumor model of human lung adenocarcinoma in nude mice to study the effect of Interstitial photodynamic therapy (IPDT) on transplanted tumors of human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells in nude mice. It is concluded that IPDT could significantly inhibit the growth of A549 transplanted tumors in nude mice, which is possibly related to down-regulating the expression of apoptotic factors Survivin and Bcl-2 genes, up-regulating the expression of Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 genes, and down-regulating the expression of angiogenesis-related VEGF and HIF-1α genes. However, the exact signal transduction pathway needs to be further studied.

Limitations

Although this study initially explored the inhibitory effect of interstitial photodynamic therapy on transplanted tumors of human lung adenocarcinoma A549 in nude mice, there are still some limitations.

Firstly, due to the limitations of the experimental period and resource allocation, the animal sample size used in this study is relatively limited, and the model type is relatively single (A549 cell line nude mouse transplanted tumor). This may to some extent affect the universality and representativeness of the research results. It is necessary for future research to expand the sample size and include more lung adenocarcinoma cell lines and animal models with different genetic backgrounds or pathological characteristics to verify and expand the reliability of this conclusion.

Secondly, during the experiment, although we have made every effort to control errors through standardized operation procedures, repeated measurements and random grouping, there may still be certain measurement deviations at the technical level. For instance, the measurement of tumor volume relies on imaging tools, which have inherent limitations in terms of accuracy. Subtle variations may also be introduced during the treatment process, such as light localization and dose uniformity. Furthermore, animal experiments are difficult to fully simulate the complex microenvironment and immune responses within the human body, and all these factors may have an impact on the results.

Finally, this study focused on the specific subtype of lung adenocarcinoma, and the conclusions drawn may not be directly applicable to other histological types of lung cancer or solid tumors. The biological behaviors, interstitial components and photosensitizer distributions of different tumors may vary, so our findings need to be verified in a broader range of tumor models.

Despite the above limitations, we believe that this study provides valuable preclinical evidence for the application of interstitial photodynamic therapy in the treatment of lung adenocarcinoma and initially reveals its potential efficacy and mechanism directions. These results can lay a reference foundation for subsequent more in-depth basic exploration and translational research, and suggest that in the future, treatment plans need to be further optimized and long-term efficacy and safety verified in multi-center, large-sample and more complex clinical scenarios models.

A hybrid spatial–frequency attention-based algorithm using efficientnet for robust and interpretable deepfake detection
Authors: Mohit Kumar, Ashwani Kumar, Vikram Yadav, Achyut Shankar, Piyush Chauhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe recent pace of generative media synthesis methods has greatly enhanced the credibility and accessibility of deepfake content, which causes serious risks to digital trust, authenticity of media, and forensic security. Existing deepfake detection methods are usually limited to either spatial domain visual cues or frequency domain artifacts, which leads to their limited robustness, poor generalization under realistic compression and poor interpretability. To overcome them, this paper will introduce a generalizable hybrid spatial-frequency deepfake detector, the proposed scheme combines both RGB-based visual representations with discrete cosine transform (DCT) frequency elements into a high-capacity convolutional network with attention-based refining. The suggested framework uses an EfficientNet-B7 backbone to identify rich hierarchical features and a convolutional block attention module (CBAM) to adaptively highlight information that is of interest to manipulation including spatial and channel-wise information. The early combination of spatial and frequency information allows the model to mutually exploit semantic inconsistencies and fine-scale high-frequency distortions added in the process of generating synthetic content. Comprehensive experiments of the FaceForensics + + C23 data set show that the proposed methodology has state-of-the-art performance with a ROC-AUC of 0.997, as well as high precision-recall balance and convergence of the training process. Further class separability is supported by feature-space analysis and prediction probability distributions and more complex CAM-based visualizations give significant forensic descriptions by identifying manipulation-prone regions of the faces. The high detection accuracy, the increased potential of generalization, and the greater interpretability are the factors that underline the efficiency of the suggested hybrid framework and confirm its appropriateness to the use in the field of the real-life deepfake forensics.Keywords: Attention mechanism, Deepfake detection, EfficientNet, Explainable artificial intelligence, FaceForensics++, Frequency-domain forensics, Media forensics, Spatial–frequency fusionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid advancement in generative modelling has greatly enhanced the level of realism, accessibility, and deepfake discounts. Modern manipulation pipelines represented by GANs, diffusion models and encoder-decoder architectures have facilitated highly convincing face-swapping, reenactment and audio-visual synthesis guaranteeing serious risks in journalism, political communication, biometric security and forensic verification. As a result, deepfake detection has emerged as a very important research area of multimedia forensics and trustworthy artificial intelligence.

Early methods for detecting deepfake videos mainly upset on handcrafted descriptors, visual artifacts and low-level inconsistencies, but these procedures did not display reliable variations1–3 as they are not robust in opposition to using up, post-processing, and cross-dataset variations. With the emergence of deep learning, convolutional neural networks increased greatly, detection performance by exploiting discriminative spatial features; however, many CNN based detectors still suffered from overfitting, poor generalization and vulnerability to unseen types of manipulation4–6. Subsequent research has also combined deeper backbones, multi-scale fusion and the use of dressing on residual learning strategies, in order to reduce these challenges, however persistent gap in generalization remains in the dataset across datasets and manipulation families7–10.

A line of separate research for a different problem was introduced to manipulate artifacts which are invisible in the raw RGB images, by using frequency-domain analysis. Several investigations have put forth that the deepfake synthesis tend to distort the high-frequency elements that are associated with the facial texture, edges, and blending boundary11–13. Frequency aware representations such as DCT spectra, wavelet transforms and the spectral residual maps proved to show better sensitivity to generative inconsistencies. Recent works have also gone a step further to develop frequency-conscious deepfake detection through the modeling of fine-grained spectral patterns and multi-frequency interactions. An example of such is hierarchical frequency-assisted interaction networks, which are suggested to encode complementary spatial and frequency information in detecting face manipulations14. On the same note, transformer architecture models at high frequencies have shown a high degree of ability to model subtle spectral artifacts that are added throughout the deepfake generation process15. Multi-spectral representations and noise-sensitive modeling have also been studied in more recent works to enhance deepfake detection and localization in more challenging manipulation situations16,17. These researches point out the significance of frequency-domain information to enhance the stability and dependability of deepfake forensic systems. Nevertheless, frequency-only detectors tend to have difficulty modeling semantic context and global structure leading to incomplete models18–20.

Hybrid spatial-frequency detection frameworks tried to overcome these downsides with a combination of both domains in achieving the rich feature encoding21–23. However, there are many existing hybrid models that will be limited by their shallow fusion strategy, lack of attention mechanisms, and through their lightweight backbone that causes a limited representational capacity24–26 Compared to the previous literature, which examines either spatial or spectral cues, the presented framework combines both RGB-based spatial features and DCT-domain frequency ones, which enables the network to learn both semantic inconsistencies and high-frequency artifacts involved in deepfake generation simultaneously. Recent research also added transformer-based models, temporal consistency learning, and multimodal cues to do so further, but such methods often bring a high computational cost, slow inference, or dependence on large-scale pretraining data27–29.

Another well-known challenge across different studies is the issue of lack of generalization across datasets whereby detection models trained on one dataset show severely degraded performance when tested on datasets which they have not seen or on compressed datasets30–33. This limitation has been attributed to dataset-specified biases, over-fitting to artefacts of particular manipulations, as well as the lack of good attention mechanisms that can signpost subtle forgery regions. Furthermore, a number of investigations have called for models that have stable performance with practical degradations such as video compression, noise, resizing and packaging artifacts typically found in real-world media streams34–36.

Despite recent progress, there has always been a lack of consensus between academia and the industry: most existing deepfake detectors fail to effectively combine spatial semantics, frequency artefacts, and attention-guided feature refinement in a backbone with high capacity which can accommodate both global and fine-grained inconsistent details. Several recent works recognize the importance of attention-based refinement37–40, but many of them are only geographical convolutional backbones and/or poorly model frequencies.

Unlike previous frameworks for detecting hybrid deepfakes which usually adopt shallow or late-stage fusion methods, the proposed method incorporates a principled mechanism of early spatial-frequency integration in a high-capacity convolutional architecture which allows the joint learning of semantic structures of faces and fine-grained spectral distortions from the lowest feature levels. Unlike previous hybrid models based on mid-tier backbones such as Xception or ResNet models, the proposed framework is based on EfficientNet-B7 (in which depth, width and resolution are compoundly scaled), which enables richer hierarchical representation learning and greater sensitivity to subtle manipulation artifacts.

Furthermore, although attention mechanisms have been studied in isolation or used superficially in previous studies, the proposed approach incorporates a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) right after the hybrid feature extraction, where the adaptive refinement of both channel-wise and spatial forensic cues can be accomplished. This design will permit precise localization of the manipulation prone regions, while suppressing background noise and dataset specific biases. Additionally, the framework explicitly adds multi-CAM explainability, which provides interpretable forensic evidence besides symbolic visualization that has been dominantly reported in the previous works.

Collectively, these design choices make the proposed framework for hybrid deepfake detector different from the existing hybrid deepfake detectors by addressing the feature fusion depth, representational capacity of the backbone network, attention-guided feature refinement, generalization robustness, and forensic interpretability at the same time, which constitute a non-trivial advancement and meaningful progress rather than incremental architectural change.

The present study targets these emerging needs by proposing a generalizable deepfake detection system that employs a hybrid convolution-based EfficientNet-B7 architecture with frequency-spatial fusion and attention with the goal of addressing these weaknesses in the existing literature. Even though some individual studies have dealt with the issues of the spatial-frequency representation and attention mechanisms, their principled combination with a high-capacity backbone as well as explainability-based forensic validation are comparatively under-researched.

Related work

The research of deepfake detection is divided into a number of methodological types, such as convolutional models, frequency-domain based, hybrid spatial-frequency combination, attention-based, and generalization-oriented models. The literature uploaded is a reflection of these trends across various families of manipulation, datasets and architectural paradigm.

Convolutional neural network based detection

The oldest deep learning direction in deepfake forensics was CNN-based. A number of studies followed the idea of using convolutional feature extractors in determining spatial inconsistencies and local manipulation traces1–4. These designs showed good behavior on single datasets but tend to be sensitive to compression, re-encoding and synthesis artifacts which vary across manipulation families5–8. Later works added depth to the networks and introduced multi-scale representation to make them more robust, but cross-dataset degradation was still a significant issue9,10.

Frequency-domain and spectral analysis

A frequency-domain study of forensics points to the significance of spectral discrepancies that are added when generating synthesis. A number of works examined variations of DCT, spectral residues, and high-frequency distortions to manipulate face detection11–13. These representations gave an additional information to spatial cues, which allowed being sensitive to manipulation artifacts that could not be perceived visually. Systems based on frequency-only detection pipelines, however, did not have semantic context and tended to reject performance in cases where synthesis pipelines maintained spectral smoothness18–20.

Hybrid spatial-frequency fusion architectures

Hybrid models consist of spatial and frequency representations, which are used to ensure robustness to domain shifts. Several of these works combined spectral characteristics and RGB features to detect complementary manipulation traces21,22. In spite of these methods having better accuracy, most of them used shallow fusion methods or small-capacity backbones, which limited features richness and generalizability23–25. Improved hybrid designs tried to go further into integration or multi-branch learning with the challenge of being able to sustain the same performance across different manipulation families26,27.

Attention-enhanced detection models

Mechanisms of attention such as channel, spatial, and combined versions have been included to highlight manipulative areas and to hold back irrelevant stimuli. Recent publications used attention blocks to refine forensic feature responses and enhance localization of small pattern artifact patterns28–30. Although significant performance improvements were observed, the effectiveness of attention modules was significantly different across backbone strength and training data properties, and some of the studies reported interpretability limitations or hyper-sensitivity to data bias31,32.

Transformer-based and time-temporal methods

The use of transformer-based architecture has brought in the global context modeling and long-range dependency capture that has made it possible to detect the temporal inconsistency of the video manipulations. Some of the works examined multi-frame attention, sequence-level forensics and transformer-based feature integration33,34. Although these models were proven to be more effective in the temporal reasoning process, they tended to be highly resource-consuming in terms of computational units, and they commonly needed a large amount of training data, preventing their utilization in practice35–37.

Robustness and cross-dataset generalization

Cross dataset generalization is a prevailing problem in the literature of deepfake detection. A variety of studies documented significant performance declines as models trained on one set are tested on unseen forms of manipulation, compression degree or acquisition pipes38–40. The results of these studies can be explained by dataset-related biases and the lack of modeling forensic-invariant features, and it is important to develop architectures that are able to learn manipulation cues transferably.

Limitations in existing literature

An integrated analysis of the research findings mentioned shows that there are a number of widespread and intersecting limitations that still pose a challenge to the efficiency of modern deepfake detection systems. The constraints extend to an architectural, representational, computational and interpretability level, and it means that the community has not yet agreed on the solid and transferable forensic paradigm.

Fragmented spatial frequency representation learning

Fragmented Spatial-Frequency Representation Learning is a learning strategy that divides a problem into sub-problems, each charged with locating certain features to the partially solved problem (i.e. distributed representation). Despite significant advances in the spatial CNN-based detectors and frequency-sensitive forensic methods, most of the current methods are limited to one representational domain. Strategies that seek to be hybrid often use superficial concatenation or shallow fusion which constrain the ability to use complementary cues. In addition, a number of hybrid architectures are based on mid-tier backbones or weak backbones that constrain representational expressiveness hence restricting their capacity to trace manipulation through a variety of synthesis pipelines21–25.

Suboptimal integration of attention mechanisms

Attention mechanisms have also been proposed as a tool to boost feature discrimination, but their combination with most earlier models is still only superficial. Some studies can also use attention blocks as light-weight extensions, instead of a globally based feature extraction hierarchy. This construction limits the selective amplification artifact of subtle manipulation and the inhibition of non-informative backgrounds of the network. Also, strategies of attention utilizing the case of low-capacity backbones do not provide substantial refinement, which leads to a low level of artifact localization and resilience28,31.

Limited cross dataset robustness and transferability

One of the most significant problems revealed in a variety of works is the strong deterioration in the detection performance when the models are tested beyond their training range. Compression level, synthesis method, camera properties, post-processing pipelines, and visual quality take up domain shifts that are challenging to most detectors to deal with. As a result, models also tend to perform strongly intra-dataset but poorly on unseen manipulation, and because of this fact, models are highly sensitive to dataset-specific biases and synthesis artifacts38–40.

Computational burden of transformer based detectors

Transformer architectures have proven themselves to be able to model long-range structural or temporal dependencies, but these have a limited applicability to real-world forensics. Numerous transformer-based designs are complex in terms of computational and memory overheads, and training data needs, and inference times are long. Such limitations make them unsuitable to use in resource-constrained or real-time settings, and it limits their availability to forensic analysts operating in operational settings33–35.

Inadequate forensic explainability

The requirement on interpretability is a major concern in the field of deepfake forensics, particularly in sensitive applications in security. Although it is an important area, not many methods have a detailed approach to visualization or explanation of the regions of the face that affect the decision to make a detection. Models that are not explainable do not give users trust and cannot be easily adopted as part of the operation to give a chance of understanding whether a decision is made based on real manipulation signals or data artifacts. This weakness highlights the fact that there remains the need to have architecture that can support transparent and verifiable decision paths.

Problem definition and research motivation

The high rate of generative models’ development has greatly contributed to the increase in the realism and variety of deepfake content, which has exponentially intensified the problems related to effective forensic detection. State-of-the-art spatial-domain detectors often fit to visual trends affecting the data set, making them inapplicable to data sets other pipelines of manipulation or natural compression artifacts. Frequency domain detectors, though good at revealing spectral distortions caused during synthesis, are generally inadequate at learning semantic links between the features or at paying attention to contextual facial patterns that provide effective forgery rejection. Partially improved methods Hybrid approaches will provide partial improvements but are usually based on shallow fusion schemes or low-capacity backbones, which limit their capacity to collaboratively leverage both spatial and spectral representations in a variety of conditions.

Attention-based architecture can also be used to further improve discriminative power by highlighting regions of interest to manipulation; nevertheless, much of the current work has added attention modules to weak backbone networks and has done so only in a superficial manner, with little refinement of forensic characteristics. Detectors based on transformers have the benefit of global context modeling, but demand a lot of computational power, large training datasets, and have long inference times, which makes them less practical to implement in practice by forensic experts. In addition, the ongoing generalization discrepancy on both datasets and types of manipulation emphasize that it is challenging to train forensic-invariant features that can retain consistent performance across cross-domain changes.

Based on such limitations, it is urgently necessary to develop a deepfake detection architecture that would meet the following requirements:

Effective spatial frequency representation learning

One should have the ability to capture semantic facial structures and manipulations of frequencies. This necessitates principled fusion scheme which combines the RGB information with frequency information in the form of DCT maps in such a way that complementary information is retained and neither of them is fused shallowly.

High-capacity backbone with strong feature hierarchies

An effective detector must also take advantage of a backbone architecture that has enough depth, width and multi-scale feature capacity to learn the finer traces of manipulation with different synthesis algorithms. EfficientNet-B7 provides an appropriate basis as it comes with the right balance of representational power and architecture efficiency.

Adaptive attention guided refinement

Attention mechanisms need to be integrated into the feature extraction pipeline in order to increase sensitivity to manipulation-relevant regions. Spatial attention modules such as channel and spatial attention modules (e.g., CBAM) may be used to narrow down on fused features, through preferential amplification of patterns of distortion and reduction of background information.

Robustness to cross dataset and compression variability

An effective forensic model should be able to be generalized to manipulations, variable compression settings, and real-world noise never seen before. This necessitates acquisition of invariant patterns that capture fundamental discrepancies that are brought about by generative synthesis, as opposed to dataset-dependent indicators.

Forensic explainability and interpretability

Visual explanations must be provided to detect outputs that are interpretable and indicate the areas that are manipulated. Tools of explainability like Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, HiResCAM, and EigenCAM can be used to support transparent forensic analysis and provide users with more confidence in the decisions made by a model.

These needs inspired the current research to propose a frequency-spatial hybrid model that relies on the EfficientNet-B7 sub-network and is added with frequency channels based on DCT, and a Convolutional Block Attention Module to perform adaptive refinement. The suggested design is expected to meet strong generalization, better discriminative power, and complete forensic interpretability–the key limitations that were found to be critical in the current literature.

Proposed methodology

The framework suggests a hybrid deepfake detector structure that entails integration of spatial and frequency-domain indicators on a high-capacity convolutional infrastructure that is assisted by attention-based refinements processes. The general design is guided by the necessity to detect the small manipulation artifacts that cut across domains of features, enhance the discriminative localization, and enhance the cross-dataset generalization. Architectural overview

The framework takes a four-channel input representation of three RGB channels and one channel of frequencies based on the DCT. The hybrid formulation of input represents complementary information: the spatial domain information is semantically and structurally contextualized, whereas the frequency channel reveals high-frequency distortions that are created during generative synthesis.

The four-channel input is driven through an EfficientNet-B7 backbone that was chosen due to the balanced scaling of network depth, width and resolution. EfficientNet-B7 has high representational power, which allows the recovery of multi-scale spatial patterns that are important in the detection of subtle or local patterns of manipulations. The backbone takes the input obtained by the fusion and processes it with its compound-scaled convolutional blocks to generate a high dimensional feature map.

In order to increase discriminative analysis of manipulation-relevant areas, a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) is used on the extracted feature representation. CBAM uses the processes of sequential channel and spatial attention to refine the feature tensor by selectively amplifying high-artifact regions and non-informative patterns respectively. This is a refining stage that enhances the model to detect any latent deviations present in a particular forensic scene that would have otherwise been subdued by a predominant semantic content.

After attention refinement, the representation is then globally pooled to combine the most salient information then transmitted through a classifier head that is aimed at giving an output of whether an input frame is real or manipulated or not.

Hybrid frequency spatial representation

Deepfake synthesis pipelines are frequently known to alter high-frequency-textures because of up sampling, warping, and blending algorithms. In order to encode such distortions, the suggested framework calculates the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) of every input frame and separates the magnitude spectrum of the significant frequency components.

This architecture allows the network to mutually learn spectral and spatial features of the lowest convolutional layers and become more sensitive to semantic inconsistencies as well as spectral abnormalities. The two input modalities are complementary to each other in the detection process. The RGB spatial representation captures semantic facial structures, contextual visual patterns, and inconsistencies in facial geometry that may arise during manipulation. In contrast, the DCT-based frequency representation highlights high-frequency artifacts introduced by generative synthesis pipelines, such as texture distortions, blending inconsistencies, and compression-related spectral anomalies. By jointly modeling these two domains, the proposed hybrid representation enables the network to capture both semantic inconsistencies and spectral manipulation traces, which improves the robustness of deepfake detection compared to single-domain approaches.

EfficientNet-B7 backbone to extract multi-scale features

The main feature extraction backbone is EfficientNet-B7 since it is high-representative, it is efficiently parameterized, and it is of multi-scale structure. The architecture employs the concept of compound scaling, depth, width, and resolution are all scaled in the same manner to add richness to the features.

Experimental results

This section contains a critical assessment of the suggested hybrid spatial-frequency deepfake detection system. The FaceForensics++ (FF++) C23 dataset is used to report quantitative measures, visual-analysis-based analysis, feature-space based exploration and explainability-based interpretation.

Dataset details

The FaceForensics++ (FF++) dataset41, which was generated by reference to the original data generation scripts, was used as an experimental evaluation. Although the FaceForensics + + dataset is widely used as a benchmark for deepfake detection research, it represents a specific data distribution and compression setting. Therefore, the experimental evaluation in this work focuses on this benchmark to establish the effectiveness of the proposed architecture under controlled conditions. Evaluating the model across additional real-world datasets (e.g., DF40, MFFI, and other emerging benchmarks)42,43 is an important direction for further validating cross-dataset generalization performance. The dataset was applied at the C23 compression depth, which is a medium-quality compression and commonly applied in the benchmark of deepfake detection in realistic post-processing settings. The files contained in the dataset are a total 7,010 files among which are 7,000 MP4 video files and 10 CSV metadata files.

Of the video samples, 6,000 videos are of manipulated (deepfake) videos and 1000 videos are of authentic (real) facial videos, which constitutes a binary classification problem. The fake videos are kept in equal distribution with six methods of deepfake production which take 1,000 videos each, that is, DeepFakeDetection, Deepfakes, Face2Face, FaceShifter, FaceSwap and NeuralTextures but the original folder has 1,000 real videos. Metadata about video identifiers, types of manipulation, and metadata about compression are given in the related CSV files.

Visual inspection of training samples

To thoroughly grasp the complexities involved in deepfake detection, the dataset is examined via feature-level and statistical attributes instead of direct visual assessment, especially considering ethical limitations on presenting recognizable face data. The FaceForensics + + dataset demonstrates significant heterogeneity across several dimensions, including lighting conditions, face orientations, backdrop settings, compression artifacts, and a range of editing approaches. This heterogeneity results in considerable intra-class variance, complicating the differentiation between authentic and altered samples. A pivotal element of contemporary deepfake creation techniques is their capacity to maintain elevated visual realism, including face structure, expressions, and overall look. Consequently, altered samples often seem convincingly to the human eye. Nonetheless, despite this visual credibility, deepfake synthesis processes generate small discrepancies at more intricate levels, especially in high-frequency elements, texture patterns, and localized face areas such as edges, blending zones, and micro-textures.

Under normal visual inspection, these differences are usually too small to see, which makes detection methods that just use spatial (RGB) information less useful. This observation highlights the fundamental limitation of purely spatial-domain analysis and motivates the integration of hybrid spatial-frequency representations. By incorporating Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)-based frequency features, the proposed framework is able to capture spectral irregularities introduced during synthesis, including compression-induced distortions and unnatural frequency distributions. At the same time, the addition of attention mechanisms (CBAM) lets the model focus on areas that are important to manipulation while ignoring unimportant background information.

Robustness and generalization discussion

Some of the issues that continue to be of concern in deepfake forensics include robustness and generalization because of the differences in synthesis pipelines, compression levels, and post-processing artifacts. Although the current study is conducted in the FaceForensics + + C23 compression mode, the key hybrid architecture employed in the current study inherently encourages generalization by learning representations that are invariant to the forensic process using joint spatial frequency modeling and attention-guided refinement. Consistent feature-space analysis of the data shows that the class separability is strong and is observed to be consistent over a variety of identities and visual conditions, as well as the calibration of confidence, which demonstrates the resistance to dataset-specific biases.

Moreover, the combination of frequency domain cues makes it more robust to the distortions caused by compression, whereas attention mechanisms promote paying attention to the manipulation of relevant facial regions instead of the background artifacts. These properties taken together indicate better transferability potential under unseen degradations. The evaluation will be extended to cross-dataset environment and different compression levels in the future to further measure the general performance of the evaluation. Even though the hybrid spatial-frequency framework, proposed, has good detection capability, some problematic situations can produce misclassification. Specifically, deepfake videos with a high compression value, post processing, or very minute manipulations can minimize spatial discrepancies, and high-frequency artifacts, increasing their challenges of detection. In this scenario, the traces of manipulation can be weak in character both visually and spectrally and this can compromise the confidence of the model. Also, deepfake generation systems, which result in very realistic textures and a balanced look of facial borders, can lead to a decrease in the number of detectable forensic cues. Note that the present experimental analysis is made with respect to the FaceForensics + + C23 data that is a popular benchmark, although will remain a specific distribution of deepfake generation methods and compression artifacts. Although the hybrid spatial-frequency representation and attention-guided feature refinement are the two implementations intended to enhance resistance to manipulation artifacts, more validation of these concepts using a series of datasets would be required to determine the full cross-dataset generalizability. The future studies will thus entail an assessment of other real-life datasets like the DF40, MFFI, and the new benchmark among others to continue testing the transferability and soundness of the suggested framework.

The ablation results further highlight the contribution of each input modality. The spatial-only model captures facial semantic inconsistencies but may miss subtle spectral artifacts introduced during synthesis. The frequency-only model detects spectral distortions but lacks contextual facial understanding. The analysis of spatial-frequency representation substantially enhances the detection, which means that the two representations play a complementary role in providing forensic cues. This proves that utilization of DCT-based frequency features increases sensitivity of the detector to manipulation artifacts, which then leads to increase in the overall sensitivity of the detection. The findings suggest that the joint use of spatial and frequency representations can be better able to detect compared to single-domain models. CBAM attention inclusion also acts to improve performance by steering the network to areas of interest manipulation. Conclusion

In this paper, there was a generalizable hybrid deepfake detector that combined spatial RGB features with DCT frequency domain cues based on a high-capacity EfficientNet-B7 backbone with CBAM attention-refining architecture. The given architecture was to address severe shortcomings of current deepfake detection algorithms such as inadequate spatial-frequency combination, poor attention-based feature localization, poor explainability, and poor generalization in real-world compression conditions. Having provided extensive experiments that are performed on the FaceForensics + + C23 data, it is shown that the framework reaches the state-of-the-art performance with a ROC-AUC of 0.997 with high balance between precision and recall, as well as providing robustness to a host of varying facial identities and visual settings as well. The results of quantitative analysis with the help of confusion matrix analysis, inspection of probability distributions, and visualization of representations in feature space prove that the representations obtained after learning are very discriminative and structured and allow the classification of genuine and distorted samples with high accuracy. Additionally, the sophisticated CAM-based explainability analysis shows that the attention-enhanced hybrid remarkably concentrates on the manipulation-sensitive areas of the face as opposed to leaf artifacts, and thus enhances the level of forensic interpretability and reliability. From an architectural point of view, the proposed framework extends the previous works by using the principled fusion of spatial and frequency information in the early fusion layers of deep convolutional backbone and using adaptive attention mechanism to refine the channel-wise and spatial information. Although the present study is at frame-level detection, the next generation of the study can be done at video-level temporal model, cross-dataset and cross-generation generalization examination through new synthesis methods, and computational optimization to provide real-time implementation in resource constraints settings. Other avenues of research are multimodal deepfake detection by using audio-visual signals and the creation of explainability-inspired forensic validation pipelines which can aid human-in-the-loop decision-making. Altogether, the suggested hybrid spatial-frequency attention-based framework demonstrates strong performance on the FaceForensics + + benchmark and provides a promising foundation for robust deepfake detection, with future work aimed at validating its generalization across multiple datasets and manipulation scenarios.

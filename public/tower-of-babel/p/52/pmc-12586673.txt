A deep hybrid inception network model with entropy based attention for automated iron ore image characterization
Authors: Shama Firdaus, Shamama Anwar, Subrajeet Mohapatra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Iron ores are an important mineral resource for the industrial development of an economy. Grading of ores is an important task involved at different stages of ore processing. The present study focuses on the grading of iron ores and uses reflected light microscopic iron ore image dataset. The ores were included from different mines of Singhbhum Craton of Eastern India. The aim of the study is to develop a robust generalized model for automating the task of iron ore characterization of ores belonging to four different grades. For this purpose a deep learning model has been developed, which implements a directed acyclic graph network architecture via hybrid inception topology. The network has been designed to combine together the feature extraction efficiencies of different pre-trained models. It implements MobilenetV2, InceptionV3, Xception models, as base classifiers, for feature extraction, followed by an attention channel for enhancement of the extracted features, and an encoder channel for dimensionality reduction of the enhanced feature set. This encoder channel helps in the making of a more generalized model. The performance of the proposed model has been compared to the existing state of art deep learning models- MobilenetV2, Inception V3 and Xception, and shows a very good performance, with a final classification accuracy of 97% in comparison to 91%, the best accuracy among the individual participating base classifiers. The individual base classifiers exhibited varying performance across different classes, with certain classes experiencing notably high misclassification rates, which posed a major concern. In contrast, the proposed model significantly reduces class-wise misclassification rates compared to the base classifiers.

Introduction

Computer vision based image classification methods have recently gained a lot of attention. This is attributed mainly to the development of high performing deep learning (DL) models. The term deep in deep learning is attributed to the deep architecture, including the depth and width of convolution layers in such networks. DL models have shown efficiency in different tasks like feature extraction, classification, segmentation, predictions etc., covering every type of data, viz. numeric, image, video, text, audio, signal. Feature extraction is the process of learning important distinguishing features in the target dataset that prove beneficial in the classification task. In a feature extraction process, essentially the input is a dataset and the output is a feature set. There are many different methods of feature extraction from image datasets like statistical, frequency domain based, geometrical analysis, but convolution methods used in DL, that exploits the spatial and channel information content in the pixel values have gained popularity on image datasets and gives it a completely new direction. Many research works have established the efficiency of DL based pre-trained models for the task of feature extraction1–4. Sometimes the feature extraction is followed by feature selection and feature enhancement processes, as an example in a study energy layer has been used with CNN as a feature enhancement layer5. Research shows that continuous attempts have been made to replicate human fovea6 based attention system in artificial neural networks for the task of feature selection. A survey7 discusses different feature selection methods. Traditionally statistics based hand crafted feature selection methods like GLCM have been used, as in8. However, recent studies show that automated DL based feature selection methods are more effective than hand crafted feature sets. In this direction, attention mechanism along with convolution, has also become popular. Entropy based unsupervised feature selection method has been outlined as an important mechanism in multiclass classification problems. This type of attention mechanism can both enhance and suppress features thereby simplifying the classification task9. All these processes, together with classification or regression, collectively form the basic machine learning activities involved in solving any problem. But, it is also a fact that DL algorithms are data greedy and are best trained on large datasets. As a solution to this, pre-trained DL models became highly popular. They are found to be very efficient with smaller datasets10. Pre-trained models are a class of deep Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models that have been trained on existing large image datasets like ImageNet, CIFAR etc., and are available with the trained weights for application into other research areas by fine tuning them11–13. The features learned by such models are portable and can be applied to a different set of problem with fine tuning of the models. It is the process of adapting a model to other types of dataset sets by topping it with a new classification layer based on the new problem design and training only a few layers while keeping the rest of the model frozen. Fine tuning these models is computationally simpler as compared to training them from scratch, as it involves training only the top few layers of the network for updating the pre-trained weights on the new dataset. These models not only come handy for feature extraction but are also very efficient for classification task. Fine tuning may result in overfitting and must be monitored using regularization techniques.

Coming to the architecture of the DL models, as generally presumed, they are not always sequential. In fact the best practices in DL involve networked architectures for best utilization of their efficiencies. One such network architecture is a directed acyclic graph. Popular topologies implementing directed acyclic graph in DL are Inception modules and residual connections. Inception is a popular network-in-network architecture where several modules are stacked in parallel branches in place of stacking them sequentially, maintaining a balance between depth and width of the network for optimal results14. This type of a network helps in learning complex patterns in the dataset much efficiently. In a residual connection, output from an earlier layer is summed to the input of a later layer creating skip connections in a sequential network. It is helpful in the integration of semantic details from high level features with fine details of low level features15. The pre-trained model Inception implements the inception technique while Xception implements the residual network, Mobilenet is an architecture implementing inception with residual connections. These are examples of a few models that implement DAG network in their architecture and have given efficient outputs over the preceding sequential networks like AlexNet.

Similar DL based application trends have also been also used in the mineral industry with the advent of Industry 4.0, where human interactions were reduced to minimal and the entire work was taken over by the Artificial Intelligence (AI). But with Industry 5.0, a balance between the use of AI and human intelligence was introduced. For bringing this into effect, explanations to the network behavior plays an important role16. It encompasses the methodologies and techniques to illustrate the behavior of so called black box DL models. The prime focus of these illustration techniques is to bring forth the decision making process of DL models. They include the visualization tools and feature importance saliency maps17. They are very helpful in improving the interpretability of a model making it more transparent and thereby increasing the trustworthiness of a DL model.

The current study aims to incorporate computer vision based DL model for the task of creating an automated iron ore characterization system. The realization of this system consists of a sequence of processes as depicted in Fig. 1. The dataset used in the study is Reflected Light Microscopic (RLM) image dataset in four different grades of iron ores that have been collected from different mines of India. A DL model has been proposed for the task of grading of the iron ores, and it implements a hybrid inception network of pre-trained inception and residual networks in a directed acyclic graph network architecture. Related work

India is a country bestowed with large mineral deposits18. Iron ore is one among the important mineral resources of the country that has contributed to its economic growth19. Grading of iron ore is an important task of mineral beneficiation20. Primarily this activity has relied upon human expertise and chemical extensive tests, which are generally cumbersome and time consuming and also prone to human induced errors21.With the advent of 5th industrial revolution a shift in technology trend has been observed from more of chemical based methods towards automation22–24. This can be accounted by the enormous development in the field of image processing and DL and have led to the adaption of reliable and time efficient applications in the mineral industry25,26. The earlier instances of image analysis of iron ore are based on Scanning Electron Microscopic images (SEM) and Reflected Light Microscopic images (RLM) outlining close relation of image based features with the physical and chemical composition of the mineral, opening the scope of image based analysis for the ores27. Later machine learning based techniques utilizing algorithm like SVM were proposed for the grading of iron ores, reporting good accuracy. It has used web camera based digital images captured from a moving conveyor belt containing ore24, but the realization of such a setup is associated with its own drawbacks and limitations. Similar task has also been done for other rocks and minerals like, sand, and soil parent material analysis28,29. These studies have used SEM in the analysis of sand whereas x-ray spectroscopy has been used in soil parent material analysis. X-ray spectroscopy produce high dimensional numerical data and machine learning algorithms proved handy in the data analysis. Similar technology has also been applied in determining the metal concentrations in iron ore concentrate30. Another study using x-ray spectroscopy, analyses the quality of iron ore and the impurities present in it31. Acquiring x-ray spectroscopic data and SEM images require trained experties. They are also associated with the risk of exposure to high beam x-rays. RLM can be safer and much easier approach. Infact a study has even outlined the efficiency of RLM in the analysis of goethetic ores, and uses machine learning based techniques to determine the grade of iron ores32. Some more related literature were reviewed, and it was observed that RLM is more cost effective imaging method that needs minimal expertise and can give useful insights for image analysis of different rocks and minerals33. Some other related studies that focused on the task of classification of iron ores were surveyed. Different literature have used different techniques to acquire dataset. These include laser induced breakdown spectroscopy34, digital web camera for image acquisition35,36, SEM and RLM microscopy . It was observed that the works mainly focused on statistical and geometrical based methods for feature extraction37 which require expert feature engineering.

A gap was observed for DL based convolution method for feature extraction in the field of minerals, as has been applied in many other types of research works. It overcomes the requirement of dedicated feature engineering. A study proposes DL based model for the surface quality inspection of copper clad laminate for defect detection38. Some studies on speech and text detection were found to implement CNN-LSTM hybrid model for improving feature understanding39,40. Some studies were also found to integrate attention mechanism with computer vision based tasks via dynamic weight updates for the features with the aim of improving the results41. Among the different popular attention mechanism that have been applied in CNNs are Squeeze and Excitation Network(SENet), Global Second-order Pooling Convolutional Networks (GSoPNet) and Bottleneck Attention Module (BAM) that have shown improvement in the results. SENet tries to model the channel inter dependencies, where the squeeze layer uses Global Average Pooling (GAP) for each channel of the input feature map and excitation layer uses fully connected layer for learning the attention value for each channel42. BAM attention layers are followed by downsampling of the output feature maps43. GSoPNet work in similar fashion as SENet but they use second order statistics for capturing global dependencies instead of GAP44. Spatial entropy based attention has been used with vision transformers and found to be beneficial for small dataset size45. Thus we see that the use of attention mechanism has proven useful in replacing the work of extensive feature engineering. Gap for similar work implementing DL models on iron ore was identified. It was also observed from the survey of the related works that, in iron ores the research works are mostly mine specific and the data discussed in the articles belong to a single mine. More specifically it could be said that the iron ore data used in the studies lack variety. There is also a lack of generalization in the available models. So, one focus of the study was also to include samples belonging to different mines and to develop a more generalized model.

Based on the observations from the surveyed literature, several key gaps were identified as limited diversity in the available datasets, minimal application of convolution-based DL techniques for feature extraction, and a lack of generalization strategies in the existing models. To address these shortcomings, a DL model crafted to cover the observed gaps as far as possible, has been proposed for the automated characterization of iron ores.

Data and materials

The data set used in the experiments contain reflected light microscopic (RLM) iron ore images and is available online. The entire process of data acquisition has been summed up in Fig. 2 and discussed in detail in46. It was prepared from iron ores belonging to different mines of Singhbhum Craton, eastern India, viz Kiriburu-Megahatuburu Mines, Noamundi Mines, Joda east Mines, and Katamati Mines under expert geologist guidance. It consists of four different grades of the iron ore samples, Blue Dust Ore (BDO), Hard Laminated Ore (HLO), Soft Laminated Ore (SLO) and Lateritic Ore (LO). The grades listed are in the decreasing order of haematite content. Figure 3a shows image of iron ore, a prepared sample for RLM imaging (Fig. 3b ) and the acquired RLM images (Fig. 3c–f ), showing one from each of the different grades of iron ores. The image acquisition process has been discussed in47. Figure 3g is the image of Leica DM2700P Digital Microscope used in the acquisition of RLM images and Fig. 3h presents a closer view of the objective lens of the microscope. After image acquisition, data pre-processing was done. In pre-processing step, the image format was converted from tiff format images as available in the repository, to png format without any significant loss of values. The bit depth was preserved during conversion. After the data pre-processing step, data augmentation was done. Data augmentation techniques were carefully selected so as to increase the size and variability of the dataset, to make it suitable for feeding into a DL model. Figure 4 lists the augmentation techniques that have been used for the augmentation of the train data. The final dataset consists of 2000 images. This dataset was split into train, validate and test set in the ratio 70:20:10. The classification model was then trained on this dataset so that it is able to recognize the grades of iron ore.Fig. 2The Process of Dataset Creation.Fig. 3The acquisition of images.Fig. 4The Applied Data Augmentation Techniques.

For the task of grading of the iron ore, three different pre-trained DL classification models were selected after a careful study and several simulation experiments. It comprised of MobilenetV2, InceptionV3 and Xception. These DL models were found to be light weight and were trained with the present dataset in lesser time, in comparison to some other pre-trained DL models. For fine tuning a pre-trained model, it was loaded into the system with the ImageNet weights. The present task is a four class classification problem, so the top layer in the pre-trained model, which has been designed for a different problem was at first excluded from the model architecture, and the base model was frozen. After this a new custom top layer for the classification of four class problem was added. Freezing the base model preserves the pre learned features, while training the new added custom layers. After the new layers have undergone initial training, few layers of the base model are unfrozen and the entire model is trained again for better adaption to the new dataset. This architecture is well suited for smooth training over small dataset and helps reduce overfitting. The loss function was categorical cross entropy loss, it is usually used with Softmax activation function and gives the measure of the divergence of the predicted values from the actual values.

Computation Environment: Intel Xeon W-2175 Processor, CPU 2.5 GHz and 128GB RAM, 14 core processors

Evaluation metrics: Different metrics have been studied for the evaluation of the model, and include classification accuracy, top-1 error, top-2 error, confusion matrix, network training loss values. These metrics were helpful in the analysis of the results. Classification accuracy represents the percentage of the test data whose class was correctly predicted by the model. The top-1 error represents the percentage of data instances where the highest probability of the predicted class is incorrect and does not match the true labels. Top-2 error represents the percentage of data instances where the top two predicted probabilities does not contain the correct class label. These error measurements are important in a multi class classification problem, specially when the classes have very similar feature. Also, a lower top-2 error in comparison to top-1 error, is an indicative that the model performance can be improved for accuracy.

The proposed model

In the basic construct of the proposed model, the three base classifiers are used together for feature extraction, the extracted features are then fused into a single feature map and then a fully connected layer is used for classification. The base classifier models are not stacked up into a sequential construct, rather they are incepted into parallel branches, in order to distribute the depth into width, making the otherwise heavy model into a lighter counterpart, as far as possible. Each of the base models themselves implement either inception or residual network structure, hence our proposed model is an inception of inception and residual networks. So, we call our model a Hybrid Inception of Networks, the HInN model. The HInN model architecture has been depicted in Fig. 6 and consists of different components. The first component is feature extraction channel. It uses MobilenetV2, InceptionV3 and Xception as base classifiers for feature extraction. The three models are run in parallel to extract the feature maps from the input image dataset. The next component of the proposed model is feature fusion channel. For feature fusion, an attention mechanism has been used as the feature engineer of the model. It implements entropy based attention channel over the output of the previous component of the model, which is a set of feature maps, where each feature map represents partial information about the image such as the textures, edges, pores etc. The entropy based supervised attention channel assigns weight to these feature map values.Fig. 6The proposed HInN model architecture.

The proposed HInN model architecture.

Entropy as the attention score:

The participating base classifiers extract meaningful features, in the form of feature maps from the image and sends them forward through the network for recognizing the grade of the ore. Each feature map contains partial information about the image properties. The receiving layer must interpret these feature maps to understand the image. Some feature map activation provide a lot of information thus helping the model distinguish between classes, while others may contain redundant and non informative feature map activations. It was observed that the variation in the activation values can be more significant than the value of the activations itself. The design of attention layer:

The attention channel was constructed as outlined in Algorithm 1. First, GAP was applied across the spatial dimensions of each participating base classifier to summarize the global activation of each channel of the feature map. Next, an attention score for each channel was computed based on its entropy, capturing the information content of that channel. These attention scores were then used to weight the corresponding channel activations, resulting in enhanced feature vectors. Finally, the attention-weighted feature vectors from all three base classifiers were concatenated into a single feature map representation. This completes the second component of the model, the feature fusion channel. This design ensures reduced complexity in comparison to any entropy based self attention, which works on every spatial input from the feature map values.

The encoder:

In the third component of the model the output feature map from the attention channel are compressed with an encoder implementing L1 regularization50. Regularization techniques are essential for preventing overfitting, and L1 regularization, in particular, reduces variance by adding a penalty to the loss function. This penalty encourages sparsity by driving many of the weights to exactly zero. In this way it helps in eliminating redundant and irrelevant features thereby yielding higher quality features51. It has also proved to improve generalization of the model. The encoder comprises of two layers (Algorithm 2). The first layer maps the concatenated feature set from the attention channel to a smaller set of more meaningful features by suppressing less important weights to zero. The second layer is a bottleneck layer, which further reduces the dimensions of the feature space. Both the layers of the encoder implement L1 regularization for dimensionality reduction as established in different literature52.

The output from the encoder channel is fed to a fully connected classification layer. In this entire architecture instead of stacking the pre-trained models, as is done in stacked ensembles and boosting techniques, the current work implements an architecture where the different pre-trained models run in parallel for feature extraction. The training process of this proposed HInN model is the same as aforementioned training process for the individual models. The images were fed in batches of 32. The loss function was cross entropy loss, with Adam optimizer and 0.001 learning rate. The initial weights were ImageNet weights that were updated subsequently with each batch of passing images and each training epoch. The hyperparameters have been summarized in Table 2. The whole process has been summed through Algorithm 3. Statistical evaluation

To support the robustness and generalizability of the proposed HInN model a 5-fold cross-validation strategy was used. In this approach, the dataset was randomly partitioned into five equal folds, each containing data belonging to each class in almost the same ratio. From the five folds, four folds were used for training and one fold was used in validation, with replacement, across five different sets of network training. The network performance was recorded over these five sets of execution, which included accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score and their mean was computed to represent the final value.

Further, to support empirical validity and robustness of the proposed model, statistical study was carried out over this performance evaluation framework and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were computed. These intervals provide a statistical estimate of the range within which the true performance of the model is likely to fall, considering the variability observed across five folds during cross-validation.

The confidence intervals were calculated using the t-distribution based on five-fold cross-validation results. This approach accounts for sample variability and prevents overestimation of the model’s performance due to chance. By reporting not just the mean metric values but also their associated confidence intervals, a more reliable and transparent assessment of model generalization is ensured. Table 6 presents the results of 5-fold cross validation and the computed mean and CI values.

From the results of the CIs computations, the claim that the proposed HInN model is robust and generalized and consistently outperforms the base classifier models was strengthened. By adding the attention channel an accuracy of 97% was observed, which is an appreciable amount of improvement over the previous one. The third variant -V3- also included the regularization & encoder channel along with the attention channel, which is the proposed HInN model. Here no change in the accuracy is observed but the training validation accuracy plot is observed to smooth out in comparison to the plot obtained in V2 of the model indicating a better trained model. Conclusion

The present study focuses on the development of a DL model for the automated grading of the iron ores. The dataset from different mines were included so as to increase variety of data. This helps in the development of a more generalized model, as opposed to most studies that are mine specific and lack variety in the data selection process. Three light weight, pre-trained models MobilenetV2, InceptionV3 and Xception, were tested individually on the dataset by fine tuning them, and were found to be adapting well with the present dataset. Though a high degree of misclassification was observed for one or the other class in the results of each of these models. As a solution to this and to minimise the class-wise misclassification the HInN model was developed which incorporates a directed acyclic graph network with hybrid inception topology. It nests the three pre-trained DL models which themselves involve inception and residual block architecture. So the resulting network is an inception of inception and residual networks, or a hybrid inception. It involves the fusion of the feature sets extracted from the three pre-trained models connected in parallel incorporated through an attention channel and then followed by an encoder channel. The attention channel employs channel wise entropy for weight updation and acts as the feature engineer of the model and the encoder helps in regularization. There were chances that the model may over fit due to the resulting enormous size of the feature map so a regularization technique was necessary. This feature fusion method shows a considerable amount of improvement in the grading accuracy. This behavior of the HInN has been explained with the saliency maps and feature importance plots for an insight into the working of the developed HInN model in the results section “The model behaviour”. For establishing the importance of different components of the models and their contribution in the model performance the results section has been backed by an ablation study presented in the section “Ablation study”.

Salient achievements of the HInN model may be summarized as follows:It shows considerable amount of improvement in the grading accuracy (97%).The performance is backed by the innovative attention module which is the feature engineer of HInN model.It achieves lower loss values as compared to the individual participating pre-trained models.A significant reduction in the top-2 error with the HInN model implementation was also observed.It reduces the misclassification rate in almost all the classes.It is a much lighter model as compared to an ensemble of similar models as it comprises of only a single FC layer.The L1 regularization was helpful in mitigating over fitting in the model.The future research has been designed to focus on three key areas. First, efforts will be made to refine the network architecture to reduce its complexity without compromising accuracy. This may demand experimenting with different pruning techniques and analyzing the computational demands of each component. Second, focus will be placed on the explainable AI, aiming to make the decision-making process of the proposed model more transparent and interpretable. Finally, the developed approach will be adapted and tested not only on other types of mineral ores but also in broader image-based applications, expanding its potential impact across domains.

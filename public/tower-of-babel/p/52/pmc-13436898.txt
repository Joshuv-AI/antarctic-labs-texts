Optic nerve sheath diameter as a prognostic tool for postdural puncture headache in pregnant women undergoing caesarean section under spinal anesthesia: A cross-sectional study
Authors: N. Amulya, Lavanya Kaparti, Suresh N. Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTContext:Postdural puncture headache (PDPH) is a common and debilitating complication following spinal anesthesia in obstetric patients, with few objective tools available for early prediction. Optic nerve sheath diameter (ONSD) measured by ultrasonography is a noninvasive surrogate marker of intracranial pressure changes and may aid in early risk stratification.Aim:To evaluate the prognostic utility of ONSD measurements in predicting PDPH among pregnant women undergoing cesarean section under spinal anesthesia.Materials and Methods:This cross-sectional study included 88 pregnant women aged between 18 and 40 years undergoing cesarean section under spinal anesthesia. ONSD was measured using ultrasonography with a 13–6 MHz probe at baseline (T0) and at 4 (T4), 24 (T24), 48 (T48), and 72 h (T72) postprocedure. PDPH was diagnosed using International Classification of Headache Disorders criteria.Statistical Analysis Used:Data were analyzed using SPSS version 22. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Student’s t-test and Chi-square/Fischer’s exact test were used as appropriate. Diagnostic performance was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, and ROC curve analysis with calculation of area under the curve (AUC).Results:PDPH occurred in 12 patients (13.6%). Baseline ONSD values were comparable between groups. Patients who developed PDPH showed significantly lower ONSD values from T4 onward (P < 0.05). The best diagnostic performance was at T72 (AUC 0.776; 95% CI 0.663–0.889) with a cutoff ≤4.52 mm, yielding 91.7% sensitivity, 69.7% specificity, and 98.1% negative predictive value. ONSD reduction from baseline at T72 was significantly greater in the PDPH group (P < 0.001).Conclusion:ONSD measurement at 72 h postspinal anesthesia is a sensitive and clinically useful prognostic tool for predicting PDPH in obstetric patients and may be incorporated into routine postoperative monitoring.

Introduction

Postdural puncture headache (PDPH) is a common and debilitating complication of spinal anesthesia in obstetric patients, adversely affecting maternal recovery, bonding, and breastfeeding.[123] It results from cerebrospinal fluid leakage causing intracranial hypotension and cerebral vasodilation, with diagnosis largely based on subjective clinical features.[45] Optic nerve sheath diameter (ONSD), a noninvasive ultrasonographic marker of intracranial pressure, correlates well with CSF pressure changes and has been studied in several neurological conditions.[678] However, physiological changes of pregnancy and perioperative factors may influence ONSD values.[9101112] This cross-sectional study evaluates the prognostic utility of ONSD for early prediction of PDPH in obstetric patients undergoing cesarean section under spinal anesthesia.

Subjects and Methods

This cross-sectional study was conducted over a period of 6 months. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants in both English and Kannada languages.

The study employed convenient sampling to recruit pregnant women aged between 18 and 40 years who were scheduled for cesarean section under spinal anesthesia. Based on sample size calculations using the area under the ROC curve methodology, a total of 88 participants were enrolled. The sample size was determined using the formula n = Z²α/2 V (AUC)/d², where the area under the curve (AUC) for predicting PDPH from ONSD at 72 h was 0.6695, derived from the study by Peng et al.[13] With a 5% alpha error, 80% power, and null hypothesis at 0.5, the calculated sample size was 88 subjects. MS Excel, SPSS version 22 (IBM SPSS Statistics, Somers NY, USA) was used to analyze data.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Participants were included if they were pregnant women between 18 and 40 years of age undergoing cesarean section under spinal anesthesia, classified as ASA I or ASA II. Exclusion criteria comprised ASA III and IV patients, those with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, contraindications to spinal anesthesia, ophthalmic diseases, neurological diseases, and patients who refused consent.

Study procedure

All ONSD measurements were performed by the same anesthesiologist using an ultrasound machine equipped with a 13–6 MHz frequency probe to ensure consistency and minimize interobserver variability. The evaluation of PDPH was conducted by a different anesthesiologist to maintain objectivity and reduce potential bias.

ONSD measurements were obtained with patients positioned supine, and two measurements were taken from each eye with the average recorded as the final value. These measurements were systematically collected at five predetermined time points: before spinal anesthesia administration (T0), at 4 h postspinal anesthesia (T4), at 24 h (T24), at 48 h (T48), and at 72 h (T72) following the procedure.

Postdural puncture headache assessment

All participants were systematically questioned about headache occurrence at each follow-up time point. PDPH was diagnosed according to the International Classification of Headache Disorders, Third Edition criteria. The assessment included the evaluation of headache characteristics, temporal relationship to the procedure, and response to postural changes.

Results

The study successfully recruited 88 pregnant women with a mean age of 28.4 ± 5.2 years, representing a typical obstetric population undergoing cesarean section. The demographic distribution showed no significant differences between patients who developed PDPH and those who did not, with P-values exceeding 0.05 for all baseline parameters including age, BMI, ASA classification, and gestational age. This demographic homogeneity strengthens the validity of subsequent comparisons, as confounding variables were effectively controlled. The overall PDPH incidence of 13.6% (12 out of 88 patients) falls within the expected range for spinal anesthesia in obstetric patients, validating the representativeness of the study population [Table 1].

The most significant finding emerged from the ONSD measurements across different time points. While baseline ONSD values showed no statistical difference between groups (4.79 ± 0.28 mm vs. 4.83 ± 0.35 mm, P = 0.714), a clear divergence became apparent starting at 4 h postspinal anesthesia. The PDPH group demonstrated consistently lower ONSD values compared to the non-PDPH group at all postprocedural time points, with statistical significance achieved from T4 onwards (P < 0.05 for all comparisons). The progressive nature of these changes suggests that CSF pressure alterations, reflected by ONSD measurements, begin early but become more pronounced over time in patients destined to develop PDPH [Table 2].

Among the 12 patients who developed PDPH, the majority (58.3%) experienced symptom onset within the first 24 h, consistent with established literature on PDPH temporal patterns. The headache severity was predominantly moderate to severe, with a mean VAS score of 6.8 ± 1.4, reflecting the significant clinical impact of this complication. Associated symptoms were common, with nausea affecting 75% of patients and photophobia present in 66.7% of cases. The need for epidural blood patch treatment in 66.7% of cases underscores the severity of symptoms and the limitations of conservative management alone [Table 3].

The analysis of ONSD changes from baseline revealed the most clinically relevant findings. Patients who developed PDPH showed significantly greater reductions in ONSD compared to those who remained asymptomatic. The mean reduction at T72 was −0.44 ± 0.25 mm in the PDPH group versus only −0.12 ± 0.19 mm in the non-PDPH group (P < 0.001). This substantial difference suggests that the magnitude of ONSD reduction, rather than absolute values, may be the key discriminatory factor. The progressive increase in the difference between groups from T4 to T72 indicates that the diagnostic utility of ONSD improves with time after the initial procedure [Table 4].

The ROC analysis demonstrated progressive improvement in diagnostic performance over time, with AUC values increasing from 0.693 at T4 to 0.776 at T72. The T72 time point emerged as optimal, achieving an AUC of 0.776 (95% CI: 0.663–0.889), which represents good discriminatory ability according to established criteria [Graph 1]. The optimal cutoff value of ≤4.52 mm at T72 provided excellent sensitivity (91.7%) with acceptable specificity (69.7%). This high sensitivity is particularly valuable clinically, as it minimizes the risk of missing patients who will develop PDPH, allowing for early intervention strategies [Table 5 and Graph 1].

The diagnostic performance metrics reveal important clinical considerations. The high sensitivity (91.7%) means that ONSD measurement at 72 h would correctly identify the vast majority of patients destined to develop PDPH. The negative predictive value of 98.1% is particularly reassuring, indicating that patients with ONSD values above the cutoff are very unlikely to develop PDPH. However, the positive predictive value of 32.4% suggests that while the test is excellent at ruling out PDPH, it has limited specificity for confirming the diagnosis. This pattern is typical of screening tests and suggests that ONSD measurement would be most valuable as a rule-out tool rather than a confirmatory diagnostic test [Table 6].

Discussion

The findings from this cross-sectional study demonstrate that ONSD measurements, particularly at 72 h postspinal anesthesia, are highly sensitive for predicting PDPH in pregnant women undergoing cesarean section under spinal anesthesia, with an optimal cutoff of ≤4.52 mm providing 91.7% sensitivity and 69.7% specificity. These results align with and extend previous literature examining ONSD as a noninvasive biomarker for PDPH risk.

Several studies have evaluated the relationship between ONSD changes and PDPH development in obstetric patients. Peng et al.[13] conducted a large prospective observational study on 156 women undergoing cesarean section, reporting that ONSD was significantly lower in the PDPH group at multiple postoperative time points. Their study found the best predictive accuracy at 24 h postprocedure, with an AUC of 0.9787 and a cutoff value of 4.0 mm (sensitivity 92%, specificity 94%), somewhat higher than the optimal AUC observed at 72 h in the present study. Their findings corroborate the progressive decrease in ONSD in patients who develop PDPH and support the use of serial ONSD measurements for risk stratification.

A Beşir et al.[14] also demonstrated that patients developing PDPH experienced a significant reduction in ONSD following spinal anesthesia, with the lowest values noted at 24 h. Their reported mean ONSD in PDPH patients ranged from 3.6 to 3.9 mm, slightly lower than the absolute values in the current study, which may reflect population or methodological differences. The timing of symptom onset and correlation with ONSD dynamics in their cohort closely matches the present findings, as most PDPH episodes occurred within the first 24–48 h postprocedure.

Another recent study by Lakshmi et al.[15] observed marginal but statistically significant reductions in ONSD at 4 and 24 h following spinal anesthesia, confirming the early onset of intracranial pressure changes postprocedure. Unlike the present study, their sample was small, and none of the patients reported PDPH, underscoring the importance of adequate power for clinical prediction. Their conclusion emphasizes the practicality of ONSD measurement as a repeatable and cost-effective screening tool in perioperative care.

The consistency in reported sensitivity and negative predictive value across published studies suggests that ONSD measurement, while excellent for ruling out PDPH, offers limited specificity and positive predictive value. The findings across research underscore its utility as a screening rather than confirmatory tool, particularly suited for early identification and targeted intervention to minimize maternal morbidity.[16]

Studies have highlighted that the magnitude of ONSD reduction, more than the absolute values, is most predictive for PDPH. As demonstrated by both the present and prior studies, a sustained decrease without recovery—particularly from baseline through early postoperative time points—is strongly associated with PDPH onset. Serial ONSD assessments improve diagnostic accuracy, especially in settings where subjective symptoms may be underreported or delayed.[17]

A notable feature across recent literature is the adoption of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis to determine optimal cutoff values for prediction of PDPH. In the present study, a cutoff of ≤4.52 mm at 72 h optimally balances sensitivity and specificity, while other studies have reported similar thresholds at earlier time points (around 4–4.5 mm at 24–48 h).

Conclusion

This cross-sectional study demonstrates that ONSD measured at 72 h postspinal anesthesia serves as a clinically useful prognostic tool for predicting PDPH in pregnant women undergoing cesarean section. With an optimal cutoff value of ≤4.52 mm, ONSD achieved excellent sensitivity (91.7%) and high negative predictive value (98.1%), making it particularly valuable for ruling out PDPH development. The progressive improvement in diagnostic performance from 4 to 72 h (AUC increasing from 0.693 to 0.776) indicates that ONSD changes reflect the evolving pathophysiology of intracranial hypotension associated with PDPH. While the moderate specificity (69.7%) limits its use as a confirmatory diagnostic test, the high sensitivity suggests that ultrasound-guided ONSD measurement could be incorporated into routine postoperative monitoring protocols to identify high-risk patients early, enabling timely intervention strategies and potentially reducing the morbidity associated with this significant complication of neuraxial anesthesia.

Real-world efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors in treating oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCAPE): protocol of a multicentre, real-world, observational, cohort study
Authors: Chenghao Wang, Qin Xie, Yan Miao, Wenwu He, Kangning Wang, Guangyuan Liu, Qiang Fang, Lin Peng, Yungchang Chen, Jin Zhou, Jiahua Lv, Yunxiang Qi, Qifeng Wang, Qiuling Shi, Yongtao Han, Xuefeng Leng

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOesophageal cancer (EC) presents a substantial global health challenge, ranking eighth in incidence and sixth in cancer-related mortality. Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the primary subtype and accounts for approximately 90% of cases in Asia. Despite treatment advances, the 5-year survival rate remains modest at 10%–30%. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, exemplified by KEYNOTE-590 and CheckMate 577 trials, have reshaped EC therapeutic landscapes. Our study addresses the critical gap in understanding the real-world impact of PD-1 (Programmed Death-1) inhibitors, conducting a multicentre, real-world, observational cohort analysis focused on ESCC. This research endeavours to provide practical insights into PD-1 treatment for EC, facilitating informed clinical decision-making and optimising patient outcomes in diverse healthcare settings.Methods and analysisThis multicentre study includes patients diagnosed with histopathologically confirmed ESCC who have consented to treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. It is structured into two distinct segments: Part A, characterised by its retrospective nature, and Part B, representing the prospective arm. Within both parts, four stratified cohorts are delineated, comprising Cohort 1 (preoperative neoadjuvant/conversion therapy), Cohort 2 (postoperative adjuvant therapy), Cohort 3 (first-line therapy for advanced ESCC) and Cohort 4 (≥2 lines of therapy for advanced ESCC). The primary endpoint is the objective response rate in diverse treatment cohorts. Secondary endpoints include pathologic complete response rate, disease-free survival, progression-free survival, overall survival, adverse events, immune-related adverse events, quality of life and the intricacies of immunotherapy patterns and hyperprogression. Furthermore, exploratory endpoints scrutinise potentially predictive biomarkers, as well as the clinical and genomic characteristics inherent to ESCC patients if possible. Introduction

According to Global Cancer Observatory 2020 estimates, there were approximately 604 100 new cases of oesophageal cancer (EC) and 544 100 deaths from the disease. EC was the eighth most diagnosed malignant neoplasm and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide.1 Oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and oesophageal adenocarcinoma are the two main histological subtypes of EC. Adenocarcinoma is the primary subtype in the United States and Europe, accounting for approximately 70% of cases. In Asia, ESCC is the primary subtype, accounting for about 90% of cases.2 Survival rates for EC patients have improved with advances in treatment, but are still unsatisfactory, with a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of only 10%–30%.3 The high mortality and poor prognosis of EC patients highlight the need for more effective therapy.

Recently, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have emerged as a successful and innovative therapy in clinical practice for treating solid tumours, heralding a paradigm shift in the management of advanced cancers, including EC.4 5 Clinical trials have increasingly showcased the potential of immunotherapy to improve the survival of EC patients.

KEYNOTE-5906 is a randomised, placebo-controlled, global, multicentre, Phase 3 clinical trial evaluating pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil+cisplatin) versus chemotherapy alone for first-line treatment of unresectable locally advanced or metastatic oesophageal or oesophagogastric junction (EGJ) cancer. It was the first immunotherapy study worldwide to report significantly improved OS in patients with advanced oesophageal or EGJ cancer. A total of 749 patients were enrolled, including 548 patients with ESCC. The results showed that patients in the chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab group had significantly better OS (12.4 months vs 9.8 months, p<0.0001, HR=0.73), higher objective response rate (ORR) (45.0% vs 29.3%) and significantly longer duration of response (8.3 months vs 6.0 months, p<0.0001) than those in the placebo plus chemotherapy group. The incidence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group was similar to that in the placebo plus chemotherapy group (72% vs 68%). These compelling results underscore the favourable efficacy and safety profile of pembrolizumab in conjunction with chemotherapy, leading to its successful incorporation into National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines and heralding a transformative shift in the treatment paradigm for advanced EC worldwide.6,11

The CheckMate 577 trial (a global, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial evaluating ICIs as adjuvant therapy in patients with oesophageal or EGJ cancer) was the first study to show that adjuvant treatment with nivolumab significantly prolonged disease-free survival (DFS). Subgroup analyses revealed a more substantial benefit, particularly among patients with ESCC and those with positive nodal status (ypN+).12

Furthermore, these findings suggest the potential utility of ICIs in the neoadjuvant treatment of locally advanced EC. Consequently, several clinical trials of EC have emerged, including PALACE-1,13 Keystone-001,14 PERFECT15 and NICE,16 which investigate the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in neoadjuvant therapies. As these trials are currently ongoing, only short-term outcomes have been published, emphasising the need for sustained follow-up to ascertain long-term results.

These clinical trials have demonstrated the notable efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in treating advanced or locally advanced EC, as well as in neoadjuvant and adjuvant therapy for EC. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that these trials were executed under meticulously controlled research conditions. The comprehensive exploration of the efficacy and safety of PD-1 treatment for EC remains incomplete in real-world settings, with a limited number of real-world studies investigating the application of PD-1 inhibitors in treating EC. To address this gap, we initiated a multicentre, real-world, observational, cohort study of PD-1 inhibitors for the treatment of ESCC.

Methods

Study aims

The study aimed to observe and describe the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in Chinese patients with ESCC at various treatment stages. This objective was pursued by collecting and analysing real-world data from Chinese ESCC patients who underwent PD-1 inhibitor treatment.

Study design and setting

This is a large, collaborative, real-world, observational cohort study. There are currently five participating centres in China.

It is divided into two distinct parts: Part A, retrospective, and Part B, prospective. Both segments are further categorised into four cohorts: Cohort 1 (preoperative neoadjuvant/conversion therapy), Cohort 2 (postoperative adjuvant therapy), Cohort 3 (first-line therapy for advanced ESCC) and Cohort 4 (≥2 lines of therapy for advanced ESCC). We will analyse the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in different cohorts of ESCC patients. PD-1 inhibitors mainly include nivolumab, pembrolizumab, camrelizumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab, toripalimab, slulizumab, etc.

The retrospective part will include patients with oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma who received PD-1 inhibitor treatment between 1 January 2019 and 31 October 2023, while the prospective part will enrol patients treated with PD-1 inhibitors between 1 November 2023 and 1 November 2025.

The objective of this study is to evaluate the real-world effectiveness and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in ESCC patients (ORR, pathologic complete response (pCR) in Cohort 1, DFS in Cohort 2, progression-free survival (PFS) in Cohort 3/4). The study will also investigate PD-1 inhibitors treatment patterns, adverse reaction management protocols and safety data encompassing the incidence, severity, onset time, response time and organ involvement of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in real-world settings. Furthermore, the analysis will include the incidence of hyperprogression, pseudoprogression and clinical management patterns. Additionally, the study will evaluate the impact of treatment on patients’ quality of life. Comprehensive data on clinical and demographic characteristics will be collected to explore the clinical and genomic features of hyperprogressive and pseudoprogressive populations, factors associated with irAEs and characteristics of the population benefitting from PD-1 inhibitors (figure 1).

Study participants

The study participants are patients with pathologically confirmed ESCC who needed and consented to treat with PD-1 inhibitors. The patients included those with locally advanced/advanced disease, those who underwent preoperative/conversion therapy and those who received adjuvant therapy.

Study sample

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in the treating ESCC within real-world settings. The study will enrol 200 ESCC patients who received PD-1 inhibitors treatment in the retrospective portion and 217 ESCC patients who will receive PD-1 inhibitors treatment in the prospective portion.

The sample size is based on the following calculations:

Neoadjuvant therapy cohort

The JCOG1109 NExT17 study revealed that the neoadjuvant chemotherapy group for locally advanced ESCC had a pCR rate of approximately 19.8%. This study aims to improve the pCR rate to 35%, setting bilateral α=0.05 and power=0.8. The sample size was calculated to be 61.0 cases using the Power Analysis and Sample Size (PASS) software. Considering the 10% dropout rate, the neoadjuvant cohort is expected to include 68 subjects.

Adjuvant therapy cohort

The CheckMate 57712 trial showed that the DFS in the observation group was only 11.0 months. However, in the group that received adjuvant therapy with PD-1 inhibitors, the DFS was extended to 22.4 months. The sample size of 34 subjects was calculated using the PASS software with bilateral α=0.05 and power=0.8. Assuming a 10% dropout rate, the adjuvant therapy group is expected to include 38 subjects.

First-line treatment cohort

The JUPITER-0618 trial found that in the first-line treatment of advanced ESCC, the ORR for patients in the chemotherapy alone group was approximately 50%. However, when chemotherapy was combined with PD-1 inhibitors, the ORR increased to 69%. The sample size of 52 cases was calculated using the PASS software with bilateral α=0.05 and power=0.8. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, this cohort is expected to include 58 subjects.

≥2 lines treatment cohort

The ESCORT19 study discovered that in the treatment of advanced ESCC beyond the first line, the group treated with chemotherapy alone had an ORR of approximately 6.4%. However, when chemotherapy was combined with a PD-1 inhibitors, the ORR increased to 18%. The sample size of 48 cases was calculated using the PASS software with bilateral α=0.05 and power=0.8. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, this cohort is expected to include 53 subjects.

Based on the cohort calculations above, a total of 217 subjects will be enrolled in the prospective portion of this study.

Follow-up

Patients will be followed up for 5 years after the completion of recruitment. For Cohort 1 and 2, follow-up will occur every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months from the third to the fifth year. Patients in Cohort 3 and 4 will undergo follow-up every 3 months.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS software, V.23.0. Continuous variables will be summarised using the number of cases, mean, median, SD, median with IQR, minimum and maximum values. Categorical and ranked variables will be described using frequencies and percentages for each category or rank, with missing values excluded from percentage calculations. Results will be presented as hazard ratios with exact 95% CI. The primary outcomes are ORR rate in different cohorts. Secondary outcomes will include pCR rate in the preoperative neoadjuvant/conversion therapy cohort, DFS in the postoperative adjuvant therapy cohort, PFS in the advanced ESCC cohort, OS, quality of life, incidence of AEs, hyperprogression, pseudoprogression, delayed reactions, etc. Exploratory outcomes involve potentially predictive biomarkers and the clinical and genomic characteristics of specific patients. The number and proportion of people who achieved ORR were calculated according to the different treatment modes. Curves and rates of DFS, PFS and OS will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier methods. Differences were considered to be significant at the 5% level of significance (p<0.05).

Discussion

PD-1 inhibitors have emerged as a crucial therapeutic approach for EC, as evidenced by multiple clinical trials that have demonstrated their efficacy and safety. Despite these trials operating under stringent conditions, real-world application extends beyond their criteria. This reality, wherein PD-1 inhibitors are administered to patients not meeting trial inclusion, underscores an unexplored dimension of efficacy and safety in ESCC. Few real-world studies currently exist, and disparities with randomised controlled trial outcomes highlight the need for further investigation. To bridge this gap, our retrospective and prospective clinical trial delves into the real-world effectiveness and safety of PD-1 inhibitors in patients with ESCC.

The CheckMate 577 trial has demonstrated that nivolumab significantly improves DFS in curative intent EC resection patients (22.4 months vs 11 months, HR=0.69, p=0.0003), particularly in ESCC and ypN+ patients. Of the patients treated with nivolumab, 34% experienced grade 3 or 4 TRAEs, but only 9% discontinued therapy.

However, a real-world retrospective study21 found that 82% (32/39) of patients faced challenges completing immunotherapy, predominantly due to disease progression or TRAEs. Survival analysis showed that adjuvant immunotherapy had no significant effect on 1-year OS. Moreover, the percentage of patients receiving neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in the treatment of EC in China in the real world is low. Further exploration is required to determine the adjuvant therapy mode for the majority of patients who do not receive neoadjuvant therapy.

Several ongoing clinical trials are assessing the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors for neoadjuvant treatment in locally advanced EC, with encouraging short-term outcomes. A real-world retrospective study22 showed similar results, and more clinical evidence is needed for the use of PD-1 inhibitors in the neoadjuvant treatment of EC.

Results from KEYNOTE-590,6 KEYNOTE-181,8 ATTRACTION-3,11 JUPITER-06,18 ESCORT,19 ESCORT-1st,23 CheckMate 648,24 RATIONALE-30225 and ORIENT-1526 collectively indicate that patients with advanced EC, in both first- and second-line therapy, derive significantly longer OS or PFS when treated with PD-1 inhibitors in conjunction with chemotherapy compared with those receiving chemotherapy alone. The KEYNOTE-590 trial showed that in patients with ESCC and Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) Combined Positive Score (CPS)≥10, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was superior to placebo plus chemotherapy in terms of OS and PFS. The incidence of TRAEs (≥3) was comparable between the two groups (72% vs 68%). However, the ESCORT and ATTRACTION-3 trials showed that patients with EC can benefit from immunotherapy regardless of PD-L1 expression levels. The absence of accurate biomarkers for predicting immunotherapy outcomes necessitates further clinical trials to explore more effective markers in EC patients.

In these clinical trials, patients consistently demonstrate high rates of treatment plan completion. Conversely, real-world clinical practice often presents challenges, leading many patients to struggle with treatment adherence due to their medical condition. This ongoing clinical trial aims to contribute invaluable real-world data by encompassing patients with diverse characteristics, such as those with poor Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status, elderly patients, comorbidities, infectious diseases, and those concurrently undergoing multiple treatments. Notably, this approach ensures the validation of real-world outcomes within the similar patient population. The trial’s comprehensive data collection strategy, capturing a broader and more representative dataset, facilitates a nuanced evaluation of the impacts of different dosage forms and treatment regimens.

The study is designed to address pivotal inquiries, investigating whether PD-1 inhibitors can sustain the substantial efficacy witnessed in earlier clinical trials when applied in real-world settings and exploring if specific patient cohorts elicit superior responses. Additionally, the study aims to meticulously assess the safety profile of PD-1 inhibitors, with a targeted focus on identifying potential safety concerns associated with their extended usage in real-world patients. The results of this real-world study are anticipated to augment our understanding of the role of PD-1 inhibitors in the treatment of ESCC, contributing to the establishment of a more reliable foundation for clinical practices.

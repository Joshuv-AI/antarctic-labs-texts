Key hub genes identification and therapeutic target prediction via multi-validation for the senescence-inflammation axis in prostate cancer
Authors: Ying Wang, Qin Liu, Mei Yi, Yan Liu, Ji’ang’shu He, Han Chao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cellular senescence remodels the microenvironment via SASP, and immune inflammation promotes carcinogenesis through DNA damage and immune remodeling; the “senescence-inflammation” vicious cycle formed by these two processes is a core driver of cancer development and progression, yet its mechanism in prostate cancer progression remains unclear. The regulatory mechanisms of “senescence-inflammation” related genes in prostate cancer were revealed, targeted drugs were predicted, and new insights were provided for the clinical treatment of prostate cancer. In this study, gene expression profile data of prostate cancer were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). Differentially expressed genes were screened out through bioinformatics analysis and verified by experiments; GO and KEGG analyses were used to explore the prostate cancer-specific biological processes and signaling pathways involved, immune infiltration characteristics were revealed by combining immune infiltration tools with single-cell technology, and finally, drugs with therapeutic potential were predicted through core gene target analysis. Prostate cancer-related differentially expressed genes were found to number 203. Notable hub genes included ACTB, RPLP0, TOP2A, TXN, HSP90B1, TAGLN, RPSA, and ANXA2. According to enrichment analysis, apoptosis, cellular senescence, inflammation, and the initiation of immunological responses are the main factors contributing to the development of bladder cancer. The immunological processes of prostate cancer are significantly influenced by memory B cells, CD8 T cells, follicular helper T cells, M1 macrophages, monocytes, and NK cells, as confirmed by immune infiltration and single-cell data. Furthermore, hub genes were found to be significantly expressed in a variety of immune cells by single-cell studies; monocytes and macrophages in particular exhibited high levels of ACTB, RPLP0, RPSA, and ANXA2. We next used Western blotting, PCR, and immunohistochemistry to evaluate these genes’ expression in clinical samples. Lastly, Miconazole, Fostamatinib, Quercetin, Resveratrol, and Regorafenib were shown to be possible important drugs or ingredients for the treatment of prostate cancer by our drug-target interaction prediction analysis. We clarified the mechanism by which immune-inflammatory and aging-related genes contribute to the pathogenesis of prostate cancer. We identified and validated four core genes, namely TXN, TOP2A, ANXA2, and HSP90B1, and predicted potential therapeutic agents, specifically resveratrol and regorafenib. These findings provide a theoretical basis for research on the molecular mechanisms of prostate cancer and its clinical treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42497-w.

Introduction

Men in industrialized nations and areas are frequently diagnosed with prostate cancer (PC), a malignancy whose incidence and progression are strongly correlated with racial, nutritional, and lifestyle factors1. In recent years, the prevalence and death rate from prostate cancer have increased. Although the precise origin of PC is uncertain, a number of factors have been linked to a higher risk of PC. The primary risk factor for PC is smoking, and tobacco contains many recognized carcinogens, including N-nitrosated chemicals and aromatic amines2. In addition, PC is also related to age, gender, genetic factors, environmental factors, etc.3. To better prevent and treat prostate cancer, the importance of detecting biomarkers has become increasingly prominent4. Currently, a variety of blood and urine biomarkers can be used to monitor or predict the occurrence, progression, and treatment response of prostate cancer. For example, in the diagnostic stage: based on PSA, combining fPSA/tPSA, PCA3, HOXC6, and DLX1 can improve biopsy accuracy; in the prognostic evaluation stage: relying on Gleason grading groups, Ki-67, ERG fusion gene (TMPRSS2-ERG), etc., to judge tumor invasiveness and progression risk; in the efficacy monitoring stage: dynamic monitoring of CTCs, ctDNA, etc., can evaluate efficacy and drug resistance5. Although a large number of potential biomarkers have been applied in the clinical diagnosis and efficacy detection of PC, their specificity still needs to be improved. In recent years, studies have shown that the development of PC is closely related to molecular mechanisms such as inflammation, immunity, and cellular senescence6,7. More and more studies are also paying attention to the application of these molecular mechanism-related biomarkers in PC. In this article, we will discuss the value of key biomarkers in the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of PC.

The primary attribute of aging organisms is cellular senescence, and an excessive build-up of senescent cells in tissues can cause a variety of age-related illnesses, including cancer8. Among the traits of the aging phenotype that are commonly seen are the chronic DNA damage response, the involvement of various cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors, increased secretion of pro-inflammatory and tissue remodeling factors, induction of anti-apoptotic genes, changes in metabolic rate, and endogenous plasma reticulum stress9. The exact role that cellular senescence plays in PC has not been determined to yet. Senescent cells can both suppress and promote tumor growth depending on the type of cancer, disease stage, and tumor microenvironment. The dual model (tumor-suppressive and tumor-promoting) is well-documented, and clear evidence of this is available in scientific literature. Senescent cells accumulate in all tissues with age in PC, an age-related illness. Senescent cells are metabolically active and, despite their inability to proliferate, they create an inflammatory milieu through the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). SASP is made up of several pro-inflammatory mediators, including as growth factors, chemokines, proteases, and cytokines, which cause the nearby non-senescent cells to age through the paracrine pathway and intensify the inflammatory milieu10,11. Numerous investigations have demonstrated that senescent cells suppress the growth of new tumors and cause PC tumors to develop, recur, and spread in later stages. Furthermore, the SASP of tumor cells explains the dual character of senescence in cancer cells12.

High-throughput sequencing and single-cell sequencing each have their own focuses and are synergistic and complementary in prostate cancer research and clinical practice. Through batch analysis, High-throughput sequencing can parse genomic driver events (such as TMPRSS2-ERG fusion, PTEN deletion, etc.), laying the foundation for molecular typing; it assists liquid biopsy in achieving non-invasive diagnosis and dynamic monitoring, and can also guide targeted therapy and preliminarily explore drug resistance mechanisms. Single-cell sequencing, on the other hand, reveals tumor heterogeneity at single-cell resolution, identifies cancer cell subsets with specific functions (such as stem cell-like subsets, therapy-resistant subsets), parses the cell interaction network in the tumor microenvironment, and thereby guides precise treatment and drug resistance reversal. The two technologies work synergistically, integrating analysis from global characteristics to local details, and promoting the upgrading of prostate cancer diagnosis and treatment towards precision and individualization13. This study obtained gene expression profile data of prostate cancer through GEO. Firstly, bioinformatics analysis was used to screen differentially expressed genes, and their biological significance was verified through experiments; combined with GO and KEGG analyses, the specific biological processes and signaling pathways of prostate cancer were explored in depth; with the help of immune infiltration tools combined with single-cell technology, the immune infiltration characteristics of tumors were revealed; finally, through the analysis of core gene targets, candidate drugs with therapeutic potential were predicted; these results will provide new ideas and schemes for the prevention and treatment of prostate cancer.

Materials and methods

Microarray data source and differential gene analysis

Prostate cancer bioinformatics data were obtained from the NCBI gene expression public database. Three data sets were selected : GSE46602, GSE104749, and GSE179321. Among them, the GSE46602 and GSE104749 data sequencing platforms are GPL 570, and the GSE179321 data sequencing platform is GPL2467614,15. To convert the chip matrix data into gene name matrix expression data, use Sangerbox 3.0’s gene name conversion tool. Fuse the three data using the batch effect removal tool. Once differential gene analysis has been performed, choose the “limma” tool to identify the genes that differ between PC samples and normal control samples. A 1.5-fold difference and a P-value of less than 0.05 were used as the thresholds for differential genes. Genes with differential expression levels (DEGs) are upregulated when the numbers are positive. Likewise, the P.value is 0.05 and the fold difference is 1.5-fold; negative values denote the down-regulation of genes with differential expression. Heat maps and volcano plots were utilized to show the outcomes of differential gene expression16.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

Using Sangerbox 3.0’s weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) tool, the gene co-expression network of GSE183021 was constructed. Based on the clustering tree, the top 50% of genes with the lowest Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) were eliminated. Determine the correlation coefficient between each pair of genes to create a similarity matrix. After that, a topological overlap matrix (TOM) was created to determine the average network link for every gene. Based on the suitable parameters of the block-dimensional module function, a dynamic tree cutting method was used to group genes with similar expression profiles into different modules. Genes that could not be assigned to any module were shown by genes in gray modules. Each module was symbolized by a distinct hue. The module eigengene (ME), which was the first principal component, was where each module’s gene expression profile began. MEs were used to evaluate the connection between modules and phenotypes. The module with the highest absolute value of correlation coefficient is the one that has been identified as the important module for further inquiry. Module membership (MM) was defined as the correlation coefficient between a gene’s expression value and a module’s ME, which illustrated the relationship between the gene and the module. The association between a gene and a phenotype was indicated by the gene significance (GS), which was the correlation coefficient between an expression value of a gene and a phenotype. The GS threshold was set to 0.1, the weight threshold was set to 0.1, and the MM threshold was set to 0.8 in order to extract hub genes16.

Prostate cancer disease gene acquisition

To study the targets and effects of prostate cancer, we used “prostate cancer” as the keyword to search the GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) disease database to obtain the target proteins associated with the disease17. It was first discovered that a key target gene for the onset of prostate cancer was the intersection of GeneCards and differential gene sets. Understanding protein-protein interactions was essential for comprehending both healthy and diseased cellular physiology, as they were crucial to cellular biological processes. This study limited the species to “Homo sapiens” with a confidence value > greater than 0.4 and performed PPI network analysis on the resulting intersection gene set using the STRING database (http://string-db.org/). Cytoscape (version 3.9.1) was used to create the PPI network18. Furthermore, a gene regulation network for the prostate cancer process was built using the CytoHubba algorithm, a plugin for the Cytoscape program19. We screened for ten more important hub genes20.

Key genes and pathways verification

To verify the credibility of prostate cancer-related genes, we used the dataset to extract the sequencing results of hub genes and performed statistical analysis. Additionally, GSEA analysis was conducted on potential hub target genes to elucidate the key signaling pathways involved in the regulation of these genes21. We obtained version 3.0 of the GSEA program from the GSEA website and utilized it for our purposes. Based on the expression levels of statistically significant hub genes, we separated the samples into low expression (< 50%) and high expression ( > = 50%) groups. Subsequently, to evaluate the relevant pathways and molecular mechanisms, we downloaded the c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt sub-collection from the Molecular Signatures Database. Considering gene expression patterns and phenotype classification, we set the minimum gene set to five and the maximum gene set to five thousand. With 1,000 resamples used as support, a P-value of less than 0.05 and an FDR of less than 0.25 were deemed statistically significant at that time.

Gene enrichment analysis of prostate cancer

PProstate cancer and pyroptosis intersection genes underwent GO, KEGG, and Reactome enrichment analyses to examine the signaling pathways and mechanisms of action associated with pyroptosis22. Select “H. sapiens” as the sole species, import the gene set, choose enrichment analysis from the tool center, and initiate the Ouyi Biological Cloud Platform. The results will be presented in various charts after submitting the common parameters and entering the gene symbol for the hub gene set.

Identification of disease immune infiltrating cells

The typical elements of the immune microenvironment include fibroblasts, mesenchymal cells, inflammatory cells, immune cells, and different cytokines and chemokines. Immune cell infiltration analysis is an essential technique for predicting how a disease will proceed and how well a treatment will work. Using RNA sequencing data, the CIBERSORT method can determine the number of immune cells in the sample. It can also use linear support vector regression to deconvolute gene expression profiles23. The CIBERSORT method in Sangerbox 3.0 software was used to identify the immune cell types of patients with different immunological patterns in the dataset. Using stacked graphs, the immune cell composition of patients with different immunological patterns was shown. Finally, a difference boxplot was used to show the statistical differences between different immune cells.

Single-cell analysis

Through the Cancer Single-cell Expression Map (CancerSCEM) database, the GSE143791 data were selected, and we chose the PRAD-063-05-1 A sample for single-cell analysis. The parameters used for this single-cell QC data setup were: resolution = 1, PCs = 1:30, MT < 10%. Cell subpopulation clustering was performed by unsupervised clustering. After cell clustering, the proportions of different cell subpopulations were displayed by circular scale plots. After selecting the functional score module of the cell module and the analysis module in the CancerSCEM database, the PRAD-063-05-1 A sample was chosen. Then, the various cell subpopulations of this sample were calculated for cytotoxicity, HLA-dependent activating receptor, HLA-dependent inhibitory receptor, HLA-independent activating receptor, HLA-dependent inhibitory receptor, inflammation, and finally, inflammation. Stress: The expression of individual genes from the chosen gene modules was functionally evaluated in the samples to shed light on how the 10 hub genes are regulated in various cell types.

Clinical samples

Informed consent from the patient’s family and clearance from Chengdu University Hospital’s Medical Ethics Committee (Ethics clearance No. PJ2023-109-15) were obtained in order to validate the aforementioned analysis. Patients who underwent radical prostatectomy for prostate cancer at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu University between October 2023 and May 2024 had their cancerous and surrounding normal tissues removed. The following were the exclusion criteria: (1) individuals with pre-existing medical illnesses, such as diabetes or cardiovascular disease; (2) individuals with infectious diseases, such as HIV, syphilis, or viral hepatitis; and (3) individuals who refused to submit to the study protocol. We ultimately obtained PC tissue and adjacent tissue from 10 clinical samples. You can find the original patient data in Supplementary Table S10.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining

The sections were hydrated with gradient ethanol and dewaxed with xylene. Subsequently, the slides were treated with hydrogen peroxide to inhibit endogenous peroxidase activity, and goat serum was used to prevent nonspecific binding sites. After that, the proper volume of primary antibody was added, and it was kept at 4 °C for the entire night. The samples were stained with diaminobenzidine and counterstained with hematoxylin on the second day after being treated for 30 min at room temperature with the secondary and tertiary antibodies. After that, pictures stained with immunohistochemistry were captured using an upright microscope.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Gene primer sequences were designed and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Sangon Biotech, China). Table 1 lists the primer sequences for every gene. Utilizing RNeasy reagents, total RNA was extracted from the tissues. The TaqMan MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (#AM2071; InvitrogenTM) was used to reverse-transcribe and purify RNA samples. 40 cycles of qRT-PCR were performed using the 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The relative expression levels of each gene were ascertained using the 2 − ΔΔCT technique, with GAPDH acting as the internal reference. Each experiment was run three times, independently.

Western blot

Using high-efficiency RIPA lysis buffer (R0010, Solarbio), samples from each group were crushed in liquid nitrogen after being rinsed three times with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). This allowed for the extraction of tissue proteins. Using a BCA protein assay kit (PC0020, Solarbio), the total protein content was ascertained. The samples were then heated to 95 °C for five minutes in order to denature the proteins. After being separated using 10% SDS-PAGE, a 20 µg protein sample was transferred to a PVDF membrane. After blocking non-specific binding for an hour in non-fat milk, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies against TXN (Abcam, 1:1500), TOP2A (Abcam, 1:800), ANXA2 (Abcam, 1:1500), HSP90B1 (Abcam, 1:1000), and β-actin (Abcam, 1:5000) for an additional night at 4 °C. The appropriate secondary antibodies were then incubated on the membranes. Using Image Lab 3.0 software, immunoblots were quantified and observed using an electrochemiluminescence detection reagent (Invitrogen).

Hub-based drug prediction

We used the Drug Signatures Database(DSigDB,http://tanlab.ucdenver.edu/DSigDB) drug prediction analysis tool to predict potential therapeutic drugs for prostate cancer based on 10 key hub genes. Subsequently, we queried the DrugBank (https://go.drugbank.com/) and traditional Chinese medicine database (TCMSP, https://www.tcmsp-e.com/) to analyze the action mechanisms of relevant drugs for prostate cancer treatment.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was utilized for thorough validation to ascertain the link between drugs and hub genes. The three-dimensional structure of the recommended docking target should be retrieved in mol2 format from the Pubchem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov//docs/news) database. Next, use AutodockTools 1.5.6 to open, process, and save the small ligand molecule as a pdbqt file. As the docking protein, get the target protein’s fundamental three-dimensional structure from the RCSB protein database (www.rcsb.org/). processed with AutodockTools 1.5.6 and stored as a file called pdbqt. Choose the box size and Vina molecular docking coordinates. The exhaustivity parameter should be set to 15. The other parameters should be left at their normal settings. Autodockvina 1.1.2 was used for semi-flexible docking, and the final docking conformation was selected by comparing the conformations with the highest affinity.

Statistical analysis

With Graphpad Prism 9.0, statistical analyses and graphing were carried out. The data are displayed as histograms that display the means from three or more separate experiments plus the standard error of the mean (SEM). When comparing two groups, a t-test was utilized if the samples had a normal distribution; if not, a non-parametric test was applied. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare the samples from the different groups. Two groups were compared using Tukey’s procedure; differences were deemed statistically significant if *P < 0.05 and highly significant if **P < 0.01.

Dataset-based target validation

Through a series of analyses, we preliminarily found that hub genes such as ACTB, HSP90AB1, RPLP0, TOP2A, TXN, HSP90B1, TAGLN, RPSA, ANXA2, CALR, etc. play an important role in the occurrence of prostate cancer by regulating cell senescence and apoptosis. In order to verify whether the relevant hub genes are credible, we used the data set for verification. Statistical analysis showed that ACTB, RPLP0, TOP2A, TXN, HSP90B1, TAGLN, RPSA, and ANXA2 hub genes were all statistically significant (* P < 0.05 ) (Fig. 5A). In addition, we used the data set to conduct G SEA analysis based on statistically significant hub genes, and the results showed that TXN, TOP2A, ANXA2, and HSP90B1 were enriched in the apoptosis signaling pathway and cellular senescence (Fig. 5B–E).

Validation of hub genes in prostate cancer

To further validate the aforementioned results, we employed immunohistochemistry, Western blotting, and qRT-PCR to assess the expression levels of hub genes (TXN, TOP2A, ANXA2, and HSP90B1) in prostate cancer tissues (PC) and adjacent tissues (controls, Ctl). We collected tissue samples from 10 patients with prostate cancer who underwent prostatectomy at our institution; some samples were designated for immunohistochemistry, while others were used for protein and RNA extraction for Western blotting and qRT-PCR analyses. The results of immunohistochemical staining demonstrated that the expression levels of TXN, TOP2A, and HSP90B1 in PC were significantly higher than those in Ctl, while the expression of ANXA2 in PC was markedly lower than in Ctl (Fig. 6A). The results of qRT-PCR analysis indicated that compared to Ctl, the hub genes regulating senescence and apoptosis, TXN (**P < 0.01), TOP2A (*P < 0.05), and HSP90B1 (**P < 0.01), were upregulated, while the expression of ANXA2 (*P < 0.05) was downregulated (Fig. 6B). Western blot analysis revealed that the expression levels of TXN, TOP2A, and HSP90B1 were elevated in the Ctl, while ANXA2 exhibited lower expression (Fig. 6C). These findings were consistent with the qRT-PCR results and aligned broadly with our analytical predictions (Fig. 5A). Collectively, these results provide preliminary validation of the stability of our predictive model, offering new directions for the early diagnosis and treatment of PC.

Enrichment analysis results

A total of 1880 entries were found in the GO enrichment results; these were broadly categorized into three categories: Molecular Function, Cellular Component, and Biological Process (Supplement Table S6). The pyroptosis-related enrichment analysis of 203 prostate cancers served as the foundation for our findings. The primary elements of biological processes (BPs) include cellular translation, muscle contraction, positive regulation of telomerase RNA localization to Cajal body, defense against cytotoxicity mediated by natural killer cells, protein folding, positive regulation of T cell mediated cytotoxicity, negative regulation of the apoptotic process, ribosomal large subunit assembly, and protein stabilization. The extracellular exosome, cytosol, focal adhesion, lumenal side of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, cytosolic ribosome, stress fiber, cell surface, cytosolic large ribosomal subunit, and actin cytoskeleton are the primary locations of Cellular Components (CC). Protein folding chaperone, unfolded protein binding, actin filament binding, protein homodimerization activity, protein binding, T cell receptor binding, structural component of muscle, structural component of ribosome, and structural component of the cytoskeleton are the main components of molecular function (MF) (Fig. 7A,B). The K-EGG signaling pathway was enriched to 216, with the key involvement being Salmonella infection, cellular senescence, drug metabolism - other enzymes, vascular smooth muscle contraction, and the estrogen signaling pathway (Fig. 7C,D, Supplementary Table S7). Finally, we concluded that there is a strong correlation between cell senescence and apoptosis in prostate cancer and the occurrence and progression of the disease based on the comprehensive enrichment data. Furthermore, we discovered that immunological modulation is a significant factor in it.

Results of single cell analysis

Cellular subpopulations were classified into 25 by analyzing the PRAD-063-05-1 A sample in the CancerSCEM database. The main subpopulations are smooth muscle cells, monocytes, erythrocytes, plasma cells, malignant cells, endothelial cells, dendritic cells, CD4 + effector T cells, CD8 + naive T cells, CD4 + naive T cells, memory B cells, neutrophils, macrophages, CD8 + Tem, CD4 + Tem, NK cells, Naive B cells, Activated B cells, and fibroblasts (Fig. 9A). Circular scale diagrams showed monocytes, NK cells, CD8+ naïve Tcells, and Naive B cells as the major cell types. Subsequently, functional score assessment of cellular modules further showed that NK cells, Endothelial cells, CD8 + Tem, CD8 + naive T cells, CD8 + effector T cells, CD8 + Tcm, and CD4 + Tem scored higher in cytotoxicity score. Macrophages, monocytes, and NK cells scored higher in the inflammation score and had higher confidence (Stress) (Fig. 9B). Taken together, monocyte, macrophage, and NK cell infiltration exacerbated the inflammatory process in single-cell analysis of PC samples, with NK cells being the most toxic (Fig. 9C). Biosignal analysis identified 10 pivotal genes involved in PC progression, and single-cell UMAP plots of the 10 pivotal genes showed that these genes were infiltrated and expressed in different cellular subpopulations to promote disease progression. Among them, ACTB, RPLP0, and RPSA were significantly expressed in all cell types in the current sample, with high expression in monocyte and NK cell populations (Fig. 10). Finally, via controlling immune cells like monocytes, macrophages, and NK cells, crucial genes like ACTB, RPLP0, and RPSA may cause inflammation and ultimately worsen the course of PC. This is the result of our current study.

Discussion

The most frequent cancer of the urinary system and the most prevalent malignant tumor worldwide is prostate cancer. Cisplatin-based chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and surgery are the most effective treatments and standard treatments for prostate cancer. To improve prostate cancer survival, many are studying biomarkers to inform prognosis and treatment response24. Therefore, in order to increase the viability of targeted medicines, trustworthy new prognostic models are desperately needed.

Using the GEO database, we conducted gene expression analysis in this work to identify 476 genes that were expressed differently in prostate cancer patients compared to normal samples. In prostate cancer, 203 associated genes with differential expression were found. Additional screening revealed that key hub genes include ANXA2, ACTB, RPLP0, TOP2A, TXN, HSP90B1, TAGLN, and RPSA. According to enrichment analysis, the key risk factors for bladder cancer include age, immune response activation, inflammation, and apoptosis. Single-cell research revealed that immune cells penetrated PC tissues, including monocytes, NK cells, CD8+ naïve T cells, and naive B cells. It also revealed that important genes like ACTB, RPLP0, and RPSA may modulate these immune cells, causing inflammation and ultimately exacerbating the development of PC.

One cyclical manifestation of the cell cycle is cellular senescence. Even though they have been exposed to mitotic signal activation, cells in the cell senescence stage are still vibrant and in a non-proliferative state25. Through a number of molecular processes, such as replication block, the production of reactive oxygen species, or DNA damage response, activated proto-oncogenes can cause cellular senescence25. As a result, oncogene-induced senescence might be crucial in stopping precancerous lesions from developing into completely malignant stages. Studies indicate a connection between age-related prostatic hyperplasia and cellular senescence, which could play a significant role in tumor suppressive mechanisms. Gene expression traits in prostate fibroblasts and epithelial cells are associated with DNA and oxidative damage, which may influence epithelial cell invasion. It is worth noting that gene expression that causes oxidative damage and DNA damage occurs not only in the aging, tumor-prone prostate stroma, but also in the active stroma of human prostate tumors25,26. Our study revealed that TXN, TOP2A, ANXA2, and HSP90B1 influence the progression of prostate cancer by regulating processes such as oxidative stress, inflammatory signaling, cell proliferation, and senescence evasion. TXN can scavenge ROS and inhibit the secretion of inflammatory factors; its high expression in prostate cancer cells protects cancer cells and promotes disease progression. TOP2A is associated with cell proliferation; the inflammatory microenvironment can upregulate its expression, aiding cancer cells in evading senescence and enhancing tolerance, and it is positively correlated with tumor invasiveness. ANXA2 is induced by components of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) from senescent cells, and it can enhance inflammatory signaling, promote cancer cell invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis. HSP90B1 is upregulated in senescent cells and can promote the secretion of inflammatory factors; in cancer cells, it can stabilize oncoproteins and enhance their stress tolerance. These four molecules act synergistically through the “oxidative stress-inflammatory signaling-cell proliferation-senescence evasion” network, and their abnormal expression may serve as potential therapeutic targets.

Tumor, stromal, endothelial, and immune cells are the various types of cells that constitute the tumor microenvironment. Increasing evidence suggests that inflammatory cytokines—such as ATP, HMGB1, IL-1β, and IL-18—released during cellular senescence may significantly impact the tumor microenvironment (TME)27. Furthermore, studies have indicated a strong correlation between the initiation of immunological responses and cellular senescence28. Thus, we looked into the connection between immune cell infiltration and cellular senescence in more detail. The CIBERSORT algorithm was utilized to obtain the results of immune infiltration. Patients with prostate cancer showed higher amounts of immune cell infiltration, according to additional research. The prostate cancer group had higher immune cell infiltration—including B cells, T cells, and macrophages—than the normal group. High levels of CD4 + or CD8 + T cell infiltration in tumor tissue are linked to longer overall life, according to a review of the literature. When T cells invade malignancies, they dampen the immune system, increasing the likelihood that patients will survive28. However, ligands on the surface of T cells bind to immune checkpoint molecules expressed by tumor cells, which will hinder T cells from recognizing tumor cells, leading to immune evasion and uncontrolled tumor growth. Immune checkpoint inhibitors can enhance T cells recognize tumor cells, thereby enhancing the immune system’s killing effect on tumors. There are many factors that affect the efficacy of immunotherapy, and the infiltration of T cells within the tumor is one of the key factors in anti-tumor immunity29. As a crucial part of the circulating immune system, monocytes produce cytokines and growth factors in response to foreign messages in the circulation. A study discovered that the secretion of CHI3L1 by circulating monocytes from individuals with metastatic PC caused pro-tumorigenic effects30. Studies of a similar nature have also unequivocally shown a link between worse patient prognosis and increased levels of monocyte immune infiltration31. Since NK cells do not require the presentation of an antigen to detect a target, they are classified as a component of the innate immune system and comprise 5–15% of circulating lymphocytes in healthy people. They perform effector roles, including as cytotoxic activity and cytokine generation, during antiviral and antitumor responses. Studies on the phenotypic and functionality of NK cells linked to prostate cancer are still scarce, nevertheless. According to one study, NK cells isolated from PC patients’ peripheral blood show pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic phenotypes and encourage the release of substances that are implicated in the extracellular matrix remodeling cascade response. Furthermore, NK cells isolated from PC patients’ peripheral blood showed pro-monocyte recruitment characteristics (CCL1, CCL2, and CCL5) as well as participation in M2-like macrophage polarization (IL-10)32.

Based on drug target prediction using hub genes, we initially identified 114 potential compounds, mainly including Resveratrol, Regorafenib, Quercetin, etc. Resveratrol is a plant-derived polyphenol with multiple anti-tumor effects. Its mechanisms include activating caspase-3, regulating the Bax/Bcl-2 protein family, arresting the cell cycle, inhibiting the PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathways, down-regulating the expression of MMPs, and suppressing the proliferation, invasion, and metastasis of cancer cells. It can also regulate the tumor microenvironment, inhibit VEGF-mediated angiogenesis, antagonize pro-inflammatory cytokines, reshape the immune microenvironment, synergize with radiotherapy and chemotherapy to enhance treatment sensitivity, inhibit EMT, and reduce drug resistance. In prostate cancer, Resveratrol at different concentrations can inhibit cancer cell proliferation in a concentration-dependent manner33,34. One possible explanation is that Resveratrol targets AR- and ER-dependent signaling pathways, as it can counteract androgen- and estrogen-induced cell growth. Furthermore, Resveratrol can enhance the apoptosis-inducing potential of TRAIL by activating FKHRL1 and its target genes35. The ability of Resveratrol to inhibit tumor growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis, as well as its ability to enhance the therapeutic potential of TRAIL, indicates that Resveratrol can be used alone or in combination with TRAIL for the treatment of PC36.

Regorafenib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that exerts its effects by inhibiting tumor angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and microenvironment-related kinases. It has been approved for the third-line or second-line treatment of advanced refractory cancers such as metastatic colorectal cancer (the CORRECT study showed that OS was prolonged to 6.4 months), gastrointestinal stromal tumors (the GRID study showed that PFS reached 4.8 months), and hepatocellular carcinoma (the RESORCE study showed that OS was prolonged to 10.6 months)37. Regorafenib is a small-molecule inhibitor that can inhibit various membrane-bound and intracellular kinases, and is involved in normal cellular functions and pathological processes, such as tumorigenesis, tumor angiogenesis, and the maintenance of the tumor microenvironment. In in vitro biochemical or cellular assays, Regorafenib or its major human active metabolites M-2 and M-5 inhibit the activity of VEGFR1, VEGFR2, VEGFR3, FGFR1, FGFR2, SAPK2, PTK5, and Abl at concentrations that have been achieved clinically38. Based on the above drug predictions and reviews of relevant literature, it can be seen that drug prediction based on hub genes has certain scientific significance and guiding significance for clinical drug use.

Conclusion

In summary, based on the pivotal genes, we developed a prostate cancer disease prediction model and found that the pathogenesis of prostate cancer patients is mainly associated with the aberrant expression of signaling pathways such as cellular senescence, inflammation, and immune regulation. Based on single-cell analysis, we found that monocytes, NK cells, and macrophages were significantly infiltrated in the development of PC, and that pivotal genes, such as ACTB,, RPLP0, and RPSA, may lead to increased inflammation by regulating these immune cells. Finally, we predicted that Miconazole, Fostamatinib, Quercetin, Resveratrol, and Regorafenib may be the key drugs or components for the treatment of prostate cancer, with the potential therapeutic mechanism of modulating immune cells and senescence process.

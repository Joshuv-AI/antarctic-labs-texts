Stress-strength reliability inference in multicomponent systems under the unit-gamma Gompertz–Weibull distribution based on progressive Type-II censoring
Authors: Zohreh Pakdaman, Reza Alizadeh Noughabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReliable assessment of multicomponent systems operating under uncertain stress is fundamental to modern engineering, environmental management, and risk analysis. In many practical life-testing experiments, observations are subject to progressive Type-II censoring, and system capacities are naturally bounded, rendering classical stress–strength models inadequate for capturing complex reliability behaviour. Despite extensive developments in parametric reliability modeling, flexible frameworks capable of jointly accommodating multicomponent system structures, bounded distributions, and progressive censoring remain limited. This study develops a comprehensive inferential framework for multicomponent stress–strength reliability under progressive Type-II censoring based on the Unit-Gamma Gompertz–Weibull distribution. The proposed model provides substantial flexibility in modeling diverse density and hazard-rate shapes while preserving analytical tractability for reliability formulation. We derive explicit expressions for key reliability measures and construct a unified estimation strategy that integrates maximum likelihood estimation, approximate likelihood methods, a Monte Carlo expectation–maximization algorithm, and Bayesian inference via Lindley’s approximation and Metropolis–Hastings sampling. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate stable finite-sample performance across varying censoring intensities and system configurations. Application to hydrological capacity data from the Shasta reservoir highlights improved goodness-of-fit and more robust reliability estimation compared with competing bounded distributions. Sensitivity and influence analyses further confirm the stability of the proposed framework. Overall, the methodology offers a flexible and practically implementable tool for stress–strength reliability analysis in multicomponent systems with censored and bounded observations.Keywords: Unit-Gamma Gompertz–Weibull distribution, Progressive Type-II censoring, Stress–strength reliability, Maximum likelihood estimation, Monte Carlo expectation maximization algorithm, Bayesian inference, Sensitivity analysisSubject terms: Hydrology, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In reliability analysis, a fundamental quantity of interest is the stress–strength reliability parameter , which represents the probability that a randomly selected unit can withstand the operational stress imposed on it. Here, X denotes the component strength, and Y represents the stress applied to the system. A unit is said to function successfully whenever its strength exceeds the experienced stress. This concept naturally arises in numerous real-world applications such as mechanical engineering, environmental modeling, biomedical studies, and industrial quality control. For instance, in structural engineering, X may represent the fire resistance or load-bearing capacity of a component, while Y denotes the severity of the load or shock to which it is subjected.

Extensive research has been conducted on the estimation of the stress–strength reliability under a wide range of lifetime models (see Saini et al.47, Zhu4,50, Hassan and Abdelghaffar13, Hassan and Morgan14, Ma et al.25, Pakdaman et al.37, Pakdaman et al.36, Pakdaman and Noughabi34, Pakdaman and Alizadeh Noughabi33, Pakdaman and Shekari35, Pakdaman et al.1,31,32, Pakdaman and Ahmadi29, and Pakdaman and Ahmadi30).

A system composed of multiple components is referred to as a multicomponent system. Suppose that the system contains k independent and identical strength components that operate under a common external stress Y. The system is said to function successfully if at least s out of the k components () continue to operate. Such a system is known as an s-out-of-k system. This configuration has extensive applications in areas including mechanical systems, industrial design, and communication networks. For example, a V8 engine can function if at least four of its eight cylinders are operating, corresponding to a 4-out-of-8 system. Similarly, a data display subsystem requiring three operational screens out of five available units can be modeled by a 3-out-of-5 system46. In structural engineering, multicomponent systems appear naturally; for instance, suspension bridges rely on multiple parallel cables, and the structure remains safe as long as a sufficient number of cable pairs withstand stresses induced by wind, heavy traffic, corrosion, and environmental degradation24.

Several authors have investigated the estimation of the multicomponent stress–strength reliability measure under a wide range of distributional assumptions for the stress and strength variables. General theoretical foundations of stress–strength models and their extensions were comprehensively discussed by Kotz et al.21, providing a unified framework for subsequent methodological developments. Early parametric studies primarily focused on classical lifetime distributions, including the Weibull model examined by McCool28 and Kundu and Gupta23, as well as the generalized exponential distribution studied by Kundu and Gupta22, Raqab et al.45, and Rao38. Alternative distributional forms have also been considered, such as the generalized inverted exponential distribution39,48, the Rayleigh distribution40, the inverse exponential distribution41, and the inverse Rayleigh distribution44. More flexible models include the Burr type XII distribution investigated by Rao et al.43, the generalized Rayleigh distribution proposed by Rao42, and the Kumaraswamy distribution studied by Dey et al.8,9,10. Bivariate extensions of stress–strength models have also received considerable attention, most notably the bivariate Kumaraswamy distribution analyzed by Kizilaslan and Nadar15

While the majority of these studies focus on inference based on complete samples, relatively limited attention has been devoted to multicomponent stress–strength reliability under censoring schemes, despite their importance in practical life-testing experiments. Progressively Type-II censored samples were considered by Kohansal16 in the context of the Kumaraswamy distribution, whereas Balakrishnan and Cramer5 provided a comprehensive treatment of progressive censoring methods and their applications in reliability analysis. Recent contributions have further expanded this line of research. For instance, Saini et al.47 developed inference procedures for multicomponent stress–strength models under progressive censoring based on the exponentiated Pareto distribution. Ahmad and El-Awady2 investigated reliability analysis under progressive first-failure censoring using the exponentiated half-logistic distribution, while Hassan and Abd Elghaffar12 proposed E-Bayesian and hierarchical Bayesian approaches under partially accelerated life testing. Moreover, Kohansal et al.18 studied multicomponent stress–strength systems with heterogeneous Lomax-distributed components under progressive censoring, and Yadav et al.49,50,51 analyzed stress–strength reliability for the xgamma distribution under generalized progressive hybrid censoring. Despite these advances, flexible stress–strength models capable of jointly accommodating multicomponent system structures and progressive Type-II censoring remain relatively scarce, thereby motivating the present study.

Progressive Type-II censoring arises naturally when units may be removed from a test before failure due to time limitations, cost reduction, or operational constraints. In a progressive Type-II censoring scheme, n units are placed on a life test, and only failure times are fully observed. At the ith observed failure time, surviving units are randomly withdrawn from the experiment. Letting , the ordered failure times are known as progressive Type-II censored order statistics. This censoring scheme includes Type-II censoring as a special case when and . An extensive review of progressive Type-II censoring can be found in Balakrishnan and Cramer6.

Recently, statistical models defined on the unit interval (0, 1) have attracted substantial interest because many types of observations are naturally bounded. Data expressed as proportions, percentages, probabilities, or normalized indices frequently occur in numerous scientific areas such as medicine, biology, hydrology, environmental studies, and finance. In these contexts, measurements are inherently restricted to a finite range due to physical, biological, or operational constraints. For instance, standardized lifetime measures, reliability scores, and capacity indicators cannot increase without bound and therefore take values within a limited domain. Distributions defined on (0, 1), commonly referred to as unit distributions, provide an appropriate framework for analyzing such bounded variables. Various unit models have been developed for this purpose, including the unit Weibull distribution26,27, the unit Burr XII distribution20, the unit inverse Gaussian distribution11, and the unit generalized half‑normal distribution19. A common strategy for constructing these models is to apply transformations that map positive random variables into the unit interval, such as or .

Among the recently proposed models, the unit gamma Gompertz (UGG) distribution introduced by Bantan et al.7 provides a flexible framework for modeling data on (0, 1). This distribution arises by transforming a gamma Gompertz random variable through and is capable of accommodating a variety of density shapes and hazard rate patterns. Such flexibility makes it particularly useful for describing bounded lifetime‑type measurements. Motivated by these features, we adopt the UGG distribution as a generator and combine it with a baseline distribution to construct the proposed UGG– class of models. The resulting family retains the bounded support inherited from the unit distribution while offering enhanced flexibility for capturing different data behaviors encountered in practical applications. Moreover, since the transformation maps into , the resulting model naturally preserves the bounded support, which provides a theoretical basis for the boundedness property of the proposed distribution.

Although several distributions defined on the unit interval are available in the literature, such as the Beta and Kumaraswamy distributions, these models may sometimes exhibit limited flexibility in capturing complex shapes of density and hazard-related functions encountered in reliability data. In particular, their parametric structures may restrict the variety of hazard rate behaviors that can arise in stress–strength contexts. Similarly, other bounded models such as the unit generalized half-normal distribution provide useful alternatives, but their shape flexibility may still be insufficient when modeling highly heterogeneous lifetime-type data observed in reliability studies.

In reliability and stress–strength modeling, especially for multicomponent systems, it is important to employ distributions that can accommodate a wide variety of density and hazard rate shapes. Generator-based constructions provide an effective mechanism for enhancing the flexibility of baseline models. In this context, the UGG generator is particularly appealing because it introduces additional shape parameters, allowing the resulting distribution to exhibit diverse density and hazard rate behaviors compared with classical bounded models such as the Beta and Kumaraswamy distributions. The additional flexibility introduced by the UGG generator is particularly advantageous when modeling stress–strength reliability measures that depend on the hazard rate structure of the underlying distributions.

Motivated by these considerations, we combine the UGG generator with the Weibull baseline distribution to construct the proposed UGG–Weibull model. The Weibull distribution is one of the most widely used models in reliability analysis due to its ability to represent increasing, decreasing, and constant hazard rate structures. By integrating the flexibility of the UGG generator with the well-established reliability properties of the Weibull distribution, the proposed model provides a flexible framework for modeling bounded lifetime-type variables arising in multicomponent stress–strength reliability systems.

The UGG– family enables diverse shapes for the density, survival, and hazard rate functions and has been shown to perform well in modeling bounded environmental and hydrological capacity measurements. In particular, choosing a Weibull baseline leads to the UGG–Weibull distribution, which we adopt in this study due to its desirable flexibility and interpretability.

To accommodate different physical interpretations of the stress–strength mechanism, two complementary modeling scenarios are considered. In the first scenario, the strength and stress variables are assumed to share a common Weibull baseline distribution, reflecting a similar underlying environmental or operational mechanism, while the UGG generator parameters are allowed to differ between the two components in order to capture potential asymmetry in their distributional shapes. In the second scenario, the UGG generator parameters are taken to be common for both strength and stress, whereas distinct Weibull baseline distributions are specified for each component, allowing for heterogeneous scale and tail behaviors arising from differing loading or degradation processes.

This double progressive censoring framework naturally arises in reliability experiments where both stress and strength units are tested simultaneously, yet may be removed at different stages of the experiment according to preassigned censoring schemes. The resulting likelihood functions are analytically intractable under both modeling scenarios, and closed-form expressions for the multicomponent reliability measure are unavailable. These challenges motivate the development of efficient computational inference procedures for parameter estimation and stress–strength reliability assessment under progressive Type-II censoring.

In this paper, we provide a comprehensive classical and Bayesian inferential framework for the multicomponent stress–strength reliability under the UGG–Weibull model based on progressively Type-II censored samples. Maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is derived, and approximate MLE (AMLE) and Monte Carlo Expectation Maximization (MCEM) algorithm are developed to overcome the absence of closed-form expressions. Bayesian inference is conducted through Lindley’s approximation and a Metropolis–Hastings (M–H) sampler. A Monte Carlo simulation study assesses the performance of the proposed methods under various censoring schemes. A real hydrological dataset from the Shasta reservoir is analyzed to demonstrate the practical relevance of the model. Moreover, sensitivity and influence diagnostic analyses are performed to evaluate the robustness of the estimates.

Prior specification

In Bayesian analysis, an appropriate choice of prior distributions is required for the unknown model parameters. In the present study, we assume prior independence among all parameters. Since the parameters of the proposed UGG–Weibull model are strictly positive, prior distributions supported on are required. For this reason, Gamma priors are assigned to most parameters because of their flexibility and their widespread use as priors for positive shape and scale parameters in reliability and lifetime models. Depending on the choice of hyperparameters, the Gamma distribution can represent weakly informative prior beliefs while preserving computational convenience in posterior inference. For the parameter , an inverse-gamma prior is adopted. The inverse-gamma distribution is also defined on and is commonly used as a prior for positive scale-type parameters in Bayesian modeling. This choice ensures compatibility with the parameter space while maintaining analytical tractability in the Bayesian estimation procedure. In this study, Gamma priors with relatively small hyperparameters are employed in order to reflect weak prior information and allow the data to primarily drive the posterior inference. Moreover, the Gamma distribution provides analytical convenience in Bayesian computation and has been extensively used in Bayesian reliability analysis and lifetime modeling. Therefore, it represents a natural and practical choice for the prior specification in the proposed model.

Simulation study

At a conceptual level, the simulation study is structured to disentangle the effects of distributional complexity and progressive Type-II censoring on multicomponent stress–strength reliability estimation. For each modelling scenario (Case 1 and Case 2), we first specify representative parameter classes designed to induce distinct hazard-rate shapes, followed by a systematic comparison of censoring schemes that vary the timing and severity of unit removals. Estimation accuracy is then evaluated using bias and mean squared error (MSE), while uncertainty quantification is assessed through credible interval length, coverage probability (CP), and MCMC acceptance behaviour. This structure allows each numerical component of the study to be interpreted in direct relation to modelling assumptions and censoring design.

Prior distributions (noninformative vs informative)

We consider two prior regimes: a noninformative prior (Prior 1) and an informative prior (Prior 2). In both regimes, we assume prior independence across parameters and employ gamma/inverse-gamma families to respect positivity.

Prior 1 (noninformative). We set all hyperparameters to zero in the gamma-type kernels, resulting in a flat/Jeffreys-like noninformative behaviour on the positive domain (implemented numerically with small stabilizing constants in the code to avoid undefined normalizing constants):

Prior 2 (informative). For the informative regime, hyperparameters are selected so that prior means match the corresponding true values in each parameter class, while keeping moderate prior variances (so the likelihood can still dominate for moderate sample sizes). Concretely, for each positive parameter we adopt a gamma prior with mean m and variance v, and for an inverse-gamma prior with matched mean/variance when needed. We use relative prior dispersion of about – coefficient of variation for generator parameters and – for baseline parameters. This yields informative yet moderately concentrated priors, which is appropriate for reliability settings where engineering knowledge exists but remains imprecise.

MLE, AMLE and numerical stability: Across all classes A–D and CSs, the MLE and AMLE columns in Tables 18, 19, 20, 21 are extremely close in both bias and MSE. This indicates that, under the present Case 2 likelihood, AMLE does not substantially alter the optimisation landscape relative to full numerical MLE, even under severe progressive censoring. Importantly, all likelihood-based estimators remain numerically stable, with finite bias and MSE throughout, confirming that the Case 2 likelihood formulation is well behaved despite the flexibility of the UGG generator and the presence of heavy early or late removals.

MCEM under progressive censoring: It should be noted that, in the present setting, the reliability functional is a highly non-linear mapping of the model parameters and depends critically on tail behaviour of the stress and strength distributions. Under progressive Type-II censoring, this non-linearity amplifies Monte Carlo variability introduced in the E-step of MCEM algorithms. As a result, even moderate Monte Carlo error in the imputed complete-data likelihood can propagate into substantial bias and variance inflation for , particularly when censoring is severe. A striking and systematic pattern across all classes is the inferior performance of the MCEM estimator in the considered configuration. For both and especially , MCEM exhibits noticeably larger biases and MSEs than its likelihood-based and Bayesian competitors, with the discrepancy being most pronounced in Classes A, C and D. This behaviour is consistent with the stress–strength setting under progressive censoring: when the Monte Carlo E-step is based on a small number of truncated completions, the induced Monte Carlo noise propagates nonlinearly into the reliability functional, leading to systematic overestimation and inflated variance. These results suggest that MCEM requires substantially larger Monte Carlo budgets to be competitive for Case 2 reliability estimation.

Bayesian point estimation and prior effects: The M–H estimators display a clear dependence on prior specification. Under the diffuse Prior 1, the Bayesian estimates are generally close to the MLE, as expected when the likelihood dominates. By contrast, the informative Prior 2 frequently reduces MSE and, in many settings, also reduces absolute bias, particularly for and in CSs that severely weaken the likelihood. The gains from Prior 2 are most evident in Classes A–C, whereas in Class D the effect is more heterogeneous, reflecting stronger sensitivity of the posterior to shrinkage when the underlying hazard-rate structure is more challenging. Overall, these results confirm the stabilising role of informative Gamma and inverse-Gamma priors for the generator and baseline parameters in Case 2.

Relative difficulty of versus : Across all parameter classes and censoring designs, estimation of is uniformly more difficult than that of . This is evidenced by systematically larger MSEs and biases in Tables 18, 19, 20, 21. From a structural perspective, depends more heavily on the joint tail and shape behaviour of the strength distribution relative to stress, and therefore it amplifies uncertainty in , especially under progressive censoring.

The results offer a clear narrative on the role of prior information in complex, censored data settings. Under the diffuse Prior 1, the empirical CPs are generally slightly below the nominal 95% level, particularly in scenarios with more aggressive censoring (e.g., designs R3/S3 and R3/S6). This under-coverage suggests that when the likelihood is weakened by censoring, the posterior distribution does not fully capture the true parameter uncertainty, leading to intervals that are located incorrectly and are often too narrow relative to the true sampling distribution.

A marked improvement is observed with the informative Prior 2. Across all parameter classes and CSs, the CPs are substantially closer to the nominal 95% level, highlighting the prior’s crucial role in regularising the posterior. More importantly, this improvement in coverage is achieved alongside a consistent reduction in the ACL. This represents a desirable outcome: the informative prior not only enhances the reliability of the uncertainty statements but also increases their precision. This indicates that the prior is not merely shrinking the posterior arbitrarily, but helping regularize the posterior towards more stable regions of the parameter space, thus yielding more accurate and efficient inferences. The MCMC ARs, while indicating a functional sampler, are in the 20–28% range, suggesting that further MCMC tuning, such as adaptive proposals, could enhance posterior exploration efficiency.

Model choice, estimation on complete data, and goodness-of-fit

Reservoir capacities are bounded and naturally supported on (0, 1) after scaling, motivating a bounded distributional family capable of representing diverse tail and hazard-rate behaviours. Accordingly, we adopt the Unit-Gamma Gompertz–Weibull (UGG–Weibull) model with a Weibull baseline generator. This choice provides a flexible yet interpretable mechanism for capturing asymmetry and tail behaviour that can differ between the stress and strength components.

Summary and perspectives

This paper developed a comprehensive inferential framework for multicomponent stress–strength reliability in the presence of progressive Type-II censoring, assuming that both stress and strength variables follow the flexible UGG–Weibull distribution. The proposed model provides a bounded and highly adaptable distributional structure, making it particularly suitable for reliability studies involving environmental, hydrological, and capacity-type observations where classical lifetime models may fail to capture complex hazard-rate behaviours.

Closed-form expressions for key distributional properties were established, and the multicomponent reliability measure for s-out-of-k systems was formulated under two complementary modelling scenarios. Since the resulting likelihood functions are analytically intractable under progressive censoring, several computationally efficient inferential approaches were investigated, including MLE, approximate likelihood-based procedures, MCEM algorithms, and Bayesian inference through Lindley’s approximation and M–H sampling.

Extensive Monte Carlo simulations demonstrated that the proposed estimators remain numerically stable across a wide range of CSs and parameter configurations. A key finding from the numerical results is the sensitivity of to the interplay between parameters: while the UGG generator parameters dictate the overall hazard rate curvature and tail flexibility, the Weibull baseline parameters significantly modulate the effective stress–strength gap. Our analysis of different parameter classes (A–D) revealed that configurations with challenging hazard structures (e.g., Class D) lead to higher MSE, emphasizing that reliability estimation in multicomponent systems is highly dependent on the joint tail behaviour induced by these parameters. In particular, Bayesian methods exhibited clear advantages in terms of reduced MSE and improved uncertainty quantification, especially under severe censoring patterns where likelihood information becomes limited and prior regularisation of the generator parameters helps stabilise the posterior distribution.

The practical relevance of the methodology was further illustrated through the analysis of a real hydrological dataset from the Shasta reservoir. The numerical results for the real data confirmed that the UGG–Weibull parameters successfully captured the distinct asymmetry and bounded support of reservoir capacities, providing a superior empirical fit compared to competing models. The UGG–Weibull model delivered stable and interpretable stress–strength reliability estimates, and sensitivity diagnostics confirmed that the final conclusions remain robust to small fluctuations in the parameter estimates.

Overall, the proposed framework offers a flexible and powerful tool for reliability assessment in multicomponent systems under progressive censoring. Future research may extend these results to dependent stress–strength structures, accelerated life-testing environments, or more general bounded generator families, thereby broadening the applicability of the methodology to modern reliability engineering and applied risk analysis.

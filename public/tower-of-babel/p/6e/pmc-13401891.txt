Haematological Recovery After Discontinuation of Co-Trimoxazole Prophylaxis Among People Living with HIV at an Urban Centre in Uganda: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Keneth Kananura, Barbara Castelnouvo, Joseph Baruch Baluku, Harriet Mayanja-Kizza, Christine Sekaggya-Wiltshire
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCo-trimoxazole (CTX) prophylaxis among people living with HIV (PLWH) significantly reduces HIV-related morbidity and mortality due to bacterial infections and malaria, especially those with advanced HIV disease. CTX prophylaxis is associated with cytopenias, particularly anemia and neutropenia. However, the proportion and factors associated with hematological recovery (resolution of cytopenias) after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis have not been fully investigated.Participants and MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted using electronic medical records of PLWH with anemia (hemoglobin [Hb]<11g/dl for females and Hb<12g/dl for males) and/or neutropenia (neutrophil count <1×103/µL) before discontinuing CTX prophylaxis, and their CBC reports were reviewed every 3 months after CTX discontinuation. Haematological recovery was defined as Hb recovery as an increase in Hb by >2g/dl or Hb improvement to ≥ 12 g/dl (males) and ≥ 11 g/dl (females), and neutrophil count recovery as an increase in neutrophil counts by ≥ 0.5×103/µL in absolute values or neutrophil count ≥ 1×103/µL after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis. Multivariable logistic regression models were used to determine factors associated with haematological recovery.ResultsThe median (IQR) age of the participants was 46 (39–52) years, and the majority were females (68.1%). Of 263 study participants, the proportions of Hb recovery and neutrophil count recovery were 42.9% and 81.1%, respectively. Overweight participants were less likely to experience Hb recovery (AOR=0.33, CI: 0.12–0.93), and males were less likely to have neutrophil count recovery (AOR=0.28, CI: 0.10–0.92).ConclusionA significant proportion of PLWH who had cytopenias before discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis experienced haematological recovery after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis. Therefore, discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis may be considered among PLWH during the management of persistent anemia and/or neutropenia.Keywords: anemia, haematological recovery, HIV, neutropenia, people living with HIV

Introduction

Primary cotrimoxazole (CTX) preventive treatment has been part of the minimum care package for people living with HIV (PLWH) in Africa since 2001. This practice was supported by clinical trials, which demonstrated that CTX prophylaxis significantly reduces HIV-related mortality, bacterial infections, malaria, and hospital admissions among antiretroviral naïve patients with advanced HIV disease.1,2 CTX prophylaxis is still being given to newly diagnosed PLWH for at least one year regardless of the baseline CD4 counts, children aged 15 years and below, and those with new suspected or confirmed WHO HIV stage 3 or 4 events in Uganda.3

CTX is associated with haematological toxicity resulting in cytopenia, especially anemia and neutropenia, and other adverse effects such as allergic reactions, photosensitivity, and interstitial nephritis.4,5 The main mechanism for hematopoietic toxicity of CTX is the reduction in tetrahydrofolate synthesis and subsequently impaired DNA synthesis. Other mechanisms include immune-mediated destruction of blood cells and idiosyncratic toxic effects on leucocytes.6–9

Prevalence of cytopenias among PLWH in Africa varies geographically, with anemia and neutropenia being the most prevalent at 1.3–95% and 10–50%, respectively.10–12 In Uganda, anemia (1–68%) and leucopenia (10–42%) are more common among PLWH.13,14

Previous studies in sub-Saharan Africa showed that CTX use was associated with an increased risk of anemia and neutropenia among PLWH taking CTX prophylaxis, which may, in turn, lead to increased morbidity and mortality.10,11 Thrombocytopenia due to CTX occurs rarely and usually in a less severe form (platelet counts ≥100×103/µL).8,12

A randomized controlled trial conducted in Uganda by Anywaine et al (2018) showed that about 29% of PLWH taking CTX prophylaxis experienced grade 3 or 4 hematological events of neutropenia and anemia.13 Persistent anemia and neutropenia among PLWH have been associated with HIV progression to AIDS, increased hospitalization and mortality, and secondary bacterial and fungal infections due to neutropenia.14–16

There is limited data about hematological resolution of cytopenias after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis due to toxicity.

Therefore, this study aimed to determine the proportion of haematological recovery and factors associated with haematological recovery among PLWHIV after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis at the Infectious Diseases Institute (IDI) in Kampala, Uganda.

Materials and Methods

Study Design, Setting, and Population

A retrospective cohort study was conducted to assess the proportion of PLWH with anemia and neutropenia who experienced haematological recovery after CTX discontinuation at the IDI, an urban HIV care clinic, in Kampala, Uganda.

IDI is an integral part of the College of Health Sciences, Makerere University, located within Mulago Hospital Complex, in Kampala Capital City. It provides comprehensive HIV care, including diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring for a catchment population of about 1.5 million people. Currently, it provides services to over 4000 PLWH. Stable patients are followed up every 3–6 months and are reviewed by doctors, while those with complications are reviewed more frequently. Response to antiretroviral therapy (ART) is monitored by annual viral load testing, while complete blood counts (CBC) are performed annually and regularly for patients who present with clinical concerns. Data for the patients, including demographics, clinical information, laboratory results, and drug prescriptions, are entered directly by the providers into an electronic database called Integrated Clinic Enterprise Application (ICEA), while laboratory results are exported into ICEA from the IDI Core laboratory database.17

We included medical records of adult PLWH (≥18 years) who were on CTX for at least 6 months, discontinued CTX prophylaxis, had anemia and/or neutropenia, and had at least one laboratory result of hemoglobin (Hb) and neutrophil count done at least one month after discontinuation from August 2015 to March 2020.

Anemia was defined as an Hb level of < 12g/dl (males) and <11g/dl (females), and neutropenia was defined as neutrophil counts < 1.0×103 cells/µL.

Participants with haematological disorders like sickle cell anemia were excluded from the study.

Data Collection

Study participants’ records were sampled consecutively from the year 2015 up to March 2020. The sample size was calculated using a proportion of 76.4% derived from a study done at Beijing Ditan Hospital in China among HIV-infected patients with cytopenias who recovered after 12 months of ART.18 A two-sided 95.0% confidence interval for a single proportion using the large sample normal approximation would extend 0.050 from the observed proportion if 277 participants were included in the study. In our study, we could include 263 participants who had complete medical records. Data were extracted from an electronic database using a data abstraction form for demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics. Variables included age, sex, BMI, WHO staging (stages I through IV, from mild disease to severe/advanced HIV disease),19 ART regimen, duration of CTX before discontinuation, duration of ART, and TB treatment during the study period. BMI was categorized as underweight (BMI <18.5kg/m2) or overweight and obese (BMI>24.9kg/m2). CBC reports done before and after discontinuation of CTX were reviewed to assess the changes in Hb and neutrophil counts. Complete blood count reports were reviewed every 3 months after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis. The data used in this study was accessed on 11th June 2022. Only the corresponding author had access to the information that could identify individual participants during or after data collection.

Study Outcomes

Haematologic recovery was categorized into Hb recovery and neutrophil count recovery. Hb recovery was defined as an increase in the Hb by >2g/dl in absolute values or Hb improvement to ≥ 12 g/dl (males) and ≥ 11 g/dl (females) after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis. Neutrophil count recovery was defined as an increase in neutrophil counts by ≥ 0.5×103/µL in absolute values or neutrophil count ≥ 1×103/µL after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using STATA version 16.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas USA).

Continuous data are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), while categorical data are presented by percentages. The differences between the two groups (haematological recovery vs non-recovery) were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U-test for continuous variables and Pearson’s Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for proportions. Logistic regression analysis was used to determine the factors associated with haematological recovery. Demographic variables (sex, age, BMI), and clinical/laboratory characteristics (WHO stage, baseline CD4, duration on ART, duration on CTX, ART regimen, and TB drugs) were included in the final logistic regression model based on variables with p<0.2 at univariate analysis or biological plausibility. A p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 10,485 electronic medical records of PLWH who were taking CTX prophylaxis from August 2015 to March 2020 were retrieved and screened for eligibility. A total of 10,222 were excluded due to various reasons, and the most frequent reason for exclusion was participants who had no Hb and neutrophil count results before and after CTX discontinuation (n=8765, 85.7%). Other reasons for exclusion were participants with no anemia or neutropenia before CTX discontinuation (n=1368, 13.4%) and those without Hb and neutrophil results after CTX discontinuation (n=78, 0.7%). Demographic and Clinical Characteristics of the Study Participants

A total of 263 participants were included in this study, of whom 173 (65.8%) had anemia only, 78 (29.6%) had neutropenia only, and 12 (4.6%) had both anemia and neutropenia before discontinuation of CTX. The median age of all participants was 46 years (IQR: 39–52 years), females constituted the majority of the participants (68.1%), and 168 (63.9%) participants were in the WHO III and IV stages. The median timing of CBC reports after CTX discontinuation was 1.2 years (IQR: 0.9–1.6 years). The median duration of CTX prophylaxis before discontinuation was 3.5 years (IQR: 1.7–3.8 years) as shown in Table 1 below. Proportions of Hematological Recovery After Discontinuation of CTX Prophylaxis

After discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis, 79 (42.9%) of 184 participants with anemia experienced Hb recovery and 73 (81.1%) of 90 participants with neutropenia experienced neutrophil count recovery.

Factors Associated with Haematological Recovery After Discontinuation of CTX Prophylaxis

Participants who were overweight were less likely to experience Hb recovery (AOR=0.33, 95% CI: 0.12–0.93, p-value=0.035) while males were less likely to experience neutrophil recovery compared to females (AOR=0.28, 95% CI: 0.10–0.92, p-value=0.037). Other factors like duration on CTX, ART regimen and WHO HIV stage were not statistically significant as shown in Tables 2 and 3.

Discussion

In this study, we found that discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis results in haematological recovery among 42.9% of PLWH with anemia and 81.1% of those with neutropenia.

We also found that overweight participants were less likely to attain Hb recovery, and males were less likely to attain neutrophil recovery after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis.

Haematological recovery among PLWH with anemia and neutropenia is expected after discontinuation of CTX due to the withdrawal of the hematotoxic effects of CTX, mainly due to the inhibition of tetrahydrofolate formation needed for the synthesis of DNA.20,21 This was demonstrated by a study conducted in Cote d’Ivoire, however in this study, participants were on both AZT-based ART and CTX. The study found that the neutrophil count improved by one month after discontinuation of CTX among participants who had persistent neutropenia.4

The relatively lower proportion of Hb recovery compared to the neutrophil recovery observed in our study may be explained by the fact that Hb levels are more likely to be affected by other factors in our settings, such as poor nutrition, co-infections with soil-transmitted helminths, malaria, and undiagnosed opportunistic infections. Among females, low Hb levels may be due to menstruation, pregnancy, and childbirth-related blood loss.22–24

We found that two factors were associated with poor haematological recovery after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis. Males were less likely to have neutrophil recovery while those who were overweight were less likely to experience hemoglobin recovery.

A study conducted in China revealed that female participants were more likely to have persistent cytopenias,18 and this contrasts with our findings. This may be due to variations in the study populations with respect to sociodemographic factors, HIV clinical stage, and health-seeking behaviour. Some studies have hypothesized that males are probably less likely to recover from neutropenia due to low levels of estradiol and progesterone hormone levels compared to females. These hormones have been shown to delay apoptosis of neutrophils through reduced activity of caspases 3 and 9.25 Although we did not collect data on alcohol use, males are also more likely to abuse alcohol, which also may lead to hematological disorders.26,27 Alcohol is known to have cytotoxic effects on the hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells and to disrupt the hematopoietic niche microenvironment, which results in reduced hematopoiesis.28–30 Chronic alcohol consumption causes significant changes in hormonal and growth factor homeostasis in the body, which promotes adipocyte accumulation in the bone marrow, hence reducing hematopoiesis.28,29

Overweight participants were less likely to experience Hb recovery. This may be explained by the fact that overweight (pre-obesity) and obesity are associated with states of low levels of chronic inflammation driven by cytokines, like interleukin-1, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, among others. This chronic inflammatory state increases hepcidin expression, which subsequently impairs iron absorption.31,32 Inflammatory cytokines can also impair erythropoiesis and hematopoiesis generally.33 Adipose tissue has been shown to express hepcidin itself, which further leads to iron trapping, hence iron deficiency.31

We did not find other clinical factors associated with either Hb recovery or neutrophil count recovery, and this was contrary to findings from other previous studies that demonstrated that AZT-based regimens, baseline CD4<200cells/µL, and WHO HIV stages III/IV, adversely affect the resolution of cytopenias after discontinuation of CTX.4,10,18,34,35 The observed differences could be explained by the fact that patients in our study had been on ART for a long duration (at least 5 years, with a median duration on ART of about 9 years) and were stable on treatment with no features of advanced HIV disease at the time of discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis.

This may also explain why being on AZT, which may induce myelotoxicity, was not associated with either Hb or neutrophil recovery in this study. Previous studies demonstrated that the myelotoxicity of AZT, commonly manifested by anemia, occurs in the early phases of therapy (within the first 6 months), and after 24 months, its hematological adverse events decline.36,37

The strength of this study lies in its longitudinal design, whereby a cohort of PLWH with anemia and/or neutropenia before discontinuing CTX prophylaxis was followed up for the improvement of the cytopenias after discontinuation of CTX prophylaxis, and our study is the first one to assess hematological recovery among PLWH who already had anemia and/or neutropenia before stopping CTX as compared to previous studies done in sub-Saharan Africa.

There are some limitations to our study. First, there is a potential inherent bias in a retrospective observational study, such as missing data, several patient records were excluded because they had no complete blood count reports before and after stopping CTX prophylaxis, which could have introduced selection bias as well. However, data for primary outcomes were available for all the included persons, and those without records for Hb and neutrophils were less likely to have cytopenias because patients are closely followed up at this HIV care clinic. Second, participants were derived from a single, urban centre of excellence for HIV care in Uganda hence, these findings may not be generalizable to all other HIV care centres, especially in rural Uganda, where other factors like malnutrition may occur more often. Thirdly, we were unable to evaluate for factors like nutritional status, helminth infections, malaria, alcohol use and other over-the-counter drugs that are potential cofounders.

Conclusion

Among PLWH with anaemia and/or neutropenia who had laboratory monitoring after CTX discontinuation, haematological recovery was observed, particularly for neutropenia. These findings support consideration of CTX prophylaxis discontinuation as one way of managing persistent cytopenias in stable ART-treated patients after assessing and ruling out other possible causes of anemia and neutropenia. Future prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings and guide clinical practice.

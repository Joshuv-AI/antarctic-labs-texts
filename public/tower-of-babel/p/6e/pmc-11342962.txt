Author response to: Comment on: Total neoadjuvant therapy versus standard neoadjuvant treatment strategies for the management of locally advanced rectal cancer: network meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials
Authors: Mark Donnelly, Odhrán K Ryan, Éanna J Ryan, Des C Winter
Published: 2024

Dear Editor

We welcome the insightful comments from Pedrazzani et al. with regard to our original article1, and congratulate their group on their own recent publication2.

Although the Stockholm III trial3 did meet our predefined inclusion criteria, we have included a repeat of the analysis excluding this trial, and the new data would not have altered our results or conclusions (Appendix S1). We acknowledge that in the interim since our final search date (14 December 2022) that new RCTs of total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) and other novel strategies for locally advanced rectal cancer have been published, and that these new data are invaluable to any contemporary evaluation of neoadjuvant management strategies.4,5 We also eagerly await the results of ongoing RCTs (NCT02843191, NCT03177382, NCT04518280, NCT05673772, KCT0007169).

We pooled TNT regimens to determine whether this rapidly expanding, and increasingly promising, treatment modality delivered on its aim of reducing distant failure rates and thereby improving survival in locally advanced rectal cancer by giving all planned chemotherapy upfront. We acknowledge that the schedule (induction or consolidation) and components (short-course radiotherapy or long-course chemoradiation, as well as the chemotherapy regimens) may affect outcomes further. We conducted subgroup analysis to identify the relative merits and flaws of TNT regimens, and, as stated in our conclusion, insist that the decision to provide a particular TNT strategy should be a personalized, risk adaptive approach for each individual patient based on this.

The CINeMA protocol we applied at the time of our analysis is available in Appendix S2 for assessment of heterogeneity for all studies for dichotomous outcomes (the framework did not and still does not support time-to-event data). Statistical analysis was performed as described in our methods section, and statistical significance was considered as two-sided P < 0.050. We do not believe our conclusion is overly categorical in nature; indeed, we advised caution in interpretation of results, while addressing the unmet need for further research to elucidate the potential role of TNT in rectal cancer. Although our analysis showed the benefits of induction and consolidation TNT, we emphasized that ‘patient selection may be important in the choice of treatment strategy’ owing to the differences in TNT regimens.

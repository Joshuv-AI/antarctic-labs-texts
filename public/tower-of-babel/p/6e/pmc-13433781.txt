A properties prediction strategy of aluminum anode foils through machine learning based on feature selection and stacking learning models
Authors: Sining Pan, Yue Zhang, Shijun Luo, Shiquan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid development of new energy vehicles, the global demand for aluminum anode foils (AAF) increases continuously. In order to improve the stability and accuracy of properties prediction for AAF, a machine learning-based property prediction strategy, integrating feature selection and a stacking ensemble model, is proposed in this study. The Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) and multicollinearity test (MT) are utilized to pinpoint the most relevant and representative features from the original datasets with 80 process parameters. The data are processed using Box-Cox transformation (BCT) to improve the normality. The stacking integrated learning model is constructed, and the Optuna algorithm is used to determine the hyperparameters of each model with the purpose of enhancing the prediction performance. The results demonstrate that the proposed model exhibits superior accuracy and stability in predicting the AAF properties in comparison to 7 baseline machine learning models. X61 (repair 2 anodizing voltage) is a key parameter of process regulation that has the strongest statistical correlation with AAF properties variation. The stepwise voltage strategy in the formation process can balance the trade-off between withstand voltage and specific capacitance. The proposed model can help to predict the two core properties of aluminum anode foils, thereby revealing the intrinsic correlations among process parameters, microstructures, and properties.Keywords: Properties prediction, Aluminum anode foils, Machine learning, Feature selection, Stacking learning algorithmSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The aluminum anode foils (AAF) are utilized extensively in capacitors, aluminum anode technology, and lithium-ion batteries due to their high specific surface area, stable anodic oxide film insulation, and excellent electrochemical corrosion resistance1. With the rapid development of global new energy vehicles, there is a continuous increase in the global demand for AAF2. AAF is produced by subjecting high-purity aluminum foils to corrosion expansion and anodizing3, the manufacturing process of AAF contains 80 full-process manufacturing parameters with strong non-linear coupling characteristics.4,5. In addition, with the continuous generation of new technologies and processes, the production process and properties requirements of AAF are in a constant state of flux, exacerbating the instability of properties control of AAF6. Therefore, predicting the properties of AAF accurately and reliably is crucial for optimizing processing technology and enhancing control over the processing system.

In existing research, prediction models include theoretical model, statistical model, machine learning model and hybrid model7–10. The hybrid model is defined as a whole process machine learning framework integrating data preprocessing, feature selection, stacking integrated prediction model and hyper parameter optimization algorithm. The hybrid model is a hot topic in current research and performs well in the prediction effect11–14. In recent years, many researchers use stacked or hybrid model architecture to integrate the advantages of different models to predict performance. Hou et al.15 proposed a stacked ensemble learning model to predict the duration of power failure during typhoon disasters. The model integrates Extremely Randomized Trees (ET), XGBoost (XGB), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LGBM), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Regression (GBR), and Decision Tree (DT) as the base learner and GBR as the meta learner, integrating the advantages of various accurate machine learning algorithms. Vakharia et al.16 developed three deep learning models (DL) using a stacking model to accurately predict the discharge capacity. These models are stacking Long Short-Term Memory networks (stacking LSTMs), Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and stacking recurrent neural networks (SRNNs). Zhangdianhua et al.17 proposed a hot-rolled strip crown diagnosis model integrating SMOTE and stacking integrated algorithm. The model integrates machine learning models such as LGBM, Support Vector Regression (SVR), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and RF, and uses SMOTE oversampling to reduce the impact of uneven data distribution and achieve an excellent prediction effect. Yin et al.18 proposed a prediction framework of anode effect based on the combination of multiple deep learning models. Different models were used to deal with the state parameters of aluminum reduction cells, and the long-term and short-term memory networks were used to identify the relationship between the parameters and the anode effect. The experiments verified that the model had good robustness and generalization19,20.

In the field of prediction models, predecessors have carried out much research and achieved remarkable results. While previous studies have made progress in performance prediction, certain limitations persist. The accuracy and stability of the prediction results of the model need to be further improved. Especially in the face of a large number of process parameters with strong nonlinear coupling characteristics, it is prone to problems such as the lack of effectiveness and representativeness of the remaining data after data cleaning and the insufficient number of features, which leads to poor performance of the prediction results of the model. In the process optimization assisted by the performance prediction model, there is more performance prediction and experimental verification, and the mechanism research between process parameters, performance parameters, and microstructure is still relatively scarce.

In this paper, aiming at the AAF whose manufacturing process contains 80 process manufacturing parameters with strong nonlinear coupling characteristics, this study proposes a stacked ensemble learning model based on multiple machine learning models and linear regression as meta-model: the most relevant and representative features are selected from the original data set containing 80 process parameters by using the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) and multiple collinearity test (MT). The Box Cox transform (BCT) is used to process the data to improve the normality. The Optuna algorithm is used to determine the hyperparameters of each model to improve the prediction performance. Even when processing datasets containing numerous, strongly nonlinearly coupled process parameters, the proposed model, following rigorous data cleaning, effectively preserves data validity and representativeness, demonstrating robust predictive performance. It provides an accurate and stable method for predicting the two core properties of AAF (voltage tolerance and specific capacitance).

With the help of the model, the key process parameters, performance parameters, and microstructure were analyzed to obtain the key process parameters affecting the performance of AAF, and a multi-stage anodic oxidation process was proposed: firstly, the dense oxide film barrier layer was formed at low voltage, the thickness of the barrier layer was increased, and the withstand voltage value was improved; Then increase the step voltage to form a porous layer, increase the specific surface area of the aluminum foil, and improve the specific capacitance. The specific capacitance can be effectively improved while maintaining high withstand voltage.

Methodology

The purpose of this study is to build an accurate and stable performance prediction model for anode aluminum foil. To achieve this, a hybrid model is adopted to analyze the influence and optimize. The whole process machine learning hybrid model integrating data preprocessing, feature selection, stacking integrated prediction model, and super parameter optimization algorithm is adopted. The specific model structure is shown in Fig. 1. Firstly, by collecting the actual production line data of the enterprise, the data set is divided into the training set and the test set according to the ratio of 8:2 by the set-aside method, and the data distribution consistency between the training set and the test set is ensured by the random segmentation technology. Then, the training set is preprocessed, descriptive statistics, visualization analysis, correlation analysis and hypothesis testing are performed, and the feature subset is screened by combining multicollinearity analysis. And then, KNN, RF, ET, GBDT, AB, XGB, and LGBM were used to construct a single prediction model, and the performance was compared by random grid search. Then, stacking ensemble learning based on these models and linear regression as meta-model was used to determine the best scheme by comparing the optimal parameters with the Optuna optimization algorithm and different stacking strategies. Finally, the accuracy and reliability of the hybrid model are verified by experiments.

The overall strategy for property prediction with ML in this work.

PCC-MT

In many studies, in order to improve the accuracy and generalization ability of the model, PCC-MT was implemented to eliminate redundant features, which verified the effectiveness of PCC-MT21–23. This method can reduce the influence of irrelevant information on the model so that it can give priority to the key and valuable features. Therefore, this study implements PCC-MT for the training set.

Pearson correlation coefficient

PCC is a parametric indicator of the dependence of two variables24. Compared to Spearman correlation coefficient (SCC) and Kendall correlation coefficient (KCC), PCC has a wider range of applications and can better reveal the linear correlation between variables. The expression of PCC is shown in Equation (Eq. ) (1).

The and are the ith parameter of the feature and the target, respectively; and are the mean values of the feature and the target, respectively; and n is the sample size. In this study, features with p-value < 0.01 and |PCC| > 0.5 are retained for subsequent analysis, where the p-value is used to test the null hypothesis that there is no linear correlation between the feature and the target variable.

Multicollinearity test

The variance inflation factor (VIF) is an important indicator used to detect multicollinearity between input features25. In MT, if there is a high degree of correlation between two or more independent variables, the variance of these independent variables will be overestimated. This multicollinearity leads to inaccurate estimation of regression coefficients. VIF is the metric used to quantify the extent of this variance inflation, shown in Eq. (2).

The is the VIF of the ith independent variable; the is the coefficient of determination (R-squared) obtained when regressing the other independent variables with the ith independent variable as the dependent variable. It should be emphasized that the VIF calculation is only performed on the input features retained after PCC screening, and the dependent target variables (withstand voltage and specific capacitance) are not included in the calculation, to avoid the interference of the linear correlation between input features and target variables on the multicollinearity judgment. Referring to existing research, features with VIF exceeding 10 are considered to have serious multicollinearity and are discarded in this study, in order to improve the stability and interpretability of the model. For the individual features with VIF > 10 but extremely high correlation with the target variable (|PCC|>0.7), we retain them after manual verification of their industrial regulatory significance, and supplement the targeted discussion on their multicollinearity in the results section.

Box-Cox transformation

The BCT is mainly applied to reduce data skewness and improve distribution symmetry26. In practice, it is often employed to solve the problem of data not meeting the normal distribution in regression analysis and analysis of variance, thus improving the accuracy and reliability of the model27. The calculation method of BCT is shown in Eq. (3).

The y is the original data and λ is the parameter of BCT. When λ =0, the logarithmic transformation is used, otherwise the transformation is performed using the BCT formula. In this study, BCT is applied to the retained features after PCC-MT screening; the transformation parameter λ is fitted on the training set, and then applied to the test set. In this study, BCT is only applied to the input features retained after PCC-MT screening, and the target variables are not transformed. To ensure the strict positivity required for BCT, a fixed positive offset is added to the individual features with non-positive values before transformation. To avoid data leakage, the transformation parameter λ is fitted only on the training set, and then the fitted λ is applied to the test set uniformly.

Optuna

Optuna is an automatic hyperparameter optimization framework for machine learning models, which can efficiently search the optimal hyperparameter combination through adaptive sampling and pruning strategies. In the context of Optuna optimization, it is first necessary to define the hyperparameter space and the objective function28. Subsequently, Optuna undertakes a continuous exploration and analysis of the hyperparameter space, based on the output of the objective function, with the aim of determining the combination of hyperparameters that minimizes the root mean squared error (RMSE) of the model on the validation set29. Finally, following this continuous trial and adjustment, the optimal combination of hyperparameters is identified, in order to achieve the optimization outcome.

Stacking integrated model

As shown in Fig. 1(Middle frame), the stacking integrated framework generally adopts a two-layer structure, with the first layer being the base learner and the second layer being the meta-learner30. To avoid data leakage, the out-of-fold (OOF) prediction strategy is adopted for model training: the training set is divided into 5 folds, each base learner is trained on 4 folds and outputs prediction results on the remaining 1 fold, and the OOF predictions of all base learners on the full training set are used as the input of the meta-learner. All base learners are finally retrained on the complete training set, and their outputs on the test set are used as the input of the meta-learner to obtain the final prediction results. First, the original dataset is divided into a number of sub-datasets and input into each base learner (RF, AB, GBDT and ET) in the first layer. Each base learner outputs its respective prediction results. Then, the output of the first layer is used as the input to the second layer learner to train the meta-learner. Finally, the final prediction results are output by the meta-learner (LGBM). The outputs of multiple models are fused to obtain an improvement in the overall prediction accuracy.

According to the above method, based on PCC-MT feature screening, BCT, and Optuna parameter optimization algorithm(abbreviated as PCC-MT-BCT-Optuna) from Subsection 2.1 to Subsection 2.3, a hybrid model with RF-AB-GBDT-LGBM as stacking strategy is proposed.

In this paper, mean squared error (MSE), root mean squared error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE), and R-squared (R2) are used as the evaluation indexes of the prediction model.

Correlation analysis

In order to enhance the quality of data features and identify pivotal process parameters, it is crucial to conduct a PCC analysis of the training set. The significance level (probability values, P-values) is employed to ascertain whether the discrepancies between features are genuine or the consequence of random factors. As illustrated in Fig. 3, PCCs for X61 (Voltage of repair 2), X42 (Voltage of formation 5), X73 (Voltage of repair 3), X51 (Voltage of repair 1) and the withstand voltage are 0.80, 0.72, 0.72, and 0.71, with all corresponding P-values less than 0.001. It indicates a strong correlation between the voltages of the formation and repair phases and the withstand voltage, with minimal influence from other factors. PCCs for X61, X62 (Conductivity of repair 2), X73, X42 and specific capacitance are − 0.71, 0.65, -0.63 and − 0.63, with all corresponding P-values less than 0.001. It indicates a strong correlation between the voltages of the formation and repair phases and the specific capacitance, with minimal influence from other factors. The results of previous research indicates a positive correlation between the thickness of the dense layer of the oxide film and the voltage of the formation and repair stage31. It can be seen that the screening of the main influencing process parameters for voltage withstand and specific capacitance is reasonable.

Multicollinearity analysis

In order to enhance the stability and interpretability of the regression model, the input features retained after PCC screening are subjected to a MT. The full results are presented in Fig. 4. It is typically deemed that a serious multicollinearity issue arises when the VIF exceeds 10. For instance, the input features X51 and X61, which are used to predict the withstand voltage and specific capacitance, exhibit significant multicollinearity issues. Although X61 has a VIF value exceeding 10, it has the strongest linear correlation with both withstand voltage (PCC = 0.80) and specific capacitance (PCC=-0.71), and is the core regulatory parameter in the actual industrial production of AAF. Consequently, X61 was retained in the final feature set due to its industrial significance, as verified manually, while X51, exhibiting a lower correlation, was discarded. In order to balance the multicollinearity and the industrial regulatory significance of the features. It has the potential to negatively impact predictive accuracy of the model. Finally, 12 core features are retained for each target variable after screening.

Key process analysis

Based on the research findings in Sect. 3.2 above, X61 (Voltage of repair 2), X42 (Voltage of formation 5), and X73 (Voltage of repair 3) are the key process parameters affecting the withstand voltage; for specific capacitance, the key process parameters are X61, X62 (Conductivity of repair 2), and X73. Through analysis, X61 exerts the most significant statistical correlation on the performance of AAF. Therefore, the relationship between X61 and both withstand voltage and specific capacitance is analyzed in detail.

In the actual production process, it is difficult to simultaneously improve the two key electrochemical performance parameters of AAF, withstand voltage and specific capacitance, which is consistent with the analysis results of key process parameters. Specifically, when the withstand voltage is improved by adjusting process parameters, the specific capacitance often decreases accordingly. Currently, further in-depth research and analysis are required to clarify the mechanism underlying the trade-off relationship between withstand voltage and specific capacitance.

Mechanism analysis and process optimization

The mechanism of how structure affects the withstand voltage and specific capacitance

As shown in Fig. 13, etched foil undergoes a multi-stage anodization process, forming an oxide film dielectric layer on its surface that can withstand a specific voltage. During the chemical synthesis process, hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in water enter the film layer under the influence of an electric field and combine with aluminum ions (Al³⁺) to form a porous hydroxide layer. The dehydration of the porous hydroxide layer into an amorphous aluminum oxide layer occurs at high temperatures. Subsequently, the amorphous Al₂O₃ layer transforms into a crystalline layer after heat treatment. During this transformation, the oxide film dielectric layer of the AAF undergoes density changes and thickness reduction. The oxygen ion concentration at the interface between the electrolyte and the oxide film is increasing, especially in the thin film regions with defects. This causes electrons to flow into the film, so the electric field exerts a force on the ions, accelerating them and leading to their impact on the film structure. There is an extremely close correlation between the spark voltage, electrolyte concentration, and electric field voltage. This indicates that the key process parameters identified by the correlation analysis meet theoretical criteria.

Typical microscopic structure of anodic oxide film: (a) FE-SEM micrograph of cross-sectional samples; (b) Schematic diagram.

Under low anodization voltage conditions, the electric field driving force is weak, and the electrochemical oxidation kinetics of aluminum atoms on the aluminum foil surface are slow, resulting in a low rate of aluminum atoms losing electrons and converting into aluminum ions. At the same time, the rate of oxygen ions in the electrolyte migrating to the aluminum foil surface via electromigration is relatively low. Under the combined effect of these two factors, the nucleation and growth processes of the aluminum oxide film are extremely slow, and the final formed oxide film is thin32,33. Under this low-voltage condition, the chemical dissolution kinetics of the oxide film by the electrolyte are relatively inhibited, and its dissolution effect is not significant, enabling the oxide film to grow in a relatively uniform and dense manner, which effectively restricts the nucleation and expansion of pores.

Under high anodization voltage conditions, the electric field intensity increases sharply. Affected by the strong electric field, the electron transfer process of aluminum atoms on the aluminum foil surface is accelerated, and a large number of aluminum ions are rapidly generated. Meanwhile, oxygen ions in the electrolyte move rapidly to the aluminum foil surface at a high migration rate driven by the strong electric field and undergo chemical reactions with aluminum ions. The combination of the two significantly accelerates the growth kinetics of the aluminum oxide film, leading to a significant increase in the oxide film thickness. Under high-voltage conditions, the rate of the chemical dissolution reaction between the electrolyte and the oxide film accelerates. The pores already present in the oxide film are subjected to more intense erosion by the electrolyte, and the pores are continuously corroded and gradually expanded, thereby changing the microstructure of the oxide film34.

Process optimization method

In the initial stage of the multi-stage anodization of aluminum foil, a lower voltage is used to allow the oxide film to grow slowly, forming a thin and dense barrier layer between the aluminum foil substrate and the porous layer35. The barrier layer serves to prevent further erosion of the aluminum foil substrate by ions in the electrolyte and is also the main obstacle to electron transport, playing a key role in the insulation performance and withstand voltage performance of the AAF. After the formation of the barrier layer, the step voltage is gradually increased to accelerate the growth rate of the oxide film. At the same time, the dissolution rate of the oxide film by the electrolyte increases, leading to the formation of a porous layer. The porous layer consists of numerous nanoscale micropores perpendicular to the aluminum foil surface, which are arranged in a honeycomb structure with pore sizes typically ranging from a few nanometers to several hundred nanometers. The presence of the porous layer greatly increases the specific surface area of the aluminum foil, enabling the AAF to store more charges, thereby improving its specific capacitance. In addition, the pore structure and pore size distribution of the porous layer also have important effects on the penetration of electrolyte in the aluminum foil and ion transport. Finally, the required total thickness of the AAF is achieved. This can effectively improve the specific capacitance while ensuring the withstand voltage performance.

In summary, the multi-stage anodization process of aluminum foil, relying on its unique reaction mechanism, achieves effective improvement of specific capacitance while maintaining a high withstand voltage through the step-by-step construction of the barrier layer and porous layer. During this process, the initially formed barrier layer lays the foundation for the subsequent growth of the porous layer, and the two act synergistically to optimize the electrochemical performance of the aluminum foil, successfully improving the specific capacitance on the premise of ensuring the withstand voltage performance.

Conclusion

In this paper, a properties prediction strategy through machine learning which combines PCC-MT feature selection with stacking learning model is proposed, in order to improve the stability and accuracy of properties prediction for AAF. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) A hybrid model combining PCC-MT-BCT-Optuna optimization method and stacking ensemble learning model is proposed and verified. It shows good stability and accuracy in predicting the core performance (withstand voltage and specific capacitance) of aluminum anode foil (AAF), a complex manufacturing product with up to 80 process parameters and strong nonlinear coupling characteristics. The proposed hybrid machine learning strategy effectively solves the problem of product performance prediction with high-dimensional and strong nonlinear coupling manufacturing process.

(2) The research reveals that the voltage at the anodizing stage (especially the voltage at the repair stage 2 × 61 ) is the key process control parameter that affects the two core properties of aluminum anode foil: withstand voltage and specific volume. The underlying mechanism is attributed to the following: under low-voltage conditions, the slowed electrochemical oxidation reaction facilitates the formation of a thin, dense insulating barrier layer, thereby enhancing the withstand voltage. Conversely, at high voltage, the intensified electric field not only accelerates the formation of this barrier layer but also promotes the rapid growth of the subsequent porous layer. The porous layer can significantly increase the specific surface area of the foil to improve the specific volume, but it may also weaken the insulation effect of the barrier layer. There is an inherent trade-off between the two.

(3) The mechanism analysis informed the proposal of a stepwise voltage anodizing strategy. That is, at the initial stage of the oxidation process, lower voltage is used, and a dense barrier layer is preferentially formed to ensure high withstand voltage; Then gradually increase the voltage to promote the rapid growth of the porous layer, so as to effectively improve the specific capacitance on the premise of ensuring the withstand voltage. This provides a clear and feasible process optimization idea and theoretical basis for solving the inherent contradiction (trade-off) that it is difficult to improve the two core performance indicators of withstand voltage and specific capacitance in the manufacture of aluminum anode foil at the same time.

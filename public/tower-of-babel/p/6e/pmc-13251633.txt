Impact of telehealth modalities on medication adherence in older adults with heart disease: Evidence from NHATS
Authors: Xiang Chen, Lixue Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAs telehealth becomes increasingly integrated into healthcare, understanding its impact on medication adherence among older adults with chronic conditions like heart disease is essential. This study investigates the effects of various telehealth modalities on medication adherence rates in this demographic.ObjectiveThe purpose of this study is to evaluate the impact of various telehealth modalities on medication adherence among older adults with heart disease, utilizing data from the National Health and Aging Trends Study.MethodsData were analyzed from 1552 participants aged 65 and older diagnosed with heart disease, sourced from the National Health and Aging Trends Study (NHATS). Telehealth modalities assessed included video consultations, phone visits, patient portals, and online refill services. Medication adherence was measured using validated self-reported questionnaires. Modified Poisson regression with robust error variance was employed to evaluate the relationship between telehealth engagement and adherence, controlling for demographic variables.ResultsFindings revealed that participants utilizing video consultations and online refill services had significantly higher medication adherence rates compared to those engaging in traditional care methods. Risk Ratios indicated that these modalities facilitated better communication and medication management, ultimately enhancing adherence.ConclusionsTelehealth modalities offer promising strategies to improve medication adherence among older adults with heart disease. The study underscores the importance of integrating digital health interventions into routine care, which can address barriers to adherence and improve health outcomes in this vulnerable population.

Introduction

The escalating prevalence of heart disease among the global aging population necessitates robust healthcare delivery models that overcome traditional barriers to access (Takahashi et al., 2022). Telehealth has emerged as a transformative solution, particularly catalyzed by the COVID-19 pandemic, by offering older adults—who often face mobility and transportation challenges—a convenient avenue for consistent medical engagement (Alvarez et al., 2021; Battistoni et al., 2024; SMA et al., 2021; Fallahi et al., 2024). While remote consultations reduce the burden of travel and improve accessibility (Perlman et al., 2026), it remains critical to understand how these digital modalities specifically influence medication adherence, a cornerstone of chronic disease management (Backes et al., 2020).

Medication adherence is a critical component of chronic disease management, particularly for patients with heart disease, where adherence to prescribed treatment plans can significantly alter disease progression and improve quality of life (Jafri et al., 2024). Despite the importance of adherence, studies indicate that many older adults struggle with these regimens due to various factors, including cognitive decline, complex medication schedules, and difficulties in managing prescriptions (Nkonde-Price et al., 2022; BT et al., 2019). Addressing these challenges is crucial for ensuring effective health outcomes and minimizing hospitalizations and healthcare costs associated with non-adherence (Schulz and Laufs, 2024).

The intersection of telehealth and medication adherence presents a unique opportunity for intervention (Zheng et al., 2024). Telehealth can facilitate better communication between patients and providers, allow for timely medication management, and enhance patient education regarding treatment regimens (Talmor et al., 2018). However, the effectiveness of these interventions in improving medication adherence among older adults with heart disease remains underexplored in the existing literature. Understanding how various telehealth modalities impact medication adherence can help refine these interventions and promote their integration into standard care practices (Qi et al., 2025).

This study utilizes data from the National Health and Aging Trends Study (NHATS), a robust longitudinal survey that gathers comprehensive data on the health and well-being of older adults. By analyzing this rich dataset, the study aims to investigate the relationship between telehealth utilization and medication adherence among older patients with heart disease. Specifically, it seeks to explore different modes of telehealth engagement—such as video consultations, phone visits, patient portals, and online refill services—and their respective impacts on medication adherence rates.

The significance of this research lies not only in its potential to enhance understanding of telehealth's role in managing chronic conditions but also in its implications for public health policy and healthcare delivery systems. As healthcare continues to evolve, recognizing the value of telehealth in improving medication adherence could inform strategies to implement and promote telehealth services more broadly. This is particularly pertinent in light of ongoing health disparities faced by older adults, especially those from low-income backgrounds or rural areas with limited access to healthcare resources.

Moreover, this study also addresses existing gaps in the literature regarding how telehealth interventions may influence the healthcare behaviors of older adults. While there is a growing body of evidence suggesting the benefits of telehealth for various patient populations, specific insights into the mechanisms through which telehealth affects medication adherence among older heart disease patients remain limited. By addressing these research gaps, the findings from this analysis could contribute to the development of evidence-based practices that better support the health management of older adults.

In addition to contributing to the empirical literature, this study also aims to provide actionable recommendations for healthcare practitioners and policymakers. By identifying effective telehealth modalities that enhance medication adherence, the research will offer insights that can be integrated into healthcare delivery models, thereby fostering a more patient-centered approach to care for older adults. These findings will encourage healthcare systems to prioritize telehealth initiatives, ensuring that older patients receive the support they need to navigate their treatment regimens successfully.

Ultimately, the successful implementation of telehealth can lead to healthier aging, improved quality of life, and reduced healthcare expenditures. As the burden of chronic diseases continues to rise, a renewed focus on innovative solutions like telehealth is essential. This study endeavors to shed light on the critical intersection of technology and healthcare, emphasizing the urgent need to harness the potential of telehealth to meet the challenges of an aging population. By exploring how telehealth services can promote medication adherence, this research not only addresses an immediate concern within chronic disease management but also paves the way for future advancements in elderly healthcare delivery.

In conclusion, as telehealth services become increasingly integral to healthcare, understanding their impacts on medication adherence among older adults with heart disease is vital. This study sets out to investigate these effects using a rigorous analytical framework, ultimately contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of how digital health interventions can improve health outcomes in an aging society.

Methodology

Study design and data source

This study employs a quantitative, observational cohort design using data acquired from the National Health and Aging Trends Study (NHATS). NHATS is a comprehensive longitudinal survey dedicated to assessing the health trajectories of older adults, focusing on disabling conditions, healthcare utilization, and various factors impacting quality of life. Established to gather detailed information about the aging population in the United States, the NHATS database is renowned for its methodological rigor, comprehensive sampling methods, and depth of data, which includes demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related measures. For this analysis, the focus primarily centers on understanding the impact of telehealth services on medication adherence among patients diagnosed with heart disease, a critical area of study given the increasing reliance on telehealth in managing chronic health conditions, particularly among older adults.

Study population

The cohort analyzed in this study consists of 1552 individuals aged 65 and older, characterized by varying degrees of heart disease. Participants were meticulously selected from the NHATS database based on stringent, predefined criteria that included a documented self-reported diagnosis of heart disease, age eligibility, and a willingness to engage with telehealth services as part of their healthcare regimen.

The demographic profile of the study cohort revealed that 52% were females (n = 809), reflecting the gender distribution often observed in older populations, where females typically outnumber males. The average age of participants was 75.4 years (SD ± 5.6), indicating a sample primarily comprised of older seniors who may face additional health challenges. In terms of educational attainment, 44% of participants completed high school or had an education lower than that, which reflects a significant portion of the population facing educational barriers. Socioeconomic factors were also evaluated, revealing that 29% of the sample reported annual incomes below $20,000, emphasizing the economic context in which these patients manage their health and the potential implications for accessing healthcare services.

Telehealth interventions

The study assessed multiple forms of telehealth interventions, categorized into five distinct modalities that reflect current practices in digital health engagement:1.Video Consultations: Utilizing secure video technology to facilitate real-time consultations between patients and healthcare providers, allowing for visual interaction which can enhance communication and patient engagement.2.Phone Visits: Conducting telephonic consultations without visual engagement, an essential option for individuals lacking access to video technology or those who prefer voice communication.3.Patient Portals: Leveraging digital platforms that enable patients to access their health information, communicate with providers, and manage their healthcare interactions autonomously.4.Online Refill Services: Platforms that allow participants to manage medication refills digitally, promoting adherence by simplifying the medication management process.5.No Telehealth Utilization: Participants who did not engage with any telehealth services served as the control group for comparative analysis, providing a baseline for assessing effectiveness of the interventions.

Data collection

Data regarding participants' demographics, health status, and levels of telehealth engagement were meticulously collected through structured surveys administered as part of the NHATS program. Key surveys primarily evaluated the following:1.Demographic Information: Comprehensive data were collected on age, gender, educational attainment, income levels, and residential status to contextualize the health behaviors of participants.2.Health Conditions: Self-reported health status was assessed, incorporating validated measures for comorbidities and mental health status, which were evaluated using established scales such as the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES—D) to provide a multifaceted view of participants' health conditions.3.Medication Adherence: Self-reported adherence to prescribed medication regimens was measured using validated questionnaires, including the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale. This tool assesses patient engagement with their medication schedule and healthcare providers via telehealth, providing insights into how virtual care impacts adherence.

Measurement instruments

Medication adherence was evaluated using validated self-report items from the NHATS standardized questionnaire. Participants responded to questions assessing their medication-taking behaviors, including whether they:

Forgot to take their medications, Were careless at times about taking medications, Stopped taking medications when they felt better, Stopped taking medications when they felt worse,

These items assess both intentional and unintentional medication non-adherence, which are key dimensions in understanding adherence patterns among older adults. Based on their responses, participants were categorized as adherent (no reported instances of missed medications) or non-adherent (any reported deviation from prescribed regimen). This assessment method has been validated in prior studies of medication adherence in older populations with chronic diseases.

All survey instruments and questionnaires used in this study were part of the standardized NHATS data collection protocol, which is publicly available through the NHATS website (https://www.nhats.org). The NHATS questionnaires were developed by a multidisciplinary team of researchers and underwent rigorous psychometric testing prior to implementation. Detailed information regarding the development, validation, and administration of NHATS instruments can be found in the NHATS User Guide and Technical Papers available at the NHATS website.

Data analysis

The data analysis involved multiple rigorous stages to assure the robustness and validity of the findings:1.Descriptive Statistics: Baseline characteristics of the cohort were summarized using means and standard deviations for continuous variables, and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, providing an overview of the study population's characteristics and health status.2.Comparative Analysis: To estimate the association between telehealth modalities and medication adherence, we utilized modified Poisson regression models with robust error variance. This approach was chosen instead of standard logistic regression to directly calculate Risk Ratios (RRs) and 95% Confidence Intervals (CIs), as Odds Ratios (ORs) can overestimate the effect size when the outcome of interest is common (prevalence >10%). The models were adjusted for potential confounders, including age, sex, socioeconomic status, educational attainment, and baseline health status.3.Sensitivity Analyses: To validate the primary findings, sensitivity analyses were rigorously performed using complete case analyses and multiple imputation methods to address any missing data. This methodology provided critical confirmation that the observed relationships between telehealth utilization and medication adherence were robust and not artifacts of data handling or missing responses.4.Statistical Significance: Statistical significance was determined based on p-values—values below 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Additionally, confidence intervals (CIs) for Risk Ratios were computed to provide context regarding the strength and precision of the associations observed in the analyses.

Outcomes

The primary outcome measured in the study was the medication adherence rate among participants, as influenced by their engagement with the different telehealth modalities. Secondary outcomes included follow-up care adherence, defined as the rate of scheduled follow-up appointments attended by participants who engaged in telehealth services. These multiple facets of outcomes are critical in establishing the functional impact of telehealth on health management and adherence behavior among older patients with heart disease.

Ethical considerations

This study was a secondary analysis of de-identified, publicly available data from the NHATS. The primary NHATS study received ethical approval from the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health Institutional Review Board (JHU IRB), Baltimore, Maryland, USA (IRB approval number: 00005031). All original NHATS participants provided written informed consent before participation in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

As this research involved only secondary analysis of publicly accessible, de-identified data without any direct participant interaction or access to identifiable information, it was classified as exempt from additional institutional review board approval under policies and 45 CFR 46.104(d) (SMA et al., 2021) of the U.S. Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects.

All data management and analysis procedures adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki principles (https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) and applicable data protection regulations, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Participant confidentiality was rigorously maintained throughout the study.

Association between telehealth modalities and follow-up adherence in telehealth interventions

The associations of telehealth interventions, indicate a significant reduction in the risk of medication non-adherence among users (Fig. 4), particularly those utilizing video consultations, which demonstrated a Risk Ratio of 0.80 (95% CI: 0.68–0.94, p = 0.010). This finding underscores telehealth's effectiveness not only in promoting medication adherence but also in facilitating comprehensive chronic disease management by overcoming geographical and logistical barriers that often hinder access to healthcare. Moreover, follow-up care adherence further illustrates the positive impact of telehealth engagement. Patients who participated in video consultations exhibited increased rates of follow-up adherence, with a Risk Ratio of 1.20 (95% CI: 1.05–1.37, p = 0.005), while those engaging in telephone consultations demonstrated a Risk Ratio of 1.15 (p = 0.034). These results reinforce the notion that telehealth fosters improved patient-provider interactions and encourages adherence to healthcare recommendations. Effective follow-up care is crucial in chronic disease management, as it ensures patients receive necessary interventions and ongoing monitoring, which are vital for maintaining health. Collectively, this data advocates for the systematic inclusion of telehealth services within chronic disease management frameworks, highlighting their importance in enhancing patient engagement and participation in health maintenance. Integrating telehealth comprehensively into healthcare systems can significantly improve overall health outcomes, making it an essential consideration for contemporary healthcare delivery models. Variability of telehealth effectiveness

The analysis presented in Fig. 5 delves into how telehealth effectiveness fluctuates across various demographic and clinical subgroups, revealing significant insights. Notably, younger patients under the age of 75 exhibited a markedly greater responsiveness to video consultations, as indicated by a Risk Ratio of 0.75 (p = 0.002). This result implies that younger populations may be more adaptable to utilizing telehealth solutions and therefore may benefit more substantially from such interventions.Fig. 5Subgroup Differences in the Effectiveness of Telehealth Modalities on Medication Non-adherence.Fig. 5

Subgroup Differences in the Effectiveness of Telehealth Modalities on Medication Non-adherence.

In contrast, for participants aged over 75 years, the analysis yielded a risk ratio of 0.85 (p = 0.150). This result indicates no statistically significant association between telehealth use and medication adherence in this age group, suggesting that the benefits observed in the younger cohort may not extend uniformly to the oldest old without additional support.

Sex-based differences also emerged in the dataset, with female participants showing more significant risk reduction at a Risk Ratio of 0.80 (p = 0.050) when utilizing video consultations. Meanwhile, male participants reflected a Risk Ratio of 0.90 (p = 0.030). These gender discrepancies emphasize the importance of integrating gender-sensitive approaches when designing and implementing telehealth interventions.

Understanding these heterogeneous effects is crucial for healthcare providers, as it enables them to refine and customize interventions that resonate with the specific needs and capabilities of diverse patient populations. By acknowledging these variations, telehealth strategies can be better designed, thus maximizing patient engagement and adherence, leading to improved health outcomes across all demographic sectors.

Discussion

Overview of key findings

This study sought to investigate the impact of various telehealth modalities on medication adherence among older adults diagnosed with heart disease, utilizing data from the NHATS. The findings illuminate significant relationships between telehealth interventions and medication adherence rates, suggesting that certain modalities can effectively enhance adherence among older patients. Specifically, video consultations and online refill services were associated with higher adherence rates compared to traditional care methods. These results align with existing literature advocating for the integration of telehealth services as critical components of chronic disease management in older populations.

Implications of telehealth modalities

The effectiveness of different telehealth modalities sheds light on how technology can serve as a facilitator of better health practices among older adults (Stargatt et al., 2022). Video consultations emerged as a particularly beneficial method, enabling visual and auditory engagement that enhances patient-provider interactions (Gentry et al., 2025). This modality not only allows healthcare providers to assess patients more thoroughly but also fosters a sense of connection that can motivate patients to be more adherent to their medication regimens. The ability to interact face-to-face, even virtually, addresses some of the emotional and psychological barriers that older adults may face when managing chronic diseases (Schütz et al., 2022; Waddell et al., 2023).

Conversely, the study found that traditional methods of care, including in-person visits without any facilitative telehealth engagement, were less effective in promoting medication adherence (Zhang et al., 2023). This is indicative of the barriers older adults face in accessing healthcare services, particularly those related to physical mobility, transportation, and potential social isolation (Bernard et al., 2022). By offering a convenient alternative, telehealth services can significantly improve adherence rates among older adults, aligning with findings from previous studies that emphasize the importance of accessibility in healthcare delivery (Huang et al., 2024).

Comparison with previous studies

Our findings regarding the positive impact of video consultations align with recent evidence from Asch et al. (Asch et al., 2022), who observed a strong association between video-based telemedicine and improved medication adherence among heart failure patients. However, our study extends this narrative by isolating the specific efficacy of administrative tools. While Paneerselvam et al. (Paneerselvam et al., 2025) emphasized the utility of multifaceted mobile apps for chronic disease management, our analysis suggests that streamlined “Online Refill Services” independently provide a substantial adherence benefit. This indicates that reducing logistical friction—simply making it easier to obtain medication—may be as critical as the educational interventions often prioritized in complex health apps.

Crucially, our divergent findings regarding older adults (≥75 years) highlight a persistent “digital ceiling.” This echoes the digital divide concerns raised by Heiney al. (Heiney et al., 2020), but our data specifically identifies advanced age as a distinct barrier, regardless of location. Furthermore, contrasting with the high variability in care delivery noted by Li et al. (Li et al., 2025), our study demonstrates a clear hierarchy where video visits consistently outperform telephone consultations. This distinction is vital; it suggests that the visual component of telehealth—facilitating non-verbal cues and physical inspection—is not merely an enhancement but a necessary mechanism for effective medication management in the geriatric heart disease population.

Online refill services and medication management

One significant aspect of the findings relates to the impact of online refill services on medication adherence. The ability for patients to manage their prescriptions digitally not only increases convenience but also allows greater autonomy over their health management (Backes et al., 2020). For older adults who sometimes struggle with complex medication regimens—due to cognitive decline or multifaceted health issues—having a streamlined, user-friendly process for medication management can lead to improved adherence rates (Paneerselvam et al., 2025). The results point to the importance of incorporating such digital solutions into standard care practices, particularly for conditions like heart disease that often require consistent medication management to prevent complications (Rana et al., 2024).

The positive impacts of online refill services tie into broader trends in healthcare toward patient-centered practices that prioritize user engagement and empowerment (Lanke et al., 2025; Ridho et al., 2022). By providing tools that facilitate easier access to medications, healthcare providers can help older adults better navigate their treatment regimens and ultimately improve their health outcomes (Tran et al., 2022). The implications of this study suggest that investment in and promotion of online refill services, alongside education around their use, could be effective strategies to enhance adherence in older populations.

Barriers to telehealth utilization

Despite the evident advantages, the study also acknowledges several barriers that could impact the successful implementation of telehealth services among older adults. Issues such as digital literacy, access to technology, and internet connectivity remain prominent challenges. Many older adults may lack the necessary skills or resources to fully engage with telehealth solutions, which could exacerbate disparities in healthcare access and outcomes. This is particularly concerning for lower-income populations or those in rural areas with limited technological infrastructure (Alzghaibi, 2025).

Critically, the reliance on digital modalities risks creating a ‘digital inverse care law,’ where the availability of efficient medical care varies inversely with the population's need. Patients with the highest burden of disease—often those with lower socioeconomic status and limited digital literacy—are the least likely to successfully navigate video visits or patient portals. Consequently, an unmitigated shift toward digital-first models could deepen the chasm between the ‘digitally included’ and the ‘digitally excluded.’ Instead of democratizing healthcare, technology may inadvertently stratify patients, granting superior access and convenience to the privileged while leaving vulnerable groups with fragmented, lower-quality care alternatives. This underscores the ethical imperative for healthcare systems to maintain robust non-digital pathways to prevent the institutionalization of health inequities.

Furthermore, certain telehealth modalities, while beneficial for some, may not be universally applicable to all patients. For instance, while video consultations enhance engagement for some individuals, others might find virtual settings intimidating or unsatisfactory compared to face-to-face consultations (Southwick et al., 2024). Healthcare providers must bear in mind individual preferences and capabilities when recommending telehealth modalities. Tailoring interventions to meet the specific needs of older adults is crucial; a one-size-fits-all approach may inadvertently alienate those who stand to benefit the most from telehealth solutions (Cho et al., 2025).

Educational initiatives aimed at improving digital literacy among older adults could help mitigate these barriers (Petretto et al., 2024). Training programs that instruct older patients on how to access and utilize telehealth services can empower them to take charge of their health management effectively. Additionally, ensuring robust support systems are in place for technical assistance can ease the transition into telehealth and encourage greater adoption among hesitant patients (Nakayama et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2023).

Role of social support and health literacy

An integral part of medication adherence, particularly among older adults, is the influence of social support and health literacy. The results of this study underscore the necessity of considering these psychosocial factors when exploring adherence to treatment regimens (Lee et al., 2021). Social support from family members or caregivers can significantly affect how older adults manage their medications, as companions play crucial roles in reminding patients to take their medications or assist them in navigating telehealth technologies (Dinh and Bonner, 2023).

Moreover, health literacy impacts how patients understand their medical conditions and treatment options. Older adults with high health literacy are more likely to comprehend their medication regimens, which in turn influences adherence (Zou et al., 2024). Therefore, healthcare providers must prioritize strategies that not only improve access to care through telehealth but also enhance the patient's understanding of their health. This can involve simplifying communication about medications, utilizing teach-back methods during telehealth consultations, or providing written materials that explain treatment plans clearly (Chamberlain et al., 2024; Wu and Sheng, 2019).

Clinical implications

The findings suggest that clinicians should actively integrate video consultations and online refill services into the management of older adults with heart disease to enhance medication adherence. However, a tailored approach is essential. Given the variation in effectiveness based on age, providers must assess digital literacy before prioritizing virtual care, particularly for patients over 75 who may not benefit as significantly as their younger counterparts. For this demographic, a hybrid care model—combining traditional in-person visits with caregiver-assisted telehealth—may be more appropriate. These insights underscore the need for personalized care plans that leverage technology without widening health disparities among vulnerable geriatric populations.

Limitations

While this study provides valuable insights, several limitations must be considered. The reliance on self-reported data for medication adherence introduces potential biases, as participants may overreport adherence due to social desirability. Furthermore, a self-selection bias may exist, as participants who voluntarily engaged in telehealth services likely possess higher levels of digital literacy and a greater predisposition toward self-management. Consequently, these findings may not be fully generalizable to the broader older adult population, particularly those facing significant technological barriers or with lower motivation for self-care. Additionally, the observational nature of the study limits causal inferences, as confounding factors could impact the outcomes observed.

Future research directions

Future research should continue to explore the efficacy of telehealth interventions across diverse populations and settings. Longitudinal studies could provide further insight into the long-term impacts of telehealth on medication adherence. Additionally, qualitative research examining patient experiences and satisfaction with different telehealth modalities could enrich understanding of user preferences and barriers, leading to improved intervention design.

Exploring the intersection of telehealth, social support, and health literacy presents another avenue for investigation. Understanding how these factors interact will be crucial for developing comprehensive strategies that support older adults in managing their health effectively.

Conclusion

In summary, the findings of this study underscore the significant potential of telehealth modalities, particularly video consultations and online refill services, to enhance medication adherence among older adults with heart disease. Despite the barriers that exist, strategic implementation of telehealth solutions, coupled with attention to increasing digital literacy and social support, can lead to improved health outcomes. As the healthcare landscape evolves, integrating telehealth into routine care is essential for effectively managing chronic conditions and promoting better health for older adults in the coming years. The urgency for such advancements is heightened as we move toward a future where aging populations will increasingly rely on innovative healthcare solutions to address their needs.

MicroRNA-21-3p regulation of NADPH oxidase 4 and vascular endothelial growth factor A contributes to hemorrhage in cerebral cavernous malformations
Authors: Xin-Xing Guo, Zhong-Run Huang, Pei-Sheng Chen, Qi Li, Jia Li, Zhong-Song Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCerebral cavernous malformations (CCMs) are vascular anomalies prone to hemorrhage, leading to neurological deficits and reduced quality of life. Current therapies are limited, and molecular mechanisms underlying vascular instability remain incompletely understood. MicroRNAs have emerged as key regulators of vascular integrity. This study investigates the role of microRNA-21-3p (miR-21-3p) in modulating oxidative stress and angiogenesis in CCM.MethodsExpression of miR-21-3p, NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4), and vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) was assessed in endothelial cells and pericytes from CCM lesions of 20 sporadic patients using fluorescence in situ hybridization. Functional assays of proliferation, permeability, reactive oxygen species (ROS), migration, and tubule formation were performed in human brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs) and pericytes with CCM2 gene depletion, following miR-21-3p mimic or inhibitor intervention. In vivo effects on cerebral hemorrhage, vascular permeability, dilation, and angiogenesis were evaluated in ccm2-knockdown zebrafish.ResultsCCM lesions exhibited decreased miR-21-3p and increased NOX4 and VEGFA compared to controls, correlating with hemorrhage. In vitro, miR-21-3p mimics suppressed proliferation, permeability, ROS generation, and angiogenic activity by directly targeting NOX4 and VEGFA. In vivo, miR-21-3p supplementation reduced cerebral hemorrhage, vascular leakage, dilation, and sprouting angiogenesis in zebrafish with ccm2 knockdown.ConclusionMiR-21-3p regulates vascular integrity in CCM by modulating NOX4-mediated oxidative stress and VEGFA-driven angiogenesis. Loss of miR-21-3p contributes to hemorrhage, while its restoration stabilizes vascular function. This study identifies miR-21-3p as a potential therapeutic target for CCM, bridging endothelial and pericyte biology with translational potential to reduce cerebral hemorrhage.

Introduction

Cerebral cavernous malformations (CCMs) are brain vascular anomalies characterized by histological features of clusters of dilated, thin-walled blood vessels, which are clinically significant due to their potential to cause cerebral hemorrhage, significantly affecting the patient's quality of life [1]. Surgical resection and stereotactic therapy remain challenges for lesions in eloquent areas and the brainstem; therefore, pharmacological therapy provides a potential venue for CCMs, given the emerging deeper understanding of these pathogeneses [[2], [3], [4], [5], [6]].

CCMs predominantly arise from deletions and mutations in the CCM1, CCM2, or CCM3 complex in endothelial cells by modulating key signaling pathways, including Krüppel-like factor (KLF) 2/4, endothelial-mesenchymal transition, Ras homolog family member A (RhoA)/Rho-associated kinase (ROCK), and Toll-like receptor 4 signaling [7,8]. Somatic mutations of PI3K catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA) and mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 3 (MAP3K3) also play a crucial role in sporadic CCMs. Functional loss mutations in the CCM complex and somatic mutations in PIK3CA enhance the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathway in endothelial cells, forming a complex mechanism similar to cancer that promotes abnormal vascular growth and leads to the formation of CCMs. The mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) inhibitor rapamycin can effectively inhibit the formation of cavernous malformations in mouse models [4,5,9].

Non-coding RNAs have a key role in the development of cerebral vascular malformations by regulating gene expression and influencing angiogenesis, inflammation, vascular integrity, apoptosis, and oxidative stress. MicroRNAs (miR), as one of the important non-coding RNAs, have emerged as a potential value for diagnosis, prognostic prediction, and therapeutic use in CCMs [[10], [11], [12]]. Several microRNAs, including miR-181a-2-3p, miR-30c-2-3p, miR-3472a, miR-95-3p, miR-361-5p, miR-370-3p, and let-7b-5p, have been shown to link with CCMs [[13], [14], [15]]. A target site blocker that prevents the interaction of miR-27a with vascular endothelial cadherin can inhibit the increase in endothelial cell permeability and reduce lesion volume, as well as cerebral hemorrhage, in a mouse model of CCM [16].

Pericytes and neuroglia are also involved in the CCM pathogenesis as non-endothelial cells. In CCM lesions, pericytes interacting with endothelial cells can activate vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA)/(VEGFR) 2 signaling, leading to increased angiogenesis and vascular permeability [5,[17], [18], [19], [20]]. MicroRNAs, which can regulate gene expression and influence several CCM-associated pathological processes in both pericytes and endothelial cells, may serve as valuable targets for reducing hemorrhage in CCM disease.

MiR-21 has been identified as modulating apoptosis and inflammation involved in the response to oxidative stress and angiogenesis in ischemic stroke and glioblastoma [21,22]. Specifically, miR-21 inhibits the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) induced by nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase 4 (NOX4), thereby suppressing the development of endothelial cell tumors [23]. In bovine ovarian granulosa cells, miR-21-3p regulates autophagy through downregulating VEGFA expression and inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway [24]. In CCM disease, the role of miR-21-3p in cerebral hemorrhage remains unclear.

Zebrafish has emerged as a powerful vertebrate model for studying cerebrovascular development, blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity, and hemorrhage. Their optical transparency and genetic tractability enable real-time imaging of brain vascular dynamics, and transcriptomic mapping has revealed conserved endothelial subtypes involved in BBB formation [25,26]. Zebrafish recapitulate key features of mammalian cerebrovascular development and BBB function, supporting translational research [27]. They also exhibit spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage and innate recovery mechanisms, providing a platform to investigate hematoma resolution [28]. Importantly, CRISPR-mediated mosaic mutagenesis of ccm2 recapitulates human CCM features and has enabled identification of therapeutic targets [29]. Collectively, these features establish zebrafish as a physiologically relevant and well-recognized model for investigating cerebrovascular integrity and CCM pathogenesis.

In this study, we hypothesize that reduced expression of miR-21-3p in brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs) and pericytes contributes to cerebral hemorrhage in CCM by derepressing NOX4-mediated oxidative stress and VEGFA-driven angiogenesis; restoring miR-21-3p will stabilize vascular integrity and reduce cerebral hemorrhage. Our study combines clinical CCM lesions, an in vitro model of human BMECs and pericytes with CCM2 gene depletion, and an in vivo zebrafish model with CCM2 gene knockdown to identify this mechanism, suggesting that miR-21-3p may be a therapeutic target in CCM disease.

Materials and methods

The study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of our institution. The Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at our institution approved the zebrafish experiment.

Human CCM samples

The clinical, imaging, and histological data of 20 sporadic CCM patients in our hospital were analyzed in this study. These patients had received the initial surgical resection of the lesion without previous radiation therapy. They had the preoperative cranial MRI examination, excluding patients with extracranial CCM lesions, vascular malformation smaller than 1 cm, coexisting other neurological disorders, and mental illness.

The vascular malformation tissues were frozen in liquid nitrogen, immediately resectioned, and stored at −80°C. Then, RNA was extracted to detect the expression of miR-21-3p, CCM1, CCM2, and CCM3 in the cavernous malformation lesions using RT-PCR. The CCM tissue specimens were embedded in paraffin and sectioned for fluorescence in situ hybridization staining to detect the expression of miR-21-3p, NOX4, and VEGFA in the endothelial cells and pericytes of vascular malformation lesions. The lesions from five patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) and glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) who received their first surgical resection without radiation therapy history were used as the control group.

Cerebral hemorrhage on MR imaging

CCM patients underwent preoperative cranial MR examination on a 3.0T magnetic resonance scanner (Achieva TX, Philips Healthcare) with scanning sequence including T1-weighted imaging, T2-weighted imaging, T2 fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, 3D time-of-flight angiography, and susceptibility-weighted imaging.

The number of CCM lesions, location, size, and concomitant cerebral hemorrhage were analyzed. The hemorrhage manifestations of CCMs on MR have different signals around the vascular malformation lesion according to the acute, subacute, and chronic bleeding period. In this study, the hemorrhage manifestations of CCMs were defined as acute or subacute bleeding signals with a thickness greater than 4 mm around the CCM lesion on T2-weighted imaging, as in the previous study [30].

Fluorescent in situ hybridization and immunofluorescent

To determine the cell-type specific expression of miR-21-3p, NOX4, and VEGFA, we combined fluorescent in situ hybridization with immunofluorescent, as previously described [31,32]. In brief, paraffin-embedded CCM sections were prehybridized for 2 h at 50°C, followed by hybridization with a digoxin-labeled miR-21-3p probe (QIAGEN, Germany) overnight at the same temperature. The sections were then washed three times with saline-sodium citrate buffer at 37°C. Next, biotinylated mouse anti-digoxin antibody was applied and incubated for 2 h at room temperature, followed by rinsing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Subsequently, Streptavidin-Biotin System Fluorescein Isothiocyanate was added and incubated for 30 min at 37°C. To specifically label endothelial cells and pericytes, rabbit anti-CD31 antibody (1:50, ab215912, Abcam) and rabbit anti-platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) -β antibody (1:50, ab51745, R&D Systems) were added, followed by rabbit anti-NOX4 antibody (1:50, ab133303, Abcam) and rabbit anti-VEGFA antibody (1:50, ab51745, Abcam), incubating overnight at 4°C. After rewarming to room temperature for 45 min, the sections were washed with PBS for 5 min. Finally, a fluorescent secondary antibody was added dropwise in the absence of light and incubated for 1 h at 37°C. Subsequently, 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) was applied to stain the nuclei for 10 min. Images were captured using a Zeiss LSM800 confocal microscope, and the expression levels of miR-21-3p, NOX4, and VEGFA in endothelial cells and pericytes were quantified as average fluorescence intensity using ImageJ.

Human brain microvascular endothelial cells and pericytes

Primary human BMECs and brain pericytes were provided by Cell System (ACBRI 376, ACBRI 498), cultured in Complete Classic Medium (Cell systems,4Z0-500) or pericyte medium with 2% fetal bovine serum and supplements (Shanghai QiDa, P4001), and used at passage 3.

Small interfering RNA knockdown and miR-21-3p intervention in CCM2 gene-depleted human BMECs and pericytes

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) was used to knock down the CCM2 gene in the human BMECs and pericytes with Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, L3000-015) as previous studies [16]. The cells were maintained overnight, and the culture medium was changed the next day. For transfection with miR-21-3p overexpression or knockdown and NOX4 or VEGFA knockdown in the CCM2 gene-depleted cells, the siRNA transfection medium was maintained for 6-8 h. Then, cells were transfected with miR-21-3p mimic, miR-21-3p inhibitor, microRNA negative control, siRNA NOX4 with Lipofectamine 3000, or siRNA VEGFA with Lipofectamine 3000. Cells were maintained overnight and changed to culture medium the next day. Human BMECs and pericytes were collected 24 or 48 h after transfection for further RNA assay, protein assay, cell proliferation assay, cell migration assay, tube formation assay, permeability assay, and ROS measurement. The optimal concentrations of the miR-21-3p mimics and miR-21-3p inhibitors were determined as 50 nM and 100 nM, respectively. The optimal siRNA sequences for CCM2, NOX4, and VEGFA were determined and shown in Supplementary Table S1. The miR-21-3p mimics and inhibitors were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). siRNA genes were synthesized by Gene Pharma (Suzhou, China).

Zebrafish model with ccm2 gene knockdown

Both AB wild-type and Tg (Fli1:eGFP; Gata1:DsRed) transgenic zebrafish were utilized in this study. All the zebrafish were bred and housed in a pathogen-free environment in a vivarium. Knockdown and overexpression experiments were performed by injecting morpholino oligonucleotides (MO) or mimics into one-cell stage embryos.

The MOs of ccm2 and miR-21-3p were synthesized by Gene Tools (Philomath, OR, USA). The miR-21-3p mimics were synthesized by Gene Pharma (Suzhou, China). The amounts used for microinjection were 200 μM ccm2 MO, 200 μM control MO, 100 μM miR-21-3p MO, 10 μM miR-21-3p mimic, and 10 μM microRNA negative control. In the rescue experiment, ccm2 MO was injected simultaneously with miR-21-3p mimic. The sequence of ccm2 MO, miR-21-3p MO, and miR-21-3p mimics used for zebrafish are shown in Supplementary Table S2.

In vivo confocal imaging for zebrafish model

To block the pigment formation of zebrafish at 1 day post-fertilization (dpf), 0.003% 1-pheny1-2-thiourea (P7629, Sigma) was added to 10% Hanks' solution. Then, 2 mg/ml pronase (3115844001, Roche) was added to 10% Hanks' solution to remove the zebrafish chorion to unify the cerebrovascular development time of zebrafish. The zebrafish embryos at 3-dpf were anesthetized by incubating in 10% Hanks’ solution containing a final concentration of 0.003% tricaine (E10521, Sigma) and embedded in 1% low-melting agarose (16520050, Invitrogen), then were subjected to in vivo imaging studies with an Olympus laser confocal microscope.

Detection of cerebral hemorrhage, brain vascular integrity, cerebrovascular dilation, and angiogenesis in zebrafish model

The cerebral hemorrhage, brain vascular integrity, cerebrovascular dilation, and angiogenesis in 3 dpf transgenic zebrafish embryos with ccm2 gene knockdown after miR-21-3p intervention were determined by in vivo confocal imaging. The fluorescence images were analyzed with ImageJ. The blood cell leakage into the brain of each zebrafish larva was marked, and the cerebral hemorrhage area was calculated using ImageJ [33,34]. The area of the primordial midbrain channel (PMBC) and the vessel branch points of the subintestinal vein (SIV) were quantified to determine the changes in cerebrovascular dilation and angiogenesis in zebrafish [35,36].

A 10-nL DAPI with a concentration of 0.8 mg/mL (C1002, Beyotime) was microinjected into the common cardinal vein of zebrafish at 3dpf. At 30 min after microinjection, fluorescence images were taken by a confocal microscope. The number of DAPI-positive parenchymal nuclei located outside the cerebral vessel was quantified to determine the brain vascular integrity in zebrafish using ImageJ [33,37].

Quantitative Real-time PCR

The total RNA was extracted from human CCM lesions, BMECs, pericytes, and zebrafish tissue using Trizol (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The human brain total RNA was provided by Takara Bio (636530). PrimeScript Reverse Transcription Kit (RR036A for mRNA, RR037A for microRNA, Takara Bio) was used for cDNA synthesis. Quantitative Real-time PCR was performed on the Applied Biosystems PCR System with TB Green Premix Ex TaqII (RR820A, Takara Bio). U6 was used as the endogenous control for microRNA quantification, as it is stably expressed and widely accepted for microRNA normalization. β-actin was used as the internal control for mRNA quantification, given its stable expression in tissues and cell lines. The stability of these reference controls was verified across the experimental groups before analysis. Each human and zebrafish tissue sample represented one biological replicate. For cell-based experiments, independent transfections were performed in triplicate. qRT-PCR reaction was run in technical triplicate. The microRNA and mRNA expression were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method. The primer sequences of miR-21-3p and mRNA (CCM1, CCM2, CCM3, NOX4, and VEGFA) are synthesized by RiboBio and shown in Supplementary Table S3.

Western blot analysis

Proteins were isolated and quantified following standard protocols. Western blot assay was conducted in accordance with established procedures. Densitometric analysis was performed using ImageJ software. The following antibodies were employed for Western blotting: rabbit anti-NOX4 (1:1000, ab133303, Abcam), rabbit anti-VEGFA (1:1000, ab51745, Abcam), and rabbit anti-β-actin (1:1000, 12620, Cell Signaling Technology). HRP-linked secondary antibodies included goat anti-rabbit IgG HRP (1:1000, 7074, Cell Signaling Technology) and rabbit anti-goat IgG HRP (1:1000, BL004A, Biosharp). The expression of β-actin served as an internal reference.

Cell proliferation assay

The cholecystokinin octapeptide (CCK8) kit (CK04, DOJINDO, Japan) was employed to assess cell proliferation. Human BMECs and brain pericytes (1 × 103 cells/well) were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated overnight at 37°C. Following transfection, 10 μL of the CCK8 solution was added to each well, and the mixture was incubated for 1 h. Subsequently, the absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Tecan, Switzerland).

Cell migration assay

Cell migration assay was conducted using Transwell chambers (353096, Corning, USA). Following transfection, 2 × 104 cells were seeded in the upper chamber, and a medium without fetal bovine serum (FBS) was added. In the lower chamber, a medium containing 20% FBS was placed. After a 12-h incubation period, the chambers were fixed with methanol and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 15 min. The cells that migrated through the membrane were observed using a microscope (Olympus, Japan) and analyzed with ImageJ.

Tube formation assay

After transfection, human BMECs were inoculated at a density of 10,000 cells per well in 24-well plates pre-coated with Matrigel matrix (356234, Corning, USA) and cultured at 37°C for 12 h. The formed tubules were monitored using a microscope (Olympus, Japan), and the number of tubules and branch lengths were quantified with the Angiogenesis Analyzer for ImageJ.

Transwell permeability assay in endothelial cells

The permeability of cell monolayers to sodium fluorescein (Sigma, USA) was measured as described previously [16]. Briefly, pre-transfected cells were plated and allowed to achieve confluence on the upper of the Transwell membrane. Subsequently, 200 μL of sodium fluorescein (46960, Sigma, USA) was added to the upper chamber at 100 μg/mL and incubated for 30 min. The fluorescence of the lower chamber medium was then measured using an enzyme labeling instrument (Tecan, Switzerland).

Reactive oxygen species measurement

The 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) (D6883, Sigma, USA) and dihydroethidium (DHE) (D7008, Sigma, USA) were utilized to detect hydrogen peroxide and superoxide anions, respectively, as previously described [38]. Human BMECs and pericytes were seeded at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in 6-well plates and cultured overnight for transfection. Subsequently, the cells were incubated with 10 μM of DCFH-DA or DHE for 30 min at 37°C. After repeated rinsing several times, the cells were placed under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan) for observation, and the average fluorescence intensity was subsequently analyzed with ImageJ.

Luciferase reporter assay

A dual luciferase assay was performed to identify the target gene of miR-21-3p. Human embryonic kidney 293T cells were transfected with control and microRNA mimics for 48 h. Following this, cells were transfected with the pGL3-NOX4-3′-UTR firefly luciferase expression construct (Genechem, Shanghai, China) or a construct carrying a mutation of the predicted miR-21-3p binding site in pGL3-NOX4-3′-UTR, together with the Renilla luciferase pRL-CMV expression construct using Lipofectamine 3000. Cell lysates were assayed using a dual luciferase reporter assay kit (Promega, USA). The data are presented as the ratio of firefly to Renilla luciferase activity.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation and were analyzed using SPSS 31.0 software. One-way ANOVA or independent-samples t-tests were employed to examine normally distributed data. The Mann-Whitney non-parametric test was utilized for non-normally distributed data. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

MiR-21-3p reduces cerebral hemorrhage, vascular permeability, vascular dilation and angiogenesis in a zebrafish model with ccm2 gene knockdown

Cerebral hemorrhage phenotype was found after knocking down the ccm2 and miR-21-3p genes. The occurrence of obvious cerebral hemorrhage in our study was classified as the hemorrhage area in the brain of more than 500 μm2. Increased miR-21-3p expression reduced the zebrafish experiencing bleeding compared to the ccm2 and miR-21-3p knockdown group, as measured by the cerebral hemorrhage rate as well as the hemorrhage area (7% versus 92%, p < 0.001; Fig. 6B). These results underscore the crucial role of miR-21-3p in mitigating cerebral hemorrhage in zebrafish with ccm2 gene knockdown.

The significantly increased DAPI-positive nuclei of brain parenchymal cells were found after ccm2 and miR-21-3p gene knockdown compared to the control group (51.7 ± 17.3 versus 3.3 ± 1.2, p < 0.001; Fig. 6C). Increased miR-21-3p reduced the increased DAPI-positive cell nuclei in zebrafish brains compared to the ccm2 and miR-21-3p knockdown group (17.3 ± 9.6 versus 51.7 ± 17.3, p < 0.001; Fig. 6C). These results suggested that increased miR-21-3p reduced brain vascular permeability in a zebrafish model with ccm2 gene knockdown.

A significant increase in the PMBC area was found after ccm2 and miR-21-3p gene knockdown compared to the control group (8655.4 ± 2385.5 μm2 versus 5487.9 ± 2057.3 μm2, p < 0.001; Fig. 6D). Increased miR-21-3p suppressed the elevated PMBC area in zebrafish brains compared to the ccm2 and miR-21-3p knockdown group (5935.7 ± 828.9 μm2 versus 8655.4 ± 2385.5 μm2, p < 0.01; Fig. 6D). These results suggested that increased miR-21-3p attenuated vascular dilation in a zebrafish model with ccm2 gene knockdown.

The significantly increased SIV sprouting was found after ccm2 and miR-21-3p gene knockdown compared to the control group (14.4 ± 3.9 versus 8.1 ± 1.9, p < 0.001; Fig. 6E). MiR-21-3p mimic significantly inhibited the increase of SIV sprouting compared to the ccm2 and miR-21-3p knockdown group (8.3 ± 2.0 versus 14.4 ± 3.9, p < 0.001; Fig. 6E). These results suggested that increased miR-21-3p inhibited angiogenesis in a zebrafish model with ccm2 gene knockdown.

Discussion

In this study, we clarify the association of endothelial and pericyte miR-21-3p with cerebral hemorrhage in human CCM lesions, in the human BMEC and pericyte models with CCM2 gene depletion, and in a zebrafish model with CCM2 gene knockdown. In the CCM2 gene-depleted or knockdown state, increased miR-21-3p inhibits cell proliferation, permeability, ROS expression, cell migration, and tubule formation by downregulating NOX4 and VEGFA, thereby reducing cerebral hemorrhage, vascular permeability, vascular dilation, and angiogenesis in the zebrafish model. Our study reveals that miR-21-3p is a potential therapeutic target for stabilizing vascular integrity and reducing cerebral hemorrhage in CCMs by regulating NOX4 and VEGFA.

Our study found that the miR-21-3p/NOX4/ROS pathway in CCM2 gene-depleted BMECs and pericytes plays a crucial role in the cerebral hemorrhage of CCM disease. Increased NOX4 expression promotes excessive ROS production, leading to the dysfunction of BMECs and pericytes, which results in increased vascular permeability and cell proliferation. Upregulated NOX4 was significantly inhibited by intervention with the miR-21-3p mimic, resulting in a reduction of excessive ROS generation in the BMECs and pericytes and alleviation of cerebral hemorrhage in a zebrafish model. Previous studies have demonstrated that mutations causing functional loss in the CCM gene can lead to various effects, including defects in autophagy, alterations in ROS homeostasis, and increased sensitivity to oxidative stress and inflammation in CCM disease [39,40]. NOX4 is primarily produced by BMECs, with abundant expression in the brain. The pro-inflammatory cytokine tumor necrosis factor-alpha regulates the expression of NOX4 in BMECs, leading to an increase in ROS and cell apoptosis. The effect of knocking down NOX4 expression or inhibiting NOX4 activity can significantly alleviate oxidative stress and cell apoptosis caused by tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and has a protective effect on BMECs [[41], [42], [43]]. CCM1 gene dysfunction in a mouse model and endothelial cells upregulates NOX4 expression, leading to excessive ROS production in endothelial cells, which exacerbates the increased permeability of endothelial cells caused by various inflammatory stimuli and ultimately impairs vascular barrier function [44,45].

Cancer cells exhibit stronger oxidative stress due to elevated levels of NOX and mitochondrial dysfunction compared to normal cells. The level of ROS derived from NOX is significantly increased in cancer cells, while miR-21-3p, miR-34a, miR-137, and miR-99a targeting NOX are significantly downregulated in ROS-driven cancer [46]. MiR-21-3p functions as an endogenous inhibitor of NOX4, which is derived from endothelial cells. Upregulating miR-21-3p can inhibit the activity and expression of NOX4, thereby reducing the excessive generation of ROS induced by NOX4 and contributing to the alleviation of oxidative stress, as well as inhibiting the growth of endothelial cell tumors in mice [23]. Recent clinical studies indicate that tempol may effectively treat symptomatic CCMs by reducing ROS levels [47]. Our findings show that the miR-21-3p/NOX4/ROS pathway regulates functions in BMECs with the CCM2 gene deletion and similarly affects CCM2 gene-depleted pericytes, thereby broadening the potential value of microRNA in regulating the oxidative stress mechanism across multiple cell types to reduce fatal cerebral hemorrhage in CCM disease.

Recent studies have shown that emerging therapies, including anti-VEGF therapy, MAPK/ERK inhibitors, and RhoA/ROCK inhibitors, yield effective therapeutic outcomes in preclinical models for arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) and CCMs. Predictive and prognostic biomarkers, such as the candidate biomarker VEGF for AVM and CCMs, could improve the ability to personalize treatment decisions [6,11]. In the CCM1 gene-deficient endothelial cell, the increased expression of VEGFA results in enhanced cell migration. In a mouse model of CCM1 gene deficiency, a VEGFA receptor inhibitor reduces the number of CCM lesions, normalizes abnormal vascular permeability, and decreases hemorrhage volume by blocking the VEGF signaling pathway, thereby improving endothelial function and inhibiting angiogenesis [48]. Additionally, in a new CCM model with the Map3k3I441M knock-in mouse, the upregulated VEGFA expression leads to the progression of CCM lesions [49]. These studies imply that microRNA-based targeting of VEGF signaling therapies may have a critical role in the management of cerebral vascular malformations. In cerebral AVM lesions, the expression of miR-18a is downregulated in BMECs. MiR-18a can attenuate the abnormal proliferation and improve functional normalization of BMECs by reducing the secretion of VEGFA. In a mouse AVM model, administration of miR-18a mimics inhibits abnormal angiogenesis, thereby suppressing the progression of AVM lesions [50].

Our findings show that elevated VEGFA expression and decreased miR-21-3p expression in the BMECs and pericytes of human CCM lesions are associated with cerebral hemorrhage in CCM disease. The protective effect against hemorrhage with miR-21-3p arises from its ability to regulate the target gene of VEGFA, as shown in the CCM2 gene-depleted BMECs and pericytes model and a zebrafish model with CCM2 gene knockdown. A previous study has also suggested an interaction between miR-21-3p and VEGFA, in which miR-21-3p mimics inhibit PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling by targeting VEGFA expression, thereby suppressing autophagy in bovine ovarian granulosa cells [24]. Recent studies offer new insights into the regulation of the VEGF pathway across multiple cell types in CCM lesions, suggesting it as a potential therapeutic target for CCM. The interaction between endothelial cells and pericytes mediated by VEGFA/VEGFR2 signaling leads to increased angiogenesis and vascular permeability [17]. An increase in the numbers of monocytes, neutrophils, and nature killer cells has been observed in CCM lesions, with enhanced intercellular communication primarily through the VEGF pathway [51]. Our findings that miR-21-3p regulates VEGFA in BMECs and pericytes present a potential therapeutic opportunity for reducing cerebral hemorrhage by targeting multiple cells in CCM lesions, with miR-21-3p as a target.

Several canonical pathways have been implicated in CCM pathogenesis, including KLF2/4, RhoA/ROCK, and PI3K/AKT/mTOR [[7], [8], [9]]. Our findings identify miR-21-3p as a novel regulator of NOX4-mediated oxidative stress and VEGFA-dependent angiogenesis in CCM. Importantly, these downstream effectors intersect with the canonical pathways at multiple levels. NOX4-derived ROS are known activators of RhoA/ROCK signaling, suggesting that miR-21-3p may modulate RhoA/ROCK activity indirectly through NOX4 regulation [52]. VEGFA is a well-established upstream activator of PI3K/AKT/mTOR in endothelial cells, indicating that the miR-21-3p/VEGFA axis may feed into the PI3K/mTOR pathway [24,53,54]. KLF2/4 have been shown to regulate the expression of multiple microRNAs in endothelial cells, raising the possibility that KLF2/4 may transcriptionally control miR-21-3p expression in CCM [40,55]. Based on these observations, the miR-21-3p/NOX4/VEGFA axis may intersect with canonical CCM pathways at multiple levels. Future studies using pathway-specific inhibitors in CCM models are needed to dissect the hierarchical relationships between miR-21-3p and these established pathways.

The therapeutic application of miR-21-3p requires efficient in vivo delivery systems, as naked microRNAs are unstable, rapidly degraded, and poorly internalized by target cells [56]. Current strategies include viral vectors, non-viral nanocarriers, and exosomes [56,57]. Lipid nanoparticles are the most clinically advanced platform, validated by siRNA therapeutics and mRNA vaccines [58,59]. For intracranial delivery, which is relevant to CCMs, exosomes offer low immunogenicity and natural BBB permeability, whereas viral vectors enable sustained expression following local administration [33,57,60,61]. Future studies would evaluate these platforms in CCM mouse models to establish the most effective and safe delivery strategy for miR-21-3p-based therapy.

Our study has certain limitations. The protective role of miR-21-3p in reducing hemorrhage was assessed exclusively in the zebrafish model, without validation in a Ccm2ECKO, Ccm1ECKO, Map3k3I441M mouse model of CCM disease, which limits the translation strength of our finding. The assessment using these mammalian models is more effective for evaluating the therapeutic effect following the CCM lesion state [9,16,49]. However, zebrafish offered unique advantages for this study, as optical transparency enabled real-time imaging of hemorrhage, and genetic tractability allowed rapid dissection of microRNA-target interactions. These features complement, rather than replace, mammalian models. Future validation using conditional knockout mouse models or patient-derived mammalian endothelial cells and pericytes is needed to confirm the therapeutic potential of the miR-21-3p/NOX4 and miR-21-3p/VEGFA axes in CCM disease.

The clinical component of this study is based on a relatively small cohort of 20 CCM patients. This sample size may limit the statistical power and generalizability of our findings. Larger, multicenter prospective cohorts are needed to confirm the clinical relevance of the miR-21-3p/NOX4 and miR-21-3p/VEGFA axes. Our study demonstrates that NOX4 and VEGFA are direct targets of miR-21-3p, and rescue experiments indicate that they are the primary functional mediators of the observed phenotypes. However, given that microRNAs often have multiple targets, we cannot completely exclude the possibility that other miR-21-3p target genes may also contribute to the development and rupture of CCM lesions. Future transcriptome-wide studies, such as RNA sequencing in mammalian CCM models, will help to map the complete regulatory network of miR-21-3p in CCM. Recent studies suggest that multiple cell types contribute to the pathogenesis of CCM disease [17,51,62]. MiR-21-3p may be involved in the cross-talk process between these cells during the development of CCM and its associated bleeding, in addition to the roles of endothelial cells and pericytes observed in our study, which warrants further research to clarify.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrates that miR-21-3p regulates vascular integrity in CCM by modulating NOX4-mediated oxidative stress and VEGFA-driven angiogenesis. Loss of miR-21-3p contributes to hemorrhage, while its restoration stabilizes vascular function, as shown in human CCM lesions, Ccm2-depleted human cells, and a Ccm2-knockdown zebrafish model. Thus, miR-21-3p could be a potential therapeutic target for CCM, bridging BMEC and pericyte biology with translational potential to reduce cerebral hemorrhage.

Value-added assessment of career planning for vocational competence based on deep learning
Authors: Wen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the static limitations of current career assessment methods and the lack of a long-term perspective in planning strategies, this study constructs an intelligent analysis framework named Deep learning-driven Value-added Career Analytics Model (DV-CAM). Integrating Deep Learning (DL) with value-added assessment, the framework aims to realize dynamic and accurate evaluation of individual professional competencies and generate personalized career planning paths that maximize the benefits of long-term development. Specifically, DV-CAM first leverages Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to extract and dynamically update individual competency profiles from multi-source time-series data. Second, it designs a value-added evaluation index system covering the absolute level of competency, growth slope, growth curvature and stability to quantitatively diagnose development potential. Finally, under the framework of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), this study takes competency status, value-added potential and job requirements as joint state inputs, and solves for the optimal sequence of career development actions by optimizing long-term cumulative rewards. Experiments based on the public Occupational Information Network (O*NET) dataset show that DV-CAM achieves a mean squared error of 0.0312 and a mean absolute error of 0.1289 for competency assessment on the test set. In planning tasks, its long-term cumulative reward and target job matching degree improvement rate reach 185.3% and 42.7% respectively, both outperforming various benchmark models. The results demonstrate that DV-CAM can diagnose competency status more accurately, and effectively guide individuals to achieve long-term career development goals through its forward-looking planning strategies. By combining the concept of value-added assessment with advanced deep learning technologies, this study provides a data-driven, dynamically perceivable and long-term oriented new paradigm for the fields of career planning and competency assessment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46485-y.

Introduction

In today’s rapidly evolving job market, the complexity of career decision-making has become increasingly prominent. This challenge is particularly pronounced in fields such as economics, which are highly dependent on quantitative and analytical abilities1,2. Students need to navigate the extensive knowledge system across multiple subfields and clarify their professional positioning in a highly competitive employment environment3,4. Traditional career counselling methods mostly rely on general advice and struggle to fully consider students’ personalized characteristics, such as unique skill sets, academic backgrounds, and development potential5,6. Therefore, constructing a data-driven method for career planning and competence assessment that can dynamically adapt to individual needs has become an important and urgent research task.

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies, especially machine learning and Deep Learning (DL), have demonstrated significant potential in the fields of career planning and educational assessment7. These technologies can mine underlying patterns from multi-dimensional data, providing individuals with more targeted development recommendations8,9. However, existing studies mostly focus on static competence matching or recommendations based on historical preferences, lacking modeling of the dynamic evolution laws of competence and systematic optimization of long-term career benefits10. In addition, students face various practical challenges in career development, such as the disconnect between theoretical knowledge and applied skills, the lack of professional network resources, the high demand for advanced quantitative analysis capabilities, and the ever-changing employment market environment11,12. These issues further highlight the necessity of constructing an integrated model that integrates dynamic assessment, potential analysis, and long-term planning13,14.

It should be noted that although existing studies have generally revealed the potential algorithmic biases and ethical risks in intelligent career recommendation systems, this study does not directly conduct explicit constraint modeling for fairness at the model level. Instead, the fairness principle is treated as an important research motivation and constraint background in system design and evaluation. The core objective of this study is to verify the theoretical feasibility and long-term optimization capability of a career planning analysis framework integrating deep learning, value-added evaluation and reinforcement learning, thereby laying a methodological foundation for the subsequent introduction of interpretability and fairness constraint mechanisms.

Literature review

With the increasing dynamics and complexity of the labor market, the use of AI technologies for scientific career planning and competence assessment has become a cutting-edge topic of common concern in both academic and industrial circles. Sun et al. (2025) achieved a significant paradigm breakthrough15. They proposed a market-aware long-term career skill recommendation framework that innovatively adopted DRL to simulate the long-term utility of skill acquisition, integrating salary returns and learning costs into the optimization objectives. Liu et al. (2025) conducted a critical analysis of the limitations of existing fit measurement methods, which only focused on a single domain (e.g., interests or skills)16. Based on the Occupational Information Network database, they developed and validated, for the first time, a holistic fit assessment system covering five core domains: interests, values, skills, knowledge, and personality. Through large-sample empirical research, their study demonstrated that cross-domain integrated assessment could significantly improve the predictive power of career success outcomes. It marked a theoretical advancement of career assessment from “skill matching” to “holistic person-job fit,” laying a solid theoretical foundation for constructing more comprehensive assessment models. As AI technologies play an increasingly central role in career decision-making, the fairness and ethics of their decisions have become unavoidable issues. A systematic review of algorithmic bias in recruitment by Albaroudi et al. (2024) pointed out that while AI improved efficiency, it was also prone to replicating and amplifying social biases embedded in historical data17. Their research summarized effective technical mitigation strategies such as vector space correction and data augmentation, and proposed a forward-looking ethical framework of “human-AI collaboration.” This provided crucial ethical guidance for ensuring the fair, transparent, and responsible development of intelligent career support systems. At the practical application level, an exploratory study by Huong (2025) demonstrated the direct application potential of machine learning and DL models in career path prediction18. By comparing various traditional machine learning algorithms with DL models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), his study confirmed the superiority of DL in capturing complex temporal and non-linear patterns in multi-source data (e.g., academic performance, skills, and interests). This provided a feasible technical pathway for transitioning from static assessment to dynamic, personalized career prediction.

In summary, while existing studies have achieved significant progress, there remains room for further exploration. Firstly, the dynamic skill recommendation proposed by Sun et al. and the holistic fit assessment developed by Liu et al. have not been effectively integrated. A comprehensive framework that simultaneously considers market dynamics, long-term benefits, and individuals’ multi-dimensional traits (e.g., values, personality) is still lacking. Secondly, although Huong’s research verified the accuracy of prediction models, its recommendation results are still inferior to Sun et al.’s interpretable DRL framework in terms of interpretability and long-term strategic value. Finally, how to deeply embed the fairness principles emphasized by Albaroudi et al. into the above complex recommendation and assessment systems is crucial to ensuring their widespread application. Therefore, this study aims to bridge these gaps and explore the construction of a new-generation intelligent system for career planning and competence assessment, integrating market awareness, holistic person assessment, long-term planning, and fairness constraints.

Research model

This section elaborates on the career planning and competence assessment model based on DL and value-added assessment. The model aims to dynamically track the evolution of individuals’ competence, quantify its growth value, and ultimately generate personalized career development paths. The overall framework of the model is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1Overall framework of model.

Overall framework of model.

It mainly consists of three core components: Firstly, a dynamic competence perception module based on multi-source data fusion and DL, which is responsible for extracting multi-dimensional competence profiles from raw behavioral data. Secondly, an assessment system that incorporates the value-added assessment theory to quantify the growth and potential of competence. Finally, a sequential recommendation model integrated with market job requirements to generate career planning strategies oriented toward long-term benefits.

Path generation of long-term career planning based on DRL

The goal of this module is to utilize the output of the preceding modules to generate a development path for individuals that maximizes their long-term career benefits. This problem is modeled as a sequential decision-making process and solved using the DRL framework19. The DRL framework is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Fig. 2DRL framework.

DRL framework.

The proposed career planning path generation framework based on DRL achieves decision optimization through continuous interaction between the agent and the simulated environment. The framework first initializes the deep Q-network and experience replay buffer, then starts the interaction loop between the agent and the environment: At each time step, the agent selects development actions via an ε-greedy strategy based on the current state (including individual competence profiles, value-added evaluation, and target job requirements). After receiving the action, the environment returns an immediate reward and new state, and stores this interaction experience in the buffer28. The system periodically samples experience data from the buffer for training, updates online network parameters by calculating target Q-values and minimizing mean squared error, and synchronizes the target network regularly to ensure training stability. After sufficient iteration of this “interaction-learning” closed-loop process, an optimal strategy that maximizes long-term career benefits is finally output, generating a customized sequence of development paths for individuals29.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study adopts the public Occupational Information Network (O*NET) database as the core data source. O*NET is a comprehensive occupational database sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, containing standardized and structured descriptive information for over 1,000 occupations. The link is: https://www.onetonline.org/. Multi-dimensional data required for model construction is extracted from it, specifically including:

Vocational competence requirement data: The level requirements of each occupation for various types of knowledge, skills, and abilities are obtained. These constitute the target job requirement vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{r}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{q}}_{j}$$\end{document} in the model.

Occupational characteristic data: Information related to occupations, such as values, interest areas, and work styles, is collected to assist in constructing the overall occupational profile.

Salary and market data: Market information including the median salary and growth rate of occupations is selected. This serves as the calculation basis for the market return \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{U}_{\mathrm{gain}}$$\end{document} in the reward function of DRL.

To simulate individuals’ dynamic career development trajectories, a dataset containing 10,000 virtual career profiles is synthesized based on the data relationships in O*NET. Each profile includes time-series data spanning 5 years (T = 20 quarters), specifically: Text descriptions of project experiences and work logs, used to simulate the input of Module 1/ Skill labels and performance scores obtained each quarter. Job change sequences and corresponding salary changes. The dataset is randomly split into a training set, validation set, and test set at a ratio of 7:2:1.

It should be specifically noted that the career development trajectory dataset constructed in this paper is synthetic data generated based on the relational data of ONET. The original design intention of this dataset is not to directly replicate the real labor market, but to serve as a controlled experimental environment for verifying the internal consistency, stability and long-term planning capability of the DV-CAM model in the context of complex dynamic decision-making problems. In the real world, an individual’s career development trajectory is often affected by a variety of uncontrollable random factors, such as fluctuations in the macroeconomic cycle (e.g., economic recession or industrial prosperity), structural disruptions in industries (e.g., the replacement of traditional jobs by emerging technologies), sudden organizational adjustments, as well as individual-level events including health issues, family changes and geographical migration. Such factors are typically characterized by suddenness, nonlinearity and low predictability, and cannot be fully depicted through the rule-based variables in standardized occupational knowledge bases (e.g., ONET). In contrast, the synthetic data constructed based on O*NET features strong structural consistency and standardization in terms of occupational competency requirements, job evolution logic and skill transfer paths, making it more suitable for analyzing the behavioral patterns of the model in an idealized labor market environment. Therefore, the experimental results of this paper mainly reflect the theoretical validity of DV-CAM under the constraints of structured occupational rules, rather than direct predictions of real career development outcomes.

Performance evaluation

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed DV-CAM model, comparative experiments are conducted between DV-CAM and multiple baseline models on the training, validation, and test sets. The evaluation focuses on two core dimensions: accuracy of competence assessment and effectiveness of career planning. The baseline models for comparison include:

Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)-Baseline: A static competence assessment model using a multilayer perceptron.

LSTM-Only: A competence assessment model that solely employs a temporal model, without incorporating value-added evaluation or DRL-based planning.

GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)-based Competence Model: A variant model that leverages GRU for temporal competency modeling, designed to compare with LSTM in terms of temporal dynamic capture capability. Through a more streamlined gating mechanism (consisting of an update gate and a reset gate), GRU filters and transmits historical information, and often demonstrates advantages in parameter efficiency and training stability.

Transformer-based Temporal Model: A temporal competency representation model constructed based on Transformer encoder layers, which utilizes a self-attention mechanism to capture long-term dependencies within sequences. This model is independent of recurrent structures, suitable for parallelized computation, and performs excellently in many long-sequence modeling tasks.

DV-CAM w/o VA (Without Value-added): An ablation experiment model with the value-added evaluation module removed.

DV-CAM w/o DRL (Without DRL): An ablation experiment model that adopts a greedy strategy instead of DRL for career planning.

Accuracy comparison of ability evaluation

This study systematically compares the competency assessment performance of the DV-CAM model and each benchmark model on the training set, validation set and test set by means of two metrics, MSE and MAE, with the results presented in Fig. 3a–c.

Fig. 3Error comparison of model capability evaluation. (a) Train Set, (b) Validation Set, (c) Test Set.

Error comparison of model capability evaluation. (a) Train Set, (b) Validation Set, (c) Test Set.

On the training set (Fig. 3a), all models are fully trained. The DV-CAM model achieved the lowest MSE (0.0297) and MAE (0.1253), demonstrating the strongest fitting capability. In comparison with the static MLP-Baseline model (MSE = 0.0452, MAE = 0.1589), its temporal variant LSTM-Only (MSE = 0.0321, MAE = 0.1325) yielded reduces errors, which confirms the necessity of temporal modeling for dynamic competency perception. Among the three temporal architectures, the GRU-based model (MSE = 0.0318) performs slightly better than LSTM-Only, while the Transformer-based model (MSE = 0.0320) showed a comparable performance. This indicates that recurrent neural networks still possess favorable representational efficiency in medium-length sequence modeling tasks. The two ablation models (DV-CAM w/o VA and w/o DRL) exhibits performance between that of LSTM-Only and the full DV-CAM model, which demonstrates that the joint optimization of the value-added assessment and deep reinforcement learning modules indirectly improves the representation quality of competency states.

On the validation set (Fig. 3b), the performance ranking of all models is consistent with that on the training set, while the errors generally rise slightly. DV-CAM still maintains the optimal performance (MSE = 0.0308, MAE = 0.1275), and the performance gap between DV-CAM and the ablation models widens further, which indicates that the full model possesses superior generalization ability and robustness to unseen data. Notably, the GRU-based model remains stably superior to LSTM-Only on the validation set, demonstrating certain structural advantages.

On the test set (Fig. 3c), the DV-CAM model outperforms all benchmark models with an MSE of 0.0312 and an MAE of 0.1289, ultimately verifying its assessment accuracy. Compared with the MLP-Baseline, DV-CAM reduces the MSE and MAE by approximately 33.8% and 20.7%, respectively. Even in comparison with the GRU-based model, the best-performing temporal benchmark model, it still achieved reductions of about 2.8% and 4.9% in the two metrics. This fully demonstrates that the integrated framework of DV-CAM, which combines dynamic perception, value-added assessment and long-term planning, exhibits distinct advantages in the accurate evaluation of individual professional competencies.

In summary, the experimental results demonstrate that the DV-CAM model outperforms traditional static methods and single temporal models and their ablation variants in competency assessment tasks, thus exhibiting its effectiveness as an integrated and forward-looking assessment tool.

Analysis on the effectiveness of career planning

The effectiveness of career planning is measured by two indicators: long-term cumulative reward and target job matching degree improvement rate. Among them, the long-term cumulative reward is the direct optimization objective of the DRL framework, while the matching degree improvement rate evaluates the practical effect of the planned path in helping individuals approach their target occupations. A comparison of career planning performance is shown in Fig. 4.

From the results of the training set, validation set, and test set, it shows that in the planning task, the long-term cumulative reward and target job matching degree improvement rate of the DV-CAM model reach 185.3 and 42.7%, respectively, which are superior to other models. This proves that its internal DRL framework has a strong ability to balance short-term costs and long-term benefits, and can formulate career development paths with a more strategic perspective.

Key role of value-added evaluation: A comparison between DV-CAM and DV-CAM w/o VA (Without Value-added) reveals that removing the value-added evaluation module leads to a significant drop in both the model’s cumulative reward and matching degree improvement rate. This indicates that the value-added evaluation module is one of the core contributions of the DV-CAM model to its excellent long-term planning performance, as it can effectively identify and invest in competences with high growth potential.

Advantages of DRL-based planning: The performance of the DV-CAM w/o DRL model (which adopts a greedy strategy) is far inferior to that of the complete model. This demonstrates that decision-making based solely on short-term interests cannot achieve global optimality, highlighting the necessity of using DRL for sequential decision-making.

Verification of practical effectiveness: In terms of the matching degree improvement rate, the DV-CAM model also achieves the best performance. This proves that the paths recommended by DV-CAM can most effectively help individuals narrow the competence gap with their target occupations, indicating significant practical application value.

In summary, the experimental results fully verify the superiority of the proposed DV-CAM model from two dimensions: “assessment accuracy” and “planning effectiveness.” Not only can it diagnose individuals’ competence states more accurately, but it can also generate personalized schemes that maximize long-term career development benefits through its unique value-added perspective and forward-looking planning capabilities.

Discussion

The experimental results indicate that the outstanding performance of the DV-CAM model stems from the synergistic effect of each module in its architecture. The improvement in competence assessment accuracy benefits not only from the representational capability of DL but, more crucially, from the value-added evaluation and DRL planning modules driving the front-end network to learn more predictive feature representations during joint training, enabling the competence profile to contain information on development potential. Value-added evaluation has been proven to be the core bridge connecting static assessment and dynamic planning. It allows the model to identify and invest in “potential stock” competences that currently have low levels but enormous growth potential, which is the key to the model achieving higher long-term cumulative rewards. At the practical application level, the model demonstrates potential as an auxiliary decision-making tool for career development. However, its performance is still limited by the difficulty in acquiring high-quality time-series data, and the decision transparency of the DRL model needs further improvement. These findings verify the theoretical value of the model and reveal the challenges in its real-world application.

Although this study has not yet incorporated real-world data on individuals’ career trajectories, the DV-CAM model is inherently endowed with a certain degree of noise absorption and uncertainty tolerance capability in terms of its structural design. Specifically, first, LSTM-based time series modeling does not merely fit the competency level at a single time point. Instead, it accumulates historical information through hidden states, thereby smoothing short-term abnormal fluctuations to a certain extent and exhibiting inherent robustness to sporadic competency declines or discontinuous changes. Second, the value-added evaluation module does not make decisions solely based on the absolute level of competencies, but comprehensively considers indicators such as growth slope, growth curvature and stability. Among these, the stability indicator quantifies the volatility of the competency evolution process via the coefficient of variation, enabling the model to automatically reduce over-investment in competency dimensions with high volatility and low stability during the planning phase, thus mitigating the interference of noise at the strategic level. Third, in the deep reinforcement learning phase, DV-CAM does not perform path planning based on the deterministic transition assumption; instead, it learns statistical response strategies to environmental uncertainty through multi-round interactions by maximizing the long-term cumulative reward. This decision-making mechanism based on expected returns allows the model to still tend to select career development paths with robust long-term benefits even in the presence of random perturbations.

Conclusion

Research contribution

By constructing the DV-CAM model, this study makes three significant contributions to the field of career planning and competence assessment: (1) it innovatively integrates deep temporal models, value-added evaluation, and DRL, establishing an end-to-end framework that spans from dynamic competence perception to long-term path planning; (2) it designs a multi-dimensional value-added evaluation indicator system encompassing competence level, growth slope, curvature, and stability, realizing a paradigm shift from static assessment to the measurement of development potential; (3) through systematic experimental validation, the study demonstrates the model’s significant advantages in assessment accuracy and planning effectiveness and deeply reveals the contribution mechanism of each module via ablation experiments. This provides a solid theoretical and practical foundation for future research.

Future works and research limitations

The research in the current stage of this study is still primarily conducted in an experimental environment based on theoretical simulation and rule-driven design, and the generalizability of its conclusions is subject to certain limitations in the context of real labor markets. As the research relies entirely on synthetic career trajectory data generated from O*NET relational data, the model has not been directly exposed to the inherently high-noise, incomplete-information, and shock-prone environment embedded in real human career behaviors. Therefore, the actual decision-making performance of DV-CAM in practical applications remains to be further verified using real longitudinal data.

From the perspective of research paradigm, the contribution of this study is more inclined to propose an extensible theoretical analysis framework and methodological path. It demonstrates that under the constraints of idealized career evolution rules, the systematic integration of deep learning, value-added evaluation, and reinforcement learning can logically achieve the unified modeling of “dynamic competency assessment-potential identification-long-term path optimization”.

In future research, on the one hand, priority will be given to incorporating longitudinal career development data of real individuals (e.g., employment tracking data of college graduates, internal career promotion records of enterprises, or long-term resume evolution data) to verify the robustness of the model in high-noise real-world environments. On the other hand, stochastic environmental models that are more consistent with the dynamics of the real labor market will be constructed by explicitly introducing random shock variables into the simulation environment (e.g., economic cycle disturbances, job elimination probabilities, or competency degradation mechanisms), thereby gradually narrowing the gap between theoretical simulation and practical application.

In addition, although this paper focuses on algorithmic bias and ethical issues at the theoretical level, the current version of DV-CAM has not yet incorporated a quantifiable fairness constraint mechanism into the model. In future research, based on real career development data, it will explore feasible paths for introducing group fairness indicators (e.g., opportunity fairness or outcome equity) within the deep reinforcement learning framework, and study the trade-off relationship between fairness constraints and long-term utility maximization, thereby promoting the evolution of the model from “competency-neutral decision-making” to “fairness-aware decision-making”.

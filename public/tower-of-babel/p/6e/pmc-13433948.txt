Design and implementation of a novel multilevel inverter for renewable energy applications
Authors: C. Dhanamjayulu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
For renewable energy applications, an optimal output with lower power electronics components requires an efficient power electronic circuit. In this paper, a novel asymmetrical thirteen-one level multilevel inverter with ideal components is suggested for use in renewable energy applications. It uses asymmetrical DC sources and fewer circuit components to produce thirteen-one output levels. This arrangement lowers the inverter’s overall size and cost. An input from a solar PV source powers the suggested inverter. A PV standalone system needs an MPPT technique to generate a stable output using the P&O algorithm in order to maintain a constant DC voltage output from the solar panels. To increase the solar PV voltage, a four-level DC–DC boost converter is used. Several dynamic tests are assessed under various loading circumstances in order to evaluate the inverter’s performance under both static and dynamic loading conditions. The suggested MLI uses less voltage stress between the switches, as demonstrated by calculations of total standing voltage (TSV) and several comparisons with contemporary designs. The suggested system is superior to the current topologies when the cost function (CF) is computed and compared. Additionally, the number of switches, sources, gate driver boards, diodes, and component count level factor are compared; the suggested architecture is found to be the most advantageous of these options. Both modelling and experiment yielded a total harmonic distortion (THD) of 3.2%, which is within IEEE guidelines. A hardware prototype is used for experimental testing of the suggested inverter, which was designed using MATLAB/Simulink.

Introduction

Carbon-based fuels become more expensive the faster greenhouse emissions grow1 in now days. Furthermore, the implementation of this scenario will cause a significant rise in pollution effects and climate change2. Since it creates zero emissions, solar PV systems are very convenient to operate since they don’t require fossil fuels3. Many people utilize it as a source of electricity for both household and industrial purposes due to its widespread application4. As the solar PV plays a major role as a source, the performance evaluation of solar standalone systems utilizing multilevel inverter with a single source are attracted5–7.

To produce electricity, solar panels convert sunlight into energy and provide power to a bus, which is then converted to DC using DC–DC converter is, reported8. The DC-AC converter is provided with load by connecting to a DC voltage source as input. Additionally, solar PV production fluctuates due to solar irradiation and temperature is discussed9. The term MPPT refers to maintaining PV modules in the state where they are harvesting maximum power, which helps maximize efficiency and effectiveness amid shifting climatic circumstances each year10. To optimize the amount of power that may be sent into a system, a DC–DC converter is used11. The implementation of a inductor less multilevel inverter structure produces 13 levels in its output voltage which reduces the component count are presented in12.

DC-AC inverter plays a vital role when it comes to solar PV systems, and that is why MLI has opted to utilize a DC-AC inverter to power the system, rather than a voltage source inverter when there is a DC-AC inverter on board13. The circuit has more advantages compared to Flying Capacitor (FC), Cascaded H-Bridge (CHB), and Neutral Point Clamped (NPC) inverters. MLI, which boosts the overall cost and circuit complexity, causes the circuit to have a greater number of components14. However, both FC MLI and NPC MLI are restricted to capacitors with identical values and voltage ratings15. The efficiency of the system is lowered with the input voltage and resulting in a higher switching frequency. At the cost of the system gets increased16. Many studies from the literature provides several new multilevel inverter topologies in order to improve the performance of the system which is suitable for renewable energy systems. Particularly about the optimization of the switching angles and the operation of fault toleration as well. These type of configurations with cascaded inverters consisting of less THD and high voltage gain are presented in17–20. The various isolated and non-isolated converter topologies are discussed in21.

A new reduced switch count type multilevel inverter topology applied for domestic solar PV applications is presented in24 where the topology utilizes less number of switches and operated in both symmetric and asymmetric operation for producing nine and thirteen levels in the output. MLIs are currently being considered as an industrial solution for high power quality and dynamic performance demanding systems, spanning through a power range of 1–30 MW25 Also, some topologies may be used to assist standalone solar systems in better handle the difficulty of controlling the DC–DC converter. Auto-control over training data, using PSO, GA, and fuzzy logic was utilized to create an auto-control about the training data, thus allowing for the power to be regulated. It is a genuine mystery as to which of the several MPPT techniques will work best for each of these applications notwithstanding their intricacy. Because they can produce low THD output voltage waveforms and higher voltages with a constrained device rating, MLIs are therefore perfect to be used in high voltage applications26. Sustainable energy sources like wind, fuel cells, and PV cells can interact with a multilevel converter system in a variety of ways27 and conventional techniques like fuzzy, INC, P&O, and fuzzy algorithms cannot extract global MPP (GMPP) under partial shading conditions. The PWM of MLIs is primarily controlled by the type of control algorithm used to define their functioning, efficiency, power ratings, and application28. For MPPT systems, it is crucial to maintain an appropriate output level due to changes in temperature, load, and solar radiation. In addition to changing throughout the day, climatic patterns also change with the seasons29.

A generalized switched-capacitor multilevel inverter topology with reduced switches is represented in30 where the topology consists of 18 switches with 4 capacitors and two DC sources in which eight number of maximum number of conducting devices and therefore the total standing voltage (TSV) of the topology becomes higher & the losses are high with which the overall cost of the inverter gets increased. The topology presented in31 is switched-capacitor based multilevel inverter for energy storage and smart grid applications where the topology consists of 12 switches with four capacitors and three DC sources in which four number of maximum conducting devices, the TSV is quite high on the switches and the overall cost of the system gets increased. A cross-switched type multilevel inverter is presented in32, it consists of 16 switches, two diodes and four capacitors excited with two DC sources providing high TSV value. A cascaded multilevel inverter integrated with wind energy conversion system is presented in33 operated with ten switches excited with four DC sources and eight number of maximum conducting switches with a nominal TSV value where the stress on the switches is low and the overall cost of the system is low compared existing MLIs. A 31-level multilevel inverter topology with reduced components is presented in34 operates with 14 switches and the circuit is excited with four DC sources with seven maximum conduction devices with an acceptable TSV value which makes the system economical.

In this paper, a renewable energy fed 31-level multilevel inverter with transformer less and transformer-based architectures are designed with a three-level DC–DC boost converter for the solar PV system. The topology is superior to the existing topologies in various parameters like switches count, overall devices count, TSV, cost function. To help manage renewable power output from the solar panels, a P&O-powered MPPT (maximum power point tracking) is utilized. To generate a certain output level, the DC voltage generated by the solar panels connected to the four-level boost converter is utilized to boost the voltage. The boosted voltage is then sent to a proposed 31-level inverter. To measure the MLI’s performance, various parameters such as power losses, efficiency, THD, cost factor, and total stand voltage (TSV), are employed. Several comparisons are made with other modern existing topologies with renewable energy systems and found that the proposed MLI holds best in all parameters. The P&O based MPPT in the developed system is implemented due to its simplicity in implementation. The algorithm works based on the perturbing the operating voltage of the photovoltaic system and observing the variation in output power. Whenever the power gets increased in the system, is hereby observed by the following perturbation, the respective operating point has been shifted from the same direction. If the power gets decreased, the direction of the respective perturbation becomes reversed. This process of iteration is continued until the optimal and efficient MPPT is reached. Hence the step size of perturbation is selected to have a balance between speed of tracking and oscillations. The MPPT provides a reference signal which varies the duty cycle of the DC–DC boost converter, which maintains the maximum power point operation for variable irradiation.

An appropriate method must be employed to track maximum power when the PV module is operating. This may be observed in a solar cell’s P-V graph. There are several ways for determining maximum power, including perturb and observe, incremental conductance, fractional open-circuit voltage, genetic algorithm, and so on. The perturb and observe technique offers several advantages in this study. It is simple to implement with many controllers such as Arduino, microcontrollers, and so forth. By changing the perturbation value, the maximum power point determination speed may be adjusted. Figure 2d represents the P&O algorithm.

Figure 1 illustrates the transformer-less configuration of the developed asymmetrical 31-level multilevel inverter integrated with the solar PV system. This configuration offers reduced cost, lower component count, and higher efficiency due to the absence of a transformer. Figure 2 presents the transformer-based configuration of the developed topology, which provides electrical isolation and improved safety, making it suitable for grid-connected and high-power applications. While the transformer less configuration is more advantageous in terms of compactness, efficiency, and cost, it lacks galvanic isolation. In contrast, the transformer-based configuration ensures isolation and enhanced protection but introduces additional cost, size, and losses. Therefore, the choice between the two configurations depends on the specific application requirements.

Conclusion

This paper provides the design and implementation of a renewable energy-fed asymmetrical 31-level three-phase MLI topology aimed at reducing system cost, size and THD in solar photovoltaic (PV) applications. The developed inverter achieves a high number of output voltage levels with a reduced component count by minimizing the switching losses and overall improvement in efficiency of the system. An effective MPPT technique based on the P&O algorithm is integrated to provide the optimal power extraction from the PV system under varying irradiation conditions along with partial shading. The developed topology demonstrates superior performance in reducing TSV, improved voltage quality and enhanced efficiency when compared with existing MLI configurations. The results with respect to simulation and experimental validate the effectiveness of the developed system, achieving a THD of 3.2%, which is under IEEE standards and a overall efficiency of 93.07%, which is suited for renewable energy and grid connected applications. Furthermore, the developed topology is extended to higher levels and by providing intelligent and efficient control techniques for the improvement in performance and reliability of the system.

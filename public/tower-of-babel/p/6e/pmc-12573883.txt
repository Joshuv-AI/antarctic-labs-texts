Identification and validation of functional markers for the superoxide dismutase gene TaSOD-B2 in common wheat
Authors: Jianpeng Wang, Yukun Cheng, Jiqing Wang, Bin Bai, Hongwei Geng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSuperoxide dismutase (SOD) is a significant metalloenzyme that affects the colour and rheological properties of wheat flour. It also enhances the stability of proteins playing a key role in coping with environmental stresses such as high temperatures, drought and salinity. Although SOD plays a crucial role in plant stress tolerance, its molecular mechanisms under various growth environments have not been fully elucidated. In addition, several SOD genes exist in wheat, but the current lack of corresponding molecular markers makes it difficult to effectively select varieties with excellent stress tolerance during breeding. Therefore, in this study, SOD activity in 273 grain sampies from different wheat regions was measured using the nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) photoreduction assay, and genetic variation at the TaSOD-B2 locus was associated with two co-dominant functional markers .ResultsThere were differences in SOD activity in different wheat regions, with the highest activity in the Southwest winter wheat region, followed by the Northern winter wheat region, the Huanghuai winter wheat region, Foreign varieties, and the Middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region. In the Foreign varieties and the Huanghuai winter wheat region, the SOD activities of materials containing TaSOD-B2b allelic variants were significantly higher than those of materials containing TaSOD-B2a allelic variants, indicating that TaSOD-B2b is the superior allelic variant type.ConclusionThe functional marker SOD2B1/SOD2B2 was effective in distinguishing varieties with different SOD activity levels. Therefore, SOD2B1/SOD2B2 can be applied to molecular marker-assisted breeding in wheat.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07643-y.

Introduction

Superoxide dismutase (SOD) is a metalloprotease with specific catalytic functions that are widely found in roots, stems, leaves and fruits of various microorganisms, animals and plants [1, 2]. According to the different metal cofactors at its catalytic site, SOD can be classified into four types: Fe-SOD, Mn-SOD, Cu/Zn-SOD, and Ni-SOD [3]. There is some similarity between Fe-SOD and Mn-SOD, which are made up of 50% of the same amino acids, and they form homodimers in prokaryotes, whereas in eukaryotes such as plants they are usually homotetramers [4, 5]. Cu/Zn-SOD is one of the most abundant classes in plants, mainly in the chloroplasts of plant cells, and consists of 154 amino acid residues carrying two metal cofactors [6, 7]. Previous studies have proposed the ability of SOD to influence the color of flour as well as the rheological properties of dough [8, 9]. SOD can release molecular oxygen in redox reactions, which converts the conjugated double bonds of pigment molecules, such as carotenoids, in wheat flour to single bonds, thereby increasing the whiteness of the flour [10]. Furthermore, SOD has been shown to enhance the whiteness of proteins by influencing the exchange reaction of molecular or intermolecular disulfide bonds within proteins, thereby resulting in the formation of a more ordered network structure and stable protein conformation [11, 12]. In addition, superoxide dismutase can further degrade the dietary fiber component of flour into oligosaccharides with prebiotic effects by dis-proportionating superoxide anions (O2−) to produce hydrogen peroxide and molecular oxygen [13]. Despite these important roles in plants, the molecular mechanisms underlying SOD activity and its impact on wheat cultivars, particularly from different growing regions, remain underexplored. Among the many SOD genes, TaSOD-B2 has been identified on chromosome 2B of wheat and shown to be closely related to superoxide dismutase activity in kernels. Previous studies indicated that allelic variation at this locus could significantly influence oxidative stress regulation and flour quality traits, making it a promising candidate gene for functional marker development. Therefore, further research into the functional markers for TaSOD-B2 genes may provide new insights for improving wheat flour quality and enhancing crop management.

Under normal growth conditions, SOD expression in plants remains relatively stable. However, when exposed to stresses such as high temperature, drought, and salt, both the expression and activity of SOD are significantly altered [14–18]. Temperature fluctuations disturb the balance between the production and removal of free radicals in the cell, causing an accumulation of superoxide anion radicals. This accumulation can lead to alterations in proteins within the cell membrane, affecting its permeability and causing damage to plant cells [19]. Studies have demonstrated that Cu/Zn-SOD genes expression is rapidly up-regulated in the leaves and roots of plants during the early stages of cold stress, but with prolonged cold exposure, the expression level in leaves decreases and becomes lower than that of the control [20, 21]. SOD plays a crucial role in cold hardening of plants, but its activity progressively declines once cold resistance has been established, indicating that the intrinsic regulatory capacity of the plant is limited [22]. Overexpression of the SOD gene in Arabidopsis thaliana can enhance cold tolerance in transgenic Arabidopsis through ABA-sensitive transcriptional regulation [23]. ABA-dependent regulation is not only limited to cold stress response but also plays a key role in drought acclimatization by maintaining reactive oxygen species (ROS) homeostasis [24]. Under drought stress, excess reactive oxygen species triggered the membrane lipid peroxidation chain reaction, whereas SOD blocked the lipid peroxidation process by disproportionating O2− and inhibiting the initial accumulation of peroxides. This mechanism significantly reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) production and attenuated membrane system damage [25]. In addition, the synergistic effect of SOD and H₂O2 metabolizing enzymes could further convert H₂O₂ into harmless H₂O, avoiding the toxic effect of hydroxyl radicals, and ultimately enhancing the tolerance of plants to drought stress [26–28]. Salt stress resulted in a significant up-regulation of SOD gene expression in plants and was further up-regulated when induced by both Zn and K [29]. In wheat and Arabidopsis thaliana, SOD genes enhance the activity of H2O2 metabolizing enzymes, which subsequently increase the activity of NADPH oxidase (NOX) and improves salt tolerance in plants [30]. Although SOD activity can be altered by the environment, it is primarily determined by genetic factors. For instance, Jiang et al. [31] conducted QTL analysis using 131 family lines from the RIL population derived from the cross “Chuan 35050” × “Shannong 483”. They identified 11 loci located on wheat chromosomes 1 A, 1B, 1D, 2D, 3 A, 4 A, 7 A and 7B, with a contribution rate ranging from 10% to 29%. Wu et al. [32] and Baek et al. [33] identified the localization of Mn-SOD activity genes in wheat seeds on chromosomes 2 A, 2B, and 2DL, and Cu/Zn-SOD activity genes on chromosomes 7 A, 7B, and 7DL, using Chinese Spring-deficient tetrasomal and bipartite materials. In this case, the Xfbb377-Xfbcd410 region on chromosome 2DL harbors an epistatic QTL locus that influences SOD content in wheat seeds. Jiang et al. [34] identified 26 SOD genes throughout the wheat genome, which are distributed on the homologous chromosomes 2, 4 and 7. In our previous work [35], we identified 29 loci that were significantly associated with SOD activity, each explaining 5%−32% of the phenotypic variance. Nine of these loci were consistently detected across three environments on chromosomes 1B, 2B, 4B, 5 A, 5B, 6B and 6D, with a contribution rate ranging from 6% to 17%. Among them, a significant locus on chromosome 2B was associated with a contribution of 14% to 17%. In this context, the gene-specific markers have been shown to facilitate the precise identification of alleles and are extensively utilized in molecular marker-assisted selection for the enhancement of wheat quality [36]. Similarly, in a recent study, Yu et al. [37] found that the TaP5CS-1 A gene is associated with drought resistance in wheat. They developed molecular marker TaP5CS-1 A-CAPS for screening and identifying drought resistance in wheat. Likewise, Sun et al. [38] utilized two functional markers, SODA1 and SODA11, to detect allelic variation at the TaSOD-A1 locus in Xinjiang wheat varieties (lines). Their findings indicated that the SOD activity of kernels containing TaSOD-A1a allele was significantly higher than that of kernels containing the TaSOD-A1b allele.

All in all, understanding the molecular mechanisms that regulate SOD activity in response to environmental stresses is essential for enhancing stress tolerance in wheat. Further research into the specific loci and genes controlling SOD activity will provide critical insights for developing wheat cultivars with enhanced resilience to cold, drought, and salt stress. The precise identification of wheat SOD activity using molecular markers not only facilitates rapid and accurate screening of germplasm resources with high SOD activity but also provides a powerful tool for elucidating the functions of key gene loci, such as TaSOD-B2 [39]. This approach significantly enhances the efficiency of marker-assisted selection (MAS) in wheat stress-tolerant breeding, laying a solid theoretical and technical foundation for developing new wheat varieties resistant to cold, drought and salinity.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and field trials

The experiment involved 273 different wheat varieties (lines) representing diverse regions, including 119 wheat varieties from the Huanghuai winter wheat region in China, among of these, 44 from the Northern winter wheat region, 35 from the Middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region, and 20 from the Southwest Winter Wheat region. Additionally, 55 varieties (lines) from European countries such as France, Romania, and Italy were also included in the experiment. All materials were provided by the Xinjiang Specialized Crop Engineering Technology Research Center, Xinjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences. And the plants were grown in the experimental field of the Manas (Xinjiang, China) in 2016–2017, 2017–2018, and 2018–2019, using a randomized complete block design with three replications and single-row planting at a row length of 2 m, and row spacing of 0.25 m. Throughout the growing season, drip irrigation was implemented for water management instead of relying solely on natural precipitation, thereby effectively mitigating the impact of interannual rainfall variability on seed SOD activity.

Superoxide dismutase activity assay

SOD activities of wheat cultivars were assessed based on the method of Wang Jiqing’s [35] After removal of impurities from wheat seeds, 10 g of wheat flour milled by 0.8 mm cyclone grinder was weighed. 0.1 g of whole wheat flour was placed in a 2 ml centrifuge tube, 1 ml of SOD extraction medium was added, shaken and mixed, and then placed in a shaker in an ice bath for 2 h. Subsequently, the supernatant (crude enzyme solution) was obtained by centrifugation at 10,000 rpm for 15 min and stored at 4℃. SOD activity was determined using the nitrosyl blue tetrazolium (NBT) photochemical reduction method with a total reaction system of 300 µL, consisting of 150 µL of 0.05 mol/L phosphate buffer, 20 µL of sterilized water, 30 µL of 130 mmol/L methionine (Met) solution, 30 µL of 750 µmol/L NBT solution, 230 µL of 100 µmol/L sodium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) solution and 30 µL of 20 µmol/L riboflavin solution. Finally, 10 µl of crude enzyme solution was added and absorbance was measured at 560 nm at room temperature. The SOD activity was repeated for each variety and retesting was performed if the difference between two measurements exceeded 10%. SOD activity was expressed as units of enzyme activity (U·g− 1) for 50% inhibition of NBT photoreduction.

Extraction and quality identification of DNA

The plant genomic DNA was extracted using the SDS method and the luminescence was separated by ultraviolet light [40]. The purity of DNA was assessed through quantification and electrophoresis. The DNA quality and quantity were detected performed using a UV spectrophotometer with 1.7 ~ 2.0 of OD260/OD280 value, then 5 µL of DNA and 1 µL of 6×Loading buffer were mixed for detection agarose gel electrophoresis.

Acquisition and bioinformatics analysis of TaSOD-B2 gene

Based on the SOD candidate gene TraesCS2B02G567600 screened by genome-wide association analysis, the corresponding gDNA and protein of TraesCS2B02G567600 were obtained by downloading from the EnsemblPlants database (http://plants.ensembl.org) Sequence. Bioinformatics analyses of protein physical and chemical properties, secondary structure, transmembrane helices, signal peptides and other related bioinformatics analyses were performed using Prot Param, SOPMA, TMHMM, Phobius, SignalP and other software available on the ExPASy (https://www.expasy.org/) bioinformatics resources website.

Validation and application of functional markers for TaSOD-B2

Previously developed functional markers for TaSOD-B2 were further validated and applied in this study [41]. The differential loci on TaSOD-B2 were screened by wheat sequencing results from the Joint Database of Genomic Variants in Wheat [42] (http://wheat.cau.edu.cn/WheatUnion/). The primers were designed for the sequence differences of the two alleles of TaSOD-B2 locus, which should make the size of the amplification products suitable for easy observation, so that they can be better analyzed by electrophoresis results on agarose gel. The PCR amplification conditions were 94℃ for 5 min, 94 °C for 30 S, 54.5 °C/56°C for 30 S, 72 °C for 30 S, 35 cycles, 72 °C for 8 min, 4 °C Storage (Table 1).

Statistical analyses

In 273 cultivars and advanced lines, the SOD activity of each genotype was measured once in each of the three growing seasons and averaged to verify the association between SOD activity and STS markers. Basic data processing was carried out using WPS Excel software, analysis of significance of differences and analysis of variance (ANOVA) method and multiple comparisons (LSD method) were performed using IBM SPSS statistics 22 software, and sequence comparison was performed using DNAMAN software.

Results

SOD activities in 273 wheat varieties

Analysis of the seed superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity in 273 winter wheat varieties (lines) from various wheat-growing regions (Table 2) revealed that the SOD activity ranged from 1354.13 U/g to 1988.86 U/g. The coefficients of variation (CV) for SOD activity ranged from 2.96% to 6.59%, with the highest CV observed in the Middle and Lower Reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region, and the lowest in the Northern winter wheat region. The SOD activity in 273 wheat materials exhibited highly significant differences among genotypes, environments, and their interactions (P < 0.001), with a heritability of 0.81. SOD activity across the three growing seasons showed a high level of consistency, with significant positive correlations between years (r = 0.72–0.75, P < 0.001), indicating that genotype performance for SOD activity was relatively stable over time(Fig. 1). The average SOD activity of varieties (lines) across different wheat regions follows this pattern: Southwest winter wheat region > Northern winter wheat region > Foreign varieties > HuangHuai winter wheat region > Middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region. Within the Northern Winter Wheat Region, Jingdong 22 exhibited the highest SOD activity among varieties (lines), while F92080G1-1/F93042G2-1 showed the highest SOD activity among foreign varieties. The variety (line) with the highest SOD activity in the Southwest Winter Wheat Region was Mianyang 19. In the Huanghuai Winter Wheat Region, the variety (line) with the highest SOD activity was Zhong 892. In the Middle and Lower Yangtze River Winter Wheat Region, the variety with the highest SOD activity was Huaimai 21. Structural and sequence analysis of TaSOD-B2

The gDNA sequence of TaSOD-B2 consists of 6017 base pairs, with a 4452 bp open reading frame (ORF) comprising 6 exons and 5 introns (Fig. 2.A). Three polymorphic sites are present in the exons, located at 69 bp, 237 bp, and 2711 bp respectively. The splice sites within the introns follow the typical GT-AG pattern (Fig. 2.B). TaSOD-B2 encodes a protein consisting of 225 amino acid residues, with a predicted molecular weight of 24.6 kD, an isoelectric point of 7.14, and an instability coefficient of 29.35. The transmembrane helices were predicted to have no obvious transmembrane structure or signal peptide. Signal peptide analysis of the N-terminal sequence further confirmed the absence of a signal peptide, suggesting that this is not a secreted protein. Functional marker site analysis of TaSOD-B2

This study employed a pair of previously developed dominant-complementary functional markers, SOD2B1 and SOD2B2, targeting a 2711 bp SNP site (A/T) between the TaSOD-B2a and TaSOD-B2b alleles of the TaSOD-B2 gene (Fig. 3). The SOD2B1 marker amplified a 643 bp fragment in varieties (lines) carrying the TaSOD-B2a allele but failed to amplify in those carrying the TaSOD-B2b allele. Conversely, the SOD2B2 marker failed to amplify fragments in varieties (lines) carrying the TaSOD-B2a allele, but produced a 1106 bp fragment in those carrying the TaSOD-B2b allele (Fig. 4). Genotyping of 273 wheat varieties (lines) using SOD2B1 and SOD2B2 revealed amplification of 643 bp and 1106 bp fragments in 127 and 146 varieties (lines), respectively (Table 3). Validation of functional markers in wheat cultivars

The validation of the developed functional markers in 273 winter wheat varieties (lines) showed that the average SOD activity in varieties (lines) with the TaSOD-B2a allelic variant was 1757.09 U/g in the Huanghuai winter wheat region, 1796.62 U/g in the Northern winter wheat region, 1772.16 U/g in the Middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region, 1824.68 U/g in the Southwestern winter wheat region, and 1722.26 U/g in Foreign varieties (lines). In contrast, varieties (lines) with the TaSOD-B2b allelic variant showed average SOD activities of 1779.75 U/g, 1809.77 U/g, 1735.55 U/g, 1806.70 U/g, and 1813.15 U/g, respectively. In general, as well as in the Huanghuai winter wheat region and Foreign varieties (lines), SOD activity was significantly higher in materials with the TaSOD-B2b allelic variant compared to those with the TaSOD-B2a variant (P < 0.05). However, no significant differences in SOD activity were observed between varieties (lines) with the TaSOD-B2a and TaSOD-B2b allelic variants in the Northern winter wheat region, the Middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region, and the Southwestern winter wheat region. These results indicate a significant correlation between the different allelic variants of TaSOD-B2 and SOD activity. Moreover, the markers SOD2B1/SOD2B2 can be effectively used in molecular marker-assisted selection breeding to enhance the accuracy of phenotypic prediction of SOD activity (Table 3).

Distribution of TaSOD-B2 allelic genes in winter wheat regions

The frequency distribution of TaSOD-B2 allelic variants was analyzed across four wheat regions and foreign varieties (lines). TaSOD-B2b is the superior allelic variant associated with high SOD activity. A 1106 bp fragment, corresponding to TaSOD-B2b, was amplified in 146 out of 273 winter wheat varieties (lines), representing 53.48% of the total, indicating the prevalence of the high SOD activity allele. In contrast, a 643 bp fragment, associated with the TaSOD-B2a variant and lower SOD activity, was amplified in 127 varieties (lines), making up 46.52% of the sample (Table 4). In the Huanghuai winter wheat region, 54 of 119 varieties (45.38%) carried the TaSOD-B2a allele, while 65 (54.63%) had the TaSOD-B2b allele. In the Northern winter wheat region, 20 out of 44 varieties (45.45%) had TaSOD-B2a and 24 (54.55%) had TaSOD-B2b. In the Middle and Lower Reaches of the Yangtze River, 22 of 35 varieties (62.86%) carried the TaSOD-B2a allele, while 13 (37.14%) carried the TaSOD-B2b allele. In the Southwest winter wheat region, 13 out of 20 varieties (65%) had TaSOD-B2a, and 7 (35%) had TaSOD-B2b. Among the 55 foreign varieties (lines), 18 (32.73%) had TaSOD-B2a and 37 (67.27%) had TaSOD-B2b. The potential for improving SOD activity in Chinese wheat varieties is considerable, especially in regions like the Middle and Lower Reaches of the Yangtze River and the Southwest, where the frequency of low SOD activity is higher. Selecting for the TaSOD-B2b allelic variant is crucial to enhance flour whiteness and product quality.

Discussion

SOD activity in wheat grains is influenced by regional and genetic factors

The SOD activity of wheat grain is closely linked to quality, influencing not only the color and nutritional value of flour but also the rheological properties of wheat dough. This makes SOD activity, an important target for the genetic improvement of wheat [43]. Wheat grain SOD activity is affected by a combination of genetic, environmental and physiological factors. Under stress conditions, the wheat plants enhance SOD expression, resulting in higher SOD activity [44, 45]. Significant differences in SOD activity exist among wheat varieties due to genotypic variation, which may be attributed to the genetic background and diversity of the varieties. Similarly, in this study, the SOD activity varied among wheat varieties (lines) from different wheat regions, which aligns with previous findings that genotype is the primary factor influencing SOD activity [46]. This substantial variation in SOD activity observed across different wheat regions highlights a promising opportunity for selection. Consequently, breeding efforts could target varieties with higher SOD activity to enhance the color and rheological properties of wheat-based products. A comparison of SOD activity across different wheat regions reveals a clear distribution pattern. Overall, the activity follows this order: Southwest winter wheat region >Northern winter wheat region >Foreign varieties >Huanghuai winter wheat region >Middle and Lower reaches of the Yangtze River winter wheat region. This pattern may be attributed to the external environment’s stress on wheat growth, which stimulates an increase in SOD activity. The Southwest region experiences milder winters but with pronounced diurnal temperature fluctuations, leading to repeated episodes of low temperature stress and sustained ROS production, thereby maintaining high antioxidant enzyme activity. Moreover, genetic adaptations of local Southwest varieties, shaped by long term selection under variable climatic conditions, may confer a more robust antioxidant system. In contrast, Northern winter wheat region primarily adapt to prolonged, stable low temperature environments. Unlike Northern winter wheat region, which enters a near dormant state in mid winter, Southwest winter wheat maintains relatively active metabolic processes, necessitating sustained ROS scavenging through elevated SOD activity. These findings highlight the importance of regional climatic and genetic factors in shaping antioxidant enzyme responses in winter wheat.

Potential mechanisms underlying the differential activity of TaSOD-B2 alleles

Superoxide dismutase is the only enzyme in nature that uses oxygen radicals as substrates [47]. It is widely distributed in microorganisms, plants and animals. Amino acids are the basic building blocks of proteins, and their content affects the stability of protein function and plays a huge role in plant growth, development, and metabolism [48]. However, most organisms show a strong preference for certain bases in tRNAs, with codons ending in U or C showing higher transfer efficiency compared to ending in A or G [49]. There are six codons encoding leucine in organisms: CTA, CTC, CTG, CTT, TTA, and TCT, with varying frequencies of usage across different studies. And CUU was found to be used more frequently than CUA encoding leucine in humans and E. coli [50–52], whereas the TTG codon is preferred for encoding leucine in chloroplasts, and the CTA is avoided as much as possible in gramineous crops [53]. In this study, TaSOD-B2a and TaSOD-B2b alleles, located on wheat chromosome 2B, exhibited a single base mutation at position 2711 of their gene sequence. This mutation changes A (associated with low SOD activity) to T (associated with high SOD activity) at position 2711 bp. It is possible that this substitution may influence codon usage preference and tRNA recognition efficiency, potentially affecting the translation rate of TaSOD-B2. Codon usage bias has been reported to influence gene expression in plants [54], but direct evidence for this effect in wheat TaSOD-B2 remains to be confirmed by further studies.

Application and limitations of TaSOD-B2 functional markers in wheat breeding

Molecular functional markers in wheat, as a key tool for modern breeding, have significantly enhanced the wheat breeding process. These markers using advanced molecular biology techniques, enable breeders to identify genes linked to important traits such as disease resistance, stress tolerance, and yield, thereby increasing both the efficiency and precision of breeding efforts. By locating key genes, breeders are able to screen for superior genotypes at an early stage, significantly shortening the breeding cycle [55]. In addition, molecular functional markers have helped to overcome the complexity of gene interactions, making joint selection for multiple traits more feasible. A pair of complementary co-dominant functional markers, SOD2B1 and SOD2B2, have been developed based on sequence polymorphisms in wheat TaSOD-B2 alleles (TaSOD-B2a and TaSOD-B2b). Functional markers have been widely used in wheat breeding to accurately differentiate between allelic variants and to improve selection accuracy. The SOD2B1 and SOD2B2 markers are closely associated with materials exhibiting low and high SOD activity, respectively. However, when applying the functional markers SOD2B1 and SOD2B2 in the winter wheat regions of the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River and southwest China, the results showed that the specific allelic variants did not always align with the expected SOD activity levels. Moreover, the SOD activity of materials containing the TaSOD-B2b allelic variant was not consistently higher than that of materials with the TaSOD-B2a allelic variant. This discrepancy may be due to the influence of other genes or quantitative trait loci (QTL) in the wheat genome, in addition to the TaSOD-B2 locus, affecting seed SOD activity. These findings further support the conclusion that wheat SOD activity is under polygenic control. Despite this, the results highlight the value of functional markers in accurately characterizing wheat SOD activity and facilitating breeding efforts.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully developed a functional molecular marker based on the sequence characteristics of the wheat TaSOD-B2 gene, enabling the effective identification of allelic variations, particularly the discrimination of the high-activity allele TaSOD-B2b. This marker not only provides a valuable tool for elucidating the genetic basis of superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity in wheat grains but can also be directly applied in marker-assisted selection (MAS) to accurately screen high SOD activity lines at early breeding stages, thereby accelerating the development of high-quality and stress-tolerant wheat cultivars. For future research, TaSOD-B2b could be pyramided with other favorable alleles previously reported for traits such as drought tolerance, salinity tolerance, cold resistance, and grain quality to achieve multi-trait improvement. In addition, integrating the marker into genomic selection (GS) pipelines combined with multi-environment field trials will facilitate a comprehensive evaluation of TaSOD-B2b performance under diverse ecological zones and stress conditions, providing systematic theoretical and technical support for wheat quality improvement and adaptation to environmental challenges.

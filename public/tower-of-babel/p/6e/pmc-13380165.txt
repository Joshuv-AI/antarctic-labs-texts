Aggressive thoracic vertebral hemangioma managed with preoperative embolization and surgical decompression: A case report
Authors: Brahim El Mahjoub, Hamza Sriri, Wiam Bougrine, Ismail Chaouche, Hajar Ouazzani Chahdi, Amal Akammar, Marouane Hammoud, Nizar El Bouardi, Badreddine Alami, Meryem Boubbou, Khalid Chakour, Mustapha Maaroufi, Moulay Youssef Alaoui Lamrani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 24-year-old male presented with a 3-month history of progressive spastic paraparesis (Frankel grade B) secondary to a T9 aggressive vertebral hemangioma (AVH) with epidural extension and spinal cord compression, displaying the characteristic polka-dot and corduroy signs on computed tomography. Spinal digital subtraction angiography confirmed marked hypervascularity predominantly supplied by the right T9 intercostal artery. Selective injection of the left T9 intercostal artery opacified an ipsilateral posterior spinal artery, contraindicating embolization on that side. Preoperative embolization was therefore limited to the right-sided feeders, using calibrated microspheres (Hydropearls 400 µm, Terumo, Tokyo, Japan) for distal occlusion followed by coil deployment for proximal hemostasis. Subsequent T9 laminectomy with posterior instrumentation and fusion resulted in low intraoperative blood loss (400 mL) with no complications. Histopathological examination confirmed a benign vascular proliferation consistent with vertebral hemangioma, with no signs of malignancy. Neurological status improved to Frankel grade D. This case illustrates that meticulous angiographic evaluation and selective unilateral embolization can achieve safe and effective hemostasis in compressive AVH when radiculomedullary arteries are identified.

Introduction

Vertebral hemangiomas (VH) are common benign vascular lesions with a prevalence of 10%-12% in autopsy series, and are typically incidental and intraosseous [1]. Approximately 0.9%-1.2% exhibit aggressive features, including cortical destruction, extraosseous extension and epidural involvement, leading to progressive myelopathy most frequently at the thoracic level [2,3].

The hypervascular nature of aggressive vertebral hemangiomas (AVH) predisposes to significant intraoperative hemorrhage exceeding 1000-1600 mL without prior devascularization [4]. Preoperative transarterial embolization using microparticles, coils, or liquid embolic agents reduces blood loss to 273-980 mL and is an established adjunct to surgical decompression [4,5]. When radiculomedullary arteries supply or adjoin the lesion, superselective techniques or deliberate abstention are required to prevent spinal cord ischemia [6,7].

Posterior decompression with instrumented fixation, occasionally combined with vertebroplasty, achieves neurological improvement in 82%-89% of patients with neurological deficit and is associated with low recurrence rates [2,8,9]. We report a case of T9 AVH in a young adult managed with selective unilateral preoperative embolization and laminectomy, with emphasis on angiographic planning when a posterior spinal artery is identified.

Case presentation

A 24-year-old male with no relevant medical history was admitted with a 3-month history of progressive lower limb weakness and heaviness, evolving to spastic paraparesis (Frankel grade B) with a sensory level below the umbilicus and brisk deep tendon reflexes. Routine blood work was unremarkable.

MRI of the thoracic spine revealed a T9 vertebral body lesion with posterior epidural extension causing significant cord compression, demonstrating T2 hyperintensity and avid gadolinium enhancement. Right-sided tumoural feeders were superselectively catheterized with a microcatheter. Calibrated microspheres (Hydropearls 400 µm, Terumo, Tokyo, Japan) were delivered to saturate distal vascular networks, followed by deployment of microcoils for proximal occlusion to ensure durable hemostasis and prevent reflux. Left-sided embolization was withheld because of the identified posterior spinal artery. T9 laminectomy with posterior instrumentation and fusion (T8-T10 pedicle screws) was performed within 48 hours of embolization. Intraoperative bleeding was predominantly right-sided, consistent with the angiographic findings. Histopathological examination of surgical specimens (2 containers of osseous and soft tissue fragments, 1-15 mm, entirely processed after decalcification) revealed medullary spaces filled by a benign vascular proliferation composed of small turgescent, mostly ectatic capillaries intermixed with medium-sized vessels lined by regular endothelial cells resting on a loose fibrous stroma. Perosseous osteoblastic cells were identified. No intravascular papillary budding, no epithelioid endothelial cells, no hematopoietic tissue, no thrombus, and no necrotic remodeling were observed. The pathological conclusion confirmed a histological appearance compatible with vertebral hemangioma, with the aggressive character affirmed by the clinical presentation and imaging (spinal cord compression), and with no signs of malignancy.

In the immediate postoperative period, neurological status improved to Frankel grade D, with independent ambulation and significant pain relief. Discussion

In our case, selective preoperative embolization of the dominant right-sided feeders achieved effective devascularization, with intraoperative blood loss of approximately 400 mL, compared to reported averages of more than 1600 mL in nonembolized AVH series [4] and 273-980 mL in embolized cohorts [4,5]. This supports embolization as a valuable adjunct even when restricted to the dominant vascular pedicle.

The identification of an ipsilateral posterior spinal artery on the left T9 intercostal artery dictated deliberate abstention on that side. This strategy is consistent with the observations of Westbroek et al. [6] who reported that atypical and aggressive vertebral hemangiomas are frequently associated with radiculomedullary arteries, and that inadvertent embolization of these vessels carries a risk of catastrophic spinal cord ischemia [6]. In our experience, unilateral devascularization of the dominant supply proved sufficient for safe intraoperative hemostatic control.

Posterior decompression with instrumented fixation provided rapid cord decompression and spinal stability, resulting in Frankel B to D improvement. This outcome aligns with multimodal series reporting neurological recovery in 82%-89% of patients with neurological deficit [2,8,9]. CT imaging features (polka-dot and corduroy signs) were diagnostic and helped exclude key differential diagnoses, including spinal metastasis, plasmacytoma, and lymphoma [10]. Histopathological confirmation further secured the diagnosis and excluded malignancy, which is an essential step in the management of any aggressive-appearing spinal lesion.

Compared to bilateral embolization strategies reported in the literature [7], our approach minimized procedural risk while maintaining surgical safety. Adjunct vertebroplasty, though not performed in this case, may be considered when significant vertebral body destruction or postdecompression instability is anticipated [4,8].

Limitations of this report include the single-case design and short duration of postoperative follow-up. Conclusion

Selective preoperative embolization followed by laminectomy with posterior fixation offers safe and effective management for compressive aggressive vertebral hemangiomas in young adults. Detailed spinal angiography is mandatory to identify radiculomedullary arteries; when present, deliberate unilateral embolization of the dominant feeders can provide adequate intraoperative hemostasis while preserving the spinal cord vascular supply, allowing safe decompression and meaningful neurological recovery. Histopathological confirmation remains essential to secure the diagnosis and exclude malignancy.

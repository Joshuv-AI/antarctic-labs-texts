Modeling mumps incidence in China: spatiotemporal clusters and evolving risk factors (2005–2020)

Published: 2025

Background

Mumps remains a significant public health threat in China, with an average annual incidence rate of 21.44 per 100,000 population in mainland China from 2004 to 2018, showing a pronounced upward trend particularly between 2004 and 2012 [1]. Epidemiological characteristics indicate that children aged 5–9 years are the high-risk group, with significantly higher incidence in males than females, demonstrating distinct seasonal peaks in spring [2, 3]. Spatial analyses reveal significant geographical heterogeneity in mumps distribution across China: spatial autocorrelation analyses identified high-value clusters in southern regions including Chongqing and Guizhou provinces [4], while local spatial scanning in Chongqing and Guangxi detected high-risk aggregation belts in central-western and northeastern areas [3, 5].

The mumps epidemic is influenced by complex socioeconomic and healthcare resource factors. Existing studies demonstrate that healthcare resource allocation (e.g., hospital beds per 1,000 population, bed occupancy rate) directly affects disease surveillance and treatment capacity, while economic development level (per capita GDP) and demographic characteristics (children dependency ratio, average household size) indirectly modify transmission risks through their impacts on health investments and residential density [4, 6]. Additionally, illiteracy rate and urbanization rate may reflect health literacy disparities and population mobility intensity, showing strong correlations with spatial diffusion patterns [1, 5]. Notably, meteorological factors (e.g., PM2.5 concentration) and interregional spatial spillover effects have been confirmed to significantly alter mumps transmission dynamics [4], highlighting the need for analytical methods capable of capturing spatiotemporal non-stationarity.

To quantify these factors’ impacts on mumps incidence, conventional methods like ordinary least squares may yield biased estimates due to ignored spatial dependence. Global spatial regression models (e.g., spatial lag model or spatial error model) can address spatial autocorrelation but fail to capture local variations [4]. Spatial panel regression integrates temporal dimensions but has limited capacity for handling non-stationary spatial relationships [4]. The temporally weighted regression (TWR) model effectively addresses temporal non-stationarity and local variations over time, though it does not account for spatial heterogeneity. Geographically weighted regression (GWR) significantly improves model accuracy by incorporating spatial non-stationarity [6], yet lacks temporal dimension processing capacity. In contrast, geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) simultaneously incorporates spatial and temporal weighting matrices, enabling more flexible analysis of dynamic changes in influencing factors across spatiotemporal scales.

While previous studies have applied spatial or temporal methods separately, few have integrated spatiotemporal scanning with advanced regression techniques capable of capturing both spatial and temporal heterogeneity in a unified framework. This study fills this gap by combining space-time scan statistics with GTWR modeling to identify persistent high-risk clusters and to elucidate the evolving drivers of mumps incidence across China. Moreover, we systematically compare GTWR with multiple benchmark models—including MLR, TWR and GWR—to quantitatively demonstrate its superiority in handling spatiotemporal non-stationarity.

Our findings provide novel insights into the persistent high-risk clusters of provinces and the dynamic effects of socioeconomic factors over time. These results can directly inform public health policy by supporting targeted vaccination campaigns, optimized resource allocation, and context-specific intervention strategies designed to address both geographic and temporal disparities in mumps transmission risk [7].

Methods

Data

This study identified eight key influencing factors building upon the theoretical framework established in the background section, covering dimensions such as economic development, education level, population structure, and healthcare resources (Table 1). The provincial-level analysis was selected primarily due to data availability constraints and policy considerations. Firstly, the authoritative mumps incidence data are systematically published only at the provincial level, ensuring nationwide consistency and comparability. Secondly, the explanatory variables from the China Statistical Yearbook are also compiled and reported with standardized metrics at this administrative level, guaranteeing perfect alignment between all variables and avoiding aggregation biases. Lastly, the provincial scale is a key unit for public health management in China, making the findings directly relevant for policy-making.

Key factors selected for analysis

The research encompassed 31 provincial-level administrative regions in China (provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the central government, excluding Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan). All data were obtained from authoritative national sources. Mumps incidence rates (2005–2020) were sourced from the Public Health Science Data Center(https://www.phsciencedata.cnShare/), and other explanatory variables were extracted from official editions of the China Statistical Yearbook for corresponding years. The dataset is complete for all provinces and years within the study period, with no missing values requiring processing. The reporting standards are uniform across all provinces, as mandated by the national authorities, ensuring data quality and consistency.

Spatial distribution analysis

Geographic Information System (GIS) technology was employed to map the spatial distribution of mumps incidence across China’s 31 provinces from 2005 to 2020, with selected years (2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020) representing 5-year intervals to capture medium-to-long-term trends while aligning with China’s Five-Year Plan cycles for policy impact analysis. These maps visually demonstrate regional disparities and spatiotemporal evolution patterns of mumps incidence.

All results were validated using Z-tests [10].

Spatiotemporal scan analysis

The space-time scan statistic was applied to analyze mumps incidence patterns across 31 Chinese provinces from 2005 to 2020. Using dynamic cylindrical spatiotemporal windows under a Poisson distribution model [11], the method detects anomalous incidence clusters. To balance the need for capturing seasonal variations with data availability constraints, the analysis was conducted using annual aggregation. Statistical significance was evaluated via log-likelihood ratio (LLR) [12], with relative risk (RR) quantifying risk levels [13]. Identified clusters were classified as primary clusters (highest LLR, p < 0.01) representing the most significant risk areas, and secondary clusters (high LLR, p < 0.05) representing moderately significant risk areas [14]. The statistical significance of the cluster hierarchies was assessed through 999 Monte Carlo simulations, where the observed cases were randomly redistributed under the null hypothesis of no space-time clustering in each replicate [15].The maximum spatial and temporal window sizes were set to 50% of the population at risk and 50% of the study period, respectively, to allow sufficient flexibility for cluster detection while maintaining computational feasibility.

Model comparison and evaluation

Model performance was comprehensively evaluated using multiple metrics including the coefficient of determination (R²), adjusted coefficient of determination (Adj.R²), and corrected Akaike Information Criterion (AICc). This multi-criteria approach enabled robust comparison of model fits and selection of the most appropriate modeling framework for analyzing spatiotemporal patterns of mumps incidence.

Software implementation

The spatial statistical analysis integrated multiple software platforms, utilizing ArcGIS 10.2 for spatial data processing and visualization, including spatial autocorrelation tests and collinearity diagnostics. The GTWR plugin within ArcGIS was employed for MLR, TWR, GWR, and GTWR modeling. Base geographic data were obtained from the National Geospatial Information Platform (Map Review No.: GS (2024) 0650). All statistical tests were conducted as two-tailed tests with significance level set at α = 0.05.

Results

Spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of mumps incidence

Temporal trend analysis revealed a fluctuating decline in mumps incidence across China from 2005 to 2020. The baseline incidence in 2005 was 27.60 per 100,000 population, which peaked in 2012 (38.49 per 100,000) before declining steadily to 10.09 per 100,000 by 2020. Notably, an anomalous surge occurred during 2011–2012, potentially attributable to surveillance system adjustments or localized outbreaks (Fig. 1).

Trends in the average incidence of mumps in China, 2005–2020

As shown in Fig. 2, spatial distribution exhibited marked regional heterogeneity, with western provinces (e.g., Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan) and certain central regions maintaining persistently high incidence rates, while economically developed eastern coastal areas (e.g., Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Guangdong) demonstrated lower rates. Municipalities with high vaccination coverage (Beijing, Shanghai) consistently recorded the nation’s lower incidence levels.

Spatial distribution of mumps incidence in China, 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020

The spatiotemporal evolution comprised three distinct phases: an expansion phase (2005–2012) where western regions emerged as significant high-incidence zones; a rapid decline phase (2012–2015) where high-incidence areas contracted though western provinces remained relatively elevated; and a stabilization phase (2015–2020) with nationwide incidence reduction and sporadic high-incidence pockets limited to western provinces. This pattern closely aligned with the implementation timeline of China’s immunization programs.

Spatial autocorrelation analysis of mumps incidence (2005–2020)

As clearly demonstrated in Table 2, Moran’s I indices demonstrated phased variations, identifying three significant clustering periods: 2007 (I = 0.351, p < 0.001), 2011–2013 (I = 0.210–0.279, p < 0.05), and 2016–2020 (I = 0.224–0.399, p < 0.001) peaking in 2020. These dynamics likely reflect temporal variations in mumps control policies, with the sustained clustering post-2010, particularly the intensified autocorrelation during 2016–2020, underscoring the need for targeted interventions in persistent high-risk western regions and optimized vaccination strategies to mitigate regional disparities.

Temporal evolution of influencing factors

As shown in Fig. 4, the analysis revealed three characteristic patterns of coefficient evolution. Fundamental directional shifts were observed in illiteracy rate and population density, transitioning from positive to negative effects, particularly notable for illiteracy rate reflecting the progressive benefits of education and health awareness.

Temporal variations in average regression coefficients of all factors

Factors showing significant intensification included household size, which evolved from weak to the strongest positive predictor, highlighting growing importance of family structure in transmission dynamics.

Fluctuating patterns characterized healthcare resource indicators (number of hospital beds per 1,000 population and bed occupancy rate), showing generally weakening effects that may reflect healthcare system improvements, while GDP per capita and children dependency ratio exhibited phase-dependent variations, with GDP transitioning from negative to positive effects and children dependency ratio oscillating between positive and negative influences.

Spatial heterogeneity of influencing factors

As shown in Fig. 5, the study revealed pronounced spatial variations in factor effects. Healthcare resources showed divergent regional impacts: number of hospital beds per 1,000 population exhibited positive effects in western China but negative in eastern regions, while bed occupancy rate displayed the opposite pattern.

Geographic distribution of average regression coefficients across study factors

Economic factors demonstrated spatial non-stationarity, with GDP per capita serving as a protective factor in western/southeastern regions but becoming a risk factor elsewhere.

Illiteracy rate had stronger positive effects in northern China but weaker or negative effects in the south.

Population density generally showed negative impacts except in certain western areas (Xinjiang, Tibet) where positive associations emerged.

The children dependency ratio shows a strong positive effect in western regions such as Gansu and Ningxia, while in northern areas like Xinjiang and Beijing, the effect is weaker or even negative.

Family structure indicators revealed west-east disparities, with household size showing stronger positive effects in western provinces but negative associations in some eastern regions (e.g., Yangtze River Delta). These spatial heterogeneities likely reflect regional differences in socioeconomic conditions, healthcare infrastructure, and demographic characteristics.

Spatiotemporal dynamics of influential factors

As demonstrated in Fig. 6, the spatiotemporal analysis identified evolving geographical patterns of factor influences. Healthcare resource risks exhibited consistent spatial patterns over time, with the number of hospital beds per 1,000 population posing higher risks in western regions, while bed occupancy rates continued to present more substantial risks in eastern areas.

Dynamic spatiotemporal patterns of mumps incidence drivers, 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020

Economic factors demonstrated temporal shifts, with GDP per capita transitioning from higher risks in eastern regions (2005) to greater risks in the southwest (2010), and finally to more substantial risks in western areas (2015 onward).

Educational factors maintained stable north-south gradients, with illiteracy rate presenting higher risks in northern regions.

The influence of population density on mumps incidence showed positive effects primarily in western regions during 2005 and 2010, while from 2015 to 2020, the positive impact became more pronounced in eastern regions.

The influence of the child dependency ratio on mumps incidence showed a positive effect primarily in eastern regions in 2005, shifted to stronger positive effects in western regions in 2010 and 2015, and returned to predominantly positive effects in eastern regions by 2020.

The influence of average household size on mumps incidence showed positive effects primarily in northern regions in 2005 and 2010, and shifted to stronger positive effects in southern regions in 2015 and 2020.

Discussion

The incidence of mumps in China exhibits distinct spatiotemporal heterogeneity [1, 22, 23]. Temporally, while the overall trend from 2005 to 2020 showed a decline, an anomalous surge occurred during 2011–2012, potentially attributable to surveillance system adjustments or localized outbreaks [1]. The subsequent sustained decrease to 10.09 per 100,000 by 2020 coincided with immunization program implementation [1, 22]. Spatially, western regions (e.g., Sichuan, Yunnan) maintained persistently high incidence [23], whereas municipalities with high vaccination coverage (Beijing, Shanghai) recorded the lowest rates [1, 22]. This spatiotemporal pattern strongly correlates with immunization program rollout [22], confirming program effectiveness. However, the persistent high incidence in western provinces despite vaccination efforts [1, 23] suggests the need for targeted control strategies considering geographical and healthcare resource disparities.

Our study revealed phased evolution in spatial clustering of mumps incidence during 2005–2020. Three significant clustering periods (2007, 2011–2013, and 2016–2020) align with previously reported epidemiological characteristics [1]. The peak clustering in 2020 may reflect inadequate outbreak control in western regions [1], while the dispersed pattern in 2009 could relate to the 2008 Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) policy [22]. The intensified spatial autocorrelation during 2016–2020 suggests potential regional disparities in vaccination strategies [24], necessitating optimized interventions for high-risk western areas [1].

China’s mumps epidemic demonstrates marked spatiotemporal clustering, with primary clusters concentrated in western/southwestern regions (e.g., Tibet, Qinghai, Sichuan), consistent with previous findings [3, 5]. The recurrent clustering in Guizhou and Chongqing (2017–2019) indicates potential persistent transmission chains or suboptimal vaccination coverage [6]. In contrast, northeastern/northern regions (e.g., Jilin, Liaoning, Beijing) exhibited transient clusters (2011–2012), possibly reflecting regional differences in population mobility, climate factors [25], or immunization strategies [26]. Independent clusters in Tianjin (2005–2008) and Gansu/Qinghai (2019) [2, 27] further corroborate China’s spatially heterogeneous mumps distribution [3, 5, 27], supporting region-specific control measures.

The GTWR analysis revealed dynamic associations between socioeconomic factors and mumps incidence. The transition from positive to negative effects for illiteracy rate and population density demonstrates successful public health education [1, 6]. The strengthened influence of household size supports household contact as a key transmission route [28], while weakened healthcare resource impacts reflect systemic improvements [1, 25]. Fluctuating effects of GDP per capita and child dependency ratio confirm nonlinear socioeconomic relationships [6, 29], highlighting how education and family structure systematically affect transmission during economic development [30].

Significant regional variations were identified in socioeconomic influences on mumps incidence. Healthcare resources showed opposing east-west effects: a positive association for the number of hospital beds per 1,000 population in western China versus a negative association in eastern regions, potentially reflecting regional efficiency differences [4, 29]. GDP per capita exhibited protective effects on mumps incidence in western/southeastern regions but was associated with increased risk in other regions, supporting economic spillover effects [4]. The north-south gradient in the impact of illiteracy rate on mumps incidence may reflect health literacy disparities [31], while more pronounced risk effects of child dependency ratio in western provinces confirm regional demographic heterogeneity [6]. These findings emphasize the need for regionally tailored strategies, particularly for healthcare resource allocation, health education, and vaccination [3].

Spatiotemporal heterogeneity was evident in the associations between socioeconomic factors and mumps incidence. Healthcare resources showed consistent east–west disparities: bed numbers had a stronger risk effect in western China, where lower absolute availability may constrain healthcare access and amplify transmission risk, while bed occupancy rate posed a greater risk in eastern regions, indicating that high resource utilization in these areas may lead to overcrowding and facilitate disease spread—suggesting fundamental differences in regional resource utilization efficiency [29]. The influence of GDP per capita on mumps incidence also varied spatiotemporally—shifting from a primary risk factor in the east (2005) to a risk driver in the west (after 2015)—which may reflect evolving regional development and population mobility patterns [3, 6]. A persistently stronger positive association was found between illiteracy rate and mumps incidence in northern China, highlighting enduring inequalities in health literacy [6]. The risk effects of population density and child dependency ratio alternated between eastern and western regions, with a notably heightened risk in the east in 2020, potentially associated with demographic changes induced by fertility policies [30]. These results demonstrate how regional development, demographic shifts, and public policies interact to influence mumps transmission, underscoring the importance of region-specific control strategies [1, 5].

These results carry important policy implications, particularly for designing differentiated public health interventions tailored to regional contexts. The divergent effects of healthcare resources between eastern and western China suggest that vaccination strategies should be regionally adapted: western regions may benefit from increased healthcare infrastructure and resource allocation to improve vaccine accessibility and coverage, whereas eastern regions might focus on optimizing existing resource use and preventing overcrowding-related transmission. Similarly, the persistent association of illiteracy with higher mumps incidence in northern China underscores the need for targeted health education campaigns in these areas to improve health literacy and vaccination uptake. Furthermore, the varying influence of household size and child dependency ratio across regions supports implementing demographic-sensitive vaccination policies, especially in western provinces where larger household sizes may facilitate transmission. By aligning vaccination strategies and educational efforts with the specific socioeconomic and demographic profiles of eastern and western regions, public health programs can enhance their effectiveness and reduce the national burden of mumps.

Our findings align with international studies demonstrating persistent spatiotemporal clustering of mumps despite vaccination programs [4, 5, 27]. Similar to observations in South Korea [6, 32] and the US [33], we identified distinct high-risk clusters, though China’s western region patterns differ from the urban residential clusters reported in MSM populations [33]. Our identified risk factors (population density, household size) parallel findings in South Korea regarding age-group correlations [6, 22]. Notably, our GTWR results reveal more complex spatial heterogeneity in socioeconomic factor impacts compared to studies using conventional models [3, 4], particularly the dual role of healthcare resources which diverges from linear associations reported elsewhere [6]. The transitioning effects of education factors mirror temporal patterns observed in Guangxi’s spatiotemporal clusters [3], suggesting universal health education dynamics across populations.

This study innovatively integrates space-time scan statistics with GTWR modeling to systematically characterize mumps spatiotemporal dynamics and drivers during 2005–2020. Key contributions include: (1) Space-time scanning not only confirmed western China as persistent high-risk clusters but also identified unique transient clusters in eastern regions; (2) GTWR enabled the first quantitative assessment of spatiotemporal heterogeneity in socioeconomic impacts; (3) Provincial-level analysis over 15 years provided robust evidence for long-term epidemiological patterns. This methodological framework advances infectious disease spatiotemporal epidemiology and informs precision public health strategies.

This study has several limitations. First, case data based on clinical diagnoses may be influenced by variations in testing and reporting rates across provinces, potentially confounding true incidence with surveillance intensity. This is particularly relevant for the observed high incidence in western China, which may partly reflect better reporting rather than higher transmission. Second, our use of provincial-level data might mask important sub-provincial heterogeneity and could introduce ecological bias, limiting the extrapolation of findings to finer scales. Third, the unavailability of data on key covariates like vaccination coverage, environmental factors, and urbanization (excluded due to collinearity) may have resulted in omitted variable bias, simplifying the complex drivers of mumps transmission. These limitations suggest that our findings represent macro-level associations, and caution should be exercised in their interpretation and application.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive spatiotemporal analysis of mumps incidence across China from 2005 to 2020. The key finding is that while nationwide incidence has declined, significant and persistent geographical disparities remain, concentrated in western China. More importantly, our analysis reveals that the drivers of mumps transmission are not static or uniform; they exhibit complex spatiotemporal heterogeneity. Factors such as socioeconomic status, healthcare resources, and population structure have varying and even opposing effects in different regions and time periods

These findings highlight the critical need for region-specific public health strategies. Vaccination efforts should be prioritized in western China to improve coverage and accessibility, while eastern regions should focus on optimizing existing resource utilization and preventing overcrowding-related transmission. Targeted health education is essential in northern China to address low health literacy, and demographic-aware interventions are needed in western provinces to address household transmission risks. Aligning interventions with regional socioeconomic and demographic profiles will enhance the effectiveness of mumps control efforts nationwide.

For future research, this study highlights the necessity of adopting dynamic and spatially explicit models, like GTWR, to better understand infectious disease dynamics. Further investigations should focus on integrating virological surveillance (e.g., genotype distribution) with these socioeconomic and spatial analyses to provide a more holistic understanding of mumps transmission and to evaluate the effectiveness of the tailored intervention strategies proposed here.

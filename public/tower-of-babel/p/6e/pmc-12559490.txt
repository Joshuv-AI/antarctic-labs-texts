The correlation of DPM1 overexpression with immune infiltration and poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Laiwei You, Yang Zhou, Yong Cheng, Huan Wang, Hao Li, Jianrong Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe DPM1 gene, crucial for glycosylation processes, has shown abnormal expression in various cancers, raising interest in its potential oncogenic role and as a biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).MethodsTranscriptomic data were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases. DPM1 expression levels were compared between HCC tissues and adjacent normal tissues. Clinical correlations were assessed using statistical analyses, including survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression. Immune microenvironment profiling was conducted to evaluate associations between DPM1 expression and immune cell infiltration patterns.ResultsElevated DPM1 levels were associated with advanced tumor stages (P < 0.001), higher pathologic T stage (P < 0.001), increased histologic grade (P < 0.001), tumor positivity (P < 0.001), tissue inflammation (P < 0.001), and elevated alpha-fetoprotein levels (AFP > 400 ng/mL, P < 0.05). Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified DPM1 as an independent prognostic factor for reduced overall survival (HR = 1.990, 95% CI 1.390–2.848). Immunological analysis revealed that DPM1 expression was positively correlated with T helper cells (R = 0.268, P < 0.001) and Th2 cells (R = 0.295, P < 0.001), and negatively correlated with plasmacytoid dendritic cells (R=-0.291, P < 0.001) and cytotoxic cells (R=-0.284, P < 0.001).ConclusionsDPM1 serves as a promising prognostic biomarker in HCC, with its expression correlating with unfavorable clinical outcomes and immune landscape alterations. Future studies should further validate DPM1’s impact on ferroptosis and immune evasion in HCC, and explore its potential as a therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03592-y.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, DPM1, Prognosis, Immune infiltration, Biomarker

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, with approximately 19 million new cases diagnosed, and nearly 10 million deaths attributed to cancer in 2020 alone [1]. Among various cancer types, HCC ranks as the sixth most prevalent cancer globally and is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths [2]. The incidence and mortality rates of HCC are particularly alarming in Asia and Africa, where they significantly exceed those observed in other regions [3]. This stark disparity highlights the urgent need for enhanced understanding and intervention strategies to combat HCC, especially in high-risk populations.

Dolichol-phosphate mannose synthase 1 (DPM1) is a gene that has garnered attention for its potential role in various malignancies, including HCC. Previous studies have indicated that DPM1 is involved in glycosylation processes, which are crucial for protein function and cellular signaling [4]. Dysregulation of glycosylation pathways has been implicated in cancer progression and metastasis, suggesting that DPM1 may contribute to the aggressive nature of HCC [5]. Furthermore, research has shown that altered expression of DPM1 correlates with poor prognosis in HCC patients, underscoring its potential as a biomarker for disease severity and therapeutic response [6]. Understanding the functional implications of DPM1 in HCC could open new avenues for innovative therapeutic strategies that target glycosylation pathways to improve patient outcomes [7].

The increasing prevalence of cancer represents a significant global health challenge, with An estimated 19.3 million new cases And nearly 10 million cancer-related deaths reported in 2020 alone [8]. Among various malignancies, HCC stands out as one of the most common forms of Liver cancer, accounting for approximately 75% of liver cancer cases and exhibiting a high mortality rate due to late diagnosis and limited treatment options [9]. The urgent need for effective biomarkers for early detection and targeted therapies has prompted extensive research into the molecular mechanisms underlying HCC.

This study focuses on the DPM1 gene, which encodes the dolichol-phosphate mannose synthase 1 protein, a critical enzyme involved in the glycosylation process, particularly in the synthesis of N-glycans [10]. DPM1 plays a pivotal role in the post-translational modification of proteins, influencing various cellular functions, including cell adhesion and signaling pathways [11]. Recent studies have highlighted the aberrant expression of DPM1 in multiple cancer types, including breast, colorectal, and notably, HCC, where its overexpression correlates with poor prognosis and aggressive tumor behavior [12]. The potential oncogenic role of DPM1, along with its capacity to serve as a biomarker for HCC, has garnered significant attention in the scientific community, suggesting that targeting DPM1 may offer new therapeutic avenues for managing this lethal disease [13]. Furthermore, the exploration of DPM1’s involvement in glycosylation-related pathways may provide insights into the broader implications of glycosylation in cancer biology [14].

This study aims to investigate the expression of DPM1 in HCC and its clinical significance. To achieve this, we will utilize transcriptomic data analysis from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases. The research will incorporate survival analysis, differential expression gene analysis, and immune infiltration analysis to evaluate the potential of DPM1 as a prognostic biomarker. By analyzing the expression levels of DPM1 in HCC tissues compared to adjacent normal liver tissues, we seek to elucidate its role in tumor progression and patient outcomes. The integration of various analytical methods will provide a comprehensive understanding of DPM1’s impact on HCC, potentially leading to improved prognostic assessments and therapeutic strategies. This research holds significant implications for enhancing the management of HCC and identifying novel biomarkers for early detection and treatment.

Methods

Data processing

Transcriptomic and clinical data for HCC and adjacent normal liver tissue were retrieved from the TCGA database, with RNA-seq data normalized to TPM format. To facilitate a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis, additional TPM-standardized RNA-seq data were obtained from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) database. For external validation of DPM1 expression differences, mRNA expression data were downloaded from the GSE14520, GSE25097, GSE121248, and GSE76427 datasets available in the GEO database via the NCBI platform.

The study included 30 liver cancer and 30 non-liver cancer specimens. These datasets provide valuable information for the analysis of gene expression in liver and non-liver cancer specimens, offering a comprehensive resource for further investigation.

DPM1 protein expression

Variations in DPM1 protein expression between HCC cells and normal hepatocytes were analyzed using data from the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC), accessible via the UALCAN platform. Additionally, immunohistochemical data on DPM1 expression in HCC specimens were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database [15].

Survival analysis

Survival analysis was conducted on the TCGA-LIHC database using Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression models. To stratify patients into high- and low-DPM1 expression groups, the ‘surv_cutpoint’ algorithm from the survminer package in R was applied. The influence of clinical variables on patient outcomes was assessed through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, with prognostic variables exhibiting a P-value of less than 0.1 in the univariate analysis included in the multivariate model. Regression results were visualized as forest plots using ggplot2.

Building and validating nomogram

Using independent prognostic factors identified from Cox analysis, we developed nomograms to predict survival probabilities. These nomograms integrate multiple predictors as scaled line segments on a common plane, effectively visualizing their interrelationships within the predictive model. To assess their validity, calibration plots were generated using the RMS package to evaluate the model’s alignment with actual clinical outcomes.

Differential gene expression analysis

Samples from the TCGA-LIHC dataset were stratified into high- and low-DPM1 expression groups based on the median DPM1 expression level. Differential gene expression (DEG) analysis was performed using the DESeq2 package in R, with DEGs identified based on an adjusted P-value < 0.05 and |log2FoldChange| >2. The relationship between DPM1 expression and the top five upregulated and downregulated DEGs was examined using Spearman correlation analysis. DEGs were visualized through volcano plots.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

To explore potential function of DPM1-interacting partners, An Analysis of protein interactions with STRING, using a confidence score greater than 0.7, generated a PPI network consisting of DPM1 proteins and 15 associated proteins [16]. Furthermore, to explore the interactions and functions of the DPM1 gene, we utilized GeneMANIA [17].

The analysis of immune infiltration within high and low DPM1 expression groups

Using Spearman’s correlation analysis, we assessed the relationship between DPM1 expression and immune cell infiltration [18, 19]. We analyzed immune cell infiltration differences between high DPM1 expression and low DPM1 expression groups using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Cell culture

The human hepatoma cell lines, Huh7, HepG2 and immortal hepatic cell QSG-7701 were obtained from the Institute of Cell Biology (Shanghai, China). These cell lines were cultured in either RPMI-1640 or DMEM medium (Gibco/Invitrogen, Camarillo, CA, United States) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (PAN-Biotech, Aidenbach, Germany). The cells were then incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 environment.

RT-qPCR

This study utilized Real-Time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) to validate DPM1 expression levels in liver cancer. The human hepatoma cell lines and immortal hepatic cells are used in this experiment. The RT-qPCR assay was performed according to the MIQE guidelines. The RT-qPCR primers were designed using Primer3 online software (https://bioinfo.ut.ee/primer3-0.4.0/) and synthesized by Sangon Biotech Co, Ltd (Shanghai, China). For cDNA synthesis, 1 µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed using the PrimeScript RT Kit (Takara Bio™ Inc., San Jose, CA, USA). This process followed the manufacturer’s protocol. Hieff UNICON®Power qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) was used to conduct RT-qPCR experiment on a Bio-Rad CFX96 System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The reaction conditions were as follows: pre-denaturation at 95℃ for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of amplification at 95℃ for 10 s and 60 ℃ for 30 s. Relative mRNA expression levels of DPM1 were measured based on the 2-ΔΔCt method with 18 S used for normalization. The primer pair were used in the following order:

Ethical review

In this study, RT-qPCR technique was used to verify the mRNA levels differences in whole blood samples between liver cancer patients and healthy controls, with 30 cases in each group. This project adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was authorized by the Medical Ethics Committee of Gongli Hospital of Shanghai Pudong New Area, Shanghai, China (Approval Number: 2020SHGLH009). All patients gave written informed consent for research purposes before participating in the study.

Statistical analysis

Data Analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 26 and R (version 4.2.1). The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to evaluate the significance of DPM1 expression in unpaired tissue samples. Furthermore, the relationship between clinical characteristics and DPM1 expression was analyzed using both the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and logistic regression analysis. To explore the correlation between the two groups, we used Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. The significance of RT-qPCR expression analysis was completed using analysis of variance (ANOVA). A P-value of less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

High expression of DPM1 in hepatocellular carcinoma

Pan-cancer analysis using the TCGA and GTEx databases revealed that DPM1 was significantly overexpressed across multiple cancer types, including hepatocellular carcinoma, bladder cancer, esophageal cancer, head and neck cancer, and so on (Fig. 1A). Notably, DPM1 expression was significantly higher in HCC compared to normal hepatocyte tissues (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1B). In a paired Analysis of 50 HCC samples and their corresponding paracancerous tissues, DPM1 was markedly overexpressed in tumor tissues (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1C). This elevated DPM1 expression in HCC was further validated at the transcriptomic level using the GSE25097, GSE121248, GSE76427 datasets, and GSE57957 (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1D–G).

High DPM1 expression is associated with adverse clinicopathologic features

Elevated DPM1 expression was significantly associated with more advanced HCC, including higher overall pathologic stage, pathologic T stage, histologic grade, tumor status, and increased AFP levels (all P < 0.05) (Fig. 2C–G). Furthermore, univariate logistic regression analysis revealed a strong correlation between DPM1 expression and key clinical pathological characteristics, particularly pathologic stage, T stage, histologic grade, tumor status, and AFP levels (all P < 0.05). Additionally, while DPM1 expression showed a potential association with vascular invasion (OR = 1.513, 95% CI = 0.951–2.407, P = 0.081), this correlation did not reach statistical significance (Table 1).

High DPM1 expression is associated with poorer prognosis in HCC

The Kaplan-Meier method was used to assess the prognostic impact of DPM1 expression in HCC. Patients were stratified into high- and low-DPM1 expression groups using the ‘surv_cutpoint’ algorithm [20]. The results revealed that elevated DPM1 expression was significantly associated with poorer overall survival (OS) (HR = 2.04, 95% CI = 1.44–2.89, P < 0.001), disease-specific survival (DSS) (HR = 2.57, 95% CI = 1.65–4.01, P < 0.001), and progression-free interval (PFI) (HR = 1.61, 95% CI = 1.17–2.22, P = 0.003) (Fig. 3A–C).

Diagnostic and prognostic value of DPM1 in HCC

The diagnostic potential of DPM1 in HCC was assessed using ROC analysis based on TCGA data. The results demonstrated that DPM1 exhibited strong prognostic performance, with An AUC of 0.857 (Fig. 4A). To further evaluate its prognostic value, we constructed a risk score model. The analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk score cohort experienced endpoint events more rapidly, underscoring the association between elevated DPM1 expression and poorer HCC prognosis, thereby reinforcing its clinical significance (Fig. 4B). Time-dependent ROC curve was constructed from the TCGA datasets, further supporting the prognostic value of DPM1 in predicting 1-,3-, and 5-year survival rates (Fig. 4C).

Predictive power of DPM1 for 1-, 3-, and 5- year overall survival (OS) by time-dependent ROC analysis in (C) TCGA. (D) Forest map based on Univariate Cox analysis for overall survival. (E) Forest map based on Multivariate Cox analysis for overall survival. (F) Prediction of 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS by nomogram. (G) Calibration plots were used to validate the nomogram model

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to determine the prognostic factors associated with DPM1 expression in HCC. Univariate analysis revealed that high DPM1 expression (HR = 1.990, P < 0.001), advanced T stage (T3 vs. T1, HR = 2.674, P < 0.001; T4 vs. T1, HR = 5.386, P < 0.001), and M stage (M1 vs. M0, HR = 4.077, P = 0.017) were significantly correlated with poorer overall survival (OS) in HCC patients (Fig. 4D). Multivariate analysis further confirmed that DPM1 overexpression (HR = 1.990, P < 0.001) and advanced T stage (T3 vs. T1, HR = 2.674, P < 0.001; T4 vs. T1, HR = 5.386, P < 0.001) were independent risk factors for OS in HCC patients (Fig. 4E).

To further validate the prognostic significance of DPM1 in HCC, we developed a nomogram incorporating independent risk factors, such as T stage and DPM1 expression, to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival (OS) rates. The cumulative score, obtained by summing the individual scores of each prognostic factor, was used to estimate OS in HCC patients. The nomogram demonstrated that a higher total score was associated with a poorer prognosis (Fig. 4F). Additionally, calibration curves were used to evaluate the alignment between the nomogram’s predicted survival probabilities and actual outcomes at different time points. The results indicated that DPM1 expression contributed to more accurate survival predictions at 3 and 5 years compared to the 1-year survival estimate (Fig. 4G).

Identification of DEGs between high and low DPM1 expression groups in HCC patients

A total of 310 differentially expressed transcriptomic genes were identified between the high and low DPM1 expression cohorts, including 274 upregulated and 36 downregulated genes (adjusted P-value < 0.05 and |log2FoldChange| >2) (Fig. 5A). The top five upregulated (ANKRD26P1, LGALS14, TUBA3C, RHOXF2B, AL139327.1) and downregulated (CYP11B2, PCSK1, SMR3A, CALCA, ANKFN1) genes were selected for further analysis based on their adjusted P-values. Heatmaps were used to illustrate the correlation between these differentially expressed genes and DPM1 expression (Fig. 5B). Utilizing GO and KEGG enrichment analyses, we carried out functional annotation of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The results indicated that the main cellular components (CC) were associated with integral component of synaptic membrane, intrinsic component of synaptic membrane and axon terminus. KEGG analysis revealed that these differential genes were predominantly implicated in pathways such as Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, Protein digestion and absorption (Fig. 5C, supplemental file).

We investigated the association between DPM1 expression and diverse biological processes by conducting GSEA using both c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt and c5.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt datasets. Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of programmed cell death that is distinct from necrosis, apoptosis, and autophagy. Unlike normal cells, cancer cells often exhibit increased iron uptake and utilization to support their growth and invasion [22]. Ferroptosis has been shown to play a pivotal role in treating HCC and has emerged as a potent therapeutic approach [23, 24]. Studies have confirmed that specific genes are related to ferroptosis in cancer [25]. Our study analyzed the correlation between DPM1 And 25 genes related to ferroptosis using the TCGA database. The findings revealed a positive correlation between the expression of DPM1 and the genes HSPA5, EMC2, SLC7A11, NFE2L2, HSPB1, FANCD2, CISD1, FDFT1, SLC1A5, TFRC, RPL8, NCOA4, LPCAT3, DPP4, CS, CARS1, ATP5MC3, ALOX15, ACSL4, and ATL1. In contrast, DPM1 expression showed a negatively correlation with MT1G, SAT1, and GLS2 (Fig. 7B, C).

DPM1 expression is considerably associated with immune infiltration

Research has demonstrated that immune cells play a significant role in cancer. We first used TIMER to investigate the relationship between DPM1 expression, tumor purity, and immune cell infiltration. The results showed a significant positive correlation between DPM1 expression in HCC and tumor purity (r = 0.068, p = 2.06e-01), B-cell infiltration (r = 0.262, p = 8.25e-07), CD8 + T-cell infiltration (r = 0.264, P = 7.49e-07), CD4 + T-cell infiltration (r = 0.233, P = 1.32e-05), macrophage infiltration (r = 0.379, P = 4.11e-13), neutrophil infiltration (r = 0.292, P = 3.27e-08), and dendritic cell (DC) infiltration (r = 0.322, P = 1.26e-09) (Fig. 8A).

Prior studies have underscored the significance of intratumoral immune cells populations in various human malignancies. Our research explored the relationships between DPM1 and 24 types of intratumoral immune cells using the ssGSEA algorithm. The findings revealed notable positive correlations between DPM1 and T helper cells (r = 0.268), Th2 cells (r = 0.295), and central memory T cells (Tcm) (r = 0.182) (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 8B, D, E). Conversely, DPM1 expression demonstrated a substantial negative correlation with dendritic cells (DC) (r=−0.260), cytotoxic cells (r=−0.284), plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC) (r=−0.291), B cells (r=−0.204), and Th17 cells (r = 0.244) (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 8B, F, G). We conducted a comparative analysis of the enrichment scores for the 24 immune cell types in groups with high and low DPM1 expression. The results indicated that the DPM1 high-expression group had higher enrichment scores in 4 types of immune cells, whereas the low DPM1 expression group exhibited higher scores in 11 types of immune cells (Fig. 8C).

The DPM1 expression levels in vitro

To validate DPM1 expression in HCC patients, we assessed DPM1 expression levels across two distinct hepatoma cell lines: HepG2 and Hub7 and one normal liver cell line (QSG-7701). The results demonstrated that the mRNA expression, as determined by RT-qPCR, indicated significantly elevated transcription levels of DPM1 transcription levels in cancerous cell lines compared to the normal groups (Fig. 9A). Additionally, the whole blood samples were collected from patients with liver cancer and from individuals undergoing normal physical examinations at our hospital for RT-qPCR experimental verification, and the result was consistent with prior prediction (Fig. 9B). This comprehensive analysis offered insights into the expression patterns. Consequently, DPM1 emerges as a promising prognostic biomarker for the survival of HCC patients.

Discussion

HCC is a highly aggressive malignancy associated with poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options. Current treatment strategies often fail to provide satisfactory outcomes, underscoring the urgent need for novel biomarkers that can aid in early diagnosis and prognosis. In this context, our study highlights the significant overexpression of DPM1 in HCC tissues, as well as its correlation with adverse clinicopathological features, establishing the necessity for further investigation into its role as a potential biomarker.

The findings from our research demonstrate that elevated DPM1 expression is consistently associated with advanced tumor stages and poorer overall survival rates in HCC patients. Using comprehensive analyses from various datasets, we established DPM1’s prognostic value, revealing its potential to serve as a reliable indicator for disease progression and patient outcomes. The identification of DPM1-related genes and pathways offers insights into the underlying mechanisms of HCC and illustrates the possible therapeutic implications. Ultimately, the promotion of DPM1 as a biomarker could not only improve prognostic accuracy but also guide personalized treatment strategies, enhancing the overall management of HCC patients.

DPM1 (Dolichol Phosphate Mannose Synthase 1) plays a crucial role in the glycosylation of proteins, which is essential for various cellular functions, including cell signaling and protein stability. In this study, the correlation between elevated DPM1 expression and adverse clinicopathological features further underscores its potential role as a biomarker for poor prognosis in HCC patients. The findings indicate that DPM1 not only serves as a critical player in glycosylation processes but also may contribute to the malignant phenotype of HCC through its regulatory effects on gene expression. The association of DPM1 with immune cell infiltration also suggests that it may play a role in the tumor microenvironment, influencing immune evasion and response, which is critical for the development of effective immunotherapies in HCC [12, 26, 27].

DPM1 and its associated genes are primarily involved in critical biological processes such as glycosylation and N-glycan biosynthesis, which are essential for protein modification and cellular signaling. Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that DPM1 and its related genes participate in various pathways, including glycosylation processes, which are crucial for maintaining cellular functions and interactions. DPM1 facilitates protein modification through the form of N-glycosylation, thus dynamic changes in the structure of N-glycans on glycoproteins contributing to the development of hepatocellular carcinoma [28, 29]. The findings from our study indicate that high DPM1 expression correlates with adverse clinicopathological features and poorer prognosis in HCC patients, highlighting its potential as a prognostic biomarker. The association of DPM1 with pathways involved in tumor metabolism and immune response further emphasizes its importance in HCC pathogenesis. For instance, the interaction of DPM1 with ferroptosis-related genes suggests that it may influence the sensitivity of HCC cells to ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death that is increasingly recognized for its therapeutic potential in cancer treatment. Enhanced O-GlcNAcylation of some proteins, such as YAP1, ZEB1 and TFRC, has the potential to increase ferroptosis susceptibility in hepatocellular carcinoma [29–31]. The inability of ferroptotic cancer cells to elicit adaptive immune responses may arise from contextual deficiencies in intrinsic aspects of the process or from microenvironmental defects imposed by ferroptotic cancer cells themselves [33, 34]. By impairing antigen-presenting cells and weakening the adaptive immune response, ferroptosis may facilitate immune evasion and undermine the therapeutic potential of its induction in HCC [35], just as was showed in the study. These insights not only enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying HCC but also suggest that targeting DPM1 and its associated pathways could provide novel therapeutic strategies for managing this aggressive cancer type [14, 26, 36].

This study presents significant findings regarding the overexpression of DPM1 in HCC and its association with adverse clinicopathologic features and poorer prognosis. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, the sample size utilized in this research was relatively small, which may affect the generalizability of the results. Additionally, the reliance on publicly available datasets, such as TCGA and GEO, may introduce batch effects and confounding variables that could skew the analysis. Furthermore, RT-qPCR data (Fig. 9A) show statistically significant but biologically modest upregulation of DPM1 in HCC patients, which weakens DPM1’s utility as a diagnostic biomarker. The effects of DPM1-induced ferroptosis and ferroptosis-related immunosuppression on hepatocellular carcinoma cells still require comprehensive in vivo analysis and validation.

The glycosylation-related gene DPM1 is involved in the N-glycan biosynthesis pathway. Glycosylation can enhance the O-GlcNAcylation of related proteins, such as YAP1 and ZEB1, potentially increasing the susceptibility of HCC cells to ferroptosis. However, ferroptotic cancer cells may fail to trigger an effective adaptive immune response, possibly due to intrinsic defects in this process, ferroptosis-induced alterations in the tumor microenvironment. By impairing antigen-presenting cells and weakening adaptive immunity, ferroptosis may contribute to immune evasion and diminish the therapeutic potential of immune-based strategies.

In summary, our results indicate that high DPM1 expression is significantly correlated with advanced disease stages and poorer survival outcomes in HCC patients, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker. The identification of DPM1 as a key player in HCC pathogenesis opens avenues for further exploration of its role as a therapeutic target. Future research should focus on elucidating the underlying mechanisms of DPM1 in HCC and evaluating its efficacy as a target for novel treatment strategies, which could ultimately improve patient outcomes in this challenging malignancy.

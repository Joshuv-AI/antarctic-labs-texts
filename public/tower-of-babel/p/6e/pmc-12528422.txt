Adaptive neural network based leader-following consensus control for a class of second-order nonlinear multi-agent systems
Authors: Fathia Moh. Al Samman, Wajiha Abdul Khaliq, Shreefa O. Hilali, Azmat Ullah Khan Niazi, Aseel Smerat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The leader-following consensus problem for a type of second-order nonlinear multi-agent systems (MASs) with input saturation, actuator faults, and sensor faults is examined in this study. An adaptive control strategy based on neural networks (NNs) is suggested to overcome the difficulties brought on by unknown nonlinear dynamics and real-world limitations. To handle the unknown nonlinear factors more precisely and flexibly than traditional approaches that depend on global Lipschitz requirements, we use the differential mean value theorem. This improvement enhances the system’s capacity to deal with nonlinear behaviors that change quickly. Additionally, sensor faults that affect location and velocity readings and actuator faults that may decrease or deform the input signal are taken into consideration in the control design. The suggested design also clearly addresses input saturation, which may restrict the control authority. Complete state measurements are not required because a distributed adaptive NN-based controller is created only on relative location and velocity data. Using stability theory and appropriate Lyapunov function construction, we rigorously demonstrate that the suggested approach leads to leader-following consensus. The efficiency and resilience of the suggested control strategy against nonlinear uncertainty, actuator and sensor failures, and saturation effects in complicated multi-agent networks are confirmed by simulation results.

Introduction

The issue of consensus control1 in multi-agent systems (MASs) has attracted a lot of attention from researchers recently because of its wide range of applications in fields like sensor networks, cooperative robotics2, and autonomous cars3. Leader-following consensus is one of the fundamental control problems in this area, where a collection of follower agents uses distributed control protocols via a communication graph to try to synchronize their states with a chosen leader4. This issue has been addressed to a great extent for both linear and nonlinear MASs under optimal circumstances5. Nevertheless, in order to guarantee strong agreement, real-world applications present a number of difficulties that must be carefully resolved, including system nonlinearities, unknown dynamics6, communication restrictions, actuator and sensor faults, and input saturation limitations.

Numerous scenarios, including fixed and switching topologies7, have been the subject of in-depth research on leader-following consensus8. For instance, integral sliding mode9 control-based distributed consensus protocols have been created for linear MASs under switching communication graphs, and their uses on quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been established10. Reinforcement learning techniques have been investigated to build optimal control strategies that ensure convergence of consensus errors while maintaining system stability in second-order MASs, where both position and velocity consensus are necessary11.

Despite these developments, the majority of research still in existence is predicated on ideal circumstances, such as fully operational actuators and sensors12, unrestricted control inputs, and well-established dynamics13. In actual use, actuator faults like bias, ineffectiveness, and total failure may affect system performance or possibly cause the MASs to become unstable14. In a similar way, sensor faults could result in inaccurate status feedback and poor control selection. Furthermore, the amount of the control signal that may be applied is limited by input saturation, a frequent constraint in physical actuators, making controller design and analysis more difficult15. If not appropriately handled, these practical issues could make it impossible to reach consensus or result in dangerous system behavior.

Adaptive control methods have been widely used in MASs research to address these issues16. Adaptive techniques work especially well when dealing with external disruptions, modeling uncertainties, and unknown system parameters17. For strict-feedback nonlinear MASs with uncertain control directions and external disturbances, adaptive control procedures have been developed, for instance18. In order to reduce the strain of constant communication while maintaining consensus convergence, event-triggered adaptive protocols have also been developed19. Finite-time adaptive control techniques have been put forth more recently in an effort to shorten convergence times and enhance temporary performance20. Neural networks (NNs)21, especially radial basis function NNs22, have been used for their strong function approximation skills in addition to adaptive approaches. NNs can be taught online to approximate unknown nonlinear functions in the dynamics of the system when combined with adaptive control. Even with modeling uncertainties, external disruptions, and incomplete system parameter information, MASs can achieve semi-globally uniformly23 ultimately bounded performance due to these neuroadaptive control techniques24.

The cooperative control of nonlinear multi-agent systems (MASs) has garnered considerable research interest due to its broad applicability in domains such as robotics, vehicular systems, and sensor networks. A particular challenge lies in the coordination of heterogeneous agents under dynamic environmental and system constraints such as actuator/sensor faults, input saturation, and unknown nonlinearities. To achieve rapid and reliable coordination, prescribed-time consensus and formation control strategies have emerged as powerful tools. In this context, event-triggered and self-triggered mechanisms have shown promise in reducing communication load while maintaining synchronization accuracy. For example, Li et al.25 developed a dynamic event-triggered protocol to ensure prescribed-time consensus of nonlinear MASs, offering both communication efficiency and convergence guarantees. Similarly, time-varying dynamic event-triggered leader-following consensus under input saturation has been effectively addressed using adaptive control techniques26. These developments demonstrate that integrating adaptive timing schemes into consensus control frameworks significantly enhances their robustness and practicality.

Furthermore, expanding coordination into more complex environments involving human inputs or mixed ground-aerial agent dynamics introduces additional challenges. To tackle these, Huang et al.27 presented a human-in-the-loop, model-free reinforcement learning approach for optimal output synchronization, capturing human intentions while maintaining stability across agent clusters. In mixed-platform environments, such as cooperative UAV–UGV teams, Barzegar and Li28 introduced a robust funnel control-based method, effectively accommodating dynamic heterogeneity. In tandem, the issue of regulating uncertain nonlinear MASs within a finite prescribed time using event-based schemes has also been explored29, establishing the groundwork for time-sensitive, resource-aware MAS applications. These contributions collectively underscore the growing importance of integrating prescribed-time guarantees, event-based triggers, and adaptive learning into MAS control to handle uncertainty, heterogeneity, and real-time constraints. The Lipschitz condition, which linearizes the system30 using a bounded gradient assumption, is a popular method for dealing with nonlinearities in MASs31.

A neural network prescribed-time observer-based output-feedback control method was proposed for uncertain pure-feedback nonlinear systems, highlighting how neural observers can guarantee state estimation and closed-loop performance within prescribed times32. The control of heavy-lift launch vehicles under actuator faults and rate gyro malfunctions was addressed by developing quantized predefined-time control, demonstrating resilience against hardware malfunctions while maintaining convergence guarantees33. Elevator fault diagnosis was explored by integrating digital twins with physics-informed neural networks and relational graph convolutional networks, providing a hybrid model that supports fault diagnosis and monitoring34. An optimal fully actuated system approach for trajectory tracking control of robot manipulators was introduced, ensuring precision in robotic operations35. Semi-global stabilization of parabolic PDE–ODE systems under input saturation was studied, offering solutions to handle distributed dynamics subject to actuation limits36. A multi-personality, multi-agent meta-reinforcement learning framework was developed for adaptive and coordinated transportation systems, enabling better decision-making in large-scale traffic systems37. A knowledge-guided self-learning control strategy was introduced for mixed vehicle platoons with delays, combining data-driven learning with domain expertise to enhance platoon stability38.

A hybrid model using neural networks inspired by vehicle lateral dynamics was designed for parameter identification and error characterization, contributing to accurate vehicle modeling39. Adaptive PI event-triggered control for MIMO nonlinear systems with input delay was proposed to reduce communication demands while ensuring system stability40. An adaptive memory event-triggered output-feedback control scheme was developed for lane-keeping of autonomous heavy trucks, with additional roll prevention mechanisms41. Adaptive pseudoinverse control was introduced for constrained hysteretic nonlinear systems, demonstrated on dielectric elastomer actuators42. Neural networks were combined with physics-based estimators for vehicle longitudinal dynamics modeling, enabling robust performance using limited driving data43. Physically interpretable wavelet-guided networks were proposed for machine intelligence fault prediction, providing interpretable deep learning models for health monitoring44. Prescribed-performance adaptive robust control was presented for robotic manipulators with fuzzy uncertainty, ensuring robustness under uncertain environments45.

Condition monitoring of discrete power devices was investigated using stress quantification and mold temperature sensing, introducing a data-driven approach for power electronics46. Adaptive fixed-time stabilization methods were contributed for uncertain nonlinear systems, offering convergence guarantees independent of initial conditions47. Contour tracking in robotic systems was enhanced through a time-varying internal model principle based approach, enabling precise tracking in dynamic environments48. Fixed-time safe tracking control for uncertain high-order nonlinear pure-feedback systems was proposed using unified transformation functions, ensuring finite-time safety guarantees49. The suggested NN-based adaptive approach is used to construct a distributed controller with reduced complexity. The suggested approach offers a scalable and reliable solution for complicated second-order systems while expanding on earlier findings on first-order MASs.

This work employs a RBFNN to approximate the unknown nonlinear terms in the adaptive consensus controller. The RBFNN is a single-hidden-layer network, with the input layer corresponding to the velocity state of the agent, the hidden layer consisting of Gaussian radial basis functions, and the output layer providing the approximation of the nonlinear function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f(v_i,t)$$\end{document}.

A star communication topology, which is frequently employed in leader-follower multi-agent systems, is depicted in Fig. 2. In this topology, all follower agents (represented by nodes F) are directly connected to a single leader agent (represented by node L) at the center. The leader provides information to each follower, but the followers do not speak directly to one another. Because of the direct connections, this centralized interaction structure allows for quick convergence to the leader’s trajectory; however, it also places a greater communication burden on the leader. Furthermore, because the followers only depend on the leader for coordination, the topology might be vulnerable to leader node failures.

Figures 11, 12, 13 and Table 1 present a comparative evaluation of the proposed NN-DMVT controller against Lipschitz-based and fault-tolerant NN baselines. In terms of consensus error, the proposed method achieves rapid convergence with a final residual error close to zero, while the Lipschitz-based method exhibits the slowest decay. The NN fault-tolerant controller performs better than the Lipschitz baseline but still lags behind the proposed approach.

Energy efficiency analysis reveals that the NN-DMVT controller consumes substantially less cumulative control effort compared to the Lipschitz-based scheme, which continues to grow over time. This indicates that the adaptive compensation in the NN-DMVT method not only improves tracking but also reduces the energy burden on the actuators.

Trajectory tracking under fault conditions further confirms the superiority of the proposed method. The NN-DMVT approach maintains smooth and accurate tracking, closely following the desired trajectory, whereas the Lipschitz baseline shows high-frequency oscillations and noticeable divergence. These results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed method in maintaining stable formation tracking despite nonlinearities and disturbances.

Conclusion

A leader-following consensus control technique based on adaptive neural networks (NNs) has been proposed in the present article for a type of second-order nonlinear multi-agent systems (MASs) that operate over an undirected and linked communication graph. The differential mean value theorem was used to manage the unknown nonlinearities in a more general and less restrictive way than standard methods that depend on global Lipschitz continuity. A number of important practical concerns that frequently show in actual MAS applications are successfully addressed by the suggested control structure. In order to maintain system safety and avoid actuator overload, input saturation limitations are specifically taken into account to make sure that control inputs stay within practical bounds. Furthermore, the control design specifically considers sensor and actuator faults, which can introduce errors in position and velocity measurements and cause a partial or complete loss of control effectiveness. In order to estimate and adjust for unknown nonlinearities and fault effects in real time, a distributed adaptive law is built using NN approximators. The suggested approach satisfies adequate conditions for obtaining leader-following consensus, according to theoretical analysis based on Lyapunov functions and other mathematical tools. The simulation results provide additional evidence of the established control approach’s resilience, flexibility, and efficiency in managing fault scenarios, saturation, and nonlinear dynamics.

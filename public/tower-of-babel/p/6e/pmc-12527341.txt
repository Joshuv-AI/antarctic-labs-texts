Papillary muscle infarction detection: unveiling the unseen with dual bright- and black-blood late gadolinium enhancement SPOT imaging
Authors: Théo Richard, Victor de Villedon de Naide, Kalvin Narceau, Thaïs Génisson, Kun He, Thibault Boullé, Guillaume Poirot, Soumaya Sridi, Pierre Jaïs, Matthias Stuber, Hubert Cochet, Aurelien Bustin
Published: 2025

Description

A 55-year-old male presented with chest pain and raised troponin level (22 000 ng/mL). Coronary angiography showed no proximal coronary occlusion. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging was subsequently performed on a 1.5-T Siemens Aera system in our hospital to investigate a suspected acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction.

Cine images showed inferomedial hypokinesis of the left ventricle and mild mitral insufficiency. Native T1 was elevated at 1080 ms. T2 mapping showed myocardial oedema with T2 being measured at 60 ms in the inferior wall, while remote myocardium T2 was measured at 42 ms. Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) using phase-sensitive inversion recovery (PSIR) showed transmural enhancement of the inferior wall with a central hypointense core, consistent with microvascular obstruction and suggesting myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary artery. In addition to these findings, joint bright- and black-blood SPOT imaging identified enhancement of the posteromedial papillary muscle (Figure 1), indicating papillary muscle infarction (PMI).

Comprehensive ventricular comparison of 2D short-axis slices of SPOT, reference PSIR and T2 mapping sequences, in a 55-year-old patient with transmural myocardial infarction, involving the right coronary artery territory and extending to the posterolateral papillary muscle. Infarction of the papillary muscle was detectable exclusively with SPOT imaging compared with PSIR.

SPOT is a recently introduced LGE technique that simultaneously acquires co-registered bright- and black-blood images. The bright-blood images allow to visualize precisely the cardiac anatomy, while the black-blood images optimally reveal the injured tissue by cancelling signal from both blood and healthy myocardium. This results in superior contrast at the blood-scar interface, which is particularly valuable for detecting subendocardial scars and PMI that are often challenging to identify using conventional PSIR.

PMI is clinically significant and has been linked with increased mortality, mitral regurgitation and ventricular arrhythmias. Thus, a fast and precise PMI detection is needed in order to guide patient management. In this case, PMI was readily detected using SPOT imaging and may explain the patient’s mitral insufficiency.

Lead author biography

Théo Richard is a PhD student in cardiac MRI at IHU Liryc—Heart Rhythm Disease Institute in Bordeaux, France. He graduated in 2024 from Télécom Physique Strasbourg with a master’s degree in biomedical engineering and a general engineering degree. His research interests focus on the development of advanced cardiac MRI techniques, including fast image acquisition and automated post-processing, for enhanced detection of ischaemic and non-ischaemic heart diseases.

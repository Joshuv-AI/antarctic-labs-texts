Association between triglyceride-glucose index and postoperative delirium following cardiac surgery: a multicenter retrospective analysis
Authors: Jia Zeng, Nanxi Li, Caifeng Ma, Yuqi Song, Weikun Tian, Ya Gao, Shuo Tang, Xiangtian Liu, Xinghan Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostoperative delirium (POD) after cardiac surgery correlates with adverse outcomes, increasing healthcare burdens and hindering patient recovery.The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, a marker of insulin resistance, shows cardiovascular prognostic value.This study sought to explore the association between the TyG index and the development of POD in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study included 1,850 patients (MIMIC-IV: n = 1,461; Yuhuangding: n = 389). A four-knot restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression analyzed the nonlinear relationship to determine the TyG threshold. Multivariable logistic regression and propensity score matching (PSM) controlled for baseline differences.Subgroup analyses were conducted.ResultsPatients with POD exhibited significantly higher TyG indices compared to non-POD patients (MIMIC-IV cohort: 9.06 vs. 8.83, P < 0.001). Using the optimal threshold derived from RCS regression, participants were stratified into two groups: Q1 (< 8.91) and Q2 (≥ 8.91). Multivariable logistic regression revealed that a TyG index ≥ 8.91 was independently associated with an increased risk of POD (adjusted OR 1.767, 95% CI: 1.406–2.219, P < 0.001). This association remained statistically significant in PSM-adjusted analyses across multiple regression models. These findings were validated in the Yuhuangding Hospital cohort. Subgroup analyses demonstrated significant interaction effects in specific patient populations.ConclusionThis study establishes a robust and independent association between an elevated TyG index and increased risk of POD in cardiac surgery patients, suggesting that the TyG index may serve as a valuable biomarker for identifying patients at risk of postoperative delirium.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02769-2.

Introduction

Postoperative delirium (POD) manifests as acute disturbances in attentional focus, consciousness level, and higher cognitive functions, typically stemming from physiological stressors rather than preexisting neurocognitive conditions [1].This complication not only extends the duration of hospital stays and raises healthcare costs but can also negatively impact cognitive outcomes in the long term [2].Patients undergoing cardiac surgery exhibit a notable vulnerability to postoperative delirium (POD), as meta-analyses have indicated incidence rates between 2.9% and 54.9%, which are significantly greater than those recorded in other significant surgical operations [3, 4].POD shares pathogenic similarities with postoperative cognitive decline (POCD). Notably, Glumac et al. showed preoperative dexamethasone reduces surgery-induced inflammation and early cognitive complications after cardiac surgery, highlighting inflammation’s role in POD [5].A comprehensive review and meta-analysis centered on cardiac surgery established that postoperative delirium (POD) is linked to several negative outcomes, such as higher mortality rates, longer durations of ICU and hospital admissions, as well as extended periods of mechanical ventilation [6].Due to its significant prevalence and severe outcomes, diagnosing, managing, and preventing postoperative delirium (POD) after cardiac surgery are essential focal points in clinical research [7].

In insulin resistance (IR), tissues exhibit a diminished response to insulin, actively contributing to irregularities in glucose and lipid metabolism. This metabolic state is triggered by reduced insulin sensitivity, leading to disrupted metabolic processes [8–10].Growing evidence indicates that insulin resistance (IR) could be significant in contributing to neurological impairment, given that insulin is crucial for neuronal survival, synaptic function, synaptic plasticity, and the uptake of glucose. Prior studies have established links between IR and neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer’s disease [11].Chronic IR may contribute to central nervous system (CNS) dysfunction through these mechanisms [11].The hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp (HEC) procedure, though considered the benchmark assessment for insulin sensitivity, sees limited clinical adoption due to technical demands, significant costs, and patient burden [12, 13].The TyG index, computed using routine fasting laboratory values (triglycerides and glucose), serves as an accessible and validated proxy for assessing insulin resistance, showing excellent concordance with gold-standard measurements [14].Existing literature consistently demonstrates the TyG index’s independent association with incidence and clinical outcomes in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disorders [14–16].

Notwithstanding the established risk factors for delirium following cardiac procedures [17–20], the possible connection between the TyG index and the risk of POD in this group has yet to be investigated. A significant independent relationship was observed linking higher TyG index levels to postoperative delirium development in cardiac surgery patients.This research intends to explore the potential of the TyG index as a new predictive biomarker for the early detection of individuals at high risk. It marks the first multicentric validation in the realm of cardiac surgery, quantitatively establishing risk gradients and addressing a significant knowledge gap in this area. These findings contribute to developing targeted strategies for POD prevention and management in cardiac surgical settings.

Methods and data

Study design and participants

Clinical records were sourced from two complementary datasets: version 3.0 of the MIMIC-IV critical care database [21]. Inclusion criteria comprised 389 sequential cardiac surgery admissions to Yuhuangding Hospital’s Cardiovascular Surgery Department, which is affiliated with Qingdao University, the observation window was March 1, 2021, through March 1, 2024. The hospital’s substantial surgical workload contributed to a diverse and representative sample.

Developed by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) researchers, the MIMIC-IV v3.0 database aggregates 14 years (2008–2022) of clinical records representing 364,627 unique patient encounters within the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center system.This study’s use of clinical records was provided by the respective IRBs of MIT and its affiliated teaching hospital, Beth Israel Deaconess.Before retrieving data, all authors who accessed the database successfully completed the NIH training course titled “Protecting Human Research Participants” and received certification (Certification ID: 11839895).

The Ethics Committee of Yuhuangding Hospital granted approval for the institutional cohort study (Approval No.2025 − 562). Due to the study’s retrospective design, the requirement for informed consent was waived, as it employed anonymized patient data that could not identify individuals, thereby presenting minimal risk to participants. All procedures complied with the ethical principles outlined by the 2013 revision of the Declaration of Helsinki (revised in 2013).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Figure 1 illustrates the study enrollment procedure through a detailed flowchart. Individuals from the MIMIC-IV v3.0 database cohort were omitted from consideration if they were younger than 18 years, or remained in the ICU for ≤ 24 h, did not have complete records of the Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU), or had preoperative neurological comorbidities such as dementia, depression, psychiatric conditions, or other central nervous system disorders. Further exclusion criteria encompassed a background of substance or alcohol use disorders, as well as any unavailable triglyceride or blood glucose data recorded during the first 24 h following ICU admission. Upon implementing these criteria, 1,461 cardiac surgery patients with full perioperative metabolic data were incorporated into the MIMIC-IV cohort.

Initially, 847 cardiac surgery patients from Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital were screened in the institutional cohort. Following application of identical selection criteria as the MIMIC-IV cohort, 389 subjects with complete data were included in the final analytical sample.

Data gathering

Data were obtained from MIMIC-IV through the utilization of Structured Query Language (SQL) via Navicat Premium (version 16.3.8, PremiumSoft CyberTech Ltd., Hong Kong, China) and PostgreSQL (version 14.5, PostgreSQL Global Development Group, United States).Variables obtained encompassed demographic information, comorbid conditions (classified on the basis of the International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision [ICD-9] codes), lab results, computed indices, surgical interventions, medications, and vital signs. The SQL code used for data extraction is publicly available in the official repository. In the cohort from Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital, patient information was gathered from the hospital’s electronic clinical management system, which contains records related to demographics, clinical variables, and vital signs.

The analysis took into account 36 different covariates. The baseline data comprised demographic details, existing health conditions, laboratory metrics, vital signs at the time of ICU entry, types of surgeries performed, specific treatments administered, and scores indicating the severity of illness. Demographic factors encompassed age and gender upon admission. The comorbidities examined included cardiac failure, peripheral angiopathies, cerebrovascular disorders, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, glucose metabolism disorder, and renal insufficiency. Laboratory parameters collected during hospitalization included complete blood counts (leukocytes, erythrocytes, thrombocytes), renal function markers (creatinine, BUN), and electrolyte levels (Na+, K+, Ca2+). Additional key parameters comprised coagulation markers (INR, PT, APTT) and differential leukocyte subsets (neutrophils, monocytes, lymphocytes). Hemodynamic and respiratory status documentation during ICU intake involved: cardiac frequency, respiratory rate, perfusion pressure (MAP), body thermoregulation data, and pulse oximetry values.Surgical interventions were classified into coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), valve repair or replacement procedures, along with various other types. Additionally, records for specific treatments, such as the use of mechanical ventilation and administration of midazolam, were also obtained. Clinical severity assessment incorporated both SAPS II (Simplified Acute Physiology Score II) and SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) metrics.

Triglyceride-glucose index

Within 24 h of ICU admission, initial laboratory measurements of serum triglyceride and fasting blood glucose levels were recorded [22, 23]. Insulin resistance was estimated via the TyG index, computed as ln(fasting triglycerides × fasting glucose/2), with both parameters measured in mg/dL units [24]. This biomarker, calculated through logarithmic conversion of fasting triglyceride-glucose multiplicative terms, has been extensively verified as a robust and practical alternative for evaluating insulin resistance across clinical and population research.

Postoperative delirium assessment

The main outcome measured was delirium, which was evaluated through the Intensive Care Unit Confusion Assessment Method (CAM-ICU) [25]. Patient consciousness status was initially evaluated with the RASS sedation assessment instrument. When RASS measurements indicated profound sedation or unresponsiveness (scores of −4 or −5), further assessment was not possible. The CAM-ICU evaluations began 24 h post-ICU admission and were conducted twice a day by healthcare professionals who had received training. This timing was chosen to avoid confounding effects from early postoperative physiological fluctuations, such as those associated with immediate anesthesia recovery, on delirium assessment. A RASS score of −3 or higher signifies lighter sedation, at which point the CAM-ICU assessment is carried out to diagnose delirium. The CAM-ICU diagnostic algorithm evaluates four key domains: (i) acute changes or fluctuating cognition, (ii) impaired focus, (iii) consciousness disturbances, and (iv) disordered thinking patterns.Delirium is clinically diagnosed upon detection of characteristics 1 and 2, plus at least one additional feature from 3 or 4.This assessment tool is preferentially employed by frontline medical staff given its operational practicality, robust psychometric properties (84–95% accuracy metrics), and validation through systematic evidence synthesis [26].

Statistical analysis

The cohort was categorized by POD development status during intensive care hospitalization.Continuous variables were analyzed appropriately for their distribution: normally distributed (mean ± SD, t-test) versus non-normally distributed (median[IQR], Wilcoxon test).Discrete data are presented as frequency distributions (n, %), with intergroup differences assessed by χ² or Fisher’s exact tests based on data characteristics.

The analysis strategy incorporated unadjusted and covariate-adjusted regression approaches, with TyG examined both dimensionally (continuous scale) and categorically (dichotomized). The binary categorization of TyG relied on the optimal cut-off value (8.91) determined from the restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression model.For multivariate logistic regression, the “Enter” method was used, where all predefined covariates were included in each model simultaneously without stepwise selection. Based on single-variable regression analysis in conjunction with a multivariable regression model, Model 1 included demographic characteristics and comorbidities. Model 2 was further refined to include laboratory variables. Additionally, Model 3 additionally accounted for vital signs (cardiac frequency, respiratory rate, MAP, body thermoregulation data, and pulse oximetry values), types of surgery (including coronary artery bypass grafting [CABG], valve repair or replacement, and others), interventions (like mechanical ventilation and the administration of midazolam), as well as illness severity metrics (SAPS II and SOFA scores).

To investigate possible nonlinear associations between the TyG index and the risk of POD, restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression analyses were conducted in both univariate and multivariate contexts. The cut-off value of 8.91, determined from the RCS model, was used to classify patients into high and low TyG index groups (TyG ≥ 8.91 vs. TyG < 8.91). To reduce intergroup disparities and control for confounders across TyG strata, we performed 1:1 propensity matching without replacement using a 0.02 caliper threshold, yielding 538 matched pairs for analysis.

Additionally, we utilized multivariable logistic regression to assess the TyG index’s predictive value for postoperative delirium development. Stratified analyses were performed to investigate the possible modification of effects by categorizing patients according to age, sex, cardiovascular comorbidities (such as congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, and cerebrovascular disease), diabetes, chronic kidney disease, midazolam administration, and mechanical ventilation use.

A post hoc power analysis demonstrated that the actual sample sizes of both the MIMIC database cohort (n = 1,461) and the Yuhuangding Hospital cohort (n = 389) exceeded the minimum required sample sizes calculated based on chi-square tests (283 and 107, respectively), indicating adequate statistical power.

All statistical computations were performed with R software (v2024.12.1 Build 563, R Development Core Team, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 using two-sided tests.

Study findings

Cohort description

From two distinct sources (MIMIC-IV and Yuhuangding Hospital), 1,850 participants were enrolled in this multicenter research (Table 1). Within the MIMIV-IV group, 70.4% were male (N = 1,029) and 29.6% were female (N = 432), with an average age of 66 years (IQR 58–74). Patients experiencing delirium exhibited significantly higher rates of congestive heart failure (53.4% versus 29.7%, P < 0.001), peripheral vascular disease (27.5% versus 15.3%, P < 0.001), and cerebrovascular disease (23.5% versus 9.4%, P < 0.001) in contrast to those not suffering from delirium. Laboratory findings indicated notably lower hemoglobin levels (11.1 vs. 11.4 g/L, P = 0.04), increased white blood cell counts (15.8 vs. 14.75 K/µL, P < 0.001), and higher platelet levels (186 vs. 173 K/µL, P = 0.012) among patients with delirium. Differences in critical interventions were marked between the two groups: exposure to midazolam was found in 20.5% of delirium patients compared to 1.6% of non-delirium patients (P < 0.001), and Propensity score matching

To minimize confounding effects, we implemented propensity-matched pairing using nearest-neighbor methodology (matching ratio 1:1) with a predefined caliper threshold of 0.01. Participants were stratified using a TyG index cutoff of 8.91 derived from restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis. Post-matching, 1,076 patients were retained, with balanced baseline characteristics between TyG groups. Propensity score distributions before and after matching demonstrated significantly improved overlap, indicating enhanced intergroup comparability (Fig. 3).After comprehensive covariate adjustment in propensity-matched analyses, multivariable models persistently demonstrated significant relationships linking higher TyG values with increased delirium susceptibility following cardiac procedures (Table 3). Compared to the low TyG group (Q1), the high TyG group (Q2) exhibited progressively increased adjusted odds ratios (ORs) across all models. After maximal covariate adjustment in Model 3, the TyG-delirium association strengthened substantially, with ORs escalating from 1.453 (pre-match, 95% CI 1.096–1.926) to 2.180 (post-match, 95% CI 1.584–3.001), establishing TyG as an unconfounded delirium risk determinant.

Discussion

Association between triglyceride-glucose index and post-cardiac surgery delirium: findings and contemporary evidence

The present investigation examined the association of the TyG index with post-cardiac surgical delirium risk by integrating multicenter datasets comprising the MIMIC-IV platform and prospectively collected surgical records from Qingdao University’s tertiary cardiovascular center.Among the included patients, the TyG index was significantly higher in the delirium group compared to the non-delirium group, a difference that was statistically significant in both databases. Collectively, the data support TyG’s utility as a preoperative risk assessment biomarker for post-cardiotomy neurocognitive complications. RCS analysis uncovered a sigmoidal correlation of metabolic index (TyG) with POD frequency, revealing continuously augmented delirium probability across TyG spectrum in the absence of threshold characteristics.Notably, POD incidence demonstrated significant elevation at TyG values above 8.91, a threshold association that persisted following PSM-based covariate adjustment. Subgroup analyses yielded robust outcomes, highlighting a close TyG index-POD risk association with significant clinical implications. This evidence supports TyG’s role as a metabolic indicator of delirium predisposition, with implications for targeted prophylactic approaches.

Prior investigations utilizing MIMIC datasets have established significant associations between TyG index levels and delirium incidence in intensive care populations, substantiating this metabolic marker’s role in neurocognitive risk stratification [27], consistent with the current results. This multicenter investigation further strengthens such evidence through large-scale heterogeneous cohorts, enhancing conclusion reliability. Research from Beijing’s largest tertiary care hospital (PLAGH) validated the TyG index’s predictive capacity for surgical delirium across multiple non-cardiac procedures, reinforcing our observations [28]. This study provided new insights and filled the shortcomings in previous research concerning the association of the TyG index with POD in cardiac surgery patients, offering new perspectives in this area. Additionally, two prior retrospective studies demonstrated TyG index-delirium associations in elderly critically ill patients [29]. This analysis corroborates such evidence while further quantifying the association strength gradient across age subgroups.Differences between age groups may be related to the physiological characteristics and comorbid conditions of patients in each age range.

The correlation between the TyG index and delirium has garnered increasing attention in recent years. Therefore, analyzing clinical records of real patients included in the MIMIC database combined with surgical cases from Yuhuangding Hospital, Qingdao University, this multi-institutional retrospective cohort analysis, leveraging substantial clinical datasets, was designed to investigate TyG index associations with post-cardiotomy delirium incidence.

Potential mechanisms linking the TyG index and POD after cardiac surgery

Cardiac surgical populations exhibit heightened vulnerability to postoperative neurocognitive dysfunction, attributable to their distinctive perioperative physiological alterations. Cardiopulmonary bypass procedures can produce microscopic emboli that potentially occlude cerebrovascular territories, inducing focal neuroinflammatory responses and cerebral swelling.The embolic material stems from both surgical manipulation and the extracorporeal circuit components, capable of inducing perfusion abnormalities and structural neuronal harm [30]. Surgically-induced hemodynamic perturbations can disrupt optimal cerebral blood flow dynamics, predisposing patients to procedure-related neurocognitive deficits [31]. Pathologically, blood pressure excursions manifest differential neurological risks: hypotension-mediated global hypoperfusion versus hypertension-induced breakdown of the blood-brain barrier and vasogenic edema [32, 33]. In the context of IR, impaired cerebrovascular autoregulation amplifies these perfusion deficits. High TyG index correlates with a 2.5-fold higher risk of cerebral hypoperfusion during CPB in cardiac surgical patients, because IR blunts the vasodilatory responses to CO₂ [34].Surgically-triggered inflammatory mediators can access the CNS through a permeable blood-brain interface, establishing a pro-delirium milieu via microglial activation and neuronal dysfunction [35]. Activation of microglia and astrocytes in this setting further propagates neuroinflammation, particularly in brain regions critical for cognition such as the hippocampus [36]. Uncontrolled postoperative nociception and circadian rhythm disruption have been implicated as synergistic promoters of postoperative neurocognitive impairment [37, 38].

Considering the substantial prevalence and multifactorial etiology of post-cardiotomy delirium, significant research efforts have focused on identifying reliable risk stratification tools for this consequential complication. The present analysis revealed an independent association between elevated TyG levels and increased POD risk among cardiac surgery recipients. The pro-inflammatory state triggered by surgical trauma is markedly exacerbated by insulin resistance, representing a principal etiological factor in delirium development [39]. The neuropathological cascade connecting insulin resistance with cognitive dysfunction encompasses: (i) hippocampal synaptic plasticity impairment, (ii) aberrant amyloid precursor protein processing, (iii) pathological tau aggregation, (iv) microglia-mediated neuroinflammatory responses, and (v) ApoE ε4 allele-related pathogenic effects [40]. Insulin’s vasoregulatory functions extend to the cerebral circulation, with diminished vasoreactivity under conditions of impaired insulin signaling potentially driving vascular cognitive pathology [40]. In cardiac surgical patients, IR-associated reductions in cerebral glucose uptake are closely related to POD. Intraoperative positron emission tomography-measured cerebral glucose uptake reduction is correlated with hippocampal hypometabolism, which is a precursor of POD [41].Empirical data from geriatric populations consistently demonstrate that heightened insulin resistance correlates strongly with greater susceptibility to postsurgical cognitive decline.Compelling evidence from bench-to-bedside research confirms that IR-focused interventions - particularly inhaled insulin protocols and supervised exercise programs - effectively preserve neurocognitive performance [42–45]. Collectively, these findings position insulin resistance as a plausible etiological contributor to postoperative neurocognitive impairment in cardiac surgical populations.

As a recently validated surrogate measure, the TyG index shows proportional relationships with graded insulin sensitivity impairment [46]. This metabolic marker’s association with insulin resistance originates from its sensitivity to altered fat partitioning: (1) accelerated peripheral fat breakdown, (2) circulating lipid excess, and (3) tissue-specific accumulation of fatty acid derivatives that disrupt canonical insulin signaling networks in major metabolic organs [47, 48]. Enhanced glycerol-3-phosphate generation contributes to systemic hyperglycemia, while chronic metabolic dysregulation in this state may precipitate insulin resistance development [49]. Compared to gold-standard hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp and HOMA-IR, the TyG index demonstrates superior clinical applicability with cost-effectiveness, excellent reproducibility, and preoperative accessibility [50].

Strengths and limitations

Firstly, this study included data from 1,461 real patients in the MIMIC database, as well as 389 cardiovascular surgery patients from Yuhuangding Hospital affiliated with Qingdao University. This pioneering multicenter investigation establishes novel associations between the triglyceride-glucose index and post-cardiotomy delirium risk. Methodologically, we conducted hierarchical regression modeling (unadjusted and fully adjusted) with external validation through the Yuhuangding cohort. Furthermore, flexible parametric modeling via restricted cubic splines elucidated the continuous TyG-delirium risk relationship.The TyG index was further converted into a categorical variable, and propensity score matching was applied to balance the baseline characteristics of the two groups. Prespecified subgroup evaluations examined whether TyG-POD relationships remained robust across demographic and clinical characteristics, while confirming the index’s discriminative capacity. Additionally, the TyG index’s foundation in standard laboratory measures enables immediate clinical translation for perioperative delirium prevention protocols.

Although valuable insights have been gained from the current study, certain limitations must be acknowledged. First, as a retrospective study, data collection relied on historical medical records, which may be subject to information bias, such as incomplete or inaccurate records, potentially affecting the reliability of the findings. Second, inherent limitations in capturing intraoperative details (e.g., surgical approaches, CPB parameters, anesthesia regimens) from both the MIMIC-IV database and Yuhuangding Hospital cohort introduce the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured surgical/anesthesia practices. Additionally, it is not possible to fully exclude confounding from other unmeasured variables, including but not limited to high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-TnT) and N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) [51].Third, the extreme odds ratios (ORs) observed in Models 2 and 3 of the Yuhuangding cohort (e.g., OR 34.8, CI 6.7–273.6) suggest potential model overfitting or instability, likely attributable to the cohort’s limited sample size and low POD incidence. These results should be interpreted with caution.Therefore, further prospective studies are needed to validate these findings. The absence of extended outcome tracking and survival impact assessments may constrain complete characterization of the TyG index’s prognostic relevance for postoperative neurocognitive outcomes.

Future studies should also incorporate more direct measures of social determinants of health (e.g., education, income level, and social support) to better clarify their influence on the TyG–POD relationship. Subsequent investigations should prioritize multicenter recruitment across diverse geographic and demographic populations, with standardized documentation of intraoperative variables (anesthesia protocols, CPB parameters) to enhance external validity. Additionally, longitudinal studies should examine how the TyG index correlates with sustained cognitive deterioration and mortality risk over time, potentially validating its prognostic role in cardiac surgery cohorts.

Conclusion

The current investigation utilized clinical records from two sources: the MIMIC-IV repository and the cardiovascular surgery database of Qingdao University’s Yuhuangding Hospital, demonstrating a robust correlation between elevated TyG values and heightened susceptibility to post-cardiotomy neurocognitive impairment. TyG ≥ 8.91 maintained significant independent prognostic value for postoperative neurocognitive impairment in cardiac surgery cohorts, with risk estimates remaining stable across all prespecified stratification analyses.These findings indicate the TyG index’s clinical versatility across diverse care environments, offering substantive utility for delirium risk stratification to facilitate precision prevention strategies that may enhance recovery trajectories and optimize resource allocation. Future studies could further explore the underlying mechanisms and potential interventions related to the TyG index and its role in POD.

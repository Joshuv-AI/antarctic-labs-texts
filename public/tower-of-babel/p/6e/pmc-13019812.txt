Combined extensile medial and sinus tarsi approaches for intra-articular calcaneus fractures: a case series
Authors: Yuefei Liu, Qianheng Jin, Chenglong Wu, Jihui Ju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of combining the extensile medial approach with the sinus tarsi approach for treating intra-articular calcaneus fractures.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 11 patients who underwent combined extensile medial and sinus tarsi approaches between January 2022 and July 2023. Preoperative and postoperative radiographic measurements of the calcaneus, including length, width, height, varus angle, Böhler’s angle, and Gissane’s angle were obtained. Functional outcomes were assessed at the final follow-up using the American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society (AOFAS) hindfoot score.ResultsAll operations succeeded, and the anatomical structure and articular surface of the calcaneus were recovered, with mean operative time 171.5 ± 29.0 min, average bleeding 97.7 ± 47.1 mL. Imaging comparisons (postoperative and final follow-up vs. preoperative) demonstrated that significant improvements were observed in calcaneal length (p = 0.042), height (p = 0.0005), width (p < 0.001), and Böhler’s angle (p = 0.001) at final follow-up compared to preoperative values. No statistically significant difference was noted in Gissane’s angle (p = 0.113), while the varus angle showed significant correction (p < 0.001). Moreover, all imaging parameters were stable from the postoperative period to the final follow-up (p > 0.05). The mean AOFAS score at one year postoperatively was 91.3 ± 7.6, indicating excellent functional recovery.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that the extensile medial approach, when strategically combined with the sinus tarsi approach, provides superior direct visualization of the medial wall and subtalar joint articular surface. The technique effectively restores calcaneal morphology and achieves high functional scores, particularly in Sander’s type III/IV fractures. These findings support its consideration as an advanced option for managing challenging calcaneal fracture patterns.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-026-03521-y.

Introduction

Calcaneal fractures represent the most common type of tarsal fractures, accounting for approximately 60% of all tarsal fractures [1]. These injuries typically result from axial load transmission during falls from height with foot-first landing, presenting clinically with localized pain, swelling, tenderness, and impaired weight-bearing function [2]. Although traditional open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with the lateral L-shaped approach has been established as an effective treatment modality, the incidence of wound-related complications remains high despite implementation of protective measures including no-touch techniques and postoperative drainage with compression dressing [3–6]. Consequently, surgical approach selection remains a contentious issue in clinical practice.

The extensile lateral approach (ELA) is widely adopted for its advantages in providing comprehensive visualization of collapsed fracture fragments, facilitating anatomical reduction of the subtalar joint surface, and enabling versatile plate fixation configurations [7]. However, this approach carries significant risks of soft tissue complications, including delayed wound healing, marginal necrosis, infection, dehiscence, and flap necrosis [8]. To mitigate soft tissue trauma, various minimally invasive reduction techniques have been developed, among which the sinus tarsi approach has gained prominence [9]. This technique allows direct visualization for subtalar joint reduction, with potential extension to expose calcaneocuboid articulations, while enabling locked plate fixation that minimizes soft tissue disruption. Nevertheless, sinus tarsi approach demonstrates technical limitations in correcting medial column displacement, often necessitating incision extension to achieve optimal reduction, which paradoxically increases complication risks [10, 11].

The medial approach involves a 5-cm longitudinal incision posterior to the neurovascular bundle along the medial plantar aspect, which has been proposed to reduce both wound complication rates and non-weight bearing duration [12, 13]. The primary treatment objectives for calcaneal fractures involve restoration of anatomical parameters including length, width, height, and articular congruity [14]. In severe fractures with lateral wall deficiency, isolated lateral wall reduction frequently fails to achieve three-dimensional anatomical reconstruction. Given the medial wall’s thicker cortical bone and predominantly split fracture pattern with distinct anatomical landmarks, medial wall reduction through extended medial approach can automatically restore calcaneal length, height, and varus angle. Combining this with sinus tarsi approach for posterior facet reduction and lateral wall adjustment enables complete dimensional reconstruction. This study therefore aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy of combined medial and sinus tarsi approaches for treating intra-articular calcaneal fractures.

Methods

Subjects

This retrospective study analyzed clinical data of all patients who underwent surgical management with combined extensile medial and sinus tarsi approaches for calcaneal fractures between January 2022 and July 2023. The study was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of Suzhou Ruihua Orthopedic Hospital (Approval No. 2021055). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Sanders type II and III closed fresh calcaneal fractures (defined as fractures within 3 weeks of injury), (2) fractures with significantly displaced, crushed medial wall and severe varus and (3) patients aged 18–60 years. Exclusion criteria consisted of (1) Sanders type I and IV fractures; (2) open, old (defined as fractures beyond 3 weeks), or pathological fractures; (3) severe osteoporosis, defined as a bone mineral density (BMD) ≤-2.5 standard deviations (SD) below the young adult peak BMD reference value accompanied by one or more fragility fractures resulting from minimal trauma (e.g., bending, falling); (4) serious internal diseases; and (5) a history of prior foot/ankle trauma or orthopedic disorders.

Eleven patients (12 feet) were treated with the combined extensile medial and sinus tarsi approach from January 2022 to July 2023. All the patients were males, with a mean age of 44.1 ± 7.6 years (range 34–58). Comorbidities included smoking history in seven patients (58.3%) and hypertension in four patients (33.3%). No patients presented with diabetes mellitus or other systemic medical conditions. The right side was involved in eight (72.7%) cases, the left side was involved in two (18.2%) cases and both sides were involved in one case (9.1%). All the cases occurred due to a high falling injury. According to the classification system of Essex-Lopresti, nine (75%) feet were classified as type IIA, while three (25%) feet were classified as type IIB. According to the classification system of Sanders, five (41.7%) feet were classified as type IIA, three (25%) feet were classified as type IIB, one (8.3%) foot was classified as type IIIAC, one (8.3%) foot was classified as type IIIBC and two (16.6%) feet were classified as type IIIAB. Five (41.7%) fractures involved the sustentaculum tail (Fig. 1). All patients presented with intact skin without abrasions or contusions. Preoperative management included limb elevation, intermittent cryotherapy, and edema control measures, with no blister formation observed. Surgery was conducted once the swelling subsided or the crease sign was observed. The mean operative time was 171.5 ± 29.0 min (range 132–216). The mean time from fracture to surgery was 8.5 ± 3.0 days (range 5–14) (Table 1).

Standard operative technique

The operation was performed under continuous epidural anaesthesia in a prone position. An incision was made starting from 1 cm above the calcaneal tubercle, moving down along the medial edge of the Achilles tendon to the plantar skin and turning to a transverse incision to 1 cm in front of the calcaneal tubercle (Fig. 2). Afterwards, a full-thickness incision was made through the skin and subcutaneous tissue to expose the abductor pollicis muscle. The abductor pollicis muscle was retracted downwards, followed by skin flap elevation. Then, a careful exposure of the medial wall of the calcaneus was performed to protect the plantar branch of the tibial nerve. The lateral incision, representing a conventional tarsal sinus approach, of about 4–5 cm from 1 cm below the lateral malleolus to the base of the fourth metatarsal was made. After the skin was incised, the fibular long and short tendon sheaths and the lateral wall of the calcaneus were sharply separated. The fibular long and short tendons were retracted downwards, and the extensor digitorum brevis muscle was stripped. The joint capsule was incised to expose the collapsed articular surface. The peroneal long and short tendon sheaths were precisely disentangled from the lateral wall of the calcaneus. Then, the peroneal long and short tendons were retracted inferiorly, followed by detaching the extensor digitorum brevis muscle. Afterwards, the joint capsule was incised to reveal the collapsed articular surface. The reduction procedure started with the medial aspect and progressed to the lateral, initially restoring the height and length of the calcaneus bone and subsequently addressing the alignment and flatness of the articular surface. A Steinmann pin 4.0 mm in diameter was inserted into the medial inferior aspect of the calcaneal tubercle. It was pulled downwards and backwards to preliminarily restore the height and length of the calcaneus bone. The calcaneal varus was corrected by applying the eversion Steinmann pin. After the reduction of the medial wall and talocalcaneal, the length and height of the calcaneus, as well as the varus and valgus, were further restored. Then, the fracture fragments were temporarily fixed with Kirschner wires. For tongue-shaped fractures, a Steinmann pin was inserted transversely at the back of the fracture fragment, and the joint surface was reduced by prying downwards. The fracture was fixed by a double-headed compression screw or locking plate through the medial incision. After rinsing, the extensor digitorum brevis muscle was repaired. Afterwards, the skin was closed with intermittent sutures, and a drainage tube was left in place for negative pressure drainage (Fig. 3).

Postoperative care

All patients were treated with limb elevation. Drainage tubes were removed 24–48 h after surgery. Afterwards, patients began to perform non-weight-bearing active ankle joint flexion and extension exercises. The calcaneus was examined with lateral and axial X-ray films at 1 week and 1, 2, 3, 6 and 12 months. At 6–8 weeks after surgery, patients were advised to bear partial weight with the aid of crutches and transitioned to full weight-bearing walking 3 months after surgery.

Postoperative follow-up and assessment

Clinical and imaging evaluations were conducted before and after surgery. The length, width, height, varus angle, Böhler’s angle and Gissane angle of the calcaneus were obtained from the lateral axial radiograph. Functional assessment was conducted at the final follow-up using the American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society (AOFAS) hindfoot scoring system [15]. This standardized outcome measure evaluates postoperative recovery across three domains: pain (50 points), function (40 points), and alignment (10 points), with a maximum total score of 100. The scoring criteria were defined as follows: excellent (90–100 points), good (75–89 points), acceptable (50–74 points), and poor (< 50 points).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Quantitative variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, and as median [range] for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables were summarized using absolute counts (N) and relative frequencies (%). The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to assess normality of distribution. For intergroup comparisons, Student’s t-test was applied to parametric data, while the Mann-Whitney U test was used for nonparametric variables. Categorical data were analysed using the chi-square test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value < 0.05.

Results

All surgical procedures were completed successfully with a mean operative duration of 171.5 ± 29.0 min (range: 132–216 min) (Table 1). The mean intraoperative blood loss was 97.7 ± 47.1 mL (range: 40–200 mL). The postoperative follow-up period ranged from 11 to 23 months (mean: 18.8 ± 3.5 months) with a 100% follow-up completion rate. Radiographic evaluation demonstrated that postoperative measurements of calcaneal length, height, and Böhler’s angle were significantly increased compared to preoperative values (p < 0.05, Table 2), while calcaneal width and varus angle showed significant reduction (p < 0.05, Table 2). Although Gissane’s angle exhibited an increasing trend postoperatively, this change did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05, Table 2). At final follow-up, comparative analysis against preoperative data revealed sustained significant increases in calcaneal length, height, and Böhler’s angle (p < 0.05, Table 2), with concomitant persistent reduction in calcaneal width and varus angle (p < 0.05, Table 2). Besides, no statistically significant difference in Gissane’s angle was observed between final follow-up and preoperative measurements (p > 0.05, Table 2). Notably, no significant alterations were observed in calcaneal length, width, height, Böhler angle, or varus angle between postoperative and final follow-up assessments (p > 0.05, Table 2; Fig. 4). At the final follow-up evaluation, the results demonstrated excellent functional outcomes with a mean AOFAS score of 91.3 ± 7.6. Functional outcomes were classified as excellent in 7 patients (8 feet), good in 3 patients (3 feet), and fair in 1 patient (1 foot) (Table 2, Supplementary Fig. 1).

Discussion

Intra-articular calcaneal fractures represent complex traumatic injuries that frequently result in prolonged functional impairment and long-term disability [16]. Current advancements in orthopedic surgery have increasingly emphasized minimally invasive techniques as the future paradigm for managing these fractures [17]. The conventional L-shaped lateral incision, while providing broad exposure, entails significant soft tissue dissection that compromises the vascular supply to the central and anterior regions of the lateral calcaneal wall, as well as disrupts the vascular network of the plantar flap [18]. This extensive surgical trauma contributes to a wound healing complication rate ranging from 11% to 24.9%, which remains a critical challenge affecting treatment outcomes [19, 20]. In contrast, the sinus tarsi approach has gained clinical favor due to its minimally invasive nature and reduced incidence of wound-related complications. However, this approach presents inherent limitations in adequately exposing the posterior and lateral calcaneal structures, which complicates intraoperative restoration of calcaneal height and width [21, 22]. The restricted visualization also increases the risk of soft tissue entrapment, iatrogenic sural nerve injury, and potential development of calcaneal varus deformity. To address these surgical challenges, percutaneous minimally invasive techniques have emerged as promising alternatives in clinical orthopedics, offering novel therapeutic strategies for intra-articular calcaneal fractures. This study innovatively developed a combined surgical approach integrating the extended medial and sinus tarsi approaches for treating intra-articular calcaneal fractures involving the medial wall. Our findings demonstrate that this hybrid technique achieves favorable outcomes in both radiological parameter restoration and functional recovery metrics.

The calcaneus possesses an arched structure with a medially curved contour. Biomechanically, the medial column primarily sustains compressive loads, while the lateral column is subjected to tensile stresses [23, 24]. This mechanical distinction corresponds to differences in cortical thickness: the medial cortex is substantially thicker and stronger, whereas the lateral cortex is relatively thin. The posterior articular surface is elevated medially and depressed laterally [25]. Under axial loading, the posterior facet tends to fail laterally, leading to comminution and collapse, while the medial wall often remains relatively intact [26]. Although the medial talus was historically considered rarely displaced in calcaneal fractures, recent studies report that talar involvement with significant displacement occurs in up to 40% of cases [27]. In our clinical series, 43% of fractures exhibited notable talar displacement.

Common surgical approaches for intra-articular calcaneal fractures—such as the extensile lateral approach, tarsal sinus approach, and arthroscopy-assisted reduction [28]—typically rely on indirect reduction of the medial wall. This often complicates the anatomical restoration of calcaneal height and alignment. Correction of varus deformity heavily depends on intraoperative fluoroscopy and surgical experience, contributing to a high failure rate in subtalar joint fractures [29]. The primary goals of calcaneal fracture surgery include anatomical reduction of the posterior articular surface, restoration of Böhler’s and Gissane’s angles, and correction of height, width, and hindfoot alignment. Hindfoot malalignment may lead to early failure, necessitating revision procedures such as osteotomy or arthrodesis [30]. The minimally invasive tarsal sinus approach was introduced by Ebraheim in 2000 for the clinical management of calcaneal fractures [31]. Located on the foot’s dorsum, this approach minimizes risk to the lateral calcaneal artery and reduces soft tissue dissection, thereby preserving flap blood supply. Wang et al. treated 18 calcaneal fractures via this approach with no reported incision complications [32]. Similarly, Mostafa et al. reported on 18 cases, with the average Böhler’s angle improving from 5.1° preoperatively to 34.6° postoperatively—a correction rate of 91.4% [33]. However, due to its limited exposure, the tarsal sinus approach does not allow full visualization of the posterior and lateral calcaneus compared to the extended lateral L-shaped incision [34]. This restricts the ability to adequately restore calcaneal height and width and complicates soft tissue handling and debris removal. In the present study, radiographic evaluation demonstrated significant postoperative improvements in calcaneal length, height, and Böhler’s angle compared to preoperative values. Similarly, calcaneal width and varus angle were significantly reduced after surgery.

A medial surgical approach enables direct visualization and reduction of fractures involving the medial wall and sustentaculum tali. However, previously described medial incisions often provide inadequate exposure of the posterior facet and carry a risk of neurovascular injury [35, 36]. Therefore, a combined medial and lateral approach allows more comprehensive fracture reduction and anatomical reconstruction. In this study, an extensile medial approach along the periosteal plane was used to minimize soft tissue injury and facilitate reduction of the sustentaculum tali. This approach effectively restores calcaneal height, length, and coronal alignment. Given its limited visualization of the posterior facet, it was supplemented with a small lateral incision to address articular impaction and lateral wall expansion. This sequential medial-to-lateral reduction strategy ensures reconstruction of calcaneal dimensions and articular congruity.

Complication rates have been consistently reported in the literature to range from 17.3% to 29% [37, 38]. Recent comparative studies demonstrate significantly lower complication rates with minimally invasive techniques compared to open reduction and internal fixation via an extensile lateral approach (4.8% vs. 20.8%) [39]. Schindler et al. reported an overall complication rate of 29%, with delayed wound healing (11%) and surgical site infection (5%) representing the most common complications [40]. Our findings revealed a more favorable complication profile compared to the aforementioned studies: only 1 patient (8.3%) developed a medial wound infection requiring pedicle flap reconstruction, while transient lateral plantar nerve palsy occurred in 2 cases (16.7%). Notably, all neurological complications resolved spontaneously by the final follow-up assessment.

Advantages

The combined medial and sinus tarsi approaches demonstrate some advantages in the management of patients with intra-articular calcaneal fractures. First, direct vision reduction and fixation can be performed for intra-articular fractures of the calcaneus involving the sustentaculum talus, avoiding the previous deficiency of the inaccurate restoration of the calcaneal height through lateral incisions. The calcaneal varus is mainly caused by the displacement of the medial wall. After restoring the medial wall, the length and height of the calcaneus naturally recover. Furthermore, the varus and valgus are corrected simultaneously, reducing the time required for intraoperative C-arm X-ray fluoroscopy, especially for the axial view. Second, the medial side of the posterior articular surface can be observed through the medial incision for Essex-Lopresti IIA tongue-shaped fractures. A Steinmann pin is inserted posterior to the calcaneal tuberosity, followed by the downwards application of pressure to reposition the posterior articular surface of the medial wall. At this stage, the lateral incision serves solely as an observational site to assess the smoothness of the posterior articular surface, allowing for necessary adjustments. Third, the medial wall of the calcaneus is thick and hard, which can provide good support and holding force for the steel plate screw after reduction and fixation. The length, height and varus deformity of the calcaneus are not easily compromised after surgery, ensuring early functional exercise. Fourth, the space on the medial side of the calcaneus is more expansive, ensuring that the insertion of a steel plate will not lead to wound closure complications. Fifth, the medial incision reveals the sustentaculum talus to enhance the precision of the sustentaculum talus screw placement.

Technical key points

The extensile medial approach combined with the sinus tarsi approach demonstrates optimal applicability in intra-articular calcaneal fractures characterized by medial wall comminution, varus deformity, and sustentaculum tali displacement, given the thicker and more robust medial cortical bone with distinct anatomical landmarks facilitating reduction. When treating intra-articular calcaneal fractures using the extensile medial approach combined with the sinus tarsi approach, the following key points should be emphasized. First, the patient should be placed in the prone position, which allows simultaneous access to both the medial and lateral aspects. Regarding the surgical incision, the longitudinal limb of the L-shaped incision should be maximized in length, as the posterior region lacks major blood vessels or nerves. The transverse limb should be kept as short as possible and extended intraoperatively only if necessary. When extending the incision across the posterior border of the medial malleolus, care must be taken to avoid injuring the plantar branches of the tibial nerve. If required, these nerve branches should be isolated and protected proactively. For screw fixation at the distal end of the plate, the same plate can be used externally as a template. A small skin incision is then made, followed by careful dissection and screw insertion. There are no absolute contraindications to this combined approach. For simpler fractures, such as isolated depressed and displaced articular surface fractures, the sinus tarsi approach alone may be sufficient. However, this combined approach is not suitable for fractures where the primary displacement involves the lateral wall with extension into the calcaneocuboid joint; in such cases, an extended lateral approach is more appropriate. Second, one longitudinal and one transverse Steinmann pin are inserted into the calcaneal tuberosity for leverage. Applying traction to the longitudinal pin helps restore calcaneal length and height, while the transverse pin aids in reducing the posterior articular surface. In comminuted fractures with bone loss or severe impaction, reduction of the medial wall alone may not adequately address lateral articular surface incongruity. In such scenarios, simultaneous medial and lateral reduction is essential. Priority should be given to reducing the depressed lateral articular fragments to restore articular congruity, followed by fine-tuning the medial reduction. Third, after medial wall reduction, the Kirschner wires used for temporary fixation should be placed as close as possible to the medial aspect of the calcaneus to avoid interfering with lateral articular surface reduction.

Disadvantages

However, this study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design and relatively short follow-up period may introduce potential biases. Second, the small number of surgical cases and the lack of anatomic plate utilization. Third, While the AOFAS score remains a widely adopted clinical metric, its psychometric properties and construct validity require further empirical validation. Therefore, future studies with larger sample sizes, longer follow-up durations, and randomized controlled designs are needed to better evaluate long-term functional outcomes and potential complications such as post-traumatic arthritis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the extensile medial approach in combination with the sinus tarsi approach represents a robust and reliable treatment strategy for intra-articular calcaneal fractures, particularly those affecting the medial wall, talocalcaneal articulation, and sustentaculum tali. This surgical technique not only improves radiographic parameters but also reduces postoperative complication rates. Therefore, this combined approach may represent a favourable therapeutic option for intra-articular calcaneal fractures. However, to fully validate its clinical efficacy, prospective randomized controlled trials with expanded sample sizes and extended follow-up periods are warranted.

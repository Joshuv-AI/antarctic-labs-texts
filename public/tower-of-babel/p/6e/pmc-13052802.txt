Association of anxiety and depression with oral health status of pregnant women attending antenatal care clinics at a tertiary care hospital, Karachi: a study protocol
Authors: Abhishek Lal, Shafquat Rozi, Wafa Zehra Jamal, Novera Chughtai, Fahad Umer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPregnancy is associated with complex physical and physiological changes that may adversely affect the oral cavity. Dental diseases such as dental caries, gingivitis and periodontitis are common occurrences in pregnancy, whereby psychological factors such as anxiety and depression may be associated with poorer oral health in pregnant women. Such poorer oral health has been associated with adverse maternal and fetal outcomes. Despite this, limited studies from Pakistan have explored the association of anxiety and depression with oral health in this vulnerable population.Methods and analysisIn this analytical, cross-sectional study, we will recruit 224 pregnant women in any trimester attending the antenatal care clinics at Aga Khan University, Karachi, Pakistan. Participants meeting the eligibility criteria will be assessed for demographics, pregnancy-related history, dental care seeking behaviours, anxiety and depression using Aga Khan University Anxiety and Depression Scale, and oral health assessment using decayed, missing, filled teeth index, gingival index and community periodontal index. The association between anxiety and depression with oral health status will be assessed using logistic and ordinal regression techniques, adjusting for confounders.Ethics and disseminationThis study’s protocol has been approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Aga Khan University (ERC # 2025-11293-35157). Written informed consent will be taken from all participants eligible, ensuring voluntary participation. The findings of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals, presented at conferences and shared with stakeholders and policymakers, emphasising the importance of oral health during pregnancy.

Introduction

Pregnancy is a state characterised by complex physical and chemical changes with an impact on almost all organs of women, including the oral cavity.1 The steroidal hormones produced by the placenta throughout pregnancy, such as oestrogen and progesterone, are responsible for maintaining pregnancy and the development of the fetus till full term. The surge in these steroidal hormones, along with the decrease in immunocompetency, is a phenomenon observed in pregnancy responsible for an increase in oral vascular permeability.2 Exacerbated permeability paves the way for the penetration of microorganisms and inflammatory mediators. Gingivitis, periodontitis, decayed teeth and xerostomia are common dental issues experienced during pregnancy.2 Oral health diseases not only affect the pregnant woman but may also harm the developing fetus if left unaddressed.3

As stated by the WHO, more than three billion individuals suffer from various dental diseases, with pregnant women being a vulnerable at-risk population due to hormonal changes.4 As per the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, more than half of pregnant women experience periodontal diseases, and one in four childbearing-age women has untreated dental cavities.5 Moreover, according to an overview of systematic reviews, the worldwide estimated frequency of periodontal diseases in this population is found to be between 11% and 100%.6 In low-middle-income countries like Pakistan, a moderate to severe burden of oral health diseases is prevalent in pregnant women, with the approximate number being relatively understudied.7 A study from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, reportedly found the occurrence of periodontal diseases between 21% and 79%.8 In Karachi, the most populous city in Pakistan, various studies have reported that one-third of pregnant women report dental caries, one-fourth with gingivitis and dental pain.9 10 Epidemiological studies have studied and observed the association of oral health status with poor pregnancy outcomes.6 11

During pregnancy, expectant mothers may face an array of dental diseases such as dental caries, gingivitis, periodontitis and xerostomia.12 Such dental diseases are pathological in nature, as the state of pregnancy with a surge in steroidal hormones worsens the already compromised oral health.13 Pregnant mothers with such dental diseases are not only at risk themselves of potentially experiencing adverse outcomes, but the developing fetus is also at risk.14 Mothers with periodontitis have been demonstrated to be at higher risk of delivering premature and low birth weight (LBW) infants.15 Moreover, maternal periodontitis may affect the mothers themselves, predisposing them to conditions like pre-eclampsia.16 In mothers with high dental caries, the infants were reported to suffer from LBW and large for gestational age.17

In terms of microbiology, a recent systematic review found higher Porphyromonas gingivalis bacteria were found in preterm delivery as compared with term deliveries.18 Literature reports transmission of oral pathogens such as Streptococcus mutans from mothers to fetus that may lead to early colonisation of such bacteria in the infant’s oral cavity.18 Early colonisation may predispose the infant to developing early childhood caries.19 Dental prophylactic treatments such as scaling and root planning during the second semester, established as a safe semester for dental treatment, may mitigate the risk of harmful outcomes for pregnant women and the developing fetus.20

One of the known factors that is associated with poor oral health during pregnancy is anxiety and depression. These psychological issues are highly prevalent globally, with 40%–70% of pregnant mothers reporting moderate to severe depressive symptoms.21 22 A local study in Pakistan concluded that 18% of pregnant women reported anxiety and depressive symptoms.23 However, such numbers are largely underreported, considering the stigmatisation of mental health support generally, even more so in pregnant women. Moreover, these psychological issues experienced during pregnancy are derived from an array of factors such as lack of partner support, current and anticipated financial burden, unplanned pregnancy, past or current pregnancy complications and so on.24 Oral health is one aspect of overall well-being that is largely avoided by pregnant women. In case of compromised oral health, the state of pregnancy exacerbates poor oral health, which may lead to poor maternal and fetal outcomes as established in the literature.

Different countries have put forth initiatives targeting the oral health of mothers during pregnancy. However, reports from different parts of the world state large disparities regarding the uptake of dental care services by pregnant women, ranging from 35% up to 75%.25 In Pakistan, there exists a significant gap in research addressing the interplay between mental health and oral health among pregnant women. Despite global evidence suggesting that poor oral health is linked to adverse pregnancy outcomes, such as preterm delivery and LBW, limited data are available on how anxiety and depression might influence these outcomes within the local context. By assessing the relationship between anxiety, depression and oral health status among pregnant women attending antenatal care (ANC) clinics at a tertiary care hospital, this study aims to provide valuable insights that could inform integrated health strategies and improve overall maternal and fetal well-being.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This will be an analytical cross-sectional study. This approach is suitable for the evaluation of the association of pregnant women’s psychological status, which includes anxiety and depression, with their oral health status, keeping in mind the scope of this research due to time and resource constraints. We will collect data from pregnant women attending the ANC clinics at the Department of Gynaecology and Obstetrics (OBGYN), at the Aga Khan University Hospital (AKUH), Karachi, Pakistan. This protocol provides a structured framework for generating local evidence to inform integrated antenatal screening strategies in low-income and middle-income settings.

Study site and setting

The Aga Khan University

AKUH is a non-profit, tertiary care private hospital that treats a wide array of simple to complex diseases, aiming to provide optimal care for patients. The patients are treated by a multidisciplinary team of doctors designed to work to meet the demands of the patients. Aga Khan University also caters to patients who are not able to afford treatments through its welfare system to ensure none are deprived of quality care.

Department of OBGYN

Department of OBGYN was established in the year 1986 and incorporated in 2009 as part of the Women and Child Health division. The reason for selecting the ANC Clinics, Outpatient Department and Department of OBGYN as study sites is the feasibility of conducting this research at AKUH. Patients from more diverse backgrounds visit the ANC clinics at AKUH. Approximately 7000 deliveries per annum are reported by the department, which ensures a good sample of patients visiting the ANC clinics.

Primary variables

The primary exposure variable will be psychological factors, which include anxiety and depression assessed by the Aga Khan University Anxiety and Depression Scale (AKUADS). The primary outcome variable will be oral health status, which consists of dental caries, gingival and periodontal health. Dental caries will be assessed by Decayed, Missing, Filled Teeth (DMFT) index, the gingival condition will be assessed using the Gingival Index (GI) and periodontal health will be assessed using Community Periodontal Index (CPI).

Other co-variables

Other co-variables that will be assessed include age, marital status, education, residence, occupation, monthly family income, family system, previous family history of mental health disorders, pregnancy-related information and dental care-seeking behaviours.

Target population: Pregnant women seeking ANC clinics at a tertiary care hospital.

Excluding such participants will ensure a direct determination of the association of psychological factors with oral health status.

Sampling technique

We will employ a purposive sampling technique (non-probability) to recruit participants following predetermined eligibility criteria. This method of sampling technique is best suited to conduct this study as it allows a targeted selection of pregnant women attending the ANC clinics at AKUH. To mitigate selection bias, all pregnant women in any trimester will be included across different clinic days and times to ensure a diverse pool of participants. Random sampling was deemed impractical for the recruitment of the participants due to the absence of sampling frames and time-constrained data collection window; hence, not opting for probability sampling. Purposive sampling offers many benefits, such as the conduct of the study keeping in mind the time and resource constraints, along with the absence of a sampling frame. Although the purposive sampling technique is not free from bias, regular monitoring of the study conduct will be done to mitigate the risk of bias. The findings of this study should be interpreted keeping in mind the limitations of potential selection bias. The results will therefore be interpreted with consideration of the potential selection bias inherent to non-probability sampling.

Sample size

The sample size will be calculated using OpenEpi software, which is a free and open source software.32 This study consists of a composite outcome variable, oral health status, which is divided into three primary outcome variables as follows: dental caries, gingivitis and periodontitis. Hence, three sample sizes will be calculated; the sample size with the highest number will be the required sample size for this study, as follows:

For dental caries,33 the parameters used to calculate sample size are as follows: level of significance at 5%, power at 80%, exposed to unexposed ratio: 0.47, per cent of unexposed with outcome: 0.69, OR: 2.49 and a 10% non-response rate. The calculated sample size was 167.

For gingivitis,27 the parameters used to calculate sample size are as follows: level of significance at 5%, power at 80%, exposed to unexposed ratio: 3;1, per cent of unexposed with outcome: 13.6%, OR: 3 and a 10% non-response rate. The calculated sample size was 179.

For periodontitis,27 the parameters used to calculate sample size are as follows: level of significance at 5%, power at 80%, exposed to unexposed ratio: 1:6.4, per cent of unexposed with outcome: 21.2%, OR: 3.3 and a 10% non-response rate. The calculated sample size was 224.

So, we will take the sample size with the highest number. For this study, the final sample size will be 224 participants.

Operational definitions

Antenatal care

According to WHO, ANC is defined as a period of care for pregnant women provided by skilled healthcare professionals throughout the duration of pregnancy.34

Dental caries

According to WHO, dental caries, often called dental cavities or tooth decay, is defined as the breakdown of the tooth structure due to acids produced by bacteria.4

Gingivitis

According to WHO, gingivitis is characterised by bleeding or swollen gums, which may lead to pain and sometimes bad breath.4

Periodontitis

Periodontitis is defined as an inflammation of alveolar bone, cementum and periodontal fibres, collectively referred to as periodontium, the supporting structures of the tooth.35

Low birth weight

According to WHO, LBW is defined as the weight of a newborn baby that is less than 2500 grams (5.5 pounds).36

Preterm

According to WHO, preterm or premature is defined as a newborn’s birth before 37 weeks of pregnancy.36

Small for gestational age

A fetus, considered small for gestational age when the predicted weight or abdominal circumference measurement is less than the 10th centile.37

Oral health

As per WHO, oral health is described as the condition of the oral cavity, teeth, gums, throat and jaw bones that allows individuals to perform routine daily tasks normally, such as eating, speaking and breathing.4

Oral health status

Oral health status is defined as the condition of the oral cavity assessed through measurable factors such as the existence or absence of gingivitis, periodontitis and dental caries.

Pre-eclampsia

Pre-eclampsia is a state of progressive pathology that involves multiple organ systems of the body, presenting primarily after 20 weeks of pregnancy with features such as novel presentation of hypertension and proteinuria or novel presentation of hypertension in combination with end-organ disorder with existence or absence of proteinuria.38

Trimester

The WHO defines trimesters as periods of pregnancy divided into three trimesters; the first trimester is from 1 week to 12 weeks, the second trimester is from 13 to 28 weeks, and the third trimester is from 29 to 40 weeks.34

Approach to study participants

Before the initiation of data collection, prior permission will be taken from the Department of OBGYN. On commencement of data collection, each day, the data collectors will obtain the daily list of appointments of patients who are scheduled for a visit to the ANC clinics at AKUH. On the first contact with the participants, data collectors will introduce the study to potential participants, explaining to them briefly the objectives and purpose of the study. The initial screening of the participants will be performed by the data collectors on the preformed screening tool guided by the eligibility criteria. Data collectors will spend sufficient time with the study participants to answer any queries before approaching them for written consent. On confirmation of eligibility for enrolment, verbal and written consent will be obtained from the participants, ensuring their voluntary participation and understanding of the objectives and purpose of the study. The participants who consent to participate will be recruited. Screening of the eligible participants and data collection will be completed in the consultation clinics to ensure privacy and confidentiality of the participants. To maintain confidentiality, prior to the interview, the participants will be asked whether they are comfortable responding to questions in front of their attendants or would like to respond privately. If the participant requests privacy, the attendant will be politely requested to step outside during the interview. Additionally, questions related to mental health will be asked discreetly and in an empathetic manner. The data collector will be trained to recognise discomfort and adjust the setting accordingly.

Data collection: structured questionnaire

The data for this study will be collected through a structured interview-based questionnaire by the data collector after completion of their training. Interview-based data collection was selected due to variable levels of literacy among the study population, as participants may require clarification and have questions, especially in the AKUADS section. Hence, an interviewer-based approach ensures unbiased and accurate collection of data, ensuring confidentiality and reducing information bias. However, if the participants express a desire to complete certain sections of the questionnaire privately, they will be given the opportunity to do so on the tablet.

All oral health assessments will be performed by a single data collector with a dental background. To maintain intra-rater reliability, the data collector will undergo training by the principal investigator and a calibration process before data collection. The calibration using the initial sample of participants will be overseen by a senior dentist. Any discrepancy during scoring when under training will be reviewed and corrected to ensure consistency in data collection. Periodic refresher checks will be conducted to ensure accurate data collection.

The structured questionnaire is designed to obtain all the information required to meet the objectives of this study.

Demographic characteristics

The demographic information will be collected from the participants, which will include age (in years), marital status, education, residence (urban or rural), employment status (either employed or housewife), estimated family monthly income, family system and a family history of mental health disorders.

Pregnancy-related information

The pregnancy-related information that will be collected from the participants will include current trimester (first, second or third), gravida status (primigravida, multigravida or nulligravida), past history of preterm, LBW or loss of an infant, frequency of ANC clinic visits and experiences of nausea and vomiting during pregnancy.

Dental-care seeking behaviours

The dental-care seeking behaviours information will be collected from the participants, which will include frequency of brushing and flossing per day, experiencing bleeding gums while brushing, visits to a dentist during pregnancy, visits to a dentist if experiencing dental pain, any dental complaints during pregnancy, receiving any information related to oral care during the antenatal period and self-rating of oral health.

Aga Khan University Anxiety and Depression Scale

The AKUADS,39 40 which has been validated locally in both English and Urdu among pregnant women in the Pakistani population aligning with local cultural norms and literacy, will be used to evaluate anxiety and depression. The sensitivity is reported to be 75% and the specificity at 81%, respectively. The scale consists of 13 physiological and 12 somatic symptoms questions assessing both anxiety and depression simultaneously. While some recognised symptoms, such as fatigue and sleep, may overlap with common pregnancy-related complaints, this validated tool in pregnant women strengthens its utility in this population. Questions are assessed on a 4-point scale, with minimum and maximum scores of 0 and 72. The data collectors will be trained to explain this distinction and administer this tool with sensitivity. However, considering this limitation and the previously proposed cut-off of 19 or greater in this population, this tool is recognised for screening purposes, not diagnostic.

A score greater than or equal to 19 represents patients possibly suffering from anxiety and depression and needing to be referred to healthcare professionals to seek treatment.39 No formal mental health diagnosis will be made solely based on this screening tool, as participants’ scores equal to or above 19 will not be labelled anxious and/or depressed in their medical records. The OBGYN care provider will be informed for further assessment and appropriate referrals if required. This ensures participants’ safety without unintended labelling and stigmatisation.

Decayed Missing Filled Teeth Index

The Decayed (D), Missing (M), Filled (F) and Teeth (T) (DMFT)41 Index is a gold standard validated tool for the caries assessment of an individual.42 This index, when used for one individual, counts the number of decayed, missing and filled teeth to reach a cumulative score. The scores of DMFT range from 0 to 28 (32 if third molars are present). The sensitivity of this scale ranges between 69.8% and 73.2%, and its specificity is approximately 69%. The DMFT index scoring will be categorised as follows: Less than 5 represents low caries experience, and greater than 5 represents high caries experience for a participant.41

Gingival index

GI43 is a gold standard validated tool that is used to assess the severity of gingivitis. GI was developed by Loe and Sillness to assess the health of the gingiva quantitatively for a patient, which includes colour, consistency, along with presence or absence of bleeding.44 The GI assesses areas around a tooth, such as mesial, distal, buccal and lingual surfaces. Each surface is scored as follows: 0 (healthy gingiva), 1 (mild gingivitis), 2 (moderate gingivitis) and 3 (severe gingivitis). Its sensitivity is approximately 61% with a specificity of approximately 84%. For a single tooth, the GI is calculated as the sum of the scores of 4 areas divided by 4. The GI of an individual can be determined by summing the scores of each index tooth and dividing by the total teeth that are examined. The final score of an individual can be categorised into one of the following categories:

Categories:

0.1 to 1. 0=Mild gingivitis: Slight inflammation without any clinical manifestations.

0.1 to 1. 0=Mild gingivitis: Slight inflammation without any clinical manifestations.

1.1 to 2.0=Moderate gingivitis: Moderate inflammation with bleeding on probing.

1.1 to 2.0=Moderate gingivitis: Moderate inflammation with bleeding on probing.

2.1 to 3.0=Severe gingivitis: Advanced inflammation with pain and discomfort, enlargement of the gingiva with spontaneous bleeding on probing.

2.1 to 3.0=Severe gingivitis: Advanced inflammation with pain and discomfort, enlargement of the gingiva with spontaneous bleeding on probing.

Community periodontal index

The CPI45 is a validated and standardised tool that is used to assess and screen for periodontal conditions in community-based settings, developed by WHO.46 A periodontal probe (UNC15) is used to measure the depth of the pocket. The CPI has a sensitivity of approximately 58%, with specificity of approximately 80.6%. The scores of this index range from 0 (health periodontium) to 4 (pocket depth of 6 mm or more). A periodontal pocket greater than or equal to 4 mm is defined as a pathological pocket.

The scores of the CPI are as follows:

0: Healthy periodontium (no bleeding or calculus, periodontal pockets greater than 3 mm).

1: Bleeding on probing, but absence of calculus and pockets greater than 3 mm.

2: Calculus with/without overhanging restorations but absence of pockets greater than 3 mm.

3: Shallow periodontal pockets (between 4 and 5 mm).

4: Deep periodontal pockets (greater than 6 mm).

X: Excluded sextant (There are less than two teeth).

9: Not recorded.

The oral cavity is divided into six sextants with teeth as follows: Upper Right Posterior (Teeth# 17-14), Upper Anterior (Teeth# 13-23), Upper Left Posterior (Teeth# 24-27), Lower Right Posterior (Teeth# 44-47), Lower Anterior (Teeth# 33-43) and Lower Left Posterior (Teeth# 34-37). From each sextant, the following index teeth are examined: Teeth# 17 and 16 (Upper Right Posterior), Teeth# 11 (Upper Anterior), Teeth# 26 and 27 (Upper Left Posterior), Teeth# 46 and 47 (Lower Right Posterior), Teeth# 31 (Lower Anterior) and Teeth# 36 and 37 (Lower Left Posterior). On each index tooth, six sites are probed, and a score is assigned to each site. For each tooth, the highest score out of the six sites is assigned as the final score. From each sextant, the highest score is assigned to that sextant. For the score of an individual patient, out of the six sextants, the highest score from all the sextants is assigned as the score for that individual patient.

Plan of analysis

The data cleaning will be performed using STATA V.17.0. The data will be checked for missing, inaccurate or out-of-range data. Any missing data will be handled depending on the severity of the missing data. For the descriptive statistics, means and SDs will be reported for the variable identified as continuous where the assumption of normality is met, whereas medians and IQRs will be reported in case of non-normality. Frequencies and percentages will be presented for variables identified as categorical. For inferential statistics as per the objectives of the study, univariable & multivariable analysis will be performed as logistic regression of anxiety and depression with DMFT and CPI, and ordinal regression of anxiety and depression with GI. GI will be treated as an ordinal variable with categories such as mild, moderate or severe using ordinal logistic regression. DMFT and CPI will be treated as binary outcomes, that is, DMFT categorised as lower caries experience or higher caries experience, and CPI categorised as presence or absence of periodontal pocket ≥4 mm, analysing using logistic regression. DMFT and CPI were dichotomised to facilitate clinical interpretability and comparability with prior studies conducted in similar settings. The multicollinearity of the independent variables will be checked with a cut-off value of greater than or equal to 0.8. Any plausible interactions between the variables will also be evaluated with a cut-off value of less than or equal to 0.1. A p value of less than or equal to 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Data management

The data will be collected by the data collectors using an interview-based questionnaire. To ensure accuracy and reliability, information from the participants will be entered into RedCap Software, a secure application on hand-held devices (tablets). The data collectors will be trained on RedCap software, eliminating any paper-based data collection to reduce the risk of any errors and enabling real-time data monitoring. Every question will be made mandatory to answer in the RedCap software to reduce the chances of missing data. Data cleaning and detection of any information shall be handled by the principal investigator, arranging data from each variable into ascending order.

To uphold cultural sensitivity, the data collection questionnaire was translated into English and Urdu languages by a translator proficient in both languages. The accuracy of the translations was ensured, and reverse translation was conducted, assuring maintenance of the initial meaning and purpose of information present in the questionnaire. A pretest will be conducted on the 5% of the study sample to identify any challenges in the data collection and to assess the need for paraphrasing and retranslation.

The personal identifiers of the patient will be omitted, with each patient assigned a unique numerical identity. All the data collected on the RedCap software will not be shared and will remain password-protected with access only to the principal investigator and research staff. Data will be backed up regularly to prevent data loss. on completion of the study, the data will be stored for up to 7 years as per AKUH’s data storage policy.

Quality assurance

For quality assurance, the data entry on the RedCap software by the data collector will be monitored daily. The data collectors will be trained by the principal investigator, followed by performing pretesting to identify and resolve any challenges in the initial sample of participants. The precision and accuracy of data, such as missing and out-of-range data, will be reviewed by the principal investigator on a daily basis. A sample of patients will be contacted by the principal investigator to ensure that they participated in the study. Validation checks in the RedCap software will also be used to reduce the chance of errors. The data will be stored for up to 7 years as per AKU’s data storage policy, with anonymised data without personal identifiers assigned unique numbers securely stored and backed up. After the retention period, all of the data will be permanently deleted using Eraser Software.

Validity issues

Internal validity

To achieve internal validity of the study, validated tools such as AKUADS, DMFT, GI and CPI will be used to ensure accurate and reliable data collection. The data collectors will be trained to reduce interviewer bias. Pretesting will be conducted to identify and manage any early challenges that might be detected during data collection. To control potential confounders, regression analysis will be performed. Since this will be a cross-sectional study, a causal association between exposure and outcome cannot be established from the conclusion. The observed data will only reflect correlations. This is an inherent limitation of the study design and will be acknowledged when interpreting the results. Although AKUADS is locally validated among pregnant Pakistani women, some somatic items (such as fatigue, sleep disturbance and appetite changes) may overlap with normal pregnancy experiences, potentially leading to misclassification. This measurement bias has been acknowledged, and trained interviewers will clarify symptom context during administration. AKUADS will be used strictly as a screening tool rather than for diagnostic purposes. Furthermore, due to the cross-sectional design, temporal relationships cannot be established, and reverse causation is possible, whereby poor oral health may also contribute to anxiety and depressive symptoms rather than the converse. Therefore, findings will be interpreted strictly as associations rather than causal relationships.

External validity

To achieve external validity, the representation of the population from diverse backgrounds, along with the use of standardised indices, will allow comparison with other populations.

Potential bias

Although the use of standardised tools, such as assessing exposure and outcomes, is used, there are potential chances of bias that may be introduced during the conduct of the study. The data collectors may influence the responses generated by the participants, potentially leading to interviewer bias. Since the non-probability sampling technique is used to recruit participants, this may steer toward selection bias. Since the participants will be asked about their previous anxiety and depressive symptoms, difficulty in recalling information may lead to information bias (recall bias). The participants may under-report or over-report their psychological symptoms. To mitigate such information bias, a standardised tool, AKUADS, will be utilised to minimise such bias. Measurement bias is another bias that may be introduced while conducting oral assessments of participants. To mitigate the effect of this bias, data collectors will undergo periodic calibration exercises to ensure accuracy while assessing oral health status using DMFT, GI and CPI indices. Additionally, dichotomisation of DMFT and CPI outcomes, while facilitating statistical interpretation, may result in loss of information and reduced analytical sensitivity. This approach was adopted to align with prior literature and simplify modelling; however, gingival outcomes will be analysed using ordinal regression to preserve clinical gradation.

Dissemination

All participants will receive a free oral health assessment as part of this study. If any dental problems, such as dental caries, gingivitis or periodontitis are identified, patients will be referred by the OBGYN care provider to dental care services. The data collected at the end of the data collection phase will be anonymised and password-protected to ensure the privacy and confidentiality of the participants. Regular backups of the data will be performed to prevent any data loss. There is minimal risk of participation in this study as the oral assessments that will be performed on the patients are non-invasive in nature. If any participant is identified as suffering from anxiety and depression symptoms, the attending OBGYN care provider will be notified, and referral to a mental health professional will be initiated through the ANC clinic, following AKUH’s routine referral protocol. The research team will not directly refer or label participants but facilitate support through the primary care provider. Moreover, in case any participant shows signs of severe psychological distress, suicidal thoughts or indications of domestic abuse, the data collector will immediately notify the principal investigator. The principal investigator will then inform the OBGYN care provider, engaging the support services with internal referrals. At any point in the study, participants can choose to withdraw from the study without any penalty.

The findings of this study shall be shared at various seminars and conferences and published in peer-reviewed journals.

Public health implications

In countries like Pakistan, mental health is largely stigmatised, even more so in vulnerable populations such as pregnant women. Literature has established the link between poor oral health of pregnant mothers and adverse maternal and fetal outcomes.47 48 Hence, there is a need for the integration of oral health services into ANC services. It is of paramount importance that pregnant women are aware of the importance of maintaining good oral health to prevent adverse maternal and fetal outcomes. Early detection and treatment of poor oral health may improve maternal and fetal outcomes. This study supports integrated ANC models by highlighting the intersection of oral and mental health in pregnancy. Incorporating brief mental health screening alongside oral health assessment into routine antenatal services could enable early identification of vulnerable women and strengthen referral pathways in resource-constrained settings such as Pakistan. Findings may inform health system strategies aimed at delivering comprehensive, patient-centred maternal care.

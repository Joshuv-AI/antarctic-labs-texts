Impact of varying levels of HepatoMax in drinking water on liver performance and blood profiles in broiler chickens
Authors: Hamid-Reza Behboodi, Morteza Asghari-Moghadam, Mehran Mehri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•HepatoMax improved growth performance and feed efficiency in broilers.•The 0.50 mL/L dose maximized carcass yield and reduced abdominal fat.•HepatoMax improved lipid profile by lowering TG, LDL, VLDL and cholesterol.•Liver enzymes and bilirubin fractions decreased, indicating better liver health.•Antioxidant status increased and lipid peroxidation (MDA) was reduced.

Introduction

The global poultry industry continually seeks to enhance broiler production efficiency and health, particularly in the face of challenges posed by high-density rearing and metabolic stress. Modern broiler chickens, selected for rapid growth and high feed efficiency, are highly susceptible to metabolic disorders and oxidative stress, which can impair liver function, compromise overall health, and negatively impact economic returns (Oke et al., 2024). The liver, as the central metabolic organ, plays a critical role in nutrient digestion, detoxification, and lipid metabolism (Tao et al., 2025). Consequently, maintaining hepatic integrity is paramount for optimal broiler performance (Corduk et al., 2007). With the global trend of reducing or eliminating antibiotic growth promoters, there has been a significant shift towards natural alternatives, such as phytogenic feed additives (PFAs), to support animal health and productivity (Raza et al., 2021). PFAs are plant-derived compounds that exert a wide range of beneficial biological activities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and hepatoprotective effects (Ashour et al., 2025).

Multi-component phytogenic-based blends composed of synergistic botanical extracts, vitamins, and organic compounds have shown notable effects on growth, reproduction, and health parameters in poultry (Asghari-Moghadam et al., 2025; Behboodi et al., 2021, 2025). Recent studies demonstrated that LibidoMax, a herbal based-blend containing sage, rose extracts, flaxseed oil, vitamins E and C, selenium, and zinc, improved reproductive hormone secretion, semen quality, hepatic enzyme function, and antioxidant status in aged broiler breeder roosters (Asghari-Moghadam et al., 2025). These outcomes confirmed that phytogenic supplementation can effectively counter oxidative and metabolic stress, enhance hepatic function, and optimize physiological performance when administered at appropriate inclusion levels. Similarly, Behboodi et al. (2025) emphasized that responses to phytogenic supplementation typically follow quadratic or broken-line patterns, reflecting dose-dependent biological thresholds beyond which efficacy plateaus or declines. The phytogenic blend evaluated in this study combines several active ingredients renowned for their metabolic and liver-supporting properties. Milk thistle (Silybum marianum), containing the active flavonoid complex silymarin, is well-documented for its potent antioxidant capabilities and its ability to protect hepatocytes from toxins and oxidative damage (Abd El-Hack et al., 2020). Artichoke (Cynara scolymus) extract is recognized for its choleretic properties, which enhance bile secretion, and its capacity to modulate lipid metabolism, often leading to reduced serum cholesterol and triglycerides (Ashour et al., 2025). Chicory (Cichorium intybus) has been increasingly investigated as a functional feed additive in poultry nutrition because of its dual antioxidant and prebiotic activities, which can positively influence gut health and metabolic function in broilers. The plant is a rich source of phenolic acids and flavonoids, which possess strong antioxidant properties that mitigate oxidative stress by neutralizing free radicals (Balaž et al., 2024). Evidence from feeding trials indicates that chicory supplementation can improve gut microbiota diversity, support intestinal morphology, reduce pathogenic bacteria like Escherichia coli, and enhance growth performance. In addition, broilers receiving chicory-enriched diets exhibited favorable blood metabolic profiles, including lower cholesterol and triglyceride concentrations (Awad et al., 2008; Tufarelli et al., 2023).

The used blend in the present study, HepatoMax, is further supplemented with l-carnitine and sorbitol. l-carnitine is an essential molecule for transporting long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for β-oxidation, thereby playing a vital role in energy production and reducing fat deposition in the liver and abdomen (Raza et al., 2021). Sorbitol can serve as an energy source and a metabolic stimulant. The combination of these ingredients may offer a synergistic effect, enhancing overall metabolic function and mitigating stress more effectively than individual components alone (Asghari-Moghadam et al., 2025). While the benefits of these individual compounds are established, comprehensive evaluations of their combined effects in a single formulation for broilers are less common.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the effects of a multi-component phytogenic blend, HepatoMax, containing milk thistle, artichoke, and chicory extracts supplemented with l-carnitine and sorbitol, administered through drinking water, on hepatic function and overall metabolic health in broiler chickens. Specifically, we examined its influence on growth performance, carcass traits, serum lipid profiles, hepatic enzyme activities, and antioxidant status during a 42-day rearing period.

Birds, housing, and experimental design

For this study, a total of 400 day-old, mixed-sex Ross 308 broiler chicks were randomly allocated into four treatment groups, each with five replicate pens containing 20 birds. The chicks were housed in pens bedded with clean wood shavings and equipped with a bell drinker and a tube feeder. During the 42-day trial, treatments were given through drinking water: a control group received a basal diet (Table 2) with untreated water, and three groups received the basal diet plus water with HepatoMax at 0.25, 0.50, or 1.00 mL/L. The basal diets were formulated according to the nutritional recommendations for Ross 308 broilers (Aviagen, 2022) and adjusted based on ingredient composition analysis to meet the expected nutrient specifications with some modifications. Performance and carcass parameter assessment

To determine body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio (FCR), birds were weighed on a pen basis at hatch and on days 7, 14, 21, 35, and 42. Mortality was recorded daily to correct FCR calculations. On day 42, four birds per pen (n = 20 birds per treatment) were randomly selected, weighed, euthanized, and dissected to evaluate carcass attributes and the relative weights of their internal organs. The liver, heart, and abdominal fat pad were weighed and recorded. Carcass dressing percentage was calculated as the ratio of eviscerated carcass weight to live body weight multiplied by 100, whereas breast and thigh meat yields were calculated as the proportion of carcass weight represented by breast and thigh muscles, respectively.

Blood collection and biochemical analyses

On day 42, blood samples from 4 birds per pen (n = 20 birds per treatment; 3 mL per bird) were collected from the brachial vein. Samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 15 min to separate the sera, which were then aliquoted and stored at −20 °C until analysis. Prior to analysis, serum samples were thawed at room temperature, gently mixed, and analyzed immediately to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.Serum concentrations of triglycerides (TG, mg/dL), cholesterol (mg/dL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL, mg/dL), low-density lipoprotein (LDL, mg/dL), and very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL, mg/dL), as well as the activities of hepatic enzymes including aspartate aminotransferase (AST, U/L), alanine aminotransferase (ALT, U/L), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP, U/L), were determined spectrophotometrically using commercial diagnostic kits (Pars Azmun, Tehran, Iran) and a Stat Fax 3200 microplate reader, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Serum total protein (g/dL), albumin (g/dL), glucose (mg/dL), total bilirubin (mg/dL), direct bilirubin (mg/dL), and indirect bilirubin (mg/dL) were also measured using Pars Azmun kits. Serum globulin (g/dL) was calculated by subtracting albumin from total protein.Antioxidant status and lipid peroxidation were assessed using Pars Azmun colorimetric assay kits by measuring total antioxidant capacity (TAC, nmol/mL), glutathione peroxidase (GPX, U/g Hb), superoxide dismutase (SOD, U/g Hb), and malondialdehyde (MDA, nmol/mL). All measurements were performed in duplicate and the mean values were used for statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using JAMOVI (Version 2.7.11.0; The JAMOVI project, Sydney, Australia). Data on growth performance, carcass attributes, and biochemical parameters were subjected to one-way ANOVA with the HepatoMax inclusion level as the main effect. The partial eta squared (η²p) was calculated as a measure of effect size to indicate the proportion of variance in the response variable explained by the HepatoMax treatments. Polynomial contrasts were used to evaluate the linear and quadratic dose–response trends to HEPATOMAX supplementation. Multivariate patterns among biochemical, antioxidant, performance, and carcass traits were explored using principal component analysis (PCA). The first two principal components were used to visualize clustering and to interpret the contribution of each variable to the observed variance. Results are expressed as mean ± SEM. To model dose-response relationships and estimate the optimal HEPATOMAX inclusion for each biological endpoint, segmented (broken-line) regression analyses were conducted, fitting linear-linear, quadratic-linear, and quadratic-quadratic two-slope models (Khosravi et al., 2016). Results

Performance and carcass attributes

The dietary supplementation of HepatoMax had significant effects on the performance and carcass attributes of broiler chickens (Table 3). While feed intake was not linearly or quadratically affected by the treatment, other key performance indicators showed significant responses. Broilers receiving 0.50 ml/L of HepatoMax demonstrated the highest weight gain (2871 g) and the most efficient feed conversion ratio (FCR) of 1.78. All measured carcass attributes were significantly influenced by HepatoMax supplementation (p < 0.0001). The 0.50 ml/L treatment group yielded the highest dressing percentage (83.7 %), breast meat yield (BMY, 52.4 %), and thigh meat yield (TMY, 41.8 %). Conversely, this same group exhibited the lowest abdominal fat pad percentage (1.66 %). Antioxidant status, serum proteins, and bilirubin

The antioxidant capacity and lipid peroxidation status were significantly modulated by HepatoMax supplementation (Table 6). The activities of key antioxidant enzymes, glutathione peroxidase (GPX) and superoxide dismutase (SOD), were highest in the 0.50 ml/L group. Concurrently, this group showed the lowest level of malondialdehyde (MDA), a marker for lipid peroxidation, indicating reduced oxidative stress. The total antioxidant capacity (TAC) was lowest in the 0.50 ml/L group. Serum protein and bilirubin profiles also showed significant improvements (Table 7). The 0.50 ml/L treatment group displayed the highest concentrations of total protein (5.73 g/dL) and albumin (2.49 g/dL). Furthermore, this group had the lowest levels of total (0.308 mg/dL), direct (0.140 mg/dL), and indirect (0.168 mg/dL) bilirubin, suggesting enhanced liver function. Supplementation with HepatoMax had a significant effect on serum glucose concentration (P < 0.001). Percentage change in responses vs. control

The administration of HepatoMax induced clear dose-dependent effects on growth performance, lipid metabolism, antioxidant status, and biochemical markers compared with the control group (Fig. 1). The administration of HepatoMax induced clear dose-dependent effects on growth performance, carcass traits, lipid metabolism, antioxidant status, and biochemical markers compared with the control group (Fig. 1). Growth performance was most improved at 0.50 mL/L, where body weight gain increased by 6.45 % and FCR improved by 7.65 %, accompanied by a slight reduction in feed intake (−1.69 %), indicating enhanced feed efficiency. In contrast, the 0.25 and 1.00 mL/L doses produced only marginal changes in growth (gain: −0.13 % and +0.96 %; FCR: −0.26 % and −0.31 %, respectively). Dressing percentage remained essentially unchanged across treatments (≤0.03 %). Liver weight decreased at 0.25 mL/L (−6.03 %) and 0.50 mL/L (−2.63 %) but increased at 1.00 mL/L (+3.07 %), while heart weight declined progressively with increasing dose (−0.67 %, −4.00 %, and −8.00 %). Carcass composition showed the most favorable response at 0.50 mL/L, with TMY and BMY increasing by 1.39 % and 1.87 %, respectively, and abdominal fat decreasing by 34.85 %. The 1.00 mL/L dose also markedly reduced abdominal fat (−35.81 %) but resulted in smaller improvements in meat yield. Lipid metabolism improved substantially, especially at 0.50 mL/L (TG −26.90 %, LDL −63.17 %, VLDL −27.70 %, cholesterol −26.51 %, HDL +81.15 %). Discussion

The present findings demonstrate that dietary HepatoMax supplementation exerted significant quadratic effects on broiler physiology. The consistent dose-dependent responses, with optimal outcomes observed at the 0.50 mL/L inclusion level, underscore a potent synergistic bioactivity. This distinct bell-shaped response (hormesis) suggests that the phytogenic constituents reach a physiological "sweet spot" at moderate concentrations. At 0.50 mL/L, the bioactive compounds likely achieve saturation of their respective receptors and transport proteins. However, at the 1.00 mL/L level, the metabolic systems may become saturated, or high concentrations of polyphenols could exert mild pro-oxidant effects or increase the metabolic cost of detoxification, thereby negating further benefits. This study provides robust evidence that a multi-component blend of milk thistle, artichoke, chicory, l-carnitine, and sorbitol acts as a metabolic modulator.

The consistent dose-dependent responses, with optimal outcomes overwhelmingly observed at the 0.50 mL/L inclusion level, underscore the potent and synergistic bioactivity of this phytogenic blend. These results align closely with previous reports on phytogenic feed additives, in breeder roosters (Asghari-Moghadam et al., 2025) and broiler chickens (Behboodi et al., 2025), where moderate inclusion levels optimized physiological responses while excessive doses yielded diminishing returns. This study provides robust evidence that a multi-component blend of milk thistle, artichoke, chicory, l-carnitine, and sorbitol can serve as an effective natural growth promoter, enhancing broiler health and productivity by targeting key metabolic and physiological pathways.

One of the primary objectives in broiler production is to maximize weight gain and feed efficiency. Rather than stimulating appetite, feed intake remained unchanged across groups, the significant improvement in FCR at the 0.50 mL/L level indicates that HepatoMax acts as a metabolic modifier. This enhanced nutrient utilization is driven by specific chemical mechanisms. Chicory’s inulin acts as a prebiotic, modulating gut microbiota and increasing the absorptive surface area of the intestine. Simultaneously, the l-carnitine component facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for β-oxidation (Behboodi et al., 2021, 2025). By optimizing the conversion of dietary fats into energy, l-carnitine spares amino acids from being catabolized for energy, allowing them to be diverted toward muscle protein synthesis. The decline in performance at 1.00 mL/L further supports the hypothesis of metabolic saturation, where the organism's capacity to utilize additional carnitine or metabolize phenolic compounds is exceeded, yielding diminishing returns. This finding is crucial, as it points towards improved metabolic function and digestive processes. The components of HepatoMax likely contributed synergistically to this outcome. For instance, chicory is known for its prebiotic properties, primarily due to its inulin content, which can modulate gut microbiota, improve intestinal morphology, and enhance nutrient absorption (Awad et al., 2011; Tufarelli et al., 2023). By fostering a healthier gut environment, chicory may have increased the availability and uptake of essential nutrients, contributing to better growth. Furthermore, l-carnitine’s role in transporting fatty acids for mitochondrial β-oxidation is critical for efficient energy production (Raza et al., 2021). By optimizing energy generation from fats, l-carnitine spares amino acids for muscle protein synthesis, directly promoting weight gain and improving FCR, a finding consistent with studies by Corduk et al. (2007). The dose-dependent nature of the growth response, peaking at 0.50 mL/L and then plateauing or slightly declining at 1.00 mL/L, is a key finding. This quadratic pattern, confirmed by broken-line regression analysis which estimated an optimal breakpoint for weight gain at 0.62 %, aligns with the concept of biological thresholds in phytogenic supplementation (Behboodi et al., 2025). Beyond a certain point, the metabolic systems may become saturated, or high concentrations of bioactive compounds could potentially exert pro-oxidant effects, negating further benefits.

The pronounced decline in serum triglycerides, VLDL, LDL, and total cholesterol, together with reduced abdominal fat, suggests a coordinated modulation of lipid metabolism. These effects can be attributed to the synergistic bioactivity of silymarin (from Silybum marianum) and artichoke-derived compounds such as cynarin and chlorogenic acid. Silymarin has been reported to enhance hepatic antioxidant defense and stabilize hepatocyte membranes, improving lipid metabolism and reducing lipid peroxidation (Abenavoli et al., 2010; Loguercio & Festi, 2011). Artichoke extract has been shown to inhibit HMG-CoA reductase—the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis—and to enhance bile acid excretion, thereby lowering circulating cholesterol levels (Bundy et al., 2008; Gebhardt, 1998). Moreover, l-carnitine plays a key role in facilitating the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondrial matrix for β-oxidation, thereby promoting fatty acid utilization rather than adipose storage (Borum, 1995; Kelly, 1998). Enhanced mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation reduces lipid deposition and improves energy efficiency. The combined action of these compounds likely improved hepatic lipid handling and redirected nutrients toward muscle protein synthesis, consistent with the observed increases in meat yield and serum albumin. Improved liver efficiency enhances lipid processing, detoxification, and metabolic integration, thereby supporting lean growth and overall performance. This repartitioning of energy supports the increased protein synthesis evidenced by higher serum albumin levels. An efficient liver can better process dietary fats, preventing their accumulation and promoting their use in other metabolic pathways that support muscle growth (Schiavone et al., 2007). Studies on silymarin have similarly shown its capacity to modulate lipid metabolism and reduce fat deposition in broilers (Yoana Petrova & Ivelina Pavlova, 2019). The enhanced protein synthesis, evidenced by higher serum total protein and albumin levels in the 0.50 mL/L group, provides the building blocks for this increased muscle yield. Therefore, the observed carcass improvements are not an isolated effect but a direct consequence of the systemic metabolic enhancements driven by the HepatoMax blend.

The liver plays a pivotal role in maintaining broiler health, functioning as the primary organ for detoxification, metabolism, and protein synthesis. However, the rapid growth rate and high metabolic activity of modern broilers often predispose them to hepatic stress and oxidative damage. In this study, supplementation with HepatoMax exhibited a marked hepatoprotective effect, as demonstrated by the significant reductions in the serum activities of AST, ALT, and ALP. These enzymes are normally confined within hepatocytes, and their elevated levels in circulation serve as classic indicators of liver cell injury or necrosis (Sultan & Gameel, 2004). The marked reduction in serum AST, ALT, and ALP is not merely a statistical result but a direct indicator of enhanced hepatocellular structural integrity. High metabolic activity in broilers typically leads to oxidative stress and membrane leakage (Tavakolinasab et al., 2020). The bioactive flavonolignan silymarin protects hepatocytes by acting as a membrane stabilizer, preventing the leakage of intracellular enzymes into the circulation (Elsayed Elgarawany et al., 2020; Ömer Faruk & Huyut, 2025). At the molecular level, silymarin stimulates RNA polymerase I activity in the nucleus, which increases ribosomal protein synthesis, the engine of liver regeneration and repair (Vargas-Mendoza et al., 2014). Concurrently, the phenolic acids in artichoke (cynarin) exert a potent choleretic effect. By stimulating bile acid secretion, these compounds facilitate the excretion of metabolic waste products, such as bilirubin, which was significantly lower in the treated groups. This confirms that the blend does not just mask liver damage but actively enhances the organ’s detoxification capacity. This hepatoprotective effect can be attributed mainly to the synergistic action of silymarin and cynarin, bioactive components of the HepatoMax formulation. The bioactive compound silymarin, derived from milk thistle (Silybum marianum), acts as a potent antioxidant and membrane stabilizer. It protects hepatocytes through several mechanisms, including scavenging of reactive oxygen species, inhibition of lipid peroxidation in cell membranes, and stimulation of ribosomal RNA polymerase I activity, which enhances protein synthesis and promotes hepatocellular regeneration (Abd El-Hack et al., 2020; Tao et al., 2025). Moreover, silymarin strengthens cellular membranes, rendering them less permeable to hepatotoxins and thereby maintaining intracellular homeostasis (Vargas-Mendoza et al., 2014). In parallel, artichoke (Cynara scolymus) extract, which is abundant in cynarin, luteolin, and other phenolic acids, complements this action through its potent choleretic and cholagogic properties (Ashour et al., 2025). By stimulating bile secretion and promoting bile flow, artichokes facilitate fat digestion and the excretion of metabolic by-products, thus alleviating hepatic workload and enhancing lipid metabolism efficiency. Enhanced bile production also contributes to improved lipid emulsification and absorption while accelerating the clearance of cholesterol and other lipids from the bloodstream, ultimately leading to a more balanced lipid profile. These combined effects of silymarin and cynarine underlie the observed reductions in serum liver enzymes and lipid fractions, confirming that HepatoMax supports liver health and metabolic stability in broilers under intensive production conditions.

The reduction in serum bilirubin fractions (total, direct, and indirect) in the 0.50 mL/L group further supports the conclusion of improved hepatic function. Bilirubin is a waste product from the breakdown of heme, and its proper conjugation and excretion are vital liver functions. Elevated bilirubin levels can indicate impaired liver detoxification pathways. The lower levels observed here suggest that HepatoMax enhanced the liver's capacity for bilirubin processing, which is consistent with the improved activity of detoxification enzymes often seen with silymarin supplementation (Marin et al., 2017). The combination of membrane stabilization from silymarin, enhanced detoxification from artichoke, and reduced oxidative stress from all plant components created a powerful synergistic effect that maintained liver health even under the demanding conditions of modern broiler production. These results are consistent with previous findings where similar phytogenic compounds alleviated liver damage in poultry exposed to various stressors (Orlowski et al., 2018).

Dyslipidemia, characterized by elevated levels of harmful lipids, is a common metabolic issue in fast-growing broilers and can lead to excessive fat deposition and associated health problems. The results of this study clearly show that HepatoMax supplementation led to a profound and beneficial modulation of the blood lipid profile. Broilers receiving the 0.50 mL/L dose exhibited the lowest concentrations of TG, total cholesterol, LDL, and VLDL, coupled with the highest level of HDL. The lipid-modulating effects observed in broilers supplemented with HepatoMax can be attributed to the synergistic actions of its phytogenic constituents, silymarin, cynarin, chlorogenic acid, inulin, chicoric acid, l-carnitine, and sorbitol, which collectively enhance hepatic function and lipid metabolism. Silymarin and cynarin improve bile secretion, promote cholesterol catabolism, and protect hepatocytes from oxidative damage, thereby reducing serum triglycerides, total cholesterol, and LDL while increasing HDL (Abd El-Hack et al., 2020; Ashour et al., 2025). By stimulating bile production, artichoke enhances the excretion of cholesterol, which is the primary precursor for bile acids (Ashour et al., 2025). This is a major pathway for cholesterol elimination from the body. Furthermore, studies suggest that compounds in artichoke can inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis (Gebhardt, 1997). Chicory further supports lipid regulation by lowering abdominal fat and serum lipids through enhanced fatty acid mobilization and improved gut–liver axis function (Khoobani et al., 2019; Tufarelli et al., 2023). l-carnitine complements these effects by facilitating mitochondrial β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids, reducing lipid deposition and improving energy utilization (Azizi-Chekosari et al., 2021; Fouad et al., 2013). Sorbitol may assist hepatic detoxification and nutrient absorption, indirectly contributing to lipid turnover (Bauchart et al., 1985). Together, these mechanisms establish HepatoMax as a potent phytogenic blend that harmonizes hepatic protection, antioxidant defense, and lipid homeostasis, resulting in improved metabolic efficiency and performance in broiler chickens.

Oxidative stress, a state of imbalance between the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the body's ability to neutralize them, is a major challenge in modern poultry production, negatively affecting health and performance. The results from this study demonstrate that HepatoMax supplementation significantly bolstered the antioxidant defense system of the broilers. This was evidenced by the increased activity of crucial endogenous antioxidant enzymes including GPX and SOD, and TAC which peaked in the 0.50 mL/L group. Critically, this enhanced antioxidant capacity translated into reduced cellular damage, as shown by the markedly lower levels of MDA, a key biomarker of lipid peroxidation (Behboodi et al., 2025). The potent antioxidant activity of HepatoMax stems from the rich profile of phenolic and flavonoid compounds present in milk thistle, artichoke, and chicory. Silymarin from milk thistle is a well-documented antioxidant that can directly scavenge a wide variety of free radicals and also chelate metal ions that catalyze ROS production (Abd El-Hack et al., 2020). Beyond direct scavenging, silymarin is known to enhance the expression of antioxidant enzymes by upregulating the Nrf2 signaling pathway, a master regulator of the antioxidant response (Tao et al., 2025). This mechanism likely explains the observed increases in GPX and SOD activities. Artichoke and chicory are also rich sources of phenolic acids (like chlorogenic acid) and flavonoids, which contribute to the overall antioxidant pool (Ashour et al., 2025; Balaž et al., 2024). The pattern observed for TAC shows an initial rise with increasing HepatoMax levels, reaching a peak around 0.5 %, followed by a moderate decline at higher doses. This suggests that moderate supplementation effectively enhanced the birds’ total antioxidant capacity, while excessive inclusion may have reduced the need for maintaining high circulating antioxidant reserves. Such a response aligns with a physiological adjustment toward redox homeostasis—once oxidative stress was alleviated and enzymatic defenses (e.g., GPX and SOD) became more active, the overall antioxidant demand decreased, resulting in slightly lower TAC values beyond the optimal level. The reduction in MDA by over 64 % at the optimal dose is a powerful indicator of this protective effect, signifying that cell membranes were better protected from oxidative damage, which is fundamental for overall health and performance.

The PCA provided an integrated and holistic interpretation of the dataset, synthesizing growth performance, biochemical, antioxidant, and carcass traits into two dominant dimensions. The first two components together explained a very high proportion of the total variance (81.8 %), indicating that the multivariate structure of the data was strongly driven by a coordinated physiological response to treatment. PC1 emerged as a clear metabolic stress versus physiological robustness axis. The positive side of this component was strongly associated with higher FCR, feed intake, lipid fractions (TG, VLDL, LDL, cholesterol), liver enzymes (ALT, AST, ALP), bilirubin fractions, glucose, and MDA, collectively reflecting increased metabolic load, hepatic stress, oxidative damage, and poorer feed efficiency. In contrast, the negative side of Component 1 aligned with higher body weight gain, breast meat yield, albumin, total protein, thymus development, and antioxidant defenses (GPX, SOD, TAC). This opposition clearly illustrates that improved growth performance occurred simultaneously with enhanced antioxidant capacity and improved protein profile, while metabolic stress markers were reduced. The PCA score distribution demonstrated a clear separation of treatment groups along this primary axis. The control group clustered toward the positive side of PC1, characterized by higher oxidative stress, elevated liver enzyme activities, increased lipid deposition, and poorer feed efficiency. Conversely, birds receiving HepatoMax, particularly at the 0.50 mL/L dose, were positioned toward the negative side of PC1, aligning with superior growth performance, enhanced antioxidant enzyme activity, improved protein status, and lower metabolic stress indicators. The intermediate positioning of the 0.25 and 1.00 mL/L groups supports a dose-dependent response, with the moderate supplementation level exerting the most balanced and beneficial systemic effect.

PC2 represented a secondary lipid deposition and treatment-related axis, strongly defined by fat pad accumulation and circulating lipid fractions on the positive side, and by HepatoMax loading negatively. The strong negative loading of HepatoMax on this axis, together with its inverse relationship with lipid traits and oxidative stress markers, further confirms that supplementation modulated lipid metabolism and reduced fat deposition. This suggests that HepatoMax not only improved growth efficiency but also influenced nutrient partitioning toward lean tissue accretion rather than adipose storage. Importantly, the PCA findings emphasize that the observed improvements were not isolated effects. Growth enhancement, improved antioxidant defense, better liver function, and optimized lipid metabolism were tightly interconnected. By mitigating oxidative stress (lower MDA; higher GPX, SOD, TAC), the supplement likely reduced the metabolic cost associated with cellular damage and inflammatory processes, thereby reallocating energy toward growth and muscle deposition. Simultaneously, improved hepatic enzyme profiles and bilirubin regulation indicate better liver functionality, which supports efficient nutrient metabolism and detoxification. Such coordinated responses reflect the synergistic action of a multifunctional additive rather than independent single-trait effects. Finally, the relatively high uniqueness values observed for heart and liver weights indicate that these organ traits were not strongly captured by the two principal components. This suggests that organ weights may be influenced by additional regulatory mechanisms, possibly including genetic, developmental, or allometric factors, that operate partly independently of the primary metabolic and antioxidant pathways modulated by HepatoMax.

Conclusion

HepatoMax supplementation via drinking water improved broiler performance, metabolic health, and antioxidant status in a dose-dependent manner. The 0.50 mL/L level produced the most consistent benefits. This dose also significantly enhanced lipid profile, reduced hepatic enzyme activities, strengthened antioxidant defenses, and decreased oxidative stress, alongside improved serum protein status and reduced glucose and bilirubin levels. Lower inclusion (0.25 mL/L) had minimal effects, while 1.00 mL/L maintained fat-reducing benefits but showed diminished improvements in growth efficiency, indicating a quadratic response. HepatoMax is a beneficial phytogenic blend rich in silymarin, chlorogenic acid, phenolics, and antioxidant compounds that supports liver function, enhances lipid metabolism, and strengthens antioxidant defenses in broilers.

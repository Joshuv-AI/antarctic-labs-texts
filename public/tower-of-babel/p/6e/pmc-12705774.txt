Effects of an enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) program in elder patients undergoing transvaginal reconstructive surgery for pelvic organ prolapse (POP): a multicenter randomized parallel open-label controlled trial
Authors: Yueyue Yan, Yuelun Zhang, Liqun Wang, Zhijun Xia, Li Hong, Qingkai Wu, Lubin Liu, Chunfang Ha, Zhijing Sun, Juan Chen, Honghui Shi, Jiangyan Zhou, Hainan Xu, Shasha Hong, Zhuowei Xue, Shentao Lu, Min Li, Shuo Liang, Lan Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo develop a locally tailored ERAS program for 60–80-year-old patients with pelvic organ prolapse (POP) undergoing transvaginal mesh (TVM) surgery in low- and middle-resource countries.MethodsIn this multicenter randomized controlled trial conducted at seven tertiary hospitals, 262 women aged 60–80 scheduled for elective TVM surgery were randomly allocated (1:1) to receive either a comprehensive ERAS perioperative intervention or conventional perioperative management (131 participants per group). The primary outcomes were postoperative length of stay, pain scores, time to first flatus, removal of urinary catheter, first assisted walking, postoperative complications, readmission, emergency visit, in-hospital costs, PGI-I, QoR-15, PONV, and residual urine volume.ResultsERAS group had significantly shorter postoperative length of stay (median 3.92 days vs. 4.99 days, HR = 1.42, 95% CI 1.11–1.82, P = 0.005), lower pain scores within 24 h, faster time to first flatus, removal of urinary catheter, and first assisted walking. There were no significant differences between the ERAS and conventional groups regarding postoperative complications, readmission, emergency visit, in-hospital costs, PGI-I, QoR-15, PONV, and residual urine volume. There was no 30-day postoperative readmission in both groups.ConclusionsERAS program can reduce length of stay and improve postoperative outcomes in patients undergoing vaginal reconstructive surgery. Recommend implementing ERAS for 60–80-year-olds in East Asia where TVM surgeries are still an option for POP.Clinical registrationChiCTR2200057422 (March 12, 2022).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00404-025-08212-2.

What does this study adds to the clinical work

This study provides the first multicentre RCT evidence that a locally tailored ERAS program markedly shortens hospital stay and speeds early recovery without raising complications or costs in 60–80-year-old women undergoing transvaginal mesh repair for pelvic organ prolapse in low- and middle-resource settings. These findings support routine implementation of ERAS pathways for this specific population where TVM surgery remains an option.

This study provides the first multicentre RCT evidence that a locally tailored ERAS program markedly shortens hospital stay and speeds early recovery without raising complications or costs in 60–80-year-old women undergoing transvaginal mesh repair for pelvic organ prolapse in low- and middle-resource settings. Introduction

The enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) concept, which was first proposed by Hehlet in 1997 [1], has challenged traditional perioperative practices with a series of evidence-based interventions involving a reduction in preoperative fasting and bowel preparation, a standardized approach in anesthetic management, limited tubes and drains, multimodal pain control, early postoperative mobilization, and enteral nutrition [2–5]. ERAS has been demonstrated to improve the functional rehabilitation of patients after surgery without reducing patient satisfaction or increasing perioperative complications [3, 6, 7]. For benign gynecological minimally invasive surgery [6, 8], gynecologic oncology surgery [4], vulvar and vaginal surgery[9], and urogynecologic surgery [10], ERAS has become a standardized surgical care model in high-income countries.

In developed countries, with the successful implementation of ERAS patients can achieve same-day discharge after urogynecologic surgery, such as vaginal hysterectomy, vaginal obliterative procedures, vaginal suspension [7, 11, 12]. However, in low- and middle-resource countries, for example, in China, ERAS implementation was limited in centers, tertiary hospitals [13] and patients undergoing urogyencologic surgery were discharged almost 5 days postoperatively [14, 15]. In order to access the gap, we aimed to develop an ERAS program based on the best currently available evidence in perioperative care and also adapt to our health care system, medical insurance, patients characters as well as local culture and custom. Furthermore, while transvaginal mesh (TVM) was banned or paused for pelvic organ prolapse (POP) repair in USA, UK, Australia, New Zealand, and France, it has remained a vital option for older women affected by or at high risk of pelvic organ prolapse(POP) relapse and contraindication for other surgical routes in Germany, Asia, and South Africa [16]. It has become increasingly important to optimize perioperative care interventions for older POP patients undergoing pelvic floor reconstructive surgeries with TVM in this region.

In this clinical trial, specific ERAS protocols and multidisciplinary team (MDT) were designed for older patients undergoing transvaginal pelvic reconstructive surgery in accordance with the items suggested by the ACOG committee[8] and the Chinese ERAS Consensus In Gynecology [17]. This study specifically compared the ERAS protocol with the conventional protocol in terms of the length of hospital stay and other perioperative outcomes in older patients who underwent TVM surgery.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval and registration

The study was approved by the institutional review board of our Hospital (date of approval: Oct 31, 2021; reference number: ZS-3216D) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. This manuscript was reported in accordance with the CONSORT statement and the Reporting on ERAS Compliance, Outcomes, and Elements Research Checklist requirements [18]. This trial was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200057422) before the first patient’s enrollment.

Participants

Potentially eligible patients were recruited by gynecologists at seven research centers between March 2022 and December 2023. Patients aged 60 to 80 years were eligible if they met all the following criteria: (1) scheduled for elective TVM surgery for pelvic organ prolapse (POP). The indications for TVM surgery were required to strictly comply with the Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of POP (2020 Edition) [19], including patients with recurrent symptomatic POP, or those over 60 years old who newly diagnosed severe prolapse, predominantly with anterior compartment prolapse, classified as at least stage III according to the ICS POP-Q system. (2) Patients were designated as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classifications I−II, and (3) they provided signed informed consent. Patients who met any of the following criteria were excluded from the trial: (1) were diagnosed with gynecological malignant tumors; (2) had acute infection, mental illness or other surgical contraindications; (3) had nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) contraindications that included peptic ulcers, a history of allergic reactions to NSAIDs, or aspirin-induced asthma; (5) had undergone emergency surgery. Patient and Public Involvement: Not applicable.

Randomization and concealment

Eligible patients were randomized into ERAS perioperative intervention or conventional management groups (control group) at a 1:1 ratio via a computer-generated random sequence with a fixed block size of 4. Potential participants were screened and enrolled by the site principal investigator. Following the acquisition of written informed consent, allocation concealment was implemented by the study statistician through a web-based interactive voice response system. Subsequently, participants were assigned to interventions by the study coordinator, which could not be altered or anticipated by the study staff. Importantly, no randomization list was ever accessible to investigators or outcome assessors, thereby ensuring complete concealment of the allocation sequence until the point of assignment. However, due to the nature of the interventions, healthcare providers and patients were not blinded to the allocation.

Surgical procedures

Patients underwent modified transvaginal mesh surgery with a self-cut mesh (lightweight, large-pore, single-stranded polypropylene mesh) by seven experienced surgeons from seven research centers. They were all qualified for grade IV gynecological operations in China and had at least ten years of experience in TVM repair. The procedures of this surgical method were detailed as published article [20]. The decision to preserve the uterus was collectively made by the surgeon's suggestions and the patient's preferences.

Interventions

Patients who were randomized to the intervention group received a perioperative ERAS program (components seen as Supplementary Table 1), including reduced preoperative fasting, omission of mechanical bowel preparation unless intraoperative intestinal damage was predicted, preoperative analgesia, total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA), bispectral index monitoring, opioid-sparing multimodal analgesia prescribed on a scheduled basis, early catheter removal and extensive ambulation, and oral intake starting on postoperative day (POD 0). The urinary catheter was removed at 6 AM on POD1 along with vaginal packing. The post-voiding residual (PVR) urine volume test was measured via ultrasound by trained nurses after patients urinated 3 times and the bladder felt empty. PVR volume less than 100 ml was considered a pass. For patients whose PVR volume test failed, functional magnetic stimulation and low-frequency electrical stimulation [21] with or without neostigmine were described. If the PVR volume still exceeded 200 ml, clear intermittent catheterization is administered.

Patients allocated to the control group received perioperative management following the most updated and available guidelines during the design of the trial. These perioperative managements included complete preoperative fasting with their last meal by midnight the day before surgery, no strict restrictions on mechanical bowel preparation or preanesthetic medication, intravenous inhalation combined with anesthesia, progressive ambulation, oral intake starting on POD 1, necessary NSAID prescription at the patient’s request, and opioid use or intravenous NSAID for rescue or breakthrough pain (components seen as Supplementary Table 1). The vaginal packing was removed at 6AM on POD1, and urethral catheter was removed within postoperative 24 ~ 72 h. The PVR volume test was done as that of the ERAS group.

Both groups adopted an intraoperative goal-directed fluid infusion regime, intravenous infusion of lidocaine, maintenance of normothermia, and prophylaxis of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) via the use of ondansetron combined with dexamethasone.

Trial outcomes

The primary outcome was postoperative length of stay, which was defined as the number of days from the day of surgery until discharge. To rule out the potential impact of the insurance policy and other nonmedical variables (i.e., sociocultural considerations and patients’ concerns) on length of stay, we set predefined criteria for discharge. The research team recommended that the attending gynecologists be discharged only when all the following conditions were met adequate pain control with oral analgesics, absence of vomiting or severe nausea, absence of body temperature ≥ 37.5 °C, tolerance of solid food, PVR volume < 100 ml, and ability to ambulate as before surgery. Independent attending gynecologists who were blinded to the group allocation made the patients’ discharge decisions based on the predetermined discharge criteria.

The secondary outcomes included a numerical rating scale (NRS) score for postoperative pain, time to first flatus, time to first assisted walk, postoperative indwelling time of the urinary catheter, incidence of PONV, postoperative 30-day complication (defined as any postoperative 30-day or in-hospital complication, according to the Clavien‒Dindo classification[22]), readmission, emergency visit, in-hospital cost, POD 30 Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI-I) score, and POD 3, 7, and 30 quality of life. Pain intensity was assessed via a numerical rating scale (NRS) ranging from 0 to 10 (no pain: 0, mild pain: 1 ~ 3, moderate pain: 4 ~ 6, and severe pain: 7 ~ 10). Quality of life was assessed with a Chinese-validated version of the Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) questionnaire [23]. Postoperative 30-day complications, including intensive care unit (ICU) admission; urine retention; bleeding ≥ 500 mL/hematoma; infection; urinary or gastrointestinal tract injury; and deep venous thrombosis; vomiting, nausea, and sedation scores at 2, 12, and 24 h postoperatively; and incision healing at 30 days postoperative, were compared as exploratory outcomes. An independent physician who was blinded to the allocation assessed secondary outcomes.

A web-based electronic data capture system was used to collect the data. During hospitalization, medical and nursing staff reported daily on the patients’ status. Patients were followed systematically at an outpatient clinic and were evaluated for any complications or readmissions occurring within 30 days after discharge. Any adverse events were reported to the safety monitoring committee for further evaluation.

Statistical analysis

We conducted a power analysis based on the findings from a multicenter perspective cohort (unpublished data) at Peking Union Medical College Hospital and the Chinese Pelvic Floor Reconstruction Surgery with Implants (Mesh) Registry (PRIMES Registry). The median postoperative length of stay was 5 days in patients who received conventional perioperative management, while we assumed that the ERAS program could shorten the median postoperative length of stay to 3.5 days. Based on these estimates, the hazard ratio for comparing the postoperative length of stay between the groups using an event-driven design was 0.7 (3.5/5), with a Type I error probability set at a bilateral 0.05, a Type II error probability of 0.2, and a 1:1 ratio of patients in the two groups. Using the event-driven design method for sample size estimation, it is calculated that a sample size of 248 cases is needed. A sample size of 124 for each group provided 80% statistical power to detect a significant difference in postoperative length of stay. Considering a 5% drop-out rate, we ultimately planned to randomize 262 patients.

Statistical analysis followed the intention-to-treat principle. If all the eligible patients were discharged alive, the postoperative length of stay was compared between groups via Cox regression, in which the time to discharge was regressed against the group allocation. The hazard ratio with a 95% confidence interval was reported as an effect size measure. A hazard ratio greater than 1 indicated faster discharge, thus favoring intervention. When all-cause mortality occurred among randomized patients, Fine and Gray’s competing risk model was used, in which the death event was regarded as a competing risk event. The time to first flatus among the secondary outcomes was compared via Cox regression. Continuous secondary outcomes, including the postoperative NRS score, PONV score, POD 3 quality of life, and in-hospital cost, were compared using t tests. Other binary secondary outcomes, namely postoperative 30-day complications and readmission, were compared via the chi-square test. We conducted a prespecified subgroup analysis based on whether the uterus was preserved, and as an additional analysis, we performed a post hoc subgroup analysis by dividing patients into those above and below 70 years of age to explore whether the impact of ERAS on the length of hospital stay varied between these age groups. No multiplicity adjustment was applied among the secondary outcomes and subgroup analysis. Therefore, these analyses were interpreted as exploratory findings. A 2-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were completed in R (version 4.4.1, R Foundation, Vienna, Austria, www.r-project.org).

Results

Between March 2022 and December 2023, a total of 276 women referred to seven research centers were screened for eligibility. A total of 3 women did not meet the inclusion criteria, and 11 women declined to participate in the trial. Among the 262 eligible women, 131 were randomly assigned to the intervention group, and 131 were assigned to the control group (Fig. 1). In the ERAS program group and the control group, 2 and 3 patients, respectively, were found by the attending gynecologist to be clinically unsuitable for TVM surgery after random allocation. As a result, other surgeries were performed instead. Therefore, these 5 patients were excluded from the ITT population. The intervention group included 129 patients, and the control group included 128 patients, forming the modified ITT (mITT) population (Fig. 1). The baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The primary postoperative outcomes are presented in Table 2. The postoperative length of stay in the ERAS group was significantly shorter than that in the conventional group (median 3.92 days [3.00 − 5.06] vs. 4.99 days [3.69 − 6.08], HR = 1.42, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.82, P = 0.005). In addition, significantly shorter values were found in the ERAS group for catheter retention duration (median 28.00 h [22.50–45.00] vs. 46.00 h [28.00–70.00], HR = 1.74, 95% CI 1.36–2.24, P < 0.001) and time to ambulation (20.00 h [13.17–24.00] vs. 23.42 h [15.11–30.00], HR = 1.85, 95% CI 1.42–2.41, P = 0.002). Patients who underwent ERAS procedures had substantially lower NRS scores and nausea scores at 2 h and 12 h after the operation. However, no between-group differences were found in terms of in-hospital cost, perioperative complications, the QoR-15 score, the PGI-I score at 30 days after the operation, or rehospitalization. With respect to the exploratory outcomes, we did not find significant differences in ICU admission, urine retention, bleeding, urinary tract infection, Clavien–Dindo complication grade, vomiting, nausea, sedation score, or incision healing (Supplementary Table 2).

Discussion

Main findings

This randomized, multicenter, open-label trial demonstrated that, compared with the conventional pathway, the ERAS pathway improved perioperative outcomes in patients who underwent TVM surgeries. Our study revealed that the ERAS pathway further improved patient outcomes, including a reduction in the postoperative length of stay, postoperative pain in the first 24 h, and urinary catheter retention duration, while maintaining quality of life recovery and not compromising morbidity or the readmission rate within 30 days after surgery.

Strengths and limitations

This is the first randomized controlled trial of the ERAS protocol in complex pelvic reconstructive surgery and has a multicenter design, which is a strength of this study. Enrolled hospitals with a large surgical volume of pelvic floor repairs from seven representative major geographic regions across China implemented the same ERAS program, facilitating the generalizability of our results to other institutions. Additionally, our study highlights the unique cultural and economic factors influencing the design and implementation of ERAS protocol in China. The cultural tendency, shaped by the structure of healthcare services and the geographical distribution of medical resources, plays a significant role in patient decision-making and recovery.

However, this study was an open-label trial, and it was therefore difficult to blind clinical practitioners or patients to interventions such as preoperative carbohydrate loading, multimodal analgesia, early postoperative diet, or ambulation in the same shared ward. Nonetheless, those possible biases were minimized by several measures: an independent physician who decided discharge time points using preestablished criteria and assessed the NRS and quality of life. Second, the generalizability of our trial is limited to institutions where same-day discharge for urogynecology surgery is common practice. The postoperative LOS is an important indicator but is not the only way to evaluate the effect of the ERAS pathway. Patient discharge might be affected by local insurance policies, the community health service system, and other nonmedical reasons. Unlike in developed countries, where ERAS is primarily aimed at same-day discharge and minimizing hospital costs, Chinese patients often prefer to extend their recovery period in the hospital due to concerns about inadequate follow-up care in local facilities. As the older population comprises a large proportion of POP patients and keeps growing in low- and middle-resource countries such as China, ensuring faster, safer, and more satisfactory recovery after pelvic floor repairs, rather than simply focusing on early discharge, remains highly meaningful.

Interpretation

In our study, we demonstrated a reduction from 4.99 days to 3.92 days in postoperative LOS in the intervention group, indicating that patients enrolled in our urogynecology-specific ERAS protocol met the discharge criteria more quickly, which aligned with previous retrospective study conducted by Xia et al.[14]. Although we cannot achieve same-day discharge as in developed countries currently [7, 24], we tried to gradually reduce hospital stay by enhancing the efficiency of perioperative care. Optimized perioperative management included avoiding routine bowel preparation, reducing preoperative overnight fasting time, multimodal analgesia, early postoperative ambulation and enteral nutrition, and timely removal of catheters. These practices and good compliance with the key elements (seen as Table 2) enhanced recovery by minimizing the physical stress of surgery, reducing discomfort, and promoting quicker functional recovery for prolapse patients aged 60–80 years who underwent transvaginal mesh surgery compared with the conventional group.

For vaginal surgery, pain was a risk factor for delay in LOS [25]. Therefore, alleviation of postoperative pain is crucial for shortening LOS. Preoperative and postoperative administration of acetaminophen and NSAIDs is a common regimen in ERAS protocols and has been shown to be effective in numerous trials in urogynecology, especially vaginal surgery [26–29]. Unless contraindicated, acetaminophen and NSAIDs should be used perioperatively to reduce postoperative pain [9]. We administrated gabapentin based on the combination of acetaminophen and NSAIDs, considering the potential injury to the small branch of obturator nerve during transvaginal mesh insertion [30]. Our specific opioid-free multimodal pain control significantly improved early postoperative pain in ERAS patients compared to the control group, without an increase in adverse effects of opioids. The lower level of pain experienced by patients in the ERAS group resulted in earlier ambulation and voluntary urination.

Urinary retention is another risk factor associated with prolonged hospital stays [25, 31]. To achieve same-day discharge, urogynecologists in developed countries preferred early removal of the urinary catheter 3 ~ 8 h postoperatively through a retro-grade voiding trial and reported that this approach decreased the risk of infection [32, 33], but it could modestly elevate the risk of recatheterization [34]. In China, urinary catheters are usually removed 3 ~ 5 days after urogynecologic surgery [14, 35]. Our study validated an alternative urinary management strategy as a key element of LOS in the ERAS pathway in low- and middle-resource countries. In the ERAS group, the median duration was 28.00 h, while it was 46.00 h in the control group. Although the ERAS group had a slightly higher rate of PVR over 100 ml after the first catheter removal, their PVR volume was improved and deceased below 100 ml by functional training with or without medicine; moreover, no significant differences were detected between the groups in terms of urinary retention and postoperative urinary tract infections. These results suggest that catheter removal on POD1, combined with the PVR volume test, could serve as an alternative to early removal in the post-anesthesia recovery unit, offering a compromise for transitioning to same-day discharge while avoiding the invasive procedure of urinary catheter replacement.

In contrast with previous studies [2, 3, 14, 36, 37], our ERAS pathway did not reduce hospital costs and, in fact, had a slightly higher cost compared with the control group, although this difference was not statistically significant. A similar finding was observed in another RCT conducted on gynecologic laparoscopic procedures in China, which reported that hospitalization costs for the full ERAS program were slightly higher than those of the control group due to the additional expenses associated with the multimodal analgesic protocol [6]. The additional costs in our trial also mainly covered prophylactic analgesics and the medication to prevent PONV. A possible reason for this cost difference is that Chinese women aged 60–80 years in the control group were reluctant to take medication due to the cultural belief that all medicines have some side effects. They preferred to endure short-term postoperative discomfort rather than take medication to alleviate symptoms as needed, resulting in lower medication costs in the control group. These findings emphasized the implementation of ERAS protocol must be adapted to different regions, according to variations across socioeconomic and cultural backgrounds [13].

Clinical research in the fields of colorectal and thoracic surgery has shown that ERAS is safe and feasible for elderly patients. According to a recent systematic review and meta-analysis involving a total of 1174 older patients, an ERAS protocol was associated with a lower incidence of postoperative morbidity (P < 0.001) and a significantly shorter hospital length of stay (P = 0.002) than a non-ERAS standard of care[38]. In the present study, we reached the same conclusion: ERAS significantly reduced postoperative pain scores within the first 24 h and improved early ambulation without increasing the incidence of perioperative complications, emergency visits or readmission within 30 days among 60 − 80-year-old patients. Another study comparing the perioperative outcomes of elderly (> 75 y/o) versus nonelderly patients (45 y/o) on ERPs undergoing laparotomy for gynecologic surgery also demonstrated the safety and feasibility of the ERAS program[39]. These findings suggest that for older patients, age alone should not determine a patient's eligibility for the ERAS program, but biological aging factors and frailty may affect outcomes with ERAS. Future studies should evaluate factors that can improve ERAS compliance in older women.

Conclusions

Implementation of the ERAS pathway for older women who underwent TVM surgeries for POP decreased length of postoperative hospital stay. The perioperative ERAS program was also associated with lower pain scores in the first 24 h, earlier removal of urine drainage, and assisted walking. We recommend considering implementation of ERAS in TVM surgeries based on evidence from this randomized controlled trial.

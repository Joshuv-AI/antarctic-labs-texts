Application study of 3D printing combined with mixed reality technology in teaching percutaneous screw fixation for scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy
Authors: Shuming Ye, Jialiu Fang, Neng Jin, Liang Hu, Changming Guo, Xin Cheng, Yue Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the application effect of 3D printing combined with MR technology in teaching the percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy, providing new ideas to enhance teaching effectiveness in this field. MethodsFrom 2023 to 2024, the trainee residents and graduate students rotating in our department were randomly divided into two groups: the MR glasses + 3D printing group (21 people) and the traditional teaching group (21 people). In the MR glasses + 3D printing group, the trainees first used Meta Quest3 glasses to learn the anatomy of the carpal bones and the operation of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy, and then practiced percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures on a 3D-printed wrist joint model. In the traditional teaching group, the trainees received traditional PPT lectures and then practiced percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures on a 3D-printed wrist joint model. Teaching effectiveness was evaluated through theoretical, operational assessments, and a self-designed questionnaire. Each item in the questionnaire was scored out of 10, with higher scores indicating better teaching effectiveness, and 0 indicating poor performance, while 10 indicated excellent performance.ResultsThe success rate of fixation in the MR glasses + 3D printing group was 90.48% (19/21), while that in the traditional teaching group was 80.95% (17/21). There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups (P > 0.05). The operation time for the MR glasses + 3D printing group was (27.43 ± 3.17) minutes, while that for the traditional teaching group was (40.00 ± 3.74) minutes, with a statistically significant difference. The questionnaire results showed that the experimental group scored higher than the traditional teaching group in aspects such as enhancing classroom atmosphere, course satisfaction, learning concentration, mastery of theoretical knowledge, and analytical problem-solving abilities (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe combination of 3D printing and MR technology improves physicians’ understanding of the theoretical basis and operational skills for percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy, enhances the fun of training, and has a promising application prospect in orthopedic education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-026-09122-w.

Introduction

Residency training is an essential step for medical graduates to acquire the core competencies required for independent clinical practice. It ensures a baseline of quality and standardization among clinicians and enhances the healthcare system’s overall performance [1]. As Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) gains traction globally, orthopedic education must adapt its teaching and evaluation strategies [2, 3]. Traditional orthopedic lecture-based classroom teaching is effective in imparting theoretical knowledge, but it has disadvantages when it comes to the practical application of knowledge in real conditions. Faces numerous challenges such as diverse trainee needs, and inadequate clinical practice opportunities, which together hinder comprehensive trainee development [4].

Visualization of Minimally Invasive Orthopedic Surgery has significantly improved communication between clinicians and patients, reducing procedure time. In particular, using artificial intelligence (AI) in orthopedics has significantly improved orthopedic imaging and minimally invasive orthopedic Surgery [5–7].Percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy is an important method of minimally invasive operation in traumatic orthopedics [8, 9]. To master the anatomical structure of scaphoid bone and the position and direction of guide pin placement is the basis of learning percutaneous screw internal fixation operation for orthopedic standardized training doctors [10]. Traditional teaching of scaphoid anatomy and percutaneous screw internal fixation mainly relies on two-dimensional materials such as scaphoid anatomical atlas. Students can only imagine the three-dimensional structure of the wrist skeleton to understand it, and it is difficult to obtain real intraoperative anatomical positioning and operation perceptual cognition. Mixed Reality (MR) technology is proposed by Professor Paul Milgram [11]. It is a technology that displays and interacts with real surgical operation and virtual image in the same visual space, and forms a reciprocal feedback information loop between reality, virtual and user, which can provide students with a more immersive real experience. To integrate theory and practice more closely [12].

In the teaching of scaphoid fracture treated by percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy, the virtual model of scaphoid fracture can improve the knowledge of the anatomy of scaphoid fracture and the minimally invasive operation technique of percutaneous screw internal fixation for standardized training doctors. However, the traditional scaphoid model has certain structural limitations, and it is difficult to truly restore the three-dimensional spatial configuration of the scaphoid in the wrist joint. 3D printing technology can produce solid models of scaphoid fracture and wrist joint according to the reality [13]. At present, many studies have successfully applied 3D printing models to anatomy and surgery related teaching [14–16].

In this study, 3D printing technology and MR technology were combined to construct a scaphoid fracture model, which was applied to the teaching of scaphoid fracture treated by percutaneous screw internal fixation, in order to improve the learning experience and learning efficiency of students.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

A total of 42 standardized training doctors who were transferred to the orthopedics Department of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University from 2023 to 2024 were selected as the research objects. Inclusion criteria: (1) certain theoretical basis of wrist joint anatomy; (2) None of them had systematically learned the technique of percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy before. Exclusion criteria: (1) trainee with experience of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fracture before admission; (2) trainee with high myopia and discomfort to wear Meta Quest3 glasses (Meta Company, USA); (3) Those who refused to complete the examination and questionnaire survey after class. The built-in random number function of Excel software was used to generate a random number for each subject, and then these random numbers were mapped into two different groups by sorting. All trainees gave informed consent and volunteered to participate. A total of 42 trainees were divided into experimental group (using the headset Meta Quest3 glasses teaching method) and control group (using the traditional teaching method) with 21 students in each group (Fig. 1). There were 14 males and 7 females in the experimental group, aged (24.00 ± 0.77) years. There were 15 males and 6 females in the control group, aged (23.67 ± 0.80) years. The subjects in both groups were postgraduate students. All of them were taught by the same associate professor with rich teaching experience. The teaching content was the treatment of scaphoid fractures by percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy.

The workflow of the software that converts CT data into the MR-ready holograms is as follows: the 3D CT data of the patient’s wrist joint (slice thickness 0.625 mm) were saved in DICOM format. DICOM data were imported into Mimics 21 software (Materialise Co., Belgium). According to the CT value of the bone, the threshold was set to 200HU, and the region grow tool was used. All adjacent and eligible pixels are automatically connected to form a preliminary skeletal “mask”. Subsequently, manual editing and fine adjustment were required to remove peripheral artifacts and isolate the scaphoid images. The processed mask generates a surface model (usually a triangular mesh, such as in STL format) using a “Compute 3D” or similar function. Through mesh smoothing, mesh simplification, hole filling and repair, the model is a closed and complete entity. The optimized STL model was imported into MR Software, and a virtual surgical learning scene of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fracture was designed. Students could freely move their heads to observe the scaphoid fracture model from any Angle through MR Head display. The virtual surgical planning (such as percutaneous screw entry point and direction) was directly superimposed on the model to achieve surgical planning.

Teaching methods

The control group was taught by the local anatomy map of wrist joint and PPT. The atlas includes anatomy of carpal bones including scaphoid bones and anatomy of intercarpal ligaments. An associate professor of the department taught the relevant content and the operation skills of percutaneous screw under C-arm fluoroscopy to the standardized training doctors through slides combined with maps, and the duration was 20 min (Fig. 2A). After the explanation, the students in the control group learned and memorized by themselves through paper maps and notes for 20 min.

In the early stage of the experimental group, the same associate professor used the same PPT and pictures as the control group to teach the standardized training doctors, and the teaching content and duration were the same as the control group. After slide teaching, students in the experimental group received immersive anatomy of carpal bones including scaphoid bone and sectional anatomy of scaphoid fracture treated with percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy with the help of Meta Quest3 equipment (Fig. 2B). The 3D carpal bone model including the scaphoid fracture and the 3D model after screw fixation of the scaphoid fracture have been imported into the Meta Quest3 device. The cases were derived from 10 patients who were admitted to our hospital, including 6 males and 4 females, with an average age of 42 ± 1.3 years. We randomly selected patients with an acute stable scaphoid fracture of type A2 located at the waist, with a fracture broken end displaced within 1mm [17]. The teaching methods assisted by mixed reality technology are: Before the operation, teachers and students wore MR Glasses to share different morphological images of scaphoid fracture before and during fixation. According to the relevant imaging information model of preoperative three-dimensional reconstruction of patients, the entry point of percutaneous screw and the direction of guide pin were determined under C-arm fluoroscopy. The virtual 3D images were rendered and updated in real time, and the direction and position of the screw penetrating the cortex and entering the fracture end were explained simultaneously. The material used for 3D printing model was polylactic acid (PLA). After adjusting for wall thickness and packing density, it was found that when the wall thickness was 2 mm and packing density was 20%, the tactility when drilling was very close to the actual bone density and drilling resistance. We removed the model support with pliers, removed the burrs from the model surface and transfixed and fixed of the five metacarpal bones with steel wires in the anatomical position for post-processing to obtain the model we used. At the same time, new models were provided to each trainee. The images in the Meta Quest3 glasses worn by the students were updated synchronously in real time following the teacher’s operation, and the students could directly understand the whole process of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures. The learning experience was consistent with the teacher. The course duration of the experimental group was 40 min, including 20 min of atlas teaching, 10 min of mixed reality teaching, and 10 min of self-study on paper atlas.

Teaching evaluation

Before and after the teaching, the theoretical level of the two groups of students was assessed by using a self-made test paper (full score 100 points, content validity was assessed through a hospital education working group review). At the end of the teaching, the 3D printing model of scaphoid fracture was used to evaluate the operation level of percutaneous screw internal fixation of the two groups of students (Fig. 2C and D), and the satisfaction of the teaching effect was evaluated by issuing a self-made questionnaire to the students. The success criteria of percutaneous screw internal fixation were as follows: the screw was located in the central axis of the scaphoid, the thread completely crossed the fracture line, and the screw head did not penetrate the scaphoid cortex. The outcome assessment was conducted by trained orthopedic doctors, who remained unaware of the allocation of each group throughout the entire study period.

Course assessment

Including theoretical assessment and operational assessment. The theoretical examination was conducted using a self-made examination paper, which included anatomy of scaphoid bone, classification of fracture and treatment methods. The total score was 100. The operation assessment included “history collection”, “imaging reading”, “position of the entrance of the percutaneous screw guide pin”, “position of the screw in the scaphoid bone”, “number of successful fixation cases” and “operation time”. Three teachers participated in the assessment, and each item was scored 100 points (The score is calculated as the average. Physicians were blinded to group assignments throughout the study period).

Evaluation scheme of training operation:

Evaluate the accuracy of screw entry point and to evaluate the position of the measuring screw: The needle entry point was located on the central axis of the scaphoid. A guide needle was inserted into the center of the scaphoid close to the lower edge of the trapezium, with a depth of the middle third of the scaphoid. C-arm fluoroscopy in anteroposterior, lateral, and oblique views showed that the guide needle was distributed along the midline of the scaphoid. Subsequently, depth measurement and drilling were performed, and a Herbert screw was inserted along the guide needle. Satisfaction evaluation of teaching effect

The self-made questionnaire was used to evaluate the overall satisfaction of the training effect of students, including five questions: satisfaction with the course, improvement of classroom atmosphere, learning concentration, mastery of theoretical knowledge, and self-evaluation of improvement of analysis and solution ability. According to their own situation, the students fill in the scores on the questionnaire, and the total score is 10.

Results

Comparison of theoretical and operational examination results between the two groups

There was no significant difference in theoretical scores between the two groups before teaching (P > 0.05). After the training, the theoretical examination scores of the two groups were better than those before the training (P < 0.05) (Table 1). In terms of the performance of operation assessment, 19 people in the experimental group were successfully fixed (the success rate was 90.48%), while 17 people in the control group (the success rate was 80.95%). All failures are caused by positioning errors. There was no significant difference in history collection and imaging reading between the two groups (P > 0.05). However, after training, the scores of the experimental group in the position of the entrance of the percutaneous screw guide pin, the position of the screw in the scaphoid bone and the operation time were better than those of the control group (P < 0.05) (Table 2 and Fig. 3A).

Discussion

The treatment of scaphoid fractures with percutaneous screw internal fixation under C-arm fluoroscopy is a minimally invasive skill that traumatic orthopedic surgeons need to master. C-arm fluoroscopy is an effective tool to assist percutaneous screw internal fixation. It has been confirmed that the use of virtual reality technology in orthopedic model teaching is helpful to improve students’ mastery of theory and operation technology [18, 19]. However, for the teaching of percutaneous screw internal fixation technique, mastering the classic bone section under C-arm fluoroscopy is only the first simple step. How to let students master the operation technique of percutaneous screw internal fixation of carpal scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy is a more challenging task.

The technique of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fractures under C-arm fluoroscopy is a difficult point in clinical teaching. Students cannot obtain an intuitive experience of the three-dimensional structure of scaphoid under X-ray fluoroscopy through the traditional atlas [20]. This study creatively applied mixed reality technology to the auxiliary teaching of carpal scaphoid anatomy under X-ray fluoroscopy. In the teaching practice of the experimental group, firstly, the basic carpal anatomy and the concept and operation skills of screw fixation of carpal scaphoid fracture under X-ray fluoroscopy were introduced through PPT and atlas, so that the students had a basic understanding of percutaneous screw fixation under X-ray fluoroscopy. After that, the mixed reality technology was used to show the dynamic image of the process of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fracture to the students, so that the students could obtain a more perceptual understanding of the operation technique of percutaneous screw fixation of scaphoid fracture under C-arm fluoroscopy. The traditional form and content of teaching are mostly text and two-dimensional picture materials. Generally, the students can only imagine the anatomical structure of the carpus and the operation of percutaneous screws by themselves under the teacher’s explanation, which is difficult to obtain good results [21]. This study introduces mixed reality teaching. Teachers can wear Meta Quest3 glasses to explain and interact with the operation of percutaneous screw fixation of wrist scaphoid bone under the guidance of C-arm fluoroscopy in real time, and realize real-time local image size adjustment through language and gestures to simulate the whole operation process. It can not only improve the students’ attention, participation and satisfaction, but also improve the students’ understanding of the position of the screws in the scaphoid bone and reduce the operation time. In this study, compared with the control group, the experimental group was better than the traditional teaching group in course satisfaction, improving subject atmosphere, learning concentration, theoretical knowledge mastery and analysis and solution ability.

There were 8 carpal bones in total, with different shapes and staggered arrangement. The spatial anatomical structure was complex, and it was difficult for students to understand them only by two-dimensional atlas. In this study, a high-simulation 3D carpal bone model was successfully produced by 3D printing technology. The model accurately reproduced the anatomical structure of carpal bones and the fracture morphology of scaphoid bone. The study found that the students gave a high evaluation on the course satisfaction, improving the subject atmosphere, learning concentration, theoretical knowledge and analysis and solution ability during the practice of using 3D printing models. For beginners, early surgical operation on the real patient’s wrist may not be possible, but practice on the high-simulation 3D model first, which has a high fault tolerance rate and is safer. It can not only improve the training efficiency of students, but also improve the satisfaction of students. In addition, the main cost of this study mainly includes the purchase of Meta Quest 3 glasses and related software, and the labor and time cost of learning related applications. The overall cost is relatively small and perfectly acceptable for the average teaching hospital.

Mixed reality technology also has certain limitations in the application of medical education [22]. First of all, the smart equipment and software used in the experimental group have a high cost and need to be purchased by teaching institutions, which limits the popularization of this technology. Secondly, the application of mixed reality technology is head-mounted equipment, students in immersive learning, there is no real operation, so the teaching process needs to be combined with actual operation, so as to improve learning efficiency. In addition, the mixed reality teaching mode is still relatively new, and there are not many teaching experiences that can be used for reference in the past. At present, most of the teachers are clinicians, who have more clinical work, so they do not have much time to learn the mixed reality teaching method, so the teaching quality may be uneven, leading to the teaching effect may be greatly reduced [23, 24]. Meanwhile, for subjective outcomes (questionnaire surveys), blinding is not feasible, which is also the current limitation. At the same time, the inter-rater reliability of the three teachers’ operation assessment was not verified, which was a limitation. Due to the current limited conditions, the number of trainees is relatively small. Therefore, a lot of curriculum education is still needed to further explore the significance of this study in practice and its long-term effects. The logic of satisfaction evaluation needs to be rethought, this is done by students themselves, and this subjective self-evaluation, only as exploratory rather than confirmatory evidence of educational effects, needs to be further evaluated. In addition, this study assessed performance on simulated models; transfer of these skills to actual patient care requires further investigation.

In conclusion, 3D printing combined with MR Technology can provide a safe and repeatable training system for standardized training doctors for the percutaneous screw internal fixation of carpal scaphoid fractures under the guidance of C-arm. It can not only improve the learning efficiency and confidence of the students, but also enhance the interaction and interest of teaching, which has broad application prospects. This teaching model is worthy of further promotion and application in orthopedic specialist training. Currently, data suggest potential benefits in engagement and simulated performance measures, but no clear advantage in knowledge acquisition or validated practical competence. This requires further exploration.

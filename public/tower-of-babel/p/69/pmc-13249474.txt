Practical Septal Pacing for Atrioventricular Block
Authors: Shun Takeuchi, Rie Aoyama, Takashi Uchiyama, Tomohiko Hayashi, Shinichi Okino, Shigeru Fukuzawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHis bundle pacing (HBP) preserves physiological pacing in atrioventricular block (AVB) but may have unstable thresholds. We evaluated practical septal pacing (PSP) for AVB.Methods and ResultsWe studied 92 AVB patients (41 HBP, 51 PSP). PSP was finalized at the narrowest attainable pQRSd with acceptable pacing parameters. Implant pQRSd was similar, while PSP had lower thresholds (0.5 vs. 1.0V; P<0.01) and remained lower at 18 months; thresholds ≥2.5V occurred less often (2% vs. 27%). No septal perforation occurred, and 1-year survival and heart failure hospitalization were comparable.ConclusionsPSP may offer a simple method with stable thresholds.

Central Figure

Right ventricular pacing (RVP) can cause electrical dyssynchrony and pacing-induced cardiomyopathy.1 Conduction system pacing (CSP), such as His bundle pacing (HBP) and left bundle branch area pacing (LBBAP), preserves physiological activation2 but may be limited by unstable capture thresholds or technical complexity and septal complications.3–5 We developed practical septal pacing (PSP) as an electrocardiogram (ECG)-guided septal pacing strategy that prioritizes narrow paced QRS and stable pacing parameters, while avoiding excessive lead advancement.

Methods

We retrospectively evaluated consecutive patients with atrioventricular block (AVB) who underwent de novo pacemaker implantation with HBP (April 2018–March 2021) or PSP (March 2021–February 2023). The primary electrical endpoints were paced QRS duration (pQRSd) and capture threshold at implantation and during follow up (1, 6, 12, and 18 months). Clinical endpoints were all-cause death and heart failure hospitalization (HFH).

PSP was defined as an ECG-guided septal implantation strategy (Figure A). A ‘practical septal site’ was first identified by pace mapping at the interventricular septum approximately 1.5 cm apical to the tricuspid annulus, aiming for p-QRSd <150 ms with a positive R wave in lead II and a biphasic R wave in lead III. The lead was then advanced intraseptally while monitoring unipolar p-QRSd and impedance. Results

A total of 92 patients (41 HBP, 51 PSP) was analyzed. p-QRSd at implantation was similar between HBP and PSP (102.1±16.0 vs. 105.7±16.7 ms; P=0.29) and remained similar during follow up. PSP had a lower capture threshold at implantation (1.00 [0.75–1.75] vs. 0.50 [0.50–0.75] V; P<0.01) and a higher impedance (768.0±198.2 vs. 514.2±85.3 ohms; P<0.01). The 1-year survival and HFH were comparable (P=0.37, P=0.87; respectively). Thresholds remained lower with PSP through 18 months (repeated-measures ANOVA; P<0.01), and a rise to ≥2.5V occurred in 11 (26.8%) HBP patients vs. 1 (2.0%) PSP patient (P<0.01). LBBAP was identified post hoc in 18 (35.3%) PSP patients; the mean left ventricular activation time was 87.8±13.7 ms. No septal perforation occurred, and procedure time did not differ between groups in a subset analysis (operator A: median 50 vs. 43 min; P=0.21). Lead revision was required in 5 (12.2%) patients in the HBP group only.

Discussion

PSP is conceptually distinct from LBBAP in that intentional left bundle capture is not required as a procedural endpoint.4 Instead, PSP provides a reproducible, ECG-guided approach with stable, long-term thresholds, which may translate into lower battery drain. Because LBBAP was identified after lead fixation rather than targeted during implantation, the PSP cohort reflects a pragmatic septal strategy rather than failed LBBAP attempts.

Study Limitations

This study has limitations. It was a retrospective, single-center, and small-scale study with differing enrollment periods, introducing potential selection bias and shorter follow up for PSP. Therefore, these findings are exploratory.

Conclusions

PSP provided paced QRS narrowing comparable with HBP with superior threshold stability and fewer lead-related reinterventions in AVB.

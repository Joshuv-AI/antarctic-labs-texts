IFPHOR0044 Local Authorities and Warm Spaces During Winter 2022-23; Insights into Decision-Making from Qualitative Interviews with Local Authority Officers in England
Authors: Gethin P Hopkin, Davara Bennett, Rachel Loopstra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCold and damp homes are associated with increased risk of several health conditions and present a serious public health challenge. Responding to concerns about rising fuel poverty in England during winter 2022-23, some local authorities opened or supported “warm spaces” for those struggling to stay warm at home. We aimed to explore how these decisions were reached for winter 2022-23 in a sample of English councils.MethodsWe conducted qualitative semi-structured online interviews during May and June 2023 with six local authority officers involved in decision-making at six different councils, undertaking thematic analysis of transcripts to identify themes. We purposively sampled councils to explore different warm spaces approaches between statistically similar areas.ResultsWe identified three core themes: contextual influences, capacity to respond and reaching decisions. Participants perceived a need and expectation to act quickly to protect residents from external forces beyond local control. According to participants, the existence of assets which could respond as warm spaces in the context of existing strategies influenced decisions. Decision-making often involved prominent senior leadership, considered the availability of and constraints on funding, and delivery relied on working across teams and sectors.ConclusionIf councils decide to establish warm spaces, working with community and the third sector appears important and senior leaders should consider an array of perspectives to inform decision-making. Central government should provide detailed funding information well in advance of delivery, including on constraints and timescales, and address the ongoing challenges of household poverty and energy costs to protect public health.

Predictive analysis of tensile strength in FDM Fabricated PLA wood composite materials
Authors: Mahendrasinh R. Chauhan, Akanksha Mishra, Praveen Oggu, Jasgurpreet Singh, Ripendeep Singh, Koushik V. Prasad, Dhirendra Nath Thatoi, Kamaljit Singh Boparai, Abhijit Bhowmik, Yalew Tamene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A computational modeling strategy was established to predict the tensile strength of polylactic acid (PLA) reinforced with wood particles fabricated using fused deposition modeling (FDM). Three different predictive approaches—Artificial Neural Network (ANN), Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), and Response Surface Methodology (RSM)—were applied to estimate the mechanical response of the printed composites. The experimental program was arranged using a Taguchi L27 orthogonal array, considering five influential printing parameters: layer thickness, infill density, printing speed, nozzle temperature, and raster orientation. The analysis revealed that the highest tensile strength of 24.70 MPa was achieved when the printing conditions were set to 95% infill density, 300 μm layer thickness, and a 0° raster angle. ANOVA indicated that raster angle (38.36%) and print speed (32.55%) were the most influential parameters, while infill density and layer thickness had moderate effects. Comparative evaluation of predictive models showed that ANFIS achieved superior accuracy (R² = 0.9906, RMSE = 0.2821, MAPE = 1.06%), outperforming ANN (R² = 0.9376, RMSE = 0.7286, MAPE = 2.67%) and RSM (R² = 0.7318, RMSE = 1.5101, MAPE = 5.39%). Residual and validation analyses confirmed the robustness of ANFIS in capturing nonlinear parameter interactions. The findings indicate that the combined application of various artificial intelligence–driven modeling approaches can greatly minimize reliance on large-scale experimental trials. This integration improves the reliability of prediction results while offering deeper understanding of process parameters, thereby supporting the efficient optimization of manufacturing conditions for durable and environmentally friendly PLA/wood composite materials.

Introduction

Additive manufacturing (AM), especially the domain of three-dimensional (3D) printing, has significantly reshaped contemporary manufacturing by enabling the fabrication of complex geometries, customized parts, and lightweight components while minimizing material wastage1–3. Among the different AM technologies, fused deposition modeling (FDM) has become widely adopted due to its relatively low operational cost and its ability to process common thermoplastics such as polylactic acid (PLA) and acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS). In recent years, growing interest in sustainability has encouraged the incorporation of renewable fillers like wood fibers into polymer matrices, giving rise to environmentally friendly composites with reduced environmental impact and enhanced biodegradability4–7. These wood–polymer composites (WPCs), especially PLA-based variants, have found applications in green design sectors including furniture manufacturing, decorative structures, and interior components8. Achieving optimal mechanical strength and precise dimensional stability in parts fabricated through FDM largely depends on the selection of appropriate processing parameters. Factors such as printing velocity, nozzle temperature, layer height, and the percentage of infill play a critical role in determining the final performance and structural integrity of the printed components9. Despite progress, issues such as anisotropic mechanical response and weak fiber–matrix adhesion continue to limit performance. To address these challenges, modern research is increasingly adopting artificial intelligence (AI)-driven approaches for modeling and optimization tasks. Techniques such as artificial neural networks, fuzzy logic–based inference systems, and evolutionary optimization algorithms have demonstrated strong capability in handling complex datasets and nonlinear relationships10–13. These data-driven tools help uncover underlying patterns within experimental results, reduce the need for extensive trial-and-error experimentation, and support accurate parameter optimization. Consequently, they facilitate faster design and development of environmentally sustainable and high-performance materials, including PLA/wood-based composites.

Current research on PLA/wood composites fabricated via FDM remains limited, particularly in relation to predicting and improving their tensile and compressive strength. Addressing this knowledge gap, Fountas et al.14 investigated the influence of key fused deposition modeling parameters on the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of PLA composites reinforced with wood flour. Test specimens were fabricated following the ASTM D638-10 standard. The researchers applied a Taguchi orthogonal array experimental design to systematically evaluate the impact of four primary printing variables: layer thickness, nozzle temperature, raster orientation, and printing speed on the resulting mechanical properties of the printed components. The resulting data were examined through ANOVA and contour mapping, which highlighted strong interactions among parameters affecting stress–strain behaviour and UTS. A quadratic regression model was established, demonstrating high predictive capability, while fracture surface analysis and benchmarking against pure PLA validated the findings. These results provided useful recommendations for optimizing FDM settings in bio-based composites. In a related study, Kelleci et al.15 investigated the properties of polypropylene (PP) composites formulated with PLA, wood flour and polyhydroxybutyrate (PHB) as blend components. Their study applied fuzzy logic together with gray relational analysis to identify the most suitable composite formulations by evaluating mechanical, thermal, physical, structural, and morphological characteristics. The findings indicated that adding PLA and wood flour improved the strength of polypropylene blends. Nevertheless, achieving a uniform dispersion of wood flour throughout the polymer matrix continued to be a significant challenge. While the incorporation of PLA and PHB had minimal impact on thermal stability, the presence of wood flour contributed to a noticeable improvement in heat resistance. Both fuzzy and gray MCDM approaches yielded consistent rankings, underscoring the robustness of the optimization framework.

Morvayová et al.16 introduced a three-dimensional thermomechanical simulation framework to refine FDM of PLA/wood biocomposites. Their approach provided accurate insights into thermal fields, residual stresses, shape deviations, and defect generation, offering a pathway to tailor processing strategies for sustainable materials. Validation through experiments confirmed the reliability of the model, showing less than 10% deviation in dimensional accuracy and effective prediction of defects. This work strengthens the knowledge base for biocomposite-oriented FDM and supports its role in eco-friendly manufacturing. In a comparable investigation, Estakhrianhaghighi et al.17 examined the effect of incorporating wood fibers into a polylactic acid (PLA) matrix to enhance both structural behavior and environmental sustainability. The fiber content was varied between 2.5% and 15%, and the results demonstrated substantial improvements in material properties. Specifically, the flexural modulus rose by approximately 60%, stiffness improved by about 72%, tensile strength increased by nearly 39%, and elongation at break improved by around 21%. Additionally, the composite exhibited a density reduction of more than 5%, indicating the potential for developing lightweight and mechanically robust bio-based materials. The introduction of the “Isoflex” cellular beam architecture significantly enhanced mechanical performance, achieving up to 130% improvement in rigidity and a 70% boost in bending behavior relative to neat PLA. These results highlight the impending of composites for high-performance AM applications. Zandi et al.18 investigated the flexural behavior of Timber Fill, a wood-fiber–reinforced PLA composite fabricated through FDM. The process parameters were optimized using an L27 Taguchi design to identify their influence on bending strength. The study indicated that layer height played the dominant role in determining flexural performance, whereas nozzle diameter and infill density contributed relatively less. Under the optimal parameter combination, the composite reached a maximum flexural strength of 47.26 MPa. However, incorporating wood fibers disrupted the uniform PLA matrix, leading to void formation that restricted further enhancement of mechanical strength. In comparison with injection-molded specimens, the FDM-fabricated parts exhibited a lower Young’s modulus (approximately 25% reduction), but they showed greater flexibility and improved resistance to deformation, highlighting the unique mechanical response associated with additively manufactured composites.

The present study addresses this research gap by developing a comprehensive hybrid framework that integrates the Taguchi design of experiments with advanced predictive modeling approaches, namely Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), and Response Surface Methodology (RSM). The primary novelty of this work lies in the systematic and comparative evaluation of these three modeling techniques under identical experimental conditions, ensuring a fair and consistent performance assessment. Unlike previous studies, which often focus on a single modeling approach or different datasets, this research provides a unified benchmarking platform for evaluating prediction accuracy. Furthermore, this study specifically focuses on the tensile behavior of PLA–wood composites fabricated via fused deposition modeling (FDM), a relatively underexplored material system in predictive modeling studies. The incorporation of wood particles introduces additional complexities such as anisotropy and interfacial bonding challenges, making accurate prediction more critical and scientifically valuable. In addition, the integration of the Taguchi experimental design with AI-based models represents a key contribution, enabling efficient data generation while minimizing experimental effort. This combined approach enhances model reliability, reduces computational and experimental cost, and improves the robustness of process optimization. Through this integrated methodology, the study not only advances predictive modeling capabilities but also provides practical insights for optimizing sustainable additive manufacturing processes involving bio-composite materials.

Materials and methods

Fabrication of specimens and study design

The test samples were fabricated using fused deposition modeling (FDM) using a commercially available wood–PLA composite filament. The filament consisted of PLA reinforced with approximately 20 wt% wood particles having irregular morphology and an average particle size ranging from 70 to 150 μm, which significantly influences interfacial bonding and anisotropic mechanical behavior. The filament was procured from a commercial supplier (e.g., eSun Wood PLA or equivalent grade) and manufactured through melt blending and extrusion to ensure uniform dispersion of wood particles within the PLA matrix. Printing was performed using a Creality K1C 3D printer, which supports 1.75 mm diameter filaments and operates at extrusion temperatures up to 300 °C. The printer is equipped with a heated build platform capable of reaching 180 °C to improve layer adhesion and can achieve printing speeds up to 600 mm/s with a dimensional accuracy of approximately 0.1 mm, enabling the fabrication of components with consistent quality. The manufacturing workflow began with the development of three-dimensional specimen models using SolidWorks CAD software (Dassault Systèmes SolidWorks Corp., SolidWorks 2023, USA; https://www.solidworks.com/), which were subsequently exported in Standard Tessellation Language (STL) format. The STL files were then processed using Creality Print slicing software (Version 5.0, Creality, China; https://www.creality.com/pages/download-software) to generate machine-readable G-code containing instructions related to toolpath generation, extrusion rate, and temperature settings. The printer subsequently executed the programmed instructions by depositing molten material layer by layer to fabricate the desired geometry. The ranges of process parameters were selected based on previous literature, pilot experimental observations, machine operating limitations, and the processing characteristics of PLA/wood composite filament. The infill density range (35–95%) was chosen to evaluate the influence of internal material distribution on tensile behavior. Layer thickness values between 100 and 300 μm were selected to investigate interlayer bonding characteristics while maintaining stable extrusion. Printing speed was varied from 40 to 90 mm/s to examine the balance between printing efficiency and bonding quality. The nozzle temperature range of 200–220 °C was selected according to the recommended processing temperature of PLA/wood filament to ensure proper melting without thermal degradation. Raster orientations of 0°, 45°, and 90° were considered to analyze the anisotropic behavior of FDM-printed composites under tensile loading. These parameter ranges ensured stable printing conditions and sufficient variation for meaningful analysis of tensile strength.

Tensile strength characterization

The tensile properties of the wood-filled PLA composite were evaluated to assess its ability to withstand uniaxial tensile loading. Test specimens were manufactured using the FDM process to ensure consistency in geometry and surface finish. The design of the specimens followed the ISO 527-Type 1 A standard, which provides internationally recognized guidelines for tensile testing of polymeric materials. Adopting this standard ensured that the results were reliable, reproducible, and comparable with findings from other studies. Each specimen was carefully prepared and mounted in the grips of the testing machine to avoid misalignment and slippage, thereby enabling uniform distribution of the applied force across the gauge section.

Tensile properties were evaluated using a Shimadzu AG-X universal testing machine equipped with a 50 kN load frame. The test was carried out at a crosshead speed of 1 mm/s, following the requirements specified in the relevant ISO standard. The instrument’s measurement accuracy of ± 0.1% ensured reliable recording of the stress–strain response during the test. Analysis of the resulting stress–strain curves provided insights into the material’s elastic response, plastic deformation, and fracture behaviour. The tensile strength (TS) of the composite was determined as the maximum stress sustained by the specimen before fracture. Beyond strength evaluation, the results also revealed important information about the stiffness, ductility, and overall deformation pattern of the wood-filled PLA composite, contributing to a deeper understanding of its structural reliability in practical applications. Predictive modeling techniques

Adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system

The Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System is a hybrid computational framework that integrates the learning capability of artificial neural networks with the reasoning mechanism of fuzzy logic systems. This combination enables ANFIS to effectively model highly nonlinear relationships and handle uncertainties in complex datasets, making it a powerful tool for prediction and optimization in advanced engineering applications20–24. In the present study, a Sugeno-type fuzzy inference system was employed to establish the relationship between FDM process parameters and tensile strength. The ANFIS model was configured with five input variables—namely infill density, layer thickness, printing speed, nozzle temperature, and raster orientation—and one output variable corresponding to tensile strength. Each input parameter was assigned three Gaussian membership functions, selected for their smooth transition characteristics and superior approximation capability. This configuration resulted in a total of 243 fuzzy if–then rules (3⁵) forming the rule base structure.

Structurally, the ANFIS model consists of a five-layer feedforward architecture, where each layer performs a distinct computational function: (i) fuzzification of crisp inputs into membership values, (ii) evaluation of rule firing strengths, (iii) normalization of firing strengths, (iv) computation of rule outputs using Sugeno-type linear functions, and (v) aggregation of all rule outputs to produce a single crisp prediction. This layered framework enables the model to capture complex interactions among input variables while retaining interpretability through its rule-based structure. The training of the ANFIS model was carried out using a hybrid learning algorithm, which combines least squares estimation for optimizing consequent parameters and gradient descent for tuning premise parameters. The initial fuzzy inference system was generated using a grid partitioning approach, and the model was trained for 100 epochs to achieve convergence with minimal prediction error. During training, the model parameters were iteratively updated to minimize the deviation between predicted and experimental tensile strength values. This structured configuration allows ANFIS to efficiently learn nonlinear dependencies among process variables while maintaining high prediction accuracy and robustness. Owing to its ability to integrate adaptability, interpretability, and computational efficiency, ANFIS serves as a reliable and effective tool for predictive modeling in FDM-based composite manufacturing. Artificial neural network

Artificial Neural Networks are powerful data-driven modeling tools inspired by the functional behavior of the human brain, capable of capturing complex nonlinear relationships within datasets25–30. In this study, a feedforward backpropagation neural network was developed to predict the tensile strength of FDM-fabricated PLA/wood composites based on key process parameters. The ANN architecture comprised five input neurons corresponding to infill density, layer thickness, printing speed, nozzle temperature, and raster orientation, followed by three hidden layers containing 10, 6, and 5 neurons, respectively, and a single output neuron representing tensile strength. This 5-(10 + 6+5)-1 configuration was selected after multiple trial iterations to achieve an optimal balance between prediction accuracy and computational efficiency.

The network was trained using the Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation algorithm, which is well-known for its fast convergence and suitability for nonlinear regression problems. A total of 1000 training epochs were employed, with a learning rate of 0.01, determined through preliminary tuning to ensure stable and efficient convergence. The dataset obtained from experimental trials was divided into 70% for training and 30% for testing, enabling the evaluation of the model’s generalization capability. To introduce nonlinearity and enhance learning capability, the hidden layers utilized a combination of logarithmic sigmoid (logsig) and tangent sigmoid (tansig) activation functions, while a linear activation function (purelin) was used in the output layer to produce continuous predictions. During training, the network weights and biases were iteratively updated to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and experimental values. Additionally, early stopping criteria based on validation performance were implemented to prevent overfitting and ensure model robustness. Through this structured configuration and training strategy, the ANN model effectively learned the complex interactions among process parameters and demonstrated strong predictive capability. The developed model provides a reliable and efficient tool for estimating tensile strength in FDM-based composite manufacturing applications. Response surface methodology

Response Surface Methodology is a statistical and mathematical optimization technique widely used to model, analyze, and optimize engineering processes in which the response is influenced by multiple input variables31. The primary objective of RSM is to establish an empirical relationship between the process parameters and the corresponding response through regression-based mathematical models. By analyzing both the individual and interaction effects of the process variables, RSM enables the identification of optimal operating conditions and facilitates prediction of responses for untested parameter combinations32. In the present study, RSM was employed to investigate the influence of FDM process parameters on the tensile strength of PLA/wood composite specimens. The methodology integrates design of experiments, regression analysis, and optimization techniques to systematically evaluate the relationship between the selected input variables and the tensile strength response. The experimental data obtained from the Taguchi L27 orthogonal array were utilized to develop a second-order polynomial regression model capable of representing linear effects, quadratic effects, and interaction effects among the process parameters.

The RSM modeling procedure involved three major stages: experimental design, mathematical model development, and response surface analysis33. Initially, experimental trials were conducted by systematically varying the selected process parameters, namely infill density, layer thickness, printing speed, nozzle temperature, and raster orientation. Subsequently, regression analysis was performed to develop a predictive polynomial equation describing the relationship between the input parameters and tensile strength. The adequacy and reliability of the developed model were statistically evaluated using ANOVA, coefficient of determination (R²), adjusted coefficient of determination (Adjusted R²), and residual analysis. Furthermore, response surface plots and contour plots were analyzed to understand the interaction behavior among the process parameters and to identify the optimal parameter combination for maximizing tensile strength. Through its structured analytical framework, RSM provides valuable insights into process behavior while reducing the number of required experimental trials. Therefore, it serves as an effective and reliable approach for process optimization and predictive analysis in additive manufacturing applications involving PLA/wood composite materials. Results and discussion

Influence of FDM process variables on tensile strength

The influence of different printing parameters on tensile strength was systematically analyzed using main effect plots derived from the Taguchi design (Fig. 7). While the experimental trends provide useful insights, a deeper understanding can be achieved by examining the underlying material behavior and interfacial mechanics governing the performance of PLA/wood composites. Among the investigated parameters, infill density exhibited a strong positive effect on tensile strength. This behavior can be attributed to the reduction in internal voids and increased load-bearing cross-sectional area at higher infill levels. From a mechanics perspective, higher infill density improves stress distribution and minimizes stress concentration sites, thereby enhancing the overall structural integrity of the printed specimen. Conversely, lower infill densities introduce porosity, which acts as crack initiation sites under tensile loading.

The effect of layer thickness demonstrated a nonlinear trend. At lower layer thicknesses, improved interlayer bonding is generally expected due to better fusion between adjacent layers. However, in the present study, the highest tensile strength was observed at 300 μm. This can be explained by the presence of wood particles within the PLA matrix, which alter the melt flow and bonding behavior. Thicker layers may promote improved particle encapsulation and reduce thermal degradation, leading to enhanced interfacial adhesion between the polymer matrix and wood fillers.

The influence of printing speed is closely related to the thermal history and bonding quality of deposited filaments. Lower printing speeds allow sufficient time for heat transfer and molecular diffusion across layers, resulting in stronger interlayer adhesion. At higher speeds, insufficient bonding time leads to weak interfacial fusion and increased porosity, thereby reducing tensile strength. This effect is further amplified in wood-filled composites, where the presence of particles restricts polymer chain mobility and makes proper fusion more critical.

Nozzle temperature plays a key role in determining melt viscosity and bonding characteristics. Although its overall contribution was relatively moderate, the observed trend suggests that excessively high temperatures may lead to thermal degradation of the PLA matrix or wood particles, weakening the interface. On the other hand, insufficient temperature may result in poor melting and inadequate bonding. Thus, an optimal temperature range is essential to balance viscosity, flowability, and interlayer diffusion.

The most significant parameter, raster orientation, directly governs the alignment of deposited filaments relative to the loading direction. At a 0° raster angle, the filaments are aligned parallel to the tensile load, enabling efficient stress transfer along continuous material paths and resulting in higher strength. In contrast, at 90°, the load is primarily supported by weaker interlayer bonds rather than continuous filaments, leading to premature failure. This anisotropic behavior is further influenced by the presence of wood particles, which can disrupt filament continuity and act as stress concentrators when poorly aligned.

From a material perspective, the incorporation of wood particles introduces both beneficial and limiting effects. While they enhance stiffness and sustainability, they also increase the likelihood of interfacial debonding and porosity formation due to poor compatibility with the PLA matrix. These microstructural discontinuities act as crack initiation and propagation sites under tensile loading. Therefore, the overall tensile performance is governed by a balance between improved stiffness and reduced interfacial integrity. The optimal parameter combination (95% ID, 300 μm LT, 40 mm/s PS, 200 °C NT, and 0° RA) reflects a condition where interlayer bonding, filament continuity, and porosity minimization are simultaneously optimized. The experimentally obtained tensile strength of 24.70 MPa under these conditions confirms the effectiveness of parameter optimization in enhancing the mechanical performance of FDM-fabricated PLA/wood composites.

The ANOVA results presented in Table 3; Fig. 8 clearly indicate that tensile strength in FDM-fabricated PLA/wood composite specimens is predominantly influenced by parameters associated with filament orientation and deposition quality. Printing speed emerged as the second most influential parameter, contributing 32.55% with an F-value of 353.68 and a p-value below 0.001. The significant influence of printing speed can be attributed to its direct effect on interlayer bonding quality and thermal diffusion between deposited layers. Lower printing speeds provide sufficient time for proper fusion and molecular diffusion, whereas excessively high speeds may lead to weak interfacial bonding, insufficient fusion, and void formation, thereby reducing tensile performance.

Infill density also demonstrated a considerable effect on tensile strength, contributing 18.05% with an F-value of 196.42. Higher infill densities increase the effective load-bearing cross-sectional area and reduce internal porosity, resulting in improved structural integrity and enhanced tensile resistance. However, compared to raster orientation and printing speed, its influence was relatively moderate. Layer thickness contributed 7.54% with an F-value of 74.91, indicating that thinner layers generally promote better interlayer adhesion due to improved fusion between adjacent deposited layers. Nevertheless, in the present study, the optimized layer thickness provided balanced deposition quality and mechanical performance.

Nozzle temperature showed the lowest contribution among the investigated parameters, accounting for only 2.51% with an F-value of 21.12. Although nozzle temperature plays an important role in controlling melt viscosity and extrusion behavior, the comparatively low contribution suggests that the selected temperature range was sufficient to maintain proper material flow and bonding throughout the experiments. The low p-value (0.002) still confirms its statistical significance on tensile strength.

The relatively small error contribution demonstrates good experimental consistency and reliability of the developed statistical model. Furthermore, the high coefficient of determination (R² = 98.88%) confirms that the selected process parameters effectively explain the variation observed in tensile strength. Results of ANFIS, ANN and RSM

The ANFIS integrates the reasoning capability of fuzzy logic with the learning ability of artificial neural networks, enabling accurate prediction of tensile strength. In this study, the model was developed using a hybrid training strategy. The antecedent (premise) parameters were adjusted through a gradient descent optimization process, while the consequent parameters were determined using the least squares estimation method to enhance prediction accuracy. A five-layered ANFIS structure was implemented, with the input variables partitioned into Gaussian membership functions, as they provided smoother transitions and superior prediction accuracy compared to triangular and trapezoidal counterparts. The number of membership functions was carefully chosen to avoid overfitting while maintaining precision in the model output. Extensive training trials determined that the hybrid optimization strategy resulted in faster convergence with minimized error. Conclusion

This study presents an integrated strategy that blends experimental investigation with computational modeling to accurately predict the tensile performance of FDM-fabricated PLA/wood composite materials. Five important printing variables—raster orientation, printing velocity, infill percentage, layer height, and nozzle temperature—were systematically evaluated to understand their influence on the mechanical characteristics of the composite. Among these parameters, raster orientation showed the strongest effect, contributing 38.36% to the observed variation in tensile strength, followed by printing velocity with a contribution of 32.55%. The level of infill was also crucial, as the highest strength was obtained when the infill density reached 95%. Experimental validation indicated that the optimal parameter configuration—95% infill density, 300 μm layer thickness, 40 mm/s print speed, 200 °C nozzle temperature, and a 0° raster angle—yielded the highest tensile strength of 24.70 MPa. These findings emphasize that careful adjustment of processing parameters is essential for enhancing interlayer adhesion, structural stability, and the load-bearing capacity of FDM-manufactured bio-composites. Furthermore, examination of residual plots demonstrated that the regression models adhered to statistical assumptions such as normal distribution, constant variance, and independence of errors, confirming the reliability and robustness of the developed predictive framework.

A comparative assessment of ANFIS, ANN, and RSM models underscored the superior predictive capability of ANFIS, which achieved the highest performance metrics (R² = 0.9906, RMSE = 0.2821, MAPE = 1.06%). ANN followed with moderate accuracy (R² = 0.9376, RMSE = 0.7286), while RSM, though providing interpretability through polynomial regression, displayed lower predictive reliability (R² = 0.7318, RMSE = 1.5101). These results validate ANFIS as the most efficient and reliable model for capturing nonlinear dependencies and parameter interactions in FDM processes. From a sustainability perspective, the findings highlight how AI-driven predictive modeling minimizes experimental trials, reduces material and energy consumption, and accelerates the optimization of eco-friendly composites. This work advances the understanding of intelligent modeling in additive manufacturing and offers a dependable framework for parameter optimization of PLA/wood composites. Future research can extend this methodology to hybrid or recycled bio-fillers, employ multi-objective optimization for strength–cost–sustainability trade-offs, and integrate adaptive feedback mechanisms for real-time process control in sustainable additive manufacturing.

Convergent Total Synthesis of Caribbean Ciguatoxin C‑CTX1 and Its C3-Epimer
Authors: Makoto Sasaki, Atsushi Umehara, Kohtaro Sugahara, Masayuki Satake, Takeshi Tsumuraya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Caribbean ciguatoxin C-CTX1 is a highly potent ladder-shaped polycyclic ether toxin responsible for ciguatera fish poisoning and has recently been detected beyond the Caribbean Sea region. Because of its extreme structural complexity and scarcity from natural sources, its structure and biological properties have not been fully validated by chemical means. We report here the first total synthesis of C-CTX1 and its naturally occurring C3-epimer through a convergent fragment-coupling strategy joining the ABCDE- and HIJKLMN-ring units, featuring late-stage radical cyclization and ring-closing metathesis to construct the central FG-rings. The synthesis provides definitive structural confirmation and practical milligram-scale access to both epimer. Comparative biological evaluation revealed a significant difference in relative neurotoxicity.

Ciguatoxins are ladder-shaped marine polycyclic ethers responsible for ciguatera fish poisoning. − Caribbean ciguatoxin C-CTX1 (1, Figure ) is a principal toxin in the Caribbean region and has recently been implicated in Atlantic outbreaks. − C-CTX1 (1) comprises 14 fused ether rings and over 30 stereogenic centers. , Related congeners include C-CTX5 (2), a proposed biosynthetic precursor, , and putative C3-epimer 3 detected by LC–HRMS analysis; neither of which have been validated by synthesis, and authentic materials are unavailable. Despite advances in Pacific ciguatoxin synthesis, − the larger size and distinct ring connectivity of Caribbean congeners have precluded total synthesis. We previously established convergent routes to the ABCDE- and HIJKLMN-ring fragments of 1 and developed a radical cyclization–ring-closing metathesis (RCM) strategy for fused ether construction. Herein, we report the first highly convergent total synthesis of C-CTX1 (1) and its C3-epimer 3, enabling structural confirmation and the first biological comparison.

With β-alkoxy acrylate 23 in hand, the central FG-ring system was constructed via sequential radical cyclization and RCM (Scheme ). Radical cyclization of 23 under our established conditions stereoselectively forged the seven-membered G-ring. The product was converted to methyl ester 24, and the newly formed C26 stereocenter was assigned by ROE analysis. Installation of the terminal olefin furnished tetraene 25, which underwent RCM with the first-generation Grubbs catalyst to form the nine-membered F-ring, completing the 14-ring ladder-shaped polycyclic ether framework 26. Consistent with reported ciguatoxin spectra, , the F-ring 1H and 13C NMR signals of 26 exhibited line broadening at ambient temperature, characteristic of slow conformational exchange in fused nine-membered ethers and supporting successful F-ring formation.

Total Synthesis of C-CTX1 (1)

Global deprotection of 26 by sequential removal of NAP groups with DDQ and acetonide cleavage with TFA furnished synthetic C-CTX1 (1), completing the total synthesis. Comprehensive NMR analysis (HSQC and HSQC-TOCSY) and comparison with reported data for the natural toxin confirmed the structure of C-CTX1 (1).

The epimeric ABCDE-ring fragment 3-epi-4 , enabled parallel synthesis of 3-epi-C-CTX1 (3) through the same convergent sequence (Scheme ). Epimer 3 showed characteristic 1H and 13C NMR differences in the A-ring region consistent with C3 inversion. Although spectroscopic data for the natural C3-epimer remain incomplete, synthetic 3 provides a reference standard for future structural and biological studies.

Synthetic C-CTX1 (1) and its C3-epimer 3 were evaluated using a Neuro-2a cell-based assay. Neither compound was cytotoxic in the absence of ouabain and veratridine (O/V−), whereas both exhibited concentration-dependent cytotoxicity under O/V+, consistent with activation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Under identical conditions, 3 displayed higher apparent neurotoxicity than 1. While semiquantitative, these results reveal a clear difference in relative activity between the two epimers.

In conclusion, we achieved a convergent total synthesis of Caribbean ciguatoxin C-CTX1 (1) and its C3-epimer 3 via esterification-based fragment coupling followed by sequential radical G-ring cyclization and RCM to construct the adjacent F-ring, thereby assembling the 14-ring ladder framework. The synthesis confirms the proposed structure of C-CTX1 (1) and provides milligram-scale access to both epimers. Direct biological comparison revealed a clear difference in apparent neurotoxicity, underscoring the influence of C3 stereochemistry on activity. This radical cyclization–RCM strategy provides a scalable blueprint for accessing complex marine ladder-shaped polycyclic ethers.

The result prediction of fluorescence in situ hybridization for breast cancer patients based on machine learning and deep learning models: a multicenter study
Authors: Cong Jiang, Dong Chen, Xiao Yu, Shentao Zhang, Yuting Xiu, Ningbin Luo, Jingjing Wu, Xuefang Zhang, Man Chen, Dechun Yang, Ziyu Zhu, Yuanxi Huang, Shipeng Ning, Shicong Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo develop and validate a multicenter ultrasound-based predictive model for fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) results in HER2 (2+) breast cancer patients, aiming to provide a convenient and cost-effective tool to support clinical decision-making.Materials and methodsIn this retrospective multicenter study, 5,888 breast cancer patients from six institutions were included. Radiomics features were extracted from ultrasound images using PyRadiomics, and deep learning features were obtained using a Vision Transformer (ViT). Feature selection was performed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression. Multiple machine learning models were developed, and their performance was evaluated with the area under the curve (AUC). The DeLong test was used for model comparison.ResultsThe proportion of FISH-positive cases ranged from 9.7% to 20.0% across the six centers. The fusion model combining ViT and radiomics signatures consistently outperformed the individual models in the training, test, and all external validation cohorts. The AUCs of the fusion model were 0.887 in the training cohort, 0.799 in the test cohort, and 0.763, 0.796, 0.734, and 0.632 in the four external validation cohorts, respectively (all P < 0.05).ConclusionThe proposed ultrasound-based fusion model enables accurate prediction of FISH assay results in HER2 (2+) breast cancer patients and may serve as a reliable decision-support tool to reduce unnecessary FISH testing in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-026-01015-x.

Introduction

Breast cancer is one of the most common cancers and the leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide [1]. Based on hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining of the tissue and histopathological analysis by immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis, the breast cancer is classified into three primary subtypes (HR+/HER2-, HR-/HER2+, and triple-negative breast cancer) [2]. The fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) is required to confirm HER2 status in cases with IHC score 2+ [3]. If the result of FISH showed that the patient has gene amplification, the targeted therapy (anti HER2 therapy) is highly recommended. Therefore, the FISH test is necessary for those special patients. However, the FISH assays are both time-intensive and expensive, highlighting the need for a rapid and accessible tool to predict FISH results.

Previous studies have developed a conventional radiomics models to predict HER2 expression status in breast cancer patients [4, 5]. However, these models primarily rely on MRI images and exhibit limited performance. Compared with MRI, ultrasound (US) is widely available and practical in clinical settings. To date, no study has established a predictive model for FISH outcomes based on US imaging.

Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) algorithms, which leverage large datasets, are increasingly utilized to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of tumor diagnosis [6]. Compared with radiomics, deep learning adopts an automated, end-to-end framework for imaging feature extraction. This strategy bypasses handcrafted feature engineering, supports the learning of complex semantic features, and alleviates the burden of manual processing [7]. As a DL architecture, Vision Transformer (ViT) models have recently gained increasing attention [8, 9].Be different with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), it enables the model to attain global features [10], and improve the generalizability of model in noisy environments [11], that could help analyze the ultrasound images. Nevertheless, no existing study has applied ML algorithms or ViT frameworks to predict FISH assay results in breast cancer.

Therefore, this study aims to develop and validate a predictive model for the prediction of FISH assay outcome, using US images, through machine learning and deep learning techniques. Additionally, a web application was developed to facilitate clinical implementation of the model.

Patients and methods

Patients

A total of 5,888 breast cancer patients were retrospectively enrolled into this study. From Jan 2021 to Jun 2024, of 3863 patients were from The Third Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University, and is randomly divided into the train set and the test set in a ratio of 7:3. The external validation cohort 1 (EV1), consisting of 831 patients from the Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital from Jan 2021 to Dec 2024. The external validation cohort 2 (EV2), consisting of 793 patients from the Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital from Jan 2015 to Dec 2022. The external validation cohort 3 (EV3), consisting of 161 patients from Yueyang Centeral Hospital and 65 patients from Ezhou Centeral Hospital from Jan 2021 to Oct 2024. The external validation cohort 4 (EV4), consisting of 175 patients from The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University from Jan 2021 to Feb 2024.

The inclusion criteria: (1) pathologically diagnosed as breast cancer; (2) the IHC result of HER2 was IHC2 + and conducted FISH assay. The exclusion criteria: (1) without US images before been diagnosed as breast cancer; (2) the result of FISH assay is missed or fail to evaluate; (3) US images with no distinct tumor.

This study was approved by the ethics committees of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (KY201944), Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital, Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University, Yueyang centeral hospital and Ezhou centeral hospital. It adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. All patients signed informed consent prior to treatment.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization assay

In an accredited laboratory, HER2 status assessed by ISH according to the ASCO/CAP guidelines [3] is interpreted as follows: a HER2/CEP17 ratio ≥ 2.0 or an average HER2 gene copy number ≥ 6 indicates HER2 positivity. A HER2/CEP17 ratio < 2.0 with an average HER2 gene copy number of 4–6 is considered equivocal; in such cases, the pathologist is advised to increase the number of counted tumor cells and repeat the ISH assessment, or to interpret the result in conjunction with immunohistochemistry (IHC) findings.

For cases with a HER2/CEP17 ratio ≥ 2.0 but an average HER2 copy number per cell < 4.0, whether these should be classified as ISH-positive remains controversial. It is recommended that such cases be annotated in the report to highlight the existing controversy, and clinicians should be advised to consider the IHC results and to communicate appropriately with the patient.

Image acquisition and evaluation

Images of specific patients are limited to the lesion side and, in the case of bilateral lesions, include the primary lesion side (the side with the maximum tumor diameter). The analyzed images are submitted in JPEG or PNG format. All the imaging parameters were standardized. The region of interest (ROI) was manually delineated in a blinded manner by three breast surgeons (5 year’ experience), using ITK-SNAP software (www.itksnap.org), and been checked by S.T.Z (Ultrasound specialist with 5 years of experience) and D.C (Ultrasound specialist with 10 years of experience). The section with the largest tumor area was selected as input to the model.

Deep learning and radiomics feature extraction and feature dimension reduction

For deep learning features, the ROI was cropped based on the largest cross-section. ViT algorithm was adopted as the base model which pre-trained on ImageNet. Images were fed into the model at the original acquisition resolution; no resampling or resizing was performed. Features were extracted from the ‘to_latent’ layer. Of 1000 ViT features were extracted based on the model.

For radiomics features, ‘Pyradiomics’ package was directly used, and of 107 Radiomics features were extracted. Including 18 first-order statistics, 14 shape features and 75 texture features.

In order to reduce computational complexity and improve model performance, standard score (Z-score) is been calculated. Then spearman analysis was been used to preliminary select features. Lasso regression was been used to further select features (Figure S1).

Machine learning models development and performance evaluation

Six familiar ML models, including eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGB) [12], Logistic regression (LR), Decision Tree (DT) [13], Random Forest (RF) [14], Support Vector Machine (SVM) [15] and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) [16], were developed to predict the result of FISH using ViT or Radiomics features. The GridSearch based on 10-fold cross validation was applied to select the best Hyperparameters, details were shown in Supplementary file. The Area under curve (AUC), Brier score and area under decision curve (AUDC) were applied to evaluate the performance of ML models.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were conducted by Python software (version 3.9.7) and R software (version 4.3.2). The performance of different ML models was compared by Delong Test. If the score of a case exceeds a given critical point, the case is considered HER2-positive. This cutoff value was determined by maximizing Youden’s index on the ROC curve. Web APP will classify the case as FISH positive if the predicting value is bigger than the cutoff value. All statistics were two sided and P-values less than 0.05 indicate significant differences.

Results

The population and study design

A total of 5888 breast cancer patients who accept FISH detection were included into this study. Of 3863 patients were from Yunnan Cancer Hospital (train and test sets) with a proportion of 20.0% FISH (+). Of 832 patients were from Guangxi Cancer Hospital (EV1 set) with a proportion of 12.9% FISH (+). Of 793 patients were from Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital (EV2) with a proportion of 12.4% FISH (+). Of 226 patients were from Yueyang and Ezhou Centeral Hospitals (EV3) with a proportion of 17.7% FISH (+). Of 175 patients were from The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (EV4) with a proportion of 9.7% FISH (+). The details showed in Table S1.

This study is conducted followed by six steps, including segmentation, feature extraction, feature selection, machine learning models development, evaluation and explanation, the prediction of FISH results based on R/V signatures and external validation of 5 different hospitals (Fig. 1).

ML models performance evaluation, validation and interpretability based on Radiomics features

Of 18 first-order statistics, 14 shape features and 75 texture features were extracted. Finally, a total of nine Radiomics features (original_firstorder_10Percentile, original_firstorder_Skewness, original_firstorder_TotalEnergy, original_glszm_GrayLevelNonUniformity, original_glszm_LowGrayLevelZoneEmphasis, original_shape_Elongation, original_shape_LeastAxisLength, original_shape_Sphericity, original_shape_VoxelVolume) were applied in six ML models to predict the result of FISH detection. Ten-fold cross validation was preliminarily used to evaluate the model performance. The learning curves presented that all six models fitted well (Figure S4).

In train set, the XGB model presented the biggest AUC value (0.722) than others models (Figure S5A), while similar result (0.622) was shown in test set (Figure S5B) (P < 0.05, Table S2). All models also showed a good discrimination for FISH result prediction (Figure S6). Each model presented a good consistency with the ideal curve, but the XGB model demonstrated the smallest Brier score (0.145, 0.156) in train (Figure S5C) and test (Figure S5D) sets, which demonstrated the better accuracy of this model. In clinical utility, the XGB model also demonstrated the biggest AUDC (0.031, 0.024), no matter in train (Figure S5E) or test set (Figure S5F).

Therefore, XGB model was selected as the optimal Radiomics feature-based model. In four external validation cohorts, the XGB model also showed good AUC values (0.6, 0.635, 0.591, 0.567, respectively; Figure S7A-D), calibration (Breier score: 0.118, 0.110, 0.148, 0.104; respectively; Figure S7E-F), discrimination (Figure S7I-L) and AUDC values (0.009, 0.009, 0.018, 0.005, respectively; Figure S7M-P).

To make the XGB model and Radiomics features easier to be understood, SHAP explanation framework was applied. The result presented that original_shape_Elongation was the most important feature (Figure S8A). The bigger original_shape_Elongation value is, the smaller impact on FISH (+) (Figure S8B). The combination of different Radiomics features influenced the result of FISH assay. Two typical patients were selected to further explain the prediction function of XGB model. A woman with FISH (-) presented a low SHAP value of -3.41 (Figure S8C) and get a low prediction score of 0.032. A woman with FISH (+) presented a high SHAP value of 0.21 (Figure S8D) and get a high prediction score of 0.551.

To make the VR model easier to be used, a Web APP was been designed. After entering the respective ViT and Radiomics features, the relevant Signatures will be output. Then Signatures will be input into the trained nomogram, a probability of FISH positive will be presented. The Web APP is available online (https://greenmood.shinyapps.io/ModelCompareNomo).

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated the feasibility of predicting FISH assay results using ultrasound images. The ViT model showed overall good discrimination across multiple cohorts, suggesting that transformer-based feature learning can capture clinically relevant ultrasound patterns associated with FISH status. Notably, the fusion model (VR Signature), which integrates ViT and Radiomics-based signatures, generally achieved comparable or improved performance compared with the ViT model alone. In addition, SHAP-based interpretation of the XGB model provided insights into feature contributions, enhancing transparency and supporting clinical interpretability of the proposed framework.

The application of trastuzumab and pertuzumab significantly improved the survival of HER2 positive breast cancer [17]. But the initial confirmation of HER2 status relies on the IHC assays, which is time-consuming. For those patients with HER2 (2+), FISH has been approved by FDA for the further assessed. It is reported that the proportion of FISH (+) was up to 24% in HER2 (2+) breast cancer patients [18]. In this multicenter study, the FISH (+) rate was from 9.7% to 20.0%. Even though the FISH assay is important, but its expensive price and time-consuming process also bring a lot of inconvenience to patients. Therefore, developing a convenient and simple tool for the FISH result prediction is necessary. Haili Wang et al. developed a MRI-based radiomics model to predict HER-2 2 + status of breast cancer [19]. However, it is only a single-center study with a small sample of 72 patients, whose credibility is very low. Another small sample study explored the performance of ultrasound-based radiomics model in predicting HER2 status of breast cancer patients [19]. Similarly, the problem of single-center data was been presented again, and too many features increase the complexity of the model. This multi-center studies also used ultrasound-based radiomics features to predict FISH result, which got an AUC value of 0.722 in train set.

In fact, many radiomics models, which based on different medical images, had been used to predict gene mutations in breast cancer [20]. However, most of them extracted features from MRI. Besides, the predicted ability showed an unstable AUC from 0.5 to 0.89. To improve the predictive performance, deep learning is gradually been applied in breast cancer. Paul Gamble et al. used DL to predict ER/PR/HER2 status, and AUCs are 0.86, 0.75 and 0.60 in slide level, respectively [21]. Meng Jiang et al. developed a deep learning radiomic nomogram for the prediction of pCR, which also showed a good predictive ability [22]. Recently, a powerful deep learning model of Vision Transformer (ViT) is gradually been applied in breast cancer, and presented a high sensitivity of 0.9 in screening the triple-negative breast cancer [23]. However, no study applied ViT in predicting the result of FISH assay in breast cancer. This multicenter study firstly used this framework to extracted ViT features, and developed six ML models to predict FISH assay result. That XGB model demonstrated powerful discrimination (AUC:0.865, 0.790, 0.754, 0.777, 0.728, 0.636, respectively), calibration and clinical utility in train, test and four external validation cohorts.

Even though the powerful predictive ability, the XGB model is hard to be understood due to the “black-box” character of ML model. Therefore, the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework was been introduced into this study [24]. It could evaluate the contribution of each feature in any ML models [25]. In our ViT-based model, ViT 446 was the most important feature. The bigger ViT 446 value is, the bigger impact on FISH (+). In our Radiomics-based model, original_shape_Elongation was the most important feature. The smaller original_shape_Elongation value is, the bigger impact on FISH (+).

To further improve the predictive performance, the joint learning was been applied in many studies. In lung adenocarcinoma, the joint model (radiomics and DL features) achieved a better ability in predicting invasiveness [26]. In breast cancer, a deep learning radiomics nomogram (DLRN) also been used to lympho-vascular invasion (LVI) status of breast cancer [27]. This study also constructed a fusion model (VR Signature) based on ViT Signature and Radiomics Signature. The fusion model also showed a good performance in predicting FISH result in train, test and four external validation cohorts. Its performance is also better than the single ViT model or Radiomics model. To make this model easier to be used by researchers, a web app is been developed. After entering ViT and Radiomics feature value, the ViT and Radiomics Signatures will be output. Then the nomogram (fusion model) could output the probability of FISH positive.

It is the first multicenter study to predict FISH assay result based on ViT and ML algorithms. Even though the good predictive ability is presented, challenges of ultrasound imaging should be noted. Because that inter-operator variability, low signal-to-noise ratio and multicenter variability may lead to the decline of model generalization ability. Besides, some limitations should be noted. First, this is a retrospective study, the prospective study is needed to further verify the model performance in the future. Second, we only focused on evaluating the performance of our model and did not directly compare it to the radiologist’s clinical presentation. Third, using only the slice with the largest tumor area may result in loss of spatial information for the entire tumor volume, the inclusion of automated Breast Ultra Sound may provide more information. Four, manual ROI delineation may lead to bias and errors, developing an auto-segmentation model would help solve this problem.

Conclusions

The convenient and cheap fusion model, which is based on ViT and ML algorithms, could accurately predict the result of FISH assay in HER2 (2+) breast cancer patients. It may be a valuable and helpful tool for patients with economic difficulties.

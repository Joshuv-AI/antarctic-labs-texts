Middle-aged female with blue nails and graying skin
Authors: Ansley DeVore, Karen DeVore
Published: 2023

Case

A 60-year-old female presented for her nail beds becoming blue over the past 3 years. Additionally, it was noted her sun exposed skin had a gray discoloration (Fig 1). She was healthy, review of systems was negative, and had no pertinent family history. She denied any prescribed medications but reported taking multivitamins and angstrom silver, both of which she took regularly for 10 years. A punch biopsy was done and confirmed the diagnosis (Fig 2).

Answers:

A.Melasma – Incorrect. Melasma is hyperpigmentation localized to the face and is common in women. It should be considered in this case especially if she had been taking hormone replacement. Additionally, melasma does not cause blue discoloration of the nails.

Melasma – Incorrect. Melasma is hyperpigmentation localized to the face and is common in women. It should be considered in this case especially if she had been taking hormone replacement. Additionally, melasma does not cause blue discoloration of the nails.

B.Wilson’s disease – Incorrect. Wilson’s disease can lead to hyperpigmentation, but this is unlikely in our patient. She was healthy, had a negative review of systems, and had an unremarkable exam besides pigmentation changes. In Wilson’s disease, we would expect patients to be positive for symptoms related to liver dysfunction including abdominal pain, lack of appetite, and fatigue.

C.Argyria – Correct. She developed argyria, a rare dermatologic condition due to exposure or ingestion of silver. Generalized argyria, in comparison to localized argyria, typically results from ingesting colloidal silver. Despite the superiority of antibiotics, silver is still taken as a homeopathic antimicrobial. This case is unique as the patient was taking angstrom silver, which is 1 million times smaller than colloidal, and has not previously been reported to cause argyria.1,2

This is unlikely as she denied the typical systemic symptoms including fatigue, weakness, appetite decrease, and polydipsia.3

Answers:

A.Deposition of brown and black granules in the dermis – Correct. Argyria shows a spared epidermis, helping distinguish it from other pigmentation disorders. The dermis will show many small brown or black granules along eccrine glands, elastic fibers, capillary walls, and nerves.1,2

Deposition of brown and black granules in the dermis – Correct. Argyria shows a spared epidermis, helping distinguish it from other pigmentation disorders. The dermis will show many small brown or black granules along eccrine glands, elastic fibers, capillary walls, and nerves.1,2

B.Hyperpigmentation of basal cell layer and pigment-laden macrophages in the dermis – Incorrect. Medications frequently cause hyperpigmentation and lead to these histological changes.4 Argyria does not cause epidermal changes and would show silver granules.1

In addition to argyria’s pigmentation being secondary to the photoactivation and metal reduction of silver, there is support of silver causing stimulation of melanin production.1 However, this is not the stereotypical histopathology of argyria.

Answers:

A.Chelation – Incorrect. Chelation has not provided results as a treatment for argyria.1

Chelation – Incorrect. Chelation has not provided results as a treatment for argyria.1

B.Topical hydroquinone – Incorrect. Hydroquinone has not shown adequate outcomes.1 Additionally, one risks further compounding the issue with prolonged exposure of hydroquinone causing exogenous ochronosis.

Topical hydroquinone – Incorrect. Hydroquinone has not shown adequate outcomes.1 Additionally, one risks further compounding the issue with prolonged exposure of hydroquinone causing exogenous ochronosis.

C.Laser – Incorrect. Limited cases have shown pigmentary improvement after cosmetic laser, specifically the low fluence 1064-nm Nd:YAG, but these were temporary with argyria recurring about 1 year after the laser.1

D.Dermabrasion – Incorrect. Dermabrasion has not provided adequate results as treatment for argyria.1

Dermabrasion – Incorrect. Dermabrasion has not provided adequate results as treatment for argyria.1

E.No treatment available – Correct. Recommending discontinuing silver products and good sun protection are the next steps as no treatments for discoloration have been adequate at this point. While argyria is a benign condition, it can cause significant psychological stress due to the lack of treatments and permanent discoloration.2

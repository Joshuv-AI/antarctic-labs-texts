Diseases burden and trends of bladder cancer attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors among global and Chinese population, 1990–2021: results from the global burden of disease study 2021
Authors: Feifan Liu, Lijun Han, Shuze Liu, Cheng Wang, Jianyu Wang, Shanhua Zhang, Shaoqiu Kong, Xiaoran Han, Haihu Wu, Fei Wu, Jiaju Lyu, Hao Ning
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBehavioral and metabolic factors, such as smoking and high fasting plasma glucose (FPG), have been recognized as important risk factors for bladder cancer. This study aims to assess the global and Chinese burden of bladder cancer attributable to these factors and analyze long-term trends.MethodsData from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study were used. Indicators such as the number and rate of deaths and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) were used to elucidate the disease burden. Joinpoint regression analysis and age-period-cohort models were applied to assess changes in the disease burden over different periods and the independent effects of age, period, and birth cohort.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2021, the global burden of bladder cancer attributable to smoking increased, with a decrease in the age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) by 38.09% and 40.78%, respectively. The global burden attributable to high FPG showed a rising trend in ASMR and ASDR, with percentage increases of 20.96% and 15.79%, respectively. In China, smoking-related trends were similar to the global pattern, but high FPG data showed more fluctuation. The age-period-cohort model showed increasing burden with age, except in the very elderly. Smoking-related burden decreased significantly across different periods and birth cohorts. For high FPG, the trend in China was more volatile. The risk was higher in earlier birth cohorts, followed by gradual improvement, but a recent rebound in risk was observed in the latest cohorts. The global cohort effect remained relatively stable.ConclusionsThis study, based on GBD 2021, highlights the significant impact of behavioral and metabolic factors on bladder cancer burden globally and in China, with differing trends. China should strengthen tobacco and blood glucose management, focus on targeted interventions for the elderly, and enhance health education for the younger population to improve public health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41043-025-01113-9.Keywords: Bladder cancer, Disease burden, Behavioral risk, Metabolic risk, Epidemiology

Introduction

Bladder cancer, a commonly encountered malignant tumor of the urinary system, accounts for approximately 6% of newly diagnosed cancers and 4% of cancer-specific deaths, ranking it as the fourth most common malignancy among male patients [1, 2]. Recent data show that, worldwide, approximately 614,000 new cases were diagnosed and about 220,000 deaths occurred in 2022 [3]. However, the burden of bladder cancer differs substantially by age and sex, with male patients representing a markedly higher proportion than female patients [4]. Bladder cancer is a multifactorial disease whose occurrence and progression are influenced by a range of behavioral, metabolic, and environmental factors, such as smoking, high fasting plasma glucose (FPG), and dietary factors [5, 6].

Worldwide, the widespread use of tobacco continues to pose a profound effect to public health, remaining a major priority in global public health efforts. According to statistics, smoking-related deaths have exceeded 200 million [7]. Smoking is considered the main risk factor for bladder cancer, with studies indicating that smokers face a 2 to 5-fold higher risk of bladder cancer in comparison to nonsmokers [8]. The normal bladder wall is composed of the mucosal layer, muscular layer, and serosal layer [9]. Various carcinogens derived from tobacco come into direct contact with the mucosal layer during urine excretion, and this prolonged exposure may contribute to an increased risk [10]. High FPG represents an abnormal metabolic condition, commonly manifesting as prediabetes or diabetes [11]. With accelerating urbanization and dramatic lifestyle changes, the burden of diseases attributable to high FPG has increasingly drawn attention [12]. Although metabolic dysregulation has been linked to various cancers, its role in bladder carcinogenesis remains to be fully elucidated. Proposed mechanisms include oxidative stress caused by chronic hyperglycemia, low-grade inflammation, and changes in the tumor microenvironment, all of which may promote tumor initiation and progression [13, 14]. Meanwhile, a growing body of clinical evidence supports an epidemiological association between high FPG and bladder cancer risk. For example, a large prospective cohort study involving 476,986 participants revealed that higher FPG was independently associated with an increased risk of bladder cancer [15]. Similarly, among patients with bladder cancer and coexisting diabetes, the cancer-specific mortality and the rates of progression and metastasis were higher compared with those without diabetes [16]. Consequently, previous findings suggest that smoking and high FPG pose major threats to patients with bladder cancer.

Coordinated by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) at the University of Washington, the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study represents the largest systematic and data-driven initiative aimed at quantifying health levels and trends [17]. In the GBD 2021, all 88 risk factors for diseases are categorized into behavioral, metabolic, and environmental factors, among which the reported risk factors for bladder cancer include a behavioral factor (smoking) and a metabolic factor (high FPG) [18]. Recent studies have explored the changing trends in the global burden of bladder cancer, emphasizing the increasing role of behavioral and metabolic risk factors [19–21]. Additionally, the majority of previous studies have relied on GBD 2019 data to assess the global burden of bladder cancer attributable to high FPG or smoking. However, differences in population demographics and risk exposure between China and the worldwide population, as well as the reliance on GBD 2019 rather than the updated GBD 2021 data, have considerably limited our capacity to investigate recent epidemiological shifts in bladder cancer. At present, a comprehensive trend analysis of bladder cancer deaths and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors in both China and worldwide, based on GBD 2021, remains unavailable.

In order to gain a more comprehensive understanding of how behavioral and metabolic factors influence the burden of bladder cancer across different age groups and sexes, this study used the latest GBD data to analyze bladder cancer deaths and DALYs from 1990 to 2021 in both global and Chinese populations attributable to smoking and high FPG. We further examined the long-term trends of these risk factors’ impact, with the goal of offering guidance for focused prevention and intervention strategies in China and other developing countries, thereby contributing to improved global health.

Materials and methods

Data sources

This analysis used anonymized data from GBD 2021 (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/), which reports the burden of 371 diseases and injuries and 88 risk factors across 204 countries and territories [18]. Data in GBD 2021 are derived from multiple sources, such as vital registration systems, hospital records, and other channels. The data heterogeneity and biases were adjusted, and uncertainty analysis was performed using 500 Monte Carlo simulations [19]. The mean of the estimates from these simulations was designated as the final estimate, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles were used to calculate the 95% uncertainty intervals (UI) [22]. The Burden of Proof Risk Function used in GBD 2021 integrates between-study heterogeneity, which can yield negative lower bounds in the 95% UI for risk attributable deaths or DALYs. To preserve the intended 95% coverage and to provide a transparent depiction of the full range of model-driven uncertainty, we have retained the complete 95% UI. The appearance of negative values reflects only statistical indeterminacy, and a positive central estimate nonetheless affirms the risk factor’s epidemiological relevance to disease burden. The definitions of each metric and the detailed procedures to enhance data quality have been thoroughly described and reported in GBD 2021 [22, 23]. To enable more meaningful comparisons of the study results, age-standardized rates were applied to account for variations in age distribution and to adjust for differences and changes in population age structures [24]. The age-standardization process uses a global standard population based on the global age distribution as a reference, providing a reliable measure for comparing age-specific rates across different regions and time periods [25]. We used the GBD Results tool (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results), developed by the IHME, to screen and extract estimates of bladder cancer deaths, DALYs, age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR), and age-standardized DALY rates (ASDR) attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors from 1990 to 2021, along with 95% UI.

Definitions

In GBD 2021, bladder cancer was identified according to the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) 9th and 10th revisions, with corresponding codes C67-C67.9, 188–188.9.9, V10.51, V16.52, and V76.3 [26]. GBD 2021 applies the comparative risk assessment framework to assess the impact of behavioral and metabolic risk factors on bladder cancer burden, independently evaluating the contribution of each risk factor by calculating the population attributable fraction and adjusting for potential confounding variables [27]. This method quantifies the proportion of disease burden attributable to each risk factor by comparing the observed exposure distribution with the theoretical minimum risk exposure level (TMREL) [28]. Smoking was defined as current or past occasional or daily use of any tobacco products. For current smokers, exposure was quantified by the equivalent number of cigarettes consumed daily and the total years of smoking, whereas for those who have quit smoking, the distribution of years since cessation was estimated [29]. According to GBD 2021 and similar previous studies, the TMREL for smoking is set to a tobacco use rate of 0%, representing individuals with no history of tobacco use [25]. High FPG was defined as an FPG concentration above the TMREL. The TMREL, ranging from 4.8 to 5.4 mmol/L, was determined by the person-years weighted average of FPG associated with the minimum mortality risk, based on pooled analyses of prospective cohort studies [21].

Statistical analysis

In this study, Joinpoint regression analysis was performed using the Joinpoint software, to examine the long-term trends of bladder cancer burden attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors. We specifically analyzed the key time points for changes in the ASMR and ASDR per 100,000 population in both global and Chinese data from 1990 to 2021. The average annual percent change (AAPC) represents the average percentage change in the age-standardized rates over the entire period (1990–2021), whereas the annual percent change (APC) describes the percentage change in the age-standardized rates during each segmented time interval. A Poisson distribution-based age-period-cohort model was employed to estimate the independent effects of age, period, and birth cohort changes, with relative risk (RR) used to quantify period and cohort effects [30]. In this study, an online tool (https://analysistools.cancer.gov/apc/) was utilized to perform the age-period-cohort analysis. In order to apply an age-period-cohort approach to analyze how age, period, and birth cohort affect bladder cancer deaths and DALYs rates attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors, we subdivided the data into 5-year age groups spanning from 30 to 34 years to 90–94 years, and an additional group for those aged ≥ 95 years.

All statistical analyses and result visualizations were performed utilizing R software (version 4.4.1), Joinpoint (version 5.1.0), along with Age-Period-Cohort Analysis Web Tool. A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

The burden of bladder cancer attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors

Between 1990 and 2021, the global number of bladder cancer deaths attributable to smoking increased from 41,114 (35100, 46965) to 58,767 (49381, 70892), while the DALYs rose from 945,486 (805856, 1076451) to 1,238,303 (1044303, 1478221). In contrast, the corresponding ASMR and ASDR both declined, with percentage changes of −38.09% (−45.40%, −27.40%) and − 40.78% (−48.01%, −29.44%), respectively. Additionally, the number of deaths and DALYs attributable to high FPG showed a marked increase, whereas the changes in ASMR and ASDR were relatively modest, with percent changes of 20.96% (11.45%, 35.25%) and 15.79% (6.56%, 30.95%), respectively. The joinpoint regression analysis

We performed Joinpoint regression analysis to segment and analyze the trends of ASMR and ASDR for bladder cancer attributable to smoking and high FPG from 1990 to 2021.

The ASMR and ASDR attributable to smoking globally decreased on average by 1.54% (AAPC = −1.54%, p < 0.05) and 1.68% (AAPC = −1.68%, p < 0.05) per year, respectively. In China, the average annual decreases were 1.23% (AAPC = −1.23%, p < 0.05) and 1.25% (AAPC = −1.25%, p < 0.05), respectively (Supplementary Table 1). Joinpoint analysis indicated a downward trend in ASMR and ASDR during most time periods (Fig. 3A, B), with a significant decrease in global ASMR during the period from 2001 to 2007 (APC = −2.30%, p < 0.05), and a similar significant decline in ASMR in China during the same period (APC = −2.95%, p < 0.05). ASDR exhibited a similar trend, with a global APC of −2.47% (p < 0.05) and an APC of −3.08% (p < 0.05) in China during the 2001–2007 period. It is important to mention that the declining trends in ASMR and ASDR have slowed in recent periods, both globally and in China. Detailed data are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

For the bladder cancer burden related to high FPG, the global ASMR increased by an average of 0.64% per year (AAPC = 0.64%, p < 0.05) between 1990 and 2021, while the ASDR increased by an average of 0.49% per year (AAPC = 0.49%, p < 0.05) (Supplementary Table 1). However, China exhibited a different trend during the same period, with ASMR and ASDR decreasing by an average of 0.64% (AAPC = −0.64%, p < 0.05) and 0.74% (AAPC = −0.74%, p < 0.05). Joinpoint analysis revealed a rising trend in the global burden related to high FPG during most periods, whereas in China, this trend showed a larger fluctuation with an overall decline. As shown in Fig. 3C, the period (1996–2000) saw a notable rise in global ASMR (APC = 2.25%, p < 0.05), after which a decrease was observed in recent period (2016–2021) (ACP = −0.44%, p < 0.05). In China, ASMR significantly increased during 1995–2000 (APC = 4.09%, p < 0.05), followed by a significant decline during 2000–2006 (APC = −4.85%, p < 0.05). Both globally and in China, the ASDR exhibited a trend similar to that of ASMR from 1990 to 2021 (Fig. 3D).

Discussion

Bladder cancer, one of the most common malignancies of the urinary system, significantly impacts the physical and mental health of individuals worldwide [31]. Bladder cancer’s development and advancement have been shown to be strongly associated with various risk factors [32]. In addition to uncontrollable factors such as genetics and age, controllable factors, including behavioral and metabolic factors, have become key contributors to the onset of bladder cancer [6]. In this research, we analyzed the burden and long-term trends of bladder cancer attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors globally and in China between 1990 and 2021, using deaths and DALYs data from the GBD 2021.

Smoking, a behavioral factor, is considered a key risk factor for bladder cancer. Smoking is an addictive behavior that poses a major public health challenge worldwide, significantly increasing the burden of disease [33]. Previous studies have identified several carcinogens in tobacco smoke, such as aromatic amines 4-aminobiphenyl, which is linked to the development of bladder cancer [10]. Despite statistical data showing an overall decrease in global smoking prevalence, the overall number of smokers is still rising as a result of population expansion [7]. Being the global main producer and consumer of tobacco, it has become an important component of the national economy of China [34]. Previous studies have found that smoking rates in China have remained high, particularly among men, with an estimated rate of around 47% [35]. Therefore, smoking remains a persistent health challenge both globally and in China. The study’s findings suggest that between 1990 and 2021, both globally and in China, the rate of deaths and DALYs attributable to smoking for bladder cancer has decreased. However, the absolute number of deaths and DALYs continues to rise, with the disease burden among male higher than that of female. Statistics from the World Health Organization suggest that the male smokers are generally higher than that of females, contributing to a heavier bladder cancer burden among male patients. The Joinpoint regression analysis in this study indicated a declining trend in the burden of bladder cancer attributable to smoking during most periods from 1990 to 2021. This decline could be linked to the global increase in health awareness and stringent measures. However, compared to the global burden of bladder cancer attributable to smoking, particularly in recent years, China has seemingly performed poorly in terms of smoking-related burden. This disparity may be due not only to differences in smoking prevalence but also to factors such as economic development levels and public health policies. Therefore, China may need to further strengthen tobacco control policies and raise public health awareness. It is important to highlight that Joinpoint regression analysis suggests a deceleration in the decline of the ASMR and ASDR in recent periods, and the age-period-cohort model results indicate that the burden of bladder cancer caused by smoking among elderly patients appears to continue to rise. This may be due to the longer duration of exposure to smoking risk factors in older individuals, resulting in cumulative effects that further exacerbate the disease burden. Therefore, it may be necessary to emphasize the establishment of interventions targeting smoking in the elderly population.

High FPG affects tumor cell metabolism and the tumor microenvironment by altering the body metabolic state, leading to an increase in tumor incidence and mortality [36]. Previous researchers found that individuals experiencing hyperglycemia have a heightened likelihood of developing bladder cancer [37]. The possible explanation for this is that high FPG-induced hyperglycemia induces oxidative stress, which accelerates the metastatic progression of tumor cells, while also inducing insulin resistance that further promotes tumor growth [21]. In recent years, due to population aging, changes in dietary patterns, and an increasing prevalence of obesity, FPG levels have gradually risen worldwide, leading to a continuous rise in the population of patients with high FPG [38]. This poses an escalating threat to global health. Our study results suggest that globally, the bladder cancer burden linked to high FPG is gradually increasing. However, the relationship between high FPG and bladder cancer remains an emerging area of research. While our study suggests a potential association, the evidence is still preliminary, and the strength of this relationship remains inconclusive. High FPG may represent a risk factor, but further research, including studies that control for potential confounders such as diet and the use of antidiabetic medications, is necessary to confirm this association and explore its causal mechanisms. The Joinpoint analysis suggests an upward trend in the disease burden during most periods, but a possible downward trend in recent years. This global trend change may be related to urbanization, improvements in quality of life, and changes in dietary patterns, which have led to an increase in the high FPG population worldwide. In recent years, the disease burden caused by high FPG has received increasing attention, with many countries implementing screening policies and stringent blood glucose management measures, which have helped to some extent in curbing the further rise of this burden. However, compared to the global trend, the data from China appear to show greater fluctuations, particularly during the period from 1995 to 2006, which experienced significant variability. These fluctuations may reflect rapid economic growth and dramatic lifestyle changes in China during this period, leading to a temporary increase in the high FPG population. Subsequently, with the advancement of public health initiatives (such as the broad application of blood glucose testing and strengthened interventions), the disease burden has improved. The age-period-cohort model suggests that, both in China and globally, the burden related to high FPG appears to rise with age, although a decline is observed in the very elderly population. However, this decline may be due to survivor bias or the smaller sample size in the very elderly group, which could cause the estimates to be influenced by random fluctuations. Additionally, it is worth noting that the cohort effect in China shows significant differences across birth cohorts, with earlier-born cohorts experiencing a higher burden, which appears to have gradually improved over time. However, more recent cohorts may have seen an increase in risk due to the influence of modern lifestyles. In contrast, the global cohort effect shows lower and more stable risk levels. These results highlight the multidimensional and complex influence of high FPG on the bladder cancer burden. Moving forward, global efforts to control blood glucose and manage diabetes need to be strengthened, while, in China, although progress has been made, there is still a need to optimize health management for the elderly population and health education for the younger generation in order to achieve stable and sustained risk control.

This research presents a comprehensive examination of the burden and long-term trends of bladder cancer attributable to behavioral and metabolic factors globally and in China from 1990 to 2021, offering valuable insights for managing bladder cancer, particularly in the elderly population. By utilizing the most recent data from the GBD 2021 and applying an age-period-cohort model, this research offers multiple analytical approaches to understanding how these risk factors have contributed to the changing patterns of bladder cancer incidence and mortality. These findings regarding the burden of bladder cancer attributable to smoking and high FPG underscore the urgent need for targeted public health strategies. These strategies should focus on both primary prevention and early detection of bladder cancer. Globally, there is a clear need for stronger tobacco control policies and smoking cessation programs to reduce the prevalence of smoking. In addition, addressing metabolic risk factors such as high FPG through public health campaigns, lifestyle interventions, and improving access to early screening can help prevent and manage the disease. In China, where the burden of bladder cancer attributable to smoking and high FPG is notably increasing, our findings suggest that targeted public health measures are needed. The government could enhance smoking cessation programs and strengthen regulatory policies to reduce smoking prevalence. Moreover, given the rise in metabolic risk factors, it is critical to promote healthier lifestyles, improve access to early screening programs, and develop public health campaigns focused on preventing and managing high blood glucose. These strategies will help mitigate the increasing burden of bladder cancer in China, particularly in the aging population.

However, we acknowledge several limitations in our research. Firstly, regarding the data, all information was obtained from the GBD 2021. Although it employed rigorous data collection and reasonable assessment methods, biases may still exist. Despite controlling for several known confounders, including age, in the analysis, residual confounding from factors such as occupational exposures, diet, and other unaccounted variables may still affect the observed associations. These uncontrolled factors could introduce bias in the burden estimates. Due to the lack of detailed individual-level data, the results of this study can only reflect changes at the population level and are not applicable at the individual level. Secondly, some of the trends observed in this analysis, particularly those related to ASMR and ASDR for bladder cancer attributable to smoking and high FPG, were not statistically significant and should be interpreted with caution. Furthermore, although our Joinpoint regression analysis and age–period–cohort models detect possible inflection points in the most recent years, these change-points may partly reflect statistical artifacts such as edge effects at the temporal boundaries rather than definitive epidemiological shifts. Nevertheless, these patterns offer valuable hypotheses about emerging directions in disease burden that should be validated in future studies with additional data. Finally, the lack of detailed staging data for bladder cancer in this study limits further exploration of the burden of bladder cancer subtypes.

Based on the findings and limitations of this study, several important avenues for future research are suggested. First, further studies should focus on exploring the mechanisms linking high FPG and smoking to bladder cancer, as understanding these mechanisms can lead to more targeted prevention and intervention strategies. Additionally, future research could address the impact of other behavioral and metabolic factors, such as alcohol consumption, second-hand smoke exposure, and obesity, which were not included in this study due to data limitations. Finally, more comprehensive cohort studies with individual-level data would help validate our findings and better capture the influence of various risk factors on bladder cancer burden at the individual level.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study, based on GBD 2021 data and employing various analytical methods, reveals the significant impact of behavioral and metabolic factors on the bladder cancer burden globally and in China, with notable differences in their trends. These findings suggest that, compared to the global trend, China still needs to strengthen tobacco control and blood glucose management strategies, particularly by implementing targeted interventions for the elderly population and focusing on health education for the younger generation, in order to effectively alleviate the bladder cancer burden and its economic impact, and to promote improvements in public health.

Clarifying the active ingredients & pharmacological mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicines via modern pharmaceutical analysis techniques
Authors: Huaxin Zhou, Fengwen Yang, Haoyun Bai, Yanni Lv, Cheng Wang, Rui Liu, Heshui Yu, Shengli Han, Zheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a widely practiced clinical medical system, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) possesses a theoretical and practical foundation accumulated over thousands of years. With growing global interest in natural therapies, TCM is currently facing a historic opportunity for globalization. However, its widespread international acceptance still faces significant challenges due to the lack of standardization, variability in herbal quality, incomplete understanding of its mechanisms of action, and gaps in meeting international regulatory requirements for medicines. With the rapid development of analytical tools and methods, advances in artificial intelligence (AI), and continuous progress in pharmaceutical sciences, comprehensive high-throughput pharmacological research, quality control, and the establishment of regulatory approaches for the complex system of TCM are gradually becoming feasible. This study systematically expounds on the current applications and challenges of modern analytical methods in TCM formulation research from the perspective of analytical technology, covering key aspects including the efficacious components of TCM, multi-component analysis of herbal medicines, application models of modern analytical techniques, correlation analysis between the bioactive constituents of TCM formulations and their pharmacological mechanisms, and the establishment of quality control methods based on efficacy-related substances.Keywords: Traditional Chinese medicine, Active ingredients, Pharmacological mechanisms, Pharmaceutical analysis techniques, Multi-component analysis

Introduction

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), a widely practiced system of clinical healthcare that has made significant contributions to human health, is gaining global recognition. An important task for TCM researchers in the modern era is to clarify the active ingredients and pharmacological mechanisms of TCM preparations. Rapid progress in pharmaceutical science and artificial intelligence (AI) has accelerated the development and improvement of pharmaceutical analysis techniques, providing new tools to elucidate the main active ingredients, the mechanisms, and the scientific basis of TCM. However, the systematic application of these advanced tools to decipher the inherent complexity of TCM remains a significant challenge. This complexity is characterized by multi-component formulations that exert therapeutic effects through multi-pathway networks. Such a “black-box” nature hinders standardization, robust quality control, and wider international acceptance, thereby creating a critical gap between the therapeutic potential of TCM and its evidence-based scientific validation.

To bridge this gap, this review intends to summarize a cohesive analytical framework. We first review TCM entities whose active ingredients have been identified, along with their intrinsic multi-component nature, to establish their material foundation. The core of the discussion then focuses on the transformative role of modern pharmaceutical analysis techniques, including multi-dimensional chromatography-mass spectrometry (MS), AI-integrated data modeling, and biomimetic screening platforms. These techniques are critically assessed for their power in correlating bioactive constituents with pharmacological mechanisms and in establishing efficacy-driven quality control paradigms. Finally, we synthesize current progress and future directions, illustrating how the integration of these analytical innovations is essential for translating TCM from an empirical practice into a precisely defined and globally integrated pharmaceutical science.

Active ingredients are the material basis for the effectiveness of TCM

Chinese medicinal materials, TCM prescriptions, and Chinese patent medicines are all widely used in clinical practice, and their effectiveness depends on specific active ingredients or active ingredient groups.

Chinese medicinal materials with elucidated active ingredients

In TCM research, classical preparations whose active ingredients have been elucidated through modern separation and analysis techniques have become benchmarks for TCM modernization. For example, researchers have accurately isolated and identified the antimalarial active ingredient artemisinin from Artemisia annua through chromatographic separation combined with biological activity tracking, clarifying its chemical structure and pharmacological mechanism on the membrane structure of malaria parasites. The discovery of artemisinin has not only saved the lives of millions of malaria patients worldwide but has also confirmed the existence of single compounds with clear pharmacological activity in TCM [1].

Classical prescriptions and Chinese patent medicines with elucidated active ingredients

The clinical effectiveness of classical TCM prescriptions has been validated through hundreds of years of practice, and modern analytical techniques provide scientific support for their therapeutic effects. For example, Tongxinluo capsule, a classical Chinese patent medicine for treating cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, is composed of various medicinal materials, including ginseng, leeches, and whole scorpions. Clinical research has confirmed that it has the effect of improving microcirculation and protecting vascular endothelial cells in the treatment of acute myocardial infarction, cerebral infarction, and other diseases. Modern analysis techniques have revealed that it contains multiple amino acids, saponins, and polypeptide components. Subsequent mechanistic studies have traced the metabolic trajectories of these constituents in the body, determining the modern biological basis of their “Tongluo Huoxue” and providing an important foundation for the secondary development of classical prescriptions [28].

The complexity of multi-component and multi-pathway compositions in TCM

The clinical efficacy of TCM stems from its material basis, formula compatibility, and multi-pathway effects. Validating the effectiveness of TCM is a complex systematic task, and clarifying the active ingredients is a key link in TCM research. Based on its complexity and degree of elucidation, a TCM composition can be classified into the three categories of compound TCM, part TCM, and component TCM.

A compound TCM, the principle and most complex form applied clinically, is composed of multiple medicinal materials of herbal and animal origin. The compatibility and activity of the ingredients in its formulation follow the principle of “monarch, minister, assistant, and guide”. Compound TCM prescriptions are a typical application scenario for modern pharmaceutical analysis. A part TCM is obtained from a single Chinese medicinal material through solvent extraction, separation and purification, as well as concentration and enrichment processes. Containing a mixture of one or several types of chemical constituents with clear pharmacological effects, the focus of its quality control is twofold. The first is to clarify the composition of the effective parts in the mixture, and the second is to determine the main active ingredient groups and their contents in these effective parts. On this basis, their pharmacological mechanisms and synergistic effects are clarified based on the main active ingredient groups. Finally, a component TCM is a single-component drug derived from a medicinal plant, and its quality control and mechanistic investigations are consistent with those of chemical drugs.

Table 3 [14,[45], [46], [47], [48], [49]] systematically summarizes 16 TCMs, including 5 compound TCMs, 4 part TCMs, and 7 component TCMs, the active ingredients of which have been clearly identified. Using modern analytical techniques, the index components (quality control markers) and active ingredients in TCM can be precisely identified, and the core TCM pain points of “complex components and ambiguous mechanisms" can be addressed. This allows the translation of traditional empirical therapeutic effects into quantifiable and reproducible modern evidence, which is key for addressing the controversy over the “scientificity” of TCM. Moreover, it lays a solid foundation for the research and development of new drugs, their standardized production, and the international recognition of TCM.

The advantages of modern pharmaceutical analysis techniques

Over the past half-century, significant progress has been made in separation science and technology. Various types of gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS), high performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS), and ultra-HPLC-MS (UHPLC-MS) analysis systems have been applied in the separation and detection of TCM constituents [60]. Moreover, the rapid development of biotechnology and AI has provided new ideas and possibilities for research on the effectiveness, composition, and constituent synergy of complex TCM systems.

Liquid/GC-MS separation and analysis techniques

HPLC-MS is one of the most widely used techniques for analyzing complex TCM systems. Combining the efficient separation ability of HPLC with the highly sensitive and selective detection of MS, it enables the simultaneous separation, identification, and quantification of hundreds of components in TCM formulae. UHPLC-MS technology has further improved separation efficiency and is more suitable for component analysis in complex TCM systems. Volatile oil substances derived from TCM formulae have attracted attention due to their unexpected pharmacological activities. GC-MS and comprehensive two-dimensional (2D) GC-MS (GC × GC-MS), which offers superior peak separation capacity, are widely used for small and medium-sized molecular components with high volatility and thermal stability. For GC-MS, many bioactive constituents in TCM are non-volatile, thermolabile, or highly polar, rendering them unsuitable for direct analysis without derivatization techniques such as silylation or methylation, which can be time-consuming and introduce artifacts. For liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS), matrix effects manifesting as ion suppression or enhancement pose significant challenges for accurate quantification.

While traditional analytical methods excel at identifying and quantifying individual components, they are largely reductionist and struggle to decipher the complex nonlinear interactions inherent to TCM formulae. In contrast, AI-driven techniques enable high-dimensional feature extraction from complex chemical fingerprints, nonlinear pattern recognition across multi-omics datasets, and integrative modeling that links chemical profiles to biological targets, capabilities that are difficult or impossible to achieve with conventional approaches. This moves TCM research from descriptive analysis toward hypothesis-driven discovery, providing a powerful framework for elucidating multi-component and multi-target mechanisms. However, despite the transformative potential of AI, its application in TCM research is not without challenges. The quality and heterogeneity of data extracted from diverse TCM formulations and experimental settings pose a significant hurdle, as inconsistent or incomplete datasets can lead to biased or non-generalizable AI models.

This analytical framework also offers concrete pathways for clinical translation. Biomarker-guided patient stratification using metabolomic or proteomic profiling can identify subpopulations most likely to respond to specific TCM interventions, while pharmacometabolomics enables personalized formulation selection and dosage optimization. Regarding practical implementation, while initial investment and interdisciplinary expertise remain challenges, the growing ecosystem of open-source AI tools and cloud-based platforms is progressively lowering barriers to entry. AI-driven prioritization of experiments can ultimately reduce research and development costs, making these technologies increasingly accessible for routine application.

With this understanding, we now turn to several representative application cases of AI-driven analysis in TCM research. The “systems biological analysis” strategy is representative. Combining data on TCM ingredients obtained through analysis techniques such as HPLC-MS with bioomics data from genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, etc., the approach constructs a component-target-pathway correlation network through AI models to achieve systematic analysis of the efficacy mechanisms of TCM materials. For instance, in a study on the compound Danshen formula, data obtained through analysis and identification of the main components were integrated with AI models to predict the targets of the components’ actions. The resulting multiple correlation networks, such as “tanshinone IIA-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-angiogenesis pathway”, clearly elucidated the synergistic mechanism of the formula in treating cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. This intelligent analysis paradigm greatly improves the efficiency and depth of research on complex TCM systems, providing core technical support for the analysis of multi-component and multi-target mechanisms.

Zuo et al. [70] focused on the comprehensive multi-component characterization and quantification of Chinese herbal medicines to understand the material basis of the drug action. The group developed a robust strategy based on LC-MS to comprehensively characterize and quantify multiple components in botanical TCM preparations. Taking wolfberry as an example, 147 constituents were identified, demonstrating the power of AI in enhancing our understanding of herbal drugs. In addition, herbal genomics is a method that combines metabolomics with herbal medicine, applying AI to evaluate the efficacy of herbal treatments and to understand their impact on biological systems [71].

Wang et al. [72] quantified interactions among the components of herbal combinations by constructing protein-protein interaction networks. This method utilizes network topology to identify the interactions of synergistic compounds, which is of great significance in understanding the complex relationships in TCM preparations.

Wu et al. [73] constructed deep learning models for three key Alzheimer's disease (AD) targets—AChE, MAO-A, and 5-HT6—as well as a model for the disease level. By integrating MS, they screened the Kaixin-San formula and identified 8 compounds that can penetrate the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and enhance β-amyloid clearance efficiency. The study elucidates the critical substances and mechanisms of action of the Kaixin-San formula in treating neurological diseases.

Gao et al. [74] proposed an AI-driven approach for active ingredient discovery and non-destructive quality control, using Xuefu Zhuyu Oral Liquid (XZOL) as an example. First, a multi-wavelength fusion HPLC fingerprint chromatogram was established to comprehensively characterize the chemical composition of XZOL. Subsequently, combined with the spectral effect relationship models of grey relational analysis (GRA), partial least squares regression (PLSR), backpropagation artificial neural network (BP-ANN), and convolutional neural network (CNN), seven active compounds that promote angiogenesis were identified, including hydroxysafflor yellow A, paeoniflorin, ferulic acid, narirutin, naringin, hesperidin, and neohesperidin.

Beyond component identification, machine learning (ML) has emerged as a distinct strategy for TCM quality control, capable of integrating multi-dimensional data to establish predictive models for quality assessment and authentication. Compared to traditional chemometric approaches such as principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), which are interpretable and well-established but limited by linear assumptions, ML methods including support vector machine (SVM), random forests, and deep learning can capture non-linear interactions and hierarchical features within complex TCM datasets. However, ML approaches face challenges including requirements for large training datasets, limited interpretability, and risk of overfitting. The most promising direction lies in synergistic integration, combining chemometric preprocessing with ML modeling to leverage the strengths of both paradigms for establishing robust, efficacy-based quality control standards.

In addition to the data-driven ML approaches discussed above, molecular docking represents a complementary in silico technique that has gained widespread application in TCM research. Docking simulations predict the preferred orientation and binding affinity of small molecule compounds to their putative protein targets based on structural complementarity. This approach is particularly valuable for validating interactions predicted by network pharmacology, providing mechanistic insight at the atomic level by identifying specific binding residues and interaction forces. High-throughput docking campaigns enable virtual screening of TCM chemical libraries against disease-relevant targets, prioritizing compounds for experimental validation. While docking predictions require experimental confirmation, they offer a rapid and cost-effective strategy for generating testable hypotheses about compound-target interactions, thereby accelerating the drug discovery pipeline from TCM sources.

Cell membrane chromatography (CMC) biomimetic detection technology

In the early 1990s, He [75] proposed CMC, a biomimetic detection technology. Under a simulated in vivo physiological environment maintaining the biological activity of membrane receptors, the interaction characteristics between drugs and membrane receptors were detected. The results reflect not only the physicochemical properties of the drugs but also their potential biological activities, providing a new analytical approach for the efficient discovery of targeted components and their action patterns in TCM. The independently developed “2D/CMC-TCM Analyzer” is a 2D chromatographic analyzer. The first dimension utilizes the recognition and retention features of CMC to selectively capture and distinguish retained components from complex TCM systems. Retained components are then transferred to an HPLC system for further analysis. Therefore, the CMC method greatly reduces the need for sample separation in complex matrix analysis, presenting a new efficient technology for biometric analysis. When utilized to solve the technical problems of drug discovery and drug quality control, it will open up new fields of biometric analysis.

Affinity chromatography (AC) technology

AC, proposed in the 1970s, is a chromatographic technique that utilizes the specific binding properties of a macromolecular stationary phase with analytes [81]. The primary strength of AC is its direct linkage to biological mechanism, as captured compounds represent potential bioactive ligands that provide a rational basis for subsequent mechanism studies. This differs fundamentally from correlation-based approaches such as spectrum-effect relationships, which infer bioactivity through statistical association rather than direct interaction. However, affinity methods have inherent limitations. Immobilization may alter protein conformation and binding behavior. Non-specific adsorption can generate false positives. Compounds requiring metabolic activation or acting through indirect mechanisms may also be missed. Furthermore, these techniques typically identify binding events rather than functional activity, necessitating complementary functional assays for validation. Based on different types of stationary phases, various AC methods have been developed and are still in use today. With the rapid development of biological science, the preparation methods for AC stationary phases have been significantly improved. The advancement of protein immobilization technology has also greatly improved the preparation methods for protein stationary phases. However, due to the diverse three-dimensional structures and biological activities of biological macromolecules, retention of their natural structures and activities after AC stationary phase immobilization can be a challenge, resulting in decreased or lost correlation with in vivo processes. Despite this, the AC method has been successfully applied in correlation analysis between TCM prescriptions and macromolecular targets, providing great potential for exploring the effectiveness of TCM preparations.

An AC column with acetylcholinesterase (AChE) as the stationary phase ligand can isolate components that inhibit AChE activity from extracts of intelligence-enhancing TCMs such as Acorus tatarinowii, providing clues for the development of AD treatment drugs. Biological macromolecule chromatography evaluates the metabolic characteristics of TCM components by simulating the in vivo binding process of drugs with biological macromolecules such as plasma proteins, providing support for the correlation research of pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics. Liu et al. [89] immobilized tyrosinase (TYR) on the surface of aminated magnetic nanoparticles (MNP) (Fe3O4-NH2). Initially, TYR was adsorbed onto the Fe3O4-NH2 surface via electrostatic interactions, followed by covalent cross-linking with glutaraldehyde (GA) to firmly anchor the enzyme to the support. This immobilized system was utilized for the screening of natural TYR inhibitors in TCM.

Liu et al. [90] screened α-glucosidase (α-Glu) inhibitors in TCM by immobilizing α-Glu on MNPs for analysis combined with capillary electrophoresis (CE). The magnetic fixation was beneficial for the reusability, stability, and separation of the enzyme substrate. The same group also immobilized α-Glu on cellulose filter paper modified with chitosan-modified cellulose filter paper (CFP/CS). In combination with CE, eleven TCM preparations were screened for α-Glu inhibitors, along with a study of the enzyme kinetics. As a new type of enzyme immobilization carrier with strong pH and temperature resistance, CFP/CS has broad development prospects [91].

Dysregulation of peptidyl arginine deiminase 4 (PAD4) is associated with various diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and AD. In a study by Zhao et al. [92], PAD4 was immobilized and stabilized on MNPs to screen PAD4 inhibitors in TCM preparations.

Chen et al. [93] synthesized a novel angiotensin converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) coupled stationary phase for screening potential ACE2-targeting components in human urine samples and Lianhuaqingwen (LHQW) extracts. The results demonstrated that eight components, including neochlorogenic acid, amygdalin, prunasin, forsythiaside I, rutin, forsythoside A, glycyrrhizic acid, and Rhubarb (or Rheum officinale), exhibited strong affinity for ACE2 (Fig. 2) [93].

Correlation analysis between the active ingredients and pharmacological mechanisms of compound TCM prescriptions

Compound TCM prescriptions play a unique therapeutic role in clinical practice, especially in high-burden conditions, such as cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, digestive system, and nervous system diseases. For Chinese patent medicines commonly used in the clinical context, the application of modern analytical techniques in composition-pharmacodynamic mechanism correlation analysis is fundamental.

Key active ingredients in TCM prescriptions include ginsenosides, tanshinol, paeoniflorin, and gastrodin, among many others, providing solutions for treating related diseases and the conditioning of sub-health. Moreover, the mechanisms of action of some TCMs have been verified to include signal pathway regulation, inhibition of inflammatory factors, and other mechanisms, indicating that modern pharmaceutical analysis techniques can detect the active ingredients in TCM. On this basis, by integrating molecular biology, cell biology, and pharmacological techniques and methods, the causal relationship between active ingredients and drug efficacy can be established, and the pharmacological mechanism of compound TCM prescriptions can be elucidated.

Quality control methods based on pharmacological substances

The reliable clinical efficacy of TCM depends on strict quality control, the key to which is to establish detection standards based on pharmacological substances and to apply these throughout the entire production process. Modern analytical techniques offer precise, reliable methods for quality control in TCM, upgrading from traditional empirical identification to precise control based on chemical constituents [125]. The key techniques are primarily fingerprint chromatography, HPLC-MS, multidimensional chromatographic technologies, and CMC.

As a classical core technology for quality control of TCM, the fingerprint chromatographic method focuses on the overall characteristics of Chinese medicine components. The method provides an exclusive “fingerprint map” of a TCM that reflects the types and relative proportions of its chemical components, ensuring stability and consistency of the TCM preparation quality.

Due to its high separation efficiency and sensitivity, the combination technology of HPLC-MS (or UHPLC-MS) has become the core means for accurate qualitative and quantitative analysis of TCM components and is widely used for quality control of single and compound TCM formulae. The technology combines the separation capabilities of HPLC/UHPLC with the precise detection capabilities of MS to achieve synchronous identification and quantification of indicator constituents, quality markers, and active ingredients.

Multidimensional chromatography involves multicolumn 2D separation, significantly improving the separation of complex components in TCM. By optimizing the selectivity of the separation system, it overcomes the difficulty of separating trace and coexisting components in complex matrices. Representative technologies include multi-column liquid chromatography (MC-LC), comprehensive 2D liquid chromatography (LC × LC), 2D liquid chromatography (2D-LC), and their combination with MS and solid-phase extraction (SPE).

CMC technology, as a targeted quality control technique, is based on the specific binding of cell membrane receptors to drug components. Focusing on the detection of components directly related to biological effects, such as allergens [126], CMC enables a correlation of quality control and clinical safety. The technology achieves specific identification and accurate qualitative analysis of allergens by immobilizing cells (such as the MrgprX2-HEK293, RBL-2H3, and LAD2 cell lines) onto chromatographic stationary phases, applying detection methods such as HPLC and MS.

Future perspective

Future research should move beyond component identification toward establishing causal, mechanism-based evidence through a multi-tiered integrated strategy. First, high-resolution MS should provide comprehensive chemical profiling, while AI-driven network pharmacology systematically maps predicted component-target-pathway networks to generate testable hypotheses. Second, these predictions require rigorous validation using biomimetic screening platforms such as CMC to confirm compound-target binding, complemented by multi-omics profiling to assess global biological impact and establish causal links between specific components and observed effects. Third, confirming interactions among active ingredients demands dedicated experimental designs, including quantitative approaches such as the Chou-Talalay combination index method for characterizing synergistic effects, and more sophisticated strategies like component knock-out/knock-in assays to dissect how individual ingredients contribute to the overall pharmacological response.

Beyond mechanistic elucidation, several priority areas warrant attention to bridge the gap between analytical capabilities and clinical applicability. Standardization and regulatory alignment require internationally recognized protocols for TCM analysis, as well as comprehensive global databases for ingredients and pharmacological data, which would facilitate regulatory acceptance. Modern analytical techniques such as high-resolution MS fingerprinting offer concrete pathways for meeting international quality standards through efficacy-linked quality control. Clinical validation of TCM demands rigorous trial designs that address product heterogeneity, blinding challenges, and the tension between standardized protocols and individualized practice. Pragmatic trials and the integration of analytical techniques into trial protocols can enhance reproducibility and provide mechanistic insight. Safety assessment must be systematically integrated using the same analytical platforms, including screening for toxic constituents, herb-drug interactions, and toxicity biomarkers. Finally, economic accessibility requires tiered analytical strategies in which comprehensive profiling identifies critical quality markers that can subsequently be monitored using lower-cost techniques, while open-source AI tools and cloud platforms progressively lower barriers for resource-limited settings.

Conclusion

In terms of chemical composition and in vivo processes, TCM constitutes a prototypical ultra-complex system, the overall landscape of which cannot be comprehensively elucidated by any single modern analytical technique. Therefore, by focusing on the principal contradictions and their dominant aspects, and through the interdisciplinary integration of chemistry, pharmacology, biology, and AI, it is both necessary and feasible to establish an integrated yet targeted modern analytical framework for drug research. Centered on the major bioactive constituents responsible for the therapeutic effects of TCM, such a framework aims to elucidate the mechanisms underlying its primary pharmacological actions. With continuous expansion and in-depth investigation, this strategy is expected to progressively uncover the scientific basis of the distinctive clinical efficacy of TCM.

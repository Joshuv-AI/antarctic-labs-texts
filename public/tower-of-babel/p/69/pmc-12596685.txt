ADGL-07 GLIOBLASTOMAS IN TOGO : EPIDEMIOLOGY, MANAGEMENT AND CHALLENGES IN A COUNTRY WITH LIMITED RESOURCES
Authors: Doléagbénou A Komlan, Kpélao E, Egu K, Ahanogbé KHM
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryIntroductionGlioblastoma (GBM) is the most common malignant brain tumor in adults. Its management is complex, and even more difficult in developing countries due to the scarcity of diagnostic and therapeutic resources.Patients and methodThis was a descriptive study with retrospective data collection, ranging from January 2018 to December 2022. All files with a histological diagnosis of glioblastoma were included in the study. The parameters studied were the diagnostic pathway, treatment modalities, available infrastructure, and socio-economic issues linked to the disease.ResultsWe identified 43 files with a histological diagnosis of glioblastoma. The average age of the patients was 48 years. The sex ratio was 1.3. Surgical treatment is limited by the lack of modern equipment. Radiotherapy is available but is expensive ($2800). Access to temozolomide also remains restricted by its cost and availability. Median survival was 4.8 months.ConclusionThe management of glioblastoma in Togo remains marked by a series of major obstacles which compromise the quality of care and the survival of patients. A multisectoral response is essential. Le glioblastome (GBM) est la tumeur cérébrale maligne la plus fréquente chez l’adulte. Sa prise en charge est complexe, et encore plus difficile dans les pays en développement du fait de la rareté des ressources diagnostiques et thérapeutiques.

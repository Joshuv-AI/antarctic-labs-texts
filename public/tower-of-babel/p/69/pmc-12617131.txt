OA2067 Fall prevention programs: A resource-efficient approach for aging populations in rural Thailand
Authors: U Suragarn, G Pfaff, C Tharakitinon, N Wuddhipreecha, E A Schecter, D M Shafer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFalls are the second leading cause of unintentional injury-related deaths globally, causing approximately 684,000 deaths annually, with over 80% occurring in low- and middle-income countries. In rural Thailand, older adults are especially vulnerable due to limited access to healthcare, rehabilitation services, and safe home environments. Despite their impact, falls are often overlooked in injury prevention, particularly in resource-limited settings.ObjectivesThis initiative aimed to reduce fall-related risks among older adults in rural Thailand through: (1) training Village Health Volunteers (VHVs) in the use of the CDC’s STEADI tool for fall risk screening and early intervention; (2) equipping VHVs to conduct home safety assessments and implement personalized fall prevention strategies; and (3) promoting social connection and health literacy through community activities, including intergenerational fall prevention fairs.ResultsTwelve VHVs completed online STEADI-based training adapted to the local context. Over one month, they conducted 439 home visits across rural Chiang Mai, identifying risks and delivering targeted interventions. Two fall prevention fairs, held in collaboration with international high school students, encouraged intergenerational engagement and public awareness. The program improved perceptions of safety, increased health knowledge, and reinforced VHVs’ roles as trusted public health agents.ConclusionTraining VHVs in evidence-based fall prevention is a scalable, cost-effective strategy to reduce injuries and promote healthy aging in low-resource communities. This model highlights the value of early intervention, local leadership, and social connection in strengthening community resilience.Key messages• Empowering Village Health Volunteers with fall prevention training and tools like STEADI enables early intervention, home safety assessments, and injury reduction among older adults in limited-resource rural communities.• Community engagement through intergenerational activities and fall prevention fairs fosters social connection, combats isolation, and promotes emotional well-being among older adults in resource-limited settings.TopicFall prevention, healthy aging, social connection

Clinical and Economic Burden of Pneumococcal Disease Due to Serotypes Contained in the 20-Valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine in the Adult Population in France
Authors: Euan Dawson, Stéphane Fiévez, Emmanuelle Blanc, Jeffrey Vietri, Mark Rozenbaum, Liem Binh Luong Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Streptococcus pneumoniae causes invasive pneumococcal disease and pneumonia. This study models the burden of adult pneumococcal disease in France. Over a 5-year period, approximately 630 000 cases and €3.1 billion of costs were attributable to pneumococcus, mostly noninvasive disease, with nearly 80% due to serotypes included in 20-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine.

Streptococcus pneumoniae causes invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD), such as bacteremia and meningitis, and noninvasive disease, primarily nonbacteremic pneumococcal pneumonia, all associated with high morbidity and mortality among adults [1]. The incidence of pneumococcal disease (PD) is higher among the elderly and individuals with chronic medical or immunocompromising conditions, but the burden is largely underestimated due to diagnostic and surveillance limitations, particularly for pneumococcal community-acquired pneumonia (pCAP), which is often incompletely microbiologically documented and may present atypically [1]. Incidence rates of PD among French adults have increased sharply in recent years, returning to pre–COVID-19 levels [2].

Over 100 pneumococcal serotypes have been identified; currently licensed pneumococcal vaccines target those responsible for most of the disease burden [3]. Pneumococcal serotype epidemiology has evolved in response to indirect effects from pediatric pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV) campaign, suppressing carriage and transmission of vaccine serotypes in children and reducing incidence of PD among adults [4]. However, adults still carry and transmit pneumococcus [5], highlighting the need for direct protection through adult vaccination.

Until 2023, France recommended vaccination only for adults with at-risk conditions, with a sequential schedule combining 13-valent PCV (PCV13) and 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine [6]. Consequently, pneumococcal vaccine uptake in France has been low with only 9.9% of the at-risk adult population fully vaccinated [7].

France simplified its adult recommendation guidelines in 2023, replacing the sequence with 20-valent PCV (PCV20) [6], and recently expanded the program to include all adults aged ≥65 years [8]. PCV20, providing coverage against 20 clinically relevant serotypes, is a third generation of PCVs which demonstrated effectiveness against IPD and pneumonia [9, 10]. In July 2025, PCV21 also obtained a nonpreferential recommendation with PCV20 [11]. Despite this policy change, vaccine coverage remains low, and the burden of PD continues to be substantial. This study aims to model the total clinical and economic burden of PD in adults in France, and to estimate the proportion attributable to PCV20 serotypes.

METHODS

Model Description

A Markov model incorporating a 5-year time horizon considered the entire adult population of France, stratified by age and risk factors as defined by the French National Immunization Technical Advisory Board [12].

Age- and risk-specific incidence rates of IPD and noninvasive disease (inpatient and outpatient community-acquired pneumonia [CAP]) were multiplied by population sizes to estimate total disease cases. Cases were then apportioned to PCV20 type using the latest serotype coverage data for IPD and CAP. Age- and risk-specific case-fatality rates (CFRs) and medical costs were applied to estimate deaths and costs. Annual cases, deaths, and medical costs were estimated for years 1–5 and accumulated to determine the 5-year clinical and economic burden of PCV20-type PD among French adults.

Model Input Parameters

Population size was derived from the French National Statistics Bureau (INSEE), and the distribution according to risk was calculated using data from a claims database study [13, 19]. Incidence rates, CFRs, and medical costs of bacteremia, meningitis, and inpatient CAP were obtained from a French claims database study using data from 2013 to 2019 [1]. To avoid double-counting of bacteremic pneumonia, inpatient CAP rates were downwardly adjusted [20]. Outpatient CAP incidence and costs were obtained from a retrospective cohort study, while it was assumed that no mortality was associated with outpatient cases [17]. General population mortality due to any cause was modeled using data from a national statistics database [18]. Separate serotype distributions were used for IPD and CAP; IPD coverage was from recent French surveillance, and CAP data were from a cohort study of French adults [14, 15]. The distributions remained constant over the time horizon since PCV13 herd effects have been fully realized while PCV20 has not yet been recommended for pediatrics.

Analysis

In the base case, inputs were combined to determine total disease cases, deaths, and medical costs due to any pneumococcal serotype and those due to serotypes in PCV20, accumulated over a 5-year horizon.

A sensitivity analysis around the proportion of pCAP attributable to PCV20-type disease was performed to account for potential uncertainty. An upper bound of 23.9% was obtained from the recent study in Spain [21], while a lower bound of 11% was used from a previous CAP’HOSPI interim analysis [22].

Clinical and Economic Burden of Pneumococcal Disease Due to PCV20-Type Disease in Adults Aged 18–99 Years in France Over a 5-year Time Horizon

Clinical Burden

Over 5 years, the estimated number of PD cases in the French adult population was 626 740, with 488 126 (78%) cases attributable to serotypes in PCV20. There were 24 338 IPD cases, of which 15 327 (63%) were due to PCV20 serotypes. The burden from pCAP cases was considerably higher, with an estimated 602 402 inpatient and outpatient cases, of which 472 799 (78%) were due to PCV20 serotypes. Beyond morbidity, the analysis estimated 49 129 disease-related deaths due to any serotype, with 37 853 (77%) attributable to PCV20 serotypes.

Financial Burden

The total direct medical cost of PD was estimated to be €3.1 billion, of which €2.4 billion (77%) was due to PCV20 serotypes (Table 2). Of the total medical costs associated with PCV20-type disease, around €0.2 billion (8%) were attributable to IPD episodes, and €2.2 billion (92%) was due to costs related to CAP, mostly from inpatient treatment.

Sensitivity Analysis

When the upper pCAP rate of 23.9% was considered, the total cases of inpatient and outpatient CAP attributed to PCV20 serotypes increased to 463 466 and 283 792 cases, respectively (Table 2). Disease-related deaths increased to 58 269 and the total medical costs to €3.7 billion. When the lower rate of 11% was used, 213 083 and 130 539 cases of inpatient and outpatient CAP, respectively, were attributed to PCV20-type cases. Disease-related deaths decreased to 28 245, and the total medical costs decreased to €1.8 billion.

DISCUSSION

This study presents the clinical and economic burden of PD in adults in France and the proportion attributable to PCV20 serotypes. Base-case results estimated that over 5 years, 626 740 cases of disease, 49 129 deaths, and €3.1 billion in direct medical costs were attributable to PD, with nearly 80% attributable to PCV20 serotypes. Most of the burden was from noninvasive disease with roughly 25 cases of CAP for each case of IPD, and costs due to CAP were more than 10 times those due to IPD. The high incidence of CAP and the frequency of pneumococcus as a primary pathogen drive this burden [23].

A key strength of this study was the use of recent data from the 2025 CAP’HOSPI study, which found that in CAP patients where pneumococcus was the causal pathogen, 78% of cases were caused by a PCV20 serotype. Previous studies estimating CAP burden due to vaccine serotypes have often relied on IPD distributions; however, recent advances have improved detection of serotypes in nonbacteremic pneumonia, increasing the evidence available regarding the CAP distribution. Given the significant burden of CAP compared to IPD, being able to use a serotype distribution specific to CAP increases the relevance and accuracy of these results.

The findings from this study support the recent policy recommending PCV20 in a single dose for the population aged ≥65 years, where there is a significant burden of disease associated with PCV20 serotypes. These results represent the maximum potential burden covered by serotypes in PCV20, with the real-world impact dependent on vaccine effectiveness and uptake. Recent real-world evidence of PCV20 suggests strong effectiveness against IPD and all-cause pneumonia among adults aged ≥65 years [10], although adult uptake in France was very low under the previous recommendation [7].

Limitations

This analysis has several limitations. Firstly, the assays used in CAP’HOSPI, while more sensitive than other methods, were not developed for surveillance and may fail to detect the pathogen in pneumonia cases, thereby underreporting the pneumococcal cases [24, 25]. Furthermore, those assays were limited to 24 serotypes, potentially underestimating the burden of pCAP due to additional serotypes. However, since the pCAP rate aligned with what has been observed in other studies [25], the difference may be minimal. Scenario analyses showed the clinical and economic burden due to PCV20 serotypes remained substantial whether the lower, upper, or base proportion was used, and lack of sensitivity to nonvaccine serotypes would not affect the estimates of burden due to types included in the assays. In addition, the current analysis was limited to the costs and consequences of pneumococcal disease, and did not incorporate vaccination costs or calculate cost-effectiveness, which has yet to be reported for French adults. Finally, this analysis focused on PCV20 serotypes since recent data for those serotypes were available from the CAP’HOSPI study. Future research is needed to assess the burden attributable to serotypes in other high-valent vaccines.

CONCLUSION

This study highlighted the considerable clinical and economic burden of PD in adults in France. Most of the burden is due to serotypes in PCV20, with CAP causing approximately 80% of the cases and economic costs.

Direct adult vaccination with PCV20 could reduce the burden of pneumococcal disease through the breadth of clinically relevant serotypes it protects against.

A unique method for one-lung ventilation in pediatric thoracic surgery: Suction catheter and single-lumen tube approach: A case report
Authors: Deema Siraj Abid, Hossam Al Oufi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Effective one-lung ventilation (OLV) ensures optimal surgical exposure and isolation of the operative lung. This case describes a novel technique for achieving OLV in pediatric thoracic surgery using a suction catheter with a single-lumen endotracheal tube (SLT). An 11-year-old girl presented with a 2-day history of hemoptysis. Chest CT showed bronchial wall thickening and multifocal nodular opacities in the right upper lobe, suggesting pulmonary hemorrhage. After standard ASA monitoring, arterial and central venous lines were placed. The patient was intubated with a size 6.0 SLT and a size 10 suction catheter, guided into the left main bronchus under fiberoptic bronchoscopy. Tube position was confirmed by auscultation and chest X-ray. Intraoperatively, adhesions indicated prior embolization. The right upper lobe bronchus and vessels were ligated with LigaSure. Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and imaging on day five showed no pneumothorax. This simple, effective method facilitates safe OLV in pediatric thoracic surgery.

Introduction

One-lung ventilation (OLV) is an essential technique in pediatric thoracic surgery, particularly during video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS).[1] VATS offers a minimally invasive approach that reduces postoperative pain, complications, and hospital stay.[2]

Effective OLV ensures optimal surgical exposure and isolation of the operative lung.[3] However, pediatric patients present unique challenges due to small airway anatomy and limited availability of appropriately sized equipment.[4]

Various modifications of OLV have been developed, including single-lumen tubes (SLTs) and alternative bronchial occlusion devices.[5] Despite these innovations, issues such as incomplete lung collapse, difficulty maintaining ventilation, and hypoxemia risk persist.[6]

This case report presents a simple and practical OLV technique in pediatric thoracic surgery that integrates a suction catheter with an SLT, offering a feasible solution where conventional devices may be unsuitable.

Case Presentation

An 11-year-old girl (BMI 16.2 kg/m2) presented with a 2-day history of hemoptysis. She had a known right upper-lobe bronchial artery-to-pulmonary artery vascular malformation, previously managed by multiple embolization’s. The most recent, performed in September 2024, was complicated by Onyx migration into the subclavian and brachial arteries, which required surgical removal after vascular surgery consultation.

On admission, the patient was alert, oriented, and not in respiratory distress, maintaining oxygen saturation of 97% on room air. Chest CT demonstrated bronchial wall thickening and multifocal nodular opacities in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe, consistent with pulmonary hemorrhage. Metallic artifacts from previous embolization were evident. Based on these findings, thoracoscopic right upper lobectomy was planned.

In the operating room, ASA standard monitoring was applied. Anesthesia was induced with intravenous propofol (2 mg/kg), fentanyl (2 µg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg). A left radial arterial line was placed for invasive blood pressure monitoring, a 5.5 F central venous catheter was inserted into the right internal jugular vein under ultrasound guidance, and two peripheral intravenous lines were established.

Intubation was performed using a McGrath video laryngoscope. A suction catheter was introduced and confirmed with a fiberoptic bronchoscope. Initial intubation with a 6.0 mm tube was unsuccessful; a 5.5 mm tube was then placed successfully. Fiberoptic bronchoscopy guided it into the left main bronchus for right lung isolation, confirmed by auscultation and chest X-ray. The patient was ventilated in volume-control mode (tidal volume 112 mL, peak airway pressure 29 cm H2O, respiratory rate 13/min, FiO2 75%, PEEP 3 cm H2O).

She was positioned in the left lateral decubitus position. Intraoperatively, vital signs remained stable (BP 100–115/55–60 mmHg, HR 119–120 bpm, SpO2 99%–100%). Dense pleural adhesions, consistent with previous embolization’s, were noted. The bronchus and vessels of the right upper lobe were ligated with LigaSure and clips, and the lobe was removed without complications. A suction catheter was used to clear bloody secretions throughout the procedure.

Before extubating, the right lung was suctioned, and the catheter withdrawn. The 5.5 mm tube was removed with the cuff inflated to aid clot removal. A clean 6.0 mm tube was reinserted for two-lung ventilation, confirmed by fiberoptic bronchoscopy. The patient was transferred to the PICU.

Postoperative arterial blood gas showed pH 7.4, PaCO2 47 mmHg, and PaO2 141 mmHg. She remained stable with no respiratory distress. By postoperative day five, imaging showed no pneumothorax. The patient tolerated a regular diet and was discharged in good condition with pulmonology and genetics follow-up.

Discussion

This case demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of using a suction catheter in conjunction with a single-lumen tube (SLT) for OLV in young patients. This novel methodology overcomes several of the issues associated with traditional procedures, including the lack of pediatric-sized double-lumen tubes (DLTs) or bronchial blockers and expertise in using bronchial blocker and double lumen tube.[57]

Using a single lumen endotracheal tube (ETT) and balloon-tipped bronchial blockers both have disadvantages, including the difficulty of achieving lung collapse, complexity of re-expansion, ease of dislodgement, and the frequent requirement for a flexible fiberoptic scope or fiberoptic intubation (FOI) equipment.

The suction catheter approach simplifies lung isolation, lowers the risk of airway trauma, and ensures enough surgical exposure.[8]

This dual capability allows for the maintenance of oxygen saturation, while successfully regulating airway secretions, making it especially useful in emergency settings or in pediatric patients with limited pulmonary reserve.[9] These benefits are consistent with the goals of OLV in pediatric thoracic surgery, which include achieving a quiet operative field, shielding the healthy lung from contamination, and maintaining adequate ventilation.[710] Furthermore, the suction catheter’s capacity to ease suctioning and flexibility to different bronchial diameters make it a viable option to more sophisticated devices.[10]

In comparison to other approaches, such as endobronchial intubation with SLTs or the use of classic bronchial blockers, the suction catheter approach is more straightforward and resource efficient.[11] It avoids the difficulties of achieving an appropriate seal and the potential of balloon dislodgement, which can jeopardize lung isolation and ventilation.[1213] Furthermore, it offers a cost-effective option in resource-constrained environments, where advanced equipment may not be easily available.[5] The technique’s usefulness is also backed by the successful use of similar approaches, such as urine catheters as bronchial blockers and modified SLT techniques.[1214] This demonstrates the adaptability of different OLV approaches in addressing the anatomical and physiological constraints specific to pediatric patients.

It is vital to combine anatomical knowledge, sizing and modification of existing equipment, and simulation training, while administering one-lung ventilation to pediatric patients.[15] More research and clinical trials are needed to confirm the efficacy and safety of this unique method in a wider range of pediatric surgical settings. Nonetheless, this case shows that using a suction catheter with an SLT is a realistic, efficient, and successful way to achieve OLV in pediatric thoracic surgery.

Conclusion

This case report describes a novel and practical approach to OLV in pediatric thoracic surgery that involves a suction catheter and an SLT. This procedure provides a practical, resource-conserving solution for lung isolation, resulting in excellent surgical conditions without the requirement for specialized equipment.

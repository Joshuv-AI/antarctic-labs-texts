Safety of Golimumab in Patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis, Psoriatic Arthritis, and Axial Spondyloarthritis: Results from a Real-World Canadian Setting
Authors: Regan Arendse, Proton Rahman, Philip Baer, Derek Haaland, Louis Bessette, Dalton Sholter, Meagan Rachich, Emmanouil Rampakakis, Anne Marilise Marrache, Allen J Lehman, Odalis Asin-Milan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis work aims to describe the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), malignancy, and mortality in real-world patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), or axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) treated with subcutaneous (SC) golimumab.MethodsThis post hoc analysis included patients treated with SC golimumab from the BioTRAC registry. Incidence rates (IR) per 100 patient-years (PYs) and time to onset of adverse events of special interest (AEoSI), including MACE, malignancies, mortality, serious AEs (SAEs), and serious infections (SIs), were assessed in subgroups based on age, sex, prior tumor necrosis factor inhibitor experience, smoking status, and baseline methotrexate and oral steroid use. All analyses were stratified by indication.ResultsOf 1231 patients included, 529 had RA, 281 had PsA, and 421 had axSpA. At baseline, mean patient age was 57.7, 52.8, and 45.7 years in the RA, PsA, and axSpA groups, respectively. Most patients with RA (76.2%) and PsA (53.7%); 40.9% of patients with axSpA were female. The IR (95% confidence interval) for MACE was 1.1 (0.6, 2.0) events/100 PYs in the RA group with no events in the PsA and axSpA groups. Malignancy IRs were 1.4 (0.8, 2.3), 0.4 (0.0, 1.3), and 1.0 (0.4, 2.1)/100 PYs. SAE incidence ranged from 7.6 (5.5, 10.3)/100 PYs in the PsA group to 11.4 (9.4, 13.6) in the RA group, and that of SIs from 1.3 (0.5, 2.7)/100 PYs in patients with PsA to 2.3 (1.4, 3.4) in patients with RA. IRs for mortality were 0.7 (0.3, 1.4; n = 7), 0.2 (0.0, 1.0; n = 1), and 0.3 (0.0, 1.1; n = 2)/100 PYs in RA, PsA, and axSpA, respectively. Older patients with RA had a significantly shorter time to MACE (p = 0.007).ConclusionsPatients with RA, PsA, and axSpA treated with SC golimumab in the real world had a low incidence of MACE, malignancy, and all-cause mortality, further confirming the safety of golimumab for the treatment of rheumatic diseases.Trial Registration Number and DateClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT00741793, August 22, 2008.Keywords: Adverse event, Axial spondyloarthritis, Golimumab, Incidence rate, Major adverse cardiovascular events, Malignancy, Psoriatic arthritis, Real-world study, Rheumatoid arthritis

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Real-world safety data from patients with rheumatic conditions initiating golimumab are limited.The study assessed the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), malignancy, mortality, and serious infections in a large observational cohort of patients in Canada with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), or axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) treated with subcutaneous (SC) golimumab.What was learned from the study?Rates of MACE among patients with active RA were low, and no cases of MACE were observed in patients with PsA and axSpA.Low rates of malignancy and mortality were seen among patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA.SC golimumab safety profile in routine clinical care was consistent across the RA, PsA, and axSpA indications, with no new safety findings.Findings from real-world evidence-based studies such as BioTRAC may have greater utility to practitioners and patients than those from the more stringently controlled phase 3 trials, aiding in shared treatment decision-making.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) are inflammatory rheumatic diseases that cause autoimmune/autoinflammatory-mediated joint inflammation leading to joint damage and deformities, pain, and disability [1, 2]. The introduction of biological therapies, including anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) agents to the treatment landscape in addition to improvements in therapeutic strategies (early treatment, treat-to-target, collaboration within healthcare teams, etc.) has, in part, improved clinical outcomes for patients with inflammatory arthritis [3, 4]. Immunomodulatory biologics inhibit specific inflammatory pathways implicated in the pathogenesis of inflammatory arthritis and offer more targeted treatment, a novel advantage over conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (csDMARDs) that act on broader targets [4].

Golimumab is a TNF inhibitor (TNFi) approved for treating RA, PsA, and axSpA [5]. Golimumab functions by blocking TNF-α activity implicated in inflammatory processes. Despite the known elevated risks of certain adverse events (AE), such as cardiovascular (CV) and infectious diseases, and elevated mortality in patients with inflammatory rheumatic diseases [6–8], golimumab has been demonstrated to be efficacious while maintaining a favorable safety profile compared with that of placebo for the treatment of all three conditions [9–12].

Accumulating real-world evidence continues to support the long-term safety profile of golimumab in the treatment of rheumatic conditions over time [13–20]. Based on the recent JAKi safety reports [21], it is important to assess the safety profile of golimumab with a particular focus on specific AEs. Therefore, the objective of this study was to describe the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), malignancy, and mortality in a large observational cohort of patients with RA, PsA, or axSpA who were treated with subcutaneous (SC) golimumab. Serious AEs (SAEs) and serious infections (SIs) in these populations were also assessed.

Methods

Study Design

This is a post hoc analysis of the Biological Treatment Registry Across Canada registry (BioTRAC; NCT00741793). Details on the BioTRAC study design and enrolled patient populations have been previously described [13–15]. Briefly, BioTRAC was a multicenter, prospective, observational study collecting real-world clinical, laboratory, safety, and patient-reported data from patients treated with infliximab or golimumab for RA or axSpA and infliximab, golimumab, or ustekinumab for PsA. Details of the registry development have been described previously [22]. The primary analyses of effectiveness and safety of infliximab, golimumab and ustekinumab from BioTRAC among patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA have been reported [13–15].

Data were collected at baseline and approximately every 6 months thereafter during the 2010–2017 treatment period to assess clinical, laboratory, safety, and patient-reported outcomes. In addition, treatment exposure (in this case golimumab injections since the last visit) as reported by patients, and treatment discontinuation as reported by physicians were also recorded on the study case report form. Patients were followed for up to 90 months with a mean time to study discontinuation of 44.1, 37.7, and 45.3 months in RA, axSpA, and PsA, respectively. In accordance with the observational nature of the registry, no protocol-defined interventions were performed for patient management, and all clinical decisions and treatments were based on routine practice and the judgment of the treating physicians.

Statistical Analysis

The present analysis utilized data from patients who initiated treatment with SC golimumab. One patient with RA who had received prior JAKi treatment was excluded. As the numbers of patients receiving infliximab and ustekinumab were low, comparisons of incidence rate (IR) among different treatments were not possible.

Baseline characteristics were assessed for all included patients. Baseline risk factors of interest were categorized by disease and were assessed descriptively with the mean and standard deviation (SD) and frequency distribution for continuous and categorical variables, respectively. The IR of AEs of special interest (AEoSI) was calculated as the number of events per 100 patient-years (PYs). AEoSI included MACE (defined as CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, or non-fatal stroke), malignancies (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer [NMSC]), all-cause mortality, SAE, and SI. Unadjusted IRs for these events, irrespective of causality to golimumab, were estimated in subgroups based on age (< 65 versus ≥ 65 years), sex, prior TNFi experience, smoking status (current smoking), baseline use of methotrexate (MTX), and baseline use of oral steroids. The impact of the aforementioned factors on time to AEoSI onset was assessed using the Kaplan–Meier estimator of the survival function and the log-rank test.

Ethical Approval

The BioTRAC study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethics approval was obtained from a central Research Ethics Board (REB) IRB Service, Ontario, Canada now known as Advarra, for private practices and from respective REBs for institutional sites: Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre REB, Winnipeg Clinical Ethics Committee, Mount Sinai Hospital REB, University of British Columbia REB, CHUS Centre de Recherche, Health Research Ethics Board of Alberta, Horizon Health Network REB, and Hôpital Maisonneuve-Rosemont. Prior to enrolment, patients were required to provide written informed consent to participate.

Results

A total of 1231 patients treated with SC golimumab were included in the analysis, of whom 529 had RA, 281 had PsA, and 421 had axSpA. At baseline, mean (SD) patient age was 57.7 (13.0), 52.8 (13.2) and 45.7 (13.3) years in the RA, PsA, and axSpA groups, respectively; analogously 31.6%, 18.1%, and 8.6% of patients were ≥ 65 years (Table 1). Most patients were female in the RA (76.2%) and PsA (53.7%) groups, as were just under half in the axSpA group (40.9%). Mean (SD) disease duration ranged from 6.0 years in the axSpA and PsA groups to 8.0 (8.3) years in the RA group. In all groups, disease activity scores were indicative of highly active disease, with mean (SD) CDAI of 26.1 (13.9) in the RA group, cDAPSA of 31.1 (18.7) in the PsA group, and BASDAI of 6.1 (2.1) in the axSpA group. A moderate impairment in physical function was noted across the respective groups, with mean (SD) HAQ-DI scores of 1.3 (0.7), 1.1 (0.7) and 1.0 (0.6). A small proportion of patients reported prior TNFi use in the RA (13.6%), PsA (22.1%), and axSpA (17.3%) groups. Proportions of patients who were current smokers were 20.0%, 18.9%, and 25.5%, in the respective disease groups. Baseline steroid use was reported by most patients in the RA (32.9%) and PsA (21.0%) groups, versus only a minority of patients in the axSpA group (12.1%). MTX use at baseline was reported by 67.3%, 61.2%, and 9.7% of patients in the RA, PsA, and axSpA groups, respectively.

Incidence Rates of Adverse Events of Special Interest

IRs of AEoSI were evaluated over 1064, 539, and 675 PYs in the RA, PsA, and axSpA groups, respectively (Table 2). The IR (95% confidence interval, CI) of MACE was 1.1 (0.6, 2.0) events/100 PYs in the RA group with no events in the PsA and axSpA groups. The incidence of malignancy in the respective groups was 1.4 (0.8, 2.3), 0.4 (0.0, 1.3), and 1.0 (0.4, 2.1) events/100 PYs. All-cause mortality IRs (95% CI) were 0.7 (0.3, 1.4), 0.2 (0.0, 1.0), and 0.3 (0.0, 1.1) events/100 PYs among patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA, respectively. IRs (95% CI) of SAEs were 11.4 (9.4, 13.6) events/100 PYs for RA, 7.6 (5.5, 10.3) events/100 PYs for PsA, and 8.4 (6.4, 10.9) events/100 PYs for axSpA. SI events occurred at IRs (95% CI) of 2.3 (1.4, 3.4), 1.3 (0.5, 2.7), and 2.2 (1.2, 3.7) events/100 PYs in the respective groups.

Incidence of Adverse Events of Special Interest by Disease and Risk Factor

Overall, across all AEoSI, no new safety signals emerged. Unadjusted analyses demonstrated numerical differences in crude IRs of AEoSI for certain risk factors across the RA, PsA and axSpA groups (Table 3). Specifically, older patients (≥ 65 years) across all disease groups had higher IR/100 PYs of malignancy (range 1.2–5.7 versus 0.1–0.5), mortality (1.2–2.9 versus 0.0), SAE (19.2–27.2 versus 4.8–7.8), and SI (3.0–8.4 versus 0.0–1.9) compared with younger patients (< 65 years). Additionally, patients with RA aged ≥ 65 years also had a higher IR (95% CI) of MACE than those < 65 years (2.4 [1.0, 4.7] versus 0.5 [0.1, 1.4]).

Males in the RA group had higher IRs (95% CI) of MACE (1.8 [0.6, 4.1] versus 0.9 [0.4, 1.9]), SAE (14.7 [10.6, 19.9] versus 10.1 [8.0, 12.6]), and SI (3.2 [1.4, 6.0] versus 1.9 [1.1, 3.2]) than females. In the PsA group, the incidence (95% CI) of malignancy was higher among males than females (0.7 [0.1, 2.5] versus 0.0 [not calculable, NC]), whereas IRs of SAE (9.3 [5.9, 13.9] versus 6.2 [3.7, 9.8]) and SI (2.4 [0.9, 5.3] versus 0.3 [0.0, 1.9]) were higher among females. IRs (95% CI) of malignancy (1.4 [0.5, 3.0] versus 0.4 [0.0, 2.3]) were higher among males than females with axSpA, while the incidence of SI was higher for females (3.8 [1.7, 7.2] versus 1.4 [0.5, 3.0]). Across disease groups, mortality rates for females ranged between 0.4 and 0.9 and 0.0 to 0.2 for males.

Current smokers versus non-smokers in the RA group had higher incidence (95% CI) of SAE (14.3 [9.6, 20.5] versus 10.6 [8.6, 13.1]) and SI (4.4 [2.0, 8.4] versus 1.8 [1.0, 2.9]). In the PsA group, current smokers compared with non-smokers had higher IRs (95% CI) of malignancy (1.1 [0.0, 6.2] versus 0.2 [0.0, 1.3]), mortality (1.1 [0.0, 6.2] versus 0.2 [0.0, 1.3]), and SAE (13.4 [6.9, 23.4] versus 6.5 [4.4, 9.4]). Higher IRs (95% CI) of SAE (12.6 [7.9, 19.1] versus 7.0 [4.9, 9.8]), and SI (3.4 [1.3, 7.5] versus 1.8 [0.8, 3.4]) were found among current smokers than non-smokers in the axSpA group.

The crude IR (95% CI) of malignancy was higher for TNFi-experienced patients with RA compared with those who were TNFi-naïve (3.2 [0.9, 8.1] versus 1.2 [0.6, 2.1]). Among patients with PsA and prior TNFi experience, the incidence (95% CI) of malignancy (1.9 [0.2, 6.8] versus 0.0 [NC]), SAE (15.1 [8.6, 24.5] versus 5.8 [3.7, 8.5]) and SI (4.7 [1.5, 11.0] versus 0.5 [0.1, 1.7]) was higher than for those who were TNFi-naïve. Similar trends were observed among patients with axSpA defined by TNFi treatment history. IRs of mortality (0.3 [0.0, 1.7] versus 0.0, [NC]), SAE (9.7 [6.6, 13.6] versus 4.3 [2.0, 8.2) and SI (2.1 [0.8, 4.4] versus 0.0 [NC]) in the PsA group were higher among patients with than without baseline MTX use, with similar trends found among patients with axSpA. Patients with PsA receiving steroids at baseline had a higher IR (95% CI) of SAE (11.5 [6.0, 20.1]) than those without baseline steroids (6.7 [4.5, 9.6]), and higher IRs of malignancy (6.6 [1.4, 19.2] versus 0.6 [0.2, 1.6)], mortality (2.2 [0.1, 12.2] versus 0.2 [0.0, 0.9]), and SAE (17.5 [7.6, 34.5] versus 7.8 [5.8, 10.3]) were found with than without baseline steroid use in the axSpA group.

Among patients with RA, the mean (95% CI) time to MACE was significantly shorter among older patients (< 65 versus ≥ 65 years: 88.8 [87.6–90.1] versus 82.0 [77.7–86.2] months, p = 0.007, Fig. 1A), with no impact of sex, prior TNFi experience, smoking status, or baseline use of steroids or MTX (Fig. 1B–F). Across all disease groups, no significant differences were observed in the time to malignancy, death, SAE, and SI for any of the risk factors of interest (data not shown).

Discussion

Safety studies of JAKi have concluded an elevated risk of MACE and malignancies compared with TNFi among patients with RA [21] prompting the present assessment of golimumab safety specifically focusing on the incidence of CV events, malignancies and infection across rheumatic diseases within the Canadian real-world setting. Findings showed low rates of MACE with SC golimumab among patients with RA (1.1 events/100 PY), and no cases of MACE reported in the PsA and axSpA populations. Rates of malignancy were also low across all rheumatic conditions (0.4–1.4 events/100 PY), as were mortality rates (0.2–0.7 events/100 PY). Incidence of infections per 100 PY ranged from 1.3 events in the PsA population to 2.3 in RA. Considering specific risk factors for AEs, older patients in all three populations had higher rates of malignancy. Older patients with RA also had a higher incidence and a significantly shorter time to MACE. Patients with prior TNFi treatment, and those with concomitant use of MTX or steroids had a numerically higher incidence of malignancy, while time to MACE was shorter among steroid users.

The results of this observational study support previous findings on golimumab safety in a real-world setting. In a prospective observational study of patients with RA (N = 474), PsA (N = 501) and ankylosing spondylitis (AS, N = 483) treated with SC golimumab conducted in Germany (April 2010-September 2015), during a 2 year follow-up period, rates of cardiac disorders (ranging from 1.1 in AS to 2.5 in RA) and malignancy (0.7 in AS to 2.0 in RA) were similar to those in this analysis [17]. Likewise, among 108 patients with RA with 2 years of SC golimumab treatment in a retrospective study in Taiwan (January 2014 to June 2019), IRs/100 PYs (95% CI) of serious infection, new malignancy, and all-cause mortality were 2.90 (0.04–0.15), 0.6 (− 0.01 to 0.04) and 0.6 (− 0.01 to 0.04) [16]. Analysis of effectiveness and safety of intravenous (IV) golimumab with and without MTX through 1 year from the AWARE observational study of 680 patients with RA conducted at 88 clinical sites in the United States, demonstrated incidence (95% CI) of serious infections, malignancies and deaths of 7.0 (4.4–10.6), 1.0 (0.2–2.8) and 0.6 (0.1–2.3) events/100 PYs with golimumab alone [23]. A comparison of AE rates through 1 year of IV golimumab compared with the TNFi infliximab showed comparable incidence of infections and malignancy with both agents [24].

The rates of AEoSI in this study were broadly consistent with those of other TNF antagonists reported in RA. An analysis of long-term incidence of safety outcomes among patients with RA initiating the TNFi adalimumab in the CorEvitas registry found IR (95% CI)/100 PYs for congestive heart failure (0.15, 0.07–0.31), malignancy (0.64,0.50–0.84), serious infections (1.86, 1.50–2.31) and all-cause mortality (0.33, 0.24–0.48) were low and did not vary substantially by duration of adalimumab exposure, which ranged from ≤ 1 to > 7 years, with majority of patients (84%) receiving adalimumab for < 3 years [25]. The Swedish Biologics Register reported no significant differences in mortality rates following treatment of RA with either of infliximab, adalimumab or etanercept (1.1 deaths per 100 PYs) [26], and no statistically significant differences were found in the incidence of solid cancers (IR/100 PYs: 0.77–0.86) and serious infections (5.35–5.56) among patients with RA following exposure to these TNFi in the British Society for Rheumatology Biologics Register [27, 28]. In CorEvitas, IRs (95% CI)/100 PYs for CV events and malignancies with TNFis including certolizumab that is typically prescribed as a later line of therapy in older patients with RA with longer disease duration and more active disease ranged from 2.05 (1.27–3.30) to 2.08 (1.29–3.34), and 1.71 (1.01–2.88) to 2.42 (1.56–3.75), respectively [29]. Real-world data on CV and cancer risks in patients with PsA receiving TNFi or anti-interleukin (IL)-12/23 therapies are limited, with one study utilizing Optum Clinformatics and IBM MarketScan (2009–2015) reporting no substantial differences in the IR of MACE among patients with psoriasis or PsA initiating the IL-12/23i ustekinumab (6.2 events/1000 PYs) compared with TNFi initiators (6.1 events/1000 PYs) [30].

One strength of our study is the relatively long duration of treatment exposure that may provide greater confidence in risk evaluation as compared with other studies [31, 32]. The observational nature of this study may provide more comprehensive information on the broader population of patients with RA in the real world, including those with significant comorbidities. Both of the aforementioned attributes allowed us to study the safety of golimumab in the presence of risk factors across regional variations in patient profiles, practice patterns, and local reimbursement policies impacting access to care over 16 years in patients across Canada. Additionally, to our knowledge, BioTRAC is one of the few registries to assess the safety of TNFi treatment across rheumatic diseases. Limitations of our study include the lack of a control group and the inclusion of patients who were mostly biologic-naïve. As the interval between assessments conducted in routine care was relatively large, the possibility of AE underreporting should be considered, particularly for non-serious AEs. Incidence of AEs across patient subgroups may reflect confounding due to differences in disease duration, levels of inflammation, socioeconomic status and other factors, measured or unmeasured. Larger studies are required to evaluate the incidence of MACE in PsA and axSpA for which no events were observed in the present study. It remains to be clarified whether the variable IRs observed across RA, PsA and axSpA are due to differences between these rheumatic diseases or lower numbers of patients with PsA and axSpA included in the analysis. Furthermore, the incidence of AEs across risk factors of interest was assessed descriptively and the independent effect of risk factors was not assessed. Finally, since expected AE rates were not available, standardized IRs were not calculated, and caution should be exercised when contrasting findings of the present analysis with existing studies.

Conclusions

Overall, this analysis of data from the BioTRAC real-world registry found golimumab to have a safety profile broadly consistent across RA, PsA and axSpA and with previous studies. No new safety concerns were identified and the rates of MACE and malignancy were in line with other real-world studies in the North American setting. The addition of these real-world data from a large sample of Canadian patients with rheumatic conditions provides further reassurance to healthcare providers who are initiating and monitoring golimumab in their patients.

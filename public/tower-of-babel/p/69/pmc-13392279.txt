Functionalization-driven modulation of electronic structure and quantum capacitance in o-MXene Y2VC2Tx (Tx = O2, F2, and OF) for high-performance energy storage
Authors: Navid Mokhtaripoor, Peiman Amiri, Amir Aliakbari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, first-principles calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) combined with the pseudopotential method are employed to investigate and compare the electronic properties, structural stability, and energy-storage potential of novel two-dimensional binary transition metal systems, Y2VC2Tx (Tx = O2, F2, and OF) o-MXenes. Structural, mechanical, and dynamical analyses confirm the potential stability of all Y2VC2Tx o-MXenes. Among them, the oxygen-terminated Y2VC2O2 layer exhibits superior energetic and mechanical stability compared to the pristine Y2VC2, fluorinated Y2VC2F2, and mixed-functionalized Y2VC2OF structures. The calculated B/G ratios for Y2VC2, Y2VC2F2, Y2VC2O2, and Y2VC2OF are above 1.75, indicating ductile mechanical characteristics in these systems. Electronic structure analyses conducted using different exchange-correlation schemes (GGA and GGA+HSE06) reveal that the studied o-MXenes behave metallically within the GGA approximation, while under the GGA+HSE06 functional, the materials Y2VC2, Y2VC2F2, Y2VC2OF, and Y2VC2O2 exhibit energy gaps of 1.62, 2.94, 2.27, and 2.48 eV, respectively, confirming their semiconducting nature. The pseudogap positioned closer to the Fermi level in Y2VC2O2 implies higher electronic robustness compared to other variants. In aqueous media, oxygen-terminated surfaces exhibit the strongest electrochemical activity, while pristine Y2VC2 shows the weakest response. Y2VC2O2 and Y2VC2OF consistently function as anodic electrodes, whereas Y2VC2 and Y2VC2F2 act as cathodic electrodes under aqueous conditions. In ionic/organic environments, Y2VC2, Y2VC2O2, and Y2VC2OF maintain anodic behavior, while Y2VC2F2 remains cathodic. Moreover, Y2VC2O2 demonstrates remarkable quantum capacitance and highly tunable electrochemical performance, largely driven by surface functionalization. These enhancements significantly improve the energy-storage capabilities of the materials, positioning o-MXenes as strong candidates for next-generation supercapacitors and battery electrodes.

Introduction

The discovery of graphene has profoundly transformed materials science, inspiring extensive exploration of a wide range of two-dimensional (2D) materials. Among these, MXenes have emerged as one of the most promising families due to their unique structural architectures and outstanding physicochemical properties. First introduced by Gogotsi and colleagues in 2011, MXenes were synthesized through the selective removal of aluminum from the layered ternary carbide Ti3AlC2 via hydrofluoric acid etching, yielding the first recognized Mxene Ti3C21. This method was later extended to other MAX phases, demonstrating its versatility in producing diverse MXene compositions2. What sets MXenes apart from other 2D materials is their exceptional integration of properties, including high electrical conductivity3,4, chemical stability5,6, remarkable charge-storage capacitance7, and large surface area8. These features have enabled MXenes to play key roles in advanced technologies such as supercapacitors9,10, photocatalytic water splitting11, high-performance energy storage systems12,13, wireless communication devices14, biomedical applications15, sensing technologies16, and lithium-ion batteries17,18. MXenes are typically generated by the chemical exfoliation of MAX phases, layered ternary carbides and nitrides with the general formula Mn+1AXn, where n = 1–319,20. Here, M denotes an early transition metal, A represents elements from groups IIIA or IVA (e.g., Al, Si)13,14,21–23, and X refers to carbon and/or nitrogen. During etching, the A-layer is selectively removed, cleaving M-A bonds and forming 2D materials with the general formula Mn+1XnTx, where Tx corresponds to surface terminations such as O, F, or OH. These terminal groups substantially influence the surface chemistry, electronic behavior, and electrochemical performance of MXenes24. Structurally, MXenes can be categorized by their stoichiometric ratios into four major formula types: M2X, M3X2, M4X3, and M5X4, corresponding respectively to 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, and 5:4 metal-to-carbon (or nitrogen) ratios25. When two transition metals are integrated, more complex layered configurations emerge, generally expressed as M′M″X (1:1:1), M′2M″X2 (2:1:2), M′2M″2X3 (2:2:3), and M′2M″3X4 (2:3:4), each representing progressively ordered heterostructural arrangements of distinct metallic components. While early MXene studies largely focused on single transition metal compositions, recent research has explored out-of-plane ordered double transition metal MAX phases, such as Cr2TiAlC2, Cr2VAlC2, Mo2TiAlC2, and Mo2ScAlC2. These discoveries pave the way for synthesizing a broader class of ordered MXenes (o-MXenes). Through first-principles calculations, Anasori et al. demonstrated the structural stability of various o-MXenes both in ordered and solid-solution forms, emphasizing their experimental feasibility26. Recent studies have further deepened insight into yttrium-based MXenes. Aliakbari et al. (2022) examined Y2C, Y3C2, and Y4C3 compounds and confirmed metallic conductivity across all phases, highlighting their potential for electronic applications27. A subsequent 2023 study expanded upon their optical properties, enhancing the understanding of yttrium carbide MXenes and their bulk counterparts28. Amoudeh et al. extended this research by evaluating multi-elemental MXenes, such as YScX (X = C, N) and YMXTₓ (M = Ti, Zr; X = C, N; Tₓ = H, O, F), revealing high structural stability and tunable electronic features governed by metal composition and surface functionalization29–31. Further contributions were made by Liu et al. (2025), who investigated pristine and functionalized Nb2C MXenes using DFT calculations. Their work unveiled a pronounced sensitivity of electrode performance to both functional group type and electrochemical environment. Notably, Nb2CO2 behaved as a symmetric electrode, whereas Nb2CF2 exhibited a reversible shift between cathodic and anodic roles depending on aqueous or ionic media32,33. Among MXenes with topologically nontrivial characteristics, compounds such as Mo2HfC2O2 and W2HfC2O2, containing hafnium and group-VI transition metals, stand out for their large band gaps and energetically favorable oxygen-terminated surfaces34,35. Their band structures exhibit time-reversal symmetry, promoting helical edge states, a hallmark of quantum spin Hall insulators36,37. Bo Wen et al. successfully synthesized two high-purity o-MAX phases, Mo2ValC2 and Mo2V2AlC3, through solid-state reaction routes and confirmed their ordered crystal structures. The corresponding o-MXenes, Mo2VC2Tx and Mo2V2C3Tx, were subsequently prepared via selective HF etching of o-MAX precursors38. In parallel, Yamin Feng et al. demonstrated through DFT simulations that oxygen-functionalized MXene electrodes exhibit improved interaction with Zn2+ ions, leading to exceptional electrochemical performance. Their assembled asymmetric hybrid Zn-ion microsupercapacitors (AZIHMSCs) delivered high energy density (130.6 µWh.cm−2), outstanding cycling stability (93.29% after 5000 cycles), and excellent flexibility (98.43% retention at 120° bending)39. A representative of o-MXene, Mo2TiC2, has previously exhibited remarkable catalytic performance40. In 2025, we successfully expanded this compositional family by synthesizing a new member, Mo2VC2Tx, thereby broadening the structural diversity and potential applications of o-MXenes41. Ordered MXenes have thus emerged as multifunctional materials, capable of not only exceptional catalysis but also enhancing metal properties when employed as performance-boosting additives, greatly diversifying their role in advanced material design42,43. However, one of the key challenges remains the complexity of synthesizing high-purity ordered MAX precursors, which limits experimental implementation44. Overcoming these synthetic barriers has become a critical focus, particularly in achieving precise structural order and optimizing selective etching methodology. It is also worth emphasizing that our previous work on YLaX (X = B, C, N) dealt with a different category of yttrium-based two-dimensional materials characterized by distinct compositional and bonding features. In contrast, the present investigation centers on ordered double-transition-metal hexagonal MXenes (Y2VC2Tₓ) with various surface terminations, where the incorporation of vanadium atoms and functional groups (O2, F2, and OF) induces markedly different electronic interactions and surface chemistry. Therefore, this study explores a new structural configuration of MXenes and offers deeper insight into how surface functionalization governs their electronic properties and quantum capacitance dimensions that were not addressed in the previously reported YLaX systems.

Despite significant progress in the theoretical and experimental exploration of MXenes, a unified and systematic understanding of yttrium-based binary MXenes with surface functionalization, particularly the ordered double-transition-metal o-MXenes Y2VC2Tx (Tx = O2, F2, and OF), remains insufficient. The novelty of this work lies in providing a comprehensive first-principles framework that enables a direct and comparative assessment of their structural stability, mechanical behavior, and electronic characteristics within the hexagonal phase. Going beyond conventional descriptors, this study places particular emphasis on quantum capacitance and surface charge-storage properties, parameters that are directly linked to electrochemical performance and energy-storage functionality. By employing density functional theory, we integrate structural, mechanical, electronic, vibrational, and electrochemical analyses into a coherent and consistent picture. This holistic approach elucidates the structure-property-function relationships in Y2VC2Tx o-MXenes and highlights their versatile potential for advanced electronic devices and next-generation energy-storage technologies.

Computational method

All first-principles calculations were carried out using the QUANTUM-ESPRESSO/PWSCF package based on the DFT method with norm-conserving pseudopotentials45. Valence electron wavefunctions were expanded in a plane-wave basis, and the exchange-correlation energy was treated using the GGA-PBE functional46. To improve the accuracy of electronic and magnetic descriptions in metallic systems, the HSE06 hybrid functional (GGA+HSE06) was additionally employed. The band structures and phonon dispersion curves were computed along the high-symmetry path (Γ-M-K-Γ) in the Brillouin zone, as illustrated in Fig. 1, using a 12 × 12 × 1 Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh47,48. A plane-wave energy cutoff of 60 Ry and an energy convergence threshold of 1 × 10− 7 Ry were employed, with Methfessel-Paxton smearing (0.01 Ry) applied to improve convergence49. All structures were fully optimized until atomic forces were below 1 mRy/Bohr, and a vacuum spacing of approximately ~ 15Å was introduced along the out-of-plane direction to eliminate interactions between periodic layers in the 2D models. The following valence configurations were used: 2s22p2 (C), 4d15s2 (Y), 2s22p4 (O), 2s22p5 (F), and 3d34s2 (V). The elastic properties of Y2VC2Tx were evaluated using the Birch and Charpin method50,51, and the corresponding elastic constants Cij were determined via the thermo_pw package52 using the volume-conserving strain approach53. Phonon and dynamical stability analyses were performed through density functional perturbation theory (DFPT)54. Structural optimization was further refined by fitting the total energy to the Murnaghan equation of state55. In this work, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{H}_{V}$$\end{document}​ is evaluated using the widely adopted semi-empirical Chen model, where G and B denote the shear and bulk moduli, respectively. This formulation highlights that Vickers hardness is more strongly governed by shear resistance than by resistance to uniform volumetric compression. As summarized in Table 5, Y2VC2​O2​ exhibits the highest bulk modulus (B = 100.81 GPa) and Young’s modulus (Y = 116.38 GPa), indicating the stiffest lattice and greatest resistance to elastic deformation among the investigated o-MXenes. However, its shear modulus (G = 40.41 GPa) is lower than that of Y2​VC2​F2​ (G = 43.71 GPa). Consequently, despite its higher stiffness, Y2​VC2​O2​ shows a lower predicted Vickers hardness (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{H}_{V}$$\end{document} = 2.95 GPa) compared with Y2​VC2​F2​, which exhibits the highest hardness among all systems (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{H}_{V}$$\end{document} ​ = 4.99 GPa). From a physical perspective, oxygen termination enhances bond rigidity and resistance to volumetric compression through strong p-d hybridization and increased cohesive strength, leading to elevated B and Y. In contrast, fluorine termination more effectively increases resistance to shear deformation, which directly controls indentation hardness. Therefore, Y2VC2​O2 can be regarded as the most mechanically robust and elastically stiff framework, whereas Y2​VC2​F2 possesses the highest Vickers hardness. This distinction confirms that stiffness and hardness do not necessarily follow the same trend, as they probe fundamentally different mechanical responses.

The bulk modulus describes a material’s resistance to volume compression, whereas the shear modulus characterizes its resistance to shape deformation. Young’s modulus represents the stiffness under uniaxial tensile loading69–71. As summarized in Table 5, oxygen termination markedly increases both the bulk and Young’s moduli of Y2VC2, leading to enhanced stiffness and overall mechanical robustness. In contrast, fluorine termination yields the highest Vickers hardness, which originates from the increased shear resistance captured by the employed hardness model. These findings demonstrate that stiffness and hardness do not necessarily follow the same trend and are governed by different combinations of elastic moduli72,73. As shown in Table 5, Y2VC2O2 exhibits a higher Vickers hardness than pristine Y2VC2, whereas Y2VC2F2 displays the highest Vickers hardness among all studied systems. Although Y2VC2O2 does not achieve the maximum hardness, it possesses the highest bulk and Young’s moduli, reflecting superior stiffness and resistance to elastic deformation. This indicates that oxygen termination primarily enhances the mechanical robustness and structural rigidity of Y2VC2-based o-MXenes rather than maximizing their Vickers hardness.

The ratio of bulk modulus to shear modulus (B/G) provides insight into a material’s ductility or brittleness. The B/G value of 1.75 serves as the critical threshold: materials with B/G > 1.75 tend to be ductile, while those with B/G < 1.75 are brittle73. The calculated B/G ratios for Y2VC2, Y2VC2O2, Y2VC2OF, and Y2VC2F2 are above 1.75, indicating that these materials are ductile.

Dynamical stability

Next, the dynamic stability of the investigated compounds was assessed using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) by calculating their phonon dispersion spectra. In a crystal unit cell containing N atoms, there exist 3 N phonon branches. Accordingly, the compound Y2VC2 with five atoms in the unit cell possesses 15 phonon branches, whereas the compounds Y2VC2O2, Y2VC2OF, and Y2VC2F2, each consisting of seven atoms, exhibit 21 phonon branches. Among these, the three lowest-energy branches at the Γ point correspond to acoustic phonon modes, while the remaining higher-energy branches represent optical modes. Furthermore, phonon frequency modes extending to higher energies indicate stronger interatomic bonding and therefore greater mechanical rigidity. Consequently, compounds with broader high-frequency ranges are considered more stable.

As shown in Fig. 3, the monolayers Y2VC2F2, Y2VC2O2, and Y2VC2OF exhibit frequency gaps, with the specific ranges summarized in Table 6. Within these gaps, vibrational modes and the propagation of heat (phonons) are prohibited in the Y2VC2Tx compounds. Importantly, no imaginary phonon frequencies are observed throughout the Brillouin zone, indicating that all phonon modes are real and stable. Therefore, the absence of imaginary frequencies confirms the dynamic stability of these compounds, suggesting that their experimental synthesis is feasible.

The results of “Structural stability” and “Dynamical stability” Section provide important theoretical insight into the potential feasibility of Y2VC2-based o-MXenes. By analogy with experimentally synthesized MXene systems, Y2VC2Tₓ (where Tₓ = O2, F2, and OF) could, in principle, be obtained through the selective removal of the A layer from a suitable ordered MAX-phase precursor, such as the hypothetical compound Y2VAlC2. Electronic properties

To investigate the electronic characteristics of Y2VC2Tx (Tx = O2, F2, and OF) o-MXene, we analyzed their total and partial density of states (TDOS and PDOS) in the hexagonal structural phase. Figures 4 and 5 present the TDOS and PDOS diagrams of Y2VC2Tx o-MXene, evaluated using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and the more precise hybrid Heyd, Scuseria, and Ernzerhof (HSE06) approaches, within the energy interval of − 5 eV to 3 eV. In each case, the Fermi energy is set to zero and indicated by a red dashed line.

Figure 4 (green bars) shows that the mentioned compounds have a metallic nature in the GGA approximation because the density of states intersects the Fermi level. The results of Fig. 4 (blue bars) show that the use of the GGA+HSE06 functional changes the nature of the compounds from metallic to semiconducting, and the o-MXenes Y2VC2, Y2VC2F2, Y2VC2O2, and Y2VC2OF have energy gaps of 1.62, 2.94, 2.48, and 2.27 eV, respectively.

The TDOS reflects the number of accessible states at a given energy. Within the GGA approximation (Fig. 4), the state densities nearest the Fermi level are 3.96 states/eV for Y2VC2, 5.19 states/eV for Y2VC2F2, 3.29 states/eV for Y2VC2O2, and 5.68 states/eV for Y2VC2OF. This approximation shows that Y2VC2F2 Y2VC2OF have higher densities of states near the Fermi level, indicating stronger metallic properties compared to Y2VC2 and Y2VC2O2. The metallic behavior of all four monolayers in the GGA approximation arises from the broad distribution of states and the absence of a band gap around the Fermi level.

It is worth noting that the Fermi level separates the bonded from the unbonded states; when this level is close to the pseudogap, and on the bonding side, the system is considered stable74. The pseudogap, a deep valley between the bonded and unbonded states, plays a pivotal role in assessing structural stability75. For Y2VC2O2 MXene, the pseudogap is closer to the Fermi level compared to the other structures, which indicates that Y2VC2O2 is more stable than Y2VC2, Y2VC2F2, and Y2VC2OF structures. It is worth noting that the structural stability of the compound increases as the energy levels get closer to the Fermi level. This finding agrees well with the structural stability calculations in the previous “Structural stability” Section. Moreover, doping of the main functional groups strongly influences the density of states, leading to a noticeable increase in the DOS near the Fermi surface.

Within a (DOS)-based first-principles framework, the quantum capacitance represents the intrinsic electronic response of the electrode material to variations in the electrochemical potential. This response is governed primarily by the availability and distribution of electronic states in the vicinity of the Fermi level. Accordingly, the peaks observed in the quantum capacitance profile originate from characteristics of the electronic structure and from variations in the DOS induced by shifts of the Fermi level, rather than from explicit interfacial electrochemical processes. Therefore, the quantum capacitance calculated in this study is regarded as an intrinsic electronic descriptor for comparing the relative behavior of different surface terminations, rather than as a direct measure of the absolute electrochemical capacitance or real experimental performance. In the DOS-based quantum capacitance framework adopted in this work, the potential φ denotes a shift of the electronic chemical potential (Fermi level) relative to its equilibrium value. Accordingly, the voltage windows of − 0.6 to 0.6 V and − 1.2 to 1.2 V are used as representative integration ranges to sample different energy regions of the electronic density of states. Conclusions

In this study, a comprehensive first-principles investigation based on density functional theory (DFT) and pseudopotential methods was performed to explore the fundamental properties of novel two-dimensional Y2VC2Tx (Tx = O2, F2, and OF) o-MXenes. The results reveal that all o-MXenes configurations exhibit structural, mechanical, and dynamical stability, indicating a strong potential for experimental synthesis. Among the studied MXenes, Y2VC2O2​ demonstrates superior thermodynamic stability, as evidenced by its more negative formation energy and higher cohesive energy. Mechanical analysis indicates that the calculated B/G ratio for all studied MXenes is above 1.75, thus indicating ductile mechanical behavior in these systems. Furthermore, the bulk modulus follows the trend Y2VC2OF > Y2VC2 > Y2VC2F2 > Y2VC2O2, indicating that the Y2VC2OF configuration exhibits the greatest stiffness among the studied compounds. Phonon dispersion analysis reveals the absence of imaginary frequencies for all systems, thereby confirming their dynamic stability and supporting the feasibility of experimental realization. From the electronic perspective, all structures exhibit metallic characteristics under the GGA approximation; however, the inclusion of hybrid-functional corrections (GGA + HSE06) introduces finite band gaps near the Fermi level, signifying semiconductor-like behavior. The presence of a pseudo-gap around the Fermi level in Y2VC2O2 reflects stronger electronic interactions compared to other compounds. Partial density of states (PDOS) analysis indicates dominant contributions from the d orbitals for the yttrium and vanadium atoms and the p orbital for the carbon atom in their electrical conductivity. Moreover, surface functionalization substantially influences the quantum capacitance and electrochemical performance of these materials. Among the studied structures, Y2VC2O2​ exhibits the highest quantum capacitance and electrochemical activity in aqueous environments, whereas pristine Y2VC2 shows the lowest activity. Overall, the calculated quantum capacitance values confirm that the Y2VC2Tx​ family constitutes promising candidates for electrode materials (both anode and cathode) in next-generation supercapacitors and advanced energy-storage devices.

Frequency of fibromyalgia in a cohort of Turkish patients with lung cancer and its effect on pain, sleep quality, fatigue and quality of life
Authors: Akin Öztürk, Rekib Sacaklidir, Firat Ulutatar
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to examine the frequency of fibromyalgia (FM) in patients with lung cancer and evaluate its effect on patients’ pain, sleep quality, fatigue, and quality of life parameters. The study was designed as a prospective cross-sectional and a total of 116 lung cancer patients were included. FM classification was made according to the 2016 ACR criteria. All patients were evaluated using a visual analog scale, fibromyalgia impact questionnaire, multidimensional assessment of fatigue, EuroQol 5D scale, Jenkins sleep scale, and Beck depression inventory. Patients were divided into FM-positive and negative groups according to the presence of FM. Demographic and clinical parameters were investigated between the groups. FM was detected in 14 (12.2%) patients. The mean age of the patients was 62.2 ± 8.4 years. There was a male predominance in 93 (80.2%) patients. No statistical difference was found between the groups in terms of body mass index, age, symptom duration, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy history. A statistically significant difference was found between FM positive and negative groups in FIQ, multidimensional assessment of fatigue, JSS, EQ-5D, visual analog scale, and Beck depression inventory scores. FM is seen more frequently in patients with lung cancer and has a negative effect on sleep, quality of life, fatigue, and mental functions. We think that physicians should not ignore the presence of FM when treating patients diagnosed with lung cancer.

1. Introduction

Lung cancer is a disease that threatens public health and causes significant mortality and morbidity. It has become more important, especially with the increase in smoking. Therefore, globally, lung cancer is the most common cause of cancer-related deaths.[1] When the diagnosis is made, an advanced stage or metastasis is usually encountered. Therefore, patients have to deal with multiple problems. Some are caused by cancer itself, while others are unrelated to cancer. Pain is one of the most common complications. Physical and social limitations, sleep, and quality of life are also adversely affected in cancer patients due to pain.[2]

Fibromyalgia syndrome (FM) is a disease characterized by widespread pain, fatigue, and sleep problems. It affects 2% to 4% of society, and this rate reaches 15% in selected populations. The actual cause of FM is unclear. However, it is thought to be a disease associated with central sensitization diseases.[3] The pain presents in the form of burning and aching and sometimes with morning stiffness. Patients complain of feeling tired even after waking up. Most patients experience sleep disruptions and then have trouble falling asleep. However, there are few studies on the frequency of FM in cancer patients.[2,4,5] According to Akkaya et al, the frequency of FM was found to be 9.9% in the population with breast cancer.[2] Eyigor et al, on the other hand, found the frequency of FM to be 10.7% in patients diagnosed with cancer who were hospitalized. However, in this study, cancer subgroups of hospitalized patients were not specified. Therefore, we do not have any information about the frequency of FM in patients with lung cancer.[4] Interestingly, Dreyer et al found an increased risk of breast cancer in those who did not meet American college of rheumatology (ACR) criteria for FM.[6] As we know, there have been no studies in the literature showing the relationship between lung cancer and FM frequency.

Our primary aim in the present study is to examine the frequency of FM in patients diagnosed with lung cancer, and our secondary aim is to examine the characteristics of patients such as pain intensity, sleep quality, and depression and to inform our colleagues about this issue.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design and study population

The study was granted approval from the ethics committee (116.2017.123) and was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki principles. The study was designed as a prospective cross-sectional. Patients who applied to the oncology outpatient clinic between April 2022 and August 2022 and were diagnosed with lung cancer were included in the study. In addition, patients under follow-up due to lung cancer were invited to the hospital for the study. At least 3 months after chemotherapy and radiotherapy treatment and normal laboratory results were included as inclusion criteria. Exclusion criteria were those with metastatic disease, rheumatologic disease, endocrinologic disease, patients continuing chemotherapy, psychiatric disease, patients who would not understand the questions, and using drugs to suppress the symptoms of FM (steroid, duloxetine, pregabalin, and tramadol). The FM classification was made according to the 2016 ACR FM criteria.[7] Pain intensity of the patients was evaluated with a visual analog score (VAS). In addition, patients were evaluated with the fibromyalgia impact questionnaire (FIQ), multidimensional assessment of fatigue (MAF), EuroQol 5D scale, Jenkins sleep scale (JSS), and Beck depression inventory (BDI). Age, height, weight, body mass index, lung cancer stage, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy history of the patients were noted. The patients were divided into 2 groups, FM positive and FM negative, according to the presence of FM. The demographic and clinical features of the groups were compared.

2.2. Measures

The pain level of the patients was measured with a 10 cm length VAS (VAS 0: no pain, VAS 10: severe pain).

The disease effect of FM was measured using the FIQ. The questionnaire scores range from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating greater involvement of the disease.[8] It has been shown that the questionnaire is valid and reliable in the Turkish population.[9]

MAF consists of 16 questions, and scores range from 1 (no fatigue) to 50 (severe fatigue). The EQ-5D index score ranges from −0.59 to 1, with high scores indicating well-being. The scales have been shown to be valid and reliable in the Turkish population.[10,11]

The JSS consists of 4 questions and results in a score between 0 and 20; higher scores reflect more sleep problems. The BDI consists of 21 questions, and 4 options are offered for each question. High scores indicate a bad mood. Turkish validity and reliability studies of the scales were made.[12,13]

2.3. Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 20.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and frequencies, while mean (standard deviation) and median (interquartile range) were used to define the continuous variables. A Chi-Square test was used for comparison of categorical variables. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used for the conformity of quantitative data to normal distribution. The Mann–Whitney U and Independent t tests were used in the analysis of non-normally distributed data and in the comparison of normally distributed data, respectively. A P value < .05 was considered statistically significant with a 95% confidence interval.

3. Results

A total of 116 patients were included in the study. The mean age of the patients was 62.2 ± 8.4 years. There was a male 93 (80.2%) predominance among all patients. The frequency of receiving chemotherapy and radiotherapy in the patients was 112 (96.6%) and 93 (80.2%), respectively. FM was detected in 14 (12.2%) patients. Among the lung cancer subgroups, adenocarcinoma 47 (40.5%) and squamous cell carcinoma 44 (37.9%) were found most frequently (Table 1).

4. Discussion

The frequency of FM in cancer patients is thought to be higher than in the general population. In addition, the presence of FM has negative consequences for cancer patients.[2,4] In the present study, the frequency of FM was found to be 12.2% in patients diagnosed with lung cancer. Also, the presence of FM was associated with worse pain, sleep quality, fatigue, and depression in cancer patients.

The prevalence of FM in the population varies between 0.5% and 6%, and it has been found to be 3.6% in Turkish women.[14] Akkaya et al showed that the presence of FM is associated with high FIQ scores and pain scores in breast cancer patients.[2] In the present study, pain score, number of trigger points, FIQ, Widespread Pain Index and Symptom Severity Scale values were found to be significantly higher in patients diagnosed with FM. The fact that these values are high in patients with FM can be associated with the presence of nociplastic pain. Nociplastic pain is a type of pain seen in patients with central sensitization subgroups, such as FM.[15] Therefore, we think that both cancer pain and nociplastic pain create a difficult situation for patients with lung cancer to cope with.

Grusdat et al showed that fatigue is more common in cancer patients due to the release of chemical mediators and radiotherapy treatments.[16] In the present study, fatigue scores were found to be high in cancer patients. In addition, MAF scores were found to be higher in patients diagnosed with FM. We also think that high pain scores in FM patients may also be the cause of fatigue. Since, the intensity of pain in these patients may affect the patient physical and mental health.

Sleep disorder is a common complaint in cancer patients.[17] It has been shown that sleep disturbances are more common in patients with chronic pain. In the present study, we found a higher level of sleep disorder in the FM group. In fact, questioning sleep disorders for FM is important in terms of diagnosis. We found that the pain score was higher in FM patients, and we concluded that the patients had sleep problems due to pain. According to Forti et al, lung function, respiratory muscle strength, and thoracoabdominal mobility were found to be lower in women with FM syndrome.[18] This decrease may have caused sleep disturbances in FM patients.

It has been shown that quality of life and mental status are adversely affected in cancer patients. However, these scores were found to be worse in FM cancer patients. Akechi et al showed that the quality of life of FM patients was negatively affected.[19] Akkaya et al showed that mental health was worse in patients with widespread pain.[2] In the present study, we showed that quality of life and mental health were adversely affected in FM lung cancer patients. This effect is also influenced by the factors of pain, sleep disturbance, and fatigue. We think that the deterioration of all these factors together affects quality of life and mental health.

There are some limitations of the present study. First, this study was designed as a single center. Second, patients who received radiotherapy or chemotherapy were not excluded. Finally, there was no healthy control group. However, the strength of the study can be shown, as it is the first study to investigate the presence of FM in patients with lung cancer.

In conclusion, FM is seen more frequently in patients with lung cancer and has a negative effect on sleep, quality of life, fatigue, and mental functions. We think that physicians should not ignore the presence of FM when treating patients diagnosed with lung cancer.

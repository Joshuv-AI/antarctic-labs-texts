Building confidence and professionalism among junior doctors in leading challenging resuscitation status conversations
Authors: Divya Kanakalingam, Jennifer Thornley, Luke Bradshaw, James Tabor, Scarlett Tankard
Published: 2023

Introduction

Effective communication around resuscitation decisions is essential for good quality patient care.1 Junior doctors (JDs) are frequently required to lead these discussions, but lack the confidence and skills to do this effectively due to inadequate training.

Objectives

The Resuscitation Workshop (RW) was designed to raise awareness of current guidance and improve JDs' confidence in leading resuscitation status conversations (RSC) using role-play scenarios.

Methods

Quantitative and qualitative data were collected from 67 JDs (foundation year 1 (A), foundation year 2 (B), general practice specialty trainees (GPST) and international trust-grade doctors (C)), using structured feedback forms with self-reported confidence ratings (SrCR) (1–10) before and after the workshop. Paired t-test scores compared mean SrCR and qualitative data were analysed using deductive thematic analysis.

Results

67.16% had led RSC, but only 29.85% (n = 20) had received RSC training during postgraduate education. Groups A, B and C showed a statistically significant increase in SrCR when leading RSC of +1.87 (t(22) = 4.3236, p = 0.0003), +1.30 (t(19) = 7.9348, p<0.0001) and +2.04 (t(23) = 4.2241, p = 0.0003) respectively. There was a statistically significant increase in SrCR of current guidelines: +3.31 (t(22) = 6.6955, p<0.0001), +2.30 (t(19) = 10.5095, p<0.0001) and +2.46 (t(23) = 4.9174, p<0.0001). Trainees reported enhanced knowledge and skills by practising challenging communication skills in a simulated environment.

Conclusion

The study demonstrated that the RW improves confidence in RSC and increases awareness of guidelines. Role-plays are increasingly prevalent in medical education and can provide JDs with opportunities to practise challenging discussions with direct feedback. This study has identified the need for further postgraduate training in complex communication skills.

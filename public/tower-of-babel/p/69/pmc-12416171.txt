Correlation between the immune response to COVID-19 vaccines and the constitutions of traditional Chinese medicine
Authors: Ping Chen, Hung-Rong Yen, Hui-Ju Lee, Mei-Yao Wu, Kyungmo Park, Ying-Chyi Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTraditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) classifies individuals into constitution types that may influence physiological responses. SARS-CoV-2 vaccines induce spike-specific antibodies and activate B and T cells, including memory subsets. This study investigates whether TCM constitution types are associated with immune responses and adverse events following COVID-19 vaccination. From March 2021 to December 2023, 78 adults received two doses of the COVID-19 vaccine, including AstraZeneca, Moderna, MVC-COV1901, and Pfizer-BioNTech. PBMCs were collected before and after vaccination. Immune parameters-cytokines, antibody titers, dendritic cells (DCs), T cells, B cells, and memory T cell subsets-were measured. TCM constitution scores were determined using the Constitution in Chinese Medicine Questionnaire (CCMQ) before vaccination. Adverse reactions were recorded through structured surveys, and correlations between constitution scores and adverse events were analyzed using the Pearson correlation. Pre-vaccination TNF-α was negatively correlated with the gentleness constitution and positively with the qi-deficiency. DCs increased post-vaccination in individuals with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions. T cell proportions increased in yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis groups. Dampness-heat scores were negatively correlated with central memory T cell (Tcm) induction. No significant correlations were found between constitution types and antibody levels or neutralization. Adverse reactions were more frequent in individuals with phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, and blood-stasis constitutions, and less frequent in those with a gentleness constitution. TCM constitutions correlate with cellular immune responses and adverse reaction profiles, but not antibody production, after COVID-19 vaccination. This supports integrating TCM constitution frameworks into personalized vaccine prediction.KEYWORDS: COVID-19 vaccine, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) constitutions, spike antibody, memory T-cell, adverse reaction

Introduction

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) causes coronavirus infectious disease 2019 (COVID-19), a pneumonia-like respiratory disease that continues to spread worldwide.1,2 SARS-CoV-2 belongs to the betacoronavirus genus of single-stranded RNA viruses and is composed of spike (S), envelope (E), nucleocapsid (N), and membrane (M) proteins.3 Upon entry into host cells, the virus binds to the angiotensin-converting enzyme-2 (ACE2) receptor on the cell membrane via the receptor-binding domain (RBD) on the S protein.4 The S protein has been the main target for developing SARS-CoV-2 vaccines, as it is essential for viral entry into host cells and is present on the outer layer of the virus. Another vaccine strategy involves the injection of mRNA encoding the S protein; the advantage of this approach is the rapid-scale production of the protein via a direct rout.5 In addition to the production of antibodies, the effects of vaccines on memory T- and B-cell responses are crucial.6,7 SARS-CoV-2 vaccines activate specific CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells, including Tcm cells and effector memory T- (Tem) cells.8 Tcm cells are found mainly in secondary lymphoid organs, such as lymph nodes and tonsils, and produce primarily IL-2, while IFN-γ or IL-4 is produced after proliferation and differentiation.9 Compared with Tcm cells, Tem cells exist in the spleen, lung, liver, and other organs; experience less proliferation; and exhibit rapid effector functions.

Currently, five SARS-CoV-2 vaccines are approved for use in Taiwan, including the AstraZeneca-Oxford AZD1222 vaccine (AZ),10 the Moderna mRNA-1273 vaccine (Moderna),11 the MVC-COV1901 vaccine (MVC),12 the Pfizer-BioNtech BNT162b2 vaccine (BNT),13 and the Novavax vaccine (Novavax).14 By July 30, 2023, the coverage of the second dose of the Taiwan SARS-CoV-2 vaccine was 89%, that of the third dose was 76.8%, and that of the fourth dose was 24.9%. Previous studies have shown that individuals who receive SARS-CoV-2 vaccines develop neutralizing antibodies and IFN-γ T-cells, and both memory T- and B-cells are produced after vaccination.15,16 Moreover, IFN-γ signaling has been recognized as an important cellular defense against SARS-CoV-2.17,18 However, several adverse reactions have been reported following vaccination, including pain, redness, or swelling at the injection site, along with fever, fatigue, headache, muscle pain, nausea, vomiting, itching, chills, and joint pain.19,20 The frequency of adverse reactions after primary vaccination with AZ was higher than that after booster vaccination.21 Conversely, individuals receiving the Moderna booster vaccination experienced more severe adverse reactions than those receiving the primary vaccination.22 No difference was observed between primary vaccination and booster vaccination with MVC.12 Our previous study also confirmed that the MVC vaccine had the lowest adverse reactions compared with the Moderna and AZ two-dose vaccines.23

TCM constitutions provide a framework for understanding an individual’s physical state, serving as a foundation for health maintenance and disease prevention.24 In the research related to TCM constitution, constitutions are often assessed using specialized questionnaires. Two commonly used questionnaires in research are the Constitution in Chinese Medicine Questionnaire (CCMQ), developed by Qi Wang,25 and the Body Constitutions Questionnaire (BCQ), created by Yi-zhang Su.26 Compared to the BCQ (four types), Qi Wang’s CCMQ (nine types) provides a more detailed classification of body constitutions. The CCMQ has also been validated in large-scale studies, demonstrating high internal consistency and structural validity, supporting its reliability in both clinical and research applications.27,28 Based on Qi Wang’s CCMQ and supported by previous research, TCM categorizes individuals into nine distinct constitution types, including gentleness, qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, blood-stasis, yin-deficiency, yang-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis, each associated with specific physiological and psychological characteristics. The gentleness constitution is considered a balanced and healthy state, characterized by emotional stability, good adaptability, and lower susceptibility to illness. In contrast, the qi-deficiency constitution presents with fatigue, shortness of breath, spontaneous sweating, and increased vulnerability to infections. Individuals with a yang-deficiency constitution are prone to cold intolerance, cold limbs or lower back, diarrhea, and edema. Yin-deficiency types often feel internal heat and exhibit symptoms such as dry mouth, flushed cheeks, and constipation. The phlegm-dampness constitution is associated with greasy skin, heaviness, and excess mucus, while dampness-heat types commonly experience oily skin, acne, bitter taste, and dark urine. The blood-stasis constitution involves poor circulation, bruising, and localized pain, sometimes accompanied by menstrual clots. Qi-depression types are emotionally sensitive, prone to low mood, frequent sighing, and chest discomfort. Lastly, the special diathesis constitution is marked by allergic tendencies and hypersensitivity to environmental changes.24,25,29–32 This classification highlights the diverse physiological and emotional patterns that may predispose individuals to specific health conditions, guiding personalized treatment and prevention strategies in TCM.

A previous study revealed that the expression levels of IL-2, IL-6 and TNF-α were extremely high in a qi-deficiency model, accompanied by gastrointestinal dysfunction and decreased immunity.33 Moreover, another study noted that treatment of qi-deficiency improved neuroendocrinoimmune disorders.34 In addition to the immune system, TCM constitutions might affect body mass index (BMI). Interestingly, a study indicated that obesity was positively correlated with the phlegm-dampness constitution but negatively correlated with qi-deficiency and yang-deficiency constitutions.35,36 Obesity can affect the concentration of neutralizing antibodies after COVID-19 infection and increase the probability of severe illness.37,38 However, no studies have demonstrated the correlation between TCM constitutions, basic immunity, antibody production, and side effects after vaccination.

Therefore, integrating constitution theory into the analysis of immune responses – particularly in the context of vaccination – can provide new insights. By evaluating correlations between TCM constitutions and immunological markers such as antibody titers, neutralizing capacity, and DC, T cell, B cell, and memory T-cell responses, we may bridge ancient theoretical frameworks with modern biomedical models of host-pathogen interaction and immune variability.

Methods

Recruitment and clinical sample collection

The underlying data of the present study originated from “The correlation between basic immunity and Chinese medicine constitution and antibody production for the COVID-19 vaccine” program, which was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of China Medical University and Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan (CMUH110-REC2-056). The ethical approval was granted on March 24, 2021, and the project was valid from March 24, 2021, to December 31, 2023. A total of 78 adults aged over 20 years provided written informed consent before enrollment. The consent process followed ethical standards, and the signed consent forms were documented and securely stored. More details on the research protocol can be found in the previous paper.23 We recruited uninfected 78 participants who were willing to receive two doses of COVID-19 vaccine, including homologous AZ (n = 38), Moderna (n = 17), MVC (n = 13), and BNT (n = 4), and a heterologous prime – boost schedule (n = 6). The clinical information and summary statistics are shown in Supplementary Table 2. Ten-milliliter peripheral blood samples were collected within one week before vaccination and four weeks after the second vaccination. The questionnaires concerning adverse reactions were collected one week after the first and second doses. In this sub-study, the Constitution in Chinese Medicine Questionnaires (CCMQs) were collected within one week prior to the first dose of vaccination, at the same time as the first blood draw, and the correlations between various underlying data and TCM constitutions were analyzed.

Constitution in Chinese Medicine Questionnaires (CCMQs)

Qi Wang’s questionnaire was used for this study, which divided the constitution into nine types.25,39 The TCM constitution score for each participant was rated on a five-point scale ranging from 0 to 4, indicating the level of each constitution from “not at all” to “very much.” The primary score for each constitution was calculated by adding all the points for the questions related to that constitution. The secondary scores were calculated using the following formula: [(primary score – number of questions)/(number of questions × 4)] × 100. Nine TCM constitutions were identified based on their secondary scores, including gentleness, qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, blood-stasis, yin-deficiency, yang-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis. The CCMQ questionnaire is composed of items designed to assess tendencies toward nine different constitution types. Constitution scores are calculated by selectively summing the scores of the items corresponding to each constitution. A score of 60 or above in a given category indicates the presence of that constitution type.

Questionnaires concerning adverse reactions

The design of the questionnaire was based on international studies regarding vaccine safety.40–42 The adverse reactions questionnaire consisted of nine questions, each rated on a five-point scale from 0 to 4, indicating the severity of the reaction (Supplementary Table S1). The questionnaire was administered one week after the first and second doses of the COVID-19 vaccine. The scores from both questionnaires were totaled and analyzed in relation to the TCM constitutions. The questionnaires were collected by clinical research staff, and a statistical technician calculated the scores.

Sample processing

The detailed procedures are described in our previous study.23 Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated using Lymphoprep gradient centrifugation (STEMCELL, Oslo, Norway). Plasma samples were stored at − 80°C for subsequent antibody analysis. A portion of the PBMCs was used for immune cell subset identification, while the remaining cells were cultured and stimulated to assess cytokine production.

Measurement of cytokine production

The detailed procedures are described in our previous study.23 PBMCs were stimulated with 50 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) (Sigma, MO, USA) and incubated for 24 or 48 hours. After incubation, the cell culture supernatants were collected, and the levels of human IL-2, TNF-α, and IFN-γ were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (BD Bioscience, CA, USA).

Detection of SARS-CoV-2-specific antibodies and neutralizing antibodies

The detailed procedures are described in our previous study.23 An ELISA using the S protein of the Wuhan strain – kindly provided by Dr. S. J. Liu of the National Health Research Institutes, Taiwan – was used to detect SARS-CoV-2-specific antibodies. Neutralizing antibody titers were determined using an Anti-SARS-CoV-2 (Wuhan strain) Neutralizing Antibody Titer Serologic Assay Kit (ACRO Biosystems, DE, USA) through an ACE2 competitive ELISA, following the manufacturer’s instructions. The neutralizing activity was expressed as the percentage of inhibition, calculated using the formula: (1 − OD₄₅₀ of sample/OD₄₅₀ of negative control) × 100%.

Flow cytometry

The detailed procedures are described in our previous study.23 Isolated PBMCs were stained with fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibodies targeting CD3, CD4, CD8, CD11c, CD19, CD45RA, and CD62L (BioLegend, CA, USA). Immune cell subsets were analyzed by Flow cytometry using a FACSVerse instrument (BD Biosciences, CA, USA).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM® SPSS® and GraphPad Prism 9 software programs. The results are presented as the means ± SDs. Differences between groups were analyzed by Student’s t test and analysis of variance (ANOVA). Significance was considered at P < .05 and indicated as *P < .05. Correlations between variables were calculated using the Pearson correlation method and represented as linear trend lines. In all figures, the p values are denoted as p, and the correlation coefficients are denoted as r.

Results

Statistical analysis of the participants’ basic clinical information

The clinical characteristics and summary statistics of the study population are presented in Supplementary Table 2. A total of 78 participants were enrolled, comprising 30 males and 48 females, with a mean age of 38 years and an average body mass index (BMI) of 23.2. Among the participants, 6 individuals had hypertension, 7 had hyperlipidemia, and no cases of hyperglycemia were reported. Regarding vaccination, 38 participants received two doses of the AstraZeneca (AZ) vaccine, 17 received two doses of Moderna, 13 were vaccinated with MVC, and 4 received the BNT vaccine. Additionally, 6 participants received two doses of heterologous vaccines. Since this study aimed to investigate the impact of COVID-19 vaccination on different TCM constitutions, the analysis was conducted without stratifying participants by vaccine brand, ensuring a broad evaluation of vaccine effects across different TCM constitutions. To minimize potential confounding effects of age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, and comorbidities on the analysis of TCM constitution types, participants were further stratified by the nine constitution groups, and the distribution of these variables across the groups was examined. No significant differences were observed in age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, or comorbidities (including hypertension, hyperglycemia, and hyperlipidemia) among the constitution types (Table 1).

Correlation between basal immunity and TCM constitutions

The study initially conducted an analysis of basal immunity, TCM constitutions, and their correlation in each participant prior to vaccination. Basal immunity was assessed by measuring the levels of TNF-α (Figure 1A), IFN-γ (Figure 1B), IL-2 (Figure 1C), and the sum of all three cytokines (Figure 1D) using a PBMC culture system. The TCM constitutions were analyzed using CCMQs. The findings revealed that the level of TNF-α prior to COVID-19 vaccination was negatively correlated with the gentleness constitution score (correlation was − 0.25, p = .027) and positively correlated with the qi-deficiency constitution score (correlation was 0.276, p = .014) (Figure 1A). However, the levels of IFN-γ (Figure 1B), IL-2 (Figure 1C), and the sum of all three cytokines (Figure 1D) prior to COVID-19 vaccination did not correlate with any TCM constitution scores. These findings suggest that healthy individuals (gentleness constitution) tend to have lower levels of TNF-α, an inflammatory cytokine, whereas those with qi-deficiency constitutions may have higher levels. To exclude the possibility that the observed correlations between TNF-α and TCM constitutions were confounded by other factors, we additionally performed Student’s t tests and Pearson correlation analyses between TNF-α levels and age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, and comorbidities. The results showed no significant associations between TNF-α and any of these variables, indicating that the correlation between TNF-α levels and TCM constitutions may not be attributable to these clinical factors (Supplementary Table S3).

Correlation between TCM constitutions and the production and neutralization ability of S-binding antibodies

We subsequently investigated the potential association between TCM constitutions and antibody production. We measured the levels of circulating S-binding antibodies in pre- and postvaccination serum samples by ELISA and calculated the fold increase in antibody levels after two doses of the COVID-19 vaccine. Our results indicated that there were no significant correlations between the fold increase in antibodies and any TCM constitution scores (Figure 2A). To further analyze these results, we divided the gentleness constitution into two grades (yes and no) and the remaining eight constitutions into three grades (major, minor, and no) and analyzed their correlations with the fold increase in S-binding antibodies (Supplementary Figure S1). However, the results for this analysis revealed no significant difference. Furthermore, we evaluated the correlations between TCM constitutions and the antibody-neutralizing ability after two doses of the vaccine. We used 40-fold dilutions of sera to compete with S proteins for binding to ACE2 and then calculated the percentage of inhibition (antibody-neutralizing ability). We found no correlation between neutralizing ability and any TCM constitution scores (Figure 2B). We obtained similar results using another algorithm of TCM constitutions and observed no associations between antibody-neutralizing ability and the nine different TCM constitutions (Supplementary Figure S2). These results suggest that there is no correlation between TCM constitutions and the production and neutralization ability of S-binding antibodies after two doses of vaccination.

Influence of TCM constitutions on immune cell responses following vaccination

Since the immune response induced by vaccination involves the activation and increase of DCs, T cells, and B cells,43 we measured the proportions of DCs, T cells, and B cells in the blood before vaccination and after two doses of the vaccine. The participants were categorized into nine different TCM constitution types to observe the changes in these immune cells within each constitution group. We observed a significant increase in the proportion of DCs following two doses of the vaccine in individuals with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions (Figure 3A). This increase may be attributed to the fact that, prior to vaccination, these constitution types had lower baseline DC proportions compared to individuals with a gentleness constitution (14.4%). Specifically, the pre-vaccination DC proportions were 13.1% in qi-deficiency, 12.2% in phlegm-dampness, 10.7% in qi-depression, 12.6% in yin-deficiency, 9.2% in dampness-heat, and 11.5% in special diathesis constitutions. Although individuals with blood-stasis (12.4%) and yang-deficiency (12.6%) constitutions also had lower baseline DC proportions than those with a gentleness constitution (14.4%), the post-vaccination increase in DCs in these groups did not reach statistical significance. In addition, we found that individuals with yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions exhibited a significant increase in T cell proportions following two doses of the vaccine (Figure 3B). This may be due to individuals with yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions had lower baseline T cell proportions prior to vaccination, with values of 41.7%, 38.0%, and 39.4%, respectively, in contrast to 48.6% observed in those with a gentleness constitution. Lastly, no statistically significant changes in B cell proportions were observed after two vaccine doses across any of the constitution types (Figure 3C). This finding is consistent with the results shown in Figure 2, which demonstrated no significant association between TCM constitution types and antibody production. To determine whether the observed associations between DC or T cell proportions and constitution types were influenced by potential confounders, we performed additional analyses using Student’s t test and Pearson correlation to evaluate the relationships between immune cell proportions and variables including age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, and comorbidities. The results showed no significant associations between these variables (except for comorbidities) and DC or T cell proportions, confirming that the observed associations with TCM constitution types may not be due to age, sex, height, body weight, or BMI. However, comorbidities (hypertension, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia) may partially contribute to the observed association between constitution type and DC proportions (Supplementary Table S3).

Correlation between the production of memory T-cells and TCM constitutions

One of the important goals of vaccination is to generate memory T-cells. Our previous study revealed that mRNA- and adenovirus-based COVID-19 vaccines could increase Tcm but not Tem cells after two doses.23 However, the relationships between memory T-cell populations induced by COVID-19 vaccination and TCM constitutions remain unknown. Therefore, we analyzed Tcm and Tem populations in PBMC samples using flow cytometry and calculated the changes in memory T-cell populations before and after vaccination. We examined the correlations between the change in Tcm cells within total T-cells and the nine different TCM constitutions. We found that individuals with higher dampness-heat scores presented lower increases in Tcm cells within total T-cells after vaccination (correlation was − 0.256, p = .024) (Figure 4A). Furthermore, we analyzed the correlations between the changes in Tem cells among total T-cells and the nine different TCM constitutions. We found no significant associations between changes in Tem cells and the nine different TCM constitution scores (Figure 4B). To ensure that the observed association between Tcm cells and dampness-heat constitution was not confounded by other variables, we conducted additional analyses using Student’s t test and Pearson correlation to evaluate the relationships between Tcm cell changes and age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, and comorbidities. The results showed no significant associations between Tcm cell changes and any of these variables, suggesting that the correlation with dampness-heat constitution may not be driven by these clinical characteristics (Supplementary Table S3). Our results suggest that individuals with dampness-heat constitution scores might produce fewer Tcm cells after vaccination.

Associations between body mass index, TCM constitutions and immune responses following vaccination

Research has shown that obese individuals may produce lower levels of antibodies in response to COVID-19 vaccination.44 In this study, we confirmed this association and explored the potential impact of TCM constitutions on the immune response following vaccination. Our study cohort was divided into two groups based on BMI, with individuals with BMIs less than or equal to 24 in one group (BMI ≤24) and those with BMIs greater than 24 in the other (BMI > 24). We found that individuals with higher BMIs (BMI > 24) produced lower levels of S-binding antibodies after two doses of vaccination (p = .028) (Figure 5A). Furthermore, we observed negative correlations between BMI and yin-deficiency (correlation −0.333, p = .003) and yang-deficiency (correlation −0.223, p = .049) constitution scores (Figure 5B), consistent with previous studies suggesting that individuals with lower BMI are more likely to exhibit yin- or yang-deficiency constitutions.35 Our data indicate that neither antibody production (Figure 2) nor memory T-cell generation (Figure 4) was impaired in individuals with these constitution types. On the other hand, this previous study also proposed that individuals with obesity tend to exhibit phlegm-dampness constitutions.35 Interestingly, although we did not observe a statistically significant positive correlation between phlegm-dampness constitution score and BMI (Figure 5B), individuals with a phlegm-dampness constitution showed a trend toward lower antibody production, albeit without reaching statistical significance (Supplementary Figure S1). These results suggest potential associations between BMI, TCM constitutions, and the immune response following COVID-19 vaccination.

Correlation between TCM constitutions and adverse reactions to COVID-19 vaccination

The severity and underlying causes of adverse reactions that occur after COVID-19 vaccination are important issues. To address this, we conducted an analysis in which we collected the severity of various adverse reactions using questionnaires one week after the first and second doses of vaccination. We investigated the correlations between comprehensive adverse reactions and single TCM constitutions, as shown in Figure 6. Our results revealed that comprehensive adverse reactions were negatively correlated with the gentleness constitution scores (correlation was − 0.248, p = .029), whereas phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, and blood-stasis constitution scores were positively correlated with comprehensive adverse reactions (p = .005, .026, and .036, respectively). However, qi-deficiency, yin-deficiency, yang-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitution scores were not significantly correlated with comprehensive adverse reactions after vaccination. Therefore, we concluded that individuals who exhibit higher gentleness constitution scores (healthier constitutions) have fewer adverse reactions after vaccination, whereas those with a tendency toward phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, and blood-stasis constitutions may be at greater risk of severe adverse reactions. To further validate that the observed associations were not confounded by other clinical variables, we additionally analyzed the correlations between adverse reactions and age, sex, height, body weight, BMI, and comorbidities using Student’s t test and Pearson correlation. The results showed no significant associations between these variables and adverse reaction scores, indicating that the observed correlations between TCM constitutions and adverse reactions may not be attributable to these demographic or clinical factors. These findings support the independent association between TCM constitution types and vaccine-related adverse responses (Supplementary Table S3).

The data show the correlations between comprehensive adverse reactions after COVID-19 vaccination and single constitutions. Lines in the graph are shown as trend lines. Statistical significance is marked in red (p represents the P value, and r represents the correlation coefficient).

Furthermore, we analyzed the correlations between nine common adverse reactions (tenderness, tiredness, headache, muscle pain, moodlessness, chillness, arthralgia, fever, and severe allergies) and TCM constitutions. As shown in Supplementary Figure S3, our results indicated that tiredness, muscle pain, moodlessness, and severe allergies were correlated with some TCM constitutions. Specifically, the gentleness constitution scores were negatively correlated with the levels of muscle pain, moodlessness, and severe allergies (correlations were − 0.264, −0.371, and − 0.308, respectively). Additionally, individuals with higher scores of qi-deficiency and phlegm-dampness constitutions were more likely to experience tiredness after vaccination (correlations were 0.256 and 0.356, respectively). The severity of muscle pain was positively correlated with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, and qi-depression scores (correlations were 0.245, 0.248, and 0.332, respectively). The severity of moodlessness was positively correlated with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, blood-stasis, and yin-deficiency constitution scores (correlations were 0.438, 0.475, 0.37, 0.365 and 0.245, respectively). Finally, the severity of severe allergies after vaccination was positively correlated with the qi-depression constitution scores (correlation was 0.372). However, there were no significant correlations between tenderness, headache, chillness, arthralgia, or fever and TCM constitutions (Supplementary Figure S4). Based on our results, individuals with gentleness constitutions had a lower probability of experiencing muscle pain, moodlessness, and severe allergies after COVID-19 vaccination, whereas qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, blood-stasis, and yin-deficiency constitutions may be positively correlated with these adverse reactions.

Discussion

This study revealed novel correlations between the nine TCM constitution scores and various immune responses following COVID-19 vaccination, including S-binding antibody levels, neutralizing capacity, DC, T cell, and B cell responses, memory T cell responses, and adverse reactions. We found that any TCM constitutions were not associated with S-binding antibodies or neutralizing ability against SARS-CoV-2. Notably, individuals with higher dampness-heat scores might produce fewer Tcm cells (Figure 4A). Another important finding was that individuals with phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, and blood-stasis constitutions might suffer more severe adverse reactions after COVID-19 vaccination.

Surprisingly, higher levels of TNF-α production were observed in individuals with more pronounced qi-deficiency constitutions before vaccination. Our previous study reported that basal immunity prior to COVID-19 vaccination, especially the levels of TNF-α and IL-2, were negatively correlated with the S-antibody titers produced after COVID-19 vaccination, indicating that individuals with higher levels of TNF-α and IL-2 produced lower S-antibodies after COVID-19 vaccination.23 However, in this study, there was no correlation between S-binding antibodies and qi-deficiency constitution. One possible explanation is that only TNF-α was significantly increased and IL-2 was not in participants with qi-deficiency constitution, which may not be sufficient to affect antibody production.

Vaccination activates both the innate and adaptive immune systems, beginning with the uptake of vaccine components by DCs, which present antigen and deliver co-stimulatory signals to initiate T cell responses. This activation leads to the differentiation of effector and memory CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and supports B cell maturation into antibody-secreting plasma cells, thereby establishing long-term immunological protection.43 Tcm cells are present in secondary lymphoid organs and blood, which require further differentiation signals to make effector cytokines and proliferate substantially after reactivation.45 In this study, we found that individuals with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions exhibited a significant increase in DC proportions following two doses of the COVID-19 vaccine. Among them, those with yin-deficiency, dampness-heat, and special diathesis constitutions also showed a significant increase in T cell proportions after vaccination. We speculate that these constitution types had lower baseline levels of DCs and T cells prior to vaccination compared to individuals with a gentleness constitution, which may account for the greater magnitude of increase observed. However, although individuals with a dampness-heat constitution showed a significant post-vaccination increase in T cells, their ability to generate Tcm cells appeared to decrease as the dampness-heat score increased. The dampness-heat constitution may exhibit a baseline pro-inflammatory immune status – characterized by elevated cytokines such as IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, and CRP – which may impair the induction or maintenance of Tcm following vaccination.46,47 These findings suggest that while all constitution types showed a trend toward enhanced cellular activity following two vaccine doses, individuals with a dampness-heat constitution may have a reduced capacity to mount protective immune responses upon re-exposure to infection. Regarding B cell responses, although there was a general trend of increased B cell proportions across all constitution types of post-vaccination, none of the changes reached statistical significance. This supports previous findings that antibody production did not significantly correlate among the various constitution groups.

A previous study revealed that obese people produce lower antibody titers and a reduced memory immune response after COVID-19 vaccination.44,48 In our study, we confirm that individuals with higher BMIs have lower antibody titers after COVID-19 vaccination. Although BMI was negatively correlated with the scores of yin-deficiency and yang-deficiency constitutions, no significant association was observed between these two constitution types and the ability to produce antibodies. It may be that BMI is just one of the factors that affect antibody production. There may be additional contributing factors. For instance, individuals with yin-deficiency and yang-deficiency constitutions may possess a baseline immunity (TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-2) that is not compromised, thus not significantly impacting antibody production.

In our study, individuals with a qi-deficiency constitution frequently reported tiredness, muscle pain, and moodlessness following COVID-19 vaccination. This is consistent with previous findings indicating that people with qi-deficiency constitution are more susceptible to fatigue and illness, possibly due to imbalances in the intestinal microbiota.49 Immune imbalance caused by gut dysbiosis may also explain similar adverse effects observed in qi-deficiency constitution after vaccination. Similarly, the phlegm-dampness constitution was associated with tiredness, muscle pain, and moodlessness after vaccination. This constitution is characterized by symptoms such as heaviness in the body, abdominal fullness, phlegm accumulation in the throat, and a sticky sensation in the mouth.50 According to TCM theory, phlegm-dampness obstructs the upper burner and interferes with the ascent of clear yang qi, causing dizziness, chest tightness, and fatigue. These mechanisms may explain why individuals with phlegm-dampness constitution experience adverse effects such as tiredness, muscle pain, and moodlessness post-vaccination. Furthermore, individuals with a qi-depression constitution also exhibited muscle pain, moodlessness, and severe allergies post-vaccination. Previous studies have shown that qi-depression is closely associated with anxiety and poor sleep quality.51 TCM theory holds that “qi is the commander of blood”; thus, when qi is insufficient or stagnant, blood, dampness, phlegm, and food may become blocked, accumulate, and generate internal heat. When this heat disturbs the clear orifices, it may lead to depression and low mood.52 This theory aligns with our findings that moodlessness was commonly observed in individuals with qi-deficiency, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, blood-stasis, and yin-deficiency constitutions. These findings emphasize the value of integrating TCM constitution types when interpreting individual differences in vaccine-induced adverse reactions and may inform future strategies for personalized vaccine recommendations.

Limitations of this study: Based on government policy, more individuals received the AZ COVID-19 vaccine than other brands due to prolonged waiting times for other vaccine brands. Given the limited sample size and uneven age distribution among participants, our study focused mainly on young individuals, which may introduce sample bias and affect the severity of adverse reactions observed after vaccination. Furthermore, because the TCM constitution and adverse reactions data were obtained from questionnaires, individual subjective differences may influence the results. Nevertheless, this study still allowed us to explore the correlation between TCM constitution and post-vaccination immune responses within a randomly enrolled age range using questionnaire-based analysis.

In conclusion, this study revealed that there was no correlation between TCM constitutions and S-binding antibody responses or neutralization responses induced by COVID-19 vaccines. However, the proportion of Tcm cells induced by COVID-19 vaccines was negatively associated with dampness-heat constitution, whereas the effector memory population was not correlated with TCM constitutions. Furthermore, the severity of adverse reactions after COVID-19 vaccination was significantly associated with TCM constitutions, particularly the gentleness, phlegm-dampness, qi-depression, and blood-stasis constitutions. These findings provide the first scientific evidence linking TCM constitutions with cellular immunity and vaccine-related adverse events, offering practical value for clinical decision-making. In the future, constitution-based evaluations may help clinicians assess vaccine tolerance and immune responsiveness, allowing for tailored vaccination strategies or pre-vaccination constitution adjustment to reduce risks and optimize protective effects.

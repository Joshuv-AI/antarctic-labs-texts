Safety and early efficacy of iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells in subacute spinal cord injury: protocol for a phase I, multicentre, open-label, single-arm trial
Authors: Mao Pang, Jun Kang, Yang Yang, Tianwei He, Senyu Yao, Bin Hu, Xiang Li, Bin Liu, Limin Rong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSpinal cord injury (SCI) frequently results in severe neurological impairment due to neural tissue destruction and scarring, representing a substantial and increasing global health burden. Current standard treatments primarily focus on preventing secondary complications but offer limited efficacy in repairing neural circuits or reversing neurological deficits. Stem cell transplantation, particularly using induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived therapies, presents a regenerative strategy that avoids the ethical constraints associated with embryonic stem cells while providing a scalable source for cellular therapeutics. Specifically, allogeneic, ‘off-the-shelf’ iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitors offer a standardised and scalable approach, supported by a dual therapeutic mechanism observed in preclinical models.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, multicentre, open-label, single-arm clinical study involving 12–24 adult patients with subacute SCI. To evaluate safety and tolerability, the trial is structured into sequential Single Ascending Dose (SAD; n=6–12) and Multiple Ascending Dose (MAD; n=6–12) phases. Both phases employ a standard ‘3+3’ dose-escalation design across two dose levels (level 1: 5×10⁷ cells/dose; level 2: 1.5×10⁸ cells/dose). Participants in the SAD phase receive a single intrathecal administration, whereas those in the MAD phase receive four biweekly intrathecal administrations. Dose escalation, cohort expansion and progression from the SAD to the MAD phase are strictly contingent on the absence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) over a 28-day observation window following the single dose (SAD) or the final dose (MAD). The primary endpoint is to determine the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs, identify DLTs and establish the maximum tolerated dose as well as the recommended phase II dose. Efficacy will be assessed by evaluating longitudinal changes in American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) Impairment Scale grade, American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) sensory/motor scores and Spinal Cord Independence Measure score at predefined follow-up visits (days 29, 71, 90, 180, 270 and 360) up to 360 days post-baseline. Exploratory objectives include characterising the potential therapeutic mechanisms and broader clinical impact through longitudinal assessment of cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers, neurophysiological function, bladder outcomes and spinal cord imaging metrics.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has received ethical approval from the Institutional Ethics Committees of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. YW2025-012-01) and the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Approval No. 2025–071-2). Furthermore, the investigational product has secured regulatory clearance for clinical trials from both the China National Medical Products Administration (Clinical Trial Notification No. 2025LP01101) and the US Food and Drug Administration (IND No. 31234). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before participation. Moreover, strict adherence will be maintained to the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines throughout the study. To ensure transparency and broad dissemination, the study results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.Trial registration numberNCT06976229.Keywords: Spine, Neurological injury, Patients, Clinical Trial

Introduction

Spinal cord injury (SCI) represents a devastating neurological event characterised by extensive cellular necrosis, axonal disruption and reactive fibrotic scar formation, stemming from both primary mechanical trauma and subsequent secondary pathophysiological cascades.1 Afflicted individuals typically endure permanent disability, encompassing motor dysfunction, sensory deficits, autonomic dysregulation and visceral dysfunction manifesting as incontinence.2 3 Global epidemiologic data consistently demonstrate persistently high SCI incidence rates.4 Our previous analysis further indicates significant global increases in SCI incidence, prevalence and years lived with disability between 1990 and 2019.5 Specifically, an estimated 574 502 new SCI cases occurred globally in 2021 (95% uncertainty interval (UI) 440 219 to 757 445), corresponding to an age-standardised incidence rate of 7.1 per 100 000 population (95% UI 5.5 to 9.4).6 Parallel to these global trajectories, China has witnessed a pronounced escalation in its absolute disease burden, evidenced by a 43.27% increase in annual SCI incidence between 1990 and 2021 to reach 99 363 new cases. While rapid population ageing within the country has precipitated a distinct demographic shift characterised by rising fall-related injuries among the elderly, motor vehicle collisions persistently drive incidence across other cohorts.7,9 Crucially, SCI disproportionately affects younger individuals within their productive working years.10 Consequently, this condition imposes profound physical and psychological morbidity on patients, alongside substantial economic burdens for affected families and broader societal systems.11 12

The current standard treatment protocol for SCI emphasises early surgical decompression; however, optimal timing remains controversial and depends on factors such as the degree of cord compression and the severity of paralysis.13,15 Alternative pharmacological intervention includes a 24-hour infusion of high-dose methylprednisolone, though current guidelines restrict its initiation to within 8 hours postinjury.16 Additional approaches involve glucocorticoids to suppress neuroinflammation, neuroregenerative strategies aimed at enhancing signal transmission and physical therapy to strengthen muscles and promote neural circuit reorganisation.17,21 On patient stabilisation, long-term rehabilitation begins immediately. Collectively, these treatments focus primarily on preventing secondary complications and optimising residual function. Despite decades of research, no therapy has definitively demonstrated the ability to repair the damaged spinal cord itself or fundamentally improve neurological deficits after SCI.22

Research into SCI regeneration, motivated by observations of fetal nervous tissue graft survival in the adult mammalian central nervous system since the late 1970s, has explored regenerative medicine approaches including drug therapy and cell/tissue transplantation.23 While drug therapies, such as granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, Rho inhibitors, riluzole and hepatocyte growth factor, have undergone clinical studies often with modest evidence of clinical benefit, stem cell therapy emerges as a promising strategy.24 25 Stem cell transplants potentially exert their effects through several mechanisms: (1) Neurotrophic support: Grafted cells secrete factors that enhance neuronal survival and promote axonal regeneration26; (2) Axonal repair: Grafted cells either facilitate repair of original neural connections or serve as interneurons bridging the injury gap27 and (3) Remyelination: Grafted cells promote myelin regeneration around damaged axons to restore neural conduction.28 These mechanisms, operating individually or synergistically, contribute to the neuroprotective, reparative and regenerative effects observed after stem cell transplantation for SCI.29

Since their inception, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) have precipitated a paradigm shift across biomedical research, rapidly evolving into a highly versatile toolkit with broad translational applications that encompass in vitro disease modelling, high-throughput pharmacological screening and the development of scalable cellular therapeutics.30 Guided by these advancements in the field, the advent of iPSC technology provides a valuable platform for generating a virtually limitless supply of specific somatic lineages, facilitating the initiation of clinical studies exploring human iPSC (hiPSC)-derived therapies.31 32 Applied to SCI, this regenerative strategy involves reprogramming a patient’s own somatic cells into iPSCs, differentiating them into neural progenitor cells or other relevant neural lineages and transplanting them into the injury site to promote neural repair, tissue regeneration and functional recovery.33 34 While early conceptualisations focused on autologous transplantation, the translational field has increasingly pivoted toward the utilisation of clinical-grade, allogeneic iPSC banks. Key advantages of this paradigm include circumventing the core ethical concerns associated with embryonic stem cells, which require the destruction of human embryos for isolation. It is necessary to distinguish these embryo-related concerns from the ethical considerations associated with human-derived somatic tissues, such as human umbilical cord blood. Although iPSC technology avoids embryo-related ethical conflicts, the utilisation of umbilical cord blood remains subject to regulatory frameworks governing human-derived biologic materials, which mandate donor informed consent, institutional review board approval, privacy protection and the verification of voluntary donation protocols. Furthermore, demonstrating multipotency for neural differentiation,32 this allogeneic approach crucially overcomes the prohibitive manufacturing delays, extreme costs and batch-to-batch variability inherent to autologous patient-specific methods. Preclinical studies show that transplanted iPSC-derived neural cells survive, integrate into the host circuitry by extending axons and forming synapses, modulate inflammation and the local microenvironment, reduce glial scarring and contribute to functional motor recovery.35 36

Preliminary clinical research supports the initial safety of hiPSC-derived cell therapies in neurological disorders like amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), Parkinson’s with work at Keio University showing promise using iPSC-derived neural stem/progenitor cells for subacute complete SCI.37,39 In this study, an ‘off-the-shelf’, allogeneic motor neuron progenitor cell therapy is manufactured through a precise two-stage derivation process: initially, clinical-grade iPSCs are generated via the reprogramming of human umbilical cord blood mononuclear cells. Subsequently, these iPSCs undergo directed differentiation into a later, more committed stage of motor neuron progenitors.40 Reflecting the ongoing clinical translation of this specific cellular lineage, a parallel phase 1 clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT07118319) is currently recruiting subjects to evaluate the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of analogous allogeneic iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells administered via intrathecal injection for the treatment of ALS. Compared with traditional methods, this direct induction approach offers key translational advantages: it achieves high-purity motor neurons more rapidly and efficiently, enhancing yield and batch consistency. Standardised, chemically defined protocols ensure cellular homogeneity and facilitate stringent quality control, while feeder-free, virus-free systems align with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards and minimise risks.41 42 These progenitors exhibit superior functional maturation and allow in vitro expansion for scalable manufacturing, critically avoiding extended pluripotent states and lowering tumourigenic risk. Employing single-cell and single-nucleus RNA sequencing, our previous preclinical research mapped the complete trajectory of spNPG cells from in vitro differentiation to functional integration in vivo. This established a novel mechanism of therapeutic action: targeted neuronal differentiation that facilitates integration into host neural circuits to enhance synaptic connectivity, alongside microenvironment remodelling that promotes neural repair. This work demonstrates iPSC-derived spNPGs as a potential treatment paradigm.43 44

This report outlines the rationale and detailed protocol for the study evaluating transplantation of iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells in patients with subacute SCI. The study protocol was approved by the respective Stem Cell Research Ethics Committees of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. YW2025-012-01) and the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Approval No. 2025-071-2). To the best of our knowledge, this constitutes the first systematically designed clinical investigation assessing both the safety and efficacy of this specific iPSC-derived cell therapy for subacute SCI (NCT06976229).45

Methods and analysis

Study objective, design and setting

This prospective clinical study is designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of human allogeneic iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells in patients with subacute SCI, specifically targeting a therapeutic intervention window of 14–60 days postinjury.

This multicentre, single-arm, open-label clinical study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov on 15 May 2025 (registration No. NCT06976229). The recruitment of participants commenced on 17 July 2025, and the study is expected to reach its primary completion by 21 February 2028. The revised study protocol (Protocol version 2.0) was released on 9 August 2025.

For this study, the sample size was not statistically driven but operationally defined. A total of 12–24 participants are planned for enrolment; 6–12 participants will be enrolled in the phase I single ascending dose (SAD) cohorts and 6–12 participants will be enrolled in the multiple ascending dose (MAD) cohorts.

This study employs a sequential ‘3+3’ dose-escalation design for both the SAD and MAD cohorts. Two predefined dose levels are investigated: dose level 1 (5×10⁷ cells/subject) and dose level 2 (1.5×10⁸ cells/subject). Escalation commences at dose level 1, progressing to dose level 2, and each participant receives only a single assigned dose level (figure 1).

In the SAD phase, each cohort will include one sentinel subject. After administration, the subject will be observed for 14 days. If no grade ≥3 adverse events (AEs) related to the investigational product (ie, definitely, probably or possibly related) or prespecified Adverse Events of Special Interest (AESIs) as assessed by the Safety Review Committee (SRC) are observed in the sentinel participants of the SAD cohort, subsequent participants may be enrolled. The composition, operational procedures and specific responsibilities of the SRC are detailed in the SRC Charter, which is provided as an online supplemental material. The severity of AEs observed during the trial will be assessed using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (NCI-CTCAE V.5.0) criteria.46

Dose escalation and phase progression within the SAD phase requires completion of the 28-day dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) observation period for the final subject in the current cohort and confirmation of safety and tolerability before progressing to the next higher dose cohort. To ensure absolute objectivity and regulatory compliance, the SRC will interpret AE severity strictly via CTCAE V.5.0, and determine causality attribution in accordance with the official ‘Technical Guidelines for Causality Assessment of Adverse Events in Clinical Trials’ mandated by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). Dose-escalation decisions are documented and only executed after safety data cleaning for the entire cohort is complete; all SRC decisions and raw data are subject to on-site regulatory inspections. Furthermore, transition from the SAD phase to the initiation of MAD Dose Level 1 is conditional on all SAD subjects completing their 28-day DLT observation period with confirmed safety and tolerability across all SAD cohorts. Subsequently, progression within the MAD phase to the next higher dose cohort requires that all subjects in the current MAD cohort complete their 28-day DLT observation period (timed from the last dose administration) and safety/tolerability is confirmed. These structured rules mandate safety reviews at each escalation step, prioritising participant safety.

Dose escalation will proceed as follows: Escalation will occur if none of the three participants in a cohort exhibits a DLT within the observation window. If a single DLT event is observed among the initial three participants, the cohort will be expanded by three additional subjects. Progression to the next dose level will require no DLTs in the expanded cohort; dose escalation will cease if ≥1 DLT occurs, deeming the level intolerable. Escalation will halt immediately if ≥2/3 initial participants experience a DLT, triggering evaluation by the SRC, which comprises sponsor medical personnel and site principal investigators, regarding potential de-escalation. on identifying the highest administered dose, if the preceding level has only three DLT-evaluable participants, three more will be enrolled. A dose level with ≤1 DLT among six evaluable participants will establish the Maximum Tolerated Dose (MTD). If the protocol-specified maximum dose is reached without MTD determination, no further escalation will occur. Following completion of the 28-day DLT assessment for the last MAD participant, the SRC and sponsor will jointly determine the recommended phase II dose (RP2D) based on phase I safety and efficacy data.

Grade ≥3 AEs assessed by the sponsor as related to the study drug (definitely, probably or possibly related), graded per the NCI-CTCAE v5.0;

To expedite the recruitment of participants and enhance the generalisability of study outcomes while minimising potential bias, this clinical study is performed at two hospitals in China. Recruitment and eligibility criteria of participants

Patients will be recruited from two hospitals in China. Written informed consents that have been approved by institutional ethics committees will be obtained from the patients before enrolment or any study-specific procedures (see online supplemental material for an English translation of the participant information and consent form).

Participants will receive no financial incentives for enrolment in this study. However, they will be reimbursed for travel expenses and compensated for the time and inconvenience associated with biospecimen collection (blood and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)). Travel reimbursement is provided at 200 RMB per visit for participants within the province and RMB500 per visit for those from outside the province. For unscheduled visits, travel reimbursement is RMB100 (within-province) and RMB200 (out-of-province) per visit, capped at 15 visits. Additionally, participants receive compensation of RMB200 per blood sampling and RMB500 per CSF collection, with the final total calculated based on the actual number of procedures completed.

Intervention

The clinical and GMP-grade iPSC-derived spinal motor neuron progenitor cell therapeutic product (XS228) was developed and manufactured by XellSmart Biomedical (Suzhou, China). This clinical trial evaluates the intrathecal transplantation of these iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells as the investigational therapeutic agent. In the SAD cohorts, participants undergo a single intrathecal administration at one of two predefined dose levels: 5×10⁷ cells or 1.5×10⁸ cells. Participants in the MAD cohorts receive repeated administrations at biweekly intervals (every 14 days) for a total of four transplantations, with the cell dose per administration corresponding to their designated dosing level from the SAD phase.

Manufacturing, storage and transport of investigational product

The XS228 product, comprising hiPSC-derived spinal motor neuron progenitor cells, is a sponsor-supplied investigational therapy manufactured under strict GMP compliance by XellSmart Biomedical (Suzhou, China). The clinical translation of this product is supported by extensive preclinical validation, including safety and efficacy profiling in both murine and non-human primate (cynomolgus monkey) models. The production uses a chemically defined, feeder-free directed differentiation protocol. Briefly, clinical-grade hiPSCs are sequentially exposed to highly specific small-molecule modulators, including dual SMAD inhibitors (SB431542 and LDN193189), Wnt agonists (CHIR99021), retinoic acid (RA), and Sonic hedgehog (Shh) pathway activators (purmorphamine), to efficiently drive neuromesodermal specification and subsequent spinal neural progenitor commitment. Detailed methodological validation regarding the generation, differentiation trajectory and characterisation of this specific XS228 cellular product has been described in our recent work.44

Following differentiation and stringent quality control testing, which incorporates preadministration release criteria including target cell concentration, strict cell viability thresholds, confirmed sterility and endotoxin limits, the final XS228 cell product is formulated in a clinical-grade cryopreservation medium, aliquoted into sterile cryovials, and subjected to a precisely controlled-rate freezing process. Long-term storage is securely maintained in the vapour phase of liquid nitrogen (≤−130°C) to guarantee maximum cellular viability and phenotypic stability. For deployment to the clinical site, the product is transported via a fully validated cold-chain logistics network using specialised liquid nitrogen dry shippers. These shippers are equipped with continuous real-time temperature monitoring devices to ensure strict adherence to the required temperature parameters during transit. Prior to administration, the cryopreserved cells are rapidly thawed at the clinical site according to standardised operating procedures (SOPs).

Study procedure

This single-arm, open-label, phase I study evaluates the safety, tolerability and early efficacy signals of the hiPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cell suspension in participants with subacute SCI. The study features sequential SAD and MAD cohorts. All participants undergo screening (days −7 to −4), a baseline period (days −3 to −1), followed by the treatment period and extended follow-up. To prevent the immune rejection of the allogeneic cell graft, systemic immunosuppression with oral tacrolimus commences on day −3 (prior to cell administration) to ensure steady-state therapeutic levels are achieved by the time of transplantation. Treatment is initiated at a dose of 0.05–0.15 mg/kg administered twice daily (online supplemental sTable 1). Given tacrolimus’s narrow therapeutic index, strict therapeutic drug monitoring is mandated. Whole-blood tacrolimus trough levels (C0) are closely monitored, with doses dynamically titrated to maintain a target therapeutic window of 7–10 ng/mL. This specific target range was selected based on established clinical allo-transplantation guidelines to achieve an optimal balance: providing localised and systemic immunosuppression to protect the graft while mitigating the risks of calcineurin inhibitor-induced toxicities (such as nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity) and opportunistic infections.48 49 Dosing duration and adjustments based on rejection risk and tolerance are recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF). SAD cohort participants receive a single intrathecal injection of cells, while MAD cohort participants receive four intrathecal injections administered biweekly (online supplemental sTable 1). After completing the treatment period, participants enter the primary follow-up phase until day 360. Following the completion of the primary 360-day observation period, all participants transition into a long-term safety follow-up phase, continuing until death, withdrawal of consent, or 2 years (day 720) after the first cell administration, whichever occurs first. In alignment with regulatory guidelines for the risk management of stem cell-derived products, this extended phase specifically monitors for delayed risks, such as tumourigenicity or ectopic tissue formation. During this period, follow-up assessments are conducted every 6 months via telephone interviews. To minimise participant burden during these long-term intervals, daily diary cards are discontinued. Instead of capturing all routine AEs over a 6-month recall period, investigators use a targeted questionnaire to systematically record only major safety milestones: patient chief complaints, emergence of tumours, subsequent treatments for the SCI, survival status (including cause of death, if applicable) and any serious AEs (SAEs) suspected to be related to the investigational product.

To ensure safety and procedural consistency across all participating study sites, the intrathecal administration of the iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells will be executed following a strict SOP. During the procedure, the participant is placed in the lateral decubitus position with maximal lumbar flexion sustained. Lumbar puncture is performed at the L3/4, L4/5 or L5/S1 interspace under local anaesthesia.

Prior to cell delivery, the opening CSF pressure is measured and recorded. To maintain intracranial volume homeostasis and prevent pressure fluctuations, a volume of CSF closely corresponding to the intended therapeutic injection volume (2 mL for dose Level 1 (1 vial: 5×10⁷ cells); 6 mL for dose level 2 (3 vials: 1.5×10⁸ cells)), alongside an additional 2–5 mL aliquot for baseline laboratory testing, is cautiously withdrawn.

Subsequently, the cell suspension is manually administered at a strictly controlled, continuous rate of 1–2 mL per minute. Following the completion of the cell infusion, the needle is flushed with 1 to 2 mL of sterile normal saline to ensure the complete delivery of the cells into the subarachnoid space. Finally, the closing CSF pressure is measured. To minimise the risk of intrathecal infusion-related complications, all participants are required to remain in a strict supine position for a minimum of 6 hours postprocedure.

All intrathecal administrations via lumbar puncture will be exclusively performed by licensed attending physicians or senior surgeons specialising in spine surgery who have completed the protocol-specific training for this clinical study. Furthermore, all protocol-specified follow-up evaluations will be conducted collaboratively by clinical doctors or physiotherapists. To ensure procedural consistency and strict adherence to the protocol, all participating medical personnel will undergo study-specific training prior to their involvement.

Assessments for safety

The primary safety objectives of this phase I study (SAD and MAD phases) are to evaluate the safety profile of iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells in participants with subacute SCI. Specifically, this includes: (1) Determining the incidence of treatment-related AEs and SAEs occurring within 28 days following the SAD or the last administration in MAD; (2) Identifying any DLTs and establishing the MTD and (3) Defining the RP2D. An AE is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a participant following provision of signed informed consent, irrespective of a suspected causal relationship to the investigational product. AEs include worsening of pre-existing medical conditions, newly emergent medical conditions or symptoms/signs, and clinically significant abnormal laboratory findings not attributable to concomitant illness. An SAE is any AE that fulfils at least one of the following criteria: results in death; is life-threatening (indicating immediate risk of death at the time of the event); requires or prolongs inpatient hospitalisation; results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity; is a congenital anomaly/birth defect; or constitutes another medically significant event requiring intervention to prevent one of the aforementioned outcomes (based on medical/scientific judgement). Investigators are required to record all AEs occurring from informed consent through the end of the planned follow-up period; however, during the long-term follow-up phase, only SAEs assessed as potentially related to iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cells will be documented. For DLT assessment, the observation window spans 28 days after the SAD or the final dose in the MAD. Participants return to the clinic on day 29 (SAD) or day 71 (MAD) for safety assessments encompassing vital signs, physical examination, laboratory tests (haematology, urinalysis, clinical chemistry, coagulation, lymphocyte subsets), 12-lead ECG, AE recording and review of concomitant medications/therapies. DLTs will be characterised based on the nature, frequency and severity of AEs and SAEs observed during this defined period and their relationship to cell administration.

Crucially, the discontinuation of treatment does not constitute a full withdrawal from the study; protocol-specified follow-up assessments will continue unless the participant explicitly revokes consent for all subsequent procedures or is deemed lost to follow-up (defined as unresponsiveness after at least three documented contact attempts). On full withdrawal, investigators will conduct a termination visit, document the reasons in the eCRF, follow-up on any ongoing AEs, and ascertain the participant’s willingness for future telephone contact. Data and biospecimens collected prior to the withdrawal of consent will be retained and used for the study unless explicitly objected to by the participant.

Statistical analysis

Analysis will use several predefined sets: the full analysis set (FAS, all enrolled/dosed participants; primary for baseline/demographics and efficacy), the safety set (SS, FAS participants with ≥1 post-baseline safety assessment; primary for safety), a DLT analysis set (assessing DLT events in defined phases/groups with sufficient dosing/safety evaluation) and an exploratory analysis set (EAS), (FAS participants with specific baseline/post-baseline biomarker/imaging data). Descriptive statistics (counts/percentages for categorical data; mean, SD, median, min, max for continuous variables) will be performed, stratified by trial stage and dose group where applicable using SAS V.9.4, with two-sided α=0.05 unless specified. Participant flow through screening, early termination and analysis sets (with reasons for exclusion) will be summarised. Baseline demographics/disease characteristics (FAS), protocol deviations (categorised and listed, with serious deviations summarised by stage/dose/type based on FAS following locked database review) and drug exposure (SS) will be reported. Efficacy endpoints (eg, ASIA impairment grade, sensory/motor scores, SCIM-III) will be summarised descriptively across stages/dose groups based primarily on FAS. Within applicable stages, between-group comparisons will use Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for ASIA grade changes and Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA)/t-tests for continuous efficacy scores.

As this is a phase I study, the primary objective is the evaluation of safety and tolerability. Consequently, given the limited sample size inherent to the dose-escalation design, all efficacy evaluations are considered exploratory. While formal statistical methods are prespecified to ensure analytical rigour, such as using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for ASIA grade changes and ANCOVA/t-tests for continuous efficacy scores across stages/dose groups based on the FAS, these analyses are primarily intended to observe preliminary therapeutic trends and establish a foundation for the design of future phase II trials.

Safety evaluation (SS) includes descriptive analyses of treatment-emergent AEs (incidence, relationships, severity, SAEs, withdrawals), laboratory parameters, vital signs, physical exams and ECGs (with summary statistics and shift tables for clinically significant findings). EAS will descriptively summarise pharmacodynamic biomarkers, neurophysiological measures, post-void residual volume and spinal cord MRI/DTI by visit.

Missing values in safety data will not be imputed. Efficacy data will be analysed using an observed-case approach.

Data management and quality control

All study personnel are bound by strict confidentiality. A quality assurance system ensures protocol adherence and data integrity per ICH-GCP and regulatory standards. Tianjin Clin-Nov Medical Technology Development, acting as the Clinical Research Organisation (CRO), is responsible for independent data verification, data cleaning, and the generation of relevant data summaries (such as tables, listings, and figures) to support the safety evaluations. Sponsor/CRO representatives retain audit rights to verify procedures and data. Source data is captured using a validated Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system managed per a predefined Data Management Plan. Trained site personnel enter data; all changes are electronically tracked. Automated logic checks and manual reviews ensure data quality by identifying and resolving discrepancies prior to analysis. Standardised medical dictionaries (MedDRA, WHODrug Global) enable consistent coding. Database locking requires resolution of all queries, verification of source data, sponsor sign-off on coding, final investigator e-signature, and formal QC checks. Post-lock changes are strictly controlled, requiring documented authorisation. These processes collectively ensure reliable, high-quality data suitable for analysis.

Discussion

A major strength of this first-in-human protocol is its evaluation of an ‘off-the-shelf’,’ allogeneic iPSC-derived motor neuron progenitor cell therapy for SCI repair, with a specific focus on motor function recovery and broad translational potential across other diseases. The trial is systematically structured, using a primary endpoint to assess safety, alongside predefined secondary and exploratory endpoints designed to capture preliminary efficacy signals and support future mechanistic investigations.

However, the study design involves several methodological limitations that must be acknowledged. First, despite its implementation across multiple clinical centres, the open-label, single-arm nature of the trial carries an inherent risk of observational bias. Without a concurrent randomised, sham, or placebo control group, it remains challenging to definitively distinguish the specific therapeutic efficacy of the XS228 intervention from the spontaneous neurological recovery that often occurs during the subacute phase of SCI. Second, as an early-phase trial, the relatively small sample size is primarily powered to evaluate safety, MTD and RP2D, which limits the statistical power required to draw definitive conclusions regarding efficacy. Third, the stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria, while methodologically necessary to ensure cohort homogeneity and safeguard participant safety in a first-in-human setting, may limit the immediate generalisability of the findings to the broader, highly heterogeneous SCI population (eg, those with chronic injuries). Finally, the requisite 12-month systemic immunosuppression regimen introduces potential confounding variables when evaluating the overall safety profile, as it may cause independent AEs. Consequently, while this study will establish critical safety profiles and capture preliminary efficacy signals, subsequent large-scale, randomised, controlled trials will be imperative for definitive efficacy confirmation.

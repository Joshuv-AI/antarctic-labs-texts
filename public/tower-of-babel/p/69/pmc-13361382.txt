The Factors Associated with the Health-Related Quality of Life in Pregnant Women in the second and Third Trimesters: Evidence from Jiangsu Province, Eastern China
Authors: Lianshuai Zheng, Xu Chen, Yao Meng, Zhongwu Hu, Pingping Tang, Lina Jiao, Dongmei Yan, Jizheng Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe health-related quality of life (HRQoL) of pregnant women has received much attention. Understanding the factors affecting their HRQoL will help health care providers develop targeted strategies for pregnancy care. This study investigated the current status of pregnant women’s HRQoL in Jiangsu Province, Eastern China, explored the impact of chronic pain and other factors on it.Materials and MethodsAn institution-based cross-sectional survey was conducted among eligible pregnant women attending prenatal care in the Obstetrics Department, Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital. A structured questionnaire referring to the characteristics of the study objects, chronic pain conditions, and HRQoL was used to obtain data. HRQoL was assessed using the European quality of life scale-5 dimensions (EQ-5D). Mann–Whitney U-test and multiple linear regression models were used to explore the factors associated with HRQoL.ResultsThe study included a total of 614 pregnant women. The mean EQ-5D utility score was 0.87±0.13, and the median score was 0.87. The mean EQ-visual analogue scale (VAS) score was 84.47±16.02, and the median score was 88.00. The incidence of chronic pain was 31.8%. Multiple linear regression models showed that pregnant women with irregular daily routines (β = −0.026 and β = −3.142, respectively, p < 0.05) and chronic pain (β = −0.105 and β = −6.767, respectively, p < 0.001) were more likely to have a low EQ-5D utility scores and EQ-VAS scores. In addition, significantly lower EQ-5D utility scores were also observed in pregnant women who had two or more pregnancies (β = −0.022, p < 0.05) and exercised irregularly or not (β = −0.022, p < 0.05).ConclusionChronic pain and daily routines were the main factors affecting HRQoL of pregnant women. When developing intervention measures to improve HRQoL, focusing on chronic pain and daily routines might be effective.Keywords: HRQoL, chronic pain, pregnant women, EQ-5D, China

Introduction

The quality of life proposed by the World Health Organization (WHO) is a multidimensional concept, including physical, psychological and social functioning, and is an evaluation of the subjective feelings of the tested person. Medical practitioners have combined this theory with medical practice to develop the concept of health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Defined as subjective satisfaction with health status under the influence of disease, physical functioning, psychological functioning, medical interventions, changes in the social environment, etc, which is linked to the economic and cultural context and value orientation.1 In clinical practice, the use of different scales and questionnaires to evaluate the HRQoL of the test subjects can better respond to the influence of the factors on it, and is an important guide to the prevention and control of diseases.2 The European quality of life scale-5 dimensions (EQ-5D) is a standardized scale for measuring health status.3 The questionnaire was developed by the European Society for Quality of Life (EuroQol) to provide a simple, universal measure of health. In the EQ-5D, after completing questions on five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, the coefficients and constants of each dimension are weighted and converted to obtain the health utility (the EQ-5D index). A health problem or abnormality in any of the five dimensions of the EQ-5D is called an incomplete health status. The scale has been validated in a Chinese population,4,5 and Huailiang Wu et al used it to assess the HRQoL of 908 pregnant women during their pregnancy in Guangdong, China.6 Therefore, using standardized tools such as the EQ-5D to assess HRQoL of pregnant women can help identify key influencing factors and guide targeted interventions.

Pregnancy is a special physiological period for women, with a series of changes in maternal body structure and function, hormone secretion, emotional state and social activities.7 HRQoL during pregnancy directly affects maternal physical and mental health, which is critical to maternal and fetal health.8 Under emerging health risks such as the COVID-19 pandemic, research on this issue becomes even more urgent.9 Previous studies have focused on the impact of more serious pregnancy complications on maternal HRQoL, such as hyperemesis gravidarum, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes mellitus, and preeclampsia.1 However, limited research has focused on the impact of “less serious” but highly prevalent problems, such as chronic pain, irregular daily routines, and physical inactivity, on HRQoL during pregnancy.10

Chronic pain, defined as persistent or intermittent pain lasting longer than 3 months, is a common symptom during pregnancy and is often overlooked as a normal physiologic response, leading to higher rates of cesarean section and increased risk of pregnancy and delivery.11,12 Currently, the prevalence of chronic pain during pregnancy and its impact on maternal HRQoL is unclear due to different descriptions of pain sites in studies, different definitions of pain (chronic pain and pain of shorter duration are usually not differentiated), as well as differences in economic and cultural backgrounds and social orientations, hindering the development of health care strategies during pregnancy.13 In addition, there are no reports on the factors influencing HRQoL during pregnancy in China, and fewer reports in other parts of Asia, which is not conducive to the provision of pregnancy healthcare support for pregnant women.1,10,13,14

To address these gaps, this study aimed to assess HRQoL and its association with chronic pain and other modifiable factors among pregnant women in Jiangsu Province, Eastern China, using the EQ-5D scale. The findings would inform the development of pregnancy healthcare strategies and support for pregnant women.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Setting

An institution-based cross-sectional survey was conducted from August to October 2022 in Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital Affiliated to Kangda College of Nanjing Medical University, Jiangsu Province, Eastern China. Pregnant women who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria and attended prenatal care in the institution’s obstetrics department were recruited using convenience sampling. During the period of this research, China was in the stage of normalizing its coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic prevention and control measures. The daily medical services of the hospital have been operating normally, with strict pandemic prevention measures in place. Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital Affiliated to Kangda College of Nanjing Medical University is the only national third-grade class-A maternal and child health hospital in Lianyungang. It is a high medical level and service quality hospital that provides a full range of health care services for local women and children. Its annual number of births reached 8458 in 2021, demonstrating strong regional representativeness.

Study Participants

The inclusion criteria for the study were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) gestational period of ≥ 14 weeks (only pregnant women in the second and third trimesters were included because chronic pain was defined as pain that lasted for at least 3 months in this study); (3) ability to communicate normally and understand the questionnaire content; (4) consent to participate in the study. The exclusion criteria for the study were as follows: (1) presence of psychiatric disorders or pregnancy complications; (2) no history of COVID-19 infection; (3) refusal to participate in the study. Pregnant women who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were invited to participate in this study. The investigators explained the purpose and process of the research in detail to the pregnant woman, and emphasized the anonymity of the study and the confidentiality of the data. Pregnant women who consented to participate were asked to self-fill out a structured questionnaire. The investigator was responsible for both guidance and interpretation. The investigators were trained, familiar with the content of the questionnaire and relatively fixed investigation teams. The training content mainly included research content, research purpose, screening of research objects, organization and implementation of field investigation, general requirements and matters needing attention. The minimum sample size for this study was calculated using the single population proportion formula: n=z2*p*(1-p))/d2. A 50% proportion (p), 95% confidence interval (CI), 5% margin of error (d), and 20% non-response rate were adopted due to the lack of relevant studies at the study site. The minimum required sample size was calculated to be 461. To minimize sampling error, a total of 620 pregnant women were recruited and completed a questionnaire, of which 6 questionnaires were excluded due to logical errors or missing data. Finally, data from 614 pregnant women were included in this study, with an effective rate of 99.03%.

Data Collection Tool

In the current study, a self-designed structured questionnaire was used to collect data. The design of the structured questionnaire was based on literature review and involved consultations with experts in relevant fields such as obstetrics, pain management, and public health. Before the formal research began, a pre-survey was conducted at the research site. Its main purpose was to adjust the wording of the questions or delete the excessive items based on the feedback, in order to enhance the rationality of the questionnaire and reduce its flaws. All the pre-survey data were solely used for questionnaire optimization and were not included in the statistical analysis of the final research. The questionnaire was in Chinese and it included the characteristics of the study objects, chronic pain conditions, and HRQoL. The characteristics of the study objects were self-designed by the researchers based on the literature review and research needs and refined under the guidance of experts in relevant fields such as obstetrics, pain management, and public health. Characteristics of the study objects included age, education level, gestational trimester, number of pregnancies, daily routines, physical exercise status, average sitting time per day, and average time of electronic screen exposure per day.

Chronic pain was defined as pain that lasted continuously or intermittently for more than 3 months.15 We screened for the presence of chronic pain by asking pregnant women “Have you had continuous pain for at least 3 months during your pregnancy”. If the answer was “yes”, the site of the chronic pain was further asked.

HRQoL was assessed by means of a simple, convenient, easy to perform, and highly reliable EQ-5D. It has been validated in the Chinese population.4,5 It included a self-rated health status questionnaire and a visual analogue scale (VAS). The former is a standardized measure of health status with five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Each dimension had 3 levels of response: 1 (no problems), 2 (moderate problems), and 3 (extreme problems). By combining the levels of each of the five dimensions, there are 243 possible combinations of health status.16 Utility scores for the 243 combinations of health status were obtained using the Chinese utility value set.17 Chinese utility scores range from −0.149 to 1. Health status 11111 has a utility score of 1, meaning no problems in any of the five dimensions, while 33333 has a utility score of −0.149, indicating the worst health status. The questionnaire had acceptable internal consistency in the current study. The latter assesses overall current health, which is represented by a vertical line. It asks respondents to rate their overall health status from 0 (worst imaginable health state) to 100 (best imaginable health state).18

Statistical Analysis

All collected questionnaires underwent double-entry verification by independent data clerks to minimize typing errors. After completeness and consistency checks, data without missing items and logical errors were imported into SPSS21.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, State of New York) for statistical analysis. Descriptive analyses were performed, with categorical variables described as frequencies and percentages and continuous variables as means and standard deviations (SDs) or medians and interquartile ranges. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to check the normality of the continuous variables. The results showed that neither the EQ-5D utility scores nor the EQ-VAS scores satisfied a normal distribution, so their medians and interquartile ranges were calculated. The Mann–Whitney U-test was used to test for differences in EQ-5D utility scores and EQ-VAS scores at different levels for each variable. Because multiple linear regression could effectively control confounding variables, multiple linear regression models were used to identify variables significantly associated with EQ-5D utility scores and EQ-VAS scores. Although the EQ-5D utility scores and EQ-VAS scores did not satisfy a normal distribution, the multiple linear regression was relatively robust to the distribution assumption of the dependent variable, especially when the sample size was large. In addition, the multicollinearity of the models was tested, and it was found that the variance inflation factor was less than 5 and the tolerance was much greater than 0.1. This indicated that collinearity was absent. All tests were two-sided and the statistically significance level was considered a p < 0.05.

Ethic Approval and Informed Consent

The Ethics Committee of Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital Affiliated to Kangda College of Nanjing Medical University approved the study (Number: LYG-ME202001). Prior to the survey, all participants were informed about the purpose and confidentiality of the study. Each participant provided written informed consent. All methods in this study were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Results

HRQoL

In the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, a total of 614 pregnant women participated in the study. The distribution of EQ-5D self-rated health status was shown in Figure 1. Pregnant women reported the highest proportion of moderate problems in the pain/discomfort dimension of the EQ-5D (56.2%), followed by the anxiety/depression dimension (24.6%), and the lowest proportion in the self-care dimension (4.1%). Pregnant women reported the highest proportion of extreme problems in the mobility dimension and anxiety/depression dimension of EQ-5D (1.0%), followed by pain/discomfort dimension (0.7%), and the lowest proportion of self-care dimension and usual activities dimension (0.3%) (Figure 1). The mean of EQ-5D utility scores obtained using the Chinese utility value set was 0.87 ± 0.13, the median score was 0.87, and the interquartile range was 0.78–1.00. The EQ-5D utility score of 1 accounted for 35.0%. The mean of EQ-VAS score was 84.47 ± 16.02, the median score was 88.00, and the interquartile range was 79.00–99.00. The EQ-VAS score of 100 accounted for 24.1%. The proportion of EQ-VAS scores in the 91–100 group was the highest (38.6%) (Figure 2).

Study Sample Characteristics

Among 614 pregnant women, the mean age was 29.49 ± 4.36. A large proportion of pregnant women (88.1%) were younger than 35 years of age, and less than one third (30.8%) had less than a college level of education. A minority of pregnant women (11.7%) were currently in their second trimester, and more than half (51.1%) were having one pregnancy. More than one fifth (22.3%) of pregnant women had irregular daily routines, and about two thirds (66.6%) did not exercise or exercise irregularly. Nearly three fifths (57.0%) of pregnant women spent an average of 5 or more hours per day in a sitting position, and more than half (53.9%) had an average of 5 or more hours per day of electronic screen exposure. Among the study subjects, 31.8% had chronic pain during pregnancy. The site of chronic pain was mainly concentrated in the pelvic girdle, followed by the lower back (Table 1 and Figure 3).

Associated Factors of HRQoL

The results of the multiple linear regression indicated that the EQ-5D utility scores of pregnant women with two or more pregnancies were lower than those with one pregnancy (β = −0.022, 95% CI: −0.043 - −0.001, p < 0.05). Compared with pregnant women with regular daily routines, those with irregular daily routines had lower EQ-5D utility scores (β = −0.026, 95% CI: −0.050 - −0.002, p < 0.05). Pregnant women who did not exercise or exercised irregularly had lower EQ-5D utility scores than those who exercised regularly (β = −0.022, 95% CI: −0.043 - −0.001, p < 0.05). Pregnant women with chronic pain had lower EQ-5D utility scores compared to those without chronic pain (β = −0.105, 95% CI: −0.126 - −0.084, p < 0.001) (Table 3). The results of multiple linear regression showed that pregnant women with irregular daily routines had lower EQ-VAS scores than those with regular daily routines (β = −3.142, 95% CI: −6.275 - −0.009, p < 0.05). Pregnant women with chronic pain had lower EQ-VAS scores compared to those without chronic pain (β = −6.767, 95% CI: −9.482 - −4.053, p < 0.001) (Table 4). Therefore, the number of pregnancies, daily routines, physical exercise status, and chronic pain were associated factors of EQ-5D utility scores, and daily routines and chronic pain were associated factors of EQ-VAS scores in pregnant women.

Discussion

In the univariate analysis, this study found that HRQoL of pregnant women was lower in the third trimester than the second trimester, which is consistent with other related studies.6,19 This is because the fetus tends to stabilize in the second trimester, the risk of abortion is lower, and pregnant women do not need to worry about the development of the fetus; early pregnancy reactions such as vomiting, dizziness, headache, etc. are relieved or eliminated in this period; the development of the fetus has not yet had an impact on the usual activities, and pregnant women feel a sense of temporary relaxation physically and mentally. In this study, 35.0% of the pregnant women had an EQ-5D utility score of 1 (no problems in any of the five dimensions), and the mean of EQ-5D utility scores was 0.87±0.13, and the mean of EQ-VAS score was 84.47±16.02. In the study of pregnant women in Guangdong Province of China by Huailiang Wu et al, pregnant women with an EQ-5D utility score of 1 accounted for 391%, the mean of EQ-5D utility scores was 0.83±0.19, and the mean of EQ-VAS scores was 87.28±9.13, the mean of EQ-5D utility scores was lower than this study,6 which may be due to the fact that pregnant women with complications in pregnancy were not excluded from their study; and the mean of EQ-VAS scores was higher, probably because this study only focused on pregnant women without complications during pregnancy (except chronic pain), whereas pregnant women with other complications that are considered more serious, such as gestational diabetes mellitus, cholestasis of pregnancy, and gestational hypertension, usually receive more clinical and nursing care, which suggests that pregnant women with chronic pain in pregnancy have not yet received enough attention. It was important to note that data collection for this study occurred between August and October 2022, during the COVID-19 pandemic in China. Lianyungang, Jiangsu Province, Eastern China maintained a stable COVID-19 situation at that time. Only five confirmed cases were identified over the three-month period, and all patients were promptly transferred to designated hospitals for isolation and treatment. As a result, no large-scale lockdown measures were implemented. Accordingly, none of the participants in this study were COVID-19 patients or subject to lockdown restrictions. Nevertheless, public health measures such as localized lockdowns in certain areas, social distancing, and increased healthcare burden may have influenced pregnant women’s psychological state and daily routines potentially affecting their HRQoL.

Chronic pain occurs most frequently around the pelvic girdle and lower back during pregnancy, which is an important factor affecting the HRQoL of pregnant women in the second and third trimesters. In this study, the prevalence of chronic pain during pregnancy in 614 pregnant women from Jiangsu Province, Eastern China, was 36.6%, of which the prevalence of pelvic girdle pain was 16.9% and lower back pain was 10.7%. According to published literature, the prevalence of pain during pregnancy ranges from 3% to 90%, with prevalence in the lower back and pelvic girdle, about 33% of women have pain that persists up to 3 months postpartum, and 8.5% have persistent pain up to 2 years postpartum, resulting in a decrease in the HRQoL during pregnancy and for a considerable period of time postpartum, women are eager to have their concerns taken seriously, and want to be given information and guidance on this symptom.14,20 However, current studies of pain in pregnancy generally do not distinguish between chronic pain and pain of shorter duration, and a significant proportion of studies investigating the site of occurrence do not distinguish between lower back pain and pelvic girdle pain, which can lead to a lack of clarity about the prevalence of site-specific pain, which affects decision-making about treatment and care, leading to a sustained decline in the HRQoL during pregnancy, and even to adverse pregnancy outcomes.12,13,21 In the treatment of chronic pain during pregnancy, analgesic medications pose potential risks to maternal and fetal safety and health. Encouragingly, some traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and exercise therapies, such as acupuncture, neurostimulation, wearing orthotic devices, massage, pelvic stabilization exercises, and spinal stabilization exercises, have been shown to help alleviate pain symptoms during pregnancy.22–26 Following evaluation of their efficacy and safety through large-scale, high-quality research, these conservative treatments are expected to be widely adopted for the management of chronic pain during pregnancy.27 Therefore, routine assessment of pain during pregnancy should be conducted in clinical practice, with clear differentiation between acute and chronic pain and precise definition of low back pain and pelvic girdle pain to guide individualized treatment. Public health policymakers should encourage research on intervention strategies for chronic pain during pregnancy, develop unified clinical guidelines, and integrate pain management into routine antenatal care services.

In addition to chronic pain, physical exercise also showed a significant association with HRQoL. Regular physical exercise was associated with higher EQ-5D utility scores during pregnancy and was not associated with self-reported EQ-VAS scores, which is in line with the study by André Souza Leite Vieira et al28 Justyna Krzepota et al found that the level of physical activity during pregnancy was positively associated with HRQoL, and that physical exercise differentiated from physical activity due to work (passive) is defined as leisure time physical activity (active).29,30 In addition, studies have shown that aerobic, resistance, combined, and group-based integrated exercises are beneficial for the enhancement of the HRQoL in pregnant women.31,32 Whereas Ana L Vallim et al found no correlation between water aerobic exercise sessions and HRQoL during pregnancy in a controlled observational study.33 Therefore, the type, intensity, and frequency of physical exercise may all potentially influence HRQoL during pregnancy. In light of this, we recommend that healthcare professionals differentiate between physical exercise and physical activity at work during prenatal care, assess the total acceptable level of physical activity, and take full advantage of the controllable nature of physical exercise in order to develop an exercise program for pregnant women.

Another important finding of this study was the role of daily routine. This study confirmed that maintaining a regular routine in all aspects of daily life is important for HRQoL during pregnancy, and that influences such as sedentariness and excessive exposure to electronic screens, adjusted for multiple linear regression analyses, are no longer influences on HRQoL during pregnancy, which was indirectly confirmed in the study by Kara M. Whitaker et al30 Regular daily routine is defined as: a stable, orderly arrangement followed in daily life, including diet, sleep, exercise, work and rest. According to TCM theory, a regular routine can maintain better physical and mental health, which is consistent with the Biological Clock theory of Western medicine. During pregnancy, a pregnant woman’s physical and mental health is under greater stress than usual. Establishing a regular diet, sleep, exercise, work and rest routine can have a positive impact on immunity, endocrine stabilization, sleep quality, better weight management, mood management, and work efficiency, thus directly improving HRQoL.

In this study, the number of pregnancies showed a association with EQ-5D utility scores during pregnancy, which was not associated with pregnant women’s self-reported EQ-VAS scores; and pregnant women’s age was not associated with HRQoL, a finding that is consistent with the vast majority of studies.1,19,34 In addition, this study found that educational level was not associated with pregnant women’s HRQoL during pregnancy, which is inconsistent with the results of a cross-sectional study of 403 pregnant women in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology at the Sandeman Provincial Hospital in Quetta, Pakistan, conducted by Rabia Ishaq et al19 and the same questionnaire was used by L. Shams on 150 Iranian pregnant women living in rural areas of Iran and 150 pregnant women in Afghanistan, and it was found that Iranian pregnant women’s mean score of HRQoL was lower than that of Afghan pregnant women’s, and educational level was associated with the HRQoL of Iranian pregnant women and not with that of Afghan pregnant women.34 This shows that the effect of education level and economic income on the HRQoL of pregnant women varies greatly in different economic and social contexts, therefore, different countries and regions should support pregnant women according to their local economic and social contexts to improve their HRQoL.35

Limitations

Although this study provided some important findings, there were some limitations that needed to be mentioned. First, the cross-sectional design precluded causal inference regarding the relationships between chronic pain, daily routines, and HRQoL during pregnancy. Second, data were collected during the COVID-19 pandemic (August-October 2022), which might have introduced bias due to pandemic-related restrictions, psychological stress, and altered healthcare access. Third, this was a single-center study conducted in Lianyungang, Jiangsu Province, Eastern China. Given regional variations in culture, healthcare systems, and socioeconomic status, the findings might not be generalizable to other populations or regions. Fourth, unmeasured confounding factors such as household income and social support, were not assessed and might have influenced HRQoL during pregnancy.

Conclusions

In the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, irregular daily routines and chronic pain were the key factors affecting the HRQoL of pregnant women in the second and third trimesters. When developing interventions to improve the HRQoL of pregnant women, greater emphasis should be placed on daily routines and chronic pain. Furthermore, healthcare providers should also pay more attention to pregnant women with chronic pain.

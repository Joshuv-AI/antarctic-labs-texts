Comparison between the Outcomes of Post-Operative Surgically Induced Astigmism between Inverted V Incision and Frown Incision for Manually Operated Small-Incision Cataract Surgery
Authors: Meenu Babber, Ahmad Husain, Sushil Ojha, Vijay Bhandari, Sayma Aleem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Due to its self-sealing suture-less incision, manually operated small-incision cataract surgery (MSICS) seems to be a glimmer of hope for addressing the cataract crisis in underdeveloped nations.Aim:This study has been conducted to compare the outcomes of post-operative surgically induced astigmism between inverted V (chevron) incision and frown incision for MSICS.Materials and Methods:100 patients with uncomplicated senile cataracts were enrolled in this prospective cross-sectional study. Patients received the Frown and the Inverted V “Chevron” incisions, respectively, after being randomly assigned to groups A and B.Results:The incidence of patients with no surgically induced astigmatism (SIA) was greater in the inverted V incision. 20 (52.63%) and 19 (45.23%) patients reported SIA (0-1) in the frown group and the inverted V incision group, respectively.Conclusion:Successful outcomes were obtained in patients who underwent MSICS using either a frown incision or an inverted incision. Our study added to the existing body of information that an inverted V incision causes less medically induced astigmatism than a frown incision.

INTRODUCTION

Despite the fact that over 95% of patients can safely undergo modern cataract surgery, problems like posterior capsule rupture and endophthalmitis can have disastrous effects on both the patient and the physician.[12] Surgically produced astigmatism is one consequence that can be handled and monitored but never completely eradicated, provided that all other problems are avoided. The results of cataract surgery are improving daily due to the introduction of new, cutting-edge cataract treatments that use contemporary equipment.[34]

However, because of its affordable price, MSICS seems to be a potential method for addressing the cataract problem in developing nations.[56] Both preoperative astigmatism and surgically accomplished astigmatism (SIA) have a substantial impact on visual results following surgery for cataracts.[78] To minimize surgically accomplished astigmatism, MSICS employs a range of scleral incisions. Straight and frown (F) incisions are the most often used incision forms in MSICS. Additionally, inverted V (Chevron) incision has been recommended to reduce surgically caused astigmatism after surgery.[89]

This study has been conducted to compare the outcomes of post-operative SIA between inverted V (chevron) incision and frown incision for MSICS.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

100 patients with uncomplicated senile cataracts were enrolled in this prospective cross-sectional study. Patients received the Frown and the Inverted V “Chevron” incisions, respectively, after being randomly assigned to groups A and B. Fifty patients were assigned to each group A and group B.

Group A: Patients received Frown incision (n = 50)

Group B: Patients received inverted V incision (n = 50)

Following surgery, patients were evaluated on days 1, 7, 4, 6, and 12 weeks. After surgery, prednisolone acetate eye drops (1%) were applied eight times a day, and gatifloxacin eye drops (0.3%) were applied four times a day. Additionally, mydriatic, along with anti-glaucoma drugs, were administered as needed. Over six weeks, prednisolone was lowered. Drug administration lasted for six weeks. At every visit, slit lamp results, best corrected visual acuity (BCVA), and uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA) were noted. At the fourth week following surgery, keratometry was performed again.

Statistical Analysis

SPSS version 22.0 statistical analysis software was used to perform a statistical analysis of the data. Significance of difference (Kruskal–Wallis test), chi-square test was used for comparing UCVA, BCVA in study groups at different follow ups.

RESULTS

There was improvement in mean UCVA at each visit in both categories, and the change at different visits was significant statistically (P = 0.021). At each visit, mean UCVA values were comparable in both the frown incision group and the inverted V incision, showing no significant difference between the two groups (P > 0.05) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

This study has been conducted to compare the outcomes of post-operative SIA between inverted V (chevron) incision and frown incision for MSICS. In our study, UCVA, BCVA, and SIA were compared at different follow-ups.

The mean SIA was lesser in the inverted V group (-0.89 ± 0.62 × 90 degrees) as compared to the frown incision group (-0.97 ± 0.72×89 degrees). These findings are also supported by other studies.

Manisha Rathi et al.[5] carried out a study and found lesser mean SIA in inverted V group (0.34D ± 0.22D) as compared to frown incision (0.575D ± 0.25D). Jauhari N et al. also supported our findings as they found reduced SIA in Chevron incision (-0.88 ± 0.61D) as compared to frown incision (-0.96 ± 0.71D) at four weeks follow-up postoperatively.[6] The findings of our study also found resemble with the findings of study by Jauhari N et al.[7] They found that with minimal astigmatism, Chevron showed the best outcomes; 48% of study participants reported astigmatism ranging 0.5 D to 1D, whereas 36% reported 0.6-1D having frown incision.

In another study, Rathi et al. evaluated 100 eyes under similar experimental conditions like our study.[8] They also reported that the mean SIA in the Chevron (inverted V-shaped incision group) and frown incision group to be 0.55D ± 0.42 D and 0.82D ± 0.42 respectively. It was found that SIA was significantly lesser in inverted V incision group. These findings seem to support the findings of our study, where mean SIA was lesser in the inverted V incision groups. Additionally, Patra R et al. also found that visual acuity was better in the inverted V incision group as compared to frown incision.[9]

CONCLUSION

Successful outcomes were obtained in patients who underwent MSICS using either frown incision or inverted incision. Our study concluded that an inverted V incision causes less medically induced astigmatism than a Frown incision.

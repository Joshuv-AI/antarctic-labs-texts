A Theranostic Study of 177Lu-Labeled Anti-ADAM9 Antibody for the Treatment of Triple-Negative Breast Cancer
Authors: Huayan Zhang, Mingming Tang, Xiaofei Hao, Binwei Lin, Huan Du, Hu Long, Jiangjiang Hu, Rui Luo, Wei Wu, Xiaobo Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is highly aggressive with limited diagnostic and therapeutic options. ADAM9 is overexpressed in TNBC and may serve as a theranostic target. Therefore, this study aims to develop [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, an ADAM9-targeted probe, and evaluate its targeting specificity, imaging capability, efficacy, and safety in TNBC models. ADAM9 expression in TNBC cells was assessed by flow cytometry, and the probe was constructed by conjugating 177Lu to the ADAM9-specific antibody 48HZ810 via DOTA, after which binding specificity, affinity, and internalization were evaluated using radioligand binding, immunoreactive fraction, and internalization assays. In vivo targeting and pharmacokinetics were further analyzed by SPECT/CT imaging and biodistribution studies, while efficacy, safety, and mechanisms were assessed via tumor growth, body weight, survival, hematology, histopathology, and immunofluorescence. High ADAM9 expression was confirmed in BT-549 cells, and [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 showed >99% radiochemical purity and stability, high affinity (K d: 3.75 ± 0.72 nmol/L), and specificity (Bmax: 422.5 ± 17.85 nmol). The tumor was clearly visualized at 72 h, with peak uptake at 96 h (8.56 ± 0.69%ID/g), and the probe significantly inhibited tumor growth (TGI: 46.04% vs antibody: 18.14%) and improved survival (70% vs antibody: 50% vs control: 0%), with no significant toxicity observed. The antitumor activity was mediated by DNA damage, apoptosis induction, and proliferation suppression. Overall, the first ADAM9-targeted theranostic probe, [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, was successfully developed, demonstrating excellent tumor targeting, antitumor efficacy, and safety in TNBC models, and validating ADAM9 as a viable theranostic target.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women worldwide and remains one of the most challenging malignancies. In 2022, it accounted for 11.6% of new cancer cases and 6.9% of cancer-related deaths, with survival depending on tumor stage and molecular subtype. Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), defined by the absence of ER, PR, and HER2 expression, represents 15–20% of all breast cancers. The lack of these receptors limits targeted therapies. TNBC is highly aggressive, with frequent recurrence and poor survival, and is considered the subtype with the worst prognosis. The five-year mortality rate reaches 40%, and due to the absence of effective targeted treatments, TNBC remains the most challenging subtype in breast cancer therapy. Developing novel therapeutic strategies is, therefore, an urgent priority in TNBC management.

Targeted radionuclide therapy (TRT) represents a cutting-edge modality in oncology that enables precise tumor delivery of radionuclides through targeting vectors such as antibodies, peptides, or small molecules, thereby achieving effective lesion eradication while markedly reducing radiation damage to normal tissues and ultimately improving the efficiency and safety of cancer diagnosis and treatment. Among available radionuclides, 177Lu has emerged as an ideal choice for theranostic applications owing to its favorable physical properties, including a 6.7-day half-life, β-particle emission with a mean energy of 133 keV, and a tissue penetration range of 670 μm, enabling the induction of DNA strand breaks and tumor cell death. With the successive FDA approvals of agents such as [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-TATE for neuroendocrine tumors and [177Lu]­Lu-PSMA-617 for prostate cancer, , TRT has transitioned to an established therapeutic paradigm. In this context, TNBC poses a major challenge within the TRT framework due to its lack of effective therapeutic targets, high aggressiveness, and poor prognosis. Therefore, applying this precision strategy to TNBC through novel radiopharmaceuticals is critical for overcoming current therapeutic limitations and improving patient outcomes.

ADAM9 is a multifunctional membrane-anchored glycoprotein that mediates proteolysis and cell adhesion and is closely associated with tumor invasiveness and poor prognosis. It is overexpressed in TNBC cells and patient samples, promoting tumor aggressiveness. ADAM9 is also overexpressed in multiple cancers − and is recognized as an important cancer biomarker and therapeutic target. , Previous studies have shown that microRNA-1298 inhibits breast cancer progression by targeting ADAM9. Theranostic strategies targeting MT1-MMP and TROP-2 have made significant progress, with preclinical studies demonstrating specific imaging and effective targeted therapy in TNBC. , Despite these advances, a systematic preclinical evaluation of an ADAM9-targeted monoclonal antibody, 48HZ810, as a theranostic probe in TNBC remains lacking. Therefore, this study successfully developed a novel ADAM9 theranostic probe, [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, and systematically evaluated its diagnostic and therapeutic potential in TNBC, offering a new strategy for precision treatment.

Materials and Methods

Cell Lines and Cell Culture

Three STR-validated human triple-negative breast cancer cell lines (BT-549, HCC1806, MDA-MB-468) were obtained from Wuhan Procell. BT-549 and HCC1806 were cultured in RPMI-1640, and MDA-MB-468 in DMEM, all with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. − Passages were limited to six months, and cells were harvested at 75–90% confluence for experiments.

Establishment of Animal Models

Female BALB/c nude mice (6–8 weeks old) were obtained from Sichuan Paxiwell and acclimatized in an SPF facility (22 ± 2 °C, 50 ± 5% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle). The xenograft model was established by subcutaneous inoculation of logarithmic-phase BT-549 cells (1 × 107) mixed with Matrigel (1:1, 100 μL) into the right axilla. Tumor volume and health were monitored daily.

Flow Cytometric Analysis of In Vitro Cell Lines

Cells (5 × 104/well) were seeded in 96-well U-bottom plates and centrifuged (1300 rpm, 5 min, 4 °C). After two washes with prechilled FACS buffer (95% PBS, 5% FBS), cells were incubated on ice with a monoclonal antibody (15 μg/mL) for 60 min. After washing, cells were incubated with a fluorescent secondary antibody for 30 min in the dark. Following a final wash, cells were resuspended in 200 μL of buffer. A total of 3000 events were acquired using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer and analyzed with FlowJo v10.8.

Modification of 48HZ810

The 48HZ810 antibody was ultrafiltered (50 kDa, 13,000 rpm, 4 °C, 10 min) to remove impurities, then washed three times with prechilled PBS (pH 7.4). The purified antibody was mixed with DOTA (10:1 molar ratio, 20 nmol/μL in DMSO) and incubated (37 °C, 70 rpm, 1 h). An equimolar reducing agent was added, and the reaction was continued for another 1 h. The final product was collected after three washes with 0.5 M sodium acetate buffer (pH 5.5) via ultrafiltration and stored for later use.

Synthesis of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810

DOTA-48HZ810 and 177LuCl3 (1:2 molar ratio) were reacted at 42 °C for 1 h with brief agitation every 10 min. Radiolabeling yield was determined by thin-layer chromatography. Aliquots (2 μL) were spotted on silica gel fiber strips and developed using 0.5 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 5.5). Radiochemical purity was further analyzed by high-performance liquid chromatography.

In Vitro Stability Evaluation

To assess in vitro stability, 5 μL of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 was mixed with 20 μL of saline, PBS (pH 7.4), or RPMI-1640 (with 10% FBS). Radiolabeling yield was measured by TLC at various time points (4 to 192 h) at room temperature to evaluate stability under different media conditions.

Radioligand Cell Binding and Blocking Assays

Radioligand cell binding assay: 5 × 104 cells were preincubated with 12 concentrations of unlabeled antibody (2-fold serial dilutions, initial 50×) at 200 rpm for 30 min. Then, 12 concentrations of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (2-fold serial dilutions, initial 5 μCi) were added and incubated for 1 h at room temperature for nonspecific binding. For total binding, cells were incubated directly with 12 concentrations of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 under the same conditions. Blocking assay: 12 concentrations of unlabeled antibody (2-fold serial dilutions, initial 50× EC50) were incubated with cells at 200 rpm for 30 min, followed by the addition of 12 concentrations of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (2-fold serial dilutions) for another 1 h. All samples were washed three times (FACS buffer, 4 °C, 2800 rpm, 4 min). Bound and unbound radioactivity was measured using a gamma counter to evaluate the targeting binding characteristics.

Immunoreactive Fraction Determination

For the unblocked group, logarithmic-phase cells (8 × 105 per 200 μL) underwent 12 2-fold serial dilutions. For the 100% blocking control, cells were similarly diluted, then incubated with 12 2-fold serial dilutions of unlabeled antibody (initial 50× EC50) for 30 min, centrifuged (3000 rpm, 5 min), and the supernatant was removed. Then, 200 μL (0.2 μCi) of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 was added to each tube, incubated (100 rpm, 1 h), centrifuged, washed twice with PBS, and cpm was measured. A scatter plot (cells/tube vs total cpm/(measured cpm – 100% blocked cpm)) was generated, linear regression (y = ax + b, R2 ≥ 0.99) was fitted, and the immunoreactive fraction was calculated as 100% × 1/b.

Internalization Assay

Logarithmic-phase cells were seeded (2 × 104/well) in 12-well plates and cultured overnight. [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (0.2 μCi) was added and incubated for 0, 4, 24, or 48 h. At each time point, the supernatant and PBS washes were collected as the extracellular fraction. Surface-bound activity was recovered with 0.1 M glycine–HCl (pH 2.7, 10 min). The internalized fraction was obtained by lysis with 1% SDS (10 min). Radioactivity in each fraction was measured with decay correction. The internalization rate was calculated as [internalized/(surface-bound + internalized)] × 100%.

Single-Photon Emission Computed Tomography and Computed Tomography (SPECT/CT)

Tumor-bearing mice (n = 3) were intravenously injected with 11.1 MBq of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (100 μL). SPECT/CT scans were performed at 4, 24, 72, 96, 144, 168, and 192 h postinjection under 2.5% isoflurane anesthesia. Quantitative analysis used AMIDE software. Standardized uptake values were calculated based on attenuation-corrected activity and body weight. %ID/g for organs was determined using spherical ROIs (2–3 mm in diameter). Time-activity curves were generated to characterize dynamic tracer biodistribution.

Biodistribution

Tumor-bearing mice (n = 3) were intravenously injected with 0.74 MBq of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (100 μL). At 4, 24, 72, 96, 144, 168, and 192 h postinjection, the mice were euthanized. Tissues (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, pancreas, stomach, intestine, muscle, bone, tumor, and blood) were collected, wet-weighed, and measured for radioactivity using a gamma counter with decay correction (177Lu half-life: 6.65 d). Biodistribution was quantitatively analyzed as %ID/g.

Dosimetry Estimation

Based on the biodistribution data, mean absorbed doses were estimated using the OLINDA/EXM software with the ICRP 89 adult male reference phantom. Time-integrated activities (MBq·h/MBq) were obtained by integrating the time–activity curves, from which the mean absorbed doses to organs (mGy/MBq) were calculated. Effective doses (mSv/MBq) were calculated based on ICRP 103 tissue weighting factors. Animal data were extrapolated to humans using organ mass scaling. In addition, tumor doses were estimated using the OLINDA/EXM spherical model (1 g, 5 g, 10 g), and these results are model-based approximations.

Radiopharmaceutical Therapy

BT-549 tumor-bearing mice (tumor volume 50–150 mm3) were randomly assigned to radionuclide therapy, antibody therapy, and control groups (n = 15 per group). Mean initial tumor volumes were 75.65 ± 20.02 mm3, 71.5 ± 19.5 mm3, and 73.79 ± 17.16 mm3, respectively. Mice received tail vein injections of 11.1 MBq [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (100 μL), 150 μg 48HZ810 antibody (100 μL), or an equal volume of normal saline. Body weight and tumor dimensions (W2 × L × 0.5) were monitored every 3 days. After 30 days, five mice per group were euthanized and blood was collected for a complete blood count using a MEK722 analyzer. Tumors and major organs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for H&E and immunofluorescence analysis. The remaining mice were monitored for survival and euthanized upon reaching humane endpoints (moribund, >20% weight loss, or tumor volume ≥1500 mm3).

48HZ810 Antibody Dose-Escalation Study

Because 48HZ810 antibody monotherapy at 7.5 mg/kg exhibited only limited therapeutic efficacy in the antitumor experiments of this study, it remained difficult to determine whether this outcome resulted from insufficient antibody dosing or reflected an intrinsic limitation of antibody monotherapy in this model. To further investigate this issue, a supplementary antibody dose-escalation experiment was conducted. Specifically, tumor-bearing mice were treated with 48HZ810 antibody monotherapy at 10 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg, and antitumor efficacy, survival benefit, and safety were systematically evaluated. BT-549 tumor-bearing mouse models with tumor volumes of approximately 40–70 mm3 were randomly assigned to a high-dose group, a low-dose group, and a control group, with five mice per group, and the mean initial tumor volumes were 57.22 ± 7.2 mm3, 54.54 ± 6.17 mm3, and 53.37 ± 9.78 mm3, respectively. Mice in the high-dose group received a tail vein injection of 48HZ810 at 30 mg/kg in a volume of 100 μL, mice in the low-dose group received a tail vein injection of 48HZ810 at 10 mg/kg in a volume of 100 μL, and mice in the control group received an equal volume of normal saline. Body weight was monitored every 3 days using a calibrated electronic balance, and tumor length and width were measured with calipers to calculate tumor volume using the formula W2 × L × 0.5. Survival was simultaneously monitored and survival curves were generated, and mice were euthanized and the experiment was terminated upon reaching humane endpoints defined as a moribund state, body weight loss exceeding 20%, or tumor volume reaching 1500 mm3.

Statistical Analysis

All experimental data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10.4.1 software. Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For comparisons between two groups, a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test was used. For comparisons among three or more groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was performed. For tumor growth and body weight data measured repeatedly over time, two-way repeated measures ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan–Meier method with the log-rank test, and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated using the Mantel–Haenszel method. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Safety Statement

All experiments involving radionuclides were conducted in strict accordance with the institutional radiation safety regulations. Appropriate shielding, contamination monitoring, and radioactive waste disposal procedures were implemented. No unexpected or unusually high safety hazards occurred during the study.

Results

ADAM9 Expression in Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

Flow cytometric analysis demonstrated significant differences in ADAM9 expression among three triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, with low expression in MDA-MB-468 cells, intermediate expression in HCC1806 cells, and markedly high expression in BT-549 cells, and flow cytometry further confirmed that the binding capacity of the antibody to target cells remained unchanged after the conjugation of the 48HZ810 antibody with DOTA (Figure A).

(B) Radioligand cell binding and blocking analysis of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 in three triple-negative breast cancer cell lines (BT-549, HCC1806, and MDA-MB-468). (C) Determination of the immunoreactive fraction of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 in ADAM9 high-expressing cells (BT-549). Synthesis and Quality Control of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810

We successfully prepared the radiolabeled conjugate of 48HZ810 by coupling 177Lu via the DOTA chelator to generate the ADAM9-targeted theranostic probe [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810. The DOTA-to-48HZ810 ratio was approximately 7.52 (Figure S1). HPLC analysis confirmed that the radiochemical purity (RCP) of 48HZ810, DOTA-48HZ810, and [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 exceeded 99% (Figures S2–S4), and TLC analysis demonstrated a labeling yield greater than 99% for [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 (Figure S5). Stability studies showed that after incubation for 8 days at room temperature in normal saline (NS), phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and RPMI 1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 maintained a labeling yield above 99% in all three media (Figures S6–S17).

Radioligand Cell Binding and Blocking Assays

Radioligand cell binding assays in three triple-negative breast cancer cell lines demonstrated that the cellular uptake of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 was markedly dose-dependent, with a maximal binding capacity (Bmax) of 422.5 ± 17.85 nmol and an equilibrium dissociation constant (K d) of 3.75 ± 0.72 nmol/L for BT-549 cells (radioligand binding: 0.83% vs blocking: 0.12%), a Bmax of 222.0 ± 9.25 nmol and a K d of 5.99 ± 1.0 nmol/L for HCC1806 cells (radioligand binding: 0.83% vs blocking: 0.24%), and a Bmax of 120.0 ± 4.0 nmol and a K d of 2.49 ± 0.40 nmol/L for MDA-MB-468 cells (radioligand binding: 0.89% vs blocking: 0.12%) (Figure B).

Cell Immunoreactivity and Internalization Assays

Cell immunoreactivity fraction analysis showed that the immunoreactive fraction of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 in BT-549 cells was 79.24% (Figure C). Internalization assays in BT-549 cells demonstrated a progressive, time-dependent intracellular uptake of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, with rates of 0.20 ± 0.16%, 0.94 ± 0.21%, 4.94 ± 0.68%, and 15.22 ± 1.30% at 0, 4, 24, and 48 h, respectively, showing statistically significant differences among groups (p < 0.001, Figure D).

In Vivo SPECT/CT Imaging of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810

Mouse SPECT/CT imaging demonstrated that at 24 h postinjection, [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 began to accumulate in tumors. At 72 h postinjection, tumors were clearly visualized. During 4–192 h postinjection, tumor uptake of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 progressively increased and remained high after 72 h, whereas radioactivity uptake in nontarget organs decreased over time, indicating significantly longer tumor retention than in nontarget organs (Figure A). Further region-of-interest delineation enabled quantitative analysis of radioactivity uptake in tumors, heart, liver, lung, muscle, bone, and intestine. The maximum tumor uptake occurred at 96 h, with a mean %ID/g of 8.56 ± 0.69, after which tumor uptake gradually declined while remaining at 6.47 ± 0.59 at 192 h. Radioactivity uptake in nontarget organs such as the heart, liver, and lung progressively decreased over time, with mean %ID/g values at 192 h of 2.09 ± 0.79 for the heart, 3.01 ± 0.14 for the liver, and 1.04 ± 0.26 for the lung. During 4–192 h postinjection, radioactivity uptake in muscle, bone, and intestine remained very low, showing a slight initial increase followed by a time-dependent decrease. The mean %ID/g ranges were 0.4567 ± 0.0611 to 1.307 ± 0.82 for muscle, 0.44 ± 0.18 to 1.023 ± 0.75 for bone, and 0.52 ± 0.21 to 1.44 ± 0.24 for intestine (Figure B).

In Vivo Biodistribution of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810

Biodistribution studies demonstrated that [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 exhibited pronounced tumor-targeting properties in vivo. Tumor uptake of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 increased in a time-dependent manner, reaching a peak at 96 h (12.35 ± 0.95%ID/g), followed by a gradual decline, while remaining at 7.71 ± 1.63%ID/g at 192 h. Blood clearance kinetics showed that peak blood uptake occurred at 4 h with a value of 6.49 ± 0.40%ID/g and progressively decreased over time, declining to 2.19 ± 0.36%ID/g at 96 h when tumor uptake peaked, and further clearing from circulation by 192 h with a blood uptake of 0.42 ± 0.13%ID/g. The liver and kidney both exhibited peak uptake at 4 h, with values of 1.84 ± 0.28%ID/g and 1.37 ± 0.14%ID/g, respectively, which were higher than those of other nontarget tissues but gradually decreased over time to 0.61 ± 0.15%ID/g and 0.41 ± 0.12%ID/g at 192 h (Figure C). From 4 to 192 h after injection of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, the tumor-to-blood uptake ratios ranged from 0.22 ± 0.05 to 19.45 ± 6.98, the tumor-to-liver uptake ratios ranged from 0.79 ± 0.20 to 13.53 ± 5.30, and the tumor-to-kidney uptake ratios ranged from 0.44 ± 1.05 to 19.28 ± 4.99 (Figure D).

Therapeutic Efficacy of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810

At day 30, the normalized tumor volume in the radionuclide therapy group was 221.2 ± 112.4 mm3, which was significantly lower than that in the other two groups (control group: 415.7 ± 158.7 mm3; antibody therapy group: 334.6 ± 154.8 mm3). The tumor growth inhibition rate (TGI%) was 46.04% in the radionuclide therapy group and 18.14% in the antibody therapy group. Statistical analysis of tumor volume revealed significant tumor growth inhibition in the radionuclide therapy group (p < 0.0001), whereas no statistically significant inhibition was observed in the antibody therapy group (p > 0.05). The difference in tumor volume progression between the radionuclide therapy group and the antibody therapy group was statistically significant (p < 0.01) (Figure A). Survival analysis revealed that the median survival time was 46 days in the control group and 68 days in the antibody therapy group, whereas survival was significantly prolonged in the radionuclide therapy group and the median survival was not reached. At the end of the 30-day treatment period, the survival rates of the radionuclide therapy group and the antibody therapy group were both 100%, whereas that of the control group was 80%. When half of the mice in the control group had died (day 46), the survival rates were 100% in the radionuclide therapy group and 90% in the antibody therapy group. At the end of the 70-day observation period, the survival rate remained 70% in the radionuclide therapy group, declined to 50% in the antibody therapy group, and reached 0% in the control group. Further statistical analysis revealed a significant difference in survival curves between the control group and the radionuclide therapy group (p = 0.0008). Compared with the control group, the hazard ratio (HR) for death in the radionuclide therapy group was 0.10, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) of 0.03–0.33. Body weight in all groups of tumor-bearing mice showed a gradual increase, and weight gain in the radionuclide therapy group was lower than that in the control group, with a statistically significant difference between groups (p < 0.05) (Figure B).

Dose-Escalation Study of the 48HZ810 Antibody

The normalized tumor volume at day 30 was 440.5 ± 97.13 mm3 in the high-dose group, 423.5 ± 165.9 mm3 in the low-dose group, and 568.2 ± 54.8 mm3 in the control group, with no significant difference between the high-dose and low-dose groups at the end of treatment, whereas tumor volumes in both treatment groups were lower than those in the control group, with statistically significant intergroup differences (p < 0.05). The tumor growth inhibition rate (TGI%) was 33.22% in the high-dose group and 26.92% in the low-dose group, indicating only mild tumor suppression in both groups. Survival analysis showed that the median survival time was 40 days in the control group and 45 days in the low-dose group, while no deaths occurred in the high-dose group. At the end of the 30-day treatment period, survival rates were 100% in both the high-dose and low-dose groups compared with 60% in the control group. At the end of the 50-day observation period, all mice in the control group had died, the survival rate in the low-dose group declined to 40%, whereas the survival rate in the high-dose group remained 100%. Further statistical analysis revealed that the high-dose group exhibited significantly prolonged survival compared with the control group (p = 0.0039, HR = 0.05, 95% CI: 0.0075–0.33), whereas the low-dose group showed no significant survival benefit (p = 0.2489, HR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.084–1.67). Body weight in tumor-bearing mice from all groups showed a gradual increase, with significantly greater weight gain in the high-dose group than in the other two groups, and the intergroup differences were statistically significant (p < 0.01) (Figure D).

Discussion

This study demonstrates for the first time the diagnostic and therapeutic value of ADAM9 as a specific molecular target in TNBCs. ADAM9 exhibits a distinct high-expression pattern in TNBC. The probe [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 enables precise detection of primary or metastatic lesions, shows significant tumor inhibition, and has a favorable safety profile. This work represents a major breakthrough in TNBC theranostics and provides a highly specific, novel strategy for TNBC patients.

Flow cytometry confirmed the specific high expression of ADAM9 in BT-549 cells, consistent with previous studies. Radioligand binding assays demonstrated the high affinity and specific binding of ADAM9 to BT-549 cells. A TNBC mouse model with high ADAM9 expression was established, and [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 was successfully synthesized. SPECT/CT enabled clear visualization of primary and metastatic lesions at 72 h postinjection with time-dependent accumulation, providing a noninvasive diagnostic tool with clinical translational potential. At 192 h, slightly higher nonspecific accumulation was observed in the liver, indicating a need for further pharmacokinetic optimization.

In the targeted radionuclide therapy experiment, a clear difference in tumor volume progression was observed between the radionuclide therapy group ([177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810) and the antibody therapy group (48HZ810). The radionuclide therapy group exhibited significant tumor growth inhibition due to precise ADAM9 targeting and sustained radiation damage from 177Lu β-particles. In contrast, the 48HZ810 antibody alone did not show statistically significant tumor suppression. However, published literature indicates that anti-ADAM9 antibodies can exert antitumor effects by suppressing downstream signaling pathways. We hypothesize that the lack of significant efficacy with the 48HZ810 antibody likely resulted from an insufficient dosage. The supplementary dose-escalation study of the 48HZ810 antibody demonstrated that 48HZ810 monotherapy at doses of 10 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg exerted only limited inhibitory effects on tumor growth in TNBC mouse models, primarily manifesting as delayed tumor progression, without significant tumor regression or a clear survival benefit. Previous studies have shown that antibodies targeting ADAM9 mainly modulate tumor progression-related phenotypes such as cell migration and invasion by suppressing phosphorylation of downstream EGFR and ERK signaling pathways rather than directly inducing tumor cell death, , a characteristic that may partially explain the limited antitumor activity observed in this supplementary study even at higher antibody monotherapy doses. Collectively, these findings further suggest that the therapeutic efficacy of 48HZ810 monotherapy in this TNBC mouse model is constrained not only by dose but also by the intrinsic biological properties of antibody monotherapy, thereby underscoring the necessity of enhancing local cytotoxic effects through radionuclide conjugation while preserving target specificity. This study systematically demonstrates for the first time the effectiveness of ADAM9 as a radionuclide therapy target in TNBC, supporting the therapeutic potential of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810.

We systematically evaluated the in vivo safety of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810. Pharmacokinetic analysis showed rapid clearance from circulation, with a 93.42% clearance rate between 4 and 192 h, reducing hematological toxicity and bone marrow suppression risk. Complete blood count and histopathological analysis confirmed no hematological toxicity, and major organs showed no radiation-induced injury. Circulating [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 exerts minimal effects on leukocytes and low cytotoxicity to blood cells and exhibits favorable in vivo safety.

This study performed the first systematic internal dosimetry assessment of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810, providing key data for evaluating its radiation safety and therapeutic window. It should be noted that tumor doses were estimated using a spherical model and represent model-based approximations and not actual patient tumor doses. Furthermore, the extrapolation of animal data to humans involves inherent variability. According to the RADAR guidelines, animal-extrapolated data tend to underestimate human organ self-doses. Therefore, human dosimetry data require further validation in clinical studies.

Further mechanistic studies showed that [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 upregulated γH2AX and 53BP1, indicating increased DNA double-strand breaks and DNA damage response activation. Downregulation of BRCA1/2 suggested suppressed homologous recombination repair, exacerbating genomic instability. Caspase-3 was robustly activated and Ki67 was markedly reduced, indicating apoptosis induction and proliferation suppression. These findings demonstrate that [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 exerts antitumor efficacy against TNBC by inducing DNA damage, disrupting repair homeostasis, promoting apoptosis, and suppressing proliferation.

The clinical significance of this study extends beyond that of TNBC. Given the ADAM9 overexpression in multiple malignancies, the [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 theranostic strategy has potential for expansion to other ADAM9-overexpressing tumors. Although mild nonspecific liver accumulation was observed, antibody optimization could improve the pharmacokinetic profile. These findings validate ADAM9-targeted therapy and provide an innovative option for patients with advanced metastatic solid tumors including TNBC.

The limitations of this study lie in the fact that the imaging and therapeutic evaluations were primarily conducted in a single-cell-line-derived xenograft (CDX) model with high ADAM9 expression, and systematic validation across models with varying ADAM9 expression levels is lacking. In addition, differences between CDX models and those of human triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) may limit their clinical translatability. Therefore, future validation in multiple CDX models with heterogeneous ADAM9 expression as well as in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models is warranted to enhance the comprehensiveness and generalizability of the conclusions. On the other hand, mild nonspecific liver accumulation of [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 may reduce its diagnostic and therapeutic performance and cause a certain degree of radiation damage to normal tissues, and future optimization of the antibody structure may improve the targeting specificity and address this issue.

Conclusion

This study systematically validates the potential of ADAM9 as a theranostic target for TNBC, and the successfully developed ADAM9-targeted theranostic probe [177Lu]­Lu-DOTA-48HZ810 not only enables noninvasive tumor visualization by SPECT/CT imaging but also exhibits pronounced tumor growth inhibition through the synergistic effects of antibody-mediated targeting and radionuclide therapy, while pharmacokinetic analysis confirms its high blood clearance rate and favorable biosafety without inducing hematological toxicity or damage to major organs. Despite limitations such as nonspecific hepatic accumulation, further optimization through antibody engineering strategies could enhance its targeting performance, and these findings not only provide a novel approach for TNBC theranostics but also lay the foundation for the development of precision diagnostic and therapeutic platforms for other ADAM9-overexpressing tumors.

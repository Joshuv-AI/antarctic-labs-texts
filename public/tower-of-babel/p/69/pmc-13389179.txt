Safety evaluation of bleomycin in ovarian cancer treatment: A pharmacovigilance analysis using Food and Drug Administration adverse event reporting system data
Authors: Xuan Zhou, Xiaohua Wang, Luyang Su, Yuntao Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimBleomycin is a cytotoxic antibiotic mainly used for the treatment of carcinoma of the ovary, skin, cervix, vagina, vulva, and penis, as well as malignant lymphoma and testicular cancer. Assessing the real-world safety of bleomycin, particularly in the treatment of ovarian cancer, is essential because of its widespread clinical application. Further investigation of its risk profile in patients with ovarian cancer remains critically important.MethodsThis retrospective, observational pharmacovigilance study was based on data from the Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System database. Data were collected from the Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System database from the first quarter of 2004 to the third quarter of 2024. The reporting odds ratio, proportional reporting ratio, multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker, and Bayesian confidence propagation neural network were used for disproportionality analyses of adverse events related to bleomycin. Background

Bleomycin, a glycopeptide antitumor antibiotic, has been a crucial drug in the treatment of various malignancies, including ovarian, cervical, vaginal, vulvar, and testicular cancers, since its discovery. 1 Despite the efficacy of bleomycin in the treatment of ovarian cancer, its adverse effects should not be overlooked. It exerts its antitumor effects by inducing DNA strand breaks, thereby inhibiting tumor cell proliferation. 2 However, the clinical use of bleomycin is associated with a range of potential adverse reactions, among which pulmonary toxicity is the most prominent, potentially leading to interstitial pneumonia and pulmonary fibrosis, and in severe cases, even life-threatening outcomes. 3 Additionally, skin reactions, fever, and allergic reactions are also commonly reported adverse events. 4 To circumvent the severe systemic toxicities and narrow therapeutic indices associated with conventional nontargeted chemotherapeutic agents, contemporary pharmaceutical research has actively pursued advanced drug delivery systems, such as functionalized polymeric or targeted nanocarriers, to enhance tumor selectivity and attenuate off-target adverse effects. For instance, folate-targeted nanocarriers and intelligent polymer formulations have shown extraordinary promise in improving the specific cytotoxicity and safety profiles of therapeutic agents such as paclitaxel and resveratrol.5,6 Nevertheless, although these next-generation drug delivery systems are undergoing rigorous development, conventional bleomycin remains an indispensable cytotoxic cornerstone of current clinical regimens for ovarian cancer. Consequently, a systematic, real-world pharmacovigilance analysis of bleomycin-induced adverse effects remains of paramount clinical importance, not only to optimize current care for patients with ovarian cancer but also to provide essential clinical toxicity baselines for designing safer, targeted bleomycin delivery formulations in the future.

With the advancement of pharmacovigilance, real-world data-based drug safety studies have gradually become an essential tool for drug risk management. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), one of the largest drug safety monitoring databases, provides valuable data resources for the analysis and detection of adverse drug reactions. 7 By mining FAERS data, researchers can identify potential risk signals, assess the frequency and severity of adverse reactions, and provide scientific evidence to support clinical drug use. 8 Nevertheless, current pharmacovigilance research on bleomycin remains limited, particularly with respect to systematic analyses based on large-scale clinical practice data.

The objective of this research was to perform a thorough pharmacovigilance evaluation of side effects linked to bleomycin, using the FAERS database, 9 evaluate its safety profile, identify potential risk signals, and explore their correlations with patient demographic characteristics, dosage, and other factors. Through this research, we hope to provide scientific evidence for optimizing the clinical application strategies of bleomycin and reducing the incidence of adverse reactions in patients with ovarian cancer, while also offering references for further improvements in pharmacovigilance research methodologies.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki, as revised in 2024.

Data source and collection

The FAERS is a publicly available database for postmarketing safety monitoring that compiles reports of adverse events (AEs) submitted by pharmaceutical companies, healthcare professionals, patients, and other stakeholders. Based on the FAERS database, we carefully organized and standardized bleomycin-related data, taking meticulous steps to eliminate duplicate reports and records. Focusing on the period from the first quarter of 2004 to the third quarter of 2024, we compiled an extensive collection of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) associated with bleomycin. We also meticulously extracted the preferred terms (PTs) across all system organ classes (SOCs). For the purpose of this study, bleomycin in the FAERS database was identified as the primary drug of interest, enabling a thorough examination of its association with AEs. This methodology enables a focused evaluation of bleomycin-associated ADRs, establishing a solid foundation for pharmacovigilance and valuable insights into the safety profile of this drug. Figure 1 illustrates the study workflow.

Data filtering flowchart.

All data used in this study were obtained from the FAERS database, which contains only de-identified patient information. Furthermore, no personal identifiers were accessed, processed, or reported in our analysis. The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 10

Data processing

To match the timeline of drug approvals, we extracted FAERS reports from 2004 Q1 to 2024 Q3 and performed the data analysis using R software. The demographic and administrative information (DEMO) table summarized the clinical details of patients with bleomycin-related AEs, 11 covering including age, sex, reporting region, reporter role, and outcomes. 12 Severe outcomes were categorized as hospitalization, disability, life-threatening events, or death. To ensure data reliability, we performed initial preprocessing steps, including deduplication and population definition, resulting in 731 adverse event reports in which bleomycin was identified as the primary suspect drug (Figure 1).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis was used to summarize the characteristics of bleomycin-related adverse event reports. Four disproportionality analysis methods—reporting odds ratio (ROR), 13 proportional reporting ratio (PRR), 14 multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker (MGPS), 15 and Bayesian confidence propagation neural network (BCPNN), 16 were employed to identify potential adverse reaction signals. An event that met the signal threshold in at least one method was considered a potential adverse reaction. The Weibull distribution was used to analyze temporal trends in adverse event occurrence. All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.2).

Results

Descriptive analysis

The analysis included 731 documented cases in which bleomycin was identified as the primary suspected drug in adverse event occurrences (1,887 total AEs). Of these, 37.48% involved female patients, whereas 54.45% involved male patients. Patients aged 18–64 years represented the majority, comprising 56.91% of the study population. Physician-reported cases constituted 27.63% of the total submissions, with nearly half (47.88%) originating from the United States. Canada and country not specified contributed 8.21% and 7.52% of reports, respectively. Additional demographic characteristics and distribution patterns are detailed in Table 1.

Classification of adverse reactions by PT categories

Bleomycin-related adverse reactions were systematically categorized based on occurrence rates and analyzed for significant safety signals. Febrile neutropenia (ROR = 38.04, 95% confidence interval (CI): 30.7–47.15), pulmonary toxicity (ROR = 300.97, 95% CI: 236.48–383.04), and respiratory failure (ROR = 22.05, 95% CI: 16.99–28.63) were among the most commonly observed adverse effects. Additionally, PTs reported with a frequency exceeding 35 occurrences involved subsequent pyrexia (ROR = 4.46, 95% CI: 3.42–5.82) and neutropenia (ROR = 7.41, 95% CI: 5.33–10.3). Moreover, unlisted potential ADRs, such as disseminated intravascular coagulation (ROR = 11.26, 95% CI: 5.05–25.09), acute myeloid leukemia (ROR = 10.71, 95% CI: 4.8–23.87), growing teratoma syndrome (ROR = 7,667.9, 95% CI: 3,059.45–19,218.09), shock (ROR = 6.14, 95% CI: 2.55–14.77), hyperthyroidism (ROR = 5.82, 95% CI: 1.87–18.06), and sarcoidosis (ROR = 17.36, 95% CI: 5.59–53.89), were also identified. Discussion

This study performed an in-depth pharmacovigilance evaluation of bleomycin-related AEs using the FAERS database. Our findings reveal significant characteristics of bleomycin-associated AEs, providing valuable insights for clinical practice in ovarian cancer.

First, our analysis confirmed known severe AEs associated with bleomycin, including pulmonary toxicity (ROR = 300.97, 95% CI: 236.48–383.04), hypersensitivity reactions, and Raynaud's phenomenon (ROR = 44.59, 95% CI: 22.26–89.33). Pulmonary toxicity emerged as the predominant severe adverse effect, aligning with findings from prior investigations. 17 A recently published randomized phase II trial (NRG Oncology study) comparing paclitaxel/carboplatin with bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP) in ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors reported that 61% of patients in the BEP arm experienced grade 4 myelotoxicity, with two deaths attributed to bleomycin-related pulmonary toxicity. 18 These prospective clinical data reinforce our pharmacovigilance findings and highlight the need for vigilant monitoring of patients with ovarian cancer receiving bleomycin, particularly those with advanced disease or receiving dose-intensive regimens. We also observed an increasing reporting rate of pulmonary toxicity over time, likely reflecting heightened clinician awareness of bleomycin-induced pulmonary toxicity and improvements in the FAERS reporting system. 19 Similarly, we detected a shock signal (ROR = 6.14, 95% CI: 2.55–14.77), which closely aligns with the reported incidence rates of approximately 1% in oncology patients treated with bleomycin 20 and is prominently featured in the prescribing information as a severe, life-threatening acute idiosyncratic reaction. Notably, an exceptionally high signal strength was uncovered for systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) (ROR = 21.17, 95% CI: 6.82–65.72), which clinically correlates with acute severe febrile reactions and hyperpyrexia reported in up to 20%–25% of patients with lymphoma and germ cell tumors during initial high-dose bleomycin infusions, representing a well-documented acute infusion storm driven by functional cytokine release. 21

Second, our study identified potential new safety signals, including acute kidney injury, acute myeloid leukemia and thromboembolic events. Among these, we captured a highly significant signal for leukemia/acute myeloid leukemia (AML) (ROR = 10.71, 95% CI: 4.8–23.87). Although this result may not be directly attributable to bleomycin monotherapy, the finding strongly aligns with the reported cumulative incidence of 1%–5% for secondary therapy-related AML in survivors receiving multiagent combination regimens, such as the BEP regimen.22,23 Although the causal relationship between these AEs and bleomycin remains unconfirmed, these findings warrant further investigation to assess their potential risks and identify possible risk factors.24,25

Additionally, our analysis highlighted specific populations at increased risks of bleomycin-related AEs. For example, older patients and patients with renal impairment were at higher risk of developing pulmonary toxicity. 26 These findings emphasize the need for careful monitoring and dose adjustment when bleomycin used in these populations.

However, this study has several limitations. First, FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system, and the disproportionality analysis used in this study is a signal detection tool rather than a method for causal inference. 27 As per FDA guidance, reports submitted to FAERS do not establish causal relationships between drugs and AEs. Future prospective studies or systematic case reviews incorporating these methods are warranted to evaluate the causal plausibility of the associations observed between bleomycin and severe AEs, including AML and sarcoidosis. Second, the lack of detailed baseline patient characteristics, concomitant medications, and disease severity limited our ability to perform more in-depth analyses.28,29

Despite these limitations, our findings remain clinically important. They provide new insights into the safety profile of bleomycin and underscore the need for continuous monitoring and evaluation of its risk-benefit ratio. Future research should focus on validating the potential safety signals identified in this study and exploring strategies to mitigate bleomycin-associated AEs.

In conclusion, this study used FAERS data to conduct a comprehensive pharmacovigilance analysis of safety profile of bleomycin. Our results confirmed known AEs and identified potential new safety signals, providing valuable insights for the treatment of patients with ovarian cancer. These findings highlight the importance of ongoing monitoring and evaluation of the risk-benefit profile of bleomycin to ensure patient safety.

Conclusion

In this study, we performed a comprehensive evaluation of bleomycin-related AEs using the FAERS database. The investigation not only confirmed previously recognized adverse effects but also identified potential adverse reactions not documented in the product labeling. These findings provide important safety insights for healthcare practitioners prescribing bleomycin and serve as a reference for the clinical safety of bleomycin use in patients with ovarian cancer. Larger-scale clinical studies and real-world investigations are needed to validate these findings and further improve the safety of bleomycin therapy.

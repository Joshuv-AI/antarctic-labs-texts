Introduction

Antimicrobial resistance was declared a global health crisis by the WHO in 2015.1 2 A key strategy for reducing antimicrobial resistance is examining penicillin allergy labels. This process is called penicillin allergy evaluation (PAE) and subsequent removal of incorrect allergy labels is referred to as de-labelling.3 4 Between 3%–10% of patients admitted to Norwegian hospitals report a penicillin allergy; however, up to 90% are disproven on examination.5 Inaccurate penicillin allergy labels are associated with increased antibiotic resistance, higher treatment costs, longer hospital stays and greater mortality.6 7 Traditionally, PAE has required a comprehensive examination including skin testing, measurements of specific IgE in serum and a clinical evaluation by an allergologist before drug provocation testing and potential de-labelling. In Norway, the limited number of allergologists and hospitals offering PAE severely restricts access to PAE.

Recently, risk stratification-based approaches for PAE have been developed for use in everyday clinical practice by non-allergists.8,10 Here, physicians use validated tools for stratifying patients into high-risk and low-risk of true penicillin allergy. High-risk patients undergo allergological assessment, while low-risk patients receive a direct oral drug provocation test (oral intake of a therapeutic dose penicillin) in their admitting ward. Patients tolerating the challenge are de-labelled. Risk stratification-based PAE has been extensively validated and is recommended in several national guidelines.11,14 Despite this, the uptake of the procedure outside clinical studies has been low.15,17 In our recent Norwegian study, clinicians expressed concern regarding psychological safety when performing PAE and emphasised that clearer guidelines and organisational support are necessary to facilitate PAE as part of their everyday practice.18 Nevertheless, clinicians demonstrated strong motivation to learn and participate in PAE, as they recognise its value in fighting antimicrobial resistance.18 Comparable findings have been reported internationally, with patients who underwent PAE describing positive experiences. Furthermore, patients who declined testing often stated they would have consented had they been better informed about the procedure and its importance.19,22 In this foregoing work, several implementation needs were detected and have informed this study protocol. To address the public health challenge of antimicrobial resistance and the lack of access to PAE in Norway, we created a multicentre study protocol to evaluate implementation of PAE in Western Norway hospitals (WNHs).

This study is guided by the Consolidated Framework for Implementation (CFIR) and the Expert Recommendations for Implementing Change strategies (ERIC).23 24 The planned outcomes are acceptability, readiness to change, fidelity, adoption, penetration, feasibility and sustainability as recommended by Proctor et al.25

Methods and analysis

Aim

To examine the implementation of the first risk stratification-based programme for evaluation of penicillin allergy labels in WNHs.

Study setting

In Norwegian hospitals, antibiotic stewardship is compulsory and penicillins are frequently the first choice of antibiotics.26 The present study examines the implementation of PAE in five WNH region hospitals. The included hospitals are Haukeland University Hospital, Haraldsplass Deaconess Hospital, Stavanger University Hospital, Haugesund Hospital and Voss Hospital. The WNH region has a total catchment area of 114 million inhabitants, equalling a fifth of the Norwegian population.27 The study includes 11 departments across university, regional and local hospitals. The hospitals are located both in urban and rural areas. Norwegian hospitals are mainly state governed and funded.

All patients above 18 years of age with a registered or self-declared penicillin allergy admitted to the participating wards are eligible for assessment. Patients with cognitive impairment are excluded, as their response to the risk stratification questions might be invalid.

Patient and public involvement

Patient representatives have been involved in the study protocol construction. The patient representatives were recruited from the established pool of patient representatives in the Western Norway Health region. The patient representatives were also members of the Norwegian patient organisation for asthma and allergy. The patient representatives were invited to all meetings concerning the development of the study, and online meetings were used to facilitate participation. Patient representatives revised and co-created the study protocol. The patient representatives and their input provided changes throughout, especially in the parts of the study considering patient information.

Design

This is a prospective, multicentre quasi-experimental study combining a stepped wedge and an interrupted time series design to evaluate the implementation of a risk stratification-based PAE programme in WNH. The study builds on previous work by our research group, where a Norwegian PAE programme was developed and tested in a single-centre pilot study, and on qualitative research identifying barriers and facilitators for implementation in WNH.18 28 The combined design enables assessment of both temporal changes in PAE practice within each hospital and contextual adaptations during implementation, such as education, training, guideline modifications and local leadership engagement. This approach allows identification of implementation strategies likely to support sustainable PAE uptake.

Implementation proceeds in three phases across the centres at predefined timepoints. The number of PAE and the number of clinicians performing them are recorded at baseline, after 3, 6 and 12 months of implementation, and at 6 months post-implementation. Their implementation plan includes the following strategies: education and training within participating units, active leadership involvement, implementation of new guidelines and de-implementation of outdated guidelines and methods. The timing of the roll-out for the departments was set after discussing the feasibility of the timeline with the units and a set roll-out timeline was determined. Description of strategies, processes and outcomes

We have constructed a multimodal implementation study for the implementation of PAE in WNH, requiring coordinated preparation in the hospital departments (inner setting) and on an organisational level (outer setting). All centres receive a standardised educational package, including lectures on the rationale for PAE, practical training in PAE and anaphylaxis management. Further lectures and training are optional and logged for each centre. Previous guidelines on national, regional and local level are to be de-implemented and replaced by harmonised new guidelines that describe the standardised PAE process. The leadership of the WNH hospitals has approved the study and the methods applied. Standard text phrases for PAE are being integrated into the electronic journal system used in WNH. Each phrase corresponds to a specific step in the PAE pathway, tested previously in the pilot study.28 The phrases can also be printed as plain text on paper if needed.

Each site has appointed a local study leader to supervise the implementation process. These leaders meet on a regular basis online or in person. The leaders all have a special interest in PAE, are experienced clinicians and have broad contextual knowledge of their departments. These leaders will also receive emails concerning the study on a regular basis and are encouraged to have a low threshold for contacting the main investigator of the study (MBA) with any questions or needs concerning the study.

We applied the CFIR and ERIC frameworks to create the study. How the ERIC strategies correlate to our implementation process can be found in onlinesupplemental files 1 2.

We chose outcomes in line with recommendations from Proctor et al which are widely used for evaluating implementation research in healthcare.23,2529 30 The primary outcome is adoption, defined as the proportion of patients undergoing a PAE label examination among all hospitalised patients with a penicillin allergy label submitted to the study centres during the study period.

To examine acceptability and readiness to change, we have chosen to compare the pre-implementation assessment from the qualitative interviews with the Implementation Process Assessment Tool (IPAT).18 31 We also plan to conduct new focus group interviews with nurses and physicians in the included department post-implementation. The semi-structured focus group interviews will contain questions concerning all outcomes, providing triangulation and a contextual understanding of the quantitative results. The implementation process will also apply the Knowledge to Action framework to guide and evaluate the implementation process itself.32

Analysis plan

For outcome measures, descriptive statistics will be performed. The number of patients submitted to the included wards declaring a penicillin allergy label or with penicillin allergy noted on their electronic health charts will be compared with the number of patients with a penicillin allergy label included in the study. This allows us to examine the chosen outcome measurements for PAE in everyday practice. Data extraction from the electronic health charts identifies all patients with a penicillin allergy label submitted to the included departments in the study period and maps changes to their allergy status, and how evaluations are done. We will use segmented regression to analyse time series trends before and after implementation. Combining the quantitative and qualitative data allows for deeper understanding of the implementation process and its outcome.

How we plan to examine each implementation outcome measurement, and for what and whom each evaluation is planned is described below: Acceptability for clinicians was examined through qualitative interviews pre-implementation.18 Continued evaluation will be performed by using the IPAT. The IPAT will be distributed to each centre before, and at 6, 12 and 18 months of implementation.

Readiness to change will be examined for clinicians and the included health system through the IPAT and qualitative interviews. IPAT results will be compared between the centres, and at the different time points analysed for differences between study centres. Interviews will be analysed with systematic text condensation.

Fidelity will be measured by how many PAE applications were delivered by clinicians as intended and is defined as the number of correct PAE evaluations in the study period.

Adoption is defined as the number of patients that undergo a PAE at each study place, compared with the numbers of patients with a penicillin allergy label submitted to the study centres in the study period. One allergy department is included in the implementation. This unit has not performed direct oral provocation tests earlier and can provide important information about implementation needs in different contexts. Two allergy units participate in the study (including the aforementioned) to follow-up patients stratified as high-risk patients.

Penetration will be examined by measuring the number of clinicians using the method out of the possible providers. On a health system level, we aim to evaluate the dissemination of the PAE method into other departments as the primary included study centres.

Feasibility will be examined through mapping adaptations to the programme during implementation, documented in study logs from each centre, and through evaluation of the electronic patient documents concerning PAE.

We will further evaluate the sustainability of the programme by estimating the number of patients that have a correct penicillin allergy label in their electronic health charts 6 months after their PAE, along with the number of patients undergoing examination 6 months after the intervention. Finally, we will evaluate the number of departments and healthcare providers still applying the method 6 months postintervention.

Study status

The background study regarding the creation, validation and safety testing of the PAE programme was performed between March 2021–2022.28 Examination of barriers and facilitators for PAE implementation in WNH was performed in 2023.18 Recruitment of study centres and local study leaders began in August 2022. Regional ethics board approval of the implementation study was granted in December 2023. The first centre started implementation in November 2024, and implementation roll-out is ongoing. The study is expected to run through 2026, with a 6 month follow-up period, and data analysis will then commence.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the Regional Ethics Committee of Western Norway, approval number 199210. Ethics approval concerning the implementation study was granted on 20 December 2023 for commencement of the clinical implementation and 17 January 2025 for the data analysis. The study is registered in the Current Research Information System in Norway project identification number: 2500736.

The departments included in the implementation study all agreed to participation after written requests to the local management. All legal and formal research requirements have been met. All included hospitals have given permission for data collection. Patient consent was waived by the regional ethics committee. Results will be published open access in peer-reviewed. Nationwide implementation of PAE is planned, informed by the study’s findings. Data collected in the study are kept in a safe data warehouse owned by WMH. Patient identifiable data is only accessible with a code key that is stored separately and only available for the study coordinator at each site. AI such as ChatGPT and Grammarly was used for English language revision, but not for content creation and all text has been revised by the authors.

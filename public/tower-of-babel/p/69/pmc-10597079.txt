Regional health and social inequalities in Bulgaria
Authors: D Tsanova, S Georgieva, M Kamburova, E Mineva-Dimitrova, B Ivanova, A Seizov, T Vitkova
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe administrative division of Bulgaria divides it into 28 regions, which differ significantly in terms of both socioeconomic development and a number of demographic indicators. The death rate is an indicator according to which our country has been at the top of the world rankings for several years. Cardiovascular diseases stand out among the leading causes of death and they are one of the most important problems facing our society and health system. The study aims to analyse the relationship between the death rate due to CVD in different regions in Bulgaria with the average annual salary of employed persons as an indicator for the socioeconomic development of the regions.MethodsThe regional differences in the death rate due to CVD and the average annual salary of employed persons were analysed based on the National Statistical Institute data. Analysis and calculations were performed by MS Excel 2019.ResultsThe death rate due to CVD in Bulgaria varies widely - from 1739.7 per 100000 in region Vidin, 1685,2 in region Montana to 878.0 in the city of Sofia, with a national average of 1163.2 /2021/. At the same time, Vidin and Montana regions are also among the regions with a very low average annual salary of employed persons - respectively 12 855 and 13 983 BGN, which is much lower than the average for the country - 21 128 and than the capital Sofia - 25 724 BGN /2021/.ConclusionsThe regional health inequalities in Bulgaria are very important in order to control and decrease the level of mortality in the country, especially the death rate due to CVD. Bulgaria is among the counties that have the most serious challenges related to high cardiovascular deaths and should be among the priorities of public policy. The profound regional differences that were found impose permanent monitoring and directing the regional social and public health policies to avoid their unfavourable effects. Society should be making major and cost-effective efforts to reduce mortality.Key messages• The elimination of regional inequalities in the country is one of the possible ways to deal with the difficult demographic situation which occurred in the years after the democratic changes.• Regional health inequalities create the need for a long-term health policy that will improve the population's health status and the effective functioning of the healthcare system in our country.

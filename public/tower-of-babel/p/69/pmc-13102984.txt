Optimizing biometric system selection via complex spherical fuzzy einstein aggregation operators
Authors: Sehar Kanwal, Umer Shuaib, Ghaliah Alhamzi, Abdul Wakil Baidar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBiometric system evaluation in the banking sector involves significant uncertainty due to sensor reliability, environmental conditions, and user behavior variability. To address these challenges, this study employs a complex spherical fuzzy (CSF) framework that represents membership, neutrality, and non-membership information in a complex-valued form, enabling the simultaneous modeling of magnitude and phase characteristics. This makes CSF modeling particularly suitable for capturing time-dependent and cyclic performance patterns in biometric systems. Within this framework, two novel aggregation operators are introduced: the complex spherical fuzzy Einstein ordered weighted averaging (CSFEOWA) operator and the complex spherical fuzzy Einstein ordered weighted geometric (CSFEOWG) operator. By employing Einstein t-norm and t-conorm based aggregation, the proposed operators provide smooth and bounded nonlinear aggregation behavior that emphasizes joint performance and limits excessive compensation among criteria. Compared with classical linear aggregation operators, the proposed approach reduces sensitivity to extreme or unreliable inputs and yields more stable and balanced decision outcomes under uncertainty. Furthermore, an improved score function for complex spherical fuzzy numbers (CSFNs) is developed to ensure unambiguous ranking of alternatives. An MCDM algorithm based on the proposed operators is constructed and applied to the selection of an efficient biometric system for preventing financial mishaps in the banking sector. Comparative and sensitivity analyses demonstrate the robustness and practical effectiveness of the proposed framework, confirming its suitability for complex and uncertain decision-making scenarios.Keywords: Fuzzy logic, Einstein aggregation operators, Multi-criteria decision making, Score function for CSFNs, Aggregation operators, Biometric system selection, Cybersecurity in financeSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Background

MCDM difficulties arise in various situations where there is a need to choose among a range of options, actions, or candidates based on a predetermined set of criteria1,2. The solution of MCDM problems with AOs is gaining popularity due to its ability to effectively address real-life problems in several fields, including engineering, environmental sciences, social sciences, and others. AOs merge many values into a single value within a defined set, allowing the resulting aggregation to include all individual values. Prior to the introduction of AOs, judgements were made using crisp sets. However, in practice, membership in a set is not always well defined, making traditional mathematical methods inappropriate, especially in social and biological sciences, psychology, economics, linguistics, and other soft sciences. In 1965, Zadeh3 proposed fuzzy sets (FSs) to overcome the shortcomings of crisp set theory by giving membership values in the unit interval [0, 1], which indicates partial truth. This idea was subsequently generalized by Yager4, who added the aggregation operators (AOs) to the fuzzy systems to improve the modeling capability of a fuzzy system. In the real world, however, there are usually cases of fuzzy sets when non-membership information cannot be represented satisfactorily by membership degrees alone. To address this, Atanassov5 proposed intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs) where membership degree (MD) and non-membership degree (NMD) are assigned. However, these values are limiting in multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) in that the values should meet the requirement that the sum of the values should be less than or equal to one. Thereafter, Yager6 suggested Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PFSs) whose sum of squares of MD and NMD is not larger than one, thus providing more flexibility and better modeling of uncertainty. The authors7 developed linguistic PyF Aczel-Alsina AOs in 2025. PyFSs have been widely applied across various fields8–11. To further enhance uncertainty handling, Yager12 introduced q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (q-ROFSs), which generalize IFSs and PyFSs by incorporating the summation of the qth power of MD and NMD, which does not exceed one, offering greater flexibility and broader applicability13,14. Gurmani et al.15 proposed linguistic q-ROFSs to optimize the performance evaluation of green supply chain management practices. The authors16 proposed Aczel-Alsina operators based on q-ROF AOs in 2025. In addition, q-ROFSs have been combined with TOPSIS/VIKOR strategies to handle complex electronic marketing and federated learning-based decision-making17.

The aforementioned theories have been shown to be effective in a variety of fields. However, there are specific circumstances in which their utility is restricted, such as voting, in which individuals may express their opinions by using alternative response options, such as ‘yes’, ‘abstain’, ‘no’, and refusal. Cường et al.18 developed a theoretical framework known as picture fuzzy sets (PFSs) to address this critical predicament. Each element of the universe is assigned three degrees by a PFS: The degree of positive MD, the degree of neutral MD, and the degree of negative MD. These sets are highly beneficial for resolving issues such as imprecise inference, clustering, decision-making, and voting. Several recent studies have proposed new similarity measures and discriminant information measures in PFSs, proving their usefulness in domains like banking site selection, pattern recognition, and medical diagnosis19–22. The authors23 introduced the concept of Einstein operators in the context of PFSs. The concept of spherical fuzzy sets (SFSs) was introduced by Gundogdu and Kahraman24. This concept broadened the scope of MD, AD, and NMD to the range [0, 1], where the sum of the squares of these degrees is contained within a closed unit interval [0, 1]. SFS theory has been the foundation of numerous successful research projects in a variety of scientific fields25,26. In 2025, Gurmani et al.27 defined cubic linguistic T-SF AOs, and utilized them to develop combinative distance-based assessment model.

Various human decision analyses depend on two-dimensional information, specifically the amplitude term and phase term. All the above approaches are unable to handle two-dimensional data. Thus, the concept of complex fuzzy sets (CFSs) was introduced in28,29. CFSs expand the conventional interval of [0, 1] to encompass the whole closed unit disk within the complex plane. A CFS comprises two elements: An amplitude term ranging from [0, 1] and a phase term between [0, 2π]. However, CFS struggles to express the complexity of non-membership degrees in complex values. In order to overcome this restriction, Alkouri and Salleh30 broadened the definition of CFSs by introducing the concept of complex IFSs (CIFSs). CIFSs. However, the issue arose when the real and imaginary components exceeded a closed unit disk. In order to address these restrictions, Ullah et al.31 proposed the concept of complex Pythagorean fuzzy sets (CPyFSs), which stipulate that the sum of the squares of the real and imaginary components of the MD and NMD must not surpass the closed unit disc. Decision-support and fuzzy modeling frameworks effectively handle uncertainty and complexity in multi-criteria decision-making problems32,33. The neutral state, a critical element in a genuine representation of human cognition, was overlooked by the previous two-dimensional models. The amplitude term and phase term parameters of MD, AD, and NMD were described by Akram et al.34, who posited a complex picture fuzzy set (CPFS). The terms must meet the condition that the sum of their real and imaginary components is restricted to a closed unit disk in the complex plane. The success of CPFSs is contingent upon the design, which facilitates the efficient processing of human opinion in two dimensions. However, when the sum of the three degrees exceeds 1, CPFSs experience complications. In 2024, Abdullah et al.35 developed a novel decision making model based on feed-forward neural networks under the double hierarchy linguistic information for the selection of the best heterogeneous wireless networks for voice, video, and data. Ullah et al.36 proposed a novel decision making model based on the fuzzy credibility graph to select the best climate change mitigation. The authors37 devised the technique for analyzing the deep learning techniques based on three-way decision under double hierarchy linguistic information and application.

Related work

The concept of complex spherical fuzzy sets (CSFSs) was introduced in38 to address these limitations. This framework enhances the concept of CPFSs by expanding the range of membership, neutrality, and non-membership grades. The CSF model is superior and prevailing compared to existing two-dimensional techniques because of its adaptable conditions. A hybrid decision-making approach using a CSF environment was presented in39. The CSFOWAOs using the CSFS VIKOR approach to address group decision-making challenges were developed in40. The CSF Dombi AOs were investigated in41 within the framework of the CSF setting for the purpose of evaluating power-battery vehicles. Akram et al. presented the concept of CSF N-soft set in42. Hussain et al.43 discussed the Aczel-Alsina operators under the CSF environment and their implementations in the evaluation of power-battery cars. Additional advancements in the CSF framework can be studied in44. Some useful approaches to solve MCDM problems within the scope of fuzzy soft sets were presented in45,46. Further developments in soft set theory can be explored in1,47. Zhang et al.48 proposed a belief-based fuzzy and imprecise clustering method for handling arbitrary data distributions.

It is important to note that CSF knowledge is more powerful than soft and hesitant fuzzy settings because it provides a richer and more flexible framework for handling uncertainty. While soft sets mainly rely on parameterization and hesitant fuzzy sets deal with multiple possible membership degrees, they both lack the ability to represent falsity and indeterminacy simultaneously. CSF settings overcome these limitations by incorporating membership, neutral, and non-membership degrees within a complex domain. This allows a more complete representation of multi-dimensional and conflicting data which is often the case in the real world. Moreover, the CSF sets are a generalization of many existing fuzzy models such as, e.g., hesitant and soft fuzzy approaches as special cases, which are enriched in expressiveness and mathematical flexibility by complex numbers. Thus, CSF knowledge offers a better and more unified tool for advanced decision-making scenarios.

One of the main advantages of this theory is that it allows decision-makers to simultaneously assess various viewpoints and criteria by incorporating both magnitude and phase terms, thereby enabling a more comprehensive and precise representation of uncertainty. In data mining, fuzzy logic and uncertainty modelling are highly reliant on AOs, which are used for amalgamate different sources of data to provide relevant insights. In ordered weighted aggregation methods, input variables will be weighted based on their positional rank, often in descending order; therefore, enhancing decision making in challenging circumstances with informed assessments. Einstein AOs enhance this methodology because they reduce the impact of outliers and give more balanced outcomes, especially in case of ambiguous or unclear data. They apply nonlinear aggregation between elements, which allows criteria to strengthen each other but restricts full compensability a helpful quality in complicated decision-making. Moreover, EOW AOs retain flexibility as well as complexity while taking into account the relative importance of inputs according to their sequence. This innovation has a strong impact on existing methodologies by overcoming limitations and increasing the strength of the uncertainty modelling, which will have a notable impact on decision science. Novel aspects of the proposed CSFEOW AOs

The CSFEOW AOs signify a notable progression in MCDM, by amalgamating the advantages of CSFs with Einstein aggregation AOs. The Einstein t-norm and s-norm offer a mathematically coherent and dependable approach for aggregating fuzzy information, minimizing distortion and enhancing realistic modelling in the presence of competing or interdependent criteria. By integrating CSF and Einstein AOs, these operators can handle complex-valued fuzzy data, incorporating both amplitude and phase information, which is very beneficial in fields related to oscillatory, periodic, or time-dependent events. CSFEOW AOs function within a high-dimensional space that encompasses membership, neutrality, and non-membership, resulting in improved discrimination and sensitivity, which facilitates more precise ranking of alternatives in comparison to the study of Einstein AOs within the scope of the theories of IFS, PSF, and SFS.

Data transformation for cross-framework comparison

To enable a meaningful comparative analysis between the proposed CSF Einstein operators and existing PF and SF AOs, it is first necessary to adapt the CSF data to the mathematical constraints of each alternative framework. Since PF and SF sets operate exclusively on real-valued membership degrees and do not accommodate complex-valued phase information, we transform each CSFN by retaining only its amplitude terms . For the PF environment, the triplet must satisfy the condition ; for the SF environment, the corresponding condition is . The phase components are therefore necessarily discarded in these comparisons. While this transformation permits the application of PF and SF-based Einstein ordered weighted averaging/geometric operators23,26 to the same case-study data, it inherently sacrifices the two-dimensional (amplitude-phase) uncertainty representation that the CSF framework is designed to capture. Consequently, the rankings obtained from PF and SF operators reflect only the behavior of the amplitude-based aggregation, and any observed differences in the final alternative order depicted in Table 7 must be interpreted in light of this information-loss limitation. This methodological step is taken not to imply direct equivalence, but to illustrate how the richer CSF representation can lead to different decision outcomes when phase information is preserved, thereby underscoring the practical value of the proposed CSF approach in scenarios where periodic, cyclic, or phase-dependent uncertainty is relevant.

Computational efficiency of the suggested approaches

When comparing computational efficiency, the PFEOW operators are the most efficient, as they deal with three membership degrees and employ the Einstein t-norm/s-norm with relatively simple arithmetic, resulting in a time complexity. The SFEOW operators introduce additional normalization to satisfy the spherical constraint, making them moderately complex. The CSEFOW operators increase computational demands further, as they incorporate both the amplitude and phase of complex-valued membership degrees, involving trigonometric and exponential computations. The CSFEOW operators are the most computationally intensive. They handle complex-valued spherical fuzzy data besides being able to combine Einstein operations, which demand iterative nonlinear arithmetic. This increases the cost over that of the standard complex spherical case but in exchange offers the greatest representational richness and accuracy in dealing with uncertainty, especially in high-level decision-making situations.

Conclusion

The CSF environment augments fuzzy logic in that it deals with uncertainty and imprecision with Einstein operators, suitable for handling nonlinear aggregation behavior and vague data in complex decision-making scenarios. This article introduces two special AOs in the CSF framework, the CSFEOWA and the CSFEOWG operators. To overcome the limitations of the existing score function for CSFNs, we have set up an advanced ranking method to facilitate the more efficient ranking and selection of optimal options. We have also covered the necessary structural characteristics of the newly defined operators. We have also designed a detailed MCDM mathematical framework on the basis of suggested AOs and the ranking mechanism. Furthermore, we have managed to utilize these strategies to resolve the MCDM problem of optimization of biometric systems to prevent financial disasters in the framework of CSF knowledge. We have also conducted a detailed comparative analysis to show the dependability and reliability of the proposed methods with respect to the current methodologies. In addition, we have demonstrated the efficiency of new operators proposed by performing the correlation analysis of these approaches with the already available methodologies using the Spearman’s and Kendall’s Tau measures. We have also shown that the suggested techniques of this article are more computationally efficient in comparison to the existing techniques.

The present research will be extended to more generalized ones, including CS dynamic fuzzy, Ct-SF settings, and complex interval-valued SF. Furthermore, we will aim to enrich the versatility and use of our newly developed models in numerous decision-making scenarios such as IoT network optimization, environmental impact, and cybersecurity.

The concepts of Ct-SF and dynamic CSF settings add a great deal to the selection of biometric systems in terms of more realistic and adaptive decision-making frameworks. Ct-SF enables experts to make uncertain, hesitant, and interdependent judgments about criteria such as accuracy, cost, usability and spoof resistance in a more precise way using its complex-valued representation, and thus improving discrimination of closely performing biometric alternatives. Meanwhile, Dynamic CSF takes into account the time-dependent nature of biometric performance, thus taking into account evolving factors such as the improvement in technology, the evolution of security threats, or changes in user preferences, and allowing for the continuous reassessment of the system selected. Consequently, the aforementioned approaches guarantee that the biometric system selection is not only robust against uncertainty but also adaptive to temporal variations, which makes the chosen solution reliable in the present and sustainable for future needs.

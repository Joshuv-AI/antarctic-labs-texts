Natural killer cell T-cell lymphoma (nasal type), a rare and aggressive type of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma: Case report
Authors: Devraj Kandel, Shishan Dhakal, Sushila Thapa, Prakash Dhakal, Surendra Rayamajhi, Jagat Baniya
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
In the sinonasal tract, diffuse large B-cell lymphomas are the predominant type non-Hodgkin's lymphoma while natural killer (NK) cell T-cell lymphoma, the nasal type, constitute only 3%-12% of NHLs in this region and is the more aggressive subtype. NK cell T-cell lymphoma mostly occurs in male at the median age of 50 years and has got a poor prognosis with a 3-years overall survival (OS) of 46.3% and a 5-years OS of 42%. We present a case of a 37 year old lady with a mass in nasal cavity extending to the nasopharynx and upper cervical lymphadenopathy. Biopsy from the nasal mass showed features of NHL, NK cell T-cell type although immunohistochemistry was not available for the definite diagnosis. For localized diseases, chemo-radiotherapy forms the mainstay of treatment with complete remission in up to 50% of cases while chemotherapy alone is given to patients with disseminated disease with a very poor outcome (5-year survival of 10%-45% only). In our case, the patient received first dose of chemotherapy and then died at home before receiving the second dose showing the aggressive nature of the disease. Timely diagnosis with typical imaging features and histological diagnosis can improve the outcome with complete cure in almost half of the cases with localized disease.

Introduction

Lymphomas are classified as Hodgkin's (10% of all cases) and Non-Hodgkin's lymphomas (NHL, 90% of all cases) [1]. NHL mainly comprise B-cell lymphomas which are primarily nodal in origin [2]. However, NHL can be extra-nodal and T-cell in origin as well. Among the extra-nodal NHLs, 8% are seen in head and neck region of which 3% are seen in nasal cavity [3]. The nasal type of extranodal natural killer T-cell lymphomas, are the most aggressive subtype of NHL in the sinonasal tract and constitute only 3%-12% of NHLs in this region [4]. Natural killer cell T-cell lymphoma mostly occurs in male at the median age of 50 years [5]. It has got a poor prognosis with a 3-years overall survival (OS) of 46.3% and a 5-years OS of 42% [6].

Case description

A 37 year old female visited a local hospital with complaints of nasal obstruction, anosmia and multiple swellings over the bilateral cervical region where she was diagnosed to have some mass in the nasal cavity. X-ray paranasal sinuses (PNS) and Computerized tomography (CT) PNS were done which also suggested mass in the nasal cavity with extension to adjacent sinuses and erosion of nasal septum. The patient was then referred to the ENT department of our hospital for further work-up and management of the case. In our center, MRI of the PNS was done where mass of the nasal cavity extending to the nasopharynx with bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy was detected. With the provisional diagnosis of sinonasal lymphoma from MRI, biopsy of the mass was taken which showed features of NK cell T-cell Lymphoma. She was then admitted in oncology ward where she received first dose of chemotherapy (CHOP regimen) and got discharged. However, the patient died at home before returning for the second dose of chemotherapy.

Histopathological findings

High and low power histological images from the biopsy specimen received from the patient show tumor cells arranged in diffuse sheet infiltrating the stroma with individual cells showing high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio, round to oval shape, hyperchromatic nuclei and prominent nucleoli in some of them. Frequent mitotic figures and large areas of necrosis are also seen. Discussion

Natural Killer Cell T-Cell Lymphoma, nasal type is a rare and aggressive type of NHL which is more common in people of Asian descent [7]. Although nasal cavities and sinuses are involved preferentially (70%), Waldeyer's ring (38%), oral cavity (14%) or the larynx, and the hypopharynx (10%) can also be involved [8]. As the clinical presentation of the disease is nonspecific, histological diagnosis with biopsy is required to reach the proper diagnosis. When available, immunohistochemistry analysis with tumor cells showing expression of CD2, CD3ε and CD56 helps to reach the definite diagnosis [9] which in our case was not available. However, certain imaging features like diffuse necrotic mass spreading to the nasopharynx from nasal cavity, destruction of midline structures like nasal septum, turbinates and palate in an Asian (as in our case) should be considered for a possible NK T-cell Lymphoma. The nearest differential of NHL of sinonasal tract is squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) which is more heterogeneous with intra-tumoral necrosis and hemorrhage, higher ADC value than that of NHL and associated with bony erosions more often than NHL. Although biopsy is the way to differentiate NHL and SCC of nasal cavity, biopsy is not easily accessible and sometimes histological differentiation also becomes not very easy, in such cases imaging characteristics are crucial for the diagnosis [10]. For localized diseases, chemo-radiotherapy forms the mainstay of treatment with complete remission in up to 50% of cases while chemotherapy alone is given to patients with disseminated disease with a very poor outcome (5-year survival of 10%-45% only) [2,5].

Conclusion

As the early diagnosis and treatment can cure 50% of NK cell T-cell lymphoma, early imaging and pathological work-up should be carried out in patients presenting with nasal obstruction and anosmia not explained with common infectious pathology.

Correction: Trajectory of the body weight after drug discontinuation in the treatment of anti-obesity medications
Authors: Han Wu, Wenjia Yang, Tong Guo, Xiaoling Cai, Linong Ji
Published: 2026

Correction: BMC Med 23, 398 (2025)

https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-025-04200-0

We thank the readers for their constructive engagement with our article [1], “Trajectory of Body Weight After Drug Discontinuation in the Treatment with Anti-Obesity Medications.” In response to the methodological points raised, we have conducted further analyses to clarify and strengthen our findings. The core conclusion—a general trend of weight regain following drug discontinuation—remains robust.

Key methodological clarifications and supplementary results are summarized below:

Primary Analysis Using a Multilevel Model:

To appropriately account for the longitudinal nature of the data and correlations within studies over time, we conducted an additional analysis using a multilevel model implemented in R version 4.5.1 with the following packages: metafor, dplyr, ggplot2, RColorBrewer, and readxl. The results of this model (Supplementary Figure S1) clearly depict a progressive increase in body weight after discontinuation across the analyzed studies, confirming the trend described in the original manuscript.

Sensitivity and Subgroup Analyses:

The discrepancy between “11 studies” and “15 studies” arose because our analysis included 11 unique studies, from which we extracted a total of 15 time-point data points.

We assessed the risk of bias due to incomplete outcome data using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (Version 1.0), following the criteria outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. In our sensitivity analysis, we excluded two studies with a potential risk of bias:

One study on bupropion (Michael Weintraub, 1999) was excluded due to its age (conducted over two decades ago), incomplete reporting of baseline BMI, and uncertainty regarding outcome data completeness.

The other, a study on fenfluramine and phentermine (J. Taylor Hays, 2001), was also excluded because it was conducted decades ago, limiting its relevance to current weight management populations, and because these medications are now rarely used for weight loss.

The sensitivity analysis showed that the pooled effect estimate remained stable (Supplementary Figure S2).

A post-hoc subgroup analysis found no statistically significant difference in weight regain between placebo-controlled and active-controlled trials (P = 0.55).

Data Transparency:

All analyses were based on summary data (mean change from baseline and its variance) extracted from the published study reports. The occasional non-alignment between weight and BMI trajectories in Figure 2 stems from the reporting of these outcomes from partially overlapping, but not identical, patient samples in the original trials.

Additional Revisions:

In the legend of Figure S1, we have explicitly stated that the shaded band represents the prediction interval.

We have corrected the misleading label “15 studies” to “15 comparisons” throughout the text and figures to accurately reflect the inclusion of 11 unique studies with multiple time-point.

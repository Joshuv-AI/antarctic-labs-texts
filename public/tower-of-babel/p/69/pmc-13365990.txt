IFPHOR0062 Global Health Promotion and Communication training Curricula: Trends and Structures
Authors: Allan Ssembuusi, David Musoke, Christine Nalwadda Kayemba, Aisha Nakiwala Sembatya, Marjorie Kyomuhendo, Ivan Lukanda, Agnes Nyabigambo, Rhoda Wanyenze
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHealth promotion and communication (HPC) are essential for behaviour change, community empowerment, and effective health information dissemination. However, HPC training opportunities are limited in many low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). This study benchmarked global postgraduate HPC programmes to inform a postgraduate HPC curriculum development at Makerere University, Uganda.MethodologyA benchmarking review of master’s-level programmes in health promotion, health communication, and combined HPC was conducted using information from official university websites and programme directories. Eligible programmes were active postgraduate degrees with a clear focus on HPC disciplines. Information on programme characteristics, modules, duration, delivery mode, and employability pathways were extracted using a structured template. Qualitative content analysis was conducted in NVivo 2020, and frequency distributions were generated and visualised using Microsoft Excel.ResultsFifty programmes were identified across multiple global regions, with most focused on health promotion (80%), followed by health communication (16%) and combined HPC (4%). The majority were offered in high-income countries (72%). A total of 555 modules were identified, with core modules accounting for 74% and electives 26%. Common modules included epidemiology, biostatistics, health planning, monitoring and evaluation, and research methods. Most programmes lasted two years and were delivered in full-time or part-time formats. Graduates were commonly employed in international organisations, academia, research institutions, and government agencies.ConclusionThere is a dominance of health promotion and foundational public health content in global HPC curricula, largely concentrated in high-income settings. There is a need for development and expansion of context-relevant HPC programmes, especially in LMICs for strengthened health workforce and improved public health outcomes.

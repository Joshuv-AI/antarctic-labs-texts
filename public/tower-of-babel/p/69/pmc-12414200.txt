Spot urinary copper/creatinine ratio: a non-invasive tool for diagnosis of Wilson’s disease in children
Authors: Fahmida Begum, Amit Kumar Datta, Khan Lamia Nahid, Tahmina Jesmin, Nadira Musabbir, Md Wahiduzzaman Mazumder, Kanij Fatema, Md Rukunuzzaman, Abu Sayd Mohammad Bazlul Karim, Faisal Chowdhury, Mohammad Azmain Iktidar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiagnosing Wilson’s disease (WD) in children remains a significant challenge. This study evaluated spot urinary copper/creatinine (Cu/Cr) ratio for paediatric WD diagnosis in Bangladesh. 60 children (30 WD, 30 non-WD by Leipzig criteria) were enrolled, and the spot morning urinary Cu/Cr and 24-hour urinary copper were measured. The spot Cu/Cr ratio was significantly higher in patients with WD (median 0.2597 vs 0.0101, p<0.001) and correlated strongly with 24-hour urinary copper (r=0.697). A cut-off of 0.01604 yielded 90% sensitivity and specificity (AUC 0.920). Spot urinary Cu/Cr ratio is an accurate, non-invasive tool for paediatric WD diagnosis, particularly in resource-limited settings. These promising initial findings highlight its potential, though further comprehensive validation in larger and diverse populations is essential to confirm its reliability and generalisability.Keywords: Adolescent Health, Genetics, Low and Middle Income Countries

Introduction

Wilson’s disease (WD) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder affecting copper metabolism, which, if untreated, can lead to severe liver and neurological damage.1 2 Therefore, early and accurate diagnosis is essential to prevent complications such as irreversible organ damage.3 However, diagnosis of WD in the paediatric population is particularly difficult due to variable clinical presentations and limitations of current investigations.2 4 Although standard diagnostic markers, such as 24-hour urinary copper excretion, are often considered key diagnostic criteria,5 they are difficult to obtain in children, resulting in collection error and unreliable results.4 Conversely, investigations like liver biopsy carry risks associated with invasive procedures, and genetic testing is often costly and inaccessible in resource-limited settings.1 2 These limitations highlight the need for a simpler, reliable, non-invasive diagnostic tool. This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of the spot urinary copper/creatinine (Cu/Cr) ratio for WD in children presenting with hepatic symptoms.

Methods

This prospective cross-sectional study was conducted from June 2021 to May 2023 at a tertiary paediatric centre in Dhaka, Bangladesh, after obtaining institutional review board approval (IRB No. BSMMU/2021/1854) and informed parental consent. 60 children (aged >3 years) with hepatic symptoms were enrolled in this study and divided into 30 newly diagnosed patients with WD (Group I) and 30 with non-WD chronic liver disorders (Group II). WD diagnosis was based on the Leipzig criteria (score ≥4).5 Patients with abnormal renal function or on d-penicillamine were excluded. Spot morning urine samples were collected for copper and creatinine measurement to calculate the Cu/Cr ratio. 24-hour urinary copper excretion and serum ceruloplasmin were also measured. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis determined the optimal Cu/Cr cut-off, sensitivity and specificity. Data were analysed using SPSS V.26.

Results

The mean age was similar between WD (9.1±2.7 years) and non-WD groups (9.4±3.2 years). Patients with WD had a significantly higher prevalence of positive family history (33.3% vs 6.6%, p=0.011), lower serum ceruloplasmin (p<0.001) and more frequent Kayser-Fleischer rings (50% vs 0%, p<0.001). The spot urinary Cu/Cr ratio was significantly higher in the WD group (median 0.2597, IQR 0.4582) compared with the non-WD group (median 0.0101, IQR 0.0144; p<0.001). A strong positive correlation was found between the spot Cu/Cr ratio and 24-hour urinary copper excretion in patients with WD (r=0.697, p<0.001) (figure 1).

ROC curve analysis for the spot Cu/Cr ratio yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.920 (95% CI: 0.840 to 1.000). An optimal cut-off value of 0.01604 demonstrated 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity for diagnosing WD (figure 2).

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate that the spot urinary Cu/Cr ratio has high diagnostic accuracy for WD in children, displaying 90% sensitivity and specificity at a cut-off score of 0.01604. This simple, non-invasive test strongly correlated with the traditional 24-hour urinary copper excretion, addressing the practical collection challenges in children.4 The results of the spot Cu/Cr ratio are comparable or potentially superior to individual components of standard diagnostic algorithms, especially considering the difficulties with 24-hour urine samples in the paediatric population.4 5 This makes the Cu/Cr ratio a valuable tool for initial screening, especially in resource-constrained settings like Bangladesh where advanced investigations are often not accessible.2 A positive spot ratio could simplify the diagnostic process, while a low ratio could aid in the exclusion of WD. It is important to emphasise that this study represents a crucial first piece of work in evaluating the diagnostic utility of the spot urinary Cu/Cr ratio in children. While our results are promising and suggest significant potential, they require substantial scientific verification.

While these results are promising, it is important to acknowledge the limitations, including the single-centre design and the relatively modest sample size inherent in a rare disease. Therefore, larger, multicentre and longitudinal validation studies are required to corroborate these findings with greater accuracy and to establish generalisable cut-off values across diverse paediatric populations. Future research should also consider evaluating the reliability of the spot Cu/Cr ratio by analysing repeat urine samples from the same patients on different days to assess intraindividual variability and consistency. Such studies would further strengthen the evidence base regarding its diagnostic utility, specifically validating its specificity and sensitivity in broader clinical contexts. Nevertheless, the spot urinary Cu/Cr ratio shows incredible potential as an accessible and reliable tool for improving the early and accurate diagnosis of WD in children, ultimately aiding timely intervention and better patient outcomes.

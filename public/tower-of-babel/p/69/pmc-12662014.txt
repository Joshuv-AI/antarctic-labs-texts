Healthcare utilization data from piedmont, Italy: An administrative dataset of care episodes (2019–2024)
Authors: Marina Andrić, Arianna Dagliati, Mauro Dragoni, Francesco Ricci
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The presented dataset comprises administrative healthcare records from the Local Health Authority of Alba and Bra, located in the Piedmont region of Italy, spanning the period from January 2019 to April 2024. It includes detailed records of inpatient admissions, ambulatory and emergency visits, residential care, home-based services, hospice care, and medication use. The dataset includes healthcare records for approximately 175 K patients, covering over 2.8 M ambulatory visits, 94 K inpatient episodes, 252 K emergency episodes, and 952 K medication-related events. It also contains 9.8 K residential assessments, 82 K home care events, and over 230 hospice care records. The dataset has been curated to support reuse by researchers interested in healthcare delivery, service utilization, and the impact of health events as the COVID-19 pandemic. Access to the dataset is available upon request, subject to ethical review.

Value of the Data

•These administrative healthcare data provide a multi-year record of healthcare utilization from January 2019 to April 2024, covering inpatient, outpatient, emergency, and medication services within the local health authority in Piedmont, Italy. Researchers interested in specific aspects of health (e.g., diagnoses, procedures, medication use, healthcare use patterns) and transitions between healthcare settings (e.g., emergency to inpatient) can benefit from access to this dataset.•The data links different healthcare events, which are helpful for analyzing patient trajectories across healthcare settings.•Because the data are standardized and enriched with external metadata (e.g., ATC drug code, facility geolocation), they are suitable for cross-regional and international comparisons.•The data are valuable for studying changes in healthcare demand and delivery before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic.

Background

This dataset was compiled as part of the TrustAlert project,1 which aims to improve the preparedness and response capacity of local public health agencies in Italy to health emergencies. The project gathers real-world administrative healthcare data, which are routinely collected to track morbidity trends and anticipate health crises. Diagnostic information in the dataset is coded using the ICD-9-CM classification system, as officially adopted in Italy. While based on the U.S. standard, the Italian version includes national extensions for COVID-19-specific codes (e.g., 480.41 - Pneumonia in COVID-19, virus identified, 043.31 - Asymptomatic COVID-19, virus identified) introduced by the Ministry of Health on October 28, 2020 [1].

Data Description

Table 1 provides an overview of the main entities represented in the dataset, including near 100 inpatient facilities, >300 ambulatory care units, one thousand ATC drug codes, and several thousand diagnosis and procedure ICD-9-CM codes. The dataset is derived from administrative flows of the Local Health Authority of Alba and Bra (ASL CN2) and covers healthcare events for residents of 75 municipalities within its jurisdiction. The number of distinct patients recorded in the dataset closely matches the size of the local population, which can be explained by the fact that the data include not only inpatient hospitalizations but also outpatient visits and pharmacy purchases. In addition to the healthcare event records, the dataset contains a number of supporting tables, i.e., facility, department, outpatient_code, diagnosis_code, intervention_code, and residence_code, which store information about distinct entities and are designed to reduce redundancy. This relational structure requires users to combine data across multiple tables to retrieve all event-level details. For example, analyzing inpatient events involves linking tables healthcare_event, inpatient_event, and associated code tables. Guidance on how these tables relate and how to use them is provided further below.

Inpatient records are derived from Hospital Discharge Records (In Italian: Scheda di Dimissione Ospedaliera – SDO), the standardized form used throughout the Italian healthcare system to document all inpatient stays. These records include administrative details (e.g., admission/discharge dates, department, costs), clinical data (primary and secondary diagnoses and procedures, coded using ICD‑9‑CM), and Diagnosis‑Related Group (DRG) codes. In Italy, DRG codes (In Italian: Raggruppamenti Omogenei di Diagnosi) are assigned at discharge using grouper software, which classifies hospitalizations based on clinical similarity and resource use. Because a single hospitalization may involve department transfers or span multiple administrative records, several SDOs can correspond to one clinical episode. To represent these events, the dataset includes tables:-inpatient_event, which stores individual hospitalization records based on each SDO, and-inpatient_episode, which aggregates related inpatient_event entries into a hospitalization episode.

The dataset includes records of emergency and ambulatory visits, captured across three main tables: emergency_care_event, emergency_care_episode, and ambulatory_event. Each emergency or ambulatory visit may involve multiple departments and services, including diagnostic imaging (e.g., radiography or CT scans) or laboratory tests, which are recorded in the outpatient_care table. These services are identified using standardized procedure codes from the national nomenclature defined by the Italian Ministry of Health, which is used for administrative and reimbursement purposes [2]. The outpatient_code table provides the mapping between these codes and their textual descriptions. Accordingly, each ambulatory/emergency record includes information: the date of service, the department involved, the procedure code, its description, and the associated cost (see Table 7). To better represent the patient’s clinical journey, temporally related emergency visits occurring on consecutive days are grouped into emergency_care_episode records (Table 8). The dataset includes medication events that document pharmaceutical services reimbursed by the Italian National Health Service. As shown in Table 9, each record in the medication_event table captures a single medication instance associated with the date of service, department, type (administered, distributed, prescribed), national medication code (based on the Italian system), a textual description, quantity, and cost. Each medication code was associated with an ATC classification to facilitate more straightforward interpretation and comparison across systems. Table 13 describes the mapped relationships between inpatient episodes and other healthcare events, capturing the temporal and clinical connections across different service types. Each row links an inpatient episode to a related event (e.g., an ambulatory visit, medication event, or emergency care episode) using unique identifiers. The relationship_type variable categorizes these links based on their timing and clinical context, e.g., a medication administered during hospitalization (inpatient_medication), or an emergency visit within 30 days post-discharge (inpatient_to_emg). A textual description field provides additional details, such as the number of days between events. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Our research team received a linked dataset containing encounter-level administrative records from the Local Health Authority of Alba and Bra, which serves a portion of the Piedmont region in Italy. The original linkage of individual-level data across multiple administrative sources was performed by Innovo Srl, which pseudonymized patient identifiers (i.e., encrypted fiscal codes) to connect healthcare events while preserving anonymity. To prepare the dataset for research on healthcare utilization and patient trajectories, we applied a series of data cleaning, enrichment, and derivation procedures. Below, we describe the methods used to process and enhance the dataset’s informational value.

Inpatient episodes. Hospitalizations may span multiple administrative records due to transfers between departments, or administrative discharges and readmissions. To capture complete inpatient stays, we grouped related records (inpatient_event) into single entries (inpatient_episode) based on patient identifier and temporal proximity. Each episode includes the derived attributes: episode start and end dates, total length of stay, number of department transfers, number of included events, and total cost.

Emergency care episode. The dataset includes cases where a single patient has multiple emergency department records on consecutive days. The underlying reasons for these repeated visits are not explicitly captured in the data. To account for this and avoid overestimating the frequency of emergency care utilization, these events are treated as a single episode of care.

Medication codes. Medication records contain textual product names, dosages, and formulations. Each entry was mapped to its corresponding ATC (Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical) classification code. The primary source was the Italian Medicines Agency (AIFA) database, which yielded 3914 matches, corresponding to 1093 distinct entries. An additional 131 ATC codes were retrieved from public sources such as myhealthbox.eu. Records without valid or mappable codes (342) were labelled accordingly. Each record was annotated with the ATC code, the source of the mapping, and, where available, commercialization status and regulatory notes.

Facility information. Original healthcare facilities were recorded as free-text entries (e.g., Ospedale Martini (Torino), Dispensario Centrale Igiene Sociale). To enrich facility information, additional attributes, such as address, postal code, standardized name were retrieved using the Google Maps API. This procedure successfully provided complete location details for the majority of entries. However, in cases where the facility name was ambiguous or not recognized, the API returned incomplete or unreliable results. These entries were annotated with null values in the corresponding fields. In total, 1311 out of 1494 entries were enriched with location metadata, representing 1051 distinct facilities. Additionally, in July 2020, the Local Health Authority (ASL CN2) reorganized hospital services by closing Ospedale San Lazzaro in Alba and opening Michele e Pietro Ferrero Hospital in Verduno, now the sole inpatient and emergency care center for the Alba–Bra area. To reflect such organizational changes, the facility table includes a unified_id field: thus, San Lazzaro and Ferrero Hospital are separate records but share a unified_id, while other facilities retain a unique identifier matching their own id.

Linked care episodes. To support the study of patient care trajectories, a derived table was constructed to represent temporal and contextual relationships between care episodes. This resource links events such as emergency visits, inpatient stays, ambulatory appointments, and medication records using relationship codes (e.g., emg_to_inpatient, post_inpatient_followup, inpatient_medication). Each link is defined by simple temporal heuristics, for example, an outpatient visit occurring within a fixed 30-day window after hospital discharge. While this table is not based on a validated care pathway model, it is provided to facilitate exploratory analyses and hypothesis generation. Limitations include potential false positives in inferred links and reliance on proximity in time rather than clinical context.

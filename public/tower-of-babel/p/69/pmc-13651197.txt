Associated factors of fetal weight estimation accuracy by obstetric ultrasound among pregnant mothers admitted to labor ward of Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, East Amhara, Ethiopia
Authors: Kaleab Belay, Mekonnen Teferi, Zelalem Mengistu, Yonas Fissha Adem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionAssessment of fetal weight is a vital and universal component of antenatal care, essential not only for the management of labor and delivery but also for growth monitoring and the management of high-risk pregnancies. Consequently, several studies have simply compared the accuracy of clinical versus ultrasound methods of fetal weight estimation while ignoring technical factors such as the experience of the sonographer. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation and its associated factors among pregnant mothers admitted to the labor ward of Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital in East Amhara, Ethiopia.MethodsA facility-based cross-sectional study was conducted from January 1 to February 28, 2023, among 360 women who delivered at Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. Systematic random sampling was used to select study participants, and data were collected through maternal interviews and record reviews. The data were coded and entered using Epi-data version 4.6 software, then exported to SPSS version 26 for further statistical analysis. Both bivariable and multivariable logistic regression models were used to identify factors associated with the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation. As a measure of association, the adjusted odds ratio (AOR) with a 95% confidence level was utilized; a P-value < 0.05 in the multivariable analysis was considered statistically significant.ResultResponses were obtained from 360 mothers (mean age 31.79 ± 6.98 years). The overall accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation (within ± 10% of actual birth weight) was 88.3%, while 80% of scans were accurate within ± 5%. The mean birth weight was 3171.67 g (SD ± 708). Multivariable analysis showed that accuracy was significantly higher for scans performed < 1 day before delivery (AOR = 5.11, 95% CI: 1.71, 15.22) and for scans conducted by gynecologists/obstetricians (AOR = 4.34, 95% CI: 1.51, 12.54) compared to second-year residents. Conversely, accuracy significantly decreased for low-birth-weight babies (AOR = 0.29, 95% CI: 0.13, 0.62).ConclusionGenerally, the percentage of accurate sonographic fetal weight estimations in this study was higher than that reported in the majority of Ethiopian research. However, further improvement is needed to increase the accuracy of weight estimations. To achieve this, it is recommended that scans be performed by the most experienced examiners and conducted near the time of childbirth.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09659-0.

Introduction

Birth weight is a critical factor influencing prenatal and neonatal morbidity, particularly in preterm and small-for-dates infants. Additionally, it is valuable in the management of breech presentations, diabetes mellitus, labor trials, macrosomic fetuses, and multiple births [1]. In the later stages of pregnancy or prior to delivery, estimated fetal weight (EFW) is frequently a significant factor in making clinical decisions, and precise estimation of fetal weight is essential for obstetric planning and treatment [2]. Although clinicians can perform EFW using Leopold maneuvers or ultrasound, the use of ultrasound to estimate fetal weight has gained popularity since the 1970s due to its perceived ability to standardize and reproduce measurements [3, 4], despite the technical challenges depending on maternal physique, uterine anomalies, or the amniotic fluid index [5].

Ultrasonographic estimated fetal weight (EFW) is determined by measuring fetal components such as biparietal diameter, head circumference, abdominal circumference, and femur length using a transabdominal approach with devices like the Voluson E8, and applying formulas such as Hadlock’s for prediction [2, 6]. Once considered a luxury, obstetric ultrasound has become a fundamental part of routine obstetric examinations, with the accuracy of EFW typically within ± 10% of actual birth weight—studies show no significant difference between clinical and sonographic estimates within this margin [7]. Despite variability, sonographic EFW remains essential in routine antenatal care and obstetric management [8].

Physicians categorize birth weight into low, normal, and macrosomic, with precise assessment at these extremes being essential due to the associated risks—low birth weight often links to neonatal complications and congenital anomalies, while fetal macrosomia can result in significant health issues [9, 10]. The accuracy of sonographic estimated fetal weight (EFW) can be influenced by various factors such as maternal weight, amniotic fluid volume, fetal sex, multiple pregnancies, gestational diabetes, fetal size, gestational age, and the interval before delivery [8, 11, 12]. The rate of clinically significant errors is also greater if an inexperienced person performs the ultrasound examination [13, 14].

This study makes an additional contribution to existing knowledge by providing context-specific evidence from Ethiopia, highlighting the significant influence of examiner experience and timing of ultrasound scans on estimation accuracy, thereby informing targeted strategies to enhance fetal weight assessment in similar resource-limited settings.Therefore, this study aimed to assess the accuracy of fetal weight estimation and its associated factors among pregnant mothers admitted to the labor ward of Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital in East Amhara, Ethiopia.

Methods and materials

Study design, area, and period

An institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted from January 1 to February 28, 2023, at the Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. The hospital is located in Dessie, the capital of the South Wollo zone, situated 401 km northeast of Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Established in 1942 GC, the hospital currently provides services to 10 million people within the South Wollo, North Wollo, Waghimra, and Oromia specialized zones. The facility maintains a staff of 868 and contains 595 beds. Based on the hospital’s last six-month report, the total number of monthly deliveries is estimated to be 600.

Population

Source population

The source population included all pregnant mothers who gave birth at Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital.

Study population

The study population consisted of all pregnant mothers who gave birth at the hospital during the specified study period.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria required that pregnancies had reached viability, the fetus was anatomically normal, and delivery occurred within seven days of an ultrasound (US) estimation of birth weight. Additionally, the actual birth weight must have been recorded immediately after delivery, and the result must have been a live birth.

Exclusion criterion

Mothers who did not have obstetric US scans.

Sampling procedure

Study participants were enrolled using systematic random sampling with a skip interval of 3 based on their order of admission. Given that the average monthly delivery rate is 600, a total of 1,200 deliveries were expected during the two-month data collection period. The first participant was selected from the first three admissions using the lottery method. Women who did not have a US scan were replaced by the next woman who delivered.

Study objective

Primary objective

To determine the proportion of accurate sonographic fetal weight estimations among pregnant mothers admitted to Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital.

Primary outcome measure

Accuracy of fetal weight estimation, defined as whether the Absolute Percentage Error (APE) was within ± 10% of the actual birth weight.

Secondary objective

To identify maternal, fetal, clinical, and sonographer-related factors significantly associated with the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation.

Secondary outcome measure

The identification of significant predictors (e.g., examiner experience, scan-to-delivery interval, and birth weight) using Adjusted Odds Ratios (AOR) with 95% Confidence Intervals.

Data collection tools and procedures

A structured questionnaire was developed to assess study variables. Following a review of relevant literature, a structured data extraction checklist was created for chart reviews. Trained health professionals performed data collection by interviewing mothers and reviewing medical records. Every operator used the Hadlock II formula, which is a globally recognized regression model for fetal weight estimation. Standardization was maintained by requiring the measurement of four specific fetal components for every scan: biparietal diameter (BPD), head circumference (HC), abdominal circumference (AC), and femur length (FL). Scans were conducted using high-quality, specialized devices, specifically the Voluson E8 and GE Logiq P6.

Data quality assurance

To ensure data quality, a structured English questionnaire was developed based on literature-based tool development, translated into Amharic, and back-translated to confirm consistency. The principal investigator provided one day of training for data collectors and supervisors regarding ethics and data collection techniques. A 5% pretest involving eighteen mothers was conducted at Borumeda General Hospital before the main study. Questionnaires were checked for completeness by collectors at the end of each session. To prevent data loss, documents were stored in locked cabinets accessible only by the principal investigator.

Data processing and analysis

Data were coded and entered using EpiData Version 4.6 before being exported to SPSS version 26 for analysis. Analytical and descriptive analyses were conducted, using the mean (standard deviation) and median (interquartile range) for continuous data and frequency distributions for categorical data. The study’s findings were presented through cross-tabulation, graphs, and frequency tables. Bivariable and multivariable logistic regression models were employed to identify factors affecting the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation. Model fitness was verified using Hosmer-Lemeshow statistics. Multicollinearity was checked by using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). Variables with a P-value ≤ 0.25 during bivariable analysis were moved to the multivariable analysis, where a P-value < 0.05 indicated statistical significance. To represent the strength of the association, the adjusted odds ratio (AOR) and crude odds ratio (COR) were calculated with a 95% confidence interval (CI).

The mean age of the mothers was 31.79 years (SD ± 6.98), with ages ranging from a minimum of 18 years to a maximum of 49 years. Out of the total 360 study participants, the majority (216, or 60%) were urban residents, 261 (72.5%) were married, and 144 (40%) were self-employed. Regarding educational status, the majority (140, or 38.9%) had attended secondary education (Table 1).

Fetal and neonatal factors

The mean birth weight of babies in this study was 3171.67 g (SD ± 708), with minimum and maximum weights of 1900 g and 4800 g, respectively. More than half of the babies (207, or 57.5%) were male, and the majority (259, or 72%) of newborns had a normal birth weight.

Sonographic and professional-related characteristics

Sonographic scans were performed by second-year residents and higher-level professionals, with nearly one-third of the scans conducted by second-year residents. The mean sonographic estimated fetal weight was 3131 g (SD ± 708), with estimated weights ranging from 1500 to 4900 g. Additionally, the majority (205, or 56.9%) of mothers gave birth within one day of their most recent scan (Table 3).

The absolute percentage error of the scans within ± 5% of the actual birth weight was 80%. The overall accuracy proportion of sonographic fetal weight estimation in this study was 88.3% (Fig. 1).

Determinants of fetal weight estimation accuracy by obstetric ultrasound

Bivariable logistic regression was performed to select candidate variables for multivariable logistic regression analysis. All independent variables with a p-value less than or equal to 0.25 were taken as candidates for a multivariable logistic regression model. Results of bivariable logistic regression showed that fetal sex, time interval between the most recent scan and childbirth, examiner level (grade) of experience, birth weight, and having the scan performed after the start of labor were candidates for multivariable logistic regression analysis with P-value ≤ 0.25. In the final multivariable logistic regression analysis, only the time interval between the most recent scan and childbirth, examiner level (grade) of experience, and birth weight were significantly associated with the accuracy of fetal weight estimation with obstetric ultrasound.

keeping all other variables in the model constant, those scans performed by third-year residents are 3.88 times more accurate in fetal weight estimation [AOR = 3.88, 95% CI (1.53, 9.88)] compared to those scans performed by second-year residents, and those scans performed by gynecologist and obstetrician are 4.34 times more accurate in fetal weight estimation compared to those scans performed by second-year residents [AOR = 4.34, 95% CI (1.51, 12.54)].

Those mothers who gave birth within the same day of the last (most recent) ultrasound scan had nearly 5 times increased odds of accuracy in fetal weight estimation compared to those mothers who gave birth four to seven days after the last (most recent) ultrasound scan [AOR = 5.11, 95% CI (1.71, 15.22)]. and those mothers who gave birth between one to three days after the last (most recent) ultrasound scan had nearly 2.87 times increased odds of accuracy in fetal weight estimation compared to those mothers who gave birth four to seven days after the last (most recent) ultrasound scan while keeping all other variables in the model constant [AOR = 2.87, 95% CI (1.02, 8.04)].

Keeping all other variables in the model constant, low birth weight babies had 71% decreased accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation compared to normal birth weight babies [AOR = 0.29, 95% CI (0.13, 0.62)] (Table 4).

Discussion

The overall accuracy rate for sonographic fetal weight estimation in this study was 88.3%. This finding is higher than those reported in studies conducted in Italy [16], Germany [17], Turkey [18], Tunisia [19], Nigeria [8, 20], and a study conducted in three teaching hospitals, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia [15]. A possible justification for this difference is the time elapsed since those earlier studies were conducted; it is believed that quality improvement activities lead to better accuracy over time. Another reason for the higher accuracy in this study may be differences in subject inclusion criteria. For instance, a German study only included term pregnancies with vertex presentations, and a Tunisian study only used scans performed by residents, both of which can lower overall accuracy.

Several factors were significantly associated with the accuracy of fetal weight estimation: the time interval between the final scan and delivery, the examiner’s experience level, and the baby’s birth weight.The scans performed by third-year residents are nearly four times more accurate, and the scans performed by gynecologists and obstetricians are more than four times more accurate compared to the scans performed by second-year residents. This finding is consistent with studies conducted in Hong Kong [21], USA [22], UK [23, 24], Switzerland [25], and Addis Ababa [15] and the possible justification for this might be due to the fact that fetal weight estimation using obstetric ultrasound is a procedure that needs better understanding of fetal biometric landmarks and incorporating those in the calculation of estimated fetal weight and having lack of better experience and advanced training leads to a lack of understanding of important parameters, which finally leads to decreased accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation.

In this study, the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation was substantially correlated with the birth weight of babies, and low-birth-weight babies had more than two-thirds decreased accuracy of weight estimation compared to babies who had normal birth weight. This finding is in line with studies conducted in Switzerland [25], Ankara, Turkey [18], and Addis Ababa [15], and the possible justification might be due to the fact that low birth weight is mainly associated with several fetal health problems that can affect some of the fetal biometrics used in sonographic fetal weight estimation, which affects the accuracy of weight estimation.

The scans performed one day before childbirth are nearly five times more accurate, and the scans performed one to three days before childbirth are nearly three times more accurate compared to the scans performed four to seven days before childbirth. This finding is consistent with studies conducted in Switzerland [25] and Germany [26]. And the possible justification for this might be due to the fact that during pregnancy, every day is associated with changes in the fetal physiology and growth, which lead to changes in the fetal weight, so, making sonographic weight estimation near childbirth is more accurate in estimating weight compared to weight estimations performed four and above days before birth. This finding implies that since every day before childbirth is a day for change in the weight of the fetus, to make an appropriate childbirth decision with an accurate estimation of fetal weight, repeat sonographic weight estimation should be performed near childbirth, especially in those extreme weight estimations.

This study showed no significant association between maternal BMI and the accuracy of sonographic fetal weight estimation. However, there was a statistically significant difference between the study groups regarding fetal weight estimation and maternal body mass index, with higher values observed in obese groups 1, 2, and 3 compared to overweight and average maternal body weight groups. This finding is also supported by studies conducted in different locations [27–29]. The literature indicates that higher maternal BMI, especially in obese groups, is associated with increased subcutaneous fat, which can hinder the ultrasound beam’s ability to accurately penetrate and visualize fetal structures, potentially leading to overestimation of fetal weight.

Strengths and limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center study conducted at a specialized hospital, the findings may not be fully generalizable to lower-level health facilities with different resources. Second, while we identified the examiner’s experience as a key determinant of accuracy, we did not conduct formal inter-observer reliability tests prior to data collection. Third, measurement bias may have occurred due to inherent technical challenges in fetal biometry, such as those related to maternal physique or amniotic fluid index. Finally, our primary limitation was the reliance on a single sonographic model (Hadlock II), which may vary in accuracy across different weight ranges. Despite these constraints, its important contribution as a source of knowledge about the associated factors of fetal weight estimation accuracy by obstetric ultrasound is one of its key strengths.

Conclusion

This study provides a contemporary benchmark for sonographic precision at Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, with an 88.3% accuracy rate that exceeds previous regional reports. These findings have direct implications for obstetric decision-making:

✓ Staffing Protocols: Given that specialist experience increases accuracy four-fold, institutional policies should prioritize senior examiners for high-risk obstetric cases (e.g., macrosomia or breech) to ensure safer delivery planning.✓ Timing of Scans: To optimize clinical accuracy, repeat scans should be mandated near the time of childbirth, as scans performed within 24 h of delivery are five times more reliable than those performed a week prior.✓ High-Risk Monitoring: Obstetricians must exercise caution when managing suspected low-birth-weight neonates, where sonographic accuracy is significantly diminished, and should integrate additional clinical measures to avoid prenatal morbidity.

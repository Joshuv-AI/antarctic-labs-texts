Pathway-level profiling of the sepsis proteome reveals immune and transcriptional dysregulation
Authors: Logan R Van Nynatten, David Tweddell, Mark Daley, Gediminas Cepinskas, John Basmaji, Marat Slessarev, Douglas D Fraser
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSepsis triggers a complex and heterogeneous host response, yet decades of biomarker studies have yielded few targeted therapeutics that improve patient outcomes. Single analyte approaches fail to capture the coordinated biological programs that drive organ dysfunction. Focusing on single or limited panels of biomarkers to endotype disease fundamentally misrepresents sepsis biology, which reflects simultaneous disruption of multiple cellular networks. However, pathway-level bioinformatic analyses interpret proteins as components of larger biological systems, enabling detection of coordinated molecular disturbances that individual biomarkers cannot capture.MethodsWe conducted an exploratory cohort study profiling 1,196 plasma proteins in 15 critically-ill adults with sepsis on ICU Day 1 and Day 3 using proximity extension assays. Background

Sepsis remains one of the most prevalent conditions necessitating admission to intensive care units (ICUs), conferring an average ICU mortality rate of 25% (Sakr et al. 2018). Exploratory studies have characterized a plethora of circulating proteins associated with clinical outcomes in critically-ill sepsis patients. Procalcitonin (Schuetz et al. 2017), suPAR (Huang et al. 2020, Donadello et al. 2012), IL-6 (Molano Franco et al. 2019, Damas et al. 1992, McElvaney et al. 2021), CRP (Koozi et al. 2020), and presepsin (Lee et al. 2022, Velissaris et al. 2021), alongside a range of other inflammatory, endothelial, and immunologic proteins, have all been linked to inflammation, disease severity, and mortality in sepsis (Pierrakos and Vincent 2010). Consequently, based on these pathobiologic findings, more than 100 randomized clinical trials have investigated whether therapeutics targeting individual molecular markers are able to sufficiently modulate the host response to infection to improve survival (Marshall 2014). Yet, these studies have not led to therapies or interventions that significantly improve patient outcomes or are pragmatic for clinical practice. Focusing on single or limited panels of biomarkers to endotype disease fundamentally misrepresents sepsis biology, which reflects simultaneous disruption of immune, endothelial, metabolic, and transcriptional networks.

High-dimensional omics assays, such as transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics, now enable the measurement of thousands of circulating plasma markers simultaneously, generating datasets in which biological signatures emerge not from individual analytes but from patterns of entire dysregulated molecular pathways. Merging these technologies with computational analyses permits an exponential capacity to recognize complex molecular signatures ushering in new frameworks for patient endotyping and phenotyping (Komorowski et al. 2022, Nynatten et al. 2022, Nynatten et al. 2023, Nynatten et al. 2025). The complexity and heterogeneity of the host response in sepsis suggests that no single marker can fully capture the biology underlying outcomes or therapeutic responsiveness. Pathway-level analyses, using frameworks such as gene set enrichment analysis, provide an opportunity to interrogate the molecular architecture of sepsis as a network phenomenon. Rather than asking which proteins differ between patients, these methods ask which biological programs differ between patients, and how these programs evolve over time. Despite this promise, pathway-centric proteomic studies in adult critical care cohorts remain scarce, and the temporal behavior of pathway activity during early sepsis, when clinical instability and therapeutic decisions are most acute, remains poorly defined.

Future efforts should therefore shift from identifying isolated biomarkers toward mapping dysregulated molecular pathways, integrating immune, endothelial, metabolic, and organ-specific responses into comprehensive biological frameworks. We set out to perform an exploratory case-control cohort study to investigate pathway-level perturbations in the plasma proteomes of critically-ill patients with sepsis by combining high-dimensional proteomics with bioinformatic analyses. Our aims were to: (1) use high-dimensional proximity extension assays (PEA) to measure the circulating plasma proteome of patients with sepsis on ICU Day 1 and Day 3, as well as in healthy controls; (2) identify proteins distinguishing patients with sepsis on ICU Day 1 and Day 3; and (3) use enrichment analyses to characterize those cellular pathways that are perturbed in sepsis. This high-dimensional pathway-centric approach, measuring 1196 plasma proteins, is amongst the largest PEA-based omic sepsis analyses to date, and moves beyond isolated biomarkers to map the coordinated biological programs that define the early host response to sepsis.

Methods

Study participants and cohort data

This study was reviewed and approved by the Western University, Human Research Ethics Board (HREB #6970). A total of 15 patients with sepsis based on Sepsis-3 (Singer et al. 2016) criteria were enrolled. Clinical demographics collected included age, sex, comorbidities, multiple organ dysfunction scores (MODS), sequential organ failure assessment scores (SOFA), medications, hematologic labs, anticoagulation status, creatinine, PaO2/FiO2 (P/F) ratios, and chest x-ray findings. Interventions recorded included use of antibiotics, antivirals, corticosteroids, vasopressors, thromboembolism prophylaxis, renal replacement therapy, invasive and non-invasive ventilation, mechanical ventilation duration and ICU duration. 15 healthy control subjects without disease were identified from the Translational Research Centre, London, Ontario (HREB #6963; https://translationalresearchcentre.com/). This study was performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation and with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975. This exploratory, hypothesis-generating pilot study was designed to detect large effect sizes in case-control comparisons and to map pathway-level structure within high-dimensional proteomic data; the study was not powered for subgroup or mortality analyses.

Blood processing

Standard phlebotomy procedures were used to collect blood. Samples were obtained from critically-ill ICU patients via indwelling venous catheters and immediately placed on ice. Maximum volumes were not exceeded per standard phlebotomy protocols. Following collection, samples were transferred to a negative pressure hood; after centrifugation, isolated plasma was aliquoted in 250 µL increments and frozen at − 80 °C. All samples remained frozen until use to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Proximity extension analysis

Plasma samples were thawed and analyzed using proximity extension assay (PEA; O-link Proteomics, Sweden) as previously described (Lundberg et al. 2011, Assarsson et al. 2014). We profiled 1,196 plasma proteins in critically-ill patients with sepsis and in age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Each protein was detected using a pair of antibodies, each coupled to a unique, partially complementary DNA oligonucleotide. The assay proceeded in three steps: (1) antibody–oligonucleotide probes bound to their target antigens; (2) oligonucleotides brought into proximity hybridized and were extended by a DNA polymerase, forming a unique DNA barcode; and (3) this barcode was amplified and quantified by real-time PCR.

Quality control included assessment of immunoassay performance, detection efficiency, and hemolysis. Intra-assay variability was minimized by robotic pipetting and normalization with three internal controls monitoring incubation, extension, and amplification. Each plate also contained external negative controls and plate controls in triplicate to ensure inter-assay precision. Protein abundance was expressed as log2-transformed normalized protein expression (NPX) values and underwent rank-based normal transformation prior to statistical analyses.

Metadata analysis and quality control

A total of 45 samples from 30 patients were analyzed, consisting of 15 sepsis patients and 15 healthy controls. Each patient with sepsis had plasma proteomes profiled on ICU Day 1 and Day 3. Age and sex information was available for all patients. The metadata comprised 45 samples and 36 variables. After filtering variables unique for each sample, 35 remained (20 categorical and 15 numeric variables).

Potential confounding among experimental design factors (clinical, demographic and laboratory values) was evaluated using pairwise univariate association tests between all factor combinations. The choice of statistical test depended on the data types involved: associations between two continuous factors were assessed using Spearman rank correlation; associations between a survival variable and a categorical factor were tested using a Cox proportional hazards model with Wald test–derived significance. All resulting P-values were transformed as –log10(P) for visualization in the heatmap.

Proteomic data quality was evaluated for sample outliers. A sample was considered an outlier if it failed at least two predefined objective criteria: (i) total Euclidean distance to all other samples, (ii) Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistic, (iii) mean Pearson correlation with other samples, and (iv) Hoeffding’s D statistic. Principal component analysis and kernel density estimates were also reviewed to visually confirm outliers. For the sum of Euclidean distances, samples falling beyond mean ± 2–3 SD of the distribution of pairwise distance sums were flagged, supplemented by the IQR method (outliers defined as > Q3 + 1.5×IQR). For the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic, a D value > 0.2–0.3 with a significant p-value (p < 0.05 after Bonferroni correction) was used as an indicator of distributional divergence. Two samples (both from sepsis patients) satisfied two of four pre-specified criteria (Euclidean distance and Kolmogorov-Smirnov testing) and were confirmed by kernel density estimation. Review of sample processing records revealed no obvious technical or handling errors for these samples, although distinguishing biological from technical outliers is inherently difficult in small cohorts. These two samples were excluded from downstream analyses.

Conventional statistics

Cohort demographic and clinical characteristics were collected. Continuous variables were reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables as counts with percentages. Comparisons of categorical variables were performed using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared using Mann-Whitney U tests or Kruskal-Wallis tests, depending on the number of groups. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (Version 8.4.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, California, USA).

Normalized PEA proteomic data was used to identify proteins that differed significantly between sepsis patients and healthy control subjects. Statistical comparisons were conducted using empirical Bayes moderated t-tests. A statistical threshold of false discovery rate (FDR)-adjusted P < 0.05 was used to define significantly differentially abundant proteins. For longitudinal comparisons (Day 1 vs. Day 3), empirical Bayes moderated t-tests were performed using a paired design with patient ID included as a blocking factor to account for within-subject correlation. Volcano plots were generated to visualize these fold changes, whereby a positive log2 (fold change) was selected to indicate up-regulation in sepsis patients relative to healthy controls, while a negative value indicated down-regulation. Heatmaps showing between-sample pairwise Pearson correlations of normalized data were generated, with Euclidean distance used for clustering.

Pathway analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the Reactome database (package: reactome.db), with the fgsea R package to examine cellular pathways differing between healthy controls and sepsis patients, utilizing proteins measured with O-link. Reactome classifies proteins through a hierarchical organization of documented interactions, including binding, reaction, and modification events. Each pathway analysis utilized a ranked protein list based on a relevant statistic. GSEA assesses whether proteins involved in specific pathways are disproportionately present at one or both the extremes of the ranked list. Pathways containing fewer than 15 or more than 500 proteins were excluded from analysis. Statistical significance, reflected as nominal p-values, was determined using an adaptive multi-level split Monte Carlo method. For each pathway, enrichment score (ES), normalized enrichment score (NES), p-value, and FDR-corrected p-value were reported. A NES greater than 0 indicates enrichment among up-regulated proteins, whereas a NES less than 0 signals enrichment among down-regulated proteins.

This GSEA approach was also applied to Gene Ontology (GO) terms (package: GO.db (v3.19.1)) to identify biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components that differ between healthy controls and sepsis patients at ICU Day 1 and Day 3. The Gene Ontology framework systematically organizes biological terms into molecular function, biological process, and cellular component categories. Molecular function describes the activities of gene products at the molecular level. Biological processes involve a series of molecular events or activities, while cellular components indicate the subcellular location of gene products.

Protein–protein interaction network analysis

Proteins differentially expressed between sepsis patients and healthy controls were investigated for protein-protein interactions using the STRING database (version 12.0, https://string-db.org/). Only protein changes exhibiting at least a four-fold difference on ICU Day 1 relative to healthy controls were included to highlight the most relevant pathways. This analysis merged both physical (direct) and functional (indirect) associations based on experimental results, curated knowledge, co-expression, and text mining. The minimal interaction confidence was set at 0.7 (high). In the resulting network, nodes represent individual proteins and edges denote their interactions, with edge thickness reflecting confidence. K-means clustering was used to detect groups of functionally related proteins in the network.

Enrichment analyses within STRING identified biological pathways and functional categories overrepresented among the differentially expressed proteins. Disease-gene relationships were examined using the DISEASES database (http://diseasesdatabase.com/) to identify disease categories associated with the protein set. Tissue expression enrichment analysis was conducted to establish which tissues and cell types express these proteins, based on data from the Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) and other curated tissue databases. Hypergeometric testing with FDR correction assessed statistical significance. Significant enrichment results (FDR < 0.05) were displayed as dot plots, where dot size indicates gene count and dot color intensity represents statistical significance.

Clinical-pathway association analysis

Association analysis was performed to uncover links between GSEA-identified dysregulated pathways and clinical characteristics. This approach, using the clinical-pathway association matrix (A = C × Gᵀ), has been developed and validated across multiple studies by our group, including analyses of both adult and pediatric cohorts (Stranges et al. 2025, Stranges et al. 2026, Fraser et al. 2025, Spagnolo et al. 2025), and provides a quantitative summary of how clinical features relate to enriched biological pathways via GSEA leading-edge gene membership. A correlation matrix (C) of clinical variables to gene expression and a gene-pathway association matrix (G), reflecting pathway membership based on GSEA leading edge genes, were used to compute the association matrix (A) as: A = C x GT. Here, C is an m x n matrix of m clinical variables by n genes, and G is a p x n matrix of p pathways by n genes. Genes not in a pathway have corresponding zero entries in G. In this study, 1,196 gene markers were evaluated; only 458 unique leading-edge genes were associated with significant pathways for Day 1 vs. Healthy Control, and 86 for Day 1 vs. Day 3, resulting in a sparse G matrix.

Cellular deconvolution analysis

Cellular deconvolution, using CIBERSORTx, estimated immune cell type proportions from bulk proteomic data. The LM22 signature matrix (547 genes spanning 22 immune cell types) served as the reference. Protein expression data were transformed from log2 to linear scale before analysis. Values are relative log₂-transformed units that allow cross-sample comparison within each protein but are not absolute concentrations and cannot be compared across proteins due to assay-specific antibody characteristics and dynamic ranges. Accordingly, proteins were mapped to gene symbols to enable compatibility with the LM22 transcriptomic signature matrix. Of the LM22 genes, 121 (22%) overlapped with the measured proteome. Given that LM22 was derived from transcriptomic data, these estimates were interpreted qualitatively as indicators of immune cell composition rather than precise quantitative measures. We have previously applied this protein-to-gene mapping strategy using a comparable Olink PEA platform in other cohorts (Iosef et al. 2023), where CIBERSORTx-predicted cell-type patterns were corroborated by orthogonal ELISA validation. Estimated immune cell proportions were visualized as stacked bar charts by sample condition. Statistical differences between cell type proportions in groups were tested using Kruskal-Wallis tests across groupings: (1) Healthy Control, (2) Day 1 ICU and (3) Day 3 ICU, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction adjusted p-values for multiple testing. Cell types with FDR < 0.05 were classified as significantly different. CD4 memory resting T cells, which had zero estimates in all samples, were excluded from testing. All analyses were conducted in Python utilizing scikit-learn, scipy, and custom scripts.

Cohort Clinical and Demographic Characteristics

Sepsis patients demonstrated significant illness severity with median MODS scores of 7 (IQR 5.0–8.0) on Day 1 and 4 (IQR 3.5-7.0) on Day 3, and SOFA scores of 7 (IQR 5.0–9.0) on Day 1 and 6 (IQR 3.5-7.0) on Day 3, with a significant improvement in SOFA scores over time (p = 0.012). Laboratory parameters revealed signs of systemic inflammation and organ dysfunction, including increased white blood cell counts, neutrophil counts, thrombocyte counts, and lactate levels on ICU Day 1 (Table 1). Most patients had pneumonia as the source of their sepsis. All patients received antibiotics, and use of vasoactive medications decreased significantly from 73.3% on Day 1 to 33.3% on Day 3 (p = 0.028). Most patients required mechanical ventilation; all patients received invasive mechanical ventilation on Day 1, and 80% continued to require it on Day 3. All 15 patients met Sepsis-3 clinical criteria for enrollment. Among the sepsis cases, 66.7% (10 patients) had suspected sepsis and 33.3% (5 patients) had microbiologically confirmed sepsis based on positive culture results; the distinction refers solely to whether a causative organism was identified by culture (all patients were included in analyses).

Proteomic data distribution

Initial proteome assessment revealed the overall structure of the proteomic dataset (Fig. 1). Figure 1A and B show the correlation analysis between technical and biological covariates. Principal component analysis of normalized protein expression data showed that patients with sepsis exhibited a proteome that was separable and easily distinguished from healthy controls (Fig. 1, Supplemental Fig. 1). Pairwise association testing among clinical, demographic, laboratory, and treatment variables revealed that significant associations were largely confined to clinically expected relationships (for example, illness severity scores with one another). Treatment variables, including antibiotics, antivirals, and corticosteroids, did not show significant associations with inflammatory laboratory parameters, suggesting that treatment allocation was unlikely to be the primary driver of the observed proteomic structure. However, subtle or unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. Sepsis patients on ICU Day 1 and Day 3 showed overlap in proteomic signatures (Fig. 1C). Sample-to-sample correlation analysis with hierarchical clustering demonstrated healthy controls exhibited high inter-sample correlation, with sepsis patients displaying greater heterogeneity consistent with clinical variability during infection (Fig. 1D).

Differential protein expression analysis

To characterize the molecular alterations associated with sepsis, we conducted proteomic profiling comparing sepsis patients on Day 1 of ICU admission with healthy controls and examined temporal changes by analyzing the same sepsis patients on Day 3 post-admission. Differential expression analysis between sepsis Day 1 samples and healthy controls revealed extensive proteomic changes (Fig. 2A, B). We identified 247 significantly differentially expressed proteins, with 156 proteins upregulated and 91 proteins downregulated in sepsis patients. Volcano plot visualization demonstrated a clear separation between upregulated and downregulated proteins, with several proteins showing both high fold-changes and strong statistical significance (Fig. 2A). Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of significantly differentially expressed proteins revealed a complete separation between healthy controls and sepsis patients (Fig. 2B), with distinct expression patterns clearly delineating the two groups.

To assess the evolution of proteomic signatures during early sepsis progression, we compared protein expression between Day 1 and Day 3 post-ICU admission in the same patients. In contrast to the sepsis versus control comparison, we observed minimal differential protein changes between these two time points (Fig. 2C, D). Direct comparison between Day 1 and Day 3 (Table 4) revealed significant temporal changes in 5 proteins, all with modest fold changes. Most notably, ALDH3A1, CR2, CD200R1, and IL1RL2 showed a significantly reduced level on Day 3 compared to Day 1, while SAA4 had a higher level on Day 3 versus Day 1. Hierarchical clustering analysis confirmed this observation, showing overlapping expression patterns between the two time points and minimal temporal clustering (Fig. 2D). Correlation analysis of the five temporally dynamic proteins with clinical outcomes revealed that SAA4 was significantly inversely correlated with vasoactive medication use, and IL1RL2 was significantly correlated with SOFA Day 3 scores (Supplemental Fig. 2). The remaining proteins (ALDH3A1, CD200R1, CR2) did not show significant associations.

Pathway enrichment analysis

To identify biological pathways underlying the proteomic changes in sepsis, we performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) using the Reactome pathway database across three comparisons: ICU Day 1 sepsis vs. healthy controls (HC), ICU Day 3 sepsis vs. healthy controls and sepsis patients on ICU Day 1 vs. Day 3. Enrichment analysis identifies whether the proteins that change in a study cluster within specific biological pathways more often than expected by chance, allowing complex molecular data to be translated into clearer, mechanism-level insights.

The Day 1 ICU versus HC comparison showed the most extensive pathway enrichment (Fig. 3A), with immune-related pathways dominating the enrichment analysis. The immune system pathway exhibited the strongest enrichment, followed by neutrophil degranulation, signaling by interleukins, and cytokine signaling in immune system (Table 5). Additional significantly enriched pathways included innate immune system, cellular responses to stress, cellular responses to stimuli, and interleukin-4 and interleukin-13 signaling. Generic transcription pathway and RNA Polymerase II transcription were also enriched. L1CAM interactions showed negative enrichment. The enrichment bubble plot for Day 1 ICU versus HC visualized these findings, with the immune system pathway representing the largest gene set among significantly enriched pathways (Fig. 3B). The Day 3 ICU versus HC comparison revealed that immune and inflammatory pathways, including immune system activation, cytokine signaling, interleukin signaling, and innate immune responses, remained significantly enriched on ICU Day 3 (Fig. 3A). In contrast, the ICU Day 1 versus Day 3 comparison showed minimal pathway-level changes (Fig. 3C; Table 6). Notably, only three transcription-related pathways reached significance: gene expression/transcription, generic transcription pathway, and RNA Polymerase II transcription, all displaying negative enrichment scores. This negative enrichment suggests that transcriptional proteins are downregulated on ICU Day 3 relative to Day 1, possibly suggesting a decline in transcriptional activity as patients progress from the acute hyperinflammatory stage toward recovery (however this requires functional validation).

Network analysis

STRING protein-protein interaction network analysis of the top differentially expressed proteins (with at least a 4-fold difference) between ICU Day 1 sepsis patients and healthy controls identified a highly interconnected network with distinct functional clusters (Fig. 5A). K-means clustering revealed four major protein groups with different biological functions: a cluster enriched for IL-10 signaling and inflammatory cytokines (red cluster), a cluster representing neutrophil-mediated cytotoxicity proteins (green cluster), a cluster containing carboxypeptidase activation and pancreatitis-related proteins (yellow cluster), and a cluster comprising cardiac proteins including NPPB and TNNI3 (blue cluster). The network displayed strong connectivity patterns, with several central hub proteins, including IL6, IL10, and CXCL8, showing extensive interactions with multiple network partners, suggesting critical regulatory roles in orchestrating the sepsis inflammatory response.

Disease-gene association enrichment analysis of network proteins revealed significant overrepresentation of respiratory diseases, including bronchial disease, lower respiratory tract disease, adult respiratory distress syndrome, COVID-19, and asthma (Fig. 5B). Cellular deconvolution

To estimate the proportions of immune cell types contributing to the bulk proteomics samples, we performed cellular deconvolution using CIBERSORTx (Steen et al. 2020) with the LM22 signature matrix (Newman et al. 2015). Analysis of the estimated cell type proportions revealed that samples were dominated by a limited subset of immune cell types (Fig. 7). CD8 and CD4 T-cells represented the most prevalent cell types across most samples, with substantial proportions also attributed to B-cells memory, regulatory T-cells (Tregs), and follicular helper T-cells. Most other cell types, including neutrophils, monocytes, and dendritic cell subsets, comprised relatively minor fractions of the estimated cellular composition. Statistical comparison between healthy controls and sepsis patients revealed that only resting dendritic cells differed significantly, with sepsis patients showing reduced proportions of resting dendritic cells (Fig. 7). No other cell types showed significant differences between conditions or across time points (Day 1 vs. Day 3) after correction for multiple testing.

Discussion

We conducted an exploratory, hypothesis-generating, pathway-focused proteomic study of critically-ill patients with sepsis. We used high-dimensional proximity extension assays (PEA) to profile the plasma proteome (1196 proteins), making this amongst the largest PEA-based sepsis proteomics studies. On ICU Day 1, plasma proteins showed significantly altered expression in sepsis patients compared to healthy controls, consistent with widespread immune and inflammatory pathway enrichment. Proteomic analysis revealed increased cytokines, acute phase reactants, and neutrophil-associated proteins, with network analysis highlighting immune response clusters centered on key inflammatory mediators dysregulated in sepsis patients. Between ICU Day 1 and Day 3, only five proteins (ALDH3A1, CR2, CD200R1, IL1RL2, SAA4) demonstrated temporal changes. Pathway analyses revealed extensive positive enrichment of immune and inflammatory pathways, with concurrent negative enrichment of transcriptional pathways on ICU Day 3 compared to Day 1. These signatures mirror the inflammatory programs underlying high-mortality transcriptomic endotypes reported in prior studies, demonstrating that circulating proteins similarly reflect the same biological pathways identified through leukocyte mRNA profiling. Overall, these hypothesis-generating results highlight that sepsis must be interpreted through a systems-level lens, as coordinated pathway disturbances, not single analytes, drive sepsis pathobiology.

Notably, the demographic and clinical characteristics of our sepsis cohort are consistent with contemporary cohorts (Kuye et al. 2021, Seymour et al. 2019, Morrissey et al. 2025, Martin et al. 2003). Although the cohort size was limited, this exploratory study is consistent with prior PEA-based sepsis proteomics investigations, which frequently employ small-to-moderate cohorts, typically ranging from ~ 20–50 patients and often using smaller targeted protein panels (Nynatten et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2023, Palma Medina et al. 2023, D’Onofrio et al. 2022, Ricaño-Ponce et al. 2022, Sguazzini et al. 2026). Notably, our study profiled 1,196 proteins: one of the largest PEA panels applied in sepsis to date. However, cohort mortality is lower than expected given the disease severity of the sepsis patients in our cohort (Goulden et al. 2018). The predominance of pneumonia as the infection source reflects the well-established epidemiology of sepsis, where respiratory infections account for approximately 50–60% of cases in most ICU cohorts (Martin et al. 2003, Vincent et al. 2009). The high rate of mechanical ventilation (100% on Day 1) and vasopressor requirements (73.3% on Day 1) further confirms the severity of illness in this population. The significant improvement in SOFA scores, lactate levels, and vasopressor requirements between Day 1 and Day 3 suggests appropriate response to therapy and clinical trajectory typical of surviving sepsis patients (Singer et al. 2016).

The plasma proteins differentially expressed in this study underscore the multifaceted nature of sepsis, involving inflammatory activation, dysregulated immunity, endothelial injury, and metabolic change. The top five upregulated proteins were IL-6, CHI3L1, OSM, PTX3 and IL-10. IL-6, the most markedly increased protein, signals through the JAK/STAT3 pathway and acts as both a key driver of inflammation and a major regulator of the acute phase response (Mihara et al. 2012). Prior work has evaluated IL-6 as a prognostic biomarker in sepsis (Damas et al. 1992, Hou et al. 2015, Remick et al. 2002), with higher concentrations associated with greater disease severity, organ failure, and mortality. Its sustained elevation through Day 3 in our cohort suggests ongoing inflammatory activity.

IL-10, also elevated in our cohort, is a principal anti-inflammatory cytokine in sepsis, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine production, reducing MHC class II and co-stimulatory molecule expression on antigen-presenting cells, and limiting T-cell activation (Saraiva and O’garra 2010, Iyer and Cheng 2012, Gogos et al. 2000). Although initially protective by curbing excessive inflammation and tissue damage, persistently high IL-10 can cause profound immunosuppression, characterized by monocyte deactivation, reduced HLA-DR expression, and increased susceptibility to secondary infections. Paradoxically, higher IL-10 levels have also been linked to increased mortality (Poll et al. 1996, Ward et al. 2008, Latifi et al. 2002, Kawai et al. 1998). The simultaneous elevation of pro-inflammatory mediators (IL-6, OSM, PTX3) and the anti-inflammatory mediator IL-10 in this cohort illustrates the complex immune dysregulation of sepsis and supports a model in which excessive inflammation and immunosuppression coexist rather than occur in distinct SIRS (systemic inflammatory response syndrome) and CARS (compensatory anti-inflammatory response syndrome) phases.

Despite significant clinical improvement between ICU Day 1 and Day 3, including reduced organ dysfunction scores, decreased vasopressor requirements, and hemodynamic stabilization, the plasma proteome remained relatively stable, with only five proteins (ALDH3A1, CR2, CD200R1, IL1RL2, SAA4) showing significant temporal changes. Given this proteome stability, our data questions whether patients who appear clinically “recovered” or “improving” may remain in a state of profound molecular inflammation, with persistent elevation of cytokines, acute phase reactants, and immune activation markers. Whether this apparent molecular-clinical dissociation reflects genuine biological persistence of the proteomic response or is an artifact of limited statistical power for within-subject comparisons remains to be determined in larger cohorts.

While individual protein changes provide important biological clues, they offer limited insight into the coordinated processes that define sepsis pathophysiology. Pathway-level analysis offers a distinct advantage over individual protein comparisons. Coordinated changes across functionally related proteins can reveal active biological processes even when absolute protein abundances remain stable, as pathway activity depends on relative expression patterns rather than individual concentrations. For example, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed profound pathway-level perturbations that extend beyond individual protein changes, including neutrophil degranulation, cytokine-mediated signaling and interleukin signaling pathways.

Our pathway-level findings demonstrate substantial concordance with established transcriptomic endotyping studies in sepsis. Leukocyte transcriptomic studies have identified molecular endotypes with prognostic and therapeutic relevance, including the SRS1/SRS2 (Davenport et al. 2016) signatures characterized by immunosuppression and T-cell exhaustion versus immunocompetence, and the MARS1-4 endotypes distinguished by differential activation of innate immunity, cytokine signaling, and adaptive immune pathways (Scicluna et al. 2017). These frameworks were recently harmonized into three consensus transcriptomic subtypes (CTS), with CTS1 defined by inflammatory pathway activation, endothelial dysfunction, and immature neutrophil gene signatures (Scicluna et al. 2025). Our proteomic enrichment of Reactome pathways including neutrophil degranulation, cytokine signaling in immune system, signaling by interleukins, and innate immune system, mirrors the inflammatory signatures defining SRS1/CTS1 endotypes (Baghela et al. 2022, Sweeney et al. 2018). Critically, high-dimensional plasma proteomics with paired transcriptomics has demonstrated that proteins differentially abundant between SRS groups enrich for neutrophil degranulation and innate immunity pathways (Mi et al. 2024), and that proteomic subphenotypes align with transcriptomic endotypes at the patient level (Mi et al. 2024). This proteome-transcriptome concordance at the pathway level suggests that molecular programs detected by mRNA profiling translate to measurable changes in circulating proteins, supporting plasma proteomics as a bridge between mechanistic transcriptomic discoveries and clinically accessible biomarkers.

Importantly, GSEA pathway analysis revealed evidence of transcriptional dysregulation during early sepsis in our cohort. Negative enrichment of generic transcription pathways and RNA Polymerase II transcription on ICU Day 3 compared with Day 1 suggests relative downregulation of transcription-related proteins over this interval. This finding aligns with emerging evidence that sepsis induces widespread epigenetic modifications and transcriptional reprogramming in immune cells (Carson et al. 2011, Binnie et al. 2020). Moreover, our findings of negative transcriptional enrichment in sepsis are consistent with findings of transcriptional dysregulation that have been observed in the context of lipopolysaccharide administration in mice models of sepsis (Rosier et al. 2022), resulting in upregulation of a variety of inflammatory pathways. The negative enrichment of transcription-related pathways also represents a proteomic correlate of the transcriptional shutdown described in leukocyte profiling studies, whereby 170 co-differentially expressed genes and proteins in sepsis, with modules related to pre-mRNA processing, splicing, and ribosomal biogenesis demonstrated coordinated downregulation (Leite et al. 2021). Transcriptomic studies have consistently reported suppression of translation initiation, ribosome biogenesis, and mRNA catabolism pathways in sepsis, interpreted as metabolic reprogramming prioritizing inflammatory responses over cellular homeostasis (Baghela et al. 2022, Calvano et al. 2005). Our finding that transcription-related proteins decline at the pathway level while inflammatory pathways remain elevated extends these observations to the circulating proteome.

Additionally, if these molecular pathways are biologically meaningful, they should manifest in measurable clinical parameters. Clinical variable-pathway enrichment analysis illustrated that dysregulated pathways correspond to biologically meaningful host variation, supporting that these findings represent true biological signal rather than technical artifact. Strong correlations between clinical inflammatory markers (neutrophil and WBC counts) and enriched immune and inflammatory pathways are consistent with the expected relationship between circulating leukocytes and systemic inflammation in sepsis. Inverse associations between platelet counts and inflammatory pathways likely reflect consumptive coagulopathy accompanying immune activation, in line with known coagulation–inflammation crosstalk. Likewise, cellular deconvolution of proteome data revealed a disconnect between pathway activity and cell composition. Despite extensive pathway dysregulation in sepsis, immune cell proportions were largely unchanged, with significant differences observed only in resting dendritic cells. This disconnect between pathway activation and cell abundance may suggest that sepsis is driven less by shifts in immune cell populations and more by functional reprogramming within existing cells, although the CIBERSORTx results should be interpreted with caution given that this represents a non-standard application using proteomic rather than transcriptomic input, with limited gene overlap (121 of 547 LM22 genes, 22%) and cross-protein non-comparability of NPX values. These deconvolution findings are therefore hypothesis-generating and qualitative; flow cytometric validation is recommended in future studies. This interpretation aligns with established concepts of immune dysfunction in sepsis, including T cell exhaustion, monocyte deactivation, and neutrophil paralysis, occurring within relatively stable cell populations (Cao et al. 2023).

These pathway-level findings have important implications for understanding sepsis biology and therapeutic development. First, the coordinated enrichment of multiple immune pathways suggests that single-target immunomodulatory therapies may be insufficient, which could explain the failure of numerous anti-cytokine trials in sepsis (Marshall et al. 1995, Cohen et al. 2015). Indeed, the recent ImmunoSep trial demonstrated that biomarker-guided stratification using ferritin and monocyte HLA-DR can identify biologically distinct sepsis endotypes and that targeted immunotherapy restores immune function and improves organ dysfunction, establishing proof-of-concept for precision immunomodulation in sepsis (Giamarellos-Bourboulis et al. 2025). However, the ImmunoSep classifications are derived from isolated surrogate markers that approximate broad immune phenotypes rather than directly targeting specific dysregulated pathways; while the trial demonstrated improvement in organ dysfunction, it did not significantly reduce mortality, underscoring both the promise and current limitations of surrogate marker-based precision strategies. Our study builds upon this paradigm by proposing that pathway-level proteomic profiling may allow a more comprehensive and mechanistically grounded assessment of immune and metabolic programs, potentially enabling finer stratification and more precise therapeutic alignment. Second, the evidence for negative transcriptional enrichment during acute sepsis raises the possibility that interventions targeting epigenetic regulation or transcriptional recovery could facilitate immune restoration. Third, the persistence of inflammatory signatures despite clinical improvement raises the possibility that resolution of the sepsis proteome is a prolonged process that extends well beyond initial hemodynamic stabilization, with potential implications for long-term immunological dysfunction and susceptibility to secondary infections (Boomer et al. 2011, Otto et al. 2011).

Prior PEA-based sepsis proteomics studies have predominantly employed 92-protein targeted inflammation panels, identifying proteins associated with severity and outcomes but constrained to pre-selected inflammatory targets (Palma Medina et al. 2023, D’Onofrio et al. 2022, Ricaño-Ponce et al. 2022). While these studies confirm the central role of cytokine signaling in sepsis, the targeted nature of such panels limits pathway-level inference. Meanwhile, recent large-scale mass spectrometry studies have substantially advanced the field at the population level: Mi et al. profiled over 1,600 patients using LC-MS/MS and identified mechanistic endotypes (Mi et al. 2024), while Palmowski et al. examined mortality-associated proteome dynamics across 363 patients from five centres (Palmowski et al. 2025). Importantly, these studies have linked proteomic profiles directly to mortality. Mi et al. identified three plasma proteome-based clusters with significantly different 28-day and 6-month survival, where SPC1 (enriched for complement activation, Fc-γ receptor signaling, and neutrophil degranulation) carried the highest mortality (Mi et al. 2024). Palmowski et al. identified 111 proteins differentially abundant between survivors and non-survivors across days 1 and 4, predominantly mapping to coagulation, complement, and tissue damage, with random forest classifiers achieving reasonable short-term survival prediction (Palmowski et al. 2025). Our cohort, was underpowered for mortality analysis; however, the pathway-level dysregulation we observe in inflammatory cascades aligns with the mortality-associated processes identified in these larger studies, supporting the biological plausibility of our findings while underscoring the need for validation in adequately powered cohorts.

Notably, MS-based profiling is inherently biased toward high-abundance plasma proteins (coagulation factors, complement components, and acute phase reactants) while systematically under-detecting the low-abundance signaling molecules (cytokines, chemokines, growth factors) that coordinate the immune response. Our 1,196-protein PEA panel bridges these approaches, combining the sensitivity of PEA for low-abundance signaling proteins with sufficient breadth to support formal pathway enrichment analysis across the full Reactome and GO databases (an analysis not feasible with 92-protein targeted panels and not accessible to the same signaling proteome via MS). Our panel extends existing observations in several dimensions. First, comprehensive Reactome enrichment reveals pathway-level coordination, including neutrophil degranulation, cytokine signaling, and innate immune system dysregulation that parallels individual protein findings from targeted panels but captures broader biological programs. Second, STRING network analysis identifies hub architecture centered on IL-6, IL-10, and CXCL8, proteins consistently implicated across 92-protein studies, but reveals their connectivity within a larger interactome. Clustering analysis revealed four functional modules: IL-10 signaling and inflammatory cytokines, neutrophil-mediated cytotoxicity, carboxypeptidase activation and pancreatitis-related proteins, and cardiac biomarkers including NPPB and TNNI3. This network-level organization has important therapeutic implications, as redundancy and interconnectedness may explain why single-target therapies have failed. Third, temporal pathway analysis demonstrates negative enrichment of transcriptional programs between Day 1 and Day 3, a proteomic correlate of the transcriptional shutdown described in leukocyte profiling (Leite et al. 2021), findings inaccessible to single-timepoint, pre-selected inflammation panels. Fourth, disease-gene association analysis links differentially expressed proteins to ARDS, vascular disease, and multi-organ dysfunction phenotypes, providing clinical relevance beyond isolated biomarker discovery.

Certainly, the results of our study must be interpreted in the context of several important limitations. Our sample size was limited, which reduced the statistical power and generalizability of our findings, thereby precluding subgroup analyses or the identification of distinct sepsis endotypes. The observational design prevents causal inferences about the relationships between proteomic changes and clinical outcomes. We did not correlate protein levels with mortality or specific organ dysfunction trajectories, which limits our ability to assess the prognostic utility of identified biomarkers. The proteome was assessed only at two early timepoints (Day 1 and Day 3); extended sampling would better characterize the temporal evolution of inflammatory signatures during sepsis resolution. Our analysis measured circulating plasma proteins, which may not reflect tissue-level molecular changes or intracellular processes that drive organ dysfunction. The cohort exhibited lower mortality than expected for the observed disease severity, potentially limiting applicability to those with more severe sepsis phenotypes. Moreover, while pathway enrichment analyses provide biological context, they rely on existing annotations that may incompletely represent sepsis-specific biology, and the predominance of pneumonia as the infection source may limit generalizability to sepsis from other etiologies. The cohort included both suspected (culture-negative) and microbiologically confirmed sepsis cases; while all patients met Sepsis-3 clinical criteria, mixing these subgroups introduces additional heterogeneity, and the lower-than-expected mortality may partly reflect the inclusion of culture-negative patients with less severe infections. Formal comparison of suspected versus confirmed sepsis subgroups is an important priority for future studies with larger sample sizes. Finally, because our workflow applies multiple layers of analysis, including differential expression, GSEA, and STRING enrichment, there is an increased risk of false discoveries; however, the consistent convergence on immune and inflammatory pathways across independent analytic frameworks is reassuring, though not definitive.

Conclusions

In this exploratory analysis, we conducted large-scale plasma proteomic profiling in critically-ill patients with sepsis using a 1,196-protein proximity extension assay to define pathway-level biological disturbances early in the disease course. Sepsis was linked to extensive disruption of immune and inflammatory pathways, including neutrophil activation, cytokine signaling, and innate immune responses, with convergent findings across differential expression, enrichment, and network-based approaches. Although patients showed marked clinical improvement between ICU Day 1 and Day 3, the circulating proteome changed very little. Pathway analyses also indicated a relative negative enrichment of transcriptional programs over time, while protein–protein interaction networks revealed tightly connected inflammatory hubs organized around canonical immune mediators. Collectively, these findings indicate that early sepsis involves persistent, coordinated pathway-level dysregulation that is not reflected by standard clinical assessments or single biomarkers. By employing one of the most extensive targeted plasma proteomic panels used in sepsis to date, this exploratory study underscores the utility of high-dimensional, pathway-focused analyses for characterizing the biological milieu of critical illness and lays the groundwork for future investigations into how these molecular signatures relate to disease course, treatment response, and patient stratification. Validation in larger, prospective cohorts with adequate mortality events and longer follow-up will be essential to confirm these exploratory observations.

Study on the influence of stress relief in close-distance coal seam Goaf and roadway excavation disturbance on surrounding rock
Authors: Zenghui Liu, Minjun Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the influence of pressure relief in the close-distance coal seam goaf and roadway excavation disturbance on surrounding rock stability, this study focuses on the large-section open-off cut of Ⅱ8224 N. Methods employed include RQD rock mass quality evaluation, FLAC3D numerical simulation, similar material simulation experiments, and field borehole camera observation. The results show the following: The RQD evaluation indicates that the immediate roof siltstone (thickness: 3.8 m) and the fine sandstone in the lower part of the main roof are classified as medium-quality Grade III rock mass, while the fine sandstone in the upper part of the main roof and the mudstone at the floor of No. 73 coal seam are extremely poor Grade V rock mass. The goaf of No. 73 coal seam exerts a significant pressure relief effect on the surrounding rock of the Ⅱ8224 open-off cut, creating a pressure relief zone with a range of 2.5–5.0 MPa. A 3 m-wide non-plastic failure zone exists between the goaf floor and the open-off cut roof, with no plastic failure occurring within the 7.4 m anchorage range of the cable bolts. The roof rock bolt zone is a tensile stress zone, while the cable bolt zone experiences compressive stress. After excavation of the open-off cut, a new surrounding rock bearing layer forms between the open-off cut and the goaf. Stepwise excavation induces secondary stress disturbance in the roof surrounding rock, and after roadway formation, stress continues to increase, with the most concentrated tensile stress occurring in the middle part of the roadway. The similar material simulation experiment shows that the roof of No. 73 coal seam fully collapses when the load exceeds 6.60 kN, with caving angles of 65 ° and 60° respectively. The stress in the goaf floor shifts to tensile stress after 300 min and stabilizes after 1200 min. Field borehole camera observation reveals that the overall integrity of the roof surrounding rock is good, with only a small number of circumferential fractures. These findings provide a theoretical basis and technical support for surrounding rock control in large-section open-off cuts under close-distance goaf conditions.

Introduction

In China, the extraction of certain coal resources has progressed to significant depths, where coal–rock masses are subjected to a true triaxial stress state. Roadway excavation creates a unidirectional free surface in the surrounding rock, inducing stress unloading and consequent stress concentration. When the induced stress exceeds the bearing capacity of the coal/rock mass, plastic yielding and crack propagation may occur. Without timely and adequate support, the surrounding rock can undergo progressive deformation and failure, ultimately leading to instability1. In deep and complex geological environments, uncertainty in surrounding-rock structures and discontinuities makes advanced geological detection and monitoring increasingly important for safe excavation. In addition, the mechanical performance of sand-powder 3D-printed rock analogs can be improved through post-processing or hybrid strengthening approaches, enabling more reliable physical simulations of fractured rocks2,3.

Domestic and international researchers have conducted extensive studies on the deformation and failure of surrounding rock in deep tunnels. Gao et al. investigated how true triaxial loading and unloading rates influence the mechanical properties of deep rock, revealing strength degradation and a transition from shear-dominated to tensile-dominated failure with changing stress paths4. Chang et al. integrated miniature cameras with acoustic emission monitoring to capture macroscopic failure processes and micro-damage signals5. Jing et al. analysed deformation and failure mechanisms of surrounding rock in deep soft-rock tunnels considering rheology and different strength criteria6. Shen et al. combined physical model tests with numerical analysis to investigate deformation and failure mechanisms of deep-buried tunnel surrounding rock, providing insights into asymmetric deformation and support performance7. In addition, true triaxial unloading tests have been used to examine deformation behaviour and failure mechanisms under unloading-dominated stress paths, showing combined shear, tensile, and splitting failures8. Cui et al. further explored rock-mass failure induced by unloading near an excavation face under true triaxial conditions, highlighting dilatancy and complex fracture evolution9. Guo et al. used numerical simulations to examine plastic-zone expansion in rectangular roadways with different aspect ratios under non-uniform stress fields10. Li et al. proposed a stepwise combined support scheme based on the large-deformation evolution process and established a theoretical analytical model11. Moreover, true triaxial tests with acoustic emission have been applied to clarify the failure mechanism of deep surrounding rock influenced by lithology and lateral constraints, providing quantitative insights into failure evolution12. Recent studies further indicate that internal fracture networks and dominant fracture orientations can significantly alter strength, deformation, and failure modes under triaxial stress states. Wang et al. proposed a mechanical method for fully grouted bolts considering bolt yielding and interface slip, while Sun et al. quantified the influence of key parameters in bolt/cable support systems on surrounding-rock behaviour13,14. Chen et al. investigated the progressive instability mechanism of special-shaped coal pillars and corresponding roadway stability control, provided an overview of ahead geological detection technologies in tunnels, and clarified the extension mechanism of directional hydraulic fractures under abutment stress based on XFEM, collectively offering complementary insights for instability assessment and control in mining roadways15–17.

In recent years, increasing attention has been paid to the mechanical response of surrounding rock under excavation-induced unloading conditions in deep underground engineering. Gu et al. studied the effect of initial stress differences on hard-rock fracture mechanisms under true triaxial excavation-unloading stress paths and found that unloading stress paths can significantly alter fracture development and failure characteristics. Li et al. and Hu et al. also reported that unloading stress paths differ from conventional loading paths, often leading to brittle failure, stress redistribution, and rapid crack propagation in coal and rock masses18–20. In particular, Li et al. and Yan et al. demonstrated through true triaxial unloading experiments that the presence of a free surface intensifies tensile–shear coupled failure and accelerates surrounding-rock instability21,22.

Moreover, roadway excavation under high in-situ stress often exhibits asymmetric deformation and failure characteristics, especially under single-sided unloading conditions adjacent to goafs or coal pillars. Wu et al. and Zhang et al. carried out relevant studies on this phenomenon23,24. Although numerical simulations and field monitoring have been widely applied to evaluate surrounding-rock deformation and support performance, experimental studies that integrate true triaxial unloading tests, cavity/excavation-disturbance simulation, and systematic support optimization remain limited. Gao et al., Wang et al., and Xu et al. also highlighted this research gap in their related studies25–27.

These studies have advanced understanding of rock-mass control mechanisms for single-sided unloading roadways through unloading mechanics experiments, deformation and failure analyses, and investigations of key control parameters28–30. However, the control mechanisms of surrounding rock in deep roadways still require further clarification. Therefore, this thesis employs a true triaxial rock mechanics testing system to conduct single-sided unloading and confining-pressure cavity experiments, combines numerical simulation to explore optimized surrounding-rock control, and links the findings with engineering practice, thereby providing references for rock-mass control in similar roadway types.

Project overview

Site conditions and roadway geometry

The large-section open-off cut of Ⅱ8224 N overlies five main (mineable) coal seams, namely No.31, No.51, No.52, No.72, and No.73. Among these, the No.72 and No.73 coal seams have been mined out. The spacing between the large-section open-off cut of Ⅱ8224 N and the goaf of the No.73 coal seam ranges from 12.0 to 18.0 m, and their spatial relationship is illustrated in Fig. 1. The total length of the open-off cut for the Ⅱ8224 N working face is 245 m, including a 225 m-long normal section with dimensions of B×H = 7.6 × 3.0 m (breadth × height); its width is approximately twice that of a conventional roadway.

The No.82 coal seam in this working face has a thickness of 1.6–2.8 m (average: 2.2 m), with relatively stable occurrence and a simple structure. The main roof of the No.82 coal seam is medium sandstone (light gray to off-white), with an average thickness of 6.6 m and an unconfined compressive strength (UCS) of 57.8–60.8 MPa. The immediate roof is siltstone, with an average thickness of 2.1 m and a UCS of 32.8–106.3 MPa. The immediate floor is mudstone (gray to dark gray), interbedded with a 0.5 m-thick No.83 coal seam, with an average thickness of 6.1 m and a UCS of 43.7–52.5 MPa. The main floor is medium sandstone, with an average thickness of 5.3 m and a UCS of 32.2–59.4 MPa. Figure 1 presents the longitudinal geological profile of the Ⅱ8224 N open-off cut.

Rock mass quality evaluation

Where:

l——Length of a single core segment (equal to or greater than 10 cm);

L——Length of the drill hole within the same rock stratum.

Based on the on-site drilled core conditions (see Table 1), the core length of the immediate roof siltstone, main roof fine sandstone, and mudstone in the Ⅱ8224 open-off cut is 3.8 m, 6.7 m, and 1.0 m, respectively. The engineering rock mass classification, derived from calculating the total length of single core segments ≥ 10 cm and the RQD of each rock stratum, is presented in Table 1. Mining of the Ⅱ7322 working face has affected the floor rock stratum within the range of 4–6 m, significantly impacting the quality of drilled cores obtained from the rock stratum adjacent to the goaf of the Ⅱ7322 working face.

Numerical simulation

Calculation model

A numerical calculation model was established based on the spatial relationship between the goaf formed by mining the No.73 coal seam and the large-section open-off cut of Ⅱ8224, as shown in Fig. 3. The No.73 goaf is located directly above the Ⅱ8224 open-off cut, with a vertical interlayer distance of approximately 12.0–18.0 m between the goaf floor and the open-off cut roof, such that the open-off cut lies within the stress-relief influence zone of the overlying goaf. Appropriate simplifications were made to the actual geological conditions during the simulation process. The simulation sequence was as follows: first, the mining of the No.73 coal seam, followed by the excavation of the Ⅱ8224 open-off cut. The Mohr-Coulomb yield criterion was adopted in the model to evaluate rock mass failure, and plastic flow was not considered. As shown in Fig. 3, this is the numerical calculation model, where the goaf of the No.73 coal seam and the roadway are depicted as marked in the diagram.

Fig. 3numerical calculation model.

numerical calculation model.

The modeling process was carried out as follows: First, the model was constructed, and a load was applied to the upper part of the model to replace the unmodeled overlying rock strata. After calculating until stress equilibrium was achieved for all elements, No.73 coal was mined and the calculation was continued until stress equilibrium (i.e., the stress distribution characteristics of the lateral coal-rock mass in the upper goaf). Subsequently, the Ⅱ8224 open-off cut was excavated, the support structure was installed, and the calculation was further performed until stress equilibrium was reached for all elements. Finally, the calculation results were processed.

Model geometry, strata layout and boundary conditions

To control variables and ensure that the model scale meets the requirements for experimental observation, a similar-material test frame with dimensions of 2000 mm × 2000 mm × 2000 mm was adopted (with an effective simulated height of approximately 1920 mm).

The strata in the model were arranged according to the combined spatial relationship between the Ⅱ8224 N open-off cut and the overlying close-distance coal seams. In the prototype, the thicknesses of the No.82 coal seam and the mudstone floor are 2.2 m and 6.1 m, respectively, corresponding to an equivalent thickness of 30.5 cm in the model. From bottom to top, the roof strata include siltstone, fine sandstone, mudstone, No.73 coal seam, siltstone, No.72 coal seam, mudstone, and siltstone, and the thickness of each layer in the model was determined by converting the prototype thickness according to the similarity ratio (e.g., siltstone 4.5 m → 11 cm; fine sandstone 5.5 m → 22.5 cm; No.73 coal seam 2.3 m → 11 cm; siltstone 9.0 m → 45 cm; No.72 coal seam 1.5 m → 7.5 cm, etc.). Caving and movement range of overlying rock in No.73 coal seam roof

Figure 10a–c show the excavation process. This similar material simulation experiment aims to study the surrounding rock stability during the excavation of a large-section open-off cut. The No.73 coal seam lies above the large-section open-off cut, and the mining of this working face will affect the state of the roof rock strata in the open-off cut. During the experiment, except for setting reserved boundaries, the No.73 coal seam was excavated in a single operation to simulate the goaf formed after the mining of the No.73 working face above the open-off cut.

At the initial stage of the similar material simulation experiment, no load was applied to the upper part of the model. The roof caving of the No.73 coal seam occurred under the action of material self-weight, and a fixed beam structure was formed on the roof of the No.73 coal seam. After the open-off cut excavation, under the condition that the original experimental parameters of the open-off cut simulation remained unchanged, loads were applied to the upper part of the model with loading levels of 1.65 kN, 3.30 kN, 4.95 kN, 6.60 kN, 8.25 kN, 9.90 kN, and 11.55 kN respectively. With the increase of loading level, the roof of the No.73 coal seam exhibited varying degrees of deformation, caving and failure.

Under different load levels, the roof caving of the No.73 coal seam had corresponding impacts on the stress, deformation and failure of the surrounding rock of the open-off cut. According to the time ratio and deformation process similarity designed in the similar material experiment, the load was transmitted from the roof caving area of the No.73 coal seam to the roof rock strata of the open-off cut. Before the load reached 6.60 kN, the roof of the No.73 coal seam evolved from a fixed beam to a hinged rock beam and then to a masonry rock beam. When the load exceeded 6.60 kN, the caving range of the No.73 coal seam roof was fully developed, with the caving angles on the left and right sides of the No.73 coal seam roof being 65 ° and 60° respectively (see Fig. 11).

The impact of mining at the No. 73 working face on the stress of the open-off cut roof surrounding rock

Two layers of pressure cells (5 cells per layer) were embedded at 45 cm and 65 cm in the floor of the No.73 coal seam, corresponding to the cable and bolt anchorage ranges of the open-off cut, to monitor the influence of No.73 coal seam mining on the floor rock strata.

Figure 12 shows the variation of surrounding rock stress in the floor caused by No.73 coal seam mining(Table 3). After the mining of No.73 coal seam, a structure is formed on the roof, and a stress reduction zone is formed in the floor rock strata under the goaf—the closer to the No.73 coal seam, the greater the stress reduction. Within 300 min after the excavation of the No.73 coal seam, the surrounding rock stress in the floor continuously adjusts, with compressive stress and tensile stress alternately appearing. Deformation and stress characteristics of the overlying strata of the open-off cut roof

To study the stress variation of the surrounding rock in the open-off cut roof after excavation, the stress monitoring data were initially processed. Fig. 13a shows the stress variation within the cable anchorage range of the open-off cut roof. After the excavation of the 180 mm left section of the open-off cut, the surrounding rock stress of the roof was monitored, with a maximum compressive stress of 0.62 kPa. With the extension of time, the surrounding rock stress of the roof continuously adjusted, and compressive stress and tensile stress alternately appeared within 252 min after the excavation of the 180 mm left section. As time increased, the overall trend of tensile stress increased accordingly; 1300 min after the excavation of the 180 mm left section, the tensile stress of the surrounding rock in the open-off cut roof tended to be relatively stable. After 1588 min of excavating the left section, the 180 mm right section of the open-off cut was excavated, causing an increase in the tensile stress of the surrounding rock in the open-off cut roof with a maximum stress of 2.56 kPa. The total excavation width of the open-off cut was 360 mm.

Figure 13b presents the stress variation of the surrounding rock within the bolt anchorage range of the open-off cut roof. The stress variation trends of the 5 measuring points at the bolt position were consistent, with tensile stress increasing slowly. Measuring Points 1 and 5 were arranged outside the open-off cut range, showing relatively low tensile stress, while Measuring Points 2, 3, and 4 were arranged directly above the open-off cut, with relatively high tensile stress. The stress monitoring data at the bolt position indicate that after the excavation of the 180 mm left section, the tensile stress at Measuring Points 1 and 2 was greater than that at other points. When the 180 mm right section was excavated, the stress monitoring data at Measuring Points 3 and 4 of the bolt position increased rapidly, and the stress increase amplitude at Measuring Point 3 was greater than that at Measuring Point 4. When the excavation width of the open-off cut reached the designed 360 mm, the stress increase rate at Measuring Points 2, 3, and 4 was higher than that at Measuring Points 1 and 5. Therefore, the stepwise excavation of the large-section open-off cut will cause secondary disturbance to the surrounding rock stress of the roof, and the surrounding rock stress of the roof will continue to increase after the open-off cut is formed into a roadway.

Results

Based on experimental research, numerical simulation, and on-site monitoring methods, the influence of pressure relief in close-distance coal seam goafs and roadway excavation disturbance on surrounding rock was studied. The main conclusions are as follows:

According to the RQD rock mass quality evaluation: the 3.8 m - thick siltstone of the immediate roof is classified as medium grade Ⅲ; the fine sandstone of the main roof is also medium grade Ⅲ, while the rock mass 2.0 m away from the floor of the No.73 coal seam is classified as very poor grade Ⅴ.Regarding the stress distribution of the rock strata between the goaf and the open-off cut: the goaf exerts an obvious stress relief effect on the surrounding rock of the open-off cut, and the open-off cut is located within the pressure relief zone; no plastic failure occurs in the 3 m-thick rock layer between the goaf floor and the open-off cut roof, nor within the 7.4 m cable anchorage range. The bolt anchorage zone on the roof lies within the tensile stress zone, while the cable anchorage range is situated in the compressive stress zone of the surrounding rock. After the open-off cut excavation, a new surrounding rock structure is formed in the rock strata between the open-off cut and the No.73 coal seam goaf, which serves as the bearing layer for the open-off cut roof; borehole peep results indicate that the surrounding rock of the roof maintains good integrity.

Determining the perceived risk of lung cancer and chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases (COPD) among hookah users in Iran
Authors: Abdolhalim Rajabi, Abdurraouf Cheperli, Ali Shahryari, Abdurrahman Charkazi, Fatemeh Zarghami
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHookah consumption is harmful to human health and can cause various diseases. Developing lung cancer and other lung diseases are one of the health consequences of hookah consumption. Measuring the perceived risk for being diagnosed with these conditions among hookah users is necessary. Therefore, this study was conducted to determine the perceived risk of lung cancer and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) in hookah users in Iran.MethodsThis cross-sectional study was conducted with a descriptive-analytical approach on 340 hookah users between January-May 2023. These people were recruited through convenience sampling. The information on the perceived risk of participants was collected through a questionnaire on the perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD. Data were analyzed through descriptive tests and multiple linear regression tests. These analyses were performed using Stata statistical software version 17.ResultsThe mean and standard deviation of the perceived risk for lung cancer was 8.41 ± 5.50 and for COPD was 8.51 ± 5.58 out of 20 possible scores. The results of multiple linear regression generally showed that the using other type of tobacco (β = 3.14, p < 0.0001), 2 or more use of hookah in past month (β=-3.41, p = 0.025), unskilled workers (β = 1.79, p = 0.017), married smokers (β=-1.31, p = 0.026), negative attitude toward hookah (β = 0.46, p < 0.0001) were the predictive variables regarding to lung cancer perceived risk. In line with COPD the using other type of tobacco (β = 3.47, p < 0.0001), once a month use of hookah in past month(β=-3.74, p = 0.041) and twice or more use of hookah in past month (β=-4.82, p = 0.008), smokers in contemplation stage (β = 3.91, p < 0.0001), female smokers (β = 3.08, p < 0.0001), unskilled workers (β = 2.42, p = 0.007), negative attitude toward hookah (β = 0.46, p < 0.0001) were the predictive variables to COPD perceived risk.ConclusionIn general, the perceived risk of developing lung cancer and COPD among hookah users was low. Therefore, it is necessary to provide widespread information about the potential harm of hookah in causing lung cancer and COPD, at the community level.

Introduction

The result of the Global Burden of Disease Study estimated that in 2019, tobacco use was responsible for approximately 7.69 million deaths and 200 million years of life with disability worldwide [1]. A hookah is a form of tobacco in which tobacco is smoked with a water pipe, a single or multi-pronged device for smoking flavored or unflavored tobacco, designed to pass the smoke through water or other liquids. Today, it is believed that 100 million people around the world smoke hookah daily [2]. The results of a study conducted on adolescents aged 12–16 between 2010 and 2019 in 72 countries globally, showed that the prevalence of hookah use in the last month was 6.8% (95% CI 6.4–7.5). The highest rate of prevalence was among European Union countries with 10.9% (95% CI 9.9–11.8) and Eastern Mediterranean countries with 10.7% (9.5–11.9) [3].

Hookah smoking is a public health concern in Iran. The results of a national and sub national study in 2021 which included 27,874 Iranian adults aged ≥ 18 years in all provinces of Iran showed that all‑ages prevalence of current hookah smoking was 4.5% overall, 3.64% among women, and 5.56% among men [4]. The results of an updated systematic review and meta-analysis aimed hookah smoking prevalence among Iranian pupils and university students which including 124 studies conducted on 155 subjects indicated that the frequency of hookah smoking within the past month/week (point prevalence) was estimated at 21.5% and 16.6% in university students and pupils, respectively [5].

Health risks of hookah consumption

Hookah consumption has many health risks for users. For instance, carbon monoxide in hookah smoke causes hypoxia and disorders in the cells of the respiratory system [6]. The presence of polycyclic hydrocarbons, which are potentially carcinogenic, and other toxic substances such as heavy metals and tar in hookah smoke are all carcinogenic substances [7]. Also, the presence of these carcinogens in hookah smoke is significantly more than in cigarette smoke [8, 9]. Similar to traditional smoking, hookah consumption is associated with harmful effects on the respiratory and pulmonary systems, and hookah users show signs of wheezing, coughing, phlegm, and shortness of breath [10–12]. People who smoke hookah have a six times higher chance of developing lung cancer, than non-smoking people [13–16].

The result of a review study showed that there is a significant relationship between hookah consumption and lung cancer, mouth cancer, and premalignant lesions of oral [17, 18]. The results of several other review studies showed that hookah consumption significantly increases the chance of developing lung cancer [18–21]. The result of a review study conducted by Darawshy et al. also showed that regular hookah consumption is associated with respiratory diseases and reduced lung capacity [22]. The results of several other studies also confirmed that the use of hookah is associated with respiratory diseases, COPD, and other diseases and conditions related to health [20].

What is “perceived risk”?

Individual interpretations and other subjective judgments about risks are known as perceived risks [23]. Risk perception is an important determining factor in health and risk-related decisions, such as adopting healthy behaviors, avoiding unhealthy behaviors, and accepting or rejecting a certain level of risk [24]. Perceived risk is one of the important constructs in health behaviors, which refers to the mental understanding of the risk of certain behavior and plays an important role in changing attitudes and consequently adopting healthy behaviors or avoiding harmful behaviors such as smoking and hookah use [25]. The low-risk perception of hookah has significant effect on hookah smoking initiation. In their study, Kuk et al. found that youth who perceived hookah to be neither harmful nor addictive were 173% more likely to initiate ever, 166% more likely to first report past 30-day use, and 142% more likely to first report fairly regular hookah use at earlier ages compared to youth who considered hookah to be both harmful and addictive [26].

Considering that hookah consumption has been increased in Iran in the last two decades, it seems necessary to inform hookah users about the dangers of hookah consumption, such as risk of developing lung cancer and COPD. The comprehensive literature review showed that there aren’t any studies on hookah perceived risk on Lung cancer and COPD among Iranian population. Therefore, to fill this knowledge gap, this study was conducted with the aim of determining the perceived risk of developing lung cancer and COPD among hookah users.

Methods

Design and process

This cross-sectional study was conducted with a descriptive-analytical approach on 340 people between January-May 2023. Based on the results of a prior study [27], the sample size was determined using G power 3.1. With a two-tailed α of 0.05 and an anticipated error of 1.81, minimum of 384 patients were required. These participants were recruited in Golestan province in the north of Iran by convenience sampling and network method. The inclusion criteria were people over 18 years of age and those who use hookah at least once a month or have used it in the past [28]. People with COPD or lung cancer were not included in the study. These people were approached in cafes and entertainment centers where hookah is usually served. After introducing the study consumers were invited to enter the study by completing the study questionnaires. They were also asked to connect the researchers to their friends and acquaintances that use hookah that are likely to participate in the study. It was explained to the participants that these questionnaires are anonymous, and their personal information will be remained confidential. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Golestan University of Medical Sciences under the number IR.GOUMS.REC.1401.336.

Measures

The data were collected using 3 main questionnaires to assess: (1) risk perception, (2) stage of change, (3) attitude and beliefs about hookah use, (4) demographic information. The information about the risk perception of hookah consumption was collected by questionnaires that were designed by Wong and Cappella and Kotz et al. regarding lung cancer, and were used in Iran by Zarghami et al. among Iranian population [29, 30]. This questionnaire has two main categories regarding the (1) perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD, (2) the probability of surviving from these two diseases, which were modified and revised for use in hookah consumption. After modification of the questions, the questionnaire was reviewed and approved by 8 experts in the field of health education and health promotion, epidemiology, and environmental health.

The first category of questions on perceived risk is: “How likely are you to develop lung cancer and COPD disease if you continue to smoke hookah, compared to non-smokers of your age group in the future?” The answers to the above questions are designed based on the scores from zero to ten, which means that a score of zero indicates that participants think they will not suffer from any of these diseases in the future, and the number 10 indicates that they think there is a high probability that they will develop one of these diseases in the future. The second category of questions asked how likely they are to survive in the next 5 years if they are being diagnosed with any of these diseases. A number of zero indicated that they think it is very likely that they will be alive after 5 years while the numbers 10 indicates that they think they will not be alive after 5 years of being diagnosed with any of these conditions (due to complications from this incidents). The sum of the scores of these two sections was considered as perceived risk, and the range of possible numerical scores was between 0 and 20.

Prochaska and Diclemente’s questionnaire of stages of change in cigarette consumption was used, to measure the stages of change in hookah consumption in this study [31, 32]. It included 5 categories; (1) precontemplation stage; people who currently are smoking and do not intent to quit it next 6 months, (2) contemplation stage; people who consider smoking abstinence in next 6 months, (3) preparation stage; people who intend to smoking quit in next month, (4) action stage; people who quitted the smoking but no more than 6 months, and (5) maintenance stage; people who have not smoked for more than 6 months. This questionnaire was used previously in Iran in the study conducted by Davaji et al. [33].

Attitude and beliefs about hookah were measured using three questions ranging from “completely agree” to “completely disagree”: (1) In my opinion, hookah smoking is not addictive, (2) I can stop smoking hookah whenever I decide, and (3) The harms of using hookah smoking are less than cigarette smoking. If a person mentions hookah smoking addictive, hard to stop hookah smoking whenever he/she decide, and harm of hookah smoking more than cigarette smoking considered as negative attitude to hookah smoking and vice versa. This questionnaire was used previously in Iran in the study conducted by Davaji et al. [33].

Also, this questionnaire has demographic variables such as age, gender, level of education, occupation, duration of hookah consumption, and current pattern of hookah consumption.

Data analysis

The collected data were entered into Stata statistical software version 17 and the results were presented by using descriptive statistics (frequency distribution, percentage, average standard deviation). Multiple linear regression analysis was used to predict the perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD, with the studied variables. In this study, p-value of lower than 0.05 was considered significant.

Findings

The average age of the participants in this study was 28.14 ± 7.22 years with an age range of 15–60 years. The duration of their consumption with the current pattern was on average 4.29 ± 3.81 years, and in the last month their average hookah consumption was 65.6 ± 7.38 times (per month). The average age of starting hookah use was 22.89 ± 6.35 years with a range of 14–42 years for the first hookah use. In terms of gender, majority of participants, (n: 286, 84.1%) were men. In terms of marital status, nearly half of the participants were single (n: 171, 50.3%).

From all the participants, 276 people (81.2%) were current hookah users and 64 people (18.8%) were hookah users in the past. Among them, 251 people (73.8%) used other forms of tobacco besides hookah. Of all participants, 188 people (55.3%) use hookah, 48 people (14.1%) used cigarettes, 8 people (2.4%) use electronic cigarettes, and 7 people (1.2%) also use pipes.

In terms of job, 67 people (19.7%) were unemployed, about two thirds of the studied people had education below diploma (n: 220, 64.6%), and 222 people (65.3%) had a low monthly income level. Also, 253 people (74.4%) used hookah 2 times (per month) or more in the last month. Regarding to the stage of change, more than half of the participants (53.2%) were in the pre-contemplation stage (Table 1).

Table 2 presents the mean perceived risk of lung cancer and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) among participants, categorized by various demographic characteristics. The results indicate that perceived risk varies significantly across demographic groups. Individuals who are unemployed have a significantly higher perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD compared to those who are self-employed, unskilled workers, or housekeepers. Individuals with lower levels of education, particularly those who have only completed primary school, perceive themselves at a higher risk of both lung cancer and COPD compared to those with higher education levels. Those who have smoked in the last month, especially those who have smoked once or twice, exhibit higher perceived risks of both lung cancer and COPD compared to non-smokers. Individuals with lower monthly income levels perceive a greater risk of both lung cancer and COPD compared to those with middle- or high-income levels. Current hookah users perceive a higher risk of both lung cancer and COPD compared to ex-users, highlighting the potential risks associated with hookah smoking. Women perceive a significantly higher risk of COPD compared to men, suggesting a potential awareness of increased susceptibility to this disease. Single individuals perceive a slightly higher risk of lung cancer and COPD compared to married individuals, although this difference is not as pronounced as other factors (Table 2).

The perceived risk for lung cancer

The mean and standard deviation of the perceived risk for lung cancer was 8.41 ± 5.50 and for COPD it was 8.51 ± 5.58 out of 20 possible scores. The results of multiple linear regression to predict the perception of the perceived risk of lung cancer showed that the perceived risk of lung cancer in the following groups is significantly higher: (1) people who use other forms of tobacco, (2) people who have not used hookah in the last month, (3) people who are in preparation and contemplation stages of hookah smoking, (4) single people, and (5) people who have a negative attitude towards hookah use. In general, these variables have provided a significant prediction of the perceived risk of lung cancer (F(21, 317) = 11.79, p < 0.001, Adj R-squared = 40%) and an average 40% to predict changes in the perceived risk of lung cancer (Table 4).

The perceived risk of COPD

The results of multiple linear regressions to predict the perceived risk of COPD showed that the following people have a significantly higher perceived risk of developing COPD: (1) people who use other forms of tobacco, (2) people who are in preparation and contemplation stages of hookah use, (3) females, (4) unskilled workers, and (5) people who have a negative attitude toward hookah use. Discussion

Hookah consumption is harmful to human health and can expose a person to lung cancer and COPD. Understanding how much hookah users perceive themselves as at risk of developing these diseases as a result of hookah use, is very critical in the prevention of these conditions or improving the disease outcome. Therefore, this study was designed and conducted to determine the perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD among hookah users. The results of this study showed that hookah users have a low perception of the risks associated with hookah consumption.

The results showed that both in terms of lung cancer and COPD, these people reported higher perceived risks than their counterpart group: (1) individuals who used other forms of tobacco, (2) people who are in preparation and contemplation stages of hookah smoking, and (3) those who have a negative attitude towards hookah use. The results of various studies have also shown that in general, women have more health motivation than men and women usually have higher perceived risks of diseases compared to men [34–37]. Results of the present study also showed that women had a higher perceived risk of COPD compared to men. The result of the study conducted by Abu-Rmeileh et al., on students in five Middle Eastern countries revealed that female students had a greater perceived risk of the harms of hookah consumption than male students in all 5 countries, which is also consistent with the findings of this study [38].

Perceived risk and use of hookah with other tobacco products

The use of other forms of tobacco along with hookah, such as cigarettes and snuff, has increased the perceived risk of hookah among the participants, which can be justified that people who only smoke hookah understand its risks less than using cigarettes alone, and believe that smoking hookah is less dangerous compared to smoking, which seems to be common in almost all parts of the world [39–42]. For instance, the result of Willis et al.‘s study conducted on adolescents showed that those who used e-cigarettes and cigarettes at the same time, reported a higher perceived risk [43]. The results of Berg et al.‘s study, which was conducted on young people also showed that the perceived risk those who used of hookah and e-cigarette at the same time, was lower than those who used only cigarette [44]. They concluded that since hookah and e-cigarettes, have recently entered the consumer market; the low perceived risk regarding these two products, could prove the effectiveness of advertisement of tobacco companies in presenting these products as safe and socially acceptable. It seems that hookah is very attractive among youth and younger people have positive perceptions toward hookah use and low the perceived risk for associated risk and its addictiveness [44].

Perceive risk and stage of change

Another finding was the increase in perceived risk among those who are in the preparation and contemplation stages of hookah smoking. This finding can perhaps be justified by the fact that people who think of quitting hookah might have been more conscious about its dangers, or it could be the other way around. This means those who for some other reasons (e.g., a family member recently being diagnosed with lung cancer or COPD, having respiratory symptoms, etc.) might have become more aware of hookah risks, and this leads them to think about quitting to avoid these risks in the long term. Since, there are very limited literature on this topic, we are not able to compare these results to other similar studies, and this is the topic that needs further exploration in the future.

Perceived risk and attitude towards hookah

On the other hand, the results showed that people who have a negative attitude towards hookah consumption consider it more harmful than others, and consider themselves more at risk of lung cancer as well as COPD. In other words, people who have a positive attitude towards hookah also think less about its dangers. This is consistent with the results of studies in different parts of the world confirming that people, especially young people, are less aware of the harms of hookah consumption than other tobacco products and have a positive attitude towards it [44–46]. In this scenario, the positive attitude towards hookah acts as a facilitator for the initiation and continuation of hookah consumption. Another cause of a positive attitude towards hookah is the peer pressure in social gatherings. Therefore, this positive attitude could be intensified through peer pressure by reducing the stigma and making the associated risks seem less harmful [45–47]. However, there are not any previous studies conducted on the relationship between attitude toward hookah and the risk perception of COPD and lung cancer, so we are not able to compare the results of the present study to of similar study.

Study strengths and limitations

A major strength of this study is investigating the perceived risk of lung cancer and COPD among hookah smokers for the first time. Most of the studies on the topic of hookah are focused on the comparing the harms associated with hookah use compared to cigarettes smoking in general. There are not any studies that have investigated risk perception among hookah users. The authors stand to contribute to the limited literature on this topic. While producing significant results, this study is subject to some limitations. First, the cross-sectional nature of the study introduces inherent limitations commonly associated with this specific study design. Second, reliance on self-reporting of participants to the questionnaires introduces potential biases inherent in such methods. Third, the use of a questionnaire with a scoring range of “0–10”, as opposed to alternative scales such as scales with the range of “0-100”, may influence the precision of risk perception assessment. Additionally, the study did not assess the family history of COPD and lung cancer or potential underlying diseases of participants that might have impacted their perception of the risk. For instance, the study did not measure psychological and emotional factors, such as anxiety and depression, which could potentially influence the perception of risk among study participants. While the study has included common demographic and sociological variables, but it did not include medical history, work environment and other potential causes of COPD or lung cancer. Finally, the lower sample of female participants was another study limitation.

Conclusion

The results of this study showed that in general, the risk perception of the participants in this study was low. Extensive education and information should be provided at the community level regarding the dangers of hookah in developing lung cancer and COPD. These public trainings specifically should target male hookah users and those who are not thinking of quitting hookah. There should also be widespread knowledge dissemination at the population level especially among youth, to correct misconceptions that hookah is harmless, non-addictive, and easy to quit. This study is providing insight into the risk perception of hookah users in Iran. Considering the limitation of the study, designing larger studies could help to gain more understating of the contributing factors. To gain a comprehensive understanding of the perceived risk of hookah users on these diseases at the national level, it is imperative to conduct more studies across different regions of the country while aiming to recruit more female participants. Conducting long-term observational studies helps to better assess the perceived risk of participants and gain more insight into this phenomenon.

Hypoxia-induced upregulation of lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1 promotes lung adenocarcinoma progression through the miR-506-5p/ASPH/NOTCH1 pathway
Authors: Weiping Yao, Kaiyuan Zhang, Wenjing Fei, Yilong Li, Keyu Cheng, Xiaoyuan Chu, Guichun Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is a major cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Understanding the mechanism of LUAD progression will aid in the development of therapeutic approaches targeting this malignancy. Hypoxia is characteristic of solid tumors and contributes significantly to LUAD progression, although the molecular mechanisms involved have not been fully elucidated. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are key regulators of cancer progression. Here, we identify SLC9A3-AS1 as a previously unrecognized hypoxia-induced lncRNA that is aberrantly upregulated in LUAD tissues. Functional analyses demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro, as well as tumor growth in vivo. Mechanistically, SLC9A3-AS1 acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) by binding to miR-506-5p, alleviating the post-transcriptional repression of aspartate β-hydroxylase (ASPH) and activating NOTCH1 signaling. ASPH overexpression or miR-506-5p inhibition counteracts the effects of SLC9A3-AS1 knockdown, restoring LUAD cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) capacity. Furthermore, we report that SLC9A3-AS1 is a hypoxia-responsive gene that is transcriptionally activated by hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) in LUAD cells. Collectively, these results identify SLC9A3-AS1 as a key hypoxia-induced regulator of LUAD progression and reveal a miR-506-5p/ASPH/NOTCH1 signaling pathway that drives oncogenic activity. These findings provide new insights for the diagnosis and treatment of LUAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-56789-8.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the second most common cancer worldwide and remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality1–3. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the predominant subtype of lung cancer4. Despite significant advances in multimodal treatment strategies (including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and molecular-targeted therapies), the overall prognosis for LUAD patients remains poor5. Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD progression, identify potential therapeutic targets, and develop innovative treatment strategies for LUAD patients.

A hypoxic microenvironment is a common feature of solid tumors and is closely associated with malignant phenotypes and poor prognoses across multiple cancer types6. Upon exposure to hypoxic stress, hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) is rapidly stabilized and undergoes accumulation, after which it binds to hypoxia-response elements (HREs) in target genes to drive a broad spectrum of tumor-promoting processes, including angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, metabolic reprogramming, chemoresistance, and radioresistance7–11. Tumor hypoxia is not merely a consequence of uncontrolled cellular proliferation within the tumor microenvironment; rather, it has become increasingly recognized as being an active contributor to tumor progression. Therefore, the elucidation of novel molecular mechanisms in hypoxic tumors to overcome hypoxia microenvironment driven tumor progression is critically important. Furthermore, accumulating evidence indicates that hypoxia is a prevalent feature of LUAD12,13.

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of noncoding transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that participate in various physiological and pathological processes via epigenetic regulation and related signaling pathways14. LncRNAs have emerged as multifunctional regulatory molecules and play critical roles in the progression of various malignancies, including LUAD15–17. Numerous studies have demonstrated that lncRNAs can function as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by sharing miRNA response elements with mRNAs, thereby competitively binding to miRNAs and regulating target gene expression18–20. In addition, accumulating evidence indicates that hypoxia contributes to tumor initiation and progression through the regulation of lncRNA expression. For example, the hypoxia-induced lncRNA known as NR2F1-AS1 upregulates NR2F1 expression and promotes pancreatic cancer progression by activating the AKT/mTOR signaling pathway8. Under hypoxic conditions, SZT2-AS1 facilitates hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression by mediating HIF heterodimerization and histone trimethylation21. Moreover, hypoxia-activated HIF1A-AS3 reprograms mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) into cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) through the miR-142-3p/miR-24-3p/PROX1/β-catenin pathway, thereby promoting chemoresistance in gastric cancer22. Therefore, the elucidation of the detailed mechanisms by which hypoxia-responsive lncRNAs regulate tumor initiation and progression is critically important.

In this study, we established a gradient hypoxia culture model in LUAD cells and performed RNA sequencing to assess transcriptomic alterations, thus revealing that SLC9A3-AS1 expression was significantly upregulated under hypoxic conditions. Notably, the expression of SLC9A3-AS1 gradually increased with the severity of hypoxia. Subsequent experiments demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 is regulated by HIF-1α in two distinct LUAD cell lines, thus resulting in the formation of a HIF-1α/SLC9A3-AS1/miR-506-5p/ASPH/NOTCH1 signaling pathway. This signaling pathway modulates LUAD cell proliferation and invasion under hypoxic conditions. In addition, we evaluated the functional role of SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD by using a mouse subcutaneous xenograft model. The goal of this study was to identify new molecular targets for LUAD and new strategies for treating the disease.

Results

SLC9A3-AS1 is significantly upregulated under hypoxic conditions in LUAD

To investigate the role of hypoxia-induced lncRNAs in LUAD, A549 cells were first cultured under normoxic (20% O₂) or hypoxic (1% O₂) conditions for 24 h, followed by transcriptomic sequencing. The results revealed that SLC9A3-AS1 expression was markedly upregulated in the 1% O₂ group (Fig. 1A). We analyzed RNA-seq data from the LUAD cohort in the TCGA database and demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 expression is higher in LUAD tissues compared with normal lung tissues, thereby suggesting that SLC9A3-AS1 may promote the progression of LUAD (Fig. 1B). We then examined SLC9A3-AS1 expression in BEAS-2B cells and a panel of LUAD cell lines (including A549, H1975, HCC827, and PC-9 cells) via quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). The results revealed significant heterogeneity in SLC9A3-AS1 expression among different LUAD cell lines, with greater expression in LUAD cell lines than in BEAS-2B cells and the highest expression observed in A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 1C). Therefore, these two cell lines were selected for subsequent experiments. A549 and H1975 cells were exposed to varying degrees of hypoxia (1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% O₂) for 24 h, and the expression levels of HIF-1α and SLC9A3-AS1 were assessed via Western blot and qRT–PCR, respectively. The results indicated that SLC9A3-AS1 expression in both A549 and H1975 cells exhibited a pronounced hypoxia-responsive pattern (Fig. 1D,E). To further determine whether HIF-1α regulates SLC9A3-AS1 expression under hypoxic conditions and to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanism, HIF-1α was silenced in hypoxic LUAD cells by using small interfering RNA (siRNA) (Supplementary Fig.S1). The knockdown of HIF-1α partially reversed the hypoxia-induced upregulation of SLC9A3-AS1 (Fig. 1F). Moreover, luciferase reporter assays revealed that hypoxia significantly increased the promoter activity of SLC9A3-AS1. HREs were predicted in the upstream region of the SLC9A3-AS1 transcription start site, and luciferase reporter constructs containing the corresponding promoter regions were generated. Compared with the empty vector, constructs harboring the SLC9A3-AS1 promoter exhibited significantly increased luciferase activity, which was attenuated upon HIF-1α knockdown (Fig. 1G,H). Site-directed mutagenesis of the predicted HIF-1α binding sites was subsequently performed to generate wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) reporter constructs. The luciferase activity was obviously increased after co-transfection of HIF‐1α and SLC9A3-AS1 WT promoter, but the luciferase activity in LUAD cells transfection of the MUT HREs showed significantly lower luciferase activity than the WT control (Fig. 1I). Furthermore, chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays provided additional evidence supporting the interaction between HIF-1α and the SLC9A3-AS1 promoter (Fig. 1J).

SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD progression in vitro

To investigate the biological function of the lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD cells, siRNAs targeting SLC9A3-AS1 were designed and transfected into A549 and H1975 cells to elicit the knockdown of endogenous SLC9A3-AS1 expression. Moreover, expression vectors harboring SLC9A3-AS1 or empty control vectors were transfected into A549 and H1975 cells to overexpress SLC9A3-AS1. And qRT–PCR confirmed that the transfection efficiency was satisfactory (Fig. 2A,B). A series of functional assays were subsequently performed. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) and colony formation assays demonstrated that the silencing of SLC9A3-AS1 significantly reduced the proliferative capacity of LUAD cells, whereas SLC9A3-AS1 overexpression markedly promoted cell proliferation (Fig. 2C–F). In addition, transwell assays revealed that the knockdown of SLC9A3-AS1 significantly inhibited the migratory and invasive abilities of A549 and H1975 cells, whereas SLC9A3-AS1 overexpression increased migration and invasion in both cell lines (Fig. 2G,H). Epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) plays a critical role in LUAD cell migration and invasion23. Accordingly, the expression of EMT-related markers (including N-cadherin, E-cadherin, vimentin, and Snail-1) was examined following the modulation of SLC9A3-AS1 expression. Western blot analysis revealed that the silencing of SLC9A3-AS1 upregulated E-cadherin expression and downregulated N-cadherin, vimentin, and Snail-1 expression (Fig. 2I). Conversely, elevated SLC9A3-AS1 expression promoted EMT in A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 2J). Collectively, these results indicate that SLC9A3-AS1 enhances LUAD cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT.

SLC9A3-AS1 functions as a molecular sponge for miR-506-5p in LUAD cells

We next investigated the molecular mechanism underlying the function of SLC9A3-AS1. The subcellular localization of lncRNAs largely determines their biological functions24. RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and subcellular fractionation experiments revealed that SLC9A3-AS1 is localized both in the cytoplasm and the nucleus of LUAD cells (Fig. 3A,B), suggesting that SLC9A3-AS1 may exert its molecular function via both cytoplasmic and nuclear pathways. Considering its cytoplasmic and nuclear localization, we proposed SLC9A3-AS1 may function via the ceRNA mechanism. To identify potential miRNAs that interact with SLC9A3-AS1, we performed bioinformatic by predictions using the miRDB (http://mirdb.org/) and ENCORI (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) databases. Notably, only one miRNA (miR-506-5p) overlapped between the two databases (Fig. 3C). We then silenced SLC9A3-AS1 to examine changes in miR-506-5p expression and observed that miR-506-5p levels were negatively regulated by SLC9A3-AS1 in both A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 3D). Subsequent experiments were conducted after confirming the transfection efficiency of miR-506-5p mimics and inhibitors by qRT-PCR (Fig. 3E). To further validate whether miR-506-5p is an actual interaction target of SLC9A3-AS1, luciferase reporter assays were performed. The results demonstrated that transfection with miR-506-5p mimics significantly reduced luciferase activity in A549 and H1975 cells expressing WT SLC9A3-AS1, whereas no significant effect was observed in cells transfected with MUT reporter constructs (Fig. 3F,G). RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were subsequently conducted in A549 and H1975 cells transfected with miR-506-5p mimics, thereby demonstrating that SLC9A3-AS1 associates with Argonaute 2 (Ago2)-containing miRNA–protein complexes, which is consistent with its molecular sponge function (Fig. 3H). In addition, FISH analysis revealed co-localization of SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p in LUAD cells (Fig. 3I). Collectively, these results indicate that SLC9A3-AS1 acts as a ceRNA for miR-506-5p in LUAD.

SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD progression by sponging miR-506-5p

We next investigated the biological effects of miR-506-5p in LUAD. CCK-8, colony formation, and transwell assays demonstrated that miR-506-5p suppressed LUAD cell proliferation, migration and invasion (Fig. 3J–L). To determine whether SLC9A3-AS1 exerts its tumor-promoting effects through miR-506-5p, rescue experiments were performed. Our data demonstrated that miR-506-5p inhibitors markedly restored cell proliferation in SLC9A3-AS1 silenced A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 4A,B). Similarly, the inhibition of miR-506-5p rescued the impaired migratory, invasive, and EMT capacities caused by SLC9A3-AS1 knockdown (Fig. 4C,D). Collectively, these results indicate that miR-506-5p inhibits the function of SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD progression.

MiR-506-5p targets ASPH in LUAD cells

After elucidating the relationship between SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p, we further investigated the molecular mechanism through which miR-506-5p functions in LUAD. Potential target genes of miR-506-5p were predicted by using the ENCORI, TargetScan (http://targetscan.org/), and miRDB databases. By intersecting these candidates with genes that are upregulated in hypoxic LUAD cells, six putative targets were identified, including ZBTB18, ANKRD1, HNRNPH3, RPS6KB1, ASPH, and YWHAZ (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, qRT-PCR analysis revealed that among these candidates, only the expression level of ASPH significantly decreased after SLC9A3-AS1 silencing (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, miR-506-5p overexpression significantly downregulated ASPH mRNA levels in A549 and H1975 cells, whereas miR-506-5p inhibition resulted in the opposite effect (Fig. 5C). The regulatory effect of miR-506-5p on ASPH protein expression was further confirmed via Western blot analysis in LUAD cells (Fig. 5D,E). Dual-luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that miR-506-5p overexpression significantly suppressed luciferase activity in cells transfected with WT ASPH reporter constructs, whereas mutation of the miR-506-5p binding site within the 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) of ASPH abolished this inhibitory effect (Fig. 5F,G). In addition, RIP assays revealed that endogenous ASPH was coprecipitated with miR-506-5p by an anti- Ago2 antibody in both A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 5H). Subsequently, rescue experiments demonstrated that overexpression of ASPH could restore the decreased proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT capacities of LUAD cells induced by transfection with miR-506-5p mimics (Supplementary Fig. S2). Collectively, these results identify ASPH is a direct downstream target of miR-506-5p, thereby regulating the progression of LUAD.

ASPH promotes LUAD progression in vitro

We subsequently sought to elucidate the biological role of ASPH in LUAD cells. A549 and H1975 cells were transfected with ASPH-specific siRNAs or ASPH overexpression plasmids, and Western blot analysis confirmed the efficient modulation of ASPH expression (Fig. 6A,B). CCK-8 and colony formation assays demonstrated that ASPH overexpression significantly promoted LUAD cell growth, whereas ASPH knockdown markedly suppressed the proliferation of A549 and H1975 cells (Fig. 6C–F). Consistently, transwell assays revealed that ASPH overexpression substantially enhanced the migratory and invasive capacities of A549 and H1975 cells, whereas the silencing of ASPH expression significantly impaired these motility phenotypes (Fig. 6G,H). Western blot analysis further revealed that ASPH overexpression decreased E-cadherin expression and increased the levels of N-cadherin, Snail-1, and vimentin in both A549 and H1975 cells. In contrast, ASPH knockdown attenuated EMT in these cells (Fig. 6I,J). Collectively, these findings indicate that ASPH promotes LUAD cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT in vitro.

The ASPH/NOTCH1 pathway mediates the effects of SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD cells

We next investigated whether ASPH mediates the functional effects of SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD. The results demonstrated that ASPH overexpression rescued the impaired proliferation of LUAD cells induced by SLC9A3-AS1 knockdown (Fig. 7A,B). Moreover, ASPH overexpression restored the migratory, invasive, and EMT capacities that were suppressed by SLC9A3-AS1 silencing (Fig. 7C,D). Previous studies have reported that ASPH promotes tumor progression in hepatocellular carcinoma and breast cancer through activation of the NOTCH1 signaling pathway25,26. Therefore, we examined whether the NOTCH1 pathway is involved in ASPH-mediated effects in LUAD. The results revealed that SLC9A3-AS1 silencing reduced NOTCH1 expression, whereas ASPH overexpression restored NOTCH1 levels (Fig. 7D). Overall, these findings suggest that ASPH mediates the activation of the NOTCH1 signaling pathway induced by SLC9A3-AS1, thereby promoting the malignant phenotype of LUAD.

SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD progression in vivo

To determine whether SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD progression in vivo, A549 cells stably overexpressing SLC9A3-AS1 and their corresponding control cells were subcutaneously injected into nude mice. Tumors derived from the SLC9A3-AS1 overexpression group exhibited a significantly increased average tumor volume and tumor weight compared with controls (Fig. 8A–C). Immunofluorescence (IF) analysis of xenograft tumor tissues revealed that SLC9A3-AS1 overexpression increased the staining intensity of vimentin, Snail-1, and N-cadherin while reducing E-cadherin expression (Fig. 8D,E). These findings indicate that elevated SLC9A3-AS1 expression significantly promotes LUAD tumor growth in vivo. Furthermore, we extracted RNA from these xenograft tumor tissues to detect miR-506-5p levels, and extracted proteins to assess ASPH and NOTCH1 expression. The results showed that stable overexpression of SLC9A3-AS1 in vivo decreased miR-506-5p levels and increased ASPH and NOTCH1 expression, which is consistent with our in vitro findings (Supplementary Fig. S3). Collectively, our results show that HIF-1α can activate SLC9A3-AS1 transcription under hypoxia conditions, and SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD growth and metastasis through the miR-506-5p/ASPH/NOTCH1 signaling axis (Fig. 9).

Discussion

Hypoxia occurs within tumors when neovascularization fails to meet the demands of rapid tumor growth. Increasing evidence indicates that hypoxia is closely associated with tumor initiation and progression27. However, the precise mechanisms through which hypoxia regulates tumor development remain incompletely understood. Recent studies have demonstrated that hypoxia modulates lncRNA transcription by inducing HIF-1α binding to HREs within gene promoters28. In the present study, we identified a previously uncharacterized hypoxia-induced lncRNA known as SLC9A3-AS1. Using the JASPAR database, we predicted putative HREs within the SLC9A3-AS1 promoter region and subsequently confirmed HIF-1α binding through dual-luciferase reporter assays and ChIP analysis.

In recent years, lncRNAs have been observed to be aberrantly expressed in a wide range of malignancies, including lung cancer, breast cancer, prostate cancer, gastric cancer, and pancreatic cancer, wherein they regulate tumor progression29–33. In the present study, we observed that SLC9A3-AS1 is upregulated in hypoxic LUAD cell lines. And the expression of SLC9A3-AS1 gradually increased with the severity of hypoxia. SLC9A3-AS1 has previously been reported to promote nasopharyngeal carcinoma progression and contribute to chemoresistance in hepatocellular carcinoma34,35. However, its precise role in LUAD initiation and progression has remained unclear. Our functional analyses demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 upregulation enhances LUAD cell proliferation, migration, and invasion both in vitro and in vivo. Collectively, these results indicate that SLC9A3-AS1 acts as an oncogenic driver.

Cytoplasmic lncRNAs often function as molecular sponges to sequester miRNAs, thereby alleviating the repression of downstream target proteins36. Nuclear lncRNAs can regulate the alternative splicing of mRNA in collaboration with splicing factors37. Moreover, some cytoplasmic and nuclear lncRNAs can respectively bind to and regulate the stability of cytoplasmic and nuclear proteins38. This study found that lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1 is distributed in the nucleus and cytoplasm of LUAD cells and promotes LUAD progression through spongy adsorption of miR-506-5p.

The dysregulated expression of miR-506-5p is associated with cancer progression39,40. However, its specific role in LUAD is not yet fully understood. In this study, we explored the functional role of miR-506-5p in LUAD. Our data demonstrate that the inhibition of miR-506-5p enhances LUAD cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT. In contrast, the overexpression of miR-506-5p leads to the opposite effects. These results suggest that miR-506-5p acts as a tumor suppressor in LUAD. The qRT-PCR results showed that there was mutual inhibition between SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p. FISH analysis demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p colocalize in LUAD cells. Dual-luciferase reporter assays and RIP experiments confirmed the direct interaction between SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p. Moreover, miR-506-5p inhibitors can reverse the effect of SLC9A3-AS1 inhibition on the proliferation and invasion ability of LUAD cells. Collectively, these results suggested that SLC9A3-AS1 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-506-5p in LUAD.

In the ceRNA regulatory network, miRNAs act as key molecular intermediates that regulate gene expression and subsequently control cellular functions by binding to the 3′UTR of target genes41. In this study, we revealed that ASPH is a key downstream target of miR-506-5p. ASPH is a member of the α-ketoglutarate–dependent dioxygenase family and catalyzes the hydroxylation of aspartyl and asparaginyl residues within the epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like domains of various proteins42. Notably, the regulation of ASPH by lncRNAs has not been previously reported. SLC9A3-AS1 promotes the expression of ASPH in LUAD cells, while miR-506-5p exhibits opposite regulatory effects. The downregulation of ASPH resulting from SLC9A3-AS1 inhibition relies on upregulation of miR-506-5p through cytoplasmic ceRNA mechanisms. In LUAD cells transfected with miR-506-5p simulators, the luciferase activity of ASPH-WT decreased, while the luciferase activity of ASPH-MUT did not change. This suggested that ASPH is a direct target of miR-506-5p. RIP experiments also showed that ASPH interacts with miR-506-5p in LUAD cells. Additional rescue assays revealed that the oncogenic effects of SLC9A3-AS1 in LUAD cells are mediated by ASPH. Previous studies have reported that ASPH promotes tumor progression in HCC and breast cancer through activation of the NOTCH1 signaling pathway25,26. Consistent with these results, our Western blot analysis demonstrated that SLC9A3-AS1 silencing reduced NOTCH1 expression, whereas ASPH overexpression restored NOTCH1 levels. Collectively, our results demonstrate that SLC9A3-AS1 acts as a ceRNA for miR-506-5p to regulate LUAD progression through the ASPH/NOTCH1 signaling axis. These findings expand upon the current understanding of LUAD regulatory mechanisms and provide a foundation for developing molecular-targeted therapies aimed at hypoxia-driven LUAD progression.

In conclusion, this study identifies SLC9A3-AS1 as a novel hypoxia-associated lncRNA in LUAD and clarifies the regulatory mechanism through which hypoxia-induced SLC9A3-AS1 facilitates LUAD progression. Specifically, SLC9A3-AS1 promotes LUAD cell growth and metastasis via the miR-506-5p/ASPH/NOTCH1 signaling axis. Moreover, HIF-1α acts as a transcriptional activator of SLC9A3-AS1 under hypoxic conditions. Overall, lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1 might serve as a promising diagnostic and therapeutic target for LUAD.

Methods

Cell culture and hypoxia exposure

Five human lung adenocarcinoma cell lines (A549, H1975, HCC827, and PC-9) and a normal human bronchial epithelial cell line (BEAS-2B) were purchased from the Shanghai Institute of Life Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; ExCell Bio, Uruguay) and maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. For the hypoxia experiment, gas mixtures with different oxygen concentrations were prepared (1% O2 + 5% CO2 + 94% N2; 5% O2 + 5% CO2 + 90% N2; 10% O2 + 5% CO2 + 85% N2; 15% O2 + 5% CO2 + 80% N2; 20% O2 + 5% CO2 + 75% N2). Cells were seeded in culture plates or dishes, and sterilized water was added to the blank wells to maintain humidity. The prepared culture plates or dishes were placed in 3 L culture bags, which were then sealed by using a heat sealer. After the appropriate gas mixture was injected, the bags were placed into a 37 °C incubator, with air exchanges being conducted every 24 h. The incubation time was chosen based on experimental requirements.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells or homogenized xenograft tumor tissues by using the TRIzol reagent (Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. One microgram (1 µg) of total RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) by using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). SYBR Green Master Mix (Thermo Fisher, USA) was used for qRT-PCR on a QuantStudio 1 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher, USA). ACTB was used as the internal control for the lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1, whereas U6 served as the endogenous control for miR-506-5p. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

miRNA, small interfering RNA (siRNA), and plasmid construction

Additionally, siRNAs, miRNA mimics, miRNA inhibitors, and overexpression plasmids were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The sequences are shown in Supplementary Tables S2, S3. Cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 1.5 × 10⁵ cells per well the day before transfection. Transfections were performed the following day by using siRNA-mate plus or GP-transfect-Mate transfection reagents according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The appropriate transfection duration was utilized based on the specific experiment.

Lentiviral transduction and generation of stable cell lines

Lentiviral overexpression vectors were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Cells were infected with lentivirus for 48 h, and fluorescence expression was monitored under a fluorescence microscope. The cells were subsequently selected with 2 ng/mL puromycin for two weeks, after which the medium was replaced every three days. Subsequently, qRT-PCR was used to verify the successful construction of the stable cell lines.

Western blot analysis

RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with a protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (1:100; NCM Biotech, China) was prepared at a 1:100 ratio. Total protein was then extracted by using the prepared RIPA lysis buffer. Protein samples were separated via SDS-PAGE and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk in TBST at room temperature for 2 h. The membranes were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: β-actin (1:20,000; Proteintech, China), Snail-1 (1:500; Proteintech, China), N-cadherin (1:5,000; Proteintech, China), E-cadherin (1:2,000; Proteintech, China), vimentin (1:20,000; Proteintech, China), ASPH (1:500; ABclonal, China), HIF-1α (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), and NOTCH1 (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, USA). After being washed, the membranes were incubated with species-specific secondary antibodies (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, USA) for 2 h at room temperature. The protein bands were visualized by using an ECL chemiluminescence protocol (NCM Biotech, China) and imaged with a Clinx ChemiScope imaging system (Clinx, China). The grayscale values of the protein bands were analyzed by using ImageJ (http://imagej.nih.gov/ij/).

Cell proliferation assay

Cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 3,000 cells per well; afterwards, 100 µL of complete culture medium was added to each well, and three replicate wells were established for each group. After 24 h of incubation, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (NCM Biotech, China) was added at the designated time points (0, 24, 48, and 72 h). After incubation at 37 °C for 1 h, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm by using a microplate reader, and a cell growth curve was plotted.

Cell cloning experiment

Eight hundred different transfected LUAD cells per well were seeded into six-well plates and cultured for 7 days at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. Colonies were then washed once with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 30 min. After the excess dye was removed and the cells were air dried, the colonies were photographed.

Cell invasion and migration assays

Cell invasion and migration abilities were assessed by using 24-well Transwell chambers (Corning, USA). Briefly, 5 × 10⁴ cells were suspended in 200 µL of serum-free medium and seeded into the upper chamber, whereas 700 µL of complete medium was added to the lower chamber. After 24 h, the cells in the chambers were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, followed by staining with 0.05% crystal violet. Cells that did not migrate through the membrane were gently wiped away with a cotton swab. The number of cells in three randomly selected fields of each sample was counted under a microscope. For the invasion assay, Transwell chambers were precoated with Matrigel matrix (Corning, USA).

RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Probes targeting SLC9A3-AS1 and miR-506-5p were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). In situ hybridization was performed by using a FISH kit (RiboBio, China). Immunofluorescence images were acquired by using a Zeiss laser scanning confocal microscope. The FISH probes used are shown in Supplementary Table S4.

Subcellular fractionation

Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNAs in LUAD cells were isolated using the PARIS Kit (Thermo Fisher, USA), and the isolated SLC9A3-AS1 level in the nucleus and the cytoplasm were measured by qRT-PCR.

Luciferase reporter assay

The WT or MUT 3′UTR sequences of the lncRNA SLC9A3-AS1 or ASPH were cloned and inserted into the pGL4.10 luciferase reporter vector (GenePharma, China). The firefly luciferase reporter plasmids containing the WT or MUT 3′UTR sequences of SLC9A3-AS1 or ASPH were cotransfected with miR-506-5p mimics into A549 and H1975 cells. The sequences are shown in Supplementary Table S5. For promoter activity analysis, the SLC9A3-AS1 promoter region containing the HREs was cloned and inserted into the pGL4.10 vector. Under hypoxic conditions, the pGL4.10-SLC9A3-AS1 promoter construct was cotransfected with si-HIF-1α or si-NC into LUAD cells. Luciferase activity was measured 24 h after cell transfection and normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

ChIP assays were performed using the ChIP-IT® Express Enzymatic Kit (Active Motif, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde and lysed, followed by enzymatic shearing to generate DNA fragments ranging from 200 to 1,500 bp. Chromatin complexes were immunoprecipitated by using an anti HIF-1α antibody or control rabbit IgG. The enriched DNA was subsequently analyzed via qRT-PCR using primers specific for the HRE region. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RIP experiments were performed by using a RIP kit (Millipore, Germany). After cell lysis, immunoprecipitation beads were prepared by using an anti-Ago2 antibody (1:5,000; Millipore, Germany) or a control IgG antibody (1:5,000; Millipore, Germany) to form RNA-binding protein-RNA complexes. The coprecipitated RNA was then analyzed via qRT-PCR.

Animal model

BALB/c nude mice aged 4–6 weeks were obtained from Hangzhou Ziyuan Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China). All of the animal experiments were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Eastern Theater Command General Hospital (Nanjing, China). The experimental protocol was strictly followed in accordance with the relevant regulations of the Animal Protection Association. A549 cells transfected with OE-NC or OE-SLC9A3-AS1 were used for the animal experiments. Transfected A549 cells (1 × 10⁶ cells per injection) were subcutaneously injected into the right inguinal region of the nude mice anesthetized with isoflurane. Tumor size was measured every two days with vernier caliper. The tumor volume was calculated using the following formula: (L × W2)/2, where L is the longest diameter of the tumor and W is the perpendicular shortest diameter. Finally, the nude mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation under anesthesia with isoflurane.

Statistical analysis

All of the data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. Statistical analyses were performed by using GraphPad Prism 10 software. The significance of the differences between two experimental groups was determined by using the Student’s t-test, and multiple group comparisons were analyzed via one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Statistical significance was confirmed when p < 0.05.

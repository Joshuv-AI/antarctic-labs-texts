A HIV status-stratified analysis of Mpox risk factors among MSM in Mainland China over two years (2023–2024)
Authors: Hui Xiang, Fangfang Chen, Peilong Li, Xinyu Hao, Jie Yang, Houlin Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe global Mpox epidemic has disproportionately affected men who have sex with men (MSM). This study describes the factors associated with Mpox infection among Chinese MSM and examines whether these factors differ between HIV-positive MSM and HIV-negative MSM.MethodsWe conducted online cross-sectional surveys in August-September 2023 and 2024, recruiting 15,848 MSM from 30 provinces in China. Weighted logistic regression models were then used to estimate the self-reported Mpox infection, stratified by HIV status.ResultsAmong 170 reported Mpox cases, 47.1% were HIV-positive. In the overall sample, higher education and income were associated with lower risk, while international travel was associated with higher risk. When stratified by HIV status, the associated factors differed. For HIV-positive MSM, having sexual partners with skin rash symptoms and STD infection were significantly associated with Mpox infection. For HIV-negative MSM, group sex and lower attention to Mpox information were significantly associated with infection.ConclusionHIV status defines divergent Mpox risk profiles among Chinese MSM. An effective response requires differentiated strategies: integration of Mpox prevention into HIV care cascade systems for people living with HIV, and the implementation of behavior-focused interventions for HIV-negative MSM. Longitudinal studies grounded in behavioral and network theories are now urgently needed to establish causal pathways, elucidate the mechanisms driving these divergent risk architectures, and evaluate the long-term effectiveness of stratified intervention approaches.Keywords: Mpox, HIV, MSM, China, Health inequalities

Introduction

Monkeypox (Mpox) is a viral illness caused by the monkeypox virus (MPXV), a member of the Orthopoxvirus genus that holds global public health significance [1, 2]. Since May 2022, the current global outbreak has resulted in sustained human-to-human transmission [1]. Mpox continues to be a major global public health threat today [1]. From January 2022 and August 2024, the World Health Organization (WHO) reported over 100,000 reported cases and over 220 death cases, across 120 countries, underscores the ongoing nature of this challenge [3]. The WHO declared Mpox was again a Public Health Emergency of International Concern at 14 August 2024 [3]. This global context is reflected in mainland China, which has reported 2760 locally confirmed Mpox cases since June 2023 [4].

The frequency of new cases had rapidly increased in 2023, raising concerns about large-scale outbreak, and a defining feature of the current Mpox epidemic is its disproportionate impact on men who have sex with men (MSM), with over 95% of cases from some reports occurring in this population [5–8]. This concentration of cases highlights MSM as a priority group for public health interventions. However, the label “MSM” encompasses a highly diverse population with varied behaviors and vulnerabilities. Treating it as a monolith may cover important internal differences in risk.

Within the MSM population, compared with HIV-negative Mpox cases, people living with human immunodeficiency virus (PLWH) may be more vulnerable to Mpox infection, experience more severe symptoms due to immunological deficiency [9]. The China Technical Guidelines for Mpox Prevention and Control (2022 edition) highighted the need to explore targeted interventions and prevention strategies for the susceptible and high-risk population [10]. However, from a social science perspective, it is critical to recognize that populations categorized as “high risk” are not homogeneous. Significant internal differences in social networks, behaviors, and health status may lead to unequal distributions of risk within the group, a phenomenon central to the study of health inequalities [11].

Surveillance data from China and globally indicate that a substantial proportion (approximately 40–50%) of Mpox cases are among PLWH [12–15]. Furthermore, clinical studies have consistently found that Mpox cases with HIV co-infection are more likely to experience severe health outcomes, including prolonged disease course [16–18]. These established facts situate HIV status as a relevant variable for examining potential disparities in Mpox epidemiology within the MSM community.

While the association between HIV status and Mpox severity is documented, it is less clear whether the correlates of Mpox infection risk are similar or different for HIV-positive and HIV-negative MSM. Previous studies and guidelines have often considered MSM as a single group or focused on HIV primarily in the context of clinical severity [11, 13, 14]. Identifying potential differences in these risk profiles is essential for uncovering patterns of health inequality and informing appropriately differentiated public health messages.

Therefore, in the present study, using data from HIV status-stratified analysis of Mpox infection, we aime to systematically describe and compare the factors associated with self-reported Mpox infection separately for HIV-positive and HIV-negative MSM, These findings will provide an evidence base for considering more tailored interventions that address the distinct needs and vulnerabilities potentially present within the broader MSM population.

Methods

Study design and participants

We conducted cross-sectional online surveys with convenience sampling targeting MSM in August–September 2023 and August–September 2024. To facilitate participant recruitment, MSM community-based organizations (CBOs) assisted in the recruitment of participants. Trained interviewers recruited MSM participants through one-on-one approaches. A custom-developed questionnaire was used, and participants were directed to scan a QR code to access the online anonymous survey hosted on Wenjuanxing Enterprise Edition. Each unique IP address was allowed to participate in the survey only once. Eligible participants were males aged 18 years or older and had provided informed consent.

Variables definitions

Socio-demographics

Age, ethnicity, education level, marital status, monthly income, occupational group, and sexual orientation were collected.

Sexual health and behavior

Participants were asked about the following over the previous three months, Frequency of sexual intercourse: categorized as 0, 1–5 times or > 5 times). Group sex: Defined as sexual activity involving at least three individuals. Sexually transmitted disease (STDs) diagnoses: including syphilis, urethral or rectal gonorrhea, urethral or rectal chlamydia. Partner’s skin rash: Any self-reported skin rash in sexual partners. International travel history: Defined as having traveled to countries or regions outside mainland China.

Mpox-related knowledge and risk perception

We assessed the following aspects of Mpox-related awareness and attitudes: Mpox knowledge: Measured using 15 multiple-choice questions covering pathogen, transmission routes, incubation period, susceptible populations, clinical symptoms, treatment, and prevention. Each correct answer received one point. A total score of ≥ 12 was defined as “adequate knowledge”, while < 12 was defined as “inadequate knowledge”. Risk perception: Participants were asked about their perceived risk of contracting Mpox, their level of attention to Mpox information, their awareness of Mpox vaccines, and their willingness to be vaccinated.

Case definition

Mpox and HIV cases were defined as participants who self-reported a prior confirmed diagnosis by a hospital or healthcare institution.

Sampling method and sample size

Surveys were administered in 30 provinces across China. The geographical regional classification is detail as follow [19]. The self-designed questionnaire collected information on demographic characteristics, including age, marital status, educational background, occupation status, residential location type, geographical region, and regarding health and behavioral characteristics including sex behaviors in the past three months, knowledge related to Mpox, Mpox infection status and HIV infection status.

Assuming Mpox seroprevalence level of 6.4 ~ 7.4% [20, 21], the sample size was estimated using the formula n=(tα)2PQ/d, where P = 0.07, Q = 0.93, α = 0.05, tα=1.96, and the permissible error d = 0.1P. The sample size was 5,102 individuals. Considering a refusal rate of 10%, the estimated annual survey sample size was at least 5,612 individuals.

Quality control of the survey questionnaire

The study used a structured questionnaire that was adapted from a previously validated instrument developed by our team for research among Chinese MSM [22]. A pre-survey was conducted to test the reliability and validity of the questionnaire. Analysis of 528 valid questionnaires confirmed that the questionnaire had good reliability and validity. The Cronbach’s α was 0.865 (P < 0.001). To minimize self-report bias, the following measures were implemented: (1) The survey was completely anonymous, with no personally identifiable information collected; (2) The cover page clearly stated the research purpose, data confidentiality, and participants’ rights, emphasizing the importance of truthful responses; (3) Key behavioral variables used neutral, non-judgmental wording based on pre-testing—for example, “In the past three months, have you participated in sexual activity involving at least three people simultaneously?”

Statistical analysis

Data from an online survey were processed using Excel 2021 and analyzed in R (version 4.4.3). Descriptive statistics were presented accordingly. Univariate analyses identified candidate variables (p < 0.10) for inclusion in multivariable models. We used multivariable logistic regression to assess the association between HIV status and Mpox infection, adjusting for demographic and behavioral factors. Effect modification by survey year was tested via an interaction term (HIV status × year). To address overrepresentation of HIV in the sample relative to population prevalence, direct benchmark weighting (DBW) was applied. All tests were two-sided with significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Between August 2023 and September 2024, a total of 15,857 participants met the eligible criteria and 15,848 (99.9%) respondents completed this survey at 30 provinces in China mainland (Table 1). One hundred twenty-seven and 43 respondents were reported Mpox infection in 2023 and 2024 respectively. The median age was 30 years, about 45% of respondents had a personal monthly income less than 2000 CNY, about 30% of respondents had an educational background of junior school or below (Table 1).

Table 3 presents the factors associated with self-reported Mpox infection in the overall sample and stratified by HIV status. In the univariate analysis, multiple sociodemographic characteristics, including marital status, educational background, monthly income, sexual behaviors, and health status factors were significantly associated with self-reported Mpox infection in the overall sample.

Multivariate analysis of the pooled data showed that higher education and higher income were protective factors against Mpox infection. Conversely, having sexual partners with skin rash, concurrent STD infection, group sex and international travel history were significant risk factors. Specifically, international travel was a consistent risk factor in both 2023 (aOR = 1.97 [95% CI: 1.04–3.60]) and 2024 (aOR = 2.57 [95% CI: 1.13–5.65]).

The stratified analysis revealed divergent risk profiles by HIV status. Among HIV-positive MSM, the factor consistently associated with Mpox infection across both survey years was having sexual partners with skin rash symptoms (2023:aOR = 17.90 [95% CI:8.70 ~ 38.22]; 2024: aOR = 9.40 [95% CI:3.09 ~ 28.66]) Notably, concurrent STD infection and being divorced or widowed were significant risk factors in 2023 (STD: aOR = 6.02 [95% CI: 2.73–13.37]; marital status: aOR = 5.28 [95% CI: 1.52–17.03]), but these associations were not observed in 2024. Among HIV-negative MSM, engaging in group sex was a consistent risk factor across both years(2023: aOR = 3.47 [95% CI:1.62 ~ 7.78]; 2024: aOR = 5.74 [95% CI:1.99 ~ 18.79]). Having sexual partners with skin rash symptoms was also significant in both years (2023: aOR = 7.26 [95% CI: 4.11 ~ 12.85]; 2024: aOR = 3.16 [95% CI: 1.09 ~ 9.02]). Lower attention to Mpox information remained a protective factor in this subgroup (2023: aOR = 0.49 [95% CI:0.25 ~ 0.93]; 2024: aOR = 0.10 [95% CI:0.01 ~ 0.37]).

Analysis by survey year showed several associations only in 2023, residing in eastern China(aOR = 0.43 [95% CI: 0.22 ~ 0.85]), higher willingness to self-pay for Mpox vaccine(aOR = 0.46 [95% CI: 0.28 ~ 0.76]; aOR = 0.28 [95% CI: 0.16 ~ 0.51]) and higher perceived severity of Mpox were protective factors (aOR = 0.47 [95% CI: 0.29 ~ 0.76]).

Knowledge about Mpox was not significantly associated with infection risk in any analysis across both years (P > 0.05) (Table 3).

Discussion

This nationwide study provides critical insights into the evolving epidemiology of Mpox among MSM in China, highlighting HIV status as a key determinant of divergent risk profiles. Our study reveals a marked decline in Mpox prevalence from 2023 to 2024 and we identify distinct risk architectures. HIV-positive MSM face risks driven by clinical and partner-related factors, whereas HIV-negative MSM are influenced by specific behavioral patterns and information awareness. These divergences underscore the inadequacy of uniform prevention approaches and necessitate HIV status-stratified public health interventions.

Our findings confirmed the concentration of Mpox cases in Eastern China, aligning with national surveillance data [14], likely reflecting regional variations in population mobility, sexual network density, and healthcare access [23, 24]. The estimated prevalence (1.6% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024) was lower than rates reported in other settings within China (2.33%) [25] and Brazil (5.8%) [26]. This discrepancy may stem from differences in epidemic phase, sampling methodology, or the effectiveness of recent public health interventions. Nevertheless, the geographic clustering emphasizes that surveillance data must be interpreted within the context of underlying population dynamics to optimize resource allocation.

The stratified analysis showed that among HIV-positive MSM, risk was significantly associated with having sexual partner with skin rash symptom. This profile aligns with global evidence indicating that HIV-positive MSM faced substantially elevated Mpox risk due to synergistic biological and network-level vulnerabilities [18, 26–28]. Immunosuppression may increase susceptibility upon exposure, while overlapping sexual networks—characterized by dense connections within HIV-positive communities—facilitate rapid transmission, consistent with social network theory [2, 29, 30]. Consequently, integrating Mpox prevention into HIV care cascade is essential. This integration include proactive screening for skin rashes during routine visits, prioritizing vaccination, and combining Mpox testing services with exsting HIV care.

Conversely, HIV-negative MSM exhibited behavioral risk patterns, where group sex and lower information attention were primary drivers [31–33]. This divergence confirms that Mpox infection manifests as distinct epidemics within MSM populations based on HIV status. Notably, this association between lower information attention and infection risk highlights a gap in risk perception among this subgroup. This pattern may reflect risk compensation mechanisms, whereby HIV-negative individuals who perceive themselves as protected against HIV through measures such as PrEP may inadvertently engage in higher-risk behaviors while deprioritizing attention to emerging threats like Mpox [32, 34]. However, given the cross-sectional design, the direction of this association remains ambiguous. It is plausible that individuals engaged in high-risk behaviors may be less inclined to seek health information, or that lack of information directly leads to riskier behaviors [35]. Future longitudinal studies are needed to disentangle these causal pathways.

Several additional findings warrant consideration. Socioeconomic factors (higher education and income) demonstrated protective effects in the overall sample but not within either HIV-positive or HIV-negative subgroups, suggesting that these beneficial effects operate broadly across the MSM population, rather than differently when stratified by HIV status. Besides, the association between international travel and Mpox infection reinforces the globalized nature of this epidemic and underscores the need for pre-travel health consultations [26, 36, 37]. However, the transient nature of certain associations including regional variations and risk perception across survey years emphasizes the dynamic character of this outbreak and the need for responsive surveillance. Besides, Mpox knowledge showed no significant association with infection in any analysis, challenging assumptions that knowledge alone modifies risk behavior. This suggests that behavioral interventions must address motivations and structural barriers beyond information dissemination.

These divergent risk profiles necessitate differentiated intervention strategies. For HIV-positive MSM, the integration of Mpox prevention into routine HIV care is paramount, emphasizing partner notification, symptom screening during clinical visits, and prioritized vaccination. For HIV-negative MSM, interventions should prioritize behavioral counseling to address specific practices like group sex, alongside theory-informed awareness campaigns to address underlying behavioral motivations and translate knowledge into protective action. Additionally, the identified association between international travel and infection risk underscores the need for pre-travel health consultations and enhanced symptom screening during peak travel seasons to mitigate cross-border transmission. Future research should prioritize longitudinal designs to establish causal pathways and evaluate the effectiveness of these stratified intervention approaches, while also incorporating biological markers for more refined confounding control. And integrate behavioral theories to deepen explanatory power regarding how network structures and risk perceptions dynamically shape Mpox transmission.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, selection bias is inherent to online convenience sampling. Although the DBW method was applied, MSM with less digital access may be underrepresented, potentially limiting generalizability. Second, reliance on self-reported data introduces risks of recall and social desirability biases, and the lack of laboratory confirmation for Mpox/HIV may lead to the underestimation of asymptomatic or mild cases. Third, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, particularly regarding the relationship between information attention and risk behavior. Finally, unmeasured confounders, such as HIV viral load and Pre-exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) use, were not fully accounted for and should be prioritized in future research.

Conclusion

HIV status defines divergent Mpox risk profiles among Chinese MSM, revealing substantial health inequalities within this population. An effective response requires differentiated strategies: integration of Mpox prevention into HIV care cascade systems for people living with HIV, implementation of behavior-focused interventions for HIV-negative MSM. These findings provide a critical evidence base for tailoring public health responses to the specific needs of these distinct subgroups. Longitudinal studies grounded in behavioral and network theories are now urgently needed to establish causal pathways, elucidate the mechanisms driving these divergent risk architectures, and evaluate the long-term effectiveness of stratified intervention approaches.

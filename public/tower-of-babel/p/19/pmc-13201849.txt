Identification of a universal three-body s-wave resonance in 10He
Authors: Y L Sun, Y Kikuchi, A Corsi, Y Kubota, M Gómez-Ramos, S Endo, G Authelet, H Baba, C Caesar, D Calvet, A Delbart, M Dozono, J Feng, F Flavigny, J -M Gheller, J Gibelin, A Giganon, A Gillibert, S Giraud, K Hasegawa, T Isobe, Y Kanaya, S Kawakami, D Kim, Y Kiyokawa, M Kobayashi, N Kobayashi, T Kobayashi, Y Kondo, Z Korkulu, S Koyama, V Lapoux, Y Maeda, F M Marqués, T Miyazaki, T Motobayashi, T Nakamura, N Nakatsuka, Y Nishio, A Obertelli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUniversality in physics describes the emergence of common features in vastly different systems. One fascinating example is the Efimov phenomenon where three-body resonantly interacting systems display universal properties. Efimov states have been observed in ultra-cold-atom systems, but their manifestation in nuclei remains elusive due to the long-range repulsive Coulomb interaction and the stringent requirement for at least two s-wave resonances in its subsystems. Recent theories propose another universality in three-body halos with only one s-wave resonance. Here, we report the identification of a precursor of this phenomenon in a neutron-rich 10He nucleus. With higher statistics and better sensitivities than previous experiments, we identify two low-lying 0+ states of 10He at ~ 1 MeV and ~ 2 MeV above its two-neutron decay threshold, and determine an s-wave scattering length of ~ − 3.5 fm between 8He and neutron. It is revealed that the lower energy state, the ground state of 10He, is a three-body state with only s-wave interactions among its subsystems. This state manifests as a resonance structure, which is a direct consequence of a universal long-range three-body repulsion. Our work sheds new light on quantum halos with finite lifetimes, providing a path toward their unified understanding across various scales and fields.Subject terms: Experimental nuclear physics, Theoretical nuclear physics

Introduction

In few-body quantum systems with short-range interactions and large s-wave scattering lengths (as) relative to the interaction range, universal low-energy phenomena can emerge1,2. One of the most prominent examples is the universality found by Efimov that there are infinite series of three-body bound states with universal energy and spatial structures when the inter-particle scattering length as is infinitely large3–5. The phenomena in atomic and molecular systems governed by the electromagnetic interactions have been extensively investigated with cold atoms (see ref. 6 and references therein). Alternatively, in the world of nuclei dominated by the strong interaction, nuclei around the neutron drip line are also expected to provide excellent opportunities to search for such phenomena (see ref. 5 and references therein).

A key aspect underlying the few-body universality is the emergence of a long-range three-body force induced by multiple s-wave scatterings7. For the Efimov state, an attractive three-body force is created from the two-body subsystems, which are marginally bound or unbound. Such a halo state can mediate attractive force between the other particles up to a substantially large distance, forming the Efimov bound states. It has also been predicted that Efimov’s universal three-body effect can extend to resonant states8 and virtual states9. Recently, it has been shown that universal geometric properties for three-body nuclear halo states may also appear when the s-wave interactions in part of the two-body subsystems are not resonant10,11. Distinct from the Efimov states, the universal halo states originate from a long-range repulsion and have not been observed experimentally in any atomic or nuclear systems to date. In the present work, we examine the low-energy properties and the related three-body effects in an unbound system in nuclear physics: the helium-10 (10He) nucleus, consisting of a 8He core and two valence neutrons.

The nature of the 10He ground state has been the focus of keen debate since its discovery in RIKEN in 199412. Up to now, two contradictory results exist for the ground state energy of 10He. The 8He–n–n relative energy (Erel) spectra from the 11Li(-p)12–14 and 14Be(-2p2n)15 knockout reactions show a prominent low-lying peak at 1.5(1) MeV (the weighted average of 1.2(3) MeV12, 1.7(4) MeV13, 1.54(11) MeV14, and 1.60(25) MeV15) with a width Γ ~1–2 MeV. The 11Li(d, 3He) reaction shows a ground-state peak at 1.4(3) MeV (Γ ~ 1.5 MeV)16. However, the 3H(8He, p)10He two-neutron (2n) transfer reaction reported a higher ground-state energy at 2.1(2) MeV (Γ ~ 2 MeV)17. Currently, the most advanced theoretical work on 10He is given in refs. 18,19. On the one hand, it has been discussed that the 10He ground state could be at ~2 MeV, as observed by the 2n transfer reaction, and has a [0p1/2]0+2 structure, while the initial-state effects due to the halo nature of 11Li or 14Be may lower the energy of 10He to ~1.5 MeV as observed in knockout reactions. Calculations with such effects qualitatively agree with the 10He invariant-mass spectrum from 11Li(-p)19, but similar calculations assuming a direct α-particle removal from 14Be cannot explain the spectrum from 14Be(-α or -2p2n)20. On the other hand, the 8He–n–n three-body model calculations predicted that 10He could have two low-lying 0+ states with [1s1/2]0+2 and [0p1/2]0+2 valence neutron structures18,21, that are built on the 1/2+ (8Heg.s. ⊗ ν1s1/2) or 1/2− (8Heg.s. ⊗ ν0p1/2) states of 9He. The observed 10He ground-state energy variation thus could be caused by two overlapping 0+ states populated with different strengths in knockout and transfer reactions18,22. The [1s1/2]0+2 component, that is characterized as a three-body s-wave resonance built from the s-wave virtual states in the n–n and 8He–n subsystems, has not been identified in any experiments so far.

Here, we show the energy spectrum and three-body correlations of 10He measured using the invariant-mass technique with higher statistics and better sensitivities than previous experiments. It is concluded that the 10He ground state is a three-body s-wave resonance, where both the n–n and 8He–n subsystems have zero relative angular momenta. Such a state shows features that arise from the long-range repulsion and represents an unbound analogue of the universal halo states predicted by Hongo and Son10.

Results and discussion

The two-neutron unbound 10He was produced through quasi-free knockout (p, 2p) reaction at ~250 MeV/nucleon, using an intense 11Li beam bombarding on the 150-mm thick liquid hydrogen target of the MINOS device23. The 8He fragment and two neutrons from the 10He decay were detected by the SAMURAI spectrometer24 and the neutron detector array NEBULA25, respectively. The same setup was used in refs. 26–30. Since 8He has no bound excited states, we do not require γ-ray detection in coincidence. The three-body relative energy Erel, which is also the energy of 10He above the 8He + 2n threshold, was reconstructed by the momenta of 8He and the two neutrons. The typical energy resolution at Erel = 1 MeV is about σ = 0.15 MeV (see “Methods”). To reject the cross-talk background mainly caused by neutron scattering within the NEBULA detectors, we applied the procedures detailed in ref. 31 (see “Methods”). The overall 2n detection efficiency as a function of Erel is shown in the upper panel of Fig. 1, which demonstrates sufficient sensitivity down to the decay threshold.

The lower panel of Fig. 1 shows the relative-energy spectrum after the efficiency correction, which has much smaller statistical uncertainties compared to the two previous experiments at 61 MeV/nucleon at RIKEN12 and at 280 MeV/nucleon at Gesellschaft für Schwerionenforschung (GSI)14. The present spectrum of 10He shows a low-energy peak at Erel ~ 1.5 MeV, consistent with the previous results in refs. 12,14. In refs. 12,14, the peak at ~1.5 MeV has been interpreted as the ground state of 10He. In addition to this low-energy peak, the current data clearly show a shoulder structure below 1 MeV, that was unresolved in the spectrum measured previously at RIKEN but was missing in the GSI result. The difference at small relative energies has been reported before in the study of 11Li32 and 6He33, which was attributed to the enhanced experimental sensitivity at low relative energies in the current setup. The low-lying shoulder indicates that the peak at ~1.5 MeV is not the ground state of 10He. Above 2 MeV, the current data, with much better quality, exhibit strength extending to higher excitation energies.

Here, we compare the data to theoretical calculations that combine the coupled-channel 9Li + n + n three-body model of 11Li and the quasi-free knockout (p, pN) reaction model. The three-body model reproduces well the ground-state properties of the 11Li nucleus, including the 2n separation energy, matter and charge radii. The dominant 2n configurations with a 9Li core are 47% [0p1/2]2 and a large s-wave mixing of 44% [1s1/2]2 that generates the neutron halo structure and strong dineutron correlations. The neutron configurations are in reasonable agreement with the recent results from the 11Li(p, pn) reaction27. In the calculation, a 0p3/2-orbital proton is removed from the 9Li core, resulting in the population of the 8He ground state that has a [0s1/2]2[0p3/2]4 neutron shell structure. The p-wave proton removal has been confirmed by comparing the experimental momentum distributions of 10He to the quasi-free knockout calculations (see “Methods”). The final 10He states were computed with the complex-scaled solutions of the Lippmann–Schwinger equation (CSLS) method34,35, which is a suitable approach to describe three-body scattering states, including virtual states, and to investigate their decay mechanisms. We adopted the 8He–n folding potential and the realistic Argonne v8′ force for the n–n potential36. Considering the uncertainty of the 8He–n interaction (see, for instance, a compilation of resonances in 9He in ref. 37), we allow variations in the parameters governing this interaction. See “Methods” for details.

The calculated Erel distributions are shown in Fig. 1, which have been convoluted with the experimental resolution detailed in “Methods”. The calculation results for the 8He–n subsystem, with the tuned 8He–n interaction to fit the current 10He spectrum below 2 MeV, produced an s-wave scattering length between 8He and neutron (acn) of ~ − 3.5 fm, in reasonable agreement with the results from some knockout reactions (−3.17(66) fm14, −3 fm < acn < 0 fm38, −2.1(6) fm or −6.8(14) fm39) and the weak 8He–n s-wave attraction from the (p, p) resonance elastic scattering40, but not with the strong one (acn ≤ − 10 fm) reported in ref. 41 and ref. 42. A recent analysis of the (p, p) resonance elastic scattering data with more advanced treatment of the isospin symmetry also suggests a small negative acn43. In addition, the current calculations produce a broad p-wave resonance for 9He at 0.76 MeV (Γ = 0.95 MeV), in reasonable agreement with the extracted 8He–n relative energy spectrum from the present experiment44.

To pin down the origin of the low-energy peak, we decomposed the Erel spectrum into contributions from different decay channels in the final scattering states. It is shown in Fig. 1 that the low-lying shoulder structure is dominated by the 8Heg.s. + [1s1/2]0+2 component, peaked at 0.96 MeV, while the peak at around 1.5 MeV in the spectrum is due to the mixture of the 8Heg.s. + [1s1/2]0+2 and 8Heg.s. + [0p1/2]0+2 components. The 8Heg.s. + [0p1/2]0+2 component gives a broad 10He resonance peaked at 1.9 MeV, consistent with the 10He 0+ state reported by the 2n transfer reaction17, but with a smaller decay width (Γ ~ 1.6 MeV). This finding resolves the long-standing puzzle about the different 10He ground-state energies obtained from knockout and 2n transfer reactions. Our work suggests that the ground state of 10He has a nearly pure [s]2 configuration. The 10He, which is traditionally considered as a doubly magic nucleus, thus appears as a “double-intruder” nucleus with the last two neutrons mainly in the 1s1/2 shell, in line with the intruder s-wave dominated ground state of 9He. The intruder dominant configuration could be understood in the shell-model framework due to the melting of the N = 8 shell closure for the neutron-rich isotopes below Z = 4. In addition, the cross-section ratio for populating the excited 0+ state relative to the ground state was extracted to be about 0.36. This value is in contrast to the estimation of a simple direct-reaction model presented in ref. 22, where a pure [sd]2-dominant configuration in the ground state of 10He was expected to yield a ratio greater than 1.5.

Our theoretical calculations reproduce the data below Erel = 2 MeV, nevertheless, they underestimate the strength at higher excitation energies. The reason might be due to higher-order transitions or higher-lying states that are not accounted for in the calculations. Indeed, the existence of 2+ state with resonance energies around 4–6 MeV14–16,45, 1− state near 5.5 MeV17 and 3− state around 7. 9 MeV45 have been suggested. Incorporation of these states into the fitting improves the agreement with the Erel spectrum in the higher energy region, but it has minimal impact on the fit results below 2 MeV. In our calculations, the spectrum’s shape is highly sensitive to the 8He–n interaction, and the observed low-energy structure is reproduced only with a weakly attractive 8He–n s-wave interaction. Furthermore, deactivating the final-state interactions (FSI) results in a shift of the excitation strength towards higher energies, coinciding with the disappearance of the [0p1/2]2 resonance and a broadening of the peak from the [1s1/2]2 neutrons. See “Methods” for details. This indicates that the 10He structure observed in the spectrum below 2 MeV primarily arises from the FSI rather than the initial-state effects.

In comparison with the theoretical calculations, both the dσ/dε around 0 in “T"-ε, shown in Fig. 3a, and the dσ/dcos(θ) around −1 in “Y"-cos(θ), shown in Fig. 3d, are enhanced. This corresponds to the so-called “di-neutron" configuration that two neutrons are emitted in opposite directions in momentum space, i.e., close in coordinate space, with a very small relative energy. The reason for the stronger enhancements than calculations is not fully understood. As mentioned before, a simple structure model was assumed for the 8He core in the calculation. However, a more complex structure is expected for the 8He core (see, for example, ref. 16), which may lead to a stronger “di-neutron" configuration than that considered in the model.

The above analyses demonstrate that the lowest-energy structure in 10He is a three-body state with zero relative angular momenta (s-wave) among its subsystems. In a two-body system, such a virtual state with orbital angular momentum L = 0, due to the absence of centrifugal barrier, can only exist as a threshold peculiarity which cannot be physically detected as a distinct peak. In contrast, the present case exhibits a well-defined resonance structure, with positive energy and finite width, which is obviously different from the threshold peculiarity. Such an s-wave resonance peak appears only in systems with ≥ 3 particles and can be attributed to a nonzero value of the effective angular momentum K, which characterizes the long-range behavior of the three-body potential2,11,18 (see “Methods” for the definition of K). Indeed, even for a noninteracting three-body system, the hyperradial (i.e., three-body effective) potential is repulsive with K = 3/2. More precisely, in our system, a large neutron–neutron s-wave scattering length of ∣ann∣ ≈ 18.6 fm47 reduces the effective angular momentum to K = 1/2 (see “Methods” for a deduction of the effective angular momentum based on the Faddeev approach). This is physically due to that a neutron occupying the halo virtual state can mediate an attractive interaction between the other neutron and the 8He core up to a large distance of ~ann, thereby effectively reducing the strength of the repulsive hyperradial potential. Nevertheless, the hyperradial potential retains its repulsive character in the 8He–n–n three-body system. This mechanism is similar to the emergence of long-range three-body attraction in the Efimov effect3,7. While K is imaginary in the Efimov effect, our system shows universal long-range repulsion with K = 1/2 because of a small core-neutron s-wave scattering length of ∣acn∣ ≈ 3.5 fm. Recently, Hongo and Son predicted that weakly bound Borromean 2n halo nuclei with small ∣acn∣ and large ∣ann∣ can exhibit a novel class of universal three-body bound states10. These states have universal energetic and structural properties distinct from the Efimov states as a consequence of the universal long-range repulsion11. Observation of the resonance structure for the three-body s-wave “virtual” state in the 8He–n–n system can be consistently interpreted as arising from this long-range universal repulsion. Thus, the present work identifies the ground state 10He as an unbound analog of the universal 2n halo states10,11. To further unveil the nature of this novel universal quantum halo, it is pivotal to scrutinize their structural properties, with concurrent developments in low-energy nuclear experiments and theoretical models on the three-body resonances.

In summary, we have reported on the low-lying states in 10He populated from the 11Li(p, 2p) knockout reactions at ~250 MeV/nucleon. Two low-lying 0+ states of 10He were identified at about 1 and 2 MeV, which have a [1s1/2]0+2 and a [0p1/2]0+2 neutron configuration, respectively, clarifying the long-standing puzzle about the different 10He ground-state energies obtained from knockout and 2n transfer reactions. An s-wave scattering length of ~ −3.5 fm between 8He and neutron was extracted from the current 10He spectrum. The existence of a three-body s-wave resonance in 10He was conclusively elucidated through the high-statistic invariant-mass spectrum and the correlation analysis among the three decay particles. The three-body effect analogous to the Efimov physics is presented as the origin for the three-body repulsive barrier. The ground state of 10He represents an unbound analog of the universal three-body halo states. While these phenomena have been observed in a nuclear system, we anticipate analogous behavior in other few-body systems, including exotic hadrons and condensed matter systems, and particularly in ultracold atomic systems, where tunable s-wave interactions between constituent particles enable systematic exploration.

Methods

Production of the 11Li beam

The 11Li beam was produced at the Radioactive Isotope Beam Factory operated by the RIKEN Nishina Center and the Center for Nuclear Study of the University of Tokyo. Cocktail secondary beam was first produced through fragmentation of a 345 MeV/nucleon 48Ca primary beam on a 9Be target. The typical intensity of the primary beam is 400 particle nA. The secondary beam was then purified and identified event by event using the BigRIPS two-stage fragment separator48. The average energy of the 11Li beam is 246 MeV/nucleon, with a purity of 70% and a typical intensity of 1 × 105 particles per second.

Cross-talk rejection

Neutrons are electrically neutral particles and are capable of generating multiple detection hits within the neutron detector array, mainly caused by neutron scattering in the detector material. In the present work, to measure the energy and momentum of two neutrons, rejection of such cross-talk background in the neutron detector array NEBULA is required. In the data analysis, we applied 6 MeVee energy threshold to the measured hits to reduce the γ background and applied the cross-talk rejection procedures following the previous work presented in Ref. 31. The parameters for the cross-talk rejection conditions were obtained using the single-neutron events taken from the experimental 11Li(p, pn)9Li + n reaction.

For the events of two neutrons detected in the same wall of NEBULA, the cross-talks due to a secondary recoil proton are identified using the following condition

where dr and dt are the distance and time difference between two hits, and parameters of dr0 = 15.8 cm, R = 15.0 cm and dt0 = 0.32 ns and T = 1.8 ns were adopted. In addition, the cross talks due to γ and scattered neutrons in larger dr and dt are rejected through a two-dimensional selection applied to Q2 and β01/β12, where Q2 is the energy deposit (unit in MeVee) of the secondary hit, β01 is the velocity (unit in speed of light) between the reaction position and the primary hit, β12 is the velocity between the primary and the secondary hits. The selection could be parameterized as Q2<108×β01/β12−60 and Q2<−12×β01/β12+80.

For the events of two neutrons detected in the different wall of NEBULA, the cross-talks are mainly due to neutron scattering from the upstream wall to the downstream one. Since the neutron’s velocity is reduced after the scattering, most of these cross-talks could be identified by a simple condition of β01/β12>1. In the current analysis, we require that Q1<250×β01/β12−200, taking into account that Q1, the energy deposit of the primary hit, becomes larger when ∣β12∣ is smaller in the neutron scattering. Finally, some remaining γ cross-talks are rejected by the condition of ∣1/β12∣ ~ 1.

After applying the above procedures, we found that 96.9% of the cross-talk is eliminated in the data, while 98.7% is eliminated in the simulation. We also confirmed that varying the cross-talk rejection conditions reasonably yields consistent relative-energy spectra, as long as the cross-talk rejection procedures are consistently incorporated into the estimation of the 2n detection efficiency. This further demonstrates that the above cross-talk rejection procedures are valid.

To calculate the distribution function W, it is important to describe the relative motion of the two valence neutrons not only for the 11Li ground state, Φnn, but also for the final 8He + n + n three-body scattering states, Ψnn. We here employ the coupled-channel 9Li + n + n three-body model53 for the initial 11Li ground state. This model well reproduces the observed quantities of the 11Li ground state, such as the binding energy, charge and matter radii, and so on. For the final scattering states, we use the complex-scaled solutions of the Lippmann–Schwinger equation (CSLS)34,35 with the 8He + n + n orthogonality condition model (OCM)21,54. The method of CSLS enables us to describe three-body scattering states accurately by using the complex-scaled Green’s function. The complex-scaled Green’s function is expanded with the completeness relation of the complex-scaled eigenstates solved in the 8He + n + n OCM. In the description of the final scattering states, we employ a realistic Argonne v8′ interaction for the n–n and a folding potential with the 8He core density for the 8He–n. Due to a lack of the 8He–n scattering data, we tune the strength of the 8He–n potential to reproduce the observed 10He spectrum, while the functional form of the 8He–n potential remains fixed as given in the folding procedure. The explicit form of the folding potential is given in ref. 54.

The calculations, either omitting all FSI or with only the n–n FSI, shift the excitation strength to higher energies, resulting in a bump peaked below 1 MeV, which is inconsistent with the low-energy structure in the experimental spectrum. We note that previous theoretical calculations of ref. 18, as shown in Fig. 6c of ref. 18, which utilized 8He–n Gaussian potentials tuned to induce a p-wave resonance of 9He around 2 MeV and an acn around +2.9 fm, also reproduce the observed low-energy structure in the 10He spectrum reasonably well. In this case, the partial decomposition in ref. 18 leads to a consistent conclusion with us that the 10He spectrum below 1 MeV is dominated by the configuration of two neutrons in the s-wave state. We also note that the universal halo states predicted by Hongo and Son10 only require a small absolute value of the core-neutron scattering length. Even if acn is slightly positive, the 10He resonance can still be interpreted as an unbound analog of the universal Hongo–Son state.

We would like to comment on the different treatment of the Pauli principle between our work and that presented in ref. 18. In the 8He + n + n OCM, the Pauli blocking in the 8He–n subsystem is taken into account by using the projection operator, the so-called pseudo potential, which excludes the Pauli forbidden states (occupied states by neutrons in the 8He core) from the 8He–n relative motion microscopically. This treatment of the Pauli blocking is important to investigate the knockout reaction, especially for the s-waves in the 8He–n subsystems, because the internal wave function is crucial to determine the distribution function W quantitatively. The analysis in ref. 18 has been performed using the core repulsion potential to exclude the Pauli forbidden states, resulting in a different internal wave function of 10He from the microscopic calculations, as shown in ref. 55. The Pauli repulsive core method is an approximate method to exclude the Pauli forbidden states from inter-cluster motions, but cannot correctly describe nodal behaviors of inter-cluster motions. By contrast, the OCM is a semimicroscopic approach in which the Pauli forbidden states are excluded accurately via projection operators.

A longitudinal cross-sectional study of changes in working attitude among primary healthcare workers across the major COVID-19 outbreaks in China
Authors: Quan Wang, Yingliu Luo, Chao Gong, Yucheng Xing, Hailan Mo, Yumeng Lv, Yaqun Fu, Jiawei Zhang, Zheng Zhu, Runzhi Han, Yixin Qin, Shan Zhang, Bingqian Wang, Mengling Feng, Xia Wei, Jing Guo, Li Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrimary health care workers play a vital role in both routine health services and responses to public health emergencies. This study aims to examine long-term changes in work attitudes and burnout among primary health care workers during the years after COVID-19 breakout in Jinan, China.MethodsWe conducted repeated cross-sectional surveys of primary health care workers in selected districts of Jinan in August 2020, 2023, 2024, and 2025. Standardised questionnaires measured job satisfaction, organisational commitment, and burnout. A total of 5,535 workers were included across four survey waves. Regression models were used to examine changes over time and associated factors. Principal component analysis was used to identify underlying vulnerability profiles. Matched subsamples were used to compare changes within the same workers across the 2023–2024, 2024–2025, and 2023–2025 periods.ResultsHere we show that recovery in work attitudes is partial and uneven. Job satisfaction declines in 2023, partly recovers in 2024, and decreases again in 2025, remaining below the 2020 level. Organisational commitment increases from 2020 to 2024 but shows a slight decline in 2025. Burnout remains a persistent concern, with depersonalisation and emotional exhaustion worsening by 2025. Severe coronavirus disease 2019 infection and newly recruited public health roles are associated with less favourable changes.ConclusionsThese findings show that the effects of pandemic-related stress continue beyond the acute crisis. Sustained mental health support, stronger organisational resources, and long-term workforce policies are needed to protect primary health care workers and support resilient health systems.Subject terms: Health care, Medical research

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic has placed an unprecedented burden on healthcare workers, both physically and psychologically1,2. The prolonged crisis, combined with heavy workloads, limited resources, and the responsibility of frontline pandemic control, has led to widespread psychological distress and professional attitude issue among them3. As estimated, 26% of US healthcare workers reported symptoms of mental illness4, while work-related challenges including burnout and diminished job satisfaction were documented across diverse healthcare systems in US5, Poland6, Turkey7, and Belgium8. These pandemic-induced psychosocial strains have manifested in concerning professional outcomes, including elevated staff turnover9,10, compromised care quality11,12, and decreased patient satisfcation12. Primary healthcare (PHC) providers, as frontline patient contacts, faced particularly acute risks of viral exposure and psychological strain, especially during infection surges3,13,14.

Although the World Health Organization (WHO) declared that COVID-19 no longer constituted a Public Health Emergency of International Concern15, the enduring occupational impacts on healthcare professionals remain poorly understood. Existing research on PHC workers’ pandemic experiences predominantly relies on single-phase cross-sectional designs13,16,17, typically capturing immediate responses to infection waves or lockdown implementations. This methodological approach fails to account for dynamic policy adaptations over the pandemic’s five-year trajectory or subsequent recovery phases. Previous research during COVID-19 has mainly focused on acute psychological distress and burnout, especially in the early phases of the pandemic2,18,19. Much less is known about how work attitudes such as job satisfaction and organisational commitment evolve over multiple years and how they co-exist with sustained burnout among PHC workers, particularly in middle-income settings.

Given that PHC workers played a pivotal role in managing public health responses under evolving pandemic policies20,21, understanding how their well-being and working attitude has changed across different phases of the pandemic is essential for informing workforce management and post-pandemic recovery strategies.

To address this gap, we conducted a longitudinal cross-sectional study in Jinan, China, examining PHC workers’ well-being at 4 critical time points: August of 2020, 2023, 2024, and 2025. These periods correspond to distinct phases of the pandemic, from the aftermath of strict lockdowns to the transition away from zero-COVID policies and the long-term adjustment to post-pandemic conditions. By analyzing changes in job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and burnout across these phases, this study provides insights into the evolving impact of pandemic-related policy shifts on the healthcare workforce, offering valuable evidence for improving resilience and preparedness in future public health emergencies.

In this study, we find that PHC workers’ work attitudes continue to change across the post-pandemic period rather than returning to pre-crisis patterns. Job satisfaction shows only partial and unstable recovery, organisational commitment improves overall but does not continue to rise indefinitely, and burnout remains substantial, with some dimensions worsening over time. Taken together, these findings indicate that the post-pandemic transition still places considerable strain on the PHC workforce and highlight the need for sustained institutional and policy responses to support long-term workforce wellbeing and system resilience.

Methods

Policy settings

China’s response to COVID-19 evolved through distinct phases, each imposing different demands on healthcare workers. In early 2020, the government implemented a nationwide lockdown, enforcing strict movement restrictions, mass quarantines, and centralized medical interventions. By May 2020, these measures had successfully suppressed the first wave, leading to the discontinuation of nationwide lockdowns22. Since then, China then adopted a “zero-COVID” strategy, characterized by mass testing, localized lockdowns, strict quarantine policies, and digital contact tracing23. While effective in controlling transmission, this approach placed PHC workers under sustained high-alert conditions, requiring continuous involvement in testing, contact tracing, isolation management, and vaccination campaigns.

A turning point came in late 2022, when the government abruptly ended the zero-COVID policy, leading to a nationwide infection wave that affected an estimated 88% of the population24. The healthcare system was overwhelmed, with PHC workers facing extreme workloads due to surging outpatient visits and home-based care demands. By mid-2023, China transitioned to an endemic management phase, with most restrictions lifted25, but PHC workers remained under pressure due to a triple epidemic of Mycoplasma pneumoniae, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and influenza26,27.

Each of these phases – the initial outbreak under strict lockdown, the prolonged zero-COVID period, and the post-2022 reopening – presented different stressors and working conditions for healthcare workers. PHC workers in particular experienced dramatic changes in their work environment, from the intense lockdown duties of early 2020, to the sustained vigilance of the zero-COVID era, and then to the challenges of a sudden surge and aftermath of policy relaxation in 2023. Study design and participants

This study follows a repeated cross-sectional design, initiated in 2020, with annual surveys planned through 2028. Surveys were conducted from August 5th to 13th in 2020 (first round), 2023 (second round), 2024 (third round), and 2025 (fourth round). In the first round, we selected one district in Jinan with a Per Capita GDP comparable to the city average. In subsequent rounds, we expanded the survey to three districts, adding one with a higher Per Capita GDP and another with a lower Per Capita GDP, ensuring a more diverse socioeconomic representation.

To recruit participants, we recommended that local administrative leaders randomly select at least 50% of PHC workers from each community health centre/station, targeting an expected response rate of 50%. Data collection was conducted through a web-based questionnaire tool, Wen-Juan-Xing (Changsha Renxing Science and Technology, Shanghai, China), allowing participants to complete the survey via computer or smartphone.

A total of 6071 PHC workers participated in the study (528 in 2020, 1860 in 2023, 2094 in 2024, and 1589 in 2025), with response rates of 53.4%, 55.2%, 56.4%, and 42.1%, respectively. After excluding low-quality responses (excessively short completion times and/or internally inconsistent answers), the final dataset included 435 responses from 2020, 1715 from 2023, 1891 from 2024, and 1412 from 2025. Additionally, by matching participants’ WeChat IDs across survey waves, we identified 724 PHC workers who completed both the 2023 and 2024 surveys, 857 who participated in both 2024 and 2025, and 494 who were present in all three rounds (2023–2025). These overlapping cohorts enable longitudinal analyses of individual-level changes over time. Check Fig. 1 for survey and sample information.

Measurements

Across all three survey rounds, we collected consistent socio-demographic information, including age, sex, job status (budgeted post vs. temporary contract labour), professional qualification certificate, technical title, highest educational degree, years of work experience, weekly working hours, working content, income, and administrative level.

In the 2023 and 2024 surveys, conducted when most of the Chinese population had been infected with COVID-19, we also gathered data on infection history and self-assessed symptom severity for both COVID-19 and influenza, recovery time for each infection, vaccination status for influenza and COVID-19, and the presence of chronic diseases.

For participants who were successfully matched across the 2023, 2024, and/or 2025 surveys, we derived secondary variables to track individual changes over time, including income changes, variations in weekly working hours, changes in the number of chronic diseases, and transitions to budgeted posts.

In this study, we draw on established constructs in organisational and occupational health research. Job satisfaction and organisational commitment are treated as core indicators of work attitudes and occupational well-being, while burnout represents a related but more strain-oriented indicator of occupational mental health. These constructs are conceptually distinct but empirically interrelated, and together they capture complementary aspects of how PHC workers experience their jobs over time. Our primary aim is not to test a specific causal model between these constructs, but rather to describe their trajectories across different policy and pandemic phases and to identify key factors associated with their levels and changes in a primary care setting.

Job satisfaction

Job satisfaction among PHC workers was assessed using the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire (MSQ) short form28. Participants rated their level of satisfaction on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (very dissatisfied) to 5 (very satisfied)29,30. The MSQ evaluates general job satisfaction, which is further categorized into intrinsic and extrinsic satisfaction30. The Chinese version of the MSQ has been widely applied in prior studies and has demonstrated strong internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s alpha ranging from 0.88 to 0.9329.

Organizational commitment

Organizational commitment was measured using the Chinese Employee’s Organizational Commitment Questionnaire (CEOCQ)31. Given the distinct characteristics of healthcare professionals, Gao et al. made minor modifications to the scale to better suit PHC workers, validating its reliability with Cronbach’s alpha values ranging from 0.78 to 0.8632. This instrument comprises 25 Likert-type questions (scored 1–5), capturing five dimensions of organizational commitment: affective commitment, normative commitment, ideal commitment, economic commitment, and choice commitment29–31.

Occupational burnout

Occupational burnout was evaluated using the Maslach Burnout Inventory Human Services Survey (MBI-HSS), a well-established tool designed to measure burnout among professionals in healthcare and human services33. Respondents rated their experiences on a 7-point Likert scale (0 = never to, 6 = every day). The MBI-HSS assesses three key dimensions of burnout: emotional exhaustion (9 items), depersonalization (5 items), and reduced personal accomplishment (8 items). The scale does not provide a combined global score, but each subscale has demonstrated high reliability, with Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.93 for emotional exhaustion, 0.89 for depersonalization, and 0.89 for reduced personal accomplishment34.

Statistics and reproducibility

We conducted both descriptive and inferential statistical analyses to assess changes in PHC workers’ job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and occupational burnout over time. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic characteristics and outcome variables across the three survey rounds. One-way ANOVA tests (two-sided) were performed to compare group differences in outcome variables across the 2020, 2023, 2024, and 2025 rounds.

For participants matched across the 2023–2024 and 2023–2024–2025 survey waves, we conducted longitudinal analyses to examine within‑individual changes in work attitudes. Multinomial logistic regression was applied to examine associations between independent variables (e.g., demographic characteristics, COVID-19 infection status, working conditions) and categorized changes in job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and burnout (i.e., increased, unchanged, or decreased). To address potential multicollinearity and reduce dimensionality, we conducted principal component analysis (PCA) using variables collected in the 2023, 2024, and 2025 rounds. We extracted five components based on eigenvalues > 1 and used varimax rotation to aid interpretability. For each component, variables with absolute loadings ≥ 0.05 could be considered to contribute meaningfully. These components were then included as predictors in multinomial logistic regression models to assess their associations with changes in the three main outcome domains and their sub-dimensions. All statistical analyses were conducted using RStudio software (version R-4.2.2), with a two-tailed significance level set at p < 0.05.

No formal a priori sample size calculation was performed, because this study was designed as a repeated cross-sectional workforce survey rather than a hypothesis-driven trial. In each survey wave, all public PHC institutions in the selected district(s) were included in the sampling frame, and local administrators were asked to recruit at least 50% of staff members from each institution. The final analytical samples included 435 participants in 2020, 1715 in 2023, 1891 in 2024, and 1494 in 2025. Matched subsamples included 724 participants for the 2023–2024 panel, 857 for the 2024–2025 panel, and 494 for the 2023–2024–2025 panel. This study did not involve biological or laboratory experiments, and therefore conventional experimental replicates were not applicable. The same validated scales were used in each round to assess job satisfaction, organisational commitment, and burnout, enabling comparison over time.

Ethic statement

The study was approved by the Medical Science Research Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital (202307001). The ethics committee approved a waiver of signature documentation, rather than a full exemption from consent. A full disclosure statement was on the questionnaire homepage so participants were fully informed beforehand, and completing the questionnaire constituted their implied consent.

The survey was fully anonymous with no personally identifiable information collected. It contained only non-sensitive content and was rated as minimal-risk research, in line with the Declaration of Helsinki and standard IRB ethical rules. Participants are free to quit at any time without any adverse consequences.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 5453 valid records were collected, including 435 in the first round (2020), 1715 in the second round (2023), 1891 in the third round (2024), and 1421 in the fourth round (Supplementary Data Sheet A). The majority of participants were female and had more than 10 years of work experience. Approximately a quarter of participant had chronic condition. More than 95% were infected with SARS-CoV-2 during the 2023 national outbreak, and approximately 35% of them also contracted influenza.

Working attitude changes

The average job satisfaction score was 80.60 in 2020, declined to 76.99 in 2023, partially recovered to 79.90 in 2024, and then decreased again to 77.59 in 2025, remaining slightly below the 2020 level (Supplementary Data Sheet B). A similar pattern was observed across all subgroups and for both dimensions of job satisfaction (extrinsic and intrinsic), with a marked decline in 2023, partial recovery in 2024, and a subsequent decrease in 2025 (Fig. 2). The only exception to this trend was observed among PHC workers with less than one year of work experience, namely new employees. Specifically, those recruited in 2023 reported significantly higher job satisfaction compared to their counterparts recruited in 2020, with an average difference of approximately 9 points. ANOVA tests confirmed a statistically significant difference in job satisfaction across the four years.

Organizational commitment among PHC workers increased steadily from 2020 to 2024, rising from 90.75 in 2020 to 93.61 in 2023 and 96.62 in 2024, before slightly decreasing to 95.61 in 2025 (Supplementary Data Sheet A). Subscale analyses of affective, normative, ideal, and economic commitment showed patterns consistent with the overall trend. The only exception was choice commitment, which continued to increase across all survey waves. Although choice commitment remained the lowest of the five dimensions throughout the study period, it also demonstrated the largest relative improvement since 2020 (Fig. 2). In contrast, other dimensions remained around 19 points across all survey rounds. The 55+ age group was an outlier in the trend, as their organizational commitment in 2023 was notably lower than in 2020, with an average decline of approximately 13 points. In addition, between the 2024 and 2025 survey rounds, newly recruited PHC workers (with less than one year of work experience) represented another exception: their organizational commitment in 2025 was higher than in the previous survey wave. ANOVA tests confirmed statistically significant differences in organizational commitment and its dimensions across the three survey periods.

Reduced personal accomplishment remained relatively stable over time, with mean scores of 31.15 in 2023, 31.26 in 2024, and 30.29 in 2025, and the difference was not statistically significant. By contrast, depersonalization increased significantly, from 4.17 in 2023 to 4.47 in 2024 and 5.64 in 2025. A similar worsening pattern was observed for emotional exhaustion, with mean scores rising from 15.79 in 2023 and 15.59 in 2024 to 17.25 in 2025, indicating a progressive deterioration in these two burnout dimensions. Longitudinal analysis of matched participants

By matching WeChat IDs of participants from the 2023 and 2024 surveys, we identified 724 individuals who participated in both rounds, with demographic characteristics comparable to the overall sample. The changes in job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and burnout followed a similar pattern to those observed in the full sample. Additionally, we calculated changes in demographic characteristics, such as income level and weekly working hours, to explore their potential influence on these outcomes. Supplementary Data Sheet B provides detailed information on these variable and result changes. We also provided ANOVA test results of matched participants at Supplementary Table S2.

We employed multinomial logistic regression, categorizing job satisfaction into three groups: increased, unchanged, or decreased. The results indicated that COVID-19 infection status was the primary factor influencing job satisfaction, with severe infections significantly increasing the likelihood of decline. Work experience beyond one year was associated with a higher probability of unchanged job satisfaction, while income changes, weekly working hours, promotion, and influenza infection were not statistically significant. For organizational commitment, COVID-19 infection was again the most relevant factor, primarily linked to unchanged commitment levels. Additionally, affective commitment was more stable among those with 2–10 years of experience, while normative commitment was influenced by COVID-19 infection. No significant associations were found for the remaining dimensions. For occupational burnout, COVID-19 infection in 2023 was the only significant factor affecting changes in burnout levels. However, serious multicollinearity was detected among several variables, which may affect the interpretation of some associations. Further details and full regression results are provided in Supplementary Figs. S1–S12.

To obtain more stable and interpretable estimates, we then applied principal component analysis (PCA) to this set of correlated variables and derived a smaller number of orthogonal components that captured the main underlying patterns of sociodemographic, employment, and infection-related characteristics. These PCA-derived components were subsequently entered into the regression models, which collectively accounting for 22% of the total variance, as summarised predictors of work attitudes and burnout. The Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant (χ² = 9562.3, p < 0.001), indicating a non-random correlation structure among covariates and supporting exploratory dimensionality reduction analyses:

1) Component I: PHC workers aged 46 + , employed on temporary contracts, with more than two years of work experience.

2) Component II: Male traditional Chinese medicine doctors who had asymptomatic COVID-19 and influenza infections in 2023.

3) Component III: Public health workers who also had asymptomatic COVID-19 and influenza infections in 2023.

4) Component IV: Workers aged 36–45 with both children and elderly dependents at home.

5) Component V: Newly recruited public health workers who experienced severe COVID-19 and influenza symptoms in 2023 and had children.

Discussion

As far as we are aware, this study provides novel evidence on the evolving work attitudes of PHC workers in China, with a particular focus on post-pandemic recovery after COVID-19 was no longer classified as a public health emergency35. Our studies quantitively testified higher risk of infection of PHC workers, compared with ordinary population: more than 97% of participants were infected by SARS-CoV-2, whereas the prevalence of ordinary population was estimated as 88%24.

Due to the unpredictability of the pandemic, we were unable to collect data on PHC workers’ job satisfaction prior to the COVID-19 outbreak or during the national restriction period. However, a systematic review of job satisfaction among PHC workers between 2009 and 2014 reported average scores ranging from 3.3 to 3.5 on a standardized 5-point scale36, which are lower than the levels observed in our 2020, 2023, 2024, and 2025 surveys. We believe that increased financial and psychological support from the government, as well as widespread societal appreciation, contributed to the higher levels of professional fulfilment and job satisfaction observed among frontline medical staff during the early stages of the pandemic—an effect also noted in previous studies37,38. It is worth noting that our study was conducted in Jinan, where the number of COVID-19 cases was very low in 2020, and there were no major infection waves among PHC workers at that time. As a result, although the lockdown policies disrupted daily life, they may have temporarily reduced PHC workers’ workloads, and the direct impact of the virus was minimal. In contrast, during the nationwide outbreak that followed, a large proportion of PHC workers were infected, and their workload significantly increased. We believe that this surge in infections and the subsequent strain on the healthcare system had a substantial negative association with job satisfaction. Even as working conditions gradually normalized, the fact that satisfaction levels remained lower than in 2020 suggests that some of the stressors introduced during the pandemic may have had enduring effects on PHC workers’ morale.

Second, organisational commitment increased steadily from 2020 to 2024 and then showed a slight decline in 2025. However, choice commitment—reflecting concerns about job security and challenges in re-employment—remained the lowest of all dimensions, while also showing the greatest improvement over time. Compared with a national survey on PHC workers, which reported scores of 90.0 for overall commitment and 19.3, 19.7, 17.7, 17.4, and 16.0 for the five sub-dimensions, respectively39, our 2020 survey showed higher levels of ideal and economic commitment, while choice commitment was comparatively lower. We attribute this pattern to the increased financial and psychological support provided to PHC workers during the initial COVID-19 outbreak. The lower choice commitment in 2020 may suggest that primary care providers gained greater confidence in their professional value and were less fearful of switching jobs, as their skills were in high demand during the pandemic. In fact, increased turnover among healthcare workers was reported in many countries during the early stages of the pandemic40,41. However, such turnover waves were not widely observed in China17. Our findings—particularly the rise in choice commitment in 2023, 2024, and 2025—support this observation. Since most primary care providers in China are employed on contract terms rather than as permanent staff, one possible explanation is that PHC jobs, with relatively stable salaries and increased social recognition, became more attractive during the pandemic, especially compared to the private sector, which experienced economic slowdown and job insecurity42.

Occupational burnout levels in 2023 and 2024 remained largely unchanged, suggesting persistent stress despite policy shifts. Unlike job satisfaction, which showed signs of recovery, burnout levels did not significantly decrease, reflecting prolonged mental and physical strain on PHC workers. In 2025, reduced personal accomplishment score declined slightly (with no statistically significant difference across the three years in ANOVA), whereas depersonalisation and r emotional exhaustion increased significantly. One possible explanation is that, after the acute COVID-19 phase ended and work gradually returned to a more routine pattern, PHC work may have become more monotonous and less “extraordinary,” reducing the sense of novelty and recognition while leaving underlying workload and structural pressures largely unchanged.

Our findings may also be interpreted in light of the Job Demands–Resources (JD-R) model43. From this perspective, PHC workers’ job satisfaction, organisational commitment, and burnout reflect the dynamic balance between job demands and available resources over time. During the early phases of the pandemic, increased social recognition, policy attention, and a strong sense of professional mission may have temporarily functioned as additional job resources, helping to sustain satisfaction and commitment despite high demands. However, as the acute crisis faded and work gradually returned to routine practice, these resources may have weakened, while workload, organisational pressures, and role strain remained. This imbalance may help explain why job satisfaction did not fully recover and why some dimensions of burnout, particularly depersonalisation and emotional exhaustion, worsened in 2025. Although our study was not designed to formally test the JD-R model, the observed patterns are broadly consistent with the view that persistent demands without sustained organisational resources can undermine the long-term wellbeing of PHC workers44.

At last, we would like to stress that although we analyse job satisfaction, organisational commitment, and burnout as separate outcomes, they should be interpreted as interrelated dimensions of PHC workers’ occupational well-being and mental health rather than as completely independent constructs.

The analysis of 724 matched participants identified COVID-19 infection status as a major determinant of changes in job satisfaction and organizational commitment. Experiencing severe COVID-19 symptoms was significantly associated with a greater likelihood of declining job satisfaction, indicating that personal health challenges may have intensified workplace dissatisfaction. In addition, newly recruited public health workers were more frequently referenced in PCA results, suggesting that, compared with experienced PHC workers and clinical doctors, they were more vulnerable to the pandemic and related policy changes.

Although we included nearly all widely recognized factors associated with work attitudes during the design phase of this study, including gender, age, work experience, and income change3,45,46, the results revealed potential omitted variable bias. Specifically, the top five components extracted from the PCA explained only about 22% of the total variance, suggesting that key factors may have been missing. This implies that while the conventional variables are associated with the overall levels of job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and occupational burnout, they may not be the primary determinants of changes in these attitudes over time. Indeed, using the same dataset, we previously found that these demographic and employment-related factors were significantly associated with the levels of job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and their respective dimensions29,30. However, the generally high baseline levels of these outcomes—such as the job satisfaction scores exceeding pre-COVID benchmarks—may have resulted in a ceiling effect, limiting the potential for further improvement. Moreover, our interviews with selected participants from the 2020 and 2023 rounds suggest that changes in work attitudes are more likely influenced by broader organizational and societal factors. Differences in motivation policies, support services, and workplace culture across institutions and districts may have played a critical role. Additionally, the broader economic slowdown likely influenced self-perceptions of working in the PHC sector, in line with the two-factor theory of motivation. Although our analysis is structured around major COVID-19 policy phases, we do not assume that the pandemic and related policies were the only or even the dominant stressors for all PHC workers. In practice, PHC staff were simultaneously exposed to multiple sources of strain, including local organisational reforms, fluctuating service volumes, economic uncertainty at the household level, and family caregiving responsibilities. Our repeated cross-sectional design allows us to describe how work attitudes and burnout evolved across key policy phases, but it cannot disentangle the independent effects of different stressors. The temporal associations we report should therefore be interpreted as patterns that coincide with, rather than being solely caused by, specific COVID-19 policies.

These findings have important policy and workforce management implications. For health policymakers, the partial recovery of job satisfaction indicates that short-term emergency support is not sufficient; more sustained investment is needed to improve working conditions, strengthen mental health services for PHC staff, and expand opportunities for career development and continuing education. For local health commissions and institutional managers, the steady increase in organisational commitment suggests that this period may provide an opportunity to consolidate workforce stability through clearer promotion pathways, fairer compensation mechanisms, and more supportive supervision. At the same time, the persistence of burnout, and the worsening of some burnout dimensions in 2025, highlights the need for more active management responses, including better workload allocation, more flexible staffing arrangements, and regular monitoring of staff well-being. Administrators should also pay particular attention to groups that may be more vulnerable to prolonged strain, such as newly recruited staff and workers facing high service demands, and provide targeted support to improve retention and long-term workforce resilience.

A major strength of this study is its repeated cross-sectional design, allowing us to capture workforce trends across multiple pandemic phases. Additionally, the matched cohort analysis provided deeper insights into individual-level changes over time. However, this study also has some limitations. First, the use of self-reported surveys may introduce recall bias or social desirability bias. Second, while we accounted for demographic and employment-related factors, other unmeasured contextual or policy variables could have influenced job satisfaction, commitment, and burnout. Finally, our findings are based on PHC workers in Jinan, which may limit generalizability to other regions with different healthcare structures or policy responses.

Although we adjusted for a wide range of individual-level sociodemographic and job-related characteristics and further reduced multicollinearity through PCA, residual confounding is still likely. We did not have detailed data on workplace climate, managerial practices, staffing levels, or family caregiving responsibilities, all of which may influence both exposure to stressors and work attitudes. Our findings should therefore be interpreted as reflecting associations after adjustment for key measured factors, but not as fully controlling for all possible sources of bias.

With regard to generalisability, our data are drawn from public PHC institutions in one large city in eastern China, and the policy context may differ from other regions. As such, the findings are most generalisable to urban public PHC systems in similar socio-economic and policy settings, and should be extrapolated to rural areas, private primary care providers, or other countries with caution. Nevertheless, Jinan shares many organisational features with other Chinese cities, and the multi-district design increases internal heterogeneity, which supports the relevance of our results for understanding post-pandemic workforce challenges in comparable PHC systems. Future research should incorporate more detailed measures of workplace environment, managerial practices, staffing conditions, and family responsibilities to better account for potential confounding factors. Multi-site studies covering rural areas, private providers, and different regional policy contexts would also help improve the generalisability of the findings.

Conclusion

As far as we are aware, this study provides novel evidence on the evolving work attitudes of PHC workers in China, particularly in the post-pandemic recovery phase. The findings suggest that job satisfaction is recovering, organizational commitment is rising, and burnout levels remain a persistent concern. COVID-19 infection status and work experience played key roles in shaping these outcomes. These insights emphasize the need for sustained workforce support, mental health interventions, and employment stability measures to ensure PHC workforce resilience in the face of future public health challenges.

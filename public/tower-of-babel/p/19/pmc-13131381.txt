JACC: Basic to Translational Science 2025 Thomas Force Young Investigator Award Winner
Authors: Matthias Nahrendorf
Published: 2026

This is the sixth consecutive year for the Thomas Force award, which recognizes a young investigator who is the primary author for a paper published in JACC: Basic to Translational Science. The award honors not only the young investigator, but also the program in which he or she is working.

This year, we received an impressive group of nominees from exceptional mentors. The editors would like to thank all of the mentors for nominating and advocating for their trainees. The caliber of both the nominees and their mentors was extraordinary, making it a challenging task to select a finalist for the award.

Because the voting was close, JACC: Basic to Translational Science chose an honorary mention:

Reo Hata, MD, PhD

Mentor: Yoshinori Yoshida, MD, PhD

Paper: Integrative Transcriptomic-Histological Analysis in Dilated Cardiomyopathy Unveils FGFR1 Inhibition as Anti-Cardiac Fibrotic and Cardioprotective Therapy

JACC: Basic to Translational Science is proud to recognize and congratulate Dr Mariam Meddeb from Dr David Kass’s laboratory as the 2025 Thomas Force Young Investigator Awardee, for his paper entitled: ATP-Citrate Lyase Supports Cardiac Function and NAD+/NADH Balance and Is Depressed in Human Failing Myocardium.

Mariam Meddeb, MD

Dr Mariam Meddeb is an Assistant Professor of Cardiology and Critical Care Medicine at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine. She received her medical degree and Master of Science training in physiology and molecular biology from the University of Strasbourg, France, and completed advanced cardiovascular and critical care training at Johns Hopkins, where she also pursued postdoctoral research in the laboratory of Dr David Kass.

Dr Meddeb’s research program investigates the molecular determinants of myocardial vulnerability to stress, with a particular focus on cardiac metabolism and lipid biology in heart failure. Informed by her clinical practice in the Coronary Care Unit and Cardiac Surgical Intensive Care Unit, her work seeks to define why patients with metabolic dysfunction exhibit heightened susceptibility to acute decompensation and whether restoring metabolic resilience can improve outcomes.

Her award-winning study identifies ATP-citrate lyase as a key regulator of cardiomyocyte redox balance and mitochondrial function, linking disordered NAD+/NADH homeostasis to impaired myocardial reserve in experimental models and human heart failure.

Mentor: David Kass, MD

David Kass, MD, is the Abraham and Virginia Weiss Professor of Cardiology, and Professor of Medicine, Biomedical Engineering, and Physiology, Pharmacology, and Therapeutics at Johns Hopkins University. He joined the faculty at Hopkins in 1986 and has been there ever since. His career has been dedicated to understanding mechanical, cellular, and molecular mechanisms for myocardial diseases and developing novel therapies to treat them. He started exploring chamber-level biomechanics and systems physiology in humans and in large animal models of heart disease. This later led to pioneering the pacemaker treatment for heart failure known as cardiac resynchronization therapy. In the early 2000s, he turned more molecular and cellular, elucidating underlying mechanisms of CRT, and studying cyclic GMP and protein kinase G signaling to identify novel pathways and mechanisms by which they are activated, are modified, and can be leveraged as therapeutics. More recently, his laboratory has returned to studies of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, grounded on molecular/cellular data from human heart failure with preserved ejection fraction myocardium. He has >545 original papers, garnering over 63,000 citations, and over his 40+ years at Hopkins has mentored over 110 postdoctoral fellows/graduate students in his laboratory.

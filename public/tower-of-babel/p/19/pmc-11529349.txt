Haglund's Deformity as a Cause of Achilles Tendon Sleeve Avulsion: A Case Report
Authors: Pramudita Angga Kartika, Tresna Angga Basunanda
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:The incidence of complete achilles tendon rupture is 18 per 100.000 patient-years and is usually presents to GP. Haglund’s deformity is believed to be a factor in achilles tendon rupture.Case Report:A 47-year-old soccer athlete came to the police clinic with complaints of pain in his left ankle after playing soccer 2 days ago. Pain was felt after the patient's left leg holds the body when going to shoot by a popping sound on the patient's left leg. The patient came after consulting with the paramedics and suspected of sprain. The results of vital sign was normal, VAS 4, BMI 24,9 kg/m2, ROM ankle joint was limited, thompson test (+) matle test (+), there was a bump on the right and left above the heel for more than 5 years show in figure 1 and 2. The patient was referred for an X-ray. From the results of the X-rays it was concluded that there was an Achilles tendon sleeve avulsion.Discussion:We report that there was an achilles tendon rupture in a soccer athlete with ideal BMI and no history of severe trauma. A ruptured Achilles tendon at the calcaneus attachment, which does not include a bone that can be fixed, is called “sleeve avulsion”, like in our case. Acute Achilles tendon rupture usually occurs at the watershed region. In the current case, the achilles tendon rupture occurred directly at the level of its distal insertion. These findings suggest that the recurrent stress load of the Achilles tendon, in particularly athletes, could be a trigger factor for Haglund’s deformity. A chronic inflammatory process reduces achilles tendon strength and can lead to tendon rupture, even in the presence of minor trauma. The first additional test that can be performed to detect a achilles tendon rupture is the thompson test. Thompson test is reported to have a sensitivity of 96% and a specificity of 93%. The next physical examination that can be used is the Matles test. The sensitivity and specificity of this test were reported to be 88% and 85% respectively.Conclusion:Underestimating Haglund's deformity can harm the patient, one of which is the occurrence of achilles tendon rupture. Anamnesa and physical examination for suspected achilles tendon rupture are very necessary, considering that most patients with achilles tendon rupture come to general practitioners. Increasing general practitioner knowledge about trauma cases in athletes will have a positive effect in the management of sports injury cases.

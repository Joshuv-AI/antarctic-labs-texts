Clinical-pathologic classification of anti-HMGCR-positive immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy
Authors: Yuyan Cao, Wei Li, Xiongjun He, Meiqi Liao, Kexin Hu, Shenghao Wu, Xin Zhang, Qianyi Liao, Ziqi Shen, Zhaoxin Liang, Chaoren Zheng, Zhuocheng Zhong, Haishan Jiang, Qin Huang, Hui Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnti-HMGCR-positive immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM), also anti-HMGCR myopathy, was initially considered as an exclusively skeletal muscular disease, observed in elderly patients with an acute onset. However, there is an increasing number of patients presenting greater clinical diversity than previously recognized. The aim of this study is to establish a comprehensive clinical-pathological classification for these patients, capturing the spectrum of disease presentations including both typical and atypical cases. We conducted a retrospective analysis and follow-up study of the clinical features, pathological manifestations, and treatment outcomes of 18 anti-HMGCR myopathy patients from Nanfang Hospital, along with data from 61 patients reported before. We defined 31 patients who presented with extra-muscular symptoms as overlap patients, while those who had merely muscular symptoms as non-overlap patients. Overlap patients had higher MMT-score, less muscular symptoms, specifically muscle weakness, and more extra-muscular symptoms, including ILD, arthralgia, weight loss and mostly skin involvement. Based on clinical, pathological, and genetic criteria, 12 peculiar patients were classified as MD-like patients. These cases, with earlier onset and longer duration, hardly had myalgia and extra-muscular symptoms, particularly skin involvement. Our study highlights the clinical and pathological heterogeneity of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM. We identified a distinct subgroup of overlap patients, as well as MD-like patients. Background

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathy (IIM), also known as myositis, encompasses a heterogeneous group of autoimmune diseases that involve skeletal muscle and multiple extra-muscular organs such as skin, lungs, joints, and heart1.

IIM was initially identified as only polymyositis (PM) and dermatomyositis (DM). However, the classification of IIM has been refined2,3 and now includes immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM), which was distinguished from polymyositis by the European Neuromuscular Centre (ENMC) in 2004. IMNM is characterized by predominant proximal muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase (CK) levels, and severe myo-fiber necrosis with little or no inflammatory cell infiltration based on muscle biopsy4. Myositis-specific antibodies (MSAs), specifically anti-signal recognition particle (anti-SRP) antibody and anti-3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A reductase (anti-HMGCR) antibody, are associated with IMNM and in 2017 were included in the diagnostic criteria for IMNM5–7. However, in approximately one-third of IMNM patients, these myositis-specific antibodies (MSAs) cannot be detected in the serum, and these cases are referred to as “seronegative” IMNM7.

Anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM, also known as anti-HMGCR myopathy, was initially observed in elderly patients with an acute or sub-acute duration, most with statin exposure6,8. However, without statin exposure, prevalence was found to be related to geographic region, genetic background, and patient age9–13. Anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM was previously considered to be a skeletal muscular disease, exclusive of extra-muscular involvement. Interestingly, more and more cases have been reported with diverse features, especially the patients with coexistence of other MSAs and/or myositis-associated antibodies (MAAs)14–16. Moreover, an increasing number of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients with slow onset has been identified. These patients unexpectedly present with proximally predominant weakness of the shoulder and hip girdle musculature due to muscle fiber dysfunction. These patients have a chronic and slowly progressive disease course from their early age and resemble limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, which is a group of genetic disorders characterized by progressive muscle weakness and wasting, diagnosed across all ages17–22. Unexpectedly, no muscular dystrophy relevant gene mutation was detected in these patients. As a result, pediatric patients resembling limb-girdle muscular dystrophy were regarded as a special phenotype of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM18. The different clinical-pathological manifestations of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM likely provide opportunities for improvement in the precision of disease classification.

The various clinical features of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients have all been published previously. However, the reports lacked detailed analysis of a diversity of symptoms, the coexistence of overlap MSAs in these patients, and as such further considerations of clinical and pathologic characteristics are needed.

In this study, we retrospectively screened our hospital for anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients. These patients, together with previous reported patients were analyzed in order to develop, for the first time, an essential and easily available clinical-pathologic classification of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM.

Methods

Patients

We reviewed anti-HMGCR antibody positive patients from Nanfang Hospital hospitalized between January 2019 and December 2021, and collected patients’ clinical features, laboratory finding, and pathologic manifestations. Clinical data from other anti-HMGCR antibody positive patients, reported in previous studies, were also reviewed and included as part of the data set. Specifically, we gathered data from 12 Chinese patients in Xiangya hospital14, 21 Chinese patients in Huashan hospital15, 13 Swede patients in Karolinska University Hospital16, 9 Japanese cases in National Center of Neurology and Psychiatry17, and 6 American cases in National Institutes of Health18.

Inclusion criteria

We enrolled confirmed IIM patients from Nanfang hospital who fulfilled the 2017 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for IIM23. Patients’ blood samples were obtained for the detection of antibodies. The 2018 European Neuromuscular Centre criteria for IMNM were applied to patients with anti-HMGCR antibody7.

Detection of antibodies

Antibody analysis was detected by EUROLINE Autoimmune Inflammatory Myopathies 16 Ag and cN-1 A et HMGCR (IgG), including MSAs: anti-HMGCR, anti-EJ, anti-Jo-1, anti-MDA5, anti-Mi-2, anti-NXP2, anti-OJ, anti-PL-7, anti-PL-12, anti-SAE, anti-SRP, and anti-TIF1-γ, and MAAs: anti-Ku, anti-PM-Scl 75, anti-PM-Scl 100, and anti-Ro52. The titer of the anti-HMGCR antibody was categorized as weakly positive (+), moderately positive (++), or strongly positive (+++) based on the signal intensity, in accordance with the manufacturer’s specifications. Serum samples from patients with anti-HMGCR autoantibodies were confirmed by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and immunoprecipitation. Anti-nuclear antibodies (ANA) were detected in all patients by indirect immunofluorescence using HEp-2 cells with a starting dilution of 1:80. Patient with dilution of 1:320, 1:160, 1:80 were respectively regarded as strongly positive (+++), moderately positive (++) and weakly positive (+).

Muscle pathology

Muscle biopsies were obtained from the quadriceps of 15 patients from Nanfang Hospital at the time of diagnosis. The other 3 patients had undergone muscle biopsies to confirm the presence of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM in other hospital. Serial 5-µm frozen sections were collected and stained with hematoxylin-eosin, NADH-tetrazolium reductase, modified Gomori’s trichrome, periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), oil red O (ORO), succinate dehydrogenase, and cytochrome C oxidase. Immunohistochemistry staining was performed for: dysferlin, dystrophin, α-sarcoglycans to δ-sarcoglycans, major histocompatibility complex class Ⅰ (MHC-Ⅰ), CD4, CD8, CD68, CD79, and the membrane attack complex (MAC).

Muscle biopsies were assessed for the following three aspects; myofiber type pathology, muscle tissue pathology, and inflammation. For myofiber pathology, the percentage of myo-fibers with atrophy, necrosis, regeneration, heterogeneity (defined as the totality of myofibers with atrophy, necrosis, and regeneration) and central nucleus were assessed, by manually counting the total number of myo-fibers. Muscular tissue pathology included the following aspects: perimysial atrophy (also perifascicular atrophy, refers to the atrophy of muscle fibers located specifically at the periphery of muscle fascicles) and endomysial fibrosis. The characteristics of inflammation were categorized into: (1) distribution of inflammatory cells as endomysial and/or perimysial; (2) distribution of MAC deposition as on sarcolemma and/or capillaries. (3) up-regulation of MHC-Ⅰ as focal and/or diffuse.

Treatment and prognosis

All patients received treatment that included: mono therapy with glucocorticoids (GC), dual therapy with GC and immunosuppressives (IS), as well as triple therapy with GC, IS and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg). Response to treatment was recorded as improvement in manual muscle testing (MMT-8) and reduction in CK24. The MMT-8 assesses eight specific muscles unilaterally, including neck flexors, deltoid, biceps, gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, quadriceps, wrist extensors and ankle dorsiflexors, with a possible score range of 0–80. For lack of common understanding of IIM patients’ response to treatment, patients’ response was recorded based on changes in CK and MMT-8 and we defined patients whose CK reduced to ≤ 500 IU/L and/or MMT-8 score improved ≥ 5 as responder. Since there is no consensus standard for evaluation of a patient’s prognosis, we defined patients who were responder without relapse as a good prognosis. Patients with disease activity or relapses were defined as a poor prognosis. Duration of observation was limited to 9 months.

Criteria for subtype of MD-like patients

MD-like anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients in our hospital were identified based on strict evaluation of clinical symptoms, pathological features and genetic test. (1) Clinical symptoms: Pediatric or teenager patients suffer from proximal muscle weakness with slowly progressive disease course. (2) Pathological features: Muscle biopsy shows severe myofiber atrophy, considerable myofibers of central nucleus and increased endomysial fibrosis, consisting with characteristic of chronic myopathy. No diagnostic changes were detected in known muscular dystrophy proteins on immunostaining. (3) Genetic test: DNA samples were obtained from peripheral blood lymphocytes using standard phenol–chloroform procedures, and whole exome sequence (WES) testing was performed by Beijing Genomics Institute (BGI) Co., Ltd (Shenzhen, China) to exclude the possibility of hereditary myopathy, including but not limited to Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD), facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD), LGMD, myotonic dystrophy, congenital myopathies, congenital myotonia and mitochondrial myopathies. No pathogenically relevant variants were detected.

Statistical analysis

SPSS software (version 24.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Qualitative variables were expressed as percentage and absolute frequency, with quantitative variables reported as means ± standard deviation. Comparison of qualitative data was performed by Chi-squared test or Yate’s correction test or Fisher’s exact test. Comparison of quantitative data between two independent samples was performed by Mann-Whitney U test or Student’s t test (two-tailed). Analysis of multiple independent samples was performed by ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis test. Furthermore, univariable Logistic Regression was used to identify predictors of prognosis. Overall, p < 0.05 was considered significantly different.

The age of onset for these patients ranged from 19 to 73 years of age (means ± standard deviation, 40.61 ± 18.28 years). Eleven patients (11/18, 61.11%) were female and seven patients (7/18, 38.89%) were male. The median duration from symptom emergence to diagnosis for anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM was 9.50 months (1month-132 months).

Seventeen patients (17/18, 94.44%) complained of muscular symptoms, sixteen patients (16/18, 88.89%) had muscle weakness, five patients (5/18, 27.78%) had myalgia, two patients (2/18, 11.1%) had dyspnea and two patients (2/18, 11.11%) had dysphagia. Seven patients (7/18, 38.9%) had extra-muscular symptoms. Among them, four patients (4/18, 22.22%) had arthralgia and four patients (4/18, 22.22%) had weight loss, three patients (3/18, 16.67%) had a rash, two patients (2/18, 11.11%) had interstitial lung disease (ILD), and one patient (1/18, 5.56%) had cardiac involvement. Nasopharyngeal carcinoma was found in one patient (1/18, 5.56%) during outpatient follow-up. One patient (1/18, 5.56%) had an exposure history to rosuvastatin.

All patients were evaluated for muscle strength based on the MMT-8. The score for theMMT-8 ranged from 49 to 80 (65.94 ± 8.17). CK levels were elevated in 17 patients (17/18, 94.44%), while one patient (1/18, 5.56%) remained normal. The serum CK level ranged from 36 to 23,462 IU/L (7341.22 ± 7177.24 IU/L).

Anti-HMGCR was strongly positive (+++) for ten patients (10/18, 55.56%), moderately positive (++) for four patients (4/18, 22.22%) and weakly positive (+) for four patients (4/18, 22.22%). Anti-HMGCR antibody overlap with other antibodies was observed in eight patients (8/18, 44.44%). The coexistence of anti-HMGCR antibody with one or more MSA and/or MAA was detected in all eight of these patients (8/18, 44.44%), including anti-SAE1 antibody (1/8, 12.50%), anti-MDA5 antibody (1/8, 12.50%), anti-NXP2 antibody (1/8, 12.50%), anti-PL-7 antibody (1/8, 12.50%), anti-Ku antibody (2/8, 25.00%), anti-OJ antibody (2/8, 25.00%), anti-PM-Scl-75 antibody (2/8, 25.00%), anti-Ro-52antibody (4/8, 50.00%), and anti-SRP antibody (4/8, 50.00%). ANA were detected in two patients (2/8, 25.00%).

Pathologic manifestations of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients

Muscle biopsy results for 15 patients are presented in Table 1. As for myofiber type pathology, the percentage of myofibers with atrophy, necrosis, regeneration, heterogeneity, and central nucleus was a range of 2%−34%, 0–19%, 1%−15%, 9–45%, and 1%−22%, respectively.

Regarding muscle tissue pathology, three patients (3/15, 20.00%) had endomysial fibrosis, three patients (3/15, 20.00%) had vasculitis, one patient (1/15, 6.67%) had perimysial atrophy.

With respect to inflammation, eleven patients (11/15, 73.33%) had endomysial inflammatory cell infiltration, while four patients (4/15, 26.67%) had perimysial inflammatory cell infiltration. Fourteen patients (14/15, 93.33%) had focal distribution of MHC-Ⅰ, while one patient (1/15, 6.67%) had diffuse distribution of MHC-Ⅰ. Sarcolemma MAC deposition was observed in all muscle biopsies (15/15, 100.00%), while capillary MAC positivity was only observed in two patients (2/15, 13.33%). Immunohistochemistry showed that CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, and CD68+ macrophages were the most common inflammatory cells. No diagnostic mutations of known LGMD associated proteins were observed by immunostaining.

Treatment and response of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients

All 18 patients received treatment (Table 1). Nine patients (9/18, 50.00%) were treated with dual-therapy of GC and IS. Seven patients (7/18, 38.89%) received triple-therapy of GC, IS, and IVIg. Two patients (2/18, 11.11%) received mono-therapy with GC. After treatment, myalgia and extra-muscular symptoms disappeared in all patients. All patients had a reduction in CK. Serum CK levels after treatment ranged from 15 to 5253 IU/L (1622.33 ± 1462.54 IU/L), with a reduction range of 31.42%−92.74% (71.29%±18.86%). Except for two patients with normal muscle strength, 14 patients (14/18, 77.8%) had variable muscle strength development, while two patients (2/18, 11.11%) exhibited stabilization. MMT-8 scores after treatment varied from 49 to 80 (70.94 ± 8.50), with an improvement variation of 0–16 (5.00 ± 3.99).

Comparison of non-overlap and overlap anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients

In our cohort, there were nearly half of patients had extra-muscular symptoms, especially skin involvement. These cases, mostly later onset, seemed to present mild muscle weakness and even normal muscle strength, corresponding with less necrotizing and regenerating myofibers on muscle biopsy. Interestingly, nearly all of them had other antibodies than anti-HMGCR antibody.

To verify our observations, we reviewed patients diagnosed with anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM who had been reported in previous researches. The geographic, demographic, clinical and serological statistics were collected from 61 cases in other 5 centers (Table 2). Six patients (6/12, 50.0%) in Xiangya hospital, eight patients (8/21, 38.1%) in Huashan hospital and eight patients (8/13, 61.5%) in Karolinska University Hospital presented with extra-muscular symptoms, such as skin rash and ILD. Specially, Japanese pediatric patients, with early onset (7.22 ± 3.90, years), in National Center of Neurology and Psychiatry were incorporated. Moreover, adolescent cases presenting limb-girdle muscle weakness with early onset (17.17 ± 6.68, years) and chronic duration (155.00 ± 97.96, months) in National Institutes of Health were also included.

Excluding patients who met the criteria for MD-like subtype, we defined patients who had extra-muscular symptoms as overlap anti-HMGCR IMNM, while we confirmed patients with only muscular symptoms as non-overlap anti-HMGCR IMNM. According to our definition, 28 patients were classified into overlap subtype and 40 patients were divided into non-overlap subtype.

There are clinical, pathological and therapeutic differences between two subgroups. The comparison of clinical data is presented (Table 3). Non-overlap anti-HMGCR patients had a higher frequency of muscular symptoms (100.00% vs. 87.10%, p = 0.026), especially muscle weakness (100.00% vs. 74.19%, p = 0.001), with lower MMT-8 scores (64.23 ± 4.36 vs. 72.14 ± 7.34, p = 0.002) than overlap patients. Furthermore, overlap anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients had a higher prevalence of extra-muscular symptoms (100.00% vs. 0.00%, p < 0.0001), including ILD (29.03% vs. 0.00%, p < 0.001), arthralgia (25.81% vs. 0.00%, p = 0.001), weight loss (25.81% vs. 0.00%, p = 0.001)), and skin involvement (77.42% vs. 0.00%, p < 0.001) than non-overlap patients. Moreover, overlap patients had higher frequency of additional MSA/MAA (63.33% vs. 11.43%, p < 0.0001), including anti-Ro52 (20.00% vs. 0.00%, p = 0.042) and anti-MDA5 (15.63% vs. 0.00%, p = 0.017).

A comparison of patients with a good and a poor prognosis was performed. By univariable analysis, overlap antibody (B = 21.0, p = 0.024) was found to be a good prognostic predictor, while MMT-8 score was not (B = 1.047, p = 0.571). ROC analysis demonstrated a good performance for overlap antibody as a predictor of prognosis, with an AUC of 0.813 (p = 0.036, 95%CI, 0.585-1.000) (Fig. 3). A cut-off value had a sensitivity of 75.0% and specificity of 87.5%.

Discussion

In this study, we collected clinical and pathologic data from anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients in our hospital and review patients’ data from previous studies. In this manner we developed an essential and easily available clinical-pathological classification of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM.

In our center, nearly half of the patients exhibited extra-muscular symptoms, particularly skin involvement. These cases, mostly later onset, generally showed mild muscle weakness or even normal muscle strength. To further investigate our findings, we gathered clinical data from patients diagnosed with anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM, as reported in previous studies. Patients presenting with extra-muscular symptoms were classified as overlap patients, while those with solely muscular symptoms were categorized as non-overlap patients.

Comparative analysis confirmed that overlap patients generally had higher muscle strength and less muscular symptoms, particularly muscle weakness. Furthermore, distinguished from general muscular symptoms, overlap patients were found to more resemble dermatomyositis, presenting with extra-muscular symptoms, including ILD, arthralgia, weight loss and mostly skin involvement.

Despite the striking similarity to dermatomyositis clinical symptoms, all muscle biopsies of overlap patients conformed to the manifestation of IMNM14–16. That’s the reason why these overlap patients were diagnosed with IMNM rather than dermatomyositis or polymyositis. These patients in our cohort present with less myofibers of heterogeneity, especially atrophy and necrosis. Reduced myofiber injury may explain why overlap patients had milder muscular symptoms.

In terms of prognosis, overlap anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients tend to have a good prognosis compared to non-overlap patients. Better prognosis in overlap patients may be explained by milder muscle injury.

Anti-HMGCR antibody was previously considered unique in IMNM in that MSAs are mutually exclusive to one another25–27, but a growing number of anti-HMGCR-IMNM patients with the coexistence of other MSAs or MAAs has been reported14–16. In our researches, additional MSA/MAAs were universally found in overlap patients, anti-Ro52 and anti-MDA5 antibody in particular. Moreover, extra-muscular symptoms in overlap patients mostly corresponded to other MSAs. Similar to the previous studies, overlap patients with anti-ARS antibodies generally showed anti-synthetase syndrome15,16, while patients with anti-MDA antibody commonly presented as skin involvement and ILD14. As anti-HMGCR antibody is almost impossible to be correlated with extra-muscular presentations, we suggested that coexisting additional antibodies is likely to be associated with extra-muscular symptoms in overlap patients, specific to the overlap antibody type, and even be a predictor of good prognosis.

The significant differences from non-overlap patients suggest that overlap anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM is probably a special subtype of anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM, presenting with dermatomyositis-like clinical features, pathological changes indicating IMNM, and better response to treatment. The coexistence of more antibodies than anti-HMGCR antibody may be the reason for distinctions.

We have also concerned about pediatric or teenager patients presents muscle weakness with chronic progression, resembling muscular dystrophy17,18. We defined 12 patients as MD-like patients by strict clinical, pathological and genetic criteria, and clarified the differences between MD-like and conventional patients. Consistent with published literature, pathological manifestations of this subtype were distinguished by considerable atrophic myofibers, endomysial fibrosis and predominant myofibers with central nucleus. These chronic myopathic pathological changes could be explained by slowly progressive disease course. Our findings indicated that MD-like patients may be isolated subgroups, and reminded that anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM could also present with slow progression other than acute onset. However, confirmation of these results requires assessment of more MD-like patients.

Remarkably, only one patient (1/18, 5.56%) in our center had prior statin exposure, a rate substantially lower than previously reported in European countries19. However, two researches from China found statin use in eight patients (8/45, 17.78%) and two patients (2/23, 8.70%) with anti-HMGCR autoantibodies15,28, which is similar to our results. This ethnic discrepancy may be attributable to variation in dietary patterns. Statins are not the only known HMGCR inhibitors, naturally occurring statins in food may contribute to IMNM in statin-naive individuals. For example, red rice, commonly consumed in East Asian diets, contain monacolin, a lipid-lowering effect of food ingredients. This may explain the absence of pharmaceutical statins.

There are limitations to this study. First, this study is retrospective for anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients, so information bias is inevitable. Second, given the rarity of this disease, we lack sufficient cases to further perform the correlation analysis between various antibodies and extra-muscular symptoms and comparison analysis between different groups of anti-HMGCR with single MSA or MAA. We look forward to more multi-center, large-sample studies in the future. Finally, proteomics, transcriptomics, and metabolomics analysis is essential for a more complete evaluation of the involved mechanisms of pathogenesis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have reported on clinical, pathological and therapeutic differences between anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients with only muscular symptoms and those overlapped with extra-muscular symptoms, and have confirmed overlap patients as a special subtype. Clinical and pathological distinctions of MD-like anti-HMGCR-positive IMNM patients indicated the probability that different subtypes exist within conventional patients. These findings challenge the traditional view of anti-HMGCR myopathy as an exclusively acute-onset condition.

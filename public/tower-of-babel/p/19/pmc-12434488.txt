Minimal clinically important difference for the S3 Noninvasive Ventilation questionnaire in individuals with COPD using home mechanical ventilation
Authors: Pedro Viegas, Leonor Roseta, Cristina Jácome, Cátia Paixão, Luísa Castro, Carla Ribeiro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe S3-Noninvasive Ventilation (S3-NIV) questionnaire is a simple tool that allows monitoring of symptoms and side-effects in individuals using home mechanical ventilation (HMV). Its longitudinal use in monitorisation is complicated by the current absence of a minimal clinically important difference (MCID), which could facilitate the application of a more tailored follow-up and therapy optimisation. We aimed to establish the MCID for the S3-NIV in people with COPD under HMV.MethodsWe conducted an observational study with adult individuals with COPD treated with HMV, followed in an HMV outpatient clinic. The S3-NIV, the Severe Respiratory Insufficiency (SRI) questionnaire and the St George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) were applied at baseline and after a 6-month period. Demographic and clinical data were collected from hospital records. The MCID was computed using both distribution- and anchor-based methods. The pooled MCID was computed using the weighted mean (two-thirds anchor- and one-third distribution-based methods).ResultsA total of 99 participants (71% male) were included, with a mean±sd age of 72±9 years and a median (interquartile range (IQR)) forced expiratory volume in 1 s of 37 (29–49)% predicted. Median time under HMV was 40 (IQR 24–84) months. The SRI (r=0.311, p=0.002) and the SGRQ (r= −0.334, p=0.001) questionnaires were correlated with S3-NIV, and therefore were used as anchors. Our pooled analysis resulted in a MCID of 0.8 points.ConclusionA MCID of 0.8 points in the S3-NIV questionnaire is proposed in stable individuals with COPD using HMV, supporting the identification of clinically significant changes and management improvement.

Introduction

COPD is a leading cause of mortality worldwide [1], and the presence of chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure (CHRF) has been shown to contribute to this unfavourable outcome [2]. Home mechanical ventilation (HMV) is a therapeutic modality with an increasing use in COPD [3] due to an expanding number of indications and organisational improvements [3–5]. HMV has been shown to improve survival, symptoms and health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and to reduce exacerbations and hospitalisations in individuals with COPD [6–9]. Despite these benefits, HMV can also have a negative impact on HRQoL [10] due to side-effects, either related to the patient–ventilator interface or to the ventilation pressure or volumes, that can severely impair patient adherence to this therapy [11]. Therefore, there is a growing interest in personalising follow-up care.

Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are increasingly used multidimensional instruments that evaluate functional status, physical, mental and social wellbeing, HRQoL or symptoms [12], and allow the measurement of the impact of the disease and therapy in patients’ lives [13]. Integrating PROMs into clinical practice can enhance the personalisation of COPD care, improve patient outcomes and foster a patient-centred approach in healthcare settings [14]. The choice of a PROM is related to the study's aim, its validity in the specific population and the establishment of other relevant measurement properties [15]. The optimal usage of PROMs relies on the evaluation of the minimal clinically important difference (MCID), which is one of the most relevant properties in clinical practice, since it is used to evaluate the impact of the smallest change (due to treatment, interventions, disease progression or exacerbation) that is meaningful to patients [16]. A number of PROMs are available for use in individuals with COPD with CHRF using HMV, such as the St George Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) and the Severe Respiratory Insufficiency (SRI) questionnaire. The SGRQ is a disease-specific HRQoL instrument developed for COPD [17, 18]. The SRI is a condition-specific HRQoL questionnaire developed for CHRF [19, 20]. Despite their usefulness, their use on daily clinical practice is complicated, as they are long, time-consuming and require extensive calculations.

The S3-Noninvasive Ventilation (S3-NIV) questionnaire, developed originally in French [21] and recently adapted to other languages [22, 23], including Portuguese [24], is a short and simple tool designed for individuals using HMV that allows monitoring of symptoms, sleep complaints and treatment side-effects. This information is pivotal in the management of these patients and could increase the identification of difficulties and optimisation of therapy [25]. The clinical responsiveness of this questionnaire in the acute setting has also been evaluated in an heterogeneous population of patients with CHRF, which included 10 exacerbated COPD patients, and a MCID between 0.63 and 1.02 was proposed [26]. However, this study focused on an acute exacerbation and the value calculated was solely related to deterioration and included a small sample of patients with COPD. The absence of a MCID for patients with COPD in a stable and chronic setting complicates the identification relevant changes that need treatment modifications [27]. Therefore, the definition of a MCID is a priority to better manage patients with COPD using this therapy, aiming for the optimisation of HMV and timely correction of side-effects. This would allow the maintenance of therapeutic benefits and avoid nonadherence patterns to HMV.

The aim of our study was to define the MCID for the S3-NIV questionnaire in stable patients with COPD using HMV, leading to a more widespread use of this questionnaire in clinical practice and interventional studies, through a more solid evaluation of relevant changes in adaptation to HMV, symptoms and side-effects.

Methods

Study design

This observational study was conducted between June 2022 and June 2024 in the Outpatient Ventilation Clinic of the Pulmonology Department at Unidade Local de Saúde de Gaia e Espinho (Vila Nova de Gaia, Portugal), a tertiary care teaching hospital, one of the three highly complex multidisciplinary units in Portugal, that follows 300–350 HMV patients a year [28]. Patients were recruited during scheduled medical visits. This study was reported according to Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guidelines [29].

Participants

Adult patients with stable COPD, with optimised inhaled therapy and controlled comorbidities, already adapted to HMV for ≥30 days were included. Exclusion criteria were refusal to participate or being unable to understand self-reported questionnaires. All consecutive patients who fulfilled the eligibility criteria were invited to participate.

Sample size

The minimum sample size was determined according to the COSMIN (Consensus-Based Standards for the Selection of Health Measurement Instruments) guidelines [30] which establishes a sample size of ≥50 participants to compute the MCID [31]. In existing studies addressing MCID for SRI, in severe stable COPD, 108 patients were included [20] and in a study addressing the SGRQ in severe COPD patients a total of 115 patients were included [32]. Therefore, a sample size target ∼100 was defined.

Data collection

At the recruitment visit (t0), demographic information (age, sex) and PROM (S3-NIV, SRI, SGRQ, EuroQol-5D, modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) scale) data were obtained. When patients were unable to answer without support (such as difficulty reading or writing), the help of the caregiver or a family member was allowed.

Clinical data (body mass index, time with HMV, exacerbations, COPD Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease severity) were collected from the patients’ electronic health records. Pulmonary function test data were extracted from the patients’ electronic health records, if obtained ≤12 months before the beginning of the study. Ventilation parameters and daily usage were recorded in the same visit by the readout of the ventilator's built-in software (ResMed AirView and Philips Care Orchestrator platforms). Daytime arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis was performed according to standard recommendations in sitting patients without ventilation, with the current oxygen flow provided. At 6 months (t6), patients completed all the PROMs again during scheduled medical visits. In cases of patient death during the study period, the date of death was collected from the electronic health records.

PROMs

S3-NIV

The S3-NIV is a validated self-administered short questionnaire assessing disease and treatment impact while addressing respiratory complaints, sleep and side-effects [21, 24]. It contains 11 items on a five-point Likert scale with the lowest possible score (0) corresponding to the highest impact of disease and treatment, while the highest possible score (10) corresponds to the lowest impact of disease and treatment. The questionnaire has two subscores: the “respiratory symptoms” subscore, calculated as the average of answered items 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7 multiplied by 2.5; and the “sleep and side-effects” subscore, calculated as the average of answered items 2, 3, 8, 9, 10 and 11 multiplied by 2.5, with the total score being the arithmetic mean of all items multiplied by 2.5. The subscores have the same range and interpretation as the total score.

SRI

The SRI questionnaire is a self-administered questionnaire containing 49 items that patients answer on a five-point Likert scale according to how true each statement has been for them in the preceding week [19]. The questionnaire contains seven subdomains: respiratory complaints, physical functioning, attendant symptoms and sleep, social relationships, anxiety, psychological wellbeing and social functioning. After recoding certain items, the final score for each subdomain is calculated by the corresponding percentage. The summary score is obtained by calculating the arithmetic mean of the subscale scores. The summary score and subscales range from 0 to 100, with a high score indicating a good HRQoL, while a low score indicates a poor HRQoL [19]. The Portuguese version used in this study has been validated with good psychometric properties [33].

SGRQ

The SGRQ [17, 18] is a disease-specific HRQoL instrument developed for individuals with COPD. It contains 76 items divided into three sections (symptoms: frequency and severity; activities: effect of disease on common daily physical activities; impact: psychosocial effects of the disease). The first section comprises several scales, while the remainder is composed of dichotomous (true/false) questions, except for the last question, which is a four-point Likert scale. The SGRQ score varies from 0 (no impairment) to 100 (extreme impairment), with higher scores indicating a worse HRQoL.

EuroQol-5D

The EuroQol-5D (EQ-5D) questionnaire is a generic HRQoL questionnaire, already validated for the Portuguese population [34]. It comprises one question for each of the five dimensions (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression), and uses a visual analogue scale to assess general health status, ranging from 0 (worst possible state) to 100 (best imaginable health state).

mMRC

The mMRC is a widely used and simple measure of breathlessness in COPD, also validated in Portuguese [35]. It comprises five items that stratify an individual's dyspnoea (0=dyspnoea only with strenuous exercise; 1=dyspnoea when hurrying or walking up a slight hill; 2=walks slower than people of the same age because of dyspnoea or has to stop for breath when walking at own pace; 3=stops for breath after walking 100 m or after a few minutes; 4=too dyspnoeic to leave house or breathless when dressing).

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 30 (IBM SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) and plots created using the R Studio software (Posit PBC, Boston, MA, USA). The level of significance was set at 0.05.

Descriptive statistics (i.e. mean±sd, median (interquartile range (IQR)) or frequencies (%)) were used to characterise the included patients in terms of sociodemographics, diagnosis, ventilation parameters and adherence to therapy. Differences between participants between t0 and t6 were compared using paired t-tests/Wilcoxon signed rank tests, when appropriate. The correlations between the mean change of the S3-NIV and the mean changes of the other measures collected were explored to determine its suitability to be used as anchors. Significant correlations (Pearson (r) or Spearman's (rs) correlation coefficient) of ≥0.3 were considered adequate [15, 36].

A combination of anchor- and distribution-based methods was used to establish the MCID of the S3-NIV. These methods were weighted at ratios of two- and one-thirds, respectively, according to the authors’ best judgement and previous work [37, 38]. The final MCID was calculated through the arithmetic weighted mean. Lastly, the pooled MCID value was used to compute the matching effect size (MCIDES) with the following formula: MCIDES=MCIDpooled/(SD2T6+SD2T0)/2. A MCIDES of 0.3–0.5 has been recommended [37].

In the distribution method the MCID was calculated as 0.5 times the standard deviation, standard error of mean, 1.96 times the standard error of mean and the 95th percentile of the minimal detectable change (MCD95) of the S3-NIV questionnaire.

Ethical considerations

This study was approved by the ethics committee of the Unidade Local de Saúde de Gaia e Espinho (CES 97-2022-1). Participation in this study required previous informed consent signature by the patient or his legal representative. Participation in this study did not include any analysis or procedures not recommended according to local practice. There were no specific risks associated with the participation in the study. The responsibility of the database belongs to the leading investigator. All data collected were included in an anonymised database specifically created for this study. The access to the database was limited to the investigators, through personal and nontransmissible access passwords. All the operations performed in the database were recorded and associated with the investigator performing this data insertion to guarantee data consistency.

These were mainly male (n=71, 71.7%), with a mean±sd age of 72±9 years, a median forced expiratory volume in 1 s of 37% (IQR 29–49%) and were established on HMV for a median of 40 (IQR 24–84) months. The MCIDs resulting from the mean change of participants improving above the MCID of the SRI was 0.6 points (supplementary table S2).

The receiver operating characteristic method was not used to compute the MCID, as the area under the curve (AUC) discrimination validity was not acceptable (<0.7) for either the SRI or the SGRQ (SRI: AUC=0.626, 95% CI 0.51–0.74; p=0.05; SGRQ: AUC=0.587, 95% CI 0.46–0.71; p=0.183). Linear regression was used to estimate the MCID for the S3-NIV using the SRI and the SGRQ. The estimated MCID for the S3-NIV using the SRI was 0.32 points (95% CI −0.09–0.73; p=0.002), and 0.35 points (95% CI 0.14–0.56; p=0.001) using the SGRQ. Discussion

Our study presents the first estimation of the MCID for the S3-NIV questionnaire in stable patients with COPD using HMV. By performing two modalities for the calculation of the measure, the distribution- and anchor-based methods, our pooled analysis established a MCID of 0.8 points.

In the baseline and 6-month evaluation we found no differences in the scores of the measured questionnaires. This is consistent with our absence of pre-defined interventions in the study and supports the intention to propose a MCID for patient-perceived changes in stable individuals with COPD using HMV. In our sample we found statistically significant changes between the ventilation parameters (IPAP, EPAP and backup respiratory rate) at 0 and 6 months, even though the median for the variation was 0. A significant difference was also found in the hours of HMV use, which could relate to changes performed during the standard follow-up, new problems or side-effects with the therapy or other unknown patient factors influencing HMV use. Patients had their medical therapy optimised before study inclusion, and no changes were performed during follow-up. Since no specific intervention was applied, and changes were only done according to our standard of care, we believe that such differences are not relevant and that a calculation of a MCID of a stable population can still be assumed.

For the estimation of a MCID, it is recommended to use validated anchors with a relevant impact on the individuals HRQoL. Our study proposed the application of several specific (SRI, SGRQ) and more generic (EQ-5D, mMRC) questionnaires to serve as anchors for our calculations. Even though the more general questionnaires could not be used due to insufficient correlation with S3-NIV, the fact that the analysis included validated specific questionnaires with already determined MCIDs [18, 20] as anchors was reassuring. The correlation between the proposed anchors and S3-NIV allowed for the necessary calculations for the determination of a MCID to be performed. The MCID of 5 points for the SRI questionnaire [20] and the MCID of 4 units for the SGRQ [32] that were used in our analysis were both calculated in populations similar to ours, further strengthening the correlation between the PROMs. Therefore, the similarity of patient populations and the correlation with other specific questionnaires with established MCID strengthens the use of S3-NIV as a simpler questionnaire that can be used effectively in clinical practice for patient monitoring.

Despite the described association between improvements in HRQoL and reduction in daytime partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PCO2) [39], which would explain the therapeutic benefits of HMV, similarly to other studies, a correlation with changes in ABG parameters (PCO2 and bicarbonate) and our questionnaire was not found [20]. Our study population included individuals with COPD already established on HMV, where optimisation of daytime hypercapnia had been done in the initial adaptation period; therefore, it would be expected to have small or no variations on ABG analysis due to the absence of intervention.

The use of distribution methods for the determination of a MCID is a relatively unbiased strategy that takes into consideration the statistical distribution of the results. For our analysis we incorporated several measurements to further strengthen the analysis, but for the calculation of the final estimation, a relatively smaller weight was attributed to this method according to published literature [37].

Despite controversies in the optimal calculation of a MCID, our pooled analysis using two different methods permitted a more robust calculation of this measure. Moreover, greater weight was given to anchor-based than distribution-based methods (two-thirds versus one-third), as recommended [36] and according to similar literature [38]. Our value is consistent with other estimates performed in the acute setting, which propose a MCID between 0.63 and 1.02 points [26], which is an impactful event in COPD patients, thus further strengthening the usefulness of our estimate in the identification of clinically significant variations. Therefore, we suggest that a variation of 0.8 points on the S3-NIV score can be viewed as a relevant change in the stable individuals with COPD using HMV.

Our study had some limitations. Firstly, even though our sample is adequate according to existing guidelines, the participation was dependent on patients' collaboration and willingness to participate due to its consecutive nature. Possible biases are present, such as a healthy volunteer bias, in which only the best controlled patients would participate, leading to an underestimation of the proposed MCID. Secondly, despite its longitudinal nature, no specific and pre-determined interventions were performed. The standard care of our centre was executed in the appointments, which could lead to no changes or changes according to patient-perceived complaints, ABG measurements or ventilator data. Therefore, our estimation pertains to a perceived change in the patients without a specific pre-determined attitude from the healthcare professionals, that is consistent with the intention of proposing a MCID for stable patients. Despite these limitations, we still believe that the proposed MCID of 0.8 points will be useful in clinical practice and allow management optimisation, as well as serve as a starting point for further studies. Further investigation should also be conducted to determine a MCID for other patient groups using HMV.

We propose the first estimation of a MCID of 0.8 points for the S3-NIV questionnaire in stable individuals with COPD using HMV, with the use of a pooled analysis of distribution- and anchor-based methods. We believe this value can be used for relevant patient-perceived changes and identification of deterioration, which could lead to follow-up optimisation, allow the maintenance of therapeutic benefits, and avoid nonadherence patterns to HMV.

Effects of phytosterol ester supplementation on egg weight, biochemical indices, liver immunity and gut microbiota of laying hens during peak laying period
Authors: Wenzi Wu, Zhuo Chen, Qixin Huang, Rui Chen, Xin Ma, Wenxin Ye, Jinghui Fan, Lichun Qian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis experiment was aimed to investigate the effects of Phytosterol Ester (PSE) supplementation on egg weight, biochemical indices, liver immunity and gut microbiota of Hy-Line Brown laying hens during peak laying period. A total of 256 healthy Hy-Line Brown laying hens were randomly allocated into 4 groups. Laying hens in the control group were fed a basal diet (CON), while those in the experimental groups received a basal diet containing 10 mg/kg (PSE10), 20 mg/kg (PSE20), or 40 mg/kg (PSE40) mg/kg PSE, respectively. We found that PSE supplementation significantly increased the egg weight in PSE20 and PSE40 groups (P < 0.05) and the serum magnesium (Mg) content in PSE10 and PSE20 groups (P < 0.05), but significantly decreased the serum calcium (Ca) content in PSE40 group (P < 0.05). Moreover, PSE supplementation significantly increased the total protein (TP) content of ovary in all experimental groups (P < 0.01) and decreased the total cholesterol (TC) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) contents of the ovary in PSE20 and PSE40 groups (P < 0.001). In serum, PSE supplementation significantly increased TP content in all experimental groups (P < 0.01) and albumin (ALB) content in PSE20 group (P < 0.05). Alkaline phosphatase (AKP) in PSE20 group, TC content in all experimental groups and LDL-C content in PSE20 and PSE40 groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05). In egg yolk, PSE supplementation significantly increased TP content in PSE20 and PSE40 groups (P < 0.01) and decreased TC content in PSE20 group (P < 0.01). In liver immunofluorescence, PSE supplementation altered the content of CD163, especially in PSE20 group. Dietary PSE significantly decreased the relative abundance of Bacteroides and Desulfovibrio, while increased the relative abundance of Faecalibacterium, g_unclassified_f_Lachnospiraceae, g_norank_f_Ruminococcaceae, g__unclassified_f__Oscillospiraceae and other bacteria. In conclusion, PSE supplementation increased the average egg weight and total protein, lowered egg yolk, serum and ovary cholesterol of Hy-Line Brown laying hens. At the same time, it can also promote serum magnesium levels, enhanced liver immunity, and improved gut microflora.Key words: phytosterol ester, gut microbiota, liver immunity, biochemical indice, egg weight

INTRODUCTION

Phytosterol esters (PSE) are the product of an esterification reaction between Phytosterols (PS) and fatty acids, and since sterol esters can be converted into sterols and fatty acids in the body, they contain the physiological functions of both PSs and fatty acids (Qing, 2023; Ye et al., 2024b), such as lowering cholesterol, anti-oxidative stress, anti-cancer, anti-inflammatory, growth promotion, and immune regulation, among others (Feng et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023). PSs and PSEs have been applied in the fields of cosmetics, food health care, pharmaceuticals, agriculture and animal husbandry, showing great prospects (Fernandes and Cabral, 2007; MS et al., 2018). Clinically, it is also used to fight digestive tract tumors (Xue et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020), treat nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (LI et al., 2020), and protect gastric mucosa. There are few studies on the effect of PSEs on intestinal microbial immunity of poultry.

At present, due to the global prohibition of feeding antibiotics, the intestinal health issues in poultry are prominent in domestic poultry production, and the intestinal health of poultry producers have been given high priority (Castanon, 2007; Xiao et al., 2020). In breeding, the accumulation of intestinal microbes in chickens can have a profound impact on various systems of the body, especially the intestinal immune system. Maintaining the normal intestinal flora of chickens is the premise and guarantee for improving benefits (Ducatelle et al., 2018; Bedford and Apajalahti, 2022; Cen et al., 2023). Therefore, the development of dietary additives to replace antibiotics and a better understanding of the impact of gut microbes on poultry immunity have become key strategies to promote poultry health and production.

This experiment was conducted to study the effects of dietary phytosterol esters on egg weight, biochemical indices, liver immunity and intestinal microflora of Hy-Line brown laying hens during peak laying period. At the same time, it provides reference for future research on phytosterols (esters), intestinal microbes, poultry immunity and so on.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animals, Experimental Design, and Diets

A total of 256 healthy Hy-Line Brown laying hens (25 wk old) with similar body weight and egg production rate (above 88%) were randomly assigned into 4 groups with 8 replicates having 8 hens per replicate. Laying hens were housed in the same closed laying hen house. Laying hens in the control group were fed a basal diet (CON), while those in the experimental group received a basal diet containing 10 mg/kg (PSE10), 20 mg/kg (PSE20), or 40 mg/kg (PSE40) mg/kg PSE, respectively. This dose was selected according to previous in vivo studies (Ding et al., 2021). A 14-d pretest period was followed by a 56-d formal test period. The laying hens were housed in 3-tier ladder-type rearing cages, with 4 hens per cage (48 cm × 34 cm × 37 cm). Incandescent lamps were used in the house with a light intensity of 30 Lx. Light duration was 15 h/day from 5:00 to 20:00. Temperature was between 18 and 24°C and relative humidity between 70 and 80%. All experiments followed the guidelines for animal care and use, and were approved by the Committee for Animal Research at Zhejiang University (Hangzhou, China) (ZJU20240055). Five days before the end of experiment, 40 eggs (5 randomly selected from each replicate) were randomly collected from each group for determination of egg quality. Egg weight were calculated. At the end of the experimental period, 2 birds of moderate weight were randomly selected from each replicate. Blood samples (5 mL) were collected from the brachial vein into a 10 mL anticoagulant-free Vacutainer tube (Greiner BioOne GmbH, Kremsmunster, Austria). After standing for 2 h at 37°C, the serum was centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, separated, and stored in a -80°C freezer. The anesthetic is a mixture of Sumianxin II and ketamine 1:1 to anesthetize laying hens (Cao et al., 2016). A 1 cm3 liver was removed and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. In additional, the cecal content, and liver, ovary tissues were collected immediately from all hens. The samples were then transferred to a freezer at -80°C for further testing.

Biochemical Indicators in Serum, Ovary and Egg Yolk

Each ovary and egg yolk sample was accurately weighed, and 9 times the volume of the double distilled water solution was added by weight. The samples were homogenized (about 60 s) using a fast homogenizer until no particles were visible in the homogenized solution. The prepared homogenized solution was centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to remove debris. The supernatant was retained for analysis.

Ovary and egg yolk tissue supernatant and serum were used to measure lipid metabolites and biochemical indicators. The total protein (TP), total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), triglyceride (TG), albumin (ALB), and alkaline phosphatase (AKP) content were determined using kits produced by the Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute.

Serum levels of Creatinine (Crea), Carbamide (Urea), Gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT), total bilirubin (TBil), Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), Ferrum (Fe) were determined by AU5831 Automatic Biochemistry Analyzer (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) in Hangzhou Second People's Hospital.

Immunofluorescence

The fixed liver samples were treated by using citric acid antigen repair solution (pH 6.0), perform antigen repair, natural cooling and cleaning. It was closed with 10% goat serum and incubated at 37°C for 30 min. The samples were preincubated with primary antibody (CD163, from Abcam, UK); then followed by incubated with secondary antibody (HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit universal secondary antibody, from DAKO, Denmark). The nucleus was stained by DAPI, the tissue was dripped with anti-fluorescent sealer, covered with a cover glass, and stored at 4°C away from light. It was observed under an optical microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) and the images were collected for analysis.

Microbial 16S rRNA Gene Sequencing Library Construction and Sequencing

DNA extraction and PCR amplification were conducted. Accordance with the instructions, microbial DNA was extracted from the samples of cecal contents using the DNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Norcross, GA, United States). Using 1.0% agarose gel electrophoresis and a ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific Inc., United States) to determine the quality and concentration of the DNA (Liu et al., 2016). The V3-V4 hypervariable region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified using primer pairs 341F (5′-CCTAYGGGRBGCASCAG-3′) and 806R (5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′) on an PCR thermocycler (ABI, CA) on 2% agarose gel, purified using the DNA Gel Extraction Kit (Axygen Biosciences, Union City, CA, United States), and quantified using a fluorometer (Promega) (Ye et al., 2024a). Sequencing was performed on an Illumina MiSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China.

Sequence processing and analysis. After demultiplexing, the sequences produced underwent quality filtration with fastp (version 0.19.6) and were merged with FLASH (version 1.2.11). High-quality sequences were denoised using the DADA2 plugin within the Qiime2 pipeline (version 2020.2) with the recommended parameters (Chen et al., 2018). This process produced single nucleotide resolution based on error profiles within samples, resulting in the generation of amplicon sequence variants (ASV) using the denoised sequences obtained through the DADA2 pipeline (Edgar, 2013; Ye et al., 2024a). The taxonomic assignments of the ASVs were made using the Naive Bayes (or Vsearch or Blast) consensus taxonomy classifier, which is implemented in Qiime2 and utilizes the SILVA 16S rRNA database (v138) (Yuan et al., 2023).

For 16S rRNA analysis, the data matrix was uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com) for data analysis. The raw sequencing reads were deposited into the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database (Accession Number: PRJNA1113832, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/1113832).

Statistical Analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± SEM. GraphPad Prism 9.5 program (GraphPad Software, San Diego, Canada) was used for all analyses except for the 16S RNA results. One-way ANOVA and LSD test determined statistical differences between trials (P < 0.05).

The effects of PSE dietary supplementation on serum trace elements are shown in Figure 1. Compared with the CON group, dietary PSE significantly increased the serum Mg content in PSE10 and PSE20 groups (P < 0.05, Figure 1B), but significantly decreased the serum Ca content in PSE40 group (P < 0.05, Figure 1C). Microbial 16S rRNA Gene Sequencing Analysis

Basic Results of 16S rRNA Sequencing. We further investigated the effect of dietary phytosterol esters on the intestinal microbial composition of cecum of laying hens in peak laying period, 25 wk old, by 16S rRNA gene sequencing. A total of 5014665 sequences were obtained, with a total length of 2096031981 bp and an average sequence length of 417 bp.

Alpha Diversity Analysis. The effects of dietary phytosterol esters on cecal microorganisms of laying hens in peak laying period were shown in Figure 4. Compared with the CON group (Figures 4 A-E), there were significant differences in the Shannon index (P = 0.037) and Simpson index (P = 0.020) of OTU levels between the groups. Chao1 index, Sobs index and Ace index of experimental groups were higher than those of CON group, but did not reach a significant level. The results showed that adding PSE increased the diversity of microorganisms in cecum.

Beta Diversity Analysis. Beta diversity analysis is an inter-group comparative analysis of species diversity among different microbial communities to explore the similarity or difference of community composition between samples of different groups. Principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) based on Bray-Curtis distance algorithm was used to analyze Beta diversity of intestinal flora. PCoA showed intestinal flora isolation among the 4 groups (R = 0.288, P = 0.003, Figure 4F). At the same time, as shown in the NMDS diagram (Figure 4G), the microbial community structure of the 4 groups (stress = 0.169, R = 0.248, P = 0.002) was significantly separated, indicating that dietary PSE supplementation could affect the Beta diversity of cecal microbial colonies and change the colony structure.

Analysis of Community Composition. The relative abundance of bacteria at the phylum and genus levels (Figures 5A, B) was assessed. At Phylum level (Figure 5A), Bacteroidota, Firmicutes and Spirochaetota were the main phyla in healthy laying hens. Among them, Bacteroidetes and Firmicutes were the most abundant, accounting for more than 80% of all microorganisms in all groups. The relative abundance of Firmicutes has increased significantly (P = 0.002), and the relative abundance of Bacteroidota (P = 0.034) and Desulfobacterota (P = 0.023) have decreased significantly (Figure 5C).

At the Genus level (Figure 5B), the top 10 genera with the highest relative abundance accounted for more than 55% of the total flora sequences. Compared with the CON group, the relative abundance of Bacteroides (P = 0.006) and Desulfovibrio (P = 0.024) in the experimental groups is significantly decreased. Faecalibacterium (P = 0.003), g_unclassified_f_Lachnospiraceae (P = 0.003), g_norank_f_Ruminococcaceae (P = 0.009) and so on showed a significant upward trend (Figure 5D).

Species Difference Analysis. LEfSe (LDA Effect Size) Analysis was performed on the relative abundance of each intestinal microflora taxa. The larger the Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) value, the more important the strain is and the more profound the influence on the group. Here, we set the LDA threshold as 2, and screened out bacteria groups with significant differences in the cecum of the CON group and the 3 experimental groups at the generic level. As shown in Figure 5E, a total of 24 different bacteria were identified in the 4 groups.

The LDA value greater than 3.5 was used as the criterion to select the iconic bacteria genera in each group, and found that g_Bacteroides and g_Desulfovibrio are enriched in the intestines of laying hens in the CON group. Two bacteria genera, including g_UCG-005 and g_unclassified_f_Oscillospiraceae, were enriched in the intestines of laying hens fed 10 mg/kg PSE. It was found that 3 bacteria genera, including g_Prevotellaceae_UCG-001 and g_unclassified_f_Lachnospiraceae, g_Fournierella were enriched in the intestinal tracts of laying hens fed 20 mg/kg PSE. It was found that 9 bacteria genera such as g_Faecalibacterium and g_norank_f_Ruminococcaceae were enriched in the intestinal tracts of laying hens fed 40 mg/kg PSE.

DISCUSSION

Phytosterols (PSs) have the functions of lowering cholesterol, anti-oxidation and preventing cardiovascular diseases. In animal production, phytosterols can promote the growth of poultry and improve the quality of livestock products(Peng et al., 2021). As one of the 10 functional nutrients recommended by the International Nutrition Society (ISN), PSs (PSEs) have a broad application prospect (Deng et al., 2014). Therefore, this study was conducted to investigate the effects of dietary phytosterol esters on egg weight, biochemical indices, liver immunofluorescence and gut microbiota of Hy-Line Brown laying hens during peak laying period.

Egg weight is one of the important indexes of egg quality. There are strong positive correlations, between poultry egg weights and hatching results (hatchability, embryonic mortality), hatching weights and subsequent performance of chicks. In which egg weight is the most important factor affecting hatch weight (Saatci et al., 2006; Hassan et al., 2005; Alkan et al., 2008). Zhang et al. (2023) reported that PSEs increased egg weight in Jingfen No. 6 laying hens at the end of lay. This is consistent with our results. Therefore, the addition of PSE is beneficial to the production of Hy-Line Brown laying hens.

Phytosterol esters are considered to be relatively safe natural compounds and are commonly found in daily diets. However, it was found that the addition of phytosterols 80 mg/kg (maximum recommended effective dose, RAD), 400 mg/kg (5 times the maximum recommended effective dose), and 800 mg/kg (ten times the maximum recommended effective dose) increased cholesterol levels in egg yolks, while there were no significant changes in serum and liver cholesterol levels (Chang et al., 2014). We suspect that feed additives may have adverse effects on animals at high doses or long-term ingestion. In order to ensure the safety of the supplemental dose and feeding duration in this experiment, the liver function and renal function indicators of laying hens were examined. The results showed that dietary PSE had no significant effects on serum GGT, TBil, Urea and Crea (P > 0.05). In serum, PSE supplementation significantly increased ALB content and decreased AKP content (P < 0.05). Feng et al. (2022) found that a nominal phytosterol ester concentration of 8.1% was considered to be the no-observed-adverse-effect level, which was equivalent to a human dose of 536.6 mg/kg body weight/day of phytosterol esters or 333.3 mg/kg body weight/day of phytosterols. The dosage and duration of the experiment of phytosterol esters added in this experiment are reasonable, which will not cause pressure or influence on the liver and kidney of laying hens, and is a relatively safe additive.

Calcium and magnesium are important factors affecting eggshell quality. It was found that at 49 wk of age, eggshell gland density decreased and the balance between immunohistochemical localization of oestrogen receptors ERα and ERβ in the eggshell gland changed. In addition, total calcium concentration in plasma increased from the beginning to the end of laying, but eggshell strength and thickness decreased significantly (Wistedt et al., 2019). Dietary magnesium deficiency inhibits the mobilization of blood calcium by uterus, which leads to the decrease of eggshell weight, eggshell thickness and eggshell strength. The addition of appropriate magnesium can improve eggshell strength, eggshell weight, eggshell thickness and eggshell strength (Kim et al., 2013; Belkameh et al., 2021). This shows that calcium, magnesium and eggshell quality are related. Previous studies have shown that adding PSE increases egg quality, such as Haugh unit and albumen height, but decreases egg breaking strength (Wu et al., 2024). In this study, it was found that the serum calcium content of the experimental groups was significantly reduced, while the serum magnesium content was significantly increased. It may be because the addition of PSE improves egg quality and increases the body's demand for calcium, resulting in a decrease in serum calcium content, and the body mobilizes Mg to promote the egg production process, resulting in an increase in blood magnesium content. Studies have shown that feeding different sources of calcium, different sizes of particles (Park et al., 2017), or different levels of calcium and phosphorus and their proportions (Küçükyilmaz et al., 2014), can directly affect eggshell quality. Therefore, when adding PSE, the calcium content in the diet of laying hens during the peak laying period should be appropriately increased to ensure normal production performance.

A large number of experiments in hyperlipidemia mice and volunteers have shown that phytosterol (ester) has the effect of lowering cholesterol and blood lipid (Wang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). In animal husbandry applications, Wen et al. showed that the addition of PSs reduced the relative mass of abdominal lipids in 30-wk-old Issa Brown laying hens and reduced TG, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C levels in the serum of 4-wk-old chickens (Wen et al., 2012). Jia et al. (2007) showed that the addition of 40 mg/kg PS to broiler diets reduced blood LDL-C and TC levels and increased serum TP content. Previous studies have suggested that dietary PSE may promote protein synthesis in the liver by affecting the Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis and amino acid metabolism pathway, thereby improving egg quality (Wu et al., 2024). These are consistent with our findings. In this study, PSE dietary supplementation increased the TP content, decreased the TC and LDL-C content of ovary, serum or egg yolk. It can be seen that the effect of PSE on reducing cholesterol and promoting protein synthesis is significant.

CD163 is a member of the scavenger-receptor cysteinerich (SRCR) protein superfamily (Zhang et al., 2014). CD163’s function is to remove hemoglobin-haptoglobin complexes from the blood circulation during intravascular hemolysis (Kristiansen et al., 2001). CD163 is also involved in immunomodulatory functions and regulates the production of anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidative substances (Nielsen et al., 2020). CD163 is involved in hemoglobin clearance as a receptor of the hemoglobin-bound globin complex and is widely used as a marker for monocytes and macrophages (Ma et al., 2018). CD163 immune cell infiltration is a marker of prognosis in certain diseases, which may be related to tissue repair and healing processes, as macrophages play an important role in promoting tissue repair and regeneration . Therefore, to explore the immunity of the liver, CD163 antibody was labeled with fluorescein and observed under microscope. With the increase of PSE concentration, the area of green fluorescence gradually increased and evenly distributed into the liver tissue cells, especially in PSE20 group. The object of this experiment is Hy-Line Brown laying hens. Avian Fatty Liver Hemorrhagic Syndrome is the most common nutrient metabolic disease in laying hens. The main characteristics are lipid metabolism disorder, liver rupture and liver bleeding. Previous studies have shown that PSE can improve fatty liver and bleeding in laying hens. Therefore, we believe that dietary PSE supplementation also plays a role in promoting the expression of CD163 and the repair and regeneration of tissues by macrophages.

Intestinal tract is the body's most closely in contact with the external environment. It is not only the digestive organ, but also the largest immune organ in the body. Intestinal microorganisms play a very important role in the regulation of host immunity (Yang et al., 2017). Dietary PSE can increase the Alpha diversity of intestinal flora, and change the Beta diversity, indicating that the number and type of intestinal bacteria have changed.

The relative abundance of Bacteroides and Desulfovibrio, common in the gut microbiota, have decreased, and they have increased the relative abundance of Faecalibacterium, g_unclassified_f_Lachnospiraceae, g_norank_f_Ruminococcaceae, g__unclassified_f__Oscillospiraceae, Fournierella, g_Prevotellaceae_UCG-001 and other bacteria. As a kind of symbiotic bacteria, Bacteroides is the main part of intestinal bacteria. It plays both beneficial and pathogenic roles in the host, usually beneficial in the gut, but opportunistic in other body locations (Wexler, 2007; Zafar and Saier, 2021). Desulfovibrio is a typical sulfate-reducing bacterium with a dual impact on host health (Zhou et al., 2024). Faecalibacterium acts as both a sensor and an activator in human intestinal health. Its anti-inflammatory properties are widely known, and changes in the composition of F. prausnitzii in the gut are thought to be related to a number of human diseases (Effendi et al., 2022; Martín et al., 2023). Members of Lachnospiraceae are among the main producers of short-chain fatty acids (Vacca et al., 2020). G_norank_f_Ruminococcaceae and Fournierella are members of the family Ruminococcaceae (Togo et al., 2017). Reduced Ruminococcaceae abundance has been associated with many inflammatory bowel diseases. It has the ability to produce butyrate and other SCFAs, so its increased abundance plays an important role in maintaining intestinal health (Gu et al., 2022). Family Oscillospiraceae is a potential keystone species in the hydrolysis of complex polymers during anaerobic digestion of biomass (Pascual et al., 2020). This shows that the intestinal flora of laying hens changed from common, dual-acting bacteria to beneficial bacteria associated with anti-inflammatory and immune effects after feeding PSE. According to the results of correlation analysis, egg weight, TP of ovary and egg yolk, AKP of serum and Mg content were also positively correlated with the increase of beneficial bacteria. In conclusion, PSE can indirectly play an anti-inflammatory role by modifying the intestinal flora to promote poultry health and performance.

CONCLUSION

PSE supplementation increased the average egg weight, lowered egg yolk, serum and ovarian cholesterol and increased total protein of Hy-Line Brown laying hens during peak laying period. At the same time, the dose of 10 to 40 mg/kg PSE did not increase the burden on the liver and kidneys, and also promoted blood magnesium levels, enhanced liver immunity, and improved gut microflora.

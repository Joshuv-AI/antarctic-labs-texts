Associations of obesity-related mammary intraductal secretions with inflammatory severity, immune dysregulation, and adverse prognosis in non-puerperal mastitis: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Wenchao Qu, Jiaye Sun, Shijun Shao, Jiamei Feng, Qingqian Gao, Lu Xie, Xueqing Wu, Hua Wan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the association between mammary intraductal secretions (IDS) and demographic characteristics, clinical manifestations, laboratory indicators and prognosis in patients with non-puerperal mastitis (NPM).Patients and methodsA retrospective analysis of 340 NPM patients who underwent surgical treatment at the Department of Breast, Shuguang Hospital, between April 2020 and December 2021 was conducted. Based on intraoperative findings of IDS, patients were divided into two groups: the IDS group (with intraductal lipid-like secretions) and the non-IDS group (without intraductal lipid-like secretions). Demographic data, clinical features, laboratory parameters, and prognostic outcomes were systematically compared between the two groups.ResultsPatients with NPM and IDS had a significantly higher median age at disease onset (33 years vs. 32 years; P = 0.047) and body mass index (BMI) (24.0 kg/m2 vs. 22.9 kg/m2; P = 0.013) compared with those in the non-IDS group. Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified older age and overweight/obesity (BMI ≥ 24 kg/m2) as independent risk factors for IDS in NPM. Although clinical symptoms did not differ significantly between the two groups, the IDS group demonstrated elevated systemic inflammatory markers, including C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and fibrinogen (Fg) (all P < 0.05). Immunological assessment revealed significantly higher levels of complement component 3 (C3), complement component 4 (C4), and total immunoglobulin E (IgE) in humoral immunity, as well as an increased proportion of regulatory T cells (Treg) in cellular immunity (all P < 0.05). No significant differences were observed between groups in prolactin (PRL) levels, lipid profiles, or cytokine concentrations (all P > 0.05). During follow-up, patients in the IDS group experienced longer healing times, higher rates of ipsilateral recurrence, and a greater incidence of bilateral disease compared with those in the non-IDS group (all P < 0.05).ConclusionsThis study demonstrates that obesity-related IDS are significantly associated with increased systemic inflammation, immune dysregulation, and unfavorable prognostic outcomes in NPM. The identification of IDS provides valuable insight for risk stratification and supports the development of personalized management strategies. Overweight and obesity were identified as independent risk factors for IDS, highlighting weight control as a potential therapeutic approach. Future investigations should focus on elucidating the underlying mechanisms and exploring targeted therapeutic strategies to improve clinical outcomes.

Introduction

Non-puerperal mastitis, a chronic inflammatory breast condition, poses significant clinical challenges, primarily due to its high recurrence rate. It is typically characterized by the rapid onset of breast swelling and pain, abscess formation, and persistent fistulas [1]. NPM accounts for approximately 4%−5% of benign breast diseases [2]. At present, no universally effective treatment has been established. Given its chronic course and tendency to recur, NPM negatively impacts breast aesthetics and quality of life, often leading to considerable physical discomfort and psychosocial distress [3]. Intraoperative observations indicate that nearly 60% of NPM patients present with abundant lipid-like secretions within the breast tissue [4]. Previous studies have suggested that these intraductal secretions are associated with localized immune-inflammatory responses in the mammary gland, potentially influencing disease progression [5, 6]. Despite existing observations, the specific associations between IDS and clinical parameters in NPM remain insufficiently defined. Key questions persist regarding which risk factors (e.g., obesity) contribute to IDS formation, whether IDS is linked to systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation, and to what extent IDS influences disease prognosis. These knowledge gaps hinder the preoperative identification of high-risk IDS-positive patients and limit the development of targeted therapeutic strategies. To address this unmet need, the first comprehensive analysis was conducted, examining the associations between IDS and demographic characteristics, clinical manifestations, laboratory indicators, and prognostic outcomes in patients with NPM.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study enrolled 340 patients with NPM who underwent surgical treatment at the Department of Breast, Shuguang Hospital, Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, between April 2020 and December 2021. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shuguang Hospital (Approval No. 2020-803-10-02).

Diagnostic criteria

NPM

The disease occurs in non-lactating and non-pregnant women and is primarily characterized by clinical manifestations such as breast masses, mastalgia, and nipple discharge. Breast masses may develop secondary acute infections, leading to abscess formation on the basis of chronic lesions. In advanced stages, abscess rupture can result in mammary fistulas, sinus tracts, or ulcers, which frequently prove refractory to treatment.

IDS

During surgery, patients were assigned to the IDS group if they met either of the following criteria: (1) intraoperative observation of abundant lipid-like secretions or comedo-like material within the glandular tissue (Fig. 1), (2) or histological confirmation of dilated mammary ducts containing pink, granular, dense material (Fig. 2) [7]. Patients who did not fulfill these criteria were categorized into the non-IDS group.

Prognosis

Complete Remission: Defined as the resolution of clinical symptoms with no palpable inflammatory lesions, complete healing of ulcers or wounds, and maintenance of these conditions for at least six months without recurrence. Imaging findings must confirm the absence of residual lesions.

Recurrence: Defined as the reappearance of local symptoms (redness, swelling, heat, pain, suppuration, or ulceration) or imaging evidence of lesions within six months after achieving complete remission in the originally affected area, following the exclusion of other causes unrelated to NPM.

Ipsilateral New lesions: Defined as the occurrence of local symptoms (redness, swelling, heat, pain, suppuration, or ulceration) or imaging evidence of lesions in either the original site or another area of the same breast more than six months after achieving complete remission, after excluding other causes unrelated to NPM.

Bilateral Disease: Defined as the presence of clinical symptoms (redness, swelling, heat, pain, suppuration, or ulceration) or imaging evidence of lesions in both breasts, after excluding other causes unrelated to NPM [8].

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) female patients meeting the clinical diagnostic criteria for NPM; (2) age ≥ 18 years; (3) hospitalized patients who underwent surgical treatment with pathologically confirmed NPM; and (4) patients who provided written informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) pregnant or lactating women; (2) patients with a pathological diagnosis of concurrent breast malignancy or other specific inflammatory conditions (e.g., tuberculosis); (3) patients with a history of systemic immune diseases, primary metabolic disorders, or malignancies; and (4) patients with substantial missing data that could affect outcome analysis.

Observational indicators

Demographic data

Demographic characteristics included age at onset, BMI, parity, and the interval between the last childbirth and disease onset.

Clinical characteristics

Clinical manifestations recorded for all included patients comprised disease duration, lesion extent, ulceration, nipple retraction, fever, and the presence of lower limb erythema or arthralgia.

Laboratory indicators

Preoperative blood tests were performed for all included patients to assess the following indicators: white blood cell count (WBC), CRP, ESR, PRL, Fg, blood lipids, humoral immunity, cell-mediated immunity, and cytokines. Values exceeding the upper detection limit were classified as elevated, while those below the lower detection limit were classified as reduced, according to the hospital’s reference standards. The analytical methods and corresponding instruments were specified as follows:

WBC: Automated hematology analyzer (Sysmex XN-9000).

CRP: Particle-enhanced immunoturbidimetry (Pumeria Medical Aristo, China).

ESR: Automated photometric method (Vastec Roller 20).

Fg: Clauss coagulation method (Sysmex CS-5100).

PRL: Chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassay (Abbott i2000).

Blood lipids: The total cholesterol (TC) level was determined using the enzymatic method, while triglycerides (TG) were measured by the GPO-PAP enzymatic method. Low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and high-density lipoprotein (HDL) were also analyzed using enzymatic assays. The peroxidase enzymatic method was employed to assess small dense LDL (sdLDL). Apolipoproteins, including apolipoprotein A-I (ApoA-I), A-II (ApoA-II), B (ApoB), C-II (ApoC-II), and C-III (ApoC-III), were quantified using the immunoturbidimetric assay. All analyses were performed on the Beckman Coulter AU5800 system.

Humoral immunity: Immunoturbidimetric analysis was conducted using the Beckman Coulter IMMAGE 800 system. Cell-mediated immunity was assessed through flow cytometry with the Mindray BriCyte E6. Cytokine levels were measured using a multiplex bead-based fluorescent immunoassay on the Beckman Coulter DxFLEX platform.

Follow-up

Follow-up was carried out through telephone interviews and outpatient visits to evaluate recovery time, recurrence, the development of new ipsilateral breast lesions, and the occurrence of bilateral breast involvement.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables with a normal distribution were reported as mean ± standard deviation (X ± S) and compared using Student’s t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (min, max) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequency (percentage) and evaluated using either the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, depending on applicability. Multivariable analysis was conducted using a logistic regression model, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was employed to assess the incidence of ipsilateral new lesions. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P-value of < 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Demographic characteristics

This study enrolled 340 patients, of whom 54.7% (186/340) presented with IDS in mammary tissue, while 45.3% (154/340) showed no evidence of IDS. The median age at onset was 33 years in patients with IDS compared to 32 years in those without IDS, suggesting that disease onset occurred slightly later in the IDS group. Overweight/obesity (BMI ≥ 24) was significantly more common among IDS patients (50.5%) than in their non-IDS counterparts (33.1%; P = 0.001), which was consistent with the higher median BMI observed in the IDS group (24.0 vs. 22.9 kg/m2, P = 0.013). Overall, 94.7% of patients were parous, with 73.2% developing the disease within 2–5 years postpartum. However, no significant differences were found between the two groups with respect to parity or the interval between disease onset and the last delivery (Table 1).

Immune function

Compared with the non-IDS group, patients in the IDS group demonstrated significantly higher levels of complement C3, C4, and total IgE in humoral immunity (Table 6). Analysis of cellular immunity further revealed a notable increase in Treg levels among IDS patients (Table 7). These findings indicate that IDS may contribute to partial activation of the complement system, thereby eliciting localized hypersensitivity reactions.

Prognostic indicators

All enrolled patients underwent surgical treatment, with follow-up completed by March 2023 (median follow-up: 2 years). The non-IDS group demonstrated higher short-term recovery rates (86.4% vs. 71.0% remission within one month post-operation; P = 0.001), suggesting prolonged healing in IDS patients. During follow-up, IDS patients exhibited significantly higher rates of ipsilateral recurrence (4.3% vs. 0%; P = 0.009) and bilateral disease development (21.5% vs. 11.7%; P = 0.017) compared with the non-IDS group (Table 9). Kaplan-Meier analysis with the log-rank test also showed a statistically significant difference in the incidence of ipsilateral new lesions between the groups (P = 0.008). The hazard ratio (HR) was 6.482 (95% CI: 1.613–26.050), indicating that IDS patients had a 6.5-fold higher risk of developing new ipsilateral lesions than non-IDS patients. To further validate this association while accounting for potential confounders, Cox regression analyses were performed. The unadjusted Cox model yielded an HR of 57.247 (95% CI: 0.189-17,294.919; P = 0.165) for IDS presence. Although Cox models did not reach statistical significance—likely due to the limited number of events (n = 8) and the resulting wide confidence intervals—the directional consistency with the significant Kaplan-Meier findings (HR = 6.482) suggests a clinically relevant association warranting confirmation in larger cohorts with extended follow-up. Bilateral disease was defined as involvement of both breasts during the follow-up period, including either (a) pre-existing contralateral NPM at enrollment or (b) new contralateral onset during follow-up. Among IDS patients, 40 cases (21.5%) developed bilateral disease with a median inter-breast onset interval of 16 months (range: 0.5–155), while 18 non-IDS patients (11.7%) developed bilateral involvement at a median interval of 12.45 months (range: 1-59.3). No significant difference in onset intervals was observed between groups (P = 0.359). Collectively, these findings suggest that IDS is associated with delayed healing and increased recurrence rates.

Multivariable analysis of IDS-related characteristics in NPM

Using the presence or absence of IDS as the dependent variable, and variables with P < 0.05 in univariable analyses of demographic data, clinical characteristics, and laboratory parameters as independent variables, a multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted. The results showed that both older age and overweight/obesity were independent risk factors for IDS (all P < 0.05). Discussion

IDS are recognized as important contributing factors to NPM. Their abnormal accumulation results in ductal dilation and rupture, thereby inducing chemical or immune-mediated stimulation that initiates inflammatory responses [9]. Cytological studies of IDS in non-lactating women have identified clusters of intact cells with morphological features characteristic of highly metabolic activity, including well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum, prominent Golgi apparatus, and abundant free ribosomes, all reflecting active lipid and protein synthesis [10, 11]. These secretions within mammary tissue may interact with mammary epithelial cells through both direct and indirect pathways.

The findings indicate that NPM patients with IDS had a higher BMI, with a greater proportion classified as overweight or obese (50.5% vs. 33.1%). Multivariable logistic regression analysis further identified overweight/obesity as an independent risk factor for IDS in NPM, with a 1.996-fold higher odds compared with the non-IDS group. Adipose tissue plays a pivotal role in mammary gland development by secreting specific adipokines that regulate epithelial cell function and mammary gland growth [12–14]. In obese individuals, the substantial increase in adipocyte number is accompanied by dysregulated adipokine secretion and functional impairment. Under stress and oxidative conditions, adipocytes undergo structural injury, degeneration, and abnormal proliferation, thereby disrupting local metabolic homeostasis and amplifying inflammatory responses [15].

Previous studies have demonstrated that obesity alters the expression of genes associated with inflammation and immune responses in mice [16]. Similarly, a retrospective study of 284 NPM patients reported significantly elevated lipoprotein levels and reduced HDL levels compared with healthy controls [17]. The untargeted lipidomic analysis of breast tissue from NPM patients revealed significant lipid metabolism dysregulation, with triglyceride metabolites identified as the predominant differential lipids and arachidonic acid metabolism emerging as the main pathway underlying these alterations [18]. Abnormal triglyceride metabolism promotes oxidative stress, excessive lipid accumulation, immune cell activation, and inflammation. Likewise, arachidonic acid can give rise to bioactive lipid metabolites, including prostaglandins and leukotrienes, which provoke strong inflammatory responses through the cyclooxygenase, lipoxygenase, and cytochrome P450 pathways [19].

In this study, 94.7% of patients with NPM had a history of childbirth, with 73.2% developing symptoms 2–5 years postpartum. It is hypothesized that intraductal secretion retention may be linked to incomplete mammary gland involution following lactation. During lactation, mammary ducts expand rapidly to form alveoli composed of secretory epithelial cells, while mammary white adipocytes can undergo transdifferentiation into intermediate phenotypes between adipocytes and epithelial cells, characterized by lipid droplet segmentation, mitochondrial hypertrophy, peroxisome proliferation, and abundant endoplasmic reticulum [20]. These modified adipocytes not only demonstrate enhanced secretory capacity but also exhibit immunoreactivity to whey acidic protein [21]. After weaning, the mammary gland undergoes involution, a tightly regulated process in which secretory epithelial cells undergo widespread apoptosis and detach into the ductal lumen, accompanied by progressive alveolar atrophy. At the same time, mammary adipocytes restore tissue structure through two mechanisms: differentiation of new mature white adipocytes and lipid accumulation-induced hypertrophy of existing fat cells [22]. These coordinated cellular events ultimately return the mammary gland to its pre-pregnancy morphology [23, 24]. The formation of large cytoplasmic vacuoles containing protein globules and lipids, along with increased vesiculation, represents a hallmark morphological feature of mammary gland involution. However, age-related changes in endocrine and metabolic function may impair complete involution, leading to the abnormal accumulation of lipid-like secretions [25, 26]. Although this study did not identify statistically significant differences in blood lipid profiles between groups, the IDS group exhibited higher rates of dyslipidemia, suggesting possible implications for altered lipid metabolism that warrant further investigation.

In this study, no significant differences in clinical symptoms were observed between the two groups, as both presented with typical manifestations of NPM, including breast masses, pain, and erythema. This suggests that the presence of IDS does not substantially influence the clinical presentation of NPM. Consequently, clinical diagnosis cannot rely solely on symptoms to identify IDS, and additional diagnostic modalities are necessary for accurate evaluation.

Patients with IDS typically exhibit characteristic ultrasound findings, including varying degrees of ductal dilation (approximately 3–5 mm or larger in diameter) posterior to the nipple, with reduced sound transmission within the ductal lumen. Lipid-like secretions often appear as hypoechoic areas, and gentle compression with the probe may reveal subtle echo movement, which can aid in diagnosis. On contrast-enhanced breast magnetic resonance imaging, enhancement of the dilated duct walls is commonly observed. Imaging characteristics vary according to ductal content: (1) serous secretions appear as linear, branching hypointense signals on fat-suppressed T1-weighted images (T1WI) and hyperintense signals on T2-weighted images (T2WI) in the retroareolar region; (2) lipid-like secretions show hyperintense signals on both T1WI and T2WI; and (3) protein-rich secretions exhibit hyperintense signals on fat-suppressed T1WI.

The findings indicate that patients with IDS exhibit significantly elevated systemic inflammatory markers. This heightened inflammatory state appears to coincide with the extensive and persistent accumulation of IDS, which contains antigenic substances capable of activating the immune system. These secretory components are linked to alterations in both humoral and cellular immune responses. Emerging evidence suggests that impaired clearance of IDS may trigger localized immune-inflammatory responses in mammary tissue, thereby accelerating disease progression [5, 6]. Lipid components and antibodies within retained secretions activate regional immune pathways, leading to epithelial barrier disruption characterized by widened intercellular gaps, basement membrane degradation, and inflammatory cell infiltration. In this study, patients with IDS demonstrated significantly higher levels of complement components (C3, C4) and total IgE. Complement activation not only facilitates opsonization and enhances macrophage phagocytosis, but also mediates vasodilation [27], tissue injury, inflammation, and chemotaxis, thereby promoting the recruitment of macrophages and neutrophils [28]. These amplified complement responses intensify inflammatory cascades and contribute to more severe clinical manifestations. Elevated total IgE levels may further reflect localized hypersensitivity reactions to retained secretory antigens. In parallel, secretory stasis leads to acinar distension, stimulates cytokine release, and activates lysosome-mediated cell death pathways [29, 30]. Accumulated intraductal pressure further compromises epithelial junctional integrity [31]. Upon ductal rupture, extravasated secretions interact with stromal immune cells (primarily macrophages), immunoglobulins, and cytokines, thereby initiating sterile inflammation [32].

Analysis of outcomes in NPM patients revealed that those with IDS experienced prolonged recovery times, higher rates of ipsilateral new lesions, and greater bilateral disease involvement compared with patients without IDS. These findings suggest that the presence of IDS is associated with increased treatment complexity and an elevated risk of recurrence in NPM. Owing to the extensive distribution of the ductal system within breast tissue, complete lesion excision remains technically challenging, and residual IDS in the breast may contribute to impaired wound healing or disease recurrence. It is hypothesized that IDS production may be influenced by systemic metabolic and immune factors, potentially explaining its occurrence in the contralateral breast and the increased frequency of bilateral disease. Supporting this, Sun et al. developed machine learning models using Random forest (RF) and XGBoost algorithms to predict postoperative recurrence in NPM patients, where BMI was identified as the strongest predictive factor in the RF model, while IDS emerged as the primary predictor in the XGBoost model [33]. Nevertheless, the precise etiology of IDS formation remains unclear, and the lack of targeted therapeutic strategies poses challenges for effective management.

This study highlights IDS as a notable feature of NPM, providing new perspectives for understanding the disease. However, the mechanisms underlying IDS formation and its associations with inflammation, metabolism, and immune regulation require further investigation. For NPM patients presenting with IDS, therapeutic strategies aimed at controlling or reducing intraductal secretions may represent an important management consideration. Moreover, given that the findings identify obesity as an independent risk factor for IDS in NPM, weight management interventions may warrant exploration as a potential preventive or therapeutic approach.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths of this study include its novelty, being the first large-scale cohort (n = 340) to systematically characterize the comprehensive associations between IDS and demographic, clinical, laboratory, and prognostic parameters in patients with NPM. The use of multivariable modeling allowed robust identification of overweight/obesity and older age as independent determinants of IDS, while also quantifying their contribution to delayed healing and increased risks of ipsilateral and bilateral disease. Limitations of this study stem primarily from its retrospective design, which may introduce selection bias and residual confounding. Important factors such as dietary patterns and lifestyle variables were not captured, and the modest number of recurrence events limited statistical precision, resulting in wider confidence intervals. Furthermore, the study establishes associative rather than causal relationships, and the molecular mechanisms linking IDS to systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation remain to be clarified in future research.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that obesity-related IDS is closely associated with heightened systemic inflammation, immune dysregulation, and unfavorable prognosis in NPM. These findings highlight the clinical relevance of IDS as a marker of more severe disease phenotype, offering opportunities for improved risk stratification and personalized management. Importantly, overweight/obesity emerged as an independent risk factor for IDS, suggesting that weight management may represent both a preventive and therapeutic adjunct. Optimizing metabolic health in this population could mitigate the burden of chronic inflammatory disease. Future research should aim to elucidate the mechanistic pathways linking obesity, IDS, and immune dysfunction, while exploring targeted therapeutic strategies to reduce secretory activity or block associated inflammatory cascades. Such advances hold promise for improving clinical outcomes in patients with NPM.

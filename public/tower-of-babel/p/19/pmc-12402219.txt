Research on real-time detection of fabric defects based on an improved Elo rating algorithm
Authors: Xiaobo Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To further enhance the detection efficiency of complex textured fabric defects and meet real-time detection demands, an improved Elo rating algorithm is proposed. Initially, the traditional Elo rating algorithm is refined, with a focus on threshold calculation and detection processes. The concept of integral images is incorporated to reduce computational demands and improve operational speed. The refined algorithm is then applied to detect three types of fabric samples, achieving an overall detection accuracy exceeding 80%. Parameter analysis indicates that selecting a sub-region count R between 10 and 30 yields optimal results, with larger sub-regions demonstrating higher detection accuracy. Comparative experiments with five object detection models are conducted, introducing two evaluation metrics: mean Average Precision (mAP) and Frames Per Second (FPS). Results show the algorithm outperforms others in mAP and FPS (102.1), thereby meeting industrial real-time detection requirements.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-17747-y.

Introduction

Fabric defect detection is a critical process in ensuring textile quality1. Traditional defect detection methods are suitable for simple textures and common defects but often fail to accurately detect complex textures, new defect types, or defects with high similarity to fabric textures, leading to misdetection and omissions. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning, have been applied to fabric defect detection. For instance, Wang et al.2 proposed a deep convolutional neural network model for defect detection, achieving over 90% accuracy for common defects. Li et al.3 introduced a multilayer perceptron optimization model for high accuracy in small sample sizes. Wei et al.4 combined data compression with convolutional neural networks to improve classification accuracy. Girshick et al.5 proposed a region-based target detection method to enhance detection speed. Liu et al.6 improved the U-Net network by incorporating an attention mechanism, further boosting detection efficiency. Zhang et al.7 proposed a YOLOv2-based method for color fabric defect detection, reducing feature extraction costs. Despite these advancements, existing methods struggle with fabrics featuring diverse textures, rich colors, and complex patterns, often resulting in lower detection rates and longer detection times. To address these limitations, the Elo grading algorithm has a dynamic scoring mechanism, supports evaluation of sub regions of different sizes, has good noise resistance, and does not require a large number of annotations, this paper proposes an improved Elo rating algorithm for real-time fabric defect detection, aiming to reduce computational costs and improve efficiency.

Improved Elo rating algorithm

Elo-rating (ER) algorithm8 was initially used to evaluate the level of athletic activities, with the continuous expansion of the application surface, ER algorithm is gradually applied to the field of defect detection, through a large number of image blocks between the competition to update the Elo-rating score, and then find the defective region, the algorithm exists in the model runtime is too long and the detection of inefficiency and other problems, especially for the complex texture and patterned fabrics, the running time of the model is directly extended due to the large amount of computation. Therefore, the traditional ER algorithm needs to be improved to increase the detection efficiency of the algorithm.

The improved Elo rating algorithm is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Sample testing and parameter analysis

Three types of fabrics are used for sample detection, namely: grey fabric samples, color fabric samples and patterned fabric samples. The size of the sample fabric images were 512 pixels Χ 512 pixels, which contained 1300 defective samples and 300 defect-free samples, totaling 1600 samples. In addition, this article also uses the TILDA dataset11 for a more extensive comparative analysis. Different types of fabric samples are shown in Fig. 2.

In order to test the effectiveness of the algorithm proposed in this paper, three types of fabric samples are detected, the parameters selected during the detection process are consistent, the number of randomly selected subregions R is 15, the constant K is 30, the subregion size m Χ n is set to 4 Χ 4, the defect detection results of the fabric are shown in Fig. 3.

As can be seen from Fig. 3, the overall detection accuracy of the three types of fabric samples using the algorithm proposed in this paper exceeds 80%, with the Accuracy indicator exceeding 90%, indicating a high detection accuracy. In addition, it is found that the detection accuracy is generally higher when the difference between the background color of the fabric and the blemish is larger; when the difference between the background color of the fabric and the blemish is smaller, the detection accuracy is reduced, the main reason is that the Elo algorithm relies on the difference between sub regions and templates for scoring. If defects are similar to the background in texture, color, or grayscale, the feature contrast is not obvious, resulting in insignificant score changes; In addition, slight differences are difficult to trigger a significant decrease in Elo scores, and the algorithm may misjudge them as normal areas. Signals with low contrast defects are easily masked by image noise, further reducing detection sensitivity. The sensitivity of the improved Elo algorithm for detecting different types of fabric defects mainly depends on the scale of the defects, the degree of texture damage, and the contrast with the background. It is most sensitive to large-scale, high contrast, and texture damage defects; The sensitivity to small and low contrast defects is low, which is also the focus of future research.

Parameter selection directly affects the accuracy of defects detection, the more influential parameters includes the number of randomly selected subregions R, subregion size m Χ n. When the subregion size m Χ n is fixed, the number of randomly selected subregions R directly affects the algorithm’s execution time and detection accuracy, four types of size types of the subregion are selected as follows: 10 Χ 9, 8 Χ 5, 6 Χ 6, and 3 Χ 3, and each size is selected as follows 100 images are detected and the algorithm execution time and detection accuracy are calculated for different sizes respectively and the results are shown in Figs. 4 and 5.

From Fig. 4, it can be seen that when the number of randomly selected subregions R increases, the execution time of the algorithms corresponding to subregions of different sizes also increases, and when R > 50, the execution time will increase rapidly. From Fig. 5, it can be seen that when 10 < R < 30, subregions of different sizes have higher detection accuracy, while when R > 40, subregions of different sizes will have their detection accuracy significantly decreased. Therefore, in order to obtain a higher detection accuracy and a shorter execution time, it is more suitable to randomly select the number of subregions R between 10 and 30. In addition, when the number of subregions R is between 10 and 30, the detection accuracy of subregions with larger sizes is higher, and the detection accuracy of subregion size 10 Χ 9 in Fig. 5 is significantly higher than that of subregions with smaller sizes. Because larger size of subregions can capture fabric periodic textures and typical defects more completely, provide more stable statistical features, and reduce noise interference; However, smaller sizes of subregions are easily affected by local fluctuations, making it difficult to cover complete defects or texture units, resulting in insufficient discrimination and decreased accuracy. So it is more suitable to select subregion size m Χ n of 10 Χ 9.

Conclusion

To further enhance the detection accuracy and meet the real-time requirements for fabric defect detection, this paper proposes an improved Elo rating algorithm. Through comprehensive algorithm analysis and comparative experiments, the following conclusions are drawn:

The traditional Elo algorithm suffers from long runtimes and low efficiency. By focusing on improvements to the threshold calculation and detection processes, and introducing the integral image technique, the proposed algorithm significantly enhances the model’s computational speed.The proposed algorithm was tested on three types of fabric samples, achieving an overall detection accuracy of over 80%. Furthermore, parametric analysis revealed that the number of randomly selected subregions, R, is optimal within the range of 10 to 30. Notably, subregions with larger sizes demonstrated higher detection accuracy.Comparative experiments with five types of target detection models were conducted. The proposed algorithm achieved a higher mean average precision (mAP) compared to other models. In terms of real-time performance, the algorithm attained an average frame rate of 102.1 frames per second (FPS), surpassing other models and meeting the real-time detection requirements for industrial applications.

Retinoic acid-related orphan nuclear receptor alpha inhibits adipogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells via activating WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway
Authors: Lei He, Zihao Chen, Tianwei He, Jiawei Di, Lang Mai, Ruijue Zhu, Depeng Wu, Liangming Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe balance between adipogenic and osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) is essential for maintaining bone homeostasis. This study aimed to investigate the role of retinoid-related orphan receptor α (RORα) in the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs.MethodsStable BMSC lines with RORα overexpression or knockdown were established. Adipogenic differentiation was evaluated using Oil Red O staining and by measuring the expression of adipogenic markers, including PPARγ2, LPL, LEP, FABP4, and ADIPOQ.ResultsTreatment with the RORα inhibitor SR3335 significantly promoted adipogenic differentiation, whereas the RORα agonist SR1078 exerted the opposite effect. Similarly, RORα-overexpressing (OE-RORα) BMSCs showed reduced adipogenic differentiation, while RORα knockdown BMSCs exhibited enhanced differentiation at 14 days after induction. During adipogenesis, PPARγ2 expression increased significantly, peaking at day 6 before gradually declining. Overexpression and knockdown of RORα accentuated this downregulation and upregulation, respectively, at days 6 and 12. The adipogenic marker genes lipoprotein lipase (LPL), leptin (LEP), fatty acid binding protein 4 (FABP4), and adiponectin C1Q and collagen domain containing (ADIPOQ) were markedly downregulated in RORα-overexpressing BMSCs at day 12. Moreover, RORα overexpression enhanced β-catenin nuclear translocation at day 1 post-induction and upregulated downstream WNT/β-catenin signaling molecules (Axin2, c-Myc, CD44) at day 6. Inhibition of WNT/β-catenin signaling with XAV-939 effectively reversed the suppressive effect of RORα overexpression on adipogenic differentiation and restored the expression of adipogenesis-related genes.ConclusionRORα suppresses adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs, at least in part, by activating WNT/β-catenin signaling.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03325-5.Keywords: Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, Adipogenic differentiation, Retinoic acid-related orphan nuclear receptor alpha (RORα), WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue, with elevated risks of bone fragility and fracture [1]. The pathogenic mechanism of osteoporosis is an imbalance between bone formation and resorption [2]. Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), a non-hematopoietic stem cell population in the bone marrow, are multipotent cells capable of differentiating into multiple lineages [3]. It is known that the differentiation balance of BMSCs can affect bone homeostasis [4]. In adults, BMSCs differentiate mainly into osteoblasts and adipocytes in the skeleton, and the balance between adipogenic and osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs is important for maintaining bone mass [5]. An increase in adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs is often accompanied by a decrease in bone formation, in turn leading to osteoporosis [6]. However, the underlying molecular mechanism remains not fully understood, and a better understanding of the regulatory mechanisms of BMSCs differentiation may provide new avenues to prevent and treat osteoporosis.

Retinoic acid-related orphan nuclear receptor alpha (RORα) is a member of the metabolic nuclear receptor superfamily and functions as a transcription factor by binding to the RORα responsive elements of the target genes [7]. It has been demonstrated that RORα plays a crucial role in a variety of physiological processes, such as lipid metabolism [7], bone homeostasis [8], cellular differentiation [9, 10], inflammation [11] and adipogenic differentiation [12–15]. Duez et al. have reported that RORα is highly expressed in human adipose tissue, and that overexpression of RORα inhibits the adipogenic differentiation, while RORα knockdown significantly promotes adipogenic differentiation [12]. Ohoka et al. found that RORα inhibits the transcriptional activity of CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein β (C/EBPβ), thereby resulting in the suppression of C/EBPβ-dependent adipogenesis in 3T3-L1 adipocytes [15]. Meanwhile, RORα also represses the expression of the lipid droplet coating protein gene perilipin [15].

Although RORα has been shown to be involved in the adipogenesis, whether RORα is implicated in the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs remains poorly understood. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate the role of RORα in the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs.

Materials and methods

Isolation, culture, and characterization of BMSCs

Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) from C57BL/6 mice were obtained from Cyagen Biosciences (Guangzhou, China). Cells were initially cultured in T75 flasks using Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and maintained at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere. The culture medium was replaced every 2–3 days. Upon reaching approximately 90% confluence, cells were passaged by washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), detaching with 0.25% Trypsin-0.53 mM EDTA solution, and neutralizing with complete medium. After centrifugation at 350 × g for 5 min, the cell pellet was resuspended and viable cells were counted using a hemocytometer with Trypan Blue exclusion. All experiments were conducted using BMSCs from a single donor at passages 3–5 (P3–P5).

Adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs and oil red O staining

The passaged mouse BMSCs were seeded onto a 6-well plate at a density of 1 × 104/cm2. BMSC identity was confirmed by flow cytometry. Cells exhibited ≥ 95% positivity for mesenchymal markers CD29 and CD44, and ≤ 2% expression for hematopoietic/endothelial markers CD31 and CD117 (Supplementary Fig. 1). Their multipotent differentiation capacity (osteogenic, adipogenic, and chondrogenic) was also verified (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Adipogenic differentiation was induced using the OriCell™ Mouse Mesenchymal Stem Cell Adipogenic Differentiation Kit (Cat No. MUXMD-90031; Cyagen) [16]. Mouse MSCs were seeded and grown to 100% confluency, followed by an additional two days of culture. To initiate differentiation, the culture medium was replaced with Adipogenic Induction Medium (Solution A) for 3 days, followed by Adipogenic Maintenance Medium (Solution B) for 1 day. This induction/maintenance cycle was repeated once. Subsequently, the cells were cultured in Adipogenic Maintenance Medium, which was refreshed every 2–3 days, for a total of 14 days. Successful differentiation was confirmed by Oil Red O staining, where cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with Oil Red O solution to visualize intracellular lipid droplets.

Osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and Alizarin Red staining

For osteogenic differentiation, BMSCs were cultured in the osteogenic induction medium (L-DMEM medium containing and 10% fetal bovine serum, and 0.1 μmol/L dexamethasone, 50 μmol/L ascorbic acid, and 10 mmol/L β-glycerophosphate). The medium was replaced every 3 days and the cells were cultured with osteogenic induction medium for 21 days. Following the removal of culture medium and PBS rinsing, cells were then fixed in a 4% neutral formaldehyde solution at room temperature for 30–60 min. After two additional PBS washes, cells were incubated with an appropriate volume of Alizarin Red staining solution (Leagene Biotechnology, BD0030) for 3–5 min. Subsequently, cells were rinsed twice with PBS and observed under a microscope. Successful induction of osteogenic differentiation was validated by the presence of calcium nodules displaying a red or orange color upon binding with the Alizarin Red dye.

For image-based quantification, stained wells were photographed at 4 × magnification. Red-stained regions were thresholded using ImageJ, and the results were expressed as the percentage of mineralized area per field.

Chondrogenic differentiation of BMSCs and Alcian Blue staining

For chondrogenic differentiation, a pellet culture system was utilized. Briefly, 2.5 × 105 BMSCs were centrifuged at 400 × g for 5 min in a 15 mL polypropylene conical tube to form a cell pellet. The pellet was then cultured in a chondrogenic induction medium consisting of high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 100 nM dexamethasone, 50 μg/mL ascorbate-2-phosphate, 1% ITS + Premix (Insulin-Transferrin-Selenium), and 10 ng/mL recombinant human TGF-β3. The medium was carefully replaced every 3 days for 21 days, without disturbing the pellet.

After 21 days of induction, the cell pellets were harvested, washed with PBS, and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. The fixed pellets were then dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. To assess chondrogenesis, the sections were stained with 1% Alcian Blue solution (pH 2.5) for 30 min to detect sulfated proteoglycans, a major component of the cartilage matrix. After washing, the sections were counterstained with Nuclear Fast Red for 5 min, dehydrated, and mounted for microscopic observation.

CCK8 cell proliferation assay

BMSCs were seeded in 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells/well). After 24 h, cells were treated with various concentrations of RAR-related orphan receptor alpha (RORα) agonist SR1078 (MCE, HY-14422) or RORα inhibitor SR3335 (MCE, HY-14413) for 48 or 96 h. Cell viability was assessed by adding 10 μL of Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) solution (Dojindo, CK04) to each well. After a 1–4 h incubation, the absorbance of each well was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader (SpectraMax). The cell viability of each treatment group was normalized to the untreated control group (0 µM) at each respective time point, with the viability of the control group defined as 100%.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR)

To assess gene expression changes during the differentiation of BMSCs, total RNA was first extracted using an RNA extraction kit (Sigma, LGCQER090150) and subsequently reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with the Reverse Transcription Kit (Takara, RR037A). The expression levels of specific target genes were then quantified using a real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR system (LightCycler 480, Roche, USA). The analyzed genes included Rorα; key adipogenesis-related genes such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma 2 (PPAR-γ2), LPL, LEP, and FABP4; crucial osteogenesis-related genes including Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and bone gamma-carboxyglutamate protein (BGLAP); and downstream target genes of the WNT pathway such as Axin2 and c-Myc. All primer sequences used for RT-qPCR are detailed in Table 1. Data were obtained from three independent experiments. The expression level of each target gene was normalized to the endogenous housekeeping gene GAPDH. The relative gene expression was calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method and presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Western blot

For protein extraction, 100 μL of protein lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B) was added to the cells, and the cell lysate was collected by cell scraping and transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 × g at 4 °C for 30 min. The resulting supernatant was collected. Protein concentration was measured using the BCA method. Equal amounts of protein sample were electrophoresed and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (Millipore, IPVH00010). After blocking with 5% skim milk in TBST buffer for 1 h, the PVDF membrane was incubated with primary antibodies against RORα (Abcam, ab278099), β-Catenin (CST, D10A8), GAPDH (Abclonal, A19056), β-actin (CST, 4970S), and histone H3 (CST, 9715S) at 4 °C overnight. After extensive washing, the PVDF membrane was incubated with the secondary antibody (Abcam, UK) at room temperature. The ECL luminescent solution (Millipore, GERPN2232) was evenly applied to the PVDF membrane. Protein signals were analyzed using the Tanon 5200 Chemiluminescent Imaging System (Tanon Science & Technology, China). The intensity of protein bands was quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA), and the relative expression of target proteins was normalized to the loading control GAPDH.

Laser scanning confocal microscope

Cells were seeded in a petri dish dedicated to laser confocal microscopy, fixed in ice-cold methanol for 20 min, followed by incubation with 0.1% Triton X-100 solution at room temperature for 30 min, and then with 5% BSA at room temperature for 1 h. After that, the cells were incubated with primary antibody β-Catenin diluted in 3% BSA at 4 °C overnight. The cells were then incubated with a secondary antibody conjugated with Alexa Fluor 488 (Thermo Fisher, USA) at room temperature in the dark for 1 h. Following Hoechst staining (10 μg/ml, Beyotime, T3204) for 10 min at room temperature, the cells were examined by laser confocal microscopy. Fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ software.

Establishment of OE-RORα and Sh-RORα stable cell lines

Mouse RORα plasmids and lentiviruses were constructed, expanded, and validated by Cyagen Biosciences (Guangzhou, China), including a lentiviral RORα overexpression vector (OE-RORα) and a lentiviral RORα interference vector (Sh-RORα). BMSCs were transduced at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 50. Cells were incubated with 1000 μL of medium containing 10 μg/ml polybrene for 1 h, and 20 μL of OE-RORα, Sh-RORα virus, or control (empty vector) lentivirus was added to each well. After 48 h, puromycin was added to each well at a final concentration of 1 μg/ml for selection, and the transfection efficiency (fluorescence intensity) was observed under a fluorescence microscope. The medium containing 1 μg/ml puromycin was replaced every 48 h until all cells in the blank group underwent apoptosis. After the cells reached confluence, the puromycin concentration was reduced to 0.2 μg/ml. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR and Western blot, and subsequent experiments were performed.

WNT/β-catenin Pathway Inhibition

To investigate RORα–WNT crosstalk, XAV-939 (Selleckchem, S1180) was continuously administered at 1 μM to BMSCs in the OE-RORα group throughout the 14-day adipogenic and osteogenic differentiation period. Fresh compound-containing medium was replaced every 3 days. Control groups received equivalent volumes of vehicle (0.1% DMSO).

Establishment of Murine Osteoporosis Model

All experimental procedures were approved by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments at South China Agricultural University (Approval No. 2021F107). Female C57BL/6 mice (8–10 weeks old) underwent bilateral ovariectomy (OVX) to establish a postmenopausal osteoporosis model. Surgical anesthesia was induced via intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (100 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg). Bilateral ovarian excision was performed using a dorsal approach under aseptic conditions, while sham-operated controls underwent the same procedure without ovary removal. Postoperative analgesia (buprenorphine, 0.1 mg/kg, SC) was administered for 72 h, with daily monitoring of recovery status. Model validation through bone density assessment and histological analysis was performed 6–8 weeks post-surgery.

Western blot analysis of bone tissue

Total protein was extracted from femoral bone tissues using RIPA lysis buffer. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay, and equal amounts of protein from each sample were subjected to Western blot analysis. Primary antibodies against RORα (Abcam, ab278099) were used, with GAPDH (Abclonal, A19056) serving as the loading control. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL system, and their relative intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

Histological validation by hematoxylin & eosin (H&E) staining

H&E staining was performed to assess trabecular bone structure and cellular morphology in decalcified bone tissues. Tibiae were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, then decalcified in 10% EDTA (pH 7.4) for 14–21 days at 4 °C. After dehydration, tissues were embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 5 μm. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and stained with hematoxylin for 5 min and eosin for 2 min following standard protocols.

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared among the groups by one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc analysis. All data shown are representative of three independent experiments and are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS software (SPSS Statistics V22, IBM Corporation, USA). P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

RORα inhibitor treatment enhanced adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs

To address whether RORα affects the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs, the RORα agonist SR1078 and RORα inhibitor SR3335 were adopted. The CCK-8 assay was performed to determine the appropriate concentration. The results showed that at concentrations of 5 to 100 μM, both SR1078 and SR3335 significantly inhibited BMSC survival at 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 96 h (all P < 0.05, Supplementary Fig. 3A and 3B). As a result, 1 μM and 2 μM of SR1078 and SR3335 were used in the subsequent experiments.

RT-PCR confirmed that 2 μM of SR1078 significantly upregulated RORα mRNA, whereas 2 μM of SR3335 significantly downregulated RORα mRNA compared with untreated controls (both P < 0.001, Supplementary Fig. 3C) in BMSCs. Consistently, western blot analysis showed that SR1078 and SR3335 treatment significantly altered the protein expression level of RORα in BMSCs (all P < 0.01, Supplementary Fig. 3D).

Adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs was assessed by Oil Red O staining. Fourteen days after induction, SR1078 (1 and 2 μM) significantly inhibited adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs, while SR3335 (1 and 2 μM) significantly promoted adipogenic differentiation (all P < 0.01, Fig. 1A).

Fourteen days after induction, SR1078- and SR3335-treated BMSCs (1 and 2 μM) were subjected to Oil Red O staining. The bar chart presents the quantitative results of Oil Red O staining. B Osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs was assessed by Alizarin Red staining. Twenty-one days after induction, SR1078- and SR3335-treated BMSCs (1 and 2 μM) were subjected to Alizarin Red staining. The osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs was evaluated by Alizarin Red staining. After 21 days of induction, SR1078 (1 and 2 μM) significantly promoted osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, whereas SR3335 (1 and 2 μM) significantly inhibited osteogenic differentiation (all P < 0.001, Fig. 1B).

Overexpression and knockdown of RORα affected adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs

Although SR3335 and SR1078 effectively modulated RORα expression, small molecules are not fully specific and may cause off-target effects. To ensure specificity and confirm that the observed effects are truly RORα-dependent, we generated lentivirus-mediated RORα overexpression (OE-RORα) and knockdown (Sh-RORα) BMSCs for subsequent experiments. RT-PCR and western blot confirmed significantly altered mRNA and protein levels of RORα in OE-RORα BMSCs and Sh-RORα BMSCs (all P < 0.001, Fig. 2A and 2B). In addition, adipogenic differentiation was significantly enhanced in Sh-RORα BMSCs but significantly inhibited in OE-RORα BMSCs at 14 days (both P < 0.0001, Fig. 2C).

Osteogenic differentiation of OE-RORα and Sh-RORα BMSCs was also evaluated by Alizarin Red staining. As shown in Fig. 2D, osteogenic differentiation was significantly enhanced in OE-RORα BMSCs but significantly inhibited in Sh-RORα BMSCs after 21 days of induction (both P < 0.05).

Knockdown of RORα upregulated adipogenic markers in BMSCs during adipogenic differentiation

To further confirm adipogenic differentiation, RT-PCR was performed to assess dynamic changes in the mRNA expression of RORα and adipogenic markers in Sh-RORα and OE-RORα BMSCs. As shown in Fig. 3A, RORα expression gradually decreased after adipogenic induction in BMSCs, which was significantly lower at days 6 and 12 than at day 1 in the negative control, OE-RORα, and Sh-RORα BMSCs (all P < 0.05). Compared with the negative control, RORα was significantly upregulated in OE-RORα BMSCs at days 6 and 12, while downregulated in Sh-RORα BMSCs at day 6 (all P < 0.05).

PPARγ2 is a marker of adipogenesis. During adipogenic differentiation, PPARγ2 expression significantly increased, peaked at day 6, and then gradually decreased. At day 6, RORα overexpression further downregulated PPARγ2 mRNA, whereas RORα knockdown correspondingly upregulated it (all P < 0.05, Fig. 3B). Western blot analyses confirmed these findings at the protein level, showing that RORα overexpression accelerated the decline of PPARγ2, whereas knockdown delayed it (all P < 0.05, Fig. 3C, D). Meanwhile, compared with the negative control BMSCs, the expression of four adipogenic differentiation marker genes—LPL, LEP, FABP4, and ADIPOQ—was significantly upregulated in Sh-RORα BMSCs at 12 days post-induction (Fig. 3E). This result was consistent with the Oil Red O staining results on day 14, further confirming the role of RORα in adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs.

The expression of osteogenic-related genes, including RUNX2, ALP, and BGLAP, was also evaluated in OE-RORα and Sh-RORα BMSCs. As shown in Fig. 3F, RUNX2, ALP, and BGLAP were significantly upregulated in OE-RORα BMSCs undergoing osteogenic differentiation on day 21 (all P < 0.01). Conversely, these genes were significantly downregulated in Sh-RORα BMSCs (all P < 0.05, Fig. 3F). These findings indicate that RORα overexpression contributes to the promotion of BMSCs' osteogenic differentiation.

Role of RORα in osteoporosis

To investigate the involvement of RORα in osteoporosis, an osteoporotic mouse model was established. Immunohistochemistry analysis revealed a significant decrease in RORα expression levels in osteoporotic mice compared with the control group (Fig. 4A, P < 0.001). Treatment with the RORα agonist (SR1078) induced an upregulation of RORα expression in osteoporotic mice (P < 0.05).

Furthermore, to assess the therapeutic potential of RORα in regulating osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs for osteoporosis treatment, additional in vivo experiments were conducted. Hematoxylin and eosin staining showed that SR1078 (RORα agonist) treatment enhanced osteogenesis in osteoporotic mice, mitigating osteoporosis (Fig. 4A).

Consistent with these findings, both immunohistochemistry and western blot analyses demonstrated a marked reduction of RORα expression in the bone tissue of osteoporotic mice compared with sham controls, which was significantly restored by SR1078 treatment (Fig. 4B, C).

RORα regulated adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs via WNT/β-catenin signaling

Since RORα was observed to regulate adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs, we further investigated the underlying mechanism. WNT/β-catenin signaling has been reported to inhibit adipogenesis in MSCs [17, 18]; therefore, we assessed whether WNT/β-catenin signaling is involved in RORα-induced inhibition of adipogenic differentiation in BMSCs.

Immunofluorescence (Fig. 5A, B) and western blot (Fig. 5C) consistently demonstrated that one day after induction, RORα overexpression increased nuclear translocation of β-catenin (both P < 0.001), suggesting activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway. By contrast, RORα knockdown significantly inhibited WNT/β-catenin signaling activation (Fig. 5C)..

The expression of WNT/β-catenin downstream targets Axin2 and C-myc was examined at days 1, 3, 6, and 12 after adipogenic induction. Both genes were modestly expressed at days 1 and 3, markedly upregulated at day 6, and decreased by day 12 in RORα-overexpressing BMSCs, whereas their expression was consistently reduced in RORα-knockdown cells (Fig. 5D).

Blocking WNT/β-catenin signaling mitigates the inhibitory effects of RORα overexpression on adipogenic differentiation

To further confirm whether RORα regulates adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs via WNT/β-catenin signaling, the WNT/β-catenin signaling inhibitor XAV-939 was used. Oil Red O staining showed that treatment with XAV-939 significantly mitigated the inhibitory effects of RORα overexpression on adipogenic differentiation (P < 0.0001, Fig. 6A). Additionally, XAV-939 treatment reversed the inhibitory effects of RORα overexpression on adipogenic-related genes (including PPARγ2, LPL, and LEP; all P < 0.05, Fig. 6B).

B RT-PCR analysis of adipogenic-related genes (PPARγ2, LPL, LEP) in OE-RORα BMSCs treated with or without XAV-939. C Alizarin Red staining of OE-RORα BMSCs treated with or without XAV-939. D RT-PCR analysis of osteogenic-related genes (RUNX2, ALP) in OE-RORα BMSCs treated with or without XAV-939. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 compared with the OE-RORα group. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3)

In contrast, XAV-939 treatment did not attenuate but rather enhanced the stimulatory effect of RORα overexpression on osteogenic differentiation (P < 0.0001, Fig. 6C). Similarly, XAV-939 treatment further upregulated the osteogenic-related genes (RUNX2 and ALP) in RORα-overexpressing cells (both P < 0.05, Fig. 6D).

Discussion

This study demonstrates that RORα suppresses adipogenic differentiation in BMSCs by activating the WNT/β-catenin pathway. Both pharmacological and genetic approaches confirmed that RORα inhibition promotes adipogenesis, while its activation impedes the process, correlating with regulation of key markers including PPARγ2, LPL, and LEP. RORα overexpression enhanced β-catenin nuclear translocation and elevated WNT target genes Axin2 and C-myc. Importantly, the WNT inhibitor XAV-939 mitigated the anti-adipogenic effect of RORα. To our knowledge, this is the first study establishing RORα-mediated inhibition of adipogenesis in BMSCs via WNT/β-catenin activation.

Previous studies have revealed that RORα participates in adipogenic differentiation in preadipocyte cell lines 3T3-L1 [12–15]. Austin et al. first found that RORα mRNA was detectable during adipocyte differentiation in preadipocyte D1 and 3T3-L1 cells [14]. Subsequent studies also confirmed the expression of RORα during adipocyte differentiation in 3T3-L1 cells [12, 15]. Consistent with these observations, we found that endogenous RORα expression was detectable in BMSCs during adipogenic induction, and gradually decreased after adipogenic induction. Regarding the effect of RORα expression, Ohoka et al. [15] and Duez et al. [12] both demonstrated that RORα overexpression blocks adipogenesis in 3T3-L1 cells. Ozaki et al. also revealed that RORα suppresses adipogenic differentiation at a later stage of differentiation [13]. These findings suggest a negative regulatory role of RORα in the adipogenic differentiation of preadipocytes. In line with these reports, our data from RORα agonist /inhibitor and RORα overexpression/knockdown experiments also showed that upregulation of RORα inhibited adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs as demonstrated by Oil Red O staining and adipogenic markers expression (PPARγ2, LPL, LEP, FABP4), whereas downregulation of RORα exhibited the opposite trend, suggesting that RORα is also a negative regulator of adipogenic differentiation in BMSCs.

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) is a member of the nuclear hormone receptor family of transcription factors, functioning as a key regulator of adipocyte differentiation [17, 18]. Our RT-PCR results showed that RORα overexpression inhibited PPARγ expression in BMSCs at 3, 6, and 9 days after induction, which is in agreement with reports by Ohoka et al. [15] and Duez et al. [12] in 3T3-L1 cells. These results further confirmed the inhibitory effect of RORα on adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs.

The WNT/β-catenin pathway is an important downstream signaling pathway of RORα [19]. The WNT/β-catenin pathway plays a critical role in promoting osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs [20, 21] and inhibiting adipogenic differentiation [22, 23]. WNT/β-catenin signaling is activated by binding WNT glycoprotein ligands to Frizzled transmembrane receptors [24], after which the β-catenin translocate into the nucleus to prevent the induction of PPARγ and CCAAT/enhancerbinding protein-α (C/EBP-α) [25]. In this study, RORα overexpression significantly enhanced nuclear translocation of β-catenin, indicating activation of WNT/β-catenin signaling. In addition, downstream molecules of WNT/β-catenin signaling were also upregulated in RORα overexpressing BMSCs. Our findings demonstrate that blocking WNT/β-catenin signaling using XAV-939 effectively alleviates the inhibitory effects of RORα overexpression on adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs. In addition, XAV-939 treatment countered the inhibitory effects of RORα overexpression on the expression of adipogenic-related genes. Since WNT/β-catenin signaling is a major pathway of inhibiting adipogenic differentiation, our observations suggests that RORα inhibits adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs via activating WNT/β-catenin signaling.

In this study, RORα overexpression enhanced osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs by day 21, accompanied by upregulation of osteogenic-related genes. Interestingly, XAV-939 did not mitigate RORα’s stimulatory effect on osteogenesis; instead, it further augmented osteogenic differentiation and increased osteogenic-related gene expression in RORα-overexpressing cells. These findings suggest that the influence of RORα on osteogenic differentiation may not solely rely on the WNT/β-catenin pathway and could potentially involve additional regulatory mechanisms. The paradoxical effect of XAV-939 (WNT inhibition coupled with enhanced osteogenesis) can be explained by its context-dependent actions. The effects of XAV-939 on MSC osteogenic differentiation appear to be cell-type dependent. For instance, while it inhibits osteogenesis in iPSC-MSCs, it enhances differentiation in BMSCs, as observed in our study. This discrepancy is achieved through modulation of signaling pathways via different mediators. As evidenced by Almasoud et al. [26], a key mediator is the adaptor protein SH3 domain-binding protein 2 (SH3BP2). As a Tankyrase inhibitor, XAV-939 prevents the degradation of SH3BP2, leading to its accumulation. This accumulation potently enhances osteogenesis, independent of the WNT pathway, by activating other signaling molecules such as the ABL-TAZ complex. Therefore, in the context of BMSCs, the strong pro-osteogenic effect mediated by the SH3BP2 pathway can outweigh the inhibitory effect of XAV-939 on the WNT pathway, resulting in a net promotion of bone formation. Notably, XAV-939 has been reported to exert divergent effects on osteogenesis depending on the cellular context, enhancing osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs while inhibiting it in iPSC-MSCs through distinct modulators of WNT/β-catenin signaling [27, 28]. This complexity underscores that multiple pathways likely interact to govern MSC osteogenic fate. While our study implicates RORα as a contributor to this process, further work is required to elucidate whether RORα also regulates osteogenesis via alternative mechanisms, such as direct modulation of RUNX2 transcription, BMP signaling, or crosstalk with other osteogenic pathways.

In addition to the findings on the role of RORα in adipogenic differentiation, this study uncovered intriguing insights into its involvement in osteoporosis. Specifically, a significant decrease in RORα expression levels was observed in an osteoporotic mouse model compared to the control group. Furthermore, treatment with the RORα agonist (SR1078) induced an upregulation of RORα expression in osteoporotic mice, suggesting a potential therapeutic avenue for mitigating osteoporosis.

Several limitations of this study should be pointed out. First, although we observed that RORα overexpression simultaneously inhibited adipogenic differentiation and activated WNT/β-catenin signaling in BMSCs, we did not provide direct evidence supporting that activation of WNT/β-catenin signaling is directly responsible for the inhibitory effect of RORα on adipogenic differentiation. In addition, the in vitro findings of this study should be validated in an in vivo animal model. Furthermore, an important limitation of this study is that the in vitro findings are based on BMSCs derived from a single mouse donor. While this approach ensures internal consistency for mechanistic investigations, it may limit the generalizability of our results across different genetic backgrounds. Future studies utilizing BMSCs from multiple independent donors are essential to confirm the reproducibility and broader applicability of our observations regarding the role of RORα in adipogenic differentiation.

Significance

In summary, our study demonstrates that RORα is a negative regulator of adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs. RORα inhibits adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs, likely through activation of WNT/β-catenin signaling.

A hybrid actor–critic and BERT framework for intelligent course recommendation in IoT-aware e-learning systems
Authors: Xia Chunqin, Wu Peixi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The necessity for recommendation models that can capture both semantic information and device-mediated learner interactions has increased due to the rapid growth of IoT-aware e-learning environments. IoT-enhanced learning in this context refers to intelligent learning platforms that continuously create and log heterogeneous interaction data, including session dynamics, access patterns across linked devices, and engagement behaviors. This work introduces a coherent hybrid framework that combines an Actor–Critic reinforcement learning agent optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with BERT-based semantic encoding. By combining textual content with context-aware interaction logs gathered from intelligent learning platforms, the method creates richer learner representations. While a Mahalanobis distance module offers correlation-aware similarity cues to enhance resilience under sparse and high-dimensional data, these representations allow the Actor–Critic agent to constantly improve its recommendation policy. The usefulness of the suggested framework for IoT-aware intelligent e-learning systems is demonstrated by experiments conducted on three public MOOC datasets, which show steady improvements over robust baselines.

Introduction

Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) have emerged as a transformative force in contemporary education by democratizing access to high-quality learning resources for learners across geographic, cultural, and socioeconomic boundaries1,2. Platforms such as Coursera, edX3, and Udemy enable millions of individuals to participate in flexible and self-directed learning experiences. However, the rapid growth of available courses has led to substantial information overload, making it increasingly difficult for learners to identify educational content that aligns with their goals, competencies, and interests4.

Alongside this expansion, modern e-learning platforms have evolved into IoT-aware digital ecosystems in which learning activities are mediated through interconnected devices and online infrastructures. These environments continuously generate heterogeneous interaction signals, including session duration, access frequency, temporal engagement patterns, and device-mediated usage logs5,6. Although such data do not necessarily originate from low-level physical sensors, they provide context-aware and behavior-rich information that is widely regarded as a practical proxy for IoT-enhanced educational environments. At the same time, the scale and diversity of these signals introduce significant challenges related to data heterogeneity, high dimensionality, sparsity, and privacy preservation7.

Traditional recommendation techniques, such as Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Content-Based Filtering (CBF), have long served as foundational approaches for personalization in educational systems8,9. Despite their effectiveness in static settings, these methods are limited in dynamic learning environments due to their reliance on fixed preference assumptions and explicit feedback signals, such as ratings or clicks. Consequently, they often suffer from data sparsity, cold-start issues, and limited adaptability to evolving learner behavior over time10. These limitations become more pronounced in IoT-aware learning platforms, where diverse and continuously generated interaction signals require adaptive and sequential modeling strategies11.

To address these challenges, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has gained increasing attention as a powerful framework for sequential decision-making in personalized recommendation systems12,13. By modeling the recommendation process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), DRL-based approaches enable continuous interaction with learners and optimization of long-term engagement objectives. Within this paradigm, the Actor–Critic architecture has demonstrated strong performance by jointly learning policy and value functions, thereby achieving a balance between exploration and exploitation and improving training stability and sample efficiency14,15.

In parallel, advances in Transformer-based Natural Language Processing (NLP) have significantly enhanced semantic understanding of textual data. In particular, the Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) model has shown remarkable capability in extracting contextualized semantic representations from unstructured text through bidirectional attention mechanisms16. In educational recommendation scenarios, such semantic representations are especially valuable for modeling course descriptions, learning objectives, and textual feedback17. While Transformer-based models have been successfully applied to general recommendation tasks, their integration with reinforcement learning for adaptive course recommendation especially within IoT-aware MOOC environments remains relatively underexplored18.

Despite their promise, IoT-aware e-learning environments raise additional concerns related to privacy, interpretability, and fairness. Continuous logging of learner interactions across connected platforms may expose sensitive behavioral patterns, necessitating compliance with data protection regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)19. Moreover, ensuring transparency through Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) mechanisms is essential for fostering trust and acceptance of intelligent recommendation systems in educational contexts20.

In response to these challenges, this study presents a hybrid Personalized Course Recommendation (PCR) framework grounded in Deep Reinforcement Learning. The proposed framework integrates BERT-based semantic representations with IoT-aware, device-mediated learner interaction features and an Actor–Critic architecture optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Unlike prior approaches that emphasize either semantic modeling or behavioral personalization in isolation, the framework unifies content understanding, contextual awareness, and adaptive policy learning within a single scalable architecture.

Related works

Intelligent recommendation in e-learning has gradually evolved from static, rule-based approaches toward adaptive and data-driven frameworks that leverage advances in deep learning, reinforcement learning, and graph-based modeling. Early course recommendation systems primarily relied on Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Content-Based Filtering (CBF) techniques, which infer learner preferences from similarities in historical interactions and content metadata20,21. Although widely adopted, these methods face persistent challenges, including data sparsity, cold-start problems, and limited scalability in scenarios with sparse user–item interactions22. Moreover, their static design restricts their ability to adapt to the dynamic and continuously evolving learning preferences typical of large-scale MOOC platforms and IoT-aware digital learning environments23.

To mitigate these limitations, hybrid and deep neural architectures have been proposed to jointly model semantic and behavioral information. Models such as DeepFM and Neural Collaborative Filtering capture nonlinear relationships between learner attributes and educational resources, leading to improved recommendation accuracy24. However, these approaches are predominantly trained in an offline manner and lack mechanisms for sequential policy adaptation based on real-time learner feedback, which constrains their effectiveness in dynamic learning settings25.

More recently, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for adaptive and sequential recommendation. DRL-based recommender systems formulate the recommendation task as a sequential decision-making problem, enabling long-term optimization of learner engagement through repeated interactions26,27. Within this paradigm, Actor–Critic architectures have demonstrated strong performance by jointly learning policy and value functions, thereby improving training stability, convergence behavior, and generalization compared to value-based reinforcement learning methods.

Graph-based and heterogeneous network models have further enhanced recommendation performance by explicitly modeling complex relationships among learners, courses, and auxiliary entities. A representative example is the heterogeneous graph-enhanced recommendation framework proposed in28, which integrates graph attention mechanisms with deep reinforcement learning to capture latent relational dependencies among courses, concepts, and instructors. In parallel, Transformer-based models such as BERT4Rec have significantly advanced semantic modeling in recommendation systems by leveraging bidirectional attention to encode contextual dependencies within user–item interaction sequences29. Extending this direction, a knowledge-aware sequence modeling approach introduced in30 combines course knowledge graphs with deep sequence encoders, including BiLSTM, CNN, and MLP components, to jointly represent structural and temporal dependencies, achieving notable improvements in NDCG and Recall on benchmark MOOC datasets.

Session-based and heterogeneous graph neural networks have also gained prominence for modeling multi-relational learner–course interactions. The SR-HetGNN framework presented in31 constructs heterogeneous graphs composed of learners, sessions, and educational items, effectively capturing both short-term and long-term user preferences from interaction logs. Similarly, a clustering-driven deep learning approach proposed in32 integrates BiLSTM and MLP architectures to group learners and courses based on behavioral similarity, thereby alleviating sparsity and cold-start issues while maintaining scalability across large educational datasets.

Hybrid architectures combining deep learning and graph-based reasoning have been explored to further enhance recommendation robustness. In33, a CNN–graph hybrid model employs convolutional neural networks to extract student performance features and graph-based link prediction to uncover latent behavioral relationships. This integration improves recommendation accuracy and mitigates cold-start effects, outperforming several baseline approaches in terms of precision and F1-score.

Beyond predictive accuracy, recent studies have increasingly emphasized the importance of privacy preservation and interpretability in intelligent e-learning recommendation systems, particularly within IoT-aware learning environments where learner interactions are continuously logged across connected platforms. Privacy-preserving paradigms such as Federated Learning and Differential Privacy have been adopted to protect distributed learner data during model training34. In addition, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) frameworks have been investigated to enhance transparency, accountability, and user trust in recommendation outcomes35.

This comparison reveals several open research gaps. First, most existing approaches address personalization from a single perspective semantic, behavioral, or structural without offering an integrated adaptive framework that jointly models all dimensions. Second, although Transformer-based models and reinforcement learning have shown strong performance independently, their combined application for adaptive course recommendation in IoT-aware MOOC environments remains limited. Third, correlation-aware similarity modeling and the integration of privacy and interpretability considerations directly within reinforcement learning–based recommendation pipelines are still underexplored.

Motivated by these gaps, this study proposes a hybrid BERT–Actor–Critic framework augmented with a Mahalanobis distance–based similarity mechanism. By unifying semantic representation, sequential decision-making, and correlation-aware interaction modeling within a scalable and privacy-conscious architecture, the proposed approach directly addresses the limitations identified in prior work and establishes the foundation for the system model described in the subsequent section.

MOOCCube dataset

The MOOCCube dataset, obtained from XuetangX, includes 706 courses, 55,203 users, and 1,200,000 interactions. The dataset comprises course metadata, including titles, descriptions, and tags, optimized for BERT-based semantic feature extraction, with user interactions such as ratings (1–5), clicks, and video views. These variables facilitate behavioral modeling and clustering by Mahalanobis Distance, hence improving recommendation relevance for various learner profiles.

edX dataset

The edX dataset, sourced from MITx and HarvardX courses, comprises 450 courses and 38,500 users, totaling 850,000 interactions. It offers demographic data (e.g., age, gender, educational qualifications) and engagement metrics (e.g., ratings, difficulty levels: beginner, intermediate, advanced). These features provide thorough user profiling and enhance scalability and customisation, rendering the dataset essential to the recommendation framework.

NTHU MOOCs dataset

The NTHU MOOCs dataset, supplied by National Tsing Hua University, comprises 320 courses, 12,800 users, and 420,000 interactions. It encompasses behavioral attributes including interaction durations (0–20 h, mean: 3.2, standard deviation: 1.5), course classifications (e.g., Computer Science, Humanities), and user actions. This dataset augments MOOCCube and edX by improving the system’s capacity to simulate changing user preferences in IoT-enabled contexts.

Dataset integration and rationale

The amalgamation of the three datasets MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs guarantees that the proposed approach generalizes proficiently across diverse e-learning ecosystems. MOOCCube offers extensive behavioral and textual diversity, edX delivers organized and demographically enhanced interactions, and NTHU MOOCs furnish contextual behavioral indicators that signify student involvement intensity. The integration of these datasets enhances cross-domain resilience and reduces dataset-specific biases, allowing the reinforcement learning model to adjust to diverse educational contexts and learner demographics.

Preprocessing

The preprocessing phase is an essential initial step in the proposed DRL-based recommender system, aimed at improving the quality and consistency of input data from the MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs datasets. This phase converts unrefined input into a structured format appropriate for further feature extraction and model training, tackling challenges such as absent values, scale inconsistencies, and textual noise. The pretreatment pipeline consists of three fundamental sub-processes: data cleaning, normalization, and text preparation, each utilizing stringent mathematical formulations to guarantee robustness and compatibility with the BERT and Mahalanobis Distance modules.

Data synchronization and feature alignment

Due to the datasets originating from several platforms, temporal and structural discrepancies may occur during integration. Timestamps are transformed to a uniform UTC format, and course identities are normalized using hash-based mapping. Feature alignment is accomplished through the application of schema unification techniques that provide uniform naming conventions and data types across all datasets. This preprocessing step ensures that input features maintain semantic consistency for both the BERT encoder and the reinforcement learning components.

IoT-aware interaction feature

In the proposed framework, IoT-aware interaction features are derived from device-mediated and platform-level interaction logs collected from smart learning platforms. The public datasets employed in this study MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs primarily contain standard web and mobile application logs that capture learners’ interactions within digital learning environments. These logs include session duration, access frequency, temporal engagement patterns, click behavior, and other context-aware usage indicators.

Although the utilized datasets are based on platform-level interaction traces, such data are widely regarded as practical and realistic representations of IoT-aware e-learning environments. In large-scale online education systems, learning activities are mediated through interconnected devices and digital infrastructures, where interaction logs serve as the primary source of behavioral and contextual information.

Accordingly, all IoT-aware features in this work are explicitly defined as device-mediated learner interaction signals generated through smart educational platforms. These features are normalized using Min–Max scaling and aggregated into a compact learner-context representation. The resulting representation is subsequently integrated with BERT-based semantic embeddings to form the final state vector used by the reinforcement learning agent. This formulation ensures conceptual consistency between the adopted IoT-aware definition, the employed data sources, and the proposed learning framework.

State representation

The State Representation module, illustrated in Fig. 1, produces a 778-dimensional vector for the Actor-Critic model. This vector integrates user features (10-dimensional) that include interaction histories and scores from MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs, course embeddings (768-dimensional) serving as semantic representations from BERT, and clustering features obtained through Mahalanobis Distance clustering. Collectively, these components encapsulate user preferences and course interrelations, thereby enabling precise suggestion generation.

Action space definition

The action space of the proposed framework comprises a discrete collection of available courses inside the dataset. Each action pertains to the suggestion of an individual course, whereas top-N recommendation lists are produced by choosing the actions with the highest probabilities from the output distribution of the Actor Network. This architecture streamlines policy learning and guarantees the interpretability of recommendations, while the implementation of probability-based ranking provides adaptability in customizing the quantity of suggestions according to user interaction frequency or session environment.

Correlation-aware similarity modeling using mahalanobis distance

To model similarity relationships among learners and courses in a high-dimensional latent feature space, a Mahalanobis distance–based similarity module is employed. This module, illustrated in Fig. 1, plays a dual role by providing correlation-aware similarity scores and by supporting clustering-based feature augmentation for the proposed DRL-based recommendation framework.

Unlike conventional similarity measures such as Euclidean distance or cosine similarity, Mahalanobis distance explicitly incorporates inter-feature correlations through the inverse covariance matrix. This property is particularly important in the proposed framework, where the state representation combines contextualized semantic embeddings derived from BERT with device-mediated learner interaction features. These heterogeneous components exhibit non-uniform variance and cross-dimensional correlations due to semantic dependencies in textual representations and co-occurring behavioral patterns in interaction logs.

Rather than assuming any strict distributional form, Mahalanobis distance is adopted as a covariance-aware metric that adaptively scales feature dimensions and mitigates anisotropy and redundancy in the latent representation space.

The resulting distance scores serve two complementary purposes. First, they are incorporated as similarity cues within the Actor Network to refine course recommendation probabilities by accounting for correlated feature structures. Training with PPO

The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) module, illustrated in Fig. 2, trains the Actor-Critic model via a systematic and iterative approach that improves the stability and efficiency of the reinforcement learning framework, thereby optimizing the performance of course recommendations within the proposed system. This approach has three essential steps: advantage computation, policy update, and value update, each facilitating the model’s effective adaptability to user interactions.

The advantage computation measures the comparative benefit of an activity against the average, expressed as:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{A}_{t}={R}_{t}+\gamma\:V\left({s}_{t+1}\right)-V\left({s}_{t}\right),$$\end{document}

The policy update utilizes a truncated objective function to avert excessive policy modifications, articulated as:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{L}^{\mathrm{CLIP}}\left(\theta\:\right)=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{m}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{n}({r}_{t}\left(\theta\:\right){A}_{t},\mathrm{clip}\left({r}_{t}\right(\theta\:),1-ϵ,1+ϵ){A}_{t}\left)\right],$$\end{document}

where r_t(θ) is the probability ratio of the new policy relative to the old policy, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{A}_{t}$$\end{document} represents the advantage, and ε = 0.2 is the clipping value that limits the update size, so providing a conservative yet effective policy refinement that reduces the chance of performance deterioration. The value update reduces the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for the Critic, aligning the projected state value V(s) with the actual returns, hence improving the accuracy of the value function across future rounds. PPO’s design facilitates stable and efficient learning by harmonizing exploration and exploitation, hence enhancing recommendation performance through superior convergence rates and flexibility to the evolving preferences of users in the MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs datasets.

The convergence of the Actor–Critic model is assessed during training by essential performance indicators, including cumulative reward, average policy loss, and critic value inaccuracy. The learning process is deemed stable when these measurements attain steady-state oscillations throughout successive epochs.

To assess the recommender’s efficacy, standard metrics Precision@N, Recall@N, F1-Score, and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) are calculated across all datasets. These metrics offer a quantitative assessment of recommendation precision and ranking efficacy. Furthermore, training stability is evaluated via variance analysis of episodic returns, validating constant policy enhancement across iterations.

Given that the system handles sensitive behavioral and IoT-derived learner data, safeguarding privacy is an essential design need.

Moreover, transparency is improved by explainable decision visualization, wherein attention weights and incentive contributions are comprehensible to both instructors and learners, so ensuring ethical adherence and cultivating confidence.

Experimental results and analysis

Experimental setup

This section delineates the experimental setup utilized to assess the proposed Deep Reinforcement Learning-based Personalized Course Recommendation architecture. All studies were performed on a workstation using an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB), an Intel Core i9 processor, and 64 GB of RAM. The models were executed utilizing PyTorch 2.1 and the HuggingFace Transformers library.

The learning rate was established at 1e − 4, the batch size at 128, and the discount factor γ at 0.99. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) was utilized with ε = 0.2 for clipping and a learning rate scheduler featuring cosine annealing. Each model underwent training for 100 epochs, with early stopping implemented when the validation loss stabilized for 10 successive epochs.

The three datasets (MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs) were randomly partitioned into training, validation, and test groups at a 70:15:15 ratio. Stratified sampling was utilized to maintain the distribution of essential characteristics, including course complexity and user interaction frequency. The validation subset was utilized for hyperparameter optimization and early termination, whereas the test subset was exclusively allocated for final assessment. This arrangement guaranteed equitable performance evaluation among all baseline models.

The BERT module utilized the bert-base-uncased pre-trained model from HuggingFace Transformers. In the fine-tuning process, only the final four transformer layers were unfrozen, while the preceding layers remained fixed to mitigate overfitting due to the limited textual data available for each course. The model had a hidden size of 768, a maximum sequence length of 128 tokens, a batch size of 32, and a learning rate of 2 × 10−5 utilizing a cosine annealing schedule.

The AdamW optimizer was employed with a weight decay of 0.01 and a dropout rate of 0.1. The [CLS] token embedding from the final transformer layer was utilized as the semantic representation for each course description, with tokenization conducted using WordPiece, incorporating lowercasing and truncation beyond 128 tokens.

Quantitative results

Evaluation metrics

To thoroughly evaluate the efficacy of the proposed BERT–Actor–Critic–PPO framework, four conventional assessment metrics were utilized: Precision@10, Recall@10, F1-score, and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR).

Precision@10 quantifies the ratio of pertinent courses within the top ten suggestions for each learner, serving as a metric for recommendation accuracy. Recall@10 measures the proportion of pertinent courses accurately retrieved within the top ten recommendations, hence indicating the system’s capacity to address a wider array of learner preferences.

The F1-score denotes the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced statistic that mitigates the influence of extreme values in either measure and acts as a more steady indicator of model dependability.

The Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) assesses ranking efficacy by calculating the average of the inverse positions of the first accurately recommended item for all learners. An elevated MRR indicates that pertinent courses are situated nearer to the apex of the suggestion list, enhancing user pleasure and engagement.

Collectively, these four metrics offer a thorough evaluation framework for assessing both the ranking quality and retrieval efficacy of the proposed reinforcement learning model, guaranteeing that enhancements encompass not only accuracy but also recommendation relevance, diversity, and robustness.

Discussion

Table 4 unequivocally demonstrates that each component significantly enhances the model’s overall performance.

The most significant decline transpires upon the removal of BERT embeddings, leading to a decrease of roughly 3.7% in Precision@10 and 4.3% in Recall@10, so affirming the critical importance of semantic feature extraction in elucidating contextual relation between learners and courses. In the absence of BERT, the model fails to represent content effectively, diminishing its capacity to generalize across diverse themes.

The lack of the Actor–Critic architecture results in a notable performance decrease of approximately 3%, underscoring its importance in the development of dynamic interaction strategies via ongoing feedback. Upon substitution with a more rudimentary DQN-based framework, the system demonstrates diminished convergence speed and reduced stability in reward trajectories.

The removal of PPO optimization results in a moderate decline (about 2.5%) in both recall and ranking precision, indicating that steady and clipped policy updates are essential for averting reward oscillation and overfitting during training.

The elimination of reward normalization results in a minor performance decline (about 1.5%), indicating the role of Mahalanobis-based scaling in stabilizing the reward gradient and facilitating equitable learning among users with varying engagement levels.

These findings confirm that the integration of BERT for semantic encoding, Actor–Critic for adaptive policy learning, PPO for stability, and reward normalization for equilibrium control produces the most precise, stable, and generalizable course recommendation framework. The collaboration among these elements allows the proposed approach to preserve interpretability while attaining consistent performance improvements across all evaluation measures.

Discussion and implications

The results from the previous experiments collectively illustrate the efficacy and resilience of the proposed BERT–Actor–Critic–PPO framework for tailored course recommendations in online learning settings.

The amalgamation of semantic representation learning via BERT with reinforcement learning techniques through Actor–Critic and PPO optimization has yielded consistent enhancements in recommendation accuracy and stability across many datasets.

The findings demonstrate that the integration of deep contextual embeddings with adaptive decision rules can proficiently tackle persistent obstacles, including data sparsity, cold-start problems, and behavioral ambiguity in learner-course interactions.

The theoretical superiority of the proposed model substantiates the significance of integrating content-aware and behavior-aware features within a cohesive architecture.

The use of semantic embeddings allows the model to discern nuanced links between course material and learner intent, while the reinforcement-based policy framework guarantees ongoing optimization informed by feedback signals.

This synergy connects static recommendation systems with dynamic, feedback-oriented decision-making frameworks, providing a more accurate representation of learner interactions with educational content across time.

The ramifications of these findings are especially pertinent for extensive MOOC platforms and e-learning environments.

The suggested model’s capacity to provide precise recommendations with minimal computing expense suggests its practical integration into real-world systems where scalability and response time are essential.

Furthermore, its stability and generalization ability across diverse datasets (MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs) indicate adaptability to different instructional environments, languages, and learner demographics.

This adaptability is essential for sustaining engagement and customisation in international online education environments.

The results emphasize the significance of adaptive policy optimization in educational recommender systems.

In contrast to conventional deterministic algorithms that depend exclusively on static feature representations, the PPO-driven framework enables the model to adapt dynamically in response to changes in learner behavior, thereby mirroring real-time interests and learning trajectories.

This adaptability corresponds with contemporary trends in AI-driven personalized learning, where responsiveness and contextual comprehension are crucial for improving learner satisfaction and retention.

The model’s effective convergence and computing scalability indicate that analogous reinforcement-based and semantics-enhanced frameworks may be applied to other areas, including skill evaluation, career trajectory forecasting, and adaptive curriculum development.

Future research may investigate cross-domain transferability, multi-modal data integration (e.g., amalgamating text, video, and engagement logs), and elucidative recommendation mechanisms to enhance transparency and confidence in educational AI systems.

Conclusion and future work

This study introduced a cohesive hybrid framework, BERT–Actor–Critic–PPO, for intelligent and adaptable course recommendations in online educational settings.

The model effectively integrates semantic representation learning using BERT with reinforcement learning-based policy optimization, capturing both contextual and behavioral aspects of learner-course interactions.

Experimental assessments across three extensive benchmark datasets MOOCCube, edX, and NTHU MOOCs revealed that the proposed framework consistently surpasses baseline models including SR-HetGNN, HGCR, CNN–Graph Hybrid, and Clustering-Driven Deep Learning, attaining significant improvements in Precision@10, Recall@10, F1-score, and MRR.

The findings validate that the amalgamation of semantic embeddings, adaptive policy optimization, and reward normalization yields a harmonious architecture that attains elevated accuracy while preserving computing economy.

The ablation investigation confirmed the significance of each component, while the convergence analysis demonstrated more rapid and consistent learning performance compared to leading approaches.

Notwithstanding a marginal rise in training duration, the suggested framework continues to be computationally feasible and scalable, rendering it appropriate for practical MOOC platforms and extensive e-learning ecosystems.

The study highlights the importance of adaptive and feedback-driven customisation in contemporary educational recommender systems, in addition to its empirical success.

The suggested model’s capacity to dynamically modify recommendations in response to changing learner behavior signifies a transition from static recommendation frameworks to continuous, policy-driven adaptability.

This adaptability significantly influences the improvement of learner engagement, motivation, and long-term retention in digital learning contexts.

Several research avenues can further advance this study. Integrating multi-modal learning signals such as video engagement, forum conversations, and assessment results could enhance learner modeling and augment contextual comprehension.

Future research may investigate cross-domain and cross-lingual generalization, allowing the model to adapt across various educational platforms and content kinds.

Furthermore, incorporating explainable AI (XAI) methods into the reinforcement learning process will improve the transparency and interpretability of suggestions, hence cultivating increased user trust.

Emerging paradigms, such as multi-agent reinforcement learning and graph neural policy networks, possess the potential to enhance collective intelligence and collaboration within extensive learner communities.

The suggested BERT–Actor–Critic–PPO model provides a robust framework for enhancing individualized education by combining deep semantic comprehension with adaptive decision-making.

Its robust generalization, speed, and adaptive learning characteristics establish it as a potential method for developing the next generation of intelligent, scalable, and context-aware recommendation systems in online education.

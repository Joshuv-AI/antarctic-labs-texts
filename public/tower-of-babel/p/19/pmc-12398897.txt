Role of ebv-circRPMS1–p53 interaction in the proliferation and clinical progression of Epstein–Barr virus-associated gastric carcinoma
Authors: Ling Yang, Meini Li, Guiming Xie, Xian Wu, Mingxiong Qin, Birong Guo, Jingyue Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Demonstration of direct binding between EBV-encoded circRNA (ebv-circRPMS1) and p53 protein in gastric cancer.•Mechanistic insight: ebv-circRPMS1 stabilizes wild-type p53, converting it into a pro-tumorigenic factor.•Clinical correlation: High ebv-circRPMS1–p53 co-expression predicts poor survival in EBVaGC patients (P < 0.05).•Translational potential: Identifies the ebv-circRPMS1–p53 axis as a novel therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker.•Multidimensional validation: Combines RIP/FISH, xenograft models, and a 70-patient cohort.

Background

Epstein–Barr virus-associated gastric carcinoma (EBVaGC) is a distinct molecular subtype of gastric carcinoma characterized by clonal EBV infection and unique oncogenic mechanisms driven by viral non-coding RNAs (Low et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2020). Among these RNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs) derived from latent EBV genes have emerged as critical regulators of tumor progression. For instance, the EBV circRNA originating from the latent membrane protein 2A (LMP2A) gene, ebv-circLMP2A, promotes cancer stemness by sponging miR-3908 and activating the TRIM59–p53 axis, thereby suppressing wild-type p53 function and enhancing chemoresistance (Gong et al., 2020). On the other hand, ebv-circLMP2A promotes angiogenesis in EBVaGC by stabilizing HIF1α through KHSRP-mediated degradation of VHL mRNA, forming a hypoxia-driven HIF1α–ebv-circLMP2A positive feedback loop that correlates with poor prognosis (Du et al., 2022). Another EBV-derived circRNA, ebv-circRPMS1, generated from the BamHI-A rightward transcript (BART) region, has recently been implicated in maintaining viral latency and modulating the host epigenetic landscape (Ungerleider et al., 2019). Xia et al. (Liu et al., 2019) found that knockdown of ebv-circRPMS1 suppresses nasopharyngeal carcinoma cell proliferation and metastasis by sponging multiple microRNAs. Specifically, ebv-circRPMS1 was shown to interact with Sam68, a splicing factor, to activate METTL3-mediated RNA methylation (m6A), thereby stabilizing oncogenic transcripts and promoting EBVaGC progression (J.Y. Zhang et al., 2022).

The tumor suppressor p53 is a central guardian of genomic integrity, yet its role in EBVaGC is paradoxical. Unlike EBV-negative gastric carcinomas (EBVnGC-), which frequently harbor TP53 mutations, EBVaGC predominantly retains wild-type p53 but exhibits impaired p53 signaling due to viral interference (Angerilli et al., 2024; Napoli and Flores, 2020). EBV employs multiple strategies to disrupt p53 activity. For example, EBNA3C recruits histone deacetylases to repress p53 transcriptional activity, while LMP1 enhances p53 ubiquitination via MDM2 activation (Wang et al., 2019; Selby et al., 2019). Notably, recent studies have revealed that non-viral circRNAs can directly interact with p53 or its mutants to regulate tumorigenesis. In breast cancer, host-derived circDNMT1 stabilizes mutant p53 to promote metastasis (Du et al., 2018), whereas circCCNB1 counteracts mutant p53 aggregation in breast cancer (Fang et al., 2018; Fang et al., 2019). However, the interplay between ebv-circRNAs and p53 remains enigmatic. While ebv-circLMP2A indirectly suppresses p53 by upregulating TRIM59, whether circRPMS1 stabilizes or destabilizes p53 remains unclear. However, the direct interaction between ebv-circRPMS1 and key tumor suppressors, including p53, remains unexplored, leaving a critical gap in understanding how viral circRNAs hijack host pathways to drive malignancy.

This study addressed these gaps by investigating the hypothesis that ebv-circRPMS1 directly binds to p53, modulating its stability and subverting its tumor-suppressive functions to drive EBVaGC progression. Our findings not only elucidate a novel viral mechanism of p53 hijacking but also highlight the clinical relevance of the ebv-circRPMS1–p53 axis as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in EBVaGC.

Methods

Study design

This study employed a translational framework integrating in vitro, in vivo, and clinical analyses to investigate ebv-circRPMS1–p53 interactions in EBVaGC pathogenesis. Mechanistic hypotheses were tested via RNA interference, RNA-protein binding assays, and functional phenotyping in EBV+/− cell lines. Xenograft models validated tumorigenicity, while a retrospective cohort (2010–2020) assessed clinical relevance. The multidimensional design was chosen to dissect molecular mechanisms while linking findings to patient outcomes, ensuring robust validation of viral-host interplay.

Cell lines and culture conditions

EBV-positive (EBV+) gastric carcinoma cell lines (SNU719) and EBV-negative cells (AGS) were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and the Korean Cell Line Bank. AGS-EBV cells were generated by infecting parental AGS cells with the B95–8 EBV strain via spinoculation (1200 × g, 2 h, 37 °C), followed by puromycin selection (2 μg/mL, 14 days). All the cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; HyClone™) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco) at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5 % CO2. For lentiviral packaging, 293T cells (ATCC CRL-3216) were maintained in ​​Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium​ (DMEM, Gibco) supplemented with 10 % FBS.

RNA preparation, cDNA synthesis, and reverse transcription quantitative PCR

Total RNA was isolated using the AG RNAex Pro Reagent protocol (AG, Changsha, China). Complementary DNA synthesis was performed with the Evo M-MLV RT Kit (AG), which includes genomic DNA elimination, following standardized protocols for subsequent RT-qPCR analysis. Quantitative PCR amplification was conducted on the LightCycler 480® System (Roche) using SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS reagents (AG) under manufacturer-specified parameters. To ensure methodological rigor, experiments included triplicate biological replicates, with triplicate technical analyses per sample. Data normalization was performed using the comparative threshold cycle method (2−ΔΔCt), with GAPDH transcription levels as endogenous controls. Primer pair sequences are archived in Supplementary Table S1.

Western blotting

Post-treatment cellular specimens were rinsed twice with 4 °C PBS before protein isolation using RIPA lysis and extraction buffer (KeyGen Biotech). Protein lysates (30 µg/sample) were electrophoresed on 8–12 % SDS-PAGE gradient gels and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (Millipore ®, Bedford,MA). Immunoblotting involved sequential antibody incubations (see Supplementary Table S2 for the dilution parameters), followed by enhanced chemiluminescence (Millipore) for band visualization.

RNA interference and transfection

The following three siRNAs targeting the back-splice junction (BSJ) of ebv-circRPMS1 were designed and synthesized by GenePharma:•si-ebv-circRPMS1#1: 5′-GGAUUCGAGCUACCGAAUA-3′;•si-ebv-circRPMS1#2: 5′-CCAGGUACUUGGAAUCUGA-3′; and•si-ebv-circRPMS1#3: 5′-UACCGAAUAGGUACCGGAU-3′.

A scrambled siRNA (5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGU-3′) served as the negative control. Cells were transfected with 50 nM siRNA using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen™) for 48h. The knockdown efficiency was validated using RT-qPCR.

The most effective siRNA was cloned into the pLKO.1 vector to generate shRNA. Stable ebv-circRPMS1 knockdown cells were established by transfecting this shRNA plasmid followed by selection with 2 μg/mL puromycin for 14 days. Successful knockdown was confirmed by RT-qPCR prior to functional assays.

Cell proliferation assay

A 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU)-based proliferation assay was performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Briefly, cells (1 × 10^5 cells/well) were seeded into 12-well plates and cultured for 24 h. Following treatment, EdU incorporation was visualized via fluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and proliferative activity was quantified by calculating the ratio of EdU-positive cells (red fluorescence) to total nuclei stained with DAPI (blue fluorescence).

RNA immunoprecipitation

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were performed using the Magna RIP® Kit (Millipore) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cell lysates were incubated with anti-p53 antibody (DO-1, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-126) or IgG control (Millipore), both conjugated to magnetic beads. Coprecipitated RNA was extracted using TRIzol™ Reagent (Invitrogen), and ebv-circRPMS1 enrichment was quantified via RT-qPCR. Linear RPMS1 RNA served as a negative control for circRNA-specific interactions. Primers flanking the circular junction (divergent) and linear region (convergent) distinguished ebv-circRPMS1 from linear RPMS1 transcripts.

The ebv-circRPMS1-MS2 and MS2-CP plasmids, constructed by GENESEED (Guangzhou, China), were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. 48 h post-transfection, RNA–protein complexes were immunoprecipitated by incubating cell lysates with an anti-Flag/MS2 antibody. ebv-circRPMS1 levels in the complexes were quantified via qRT–PCR, while associated proteins were detected by Western blot.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization and immunofluorescence staining

Cy3-labeled probes targeting the BSJ of ebv-circRPMS1 (Exon 2–4 junction; Servicebio®) were hybridized to fixed cells using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), followed by immunofluorescence staining with anti-p53 antibody and Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody (Invitrogen). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM 880 confocal microscope.

Animal experiments

To validate the ebv-circRPMS1 role in vivo, xenograft models were established by subcutaneously injecting 5 × 10^6 EBV+ (AGS-EBV) or EBV− (AGS) cells into female NOD/SCID mice (6 weeks old, n = 8/group). The tumor volumes were measured weekly using calipers and calculated as V = 0.5 × length × width2. The mice were sacrificed at 28 days, and the tumors were harvested for immunohistochemistry (IHC) and BaseScope analysis. This animal study was approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of the Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University. All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee and were conducted in strict accordance with the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines.

Clinical specimens

The clinical significance of ebv-circRPMS1–p53 co-expression in EBVaGC prognosis was analyzed in 70 surgical resection specimens of EBVaGC collected from Guigang City People’s Hospital between 2010 and 2020. The specimens underwent formalin fixation, paraffin embedding, and subsequent sectioning for hematoxylin-eosin staining. They were further analyzed to assess the expression levels of p53. The expression of ebv-circRPMS1 was detected using BaseScope 2.5 HD assay (ACD Bio) with a custom-designed probe. Clinical and pathological characteristics of the patients were recorded, including age, gender, tumor dimensions, tumor localization, differentiation grade, TNM staging, and other relevant factors. All the patients provided informed consent, acknowledging their participation in the study. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guigang City People’s Hospital.

Immunohistochemistry and in situ hybridization

Paraffin-embedded tissue specimens were sectioned at 4 μm thickness for immunohistochemical analysis. The Novolink Polymer Detection System (Leica Biosystems, UK) was used following the manufacturer’s standard protocols. Evaluation of p53 immunostaining was conducted through dual-pathologist consensus assessment, considering both the staining intensity and the proportion of positive cells. The intensity grading followed established protocols (J.Y. Zhang et al., 2022), with the following four-tier criteria: 0 (no staining), 1 (yellow), 2 (light brown), and 3 (dark brown). Positive cell quantification similarly stratified into four categories: 0 (<10 %), 1 (10–40 %), 2 (40–70 %), and 3 (≥70 %). The composite score was calculated by multiplying the intensity and proportion scores, with the final classification determined as: 0 (−), 1 (+), 2 (++), and 3 (+++). Further details regarding the p53 antibody are available in Supplementary Table S2.

An in-situ hybridization (ISH) assay was performed with a commercially available EBV oligonucleotide probe complementary to EBV-encoded small RNAs (EBER) (PanPath, Amsterdam, Netherlands), as previously described by Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2010).

BaseScope assay

BaseScope assays were performed according to the guidelines provided by the manufacturer (Advanced Cell Diagnostics, Newark, CA). The experimental protocol was performed according to the method described by Zhang et al. (J.Y. Zhang et al., 2022).

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as means ± SDs. Comparisons between groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test (two groups) or one-way ANOVA (multiple groups). Survival curves were generated via Kaplan–Meier analysis, and differences were assessed using the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox regression was performed to identify independent prognostic factors. All statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS 26.0 (IBM) and GraphPad Prism 9.0. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

ebv-circRPMS1 and p53 are co-upregulated and promote cell proliferation in EBVaGC cells

Our previous investigations identified a distinct expression pattern of circRPMS1, with significantly elevated levels in EBV-positive cells (SNU719) compared to its absence in EBV-negative cells (AGS) (Huang et al., 2019). To further investigate the influence of circRPMS1 in SNU719 cells, we initially designed three circRPMS1 interference sequences. RT-qPCR validation confirmed the efficacy of si-circRPMS1#2 and si-circRPMS1#3 in effectively knocking down circRPMS1, with si-circRPMS1#2 showing the most pronounced effect (Fig. S1). Therefore, si-circRPMS1#2 was used for subsequent experiments. On the other hand, we transfected AGS cells with circRPMS1-overexpressing plasmids and selected stably expressing cell lines for subsequent experiments (Fig. S2). To confirm the p53 expression in GC cells, RT-qPCR and Western blotting analysis revealed that ectopic expression of circRPMS1 in AGS cells significantly upregulated both ebv-circRPMS1 (P < 0.001 vs. Negative Control) and p53 mRNA/protein levels, whereas siRNA-mediated knockdown of ebv-circRPMS1 in SNU719 cells markedly reduced its expression (P < 0.01 vs. NCi) and concurrently suppressed p53 transcription and translation (Fig. 1A–C). In functional studies that employed EdU incorporation assays, circRPMS1 overexpression robustly enhanced AGS cell proliferation (P < 0.01 vs. NC), while ebv-circRPMS1 depletion in SNU719 cells attenuated proliferative capacity (P < 0.05 vs. NCi). Direct interaction between ebv-circRPMS1 and p53 in EBVaGC cells

In Fig. 1, we observed that circRPMS1 modulates P53 expression. Subsequently, we examined whether they co-localize in SNU719 cells. The RNA-FISH assays suggests that ebv-circRPMS1 shows significant colocalization with p53 within the nucleus, as highlighted by the yellow arrows and areas in Fig. 2A, suggesting spatial interaction between the two molecules. Furthermore, RIP assays using an anti-p53 antibody demonstrated reduced enrichment of endogenous ebv-circRPMS1 in p53-precipitated complexes compared to the IgG controls (Fig. 2B), indicating direct RNA–protein binding. This interaction was further validated by the RIP assays, wherein MS2 tag-labeled ebv-circRPMS1 specifically coprecipitated with Flag-tagged p53 in transfected SNU719 cells (Fig. 2C). ebv-circRPMS1–p53 axis associated with tumor growth in vivo

Subsequently, we investigated whether circRPMS1 affects SNU719 cell proliferation in vivo. SNU719-NC (circR-NC) cell, or SNU719-si-circR(si-circR) cells were injected into the thigh root of NOD/SCID mice. The tumors harvested from the NOD/SCID mice revealed significantly smaller volumes of si-circR tumors compared to their circR-NC counterparts (J.Y. Zhang et al., 2022). The ISH analysis of EBER confirmed robust EBV infection in xenograft tumor (Fig. 3A). Concurrently, the IHC analysis demonstrated diminished accumulation of p53 protein in the si-circR tumor tissues (Fig. 3B). The BaseScope assays showed significantly reduced expression of ebv-circRPMS1 in the si-circR tumors compared to the circR-NC controls, with the staining intensity correlating negatively with the p53 levels (Fig. 3C). Clinical relevance of ebv-circRPMS1–p53 co-expression in EBVaGC prognosis

We collected 70 EBVaGC clinical samples, p53 expression was evaluated in both

EBVaGC and EBVnGC using IHC. The results showed that p53 expression in the EBVaGC was higher compared to the EBVnGC (Fig. 4A). Based on the immunohistochemical intensity of p53 in cancer stroma, samples were divided into high and low expression groups. The ISH analysis for EBER confirmed EBV infection in EBVaGC tissues, while the BaseScope assays revealed significantly elevated ebv-circRPMS1 expression in EBVaGC compared to the EBVnGC controls (Fig. 4A). The IHC analysis demonstrated concurrent upregulation of nuclear p53 protein in the EBVaGC tissues (Fig. 4B), with the staining intensity mirroring the ebv-circRPMS1 expression gradient (Fig. 4C). Discussion

This study found that ebv-circRPMS1 directly binds to p53 protein, thereby enhancing tumor proliferation. Clinically, ebv-circRPMS1–p53 co-expression correlates with poor survival in EBVaGC patients. The direct interaction between ebv-circRPMS1 and p53 contributes to the emerging paradigm of viral non-coding RNAs rewiring host tumor suppressor networks.

Similar mechanisms have been observed in other oncoviruses. HPV16-derived circE7 enhances RAD21 stability to promote cervical cancer metastasis (Ge et al., 2024), while circTP53 drives hepatocellular carcinoma by sequestering miR-1233–3p to upregulate MDM2 (Ma et al., 2021). This spatial regulation of p53 by circRNAs is exemplified by host-derived CDR1as, which stabilizes p53 through cytoplasmic MDM2 binding (Lou et al., 2020), while nuclear circPVT1 promotes p53 ubiquitination via miR-125b and miR-200a recruitment (Luo et al., 2021). These findings position viral circRNAs as precision tools for hijacking host proteostasis networks, with ebv-circRPMS1 representing a novel archetype that converts p53 from tumor suppressor to oncogenic collaborator.

The stabilization of wild-type p53 induced by ebv-circRPMS1 challenges the conventional belief that p53 gain-of-function (GOF) strictly requires genetic mutations. Recent studies have revealed that p53 can undergo non-mutational activation through liquid–liquid phase separation mechanisms (Yang et al., 2017). Specifically, mutant p53 forms oncogenic protein condensates via its prion-like domain (Lama et al., 2022; Petronilho et al., 2024). Furthermore, post-translational modifications (PTMs) play critical roles in regulating p53 activity: lactylation of p53 at lysine 120 (K120), mediated by AARS1, promotes chemoresistance in colorectal cancer (Zong et al., 2024), whereas SETD1A-mediated p53 methylation at lysine 372 (K372) enhances cancer stemness in breast malignancies (Ivanov et al., 2007). The potential of ebv-circRPMS1 to influence p53 PTMs) may synergize with lactylation or acetylation to lock p53 into a transcriptionally dysregulated state. Future studies should map ebv-circRPMS1’s impact on p53 PTMs using site-specific antibodies or proteomics, similar to circ-Amotl1’s enhancement of c-Myc phosphorylation in glioblastoma (Yang et al., 2017).

Targeting the ebv-circRPMS1–p53 interaction presents unique challenges and opportunities. Antisense oligonucleotides against ebv-circRPMS1’s BSJ could exploit viral sequence specificity, minimizing off-target effects on host circRNAs. Studies of ebv-circRPMS1–p53 complexes, analogous to circNSUN2/IGF2BP2 structural mapping (He et al., 2021) could identify druggable interfaces. Small molecules mimicking ebv-circRPMS1’s β-sheet domains might competitively inhibit p53 binding.

This study identified ebv-circRPMS1 as a novel viral oncogene that directly binds to and stabilizes p53, subverting its tumor-suppressive functions to drive EBVaGC progression. This interaction enhances p53’s GOF properties, promoting tumor proliferation in vitro and accelerating xenograft growth in vivo. Notably, the correlation between high p53 expression and poor prognosis suggests a GOF mechanism of wild-type p53 in EBVaGC rather than mutational activation. Clinically, ebv-circRPMS1–p53 co-expression correlates with reduced overall survival in EBVaGC patients, establishing its prognostic significance. While significant co-expression patterns suggest functional interplay, p53 rescue experiments remain essential to confirm causality.

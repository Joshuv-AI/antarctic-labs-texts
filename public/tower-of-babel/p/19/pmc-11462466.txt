A new pricing method for integrated energy systems based on geometric Brownian motions under the risk-neutral measure
Authors: Jun Liu, Lihong Zhou, Hao Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn integrated energy system (IES) is a promising energy-saving technology. With the advancement of technology and deregulation of the energy market, numerous distributed energy systems (DESs) will be accessed in the IES with various trading modes in the future. This makes it even more challenging to evaluate the fair energy selling price of IES, which is of great concern to market regulators and participants. To address this issue, this study proposes a risk-neutral pricing method for IES based on geometric Brownian motions. First, geometric Brownian motions are introduced to characterize the uncertainty feature of the energy price of the DESs. Then, we propose a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) for the energy price of an IES by utilizing the Ito lemma and the properties of the martingale under the risk-neutral measure. Finally, sensitivity analyses of the proposed SPDE on the volatilities and two types of correlation coefficients are conducted by implementing the proposed model in the given scenario.Keywords: Integrated energy system, Distributed energy system, Risk-neutral measure, Geometric Brownian motion, Stochastic partial differential equation

Introduction

Motivation and literature review

The integrated energy system (IES) is a hot energy-saving technology that can transfer, convert, and couple various resources such as electricity, gas, and heat before selling energy to users [1]. Compared with the traditional single energy market, the integrated energy market within IES coexists with multiple distributed energy, such as photovoltaic [[2], [3], [4], [5]]. In a highly market-oriented energy market, by publicly disclosing the electricity sales information and participating in market competition, multi-agent participation in the distribution market has been formed. Due to the competitive relationship between distribution networks and distributed energy systems (DES) in energy sales, there is a correlation between energy sales prices. IES is the most crucial trading entity in the market. Therefore, it is of great significance for fair trading in the energy market to determine the fair price for energy sales of the IES [6,7]. The fair price of the IES provides a pricing benchmark, which is suitable for energy trading markets with multiple market entities participating [8].

Traditional energy pricing methods generally adopt fixed or negotiated pricing models, which cannot reflect the dynamic price characteristics of energy as a commodity. At present, some literature has studied the dynamic pricing of energy. For example, Yang et al. [9] proposed a novel distributed power agency trading based on dynamic game theory, which can optimize pricing strategies considering participants’ expectations of the trading price. Wang et al. [10] presented a novel distributed peer-to-peer energy transaction method based on the double auction market for diversified prosumers in urban IES. Dong et al. [11] applied game theory to generate smart trading contracts and determine energy prices through blockchain-based peer-to-peer transactions. The papers [[12], [13], [14], [15]] investigated energy management according to uncertainty modeling. Mohammadali et al. investigated the renewable networked micro-grids flexibility in Ref. [16] and proposed flexibility pricing of integrated unit of electric spring and electric vehicles parking in microgrids in Ref. [17]. However, in these studies, the pricing methods are only effective for participating entities in some given scenarios, and not universally applicable to the comprehensive energy market with multiple trading entities and diverse trading scenarios. Furthermore, previous pricing methods cannot reflect the IES fairness of the comprehensive energy market or the randomness and volatility of energy prices as a commodity.

Regarding some relevant research on the applicability of dynamic pricing, some stochastic pricing models based on geometric Brownian motions have been applied to evaluate the fair asset price in recent years. Geometric Brownian motion (GBM) is a continuous-time stochastic process in which the logarithm of a random variable follows Brownian motion. Its characteristics lie in three aspects: (1) The expectation is independent of the price of stochastic processes, which aligns with the observation of the actual market. (2) It only considers cases where prices are positive, just like actual energy prices. (3) It exhibits the same "roughness" as the price trajectory observed in the energy market. For example, S. Borovkova and M. D. Schmeck developed an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with a jump component to electricity price modeling based on the powerful technique of stochastic time change [18]. Fernandes et al. [19] and Koalova [20] used the real option approach in the investment in the energy sector and summarized the real option in the fields of renewable energy. Liu et al. [21] evaluated the carbon option to cope with the uncertainty of demand and price of carbon emission rights based on a geometric Brownian motion model with regime change. Xiao et al. [22] devised a barrier option for wind power based on a geometric Brownian motion to hedge fluctuation and randomness. Stute and Kuhnbach [23] proposed a dynamic pricing method, which was seen as an option to incentivize flexible and responsive use of household consumers. Ma et al. [24] used the valuation of real options to solve the optimal investment problem for subsidizing residential battery installations assuming that the power demand and the cost of the battery follow a geometric Brownian motion. Donghyun et al. [25] investigated uncertainty factors in the integration market of renewable energy and evaluated the price of renewable energy using the Monte Carlo simulation. Xue et al. [26] constructed a renewable energy price index and priced the option based on this index by the improved Monte Carlo option pricing method.

Overall, a summary of the studies done in the literature reviews is presented in Table 1. These studies provide ideas for solving the dynamic pricing problem of the IES with various DESs under the risk-neutral measure. The risk-neutral pricing theory expresses the conclusion in the capital market that under the no-arbitrage condition, the price of the derivative security is independent of the risk attitude of investors if the price of the derivative security still depends on an underlying security. This conclusion is mathematically manifested as the differential equation for pricing derivative securities does not include variables influenced by investors' risk attitudes, especially the expected return rate. Therefore, this paper investigates the fair energy price of the IES based on geometric Brownian motions [27,28] under the risk-neutral measure.

Considering the fairness of market transactions in an actual energy market, investors in DESs or IES must bear more risks to obtain higher returns. That is, the greater the risk, the higher the expected value. According to economic theory, if the commodity market is efficient, investors will perform risk-free arbitrage once the price deviates from the fair price. Under the no-arbitrage condition, the risk-neutral pricing theory implies that in the capital market the price of the derivative security is independent of the risk attitude of investors if the price of the derivative security still depends on an underlying security. This conclusion is mathematically manifested as the differential equation for pricing derivative securities does not include variables influenced by investors' risk attitudes, especially the expected return rate. Risk neutrality is a concept relative to risk preference and risk aversion, and risk-neutral investors do not require risk compensation for the risks they bear. A world where everyone is risk-neutral is called a risk-neutral world. In this world, investors do not compensate for risk, and the expected returns of all securities are risk-free interest rates. It should be emphasized that the valuation of derivative products obtained under the assumption of risk neutrality can also be applied to the real world. Despite differences in risk preferences among investors in the real world, when arbitrage opportunities arise, investors will adopt arbitrage behavior regardless of their risk preferences. Consequently, the commodity price must assume risk neutrality to ensure market fairness [29]. Therefore, considering the commodity nature of energy, the energy price in the risk-neutral world is the energy price in the real world.

Paper organization

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, the fair pricing models of an IES are derived under the risk-neutral measure assuming DESs follow a geometric Brownian motion. In Section 3, numerical experiments are performed to analyze the influences of volatility parameters and correlation coefficient parameters in DESs on the fair energy price of the IES. Section 4 summarizes the main conclusions.

The pricing model for IES under the risk-neutral measure

Due to the competitive relationship between distribution networks and distributed energy systems in electricity sales, there is a correlation between the electricity sales prices of distribution networks and distributed energy systems. Therefore, this section first considers the randomness and volatility of distributed energy electricity sales prices and uses a geometric Brownian motion model to characterize the electricity sales prices of DESs by simulating the asset price process. When the IES has only the electrical energy, the electricity fair pricing model of the distribution network is derived by utilizing the Ito lemma [30] and the properties of martingale [31] considering the relationship among the DESs. The result is Theorem 2. Furthermore, the fair energy pricing model of the IES comprising the electricity network, gas network, and heat network is proposed, which considers the relationship among electricity, gas, and heat in the same DESs and that among the different DESs. The result is Theorem 3.

Conclusion

This paper derives a novel model for the fair energy price of IES with large amounts of DESs, which could be exploited as a powerful dynamic pricing tool. Geometric Brownian motions have been introduced to capture the randomness of the energy price of DESs accurately. Based on this model, an SPDE followed by the fair electricity price of IES is proposed using the Ito lemma and properties of martingale. Sensitivity analyses are performed to investigate the influence of model parameters on the fair energy selling price of IES. Experimental results are as follows.

(1)The volatility of the fair price of the IES significantly decreases, and the price becomes more stable when the photovoltaic power generation is accessed in the IES from 8:00 to 17:00. Therefore, to improve competitiveness and suppress fluctuations in fair electricity prices of IES, more DESs with peak shaving and valley filling functions should be accessed.

The geometric Brownian motion has unique advantages in characterizing the randomness and volatility of energy prices. However, as a continuous stochastic process, it cannot reflect the jumping phenomenon and the time-varying volatility in the price process. Meanwhile, the energy pricing model within the IES did not consider the impact of the coupling of electricity, gas, and heat within the system. In future works, the fair pricing problem in case of the coupling among electricity, gas, and heat in the IES will be investigated by the jump-diffusion model with stochastic volatility.

Other correlation coefficients can be obtained using the same method.

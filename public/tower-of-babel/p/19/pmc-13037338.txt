Appropriateness of stress ulcer prophylaxis and associated factors among patients in medical, surgical, and intensive care units at Wolaita Sodo university comprehensive specialized hospital, Ethiopia: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yetenayet Tadele, Aliyi Gerina, Zewde Zema Kanche, Temesgen Sidamo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStress-related gastric ulcer (SRGU) is a common complication among critically ill patients, and inappropriate use of stress ulcer prophylaxis (SUP) remains a global challenge in hospitalized populations.ObjectiveThis study aimed to assess the appropriateness of SUP practice and its associated factors among patients admitted to medical, surgical, and intensive care units (ICUs) at Wolaita Sodo University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Southern Ethiopia, in 2023.MethodsA hospital-based cross-sectional study was conducted using a retrospective chart review of 370 patients. Data were collected from July to September 2023 using a structured and pretested data abstraction tool. The data were entered into Epi Data version 4.6 and analyzed using SPSS version 25. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and SUP practices. Bivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify candidate variables (p < 0.25), which were subsequently entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to determine factors independently associated with inappropriate SUP use. Statistical significance was declared at p < 0.05.ResultsOf the 370 patients included in the analysis, 232 (62.7%) received SUP inappropriately. Factors independently associated with inappropriate SUP use included hospital stay longer than seven days (AOR = 2.50; 95% CI: 1.42–4.39), absence of hypoperfusion (AOR = 0.35; 95% CI: 0.22–0.58), mechanical ventilation for more than 48 h (AOR = 3.88; 95% CI: 1.90–7.92), and delayed initiation of SUP (> 1 day after admission) (AOR = 0.34; 95% CI: 0.20–0.58).ConclusionThe prevalence of inappropriate SUP use was high in the study setting. Implementation of institutional guidelines, targeted prescriber training, and pharmacist-led stewardship interventions are recommended to promote appropriate SUP utilization.Keywords: Stress ulcer prophylaxis, Appropriateness, Acid suppressive therapy, Hospitalized patients, Ethiopia

Introduction

Stress-related gastric ulcer (SRGU) refers to acute gastric mucosal damage that develops in patients with severe or life-threatening illnesses in the absence of pre-existing gastrointestinal pathology [1]. Critically ill patients are particularly vulnerable to stress-related gastric bleeding (SRGB) due to physiological stress, hypoperfusion, and invasive medical interventions. Without appropriate prophylaxis, stress-related mucosal damage may progress to clinically significant gastrointestinal bleeding, leading to increased morbidity and mortality [2].

Previous studies have reported that approximately 5–25% of critically ill patients who do not receive stress ulcer prophylaxis (SUP) develop SRGB, depending on illness severity and exposure to risk factors [3]. Stress-related gastrointestinal complications (SRGICs) are associated with prolonged intensive care unit (ICU) stays, increased healthcare costs, and mortality rates up to four times higher than those observed in patients without gastrointestinal bleeding [4]. Consequently, appropriate use of SUP is a critical component of care in high-risk hospitalized patients.

The American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) provides widely used guidelines for SUP, recommending prophylaxis for patients with at least one major risk factor or two or more minor risk factors [5]. Major risk factors include coagulopathy, mechanical ventilation for more than 48 h, severe head injury, multiple trauma, spinal cord injury, hepatic failure, and extensive burn injury. Minor risk factors include sepsis, prolonged ICU stay, occult or overt gastrointestinal bleeding, use of antiplatelet agents, and high-dose corticosteroid therapy [5].

Despite these guidelines, inappropriate use of acid-suppressive medications (ASMs), including proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and histamine-2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs), remains common. Overuse of SUP in low-risk or non–critically ill patients exposes individuals to unnecessary adverse effects such as Clostridioides difficile infection, hospital-acquired pneumonia, osteoporosis, and drug–drug interactions. Globally, studies report that 20–92.9% of hospitalized patients receive SUP inappropriately, either without indication or despite meeting criteria for prophylaxis [6–8]. Conversely, underuse of SUP in eligible patients increases the risk of stress-related gastrointestinal bleeding.

Some Previous study shows increase inappropriate practice of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) as SUP, even if in non critical ill patients. This improper practice of PPIs as SUP can expose patients to potential adverse effects like: Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea, Community-acquired pneumonia, Osteoporosis and hip fractures [9, 10]. A study conducted at Palestine hospital in 2019 found that 96.4% of patients received SUP. Of these, 11.3% developed adverse effects, including: 31.25% had hospital-acquired pneumonia, 50% had Clostridium difficile-associated infections and 18.75% had both infections [4].

A considerable number of hospitalized patients with risk of developing SRGU or fulfilling guideline criteria did not get this prophylaxis’s [11]. A cross-sectional study conducted in a China hospital in 2021 shows that 397 (33.1%) patients received SUP without indication (overpractice), and 112 (9.3%) fulfilled guideline criteria for SUP did not received SUP (underpractice). This is a big problem for the patient because SRGB can increase patient hospitalization, mortality, and cost [12].

Evidence from both high-income and low- and middle-income countries demonstrates that inappropriate continuation of SUP beyond ICU admission and into general wards is particularly common. In Africa, studies have reported that 20–73.5% of patients receive SUP without appropriate indication [7, 13, 14].

In Ethiopia, data regarding SUP practice based on ASHP guidelines are limited, and evidence from comprehensive specialized hospitals remains scarce. To the best of our knowledge, no prior study has evaluated the appropriateness of SUP use and its associated factors at Wolaita Sodo University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the magnitude of inappropriate SUP practice and identify associated factors among patients admitted to medical, surgical, and ICU settings in this hospital.

The findings of this study are expected to provide valuable insights into existing gaps in SUP utilization, inform the development of institution-specific guidelines, and support pharmacist-led interventions to optimize prophylactic therapy. Ultimately, improving the appropriateness of SUP use may reduce preventable adverse drug effects, healthcare costs, and patient morbidity and mortality in the Ethiopian healthcare setting.

Materials and methods

Study setting and period

The study was conducted from July to September 2023 in the medical, surgical, and intensive care unit (ICU) settings of Wolaita Sodo University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (WSUCSH), located in Wolaita Sodo City, Southern Ethiopia, approximately 329 km south of Addis Ababa. The hospital has a total capacity of approximately 102 beds, including 42 beds in the medical ward, 40 beds in the surgical ward, and 20 beds in the ICU. WSUCSH is a tertiary-level teaching hospital affiliated with Wolaita Sodo University College of Health Sciences and provides a wide range of services, including internal medicine, surgery, emergency care, obstetrics and gynecology, pediatrics, oncology, psychiatry, neurology, and dermatology (HMIS of WSUCSH, 2023).

Study design

A hospital-based cross-sectional study design was employed to assess the appropriateness of stress ulcer prophylaxis (SUP) practice and associated factors among patients admitted to the medical, surgical, and ICU wards of WSUCSH during the study period.

The calculated sample size was proportionally allocated to the medical, surgical, and ICU wards based on patient admission data from the preceding three months. Eligible patient charts were identified using ward admission logbooks and selected using a simple random sampling technique (Fig. 1).

Data collection procedure and quality control

Data collection tools and procedure

Data were collected using a structured and pretested data abstraction tool developed after reviewing relevant literature. The tool captured socio-demographic information, clinical characteristics, risk factors for stress-related gastric ulcer, and details of SUP use.

Appropriateness of SUP was assessed using ASHP guidelines, which recommend SUP for patients with at least one major risk factor or two or more minor risk factors. Major and minor risk factors were identified through careful review of patient medical records. Three trained pharmacists independently reviewed patient charts, and ambiguous cases were resolved through discussion with a senior clinical pharmacist.

Data quality control

Data collectors and supervisors received one day of training on study objectives, data collection procedures, and confidentiality. The data collection tool was pretested on 5% of patient records at a nearby hospital, and necessary modifications were made before actual data collection. Completed data collection forms were checked daily for completeness and consistency.

Data entry and analysis

Collected data were entered into EpiData version 4.6 and exported to SPSS version 25 for analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and SUP practices. Bivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify variables associated with inappropriate SUP use at a p-value < 0.25. Variables meeting this criterion were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to identify factors independently associated with inappropriate SUP use. Model fitness was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, and multicollinearity was evaluated using variance inflation factors (VIFs). Statistical significance was declared at p < 0.05.

Indications to practice stress ulcer prophylaxis

According to ASHP guidelines, patients must have at least one major risk factor or two or more minor risk factors to be eligible for SUP. In this study, the most frequently identified major risk factors were a history of gastrointestinal ulcer within one year prior to admission (55, 14.9%), mechanical ventilation for more than 48 h (54, 14.6%), and coagulopathy (52, 14.1%).

Among minor risk factors, hypoperfusion was the most common (170, 45.9%), followed by high-dose corticosteroid therapy (37, 10%) and ICU stay longer than one week (27, 7.3%) (Table 4).

Pharmacologic agent used as SUP

Of the 370 patients included in the study, 254 (68.6%) received stress ulcer prophylaxis. Among these, 229 (61.8%) received SUP via the intravenous route, while 25 (6.8%) received oral therapy. Histamine-2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs) were the most frequently used agents, administered to 204 (55.2%) patients, primarily via the intravenous route. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) were used in 50 (13.5%) patients.

In the medical ward, 113 (30.5%) of 165 admitted patients received SUP, predominantly intravenous cimetidine. In the surgical ward, 102 (27.6%) of 153 patients received SUP, again mainly intravenous cimetidine. In the ICU, 39 (10.5%) of 52 patients received SUP, with intravenous cimetidine being the most commonly used agent (Table 5).

Magnitude of inappropriate practice of stress ulcer prophylaxis

In the medical ward, 102 (27.6%) patients received SUP inappropriately, while 63 (17.0%) received appropriate prophylaxis (Fig. 4). In the surgical ward, 111 (30.0%) patients received SUP inappropriately, compared with 42 (11.4%) who received appropriate SUP. In the ICU, appropriate SUP use was more common, with 33 (8.9%) patients receiving guideline-concordant therapy. Overall, 232 of the 370 patients received SUP inappropriately, resulting in an overall prevalence of inappropriate SUP use of 62.7% (95% CI: 57.6%–67.6%) (Table 6).

Variables meeting the bivariable selection criteria were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model. Four factors remained independently associated with inappropriate SUP practice.

Patients with a hospital stay longer than seven days had significantly higher odds of receiving inappropriate SUP compared with those hospitalized for seven days or less (AOR = 2.50; 95% CI: 1.42–4.39; p = 0.002). Patients who were mechanically ventilated for more than 48 h had higher odds of inappropriate SUP use (AOR = 3.88; 95% CI: 1.90–7.92; p < 0.001).

In contrast, patients without hypoperfusion had significantly lower odds of inappropriate SUP use compared with those who had hypoperfusion (AOR = 0.35; 95% CI: 0.22–0.58; p < 0.001). Additionally, delayed initiation of SUP (more than one day after admission) was associated with lower odds of inappropriate SUP use compared with immediate initiation (AOR = 0.34; 95% CI: 0.20–0.58; p < 0.001) (Table 8).

Discussion

This study assessed the appropriateness of stress ulcer prophylaxis (SUP) use and identified associated factors among patients admitted to medical, surgical, and intensive care units at Wolaita Sodo University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. The findings demonstrate a high prevalence of inappropriate SUP use, despite the availability of well-established clinical guidelines, highlighting an important gap between evidence-based recommendations and real-world clinical practice.

This study showed an overall magnitude of inappropriate practice of SUP were 62.7% with a 95% confidence interval of (57.6%, 67.6%). This finding was in line with the previous study conducted in Beirut university hospital, which indicated that the prevalence of inappropriate practice of SUP were (58%) [16]. University Medicine Greifswald Germany indicate inappropriately practice of SUP were (60%) [17]. In addition to this, Studies that reported a higher proportion of inappropriate practice of SUP were Jordan University; Department of Clinical Pharmacy reported (86%) patients received SUP without indication [18], Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Iran (80%) of patients received SUP without indication [19]. Study that Contradicted to this study results was study conducted in Namazi Hospital, Iran indicated (74%) patients received SUP appropriately with indication [20].

In Africa, studies reported higher proportion of inappropriate practice of SUP were study conducted in Sudan indicate (73.5%) patients received SUP without indication [14]. Local studies in Ethiopia studies that in line with this finding were study conducted University of Gondre hospital indicate (63.4%) inappropriately practice SUP [13] and Debre Berhan university hospital 66% inappropriately received SUP without indication [21]. Also studies reported higher proportion of appropriate practice of SUP, in Dessie Referral Hospital indicate (50%) patients received SUP with indication [22]. The reason for the variation in results may be lack of their own comment guideline.

In this study, H2RAs were the most commonly used pharmacological agent as SUP, around 204 (80.3%) patients received it to prevent SRGU. This result is consistent with the studies conducted in the Gaza Strip hospitals, Palestine indicate (86.9%) patients received H2RAs [23], Swedish (88%) [24],, Kenyan Referral Hospital (76.4%) patients received H2RAs [7], Dessie Referral Hospital (76.0%) patients received H2RAs [22]. The possible reasons why our clinician’s preferred IV dosage form of H2RAs in our hospital might be the lower cost of the IV dosage form of H2RAs compared to the IV dosage form of PPIs. However Studies that did not conform to the findings of this study were those conducted in Saudi Arabia indicate (68.1%) patients received PPIs [25] and Gonder Referral Hospital indicate (92.7%) patients received PPIs as SUP [13].

In our study, coagulopathy (14.9%), a history of GIU within a year before admission (14.9%), and mechanical ventilation (14.6%) were the three most common major risk factors for the indication to practice of SUP; hypoperfusion (45.9%), and steroid therapy (10%) were the two most common minor risk factors for the indication to practice of SUP. However in our finding, no patient was indicated to practice SUP due to burning more than 35% of body surface area.

Associated Factors were exist between patient’s stay hospital > 7 days, hypo-perfusion, patients on mechanical ventilation for > 48 h, patients concurrently taking anticoagulant and duration of SUP start with inappropriate practice of SUP. Patients who stayed > 7 days in the hospital were 3.202 times more likely to be inappropriately received SUP than patients who stayed < 7 days. This finding was similar with a study conducted in Iran [19], and Gonder [13]. The possible reason may be that patients who stay for long time in the hospital with complicated medical conditions are exposed to a higher number of medications; due to this, physicians may think those medications could expose patients to GI ulceration, so they start giving SUP to prevent GI ulceration with or without consideration of risk factor.

In addition to this, patients who did not have hypo-perfusion related complications were 0.354 times less likely to be inappropriately received SUP than those who had hypo-perfusion related complications. This finding were consistent with a previous study conducted in China [12]. Patients who stayed > 7 days in the hospital had a 2.496 time more likely inappropriately receive SUP than patients who stayed < 7 days. Patients who started SUP after one day was 0.343 times more likely to be inappropriately received SUP than those who started it immediately after admission. Patients who were on mechanical ventilation for > 48 h were 3.882 times less likely to be inappropriately received SUP compared to those who were not on mechanical ventilation for > 48 h, this finding was consistent with a previous study conducted in Brazil [26].

Limitations of study

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causal relationships between identified factors and inappropriate SUP use. Second, the study relied on retrospective medical record review, which may be subject to incomplete documentation and misclassification of risk factors.

Third, the study was conducted at a single tertiary hospital, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other healthcare settings. Additionally, clinical outcomes such as gastrointestinal bleeding, Clostridioides difficile infection, or pneumonia were not assessed, preventing evaluation of the direct clinical consequences of inappropriate SUP use.

Despite these limitations, the study provides valuable evidence on the magnitude of inappropriate SUP practice and associated factors in a resource-limited setting. The findings may serve as a basis for future interventional studies and quality improvement initiatives aimed at promoting rational SUP use.

Conclusion

This study found a high prevalence of inappropriate stress ulcer prophylaxis use among patients admitted to medical, surgical, and intensive care units. Inappropriate prescribing was more common outside the intensive care setting and was associated with prolonged hospitalization and mechanical ventilation. These findings emphasize the importance of routine reassessment of SUP indications and adherence to evidence-based guidelines. Strengthening multidisciplinary collaboration, including clinical pharmacist involvement, may help promote rational SUP use and optimize patient care.

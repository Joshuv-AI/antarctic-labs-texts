Promoter role of putrescine for molecular and biochemical processes under drought stress in barley
Authors: Selma Tabur, Serkan Ozmen, Signem Oney-Birol
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDrought, which adversely affects plant growth and continuity of life and reduces product yield and quality, is one of the most common abiotic stresses at the globally. One of the polyamines that regulates plant development and reacts to abiotic stressors, including drought stress, is Putrescine (Put). This study compared the physiological and molecular effects of applying exogenous Put (10 µM) to barley (Hordeum vulgare cv. Burakbey) under drought stress (− 6.30 mPa PEG 6000). The 21-day drought stress imposed on the barley plant had a strong negative effect on plant metabolism in all experimental groups. Exogenous Put treatment under drought stress had a reformative effect on the cell cycle (transitions from G0-G1 to S and from S to G2-M), total protein content (almost 100%), endogenous polyamine content, malondialdehyde (MDA) (70%), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX) (62%) levels compared to the drought stress plants. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) (12%) and catalase (CAT) (32%) enzyme levels in the same group increased further after exogenous Put application, forming a response to drought stress. Consequently, it was discovered that the administration of exogenous Put in barley raises endogenous polyamine levels and then improves drought tolerance due to increased antioxidant capability, cell division stimulation, and total protein content.Keywords: Abiotic stress, Biochemical enzymes, Cell Cycle, Hordeum vulgare L., Total protein contentSubject terms: Biochemistry, Cell biology, Genetics, Plant sciences, Environmental sciences

Introduction

Drought, which has emerged as a result of the deterioration of the ecological balance over time, has become a negative factor with serious consequences for humanity and hindering agricultural production. While drought stress shows quite different effects depending on plant species, it generally has negative effects on physiological, morphological, biochemical and molecular changes1. Plants react to drought stress by regulating plant metabolism, phytochemical activity, RUBISCO, stress metabolites (glutathione, glibet, polyamines) and antioxidant enzyme levels under these adverse conditions2. In addition, drought causes more reactive oxygen species (ROS) to accumulate than normal. While these ROS directly interact with membrane lipids and many macromolecules, resulting in decreased yield and quality in plants, they also increase the content of malondialdehyde (MDA)3. Apart from all these effects, previous studies have determined that drought stress negatively affects stomatal conductivity, cell swelling, transpiration, photosynthesis, respiration, proline and soluble sugar production processes4–6. All abiotic stresses, including drought stress, produce different signal stimuli, such as an increase in kinase accumulation, overexpression of ROS, and synthesis of hormones that stop mitosis (ABA, JA). The resulting signal molecules cause stimulation at checkpoints in the cell cycle, disrupting the transitions in cell cycle phases. As a result, there is a decrease in DNA replication in the cell structure and disruption occurs in the transition of cells to the mitotic division phase7.

In drought conditions, plants first tend to change their water potential with the help of hydraulic and electrical signals. These resulting signals cause biochemical changes in the structure of plants directly or indirectly because of drought, causing the generation of substances such as ABA, Ca2+, and NO. These signals can change plant status indirectly by causing an increase in functional gene products such as proline, glycine betaine, soluble sugar, LEA proteins, and aquaporin in plant metabolism. Additionally, regulatory gene products such as CDPKs, MAPKs, HD-zip/bZIP, AP2/ERF, NAC, MYB, and WRKY regulate signal transduction pathways, ensuring the successful survival of plants in an arid environment8.

Polyamines, which exist in all living organisms, are molecules with low molecular weights that fall into the biogenic amine group. Positively charged polyamines mitigate cell damage by interacting with proteins, nucleic acids and components in the cell wall against adverse stress conditions9. While the increase in the amount of polyamine during germination and the change in polyamine levels during seedling growth in some plants indicate that polyamines act as plant growth regulators. It is known that polyamines increase RNA and protein synthesis before pollen tube growth and inhibit senescence by inhibiting RNase and protease activity in plants. In addition to the stated properties, it has been stated that polyamines are important for different processes such as cell division, continuity of the life cycle, cell signaling, morphogenesis, secondary metabolism, aging, and planned cell death.10.

In today's conditions, one of the biggest difficulties encountered in advanced agricultural practices is meeting the nutritional needs of people despite the presence of negative environmental pressures. For this reason, plants with advanced properties have developed different mechanisms to cope with the negative effects of environmental stresses11. There are many studies showing that abiotic stresses negatively affect the amount of endogenous polyamines and biochemical enzyme activities in various plant species12–15. Polyamines, which have an important role among these mechanisms, act as the first messengers when stress affects the plant body16. In addition, polyamines play vital roles in plants responding to a variety of environmental stresses, including metal toxicity, oxidative stress, drought, salinity, and cold stress16–20.

Synthesis of polyamines takes place as a result of reactions involving l-arginine and l-methionine. The genes encoding this enzyme have been identified in the biosynthesis stages of polyamines, which are synthesized in two different ways in plants21. Diamine putrescine is synthesized from arginine by arginase and from ornithine by ornithine decarboxylase (ODC). Moreover, putrescine can be synthesized from agmatine by three sequential reactions catalyzed by arginine decarboxylase (ADC), agmatine imino hydrolase (AIH), and N-carbamoyl putrescine amido hydrolase (CPA), respectively22. The relationship between biosynthetic genes involved in the synthesis of polyamines and their resistance to adverse environmental conditions has become an important issue. It has been determined that transferring the ADC from Jimsonweed to rice protects the plants due to the formation of higher amounts of Put, Spd, and Spm in arid conditions23. Similarly, the accumulation of Put after the transfer of the ADC2 gene in Arabidopsis proves that it increases stress tolerance9.

Barley, which has been used as an agricultural crop for approximately 10,000 years and is one of the most significant grain products, is mostly used in animal feed and beer production today, but it can still be used to feed millions of people24. The 2023 global crop harvest of barley, which ranks 4th in the world in terms of cultivated agricultural areas and products obtained, is almost 142 million tons25. Barley is very important economically, and it is necessary to carry out studies on it because it is a model plant, as well as due to its ability to easily adapt to different environmental conditions and its have very large production areas. Considering that polyamines are defined as substances that can be added to the cell externally as plant growth regulators or are found in the metabolism and respond to abiotic stress, it is concluded that polyamines can be used to make plants more resistant to drought. In literature research based on previous studies, no study was found that revealed the effects of exogenous Put application on barley plants under drought stress on the cell cycle, endogenous polyamine amounts and biochemical enzyme changes as included in this study. As a result of this study, the biochemical and molecular effects of exogenous putrescine application in barley under drought conditions have been explained for the first time, and the results obtained will form the basis for the development of barley varieties that are resistant to stress against due to global climate change, which is becoming more severe day by day.

Materials and methods

Barley seeds (Hordeum vulgare cv. Burakbey) were sent by the Ankara Agricultural Research Institute Directorate (in Turkey). Put used as a growth regulator in the study, and PEG 6000, which showed the effect of drought stress, were purchased from Merck. Put to be applied exogenously was prepared as a 500 mL–100 µM stock solution by dissolving in distilled water. After preliminary studies, it was determined that the PEG 6000 concentration, which reduced the germination percentage below 50%, was − 6.30 mPa to show the impact of drought stress in barley seeds. The next step after defining the PEG 6000 concentration was to determine the Putrescine concentration that best improved the negative effect of stress on germination, which was found to be 10 µM. Before the seeds were planted in petri dishes, they were subjected to sterilization and pretreated in distilled water (control) and in the medium with Put for 24 h at room temperature. Then the seeds were sown in sterilized glass petri dishes, including 7 mL of distilled water and PEG-6000 solutions, and left to germinate in an incubator at 20 °C. The seeds germinated in the incubator for a week, were transferred to the peat soil in 9 × 5 viols, and were then grown in the climate cabinet for 21 days. Plant specimens grown for seven days were grown in a climate cabinet at constant temperature (24 °C), 16/8 day and night periods and constant humidity conditions. Control group plants were irrigated regularly with distilled water every 3 days, and drought-treated plants were irrigated with PEG 6000 on the tenth day. All the studied plant tissues were taken from the climate cabinet at the end of the 21st day, and after they were decomposed with liquid nitrogen, they were stored at − 80 °C for analysis. This study was divided into four groups: Group I (C, Distilled water), Group II (distilled water + 10 µM Put), Group III (− 6.30 mPa PEG 6000) and Group IV (− 6.30 mPa PEG 6000 + 10 µM Put) according to applied drought stress and exogenous Put applications (Fig. 1.). For the analysis of all plant groups used in the study, all samples were obtained from three different plant tissues.

Figure summary of all experiments performed in the study.

Cell cycle assay

In order to analyze the phases of the cell cycle, the samples taken from the germinated seeds were evaluated using the Flowing software program with the CyFlow Space (Sysmex Partec) device in accordance with the Otto26 protocol. After cutting approximately 2.5 cm from the root tips grown for seven days, the obtained tissues were taken into petri dishes on ice. Tissue cells taken on ice were dissected in 5 mL of Otto I solution. After the textures that were broken into pieces were filtered with the help of a 33 µm mesh and 10 mL of Otto II solution was added, they were analyzed in flow cytometry, and the cell cycle was determined. The differences in the cell cycle were revealed as a result of taking samples from 3 different plants from each group and comparing the results.

Endogenous polyamine assays

After 7 days of germination, barley seeds were transferred to viols with peat soil and after 21 days of growing, fresh leaf tissue and the amount of endogenous polyamines were analyzed. Anlı et al.27 method was modified and adapted for barley plant in order to reveal how exogenous Put application impacts on endogenous polyamine contents under drought stress and control conditions. According to this protocol, approximately 3 g of leaf tissue was divided into small pieces with 25 mL of 0.4 M HclO4 with a scalpel. Acid lysed leaf tissues were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm at 3 °C for 10 min. Then, 400 µL of the sample filtered with a 0.45 µm filter was taken, 400 µL of Na2CO3 and 400 µL of dansyl chloride were added and kept in a 40 ºC water bath for half an hour. 200 µL of Na-l-glutamate monohydrate was added to the mixture, which was kept in the water bath, and it was kept under the same conditions for 1 more hour. Finally, 1 mL CH3CN was added to the samples taken from the water bath and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min, and the samples taken in the upper phase were analyzed in HPLC.

Biochemichal analyses

Total protein assay

In order to determine the total protein content, 0.3 g of leaf tissue was homogenized with 4 mL of 50 mM Potassium Phosphate bafir solution (pH: 7.0) containing 2 mM Na-EDTA and 1% PVP, then centrifuged at 4 °C at 10,000 rpm for 10 min. centrifuged and the resulting supernatants were used for protein analysis28. Using the mixture obtained for protein analysis, total protein contents were determined by applying the protocol of the Bio-Rad extraction kit29.

MDA assay

The measurement of the amount of MDA for the specification of the lipid peroxidation level was made according to the Li30 method. According to this method, after 200 mg of fresh leaf tissue was crushed in a mortar with 5 mL of TCA solution, the homogenates were centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 25 min. After 2 mL of each concentration of the supernatants was taken, 2 mL of 0.6% TBA solution in 10% TCA was added, and the mixture was boiled in a steam bath for 15 min., and then instant cooling was applied in an ice bath. Finally, after the obtained material was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min, the absorbance of the supernatant was determined at 600, 532 and 450 nm levels in spectrophotometry. As a result, MDA calculation is; Calculated according to 6.45 (A532–A600)–0.56 A450.

SOD, CAT and APX assays

Extraction processes were carried out on ice for the determination of antioxidant enzyme amounts. After washing 0.2 g of fresh leaf tissue in distilled water, it was homogenized with 5 mL of Sodium Phosphate buffer (50 mM) taken from the refrigerator. Then, enzyme analyses were performed after the fragmented tissues were centrifuged at 10.000 rpm for 25 min.

The process followed to determine the SOD enzyme level belongs to Beauchamp and Fridovich31. In the mixture prepared for enzyme analysis, 1.5 mL Sodium Phosphate Buffer (pH 7.8), 300 µL C5H11NO2S, 300 µL nitroblue tetrazolium chloride, 300 µL EDTA-Na2, 300 µL Riboflavn, 10 µL polyvinylpyrrolidone, 280 µL water, and 10 µL enzyme were located. 3 ml of the mixture with the final concentration was kept in a 2X15W fluorescent lamp for 10 min and measured at a wavelength of 560 nm 10 min after it was placed in the dark environment.

For the determination of CAT enzyme level, the method belonging to Beers and Sizer32 was adapted for the barley plant. The solution to be analyzed according to this method contains 1.5 mL of Sodium Phosphate Buffer, 1 mL of distilled water, 0.3 mL of H2O2 and 0.2 mL of enzyme extract. The catalase enzyme level was determined by calculating the change that occurred as a result of the 4-min kinetic reading of the mixture at 240 nm.

2 mL mixture was prepared according to the APX enzyme level determination study, which was done by modifying the method used by Kato and Shimuzu33. This mixture contains 1 mL of Potassium Phosphate Buffer, 500 µL of EDTA-Na2, 250 µL of Ascorbic acid, 200 µL of H2O2 and 50 µL of sample. The APX enzyme level was specify by calculating the change that occurred as a result of the 4 min kinetic reading of the obtained mixture at 290 nm.

Statistical evaluations

Statistical results were obtained as a result of three replications of the samples obtained from three different tissues for analyses of all parameters examined in the study. In order to determine the statistical differences, SPSS program was used, and Duncan test analysis of the program was performed. While the "corrplot" library in the Rstudio program was used to perform correlation analyses, the "factoextra" libraries in the same program were used for PCA analysis.

Results and discussion

In this study, the effects of exogenous Put application on various biochemical and molecular parameters in Hordeum vulgare cv. Burakbey under both normal and drought stress were investigated. The effect of exogenous Put treatment on the cell cycle is shown in Fig. 2. Accordingly, when group I plants were examined, there was 21% cell density in G1, 61% in S and 18% in G2, among the stages of the cell cycle. When applied to the distilled water medium, Put showed an increasing impact on cell division by reducing cells in S and increasing cell accumulation in G2 compared to control plants. It was determined that the cell cycle was inhibited in plants grown in an arid environment (Group III) compared to plants germinated in distilled water medium. In Group III, while drought stress increased cell density by more than 50% in G1 and decreased cell density by approximately 50% in S phase. As indicated in Fig. 2, it is clearly seen that the additional treatment of Put had a affirmative impact on the cell cycle in Group IV plants. Put applied under drought stress not only abolished the inhibitory effects of this stress in G1 and S stages, but also increased cell accumulation in G2 significantly (50%).

Abiotic stresses, including drought, have inhibitory effects on the vital activities of plants, and plants grown under these conditions are noticeably smaller than plants grown in an environment without stress. One of the biggest reasons for this change in plant sizes is the decrease in cell numbers34. One of the reasons that drought stress reduces the number of cells is that the cells remain stable at the G1/S and G2/M checkpoints in the cell cycle. Another reason is that the transition of Cyclin Dependent Kinases (CDK) to M phase is slowed down due to the prolongation of the process in the S phase35,36. Cyclins, CDK and regulatory subunits control the processes involved in the cell cycle37. Complexes formed by cyclin-dependent kinases with Cyclin D control G1-S transition, while complexes formed with Cylin A and B control S-G2 and G2-M transitions38. In addition, drought stress stimulates cell cycle checkpoints with the effects of signal molecules formed as a result of accumulation of antimitogenic hormones and prevents cells from accumulating in S phase during cell cycle7. In their studies to date, researchers have shown that abiotic stresses have reduced impacts on plant growth and cell cycle in maize and Arabidopsis plants39,40. It is known that there is an increase in cell densities in the G1 and G2 stages in wheat and maize, especially in studies examining the effects of drought on cell cycle41,42. According to the results obtained from this study, it was understood that drought stress disrupted the cell cycle process in barley, and this situation was parallel to other studies in the literature. Various differences occurred in cell cycle stages in all the remaining study groups except the control group (Group I). The increase in the G2 phase in Group II plants and the S and G2 phases in Group IV plants caused by exogenous Put application is the result that exogenous Put application stimulates cell cycle, both under normal and stress conditions. As a result of the suppression of cyclin D, which provides the G1-S transition of cells in the cell cycle, the cell density in the G1 phase of the drought stressed group III plants is 27% higher than that of the group I plants. This may be due to the fact that the increase in S and G2 phases after exogenous Put application in Group IV plants may promote D-type cyclins suppressed by both drought stress and A-type cyclins. Polyamines are known to govern many processes, including promoting cell proliferation and cell cycle progression43. Parallel to these properties of polyamines, the addition of Put in barley control group samples caused an increase in S-G2 cell cycle. Despite the inhibitory effects of drought stress on the cell cycle process, exogenous Put application showed an increased effect on S and G2 transitions (Figs. 2 and 3.). The results clearly show that the application of exogenous Put together with drought stress improves the disruptive effect of stress on the cell cycle.

Polyamines help the development of various processes such as seed germination, enzyme activities, and fruit development, which are necessary for plant development. Polyamines, which take part in processes that are vital for plants, are very important in defense mechanisms against environmental stresses thanks to these properties44. It is known from previous studies that there is an increase in the internal levels of Spd and Spm in maize and wheat with the effect of drought.45–47. Studies examining the effects of exogenous Put on endogenous polyamine contents under the influence of drought stress are very rare. Ebeed et al.48, stated that drought increased the amount of endogenous Put and Spm while decreasing the amount of endogenous Spd in Triticum avestium. In the same study, the results of the addition of exogenous Put against the effects of drought on the internal level of polyamines were investigated. In this study, it was concluded that while the addition of Put under stress increased the amount of endogenous Spm and Spd, it decreased the amount of Put. According to the results obtained from this study, exogenous Put application in barley raised the amount of endogenous Spd and Spm and decreased the amount of endogenous Put and Cad. The first initiation of the synthesis of polyamines occurs with L-arginine and L-methionine. Diamine putrescine is synthesized from arginine by arginase (ADC) and from ornithine by ornithine decarboxylase (ODC). Spd and Spm are formed by the addition of aminopropyl groups to putrescine in reactions catalyzed by spermidine synthase (SPDS) and spermine synthase (SPMS), respectively22. It is not known exactly how these enzymes, which regulate the synthesis of polyamines, will affect different plant species and changing environmental conditions. This work, which was carried out for these reasons, investigated the effects of externally giving Put, which is in plant metabolism, on the amount of polyamines in the plant in both dry and normal conditions. The increase in Spd and Spm amounts due to the decrease in the amount of Put in barley due to the effect of drought shows consistency according to the steps in the synthesis mechanism of polyamines. This study proved once again that the change in endogenous PA levels in barley exposed to drought stress is important for polyamines to cope with harsh environmental conditions for plants. It has been investigated how endogenous polyamines, which play an vital role in the response mechanism against drought, show changes with the effects of exogenously applied Put, and as a result, it has been determined that exogenous Put shows healing effects under drought stress.

The effects of exogenous Put application against drought stress on total protein content are given in Fig. 5. Total protein content in Group I plants was found to be 0.39 mg/mL−1. According to the results of the study, total protein contents gradually increased in all remaining study groups. All of these increases are very significant, both statistically and numerically. Total protein contents in the application groups were 0.69, 1.31 and 2.60 mg/mL−1 in Group II, Group III and Group IV plants, respectively. The obtained results reveal that the amount of total protein was highest after exogenous Put application under drought stress. Drought stress prevents plants from taking up water, causing damage to plants at the cellular, textural, and organ levels. Therefore, plants have developed various defense mechanisms against drought stress. Some of these mechanisms are the closure of stomata by decreasing photosynthesis and respiration, the synthesis of new proteins, and the aggregation of osmolytes49. In addition to being present in many physiological processes in plants, polyamines are also involved in the regulation of defense mechanisms against environmental stresses such as drought stress50. Polyamines have been associated with tolerance to abiotic stresses because they play a role in processes such as scavenging free radicals, membrane stability, nucleic acid, protein synthesis, enzyme activity, and ethylene biosynthesis51. As a result of the studies carried out to determine the total protein content, the researchers could not reach a common conclusion. Studies have shown that drought stress reduces the total protein content in cotton and tomatoes, has no effect on garlic, and increases it in peanuts, wheat, rice, and chickpeas52–54. No study was found on the total protein amount of Put exogenously applied under drought stress. Some researchers have examined the effects of exogenous Put application against the negative effects of drought stress on total protein contents. Shallan et al.55 in cotton, Mohammadi et al.56 in thyme, and Hassan et al.57 determined that in wheat, exogenous Put they applied under drought stress showed increasing effects on total protein contents. With this study, it has been proven once again that the application of exogenous Put under drought stress increases the total protein content. It is obvious that drought stress affects the total protein contents differently, depending on the type of plant used, the parts of the plant from which protein is obtained, and the severity of drought stress. In this study, the stress response mechanism developed as a result of the increase in total protein amount under drought stress. It is thought that exogenous Put applied under drought stress may help to alleviate the effect of drought stress and ensure the continuity of life of the plant by causing an increase in the amount of protein that plays a role in the response mechanism to drought stress.

The effects of drought stress and exogenous Put treatments on MDA, SOD, CAT and APX parameters in Hordeum vulgare cv. Burakbey are given in Fig. 5. While the amount of MDA was measured at 0.12 µmol/L−1 in Group I plants, it was determined that exogenous Put application in Group II plants did not change the MDA amount so much. The MDA level in Group III plants to which drought stress was applied increased approximately 2.5 times and reached 0.41 µmol/L−1 levels. Exogenous Put application in Group IV plants, on the other hand, caused a decrease in MDA levels by approximately 75%, allowing the MDA level to decrease almost to the control group amounts. Another biochemical, SOD enzyme level, was measured at 0.37 µmol/L−1 in Group I plant samples. Both drought stress (Group III) and exogenous Put applications (Group II, IV) increased SOD levels, and the highest amount of SOD was reached in Group IV plants with 0.44 µmol/L−1. The change in the CAT enzyme level was similar to the change in the SOD enzyme level. As a result of the application of drought stress, the amount of CAT increased. Accordingly, the CAT enzyme amounts in Group I and Group II are 10.69 and 12.09 µmol min−1/mg, respectively. The results obtained from the study showed that exogenous Put application significantly increased the amount of CAT enzyme under stressful and non-stressful conditions, causing the enzyme amounts to increase up to 16 µmol min−1/mg levels. When the APX enzyme levels were examined, while the amount of APX in Group I plants was 109.26 µmol min−1/mg, the amount of APX in Group II decreased to 102.46 µmol min−1/mg after exogenous Put application. Drought stress caused the amount of APX to increase compared to the control group, as in all other biochemical parameters. That is, the application of exogenous Put under stress conditions in Group IV plants decreased the amount of APX by 60% and showed a reducing effect up to 47.38 µmol min−1/mg levels.

Under normal conditions, various ROS required for photosynthesis and respiration are produced in cell organelles such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes in plants. In this way, plants maintain the plant life cycle by maintaining homeostasis. In plants, SOD, CAT, APX and MDA are the main enzymatic antioxidants, and these enzymes are involved in response mechanisms against abiotic stresses such as drought stress58. An increase occurred in the amount of MDA, one of the end products of lipid peroxidation, which is one of the primary effects of drought stress, after the application of drought stress in tomato59, wheat60, barley61, mustard62, thyme56 and cotton55. As a result of the study, it was determined that drought stress increased the MDA level, as in previous studies. Application of exogenous Put under drought stress caused a decrease in MDA levels and ameliorated the negative effect of stress. After applying drought stress (Group III), higher SOD, CAT, and APX activities were observed in barley plants compared to control group plants (Group I). After applying drought stress, higher SOD, CAT and APX activities were observed in barley plants compared to control group plants. While the conversion of oxygen to hydrogen peroxide is carried out by the SOD enzyme, the CAT enzyme tries to protect cells from stress by directly converting the harmful H2O2 that occurs under stress conditions into H2O and O2. This situation can explain the high positive correlation between the amounts of CAT and SOD observed in our study. From this study, it was determined that drought stress increased both SOD and CAT enzyme amounts. When the studies in the literature were examined, drought stress increased the amount of SOD in mulberry, thyme, tomato, and chickpea56,63–65. It was determined from the previous studies that CAT enzyme activity under drought stress decreased in thyme, wheat, and cotton plants, while an increase occurred in barley55–57,60. In Group IV plants responded to drought stress by increasing the amount of SOD and CAT enzymes more than their own control groups (Group III). In other words, the application of exogenous Put under drought stress promoted the amount of SOD and CAT enzymes. Playing an important role in ROS scavenging mechanisms, APX takes part in reactions in the ascorbic acid cycle that detoxify hydrogen peroxide, which is produced under stressful conditions and is harmful to the plant body66. As with all other biochemical enzymes, drought stress increased the amount of APX in barley. Researchers have stated that drought stress increases the amount of APX in various plants such as corn67, wheat68, and cypress69, as in our study. In addition, Put applied exogenously under drought stress decreased the amount of APX compared to stress control and played a very important role in the response to drought stress. While drought stress is known to cause an increase in MDA, CAT, SOD, and APX levels, researchers have tried to alleviate the negative effects of stress by using bacteria, chemicals, and various growth regulators. However, studies showing the effects of exogenous Put application against drought stress on these enzyme parameters are very limited. Accordingly, exogenous Put application under drought stress showed decreasing effects on MDA levels, while increasing the amount of SOD, CAT and APX in corn, wheat, barley and thyme55–57,60. As a result of this study, it was determined that Put applied in a stress-free environment increased all parameters except APX (Group II). Exogenous Put application under drought stress, on the other hand, showed mitigating effects of the negative effects of drought stress, especially by reducing the amounts of MDA and APX and causing changes in other parameters. In conclusion, in this study, the effects of exogenous Put application on Hordeum vulgare cv. Burakbey under drought stress on total protein content, MDA, SOD, CAT and APX were given for the first time. Obtained results show that according to the plant type to be used, the application method, and the severity of drought stress, exogenous application in an appropriate dose amount has curative effects against the negative effects of drought stress.

Correlation changes in all investigated parameters of exogenous Put application under drought stress are indicated in Figs. 6 and 7. On the figure, parameters with positive correlation are marked in dark blue, parameters with negative correlation are marked in red, and the color intensities in the figure are adjusted proportionally to the correlation intensity. After applying exogenous Put against drought stress, the data value of the primary components (Dim1) was 40.4%, while the secondary data variability value (Dim2) was 28.9% (Fig. 7). The results of the G1 phase correlation analysis of the cell cycle showed a very strong positive correlation with the S phase, endogenous Spd amount, CAT and APX enzyme levels, and a very strong negative correlation with the G2 phase and total protein content. The S phase of the cell cycle showed a very strong positive correlation with the amount of endogenous Put, in addition to showing similar results to the parameters in the G1 phase. As a result of the study, it was revealed that exogenous Put application under drought stress showed very strong positive or negative correlation in most of the parameters examined. These results show us to what extent the investigated parameters are affected by the interactions between each other. As a result, exogenous Put application showed positive effects against the negative effects of drought stress and these effects were once again supported by the results of the correlation analysis. As can be seen from the analysis results, exogenous Put application in the presence of drought stress created a plant stress resistance mechanism by affecting the physiological and molecular parameters examined. In light of all the data obtained, it has been clearly demonstrated that exogenous Put application affects not only the polyamine contents in the plant but also the changes in cell cycle, biochemical enzyme activities, and total protein amounts.

Conclusions

The inhibitory effects of adverse environmental conditions on plant growth and the increasing speed and intensity of global climate change have become even more frightening in recent years. Drought, the most common of these environmental stresses, damages arable land in the world, and as a result, it is inevitable to experience a shortage of agricultural products necessary for the increasing world population. In order to supply the nutritional needs of the growing world population, one way to overcome this problem is to make food resources more resistant to drought. For this purpose, by using barley seeds, which is an economically important model plant, the molecular and biochemical interactions of Put application in both normal and drought conditions are comparatively explained. The large changes in endogenous polyamine amounts under drought stress are evidence of the importance of polyamines in the stress resistance mechanism. Application of exogenous Put, one of the polyamines that is so successful in resistance to stress, under drought stress is important in revealing the response of barley and other plants to stress. As a result of the study, it is explained to what extent exogenous Put application affects total protein content, endogenous polyamine content, cell cycle, and biochemical enzyme levels against drought stress, which has an almost lethal effect for plant growth and development. Finally, it was concluded that the external addition of Put, which is an important member of polyamines, greatly reduces the negative effects of drought stress on the plant and will form the basis for future studies to combat drought stress.

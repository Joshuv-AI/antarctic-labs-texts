Dall-E in hand surgery: Exploring the utility of ChatGPT image generation: Correspondence
Authors: Hinpetch Daungsupawong, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

The publication on “Dall-E in hand surgery: Exploring the utility of ChatGPT image generation [1]” is hereby discussed. The study on the potential of AI tools such as ChatGPT and DALL·E in hand surgery makes intriguing observations about employing LLM (Large Language Models) technology to boost education for patients and medical workers. However, the study approach has some shortcomings that may jeopardize its dependability and widespread use. For example, utilizing Google Trends to select search phrases may not accurately reflect the clinical context's real queries or difficulties, or the individual needs of each patient population.

Furthermore, while common tools such as PEMAT, DISCERN, and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level are widely used, they do not reflect the specifics of assessor scoring, if there is consistency, or whether specialists are involved in the assessment. Furthermore, providing the average score without confidence intervals or rigorous assessment of differences (e.g., t-test or ANOVA) may result in a statistically insignificant comparison between models.

Another observation is the display of results from GPT-4o and DALL-E, which claim that GPT-4o generates images more accurately, but do not detail the criteria used to evaluate the images, the clarity of the images to patients, or the scoring by real users, such as patients or doctors, if qualitative measures are also provided. This will provide another dimension to the evaluation of the value of visuals in learning.

The following question is, “Should these AIs be used as a supplement or replacement for the role of physicians in providing education?” or “Can the assessment tools truly reflect the quality of understanding of real-life patients?”. Reinterpreted, the study may show that, despite AI's potential, a thorough assessment system is required that is both quantitative and qualitative, with a higher emphasis on real user participation, to ensure that AI becomes a true assist rather than a statistically sound solution.

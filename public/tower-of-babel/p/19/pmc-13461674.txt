Predication of ICU Length of Stay and External Validation in a Tertiary Level ICU Using Artificial Intelligence (AI) Prediction Model
Authors: Sohini Roy, Pradeep Rangappa, Karthik Rao, Sabyasachi Sensarma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe length of stay (LOS) of a patient in (ICU) is a process measure of ICU efficiency that is commonly used as an indicator of ICU performance.1 While premature early release from the ICU might result in medical issues and an increased risk of readmission to the ICU or even greater mortality, longer stays in the ICU are linked to higher care costs and resource utilization.2 Accurate prediction of intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay (LOS) is crucial for resource planning. Numerous studies have focused on the creation of ML models for predicting LOS in the ICU. ML research in healthcare, however, has many challenges like heterogeneity of clinical situations, patient demographics etc.3ObjectiveTo develop and validate an AI-based predictive model for ICU LOS.Materials and methodsPublicly accessible MIMIC-IV version 2.2 database with ICU subset was used. We developed three Random Forest models using ML to address varying data availability scenarios: a comprehensive model with 183 features (V1) representing well-resourced tertiary ICUs, a simplified model with 13 features (V2) representing resource-limited settings, and an enhanced model with 16 features (V3). The simplified V2 model used only routinely available parameters collected in the first 24 hours: demographics (age, sex), vital signs (heart rate, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, temperature, SpO2), laboratory tests (glucose, creatinine, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, urea), and comorbidities. The V3 model added 3 features to V2: number of procedures performed, lactate levels, and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS). Models were externally validated on 34 patients from an Indian tertiary care ICU.ResultsOn MIMIC-IV internal testing, V1 achieved RMSE 2.81 days (R² 0.44), V2 achieved RMSE 3.28 days (R² 0.24), and V3 achieved RMSE 3.38 days (R² 0.19). However, external validation revealed a striking reversal: V2 (13 features) significantly outperformed V3 (16 features), achieving RMSE 5.80 days with 8.8% accuracy within ±1 day, while V3 deteriorated to RMSE 6.86 days (18% worse) with 0% accuracy within ±1 day. Model performance varied substantially across LOS categories. For medium-stay patients (4-7 days, n = 12), both models showed better accuracy (MAE 4.7-4.8 days). However, for short-stay patients (1-3 days, n = 15), V3 severely overpredicted by 7.9 days, while for long-stay patients (>7 days, n = 7), V3 underpredicted by 3.8 days, converging predictions around 10 days regardless of actual acuity. V3 exhibited severe systematic bias (+4.36 days overprediction).ConclusionsOur study showed simplified prediction models using only 13 routine clinical parameters perform better than complex models in resource-limited settings. The V2 model, requiring only basic features outperformed the 16-feature model by 18% (RMSE: 5.80 vs 6.86 days) in external validation. We recommend the 13-feature V2 model for deployment in resource-constrained ICUs. This has scope for implementing AI driven predictive analytics in primary and secondary-level ICUs in resource limited settings.Keywords: ICU length of stay(LOS), AI, ML, Predictive analysis

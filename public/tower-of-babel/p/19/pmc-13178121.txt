Dear Editor,

We thank the reader for their insightful comments regarding our study published in the Indian Journal of Critical Care Medicine (IJCCM). Their proposal to transition from simple tele-ICU “alerts” to a robust framework of “assurance” is timely and aligns with our vision of remote monitoring as an essential, hospital-wide safety layer.

We strongly agree that standardizing a minimum viable safety dataset (MVSD) is a logical next step to ensure that tele-ICU surveillance is consistently actionable and auditable across different resource settings. Our multicenter study utilized a standardized protocol where automated alerts were reviewed in real time by tele-ICU intensivists. This adjudication process achieved a sensitivity of 79.2% and a specificity of 80.1%, which we believe provides the “decision-grade” foundation the reader describes.1

Regarding the specific points raised by the reader:

Workflow and human factors: We concur that human-factors outcomes are critical and should be treated as primary results. In our cohort, the median time from alert generation to bedside intervention was 12 minutes, with 89.4% of interventions occurring within 30 minutes.2

Time-to-first-beneficial-action: This is indeed a globally meaningful metric that directly tests patient-centered outcomes. Our multivariable analysis showed that early intervention (≤15 minutes) was independently associated with a lower risk of in-hospital mortality (aOR: 0.65; 95% CI: 0.52–0.81, p < 0.001).1

While we acknowledge the limitations of our observational design, the observed 47% reduction in in-hospital cardiac arrest (IHCA) incidence and a risk-adjusted mortality ratio of 0.69 suggest that current tele-ICU systems are already delivering tangible safety benefits in middle-income healthcare environments.

We embrace the reader's suggestion to incorporate these rigorous reporting spines into future evaluations to accelerate the responsible scale-up of tele-ICU technology.

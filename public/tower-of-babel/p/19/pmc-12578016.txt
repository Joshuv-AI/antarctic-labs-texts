Leveraging machine learning to detect subclinical seizures in Dravet syndrome: a step toward early diagnosis and SUDEP prevention
Authors: Syeda Tamkeen Zahra, Aliha Iftikhar, Muhammad Talha, Laiba Shamim, Sakan Binte Imran
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

Dravet syndrome (DS) is an uncommon, premature transitional epileptic encephalopathy with an approximate incidence of 1 in 22 000 to 1 in 45 000 and a high mortality rate, primarily due to prolonged seizures and Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy (SUDEP). Dravet Syndrome is characterized by refractory seizures and massive neurodevelopmental decline and several complications like cognitive impairment, behavioral issues with autism spectrum features, abnormal gait, muscle flaccidity, sleep disturbance, sudden death, brady/tachycardia, autonomic dysfunction, repeated infections, and growth retardation. Due to its intractable clinical course and raised risk of fatal complications, in advance documentation is crucial to take the first step in initial appropriate management and reduce the risk of long-term morbidity and mortality[1]. This article is in line with the TITAN Guidelines on the need for transparency in AI use in healthcare[2].

Subclinical seizures in DS frequently make a gateway from clinical detection, accompanying silently to cognitive impairment and increased mortality risk. Latest advances in machine learning (ML) have opened new pathways for analyzing EEG recordings with greater accuracy and automation. By identifying subtle, preictal EEG patterns that precede undisguised seizures, ML algorithms extend the prospects to detect hidden seizure activity and forecast episodes before clinical onset[3]. Modern research has illustrated the application of ML models in accurately recognizing epileptic seizures from EEG data, achieving a remarkable 98.4% accuracy. This highlights the importance of Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven diagnostics to ease the burden on neurologists and enable timely, effective intervention[4].

Advanced ML techniques help uncover disease mechanisms like seizures by utilizing large, complex datasets from genetic, imaging, and clinical sources. Greener et al recently provided a valuable guide for biologists, featuring visual explanations of core ML concepts and outlining suitable methods for analyzing different types of biological data[5]. This approach, though currently limited, can be expanded to aid diagnosis and treatment monitoring of rare seizures like DS using EEG. More comprehensive data and continually improving algorithms are needed to evaluate both short- and long-term benefits, as well as potential complications and limitations.

Despite the availability of large medical datasets and suitable learning algorithms for years, and hundreds of papers applying ML to healthcare data, only a small number have had a significant impact on clinical practice[6]. It can be a potent tool for solving problems in healthcare. Researchers need to familiarize themselves with new machine learning models to provide new solutions to the existing problems in this area by using reinforcement learning techniques. Machine learning in healthcare faces challenges such as obtaining large, high-quality datasets and applying methods that are necessary, efficient, while protecting patient privacy. Further research should improve data quality, dimensionality reduction, and privacy safeguards[7].

In conclusion, recent machine learning models applied to EEG analysis show great potential for identifying subclinical seizures in DS, supporting earlier treatment and better patient outcomes. Nonetheless, achieving reliable accuracy across varied populations is a significant hurdle. Combining these ML techniques with neurotechnology for real-time monitoring could transform management. Ongoing research and interdisciplinary collaboration are vital to enhance these methods and overcome current challenges to ensure real clinical benefits to those with DS.

Association of interleukin-6 (IL-6) with suicide attempt
Authors: Iqra Rasheed Shah, Sabah Manzoor, Arshad Hussain, Sabhiya Majid, Khawar Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Suicide is a major cause of death among young adults, but few biological markers have been linked to suicidality. Interleukin-6 (IL-6) may play a role in elevating the risk of depression and suicidal tendencies. However, limited research has explored the connections between IL-6 and suicidal thoughts or actions.Aim:This study was conducted to investigate the association between IL-6 levels and suicide attempts.Materials and Methods:In this observational study, 31 patients undergoing clinical follow-up after a suicide attempt were enrolled. The Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) was used for assessment of suicidal behavior. IL-6 levels in patients were measured using the ELISA technique with the IL-6 Human ELISA Kit (Invitrogen, Cat. No-BMS213-2) following the manufacturer’s protocols.Results:The mean level of IL-6 in the patients was 2.75 ± 6.50 (range: 0–36.6) pg/mL. Women exhibited higher IL-6 levels than men (women = 4.04 ± 8.97; men = 1.54 ± 1.86) pg/mL. IL-6 levels significantly increased with higher severity levels of suicidal ideation, with a P value of 0.0001, indicating a positive association. A significant positive correlation was observed between IL-6 levels and the intensity of suicidal ideation, with higher intensity associated with elevated IL-6 levels (P value = 0.0113). A significant positive correlation was observed between IL-6 levels and the lethality of suicidal ideation, with higher levels of lethality associated with elevated IL-6 levels (P value = 0.0129).Conclusion:This study provides evidence that elevated IL-6 levels are associated with suicide attempts, supporting its potential role as a biomarker for suicidal behavior.

Suicide remains a critical global public health concern, particularly among adolescents and young adults. According to the World Health Organization, over 700,000 people died by suicide in 2021, making it the second leading cause of death among individuals aged 15–29 years.[12] For every completed suicide, around 20 attempts are made, though fatality rates vary by country, with 8.5%–13% of attempts resulting in death.[3] In India, a total of 170,924 deaths by suicide were recorded in 2022, the highest since 1967, with the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir reporting the highest number of attempted suicide cases relative to its population.[4]

Although various clinical, psychosocial, and sociodemographic risk factors have been identified, their predictive value is low and many are non-modifiable.[5] Current psychosocial indicators are inadequate for reliably identifying high-risk individuals, highlighting the need for objective, modifiable markers, such as biomarkers.[6] We need better insight into the neurobiological processes to offer a novel understanding of the pathophysiology of suicide and identify new treatment targets and approaches.[7] One such promising area of investigation involves inflammatory biomarkers, particularly pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). These molecules are known to influence neuroinflammatory pathways that may underlie psychiatric disorders and suicidal behavior. Several studies, including meta-analyses and post-mortem investigations, have reported elevated IL-6 levels in individuals with depression, suicidal ideation, and suicide attempts.[8] These findings suggest that cytokine dysregulation and neuroinflammation may contribute to psychiatric conditions associated with suicide.[9] For instance, IL-6 has been implicated in psychiatric disorders such as anxiety, depression, and suicidal behavior, with studies reporting varying levels of IL-6 in plasma, serum, and cerebrospinal fluid among affected individuals.[10] Systemic IL-6 levels are strongly associated with IL-6 activity within the brain, particularly in conditions of acute brain injury or inflammation. Systemic IL-6 can contribute to brain inflammation and affect neuronal function, while brain IL-6 also plays a role in brain development, survival, and injury response.[11] Elevated systemic IL-6 levels may be associated with poorer cognitive performance, potentially due to the influence of IL-6 on the blood-brain barrier, according to Frontiers.[12] Elevated IL-6 levels have also been observed in children with suicidal tendencies, correlating with ongoing suicidal behavior.[13] However, discrepancies in findings indicate that the role of IL-6 in suicidal behavior is not fully understood. In this sense, this study was conducted to investigate the association between IL-6 levels and suicide attempts.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This study was conducted at the postgraduate Department of Psychiatry, Institute of Mental Health and Neurosciences (IMHANS), GMC Srinagar, between 2022 and 2023. In this observational study, 31 patients undergoing clinical follow-up after a suicide attempt were enrolled. Exclusion criteria were designed to eliminate conditions that may independently alter cytokine levels or complicate assessment of suicidality.

All participants were briefed about the study’s objectives and procedures. Informed consent was obtained from all participants. At admission after a suicide attempt, their samples were sent along with other baseline investigations. Efforts were made to maintain confidentiality and provide a supportive environment to address the potential psychological impact of recalling traumatic experiences. A suicide attempt was defined as a nonfatal, self-injurious act undertaken with the intent to die.[14]

Participants underwent evaluation by a trained psychiatrist using the Structured Clinical Interview based on DSM-5 criteria.[15] Sociodemographic information, including age, gender, marital status, occupation, education, and substance use, was gathered through interviews. Suicidal ideation and behaviors were assessed using the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS),[16] which comprises four subscales: (a) severity, (b) intensity, (c) behavior, and (d) lethality.[17] The C-SSRS was administered in English or translated into the local language (Kashmiri/Urdu) by the evaluating psychiatrist in real time, ensuring comprehension for non-English-speaking participants while maintaining the scale’s content and clinical validity. IL-6 levels were determined using the ELISA method with the IL-6 Human ELISA Kit (Invitrogen, Cat. No. BMS213-2) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean and standard deviation, while categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Pearson’s Chi-square test was used for inferential analysis, with two-sided P values reported; a P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data analysis was performed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS, Version 20.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois, USA).

After performing linear regression analysis to control for the potential confounding effect of gender, IL-6 levels were found to be significantly associated with both the severity and intensity of suicidal ideation. Specifically, higher IL-6 levels predicted higher severity scores (B = 0.072, P = 0.025) and higher intensity scores (B = 0.431, P = 0.022), indicating a meaningful positive relationship. In both models, gender was not a significant predictor, suggesting that the observed associations between IL-6 and suicidal ideation subscales were independent of gender. However, in the model assessing lethality, IL-6 did not show a statistically significant association (B = 0.037, P = 0.129), and gender also remained non-significant. These results indicate that while IL-6 is a significant biomarker associated with the severity and intensity of suicidal ideation, it does not appear to predict lethality in this sample [Table 3].

DISCUSSION

Our study demonstrates that individuals who attempted suicide had elevated serum IL-6 levels, supporting the hypothesis that systemic inflammation may contribute to the pathophysiology of suicidal behavior. The mean IL-6 level among suicide attempters in our sample was 2.75 ± 6.50 pg/mL. This finding is consistent with previous studies that reported increased IL-6 levels in individuals with suicidal ideation or attempts, as well as post-mortem studies in suicide victims.[101318]

In our study, female patients exhibited higher IL-6 levels than male patients. This aligns with literature suggesting that women exhibit stronger IL-6 responses to acute psychological stress, potentially due to hormonal or immune system differences.[1920] This finding also reflects the broader trend that women are more prone to nonfatal self-harm and suicidal ideation, indicating that gender-based biological differences may play a role in inflammatory responses associated with suicidality. Demographic analysis revealed that most attempters were unemployed (87.10%) and unmarried (58.06%), corroborating existing evidence that social isolation and economic stressors are significant psychosocial risk factors for suicidal behavior.

In this study, we assessed the severity, intensity, and lethality of suicide attempts using the C-SSRS to evaluate the association of C-SSRS subscales with IL-6 Levels. Importantly, our study found a positive correlation between IL-6 levels and the severity, intensity, and lethality subscales of the C-SSRS. Participants with higher IL-6 levels scored significantly higher on all three dimensions. This association supports the idea that IL-6 may serve as a biomarker reflecting the neuroinflammatory burden in individuals experiencing more severe suicidal ideation or attempts. These findings are in line with those of Aguglia et al.,[3] who also reported significant associations between IL-6 levels and suicidal severity indices. High severity, intensity, and lethality in suicide attempts are considered a strong predictor of future completed suicide, making it crucial to investigate its neurobiological underpinnings to better understand the pathophysiology of suicide.[21] Identifying subscales of the C-SSR scale in suicide attempts is vital for early detection and timely referral of patients to specialized care.[22]

Biological mechanisms of IL-6 in suicidal behavior

IL-6 plays a pivotal role in immune response and neuroinflammation and has been implicated in various psychiatric disorders, including depression, anxiety, and suicidal behavior. Several mechanisms have been proposed: IL-6 can cross the blood-brain barrier and affect brain regions implicated in mood regulation, such as the prefrontal cortex and limbic system. IL-6 is known to activate the HPA axis, leading to increased cortisol production; thus, prolonged HPA activation and elevated cortisol levels are associated with depression and suicidal ideation. IL-6 modulates neurotransmitters such as serotonin, dopamine, and glutamate, which play critical roles in mood and behavior, resulting in altered neurotransmitter levels that may contribute to impulsivity, hopelessness, and other psychological states linked to suicidal behavior.

Clinical implications

The findings of our study preliminary suggest IL-6’s potential utility as a biomarker for identifying individuals at risk for suicide attempts and screening for IL-6 levels in clinical settings may help stratify high-risk individuals and enable early intervention. Our findings demonstrate that therapeutic interventions (anti-inflammatory therapies) targeting cytokines, including IL-6, could be explored as adjunctive treatments for patients with suicidal behavior, and agents such as IL-6 inhibitors or lifestyle interventions that reduce systemic inflammation (e.g., exercise and dietary modifications) may hold promise.

Limitations

The first limitation of our study was the small sample size, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Larger, multicenter studies are required to validate the results. Second, the study design precludes establishing causation. Longitudinal studies are required to examine how IL-6 levels fluctuate over time and their direct impact on suicidal behavior. The study excluded patients with certain psychiatric and medical conditions to minimize confounding, but other factors, such as stress and socioeconomic variables, might still influence IL-6 levels. Future research should examine other pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNFα and IL-1β) to better understand the cytokine network and its role in suicidal behavior.

Strengths

Suicide is a significant global public health concern, and the study addresses the critical need for biomarkers to improve risk assessment and intervention strategies. In our study, investigating IL-6 levels provides an objective biological marker to complement existing psychosocial and demographic risk factors for suicide attempts and clear criteria for patient selection minimized confounding factors, such as comorbid conditions (e.g., schizophrenia, concurrent infections, chronic illnesses, or medications), which could influence cytokine levels. The inclusion of multiple C-SSRS subscales allowed for a detailed analysis of the association between IL-6 levels and different dimensions of suicidality.

CONCLUSION

This study highlights a significant association between elevated IL-6 levels and the severity and intensity of suicidal ideation among individuals who recently attempted suicide. While IL-6 levels did not significantly predict the lethality of suicide attempts, the findings suggest that IL-6 may serve as a promising objective biomarker for identifying individuals at heightened risk of intense and severe suicidal thoughts, independent of gender. These results support the growing evidence linking neuroinflammatory processes to suicidal behavior and underscore the potential of targeting inflammatory pathways in the assessment and management of suicidality. Further research with larger, longitudinal cohorts is needed to confirm these associations and explore the clinical utility of IL-6 in suicide risk stratification and intervention.

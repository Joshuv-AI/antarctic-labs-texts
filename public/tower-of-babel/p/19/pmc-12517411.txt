First-line treatment with polatuzumab vedotin improves efficacy and survival in newly diagnosed patients with advanced diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a multicentre retrospective analysis
Authors: Si-Jun Bai, Xu-Liang Shen, Wei-Wei Tian, Zhao-Yu Liu, Jun-Yuan Lang, Yi-Nan Gao, Cai-Xia Zhang, Ya-Ru Wang, Ye Geng, Yin-Xue Shi, Qi Liu, Jian-Xia He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPolatuzumab vedotin (Pola) is an anti-CD79b monoclonal antibody–drug conjugate that has shown remarkable efficacy and safety in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) patients. Based on the results of the phase III POLARIX trial, it has been approved for first-line treatment of DLBCL. However, real-world studies are limited, particularly those assessing the efficacy differences between Pola-based regimens and traditional chemotherapy.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective study analyzed clinical data from 64 newly diagnosed patients with advanced-stage, high-risk, or bulky DLBCL. Patients were categorized into the Pola plus chemotherapy (Pola) and traditional chemotherapy groups based on induction therapy.ResultsAfter excluding 6 patients who received fewer than four cycles of chemotherapy, 58 patients remained for efficacy analysis. The objective response rate after four cycles was 100% in the Pola group compared with 74.1% in the traditional chemotherapy group (p = 0.009). After a median follow-up of 13.5 months (range, 11.0–16.0 months), the median overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and duration of response (DOR) in the Pola group were significantly longer than those in the traditional chemotherapy group (all p < 0.05). Among patients with advanced disease, Pola plus chemotherapy was superior to traditional chemotherapy in terms of OS, PFS, and DOR (all p < 0.05). For patients aged >60 years with advanced disease, the Pola group had superior OS, PFS, and DOR (all p < 0.05). Further subgroup survival analysis of patients receiving Pola-based therapy revealed that although the combination considerably improved PFS and DOR in advanced-stage patients, it did not overcome the poor prognosis associated with high P53 expression.ConclusionPola plus chemotherapy demonstrates superior efficacy and may prolong survival in patients with newly diagnosed advanced or high-risk DLBCL. However, it does not mitigate the unfavorable prognosis in patients with high P53 expression.Keywords: Polatuzumab Vedotin, first-line treatment, advanced diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, efficacy, survival, P53 expression

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a highly heterogeneous and aggressive form of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma [1,2]. The R-CHOP regimen, which combines rituximab with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone, has significantly improved outcomes in patients with DLBCL, leading to long-term survival rates of 50%–60% [3–5]. However, disease progression still occurs in one-third of patients [6,7].

For high-risk patients with DLBCL, the 5-year overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) rates following R-CHOP treatment are only 45.7%–58.7% and 41.1%–55.6%, respectively, considerably lower than the 85.5%–87.7% OS and 79.9%–83.9% PFS rates observed in low-risk patients [8–10]. A 13-year multicentre retrospective study in China that examined the impact of various clinical characteristics on the survival of patients with DLBCL during the rituximab era revealed that the 5-year OS and PFS rates for patients with Ann Arbor stage III/IV were 51% and 37.3%, respectively. These rates were significantly lower than those for Ann Arbor stage I/II patients (83% and 77.4%, respectively) [11]. In addition to the patients with advanced disease and high risk, the study [12] confirmed that stage I/II patients with bulky disease had a worse prognosis than those with non-bulky disease, showing a 5-year PFS of 44% compared to 90%. Song et al. [13] investigated the efficacy of R-CHOP in treating primary extranodal DLBCL and found that patients with extranodal tumors measuring ≥7.5 cm in maximum diameter had significantly lower PFS and OS rates than those with tumors <7.5 cm (PFS, 56.4% vs. 86.0%; OS, 64.1% vs. 87.7%). Therefore, the efficacy of R-CHOP in the treatment of advanced-stage or high-risk DLBCL or DLBCL with bulky disease needs to be improved. Intensive treatment with R-CHOP-like regimens and clinical trials combining new drugs based on R-CHOP are continuously conducted to improve the survival of patients with high-risk clinical features [14–17].

CD79b, a B-cell surface antigen, is expressed in almost all B-cell lymphomas [18,19]. Polatuzumab vedotin is an antibody–drug conjugate consisting of a conjugated anti-CD79b monoclonal antibody [20]. A pivotal study [21] evaluated the efficacy and safety of Pola-BR in treating patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) DLBCL. This combination regimen significantly improved the response rate and prolonged the survival of patients. Furthermore, a 5-year endpoint analysis [22] demonstrated that the Pola-BR regimen could provide long-lasting benefits to patients who responded to treatment while maintaining a manageable safety profile. As a result, the 2022 National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines included the Pola-BR regimen as a level II recommendation for the treatment of R/R DLBCL. The results of the phase 3 POLARIX clinical trial [23] further established the status of Pola-R-CHP in primary high-risk DLBCL. Consequently, the FDA and the 2023 NCCN guidelines included the Pola-R-CHP regimen for first-line treatment of newly diagnosed DLBCL. However, there are few real-world studies of this treatment option. To investigate the real efficacy and safety of the Pola combination regimen in treating patients with DLBCL exhibiting high-risk clinical features, we retrospectively analyzed the clinical characteristics of patients with newly diagnosed advanced-stage or high-risk DLBCL or patients who had DLBCL with bulky disease and further explored the efficacy and safety of the Pola combination regimen.

Materials and methods

Patients

This multicentre retrospective cohort study analysed the clinical data of 64 patients newly diagnosed with advanced, high-risk, or bulky DLBCL who were treated at the haematology departments of five hospitals in Shanxi Province between January 1, 2022, and November 1, 2024. The five hospitals were Shanxi Provincial People’s Hospital, Heping Hospital affiliated to Changzhi Medical College, Shanxi Bethune Hospital, Second Hospital of Shanxi Medical University and Jincheng People’s Hospital. Among them, 35 received polatuzumab vedotin (Pola)-based combination therapy, while 29 received the traditional R-CHOP chemotherapy regimen. All patients met the diagnostic criteria of the 2020 WHO 5th edition classification of haematolymphoid tumours [24].

Inclusion Criteria:

Histologically confirmed DLBCL;

Disease stage III–IV, International Prognostic Index (IPI) score ≥4, Ki-67 ≥ 90%, or presence of bulky disease (maximum lesion diameter ≥5 cm);

Disease stage III–IV, International Prognostic Index (IPI) score ≥4, Ki-67 ≥ 90%, or presence of bulky disease (maximum lesion diameter ≥5 cm);

Absence of grade 2 or higher peripheral neuropathy;

Absence of grade 2 or higher peripheral neuropathy;

Receipt of at least two cycles of chemotherapy;

Receipt of at least two cycles of chemotherapy;

Completion of at least one response evaluation.

Exclusion Criteria:

Primary central nervous system lymphoma

Primary central nervous system lymphoma

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanxi Provincial People’s Hospital (2024-kY-01) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective study design.

Treatment regimens

Of the 64 DLBCL patients, 35 received Pola-based combination regimens. Among them, 32 were treated with Pola-R-CHP, which comprised Pola at 1.8 mg/kg on day 0, rituximab at 375 mg/m2 IV on day 0, cyclophosphamide at 750 mg/m2 IV on day 1, doxorubicin at 50 mg/m2 IV on day 1, and prednisone at 100 mg PO on days 1–5. One patient with CD20-negative only received only Pola-CHP. One older patient received a dose-adjusted Pola-R-CHP with a one-third reduction in the CHP dose. Additionally, one patient with cardiac involvement was treated with Pola-R-CEP (where etoposide replaced doxorubicin) due to reduced cardiotoxicity, consisting of Pola at 1.8 mg/kg on day 0, rituximab at 375 mg/m2 IV on day 0, cyclophosphamide at 750 mg/m2 IV on day 1, etoposide at 100 mg/m2 IV on days 1–3, and prednisone at 100 mg PO on days 1–5. Twenty-one patients received the traditional R-CHOP regimen, which included rituximab at 375 mg/m2 IV on day 0, cyclophosphamide at 750 mg/m2 IV on day 1, doxorubicin at 50 mg/m2 IV on day 1, vincristine at 1.4 mg/m2 IV on day 1, and prednisone at 100 mg PO on days 1–5. Eight older patients received a dose-adjusted R-CHOP regimen with a one-third reduction in the CHOP dose. All patients or their families signed informed consent before treatment.

Endpoints and evaluation

The follow-up cutoff date was February 1, 2025. According to the 2014 Lugano classification [25], PET-CT and/or CT or bone marrow evaluations were performed every two cycles to assess treatment response, which was categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease, or progressive disease. The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved CR or PR. The primary endpoint was the last follow-up date or death. OS was defined as the time from diagnosis until endpoint. PFS was the time from diagnosis to disease progression, relapse, death from any cause, or last follow-up. The duration of response (DOR) was calculated as the period from the first documented ORR to disease progression or death. Adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs were graded using the NCI CTCAE v5.0 (https://ctep.cancer.gov/protocoldevelopment/electronic_applications/ctc.htm).

Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). Categorical data were described using counts (N) and percentages (%), with group comparisons conducted using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. The Shapiro–Wilk test was employed to assess the normality of continuous variables. Normally distributed data are expressed as mean ± SD and were compared using an independent-sample t-test. Non-normally distributed data are presented as median (interquartile range) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. The Kaplan–Meier method was used for OS, PFS, and DOR analyses, with the log-rank test used for comparisons. A p-value of ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant. The chi-square test was used to identify potential confounding factors between treatment groups, and multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to control for potential confounder bias due to group imbalance. The subgroup analysis, including subgroups of age, gender, cell of origin, MYC/BCL2 double expression, ECOG score, number of extranodal involved sites, B symptoms, IPI score, ALB status, LDH, Ki67 ratio, expression of CD5, P53 ratio, Ann Arbor stage, maximum diameter of lesion, and G-CSF usage, were applied for the CR (pola and chemotherapy vs. traditional chemotherapy) and their 95% CI were calculated. The interaction P values for the subgroups were calculated.

Results

Baseline and treatment characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the 64 patients are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 65 years (range, 33–86 years). Cell of origin was determined using the Hans algorithm based on immunohistochemistry. One patient had an undetermined pathological subtype. Among the 63 evaluable patients, 20.3% (13/63) were double expressers. One patient had MYC and BCL6 rearrangements, three had BCL6 rearrangements, and one had a BCL2 rearrangement. Central nervous system involvement was observed in one patient, who also exhibited abnormal B-cell immunophenotyping in cerebrospinal fluid. Among all patients, 54.7% (35/64) received Pola-based regimens, with a median of four treatment cycles (range, 2–6). One patient underwent salvage treatment and autologous stem cell transplantation after disease recurrence. Seven patients received maintenance therapy: two with lenalidomide, two with rituximab, one with R2 (rituximab + lenalidomide), one with zanubrutinib, and one with chidamide. When comparing baseline data between the Pola plus chemotherapy (Pola) and traditional chemotherapy groups, statistically significant differences were noted only in sex, CD5 positivity, albumin, and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) use: more female patients (54.3%, 19/35), fewer CD5-positive patients (24.2%, 8/33), more patients with hypoalbuminemia (58.8%, 20/34), and more frequent G-CSF use (74.3%, 26/35) were observed in the Pola group.

Response

Based on induction therapy, patients were categorized into the Pola group (35 patients, 54.7%) and the traditional chemotherapy group (29 patients, 45.3%). After excluding 6 patients who received fewer than four cycles, 58 patients remained for analysis. Among these, the ORR after four cycles was 100% (31/31) in the Pola group and 74.1% (20/27) in the traditional group (p = 0.009). The CR rates were 71.0% and 48.1%, respectively (p = 0.076) (Table 2). In subgroup analyses, the CR rates of the Pola group and the traditional chemotherapy group were calculated for each subgroup. The results showed no significant differences in CR rates between the two induction chemotherapy groups in any subgroup (Figure 1).

Survival analysis

The median follow-up was 13.5 months (range, 11.0–16.0). At the end of follow-up, the median OS, PFS, and DOR in the Pola group were significantly longer than those in the traditional chemotherapy group (p = 0.036, p = 0.023, and p = 0.030, respectively) (Figure 2; Tables S1–S3). Furthermore, variables with p < 0.1 in the between-group difference analysis were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The results showed that maximum lesion diameter ≥7.5 cm and Pola-combination chemotherapy were independent prognostic factors for OS and PFS, while sex, maximum lesion diameter ≥7.5 cm, and Pola combination chemotherapy were independent prognostic factors for DOR (Table 3).

Safety

The incidence of AEs in the Pola group and the traditional chemotherapy group was 85.7% (30/35) and 65.5% (19/29), respectively. Hematologic toxicity remained the most common adverse reaction in both groups. Statistical analysis showed that the incidence of neutropenia and nonhematologic toxicity was higher in the Pola group than in the traditional chemotherapy group. However, there was no significant difference in the incidence of grade ≥3 AEs between the two groups (Table 4).

Discussion

Improving the survival of patients with DLBCL who exhibit high-risk clinical factors has been a longstanding objective for haematologists. The phase 3 POLARIX clinical trial [23] compared the efficacy and safety of Pola-R-CHP with the standard R-CHOP regimen in previously untreated patients with intermediate- to high-risk DLBCL. The trial demonstrated that with comparable safety, the Pola group had a lower risk of disease progression, relapse, or death. Our multicenter retrospective real-world study selected newly diagnosed DLBCL patients with high-risk clinical characteristics, such as advanced Ann Arbor stage, high IPI scores, and bulky disease, and compared the clinical features and treatment outcomes between Pola plus chemotherapy and traditional chemotherapy. Although the CR rate after four cycles did not differ significantly between the two groups, the ORR after four cycles was notably higher in the Pola group.

In our study, there were no statistically significant differences in AEs or serious AEs between the Pola group and the traditional chemotherapy group. However, the incidence of neutropenia and nonhematologic toxicity was higher in the Pola group, which may not be entirely consistent with the comparable safety outcomes reported in the POLARIX trial [23]. The most common AEs of any grade and grade ≥3 in the Pola group were hematologic toxicities, particularly neutropenia, with incidences of 80% and 51.4%, respectively. These rates were significantly higher than the 30.8% and 28.3% observed in the POLARIX trial. Similarly, in the subgroup analysis of the POLARIX Asian population [26], the incidences of neutropenia and grade ≥3 neutropenia were 35.7% and 30%, respectively, which were still lower than the rates observed in our study. This discrepancy may be related to the significantly lower proportion of patients receiving primary prophylactic G-CSF in our study compared to POLARIX.

Interestingly, peripheral neuropathy occurred in only 8.6% of the 35 patients in our study, all of which were below grade 3, which is much lower than the 44.3%–52.9% reported in previous clinical trials [23,26]. This may be because some patients did not complete the full course of treatment; this issue requires further follow-up. The most common nonhematologic AE in the Pola group was pneumonia, with grade ≥3 accounting for 8.6%. One patient died from severe pneumonia during the neutropenic period, emphasizing the importance of G-CSF primary prophylaxis in reducing the risk of neutropenia and infection during Pola-based therapy. Notably, 10 patients in the Pola group experienced chemotherapy delays due to AEs, but none required dose reductions of Pola.

Although the POLARIX trial [23] demonstrated a clear PFS benefit for the Pola group compared with the traditional chemotherapy group, it did not show a significant difference in OS. In contrast, survival analysis in this study demonstrated that the Pola group had superior OS, PFS, and DOR. Multivariate analysis further indicated that Pola could serve as an independent prognostic factor after adjustment for confounding variables between the two groups. However, given the small sample size, larger studies are needed for confirmation. Notably, because of the high proportion of advanced-stage disease in our cohort, subgroup analysis confirmed that the Pola group achieved significantly better OS, PFS, and DOR in patients with advanced disease, consistent with the findings of a real-world multicentre study reported by Zhao [27].

Older patients with DLBCL often have poor physical conditions, comorbidities, and reduced bone marrow reserve, making it challenging to tolerate full-dose chemotherapy, which can lead to poor efficacy and shorter survival. Previous research [28] has reported 5-year relative survival rates of 79.7%, 70.3%, and 55.1% for patients aged <55, 55–64, and ≥65 years, respectively, indicating poorer prognosis with increasing age. Therefore, new treatment strategies are urgently required to improve survival in older patients. A retrospective study by Sato [29] involving DLBCL patients aged > 80 years treated with Pola and reduced-dose CHP showed 12-month OS and PFS rates of 86.2% and 78.5%, respectively, suggesting that this regimen is an effective treatment option. In our study, we compared survival outcomes between Pola-based and traditional chemotherapy regimens in older patients (≥60 years old). The 1-year PFS rates were 91.1% for the Pola group versus 70.6% for the traditional chemotherapy group, while the 1-year OS rates were 95.7% versus 73.8%. Although OS did not reach statistical significance, the Pola group demonstrated an observed survival advantage in PFS. Further analysis of PFS and DOR in older advanced-stage patients also demonstrated significant benefits in the Pola group, which might suggest a promising treatment option for this population.

Survival analysis of the Pola subgroup further confirmed that advanced-stage patients experienced greater benefits in terms of PFS and DOR. Previous studies [30] have shown that patients with ≥50% P53 expression have poorer OS. Our study also confirmed that even with Pola-based chemotherapy, the poor prognosis associated with high P53 expression could not be overcome. This highlights the urgent need for novel therapies targeting patients with high P53 expression or TP53 mutations.

CD20-negative DLBCL is a rare subtype often linked to aggressive pathology, high chemotherapy resistance, and poor survival rates. Studies have reported 1-year OS rates ranging from 24% to 66% for different subtypes of CD20-negative DLBCL [31]. Notably, our study included one patient with CD20-negative DLBCL, characterized by immunohistochemistry, Ki-67 > 90%, P53 expression of 30%, and multiple giant gastric ulcers at diagnosis, indicating several poor prognostic factors. This patient achieved CR after two cycles of Pola-CHP and subsequently received four cycles of CHOP due to financial constraints. As of the last follow-up (over 6 months later), the disease remained in remission, suggesting that the Pola combination regimen may provide a viable treatment option for rare DLBCL subtypes.

This multicentre retrospective study explored the differences in efficacy and safety between Pola-based and traditional chemotherapy regimens for newly diagnosed DLBCL in a real-world setting, providing real-world evidence for the use of Pola in treating newly diagnosed DLBCL. However, the study has limitations, including potential selection and information biases. Because the patients included in this study were advanced, high-risk, and mostly older adults, some received maintenance treatment with agents such as lenalidomide due to insufficient dose intensity of prior chemotherapy. Currently, major guidelines do not recommend maintenance therapy for most patients with DLBCL; it is mentioned only in certain ongoing studies or in single-arm, real-world studies of special populations [32]. Although multivariate analysis indicated that Pola may serve as an independent prognostic factor for DLBCL, the small sample size highlights the need for larger studies with more rigorous designs to account for the influence of this subgroup of patients. Due to the limited sample size, subgroup analyses of patients with advanced disease and older patients focused only on survival outcomes. Although favourable results were observed, they are prone to confounding bias given the lack of multivariate Cox regression analysis and therefore do not yet allow the conclusion that Pola is an independent prognostic factor in these patient populations. Moreover, the short follow-up period may have limited our ability to detect statistically significant results, such as OS differences in older patients, which may become apparent with larger samples or extended follow-up.

Conclusions

This multicentre real-world study demonstrates that the Pola-based regimen may offer promising efficacy and manageable safety in the treatment of patients with DLBCL, including those with advanced-stage, high-risk, or elderly disease, but it does not mitigate the poor prognosis associated with high P53 expression.

Comparison of early curative effects of lateral unicompartmental knee arthroplasty with total knee arthroplasty and the legacy constrained condylar knee arthroplasty in the treatment of isolated lateral osteoarthritis: a single-center retrospective study
Authors: Hui Yu, Jiahong Meng, Yibo Xu, Kai Xu, Changjian Lin, Jiapeng Bao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAims of this study were to compare the functional and radiographic results, perioperative complication after lateral unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (LUKA) with total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and the legacy constrained condylar knee (LCCK) arthroplasty for the treatment of lateral compartmental knee osteoarthritis (LCKOA), and to provide a basis for the treatment of LCKOA.MethodsFrom January 2021 to January 2024, analyzed retrospectively clinical data of 74 patients with LCKOA treated with LUKA, TKA and LCCK arthroplasty. According to the operation plan, the patients were divided into three groups: LUKA group (23 cases), TKA group (23 cases) and LCCK group (28 cases). The changes of hip-knee-ankle (HKA) angle of the affected knee before and after operation were measured. All patients were assessed using the operation time, blood loss during operation, incision length, visual analogue scale (VAS) score before operation and after operation, Hospital for Special Surgery score (HSS), Oxford Knee Score (OKS), range of motion (ROM).ResultsAll 74 patients were followed up for (2.38 ± 0.33) years. The operation time, blood loss during operation, incision length and VAS score after operation in the LUKA group were significantly less than those in the TKA group and LCCK group (P < 0.05); HKA valgus angle, ROM, HSS score and OKS score of the three groups after operation were significantly better than those before operation (P < 0.05); There was no significant difference among the three groups in HSS score and OKS score (P > 0.05).ConclusionLUKA, TKA and LCCK arthroplasty have good clinical effect in the treatment of LCKOA; Compared with TKA and LCCK arthroplasty, LUKA has the advantages of less trauma, less bleeding, less pain and quick recovery. The short-term effect is satisfactory, and the long-term effect remains to be observed.

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is the most common joint disease in the elderly. Some scholars have found that the incidence of symptomatic knee osteoarthritis is 8.1% through a questionnaire survey of people over 60 years old, among which women are more common than men, and gradually increase with age until they tend to be stable around 70 years old [1]. For patients with knee osteoarthritis who are ineffective in conservative treatment, it is recommended to give priority to surgical treatment. At present, the commonly used surgical methods include arthroscopic surgery, high tibial osteotomy, proximal fibular osteotomy, patellofemoral joint replacement, unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) and TKA [2]. Among single compartment osteoarthritis, medial compartment is more common than lateral compartment. In most series, the incidence of solitary arthritis in lateral compartment of knee joint is less than 10% [3]. In the early literature, LUKA was less effective than medial unicompartmental knee replacement [4]. With the progress of surgical technology and the improvement of prosthesis design, recent studies have found that compared with TKA, OKS, HSS, Knee ROM and complications of LUKA are obviously better than TKA, and the survival rate of prosthesis is similar [5]. However, few data have been available to date comparing LUKA with TKA and LCCK for LCKOA. In addition, because each of the three treatment options has its own advantages, in which patients each option is preferred remains undetermined. The surgical data of 74 patients with LCKOA treated with UKA, TKA and LCCK arthroplasty in our department were retrospectively analyzed, and the early postoperative curative effects were compared and the surgical experience was summarized.

Materials and methods

General information

Participants

Retrospective analysis of clinical data of patients with LCKOA treated with LUKA, TKA and LCCK arthroplasty in our department from January 2021 to January 2024. A total of 102 patients were included in the study and 17 patients were excluded from the final analysis because they could not complete the follow-up due to forgetting the follow-up time, considering the cost, or leaving their original place of residence. In terms of study design, using propensity score matching (PSM) can effectively reduce confounding biases. SPSS 24.0 statistical software was used for analysis. In our propensity score (PS) model, 6 baseline characteristics (Age, Height, Weight, BMI, Preoperative Valgus, Preoperative ROM) were used as covariates, the calipers set to a maximum width of 0.4 standard deviations of the estimated PS. Standardized differences for all covariates were used to compare the balance of baseline covariates between the two groups before and after matching. Finally, 11 patients were excluded from propensity score matching (PSM) due to the need to correct age differences (Fig. 1), leaving 23 patients who received LUKA (Zhengtian; Tianjin; China), 23 patients who received TKA (Chunli Zhengda; Beijing; China), and 28 patients who received LCCK (Zimmer Biomet; Indiana; USA). All the operations were performed by the same experienced surgeon. There were 29 males and 45 females, 27 cases of left knee, 47 cases of right knee, age (68.18 ± 6.44) years old, body mass index (BMI) (24.09 ± 3.19) kg/m². Before operation, full-length anterior position of both lower limbs, anterior-lateral position of weight-bearing knee joint and MRI examination of knee joint were completed, so as to clarify the degeneration of each compartment of knee joint. The patients were divided into 3 groups according to the operation plan, 23 cases in the LUKA group, age (66.91 ± 5.82) years, follow-up time (2.36 ± 0.33) years; The TKA group (23 cases), age (69.30 ± 5.46) years, follow-up time (2.36 ± 0.40) years; In the LCCK group, 28 patients (68.29 ± 7.62) years old were followed up for (2.41 ± 0.28) years. There was no significant difference in baseline data among the three groups (P > 0.05).

Inclusion criteria

(1) LUKA, TKA or LCCK arthroplasty was performed to treat isolated LCKOA, and the cartilage degree of medial compartment was basically normal; (2) Preoperative imaging evaluation showed lateral compartment degeneration; (3) The collateral ligament is obviously relaxed and the knee joint is unstable, the LCCK arthroplasty are preferred; (4) No severe patellofemoral joint symptoms; (5) Follow-up data are complete.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Uncorrected valgus deformity; (2) Severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency, which is not enough to tolerate surgery; (3) Patients who are unwilling to accept follow-up.

Implant and surgical technique

LUKA group

Using fixation platform system, choosing lateral parapatellar approach, exposing lateral joint space, the tibial osteotomy plate was placed. The sagittal plane and coronal plane of the force line rod of the osteotomy plate were parallel to the long axis of tibia at the same time, and the tibia was rotated 15° and clung to the lateral edge of tibial intercondylar crest for vertical osteotomy. The horizontal osteotomy was 2–4 mm below the lesion wear, with a horizontal osteotomy degree of about 7–9 mm and a backward inclination angle of 3°-5°. The knee was flexed 45°, the midline of the femoral prosthesis was marked, drilling was performed, posterior condyle, posterior oblique osteotomy, grinding, mold test and upper column grinding were performed. Install the matching prosthesis with bone cement. The knee joint is stable without impact, and the force line of the lower limbs is normal.

TKA and LCCK group

The anterior median longitudinal incision of the knee was selected, the knee joint cavity was completely exposed. Soft tissue release, pay attention to protect the medial and lateral collateral ligaments. The anterior and posterior osteotomy of distal femur was performed by eversion 5° and external rotation 3°. After extreme flexion of knee joint, tibial extramedullary locator and proximal osteotomy plate was installed, tilted back 5°, proximal tibial osteotomy was performed, and the tibial gasket thickness was 10–14 mm. Install the test mold, move the knee joint and balance the soft tissue. Implant each prosthesis and keep it under pressure until the bone cement is solidified. Check the extension and flexion of joints, and the balance is satisfactory.

The postoperative protocol was the same in three groups. Postoperative routine anti-infection and anticoagulation to prevent venous thrombosis of lower limbs, multi-mode analgesia. On the same day after replacement, the patient was instructed to actively contract quadriceps femoris and raise straight legs, and the next day, he began to walk weight bearing with the help of walking aids. After discharge, guide patients to strengthen lower limb strength training and knee joint activity function exercise.

Main observation indicators

Follow-up was carried out 3 months after operation, and the full-length standard X-ray films of both lower limbs and the standard anterior and lateral X-ray films of the affected limb were taken to evaluate whether the knee prosthesis had complications such as wear, looseness and dislocation (Fig. 2), and record the VAS score of the patients before and 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months after operation. The preoperative and postoperative OKS and HSS indexes were evaluated. The HKA angles were measured before and after operation to evaluate the recovery of knee joint function (Fig. 3).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 24.0 statistical software was used for analysis. The measurement data were expressed by mean ± standard deviation (SD). Paired sample t-test was used to analyze the indexes before and after operation, and ANOVA analysis of variance was used for comparison between groups. The difference was statistically significant with P-value < 0.05.

Results

Seventy-four patients with LCKOA were included, including 23 cases in LUKA group, 23 cases in TKA group and 28 cases in LCCK group. Through telephone follow-up and outpatient follow-up, 74 patients were followed up for (2.38 ± 0.33) years.

There was no significant difference between the baseline data of the LUKA groups and TKA groups (P > 0.05), it was comparable. The valgus angle of LCCK group was significantly greater than that of LUKA group and TKA group (Table 1).

Discussion

Early LUKA has high technical difficulty, poor surgical effect and high incidence of complications [4], so TKA is the main way to treat LCKOA. However, with the maturity of medical technology and the progress of surgical prosthesis design, the clinical effect of LUKA has gradually become prominent. It is reported in literature that OKS, HSS, ROM, Hospitalization Time are shorter after LUKA, and satisfaction is higher than that of matched TKA group [5]. Moreover, LUKA treatment of isolated LCKOA resulted in an acceptable symptom state in activities of daily living and physical activities in > 80% of patients. The average prosthetic survival time of the whole cohort is estimated to be 15.3 years [6]. LUKA prosthesis can be divided into fixed bearing (FB) and mobile bearing (MB) according to different types of polyethylene gaskets, both of which have their own advantages and disadvantages. Pronk analyzed the data of 163 patients who received FB and MB implants respectively showed that there was no significant difference in postoperative satisfaction, pain relief and functional improvement between the two groups [7]. However, the common postoperative complications of the two platform prostheses were different. Kim [8] found 1441 of the most common postoperative complications after using MB prostheses is bearing dislocation (2.9%), followed by component loosening (1.2%). In 135 knee joints using FB prosthesis, the most common complication was component loosening (4.4%), followed by fracture of medial tibial condyle of knee joint (0.7%). Although there is no significant difference in the long-term functional outcome between the two platform prostheses, the revision rate of MB is higher than that of FB in short-term and medium-term survival rate. In one study, the average follow-up time for fixed and mobile-bearing studies was 7.5 and 3.9 years, respectively. FB designs had an annual revision rate of 0.94 (95%CI 0.66–1.33), while MB had an annual revision rate of 2.16 (95%CI 1.54–3.04) [9, 10]. Therefore, we recommend the use of FB prosthesis, and there are no obvious complications in the patients currently used.

At present, there are mainly lateral parapatellar approach and medial approach for LUKA. Edmiston analyzed the data of 65 patients who received LUKA, and found that there was no difference in knee joint score or revision rate of TKA between medial approach group and lateral approach group, and both surgical approaches could achieve satisfactory results [11], which mainly depended on the familiarity of the operator with the surgical approach in clinic. In this study, the incision length of lateral parapatellar approach is about 8.5 cm, which can fully expose the lateral compartment and facilitate operation. At the same time, the possibility of medial structure injury is lower, the soft tissue balance can be destroyed as little as possible, and patella valgus is not needed. The infrapatellar fat pad is reserved as much as possible to cover the prosthesis and reduce the incidence of infection.

TKA as a traditional surgical method for the treatment of KOA, can correct valgus deformity and achieve good therapeutic effect in the treatment of LCKOA [12]. However, TKA has large trauma, large amount of bleeding, long operation time and relatively high perioperative complications [5]. In this study, the three groups of surgical schemes after the HKA valgus angle, the ROM of knee joint, HSS score, OKS score were significantly better than before operation, lower limb force line recovered well, have achieved good clinical results. However, there was no significant difference in HSS score and OKS score between the three groups (P > 0.05). The operation time, blood loss, incision length and VAS score in 3 months after operation in the LUKA group were better than those in the TKA group and LCCK group, and the difference was significant (P < 0.05). Therefore, this study believes that for patients who meet the indications of LUKA, choosing LUKA can correct valgus deformity and obtain the same clinical effect as TKA, it also has the advantages of less trauma, shorter operation time and less pain.

The success of LUKA depends first on the screening of patients. Early Kozinn and Scott put forward classic indications including: single compartment non-inflammatory arthritis, age > 60 years old, weight < 80 kg, no heavy physical labor, preoperative range of motion ≥ 90°, correctable deformity < 15°, patellofemoral joint and medial compartment cartilage normal, cruciate ligament intact [13]. Following this indication, Scott reported that the 9-year survival rate of the prosthesis was 90%, which was significantly higher than before [14]. With the progress of medical knowledge, surgical techniques and prosthetic implants, the indications of LUKA are relaxed, which is mainly suitable for painful osteoarthritis, osteonecrosis or post-traumatic arthritis confined to the lateral compartment of knee joint, knee joint ROM ≥ 100°, lower limb valgus deformity < 15° and complete anterior cruciate ligament function [15]. At present, it is generally believed that age, obesity and asymptomatic patellofemoral joint degeneration are not contraindications for LUKA. The contraindications are mainly rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory arthropathy, anterior cruciate ligament injury, uncorrected valgus deformity, symptomatic patellar arthritis and so on [16]. Although there have been successful reports of UKA with anterior cruciate ligament loss and severe joint deformity in recent years, it is still suggested that patients should follow a conservative concept in preoperative selection [17, 18]. In this study, 26 patients successfully completed LUKA, and the knee joint function recovered satisfactorily under the condition of satisfying the indications. However, the degree of valgus before operation was different among the three groups. The degree of valgus in the LUKA group and TKA group was smaller than that in the LCCK group. When assessing inclusion criteria, LCCK is the first choice for patients with obvious collateral ligament relaxation and knee instability. In such patients, the valgus angle tends to be larger. So the LCCK group is what we think of as a background reference, the main research focus is still on the comparison between the LUKA group and the TKA group. According to Rosso’s article [19], the author thinks that LUKA is suitable for patients with LCKOA with valgus angle < 15°. When valgus angle > 15°, TKA or LCCK prosthesis is recommended to obtain better lower limb force line and clinical effect.

Early complications of LUKA include incision infection, deep vein thrombosis, nerve injury and so on. Long-term complication package including the progress of osteoarthritis of medial compartment and patella joint, dislocation of gaskets, aseptic loosening of prosthesis, etc [20]. Among them, dislocation of gaskets is more likely to occur in the lateral compartment, because the lateral compartment is more relaxed when flexing than the medial compartment. In addition, the lateral condyle of tibia is convex, while the medial condyle is concave. Therefore, during deep flexion, the medial femoral condyle is located above the tibia, while the lateral femoral condyle is subluxated to the tibial plateau when it rotates outward relative to the tibia [21]. In this study, only one patient who underwent LCCK prosthesis had incision infection, and the incision healed after debridement and anti-infection treatment. One patient had contralateral lower limb venous thrombosis, and the rest patients did not have intraoperative fracture, prosthesis loosening and detachment during the whole study. This may be related to the small number of cases included in this study and the short follow-up time.

According to the current literature, LUKA is an effective solution for the treatment of LCKOA, and its survival rate and satisfaction are equivalent to or even better than medial UKA and TKA [6, 22, 23]. Compared with TKA, LUKA has less damage to knee ligament, meniscus and knee structure during operation, and its operation time, intraoperative blood loss and postoperative pain are better than those of TKA group, which can restore joint function early and is more easily accepted by patients, so it is more suitable for KOA patients who meet the indications of LUKA [24, 25]. CCK implants in primary TKA can be considered a viable option in cases of severe coronal deformities or when intraoperative stability is not achievable with standard implants (CR, PS, MS). The indications were: (1) valgus deformity type 2–3 partially correctable; (2) severe varus deformity with varus thrust; (3) post-traumatic deformity with major ligamentous insufficiency and any case of intra-operative ligament insufficiency [19, 26].

There are still some shortcomings in this study: First, although all the patients who meet the standards have been included in this study, the incidence of KOA in the lateral single compartment of the knee is low, which leads to a small sample size in this study; Secondly, the follow-up time of this study is limited, and only the early treatment after three kinds of joint prosthesis are reported; Thirdly, patients with indications for LUKA and LCCK are different greatly, the collateral ligament is obviously relaxed and the knee joint is unstable, the LCCK arthroplasty are preferred. Therefore, the long-term efficacy of LUKA, TKA and LCCK needs further analysis by a large sample multicenter randomized controlled study.

Conclusion

To sum up, LUKA, TKA and LCCK prosthesis for the treatment of LCKOA have good clinical effects; Compared with TKA and LCCK prosthesis, LUKA has the advantages of less trauma, less bleeding, less pain and quick recovery, but the indications and contraindications of surgery should be strictly evaluated, and the short-term effect is satisfactory, while the long-term effect remains to be observed.

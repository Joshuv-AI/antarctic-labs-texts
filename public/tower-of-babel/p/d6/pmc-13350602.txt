Dog Owners’ Willingness to Pay for Rabies Vaccine and Associated Factors in Selected Zones of Amhara Region, Ethiopia
Authors: Samuel Derso Tezera, Belayneh Zelalem, Atsedemariam Nigus Gedamu, Shewatatek Melaku Asefa, Jean Claude Manirambona, Sefinew Alemu Mekonnen, Inne Nauwelaers, Peter Delputte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo understand the dog owners’ willingness to pay (WTP) for rabies vaccines to control rabies and influencing factors in selected zones of Amhara region, Ethiopia.Methods Questionnaire data were collected from 403 dog owners selected for this purpose. Contingent valuation method was used to evaluate WTP. The interval regression analysis was used to identify factors associated to owners’ WTP for rabies vaccine.Results The majority of dog owners (65%) were WTP for suggested price bid amounts, but some had shown variability when it comes to specific price bid amounts, namely their WTP decreased when the price of the vaccine increased. The mean WTP for rabies vaccine was 38.61 ETB (0.471USD) per year. Government employees were 18.5 (95% CI: 5.55-26.10, P value <0.003) times more likely WTP for rabies vaccine compared to farmers. Respondents who believe that vaccines prevent diseases were 19.5 times more likely to be WTP for rabies vaccine.Conclusion Although majority of dog owners were WTP for rabies vaccine, increasing awareness of the importance of vaccination in disease control and increasing availability of rabies vaccine by low price would increase the chances of controlling rabies through vaccination.

Introduction

Rabies causes a significant number of human and animal deaths. Globally, an estimated 59,000 people die from rabies each year [1]. Rabies vaccine is widely used in dogs in many parts of the world for the prevention and control of rabies [2]. Rabies is a lethal viral zoonotic disease caused by the rabies virus which causes encephalitis in warm-blooded animals and humans [3]. Rabies virus is a member of genus Lyssavirus within the Rhabdoviridae family [1]. Rabies virus is a negative-stranded RNA virus that is enveloped and non-segmented. Its genome is 12 kb in size and encodes five proteins, which are: matrix protein (M), glycoprotein (G), phosphoprotein (P), nucleoprotein (N), and RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (L) [4].

Rabies results in 8.6 billion USD in economic losses annually and causes 3.7 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) [5]. Rabies is an endemic disease in developing countries of Africa and Asia with the most human deaths occurrence ratios in Africa and Asia, 36.4% and 59.6, respectively [6]. Africa accounts for the majority of human deaths from rabies globally, with an estimated 21,000 to 25,000 human deaths occurring annually [1]. The burden of rabies falls disproportionately on rural and marginalized communities where access to post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) and healthcare services are limited [7]. This results in preventable deaths, particularly among children who are more likely to be bitten by dogs [1].

Ethiopia has one of the highest rabies death rate in the world [8]. It was estimated that 2,700 people die from the disease each year due with domestic dogs as the major source of infection [5]. The disease is endemic in many parts of the country and it is a constant threat to human and animal health [9]. The high prevalence of free-roaming and unvaccinated dogs in both urban and rural areas further exacerbates the risk of rabies transmission in the country [10]. Additionally, the limited access to PEP for individuals bitten by suspected rabid animals contributes to the burden of rabies in Ethiopia [11]. Overall, dog rabies remains a critical public health issue in the country, necessitating sustained efforts to improve vaccination coverage, enhance surveillance systems, and raise awareness about responsible dog ownership and rabies prevention [12].

Vaccination is the most effective way to prevent rabies in dogs and protect both animals and humans from this deadly disease [13]. Rabies vaccine is widely used in dogs, the main reservoir hosts for rabies virus, in many parts of the world for the prevention and control of canine rabies [14]. The purpose of rabies vaccination in domestic animals is mainly to prevent death of animals, in-contact humans and minimize economic losses [15]. Vaccination coverage of approximately 70% of the vector population is estimated to be sufficient to block rabies transmission [16]. While dog vaccination is an effective method in preventing rabies, the number of dog owners participating in rabies vaccination campaigns is not significant, and successful vaccination needs collaboration of dog owners and other stakeholders [17]. Economic factors are stated as one reason for willingness to pay (WTP) for rabies vaccine [18]. However, there are also non-economic factors that affect dog owners’ participation in anti-rabies vaccination. Therefore, it is essential to explore the potential barriers and facilitators that affect dog owners’ WTP for the rabies vaccine. The aim of this study was to determine the dog owners’ WTP for rabies vaccine and identify factors associated with their WTP in selected zones of Amhara region, Ethiopia.

Materials and Methods

Study Area

The study was conducted in selected districts from three zones of Amhara region, Ethiopia. The area was selected based on the high prevalence of animal and human rabies [19]. The area covers Bahir Dar town from Bahir Dar special zone, Hulet Ej Enese from East Gojam zone and Yilmana Densa from West Gojam zone (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The map of Bahir Dar town, Yilmana Densa and Hulet Ej Enese districts, Northwest Ethiopia

The map of Bahir Dar town, Yilmana Densa and Hulet Ej Enese districts, Northwest Ethiopia

The study populations were dog owners in two districts and in Bahir Dar town in Amhara region, Ethiopia. According to official population projections listed on CityPopulation.de (using Ethiopian Statistics Service data), the population of Bahir Dar as of 1 July 2022 was 455,901 people. This figure is widely used as an official estimate for the city’s current size [20]. Yilmana Densa district has a total human population of 214,852 [21]; and Hulet Ej Enese district has a total population of 275,638 [22]. Most dogs get their food from houses prepared for human food. The purpose of owning dogs is for hunting and guarding.

Sampling Methods

Study participants were selected by using the purposive sampling technique. First, list of dog owners was created in each district based on knowledge of informants and veterinary technicians working in the nearby veterinary clinics and persons working in Kebeles. Dog owners were approached based on the order of the list for interview. When a dog owner was not willing to be interviewed, he was skipped and the dog owner with the subsequent number on the list was approached. In that way dog owners who were volunteered to participate in the study were interviewed until 423 respondents were included in the study. An English questionnaire was prepared and translated into Amharic (the native language), and then back into English for analysis. With the use of a debriefing form, data were gathered once verbal informed consent was obtained.

The Contingent Valuation Method

The contingent valuation method (CVM) is a survey-based technique that allows one to estimate how survey respondents’ trade-off private consumption for a non-marketed product in terms of money [23]. It works by eliciting preferences for non-marketed commodities [24]. A vaccination service might be deemed to be a private good and potentially marketable. The use of vaccine and veterinary pharmaceuticals involves externalities and many animal health inputs are neither purely private nor purely public [25].

Contingent valuation is widely used in economic valuation of non-marketed goods as it uses choice and consumer preference as its underlying logic of valuation [24]. Selection of elicitation format is one of the contentious aspects of CVM [25]. Possible bid design techniques are payment card open-ended question, and single (double) bounded dichotomous choice methods [26]. Dichotomous choice methods are important in that they have less starting bias and require more complex analysis, and also have an advantage in data efficiency [27]. Therefore, the choice of the method for this study was measured by using a one-item question and CVM by constructing bidding? design (what is maximum amount you are willing to pay for rabies vaccine per dose?). The second part of the questionnaire addressed determinants that influence dog owners’ WTP, such as employment status, household income, the existing knowledge of rabies and status. It also explored the participants’ awareness of rabies transmission, exposure and effectiveness of rabies vaccine. WTP questions were included in this second category [10]. The WTP questionnaire consisted of introductory statements explaining the purpose and importance of dog vaccination and comprised of sets of questions that aimed to explore determinants of WTP and explored particular elicitation methods; a payment or bidding game [29] was used with a series of yes/no questions that aimed to find the maximum WTP.

During the distribution of the questionnaire, the first bid amounts were dispersed at random among the responders. Depending on the response to the first bid amount, second bid questions were asked, each with a bid amount that was 25% of the initial bid amounts, plus or minus. The follow-up bid amount would increase by 25% if the initial bid amount was accepted, and it would decrease by 25% if the initial bid amount was rejected.

According to Table 1, the first bid question amounts were 20, 40, 60, and 80 Ethiopian birr (ETB). Based on information obtained from relevant officials in the area chosen for this study and through communication with officials of the National Veterinary Institute, these starting prices were suggested. The quality standard rabies vaccine has a protection level greater than 99.99% according to WHO, 2019. In this study, local veterinary officials requested dog owners to bid on a hypothetical vaccine that had protection effectiveness of nearly 100% when given as a first dose at 3 months and above, a first booster dose at 1.5 years, and additional boosters every 3 years.

Study Variables

In this study a total of 18 variables that could possibly affect dog owners’ WTP for rabies vaccine were evaluated. The study variables had two to five possible answers. The list of variables and their level are presented in Table 2.

Data Management and Analysis

The data were entered into Excel spread sheet and checked for completeness. Descriptive analysis and stepwise model regression analysis were conducted using STATA software version 14. A p-value of 0.05 was used as the significance threshold. Interval regression analysis was employed to estimate the mean dog owners’ WTP and to identify factors affecting WTP. Four binary responses for double bound dichotomous contingent valuation question were used: the dog owners were not willing to purchase the initial bid amount and the lower bid amount (“no”, “no”), the respondents were not willing to pay the initial bid amount but accepted the lower bid amount (“no”, “yes”), the dog owner willingness to purchase the initial bid amount of rabies vaccine but not the higher follow up bid amount (“yes, no”) and the last response was accepting both the initial and second bid amounts (“yes”, “yes”).

Greater follow-up bid amounts resulted in four potential intervals, and three different censorship techniques (left, interval, and right) were applied. The WTP’s descriptive statistics and related variables were examined using STATA software version 14. Stepwise regression analysis was employed in the interval regression modeling, with the retention of relevant variables.

Multicollinearity was checked with the Pearson correlation coefficient using pairwise regression analysis. Correlation coefficient > 7 was considered a significant correlation.

Descriptive Statistics

Majority of respondents were from Hulet Ej Enesse followed by Yilmana Densa while the lowest were from Bahir. The majority of the respondents were males (62%), government employees were few, and the majority of participants were illiterate. 57% (57%) of the dog owners stated that they had vaccinated their dog(s) at least once within the past two years and they had previously paid for rabies vaccination. With regard to rabies awareness, almost all of the respondents (96%) had heard of rabies and the majority (78%) knew how to recognize the clinical signs of rabies in dogs and could identify the routes of transmission (85%). Discussion

Willingness to Pay for Rabies Vaccine

This study was conducted to assess dog owners’ willingness to pay for rabies vaccine and to identify factors that influence dog owners WTP for rabies vaccine in selected zones of Amhara region. We found that most of dog owners (65%) were WTP for suggested price bid amounts for rabies vaccine. The finding in the current study supported the work of Birhane and his colleagues [6]. This is not surprising as zoonotic importance and severity of rabies are generally known by dog owners, but access to rabies vaccine for dogs is limited. However, the proportion of dog owners WTP for the vaccine showed great variability when it comes to specific price bid amounts. The majority of dog owners (67%) were WTP the initial price bid amount (20 ETB), but the proportion decreased to 46% when the bid amount increased to 25 ETB, and it further decreased to 42% when the bid amount increased to 80 ETB.

Dog owners are WTP “a certain price” for rabies vaccine but their WTP is variable when the price becomes more tangible. It is not surprising that, although dog owners are WTP for rabies vaccine, it is dependent on the price of the vaccine. As prices increased, the proportion of dog owners WTP to the vaccine decreased. The reason for this could be related to financial resources to pay the vaccination fee as well as the cost of rabies vaccine [18, 30].

The mean WTP price for the vaccine as estimated using the interval regression model parameterized from the double-bound dichotomous questionnaire data was ETB 38.61 (0.48 USD) per year. This finding agrees with the report of WTP survey conducted in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia where 76.2% of respondents were willing to pay 20 ETB for rabies vaccine [31]. This WTP estimate is significantly higher than 20 birr per dose for annual vaccination charged currently for vaccines produced by the National Veterinary Institute in Ethiopia. The higher level of WTP for the vaccine may not reflect the actual price value of the vaccine. It may be due to the limited availability of the vaccine as shortage in supply causes increased prices by higher demand.

The present study showed that there were certain factors that could influence dog owners’ WTP for dog vaccination. Occupation of dog owners was significantly associated (p < 0.05) with WTP for vaccination to control rabies. The odds of WTP of Government employees were higher than that of the odds of WTP of farmers for rabies vaccine. Government employees are more likely to be aware of the risk of rabies than farmers because they have a better educational background than farmers. This stresses the importance of creating awareness. Availability and ease of implementation of anti-rabies dog vaccination are important to farmers as well. This finding is in line with the report from Tigray region [32], and in Northwest Ethiopia [10]. Proactive and sustainable vaccination programs have proven their efficacy in the eradication of domestic dog rabies which motivates the fight against rabies control. This report gives policy makers an indication that many dog owners are WTP for rabies vaccines. Nevertheless, the conclusions should also be carefully interpreted, as it could also be related to income, since dog owners working as government employees, in Ethiopian circumstances, in general have higher income compared to farmers. Income has been shown to affect WTP for rabies vaccination in South Gondar [11], and in Lamely Maychew district, North Ethiopia [33].

According to the theory of positive income elasticity, income and WTP are related, which is supported by the positive correlation found between people’s income level and the amount they were prepared to pay for dog vaccinations [10]. On the other hand, this could also suggest that those in lower income categories would not be able to pay as much as those in higher income categories. For this reason, in order to maintain equilibrium, premium adjustments for individuals in the low-income category may need to be made through subsidies [34].

The present study also found that WTP for dog vaccination was influenced by pre-existing knowledge of importance of vaccination in preventing disease. Respondents who believe that vaccine prevent diseases were 19.5 times more likely to be WTP for rabies vaccine. It is not surprising that knowledge influences belief. Knowledge and belief refer to qualitatively different aspects of the mental representation: knowledge refers to the representation of a proposition, and belief refers to the representation of a truth-value associated with a proposition [35]. In turn, belief guides action which also embodies information received from perception.

The odds of WTP of respondents in Bahir Dar was higher compared to that in Hulet Ej Enese. The explanation for this could be related to knowledge of risk of rabies. The level of education and school access are likely to be higher in urban areas than in rural population. Therefore, respondents who know the risk of rabies are more likely to be WTP compared to respondents who have lower levels of knowledge of the risk of rabies. However, the odds of WTP of respondents in Yilmana Densa district was also higher than the odds of WTP of respondents from Hult Ej Enese. This could be related to difference in prevalence of rabies in the dog population, so they may have a different level of rabies exposure [36]. In general, the study has shown that dog owners are WTP for rabies vaccines although the number of respondents who were WTP increased when the price of the vaccine decreased. This information can be used by veterinary services and public health agencies when planning dog vaccination programs in controlling rabies. If the government would subsidize the vaccine, this study suggests it is possible to increase the number of participants in rabies vaccination campaign.

In conclusion, the majority of dog owners were WTP the suggested price bid amounts for rabies vaccine. However, the number of respondents who were WTP decreased when the presented bid amount for rabies vaccine increased. Occupation, district and knowledge of the capacity of vaccines preventing the disease were associated with WTP for rabies vaccine. Providing anti-rabies dog vaccination at lower price, for instance through subsidizing the cost of vaccine, improves participation chances of dog owners to anti-rabies vaccination programs. Increasing awareness of dog owners on the importance, availability and ease of implementation of anti-rabies dog vaccinations are suggested.

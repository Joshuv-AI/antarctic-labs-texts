Comparison of Sealing Ability of Biodentine, Biostructure Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair as Perforation Repair Material: An Inverted Laser Scanning Confocal Microscopic Study
Authors: Langpoklakpam Monica Devi, Gunjan Yadav, Amit Rai, Sonali Saha, Kavita Dhinsa, Anshul Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundOne of the endodontic errors that might happen during a root canal procedure is perforation in the furcation area. To prevent bacterial contamination, it should be repaired as quickly as possible with a biocompatible material. The study sought to assess and also compared the ability of the materials, i.e., Biodentine, Biostructure Mineral Trioxide Aggregate, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair, to seal the perforation in permanent molars as materials of choice for perforation repair.Materials and methodsIn the present study, 65 samples were made ready using Rhodamine B dye and distributed into five groups of thirteen samples each: group I (perforations left unsealed, i.e., positive control), group II (perforations sealed using Biodentine), and group III [perforations repaired with Biostructure mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA)]. In group IV, Theracal LC was used to seal the perforations. Similarly, in group V, Bio-C Repair was applied to repair the perforation. Samples were observed in an Inverted Laser Scanning Confocal Microscope. The degree of the penetration of the dye was estimated in mm (millimeters) with the help of the Software IMAGE J. Data obtained were subjected to Post hoc Tukey's test and One-way Analysis of variance test.ResultsThe mean depth in regard to penetration was found to be maximum in group I, then group V, and minimum in group II. Overall, there was a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001) among the mean depth of penetration of all five study groups.ConclusionBiodentine, Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair showed microleakage, while Biodentine showed the least marginal leakage amongst all, and Bio-C Repair exhibited the highest microleakage.Clinical significanceIn order to seal and manage furcation perforation effectively, this study will educate dentists on how to choose and apply the best perforation repair material to achieve a good prognosis.

Introduction

Maintaining healthy dentition is crucial for natural esthetics and proper function. Any disruption in this balance necessitates dental treatment, particularly endodontic treatment. Sometimes, in the field of endodontics, procedural mishaps occur that have an influence on the result of the root canal therapy. Endodontic perforation is one of these procedural mishaps.1 The American Association of Endodontics (AAE) Glossary of Endodontic Terms (2003) defines a perforation as a pathological or mechanical communication between the exterior tooth surface and the root canal system that is brought on by iatrogenic causes, resorption, or caries.2

Perforations can happen at any point in the process, such as during instrumentation operations that result in root perforations in the apical, mid-root, or cervical regions, or during access cavity preparation that results in lateral surface or furcation perforations.1 As quickly as feasible, perforations should be repaired using a biocompatible material to prevent bacterial contamination. Serious consequences from perforations may potentially result in extraction. The tooth or teeth may survive for a long time if the perforation is identified early and appropriate treatment is administered.3

A variety of materials have been developed, evaluated, and standardized in dental science for the repair of perforations. Bio-C Repair (Angelus) is one such unique material. The newly introduced calcium (Ca) silicate-incorporated materials have been reported for improved working characteristics, but evidence is lacking to support these material improvements from MTA. Multiple studies have been found about comparison of MTA, Biodentine, and other calcium silicate-based materials. However, till date, no study has been found about the comparison of Biodentine, Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair as material of choice for perforation repair.

Thus, the purpose of this study was to evaluate and compare the sealing ability of Biodentine, Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair as perforation repair material.

Materials and Methods

The research study was carried out in Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Sardar Patel Postgraduate Institute of Dental and Medical Sciences, Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh, with permission from the Institutional Ethical Committee (IEC PROTOCOL NO. PEDO/03/222353/IEC). This was done in collaboration with the University Sophisticated Instrumentation Center, Baba Saheb Bhimrao Ambedkar University, Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh, which was well equipped with the facilities required for the study. The study aimed to compare the sealing ability of Biodentine, Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair in the repair of furcal perforation by measuring dye penetration under an inverted confocal laser microscope.

The study was an in vitro experimental study. Out of the 65 samples, all of the 5 groups had 13 samples each, which was found to be sufficient for an alpha of 0.05, power of 80%, and 0.45 as effect size (assessed for difference in mean dye leakage).

Sixty-five freshly extracted permanent molars were collected from the Department of Pedodontics, SPPGIDMS, and also from dental clinics in and around Lucknow city, Uttar Pradesh.

Inclusion criteria were slightly carious or noncarious permanent molars with two or more roots and teeth with their furcations intact. Permanent molars with severe caries, fused roots, cracks, and dilacerations were excluded.

The study was conducted in two phases.4

Phase 1

In phase 1, samples were prepared using Rhodamine B dye. The procedure was as follows:

Sixty-five extracted human upper and lower molar permanent teeth were gathered, preserved, and later cleaned by soaking them in 5% NaOCl for half an hour. They were then cleaned with tap water to get rid of all the debris and stored in regular saline until the next phase. A tapered diamond bur was used to amputate the molar roots 3 mm below the furcation area. All molars had access cavities made with a round bur no. 2, after which the root canal orifices were found (Fig. 1). The orifices of each canal, along with the sectioned root surface and pulpal floor, were sealed with sticky wax. Then, in an effort to improve the marginal seal, two additional coats of varnish were applied. Each perforation was positioned centrally between the roots, and its location was indicated with a black marker pen. A high-speed handpiece with air and water coolant and a no. 2 round carbide bur was used to make an artificial perforation from the tooth's exterior that was 1 mm in diameter. All of the samples' chambers and perforations were cleaned using water and allowed to dry. Afterwards, the specimen teeth were split into five groups of 13 each: group I (perforations left unsealed, i.e., positive control), group II (perforations repaired with Biodentine), group III (perforations repaired with Biostructure MTA), group IV (perforations repaired with Theracal LC), and group V (perforations repaired with Bio-C Repair).

Access openings performed and located root canal orifices

Following the initial setting of the repair materials, each group's samples were put in a different petri dish with Rhodamine B dye so that every tooth was submerged in dye up to the cementoenamel junction (CEJ) for a full day of the retrograde dye challenge. Rinsing of the teeth was done underneath running water for forty minutes after being taken out of the dye, and a polishing disc was used to get rid of any remaining varnish. Sectioning of all the teeth was done both in longitudinal and mesiodistal direction with a diamond disk to obtain two separate halves.

Phase 2

In phase 2, samples were observed in an inverted laser scanning confocal microscope (ILSCM). Using an ILSCM (Model: LSM 900, Zeiss Germany), oil immersion lenses, and a green filter (wavelength 546 nm), the degree of dye penetration was examined. When Rhodamine B dye was stimulated by green light with a wavelength of 546 nm, it produced a reddish-orange fluorescence, which showed the extent of depth of penetration (Fig. 2). The quantity of dye penetration was measured in mm using the IMAGE J software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland, USA). The collected data were analyzed statistically.

Data Analysis

Data was interpreted using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 21. The study variable, i.e., depth of penetration, was a continuous variable, thus, summarized as SD and mean. Microsoft Excel was used for the preparation of graphs. One-way ANOVA test and Tukey's post hoc test were performed. The level of significance was set at p < 0.05.

Group I (positive control) exhibited the highest mean depth of penetration, followed by group V (Bio-C Repair), group IV (Theracal Light Cured), group III (Biostructure MTA), and group II (Biodentine), respectively (Fig. 3).

Discussion

The outcome of endodontic therapy may be impacted by perforations, regardless of their location or cause. As soon as feasible, a biocompatible material should be used to block the iatrogenic, pathophysiological, or mechanical interaction between the external tooth surface and the root canal system.5 The preferred material for repairing perforations should be biocompatible, radiopaque, bacteriostatic, offer a sufficient seal, have properties to induce cementogenesis and osteogenesis, reasonably priced with good handling properties, nontoxic, and anticariogenic. None of these qualities is present in any material. Over the years, a variety of sealing materials and methods have been tested and tried with differing degrees of success in an attempt to find the perfect one.6 The outcomes of this current research study on intergroup comparison of mean dye penetration depth showed that the mean depth of penetration was found to be least for Biodentine followed by Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC and Bio-C Repair.

A novel bioactive dentine alternative, biodentine has surfaced in recent years. Biodentine is now widely used in dentistry due to its many clinical uses, enhanced handling, and better physical qualities. Biodentine (Septodont, Saint-Maurdes-Fosses, France) is a two-part dental substance that has distinct liquid and powder compositions. The primary ingredient of the powder is tricalcium silicate. Fillers include calcium oxide and carbonate. Iron oxide is utilized for shade, while for X-ray visibility, zirconium oxide is added to the powder. Calcium chloride, which is present in the liquid, serves as a water-reducing and setting accelerator. Calcium chloride speeds up the material's curing time and stops cracks from forming, which is a common problem with cement that contains a lot of water. Biodentine takes nine to 12 minutes to set initially.7 However, Biodentine is highly costly and technique-sensitive, despite its many outstanding qualities.8

When used for perforation repair, MTA, being the preferred and gold standard material for repairing perforations, has a greater sealing ability because it is biocompatible with the periradicular tissues and promotes cementoblasts to develop the matrix needed for cementum production.2,9 Despite MTA's appealing qualities, it has a number of clinical drawbacks, including high cost, lengthy setting time, difficulty manipulating, and the tendency to discolor tooth structure. Due to the high cost of the various varieties of MTA, Biostructure MTA (SafeEndo) has been introduced as an alternative.

Biostructure MTA (SafeEndo) is a novel MTA that comes in liquid and powder form. Mineral trioxides (dicalcium silicate, tricalcium aluminate, and tricalcium silicate) and zirconium oxide are the main constituents of the powder, while the liquid is composed of calcium chloride in filtered water. Biostructure MTA is available in a packaging of powder (1 gm) and liquid (2 mL). The time a powder takes to solidify is determined by the ratio of its oxides. When the liquid and the powder are mixed in a ratio of 3.1–2.8 for a duration of 1 minute at a humidity of (50 ± 10) % and room temperature (18–23°C), a plastic paste that is easy to use is produced. A fast-setting product takes 2–4 minutes to operate, and it takes 15–18 minutes to harden. 10 to 12 minutes is the operational time of a prolonged hardening product. Under conditions of a mouth cavity, the product hardens in the canal after 24 hours.10

A new resin-modified, light-cured, calcium silicate-filled base/filler material called Theracal LC (Bisco) was released in 2011 to address the drawbacks of MTA. In contrast to MTA, the property of Theracal LC that is light-cured results in instant polymerization and shorter treatment, which affects patient compliance in the case of children. Calcium hydroxide is released as a hydration byproduct as the material sets, and an alkaline environment is produced that stimulates the creation of apatite, which is followed by the formation of new dentin, as well as cell growth and proliferation.11,12

The repair cement Bio-C Repair (Angelus) is a bioceramic putty that is all set to use. In addition to its stimulation of tissue regeneration, antibacterial activity, and suppression of bacterial infiltration, the bioceramic formulation offers a significant advantage over conventional cements in that it does not require mixing. The product is presented in a screw-in syringe, which makes it easier to remove and apply to the preparation site. This simplifies the process and saves a significant amount of time. Since Bio C Repair contains no bismuth oxide, it is also bioactive and stain-free.13

The findings from the current research inferred that Biodentine showed greatest ability to seal as perforation repair material followed by Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC and Bio-C Repair in descending order. The results of our study were in agreement with the studies of Kumar et al.4, Aggarwal et al.,5 Guneser et al.,14 Yazdi et al.,15 Samuel et al.,16 Ajas et al.17 and Sinkar et al.,18 which demonstrated that the sealing ability of Biodentine was better than MTA.

Kumar et al.4 cited that it also minimizes the space between the tooth and the biodentine by forming tag-like structures made of crystalline deposits that accumulate over time and are rich in calcium or phosphorus and connect chemomechanically with the tooth which was in accordance to the results of our study. According to Aggarwal et al.,5 in furcal perforation repairs, Biodentine demonstrated a greater push-out bond strength than MTA plus and ProRoot MTA, with the added benefit that its bond strength was unaffected by blood contamination. In comparison to MTA, which is consistent with our work, Biodentine demonstrated noteworthy effectiveness as a material for perforation repair according to the results of a study carried out by Guneser et al.,14 even after being subjected to different endodontic irrigants. In a research by Yazdi et al.,15 Biodentine outperformed MTA, calcium enriched mixture (CEM) Cement, and Endosequence root repair material (ERRM) putty in terms of push-out bond strength in the presence of blood. According to Samuel et al.,16 Biodentine can be a good substitute for MTA because it demonstrated less microleakage than MTA, which was in accordance with our study. Ajas et al.17 also stated that compared to MTA, biodentine has a greater capacity for biomineralization and a broader layer rich in calcium and silicon at the material–dentin interface. Sinkar et al.18 found that Biodentine outperforms ProRoot MTA in terms of sealing ability, which was similar to the results of our study.

However, findings reported in the current experimental research were in contrary to the studies of Mohammed et al.,19 Francis et al.,20 Shrestha et al.,21 and Rudranaik et al.22 in which it was observed that Biodentine and MTA had similar sealing abilities.

The outcomes of this present in vitro study were same with the studies of Alazrag et al.,8 Yamamoto et al.,23 Arandi and Thabet,24 Han and Okiji25 and Camilleri26 in which it was observed that the sealing ability of Theracal LC was inferior to that of Biodentine and MTA.

It was inferred from this in vitro study that Theracal LC exhibited higher dye penetration depth than Biostructure MTA and Biodentine. According to Alazrag et al.,8 when compared to MTA and Biodentine, Theracal LC displayed the highest frequency distribution of gap, which was consistent with the findings of our current investigation. Yamamoto et al.23 also found that the ProRoot MTA produced a greater amount of Ca2+ ions compared to Theracal LC. Theracal LC does not generate Ca(OH)2 after setting, but it does release calcium ions and forms precipitated calcium apatite layer, according to the study. Arandi and Thabet24 stated that in contrast to other materials such as mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA), resin-modified calcium silicate, and calcium hydroxide (CH) cement, Theracal LC, biodentine is notable for their high amounts of calcium ion release, which was in agreement with our study.

According to Han and Okiji,25 dentin's absorption of calcium and silicon ions causes tag-shaped regions to form in Biodentine that are larger than in MTA. The improved powder composition of Biodentine, which includes softeners and setting enhancers, is another factor contributing to its superior seal. According to Camilleri J et al.,26 a novel predosed capsule composition intended to be used in an amalgamation device primarily enhances the physical attributes such as the agent's ability to seal. Biodentine particles are smaller than MTA as well as the reduced pore volume and porosity at the sealing interface of the Biodentine material render to improve the sealing capacity. The above reasons could be attributed to superior sealing ability of Biodentine compared to the other materials.

Furthermore, the inferences of our research study indicated that Theracal LC has less sealing abilities with MTA. These were in contrary to the studies of Umale et al.12.

Outcomes of the current study reported that Bio-C Repair had the highest mean dye penetration depth, which was in agreement with the studies of Ghilotti et al.,27 De Toubes et al.,28 Rodrigues et al.29 and Campi et al.30

According to Ghilotti et al.,27 carbon (34.81%) and oxygen (34.51%) were found to make up the majority of Bio-C Repair. De Toubes et al.28 observed that while calcium was abundant in the MTA and Biodentine samples, it was represented in Bio-C Repair in lesser amounts (comparable to zirconium, which serves as a radiopacifier) and at a lower concentration than all other biomaterials, which are mostly composed of calcium and oxygen. This could be the cause of the increased dye leaking in the Bio-C Repair case, which was consistent with the conclusions of our study. Rodrigues et al.29 reported that the Biodentine bioceramic cement exhibited superior binding strength to root dentin only to Bio-C Repair cement, as well as greater resilience to compression compared to Bio-C Repair and MTA Repair HP. Whereas bonding failure was frequently seen for Bio-C Repair and MTA Repair HP, the most prevalent failure mode for Biodentine was mixed, which supports our findings. According to Campi et al.,30 Bio-C Repair demonstrated bioactivity and biological compatibility comparable to MTA and Biodentine, as well as the ability to promote alkaline pH, indicating potential for usage as a reparative material and supporting our study.

Using a dye is said to be the simplest and most economical way to find leakage. Methylene blue, basic fuchsin, rhodamine, silver nitrate, and India ink are among the dye types that have been suggested for evaluating microleakage. The amount of dye leakage depends on molecular size, chemical interactions, and dye pH.31,32 Our study used Rhodamine B dye because it makes it simple to apply linear measurement techniques to quantify the region of dye penetration.4 Since MTA has antibacterial properties, and the application of the fluid transport model for determining sealing efficiency in the repair of furcation perforation has not been clearly defined, we did not choose bacterial leakage.33 Due to its simplicity of usage compared to the complexity of other current methods, the technique of penetration of dye has been utilized for a long time in endodontics. Since both techniques rely on quantitative evaluation of liquid flow inside interfaces, the dye penetration methodology used in this work produced results that were analogous to those observed using the technique of fluid-filtration, according to Camps J and Pashley.34,35

There are many ways to evaluate furcation repair material using certain microscopes, such as scanning electron microscope (SEM) or TEM. However, spectrophotometry is widely applied for dye determination due to its low cost, higher sensitivity, and very high detection power.32 Since it is an easy way to test how well dental materials adapt to the dentin, ILSCM was employed for analysis in this study. It offers a number of benefits, including the samples' nondestructive analysis; samples do not need to be dehydrated because ILSCM or CLSM eliminates the need for drying, which is essential for traditional SEM or TEM, reducing the possibility of shrinkage or additional artifacts from drying; no particular sectioning method is necessary. This lessens the chance from developing irregularities arising from dehydration and sputter-coating processes used to prepare the specimens for SEM analysis, and additionally, there is the option of rapid sampling.4,36–38

The primary drawback of this current in vitro study is that they necessitate prolonged observation and does not replicate the circumstances seen in the mouth cavity. Further, in vivo studies with a large sample size have been recommended to analyze the sealing properties of the tested material because the findings of this in vitro investigation might not fully illustrate the therapeutic applicability of the materials used for sealing perforation defects.

Conclusion

Considering the constraints of the present in vitro study, while comparing Biodentine, Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC and Bio-C Repair as perforation repair materials, Biodentine demonstrated noticeably less microleakage than Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC and Bio-C Repair. Biodentine, a calcium silicate-based substance that claims superior qualities over MTA, has the capacity to seal perforations more effectively than Biostructure MTA, Theracal LC, and Bio-C Repair.

Clinical Significance

The outcome of endodontic treatment may be impacted by perforations, regardless of their location or cause. A biocompatible substance should be used right away to seal any iatrogenic, pathological, or mechanical connection between the outer surface of the root and its canal system. In order to seal and manage furcation perforation effectively, this study will educate dentists on how to choose and apply the best perforation repair material to achieve a good prognosis.

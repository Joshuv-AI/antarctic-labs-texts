Carvedilol for Prevention of Chemotherapy-Induced Cardiotoxicity
Authors: Silvia Moreira Ayub-Ferreira, Mônica Samuel Avila, Mauro Rogerio de Barros Wanderley, Fatima das Dores Cruz, Sara Michelly Gonçalves Brandão, Vagner Oliveira Carvalho Rigaud, Cecilia Beatriz Bittencourt Viana Cruz, Guilherme Veiga Guimaraes, Marcio Sommer Bittencourt, Edimar Alcides Bocchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical Abstract

Despite advances in oncology, cardiovascular toxicity associated with chemotherapeutic agents, particularly anthracyclines, remains a concern.1 The role of β-blockers in the primary prevention of chemotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity remains debated. In the CECCY (Carvedilol for Prevention of Chemotherapy-Induced Cardiotoxicity) trial, carvedilol did not reduce the incidence of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) decline at 6 months; however, carvedilol was associated with lower troponin levels and a lower incidence of diastolic dysfunction (DD).2

The aim of the present study was to investigate the cardioprotective effects of carvedilol in patients undergoing anthracycline-based chemotherapy; methods were described previously.2 The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01724450). The trial randomized 192 participants in a 1:1 ratio.

Both carvedilol and placebo were up-titrated every 3 weeks to the maximum tolerated dose or until predefined safety thresholds were met (intolerable symptoms, heart rate ≤60 beats/min, or systolic blood pressure ≤110 mm Hg). Carvedilol and placebo were continued throughout the chemotherapy regimen through week 24. Late follow-up extended to 96 weeks.

Sequential transthoracic echocardiography was performed at weeks 6, 12, 24, 48, and 96. Experienced clinicians interpreted all echocardiograms using standard imaging protocols. Troponin I levels were measured through week 24 but were not collected thereafter. The primary endpoint was a decline in LVEF of 10% or more from baseline through week 96. The secondary endpoint was progression of DD, evaluated according to guideline-based criteria available at the time of study design.

Baseline clinical characteristics were well balanced between the carvedilol and placebo groups.2 Reductions in LVEF of 10% or more from baseline were observed in 10 patients (10.4%) in the carvedilol group and 11 patients (11.4%) in the placebo group (P > 0.99, chi-square test). No significant differences in LVEF trajectories were observed between groups over the 96-week follow-up period (P = 0.79, longitudinal mixed-effects model).

At week 24, patients in the placebo group exhibited a greater increase in left ventricular diastolic diameter (LVEDD) than those receiving carvedilol, although the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.52, longitudinal mixed-effects model). This difference was not observed at week 96.

Additionally, the incidence of DD increased in the placebo group during anthracycline treatment, whereas it remained stable in the carvedilol group (P = 0.052) (Figure 1). Menopausal status did not influence outcomes. Clinical events were analyzed exploratorily, and no significant differences were observed between groups. Overall, carvedilol did not reduce cardiotoxicity as measured by LVEF decline through week 96; LVEDD increased more in the placebo group at week 24 but not at week 96.

Neither the PRADA (Prevention of Cardiac Dysfunction During Adjuvant Breast Cancer Therapy) trial,3 which evaluated metoprolol, nor the CECCY trial, which investigated carvedilol, demonstrated a significant impact on LVEF. Both studies enrolled patients with low cardiovascular risk and low cumulative anthracycline doses, for which the expected incidence of cardiotoxicity is low. In contrast, the SAFE (Cardiotoxicity Prevention in Breast Cancer Patients Treated With Anthracyclines and/or Trastuzumab) trial reported a reduction in subclinical cardiac injury among patients with breast cancer receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy, suggesting that cardioprotective strategies may be more effective in higher risk populations or under more intensive treatment regimens.4

Although carvedilol did not significantly prevent LVEF reduction in this cohort, its use attenuated the increase in LVEDD at week 24. This finding may suggest a role for carvedilol in modulating cardiac chamber remodeling during anthracycline exposure.

A recent meta-analysis identified DD as a predictor of long-term left ventricular dysfunction in the setting of chemotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity.5 The reduced incidence of DD in the carvedilol group may reflect a protective effect against early myocardial injury and subsequent progression to clinically significant ventricular dysfunction.

In the early CECCY analysis,2 troponin elevation was minimal, only slightly exceeding the upper limit of normal, suggesting mild myocardial injury. Troponin elevation during anthracycline treatment has emerged as a key predictor of left ventricular dysfunction and an increased risk for adverse clinical outcomes. Troponin may help identify higher risk individuals who could benefit from cardioprotective interventions.

However, the mild myocardial injury observed may have been insufficient to induce a measurable decline in LVEF, and no significant effect of carvedilol in preventing overt cardiotoxicity was observed. Conversely, DD may represent an early subclinical manifestation of cardiotoxicity, and carvedilol may exert a protective effect in this context.

Our study had limitations. This single-center clinical trial observed a 10.7% incidence of cardiotoxicity, lower than initially anticipated, which may have limited statistical power to detect certain effects of carvedilol. Interobserver variability may have influenced serial LVEF measurements, but any effect would be expected to be nondifferential between arms.

In conclusion, the incidence of cardiotoxicity with low-dose doxorubicin was lower than expected. Although carvedilol did not prevent LVEF decline over 96 weeks, it was associated with possible attenuation of DD and a smaller (nonsignificant) increase in LVEDD, suggesting an effect on cardiac remodeling. Further studies targeting high-risk populations, particularly those with elevated troponin levels, are warranted to confirm these findings.

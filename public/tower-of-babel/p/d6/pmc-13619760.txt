Post Hoc Subgroup and Body Region Analyses of VASI Outcomes Among Patients with Vitiligo in a Phase 2 Povorcitinib Trial
Authors: Thierry Passeron, Amit G. Pandya, Khaled Ezzedine, Nanja van Geel, Lois Erskine, Leandro L. Santos, Zhenyi Xue, Andrew Blauvelt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionA phase 2 trial (NCT04818346) of the oral Janus kinase 1 inhibitor povorcitinib demonstrated substantial total body and facial repigmentation over 52 weeks in adults with extensive nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV). Here, post hoc analyses evaluated povorcitinib efficacy in patients based on demographic and clinical characteristic subgroups, as well as affected body regions.MethodsData were pooled for 103 patients in the extension-evaluable population who received povorcitinib (15, 45, or 75 mg once daily) throughout the 52-week study. Efficacy among subgroups was evaluated using achievement of ≥ 50% reduction from baseline in total Vitiligo Area Scoring Index (T-VASI50) and ≥ 50%/≥ 75% reduction from baseline in facial VASI (F-VASI50/F-VASI75). Efficacy in the affected body regions was evaluated using VASI50 responses. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, and missing values were imputed as nonresponders.ResultsAt Week 52, 34.0% (35/103) of patients achieved T-VASI50, 61.2% (63/103) achieved F-VASI50, and 45.6% (47/103) achieved F-VASI75. Repigmentation occurred across subgroups regardless of patient age, sex, race, Fitzpatrick skin type, affected body surface area, other autoimmune comorbidities, disease duration, or prior NSV treatment. In addition, VASI50 response rates increased consistently and, in most cases, without plateauing for each body region, ranging from 25.8% for the feet to 57.3% for the head and neck (excluding the face). Decreased rates of disease activity were also evident across body regions.ConclusionIn this exploratory post hoc analysis of adults with extensive NSV, oral povorcitinib produced clinically meaningful repigmentation (as measured by achievement of T-VASI50 and F-VASI75) across patient characteristic subgroups and body regions through 1 year of treatment. Response rates varied by body region, reinforcing the lengthy repigmentation process, especially in nonfacial areas, and highlighting the need to manage patient expectations and encourage adherence to long-term therapy.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT04818346.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-026-03673-6.

Introduction

Vitiligo is a chronic, inflammatory autoimmune disorder characterized by melanocyte loss and skin depigmentation, often leading to substantial psychosocial burden [1]. Effective therapies aim to achieve repigmentation [2, 3], but treatment efficacy may vary based on demographics and clinical characteristics such as age, skin type, and disease duration [1, 4]. In addition, repigmentation can vary across body regions, with facial and proximal regions generally responding more favorably to treatment than more resistant sites like the hands and feet [4–6].

Povorcitinib is an oral, small-molecule, selective Janus kinase 1 inhibitor that demonstrated substantial repigmentation in patients with extensive nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV) through 52 weeks of treatment in a phase 2 dose-ranging study (NCT04818346) [7]. Povorcitinib was generally well tolerated, with no serious adverse events related to treatment through Week 52 [7]. All doses of povorcitinib produced a statistically superior percentage change from baseline in the total Vitiligo Area Scoring Index (T-VASI [all body regions, including the face]; primary endpoint) compared with placebo. Additionally, numerically higher percentages of patients treated with povorcitinib achieved ≥ 50% reduction from baseline in T-VASI (T-VASI50; key secondary endpoint) and ≥ 50%/≥ 75% reduction from baseline in facial VASI (F-VASI50/F-VASI75; exploratory endpoints) versus placebo. Attainment of F-VASI75 and T-VASI50 is considered an indicator of treatment success by both patients and clinicians [2]. Here, post hoc analyses were conducted to assess the efficacy of povorcitinib treatment across patient characteristic subgroups and body regions.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

The phase 2 study methodology has been published previously [7]. In brief, the study included 171 adults (18–75 years old) with a clinical diagnosis of NSV, depigmented areas affecting ≥ 0.5% of facial body surface area (F-BSA) and ≥ 8% of total BSA (T-BSA), and scores ≥ 0.5 on F-VASI and ≥ 8 on T-VASI. Patients were randomized 1:1:1:1 to receive one of three doses of povorcitinib (15, 45, or 75 mg) or placebo once daily for 24 weeks of double-blinded treatment. Thereafter, during the extension period, patients initially randomized to placebo or povorcitinib 15 mg were allocated to povorcitinib 75 mg, and patients initially randomized to povorcitinib 45 mg or 75 mg maintained their assigned dose through completion at Week 52. These post hoc analysis pooled data for the 103 patients originally randomized to povorcitinib (i.e., patients who received povorcitinib 15, 45, or 75 mg from Day 1) who continued into the extension period (i.e., extension-evaluable population; Figure S1).

The trial was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, study protocol, applicable Good Clinical Practice, and local regulatory requirements. An institutional review board or independent ethics committee approved the study protocol and amendments at each site. All patients provided written informed consent before participating in the trial, including for publication of clinical photos.

Assessments

Percentages of patients achieving T-VASI50, F-VASI50, and F-VASI75 at Week 52 were assessed using baseline patient demographics and clinical characteristics. Analyzed subgroups were age group (≤ 40 years, > 40 years), sex (male, female), race (white, non-white), Fitzpatrick skin type (I–III, IV–VI), F-BSA (≤ 1.5%, > 1.5%), T-BSA (≤ 20%, > 20%), other autoimmune disorders (no, yes), disease duration (< 10 years, ≥ 10 years), and previous NSV therapy (no, yes; topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors, phototherapy). In addition, percentages of patients achieving VASI50 through Week 52 were assessed by affected body region (face [including nose and eyelids, but excluding lips, scalp, ears, and neck], head and neck [excluding face], trunk [including genitalia], upper extremities [including axillae], hands, lower extremities [including buttocks], and feet), and all body regions combined (separately analyzed including and excluding the face, because it is considered most responsive to repigmentation). Clinical signs of disease activity (confetti-like depigmentation, Koebner phenomenon, and/or hypochromic areas or borders) and therapeutic prognosis (leukotrichia) were also evaluated by body region at baseline and Week 52.

Statistical Analysis

For subgroup and body region analyses, missing values were imputed as nonresponders. Data were summarized using descriptive statistics, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. Only patients with baseline VASI > 0 for each body region were included in the respective body region analyses.

Efficacy of Povorcitinib by Subgroup

At Week 52, 34.0% (35/103) of patients who received any dose of povorcitinib from Day 1 achieved T-VASI50, 61.2% (63/103) achieved F-VASI50, and 45.6% (47/103) achieved F-VASI75 (Figs. 1, 2, 3). T-VASI50, F-VASI50, and F-VASI75 responses were observed across all demographic and clinical characteristic subgroups. Notably, response rates appeared similar between patients with Fitzpatrick skin types I–III and IV–VI (fairer vs darker skin), those with or without other autoimmune diseases, those with lesser or greater affected T-BSA (≤ 20% vs > 20%), or those with shorter or longer disease duration (< 10 vs ≥ 10 years). Numerically higher response rates (≥ 15% difference) were observed in select subgroups; however, these were inconsistent across T-VASI50, F-VASI50, and F-VASI75 endpoints. T-VASI50 response rates were numerically higher among female (41.0% [25/61]) versus male patients (23.8% [10/42]; Fig. 1). Trends toward higher F-VASI50 response rates were observed among patients with baseline F-BSA ≤ 1.5% (66.7% [46/69]) compared with F-BSA > 1.5% (50.0% [17/34]; Fig. 2). F-VASI75 response rates were numerically higher among patients who had received any prior NSV treatment (47.3% [44/93] vs 30.0% [3/10] of patients without prior NSV treatment; Fig. 3). Efficacy of Povorcitinib by Body Region

Among patients treated with povorcitinib from Day 1, VASI50 response rates increased steadily through 52 weeks across all body regions and mostly without plateau (Fig. 4). VASI50 score for all body regions (including face) was achieved by 7.8% (8/103) of patients at Week 24 and increased to 36.9% (38/103) at Week 52; VASI50 for all body regions (excluding face) was achieved by 8.7% (9/103) and 34.0% (35/103) of patients, respectively. The highest response rates were observed in the face and the head-and-neck regions, where more than half of patients achieved VASI50 responses at Week 52 (61.2% [63/103] and 57.3% [59/103], respectively). This was followed by improvements in the upper (40.8% [42/103]) and lower extremities (38.2% [39/102]) and the trunk (34.3% [35/102]). Response rates in traditionally challenging areas were also notable, with 37.0% (37/100) of patients achieving VASI50 on the hands and 25.8% (25/97) on the feet. Additionally, decreased rates of disease activity indicators and leukotrichia were evident across body regions at Week 52 versus baseline (Table 2). Discussion

Following 52 weeks of treatment with oral povorcitinib, many patients with extensive NSV achieved clinically meaningful repigmentation as measured by T-VASI50 (34.0%) and F-VASI75 (45.6%) responses [2, 8]. In this exploratory post hoc analysis, patients achieved meaningful improvements across subgroups defined by demographic and clinical characteristics, including those with longstanding and extensive disease and those with prior treatment history. There was some variability in treatment responses among certain patient subgroups, likely influenced by small sample sizes, underscoring the need for further investigation in larger, more balanced cohorts.

This analysis also showed improvements in repigmentation across affected body regions through 1 year of povorcitinib treatment, as indicated by VASI50 responses. The extent of repigmentation varied by anatomic location, with the highest response rates observed for the face and head-and-neck, areas known to be more responsive to therapy [4–6, 9]. Encouragingly, even in the repigmentation-resistant hands and feet [4, 5], VASI50 responses were achieved in 37.0% and 25.8% of patients, respectively. Notably, however, VASI scoring does not provide granularity regarding repigmentation in specific areas of the hands and feet that are known to be highly treatment resistant, such as the inner wrists and tips of the fingers and toes.

Vitiligo is a chronic condition, and the repigmentation process usually takes several months to years and varies depending on the location of the depigmentation [10]. This process begins with suppression of the immune response (i.e., halting melanocyte loss), followed by activation of melanocyte precursors, mainly located in the hair follicle bulge and infundibulum, which proliferate, migrate to the epidermis, and differentiate into melanocytes to repopulate and repigment the skin [11, 12]. Repigmentation is often more limited or delayed in nonfacial body regions with low hair follicle density, or regions with leukotrichia, indicating depletion of follicular melanocyte precursors [11, 12]. These observations underscore the importance of managing patient expectations and encouraging adherence to long-term therapy to achieve successful outcomes [13, 14]. Notably, the absence of a repigmentation plateau at Week 52 in this study suggests that further improvement may be achievable with continued therapy beyond 1 year. Although repigmentation may be accelerated with the addition of phototherapy in some patients [15, 16], a commitment to consistent, long-term treatment remains critical.

Limitations of these analyses include the exploratory post hoc design, small sample sizes in some subgroups despite pooling of povorcitinib treatment groups, use of descriptive analyses, and lack of a comparator arm, which limit meaningful interpretation and generalizability. Including only patients who were randomized to and continued povorcitinib in the extension-evaluable population may have introduced selection bias. Moreover, these analyses were based on clinical trial data, which may not be generalizable to real-world clinical settings. The trends identified here require confirmation in studies evaluating larger real-world patient populations. Finally, because the analysis includes outcomes up to Week 52, longer-term follow-up is needed to better understand the upper limits and durability of repigmentation with prolonged povorcitinib treatment.

Conclusion

Findings from this exploratory post hoc analysis highlight the potential efficacy of oral povorcitinib across a range of subgroups, with many patients of varying demographics and clinical characteristics achieving repigmentation indicative of treatment success (i.e., T-VASI50 and F-VASI75) [2, 8]. Importantly, repigmentation per VASI50 response was observed across all body regions. Although repigmentation rates varied by body region (e.g., slower for nonfacial and distal body regions), continued improvement was observed without plateau in most body areas and may increase further with longer duration treatment. These findings may be helpful in managing realistic expectations among patients and their caregivers, ultimately promoting adherence to long-term treatment.

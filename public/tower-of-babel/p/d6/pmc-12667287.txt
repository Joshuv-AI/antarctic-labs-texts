Upholding integrity and advancing innovation: development of scientific journals at the Institute of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences
Authors: Qun Yan, Lei Cai, Wen Wu, Hongyu Chen, Wei Qian, George Fu Gao
Published: 2025

For scientific research, scientific journals serve as both the “tail” and “head” of the Chinese Secred Dragon (Loong), thus contributing significantly in advancing knowledge and fostering innovation (Lu 1990; Yan et al. 2024, 2025). They are essential in science communication, not only for summarising existing knowledge but also for inspiring new ideas that drive scientific progress and contribute to socioeconomic development and human health.

Despite their small size, microorganisms significantly affect the studies on agriculture, environment, industry, and medical sciences. Established on 3 December 1958 through the merger of two Chinese Academy of Sciences institutes—the Institute of Applied Mycology and the Beijing Laboratory of Microbiology—the Institute of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (IMCAS) has been committed to advancing all aspects of microbial research for nearly seven decades. The former President of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Yongxiang Lu, inscribed the following vision for the IMCAS: “Microbes, high-tech, and big business” (Figure 1). This vision underscores the indispensable role of microorganisms in advancing fundamental life sciences and medical research as well as their significant influence on industrial development (Anon. 2005; Gao 2022). Adhering to this vision, the IMCAS has conducted sophisticated investigations on microbial taxonomy and evolution, synthetic biology, plant pathology, pathogen infection, and immunity. By advancing breakthroughs in fundamental theories and core technologies, the IMCAS fosters the growth of emerging industries and contributes significantly to achieving high-level self-reliance and self-improvement in science and technology.

Notably, the IMCAS has prioritised the development of scientific journals. It has sponsored seven scientific journals in collaboration with academic societies. These include four Chinese-language titles, i.e., Acta Microbiologica Sinica (微生物学报, launched in 1953), Microbiology China (微生物学通报, launched in 1974), Mycosystema (菌物学报, launched in 1982), and Chinese Journal of Biotechnology (生物工程学报, launched in 1985). In recent years, three new international English-language journals have been successfully launched: Mycology (launched in 2010), mLife (launched in 2022), and hLife (launched in 2023) (Figure 2). Among these journals, Acta Microbiologica Sinica boasts an impressive 70-year publication history—even predating the establishment of the IMCAS. Additionally, Microbiology China is the nation’s only continuously published microbiology journal with over half a century of scholarly contributions. Meanwhile, the youngest member of this group, Chinese Journal of Biotechnology, has maintained an active presence for more than four decades. The three English-language journals, which were established within the past 15 years, reflect the institute’s commitment to global scientific exchange and data sharing. Collectively, these journals form a complementary academic communication platforms that support domestic research and foster international collaboration.

Prior to the 1990s, China’s scientific achievements were predominantly published in domestic journals. Breakthroughs such as the artificial synthesis of crystalline bovine insulin and the discovery of artemisinin were initially published in Chinese journals (Niu et al. 1964; Artemisinin Structure Research Collaboration 1977). Similarly, significant microbiological research has been published in IMCAS journals. For example, the article of Yin et al. (1980), “Two-step fermentation process for vitamin C production,” published in Acta Microbiologica Sinica, won the Second Prize of the National Invention Award in 1983. The related patent was licensed to Roche for $5.5 million in 1985, marking a pivotal moment in the development of Chinese biotechnology. Representative results for the Streptomyces lactis peptide project were published in the Chinese Journal of Biotechnology (Jia et al. 1998), and the progress won the National Science and Technology Progress Award (Second Class). Meanwhile, the discovery of the new fungal order Umbilicariales (lichen-forming fungi) was first published in English by Chinese researchers in Mycosystema on 22 February 2007. This taxonomic proposal has subsequently gained international recognition under the Principle of Priority in Fungal Nomenclature (Wei 2021).

In the 1990s, China gradually acts as one of the major contributors in most aspects of scientific studies in the world. Chinese research institutions then adopted a scientific evaluation system based on journals impact factor, thus prompting researchers to publish their results in international journals. This fact, coupled with a shortage of influential English-language journals in China, weakened the domestic publishing industry and diminished China’s voice in the global scientific discourse. In 2006, the Project for Enhancing International Impact of China STM Journals (PIIJ) was initiated by the China Association for Science and Technology (CAST) to provide extensive support and fund to enhance the quality and impact of China-based scientific journals. Since 2013, the CAST has collaborated with other departments and ministries to formulate plans for supporting the internationalisation of scientific journals in China, from financial expenditures to policies, such as the “China Science and Technology Journal Excellence Action Plan”. The number of high-quality journals in China, particularly new English-language journals, has continued to increase. Not only have these journals significantly enhanced China’s international academic influence but also greatly improved the journal development ecosystem, leading to an unprecedented increase in confidence within China’s scientific journal community. This strengthens China’s role in promoting global sound governance.

To advance microbiology both domestically and globally while addressing health and wellness challenges, the IMCAS launched three English-language journals: Mycology (An et al. 2010), mLife (Huang and Zhou 2022), and hLife (Gao et al. 2023). Mycology, which was initiated by Professors Xingzhong Liu and Lei Cai in 2010, leverages the IMCAS’s leading fungal research. Expanding beyond fungi, mLife was launched to encompass a broader field of microbiology. hLife was established to promote biomedical research by bridging laboratory and clinical studies, with emphasis on translational and transformative medicine, which coincided with the United Nations’ adoption of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). For instance, hLife serves as a platform for academic exchange, it publishes not only scientific findings such as Articles (Song et al. 2023) or Letters (Liu et al. 2023) but also shares stories pertaining to the research and its practitioners. Through innovative sections such as “Dialogue” (Dong and Cooper 2023) and “History” (Wu et al. 2025), hLife enhances disciplinary popularisation. These three journals have obtained widespread attention in scientific community and received unanimous praise from scientific peers.

Recently, Chinese scientific journals—particularly English-language ones—have experienced unprecedented growth. However, establishing world-class scientific journals in China demands equal commitment from both English- and Chinese-language journals, as each serves a distinct yet complementary role in global knowledge exchange. Developing high-quality Chinese-language scientific journals is not merely an additional step but a strategic necessity, i.e., a “foundational mission” for safeguarding China’s scientific discourse and cultural identity.

Guided by the principle of balancing integrity with innovation to promote coordinated development, the IMCAS’s journal initiatives remain committed to addressing national strategic priorities. By enhancing academic quality and social impact, the IMCAS continues to contribute to the high-level development of scientific journals.

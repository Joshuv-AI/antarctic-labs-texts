Research on strategies for enhancing system security resilience of prefabricated building engineering
Authors: Xiaogang Song, Zehao Li, Yaohua Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the construction industry’s deepening development towards digitalization, intelligentization, greening, and industrialization, prefabricated building is poised for significant promotion due to its characteristics of energy efficiency, environmental friendliness, rapid progress, and high quality. Current safety management models for prefabricated construction primarily focus on preventing safety accidents, lacking a holistic, systematic analysis of the system’s comprehensive capability when subjected to risk disturbances. This study adopts a resilience perspective, focusing on the safety resilience of prefabricated construction engineering systems. Utilizing accident case studies and academic literature as sources, it preliminarily identifies 24 influencing factors of safety resilience based on the four types of resilience. The rationality of the indicator system is validated through confirmatory factor analysis, including structural validity, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Based on the screened and validated safety resilience indicator system, a Bayesian Network model for the safety resilience of prefabricated construction engineering is established. Through forward causal inference, backward diagnostic inference, and sensitivity analysis within the Bayesian Network model, critical influencing pathways and sensitive nodes are identified. Targeted countermeasures and suggestions to enhance the safety resilience of prefabricated construction engineering systems are proposed. This approach can effectively improve the system’s risk resistance capability and post-disaster rapid recovery capacity, thereby strengthening its overall safety resilience.Keywords: Prefabricated building, System security, Safety resilience, Bayesian networkSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Compared to traditional construction methods, prefabricated construction offers distinct advantages in energy efficiency, environmental sustainability, accelerated project timelines, and enhanced quality control. It significantly reduces labor requirements and mitigates safety risks1,2. The development of digital technology has enabled information transmission and collaborative interaction in the design, manufacturing, assembly, and operation and maintenance of building components, achieving precise geometric control and effectively improving the quality, progress, and environmental benefits of prefabricated buildings3,4.The imperative for green and sustainable development has provided clearer strategic direction for the construction industry, accelerating the adoption and advancement of prefabricated building systems.However, inherent limitations in design sophistication and construction techniques associated with prefabricated construction have led to the emergence of novel safety management challenges5,6. Consequently, conventional safety and risk prevention paradigms are increasingly inadequate for addressing the safety management requirements of prefabricated construction engineering7.In recent years, safety resilience theory—an interdisciplinary conceptual framework emerging from multiple fields has been progressively applied across diverse domains including urban planning, ecological management, disaster risk reduction, and socio-economic systems, yielding substantial research advancements8. A systematic investigation of safety issues through the lens of resilience theory offers a novel methodological approach for safety management in prefabricated construction engineering.

Currently, domestic and international scholars predominantly investigate safety management in prefabricated construction from a risk perspective, with research adopting a resilience lens remaining relatively scarce. Regarding safety management and evaluation in prefabricated construction: Song LL identified safety risk factors through expert interviews and validated the efficient propagation and diffusion of these risks9.Fard MM, et al. analyzed 125 prefabricated construction-related accidents, finding that safety assessments during the manufacturing and on-site phases within prefabricated construction departments can serve as a starting point for enhancing safety performance10.Song YH et al. evaluated a lifting operation risk model for prefabricated buildings based on the Human Factors Analysis and Classification System (HFACS)11.Yang and Li (2023) utilized the bidirectional inference capability of Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) to identify critical risk factors leading to lifting accidents12.Li and Zhang (2023) et al. conducted quantitative analysis and assessment of safety risk factors using a Bayesian Network (BN) algorithm, improving the identification and analysis capabilities for safety risks in prefabricated construction projects13.He and Tian (2023) applied the Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) method to determine the importance ranking of safety risk factors based on the analysis of numerous prefabricated construction accident cases14.Li and Chen (2022) developed a safety evaluation model for prefabricated construction using an improved Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) method based on entropy-weighted Best-Worst Method (BWM)15.Wang, et al.(2022) performed safety risk assessments for prefabricated construction based on the IM-FCM method16.Fan et al. (2022) conducted safety risk assessment research on prefabricated construction models using game theory-regret theory17.Wang, et al.(2022) carried out safety risk analysis for lifting operations in prefabricated construction based on System-Theoretic Process Analysis (STPA) and fuzzy Bayesian Networks18.Chang, Yan, and Li (2018) constructed system dynamics flowcharts for prefabricated construction safety risks to facilitate safety risk analysis and evaluation19.Bao developed a safety control system and employed evaluation methods to verify its effectiveness for safety management in prefabricated construction20.Regarding system safety resilience: Trinh, MT et al. examined the interactive effects of safety resilience culture and project complexity on safety performance in construction projects21.Shi-Zhi Chen et al. proposed a Prior Knowledge-integrated Neural Network (PKNN) to achieve efficient structural seismic response prediction and low-computational-cost seismic safety assessment, enhancing system safety resilience22. De-Cheng Feng et al. proposed a rapid assessment method for overall damage level of buildings after earthquakes based on component images and deep learning, improving safety recovery resilience23.Song (2023) et al. introduced resilience theory into the safety operation management of water conservancy engineering projects24.Sun (2020) et al., utilizing factor analysis, found that information management plays the most significant role in enhancing engineering safety resilience throughout the entire accident process, particularly when the complex engineering system (composed of human, material, and informational components) is subjected to disturbances from the external environment25.Tang (2023) et al. conducted a safety resilience evaluation of metro station fires based on an improved variable-weight matter-element extension (MVMEE) model, incorporating dimensions such as “4M1E”26.

Based on the above analysis, few scholars have conducted a systematic analysis of the whole-process safety management of prefabricated buildings from the perspective of resilience. This study, grounded in the perspective of resilience, introduces the theory of safety resilience into the safety management model of prefabricated building projects, exploring its safety resilience during implementation27.This theoretically enriches the safety management of prefabricated building projects and effectively enables prefabricated building projects to better predict, respond to, quickly recover from, and learn from risk disturbances in future safety management.

Methods

Literature review method, case analysis method, and Delphi method

The literature review method and case analysis method involve analyzing recent accident cases involving casualties in prefabricated construction projects, and combining this with relevant literature and management standards on safety management of prefabricated construction projects, to identify factors affecting safety resilience.This study initially conducted a comprehensive and systematic analysis of prefabricated construction-related literature, national standards, and safety accident case studies. Through this process, manifestations of risk disturbances within prefabricated construction engineering systems were identified, culminated in the compilation of preliminary safety resilience influencing factors. Then, through the Delphi method, a Delphi expert group consisting of 22 experts and scholars with at least 3 years of work experience in construction project management, construction site management, safety management, etc., from construction units, design units, construction units, engineering consulting units, and research institutes, was invited to rate the importance of influencing factors using the Likert five-point scale. This Delphi method underwent two rounds of scoring. In the first round, survey questionnaires were distributed to the experts, asking them to rate the influencing factors. In the second round, the results from the first round were summarized and fed back to the experts, inviting them to make further choices and judgments.

Confirmatory factor analysis

CFA (Confirmatory Factor Analysis) is a statistical methodology employed to validate the structural relationships within theoretical models, specifically examining the associations between latent constructs and their observed indicator variables. Unlike exploratory approaches, CFA tests a priori theoretical assumptions regarding measurement structures, focusing on assessing the goodness-of-fit of specified theoretical models. In this research, CFA is utilized to empirically validate the structural rationality of the identified safety resilience influencing factors.

To empirically validate the rationality of the safety resilience indicator system, this study employed a questionnaire survey method to conduct Confirmatory Factor Analysis on the preliminarily identified indicators. The resilience categories were treated as latent constructs, while the influencing factors served as observed variables. Systematically designed measurement items, assessed using a five-point Likert scale to gauge perceived impact levels, formed the core of the questionnaire.The survey specifically targeted practicing professionals engaged in construction project management, on-site construction management, and safety management roles within: Construction developers, Design institutes, Contracting organizations, Supervision companies, Related entities.Utilizing a combined online and offline distribution approach, 274 questionnaires were successfully collected. Following data screening, 252 valid responses were retained, yielding a high valid response rate of 91.97%.

Bayesian network

BN (Bayesian Network) stands as one of the most effective theoretical models for uncertain knowledge representation and probabilistic reasoning. In this study, we employ Bayesian networks to assess the safety resilience of prefabricated construction engineering systems. By conducting bidirectional reasoning analysis and sensitivity analysis using the established Bayesian network model, we ultimately identify key influencing factors.

Establishment of bayesian network model structure

After obtaining a “validated” measurement model structure, this study converts the CFA measurement model into a Bayesian network topology. In the BN structure, “Prefabricated Construction Engineering System Safety Resilience” is taken as the parent node in the BN, the observed resilience influencing factors are taken as the root nodes in the BN, the latent variable safety resilience index is taken as the intermediate node in the BN, and the paths from the latent variables to the observed indicators are taken as directed edges between nodes in the BN. Netica6.08 (https://www.norsys.com/netica.html) is used to establish the BN model structure, resulting in the BN topology diagram. In Bayesian network models, the direction of arcs between nodes is preset by existing theoretical literature. The statistical validation results of CFA support the validity of this theoretical assumption on this dataset, thus providing a statistical basis for transforming these measurement relationships into arcs in BN.This diagram precisely represents the interrelationships between the influencing factors within the indicator system and their corresponding resilience categories. The computational inference mechanism of the Bayesian Network reflects and propagates probabilistic relationships among the nodes.

Determination of bayesian network model parameters

Owing to the scarcity and limited accessibility of empirical data on safety resilience in prefabricated construction engineering systems, this study primarily employed an expert questionnaire survey. Expert knowledge and experience were leveraged to establish prior probabilities for root nodes and conditional probability tables (CPTs) for intermediate and leaf nodes within the Bayesian Network.Due to the strong professionalism of this survey questionnaire, the quality of the recipients is more important than the quantity of questionnaires distributed.Survey participants were strategically selected: Priority was given to frontline practitioners directly involved in prefabricated construction projects within contracting organizations.Senior professionals from project developers, design institutes, construction contractors, and supervision companies were also systematically consulted.

Of the 74 questionnaires distributed,27 were deemed invalid based primarily on inconsistent response patterns, supplemented by exclusion criteria of work experience under one year or insufficient domain-specific knowledge.Ultimately, 47 valid questionnaires were collected, with a response rate calculated as 63.51%, satisfying the empirical criterion of “having at least 5–10 observation samples under each parent node combination”.Among the 47 respondents surveyed, over two-thirds have more than four years of work experience, and over half are members of the management team of their respective organizations, meeting the quality requirements for questionnaire samples.Through statistical aggregation and expert evaluation of the collected data, Prior probabilities for all safety resilience influencing factors were determined. These priors were subsequently used to compute conditional probabilities for intermediate and leaf nodes.This empirically derived probability framework provides robust data support for subsequent analysis of safety resilience influence pathways.

Analysis of bayesian network model

Bayesian network security resilience prior probability analysis

In the Netica 6.08(https://www.norsys.com/netica.html), edit the states of each node. For instance, the state indicating whether a safety resilience-influencing factor is active can be represented by two options: “Yes” for active and “No” for inactive. After creating all nodes, input the prior probability parameters derived from questionnaire analysis. Once data entry is complete, utilize the Netica 6.08(https://www.norsys.com/netica.html) to compute the probabilities for each node within the model, resulting in a prior probability diagram.The prior probability diagram indicates that within the constructed Bayesian network, the predicted resilience (PR) of the prefabricated building construction system has a probability of 51.0% of being active. The response resilience (RR) has a probability of 48.6% of being active, the rapid recovery resilience (FR) has a probability of 69.1% of being active, and the learning resilience (LR) has a probability of 69.2% of being active. The overall safety resilience of the prefabricated building construction system has a probability of 65.6% of being active.

Analysis of the prior probability results indicates that the current operational level of safety resilience within existing prefabricated building construction systems is relatively low. Among the four resilience dimensions, rapid recovery resilience (FR) and learning resilience (LR) demonstrate relatively commendable performance. Conversely, response resilience (RR) exhibits the lowest probability of being active (48.6%), signifying that the system’s resistance capability requires the most significant enhancement.This suggests that, at the current stage, when risk disturbances occur during the implementation of prefabricated building construction, the site demonstrates an ability to respond swiftly to emergent hazardous situations, conduct repairs, and restore work areas to normal operations rapidly. However, the system’s capacity to resist incidents and disasters is weak; it fails to effectively withstand initial impacts. Consequently, enhancements to the system’s robustness, redundancy, and managerial agility are imperative.Furthermore, the probability of predicted resilience (PR) being active is also comparatively low (51.0%). This implies that the system inadequately perceives changes in its internal and external environment. Its ability to proactively identify vulnerabilities within subsystems prior to the occurrence of natural disasters or safety incidents, and implement preemptive protective measures to mitigate the likelihood or severity of system damage, is insufficient.

This signifies that considerable room for improvement exists for numerous safety resilience influencing factors within the prefabricated building construction process.

Analysis of posterior probability diagnostic reasoning in bayesian networks

The functionality of Bayesian networks in diagnostic reasoning lies in their capacity to inferentially calculate the probability of occurrence for each node. This is achieved by analyzing the impact of the known probability of a specific node occurring on the probability changes of other related nodes, demonstrating their efficacy in providing decision support under uncertain conditions. This tool enables reverse inference, tracing from known outcomes back to potential causes. It is extensively applied in critical domains such as risk quantification, intelligent decision-making, and anomaly detection. Through iterative feedback and refinement, users can significantly enhance model performance, thereby furnishing more precise and reliable data support for the decision-making process. For instance, in the Netica 6.08(https://www.norsys.com/netica.html), setting the probability of the “not activated” state for leaf nodes to 100% yields the posterior probability maps.

In the Predictive Resilience (PR) module, when the probability of failure for the safety resilience of the prefabricated construction engineering system is set to 100%, the probability of failure for the safety inspection of material stacking at location PRW1 increased from 59.3 to 62.7%, representing a 3.4% increase. This node exhibited the largest magnitude change within the module, signifying that it demonstrates the highest volatility among all assessed parameters. These changes in probability values indicate this node is the most unstable contributor in the system.This analysis demonstrates that enhancing the frequency of regular safety inspections for material stacking constitutes a highly effective preventive measure for both identifying potential hazards and mitigating associated risks, enabling the proactive forecasting of potential safety incidents. To effectively manage this critical vulnerability, project leadership should Intensify the frequency of routine safety production meetings and heighten safety awareness among personnel responsible for safety management and material handling.In addition, project leaders should implement standardized and regular inspection protocols for the storage conditions of all delivered materials.

Insufficient proficiency coupled with a lack of experience in handling diverse contingencies substantially elevates the risk of precipitating mechanical hazardsduring practical operations.To address this vulnerability, project management must mandate strict compliance from subcontractors responsible for prefabricated component manufacturing and on-site personnel by ensuring Comprehensive development and implementation of standard operating procedures (SOPs) for every piece of heavy machinery and rigorous verification that all workers demonstrate thorough mastery and consistent application of these procedures.

In the Learning Resilience (LR) module, when the probability of failure for the safety resilience of the prefabricated construction engineering system is set to 100%, the probability of failureassociated with the proactive enhancement of personnel safety awareness at location LRR1 increased from 33.0 to 39.8%, representing a significant rise of 6.8% points.Consequently, the proactive enhancement of safety awareness constitutes the most direct mechanism for improving learning resilience. OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration in the United States), HSE (Health and Safety Executive in the United Kingdom), and China’s “Standard for Safety Management of Construction Site” all provide extensive details on the requirements for stacking height, spacing, and support of various materials (such as steel bars, blocks, and formwork). The existence of these mandatory provisions is itself an acknowledgment by the industry of the high importance of the risk point of “material stacking”. Elevating the “PRW1” node from an analytical variable to the level of mandatory enforcement by regulations demonstrates its fundamental status. Standards worldwide (such as China’s “Regulations on Safety Management of High-Risk Divisional and Sub-divisional Works”) all mandate the preparation of specialized construction plans and the organization of expert demonstrations. The regulations explicitly make “expert demonstration” a prerequisite for the commencement of such projects, which directly confirms that “expert demonstration of specialized construction plans (PRF3)” is a key decision node in preventing systemic collapse. Therefore, it is highly reasonable to consider PRW1 (safety inspection of material stacking) and PRF3 (expert demonstration of specialized construction plans) as the most critical root nodes for predicting safety resilience.The project department should place special emphasis on material management and the review of construction plans for sub-divisional works with high risks, in order to mitigate safety risks at their source.

Conclusions

To enhance the system safety resilience of prefabricated construction projects, this study identified 24 influencing factors of system safety resilience in prefabricated buildings through literature review, case analysis, and Delphi method, and conducted a confirmatory factor analysis on these factors. Based on the results of the factor analysis, a Bayesian network model structure was established, and a sensitivity analysis was conducted on the influencing factors to identify key influencing factors. Based on the results and discussion, the following conclusions were drawn:

To enhance predictive resilience, the project department should prioritize safety inspections of material stacking and expert verification of specialized construction plans. Regarding safety inspections of material stacking, the project department should establish a management organizational system and management regulations, clarify responsible entities and job responsibilities, arrange full-time safety management personnel to conduct on-site inspections according to safety management regulations, and track and recheck potential safety hazards throughout the entire process. For expert verification of specialized construction plans, it is necessary to strictly control the expert members, approval, and execution processes of the verification, ensuring that the content of the specialized plan is complete and feasible, that the calculation sheets, verification bases, and construction drawings in the specialized plan comply with standards and specifications, and that the specialized construction plan can fit the actual situation on site.

To enhance predictive resilience, the project department should prioritize safety inspections of material stacking and expert verification of specialized construction plans. Regarding safety inspections of material stacking, the project department should establish a management organizational system and management regulations, clarify responsible entities and job responsibilities, arrange full-time safety management personnel to conduct on-site inspections according to safety management regulations, and track and recheck potential safety hazards throughout the entire process. For expert verification of specialized construction plans, it is necessary to strictly control the expert members, approval, and execution processes of the verification, ensuring that the content of the specialized plan is complete and feasible, that the calculation sheets, verification bases, and construction drawings in the specialized plan comply with standards and specifications, and that the specialized construction plan can fit the actual situation on site.

To enhance response resilience, the focus should be on the operational skill level of workers and the configuration of project safety protection facilities. To improve the operational skill level of workers, the project department should attach importance to pre-job training and continuing education, allowing workers to be familiar with laws and regulations, technical standards, and normative documents, and to master various safety operation procedures and protective measures. At the same time, dynamic safety supervision should be implemented in combination with information technology such as the Internet of Things. For project safety protection facilities, the construction of prefabricated building projects and the construction of safety protection facilities should be carried out simultaneously and strictly implemented. In the event of a safety accident, safety protection facilities will minimize the casualty rate and direct economic losses to the greatest extent;

Based on the theory of safety resilience, this study enriches the dimensions of safety management research in prefabricated construction engineering and provides new ideas for analyzing and effectively controlling safety risks in prefabricated buildings. However, there are still limitations in the research. (1)given the challenges of data collection in prefabricated construction projects, this study adopted the method of designing a questionnaire to estimate the parameters required to construct a Bayesian network. This process is inevitably constrained by the personal biases of experts. In future research, integrating historical monitoring data will enable a more precise evaluation of resilience indicators, thus providing a more objective and comprehensive quantitative method.(2)Although the BN structure in this study is grounded in a robust theoretical framework, the causal relationships it presents are primarily theory-driven, rather than purely data-driven. The role of CFA is to verify that observed variables can reliably represent underlying concepts, thus laying a solid foundation for constructing the BN model. Future research could collect data at different time points to more rigorously test these causal paths, or employ experimental methods to manipulate key variables to provide stronger causal evidence.

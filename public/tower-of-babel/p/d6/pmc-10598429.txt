Effect of interleukin‐12 gene expression on insulin resistance in patients with acne vulgaris
Authors: Ahmed Ibrahim AbdElneam, Mohammed Saleh Al‐Dhubaibi, Saleh Salem Bahaj, Ghada Farouk Mohammed, Ahmed Kaid Alantry, Lina M Atef
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInsulin resistance (IR) is a condition where cells become resistant to insulin, causing impaired glucose uptake and increased blood glucose levels. Interleukin‐12 (IL‐12), a cytokine, regulates the immune system. High levels of IL‐12 can lead to chronic inflammation, exacerbate resistance to insulin, and contribute to type 2 diabetes. Also, link IR to acne vulgaris (AV), as it reduces tissue sensitivity to insulin, causing increased insulin levels and sebum production, which can contribute to acne development.AimTo explore the role of IL‐12 gene expression on IR in AV patients and to study the role of IL‐12 gene in the development of AV.Subjects and MethodsA case–control study was performed on 68 AV patients and 68 healthy controls. The biochemical analysis included fasting glucose, fasting insulin, (HOMA‐IR), and serum IL‐12 level. IL‐12 gene expression was performed by quantitative real‐time PCR for both two groups. In addition, folding change was calculated by using the standard 2–(∆∆Ct) method.ResultIL‐12 level, IL‐12 folding change, fasting insulin, and IR were all increased in acne patients. A highly significant linear correlation was found between IL‐12 folding change and both IL‐12 levels and IR. There is a substantial positive significant simple linear association between IL‐12 level and IL‐12 folding change, as well as IR and IL‐12 folding change, in moderate and severe acne.ConclusionIL‐12 gene has an important role in IR and the development of acne in AV patients.Keywords: acne vulgaris, IL‐12 gene expression, insulin resistance, serum interleukin‐12

INTRODUCTION

Acne vulgaris (AV) is a long‐term, inflammatory skin condition that affects the pilosebaceous unit. 1 According to estimates, it has a 9.4% global prevalence, making it the eighth most common disease in the world. 2 , 3

Insulin resistance (IR) is a condition in which cells in the body become resistant to the effects of insulin, leading to impaired glucose uptake and increased blood glucose levels. 4 Interleukin‐12 (IL‐12), on the other hand, is a cytokine that is involved in regulating the immune system. 5

Researchers suggest that there may be a relationship between IR and serum IL‐12. They indicated that high levels of IL‐12 can lead to chronic inflammation, which may be an important factor in IR. 6

Furthermore, IR also is linked to high levels of many inflammatory markers. 7 These markers have been shown to promote the production of IL‐12, which can exacerbate IR. 8

There is some evidence to suggest that AV and IR have an association. IR is a condition in which the body's tissues become less sensitive to insulin, the hormone that helps regulate blood sugar levels. 9

IR can lead to an increase in insulin levels in the blood, which in turn can stimulate the production of androgen hormones 10 leading to an increase in sebum production. Increased sebum production can contribute to the development of acne. 11

Several studies have suggested that IL‐12 may be involved in the development of IR. 11 , 12 , 13 However, more researches are needed to fully understand the relationship between these two conditions. Therefore, our main aim is to explore the role of IL‐12 gene expression in IR in patients with AV.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Subjects

This study is a case–control study that involved 68 AV patients and 68 healthy controls. The Shaqra University Ethics Committee accepted the study, which has the agreement reference number ERC_SU_20220088, and all participants provided written consent.

The study included a detailed medical history of both AV patients and controls, which included information on demographic characteristics, acne history, and medication use. The acne condition was classified according to severity and based on the number of comedones and inflammatory lesions. 14

Inclusion criteria: This study included patients aged 15 to 45 of any gender with AV patients who did not receive acne treatment for at least the past six weeks, and those who have not responded well to typical topical or systemic antibiotic treatments.

Exclusion criteria: This study excluded pregnancy, breastfeeding, use of oral contraceptives, or being postmenopausal. Patients with conditions that affect other systemic diseases, those with chronic systemic diseases, those on regular medication for other diseases, and those who have taken oral isotretinoin within the last three months.

RNA EXTRACTION GENE EXPRESSION

Purification and extraction of RNA

Total ribonucleic acid (RNA) was isolated from blood samples utilizing a Qiagen QIAamp RNA Blood Mini Kit (Catalog number: 52 304), as well as the level of purification of the collected RNA was determined utilizing a Nanodropper 2000 (ThermoScientific).

Synthesis of cDNA

RNA reversed into cDNA with cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit from Invitrogen, Life Sciences.

Quantitative real‐time PCR

Maxima SYBER Green Q PCR Master Mix is used for real‐time quantitative PCR. After the initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 min, the PCR consisted of 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 45 s, annealing at 57°C for 45 s, and extension at 72°C for 45 s. The data was normalized based on the level of expression of the housekeeping gene beta‐actin. The beta‐actin primer sequence is Forward primer 5′: TGTATGAAGGCTTTTGGTCTCC‐3′, Reverse primer 5′: TGTATGAAGGCTTTTGGTCTCC‐3′ −3′ CTGGTCTCAAGTCAGTGTACAGGT. IL‐12 priming 5′: AAAGGACATCTGCGAGGAAAGTTC‐3′ and opposite 5′: CGAGGTGAGGTGCGTTTATGC‐3′. 15

Magnitude of changing with mRNA expression toward candidate genes was calculated by utilizing the standard 2–(∆∆Ct) process. 16

BIOCHEMICAL ANALYSIS

Serum glucose determination using the kit supplied by Bio System, Spain. The measurement of insulin was carried out using an ELISA kit, which was provided by Demeditec, a German. 17

The homeostasis‐based model for assessing IR (HOMA‐IR) is used to estimate IR. IR = fasting insulin (mlU/L) X fasting glucose (mmol/L)/22.5. 18 Serum IL‐12 levels estimation using enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) technique IL‐12 p70 Human ELISA Kit—Invitrogen–ThermoFisher.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

The data was analyzed by the Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) software version 16 (Chicago, IL, USA). The accuracy of IL‐12 expression, IR, fasting glucose, and fasting insulin levels was assessed with receiver operating characteristics (ROC curve). The best cutoff values of IL‐12 expression, IR, fasting glucose, and fasting insulin selected with maximum sensitivity and specificity for prediction. Criteria of qualification for the area under the curve (AUC) were as follows: 0.90−1 = excellent, 0.80−0.90 = good, 0.70−0.80 = fair 0.60−0.70 = poor, and 0.50−0.60 = fail. All tests were two‐sided. A P‐value below 0.05 is considered significant. Independent t‐tests were used for comparison between two groups, and the Chi‐square test was for comparison between two or more groups of categorical data. One‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test was used to compare more than two groups with multiple comparisons, Person correlation coefficient test was used to examine the linear relationship between different continues data, (+) sign indicates a positive correlation and (−) sign indicates a negative correlation.

DISCUSSION

Acne is an inflammatory condition and a component of many systemic diseases or syndromes linked to IR. 19

Epidermal keratinocytes express insulin and insulin growth factor receptors, and hyperinsulinemia results in an increase in keratinocyte proliferation and failure of follicular corneocyte terminal differentiation, which contribute to the pathogenesis of acne. 20 , 21 Insulin also stimulates the synthesis of androgens and increases sebum production, both of which are correlated with the severity of acne. 20 , 21

Since high body mass index (BMI) is a component of metabolic syndrome, AV patients exhibit an increase in the level of serum glucose and insulin as well as IR. 22 , 23

IL‐12 is a cytokine that can be induced by Corynebacterium acne through Toll‐like receptor 2 (TLR2) which causes human monocytes to release IL‐12, which activate production of IFN‐γ. 24

In inflammatory conditions such as acne, inflammatory mediators such as cytokines can induce IR through the activation of insulin receptor substrates (IRS) kinase through the activation of p38MAPK. 25

Although there are a lot of publications on the relation between acne and IL‐12 and about acne and IR the role of the IL‐12 gene expression and its role in IR in acne have not been studied or elucidated.

Various factors contribute to the predisposition of AV and the severity of skin symptoms. Genetics being one of the most significant factors many genes were studied in relation to acne presentation and severity as Apo B‐48 gene expression. 26 Many genes were studied including tumor necrosis factor (TNF) family, Interleukin family, and cytochrome p450 gene families from the interleukin family like IL‐1A, IL‐1B, IL‐4.IL‐6, IL‐8, IL‐10, IL‐17A, and IL‐17F have been studied by Heng et al. 27

Nevertheless, IL‐12 gene has not been studied in relation to acne and acne severity, so we have tried in our study to highlight the role of IL‐12 gene expression in AV patients and its relation to IR in these patients.

Our results indicated that acne patients' IL‐12 gene expression was with good diagnostic accuracy in predicting AV patients with severe AV patients have greater levels of IL‐12.

These findings suggested the possible role of IL‐12 and IL‐12 gene expression in the pathogenesis of the disease, how that proinflammatory cytokine can affect our immunity and cause many changes on the level of the pilosebaceous unit by causing more inflammation and increasing the severity of acne and inducing IR through receptors and signaling pathways.

In agreement with our results, a study by Marwa et al., found that serum levels of IL‐12 were significantly higher in patients with AV although they found no significant correlation between IL‐12 and acne severity in difference with our study, which shows a significant correlation between IL‐12 and acne severity. 28

Also, Mohamed et al., who performed a study on 27 AV patients and 27 matched controls showed significantly higher serum levels of IL‐12 than the control. They found a lack of correlation between IL‐12 levels and the severity of the disease; they suggested that IL‐12 is essential for the development of all inflammatory papules independent of the degree of severity. 29

Also, Murlistyarini et al., who studied on 68 patients with AV‐assessed serum IL‐12, IL‐17, and IL‐37 found no correlation between IL‐12 and disease severity, and they said that there is a possibility due to a considerable number of confounding factors that are unknown in that study. 30 Also, a study done by Sibero et al., who took 24 moderate AV patients and 39 severe AV patients with no mild patients found no significant difference in mean levels of IL‐12 level in moderate and severe AV patients but it was higher in severe acne compared with moderate acne. 31

Also, in our study, patients with severe AV had greater levels of IL‐12, IL‐12 folding change, fasting insulin, and IR with significant values. Moderate and severe AV has a significant positive simple linear correlation between IL‐12 and IL‐12 folding change, and IR and IL‐12 folding change. This result is in the same line with Nagpal and his colleagues who suggested that AV patients had, higher plasma glucose levels, and higher IR compared to controls but the overrepresentation of IR in severe acne compared to milder acne was not observed 32 , also Kartal et al., founded a positive relation between female acne and IR. 33

Del Prete et al., Balta et al.—with a study on post‐adolescent acne, and Emiro˘glu et al., who carried out the study on severe AV patients only, suggested that the patients' group had considerably higher fasting insulin levels than the control group. Furthermore, there was a highly significant difference in HOMA values between the patient and control groups and this explained that high insulin exacerbates acne by increasing the proliferation of basal keratinocytes and stimulating androgens, which cause sebum production, which correlates with acne severity. 22 , 34 , 35

On the other hand, a study done by Munichandrappa and his research team indicated that no significant correlation between acne and IR in the Indian population may be due to the small sample size and the bulk of patients with mild acne and different food habits in this population. 36

CONCLUSION

Our results might suggest a possible relation between IL‐12 gene expression and IR in acne in the Saudi patient population, further studies on large scales and different populations with different habits may be to group patients into obese and non‐obese patients to see if IL‐12 expression will differ in the two groups or not.

Serum IL‐12, IL‐12‐fold change, IR, and serum insulin levels were higher in AV. Severe AV have a positive relation between IL‐12 gene expression and IR.

AI-assisted treatment decisions for femoral neck fractures: a simulated assessment of ChatGPT-4’s accuracy and comprehensiveness
Authors: Jing Li, Boyuan Shi, Fangye Shi, Anan Sun, Jiyue Huang, Xitao Wu, Zhipeng Yao, Chengjian Wu, Lei Chen, Jiandong Yuan, Zhiqiang Xue
Published: 2025

Introduction

Femoral neck fractures (FNF) are prevalent and severe traumatic injuries, especially among older individuals. These fractures are characterized not only by high incidence but also by significant mortality and healthcare costs [1–3].The diversity of patients and the heterogeneity of their demographic characteristics complicate the decision-making process for treating these fractures. Traditionally, the decision-making process integrates clinical guidelines, the expertise of orthopedic surgeons, and various patient health factors [4–6]. However, the variability in patient outcomes necessitates more personalized and scientific decision-making methods. In different fields, AI based on natural language processing (NLP) has demonstrated remarkable capabilities, and its applications in healthcare are no exception [7, 8]. AI has become a transformative tool that improves diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis [9–11]. ChatGPT-4, a conversational AI based on GPT-4, utilizes reinforcement and supervised learning to understand and address complex user needs and intentions, including those related to medical queries [12].

As technology advances, medical knowledge has become more democratized and accessible. An increasing number of patients now rely on search engines and AI chatbots for medical information [13, 14], a trend that reduces dependence on traditional healthcare systems and sometimes even challenges physicians'recommendations. ChatGPT-4 is not specifically designed for healthcare and lacks domain-specific training [15, 16]. The illusion that ChatGPT-4 can generate seemingly reasonable but evidence-based treatment suggestions is particularly dangerous in specialized decision-making [17]. Existing studies have raised concerns about its accuracy in triage and diagnosis [9, 18]. Although AI holds substantial potential in healthcare, integrating it into clinical decision support systems, especially for decisions regarding FNF treatment, remains insufficiently studied. ChatGPT-4 can process vast amounts of data, clinical parameters, and demographic information to provide personalized treatment recommendations [19, 20]. Currently, research on the application of AI in FNF treatment decisions is limited, with a lack of evaluation reports on the accuracy of ChatGPT-4 recommendations in this field. This study aims to explore this issue.

Previous research primarily focused on single-option and closed-ended questions, failing to address the needs of individualized treatment decisions [21, 22]. In this study, orthopedic surgeons from various medical centers were recruited to conduct a preliminary assessment of ChatGPT-4 accuracy and comprehensiveness in responding to medical queries from simulated patients with FNF. Additionally, our study provides subjective, open-ended explanations to inform treatment decisions for FNF. Professional physicians rated the treatment recommendations and explanations generated by ChatGPT-4, allowing for an analysis of its limitations in producing medical information. This evaluation provides preliminary evidence for the reliability of ChatGPT-4 in clinical fracture treatment.

Methods

The study was undertaken in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, as well as follows the Standards for Reporting of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies (STARD 2015) guidelines. The ChatGPT-4 response portion of the study was completed by November 1, 2024. Responses were recorded after the initial query, with no follow-up or repeated queries, and data analysis was completed by February 20, 2025. As this study utilizes simulated patient data, it did not require review and approval by the Ethics Review Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University.

Simulated FNF patient

To simulate real-world clinical scenarios of FNF patients, we constructed profiles for 144 virtual patients using combinations of variables such as gender, age, Garden classification of FNF, BMI, and medical history, as specified in the Supplement (Appendix). To simplify the model and enhance data handling consistency, we categorized patients with BMI > 30 as BMI ≥ 25, reducing the number of categorical variables and improving the model's stability and interpretability. Considering the complexity of health conditions in actual clinical cases, we selected hypertension and diabetes, the two most common and significantly impacting comorbidities, to reflect their prevalence and critical influence in clinical decision-making for FNF patients.

Prompting ChatGPT-4

In this study, we initially configured ChatGPT-4 as an orthopedic expert specializing in hip fractures, particularly FNF, to facilitate learning and updating relevant knowledge in this field. To this end, we developed a universal natural language prompt template, which was tailored based on the response characteristics of ChatGPT-4, as detailed in the Supplementary (Table 1). The final template is as follows:"What is the best treatment approach for an FNF patient with < five different factors > and provide a brief rationale for the treatment recommendation."For instance, in a case with Garden classification 3, male, age > 65 years, BMI < 18.5, and a previous history of diabetes or hypertension, we inquired about the optimal treatment recommendation and its rationale from ChatGPT-4. Clinical interpretation of patients with simulated femoral neck fracture

Seven attending physicians with more than 5 years of clinical experience in orthopedics were recruited from three clinical centers. These experts were instructed to manually recommend treatment options for all combinations of simulated patients according to the previously described workflow. A double-blind design was employed to prevent bias, where the experts were unaware of ChatGPT-4 recommendations for each simulated patient, ensuring that their recommendations were based purely on their clinical insights. This real-world data from clinical experts served as the gold standard to evaluate the treatment recommendations generated by ChatGPT-4, which were used for subsequent quantitative analysis to determine the accuracy and clinical utility of ChatGPT-4 in making treatment decisions for FNF.

Score based on ChatGPT-4 treatment style and interpretation

To comprehensively assess the effectiveness and clinical applicability of ChatGPT-4 in making treatment decisions for FNF, we developed two scoring systems for the seven participating orthopedic surgeons, detailed in Supplementary Table 2. The doctors rated each treatment recommendation by ChatGPT-4 on a scale from 0 to 4, where 0 indicates complete disagreement and 4 indicates a strong endorsement. Additionally, the clarity of treatment explanations provided by ChatGPT-4 was rated from 1 to 4, with 1 indicating dissatisfaction and a need for further clarification and 4 signifying that the explanation was excellent and required no additional detail [23], as detailed in Supplementary Table 3.

Statistical analysis

This study evaluates the consistency between treatment plans generated by ChatGPT-4 and those proposed by seven orthopedic surgeons using Precision, Recall, and F1 Score as performance metrics. Precision reflects the proportion of ChatGPT-4-recommended treatment plans that the doctors endorsed, while Recall indicates the proportion of correct treatment choices recommended by the doctors accurately identified by ChatGPT-4. The F1 Score, the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, measures the balance between these metrics. TP (True Positive) represents ChatGPT-4 recommendations endorsed by doctors, FP (False Positive) denotes ChatGPT-4 recommendations not endorsed by doctors, and FN (False Negative) refers to doctor-recommended treatments not identified by ChatGPT-4. If ChatGPT-4 generated two treatment modalities and one agreed with the doctors, it was considered an agreement. To further assess ChatGPT-4 performance, we normalized the seven doctors'treatment choices using the majority rule principle and recalculated the metrics. We analyzed the distribution of treatment choices made by all participants and ChatGPT-4 and studied their consistency and correlation through overlap analysis.

A random forest algorithm was employed to identify factors leading to discrepancies between ChatGPT-4 and clinicians'recommendations. The dataset was split into 70% training and 30% testing sets, and a random forest model with 100 decision trees was constructed using all available predictor variables for prediction and feature importance evaluation.

Lastly, using two rating systems, clinical doctors scored ChatGPT-4 based on the accuracy of its treatment choices and explanatory capabilities. All data analyses were performed using R version 4.3.0, with a significance level of P < 0.05.

Result

Based on a combination of variables listed in Table 1, we designed 144 simulated patients with FNF. ChatGPT-4 generated 156 treatment recommendations along with 144 explanatory statements. Seven orthopedic surgeons provided 1,086 treatment recommendations. The treatment recommendations and explanatory notes produced by ChatGPT-4 were also submitted to these surgeons, resulting in 1,008 feedback entries on treatment recommendations and explanations (144 cases × 7 physicians), with no missing data.

Based on the treatment choices of each expert physician, the F1 scores for ChatGPT-4 were as follows: 0.843, 0.748, 0.697, 0.829, 0.777, 0.810, and 0.819 (Fig. 1). Adhering to the majority rule principle, the combined treatment approaches yielded an F1 score of 0.834. Discussion

This study evaluated the performance of ChatGPT-4 in generating treatment recommendations for patients with FNF within simulated clinical scenarios. The model demonstrated encouraging accuracy, and its recommendations were largely consistent with those of orthopedic experts. Moreover, the explanatory rationale provided by ChatGPT-4 was generally perceived as reasonable by clinical evaluators. However, some notable discrepancies were observed between the model's suggestions and expert consensus, indicating possible biases or limitations in its underlying decision-making framework. Furthermore, while often coherent, the explanations lacked sufficient depth and clinical nuance when addressing more complex or borderline cases, underscoring the need for further refinement before clinical application.

Compared to orthopedic specialists, its precision, recall, and F1 scores have reached high levels. Given the complexity and multiple influencing factors involved in decision-making for FNF, ChatGPT-4 can identify key decision-making factors when processing large datasets and, in most cases, provides treatment recommendations consistent with those of clinical doctors, demonstrating its potential in clinical decision support. A high recall rate indicates that ChatGPT-4 will likely identify a wide range of relevant treatment options, thus minimizing the risk of omitting critical therapeutic measures despite occasional errors. However, further validation is required to confirm this reliability in clinical practice. The F1 score of ChatGPT-4 was more than 0.7 in evaluating individual experts, and reached 0.834 in evaluating standardized treatment plans, which achieved a satisfactory balance between accuracy and generalization. This result suggests that in the face of big data and diverse patient information, the ChatGPT-4 model may be able to identify commonalities and trends in treatment approaches, provide more consistent recommendations, and effectively reduce misdiagnosis and missed diagnosis. However, how to integrate ChatGPT-4 into clinical decision support systems is a challenging research. ChatGPT-4 can assist physicians in initial clinical decision-making by integrating electronic health record (EHR) systems and combining patient medical history, laboratory results, and imaging data. In addition, by combining with the clinical expert system, ChatGPT-4 can collaborate with multidisciplinary teams, improve the synergy effect of interprofessional diagnosis and treatment, and make accurate decisions. Nevertheless, the output of ChatGPT-4 requires human oversight to ensure that its recommendations are consistent with standards of clinical practice. Through continuous model training and feedback mechanisms, its adaptability and accuracy in the clinical environment can be continuously optimized, and its effectiveness as a decision support tool can be improved.

However, it is crucial to recognize that, although a high F1 score indicates that ChatGPT-4 can provide valuable assistance to physicians, it cannot fully capture the complexity of clinical decision-making. In actual clinical practice, treatment decisions are not solely based on statistical data; they also consider factors such as the physicians'experience and patient preferences [24].This study finds significant variations in treatment preferences among physicians, particularly in the choice between conservative treatment and total hip arthroplasty. These differences may relate to individual physician experience, interpretations of guidelines, and judgments about patient prognosis. In contrast, as a data-driven tool, ChatGPT-4 recommendations tend to follow data patterns. They are less influenced by subjective factors, which may sometimes lead to differences in physician judgments.

This study also employed a random forest model to explore factors contributing to discrepancies between ChatGPT-4 and physicians'treatment recommendations. The results indicate significant factors leading to these discrepancies include Garden IV fractures, patients younger than 50, and those older than 65. Not only does this validate the effectiveness of the random forest model in processing medical decision-making data, but it also reveals potential factors influencing the differences in decision-making between ChatGPT-4 and physicians. This provides critical insights for enhancing the ChatGPT-4 application in clinical decision support. There are differences in the treatment decisions for Garden IV fractures, which are influenced by multiple factors, highlighting the complexity of clinical diagnosis and treatment decisions [25].Treatment guidelines from the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) emphasize age as a key determinant. In young, active patients, especially those with good bone mass, internal fixation is often preferred to preserve the hip joint and maintain function. However, in older patients, especially those with osteoporosis and poor functional status, hip replacement is generally recommended because of its potential to improve long-term results and reduce the risk of nonunion or malunion [26, 27]. However, based on its training data, ChatGPT-4 may still recommend relatively conservative treatments, such as internal fixation in some cases, possibly because ChatGPT-4 relies more on statistical patterns without fully considering the specifics and severity of Garden IV fractures. Additionally, ChatGPT-4 may lack sufficient data to fully understand the natural history and prognosis of Garden IV fractures, thus underestimating the risks of nonunion and necrosis [28, 29]. These findings help developers to specifically optimize algorithms, such as refining strategies for specific fracture classifications or patient demographics. Simultaneously, this information cautions physicians to remain vigilant when using ChatGPT-4 support systems, especially when handling complex or marginal cases.

While providing treatment recommendations, ChatGPT-4 also offers explanations tailored to various complex patient scenarios, which enhances interpretability and builds trust in decision support systems [30, 31]. This study analyzes explicitly orthopedic physicians'assessments of ChatGPT-4 treatment recommendations and explanations. The results show that expert physicians highly rate ChatGPT-4 treatment recommendations and their explanations, with average scores of 3.25 and 3.26 out of 4 for the recommendations and explanations, respectively. This indicates that ChatGPT-4 treatment plans are usually in line with expert opinions and are considered appropriate in most cases. Physicians generally recognize the quality of ChatGPT-4 explanations, demonstrating its capability for effective communication and reasoned support of treatment recommendations in simulated clinical environments. This is particularly important for establishing clinical physicians'trust in the ChatGPT-4 decision support system.

However, we also observe variations in treatment recommendation scores among expert physicians. For example, Doc6 gives full scores more frequently than other physicians, while Doc7 never gives a full score. In some instances, Doc5 has given zero scores, possibly reflecting serious concerns or differing opinions regarding certain ChatGPT-4 recommendations. These variations indicate that even among experts, there may be significant differences in the level of acceptance of the same treatment recommendation. This suggests that when applying ChatGPT-4 recommendations to clinical practice, we must integrate each physician's professional judgment and understanding of the unique patient situation. Additionally, while physicians generally acknowledge the treatment explanations provided by ChatGPT-4, there is a relatively large standard deviation in the quality ratings of explanations. This implies that there is some inconsistency in perceptions of explanation quality. These rating discrepancies may be related to subjective interpretations of"good explanations"by raters. Alternatively, they may reflect individual differences among physicians in their explanation requirements. This suggests that in developing ChatGPT-4-assisted decision support tools, customizable explanations should be provided to meet the needs and expectations of different physicians.

Although ChatGPT-4 performed well in this study, some limitations must be acknowledged. First, simulated Settings may lack the complexity and variability of real clinical Settings, where patient conditions and treatment responses are more diverse and less predictable. Second, although simulated cases can provide valuable insights into standardized comparisons and model validation, they inherently lack the dynamic changes seen in real clinical Settings—such as the evolution of a patient's condition, subjective physician preferences, physical examination findings, imaging diagnoses, and multidisciplinary consultation. An inherent limitation of ChatGPT-4 as a text-based model is its inability to process imaging data, such as radiographs, CT scans, or MRI, which are critical for orthopedic decision making. In addition, ChatGPT-4 cannot perform or interpret the results of a physical examination, which is essential for assessing musculoskeletal conditions. In addition, while experts highly rated ChatGPT-4's treatment recommendations and explanations, these evaluations may be influenced by the subjective judgment of physicians. This highlights the need for further research to assess whether interactive conversational prompts improve the accuracy and reliability of treatment recommendations. Although these evaluations indicate the effectiveness of ChatGPT-4 in simulation studies, they cannot fully replicate actual clinical decisions, which must take into account more clinical experience and individual differences in patients.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates ChatGPT-4 potential in personalized orthopedic diagnosis and treatment decision support, offering new possibilities for clinical decision-making assistance. ChatGPT-4 performs well in Precision, Recall, and F1 scores for treatment recommendations and receives positive expert evaluations. However, limitations exist due to the use of simulated cases. Future research should validate ChatGPT-4 reliability and robustness in clinical practice and explore its integration with doctors'professional judgment and patients'preferences to optimize decision-making.

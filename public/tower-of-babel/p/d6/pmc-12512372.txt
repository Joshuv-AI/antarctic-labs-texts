Postabortion contraception in Brazil: experience in a reference service during the covid-19 pandemic
Authors: Carolina Braga Trabach, Nelio Neves Veiga-Junior, Priscilla Brenda Fonseca Dantas, Aline Aparecida Junqueira, Carina Cordeiro Nunes, Ana Luiza Sturião Vieira Santana, Ana Kaori Fecchio Matsubara, Luiz F. Baccaro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDuring the covid-19 pandemic, despite sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services being considered essential, in Brazil, only 55% of abortion services remained in operation. This restriction of access could lead to an increase in unplanned pregnancies, unsafe abortion practices, and, consequently, an increase in the maternal mortality rate. In addition to the difficulty of consolidating information for monitoring and evaluating SRH services. In this scenario, postabortion contraception (PAC) during hospital admission is essential, especially with the postabortion use of long-acting reversible methods (LARC), such as intrauterine devices (IUDs), since they are effective, economical, safe and with immediate contraception.ObjectiveTo evaluate postabortion contraceptive methods, mainly LARC, during hospital admission after spontaneous abortion and legal termination of pregnancy and their associated factors through the abortion surveillance network.Methodsa cross-sectional study with women of any age admitted for spontaneous abortions or legal interruption procedures at the Women’s Hospital at State University of Campinas (UNICAMP), Brazil, between July 2017 and November 2022. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee from UNICAMP. The dependent variable was IUD insertion. The independent variables were clinical, sociodemographic characteristics, vulnerable groups and period of hospitalization (pre-pandemic: until 10/03/2020; pandemic: from 11/03/2020). For statistical analysis, Cochran-Armitage trend tests and an adjusted logistic regression model were used, with the significance of the coefficients evaluated by the p-value, obtained through the Wald statistics associated with each parameter, as well as the confidence interval.Results675 women were hospitalized during the period, 547 for spontaneous abortions and 128 for legal termination. Of these, 499 (79%) had at least one vulnerable factor and 45.2% started PAC during hospital stay, as injectables (48.5%), oral contraceptive pills (34.8% ) and IUDs (13.4%). There was a trend towards increased use of IUDs before hospital discharge (p < 0.01), especially in hospitalizations that occurred during the pandemic period, in the most vulnerable groups (p < 0.01) and women with previous abortions (p < 0.05). The factors independently associated with a higher frequency of IUD insertion were presence of 3 or more vulnerability factors (coefficient 4.50; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.12 to 7.88; p = 0.01); hospitalization during the pandemic period (coefficient 2.56; CI 1.14–3.99; p < 0.01) and the number of abortions (coefficient 0.61; CI 0.09–1.13; p = 0. 02).ConclusionReducing access barriers to highly effective and long-acting contraceptives is an important step towards curbing the rates of unplanned pregnancies. Increasing rates of postabortion IUD use in vulnerable populations and in the context of the pandemic reinforce the importance of the epidemiological surveillance strategy in reducing exposure to preventable risks such as unsafe abortion and managing SRH policy strategies to reduce the maternal mortality rate.

Background

Abortion is among the leading causes of maternal death worldwide [1]. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) [2], to reduce the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) by 2030 and achieve one of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), it is essential to eradicate unsafe abortion and ensure universal access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH), which includes access to broad, safe, and affordable contraception [2, 3].

Despite the global reduction in MMR between 2000 and 2020, seven countries in Latin America, including Brazil, experienced a significant increase in this indicator [4, 5]. Furthermore, during the public health emergency caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, which rapidly increased maternal deaths worldwide and overloaded health systems globally, particularly in Brazil, one of the most impacted countries, MMR increased by over 90% in 2021 [6] making it even more difficult to achieve the SDG. This was due to the lack of access to contraceptive methods and the inequity in access SRH services, as only 55% of the 76 hospitals that reported providing abortion services in 2019 remained operational and were located in 3.6% of municipalities of the country [7]. This affected women’s access to safe abortion services and also hindered the consolidation of information for monitoring and evaluation of health service offerings [8].

In Brazil, where abortion is illegal except in cases of sexual violence, risk to maternal life, and fetuses with anencephaly, and 1 in 7 maternal deaths occurs due to unsafe abortion [7, 9], efforts in postabortion contraception (PAC) immediately following the expulsion of the products of conception are crucial to decrease rates of unplanned pregnancies, abortion recurrence, and reduce abortion-related morbidity and mortality [10, 11]. Studies demonstrate that long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs), such as the intrauterine device (IUD), are reversible, cost-effective, safe and when initiated immediately postabortion, they provide timely contraception, higher continuation and satisfaction rates, and lower rates of unintended pregnancy [1, 12–14]. Thus, the objective of this study is to evaluate PAC, mainly the IUD insertions, during hospital admission following spontaneous abortion and legal pregnancy termination, and their associated factors through an abortion surveillance network.

Methods

MUSA network

Created by the Latin American Center for Perinatology (CLAP), the multicentric MUSA (Mujeres en Situacion de Aborto/ Women in Abortion Situation) Network promotes and improves care for abortion in Latin America and the Caribbean [15]. This network includes several hospitals, the sentinel centers, that might send frequently data registered in the Perinatal Computerized System (SIP), a CLAP software, that record data related to pregnancy and epidemiological monitoring. Our institution, the University of Campinas Women’s Hospital (UNICAMP), a tertiary referral hospital located in an urban setting in southeast Brazil, for cases of complications related to pregnancy in municipalities in the region and experiences an average of 250 births and 20 cases of first trimester pregnancy loss per month and has been a sentinel center of the CLAP MUSA-Network since July 2017.

The protocol of the CLAP MUSA-Network and specific methodological details for implementation of the research proposal are published elsewhere [15]. Briefly, each sentinel centers of the CLAP MUSA-Network regularly provide data on maternal morbidity in abortion, miscarriage and early pregnancy loss, methods of uterine evacuation, abortion-related complications, postabortion contraception. In addition to enable epidemiological monitoring and compares clinical changes patterns over time, representatives from all sentinel centers attended regular webinars with scientific topics to promoted changes in local context and includes evidence-based interventions that are required and recommended by WHO to improve women’s care and encourage good clinical practices for safe abortion.

Intrauterine device in postabortion contraception

The Brazilian Ministry of Health’s Technical Care Manual for abortion management that are used in health facilities recommends PAC’s provision and counseling [16]. The clinical practice in our institution follows the Eligibility Criteria established by WHO [1, 17] and all medical staff members, including medical residents in gynecology and obstetrics and the assistant doctors, had technical standards and training curriculum to insert IUDs, including in postabortion care. Registered nurses or others healthcare professionals were not allowed to perform IUD insertion, since the Brazilian Ministry of Health, in 2019, decreed that the insertion of IUDs is a medical task and therefore must only be the responsibility of physicians [18]. The recommends.

Throughout the pandemic, various clinical meetings were conducted by the MUSA team to ensure that the medical team started immediately PAC counseling during hospital admission. Thus, in 2021, a theoretical-practical in-person training was held, aimed at obstetricians and gynecologists, reinforcing the Medical Eligibility Criteria for postabortion IUD use [1, 17] (both copper-bearing IUDs [Cu-IUD] and levonorgestrel-releasing IUDs [LNG-IUD]) e reassure the insertion technique and the technical standards. Informative posters were displayed inside the operating room and in the nursing station. Additionally, explanatory materials were made available to the professionals both electronically and physically.

Study design and data collection

This cross-sectional prospective study was conducted between July 2017 and November 2022. All women admitted to our institution for postabortion care (induced abortion for any legal reason and others postabortion circumstances, as miscarriage and early pregnancy loss) and with their data inserted in the the SIP-Abortion database were included. The exclusion criteria were bleeding during pregnancy with no confirmed abortion and ectopic or molar pregnancies. Recording SIP data are collected through in-person interviews with women before hospital discharge and with revised medical records. All participants provided written informed consent and for the minors and their legal guardians, both provided informed assent and consent, respectively. The Research Ethics Committee of our institution (CEP UNICAMP) approved the study protocol (approval number: 93060618.9.1001.5404).

Before hospital discharge, the women were counseling about the available contraceptive methods in the institution (combined oral contraceptives [COCs] progesterone-only pills [POPs], combined injectable contraceptives (CICs), depot medroxyprogesterone acetate [DMPA] injectable, copper-bearing IUDs [Cu-IUD] and levonorgestrel-releasing IUDs [LNG-IUD]). The condoms and others barrier methods were offered for all women to prevent sexually transmitted infection.

For the present analysis, the dependent variable was IUD insertion after uterine evacuation and before hospital discharge in cases of spontaneous abortion or legal termination of pregnancy. The contraceptive methods were analyzed individually as oral contraceptive (COCs and POPs), injectable (CICs and DMPA), and intrauterine device [IUD] (Cu-IUD and LNG-IUD]). The independent variables were clinical and sociodemographic characteristics: abortion (legal; spontaneous), age (< 20 years; ≥20 years); literate (elementary or higher; no education); living alone (yes; no); previous births (0; 1; ≥2); previous abortions (0; 1; ≥2), planned pregnancy (yes; no), pregnancy resulting from contraceptive failure (yes; no); history of gender-violence (yes; no), presence of vulnerable factor ( woman were classified with none, one, two, or three or more less favorable social factors, according to the Commission on Social Determinants of Health [19]: being under 20 years of age; having studied at most up to elementary level; having a history of gender-based violence, living alone, unplanned pregnancy), uterine evacuation (yes; no); type of uterine evacuation (medical abortion, dilatation and curettage [D&C] and manual vacuum aspiration [MVA]), period of admission for abortion, (pre-pandemic: until March/2020; pandemic: since March/2020).

Statistical analysis

A descriptive analysis of the data was performed. Continuous variables were assessed as mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, maximum, and quartiles. For categorical variables, the relative frequencies were calculated. A Cochran-Armitage trend test with was used to evaluate the change in the trend in the occurrence of the outcome variables. To evaluate the association between IUD insertion and the independent variables, a logistic regression model was adjusted, with the significance of the coefficients assessed by the p-value, obtained through the Wald statistics associated with each parameter, as well as the confidence interval. The level of significance assumed was 5%. The software used for the analyzes was R version 4.2.1 (2022-06-23), Vienna, Austria [20].

Results

During the analyzed period, 675 women were admitted for abortion with 128 (19%) undergoing abortion for any legal reason and 547 (81%) for spontaneous abortion, and regarding the period, 379 (56.1%) were hospitalized before the pandemic and 296 (43.9%) during the pandemic period. The mean age of the women was 29.87 ± 7.63 years, and the mean gestational age was 10.8 ± 3.6 weeks. For 416 (61.7%) and 208 (30.8%) women had, respectively, at least a birth and one abortion, 472 (70%) women did not use contraception and 142 (30.7%) reported contraceptive method failure. Among the vulnerable factors studied, 469 (69.9%) women the pregnancy was unplanned, the gender-based violence occurred in 108 (16%) women, 88 (13%) were under 20 years old, 83 (13%) lived alone, 9 (0.6%) were not literate. Nine out of ten women underwent some type of intervention for uterine evacuation, among these 108 (19.1%) performed MVA, 402 (71.3%) required D&C and 54 (9.6%) had medical abortion with misoprostol. Since the implementation of the surveillance network, 305 women (45.2%) started some form of contraception before hospital discharge. The most used methods were injectable contraceptives (n = 148, 48.5%), followed by oral contraceptive pills (n = 106, 34.8%), and IUD (n = 41, 13.4%). During the analyzed period, an increasing trend in the use of contraception before hospital discharge was observed (Cochran-Armitage test: z = 6.07; p < 0.01), as well as a trend of increased insertion of IUD (Cochran-Armitage test: z = 8.07; p < 0.01).

A higher frequency of IUD insertion before hospital discharge was observed in women who presented three or more vulnerability factors (coefficient 4.50; 95% CI 1.12 to 7.88; p = 0.01), women who were hospitalized during the pandemic period (coefficient 2.56; 95% CI 1.14–3.99; p < 0.01), and those with a higher number of previous abortions (coefficient 0.61; 95% CI 0.09–1.13; p = 0.02). Women living alone were associated with a lower chance of having an IUD inserted before hospital discharge (coefficient − 2.34; 95% CI -4.61 to -0.07; p = 0.04) (Table 2).

Discussion

This study evaluated postabortion contraception during hospital admission and its associated factors through the abortion surveillance network. Since the implementation of the CLAP MUSA Network, there has been an increasing trend in PAC. Despite challenges in maintaining essential health services during the pandemic, our setting adapted to WHO recommendations [1] and demonstrated that the use of IUDs immediately inserted postabortion increased during the pandemic. This increase was associated with women in vulnerable situations and those with a higher number of previous abortions.

Immediate postabortion contraceptive counseling and same-day initiation strategies can lead to higher adherence rates to LARC methods [21], as observed in our study once the PAC uses integrated long-acting methods, are less prone to user errors and not requiring repeated prescriptions [22]. Differently from Kenya’s health services, which compared PAC in the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods, there a 15% of reduction in PAC access for women under 20 years and a 30% drop in IUD insertion during the pandemic period [23], in our study, we observed higher IUD insertion during the pandemic period. This likely resulted from stronger actions to provide this contraceptive method in 2022, rather than being directly caused by the pandemic itself, although the difficulty in accessing family planning during its critical period may have contributed to greater adherence to LARCs. Similarly, a hospital in South Africa that implemented PAC strategies during the pandemic reported that most women opted for injectable contraceptives. Although only 2% chose IUDs, there was a 70% increase in IUD utilization during the COVID-19 period [24].

Studies have shown that the disruption of SRH services during the COVID-19 pandemic impacted PAC provision in health services [7]. Endler et al. [25], that conducted an online survey in 29 countries, where 8 out of 10 healthcare professionals reported that access to contraceptive services was reduced or much reduced due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and according to the responders, Brazil, classified as a country with severe abortion restriction hasn’t implemented any changes in contraceptive policies in response to the Covid-19 pandemic. Despite this, our health service showed an increase in PAC. The continuous monitoring carried out in our study during the pandemic might facilitated the implementation of strategies to promote PAC, including the LARC use immediately after abortion.

The burden of social inequities in Brazil also impacts sexual and reproductive health [26]. Vulnerable populations face significant barriers to accessing healthcare, making hospitalization a strategic opportunity to initiate long-term contraception [9]. Em uma revisão que avaliou os impactos da COVID-19 nos serviços de contracepção e aborto em países de baixa e média renda destacou a falta de disponibilidade de dados representativos para abordar a situação de grupos vulneráveis [27]. The National Abortion Program in Brazil revealed that, among women who experienced two or more abortions, three out of four were Black, and 50% had their first abortion before the age of 19 [28]. Immediate postabortion IUD insertion can prevent abortion recurrence by 40% compared to delayed insertion [8], particularly among women under 20, where pregnancy and abortion rates can decrease by up to 75% [29].

Our study presents limitations, primarily being a cross-sectional study, which does not allow us to establish cause-and-effect relationships. Additionally, it includes only women hospitalized due to abortion complications or legal pregnancy termination, excluding those who did not require hospitalization and not differentiating between spontaneous and induced abortions outside the legal context. Moreover, not all contraceptive methods, such as the subdermal implant, are available in our service. Despite these limitations, the establishment of an epidemiological surveillance network proves essential for identifying areas for improvement and monitoring the outcomes of implemented measures over time, especially the postabortion contraception provision is aa highly effective public health practice.

The CLAP MUSA-Network has encouraged PAC. This strategy enabled an increase in postabortion contraception during the pandemic, particularly the use of long acting and safe methods. These methods directly impact the reduction of unplanned pregnancies and, consequently, maternal mortality. Moreover, they contribute to ensuring universal access to sexual and reproductive health services for women.

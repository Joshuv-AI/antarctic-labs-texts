Metastatic Ewing Sarcoma, Patterns of Care and Outcomes of Patients in a Real‐Life National Setting Over a Decade
Authors: Coline Ducrot, Derek Dinart, Maeva Bonneau, Max Piffoux, Marie Sophie Minot, Juliane Mignot‐Posson, Thomas Grinda, Jordane Chaix, Sarah Winter, Pauline Gerard, Romain Duong, Berengère Narciso, Natacha Entze Werle, Cécile Vérité, Valérie Laurence, Axel Le Cesne, Pablo Berlanga, Camille Tlemsani, Pascaline Boudou‐Rouquette, Emmanuelle Bompas, Thibaud Valentin, Nadège Corradini, Mehdi Brahmi, Carine Bellera, Maud Toulmonde
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMetastatic Ewing sarcoma (MES) has a poor prognosis. This multicenter observational study provides real‐world data on treatment patterns of patients with MES in France. Treatment characteristics, outcomes such as time to next treatment (TTNT) and overall survival (OS), and prognostic factors of patients aged ≥ 12 years treated for a MES in 11 French reference network centers were retrieved from our national database. From 2008 to 2018, 156 patients with MES were included: 82 were metastatic at diagnosis (upfront metastatic cohort), 74 had developed secondary metastases after treatment of a localized disease (metastatic relapse cohort). 94% of patients received systemic treatment, with a median of three lines (1–11), 61% had at least one loco‐regional procedure and 42% of patients participated in a clinical trial in the metastatic setting. Median OS from metastatic diagnosis was 20.3 months [95% CI 14.0; 27.7] in the metastatic relapse cohort and 31.4 months [95% CI 26.5; 42.5] in the upfront metastatic cohort (p = 0.02). Median TTNT in first metastatic line was 16.8 months [95% CI 12.9; 21.3] in the upfront metastatic cohort and 7.4 months [95% CI 5.0; 11.1] in the metastatic relapse cohort, 5.8 months [95% CI 3.6; 7.3] in 2nd line and 3.8 months [95% CI 2.8; 5.7] in 3rd line, without significant difference between cohorts and treatment regimens. Patients with upfront MES have a longer OS than patients with relapsing disease, mainly due to first metastatic line dose‐dense polychemotherapy and loco‐regional procedures in selected patients. Main regimens used at relapse are associated with the same range of benefit and survival, while TKIs such as regorafenib or cabozantinib have modest activity. Inclusion in clinical trials should be prioritized.

Introduction

Ewing sarcoma is the second most frequent bone cancer in adolescents and young adults. It arises in young patients with a median age of 14 years, and 70% of patients diagnosed are under 20. ES is characterized by a specific translocation leading to a fusion gene combining EWSR1 (Ewing Sarcoma RNA binding protein) and FLI1 (Friend Leukemia Integration 1) in 80% of cases, or members of the ETS (Erythroblast Transformation Specific) oncogene family [1].

About one‐fourth of Ewing sarcoma patients are metastatic at diagnosis and 30%–40% of Ewing sarcoma patients with localized disease will develop metastasis. Metastatic Ewing sarcoma has a poor 5‐year survival rate of 15%–38% [2]. Current regimen used in first line of upfront metastatic Ewing sarcoma is the same as for localized Ewing sarcoma, based on dose‐dense ifosfamide and etoposide (IE) alternating with vincristine, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (VDC) every 2 weeks [3, 4]. Though studies like rEECur are attempting to address this, there is no clear consensus of what regimen to use at first relapse of metastatic disease [5, 6, 7]. Despite a large number of new drugs explored in Ewing sarcoma in the last 15 years and conventional regimens used in routine, there is no clear consensus for first‐line in metastatic recurrent Ewing sarcoma [8]. Reported poor prognostic factors for Ewing sarcoma are age at diagnosis, axial location of primary tumor, initial tumor volume, unresectable tumor, poor pathological response to neoadjuvant treatment and the presence of metastasis at diagnosis [9].

Large observational studies are needed to improve patient care and clinical trials design. Available real‐world data for metastatic Ewing sarcoma patients is limited, with monocentric studies, studies restricted to patients above 40–50 years or to rare subtypes of Ewing sarcoma only [10].

In this context, we report a national multicenter observational real‐world study that describes treatment characteristics, outcomes, and prognostic factors of patients ≥ 12‐year‐old treated for a metastatic Ewing sarcoma in 11 French national reference network centers from 2008 to 2018.

Material and Methods

Patients aged ≥ 12 years (1) with a diagnosis of Ewing sarcoma made by an expert pathologist from one of the French network centers (RESOS), (2) with an initial diagnosis made from 2008 to 2018, (3) with a metastatic disease (either upfront or relapsing), (4) treated in one of the 11 participating national reference centers designated by the French National Cancer Institute for the management of bone sarcomas, (5) with no objection against inclusion in the Groupe Sarcome Français & Groupe d'Etude des Tumeurs Osseuses (GSF/GETO) database and who gave their informed consent to inclusion the study after written information, according to Loi Jardé no. 2012‐300, were included in the study.

The primary objective was to describe characteristics and modalities of treatment of patients with metastatic Ewing sarcoma, including systemic treatment regimen, access to clinical trials and loco‐regional procedures. Secondary objectives were to assess patient's outcome in terms of time to next treatment (TTNT), and overall survival (OS) from metastatic setting for each line of systemic treatment, as well as prognostic factors for OS and TTNT. TTNT for each line was defined as the time from initiation of systemic treatment to the initiation of next systemic treatment or death due to any cause. When neither death nor new systemic treatment was observed, TTNT was censored at the date of last patient contact. OS was defined as the time from diagnosis of metastatic disease to death. When death was not observed, OS was censored at the date of last patient contact. OS was also calculated from the date of each line of systemic treatment. Factors included in the prognosis analysis are listed in the Data S1.

Demographic data, clinical and tumor (primary and metastatic) characteristics, and treatment modalities were described per study population. Descriptive statistics were used to show the distribution of variables in the population. Cox proportional hazard models were used to identify clinical factors associated with the survival and TTNT after the diagnosis of metastases. Factors with a significance level of 0.2 (p < 0.2) in the univariate analysis were incorporated into the multivariate analysis and selected using a backward stepwise selection method (p < 0.05). Survival (follow‐up) times were described as median values based on the (inverse) Kaplan–Meier estimator. Data were analyzed using SAS v9.4 software. This study was approved by all participating centers' internal ethics committees and ethics committee of Institut Bergonié, promoter of the study.

Results

A total of 174 patients with metastatic Ewing sarcoma were screened in 11 participating centers, of whom 156 had confirmed metastatic Ewing sarcoma: (i) 82 patients were metastatic from diagnosis (upfront metastatic cohort), (ii) 56 patients had metastatic relapse after standard treatment of a localized disease and (iii) 36 patients had a locally advanced unresectable disease, of whom 18 developed secondary metastases later on (iiiM). Patients from the (ii) and (iiiM) joined together represent the metastatic relapse cohort (n = 74) (Figure S1). Patients' characteristics at diagnosis are reported in Table S1. The median (range) age at diagnosis was 21 (12–86) years, 61 patients (35%) were diagnosed before the age of 18 years and 11 (6%) after 50 years. Patients' characteristics at diagnosis of metastatic Ewing sarcoma and their management are given in Table 1. Overall, 100 patients (64%) had one metastatic site at metastatic diagnosis, which was the lung only for 53 patients (34%) and bone only for 36 patients (23%).

Systemic treatment was given to 147 (94%) patients overall. The median number of lines was 3 (1–9), and 76 (49%) patients received more than 2 lines. Main systemic treatment regimen given in each line to patients in the upfront and the relapse cohorts are shown in Tables S2 and S3. In the whole cohort, 62 patients (42%) participated in a clinical trial at least once in the metastatic setting, 35 (24%) in first line, 25 (17%) in second line, and 16 (11%) in third line.

The Upfront Metastatic Cohort

First metastatic line was dominated by Vincristine, Doxorubicin, Cyclophosphamide/Ifosfamide—Etoposide‐based (VDC/IE or VIDE/VAI) polychemotherapy regimen (95%). Second line consisted of Temozolomide and Irinotecan (TEMIRI) in 42%, Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs, regorafenib, cabozantinib or pazopanib) in 13%, followed by Topotecan and Cyclophosphamide (TOPO‐CP) (6%) and Vincristine, Actinomycine, Cyclophosphamide (VAC)‐based regimen (6%). Third line was dominated by TKIs, representing 22%, TEMIRI (17%) and TOPO‐CP (15%), whereas 17 patients (36%) received other miscellaneous regimens such as docetaxel + gemcitabine, carboplatin + etoposide or cyclophosphamide + vinorelbine. Loco‐regional procedure on metastasis was proposed to 54 patients (66%): two patients (4%) underwent surgery for their metastases, 32 (60%) had radiation therapy, 2 (4%) had interventional radiology such as radiofrequency or cryoablation, 17 patients (31%) had multiple procedures.

The median follow up from metastatic diagnosis was 76.8 months [95% CI 66.7; 105.9]. The median OS in the metastatic setting was 31.4 months [95% CI 26.5; 42.5] (Figure 1B). The 3‐year OS in was 46.7% (SD = 0.055). The median TTNT in 1st metastatic line was 16.8 months [95% CI 12.9; 21.3] (Figure 2 and Table 2). In this upfront metastatic cohort, VDC/IE or VIDE/VAI polychemotherapy used in first metastatic line reached a TTNT of 16.8 months [95% CI 12.9; 21.3]. The TTNT in second line was 5.9 months [95% CI 3.6; 7.7] with TEMIRI, 5.2 months [95% CI 1.2; 7.3] with TKIs and 2.5 months [95% CI 1.2; 11.3] with other rarer regimens. The TTNT in third line was 7.4 months [95% CI 2.1; 16.7] with TOPO‐CP, 6.2 months [95% CI 1.1; 11.9] with TKIs, 4.0 months [95% CI 1.6; 6.4] with TEMIRI, and 3.7 months [95% CI 2.2; 6.5] with other rarer regimens (Table 3).

Details Regarding the Upfront Lung Only Metastatic Cohort

First metastatic line was dominated by Vincristine, Doxorubicin, Cyclophosphamide/Ifosfamide—Etoposide‐based (VDC/IE or VIDE/VAI) polychemotherapy regimen (27/28, 96%). Loco‐regional procedure on metastasis was proposed to 19 patients (68%): one patient (5%) underwent surgery for their metastases, 8 (42%) had radiation therapy, whereas 10 patients (53%) had multiple procedures. The median OS reached 44.3 months [95% CI 26.5; Not Reached] in this specific cohort with lung metastasis only, with a total of 18 deaths registered. The 3‐year OS was 60.7% (SD = 0.092). The 28 patients reached a median TTNT of 23.6 months [95% CI 15.6; 36.1] in first line.

The Metastatic Relapse Cohort

First metastatic line was dominated by TEMIRI (45%), followed by TOPO‐CP (11%) and TKIs (8%). Second line consisted in a TKI for 39% of patients, TEMIRI for 17%, TOPO‐CP and Ifosfamide regimens for 6% whereas 26% of patients received other miscellaneous regimens such as docetaxel + gemcitabine, carboplatin + etoposide, or cyclophosphamide + vinorelbine. Third line was also dominated by TKIs with 11 patients (38%) receiving a TKI and five patients (17%) receiving TEMIRI whereas two patients received TOPO‐CP (7%) and 10 patients received other regimens (34%).

The median time from initial diagnosis of localized disease to metastatic relapse was 20.6 months [95% CI 19.1; 24.2] and the median follow up from metastatic diagnosis was 76.8 months [95% CI 35.2; 94.3]. The median OS was significantly shorter in the relapse cohort than the upfront cohort: 20.3 months [95% CI 14.0; 27.7] versus 31.4 months ([95% CI 26.5; 42.5], p = 0.02), respectively (Figure 1B). The 3‐year OS was 32.3% (SD = 0.058). The median TTNT in 1st metastatic line was 7.4 months [95% CI 5.0; 11.1] (Figure 2 and Table 2). TEMIRI and TOPO‐CP used in first metastatic line reached a TTNT 8.1 months [95% CI 4.0; 11.1] and 6.8 months [95% CI 2.3; 15.4], respectively, whereas TKIs and other rarer regimens used in first metastatic line reached a TTNT of 4.0 months [95% CI 1.1; 61.5] and 5.3 months [95% CI 1.4; 10.1]. The TTNT in second line was 7.2 months [95% CI 2.3; NC] with TEMIRI, 7.1 months [95% CI 2.3; 17.6] with TKIs and 2.4 months [95% CI 0.6; 9.7] with other less common regimens.

Prognostic Factors

The prognostic factors associated with OS in univariate analysis were pathological response to neoadjuvant treatment, location of first metastasis and loco‐regional treatment of metastasis (Table S5) while timing of metastasis (upfront or relapse), location of first metastasis and loco‐regional treatment of metastasis were independent factors in the multivariate analysis (Table 4). The prognostic factors associated with TTNT in univariate analysis were timing of metastasis, pathological response to neoadjuvant treatment, location of first metastasis, and loco‐regional treatment of metastases (Table S6) while timing of metastasis, pathological response to neoadjuvant treatment, and loco‐regional treatment of metastasis remained significantly associated with TTNT in the multivariate analysis (Table 4).

Discussion

Our study is a large multicenter study using national real‐world data to assess outcomes of 156 metastatic Ewing sarcoma patients. In our study, the median age was 22.5 years (12–72), with two‐thirds of adult patients. Although outcomes have improved in recent decades for patients with localized Ewing sarcoma, there has been no improvement for patients with metastatic Ewing sarcoma overall, despite complexification of alkylator and anthracycline based regimens, or introduction of high‐dose chemotherapy intensification with autologous stem‐cell rescue [11, 12, 13, 14]. The dose dense VDC/IE regimen has replaced the VIDE/VAI previous regimen as an international standard of care for first line of all stages of Ewing sarcoma [3]. Our study is in line with these data, with 95% of patients from the upfront metastatic cohort receiving a VDC/IE or VIDE/VAI regimen, and performing a long TTNT (16.8 months). Overall, nine patients received busulfan‐based intensification in the first or second metastatic line, either in the upfront or relapse setting, and reached a TTNT of 19.7 months. The role of high dose therapy (HDT) with autologous stem cell rescue is controversial in metastatic Ewing sarcoma. Dirksen et al. reported no statistical difference in survival between HDT and VIDE/VAI + whole lung radiation in lung metastatic Ewing sarcoma < 50 years, with more toxicity [13]. Very recently, Corradini and colleagues reported the results of the COMBINAIR3 study, assessing an intensified protocol in selected disseminated extrapulmonary Ewing sarcoma patients [15]. Patients aged 6 to 47 years were initially treated by VDC/IE, switched to TEMIRI after four courses if no response, received HDT if good responders after the induction sequence, local treatment to metastasis if possible and were proposed 2 years of oral maintenance. Overall, 45% had a pelvic primary, 83% bone or bone marrow involvement at baseline; 33 out of 42 (78.5%) patients received HDT and 31 had surgery of their primary. Three‐year EFS and 3‐year OS rates were 53.7% [95% CI: 40.4–71.3] and 65.5% [95% CI: 52.4–81.9], respectively in these very selected not lung‐only metastatic patients.

In our real‐world cohort, outcomes differed markedly according to the timing and pattern of metastatic presentation. Patients with upfront metastatic disease achieved a median OS of 31.4 months, with prolonged disease control in first line, while subsequent lines were associated with progressively shorter TTNT and OS, reflecting diminishing therapeutic benefit. Notably, patients presenting with lung‐only metastases had more favorable outcomes, with a median OS exceeding 44 months and a prolonged first‐line TTNT, suggesting a potentially distinct prognostic subgroup and supporting the role of aggressive systemic and loco‐regional approaches in selected cases. In contrast, the metastatic relapse cohort demonstrated significantly poorer survival (median OS 20.3 months), shorter TTNT in first line, highlighting a more aggressive disease biology and/or reduced chemosensitivity and/or less treatment options available in the relapse setting.

However, TTNT and OS from second line were similar among cohorts, illustrating that dose‐dense protocols with polychemotherapy can achieve longer TTNT and survival in selected population of upfront metastatic Ewing sarcoma, but that prognosis becomes similar to that of metastatic relapse from subsequent progression.

Despite several protocols available and a large number of new drugs tested in Ewing sarcoma in the last 15 years, none has made a consensus for successful routine implementation in first or further lines of recurrent metastatic Ewing sarcoma [5, 8]. Main drug regimens used in routine practice are alkylating agents' combination such as TOPO‐CP, VAC, high‐dose ifosfamide, TEMIRI or gemcitabine‐docetaxel association, based on small studies. These different regimens were recently compared in the REECUR phase II/III, in which 451 patients were randomly assigned between 2014 and 2021 to TOPO‐CP, TEMIRI, gemcitabine and docetaxel or high‐dose ifosfamide. High‐dose ifosfamide was considered more effective than TOPO‐CP, gemcitabine‐ docetaxel and TEMIRI, which led to the phase III comparing TOPO‐CP and high‐dose ifosfamide. The median EFS was 3.7 months (95% CI, 2.1–6.2) for TOPO‐CP and 5.7 months (95% CI, 3.8–7.0) for high‐dose ifosfamide [6, 7]. In our study, main regimen used in first metastatic line after relapse was TEMIRI, with a TTNT of 8 months, followed by TOPO‐CP, that reached a TTNT of 7 months. Ifosfamide was used in a handful of patients only, which precluded any analysis. On the other hand, TKIs such as regorafenib, cabozantinib or pazopanib were given to 52 patients in up to fourth line and reached a TTNT of 2.8 to 7 months. This is in line with what has been published in the literature [16, 17, 18]. The CABONE study assessed cabozantinib in heavily pretreated metastatic Ewing sarcoma patients and showed an ORR of 26% and a median PFS of 4.4 months [16]. Two studies also assessed the oral TKI regorafenib in Ewing sarcoma patients with metastatic relapse. In the non‐comparative randomized study REGOBONE, median PFS was 11.4 weeks with regorafenib and 3.9 weeks with placebo, whereas the SARC024 reported an ORR of 10% and a median PFS of 14.8 weeks with regorafenib [17, 18]. According to this body of evidence, TKIs such as cabozantinib or regorafenib could represent an alternative to chemotherapy regimen in metastatic Ewing sarcoma not amenable to curative‐intended loco‐regional treatment to metastasis, with less toxicity, notably hematological. Combination trials with new drugs comprising TKIs represent a compelling strategy for future phase II trials in Ewing sarcoma. The combination of regorafenib with VDC/IE is currently being explored in a phase Ib (NCT05830084) in patients with upfront metastatic Ewing sarcoma and will be followed by a VDC/IE ± regorafenib randomization in the future Euro Ewing Consortium trial Inter‐Ewing‐1 in the same indication. Similarly, the combination of ifosfamide and lenvatinib is currently assessed in the REECUR trial in recurrent metastatic Ewing sarcoma (ISRCTN36453794). However, biological rationale for such combinations needs to be strengthened [19].

The limited activity of regimens proposed in this setting underlines the urgent need for new therapeutic options. In our study, 40% of Ewing sarcoma patients participated in a clinical trial at least once in their metastatic disease course. This high inclusion rate is obtained in a study performed in reference centers using pediatrician‐adult oncologist international networks. However, new drugs availability for Ewing sarcoma patients remains a worldwide issue. Felix et al. recently published a systematic review on phase I/II trials in metastatic/relapsed Ewing sarcoma from 2005 to 2018. They identified 146 trials ‐only 23 with an Ewing sarcoma specific stratum. According to their estimates, only 10% of the theorically eligible Ewing sarcoma patients from the European and USA population were included in a phase 2 trial, and only 12% of the reported studies covered the 90% age range of first Ewing sarcoma recurrence (8–38 years). Half of the available phase II trials were only open in the US, and only 30% were international collaborative trials. Main classes assessed over these last decades in Ewing sarcoma were anti‐IGF1/IGFR inhibitors, checkpoint inhibitors or antiangiogenics. These trials reported a global ORR of 10%, a median PFS of 1.9 months (1.3–14.7) and a median OS of 7.6 months (5–30). Among the 11 trials considered positive, the median PFS and OS were 4.5 months (1.3–10) and 16.6 months (6.9–30), respectively [8]. Beyond the availability of new drugs, these data highlight the importance to support international initiatives such as the Euro Ewing Consortium to increase knowledge on Ewing sarcoma biology, discover and preclinically test new pathways and promote drug discovery. Only with stronger preclinical evidence of activity will we be able to establish successful, innovative clinical trials and improve patient outcomes.

Independent risk factors identified in this study align with those reported in the literature, such as the pathological response to neoadjuvant treatment, location of metastasis and loco‐regional treatment of metastasis. This illustrates the fact that tumor biology is driving the prognosis of these patients together with the possibility to propose loco‐regional treatment of metastasis. Approximately 60% of patients had loco‐regional procedure for their metastasis in this study, over 50% received radiation therapy, and nearly one‐third had multiple procedures. Ewing sarcoma is considered radiosensitive and radiation to metastatic sites is recommended when feasible, particularly in symptomatic disease [20].

This study has several limitations. We chose to assess TTNT as it is a more robust endpoint than PFS in retrospective design. However, loco‐regional procedure given in “cloture” may lead to overestimation of TTNT. Nonetheless, this reflects real‐world patterns of care. Besides, TTNT has shown a better correlation with OS than PFS in retrospective studies [21, 22]. Our study used real‐world data from a national prospective registry. Real‐world data provide valuable insights in situations where randomized controlled trials are not feasible, especially in rare tumors such as Ewing sarcoma. When the outcomes from real‐world data studies align with those from clinical trials, this reinforces evidence and can help extend inferences from existing trials to other patient populations. However, real‐world data studies are prone to multiple biases and one should be cautious about adopting new therapies only based on such evidence. This retrospective study comprises patients with heterogeneous profiles and diverse chemotherapy regimens, leading to small numbers in the subgroup analysis on TTNT for specific lines and systemic treatment regimens. However, it gives a good overview of real‐world therapeutic strategies proposed to Ewing sarcoma patients in main French national sarcoma expert centers.

Conclusion

Metastatic Ewing sarcoma is a rare disease with a poor OS and an unmet medical need. In this multicenter national study, we assessed patterns of care of patients with upfront and relapsing metastatic Ewing sarcoma from 12 to 71 years old. Our findings show that patients with upfront metastasis had a longer OS compared to those with metastatic relapse (not accounting for the median time from diagnosis to metastasis in the relapse cohort of 20.6 months), mainly due to the possibility to offer aggressive approaches combining dose dense polychemotherapy with loco‐regional procedures in highly selected patients during first metastatic treatment line. From progression or metastatic relapse, prognosis remains poor with a median OS of less than 2 years. Various agents and regimens like TOPO‐CP, TEMIRI or Ifosfamide are associated with similar outcomes. Loco‐regional treatment of metastasis is associated with better survival and should always be considered in the therapeutic strategy when feasible.

TKIs such as cabozantinib or regorafenib can represent an alternative to chemotherapy regimen in metastatic Ewing sarcoma not amenable to curative‐intended loco‐regional treatment to metastasis, with less toxicity, notably when hematological toxicity is a concern. Combination trials with new drugs comprising TKIs represent a compelling strategy for future phase II trials in Ewing sarcoma. There remains a limited number of new treatment opportunities offered to patients with metastatic Ewing sarcoma. Stronger biological research, academic and industrial collaborations, as well as patient inclusion in clinical trials are needed to improve access to new active treatment strategies, and ultimately improve OS.

Response to “Is it time to revise criteria and treatment of type 2 AIP?”
Authors: Nicolò de Pretis, Luca Frulloni
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the letter of Lorenzo and Rebours 1 about the article recently published by our group on type 2 AIP. 2 Considering the benign clinical presentation and the need of minimizing systemic steroids in IBD patients, Lorenzo and Rebours proposed reviewing the International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria for type 2 AIP, 3 reconsidering the need of steroid administration in asymptomatic patients (especially if concomitant IBD is diagnosed) to achieve a definitive diagnosis of type 2 AIP.

This topic is of great interest. Response to steroids is considered a cardinal criterion for AIP diagnosis according to the ICDC. In patients without a definite pathological diagnosis of AIP, steroids are administered with a dual purpose of achieving diagnosis and inducing remission.

If imaging is typical for AIP (diffuse forms) and pre‐existing or concomitant IBD is observed, the diagnosis of probable type 2 AIP is achieved only in case of clinical/radiological response to steroids. In these patients, who are generally young (3rd–4th decade), there is no issue of differential diagnosis with pancreatic cancer, even though pancreatic lymphoma with diffuse enlargement of the pancreatic gland has rarely been described. Therefore, considering that asymptomatic AIP and spontaneous remission have been described, steroid administration could be avoided in this specific setting. However, even in asymptomatic patients, the exocrine function of the pancreas might be impaired, and the available literature suggests that steroids improve exocrine function in a significant proportion of patients. 4 Moreover, imaging and an accurate organ function evaluation during follow‐up is needed if no therapy is administered, because the persistence of the disease at imaging probably will suggest the use of steroids. Furthermore, the steroid treatment is short (up to 3 months generally) and type 2 AIP patients have a low relapse risk 5 and the probability of re‐administering steroid therapy is low over the lifetime, without long‐term steroid‐related side effects described in the literature.

If imaging is atypical for AIP (focal form) even in the presence of IBD, steroid therapy is mandatory to definitively exclude cancer in asymptomatic patients with negative or inconclusive EUS‐FNB, even if patients are generally young with a low risk of pancreatic cancer, and to make a diagnosis of type 2 AIP.

Despite the lack of serological biomarkers, and the unsatisfactory performance of FNB for diagnosis of type 2 AIP, 6 we agree that the presence of typical imaging associated with the diagnosis of IBD might allow the diagnosis of probable type 2 AIP even before steroid treatment. However, solid data supporting safety and long‐term outcomes of conservative management without steroid administration are lacking and further studies are needed to investigate this strategy.

Sincerely,

Nicolò de Pretis and Luca Frulloni

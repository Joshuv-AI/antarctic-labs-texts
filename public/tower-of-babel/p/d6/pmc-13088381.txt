Esketamine vs. sufentanil for anesthesia induction in elderly patients undergoing transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB): effect on hemodynamic stability, airway reactivity, and oxygenation
Authors: MingChong Wang, WenHua Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundElderly patients undergoing transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) face anesthesia-associated complications from cardiopulmonary impairment and comorbidities, so geriatric TBLB anesthetic management is crucial.AimTo compare esketamine and sufentanil on hemodynamics, airway pressure, bronchospasm incidence and oxygenation in elderly TBLB induction.MethodsUsing a retrospective propensity score matching (PSM) cohort study design, We enrolled recruited elderly patients undergoing TBLB at the Hospital between January 2024 and August 2025. Patients were divided by anesthesia induction protocol: control group (Group S, sufentanil combination) and experimental group (Group E, esketamine combination). A 1:1 PSM method was used to match 40 patients in each group. Primary outcomes included heart rate (HR), mean arterial pressure (mBP), blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂) and airway pressure (Paw) at the time of entering the operating room (T0), LMA insertion (T1), bronchoscopic biopsy (T2), the end of operation (T3), and LMA removal (T4). The incidence of bronchial smooth muscle spasm was observed under the microscope. Secondary outcomes were extubation time, recovery time, and PONV incidence.ResultsAfter PSM, baseline characteristics were comparable (P>0.05). At time points of high stimulation (T1, T2), Group E had significantly higher HR and mBP than Group S (P<0.05). This translated to a markedly lower incidence of clinically significant hypotension requiring intervention in Group E (12.5% vs. 45.0%, P = 0.001). Group E also exhibited significantly lower Paw at T1-T3, higher SpO₂ at T2, and a markedly reduced incidence of bronchospasm (5.0% compared to 22.5%, P<0.05). Recovery profiles favored Group E, showing shorter extubation and recovery times alongside a lower incidence of PONV (P<0.05).ConclusionsIn this retrospective study, esketamine showed associations with improved hemodynamic stability, lower incidence of bronchospasm and airway pressure, reduced PONV, and better oxygenation during induction in elderly TBLB patients, suggesting its potential as a viable alternative.

Significance & Innovation

This PSM study indicates that, compared with sufentanil, esketamine may support more stable hemodynamics, lower airway reactivity, improved oxygenation, and enhanced postoperative recovery in elderly patients undergoing TBLB. The findings provide observational evidence for optimizing anesthesia regimens in this clinical setting.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the most common and deadly malignancy worldwide. The increasing use of CT scans has led to earlier detection of pulmonary nodules. Transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) is a minimally invasive technique for sampling peripheral lung lesions. Compared to percutaneous biopsy, it is safer for central lesions or those near critical structures, with a lower pneumothorax risk [1]. Modern TBLB often utilizes image guidance such as radial ultrasound to enhance accuracy [2]. However, TBLB is a highly stimulating procedure involving prolonged operation, repeated breath-holds, and significant airway manipulation. Thus, optimal anesthesia must provide deep sedation and analgesia to ensure patient stillness and respiratory stability, while minimizing cardiorespiratory depression to ensure safety and allow rapid recovery—presenting a major challenge in anesthetic management. Elderly patients are the main population undergoing TBLB, accounting for approximately more than 70% of such procedures. This group is often accompanied by cardiopulmonary dysfunction, insufficient physiological reserve and a variety of underlying diseases, and is more prone to complications such as hemodynamic instability, hypoxemia, bronchospasm, delayed postoperative recovery and cognitive dysfunction during anesthesia induction and surgery [3–5]. Therefore, it is crucial to select an induction agent for elderly TBLB patients that provides deep sedation and analgesia, stabilizes circulation, suppresses airway reactivity, and minimizes respiratory impact.

In the selection of anesthesia induction drugs for TBLB, sufentanil, as a classical and highly selective µ-opioid receptor agonist, has been widely used in general anesthesia induction and intraoperative maintenance due to its strong analgesic effect, rapid onset and effective inhibition of cough reflex [6, 7]. Studies have shown that sufentanil has a lipid-soluble receptor affinity 5–10 times higher than that of fentanyl. Rapid blood-brain barrier penetration confers advantages in hemodynamic stabilization and anesthetic-sparing effects perioperatively [8]. However, the limitations of its clinical application have become increasingly prominent: sufentanil induces a dose-dependent respiratory depression, and the risk of hypoxemia after use is significantly increased in elderly patients due to decreased lung compliance and functional residual capacity [9]. At the same time, Its associated adverse reactions include dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, vomiting, and skeletal muscle rigidity, as well as the potential for withdrawal reactions and addiction, so it needs to be combined with other drugs to balance efficacy and safety in clinical practice [10]. In elderly patients with TBLB, which requires high respiratory function and recovery quality, the risk of respiratory depression and postoperative adverse reactions of sufentanil has become a key factor restricting its application.

In recent years, distinguished by its enhanced sedative activity and improved tolerability, esketamine has emerged as a notable alternative to racemic ketamine., and its application potential in the field of anesthesia in elderly patients [11–13]. Compared with traditional ketamine, esketamine is excreted through the kidney, the body clearance rate is significantly improved, and the plasma concentration can be rapidly decreased 2–4 h after administration, which reduces the risk of adverse reactions caused by drug accumulation [14]. More importantly, esketamine has unique pharmacological properties: on the one hand, its sympathomimetic activity can activate the central β-adrenergic pathway, which is suitable for elderly patients with hemodynamic instability. It can provide sedative and analgesic effects while reducing fluctuations such as sudden drop in blood pressure and slow heart rate(HR). On the other hand, it has a slight effect on respiratory depression and can maintain respiratory drive and airway protective reflex, especially for elderly patients with COPD or sleep apnea syndrome [11, 15]. Furthermore, the neuroprotective properties of esketamine have been extensively documented in preclinical and clinical investigations, suggesting its potential to mitigate the incidence of postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD), while its antidepressant effect can reduce the risk of postoperative depression and further improve the prognosis of patients [16–18]. When administered adjunctively with propofol and remifentanil, subanesthetic-dose esketamine (0.15–0.3 mg/kg) exhibits a significant anesthetic-sparing effect, shorten the postoperative recovery time, and maintain hemodynamic stability in the routine fiberoptic bronchoscopy examination of elderly patients [19]. Although esketamine has shown a good prospect in airway anesthesia, the existing studies mostly focus on fiberoptic bronchoscopy, and the relevant evidence for TBLB is still lacking. Compared with conventional bronchoscopy, TBLB is more delicate to perform, has stronger airway stimulation, and requires longer anesthesia maintenance time, which puts forward higher requirements for the depth of sedation, analgesic effect and respiratory safety of anesthetic drugs. At present, there is no systematic report on the direct comparison of esketamine and sufentanil in the induction of anesthesia in elderly patients with TBLB. The differences between the two in maintaining hemodynamic stability, reducing airway hyperresponsiveness, improving oxygenation function and promoting postoperative recovery are not clear.

Based on the above background, the present study used a retrospective propensity score-matched cohort study design to systematically compare the effects of esketamine and sufentanil on hemodynamic stability, airway responsiveness and oxygenation during TBLB anesthesia induction in elderly patients, and the following hypotheses were proposed: In elderly patients with TBLB, compared with sufentanil combined with remimazolam and rocuronium, esketamine combined with the same adjuvant drug regimen, It can more effectively maintain intraoperative hemodynamic stability (reduce the fluctuation range of HR and mBP), reduce airway hyperresponsiveness (reduce the incidence of bronchospasm and Paw), improve intraoperative oxygenation function (improve SpO₂ at key operation time points), and shorten postoperative extubation time and recovery time, Its mechanism contributes to a marked reduction in postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), alongside a decrease in other related complications. The findings are expected to provide preliminary observational evidence to support clinical anesthesia drug selection and perioperative management optimization in elderly patients undergoing TBLB.

Materials and methods

Study design

A cohort of elderly patients undergoing TBLB was established using propensity score matching, drawing from data collected at the Hospital between January 2024 and August 2025. The data of 110 patients who met the initial screening criteria were extracted through the electronic system. According to the anesthesia induction protocol, the patients were divided into control group (group S, sufentanil combined with remimazolam and rocuronium) and experimental group (group E, esketamine combined with remimazolam and rocuronium). To mitigate the effects of intergroup confounders, 1:1 PSM was used: taking age, gender, BMI and ASA classification as the main covariates, the nearest neighbor matching method was used, and the caliper was set at 0.02. Finally, 80 participants were successfully matched, including 40 in group S and 40 in group E. The design operation process is shown in Fig. 1.

Ethical statement

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Qiqihar Medical University(Approval No. 2024LL-189). The principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (DH) were strictly followed, and all study procedures complied with international ethical standards. As a retrospective analysis based on archived clinical data, the Ethics Committee granted a waiver of informed consent. All data were anonymized to fully protect participant privacy and confidentiality throughout the research.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: ① Age ≥ 65 years old; ② Body mass index (BMI) 18–25 kg/m²; ③ American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade ⅱ-ⅲ; ④TBLB is required for clinical diagnosis without surgical contraindications; ⑤Complete medical records including intraoperative vital signs, airway reactions and postoperative recovery data.

Exclusion criteria: ①Allergy or contraindication to esketamine, sufentanil, remimazolam, rocuronium and other study-related drugs; ②Complicated with severe hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥ 180 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 110 mmHg, not effectively controlled), acute myocardial infarction, severe arrhythmia and other cardiovascular diseases; ③Severe liver and kidney dysfunction (Child-Pugh grade C or glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min); ④ difficult airway (Mallampati grade Ⅳ, mouth opening < 3 cm or limited neck movement); ⑤ History of acute respiratory infection, increased intracranial pressure (> 20 mmHg), glaucoma or mental illness; ⑥Missing data of key observation indicators in medical records.

Anesthesia protocol

Preoperative preparation

All patients were instructed to observe an 8-hour fast and a 4-hour clear fluid fast preoperatively. Upon entering the operating room (T0), baseline airway pressure (Paw) was recorded via the anesthesia machine’s pressure sensor during mask oxygenation and spontaneous breathing. Peripheral venous access was then established. Continuous monitoring was initiated, including heart rate (HR), mean arterial pressure (mBP), oxygen saturation (SpO2), Paw, electrocardiogram (ECG), and bispectral index (BIS) to assess anesthesia depth [20].

Anesthesia induction

Drug dosing was based on institutional anesthesia practice, prescribing information, and literature supporting low-dose esketamine for induction in elderly patients [13]. Sufentanil was titrated cautiously in accordance with general guidelines for geriatric administration [21].

Group S: sufentanil (50 µg/mL) was slowly administered intravenously at 0.2 µg/kg (infusion time > 1 min), followed by remimazolam (2 mg/mL) 0.2 mg/kg and rocuronium (10 mg/mL) 0.6 mg/kg.

Group E: esketamine (25 mg/mL) was slowly administered intravenously at 0.5 mg/kg (infusion time > 2 min), with the same dosing regimen for remimazolam and rocuronium as in Group S.

Anesthesia maintenance

Both groups were maintained by total intravenous anesthesia. Propofol (10 mg/mL) was continuously infused at 4–6 mg·kg− 1·h− 1 and remifentanil (1 mg/mL) at 0.1–0.2 µg·kg− 1·min− 1 via an infusion pump According to the monitoring results of muscle relaxation monitor (Danmeter company, Denmark, model: TOF-Watch SX), rocuronium 0.15 mg/kg was intermittently injected intravenously to maintain four train stimulation (TOF) at 0–1 response. The BIS value was maintained between 40 and 60 to ensure the appropriate depth of anesthesia.

Intraoperative management

After induction, a laryngeal mask airway was inserted for mechanical ventilation. Proper placement and seal were confirmed by symmetrical bilateral breath sounds, chest rise, and a normal airway pressure curve without leak. Ventilator settings: tidal volume 6–8 mL/kg, respiratory rate 12–14 breaths/min, I:E ratio 1:2, FiO2 60%–80%, maintaining PetCO2 at 35–45 mmHg. Intraoperative hypotension was defined as mBP decrease > 20% from baseline; bradycardia as HR < 50 beats/min. Hypotension was treated with rapid infusion of 200–300 mL compound sodium chloride solution or intravenous ephedrine (30 mg/mL) 6 mg. Bradycardia received atropine (0.5 mg/mL) 0.5 mg intravenously.All procedures were performed by attending physicians with over five years of experience in interventional pulmonology. Using digital subtraction CT real-time fluoroscopy and an ultrasound-guided sheath, samples were obtained via flexible bronchoscopy with biopsy forceps. Following standard protocol, the forceps tip was kept ≥ 2 cm from the pleura, and 3–5 tissue specimens (1–2 mm in diameter) were collected for pathological analysis [22].

Postoperative recovery

After the end of the operation, the infusion of propofol and remifentanil was stopped, and the laryngeal mask airway was removed when the patient’s spontaneous breathing recovered, the tidal volume was > 5 mL/kg, and the patient was conscious and able to follow the instructions. The patient was transferred to the post anesthesia care unit (PACU) and observed for 30 minutes.

Observation indicators

Baseline data

The baseline data of patients were collected before operation, including demographic characteristics, major comorbidities, pulmonary function indexes, surgical related indexes and laboratory examination indexes.

Hemodynamics

The HR (BPM) and mBP (mmHg) of the two groups were recorded at the time of entering the operating room (T0), LMA insertion (T1), bronchoscopic biopsy (T2), the end of operation (T3), and LMA removal (T4). The differences between each time point and T0 were calculated to evaluate the hemodynamic fluctuations.

Airway related indicators

Paw (cmH₂O) was recorded at each time point from T0 to T4. The incidence of bronchial smooth muscle spasm was recorded by direct observation under fiberoptic bronchoscopy (criteria [23]: congestion and edema of bronchial mucosa, stenosis of lumen, significant increase in airway resistance during ventilation, drug intervention or adjustment of ventilation parameters).

Oxygenation function indicators

SpO₂ (%) was recorded at each time point from T0 to T4 to evaluate intraoperative oxygenation maintenance.

Postoperative recovery period indicators

The time of extubation (from the end of the operation to the removal of the laryngeal mask), the time of recovery (from the cessation of anesthetic drugs to the call of energy), and the rate of PONV at the 30-minute mark after surgery (nausea: subjective feeling of stomach discomfort and vomiting; vomiting: gastric contents discharged through the mouth) were recorded.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was estimated based on preliminary retrospective data from eligible cases analyzed prior to this study. Initial findings suggested an intraoperative bronchospasm incidence of approximately 23% in the sufentanil group versus about 5% in the esketamine group. The sample size was estimated by G-power software, the significance level (α) was set at 0.05 (two-tail test), and the statistical power (1-β) was set at 0.8. Based on these parameters, the power analysis yielded a requirement of 35 subjects in each group, and the total sample size was 70. Given the retrospective design, potential case loss due to incomplete data or covariate imbalance during PSM was anticipated. Assuming an approximate 80% matching efficiency, the initial sample was expanded to 110 to ensure sufficient post-matching size. Following 1:1 PSM, 80 patients (40 per group) were successfully matched, meeting and exceeding the pre-specified sample size requirement.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0. Normally distributed continuous data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s), and inter-group comparisons were made with the independent samples t-test. For data deviating from a normal distribution, the median (interquartile range) is reported, and the Mann-Whitney U test was employed for comparisons. Analysis of categorical data, presented as n (%), utilized the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for expected frequencies below five. Propensity score was calculated using logistic regression to construct a propensity score model, and matched 1:1 according to the nearest neighbor method with a clamp value of 0.02. After matching, balance between groups was evaluated by standardized mean difference (SMD) (SMD < 0.1 was considered as good balance). The results of HR, mBP, Paw, SpO₂, extubation time and recovery time were expressed by x̄±s. The incidence of PONV was analyzed by chi-square test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value < 0.05 for all tests conducted.

Results

Comparison of baseline data

A total of 110 elderly patients who met the criteria for TBLB surgery were screened in this study. According to the anesthesia induction regimen, The initial allocation resulted in Group E (n = 55) and Group S (n = 55). As detailed in Table 1, the two groups exhibited substantial differences in several baseline characteristics before PSM, including age, pulmonary function indexes, surgery-related indexes, and laboratory test indexes (P < 0.05). To control for bias from these baseline imbalances, the matching was implemented via a 1:1 nearest-neighbor algorithm, incorporating age, sex, BMI, and ASA physical status as the core matching variables, with a caliper value set at 0.02. After matching, 40 patients in group E were successfully matched to 40 patients in group S with highly similar baseline characteristics, forming the final analysis cohort (n = 80). After PSM, the SMD of demographic characteristics, major comorbidities, pulmonary function indexes, surgery-related indexes and laboratory examination indexes included in the comparison were all < 0.1, and the balance between the groups was good, and there was no statistically significant difference (P > 0.05).

Comparison of hemodynamic indexes

The two groups demonstrated comparable baseline hemodynamics, with no significant differences in mBP or HR at T0 (P > 0.05). At T1, group S demonstrated significantly lower mBP and HR values compared to group E (P < 0.05), mBP and HR remained stable in group E, At T2, HR and mBP in group S were still significantly lower than those in group E (P < 0.05), while the mBP and HR in group E remained stable, close to the baseline level. At T3 and T4, mBP and HR tended to recover in the two groups, and there was no significant difference between the two groups (P > 0.05). (Table 2).

Differences in hemodynamic parameters translated directly into clinically significant event rates (Table 3). Intraoperative hypotension occurred in 45.0% of the sufentanil group, significantly higher than the 12.5% in the esketamine group (χ² = 10.114, P = 0.001), with 37.5% of these cases requiring ephedrine. The sufentanil group also had a higher incidence of bradycardia (22.5% vs. 5.0%; χ² = 5.165, P = 0.023). Overall, the proportion of patients needing any vasoactive agent was significantly lower in the esketamine group (12.5% vs. 50.0%; χ² = 13.333, P < 0.001).

In conclusion, compared to sufentanil, esketamine provided more effective hemodynamic stability during key procedural phases of TBLB, significantly reducing the risk of clinically significant hypotension and bradycardia requiring pharmacological intervention.

Comparison of airway related indicators

The baseline levels of Paw were comparable between groups E and S(P > 0.05). Discussion

In this study, a retrospective PSM cohort design was used to compare the clinical effects of esketamine and sufentanil combined with remimazolam and rocuronium for anesthesia induction in elderly patients with TBLB. Compared to conventional sufentanil, esketamine (0.5 mg/kg) induction provided a superior hemodynamic profile in elderly TBLB patients, characterized by enhanced stability and a lower incidence of hypotension and bradycardia. It can effectively reduce Paw, reduce the risk of bronchospasm, and improve oxygenation guarantee during operation. It can promote rapid postoperative recovery and reduce the incidence of PONV. These findings provide preliminary observational evidence for optimizing anesthetic management in elderly patients during such irritating airway procedures.

Elderly patients are prone to hemodynamic fluctuations during anesthesia induction, a risk heightened by the intense stimulation of TBLB [24]. This study, along with previous research, demonstrates that esketamine’s unique sympathomimetic properties help better maintain blood pressure and heart rate during induction [25–27]. For instance, studies by Zhao et al. [25]and Ji et al. [26] confirmed that low-dose esketamine stabilizes circulation in elderly patients during airway manipulation. Our findings further quantify this advantage as significantly lower rates of clinically significant hypotension and bradycardia requiring intervention. In contrast, sufentanil, a potent opioid, effectively suppresses stress responses but may cause dose-dependent circulatory depression due to sympatholysis and vagal activation [27, 28], consistent with the marked decreases in BP and HR observed in Group S during high-stimulation phases. It should be noted that ensuring hemodynamic stability in elderly patients can be approached through multiple strategies. Besides esketamine, α2-adrenergic agonists such as dexmedetomidine have also been widely studied for attenuating tracheal intubation stress, albeit via different mechanisms [29]. The transient and manageable sympathomimetic effects of esketamine observed in this study offer a viable complementary option for elderly patients with cardiovascular risks.

Optimal airway conditions are crucial for successful TBLB. This study showed that the esketamine group had lower intraoperative airway pressure, a significantly lower incidence of bronchospasm (5.0% vs. 22.5%), and better oxygenation maintenance during biopsy. These findings align with reports by Zhang et al. [30] and Li et al. [31] in different surgical contexts, suggesting that esketamine may reduce airway hyperreactivity through anti-inflammatory effects and direct action on bronchial smooth muscle In contrast, while opioids suppress cough reflexes, they may increase hypoxemia risk due to respiratory drive depression and histamine release [32]. In bronchoscopic anesthesia, multiple strategies exist to maintain airway stability and respiratory safety. For instance, remifentanil is often utilized for its rapid onset and titratability in procedures requiring quick adjustments of anesthesia depth, balancing patient immobility against respiratory depression [33]. Our results indicate that esketamine may serve as a valuable component in such balanced approaches.

Promoting rapid and smooth recovery in elderly patients is a key anesthesia goal. In this study, the esketamine group demonstrated shorter extubation and awakening times, along with a lower incidence of PONV. These benefits are associated with its rapid metabolism, low accumulation risk [34], and “opioid-sparing effect” that reduces opioid requirements. Reviews by Lü et al. [35]and Wang et al. [36]support esketamine’s role in improving postoperative recovery metrics. In contrast, sufentanil’s prolonged effect and greater accumulation tendency in the elderly may contribute to delayed awakening and higher PONV risk [37]. Therefore, the advantages of esketamine hold clear practical significance for reducing postoperative complications and accelerating recovery in elderly patients.

Conclusion

In summary, this study suggests that in elderly patients undergoing TBLB, the esketamine-based induction regimen may be associated with better intraoperative hemodynamic stability, reduced airway reactivity, and improved postoperative recovery compared to the sufentanil-based regimen. These findings offer a clinically useful alternative, particularly for patients susceptible to hemodynamic fluctuations or at higher risk of airway hyperreactivity. Despite the positive results of this study, there are still some limitations. First, as a retrospective study, although we used PSM to control known confounding factors as much as possible, we still cannot avoid the possible bias caused by some unmeasured factors. Second, this study was a single-center study with a relatively limited sample size, which may affect the extrapolation of the results. Third, the effects of different doses of esketamine were not compared, and the optimal dose could not be determined. Finally, airway responsiveness is mainly assessed by indirect indicators such as hemodynamics In the future, multi-center and large-sample trials are needed to further verify our conclusions. Samples from different regions and races were included to improve the generalization and accuracy of the results, and to implement individualized and precise anesthesia management for patients.

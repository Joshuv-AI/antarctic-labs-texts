Characteristics and functionalities of digital tools supporting motivation and adherence to physical activity in older adults with health conditions: a protocol for a scoping review
Authors: Simone Burin-Chu, Nounagnon F Agbangla, Isabelle Caby, Farole Bossede
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRegular physical activity declines with age, particularly among older adults with health conditions. Digital tools, including wearable devices, mobile applications, virtual reality systems and tele-exercise platforms, offer promising ways to promote physical activity. However, the technological components of these tools and their reported associations with motivation, adherence and physical activity levels remain underexplored. This scoping review aims to map the characteristics and functionalities of digital tools used to promote physical activity among older adults with health conditions and to examine how these components are described in relation to psychobehavioural outcomes.Methods and analysisThis review will follow the Joanna Briggs Institute methodological guidance. Searches will be conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, PsycInfo and SportDiscus. The reference lists of included studies will also be screened to identify additional relevant articles. Eligible studies will include adults aged 60 years or older with health conditions using digital tools for physical activity. Two reviewers will independently select studies and extract data on the components of digital tools. A descriptive synthesis will summarise the key technological characteristics, and a content analysis will identify and categorise tool components, describing how they are reported in relation to psychobehavioural outcomes and theoretical constructs.Ethics and disseminationThis review does not involve human participants, so ethical approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and stakeholder summaries.

Introduction

Regular physical activity is a key determinant of physical, cognitive and mental health in older adults.13 It contributes to the maintenance of mobility and autonomy,4 prevents and manages chronic diseases,5 reduces the risk of falls6 and improves quality of life4 and social participation.7 8 However, physical activity levels tend to decline with age,9 making it difficult for many older adults to sustain regular exercise participation.10 11 Despite global initiatives such as the WHO’s12 ‘Global Action Plan on Physical Activity 2018–2030’, achieving sustained behaviour change among older adults remains a major public health challenge.13 Barriers include physiological limitations, pain, fatigue and psychological or social obstacles such as low self-efficacy, limited facility access, insufficient social support and low interest in physical activity.1315

Motivation and adherence are widely recognised as key determinants or predictors of success in physical activity programmes.16 17 Adherence refers to the consistent maintenance of a target behaviour over time,18 while motivation reflects the internal and external forces that initiate, guide and sustain actions toward objectives.19 Several theoretical frameworks have been proposed to explain motivation and adherence in behavioural change. For instance, Self-Determination Theory (Ryan and Deci, 1985)19 postulates that human behaviour is motivated by the satisfaction of three basic psychological needs, namely autonomy, competence and relatedness. The Transtheoretical Model of Change by Prochaska & Di Clemente (1982)20 defines behavioural change as a cyclical process. The previously mentioned and other theoretical models, such as Self-Efficacy Theory21 and the Theory of Planned Behaviour,22 are widely used to explain how people adopt and maintain physical activity. In addition, experiencing enjoyment and valuing physical activity can support long-term engagement in the practice.23

Digital tools have emerged as promising approaches to support physical activity, particularly among inactive or sedentary populations.24 The WHO25 defines digital health as the use of information and communication technologies for health, including eHealth, mHealth, big data and artificial intelligence. Various digital tools have been used to promote physical activity, such as smartwatches and wearable sensors,26 mobile health applications,27 exergames,28 virtual reality systems29 and tele-exercise platforms.30 They can provide real-time feedback to visualise performance and progress, support goal setting and remote monitoring, encourage social interaction and enhance autonomy through personalised and adaptive experiences.31 32 Collectively, these components have been reported to support motivation and adherence.33 Many are also embedded in playful and engaging environments that incorporate gamification, defined as the use of game design elements in non-game contexts.34

Previous reviews have examined the effects of digital tools on physical activity, reporting outcomes such as self-reported activity levels,35 36 step counts,37 reductions in sedentary behaviour38 and changes in motivational or behavioural processes.39 Valenzuela et al33 reported high adherence to technology-based interventions. However, existing reviews have primarily focused on intervention outcomes rather than on the characteristics of digital tools and how these may relate to motivation, adherence and physical activity levels. This focus limits understanding of how these technological components may be prioritised and applied in the development or implementation of digital tools to promote physical activity among older adults with health conditions. To address this gap, we propose a scoping review to systematically identify and categorise the components of digital tools, including their technical characteristics, functionalities, features, design elements and reported usability or limitations, as described in empirical studies involving this population. Rather than evaluating intervention effectiveness, this review will map the existing evidence and examine how functional and design components of digital tools are reported in relation to psycho-behavioural and physical activity outcomes.

Objectives

The specific objectives of this scoping review are to (1) identify the different types of digital tools and describe their characteristics, features, functionalities, design elements and usability; (2) examine how these components are described in relation to motivation, adherence and physical activity outcomes and (3) identify key elements and reported limitations of digital tools relevant to the practice of physical activity. In this context, we consider functionalities to be the actions performed by the digital tool, features to be its options or integrated attributes, and design elements to be the interface, interaction and visual components that optimise its use. Usability and limitations refer to factors such as adverse effects, dropout rates and user experience.

Methods

We considered the scoping review methodology to address our objectives. This study design allows systematic mapping of the available literature on a topic. It enables identification of key concepts, sources of evidence and research gaps.40

A five-step process will be used: (1) identify research questions; (2) identify relevant studies; (3) select studies; (4) chart the data and (5) synthesise and summarise. The optional stakeholder consultation stage will not be adopted.41 42 As this scoping review aims to map the functional and design components of digital tools as described in published empirical studies, it does not seek to generate new stakeholder perspectives. Furthermore, risk of bias assessment will not be performed, as the aim is to map the breadth of available evidence rather than evaluate study rigour.43 The five-step process will follow the recommended framework by Arksey and O’Malley,41 later refined by Levac et al,42 and recently updated by Peters et al.40 The recommendations from the Joanna Briggs Institute Evidence Synthesis Manual will also be followed. The final report will be drafted based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA) checklist.44 The protocol for this review has been prospectively registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF; DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/WAT75).

Stage 1: determining the research question

This scoping review addresses four main questions: Which digital tools are currently used to support motivation and adherence to physical activity among older adults with health conditions? How are these digital tools described in the studies? What characteristics and functionalities of digital tools are reported, and how are they described in relation to motivation, adherence and physical activity levels in this population? Do studies report usability and limitations factors, and if so, what are they?

Stage 2: identifying relevant studies

Searches will be conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, PsycInfo and SportDiscus databases. A tailored electronic search strategy has been developed for each database. Search terms were derived from previous studies and then grouped by concept (population, intervention, digital tools and outcomes). The terms were then combined using the Boolean operator “OR”, and the matched groups were linked with “AND.” Table 1 summarises the search terms. To ensure consistency, the review team conducted a pilot test of this strategy between October and November 2025, and the approach was subsequently refined and updated in February 2026. The search strategy is presented in online supplemental appendix 1.

Search terms

In addition to electronic database searches, the reference lists of all included studies will be systematically screened, with backward citation chasing to identify potentially relevant articles not captured by the initial search strategy. Forward citation chasing will also be performed to identify studies citing the included articles, using Web of Science and Google Scholar.45 Grey literature will not be included in the search strategy because the review is restricted to peer-reviewed empirical studies to ensure consistency in methodological reporting and data extraction, despite the potential value of these sources for technical or implementation details.

Stage 3: study selection

The main search will be conducted between March and May 2026, and studies published up to the date of the final search will be considered eligible. The date of the final search will be reported in the final manuscript, including any updates conducted before submission or publication. After identifying studies, duplicates will be removed. Two independent reviewers will screen the titles and abstracts to determine whether studies meet the review’s inclusion and exclusion criteria. These reviewers will also read the full texts of selected studies for final inclusion. Disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer. Study selection will be conducted using Rayyan software. The selection process will be presented using a PRISMA flow diagram.44 Reasons for excluding reports will also be listed.

The inclusion criteria were defined using the Population, Concept and Context mnemonic, as recommended for scoping reviews.40

Population: Studies will be eligible if they explicitly target adults aged ≥60 years or report a mean sample age at or above this threshold. Mixed-age studies will be included only when data specific to this age group can be extracted or when most of the sample (≥50%) falls within this group. Participants may be living in community or institutional settings (eg, nursing homes) and present with health-related conditions. For this purpose, health conditions will be defined as chronic or age-related conditions that may influence mobility, function or participation in activities of daily living.46 This includes physical or chronic diseases (eg, sarcopenia, cardiovascular disease), neurodegenerative disorders (eg, Alzheimer’s disease) and mental health conditions (eg, depression).

Concept: Studies will be included if they use digital tools, as defined in the introduction, such as connected or wearable devices, mobile health applications, exergames, virtual or augmented reality systems, or tele-exercise platforms designed to promote physical activity. They can be used individually or collectively. Digital tools for monitoring adherence to exercise programmes or managing exercise prescriptions are also eligible. The term physical activity will encompass the definition that includes both structured exercise and sports.47 Included studies must aim to assess the effects of digital tools on physical activity levels and/or outcomes related to motivation or adherence.

Context: Eligible studies should deliver physical activity in settings such as nursing homes, community centres, rehabilitation centres, clinical environments or home-based settings.

Type of evidence sources

Only empirical and interventional studies assessing digital tools in the context of physical activity will be included. Eligible designs include randomised and non-randomised clinical trials, pre-intervention and post-intervention studies, and feasibility studies. The search will cover studies published since 2000. This cut-off reflects the period of key technological developments related to the digital tools of interest.25 No language restrictions will be applied. Single case studies, systematic reviews with or without meta-analysis, conference or seminar abstracts, incomplete or non-peer-reviewed research, unpublished manuscripts, theses, dissertations and book chapters will be excluded.

Stage 4: charting the data

A structured data extraction form will collect relevant information from the included studies, following the topic framework provided in online supplemental appendix 2. As recommended by Pollock et al,48 reviewers will pilot the form on five studies randomly selected to ensure clarity and consistency before formal extraction. Data extraction will then be conducted independently by two reviewers, with disagreements resolved through discussion or by a third reviewer. Data will be extracted from any section of the reports, including the introduction, results, discussion, conclusions or supplementary information.48 If essential data are unclear or missing, the study authors will be contacted for clarification, with up to two attempts over a 4-week period.

Data extraction will include study characteristics and the components of the digital tools. Study-level information will include author, year, country, study design and population details (age, sex and health condition). Given the expected heterogeneity of health conditions, clinical profiles will be categorised a priori into broad predefined groups (eg, chronic diseases, neurodegenerative conditions and mental health conditions). Key descriptors, including the primary diagnosis and functional limitations, will be extracted to support the contextual interpretation of digital tool components. Intervention characteristics (duration, session frequency and setting), assessment tools, reported outcomes for physical activity (eg, step count), motivation (eg, questionnaire scores), adherence (eg, percentage of sessions completed) and follow-up research will also be collected. When available, theoretical frameworks or psychological models used to interpret the results will also be recorded.

For the digital tools, the extracted data will include the technology type, developer or company, functionalities, technical and design aspects, interface characteristics, intended purpose, methods of user interaction, planning and a description of physical activity. Additional information on limitations, adverse effects, dropout and usability will be extracted only if reported in the included interventional studies. The aim is to describe reported limitations in the included studies. To ensure clarity and consistency across multiple metrics, predefined operational rules will be applied during data extraction. Specifically, adherence and dropout will be treated as distinct constructs. Exercise adherence refers to participation in the prescribed physical activity, whereas tool-use adherence refers to compliance with the technology. Dropout will be classified as either in the exercise programme or with the digital tool. When multiple metrics are reported, each will be categorised according to its primary behavioural target, either physical activity behaviour or technology use, without aggregation.

Stage 5: collating, summarising and reporting the results

Data from study characteristics, population, interventions, outcomes and digital tool characteristics will be presented in descriptive and tabular form. The different types of digital tools will also be categorised according to their reported components through a content analysis48 49 conducted using Atlas.ti. To ensure a consistent coding approach, a hierarchical analytical framework will be applied. Digital tools will constitute level 1 of the analysis, while their components will be organised at level 2 into three parallel domains, including functionalities, features and design elements. The unit of analysis will be the smallest explicitly described technological component that performs a single function or interaction.50 When multiple components are described within the same sentence or paragraph, each will be extracted and coded separately. Study-level outcomes relating to motivation, adherence and physical activity levels will be extracted independently and, during the synthesis phase, descriptively mapped to the identified technological components to explore reported associations. Usability and reported limitations will not be included in the hierarchical coding of technological components but will be extracted separately and presented descriptively when reported.

Furthermore, the components of digital tools will be categorised according to key determinants of motivation and adherence. This analysis will be guided by Self-Determination Theory, focusing on the three basic psychological needs of autonomy (a sense of volition and choice), competence (a sense of effectiveness), and relatedness (a sense of connection with others).19 This analysis will also use the Behaviour Change Techniques taxonomy proposed by Abraham and Michie,51 which standardises components like goal setting, action planning, feedback, reinforcement and social support. This taxonomy is widely used in health and physical activity research and has been applied to identify techniques in exercise-related digital applications.52 Coding will follow predefined operational criteria derived from the constructs of both approaches. Multilabel coding will be permitted when a digital component explicitly reflects more than one theoretical construct, supported by distinct textual evidence.

In both phases of the content analysis, a pilot coding exercise on three studies will be conducted to calibrate interpretation and refine mapping criteria through team discussion. Following this, one reviewer will perform the primary coding, while a second reviewer will independently check all coding decisions. Any discrepancies will be resolved through discussion and, if necessary, consultation with a third reviewer. The results of this content analysis will then be discussed with the full scoping review team prior to final reporting.48 All findings will be presented in tabular format and complemented by a narrative synthesis. Therefore, the outputs of this scoping review will include a structured taxonomy of digital tools, a classification matrix mapping their functional and design components in relation to psychobehavioural outcomes in physical activity practice, and a descriptive summary of reported usability factors and contextual limitations.

Discussion

This scoping review aims to systematically map the existing evidence on digital tools used to promote physical activity among older adults with health conditions, examining their characteristics and functionalities, and how these are reported in relation to psychobehavioural outcomes. It will also explore barriers and usability issues, including dropout, user experience and reported adverse effects, when available.53 While previous reviews have primarily focused on intervention effectiveness, few have systematically mapped the functional and design components of digital tools in relation to motivation, adherence and physical activity among older adults with health conditions. Accordingly, this scoping review aims to address this gap by providing a structured, comprehensive evidence map.

Another original contribution is the consideration of theoretical models and behaviour change approaches in the context of digital tools, which remain underexplored in technology-based interventions for older adults.54 By linking digital resources to motivational and behavioural frameworks, this review is expected to provide a structured overview of how design features support physical activity engagement. In this context, it is expected to inform decision-making among professionals and developers working on digital innovations for healthy ageing. It may also highlight gaps in the evidence and potential adverse effects, informing the design of future digital innovations tailored to older adults.

This review has some limitations. The inclusion of a variety of study designs and digital tools is likely to introduce methodological heterogeneity, potentially limiting direct comparisons between studies. In line with scoping review methodology, no risk-of-bias assessment will be performed, so the review will not support firm conclusions about the causal effects of digital interventions on behaviour or physical activity levels. Finally, we chose to include only older adults with health conditions. Although this choice allows us to better target our objectives, there is a risk of losing relevant information from research conducted with healthy older adults, as many validations of physical activity technologies are performed in this population. Despite these limitations, the evidence synthesised will provide a comprehensive overview of current knowledge and practice. The findings may contribute to the evidence base for digital tools for healthy ageing, at a time when technology is increasingly important in supporting long-term adherence to physical activity programmes.

This protocol outlines a structured and transparent approach to addressing the research questions through systematic evidence mapping. The review will identify and summarise digital tools that support motivation, adherence and physical activity levels among older adults with health conditions and develop a taxonomy of these tools, together with a classification of their functional and design components. It will also provide a descriptive mapping of how these components are reported in relation to psychobehavioural outcomes, along with a summary of reported limitations.

Ethics and dissemination

This review does not involve human participants, so ethical approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and stakeholder summaries.

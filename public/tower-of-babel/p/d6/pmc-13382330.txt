Perirenal bronchogenic cyst with dermoid component: A rare urological case report
Authors: Shuai Luo, Xin Zhang, Yu Yang, Hailin Zhao, Xingzhuang He, Jianfeng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Combined bronchogenic and dermoid cyst is an extremely rare entity, with only three previously documented cases localized to the back and oral cavity. We herein present the first case arising within the clinically concealed perirenal space. A large perirenal cyst was incidentally detected on imaging in a young female patient and preoperatively considered as teratoma. Complete surgical excision was performed, and the final histopathological examination confirmed the mixed cystic lesion. This case highlights that urologists should include this rare combined lesion in the differential diagnosis of fat-containing perirenal masses besides teratoma.

Introduction

Dermoid cysts arise from ectodermal tissue entrapment along embryonic fusion planes, with cyst walls lined by epithelium and containing fully differentiated cutaneous structures.1 Bronchogenic cyst originates from abnormal development of bronchopulmonary tissue in embryonic stage, usually located in mediastinum and lung, and some bronchial tissue deviates from normal position to form closed cyst cavity.2 It is speculated that cells are trapped in fusion and continue to proliferate in ectopic state after some stimulation.3 Combined bronchogenic and dermoid cysts are exceedingly rare, with all previously published cases confined to the oral cavity and subcutaneous back tissue. We report the first such composite cyst arising within the perirenal space. The lesion was preoperatively considered as teratoma on cross-sectional imaging; definitive diagnosis was confirmed via postoperative histopathology following complete surgical resection, and the patient recovered uneventfully with hospital discharge.

A 19-year-old female was hospitalized three days after incidental identification of a perirenal mass. She initially presented with a foreign-body sensation in her throat, and an initial non-contrast CT unexpectedly demonstrated a large lesion within the left perirenal space. The patient had no significant past medical history, previous abdominal surgery, or relevant family history. She was not taking any regular medications. She was entirely asymptomatic, and no abdominal mass was palpable on physical examination. Laboratory testing revealed an elevated CA19-9 level of 84.34 IU/mL (reference range: 0–37.0 IU/mL), with all remaining blood parameters within normal limits.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (Fig. 1) demonstrated a mixed-attenuation mass measuring 11.1 cm × 10.4 cm × 11.7 cm within the superior left perirenal space. The lesion was predominantly fatty with focal hyperdense components and displayed no appreciable contrast enhancement. Its medial margin was intimately apposed to the left renal vessels. Radiologically, a benign neoplasm was favored, with mature teratoma as the leading differential diagnosis.Fig. 1Enhanced CT scan of a 19-year-old patient.

Via a left paramedian pararectus incision, retroperitoneal dissection was performed to access the perirenal space. The cyst was meticulously enucleated with careful preservation of the left renal vasculature and collecting system. Gross pathological examination (Fig. 2a) revealed a grayish-yellow cut surface with multiple locules filled with bean dreg-like and mucoid gelatinous material. Immunohistochemical staining showed positive expression of CK5/6 and WT1, whereas CK7, CK20, CEA, CR, PAX-8 and Desmin were negative; the Ki-67 proliferation index was approximately 1%. Histopathological examination demonstrated two distinct epithelial components. One component was lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with focal cartilaginous tissue, consistent with a bronchogenic cyst. The second component showed stratified squamous epithelium with keratinized material, consistent with a dermoid cyst component. The final diagnosis was established based on the combined histopathological and immunohistochemical findings. The patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 14 with no perioperative complications. One month after surgery, telephone follow-up confirmed that she remained asymptomatic and had resumed normal daily activities. Discussion

Only three cases of combined bronchogenic and dermoid cysts have been previously documented in published literature 4, 5, 6. All three patients shared similar clinical characteristics. First, lesions arose in the cutaneous or oral regions and presented with noticeable symptoms, enabling early diagnosis. Second, the lesions were either congenital or long-standing, gradually causing local compression and organ dysfunction with disease progression. All patients ultimately received complete surgical resection, with final diagnoses confirmed via histopathology.

In contrast to prior published cases, the present lesion arose within the perirenal compartment, reminding urologists to stay vigilant for rare mixed cystic perirenal lesions. Our patient was a young asymptomatic female with unremarkable physical findings. Deep perirenal location and soft consistency of the cyst likely accounted for the absence of compressive manifestations and contributed to its occult presentation. As proposed by Ulkumen et al.,7 elevated CA19-9 correlates with tissue necrosis or cyst fluid leakage secondary to dermoid cyst wall rupture. Only mild CA19-9 elevation was seen in our patient, implying intact cyst walls without rupture or intra-abdominal necrosis.

Differential diagnoses for perirenal mixed cysts are broad, including teratoma, liposarcoma and schwannoma, alongside lesions arising from the urinary tract 8, 9, 10, 11. Color Doppler ultrasound enables cost-effective screening of lesion location and dimensions. CT delineates tumor margins, identifies tiny occult lesions and facilitates preliminary benign–malignant discrimination. As reported by Chawda et al.,12 progressive enlargement of dermoid cysts may induce adjacent bony remodeling or scalloping, which can be evaluated on CT. Furthermore, MRI helps define the potential origin, vascularity and invasive characteristics of the mass.13

Definitive diagnosis cannot be established based solely on imaging; histopathology remains the diagnostic gold standard. Histologically, dermoid cysts are characterized by a squamous epithelial lining and may contain mature cutaneous adnexal structures such as hair follicles, sebaceous glands and sweat glands.14 In the present case, the dermoid component was identified by the presence of stratified squamous epithelium with keratinized material on histopathological examination. Bronchogenic cysts feature an inner lining of pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium, with stromal smooth muscle, mucous glands and sporadic cartilaginous tissue.15 Unlike teratomas that derive from all three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm),16 our specimen predominantly consisted of a typical bronchogenic cyst with only focal dermoid elements. The bronchogenic and dermoid components were identified as distinct epithelial populations in separate histological fields, without evidence of extensive heterologous tissues typically seen in mature teratoma.

Surgical excision is recommended for asymptomatic large benign perirenal cysts.17 Resection enables definitive histologic diagnosis and prevents complications including progressive mass enlargement with adjacent organ compression, secondary infection, or malignant transformation.18,19 This report expands available clinical data on this uncommon ectopic composite cyst, improving urologists’ awareness and serving as a reference for future differential diagnosis. When assessing fat-containing perirenal cystic lesions, rare composite cysts should be included alongside teratoma in the differential workup. Histopathology is confirmatory, and early surgical intervention yields favorable prognosis.

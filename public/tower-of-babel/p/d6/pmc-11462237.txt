Protocol for developing an explosion-propeller hybrid driving underwater robot for AI-based concrete overhaul in real marine environments
Authors: Chenjie Zhang, Zhaochang Chen, Pengcheng Jiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryWe recently developed an explosion-propeller hybrid driving underwater robot combined with an AI-based concrete damage detection technique for concrete overhaul in real marine environments. Here, we describe steps for establishing a detection dataset, optimizing and testing the algorithm, and preparing the explosive module. We detail procedures for setting up the robot body, assembling, and preparing other tools. Finally, we outline steps for testing in the designated sea area, collecting data, and processing and analysis.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhang et al.1

Before you begin

Background

Concrete faces various challenges in the marine environment, which can lead to corrosion and damage of concrete structures.2 Therefore, regular overhaul and maintenance are essential to ensure the safety, reliability, and long-term sustainability of offshore structures. In recent years, researchers have applied AI techniques to concrete overhaul,3,4,5 but no one has yet publicized the standard steps for algorithm establishment and refinement. Meanwhile, in terms of hardware, as the carrier of visual input, the preparation of high-performance underwater robots6,7,8,9,10,11,12 is also a key work related to the efficiency of machine maintenance.

We recently developed an explosion-propeller hybrid driving underwater robot, combined with AI-based concrete damage target detection method for concrete overhaul in real marine environments.1 The work has achieved encouraging results in both the software and hardware parts, and finally succeeded in the testing of the actual sea area, truly realizing the integration of hardware and software, and realizing the practical assistance of scientific research to engineering.

The protocol discussed here specifically details the process of building up the concrete damage target detection model, preparing the robot and site trial in real marine environments.

2.Select related images from the Internet to expand the dataset.a.Find images of concrete with different types of damages.Note: Due to the limitations of conditions, if you do not have a previous research foundation, it is very difficult to achieve a model size of more than 1000 images per type of damage. For example, if you are dealing with concrete damage identification problems, datasets such as Crack: Bridge,13 CSSC:2017,14 SDNET:201815 and CODEBRIM:201916 would be helpful.b.There are a relatively large number of images of common damage types such as cracks on the Internet. Different lighting conditions may affect the training effect of the model, so images with similar lighting conditions should be selected.iii.Data relevance: Make sure the images selected from the public data set are relevant to your concrete damage task. The content and context of the data should match your target detection task to avoid introducing irrelevant or inapplicable data (for example, rail damage images are very similar to concrete damage, but please avoid combining the two images).Note: Please try to ensure that the number of different types of damage pictures is similar (if not, model bias will arise, where the model performs well on more frequent damage types but poorly on less frequent ones, diminishing overall detection accuracy), and increase the proportion of pictures containing multiple types of damage and multiple damage points, both of which are beneficial for subsequent model training.

For i from 1 to num_splits:

Shuffle(data)

# Calculate the number of samples for each set

total_samples = length(data)

training_size = floor(total_samples ∗ 0.8)

validation_size = floor(total_samples ∗ 0.1)

test_size = total_samples - training_size - validation_size

# Split the dataset

training_set = data[0:training_size]

validation_set = data[training_size:training_size + validation_size]

test_set = data[training_size + validation_size:]

# Store the splits

Add (training_set, validation_set, test_set) to split_datasets list

Return split_datasets

Limitations

The key issue in this paper is concrete overhaul in real marine environments. In order to realize automatic overhaul, we proposed a method of using underwater robots to shoot images in the target area, and then automatically identify the damaged area through AI algorithm. This method has certain universality, and the expected effect has been preliminarily achieved from the engineering point of view, but there are still many limitations.

In terms of hardware, cable work limits the flexibility of the robot, which can cause trouble with cable entanglement. In addition, the explosion drive module still relies on a hard floor for launch, which limits the robot’s escape potential when it is confined by water weeds. What’s more, the overall mass of the robot is relatively light, resulting in waves and other loads having a greater impact on the stability of the robot.

In terms of software, first of all, high-quality data sets are still missing, and we call for deep global collaboration and sharing in this area. Secondly, the damage situation of concrete is complicated, and our current classification standard is not excellent. If experts in the field of concrete can discuss together and finally determine the most reasonable classification method and labeling standard, it will help us get a more convincing result. Finally, the object detection algorithm is still unable to analyze the damaged area at pixel level, and the introduction of semantic segmentation algorithm will become a new growth point in this field.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

AI object detection algorithms cannot be deployed to the edge (related to Step 6 in preparation).

Potential solution

This problem can be solved from two perspectives, one is to improve the level of computing power at the edge under the premise of hardware and economic conditions, and the other is to reduce the amount of computation of the AI model. The algorithms to reduce the computation amount of YOLO model mainly include model compression and pruning, model quantization, knowledge distillation, reduce input image resolution, reduce detection layers, optimize convolution operations, etc.

Problem 2

Target detection model cannot achieve satisfactory results of concrete damage identification (related to Step 10 in preparation).

Potential solution

First make sure that your model is correct and not out of fashion, you can test it on other publicly available datasets, and if the model works well on publicly available well-known datasets, it is almost certain that there is a problem with your dataset. In general, when there are a large number of identification errors, please first check whether there is a problem with the annotation file, whether the name of the annotation file corresponds to the picture one by one, whether the category of damage in the annotation file corresponds to the model setting, in addition, please check whether the data set pictures you choose are representative and extensive.

Problem 3

Robot explosion module malfunction (related to Step 2 in methods).

Potential solution

First, check whether there is an obvious break or poor connection of the connection line, then empty the gas in the explosion chamber, turn on the explosion switch, listen closely to whether there is a continuous spark ignition sound in the gas room, and finally replace the new mixed gas to try again.

Problem 4

Ejection actuator seal failure (related to Step 2d in methods).

Potential solution

Based on the results of air tightness test, first of all identify the location of the bubble forming, then carry out the corresponding treatment according to different situations. If the surface of the soft membrane produces bubbles, it needs to reprepare the soft membrane. If bubbles are generated at the edge of the steel chamber, it is necessary to wrap a sealing film tape on the threaded surface of the top steel chamber. If bubbles are generated around the air valve or spark generator, sealant needs to be applied.

Problem 5

Robots can’t take satisfactory underwater images (related to Step 5 in methods).

Potential solution

First, confirm the main problems of the current image, the possible problems include the picture is too dim, the picture is too fuzzy and so on. If you find that the picture is too dim, you need to consider increasing the power of the fill light or reducing the depth of the operation, or choose to test when the natural light is more abundant. If you find that the picture is too fuzzy, please first confirm the water quality of the target water area, in addition, consider whether the wind and waves during the operation are too large, which will affect the balance of the robot, then violent shaking affecting the stability of the shooting.

Problem 6

The cable disconnects when the robot retrieves (related to Step 14 in methods).

Potential solution

First, maintain an appropriate distance between the inspection ship and the robot, so that the robot cannot stay away from the hull, but also prevent the robot from entering the bottom of the inspection ship with the wave flow. Secondly, fishing nets and other fishing equipment are used to salvage the robot. Finally, if the above method fails, or the corresponding tools are lacking, divers are dispatched to manually recover it under the premise of ensuring safety.

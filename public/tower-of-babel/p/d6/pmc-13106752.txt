BDSet: A First-ever BDS Satellite-based PPP Augmentation Dataset Since 2023
Authors: Chenhao Ouyang, Junbo Shi, Cheng Hou, Wenjie Peng, Jiming Guo, Yibin Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe BDS precise point positioning (PPP) service broadcasts state space representation (SSR) corrections via PPP-B2b augmentation signals. To maximize research potentials of the service, this study develops a first-ever BDS PPP-B2b augmentation dataset since January 2023, named BDSet. This dataset originates from PPP-B2b augmentation signals collected in Wuhan, China, and covers the processing flow of ‘Raw data → Standardized data → Decoded data → Final products’, i.e. raw data from receiving chip, standardized PPP-B2b augmentation messages, decoded PPP-B2b SSR corrections, and recovered PPP-B2b precise products which can be directly utilized in positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) applications. The developed BDSet has been continuously updating since January 1, 2023. The dataset in 2023, 2024 and 2025 occupies a storage memory of 142.7 GB, containing 255,405,156 PPP-B2b augmentation messages. Message amounts for BDS C59, C60, C61 and C62 satellites are 87,080,707, 80,148,878, 13,107,885 and 75,067,686, respectively. The developed BDSet provides a substantial data base for applications in GNSS PNT, geoscience, meteorology and so on. Meanwhile, the three-year dataset can better facilitate artificial intelligence (AI)-empowered BDS big data mining in the future.Subject terms: Atmospheric science, Solid Earth sciences

Background & Summary

Satellite-based precise point positioning (PPP) services enable high-accuracy positioning for massive users by broadcasting state space representation (SSR) corrections via satellite communication links. Due to the property of satellite communication links, PPP services can be applied in regions with limited or even no terrestrial internet coverage, such as at sea and in less-populated areas. Existing satellite-based PPP services can be categorized into commercial and non-commercial types. Commercial services require user subscription fees, such as Trimble’s CenterPoint RTX, Hemisphere’s Atlas, NovAtel’s TerraStar, VERIPOS’s APEX, FUGRO’s MarineStar, Hi-Target’s Hi-RTP, Qianxun SI’s XStar and so on. In contrast, non-commercial services are available for free, such as Centimeter-Level Augmentation Service (CLAS) of QZSS1, High Accuracy Service (HAS) of Galileo2, and PPP service of BDS3.

On December 27, 2019, the first version of BDS PPP-B2b signal interface control document (ICD) was released, marking official launch of the BDS PPP service4. This service is designed as a unique component along with regular positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) services. Specifically, the BDS PPP service generates BDS/GPS satellite orbit, clock offset, and differential code bias (DCB) corrections at intervals of 48/6/48 s, respectively, based on 12 continuous tracking stations located within China5. These generated SSR corrections are encoded into PPP-B2b augmentation messages, which are then uploaded to BDS geostationary earth orbit (GEO) satellites. GEO satellites subsequently modulate augmentation messages into PPP-B2b augmentation signals which are broadcast to the earth. Once these augmentation signals were received, users can obtain improved performance over the traditional single point positioning using a standalone receiver6.

Currently, several types of receiving chips are reported to support the reception of PPP-B2b augmentation signals, such as ComNav K803, K823, and M300 Pro used by Ouyang et al.7, Xu et al.8, and Yang et al.9, the FRII-Plus used by Xu et al.10, Xu et al.11, and Tao et al.12, and the Septentrio Mosaic X5 used by Zhou et al.13. Additionally, the Redmi K60 Ultra smartphone used by Wang et al.14 has also achieved the PPP-B2b signal reception. Once the receiving chip tracks PPP-B2b augmentation signals, it decodes the message to obtain SSR corrections, which are then combined with the broadcast ephemeris to recover precise orbits, clock offsets and DCBs15. The detailed recovery procedure is provided in the official BDS PPP-B2b ICD on pages 33 and 344.

Based on the recovered PPP-B2b precise products, numerous studies have been conducted, focusing on product accuracy evaluation, positioning algorithm development, as well as zenith tropospheric delay (ZTD) and precipitable water vapor (PWV) estimation. Regarding the product accuracy evaluation, existing studies have used various post-processed precise products as references to evaluate PPP-B2b precise products. Taking the final products from Wuhan University as references, Tao et al.16 evaluated one-week PPP-B2b precise products in August 2020, obtaining orbit root mean square errors (RMSEs) of 0.065/0.240/0.210 m and 0.062/0.345/0.213 m in radial (R)/along-track (A)/cross-track (C) directions for BDS and GPS satellites, respectively. The standard deviations (STDs) of clock offsets were 0.11 ns and 0.13 ns, respectively. Using final products from the International GNSS Monitoring and Assessment System (iGMAS) as references, Xu et al.17 evaluated one-month PPP-B2b precise product during October 2020, yielding orbit RMSEs of 0.068/0.334/0.366 m and 0.156/0.398/0.518 m in R/A/C directions for BDS medium earth orbit (MEO) and inclined geosynchronous orbit (IGSO) satellites, respectively, and a clock offset STD of 0.2 ns. Referencing rapid products from the German Research Centre for Geosciences (GFZ), Ren et al.18 evaluated one-week PPP-B2b precise products in August 2020, reporting R/A/C orbit RMSEs of 0.072/0.248/0.259 m and 0.073/0.310/0.219 m, and clock offset STDs of 0.05–0.18 ns and 0.05–0.15 ns for BDS and GPS satellites, respectively. Meanwhile, differences between the PPP-B2b DCB and International GNSS Service (IGS) DCB products were reported to be less than 2 ns. Based on one-year PPP-B2b precise products from May 1, 2021 to May 1, 2022, Sun et al.19 assessed the long-term performance of BDS PPP service, obtaining R/A/C orbit RMSEs of 0.078/0.325/0.356 m and 0.086/0.459/0.362 m for BDS and GPS, respectively, and clock offset STDs of 0.16 ns and 0.20 ns. It should be noted that all previous studies have proven that the improvement of PPP-B2b precise products over broadcast ephemeris.

In terms of general positioning algorithms using PPP-B2b precise products, existing researches mainly focus on optimizing functional and stochastic models for open sky and obstructed environments. In open sky environments, Xu et al.10 proposed an improved functional model that treated signal in space range errors as unknown parameters. This model reduced the impact of PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset/DCB errors on coordinate parameter estimation, resulting in an average positioning accuracy improvement of 21.1%/25.4% in horizontal/vertical directions for vehicular and maritime kinematic positioning. Dai et al.20 introduced an enhanced stochastic model based on multipath errors, which shortened the PPP convergence time by 10%/14%/9% in East (E)/North (N)/Up (U) directions, achieving E/N/U accuracies of 9.9/6.5/14.0 cm based on PPP-B2b precise products. Tao et al.12 realized integer ambiguity fixing by estimating phase observable-specific biases (OSB) based on PPP-B2b precise products, reducing PPP convergence time from 26.1/14.1 minutes to 3.4/4.3 minutes in horizontal/vertical directions, and improving positioning accuracy by 68.2%. To maintain positioning continuity during correction interruptions, Shi et al.21 trained a neural network for PPP-B2b clock offset prediction. Using predicted versus real clock offsets results in less than 2.6/4.9 cm positioning accuracy difference in horizontal/vertical directions after convergence. In obstructed environments, existing studies mainly focused on optimizing functional models formed by the combination of GNSS and other sensors. Xu et al.22 developed a GNSS/inertial navigation system (INS) integrated system based on PPP-B2b precise products. In vehicular experiments under urban environments, accuracies of GNSS-only positioning were 1.14/2.00/2.44 m in E/N/U directions, which were improved by 64.1%/77.8%/73.8% when GNSS/INS integration was used. Shang et al.23 developed a GNSS/INS/camera integrated system based on PPP-B2b precise products, achieving lane-level positioning with smartphones. In urban environments, positioning accuracy was improved from 1.48 m to 0.99 m. Guo et al.24 proposed a GNSS/INS/light detection and ranging (LiDAR) integrated system based on PPP-B2b precise products, improving positioning accuracy from meter- to sub-meter-level in areas surrounded by buildings and trees.

Previous studies have demonstrated the applicability of PPP in meteorology25,26. Using PPP-B2b precise products, several researches have evaluated the accuracy of PPP-derived ZTD and PWV. For example, Wang et al.27 derived ZTD for 44 IGS stations from September 7 to 13, 2022, based on PPP-B2b precise products. The results showed ZTD RMSEs ranging from 10–20 mm for stations in China and 15–30 mm for stations in Europe. Zhou et al.13 derived ZTD based on PPP-B2b precise products for 15 IGS MEGX stations between June 30 and August 17, 2023. Compared with ZTD generated from WUM post-processed precise products, IGS final ZTD products, radiosonde data, and European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts Reanalysis v5 (ERA5) data, the RMSEs were 19.2 mm, 21.5 mm, 25.7 mm, and 28.2 mm, respectively. Cao et al.28 assessed the PWV derived from PPP-B2b precise products at a station in Beijing, China, reporting a RMSE of 1.8 mm during January 2023. Xu et al.11 generated PWV for 17 coastal stations in China between June 6 and 10, 2022, using PPP-B2b precise products, reporting mean absolute errors of 2.10 mm, 2.60 mm, and 2.48 mm for stations in the North, South and East China Sea regions, respectively.

Although the BDS PPP service has been explored from the aforementioned aspects, in-depth studies are still hindered by the following issues. First, the format of PPP-B2b augmentation data are not unified for various receiving chips. Second, processed PPP-B2b precise products are directly used, without investigating error patterns of underlying raw augmentation data. Third, the longest duration of PPP-B2b augmentation data in previous studies was one year, which cannot timely reflect the spatiotemporal variability of BDS PPP service during different operational phases. Fourth, algorithmic researches primarily focus on limited scenarios, lacking validation of generalization ability across diverse scenarios such as deformation monitoring29, marine vessel positioning30 and so on.

To address these issues, this study designs and implements a first-ever BDS PPP-B2b augmentation dataset, named BDSet, which covers the full data processing flow of ‘Raw data → Standardized data → Decoded data → Final products’, i.e. raw messages output by receiving chips, standardized PPP-B2b augmentation messages following the BDS PPP-B2b signal ICD, decoded PPP-B2b SSR corrections, and recovered PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset/DCB products based on SSR corrections and broadcast ephemerides. In the following section, the developed platform for PPP-B2b augmentation data acquisition is first introduced. Next, the dataset design is explained, detailing the data processing workflow and data classification scheme. Then, a three-year dataset from Jan. 2023 to Dec. 2025 is developed with implementation details including naming conventions, storage paths, and formats of various files. Finally, the developed dataset is validated from several aspects, such as data size, message amount, spatiotemporal patterns of PPP-B2b SSR corrections, and accuracies of recovered PPP-B2b precise products.

Methods

This section first introduces the developed platform for PPP-B2b augmentation data acquisition. Then, procedures used to create the dataset are explained.

Dataset acquisition

Figure 1 illustrates the environment and platform for PPP-B2b augmentation data collection. The collection site locates on the rooftop of a building in the city of Wuhan, China, providing an open sky environment. BDS GEO satellites broadcasting the PPP-B2b augmentation signals are C59, C60, C61, and C62, positioned above the equator at longitudes of 140°, 80°, 110.5°, and 160°E, respectively (https://www.bdsupport.cn/status/constellation). The data acquisition platform consists of both hardware and software components. The hardware includes a Harxon GPS1000 antenna, a ComNav K803 receiving chip with firmware version 620F1.23924.1, and a data processing server. The antenna is connected to the receiving chip via cables, and the receiving chip communicates with the server through an RS232 serial port. The software deployed on the server collects PPP-B2b augmentation messages and broadcast ephemerides.

Dataset design

Figure 2 illustrates the classification of PPP-B2b augmentation dataset. Generating PPP-B2b precise products from raw data requires four stages, each correlating to a specific type of PPP-B2b augmentation dataset. Therefore, this study classifies the PPP-B2b augmentation dataset into four levels: Levels 0–3 correspond to raw messages from the receiving chip, standardized PPP-B2b augmentation messages following the ICD, decoded PPP-B2b SSR corrections, and recovered PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset/DCB products, respectively.

Classification of PPP-B2b augmentation dataset.

Level 0: Raw messages from receiving chip

The receiving chip outputs binary messages containing both the PPP-B2b augmentation messages and BDS/GPS broadcast ephemerides in a chip-specific format.

Level 1: Standardized PPP-B2b augmentation messages

Augmentation messages in Level 0 are not organized in the ICD-defined format. To standardize these messages, PPP-B2b augmentation messages are first extracted from raw messages. Then, they are re-encoded according to the format defined in the BDS PPP-B2b signal ICD.

To better understand the re-encoding process, this study takes ComNav K803 receiving chip-defined format as the example and compare PPP-B2b augmentation message structures of Level 0 and Level 1 in Fig. 3. Both messages contain identical 984 bits, corresponding to bits 257–1,240 in Level 0 and bits 17–1,000 in Level 1. The re-encoding process involves removing the first 256 bits and the last 32 bits of PPP-B2b augmentation message in Level 0, and adding a 16-bit preamble (0xeb90) to the beginning. The standardized PPP-B2b augmentation messages in Level 1 contain five types: Type 1 (satellite mask), Type 2 (orbit corrections), Type 3 (DCB corrections), Type 4 (clock offset corrections) and Type 63 (null message).

Structure of PPP-B2b augmentation messages in Level 0 and Level 1. The green parts of the two messages are identical.

Level 2: Decoded PPP-B2b SSR corrections

Type 1/2/3/4 messages in Level 1 are decoded to obtain PPP-B2b SSR corrections in American Standard Code for Information Interchange (ASCII). Types 2 and 3 include satellite Pseudo-Random Noise (PRN) codes, enabling direct association of satellites with their orbit/DCB corrections. While Type 4 does not contain satellite PRN codes, satellite mask in Type 1 is required to associate satellites with their clock offset corrections.

Level 3: Recovered PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset/DCB products

By combining broadcast ephemerides from Level 0 and PPP-B2b SSR corrections from Level 2, and performing multiple Issue of Data (IOD) matchings15, PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset/DCB products are recovered and can be directly used for subsequent PNT applications.

The IOD matching serves to associate SSR corrections with broadcast ephemerides31. PPP-B2b SSR corrections contain four types of IOD: IOD SSR, IOD PRN mask (IODP), IOD Navigation (IODN), and IOD Correction (IOD Corr). Figure 4 illustrates relationships among these four IODs: IOD SSR associates Type 1 with Type 2/3/4, IODP associates Type 1 with Type 4, IODN associates Type 2 with broadcast ephemerides, and IOD Corr associates Type 2 with Type 4. With all IODs matched, the broadcast orbit/clock offset are first calculated, followed by the PPP-B2b orbit/clock offset calculation32.

Relationships among various IODs in PPP-B2b augmentation messages.

The implemented dataset is currently available at 10.57760/sciencedb.cast.0000933. Technical Validation

Based on the dataset implementation described in the previous section, this study has developed the BDS PPP-B2b augmentation dataset, i.e. the BDSet. The dataset started since January 1, 2023, and will be updated on a monthly basis. For the year of 2023, 2024 and 2025, the dataset occupies a storage memory of 142.7 GB, containing 255,405,156 PPP-B2b augmentation messages. Message amounts for BDS C59, C60, C61 and C62 satellites are 87,080,707, 80,148,878, 13,107,885 and 75,067,686, respectively. Taking data in 2023 as an example, this section provides an initial evaluation of the dataset.

File size

Evaluation of Level 2

This sub-section evaluates the PPP-B2b SSR corrections in the sub-folder Level_2 from both spatial and temporal perspectives. From the spatial perspective, the available satellites within PPP-B2b SSR correction are extracted, and the ground tracks of extracted satellites are calculated to monitor the coverage of BDS PPP service. Figure 12 presents the service coverage during January 1-7, 2023. It can be observed that the service coverage is within the latitude from 45.17° S to 57.33° N and longitude from 16.06° W to 98.64° W. This is due to the fact that the BDS PPP service utilizes regional tracking stations to generate PPP-B2b SSR corrections37. The identical service coverage is observed from February to December, 2023 in the dataset, and also aligns with the spatial coverage in Ouyang et al.7 based on the PPP-B2b SSR corrections during June 17–21, 2022. This consistency indicates that the BDS PPP service coverage does not change from June 2022 to December 2023.

Code availability

The code for generating BDSet is available at the repository: 10.57760/sciencedb.cast.0000933. The code is developed in C++ and compiled with Microsoft Visual Studio Community 2019 under the Windows 10 Professional operating system (Version 22H2). Following successful compilation, the dataset is generated through a two-step procedure: first, the daily raw data from receiving chip and mixed broadcast ephemerides are stored in two separate directories as input preparation. Then, the compiled program is executed in command interpreter, with five input arguments: the directory of the raw data, the start and end date of data collection in YYYYDOY format, the directory of the mixed broadcast ephemeris, and the output directory for the dataset.

The outcome of stenting and its duration after pyeloplasty in children with ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO): a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Made Kresna Yudhistira Wiratma, Tanaya Ghinorawa, Prahara Yuri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe complication related to the use of stent and its duration are still considered in the treatment option of UPJO despite the high success rates of several pyeloplasty techniques.MethodsA systematic search was performed using PubMed/MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, and Cochrane databases, selecting cohort and controlled trial studies until April 2023. The following medical subject headings (MeSH) were included in the search: stent, ureteropelvic junction obstruction, pyeloplasty and children. Complication and success rate related to the stent placement after pyeloplasty were recorded.ResultsEleven studies including 1072 patients were included. Eight studies compared stented versus non-stented pyeloplasty. Three studies compared 4-week stenting vs ≤ 2-week stenting. The total complication risk is decreased in stented group compared to non-stented group (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.59–1.13). According to subgroup analysis, urinary leakage (OR 0.21 95% CI 0.10–0.47) and obstruction (OR 0.69 95% CI 0.32–1.47) are decreased in stented group. The total risk of complication is increased in 4-week stenting period (OR 5.11, 95% CI 2.43- 10.74) compared to ≤ 2-week stenting period, with the OR of each subgroup analysis is 4.64, 95% CI 1.8–11.97; 4.36, 95% CI 0.5–38.3 and 6.78, 95% CI 1.65–27.92 for fever, stent migration, and irritative symptom, respectively. There were no differences of success rate among the groups (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.04–5.62).ConclusionThe use of stent can reduce the risk of urinary leakage after pyeloplasty in children. Stenting duration ≤ 2 weeks carries a smaller risk of complications with the same post operative success rate as stents with a longer duration.Keywords: Stent, Ureteropelvic Junction Obstruction, Pyeloplasty, Complications, Pediatric Patients

Introductions

Hydronephrosis, a prevalent urological issue in children, is frequently attributed to ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO), which stands as its most common cause. Pyeloplasty remains the primary approach for addressing UPJO conditions, with the Anderson-Hynes technique being the predominant method. Post-pyeloplasty, adjunctive measures like stent placement are often employed to enhance urinary drainage patency. However, this practice carries an elevated risks such as infection, and urinary symptoms [1–4].. This study aimed to compare outcomes associated with post-pyeloplasty stenting in children and determine the optimal duration for maintaining the stents.

Methods

Search strategy

According to PRISMA criteria, this study was carried out. Research evidence on stent placement after pyeloplasty on pediatric patient was included. These studies consisted of the hospital-based randomized control trial and cohort studies. Only full-text articles published in English were considered; reviews, case reports, duplicate publications, studies with inadequate control group data, and studies with poor methodology were removed.

A systematic search was performed using PubMed/MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, and Cochrane databases, selecting cohort and controlled trial studies until April 2023. The following medical subject headings (MeSH) were included in the search: stent, ureteropelvic junction obstruction, pyeloplasty and children. Complication and success rate related to the stent placement after pyeloplasty were recorded.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The articles were all reviewed separately by two authors using the pre-established standards for exclusion and inclusion. First, duplicates were checked in the titles and abstracts of recognized records. Second, for the systematic review and meta-analysis, the same studies were used to choose the full-text articles. All disagreements were settled through debate until the authors agreed.

Inclusion criteria (the guidelines to select the eligible studies which could be included in the process of the analysis) and exclusion criteria were chosen as follows.

The inclusion criteria in this study were: (1) the participants were all UPJO patients who had been treated for pyeloplasty; (2) the study design was a randomized controlled trial or a cohort study (prospective or retrospective); (3) studies that analyzed pediatric patients; (4) Primary repair pyeloplasty; (4) All pyeloplasty techniques; (5) If multiple studies from the same institution or author were found, only the latest literature was included for analysis to avoid patient overlap.

The exclusion criteria in this study were: (1) articles that only focused on patient preparation, preoperative imaging studies, technical instructions, specific instruments, surgeon learning curve, or cost analysis were excluded; (2) second procedure; (3) review articles, reviews, case reports, letters to editors, and abstracts of scientific meetings in languages other than English.

Data synthesis and analysis

Two writers independently retrieved study characteristics. The first author's name, the year of publication, country, study design, number of participants, mean age, stenting time, mean follow up, complications, postoperative renal function, and other information were all included. Funnel plots were use to assess the risk of publication bias. The quality of the studies was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale. Selection, comparability, and result assessment biases were among the assessment domains. Sensitivity analysis was used to determine the impact of bias on the effect estimates after the risk of bias assessment. The current review's Methods, Results, and Discussion portions were reported using the PRISMA statement. Then, categorized extracts from each included article were used.

Results

Overview of literature searching

There were 1349 studies identified through database searching from Pubmed, Sciencedirect, and Cochrane. After removing duplicates, we screened 1288 titles and abstracts, leaving 11 studies to be selected and then analyzed for data synthesis (Fig. 1). The search yielded 11 studies involving 1072 participants. Eight studies compared outcome between stented versus non-stented pyeloplasty (Table 1). Three studies compared complication and success rate between 4-week stenting vs ≤ 2-week stenting (Tables 2 and 3).

Complication risk

The risks of complications compared in this study were fever [4–7], infection [3–5, 7–9], urinary leakage [3–6, 8–10], obstruction [3–6, 8, 9], need for secondary procedure [3, 4, 6]. Overall, total complications occurred less frequently in patients in the stented group (OR 0.81 95% CI 0.59–1.13 I2 34%). In the subgroup analysis, the odds ratio was assessed based on each aspect (Fig. 2), fever (OR 1.16 95% CI 0.59–2.30), infection (OR 2.00 CI 0.99–4.05), urinary leakage (OR 0.21 95% CI 0.10–0.47), obstruction (OR 0.69 95% CI 0.32 −1.47), need for secondary procedure (OR 1.72 95% CI 0.54–5.47). According to subgroup analysis, urinary leakage was significantly lower in stented group. Obstruction was also less frequent in the stented group, but the difference was not significant.

Discussion

During the last decade the use of stents has become very widespread. The use of catheterization in the ureter was first used in Egyptian era using a tube inserted into the ureter through the open bladder surgery by Gustav Simon in 1900. The use of ureteral stents, which more or less resemble the stents that are often used today, was first introduced by Joaquin Albarano in early 1900s. The main function of stent installation is to maintain the patency of urinary drainage in conditions where the upper urinary tract is blocked either due to compression from inside or outside the urinary tract [12].

The use of stents is not without complications. Some side effects can arise from the use of the stent itself, especially if it is used or maintained for a long time. Bacterial colonization and encrustation are two things that are very common and cause infection and obstruction caused by the presence of the stent. Stents in the ureters can cause inflammation and irritation in the bladder, resulting in hematuria, pain in the bladder, a sensation of urgency and other complaints related to urination in patients. Reflux to the kidneys also causes pain in the kidneys. Ureteral stents also have an effect on ureteral peristalsis and pressure on the kidneys which causes an uncomfortable sensation when urinates [7, 12].

The necessity for upper urinary tract (UUT) drainage after UPJO repair is still a matter of debate, especially in children whom the diameter of the ureter is relatively small. The main objective of draining the UUT after pyeloplasty is to prevent urine leakage through the anastomosis, which may cause stricture formation [7, 10]. Though Anderson-Hynes pyeloplasty has become established as the gold standard for management of PUJO, the issues of stenting the pelvi-ureteric anastomosis are still unresolved. What is interesting is that from the beginning the introduction of this technique was without stents because it was considered contrary to the principles of surgery where infection would cause fibrosis in the anastomosis which would result in strictures in the future. However, experts still think that having a stent is an important step because it guarantees good urine drainage and makes wounds heal quickly and hydronephrosis improves [1, 2, 5, 9].

There are several types of stents used for post-pyeloplasty. The options are percutaneous nephrostomy, DJ stent or pyeloureteral stent [1, 8]. The principle is that the size of the stent must match the size of the ureter without excessive pressure on the ureter which tends to be small in children. Use of appropriate stents will prevent local ischemia or pressure necrosis. Nephrostomy can also be used to ensure the anatomy is dry. Many studies have used DJ stents as a post-pyeloplasty stenting option [10].

The first question that must be answered is whether a stent is needed after pyeloplasty surgery. In our study total complications occurred less frequently in patients in the stented group (OR 0.81 95% CI 0.59–1.13 P = 0.21). What is important to highlights is the significantly lower difference in terms of urine leakage in patients with stents. This supports the role of post-pyeloplasty stents. In the subgroup analysis, there were also no significant differences in terms of fever, infection, obstruction and need for secondary procedures.

The next question that must be addressed is how long the stent is maintained. Based on this study, it was found that there was a significant increase in complications in patients with stents of 4 weeks duration, such as infections and irritative symptoms. The infection criteria used in the included studies used different standards. For example, Abdelwahab et al. used urine culture and urinalysis 1 month after surgery to diagnose infection due to non-specific complaints in children [2].

The dilemma between the duration of the stent that must be maintained and the complications that arise must be considered when making the decision to use a stent after pyeloplasty. An aspect that is also taken into consideration is the success of a short stent duration compared to a longer stent duration. The success of the pyeloplasty procedure is important. There are several indicators to assess success, including: postoperative renogram, anteroposterior diameter and parenchymal thickness on ultrasound [6, 11]. In this study, it was also found that there was no difference in terms of postoperative success between the 4-week vs. ≤ 2-week stent use groups.

Variations in the type of stent used, operator bias, and the majority of data being retrospective studies indicate the need for deeper studies that focus on these issues to obtain a more accurate picture regarding stent use and also the optimal duration to obtain maximum results after pyeloplasty.

Conclusions

In summary, our analysis of currently available clinical evidence indicated that the use of stent can reduce the risk of urinary leakage after pyeloplasty in children. Stenting duration ≤ 2 weeks carries a smaller risk of complications with the same post operative success rate as stents with a longer duration.

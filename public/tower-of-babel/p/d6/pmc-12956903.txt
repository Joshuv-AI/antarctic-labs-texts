Harnessing Cytomegalovirus DNAemia and CMV-Specific Cell-Mediated Immunity in Pediatric Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant Recipients
Authors: Sanghoon Lee, Gahee Kim, Jung Hwa Kim, Eun Seok Choi, Sung Han Kang, Hyery Kim, Kyung-Nam Koh, Ho Joon Im, Jina Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecovery via cytomegalovirus (CMV)-specific cell-mediated immunity (CMI) in pediatric hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) recipients is associated with a lower incidence of significant CMV DNAemia and disease. This study aimed to determine whether early post-HSCT CMI monitoring could predict CMV infection outcomes and identify factors that influence CMI recovery.MethodsPediatric patients (≤ 19 years) undergoing allogeneic HSCT at Asan Medical Center Children’s Hospital between August 2018 and February 2020 were prospectively enrolled. CMV-specific CMI was assessed using the enzyme-linked immunospot assay for phosphoprotein 65 (pp65) or immediate-early protein 1 (IE-1) antigens at pre-transplantation, and 1 and 3 months post-transplantation. Plasma CMV polymerase chain reaction monitoring was conducted regularly, and the incidence of CMV DNAemia and CMV disease was evaluated over a two-year follow-up period.ResultsOf the 55 enrolled pediatric recipients, CMV DNAemia occurred in 74.5%, with 21.8% of cases progressing to significant CMV DNAemia. Cumulative CMI recovery rates at 3 months were 61.8% for pp65 and 43.6% for IE-1. Recipients with a cumulative recovery of pp65-specific CMI exhibited a significantly lower incidence of significant CMV DNAemia and CMV disease. In contrast, the cumulative recovery of IE-1-specific CMI was not associated with CMV outcomes. In the multivariate analysis, haploidentical donor status and post-HSCT CMV DNAemia were independently associated with impaired cumulative recovery of both pp65- and IE-1-specific CMI.ConclusionMonitoring pp65-specific CMI recovery within three months post-HSCT is valuable for predicting significant CMV infection in pediatric recipients. Haploidentical HSCT recipients demonstrated impaired CMI recovery, highlighting the need for careful monitoring and tailored prophylactic strategies.

INTRODUCTION

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection is a common complication among pediatric allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) recipients and is associated with non-relapse mortality.123 Thus, CMV serostatus is a critical risk assessment tool, with seropositive recipients at high risk for CMV reactivation.12 Moreover, without prophylaxis, up to 80% of CMV-seropositive recipients may experience CMV infection post-HSCT.1

CMV-specific cell-mediated immunity (CMI) plays a pivotal role in controlling CMV infection by inducing cytokines, such as interferon-gamma (IFN-γ).124 Studies have suggested that CMI recovery correlates with favorable CMV outcomes in HSCT recipients.567 Given the cost and labor associated with CMI monitoring, understanding how CMI assessment can improve the management and outcomes of transplant recipients is crucial.56 Our previous study indicated that full CMI recovery, defined as ≥ 50 spot-forming cells (SFCs)/2.0 × 105 peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for either phosphoprotein 65 (pp65) or immediate-early protein 1 (IE-1), reduced the risk of clinically significant CMV infections following pediatric allogeneic HSCT.7 Notably, most recoveries via CMI occurred within three months post-transplantation.

Pre-emptive therapy (PET) enhanced CMV-specific CMI in high-risk D+R− (donor CMV-seropositive/recipient CMV-seronegative) liver transplant recipients compared to universal antiviral prophylaxis (AP), potentially minimizing the incidence of late-onset CMV disease.8 However, no studies have directly compared CMV-specific immune responses between pediatric allogeneic HSCT recipients who receive AP vs. those who receive PET.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate CMV-specific CMI recovery within the first three months following allogeneic HSCT in pediatric recipients and its association with significant CMV DNAemia, defined as prolonged, recurrent, or late-onset CMV DNAemia, as well as CMV disease. The clinical significance of differences in CMI recovery between significant and non-significant CMV DNAemia groups was also analyzed. Additionally, this study sought to assess the individual utility of pp65 and IE-1 as markers of CMV-specific immune recovery and to evaluate the differences in CMV-specific immune responses according to CMV prophylaxis strategies (AP vs. PET).

METHODS

Study population

Pediatric allogeneic HSCT recipients, aged ≤ 19 years at Asan Medical Center Children’s Hospital between August 1, 2018, and February 28, 2020, were prospectively enrolled. Additionally, pediatric assent forms were obtained for participants aged 7 to 15 years. Cases with D−/R− CMV serostatus, engraftment failure, relapsed disease, or death within three months post-HSCT were excluded.

Demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, underlying diseases leading to HSCT, HSCT protocol, CMV serostatus, and CMV infection or disease status, were recorded from the electronic medical records; a follow-up period of up to two years post-HSCT was applied.

Myeloablative conditioning regimens included 1) cyclophosphamide (60 mg/kg/day for 2 days) combined with busulfan (3.2 mg/kg/day for 4 days); 2) cyclophosphamide (60 mg/kg/day for 2 days) combined with total body irradiation ≥ 12 Gy, typically delivered in fractionated doses; 3) fludarabine (30 mg/m2/day for 5 days), treosulfan (14 g/m2/day for 3 days), and thiotepa (10 mg/kg for 1 day).910 All other regimens, typically comprised lower-intensity agents or reduced dosages, and were categorized as reduced-intensity conditioning. Rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin was included in the conditioning regimens as a serotherapy. Rituximab was administered to haploidentical HSCT recipients on day 1 following stem cell infusion.10 T-cell depletion was performed by negative selection using the CliniMACS® system (Miltenyi BioTec, Bergisch-Gladbach, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.11

Acute graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) was graded within 100 days post-HSCT based on a previously reported grading system.12 For GVHD prophylaxis, haploidentical HSCT recipients received mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), while matched HSCT recipients were treated with a combination of cyclosporin and methotrexate (MTX).

Outcomes

Primarily, this study aimed to evaluate whether the recovery of CMV-specific CMI in pediatric recipients within three months post-HSCT could predict the development of significant CMV infection and/or CMV disease over the two-year post-HSCT period, and to assess the individual utility of pp65 and IE-1 as markers of CMV-specific immune recovery. The secondary outcomes were to identify clinical factors associated with CMV-specific CMI recovery and to analyze differences in CMV-specific immune responses according to CMV prophylaxis strategies (AP vs. PET).

Definition of CMV infection and evaluation strategies

Plasma CMV DNAemia was monitored using real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with the Abbott Real-Time CMV Amplification Reagent kit (Abbott Molecular Inc., Abbott Park, IL, USA). Regardless of the prophylactic strategy (AP or PET), CMV DNAemia was assessed weekly or at every outpatient visit from the time of stem cell infusion to day 180 post-HSCT. In patients with chronic GvHD or a history of CMV reactivation, monitoring was continued weekly or at each outpatient visit until day 365.

CMV DNAemia was defined as a plasma CMV PCR level above the detection limit (≥ 1.5 log IU/mL). Significant CMV DNAemia was defined as the presence of prolonged, recurrent, or late-onset CMV DNAemia. Prolonged CMV DNAemia was defined as a CMV PCR level ≥ 3.0 log IU/mL persisting for ≥ 14 days. Recurrent CMV DNAemia was defined as reactivation occurring ≥ 4 weeks after resolution of the previous episode, and late-onset CMV DNAemia was defined as CMV reactivation occurring after day 100 post-HSCT. CMV disease was diagnosed according to the definition by Ljungman et al.13

CMV-specific enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assay

CMV-specific CMI was evaluated using the Human IFN-γ ELISpot kit (ALP) (MABTECH, Stockholm, Sweden) at three timepoints: pre-conditioning, and 1 and 3 months post-HSCT. PBMCs were stimulated using phytohemagglutinin (GibcoTM, Life Technologies Corporation, Grand Island, NY, USA) as a positive control; pp65 and IE-1 peptide pools (PepMixTM, JPT Peptide Technologies, Berlin, Germany) as CMV antigens; medium alone as a negative control. Spots were enumerated after 12 hours of incubation using an automated microscope. Results were expressed as SFCs per 2.0 × 105 PBMCs (hereafter referred to as SFCs, unless otherwise specified). CMI recovery was arbitrarily defined as ≥ 50 SFCs for both pp65 and IE-1.7 Cumulative recovery was defined as the detection of at least one CMI recovery at either 1 or 3 months post-HSCT.

CMV prophylaxis

CMV infection following HSCT was prevented using either AP or PET, according to the institutional protocol. PET was implemented as the CMV prevention strategy for matched HSCT recipients; meanwhile, universal prophylaxis against herpes simplex virus (HSV) and varicella-zoster virus (VZV) was administered via intravenous or oral acyclovir for at least 30 days based on the HSV or VZV serostatus of the recipient.

For haploidentical HSCT recipients, AP was applied based on the CMV serostatus of the donor and recipient. In CMV R+ cases, ganciclovir (5 mg/kg every 12 hours) was administered from the start of conditioning until day −2, followed by intravenous acyclovir (5 mg/kg every 8 hours) until engraftment. Oral valganciclovir (25 mg/kg once daily) was continued until day 100 post-HSCT or until the CD4+ T cell count exceeded 100 cells/µL, after which oral acyclovir (600 mg/m2 twice daily) was continued until day 365. In CMV D+/R− haploidentical cases, intravenous acyclovir (5 mg/kg every 8 hours) was given until day −1, followed by foscarnet (90 mg/kg once daily) from the time of stem cell infusion until engraftment, and then oral acyclovir until day 365. Meanwhile, acyclovir (5 mg/kg every 8 hours intravenously or 600 mg/m2 orally) was used instead of ganciclovir or foscarnet in CMV D−/R− haploidentical cases.

Antiviral agents were initiated when the plasma CMV level exceeded 3.0 log IU/mL or when the level was above 2.5 log IU/mL in the presence of risk factors for CMV disease (e.g., haploidentical group, high-dose corticosteroid use, etc.).

Statistical analysis

The Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test was employed for inter-group comparisons of continuous variables, while the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test was used for comparing categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with CMI recovery or progression to significant CMV DNAemia or disease. Covariates with a P value < 0.07 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis.

Differences in post-transplant event occurrence between groups were analyzed using time-to-event analysis, and log-rank P values were calculated. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 26 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Asan Medical Center (IRB No. 2018-0889). Written informed consent was obtained from their parents or guardians.

RESULTS

Study population

Of the 74 allogenic HSCT recipients, 19 were excluded for the following reasons: lack of informed consent (n = 10); engraftment failure, relapsed underlying disease, or death within 3 months (n = 6); CMV D−/R− serostatus (n = 2); absence of ELISpot assay results (n = 1). A total of 55 recipients were enrolled, with a median follow-up of 24 months (interquartile range [IQR], 22–24 months) (Table 1).

The median age at HSCT was 11.1 years (range, 3 months to 19.5 years), including nine children under 24 months of age. Malignancies accounted for 45.5% of HSCT indications. Peripheral blood was the source of hematopoietic stem cells, and 50.9% of recipients underwent myeloablative conditioning. Nineteen recipients (34.5%) received allogeneic grafts from haploidentical donors. Regarding CMV serostatus, 70.9% (39/55) were D+/R+, 20.0% (11/55) were D−/R+, and 9.1% (5/55) were D+/R−. All haploidentical HSCT recipients were either D+/R+ (n = 17) or D+/R− (n = 2).

For CMV prophylaxis, 16 recipients (29.1%) received AP, 15 of whom were haploidentical HSCT recipients; among these, 12 (75.0%) developed CMV DNAemia with a median onset of 13 days (IQR, 5–24 days) post-HSCT, and four experienced significant CMV DNAemia (Supplementary Table 1). PET was administered to 39 recipients, with a significantly higher proportion among matched HSCT recipients (97.2%, 35/36) compared to haploidentical recipients (21.1%, 4/19) (P < 0.001). The incidences of CMV DNAemia, significant CMV DNAemia, CMV disease, and all-cause mortality did not differ significantly according to the prophylaxis strategy.

For GVHD prophylaxis, 37 recipients received two immunosuppressive agents (cyclosporine plus MTX), 14 received MMF alone, and four did not receive any immunosuppressive therapy. High-dose steroids were administered to 24 recipients (43.6%). An acute GvHD of grade ≥ 2 occurred in 19 recipients (35.2%), and relapse of underlying disease occurred in eight (14.5%). The two-year all-cause mortality rate was 9.1% (5/55), with 2.9% among matched HSCT recipients and 22.2% among haploidentical HSCT recipients (P = 0.040).

CMV infection post-HSCT

Any level of CMV DNAemia occurred at a median of 13 days post-HSCT (IQR, 5−24 days) in 74.5% of recipients, whereas significant CMV DNAemia occurred at a median of 16 days (IQR, 4−24 days) in 12 recipients (21.8%). Significant CMV DNAemia included prolonged CMV DNAemia (n = 9), recurrent CMV DNAemia (n = 6), and late-onset CMV DNAemia (n = 7). No statistically significant differences were observed in the incidence of any CMV DNAemia, significant CMV DNAemia, or CMV disease between the haploidentical and matched groups (P = 1.000, 0.733, and 0.405, respectively). However, late-onset CMV DNAemia was more frequently observed in the haploidentical group (5.6% [2/36] in the matched group vs. 26.3% [5/19] in the haploidentical group; P = 0.041).

CMV disease was diagnosed in eight instances among six recipients (10.9%), including retinitis (n = 2), pneumonia (n = 2), gastrointestinal disease (n = 1), and a combination of retinitis, pneumonia, and central nervous system disease (n = 1). The median time from HSCT to CMV disease onset was 116 days (IQR, 66−183 days).

In the time-to-event analysis (Fig. 1), recipients with a cumulative CMI recovery of pp65 had a significantly lower incidence of prolonged CMV DNAemia (log-rank P = 0.010) and CMV disease (P = 0.036). In contrast, no significant differences were observed in association with IE-1 recovery (P = 0.942 and 0.888, respectively).

CMV-specific CMI recovery following HSCT

Among the 41 recipients who experienced CMV DNAemia, ELISpot assays were performed after the onset of CMV DNAemia in 38 (92.7%) at 1 month and in all 41 (100%) at 3 months post-HSCT. Compared with recipients without CMV DNAemia, those with CMV DNAemia had higher cumulative CMI recovery rates: 80.5% vs. 7.1% for pp65 (P < 0.001), and 56.1% vs. 7.1% for IE-1 (P = 0.002). This pattern was consistent across both the haploidentical and matched HSCT subgroups. However, among haploidentical HSCT recipients, pp65 and IE-1 SFCs were generally lower than those observed in the matched group, and the impact of CMV DNAemia on CMI SFCs was evident only for pp65 at 3 months post-HSCT (42 SFCs vs. 1 SFC; P < 0.001).

Impact of CMV-specific CMI recovery on significant CMV DNAemia and/or CMV disease

Recipients with cumulative recovery of pp65 experienced significantly fewer episodes of significant CMV DNAemia compared with those without recovery (odds ratio [OR], 0.07; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.01–0.46), including prolonged (OR, 0.08; 95% CI, 0.01–0.49), recurrent (OR, 0.07; 95% CI, 0.01–0.47), and late-onset CMV DNAemia (OR, 0.04; 95% CI, 0.01–0.29) (Table 3). Additionally, these recipients were less likely to develop CMV disease (OR, 0.11; 95% CI, 0.01–0.81). In contrast, cumulative recovery of IE-1 levels was not associated with the development of significant CMV DNAemia (OR, 1.14; 95% CI, 0.29–4.44) or CMV disease (OR, 1.20; 95% CI, 0.18–8.07).

Comparison between non-significant and significant CMV DNAemia groups

DISCUSSION

In this prospective observational study involving pediatric allogenic HSCT recipients, 75% experienced CMV DNAemia following HSCT, with 22% of cases classified as significant CMV DNAemia. By three months post-HSCT, 62% of recipients had recovered CMV-specific CMI for either pp65 or IE-1, with higher recovery rates observed in those who experienced CMV DNAemia. Importantly, recovery of pp65-specific CMI was significantly associated with a lower risk of progression to significant CMV DNAemia or CMV disease.

Previous studies have shown that recovery of CMV-specific CMI typically occurs shortly after the detection of CMV DNAemia or antigenemia.1415161718 Controlled CMV DNAemia may enhance CMI recovery, subsequently reducing the risk of significant CMV complications. In contrast, uncontrolled CMV DNAemia, particularly early post-HSCT or at higher viral loads, has been associated with increased mortality.1920 Thus, achieving “permissive CMV DNAemia”—defined as low-level DNAemia that does not progress to disease and resolves spontaneously without the need for antiviral treatment—could optimize CMI recovery while minimizing adverse outcomes.

CMI recovery was notably hindered in haploidentical HSCT recipients, approximately 80% of whom received AP, and who exhibited higher mortality rates and a higher incidence of late-onset CMV DNAemia. This impaired recovery likely reflects the effects of in vivo and ex vivo T cell depletion strategies, including rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin and ex vivo αβ T cell depletion.21 The potential benefits of PET are thought to arise from allowing controlled CMV DNAemia, thereby promoting the development of CMV-specific immunity. Indeed, matched HSCT recipients, most of whom received PET, were expected to experience CMV DNAemia more frequently than haploidentical HSCT recipients, potentially facilitating the establishment of CMV-specific CMI through permissive CMV DNAemia. However, this study observed a similar overall incidence of any level or significant CMV DNAemia across donor types and anti-CMV strategy. Furthermore, after adjusting for other factors, the type of anti-CMV strategy (AP or PET) itself did not significantly impact CMI recovery.

Along with substantial evidence indicating that HSCT recipients with CMV-specific CMI recovery effectively control CMV DNAemia and CMV disease,567 our study found that recipients with CMI recovery for pp65 showed improved clinical outcomes following HSCT. Although the optimal threshold for defining CMI recovery remains controversial, thresholds of 50 to 100 SFCs per 2.5 × 105 PBMCs have been previously proposed.567 El Haddad et al.6 suggested that recovery of CMI ≥ 100 SFCs/2.5 × 105 cells for pp65 or ≥ 50 SFCs/2.5 × 105 cells for IE-1 was associated with a decrease in the progression of clinically significant CMV DNAemia in adult HSCT recipients. Thus, building upon prior findings at our institution,7 this study adopted a threshold of ≥ 50 SFCs per 2.0 × 105 PBMCs, and further strengthened the definition of cumulative recovery by assessing two time points within the first three months.

We utilized pp65 and IE-1, two well-established CMV antigens, for ELISpot-based immune monitoring. Mechanistically, the pp65 antigen plays a role in assisting CMV DNA in evading the host’s immunity, either by suppressing IFN-γ-inducible protein 16, which aids the host in identifying viral DNA,22 or by weakening the IFN type I signaling pathway through the suppression of IFN regulatory factor 3.23 Conversely, IE-1 contributes to CMV survival by preventing apoptosis of infected cells.24 In this study, pp65-specific immune responses showed more dynamic changes during the first three months post-HSCT and were more strongly associated with the prevention of significant CMV DNAemia and CMV disease than IE-1-specific responses.

The practical significance of monitoring CMI responses, particularly for pp65, lies in its potential to refine CMV management strategies. According to our findings, recipients who demonstrated cumulative recovery of pp65-specific CMI within three months post-HSCT exhibited a significantly lower incidence of significant CMV DNAemia or CMV disease. Although routine CMV DNAemia monitoring may still be necessary for a certain period, early recovery of pp65-specific CMI may permit higher thresholds for initiating antiviral therapy or earlier discontinuation of antiviral treatment, thereby promoting a more judicious use of antiviral agents. Hence, future studies are warranted to determine whether clinical guidelines, such as the frequency of CMV monitoring or the thresholds for antiviral initiation, can be adapted based on CMI recovery status.

This study has several limitations. First, the transplantation process was determined using a pre-established protocol based on the type of HSCT, which inherently linked key factors and potentially biased the evaluation of CMI recovery. Although a stratified analysis was considered, this method was limited by the small sample size. Additionally, no interventions were made based on CMI status. Second, we did not evaluate the exact CMV DNAemia level, meaning the threshold that defines “permissive” DNAemia remains unclear. In the non-significant CMV DNAemia group, cumulative recovery of pp65 was observed in more than 90% of recipients, while none progressed to CMV disease. Considering that the median peak CMV DNAemia level in this group was 2.91 log IU/mL, a tentative suggestion is that a threshold below 3.0 log IU/mL may serve as a provisional criterion for permissive CMV DNAemia. However, further research is required to validate this threshold, particularly in high-risk populations such as haploidentical HSCT recipients. Third, the ELISpot assay was conducted only twice following HSCT, which may have limited the ability to capture the dynamic changes in CMI over time. Additionally, this study did not explore the CMV-specific antigen response beyond pp65 and IE-1, nor did it evaluate the role of neutralizing antibody responses. Although the field of CMV prevention and immunity has predominantly emphasized T cell responses, it is worth noting that neutralizing antibodies may also play a significant role in CMV control. Nonetheless, the strengths of this study lie in bridging an existing gap in the current literature regarding the dynamics and clinical relevance of CMI recovery in pediatric HSCT recipients. Moreover, this study highlights the differences in CMV-specific CMI recovery according to the occurrence of CMV DNAemia, irrespective of the CMV prophylaxis strategy. Furthermore, this study underscores the distinct clinical implications of pp65- vs. IE-1-specific immune recovery.

In conclusion, early recovery of CMV-specific CMI, particularly targeting pp65, was significantly associated with a reduced risk of significant CMV DNAemia and CMV disease. Non-significant CMV DNAemia within a permissive viral load range may enhance CMV-specific CMI without progressing to disease. However, the anti-CMV prophylaxis strategy, whether PET or AP, did not significantly impact CMI recovery in this study, meaning further investigations are warranted, especially for haploidentical HSCT recipients who exhibited hindered CMI recovery and may benefit from tailored approaches. Despite the challenges associated with integrating CMV immune monitoring into routine clinical practice, early post-HSCT monitoring of pp65-specific CMI responses could serve as a valuable surrogate marker for predicting serious CMV infections and related outcomes.

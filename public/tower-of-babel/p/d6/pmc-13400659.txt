Protocol for high-throughput droplet-engineered TIL-retaining tumor organoids in triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Xiaolong Zhou, Yu Zhu, Wanlong Wang, Yongde Cai, Zhiyuan Liu, Yingshi Su, Shaohua Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol for generating high-throughput, droplet-engineered tumor organoids from triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) tissues using the OrgFab® micro-sphere printing system. We describe tumor dissociation, bioink preparation, and automated organoid fabrication. We further detail rho-associated coiled-coil kinase (ROCK) pathway modulation to preserve tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and enhance T cell function, as well as flow cytometry-based immune characterization (Figure 1). This protocol enables scalable production of immune-competent tumor organoids for immunological analysis and drug-screening applications.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhou et al.1

Before you begin

Tumor organoids have emerged as physiologically relevant 3D models that recapitulate key structural and functional features of native tumors.2 In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), a highly aggressive subtype lacking effective targeted therapies,3,4 immunotherapy has become an important treatment strategy5; however, clinical response rates remain limited,6 largely due to the complexity of tumor–immune interactions and insufficient tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) activity.7,8,9,10,11,12

A major limitation of standard organoid systems is the rapid loss of endogenous immune components, particularly TILs, during tissue dissociation and in vitro expansion. Tumor cells typically outcompete immune cells under standard culture conditions, and existing organoid platforms often fail to support TIL survival and function. Co-culture systems with engineered immune cells or peripheral blood-derived immune cells partially address this issue but lack sustained in situ antigen exposure.13 Although air–liquid interface (ALI) cultures can preserve parental tumor immune cells and recapitulate tumor microenvironment architecture,14 these systems are limited in scalability, uniformity, and high-throughput applications.

To address these challenges, we developed a high-throughput droplet-engineered tumor organoid platform based on cascade-tubing microfluidics (CTM).15,16,17 This system enables standardized generation of uniform cell-laden micro-spheres with controlled size and cellular composition, preserving heterogeneous cell populations derived from primary tumor tissues.

In this protocol, we describe a workflow for generating TNBC tumor organoids that retain endogenous TILs through optimized tissue processing and culture conditions. Specifically, modulation of the Rho-associated coiled-coil kinase (ROCK) signaling pathway is used to balance tumor cell survival and immune cell preservation. Removal of the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 at day 2 (D2) enables TIL retention, whereas subsequent supplementation with pentanoic acid (PA) enhances T cell functional activity.1

The resulting system provides a scalable platform for constructing immune-competent tumor organoids suitable for immunological analysis and immunotherapy-oriented drug screening. Innovation

This protocol establishes a high-throughput droplet-engineered tumor organoid platform that preserves endogenous tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), addressing a major limitation of conventional organoid systems that rapidly lose immune components during culture. By integrating automated micro-sphere fabrication using the OrgFab® platform with optimized tissue processing, this approach enables reproducible generation of uniform tumor organoids while maintaining heterogeneous cellular composition from primary tumor tissues.

A key innovation of this protocol is the use of ROCK pathway modulation to balance tumor cell survival and immune cell preservation. Specifically, timed withdrawal of Y-27632 at day 2 (D2) enables retention of endogenous TILs, while subsequent supplementation with pentanoic acid (PA) further enhances T cell functional activity within the reconstructed tumor microenvironment.

Compared to existing methods, this protocol combines high-throughput engineering with immune-competent tumor modeling, providing improved reproducibility, scalability, and biological relevance. Importantly, it extends organoid applications from tumor growth modeling to immunotherapy-oriented drug screening and enables systematic evaluation of combination treatment strategies.

Dissociate tumor tissues and prepare tumor-derived bioink

Timing: 1.5–2.5 h

In murine TNBC organoids, successful establishment of the DEO-Y condition is characterized by preservation of endogenous tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) despite overall organoid growth. During culture, total cell numbers typically increase, with approximately a two-fold expansion by day 5 (Figure 6B). Although the relative proportion of CD3+ T cells appears reduced compared to parental tumor tissue (Figure 6C), this decrease reflects dilution due to tumor cell expansion rather than loss of T cells. Consequently, the absolute number of CD3+ T cells is largely maintained in DEO-Y organoids, indicating effective retention of endogenous TILs.

Upon further ROCK pathway modulation, including supplementation with pentanoic acid (PA) from day 2 (DEO+PA), tumor organoids are expected to exhibit enhanced immune cell retention and functional activation. This is reflected by increased frequencies of leukocytes and CD3+ T cells, as well as elevated activation markers (e.g., CD25+, CD107a+) and modulation of exhaustion-associated markers such as PD-1 (Figures 6F–6J), indicating improved T cell functional status.

In patient-derived organoids (PDOs), similar outcomes are expected across independent samples. PDOs typically maintain consistent morphology during culture (Figure 7A) and retain measurable immune cell populations, including CD3+, CD8+, and activated T cells. Comparative analysis across control, DEO-Y, and DEO+PA conditions demonstrates enhanced immune cell retention and activation following ROCK modulation (Figure 7B), supporting the translational applicability of this system.

Overall, this protocol enables the generation of immune-competent tumor organoids that preserve endogenous TILs and support their functional modulation. This platform is suitable for downstream immunological analysis and immunotherapy-oriented drug screening, providing a scalable system to investigate tumor–immune interactions and evaluate combination treatment strategies.

Limitations

This protocol enables generation of immune-competent tumor organoids with preserved endogenous tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs); however, several limitations should be considered. First, successful retention of TILs is highly dependent on the quality and freshness of tumor tissues. Patient-derived samples that are processed with delay or subjected to cryopreservation may show reduced immune cell viability and lower efficiency of TIL retention. In addition, variability in immune cell abundance across tumor samples, particularly in low-infiltration tumors, may lead to inconsistent TIL representation in organoids.

Second, this protocol is currently optimized and validated for short-term culture of immune-competent tumor organoids, up to 5 days. Reliable long-term culture or passaging conditions that maintain endogenous TILs have not yet been established. In our experience, passaging the organoids leads to progressive loss of immune cells, and the passaged organoids no longer faithfully preserve the original endogenous TIL population. Therefore, this system is best suited for short-term functional interrogation of tumor-TIL interactions rather than long-term expansion of immune-containing organoids. Nevertheless, because organoids generated using the OrgFab® system mature rapidly, they can be used for drug-response assessment by day 5, which may facilitate rapid patient-specific tumor modeling and ex vivo therapeutic screening.

Third, the balance between tumor cell survival and immune cell preservation is sensitive to enzymatic digestion and culture conditions. Over-digestion or excessive mechanical stress during tissue processing can reduce lymphocyte viability, whereas insufficient digestion may compromise bioink homogeneity and organoid formation. Similarly, precise timing of ROCK pathway modulation is critical; deviations from the optimized schedule (e.g., delayed removal of Y-27632 or improper PA supplementation) may impair TIL retention or functional activation.

Fourth, although the OrgFab® system enables high-throughput and uniform organoid generation, micro-sphere formation can be affected by technical factors such as bioink viscosity, temperature control, and tubing stability. Variations in these parameters may lead to inconsistent organoid size or structure, which can influence downstream analyses.

Finally, while this protocol preserves endogenous TILs and supports functional interrogation, it does not fully recapitulate all aspects of the in vivo tumor microenvironment, such as vascularization, systemic immune interactions, or long-term immune dynamics. Therefore, results obtained from this system should be interpreted in conjunction with in vivo models when possible.

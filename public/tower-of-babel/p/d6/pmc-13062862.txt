Real world outcomes of darolutamide activity and safety in treating hormone-sensitive prostate cancer
Authors: Shuo Liang, Xuyu Zhang, Tong Lu, Zhigang Wu, Hongqian Guo, Junlong Zhuang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDarolutamide is a next-generation androgen receptor (AR) inhibitor, used in the treatment of prostate cancer. There is currently limited real-world data available on its activity and safety. In our research, we collected real-world data to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of darolutamide in patients with castration-sensitive prostate cancer.MethodsThis study retrospectively collected baseline information from patients with non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (nmHSPC) and metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) who received darolutamide treatment at Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital between January 2022 and January 2024. We recorded the time at which patients reached their lowest prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels and documented information of treatment discontinuation, dosage reduction, and adverse events during the treatment.ResultsA total of 188 patients were included: 71% (n=133) with nmHSPC and 29% (n=55) with mHSPC. Both groups reached the lowest PSA levels in a median of 5 months, with most patients achieving a PSA90 reduction. At 12 months, biochemical progression-free survival (bPFS) rates were 72.8% for nmHSPC and 91% for mHSPC. Discontinuation due to voluntary choice was 2.3% (n=3) for nmHSPC and 1.8% (n=1) for mHSPC; due to insufficient efficacy, 0.8% (n=1) for nmHSPC and 7% (n=4) for mHSPC. Adverse events led to dose reductions in 4.5% (n=6) of nmHSPC and 1.8% (n=1) of mHSPC patients. Among the 51.1% (n=68) of nmHSPC patients with available surgical data, 81% (n=55) had negative margins, and 28% (n=19) achieved pathological complete response (pCR) or minimal residual disease (MRD). Common side effects included rash, fatigue, hot flushes, and sweating, with 7.4% (n=14) experiencing grade 2 adverse events and 0.5% (n=1) with grade 3.ConclusionsOur real-world data demonstrate the activity and safety of darolutamide in patients with nmHSPC or mHSPC.

Introduction

Prostate cancer is the fifth leading cause of cancer-related deaths in men globally, significantly contributing to mortality among male cancer patients (1). In recent years, its incidence has been steadily rising. The management of prostate cancer typically involves surgical procedures, drug therapies, chemotherapy and radiotherapy. For those who are not candidates for surgery or radiotherapy, the primary treatment options include drug therapies, such as androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), doublet therapy, and triplet therapy (2).

ADT reduces androgen production by the testes but is ineffective against androgen produced by the adrenal glands and tumors (3). Multiple clinical studies have demonstrated that combining ADT with androgen receptor signaling inhibitors (ARSIs), such as apalutamide, enzalutamide, and darolutamide, can result in improved survival outcomes (4). Notably, several trials have indicated that darolutamide is associated with fewer side effects compared to abiraterone, enzalutamide, and other ARSIs (5).

Recent studies, including the ARAMIS and ARASENS trials, have demonstrated that darolutamide significantly improves survival outcomes (6-8). However, real-world data on the activity and safety of darolutamide remain limited. To address this gap, we conducted a single-center, real-world retrospective study. In this study, we closely monitored key clinical outcomes, including time to lowest prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, treatment discontinuation, dosage reductions, progression-free survival (PFS), and adverse events. The primary goal of this study is to evaluate the activity and safety of darolutamide in patients with non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (nmHSPC) and metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC), thereby providing valuable evidence and guidance for the treatment of prostate cancer. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-1-897/rc).

Methods

Patients and treatments

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethical Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (No. 2022-030-01) and all patients provided written informed consent.

Included patients were males aged between 38 and 98 years, diagnosed with mHSPC or nmHSPC, and deemed suitable for darolutamide treatment at Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital. Data were collected between January 2022 and January 2024. The clinical data included 68 patients with nmHSPC who received neoadjuvant therapy. These patients received darolutamide plus ADT with a median treatment duration of 6 months. Subsequently these patients underwent surgery. Another 65 nmHSPC patients were treated with darolutamide plus ADT, also for a median of 6 months. Additionally, 55 mHSPC patients were also treated with darolutamide plus ADT, with a median treatment duration of 11 months. None of the patients received docetaxel or radiotherapy during the study period. The inclusion criteria for patients were the following: patients aged ≥18 years, a diagnosis of prostate cancer confirmed by histopathological or cytological examination. Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria were the following: non-adenocarcinoma pathology, patients with castration-resistant prostate cancer, lost to follow-up or incomplete data, received chemotherapy or other ARSIs (Figure 1).

Follow-up and observation indicators

The clinical data were collected through the Hospital Information System (HIS) electronic medical record system, including patients’ age, PSA levels, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), Charlson Comorbidity Index scores, Gleason scores, tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, number of metastatic lesions, and risk stratification. The follow-up duration was 22 months. Additionally, we tracked the median duration of treatment, time to reach the lowest PSA level, biochemical PFS (bPFS), defined as the time from initiation of treatment to a PSA increase to 0.1 ng/mL, PFS, time to a PSA reduction of 50%, PSA decrease ≥90%, and PSA <0.2 ng/mL, as well as surgical information. Furthermore, we recorded treatment discontinuation status and reasons, dosage reduction causes, and adverse events to assess the safety of darolutamide. The grading of these events was determined in accordance with version 5.0 of the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

Statistical analysis

Measurement data following a normal distribution are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical data are described as frequency (N) and percentage (%). The Kaplan-Meier method was used to analyze the time to reach the lowest PSA level, treatment discontinuation, dosage reduction, and adverse events during treatment. Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8, SPSS (Version 27.0), or R (Version 4.0.2).

Results

Baseline data of 188 patients

The median age of the included patients was 74 years (range, 38–98 years). Most patients had a Gleason score of ≥8 (n=135, 71.8%), and 91% (n=171) had an ECOG PS score of 0–1. Baseline PSA levels were 76.15±132.45 ng/mL for nmHSPC and 385.31±1,316.39 ng/mL for mHSPC. The majority of patients had a Charlson Comorbidity Index score of ≥3 (n=172, 91.49%). Of the 133 nmHSPC patients, 108 (81.20%) had lymph node metastasis. Additionally, among the 55 mHSPC patients, 49 (89.1%) had no invasion of tissues other than the seminal vesicles, and 31 (56.36%) developed lymph node metastases. All mHSPC patients had distant metastasis, with 32 (58.18%) classified as oligometastatic. Furthermore, 109 (81.95%) nmHSPC patients and all mHSPC patients were categorized as having very high or locally advanced disease (Table 1).

PSA response rate and surgical outcomes

The median duration of treatment for nmHSPC patients was 6 months, while mHSPC patients had a median treatment duration of 11 months. The median time for both nmHSPC and mHSPC patients to reach the lowest PSA levels was 5 months (Figure 2). Notably, 99.2% (n=132) of nmHSPC patients achieved PSA90, while 96.4% (n=53) of mHSPC patients reached PSA90. The 12-month bPFS rates were 72.8% for nmHSPC and 91% for mHSPC (Figure 3). A total of 70 (52.6%) nmHSPC patients achieved significant PSA reduction (PSA <0.2 ng/mL), while 32 (60.4%) mHSPC patients achieved significant PSA reduction (PSA <0.2 ng/mL) (Table 2). Of the 133 nmHSPC patients, 68 (51.1%) underwent radical prostatectomy, all of whom received neoadjuvant therapy. Margins were negative in 81% (n=55) of patients, while 19% (n=13) had positive margins. Regarding postoperative pathology, 10 (15%) patients achieved pathological complete response (pCR), and 9 (13%) patients had minimal residual disease (MRD) (Table 3).

Discussion

Our real-world study demonstrates that darolutamide is well tolerated and therapeutically effective. The median time for patients to reach the lowest PSA levels was 5 months. After several months of treatment, most patients achieved PSA90. The longer median duration of treatment in patients with mHSPC compared with those with nmHSPC may, at least in part, explain the higher radiographic PFS (rPFS) rate observed in the mHSPC cohort. Furthermore, 68 (51.1%) nmHSPC patients underwent radical prostatectomy, and postoperative pathological findings indicated that 28% of these patients achieved either pCR or MRD. In the ARANOTE trial, 63% of patients with mHSPC achieved a PSA level below 0.2 ng/mL, which is comparable to the proportion observed in our study (60.4%) (9). Only 41 (21.81%) patients experienced adverse events, and 16 (8.51%) patients either discontinued treatment or reduced their dosage.

Darolutamide is a novel second-generation androgen receptor (AR) inhibitor. Its unique structure enables darolutamide to bind directly to the AR ligand-binding domain (10). Our data indicate that darolutamide achieves good clinical efficacy in patients with mHSPC and nmHSPC. However, docetaxel was not included in our study. Randomized controlled trials have shown that mHSPC patients treated with triplet therapy consisting of ADT, ARSI, and docetaxel achieve better survival outcomes (5,11). Therefore, future research will include patients receiving triplet therapy. Moreover, there are currently no prospective trials comparing darolutamide with other second-generation AR inhibitors. A systematic review and meta-analysis showed that darolutamide, apalutamide, and enzalutamide (all combined with ADT) have similar efficacy in terms of metastasis-free survival (MFS) (12). In terms of PFS, new-generation ARSIs show no significant differences (13).

Drug-induced adverse events can negatively impact patients’ quality of life, potentially decreasing patient compliance. Additionally, medications with fewer side effects align better with patient expectations. Compared with enzalutamide and apalutamide, darolutamide demonstrates a superior safety profile regarding adverse events (4). Its low penetration of the blood-brain barrier contributes to fewer central nervous system (CNS) effects (14). Unlike abiraterone, darolutamide does not require concomitant use of glucocorticoids, thereby avoiding the associated side effects of glucocorticoids. In our study, only 41 patients reported adverse events, and only one patient experienced adverse events graded over 2, indicating an advantage over other new-generation ARSIs. In the ARASENS trial, the incidence of adverse events in the darolutamide group was comparable to that in the placebo group, although slightly higher in older patients, further supporting the favorable safety profile of darolutamide (15). However, further follow-up is necessary to evaluate the long-term safety of darolutamide.

Numerous studies have demonstrated that darolutamide plays an important role in patients with nmHSPC and mHSPC (8,16). However, data on the use of darolutamide in neoadjuvant therapy remain limited. Existing studies have shown that abiraterone (17), enzalutamide (18), and apalutamide (19) can reduce tumor staging and improve pathological outcomes, such as positive surgical margins, lymph node metastasis, and extracapsular invasion (20). Our study indicates that the majority of nmHSPC patients achieved negative surgical margins following neoadjuvant therapy with darolutamide. Although our sample size is small, these findings are promising. Further research is necessary to provide additional evidence supporting the benefits of darolutamide in the neoadjuvant setting.

There are several limitations in this study. First, all patients were recruited from Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, resulting in a lack of data from different centers. Additionally, the study included only 188 patients, so future studies will involve a larger sample size. Furthermore, as this is a retrospective study, prospective studies are needed to validate our findings. We are unable to account for selection bias and other unquantifiable confounding factors. Finally, the short follow-up period calls for ongoing research to further assess the long-term efficacy and safety of darolutamide.

Conclusions

Our study shows that darolutamide is both effective and safe for patients with nmHSPC and mHSPC. Most patients achieved PSA90, and over half experienced significant PSA reductions. With a lower incidence of adverse events, we anticipate that darolutamide will provide greater benefits to a larger number of prostate cancer patients.

Effects of home disease management strategies based on the dyadic illness management theory on elderly patients with chronic heart failure and informal caregivers’ physical and psychological outcomes: protocol of a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yaoyao Hu, Lu Yu, Lingyun Zhu, Li Li, Xiaojing Li, Xiaoyan Wang, Taihua Zhou, Danfeng GU
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrently, most elderly chronic heart failure (CHF) patients go home for rehabilitation after certain treatment in hospitals. However, the results of their rehabilitation at home are not satisfactory. According to studies, dyadic treatments can increase the efficiency of home rehabilitation, enhance both partners' quality of life, lessen the caregiver's load of care, and alleviate the strain of medical resources. Thus, the aim of our research is to design a study protocol that included elderly CHF patients and their informal caregivers as an intervention unit and to explore the impact of the protocol on their health and physical outcomes.MethodsThis is a prospective randomized controlled trial conducted in a triple-A hospital. In total, 80 elderly CHF patients and informal caregivers (80 dyads) will be recruited with informed consent. Based on the randomized numbers, they are divided into a control group (40 dyads) and an intervention group (40 dyads), subjects in the control group will receive usual care, and subjects in the intervention group will receive a home-based disease management program based on the Theory of Dyadic Illness Management on the basis of the control group. The duration of the intervention is 3 months, and the follow-up is 6 months. Data is collected at enrolment, 3 months after the intervention, and 3 months after the end of the intervention. The primary outcome is patients’ quality of life and readmission. Secondary outcomes include patients’ self-management behaviors, anxiety, and depression and caregivers’ quality of life and care burden.DiscussionThis study focuses on whether this home-based disease management program can improve the quality of life of elderly patients with CHF, reduce the readmission rate, enhance their self-management capacity, reduce negative emotions, and reduce the burden of informal caregivers. It can provide a new perspective on home management and cardiac rehabilitation of heart failure disease in the elderly, as well as alleviate problems such as the burden of healthcare resources.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trials Registry ChiCRT2300068026. Background

Heart failure can be divided into acute heart failure and chronic heart failure (CHF), among which CHF is the most common, especially in the elderly. CHF is a persistent state of heart failure that can be stable, worsening, or decompensated, characterized by dyspnea, fluid retention, and fatigue, and is one of the leading causes of death in elderly people [1]. Based on research studies, heart failure affects about 64 million persons globally, with developed countries having as high as 2.1% prevalence among those 65 years of age and above [2, 3]. Conservative estimated that China’s heart failure patients up to 4 million cases, 60–79-year-old people with heart failure accounted for about 3.09%, the average number of heart failure patients hospitalized 3.3 times/year, the average per capita hospitalization cost of 29,746 yuan/year [1, 4]. With the progress of population aging in China, the prolongation of life span per capita, and the increase in the diversity of cardiovascular risk factors, the prevalence of heart failure in the elderly has shown a sustained trend of increase, and the elderly patients with heart failure are at a high risk of disease deterioration and re-admission to the hospital, and advanced age is even more of a major risk factor for the poor prognosis of patients with heart failure.

However, there is no effective cure for heart failure, and the majority of patients are discharged after a period of treatment in hospitals for home recovery and rehabilitation. The AHA/ACC/HFSA Guidelines for the Management of Heart Failure, the European Society of Cardiology (ESC), and the Chinese Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis and Management of Chronic Heart Failure in the Elderly all emphasized the need for self-management of patients with heart failure while at home in an attempt to reduce the risk of readmission, death, and improve quality of life (QoL) [5–7]. Self-management is an individual’s ability to manage symptoms, treatment, physical, and psychosocial changes in response to chronic disease [8]. Due to the diversity of clinical syndromes of CHF, the complexity of the elderly patients’ own conditions, and the relative lack of evidence-based non-pharmacological management in the elderly with CHF, all these make the disease self-management a challenging task [9]. A large number of studies have demonstrated that good self-management is effective in improving the prognosis of patients with CHF and thus reduces health care costs and the use of health resources [10–12]. However, the self-management level of elderly CHF patients in China has been reported in most studies to be at a low to moderate level [13, 14]. Therefore, it is urgent to improve the self-management ability of elderly CHF patients.

Research has indicated that the inclusion of informal caregivers (ICs) in the management of patients’ diseases can improve the effectiveness of disease management, boost patients’ physical and mental well-being, and lessen the burden upon medical resources [15]. However, in the process of caring for patients with CHF, caregivers are faced with many problems such as heavy care burden, insufficient care capacity, and lack of external support [16]. Domestic scholars have also noted out that caregivers of CHF have heavy caregiving burdens, insufficient caregiving knowledge and competence, and lack of external support in caring and that they are in dire demand for greater knowledge and skill support in disease management [17, 18]. A systematic review has shown that an overburden of care by family caregivers of HF patients adversely affects their own health outcomes and those of their patients [19]. Given the significant role that ICs play and the difficulties that come with providing care, it is imperative that novel models of interventions be developed to help them in order to lessen the burden of providing care, enhance their QoL, and ultimately improve their own health outcomes and those of the people they care for.

Dyadic intervention is the simultaneous intervention of patients and caregivers. Compared to the single target of intervention, dyadic interventions are more cost-effective and can largely lead to better outcomes for both parties [20]. The Theory of Dyadic Illness Management is a recent update to dyadic theories proposed by Lyons and Lee. The fundamental principle of this theory is that, rather than emphasizing a single level of patients or caregivers, disease management is a dyadic phenomenon that centers on patients with chronic illnesses and family members who provide care for them [21]. The theory consists of three main elements: dyadic appraisal, dyadic management behaviors, and dyadic health, and optimizing dual health is its central goal. Dyadic appraisal refers to the evaluation of patients and caregivers based on symptoms, care values, and preferences. Dyadic management behaviors encompass the various ways members of the dyad work as a unit, including decision-making, managing changes in function or worsening signs and symptoms, and general health behaviors of both members. Dyadic appraisal and dyadic management behaviors both influence the health of the dyad. In turn, the health of the dyad can influence how the patient and care partner appraise and manage illness together over time.

Overseas-related researches have shown that disease management based on patients and caregivers (i.e., dyadic disease management) enhances communication and emotional management between the two and increases knowledge and skills in disease management [22, 23]. In addition, the discharge readiness service program developed by our scholars based on the Theory of Dyadic Illness Management has improved the level of discharge readiness and support for coping in elderly lung cancer patients and also meets the comprehensive needs of caregivers [24]. The simultaneous intervention by Chinese scholars for HF patients and their spouses not only improved the of the patients but also improved the intimacy between the couples [25]. As such, in the context of CHF disease management, there is a need to shift the focus to a dyadic orientation, that is, to manage the disease as a unit for both the patient and the caregiver and thus to promote sustained collaborative management behaviors that will have a lasting impact on the physical and mental health of the patient and their caregivers.

Due to the complex and recurrent nature of CHF symptoms, it poses some challenges for home-based disease management. Most studies on CHF disease management in China have focused on a single aspect of the patient or informal caregiver, with only a few interventions focusing on heart failure patients and their ICs as a single unit and even fewer on elderly patients and their ICs in particular. Currently, healthcare professionals’ interventions for disease self-management of CHF patients only focus on health education during hospitalization and do not continue to intervene after patients are discharged from the hospital, resulting in a lack of continuity of interventions. After release from the hospital, with the passage of time, there is often a fading of knowledge about the illness, and their level of adherence to treatment and self-management maintenance will gradually decline [26, 27]

As a consequence, the purpose of this trial is to explore the impact of a home-based disease management program based on the Dyadic Ill Management Theory on the physical and mental health outcomes of elderly CHF patients and ICs and to contribute to the basis for the formulation of a disease management and rehabilitation program for elderly patients with CHF.

Study aim

This study includes elderly patients with CHF and ICs as an interventional unit, primarily to understand the effectiveness of a home-based disease management program.

The principal aims of the study are to explore whether home-based disease management can improve patients’ QoL and readmission rates. The secondary objectives are to explore whether home-based disease management could improve patients’ self-management behaviors, reduce their somatic symptoms, lessen anxiety and depression levels, and promote QoL and reduce the burden of care for ICs.

Methods

Study design

This prospective, randomized controlled superiority trial is conducted at the Department of Cardiology, the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University, Wuxi, China. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 80 pairs of elderly patients with CHF and ICs who meet the eligibility requirements of the study will be selected, and after they have signed the informed consent form, the researcher will randomly divide them into the control group and the intervention group (1:1) according to the randomized numerical table method. The control group is given routine nursing care measures, and the intervention group implements home-based disease management strategies based on routine nursing care, with a duration of intervention of 3 months. Both groups will be followed up for 6 months each. Based on the Standardized Protocol Items and the SPIRIT reporting guidelines [28], the study protocol was designed (more information is given in Additional file 3). Setting

Department of Cardiovascular Medicine of Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University is the key specialty of clinical medicine in Wuxi, National Chest Pain Center and National Heart Failure Center. There are 130 beds in the department, which consists of 3 wards of cardiovascular medicine and 1 ward of CCU. The average annual outpatient volume is 100,000, and the number of inpatient hospitalizations is more than 4400.

Sample size calculation

The sample size formula for comparing the means of two samples is adopted to calculate the sample size [29]: n1 = n2 = 2[(Zα/2+Zβ)δσ]2, n1 and n2 are the required contents of the two samples, respectively. Zα and Zβ are the Z values corresponding to the probability of type I and type II errors, respectively. σ is the standard deviation of the study indicator and δ is the difference between the two overall means of the study indicator. In this study, the QoL of CHF patients is the primary outcome indicator, and by reviewing similar literature, we learned that σ=5.01, δ= 8.15 [25]. At the test level: α = 0.05, 1-β = 0.8 level, n1 = n2 = 33 is calculated using the above sample size formula (n1 = n2 = 2[(Zα/2+Zβ)δσ]2) [29]. Considering also the 20% sample missing rate, the sample size (patients) for each group is formulated in this study as 40 cases. At the same time, 40 matched caregivers in each group would be included. That's a total sample size of 80 pairs.

Participants and recruitment

Participants in the study will be enrolled at the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University. To recruit individuals to participate, the trial will be advertised on the hospital’s WeChat public number and the Department of Cardiology bulletin board. Two trained researchers will explain to the participants in detail about the trial: (1) the purpose, method, and procedure of this study, the significance, the fact that there is no financial cost to participate, and whether there are any risks and discomforts, etc. and (2) the confidentiality of personal information of the subjects and the information about the measurements related to the study will be protected. Subjects who meet the inclusion exclusion criteria and agree to participate in this study will voluntarily sign an informed consent form.

Randomization and allocation concealment

An intervention group or a control group will be randomly assigned with a 1:1 allocation ratio for eligible participants who will provide written consent to participate in the clinical study. The random numbers are generated by a statistician using spss26 software, the statistician only helped to generate the random numbers and was not involved in this study, and the random numbers will be sealed in opaque envelopes to be kept by a person and placed in a safe place. No one knows the details of the envelope. The envelope will be unsealed in the order that participants are visited, and depending on the groups listed in the envelope, participants will be randomly assigned to the intervention or usual care group by a person who is not involved in the intervention and data collection.

Blinding

Due to the nature of the intervention, neither participants nor the medical staff involved in the intervention can be blinded to the allocation. However, data gatherers, outcome evaluators, and data analysts will be blinded until the statistical analysis has been completed. The results data are entered in separate sheets to enable for analysis without exposing the allocation status. There is no need to unblind because the subjects and intervention implementers are not blinded in this trial.

Intervention

Control group implements conventional nursing, and the intervention group implements home disease management on the basis of the control group, that is, participants will continue to receive routine medical care throughout the study period. The intervention will last for 3 months. Both groups will be followed up for 6 months respectively.

A multidisciplinary collaborative team is formed. There are ten members of this study team, including one chief cardiologist, one attending physician, one chief cardiology nurse, four primary nurses, one psychological counselor, one dietitian, and one master’s degree in nursing. The nurse leader serves as the team leader and, together with the project leader, is responsible for the coordination and quality control of this study intervention. The physicians, psychological counselor, and dietitian are responsible for the development of the therapeutic, psychological, nutritional, and other professional rehabilitation components for CHF patients and ICs. Two nurses conduct the intervention, two nurses collect the data and follow-up, and the master of science in nursing student organizes and analyzes the data.

The intervention is divided into two phases, the first phase is in-hospital intervention (0–2 weeks), which used face-to-face format for two times. The second phase is an out-of-hospital intervention (2–12 weeks), which is conducted in the form of online, once every 2 weeks, for a total of five times. The total intervention length is 12 weeks, with seven times of approximately 60 min each. During the implementation of intervention, evaluate the disease symptoms and preferences of dyadic objects at any time, in order to adjust intervention measures in a timely manner and improve the management behavior of both. For keeping track of both parties’ mastery of knowledge and skills related to disease management, an interactive session with the participants is held prior to each online meeting.

After discharge, follow-up visits are made once a week for the first month and then once a month, each lasting between 5 and 10 min. A committed researcher keeps track on the follow-up patients’ dynamics to determine whether dyads are experiencing any new fundamental management issues and to provide prompt feedback and remedies.

Outcome

The main outcome measures of this study are QoL and readmission rate of elderly patients with CHF. The secondary outcome measures are self-care behavior, physical symptoms, anxiety, and depression level in elderly patients with CHF, and QoL and care burden of ICs. The evaluation schedule is shown in Fig. 2.

Primary outcome

QoL: The Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ) will be used to evaluate the QoL of patients. The instrument was developed by Rector et al. in 1987 and consists of 21 items in 3 dimensions (somatic, emotional, and other domains). Each entry is rated on a 6-point Likert scale (0–5), with “0” meaning “no effect” and “5” meaning “very severe effect”. The total score ranges from 0 to 105, with higher scores indicating poorer QoL for patients. The MLHFQ is a translated and validated instrument, the total Cronbach’s α coefficient of MLHFQ was 0.88 [38]. The data will be collected at enrolment (-t1), 3 months after the intervention (t6), and 3 months after the end of the intervention (t7).

Secondary outcome

Self-management behaviors: The Chinese version of the Self-Care of Heart Failure Index Scale (SCHFIv6.2) will be used to measure the self-care behavior of patients with heart failure [40]. The scale contains three subscales: self-care maintenance, self-care management, and self-care confidence subscales. Self-care behaviors of elderly patients with chronic heart failure are evaluated by self-care maintenance and self-care management subscales. ① The self-care maintenance scale is a 10-item scale with a 4-point Likert scale, with 1 being “never” and 4 being “always,” which measures patients’ adherence to treatment and symptom monitoring behavior. ② The self-care management scale is a survey of heart failure patients’ recognition of symptoms, management, and post-management evaluative behaviors over the past month. Since the number of entries and scores for each subscale is different, therefore, it is necessary to convert to standard score according to the formula, formula: standard score = [(actual score − lowest score)/(highest score − lowest score)] × 100. The total score of the scale ranges from 0 to 300, and more than 70 points for each subscale indicate that the patient’s self-care status is good. The Cronbach’s α coefficients for the self-care maintenance subscale and self-care management subscale were 0.74 and 0.87 [40], respectively. The information will be gathered at -t1, t6, and t7.

Safety evaluation

Safety assessment will include adverse event reports, serious adverse events, and subsequent assessments on all enrolled subjects. This trial requires all participants to report any adverse events that occurred during week 1–week 24 (t1–t7). After the study starts, any adverse events (including serious adverse events) that occur, whether or not those are related to the intervention, should be documented on a case report form, and the subjects should receive the necessary medical attention. If a serious adverse event occurs (e.g., symptoms of chronic heart failure severe enough to require hospitalization), it will be reported to the principal investigator and the ethics committee within 24 h and a determination will be made as to whether the participant should continue the trial. Serious adverse events occurring during the trial will be summarized, analyzed for causality, and combined with anticipatory judgments to determine relevance to the intervention strategies. Every adverse event that is gathered will be faithfully reported in future publications.

Compliance monitoring and management

A WeChat follow-up group is established to push out regular weekly health knowledge tweets; after subjects are discharged from the hospital, they are followed up by specialized personnel to dynamically discover whether there are any new disease management problems for both the patient and the caregiver and to give timely feedback and solutions to improve their motivation and adherence. Compliance monitoring: after the study is conducted for 2 weeks, the researcher collects the participation of the study subjects in order to calculate compliance. Compliance rate is calculated as: Compliance rate (%) = [Continuous participants/total number of interventions] × 100%. The trial continues only if adherence is ≥ 80%; otherwise, reasons are sought for programmatic corrections.

Data management and quality control of data

Only persons involved in the project are allowed to access data. Individual participant data will be managed via a web-based electronic database and the case record form (CRF). All data will be input by the research team. Information relevant to the study will be safely stored in a file cabinet in the research room. All computers used for data entry and analysis are password protected. Each participant will be assigned a unique number and their name will not be mentioned in any study reports. Clinical experts and biostatisticians, independent of the trial site and without any conflict of interest with the trial, will review the process and data throughout the study to enhance safety monitoring. After the trial, the participant’s information will be kept for 5 years in accordance with medical record keeping regulations.

All data are dual entered into the computer using Epidata 3.0 software. After completion of data entry and query processing, the database will be locked at the command of the subject leader. No one was allowed to view the database without authorization.

Auditing

A monthly meeting of the project management team, which consists of project administrators, will be held to review status of project progress, project compliance, and identify any problems. The trial’s conduct and findings will be reviewed by the Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University.

Statistical analysis

The data will be analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics V.26 software. The researcher conducting the data analysis shall be blinded to the group sequence number. The normal distribution of continuous variables will be examined using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Continuous variables such as age, body mass index, ejection fraction, blood pressure, and blood test indices of the subjects are described using mean ± standard deviation or median (quartiles). Information on categorical variables such as gender, beliefs, literacy, health insurance status, cardiac function classifications, and comorbidities of the participants are described using frequency (n) and percentage (%). Comparison of continuous measures between or within groups before and after the intervention: data for measures conforming to normal distribution are analyzed using the two independent samples t-test, the t-test for paired samples or repeated measures analysis of variance; non-normally distributed data are analyzed using the non-parametric statistical Mann–Whitney U test. Comparison of count data between or within groups: data are analyzed using the χ2 test in a four-cell or R × C (Row × Column) table, Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided P < 0.05 indicates a statistically significant difference.

Discussion

Chronic heart failure (CHF) is the result of several cardiovascular diseases and is most common in the elderly population [1, 2]. In recent years, although the continuous development of medicine, the constant updating of drug types and the improvement of prevention and treatment methods have led to great progress in the treatment of CHF; the prognosis of patients is still poor. Due to its high hospitalization rate, high readmission rate, and death, CHF places a significant financial strain on society and families [4]. After obtaining treatment in a hospital, the majority of patients with CHF are now discharged for home rehabilitation. However, elderly patients with chronic heart failure are often characterized by old age, delayed response time, poor comprehension, and deficiencies in self-care, which makes managing their disease at home a very challenging task [9].

It has been noted in several studies that disease management with the involvement of informal caregivers can help improve the physical and mental health of both parties and reduce the burden on medical resources [15, 46]. However, a review of the literature domestically and internationally revealed that only a few studies have considered elderly patients with CHF and their informal caregivers as a unit for intervention, particularly in China, where there have been no studies on this topic.

The study protocol was designed based on the theory of dyad disease management. This study, which is the kind to be conducted in China, aspires to intervene with elderly CHF patients and ICs as a unit. We expect that this study will improve the QoL for elderly CHF patients, lower their readmission rates, enhance their ability to manage their own condition, and lessen negative emotions. Furthermore, we hope that our trial will help ICs to live better lives and carry less of the burden of caregiving. By doing so, it can allegedly offer refreshing perspectives on the home management of CHF disease in the elderly and cardiac rehabilitation while also easing issues like the burden on medical resources and is else in focus with what the European Society of Cardiology and expert consensus both need [5, 6].

This program of study has some limitations. First, this is a single-center randomized controlled study that only enrolled elderly patients with CHF from a tertiary hospital in Wuxi as the subjects and may be somewhat limited in extending the study results. It is recommended to carry out multicenter and chronic disease categories in the future to validate and refine this study protocol. Second, this study will only gather data for 3 months after the intervention due to time, energy, and human resource limitations. Long-term interventions and follow-up visits are recommended for the future in order to dynamically and consistently gain an understanding of the unique experiences and needs of elderly CHF patients and ICs in home-based disease management, in hopes of better improving the physical and mental health outcomes for both.

Trial status

The first version of study was registered with Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (registration number: ChiCRT2300068026) on 3 February 2023. The experiment began on February 20, 2023. However, due to COVID-19 pandemic, enrollment in this study was paused for a period of time midway through the study and re-enrollment began in October 2023, and to March 2024, 26 pairs of subjects have been recruited who meet the inclusion exclusion criteria. Recruitment for this trial is scheduled to be completed by October 2024.

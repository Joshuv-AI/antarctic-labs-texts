Effects of different telehabilitation methods on postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation of patients with lung cancer: a systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials protocol
Authors: Junna Liu, Hongmei Niu, Jinmeng Li, Qiang Chen, Hongyan Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionPostoperative pulmonary complications affect approximately 40% of individuals undergoing lung cancer surgery. A number of clinical trials and meta-analyses have shown that postoperative telehabilitation may reduce the incidence of postoperative adverse events while reducing transport time and outpatient costs and improving patient motivation. However, optimal telehabilitation strategies remain undetermined. The primary objective of this protocol is thus to compare the effects of different telehabilitation methods used after pulmonary surgery in patients with lung cancer by conducting a network meta-analysis.Methods and analysisTwo researchers will independently search six databases (PubMed, the Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science, Medline and the Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI)) and two clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP). The time range will be from database inception to March 2026. EndNote X V.21 will be used for screening based on predetermined inclusion criteria. All Chinese and English randomised controlled trials examining the effects of telehabilitation on lung function after lung cancer surgery will be included. The primary outcome measures will be forced vital capacity (FVC), forced expiratory volume over 1 s (FEV1), FEV1/FVC and 6 min walking test. The secondary outcome measures will be the incidence of adverse events, intervention completion rate, incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications and European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30) score. Risk of bias assessment will be performed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias V.2 (RoB V.2.0) tool, while certainty of evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment and Evaluation (GRADE). Stata V.18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas) and R (V.4.1.2) will be used to conduct traditional pairwise meta-analysis and network meta-analysis.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required for this study. The results will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal or conference.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251275899.

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC), malignant tumours arising from the bronchial mucosal epithelium, has the highest global cancer incidence (12.4%) and mortality (18.7%) among all malignancies, accounting for nearly 1.8 million deaths annually.1 Surgery remains the primary curative treatment for early-stage patients, offering the best chance of long-term survival.2 3 However, the postoperative course is frequently complicated: up to 40% of patients develop postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs), including pneumonia, atelectasis and respiratory failure, which prolong hospitalisation, increase healthcare costs and significantly impair quality of life and long-term prognosis.4 5 Effective strategies to optimise postoperative recovery are therefore urgently needed.

A number of studies have shown that pulmonary rehabilitation can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative complications in patients with LC, improving lung function and activity tolerance in such patients, and improving their happiness and quality of life after surgery.6 7 However, traditional hospital or outpatient-centred pulmonary rehabilitation models now face significant challenges,8 including high costs and limited flexibility in terms of time and location as well as a lack of compliance due to the monotony of the rehabilitation process.9 10 Consequently, only 60% of patients complete pulmonary rehabilitation,11 with the majority resorting to home-based rehabilitation.12 Yet, home-based rehabilitation lacks real-time feedback, professional interaction and standardised protocols, resulting in suboptimal compliance and unreliable efficacy.

To address these limitations, telehabilitation has emerged as a promising solution for postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation,1316 facilitated by advances in digital technology and mobile health. This approach delivers personalised care via mobile apps,1720 WeChat mini-programmes,21 video platforms,22 telephone programmes2325 or multimodal combinations. Transcending geographical and temporal barriers, it enables real-time guidance and continuous monitoring to optimise home-based rehabilitation and in-hospital intervention outcomes. While a large number of studies confirm its feasibility and effectiveness,26 head-to-head comparative evidence among these modalities remains insufficient. No study has evaluated their relative effects on lung function, exercise capacity, complication rates, quality of life or safety following LC surgery, and network meta-analysis (NMA) can effectively fill this evidence gap.

Therefore, this study will be the first to use the NMA method to systematically evaluate the clinical efficacy and safety of different telehabilitation methods (compared with conventional postoperative care, traditional face-to-face rehabilitation or other forms of telehabilitation) in patients after LC surgery and rank the effectiveness and safety of these telehabilitation approaches, aiming to provide a more accurate reference for individualised, evidence-based pulmonary rehabilitation decision-making, thereby improving postoperative outcomes of LC patients and promoting the wider application of telehabilitation in postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation.

Methods and analyses

A systematic review and NMA will be performed within the Bayesian framework. A protocol following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses protocol (PRISMA-P) and the relevant guidelines of Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for NMA (PRISMA-NMA)27 has been developed, and the proposed study was registered on the PROSPERO platform on 30 December 2025 under reference CRD420251275899. Any subsequent revisions to the protocol will also be added to the PROSPERO platform.

Inclusion criteria

Types of studies

Only randomised controlled trials (RCTs) published in English or Chinese will be included. Included studies must also clearly note the use of a random allocation method, incorporating terms such as ‘randomised controlled’ or ‘random’ or noting the use of specific randomisation methods such as ‘random number table’.

Types of participants

The inclusion criteria for participants are as follows:

Postoperative LC survivors aged ≥18 years, regardless of gender, ethnicity, race or educational background.

Postoperative LC survivors aged ≥18 years, regardless of gender, ethnicity, race or educational background.

Participants must have no mental disorders, normal comprehension ability and proficient in using intelligent devices.

Participants must have no mental disorders, normal comprehension ability and proficient in using intelligent devices.

Types of interventions

Participants in the experimental group will be allocated to a telehabilitation intervention. According to the preliminary searches for this study, such telehabilitation may include the use of any or all of mobile apps,1720 WeChat mini programmes,21 video links22 and telephone contact.2325

Types of control groups

The control group will perform face-to-face pulmonary rehabilitation treatment, such as routine care and routine pulmonary rehabilitation training (excluding telehabilitation).

Types of outcome measures

Primary outcomes

Pulmonary function tests will include forced vital capacity (FVC), forced expiratory volume over 1 s (FEV1), and the resulting FEV1/FVC ratio. Based on the 2023 European Respiratory Society/American Thoracic Society (ERS/ATS) technical standards, the interpretation of lung function follows a clear process: The FEV₁/FVC ratio is evaluated initially, and if this ratio is lower than the lower limit of the normal (LLN) range, airflow limitation is indicated. Subsequently, the FVC is evaluated, as if the FVC is normal, the limitation may be determined as being a simple obstruction. If the FVC is also lower than its LLN; however, this may indicate a mixed disorder. The latter case requires measurement of the total lung capacity to confirm whether there are other limiting components.28

Data sources and search strategy

Two researchers (Ju-L and Ji-L) will independently search eight online sources. These will include five English databases (PubMed, the Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science and Medline), two clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (WHO ICTRP)) as well as one Chinese database, the Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). The search will involve identifying relevant records in the period from each database’s inception through to March 2026. Searches will be triggered by pairing Medical Subject Headings with free-text keywords to capture all relevant literature. The Boolean operators ‘AND’, ‘OR’ and ‘NOT’ will be used to link the identified keywords to form a systematic search. The search keywords will include ‘lung neoplasms’, ‘lung cancer’, ‘telehabilitation’, ‘remote rehabilitation mobile health care’, ‘telemedicine’, ‘FVC’, ‘FEV₁’, ‘6MWT’, ‘Adverse Events’, ‘PPCs’ and ‘EORTC QLQ-C30’. Appropriate corresponding search formulas will be developed to match the characteristics of each individual database.

A process of backward citation will also be applied by searching the references and related systematic reviews of the included works, with special attention being paid to those references with higher citation frequencies (citation frequency ≥3 times) to ensure important pieces of work are not omitted. The detailed and comprehensive search strategies for each database will be outlined in greater detail in online supplemental materials.

Study selection

The literature screening work will be independently carried out by two researchers (Ju-L and Ji-L) using EndNote V.21 (Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania) to achieve efficient deduplication. Subsequently, an initial assessment will be performed based on examining the resulting titles and abstracts to eliminate any literature featuring incorrect research types, subjects or intervention measures, as well as any truly irrelevant literature. Finally, an independent full-text in-depth reading of the remaining literature will be undertaken by each researcher. The rationality of the experimental design and the completeness of the target outcome measures will be evaluated, and studies that do not meet the predefined criteria will be excluded, with justifications recorded for each exclusion. Where literature that might otherwise be reasonably included contains missing, unreported or unclearly expressed data (eg, SD, sample sizes, intervention details), attempts will be made to contact the relevant author to obtain complete outcome data. During the screening process, all results will be cross-checked between researchers, and if any differences arise, these will be discussed, with any remaining discrepancies determined by the third researcher (QC). Ratings for each domain (low risk, some concerns, high risk) will follow the tool’s guidance. The overall risk of a study will be defined as ‘high’ if either (1) at least one domain is high risk or (2) several domains raise concerns about risk of bias. The overall risk will be assessed as ‘some concerns’ if at least one domain suggests some risk of bias, but there are no high-risk domains. The overall risk will thus be judged ‘low’ only if all domains are low risk.

Discrepancies will be resolved by asking the third investigator (QC) to coordinate discussions and lead consensus. A risk-of-bias figure will also be drawn to visually represent the bias status of each study.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

Pairwise meta-analysis

A traditional pairwise meta-analysis will be performed in Stata V.18.0 (StataCorp, University Town, Texas). Only the incidence of adverse events and postoperative complications and the completion rate of interventions are dichotomous variables in this study; the effect size for these will thus be expressed as relative risks (RR) with 95% CIs. All other outcomes will be treated as continuous variables. For continuous variables, the mean difference (MD) and its 95% CIs will be used if the measurement instrument is the same. The standard MD and its 95% CIs will be used if the measurement instrument is different.

The χ² test and the I² statistic will be employed to assess and quantify statistical heterogeneity, respectively.35 Low heterogeneity, defined as p>0.10 and I²≤ 50%, will lead to the application of a fixed-effect model for pooling. Conversely, evidence of high heterogeneity (p≤0.10 or I²> 50%) will prompt the use of a random-effects model. For instances where substantial clinical or methodological heterogeneity is present, or if too few studies can be included to permit quantitative synthesis, descriptive analysis will be performed following the relevant comprehensive guidelines.36

Network meta-analysis

R (V.4.1.2) will be used for NMA. The gemtc and rjags packages will be utilised to construct the necessary Bayesian model and to perform statistical calculations using the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method. Each model will run with four Markov chains, with a step size set to one, and iterations set to 50 000. The first 20 000 iterations will be removed to represent a burn-in period, leaving the last 30 000 iterations to be used to calculate the sample.37

Network relationships between interventions will also be plotted. Nodes in the resulting network diagram will represent different interventions, and their areas will be positively correlated with the sample sizes of the interventions. The lines between nodes will indicate the presence of direct comparison studies, and wider lines will indicate higher numbers of original studies comparing the two interventions. In addition, a league table for all pairwise comparisons will be conducted, with the Surface under the Cumulative Ranking curve (SUCRA) being calculated for each intervention to generate the ranking and cumulative ranking probability plots. A higher SUCRA value indicates a greater probability that a given intervention is optimal.38

Assessment of similarity and consistency

Similarity and consistency assumptions will be evaluated to ensure the reliability of all comparative results. Any closed loops in the network relationship diagram will be subjected to the global inconsistency test in R (V.4.1.2), comparing the deviation information criterion (DIC) of the comparison consistency and inconsistency models. A DIC difference of less than one between these two models will serve as an indicator of no inconsistency, allowing the application of the consistency model. If inconsistency is indicated, the node splitting method will be employed to conduct local inconsistency testing to evaluate consistency between each direct and indirect comparison.39 In local tests, p>0.05 will be considered to indicate no inconsistency in the local area. If p<0.05, this will indicate that the results should be treated with caution. If the network graph is not a closed-loop structure, inconsistencies in results will be directly identified and determined.

Once all Bayesian models have been run, convergence diagnosis will be conducted. Convergence diagnosis will be quantitatively evaluated by calculating the Brooks-Gelman-Rubin statistic (R-hat), with results being visually verified by examination of trajectory maps and density maps. Any potential scale reduction factor close to one will be regarded as evidence of good convergence, based on this demonstrating the stability and reliability of the statistical results.

Subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis

The original data entry will be checked initially if any evidence indicating significant statistical or clinical heterogeneity emerges. After that, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be conducted, using Stata V.18.0, to explore the origins of heterogeneity and inconsistency in the included literature. The inspection levels will be all set at α=0.05.40 Where significant heterogeneity emerges, subgroup analyses based on the average age, gender, illness duration and treatment time of the subjects will be implemented to identify the origins of such heterogeneity.

A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis will be conducted for each study, with studies judged as having a high risk of bias overall being excluded. If effect size and statistical significance do not change substantially after the removal of a given study, the conclusions of the current meta-analysis will be assumed to have good methodological robustness.41

Assessment of publication bias

Publication bias can undermine the credibility of results. If an intervention comparison includes more than 10 studies in the NMA, the funnel plot, corrected using Egger’s test, will be used to assess potential publication bias and small-study effects. Any sample size of fewer than 10 items is associated with a significant reduction in the statistical power of the test; however, so in such cases, formal bias assessment will be replaced by application of the Bootstrap method to calculate the skewness CI as a reference. If the scatter points in the funnel plot are symmetrical and concentrated around the null line, this would indicate that the interstudy heterogeneity is low and the risk of publication bias is controllable; otherwise, significant publication bias is possible.42

Egger’s test relies on using a linear regression model to quantitatively assess funnel plot asymmetry. The test level will be set at α=0.05. Any p value of the intercept less than 0.05 will thus indicate clear asymmetry in the funnel plot. To enhance testing efficiency, the p values of the Egger’s test and skewness test will be combined for further testing.43

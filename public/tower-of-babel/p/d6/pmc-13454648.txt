Spontaneous Takotsubo Syndrome and Its Recurrences: Pathogenetic Role of Intraventricular Mechanical Factors
Authors: Yu-ki Nishimura, Yoshinori Doi
Published: 2026

Yalta et al. suggest the possibility of sudden intraventricular obstruction as a subtle trigger of spontaneous takotsubo syndrome (TTS) episodes, and that TTS due to this sudden intraventricular obstruction, mostly arising in the absence of an overt stressor, might also be recurrent in the long term. However, we would like to make it clear that it was not the case in our study patients with recurrence. None of our 13 patients with recurrence demonstrated any hemodynamic changes indicative of sudden intrventricular obstruction at initial episodes or at recurrence. Thus, a most important and interesting findings of our study is that the absence of a clear emotional or physical trigger at the initial presentation of TTS is a strong and independent predictor of recurrence.1 We suspect that it is possible that an underlying subclinical vulnerability, such as enhanced myocardial catecholamine sensitivity, chronic autonomic dysfunction, and/or impaired central autonomic regulation, may predispose certain patients to recurrent episodes, independent of external acute stressors.2

The definition of TTS in our study was based on the revised version of the Mayo Clinic diagnostic criteria,3 together with international Takotsubo diagnostic criteria for this condition.4 Furthermore, obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy was firmly excluded from our study when it was present at initial episode, because it is known that transient apical ballooning masquerading as TTS may at times be produced in the patients with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy when intraventricular pressure gradient suddenly increases.5 Although it morphologically resembles TTS of apical phenotype, we believe that it should not be included in the original condition of TTS. Thus, although we think that the suggestion made by Yalta et al. is not relevant to the matter at hand, we agree that we should be cautious in the diagnosis of TTS in patients with pre-existing myocardial conditions.

In addition, other important messages from our study include the lower prevalence of typical ECG changes at the initial presentation of TTS in recurrent cases, and the reproducibility of the ballooning patterns between initial and recurrent TTS.

Finally, we would like to once again emphasize the importance of evaluating the presence or absence of a clear emotional or physical trigger at initial episodes TTS in all patients, because the absence of a definable trigger is an independent and important predictor of TTS recurrence.

Davunetide sex-dependently boosts memory in prodromal Alzheimer’s disease
Authors: Illana Gozes, Jason Blatt, Alexandra Lobyntseva
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe tauopathy inhibitor, davunetide shows sex-dependent efficacy in women suffering from progressive supranuclear palsy. Extending these findings to prodromal Alzheimer’s disease, we submitted a double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12 weeks/16 weeks follow-up, davunetide clinical trial results in amnestic mild cognitive impairment (ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00422981), to a sex-dependent analysis.MethodsOne hundred forty-four individuals, separated into eight groups (1:2 placebo—and 2 doses, 5 mg davunetide/daily or 15 mg davunetide/twice-daily, with matching placebo intranasal volumes), were evaluated.ResultsSignificant dose-dependent cognitive increases were observed in men compared to women with a test of delayed (12 ss) visual matching to the sample. In a test of semantic working memory and attention (digit span), women showed a significant low-dose placebo effect, ensuing in a high dose significant davunetide improvement, over the matched placebo. Correlating anxiety with cognition showed sex-opposing results, with women depicting significant anxiety correlations with delayed matching to sample.DiscussionIn conclusion, sex-specific prodromal Alzheimer’s drug development is encouraged, with davunetide playing a lead initiative role.

Introduction

We recently showed a significantly slower deterioration of intranasal davunetide-treated women suffering from progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP), compared to placebo-administered females, over a year of twice-daily drug administration. Mechanistically, davunetide, while enhancing Tau-microtubule interaction, protects against tauopathy and consequent neurodegeneration (as measured by enlargement of ventricular volumes), a core pathology of PSP. The PSP sex-dependent difference was explained by a significantly faster neurodegeneration and functional loss in women coupled with a potential over-dose in men (30 mg davunetide twice daily/52 weeks) [1]. Furthermore, in the same woman population, davunetide protected against geriatric depression [1]. With Alzheimer’s disease (AD), the most prevalent tauopathy, being twice more abundant in women than men, and with tauopathy progression associated with menopause [2], also encompassing geriatric depression as a major risk factor, we hypothesized that women suffering from prodromal AD will present the ideal population for davunetide treatment.

To put our hypothesis into a further context, davunetide (also known as NAP, AL-108, CP201) is the smallest neuroprotective fragment of activity-dependent neuroprotective protein (ADNP), serving as a replacement/enhancement therapy when ADNP is deficient or mutated [3, 4]. We have discovered that ADNP serum levels in elderly adults at risk for AD correlate with performance in intelligence tests [5]. Complete serum proteomics of individuals suffering from early AD, identified ADNP as a major AD-related down-regulated protein, with davunetide providing protection in relevant cell culture models [6–8]. Complementarily, we have also shown davunetide (NAP) protection in amyloid/tauopathy AD transgenic mice [9, 10]. Directly connecting tauopathy to ADNP in humans, we have revealed somatic mutations in ADNP in postmortem AD brains with augmented mutation frequency correlating with increased tauopathy [11], as well as surprising tauopathy in young ADNP-mutated deceased child brain postmortem analysis [12]. Davunetide (NAP) protects against mutated ADNP-related tauopathy in cell cultures and in ADNP-mutated or deficient animal models, with potential increased tauopathy in young ADNP mutated males [4, 11, 13]. Our results also indicate increased ADNP expression in the male compared with female hippocampi [14], ADNP regulation of sex steroid enzyme transcription [12], and in-turn modulation by the estrous cycle further suggested sex-specificity [15]. Lastly, ADNP/NAP associate and regulate the aging-protecting protein, sirtuin1 (SIRT1) [16] and the autophagy and microbiome resilience-linked FOXO3 [4]. Furthermore, we have shown that davunetide (NAP) also enters the cell nucleus interacting with an ADNP zinc finger [17], protecting ADNP transcriptional dysregulation, with ADNP mutations and dysregulation associated with decreases in microtubule function/axonal transport in a sex-dependent manner [18].

The mechanism of action of davunetide (NAP, single aa letter code, NAPVSIPQ) is through its interaction with the cellular skeleton (cytoskeleton) with the major building blocks, microtubules, providing cell structure and function. As such, the NAPVSIPQ—SIP motif interacts with the microtubule end binding proteins (EB1 and EB3), enhancing ADNP-EB1/EB3 binding [19], Tau-EB1/EB3 and SIRT1/EB1/EB2-Tau-microtubule association [16], thus, protecting against tauopathy and brain aging [4]. NAP further enhances microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3) interaction with ADNP, thereby augmenting LC3-driven autophagosome formation and autophagy, in association with microtubule protection [20].

The NAPVSIP motif interacts with proteins containing SH3 domains essential for cytoskeletal function [13], augmenting the protection against tauopathy and enhancing synaptic organization. The entire NAPVSIPQ binds the armadillo domain of beta catenin important for WNT signaling critical for development [21, 22]. Thus, ADNP/NAP regulates AKT [4], with the Phosphatidylinositol 3-Kinase (PI3K)/AKT and WNT signaling pathways, converging on Glycogen Synthase Kinase 3β (GSK3β) and β-catenin [23], and with ADNP/NAP inhibiting Tau hyperphophorylation [24], tauopathy and neurodegeneration [1]. As testosterone further protects against Tau hyperphosphorylation through PI3K-Protein Kinase B-GSK3β signaling [25], we hypothesize that davunetide dosing and protection will be sex-dependent in all indications, paving the path to separating men and women while specifically tailoring medications against AD and related tauopathies. To prove our hypothesis, we reanalyzed here, an original clinical trial [26, 27] assessing cognitive skills in men and women suffering from amnestic mild cognitive impairment (aMCI) and exposed daily or twice daily to davunetide for 12 weeks.

Methods

Clinical trial reanalysis

As indicated in the Introduction, based on our recent post-hoc evaluation of a clinical trial assessing davunetide in PSP, including our discovery of sex-specific efficacy in women, we opted to follow a similar sex-specific analysis for another clinical trial performed on a population of elderly individuals exhibiting aMCI (ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00422981). The trial was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study, evaluating a 12-week-period of intranasal davunetide (AL-108, 5 mg once daily and 15 mg twice daily) compared to placebo in adults aged 55–85 years. The study was further extended to an additional 4-week follow-up, evaluating long-lasting effects. We originally published the trial highlights while performing analysis on a mixed male/female population [26]. The same sex-indiscriminate detailed analysis of the results of the trial, performed in 17 clinical research sites in the USA was later published [27]. The drug substance was synthesized by Bachem, and the drug product and matching placebo were manufactured by Patheon Inc. using Pfeiffer metered nasal spray pumps delivering 100 microL /spray (2.5 mg of davunetide or placebo/ spray). This placebo mimicked placebo used in preclinical studies [28]. As for davunetide, 5 mg was administered by 1 spray per nostril and 15 mg was 3 sprays per nostril. Ethics approval and consent to participate

All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. As indicated above, studies were conducted at 17 independent US clinical sites. For details on the study sites, please see ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00422981. All study documents were approved by the above-mentioned institutional review boards/independent ethics committees (IRB/IEC). As part of the inclusion criteria, all study participants provided informed consents. A 4-week screening period was included, and eligible subjects were randomized and assessed every 4 weeks (±5 days) at the investigational site. The drug was proven safe and well tolerated as emphasized in detail before [27].

Additional details enhancing the reproducibility and transparency of the study including participant selection criteria, randomization process, and detailed protocol for cognitive assessments were presented before [27] and further described below.

Participant selection criteria

Study participants met the following inclusion criteria: age between 55 and 85 years, presence of a self-reported memory complaint corroborated by spouse or companion, Logical Memory (LM) subtest of the Wechsler Memory Scale (WMS) WMS-III LM-II score ≤ 5 (as updated [29]), Mini-Mental State Exam [30] (MMSE) ≥ 24, Center for Epidemiologic Studies—Depression (CES-D) [31, 32] score <27. Subjects were required to have no obvious alternative cause for their memory complaint, agree to provide informed consent, and be fluent in English. Females could not be of child-bearing potential. Excluded subjects were those showing significant/untreated psychiatric illness or other neurological conditions that could interfere with the study evaluations/procedures. Subjects with deformities of the nasopharynx were also excluded apart from those who had a surgical correction for a deviated nasal septum.

Randomization

Patients were randomized to receive 5 mg once daily (q.d.) of davunetide or placebo in a 2:1 drug/placebo ratio (Table 1). After safety was established, a second arm was enrolled in which patients received 15 mg twice daily (b.i.d.) of davunetide or placebo (Table 1). Randomization was performed using permuted blocks (n = 6) with each subject receiving identical bottles identified only by kit number. The randomization sequence was generated by a contract research organization and subjects were assigned to treatment groups using an interactive response system (http://www.nextrials.com/).

Cognition was evaluated using four tests, as described below:

Delayed matching (match) to sample

The first cognitive evaluation, described herein, is composed of a test of delayed and recognition memory using simultaneous and delayed visual matching to sample (Cambridge Cognition—CANTAB [33]). In this test, the individual is exposed to a complex visual pattern on a computer screen including rapid exposures at 0-, 4- and 12 s delays, when four different patterns are displayed allowing the individual to select the pattern that matches the original visual pattern (sample). The respective percentages of correct choices (positive scores) are measured.

Digit span

The second test described herein was divided into the forward digit span task, a measure of general attention, and a backward digit span task, a measure of verbal working memory. The combined form is a widely used test of working memory and attention. In these tests, the individual is read number sequences of increasing length (while listening) and then asked to repeat the number, either forward or backward. The individual score is based on the number of sequences correctly repeated [34].

Cambridge cognition (CANTAB) spatial working memory

A more complex evaluation included the Cambridge Cognition spatial working memory [35], a self-ordered task that measures strategy (sensitive to the frontal lobe and executive dysfunction). In this test, several colored boxes are presented on the screen with one containing a hidden blue token. The test administrator responds to the first problem to demonstrate the correct solution. In short, the participant is instructed to touch the boxes in turn to search for the blue token that has been hidden. When the tested individual finds this blue token, it needs to be placed on the side of the screen. Another blue token is then hidden in another different color box and the tested individual needs to find it. After two practice problems are administered, each with three boxes, the test implemented consisted of two problems with four boxes, two problems with six boxes, and two problems with eight boxes. The key task instruction is that the computer will never hide a blue token in the same-colored box twice in the same problem. A higher error score represents a worse outcome.

Cambridge cognition (CANTAB) paired associates learning (PAL)

PAL measuring visuospatial associative learning and memory (reflecting medial temporal lobe functioning), was the most complex test used here [36]. Paired associates learning was suggested to predict dementia conversion at a later stage [37]. In short, in this test, boxes are displayed on the screen and open sequentially to reveal several patterns. After trying to remember which pattern belongs to each box, each pattern is randomly shown in the center of the screen, and the tested individual needs to identify the original box in which the pattern was located. The test begins with a practice session where six boxes open to reveal two patterns. The test then follows by sessions starting with two and gradually proceeding to eight patterns. At each session, the participant may have up to six attempts to respond correctly and proceed to the next session. Like in the spatial working memory test, the number of errors is counted, meaning that a higher score represents a worse outcome.

Anxiety assessments

The Spielberger State-Trait Anxiety Inventory [38] separately measures “state” and “trait” anxiety. Thus, two tests were implemented differentiating between the temporary condition of “state anxiety” and the more general and long-standing quality of “trait anxiety”. As the trial was during a relatively short period of time, the “state anxiety” test seems to be more appropriate, although both tests were implemented. It should be noted that these tests are sensitive to sex/gender and age [38].

Finally, as davunetide was applied intranasally, a smell test [39] was also implemented to complete the clinical picture, although smell measurements were limited for week 12 (trial termination) and week 16 (follow up).

Statistical analysis

Analyses using GraphPad Prism 10.2.3 software included D’Agostino and Pearson tests for normality coupled with log10 transformation to achieve normal distribution. Two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed post hoc by Tukey’s Honest Significant Difference (HSD) test was further used to assess significant differences. Unless otherwise stated, intercept differences between groups were calculated for significance. Pearson correlations were used to assess the impact of anxiety on the outcome of the cognitive tests.

Results

Differential davunetide effects on anxious men and women suffering from aMCI

Detailed subject characteristics were previously described while mixing males and females [27]. Here, results separating men and women are depicted for the first time. While baseline capabilities showed no difference (Fig. S1), further assessments below indicated a sex-specific significant impact on cognition for davunetide treatment encompassing dose-dependency in men, coupled with opposite sex-specific placebo effects on state anxiety, affecting cognitive outcomes in women, with davunetide also protecting against heightened anxiety.

Specifically, Fig. 1 assesses the percent change from baseline in the delayed match to sample test (12 s delay) described in the method section. Linear regression and Two-Way ANOVA plots comparing placebo and davunetide groups, plotted at weeks 0, 4, 8, 12, and 16. Fig. A1 and A2 male placebo group comparisons, showed individual variations (A1) and a trend for high dose placebo effect increasing with the duration of treatment (A2, #P = 0.055). The high-dose and low-dose placebo groups were further compared to the davunetide dosage groups. Results showed significant davunetide effects indicative of dose dependency, with high-dose groups performing significantly better from week 4 and onward (Fig. 1B). These results were verified in Fig. 1C1 comparing male placebo groups and davunetide 5 mg and showing no drug effect at the low dose, while male placebo groups and davunetide 15 mg showed a significant intercept difference (Fig. 1C2, *P < 0.03). A similar analysis was performed for women (Fig. 1D1–F2). Discussion

An original work shows that male and female brains are generally structured somewhat differently, with males exhibiting greater within-hemispheric connectivity facilitating connectivity between perception and coordinated action, whereas between-hemispheric connectivity predominates in females to facilitate communication between analytical and intuitive processing modes [42]. These findings agree with sex-differential drug effects in AD, with the newly approved amyloid antibody drug, leqembi, being apparently more efficacious in men than women [43]. Similarly, safinamide, an MAO-B inhibitor that extends the effects of levodopa and may help to slow the progress of Parkinson’s disease, was shown to differentially affect men and women, highlighting bodily discomfort and communication [44]. Further regarding Parkinson’s disease, female and male patients have distinct preoperative nonmotor and motor profiles, which should be considered for deep brain stimulation treatments [45]. These findings also link with our recently published results showing efficacy for 30 mg twice daily davunetide in women and not in men suffering from PSP [1] further highlighting davunetide neuroprotection against increased ventricular volumes in women over a year of clinical trial. In this respect, leqembi also increases brain ventricular volumes, indicative of a neurodegenerative process [46], and the idea of combination therapy, especially with potentially lower efficacy of leqembi in women is of interest.

Focusing on AD, a recent review stresses that sex and gender significantly impact the prevalence of protective and risk factors, influencing the burden of AD pathologies, which ultimately shape cognitive trajectories. The authors have adopted a multidisciplinary approach to provide foundations and recommendations for future research into sex- and gender-specific drivers of resilience, including risk factors, genetics, brain structure and function, and animal research, strongly advising researchers in the field to adopt a sex/gender-approach to resilience throughout disease stages [47]. An example of a biological process associated with resilience, or susceptibility is for example, sex-dependent S-nitrosylation [48].

Differentiation between men and women in AD neurodegeneration is not only in resilience and drug efficacy but also in biomarkers. For example, a recent study assessed plasma ceramides as potential markers for MCI in middle-aged men. Higher levels were associated with MCI in men, while no associations were found among women. In addition, higher levels of ceramide were associated with a larger volume of the hippocampus after controlling for covariates, independent of MCI [49]. Regarding ceramides, pituitary adenylate cyclase-activating polypeptide (PACAP) regulates ADNP, and its receptor PAC1 (Adcyap1r1) is controlled by ADNP/NAP, in a sex-dependent manner [50, 51]. Curiously, increased PACAP gene expression in the prefrontal cortex in schizophrenia was recently related to suicide, and an increase in all PACAP-related genes was present in schizophrenia male patients compared to schizophrenia females patients who died naturally [52]. These findings also correlate with ADNP/ADNP2 dysregulation in the schizophrenia male postmortem brain [53]. ADNP deficiency is associated with a sex-dependent reduced ability to cope with stress, which could be partially reversed by PACAP treatment [50]. Most importantly, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), a risk for AD [54] was linked to PACAP in a sex-dependent manner. For example, when genotyping for the ADCYAP1R1 CC rs2267735 variant in saliva, in girls, this variant genotype was associated with PTSD, while in boys, an opposite effect was apparent, with the variant genotype attenuating risk for PTSD. A further association between ADCYAP1R1 and arousal was suggested [55]. Similarly, in adults, higher somatic anxiety symptom severity in females but lower somatic anxiety symptom severity in males were discovered with the same PACAP variant [56].

Here, we have observed sex-dependent placebo effects on anxiety state in aMCI patients, with davunetide treatment offering protection, accentuated in women. Similarly, davunetide provided protection to female PSP patients against geriatric depression [1]. Protection by davunetide (NAP) against anxiety was also observed in the preclinical setting [28]. The association of Tau, sex and ADNP was recently reviewed [57], and now, we show, for the first time, differential anxiety, and cognitive effects of davunetide in aMCI patients. Despite the study limitations of a small cohort size (Table 1), we still detected significant sex-dose- efficacy in most study outcomes. We also discovered sex-specific placebo effects, suggesting that olfactory stimulation is an interesting target for cognitive enhancement [58], although we did not see differences in the smelling test. We did not use the composite cognitive memory score (CCMS), combining all tests described above and originally included in the study design [27], as our results indicated patient variations, extensive trial difficulty in the PAL test, for example, and possibly a cohort that is too small for such sophisticated analyses.

The differential sex-dependent effects on visual and verbal attention/memory found in our studies agree with findings implicating increased spatial memory in men with increased recognition and semantic memory in women [59, 60]. While baseline differences were not observed here, exercise was suggested to increase memory performance [59]. In our case, the tested individuals exercised using the specific tests, with the addition of differential nasal stimulation and davunetide efficacy further enhancing memory. From a mechanistic point of view, we have shown that vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), a peptide homolog to PACAP, originally discovered to regulate ADNP [3], is highly responsive to physical exercise. In the hippocampus of normal adult rats VIP expression is restricted to interneurons, however, when exposed to a 60-minute walk in an activity wheel, transient expression in most pyramidal and granular neurons of the hippocampus is detected. This, coupled with a dramatic increase in VIP immunoreactivity in the motor cortex, potentially influences learning and memory processes [61]. In this respect, in the rodent hypothalamus, VIP is regulated by estrogen and not by testosterone [62] and ADNP expression oscillates with the estrous cycle [15]. Furthermore, our mouse model studies showed immediate effects of davunetide (NAP) treatment on microtubule-dependent axonal transport [63] and sex-specific enhancing effects on visual evoked potential in males, correlated with protection against tauopathy [4].

Interestingly, as detailed in the Introduction, we directly connected tauopathy to ADNP in humans, revealing somatic mutations in ADNP in postmortem AD brains with augmented mutation frequency correlating with increased tauopathy [11], as well as extensive tauopathy in young ADNP-mutated deceased child brain postmortem analysis [12]. Indeed, ADNP is a lead gene that when mutated de novo drives the developmentally delayed autistic/intellectually disabled ADNP syndrome. Our most recent longitudinal assessment of communicative abilities in these ADNP syndrome children as well as young adults, highlights mutated protein length and structural characteristics correlation with communicative abilities that was accentuated in males, emphasizing ADNP/davunetide involvement in sex differences [64]. Similarly, other autism-related genes were found to be somatically mutated in the AD postmortem brain [11], and while autism is more prevalent in boys compared to girls and AD is more prevalent in women compared to men, both sexes should be evaluated [65]. This assertion is highly relevant in the case of ADNP and davunetide, showing marked sex differences [1, 4, 9, 18, 57].

Finally, the dose-dependent robust davunetide effect seen in men in the delayed matching to sample visual memory test, versus the attention and verbal memory test being performed better by davunetide-treated women, was correlated with the opposite anxiety effects differentiating the two sexes, with davunetide providing protection against anxiety [28]. In conclusion, davunetide, augmenting synaptic plasticity [66], is positioned here as a sex-specific memory enhancing and anxiolytic drug in prodromal AD. Coupled with long-term brain protective effects identified in women suffering from PSP [1], davunetide is proposed as a disease modifying investigational drug.

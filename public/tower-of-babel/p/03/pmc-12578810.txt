Millimeter-wave broadband dual-circularly polarized array antenna loaded with metasurface
Authors: Ting Wu, Zikang Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a millimeter-wave broadband dual circularly polarized (DCP) linear array antenna loaded with a linear to circular polarization converting metasurface. The feeding element employs a circular patch into which a symmetric cross-shaped slot is etched, dividing the patch into four electrically isolated, equal-area sectors. The original electric-field distribution is reconfigured by the proposed geometry, yielding simultaneous horizontal and vertical linear polarization. The linearly polarized electric field is converted to a dual circularly polarized electric field by the metasurface. Compactness and simplicity offer excellent scalability. A 1 × 4 array is fabricated and measured to verify the validity of the design. The measured results show good agreement with the simulation. Measured results show an impedance bandwidth (IBW) of 24.2% (26.5–33.8 GHz), an axial ratio (AR) bandwidth of 21.6% (27.2–33.8 GHz), and a gain of 13.4 dBic at the center frequency.Subject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

With the rapid development of 5G/6G communications, the millimeter-wave band (30–300 GHz) has become a main choice for next-generation wireless systems, due to ultra-wide bandwidth, high-data-rate transmission, and high spatial resolution1–4. Circular polarization antennas possess a distinct edge when it comes to addressing issues arising from multipath propagation and polarization mismatch3. Therefore, they have been widely studied. Left-handed circular polarizations and right-handed circular polarizations enhance spectral efficiency, enabling bidirectional communication and fulfilling the requirements of MIMO systems5–9. The artificial electromagnetic structures can flexibly control the amplitude, phase, polarization, and propagation modes of electromagnetic waves, offering new solutions for antenna design10–12. Metasurfaces can achieve miniaturization, broadband operation, high gain, and low profile of antennas and have been widely applied to enhance the performance of millimeter-wave antennas13–16. Researchers have conducted extensive studies on millimeter-wave circularly polarized antennas based on microstrip patches, substrate integrated waveguides (SIW), and slot structures17–19. A circularly polarized antenna with a circular patch radiator is introduced in20. The radiator’s metasurface (MTS) characteristics are enhanced by integrating multiple split-ring slots. The isolation between radiating elements is 17 dB, which causes signal interference in MIMO systems. Although the antenna achieves bidirectional radiation and polarization conversion, the band stealth performance is narrow. A practical design is presented in21 to achieve bidirectional radiation and polarization conversion in Fabry-Perot resonant antennas. However, the bandwidth only covers a narrow band around 25 GHz and cannot support the core frequency band of 5G millimeter waves.

A broadband millimeter-wave Ka-band array antenna with DCP based on metasurfaces is presented in this paper. First, a broadband dual-linearly-polarized antenna element is designed. Second, a 1 × 4 array is constructed, and the element layout is optimized to reduce mutual coupling. Finally, a linear to circular polarization converting metasurface is loaded, combined with a gradient impedance matching and an orthogonal feeding network to achieve broadband DCP. The array antenna, with broadband, wide ARBW, and low profile, fulfills the requirements of millimeter wave communication and radar systems.

Dual linear polarization unit design

The configuration of the dual linearly polarized electric field generation structure is shown in Fig. 1. The structure consists of a four-layer stacked printed circuit board. The substrates have a relative permittivity of ℇr = 2.2, and their thicknesses are 0.508 mm, 0.508 mm, 0.254 mm, and 0.760 mm, respectively, from the bottom layer to the top layer.A circular patch on the top surface of substrate Ⅰ is divided into four equal, disconnected segments by two perpendicular slots that intersect to form a cross-slot structure. The longitudinal slot on the patch modifies the E-field distribution of the TM20 mode to produce a broadside radiation pattern, with negligible influence on the E-field distribution of the TM10 mode.Similarly, the other orthogonal linear polarization is achieved using the TM02 and TM01 modes. The four layers are assembled together by bonding them with three layers of Rogers 4450 bonding film, which are placed between substrate Ⅰ, substrate Ⅱ, substrate Ⅲ, and substrate Ⅳ.

Ground serves as the energy coupling interface between the feeding network and the top-layer radiating patch, with the etched cross-shaped slot acting as the key channel for energy transfer. The signals transmitted by the lower-layer SICL feeding network (feeding I/II) form an alternating electromagnetic field beneath Ground I. Through the field penetration effect of the slot, energy is coupled to the top-layer radiating patch, exciting the current distribution on the patch surface and ultimately converting it into spatial electromagnetic wave radiation. The cross-shaped slot, with its tapered edges, reduces impedance discontinuities to broaden the coupling bandwidth. Its size regulates the coupling strength, ensuring impedance matching between the radiating element and the feeding network. As a metallic conductive layer, Ground I reflects the reverse electromagnetic field generated by the radiating patch and blocks stray electromagnetic fields from the lower-layer feeding network from interfering with the radiating element, forming an isolation barrier between the radiation and the feeding field to minimize gain loss or pattern distortion caused by electromagnetic crosstalk.

Ground I, in conjunction with the lower-layer Ground III, constructs a closed metallic cavity for feeding II (horizontal polarization feeding). Feeding II is enclosed within the space formed by Ground II, Ground III, and the surrounding metal vias, constituting a substrate-integrated coaxial line (SICL) structure. This enclosed environment confines the electromagnetic waves around the feeding line, preventing signal loss due to radiation or stray coupling during transmission.

Furthermore, Ground II physically separates feeding I and feeding II. The electromagnetic shielding effect of the metal layer blocks direct electromagnetic coupling between the two orthogonal feeding networks. Combined with the lateral isolation provided by the via array, this design increases the isolation between the horizontal and vertical polarization ports to over 33 dB, ensuring independent transmission of dual-polarization signals. The top views of the Ground I/II structures are shown in Fig. 2(b) and (c).

Feeding I and II leverage the quasi-TEM modal transmission characteristics of a substrate integrated metal strip to enable low-loss millimeter-wave signal transmission. Their characteristic impedance is determined jointly by the strip width, thickness, and the substrate dielectric constant. The structure is shown in Fig. 3. Plated-through vias or coaxial probe posts penetrate the dielectric substrate vertically, providing signal transition from planar microstrip lines to different layers and elements.

Design of linear-circular polarization converter

Figure 6 depicts a metasurface that leverages the electromagnetic anisotropy of a meander-line grating to achieve differential phase control over the orthogonal components of an incident linearly polarized wave, ultimately synthesizing a circularly polarized wave. The horizontal electric-field component, aligned with the meander-line axis, experiences a phase lag due to the inductive nature of the structure, whereas the vertical electric field component, perpendicular to the axis, undergoes a phase advance induced by the structure of capacitive behavior. This phase disparity gives rise to a differential phase shift, which is the key mechanism enabling linear-to-circular polarization conversion. The equivalent circuit diagram is shown in Fig. 6(c).

While a four-layer grid slightly improves the S-parameters relative to the three layers, its ARBW is inferior. Consequently, the final antenna employs a three-layer polarizing grid.

Measured results and discussion

To validate the design of the antenna, a prototype is manufactured according to the simulation model and is depicted in Fig. 13. The dimensions of the antenna are 2.4λ × 0.6λ × 0.25λ. The simulated and measured results are illustrated in Fig. 14. The results indicate that the curves essentially coincide, with an impedance bandwidth of S11< −10 dB achieving 24.2% (26.5–33.8 GHz) and bandwidth of AR < 3 dB reaching 21.6% (27.2–33.8 GHz).

Fabricated prototype of the proposed antenna.

Simulated and measured S-parameters, AR and gains of the proposed antenna.

The analysis of the results has verified that the structure can achieve dual circular polarization. However, there are significant differences between the simulated and measured S-parameter curves and axial ratio curves. The main reasons are the different feed structures and the mutual coupling effect between the elements. Since the array adopts a 1 × 4 horizontal configuration with four elements, mutual coupling between adjacent elements and fabrication tolerances (e.g., deviations in substrate thickness or metal pattern dimensions) collectively lead to the observed discrepancies.

For the simulation-internal inconsistency (between S11 and S22), it arises because electrical signals input from Port Ⅰ and Port Ⅱ induce different electric fields on the element surface, leading to varying mutual coupling effects between the elements. For the discrepancies between simulation and measurement, the primary factors are the additional mutual coupling caused by the array layout and fabrication tolerances. These two factors collectively lead to the differences between the measured and simulated results.

Conclusion

An aperture-coupled broadband DCP antenna has been introduced. The antenna features two orthogonal slots etched onto a circular patch. Dual circular polarization is realized by the metasurfaces. The antenna achieves a wide impedance bandwidth of 24.2% and an AR and width of 21.6%. Compared to similar designs, the antenna demonstrates significant performance improvements. The close match between the measured and simulated results confirms the effectiveness of the proposed design.

Robotic versus laparoscopic approaches to neonatal annular pancreas: a comparative analysis of surgical outcomes and innovation trade-offs
Authors: Yuanzhu Gan, Zebing Zheng, Huijuan Wang, Yu Liao, Zeping Li, Chengyan Tang, Daiwei Zhu, Xingrong Xia, Lu Huang, Qing Du, Wankang Zhou, Yuan Gong, Xilin Liao, Yuanmei Liu, Zhu Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the safety and to efficacy of robotic-assisted duodenoduodenostomy (RAD) and laparoscopic-assisted duodenoduodenostomy (LAD) for neonatal annular pancreas and analyze its associated learning curve.MethodsA retrospective analysis of 59 neonates undergoing minimally invasive DS (2019–2024) was performed. Patients were stratified into robotic (RAD, n = 25) and laparoscopic (LAD, n = 34) cohorts. Perioperative outcomes (operative time, anastomosis time, blood loss, drainage duration, complications) were compared. Learning curves for console docking and intracorporeal manipulation were assessed using cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis.ResultsRAD demonstrated significantly shorter anastomosis time (35.6 ± 2.4 min vs. 47.0 ± 4.1 min, P < 0.05), reduced blood loss (median 4.0 ml vs. 6.0 ml, P < 0.05), and earlier drain removal (median 4.0 vs. 5.0 days, P < 0.05) compared to LAD. operative time was longer for RAD (179.3 ± 21.1 min vs. 156.9 ± 23.1 min, P < 0.05), attributed to docking time (22.2 ± 5.9 min) and the learning curve. Complication rates were comparable (16.0% vs. 26.5%, P = 0.537), with no anastomotic leaks, strictures or reoperations in either group. CUSUM analysis identified proficiency thresholds: docking time plateaued at case 13, and intracorporeal manipulation stabilized after 10 cases. Preliminary analysis within the first 20 cases suggests a 20-case threshold may be critical procedural mastery.ConclusionRAD is a safe and effective approach for neonatal annular pancreas, demonstrating superior precision to laparoscopy. While requiring a longer initial operative time, RAD provides significant advantages in anastomotic efficiency and hemostasis once the learning curve is overcome.

Annular pancreas (AP) is a congenital gastrointestinal malformation with an estimated incidence of approximately 3.4 per 100,000 live births [1]. The defining pathological feature is characterized by an anomalous annular band of pancreatic tissue encircling the descending duodenum, leading to induce luminal narrowing or obstruction, the condition typically manifesting clinically as persistent vomiting [2]. Notably, more than 40% of cases are associated with duodenal atresia or other forms of complete duodenal obstruction, which significantly increases perinatal morbidity and mortality risk [3].Prenatal ultrasonography findings of polyhydramnios, combined with a hyperechoic pancreatic tissue ring surrounding the duodenum should raise strong suspicion for AP [4]. In neonates with RAD biologically confirmed duodenal obstruction (as evidenced by upper gastrointestinal contrast studies), early surgical intervention is critical to optimize outcomes. Although robotic-assisted surgery has demonstrated procedural safety in complex pediatric abdominal interventions (e.g., biliary atresia and Hirschsprung disease), its application in neonatal AP management remains under-investigated [5, 6].

This study aims to systematically evaluate the technical feasibility and clinical outcomes of using the robotic system for the surgical management of these complex neonatal malformations.By performing comparative analysis of perioperative outcomes between robotic-assisted and laparoscopic DS, we intend to identify elucidate the potential advantages of robotic approaches in these delicate procedures.

Materials and methods

Materials

Clinical data

The medical records of 25 neonates who underwent RAD and 34 neonates who underwent LAD between January 2019 and December 2024 were enrolled in this retrospective study, which was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University (Approval No. KLL-2024-691), with written informed consent obtained from all legal guardians.

Clinical data were collected from patients diagnosed with annular pancreas who underwent da Vinci robotic-assisted or laparoscopic diamond-shaped duodenal anastomosis between January 2019 and December 2024.

Inclusion criteria: Duodenal obstruction was diagnosed; intraoperatively, AP was identified and DS was performed.

Exclusion criteria: Patients with concomitant gastrointestinal anomalies (e.g., intestinal malrotation, imperforate anus, esophageal atresia, intestinal atresia, ectopic pancreas, or Meckel’s diverticulum).

Following detailed counseling on RAD and LAD for neonatal duodenal obstruction, families retain final decision-making authority. RAD offers improved 3D visualization and enhanced instrument articulation, potentially increasing precision for intricate suturing in the confined neonatal abdomen while minimizing tremor. Disadvantages include significantly higher costs, potentially prolonged operative times (especially during the learning curve), and limited working space challenges in smaller neonates. Surgeons assist families in weighing RAD's technical advantages against these practical constraints for collaborative decision-making.

The following parameters were collected data on infant gender, corrected gestational age, body weight, clinical manifestations, total comorbidities, operative duration, duodenal anastomosis time, intraoperative blood loss, hospitalization costs, and postoperative complications. Postoperative recovery outcomes, including time to enteral nutrition, time to full oral feeding, drainage tube removal time, and Postoperative hospital stay along with follow-up data (complications, reoperation/readmission rates, and mortality) were systematically analyzed. Follow-up evaluations were conducted every 3–6 months via outpatient clinics or telemedicine consultations.

Methods

Operational protocol for RAD group

The RAD procedure was performed using the Da Vinci Xi™ surgical system (Intuitive Surgical, Inc. USA). Following standard general anesthesia induction and gastric decompression via nasogastric tube, the patient was positioned supine. A 0.8-cm vertical incision was created at the umbilical crease, through which a Veress needle was inserted to establish CO₂ pneumoperitoneum (maintained at 6–8 mmHg). A trocar sleeve was inserted for laparoscopic visualization. Subsequent trocar sleeves were placed under direct vision in the left and right abdominal quadrants as working ports, with an additional 3-mm trocar inserted in the right lower quadrant as an auxiliary port (Fig. 1). The robotic arms were docked and the robotic surgical system was activated. ICG (0.2 mg/kg) was intravenously administered to assess tissue perfusion. (Intraoperative exploration identified complete encirclement of the second part of duodenum by pink pancreatic tissue, with marked proximal duodenal dilation, thickened intestinal walls, and a narrow distal bowel, confirming the diagnosis of AP. A robotic electrocautery hook was used to incise the lateral peritoneal reflection of the duodenum, fully mobilizing the proximal and distal segments of the descending duodenum. A 1.2-cm transverse incision was made 0.5 cm proximal to the pancreatic margin on the dilated proximal duodenum, and a 1.2-cm longitudinal incision was created distal to the pancreatic margin. Using 5-0 absorbable sutures, continuous suturing was performed from the left apex of the transverse incision to the left endpoint of the longitudinal incision to approximate the posterior wall. The gastric tube was advanced through the gastric cavity and positioned in the proximal jejunum via the anastomosis. The remaining intestinal wall was sutured from the left side to complete the anterior wall closure. A drainage tube was placed posterior to the anastomotic site and exteriorized through the right trocar site. Following the procedure, local infiltration with ropivacaine (2 mg/kg) was routinely administered for postoperative analgesia.Fig. 1Surgical procedure diagram for robotic duodenal rhomboid anastomosis in children with AP

Operational protocol for LAD group

Following successful induction of general anesthesia, the patient was positioned supine with routine nasogastric tube placement. A three-port minimally invasive technique was employed: a 3 mm trocar was initially inserted through a 0.3 cm incision at the left umbilical margin for optical access, followed by CO₂ insufflation to establish pneumoperitoneum (pressure maintained at 6–8 mmHg). Two additional 3 mm operative trocars were placed in the right mid-abdominal and right upper abdominal regions under direct visualization (Fig. 2). Intraoperative exploration demonstrated complete circumferential encasement of the second part of duodenum by pancreatic tissue, with characteristic findings of proximal duodenal dilation, thickened intestinal walls, and distal luminal narrowing, confirming AP diagnosis. Subsequent anastomotic procedures mirrored those described in the robotic-assisted approach, including duodenal mobilization, creation of transverse and longitudinal incisions, continuous 5-0 absorbable suture anastomosis, nasogastric tube placement across the anastomosis, and posterior drainage tube insertion. Learning curve and prediction model monitoring

To assess the learning curve for intracorporeal manipulation time and console time in the RAD group as surgical experience accumulated (sequentially by case number), and to monitor performance trends of prediction models (for length of stay, duodenal anastomosis time, and intracorporeal manipulation time), this study employed the cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis method [7]. The CUSUM technique effectively identifies subtle learning effects or systematic deviations/unanticipated trends in model predictive performance arising during the procedural sequence by calculating the cumulative deviation of the actual intracorporeal manipulation/console times from their target values (model-predicted values) across consecutive cases-trends often undetectable by standard analytical methods. To control for confounding factors (e.g., neonatal age, weight, hyperbilirubinemia, prematurity) and mitigate the risk of selection bias, relevant covariates were incorporated during the study design phase. These factors were subsequently adjusted for in the analysis using stratification, matching, or multivariate modeling. The learning curve was visualized by plotting the CUSUM values against the sequence of surgical cases.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 29.0 software. Continuous variables conforming to normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared between groups using independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range, Q1–Q3) and analyzed with Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical data were described using frequencies and proportions, with between-group comparisons conducted via χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Ordinal variables were compared using nonparametric rank-sum test. A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Learning curve

Analysis of the robotic surgical procedures revealed distinct learning curve patterns. Intra-abdominal time and docking time decreased significantly with case accumulation (r = − 0.931, P < 0.001; r = − 0.979, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A, B). Multivariate regression analysis

By incorporating and adjusting for potential confounding variables—including neonatal age, body weight, hyperbilirubinemia, and prematurity—within the multivariate predictive model, the extreme values (both maximum and minimum) of the model residuals converged toward zero compared to a simple regression model considering only the independent and dependent variables (e.g., intra-abdominal operation time). This demonstrates that adjusting for these confounders effectively reduces the risk of systematic bias in model estimates, yielding predictions that more accurately reflect the true underlying relationship and diminishing the occurrence of extreme prediction errors arising from the omission of critical influencing factors (Fig. 6A–F).Fig. 6A–F The impact of confounding variables (including neonatal age, birth weight, hyperbilirubinemia, and prematurity) on intraoperative time, intestinal anastomosis time, and length of hospital stay was evaluated.

Perioperative management

A standardized prophylactic antibiotic protocol was implemented, comprising a single preoperative intravenous cefazolin bolus (50 mg/kg), followed by postoperative intravenous cefotaxime (50 mg/kg) for 6–10 days. Enteral nutrition initiation commenced within 24–48 h postoperatively via nasogastric tube, delivering minimal trophic feeds (0.5–1 ml/kg/hour) utilizing breast milk or extensively hydrolyzed formula. Feed volumes were advanced incrementally at 20–30% increments every 12–24 h, targeting full enteral nutrition (FEN; 100% nutritional requirements) within 5–7 days. Neonates with birth weight < 2.5 kg or significant medical complexity were managed under a modified protocol targeting ≥ 70% goal enteral intake by postoperative day 7.Concurrently with trophic feeding initiation, structured oral water trials (5 ml/feed, 5 times daily) were instituted. Upon FEN achievement, nasogastric supplementation was systematically weaned to transition to exclusive oral feeding—defined as sustained oral intake meeting 100% nutritional requirements post-tube discontinuation. Acid suppression therapy (cimetidine) was administered throughout this transition and discontinued upon confirmed sustained oral nutritional autonomy.

Definition

Operative time was defined as the interval from the attending surgeon's initial positioning at the operating table until completion of wound closure. Time to full oral feeding was defined as the successful oral intake of the prescribed daily milk volume without requiring tube feeding and without experiencing adverse reactions during feeding that did not resolve spontaneously, sustained for over 48 h. Postoperative hospital stay was defined as the duration from surgery completion until meeting discharge criteria, including being clinically stable, tolerating a liquid diet, and demonstrating satisfactory wound healing without signs of infection (e.g., erythema, swelling, heat, or pain).

Discussion

Annular pancreas (AP), a congenital anomaly requiring surgery, is optimally managed by the Kimura duodenoduodenostomy (DS) technique [8]. Established as the gold standard for congenital duodenal obstruction, DS offers anatomical alignment, intestinal continuity preservation, and a favorable complication profile [9]. Minimally invasive DS, validated since 2001, provides established benefits over open surgery [10]. While robotic surgery demonstrates safety in complex pediatric procedures, its application for neonatal AP and in high-risk infants (< 6 months, < 3 kg) remains under-investigated and controversial [11, 12]. This report details our robotic technique for AP in newborns (< 30 days).

The operative time in the robot-assisted group was significantly longer than that in the laparoscopic control group (Robot-assisted: 179.3 ± 21.1 min; Laparoscopic control: 156.9 ± 23.1 min; P < 0.05), with the console docking phase accounting for an additional 22.2 ± 5.9 min. This difference reflects the anatomical constraints of neonates and a significant learning curve effect. Learning curve analysis using the cumulative sum (CUSUM) method revealed a non-linear progression pattern: Intra-abdominal operation time demonstrated a significant improvement in operative fluency within the first 10 cases, while proficiency in console docking was achieved by the 13th case, followed by skill consolidation over the subsequent 15 cases. These findings confirm that operative time, particularly intra-abdominal operation time, is a key metric for measuring the learning curve [7, 13]. The curve morphology is primarily influenced by three factors: the surgeon’s prior experience (> 50 laparoscopic procedures); procedure frequency (≥ 3 times per week); and anatomical complexity. Preliminary analysis of the first 20 cases suggests that a threshold of 20 cases may be crucial for achieving safe and proficient operation. Accordingly, we propose: (1) Intensive phase: Complete ≥ 10 cases intensively within 2 weeks; (2) Consolidation phase: Complete 15 cases over 4–6 weeks. Future studies should focus on validating the proposed “docking proficiency threshold” (13 cases) and investigating the mechanistic impact of cognitive factors (e.g., three-dimensional spatial perception) on learning curve morphology.

Robotic-assisted surgery provides significant advantages for neonatal annular pancreas (AP), characterized by abnormal proximal duodenal muscular thickening (2.0–3.0 mm versus distal thickness of ~ 1 mm) [14]. Given the inherent fragility of the neonatal intestinal wall, traditional laparoscopic anastomosis carries substantial tissue tear risk when suture tension exceeds 0.5 N. Conventional laparoscopy is further limited by two-dimensional visualization (lacking stereoscopic depth) and restricted instrument articulation, prolonging anastomotic time. These limitations are addressed by the da Vinci® robotic system through multiple technological innovations: (1) EndoWrist® instruments with 540° articulation enable dexterous suturing; (2) Integrated three-dimensional imaging with motion scaling facilitates microsurgical precision; (3) Tremor filtration minimizes operator-induced error; (4) Real-time synchronization with automated collision-avoidance enhances safety; (5) Ergonomic console positioning reduces surgeon fatigue [15]; (6) Intraoperative indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence allows real-time obstruction localization.

Comparative studies confirm robotic approaches yield shorter anastomotic times, reduced intraoperative blood loss, and decreased postoperative drainage duration versus laparoscopy. This superiority is mechanistically attributed to 10× optical magnification combined with 1080P stereoscopic visualization for microvascular clarity, augmented by ICG fluorescence with vascular injury alerts enabling precise dissection while minimizing iatrogenic trauma. Prior investigations further document lower postoperative inflammatory markers (specifically IL-6 and CRP) and reduced exudation in robotic cohorts, correlating with diminished anastomotic leakage and intra-abdominal infection risks [11, 12].

Despite growing pediatric applications of indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence imaging [16], its systematic implementation in neonatal annular pancreas (AP) procedures remains inadequately described. This study standardized ICG administration in all infants undergoing robotic-assisted AP repair. Pre-anastomosis, ICG solution (0.1 mg/kg; maximum ≤ 2.5 mg) was delivered via intraluminal catheter. Given annular pancreas pathology—where pancreatic tissue encircles and integrates into the duodenal muscularis—this technique provided intraoperative navigation through dual mechanisms: (1) Fluorescence accumulation within the dilated proximal bowel segment served as an obstruction site marker; (2) Dynamic visualization of ICG penetration boundaries into the distal bowel enabled quantitative assessment of obstruction extent and severity.

This study demonstrated the feasibility of robotic-assisted surgery through successful procedures in 25 infants with low birth weight (minimum 2.9 kg) and early postnatal age (youngest: 2 days), with no conversions to open laparotomy. These outcomes indicate that robotic-assisted surgery represents a viable approach for rigorously selected annular pancreas cases—excluding severe congenital anomalies, requiring hemodynamic stability, and tolerance to pneumoperitoneum and anesthesia—when performed by specialized robotic surgical teams. Additionally, three cases (12.0%) experienced instrument arm collisions due to restricted neonatal working space, necessitating intraoperative trocar repositioning. To address pediatric anatomical constraints, we overcame inherent compatibility limitations of standard robotic systems by modifying trocar placement (reducing inter-port spacing from 8 to 4 cm).

Furthermore, this study developed a multivariable predictive model incorporating potential confounding variables (e.g., neonatal age, weight, hyperbilirubinemia, prematurity). Compared to ordinary least squares regression, the multivariable model demonstrated significantly reduced magnitude and range of residuals (P < 0.01), indicating effective mitigation of unmeasured confounding and enhanced model robustness.Notably, the following limitations warrant consideration: Nevertheless, our study has limitations: (1) As a single-center retrospective case series, data collection and processing may contain inherent biases that could influence statistical outcomes. (2) The higher cost of robotic surgery may introduce selection bias, as patients with better economic resources might preferentially opt for robotic procedures. To address this selection bias, our institution implemented a Congenital Malformation Assistance Program, providing partial subsidies for eligible patients demonstrating financial hardship, thereby enhancing equitable access to the technology.

Conclusion

RAD is a safe and effective approach for neonatal acute pancreatitis, demonstrating superior precision to laparoscopy. While requiring a longer initial operative time, RAD provides significant advantages in anastomotic efficiency and hemostasis once the learning curve is overcome.

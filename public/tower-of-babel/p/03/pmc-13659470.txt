Peripartum ALT flares predict earlier postpartum HBeAg clearance in highly viremic HBV-infected women: A long-term follow-up study
Authors: Wan-Hsin Wen, Chun-Jen Liu, Ming-Wei Lai, Ming-Chieh Tsai, Shu-Chi Mu, Chyi-Long Lee, Shao-Wen Cheng, Jenn-Jeih Hsu, Yu-Cheng Lin, Jen-Jan Hu, Chien-Ting Hsu, Ming-Wei Lin, Yao-Jong Yang, Kuang-Chun Hu, Rong-Nan Chien, Sung-Chu Ko, Yu-Chun Chen, Jin-Chung Shih, Chien-Nan Lee, Huey-Ling Chen, Mei-Hwei Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsCurrent guidelines recommend antiviral therapy for pregnant women with chronic HBV infection and high viral load to prevent mother-to-child transmission (MTCT), but data on long-term maternal outcomes remain limited.MethodsWe analyzed 293 HBeAg-positive pregnant women with high viral load (>200,000 IU/ml) enrolled in previous trials of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate or tenofovir alafenamide for MTCT prevention. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels were serially monitored from late pregnancy through 6 months postpartum. Peripartum ALT trajectories (flare vs. stable) were identified using previously validated group-based trajectory modeling. After 6 months postpartum, follow-up and initiation of antiviral therapy were guided by national reimbursement criteria and shared decision-making as part of routine clinical care. Cox proportional hazards regression, adjusted for multiple covariates, was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs for postpartum HBeAg clearance.ResultsAmong 204 women who received antiviral therapy during pregnancy and 89 who did not, 50 achieved HBeAg clearance over a median postpartum follow-up of 27.1 months (range 0.03–147.8), corresponding to an incidence of 5.03 per 100 person-years. No adverse hepatic outcomes were observed. A flare-type peripartum ALT trajectory was a strong independent predictor of earlier HBeAg clearance (HR 10.37; 95% CI 5.44–19.76; p <0.001), independent of age, HBV genotype, baseline or delivery viral load, postpartum antiviral therapy, and subsequent pregnancies. Cumulative HBeAg clearance rates were significantly higher in the flare group (n = 43) than in the stable group (n = 250) at 12, 24, and 60 months (2.3%, 25.3%, and 85.9% vs. 0.4%, 3.9%, and 11.9%, respectively).ConclusionsPeripartum ALT flares are strongly associated with accelerated HBeAg clearance. Longitudinal ALT monitoring around childbirth provides a critical window to guide individualized postpartum treatment strategies.Impact and implicationsThis study provides long-term follow-up data in highly viremic women receiving antiviral therapy during pregnancy to prevent mother-to-child transmission, with a focus on postpartum HBeAg clearance. Peripartum ALT flares are associated with significantly earlier HBeAg clearance, suggesting ALT monitoring around delivery to inform postpartum management. Our findings also demonstrate favorable treatment experiences among HBV-infected women, a population in which treatment uptake remains suboptimal. Beyond the prevention of mother-to-child transmission, these results support broader implementation of antiviral therapy to improve care for HBV-infected women and contribute to global hepatitis elimination efforts.Clinical trial numbersNCT 01312012, NCT 03695029.

Introduction

HBV infection remains a major global health problem, affecting an estimated 254 million people worldwide.1 Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) is the predominant route of HBV transmission.2 Although hepatitis B immunoglobulin and vaccination prevent 90–95% of MTCT, infants born to highly viremic mothers remain at risk of infection.[3], [4], [5]

International guidelines therefore recommend antiviral prophylaxis during pregnancy for women with HBV DNA ≥200,000 IU/ml or positive hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg).2,6,7 Despite established MTCT prevention efficacy, data on long-term maternal outcomes remain limited.[8], [9], [10], [11], [12]

Pregnancy-related immune modulation and postpartum immune reconstitution influence the course of chronic hepatitis B.[13], [14], [15] However, the association between postpartum aminotransferase (ALT) flares and HBeAg clearance remains inconclusive.6 As antiviral therapy during pregnancy becomes increasingly common, long-term maternal outcomes are not well defined. Using a prospectively followed cohort, this study evaluates long-term outcomes, with a primary focus on postpartum HBeAg clearance, in highly viremic HBV-infected women treated during pregnancy to prevent MTCT.

Patients and methods

Study cohorts and MTCT prevention trials

Since 2011, highly viremic HBV-infected pregnant women identified through Taiwan’s universal first-trimester screening (hepatitis B surface antigen [HBsAg] and HBeAg positive) were recruited for MTCT prevention trials. HBV DNA enrollment thresholds were: ≥7.5 log10 IU/ml (2011–2015), ≥6 log10 IU/ml (2016–2021), and ≥200,000 IU/ml (2022–present). Group allocation (treated during pregnancy vs. untreated) followed patient preference. Exclusion criteria included baseline ALT >5x the upper limit of normal (ULN), hepatitis C or HIV co-infection, cirrhosis, malignancy, systemic disease, or fetal anomalies.8,11,16,17

The treated during pregnancy group received tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (300 mg/day, 2011–2018; NCT01312012) or tenofovir alafenamide (25 mg/day, 2019–present; NCT03695029) from the third trimester until 2–4 weeks postpartum. ALT and HBV DNA were monitored at baseline (D0), 1-month post-baseline (D1M), delivery (P0), and months 1, 2, 4, and 6 postpartum (P1M–P6M). Quantitative HBsAg (qHBsAg) data were available for a subset of participants: 86 women treated during pregnancy (baseline and delivery) and 82 untreated women at baseline (85 at delivery).

Flare vs. stable peripartum ALT trajectory

Using a previously validated group-based model, patterns of ALT trajectories (ALT at D0, D1M, P0, P1M, P2M, P4M and P6M) were mapped.11 For the current analysis, we consolidated our original four-pattern model into a binary classification (flare vs. stable), as the stable pattern predominated (85.3% of the cohort).

Postpartum follow-up

After 6 months postpartum, women from the prior MTCT trials were enrolled for long-term follow-up under standard chronic hepatitis B care. Routine outpatient visits occurred every 6–12 months and included ALT and HBeAg testing, alongside cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance via abdominal ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein testing. Postpartum antiviral initiation was primarily guided by Taiwan's National Health Insurance reimbursement criteria, complemented by shared decision-making.

The primary outcome, HBeAg clearance, was defined as the first documented negative result and measured as time from delivery. For this analysis, participants were censored at the time of HBeAg clearance but continued follow-up for other outcomes. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Boards.

Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed using Stata (version 14.0; Stata Corporation). A two-sided p value ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant. Group comparisons were conducted using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables and the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

HBeAg clearance was assessed using two methods. First, incidence rates were calculated as events per 100 person-years of follow-up from delivery. Second, the cumulative probability of HBeAg clearance was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with the log-rank test to compare differences.

Predictors of HBeAg clearance were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression. Due to high multicollinearity (variance inflation factor >10) between HBV DNA at delivery and pregnancy treatment, HBV DNA was prioritized over treatment based on model-fit diagnostics. Other covariates included age, HBV genotype, baseline HBV DNA, peripartum ALT trajectory, postpartum antiviral therapy, and subsequent delivery. A secondary sensitivity analysis was performed on the subset with available qHBsAg data.

Postpartum HBeAg clearance

Over a median follow-up of 27.1 months (range, 0.03–147.8), 50 women (17.1%) cleared HBeAg at a median of 35.3 months postpartum, representing an incidence of 5.03 per 100 person-years. HBeAg clearance rates (15.7% vs. 20.2%, p = 0.342) and follow-up durations were comparable between treated during pregnancy and untreated groups (Table 1). Among the 50 women who achieved HBeAg clearance, 46 (92%) developed anti-HBe positivity, either at the time of HBeAg loss (68%) or during subsequent follow-up (24%). Among women who achieved HBeAg clearance, the median HBV DNA level at the last follow-up was 2.5 log10 IU/ml (range 0–5.4). Additionally, over 1,097.3 person-years of follow-up, no participants developed cirrhosis, hepatic decompensation, or hepatocellular carcinoma.

Kaplan-Meier analysis estimated the probability of remaining HBeAg-positive at 12, 24, and 60 months postpartum to be 99.3%, 92.9%, and 74.1%, respectively. Predictors of HBeAg clearance

In the primary cohort (n = 286 after excluding 7 cases lacking HBV genotype data) a flare-type peripartum ALT trajectory was identified as the only independent predictor of HBeAg clearance (hazard ratio [HR] 10.37; 95% CI 5.44–19.76; p <0.001) after multivariable adjustment. Lower baseline HBV DNA showed a borderline association with HBeAg clearance (HR 0.52; 95% CI 0.27–1.01; p = 0.054). In the sensitivity analysis of the qHBsAg subset (n = 165), lower baseline qHBsAg showed a marginal association with clearance (HR 0.46; 95% CI 0.18–1.15; p = 0.097), while delivery qHBsAg did not (HR 0.65; p = 0.350). Notably, the predictive power of the flare-type ALT trajectory remained robust within the subset (HR 13.09; 95% CI 5.05–33.95; p <0.001). Detailed results are provided in Table S1.

Peripartum ALT trajectory and HBeAg clearance

Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that women with a flare trajectory (n = 43) cleared HBeAg significantly earlier than those with a stable trajectory (n = 250; log-rank p <0.001), consistent with multivariate findings. At 12, 24, and 60 months postpartum, cumulative HBeAg clearance rates were 2.3%, 25.3%, and 85.9% in the flare group, compared to 0.4%, 3.9%, and 11.9% in the stable group (Fig. 1B).

Discussion

While current guidelines emphasize antiviral prophylaxis for highly viremic pregnant women to prevent MTCT, long-term maternal outcomes remain insufficiently characterized due to small cohorts and short follow-up.9,18,19 In this prospective longitudinal study, a flare-type peripartum ALT trajectory was identified as the sole strong independent predictor of HBeAg clearance, after adjusting for multiple covariates associated with clearance.20 Our findings suggest that immune reactivation around delivery may enhance functional immune control, positioning the peripartum period as a critical window for the transition from immune tolerance to activation. Monitoring peripartum ALT trends may therefore facilitate personalized postpartum management.

Two recent retrospective studies have investigated the predictors of postpartum HBeAg clearance with varying results. One identified baseline viral load <8 log10 IU/ml, baseline ALT ≥2x the ULN, and age <35 years as independent predictors.21 A second study developed a nomogram incorporating pregnancy ALT flares ≥2x ULN, postpartum antiviral therapy, and lower delivery HBsAg/viral loads to predict HBeAg clearance.22 Compared to these retrospective studies, our prospective study offers the advantage of extended follow-up and a nuanced longitudinal assessment of ALT trajectories rather than a single-timepoint measurement. Importantly, all three studies consistently identify peripartum ALT elevation as a predictor of HBeAg clearance, underscoring the role of immune reactivation. However, unlike ALT flares, low HBsAg and viral loads likely represent markers of reduced viral replication under host immune activation rather than acting as true predictors of HBeAg clearance.

In this study, we also found that women who received antiviral therapy during pregnancy experienced fewer peripartum ALT flares and were more likely to initiate postpartum antiviral therapy than untreated women. These findings suggests both a positive clinical response and a favorable treatment experience during pregnancy. Current evidence indicates that HBV treatment coverage in many regions remains suboptimal, particularly among women.1,23 Therefore, our results underscore the importance of improving awareness among both patients and providers regarding the broader health benefits of antiviral therapy beyond MTCT prevention.

A potential limitation of this study is the absence of quantitative HBeAg (qHBeAg) measurements. Emerging evidence suggests that qHBeAg has value in predicting HBeAg clearance and monitoring treatment response.24,25 However, qHBeAg is not yet routinely implemented in clinical practice or recommended by the latest management guidelines.6,7 Our findings reflect current real-world clinical standards and remain highly applicable to practitioners operating under existing frameworks.

In summary, this study provides long-term data on HBeAg clearance among highly viremic HBV-infected women receiving antivirals to prevent MTCT. While antiviral therapy initiated during pregnancy effectively induced viral suppression, low viral load at delivery does not independently predict postpartum HBeAg clearance. Instead, a flare-type peripartum ALT trajectory emerged as the sole robust predictor of HBeAg clearance after adjusting for covariates, suggesting that monitoring ALT trends around childbirth can inform personalized management strategies. Furthermore, our findings highlight favorable treatment responses and experiences in this population, where care remains suboptimal. Beyond MTCT prevention, these results support the broader implementation of antiviral therapy to improve maternal outcomes and accelerate progress toward global hepatitis elimination.

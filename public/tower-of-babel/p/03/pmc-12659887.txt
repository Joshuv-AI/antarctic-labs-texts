1. DIAGNOSTIC PERFORMANCE AND CHALLENGES OF FNAC IN BONY LESIONS - A ONE YEAR STUDY IN A TERTIARY CARE HOSPITAL

Manaswi Kalita, Aditi Sharma; Department of Pathology, Assam Medical College, Dibrugarh, Assam, India

Introduction: Modern healthcare improvements, though beneficial, often entail higher time and financial investment because of increased procedural complexity and the requirement for specialized staff to operate expensive equipment. FNA pleasantly contrasts with this trend, being a simple procedure that can be performed almost anywhere. FNAC is less popular for bony lesions, as many doubt it’s ability to provide adequate sampling. In spite of this, conditions such as inflammation, cyst, neoplasms, hemorrhage, and malformations often soften bone, allowing needle passes easily. Aims and Objective: 1.To evaluate the efficiency of FNAC in diagnosis of bony lesions 2. To study the cytomorphological features of different lesions of bone. Materials and Methods: This is a cross-sectional study conducted from June 2024 to May 2025.58 cases of bony lesions taken from OPD and IPD in AMCH were thoroughly investigated and clinico-radiologically examined. Aspiration was carried out by 22 G needle of varying length with 10 mL syringe, then stained by MGG/PAP. For suspecting Tuberculosis, ZN stain and CBNAAT was done. Histopathology was taken as a gold standard. Few diagnostically challenging cases were confirmed by Ancillary technique. Results: Out of 58 cases, 34(58.62%) were primary bone tumors, 11(18.96%) metastatic lesions, 6(10.34%) granulomatous lesions, and 7(12.06%) cases of inflammatory lesions. Among the primary tumors, Giant cell tumor was of maximum cases13 (22.41%) followed by osteosarcoma. There was 34 true positive cases and 7 false negative cases. Sensitivity in this series was found to be 82.92% and specificity is not required in this study series as there were no true negative cases in the study. The overall accuracy in this study was found to be 82.92%. Conclusion: Cytopathologic assessment with the clinical and radiologic findings, together, can yield almost the same diagnostic accuracy in the majority of lesions when compared with that of histopathology diagnosis.

2. IMAGE ANALYSIS: BRIDGING MORPHOLOGY AND AI IN BREAST CYTOPATHOLOGY

Jai Kumar Chaurasia1, Vaishali Walke1, Vijayshri Chaurasia2, Deepti Joshi1, Shakti Kumar Yadav1, E Jayashankar1, Vivek Patel2, Neelkamal Kapoor1; 1Department of Pathology and Lab Medicine, AIIMS, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India, 2Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering, MANIT, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Breast cytopathology is challenging. Considering the interobserver variability in diagnosis, attempts to automate it with software aided diagnosis has gained attention in recent years. Image analysis using Fractal Analysis (FA) is one such technique which uses the computational image processing. It can translate subjective observations into quantitative values and represents the changes in structure of the tissue fragment with progression of the degree of complexity of tissue from benign to malignant. Objectives: To study the role of Image Analysis using FA in distinguishing benign and malignant lesions of breast on fine needle aspirate cytology smears. Materials and Methods: A retrospective analysis of Papanicolaou (PAP) stained smears of breast lesions from year 2020 to 2023 was performed on 50 cases (25 benign and 25 malignant) with available follow-up histopathology. Atleast 10 tissue fragments per case were acquired on 40× objective lens using Leica DFC 295 microscope, resulting in 500 epithelial fragments which were subjected to FA. The images were extracted and converted to gray scale using image J software developed at the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and laboratory for Optical and Computational Instrumentation (LOCI, University of Wisconsin). The analysis of extracted image was done by using progamming language Python (version 3.13, 2024, Wilmington, USA). The fragments were analysed for size, luminance, shape unevenness and complexity of inner structure. The χ2 and Mann–Whitney U statistical tests were applied using SPSS (version 28). A P value of < 0.05 was considered as significant. Results: Significant P value was obtained for Fragment chromasia/ luminance (P = 0.007), fragment unevenness (P = 0.04), complexity of inner structure (P = 0.01) for distinguishing benign and malignant lesions. Conclusions: Image Analysis using Fractal Analysis is an useful tool which can quantitate subjective observations and can aid in differentiating between benign and malignant lesions of breast.

3. ENHANCING FNAC DIAGNOSIS OF BREAST LESIONS WITH AI-BASED NUCLEAR MORPHOMETRIC ANALYSIS

Neha Singh, V M Mimna, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC)plays a pivotal role in early detection of malignancies. While traditional nuclear morphometry aids in distinguishing benign and malignant lesions, its interpretation is limited by Inter-observer variability. Quantitative nuclear morphometry supported by artificial intelligence (AI) offers an objective approach to cytological evaluation. Objectives: 1. To assess the role of AI-driven nuclear morphometric analysis in differentiating benign from malignant breast lesions on FNAC smears and 2. To determine its correlation with Robinson’s cytological grading in malignant cases. Materials and Methods: A total of 60 FNAC cases were studied (30 benign, 30 malignant). Smears were digitized and analyzed using an AI-based image analysis platform for nuclear segmentation and extraction of morphometric features, including nuclear area, perimeter, circularity, and chromatin texture. Results were statistically compared between benign and malignant groups, and correlations with Robinson’s grading were tested using concordance analysis. Diagnostic performance indices were calculated. Results: AI-driven morphometry demonstrated significant differences between benign and malignant lesions across all major nuclear parameters (P < 0.005). Diagnostic performance was high, with sensitivity of 93.3%, specificity of 91.7%, and overall accuracy of > 90% for distinguishing malignant from benign cases. Within malignant cases, morphometric indices showed strong positive correlation with Robinson’s cytological grades (concordance coefficient = 0.86, P < 0.005). The AI-generated morphometric score reliably paralleled increasing nuclear atypia across Grade I–III categories. Conclusion: AI-assisted nuclear morphometry on FNAC smears provides an accurate, reproducible, and objective adjunct to routine cytology. With diagnostic sensitivity and specificity exceeding 90% and strong concordance with Robinson’s grading, this approach has the potential to enhance preoperative breast lesion assessment and reduce subjectivity in cytological reporting.’

4. ASSESSMENT OF THE INTRAUTERINE CONTRACEPTIVE DEVICE (IUCD) RELATED CHANGES IN PAP SMEARS AND THEIR SIGNIFICANCE

Sneh Singh1, Rajnish Kalra2; 1Department of Pathology, Civil Hospital, Panchkula, Haryana, India, 2Department of Pathology, PGIMS, Rohtak, Haryana, India

Introduction: Intrauterine contraceptive devices (IUCDs) are among the most widely used reversible contraceptives. Their long-term presence may induce cytological changes and predispose to genital tract infections detectable on Pap smears. Objectives: To assess IUCD-related cytological alterations and microbial infections in Pap smears and compare them with non-IUCD users in order to achieve higher acceptance. Materials and Methods: A prospective study was conducted on 200 women aged 18–45 years using IUCDs and 100 age-matched controls without IUCD usage attending the Postpartum Centre, PGIMS Rohtak. Cervicovaginal smears were prepared and stained by the standard Papanicolaou method. Smears were evaluated for adequacy as per the Bethesda system (2014) and screened for cyto-hormonal patterns, cytological alterations, and microbial agents. Statistical analysis was performed using the Chi-square test. Results: Cytological alterations were observed in 60% of IUCD users compared to 15% of controls (P < 0.001). The most frequent changes included benign atypia (28%), karyomegaly (11%), and IUD-like cells (9.5%). Psammoma bodies were detected in 5.5% of IUCD users. There was exfoliation of endometrial cells along with histocytes and giant cells in 31.5% of IUCD users versus 15% of controls, with shedding noted even beyond the 10th day of cycle. Microbial infections were significantly higher in IUCD users (25.5%) than controls (3%) (P < 0.001). Gardnerella vaginalis (8.5%), Actinomyces (7.5%), and HPV-related changes (4.5%) were the predominant. The highest incidence of infections (37.6%) was observed in women with 3–10 years of IUCD use. Conclusion: IUCD usage is associated with a significantly increased incidence of cytological alterations and genital tract infections detectable in Pap smears. Most changes are benign and reactive, emphasizing the importance of thorough cytological evaluation with relevant clinical history to avoid misdiagnosis and increase acceptance among the users.

5. EFFICACY OF CYTOLOGICAL SMEARS IN DIAGNOSIS OF ENDOMETRIAL LESIONS ASPIRATED BY KARMAN’S CANNULA

Sushanta Karmakar, Adity Sharma; Assam Medical College, Dibrugarh, Assam, India

Introduction: Endometrial aspiration cytology is an easy, secure, and trustworthy outpatient method for assessing endometrial normalcy and identifying premalignant and malignant lesions. It generates little discomfort and doesn’t require anaesthetic or cervical dilation. Aim and Objectives: To evaluate the efficacy of endometrial cytology using Karman’s cannula and to study the cytomorphological features of different endometrial lesions and confirmed by histopathological examination. Materials and Methods: A hospital-based cross-sectional study carried out in the AMCH Departments of Pathology and Obstetrics and Gynaecology between March 2024 and Feb 2025. 144 cases were included and patients presented with menorrhagia, Metrorrhagia, Polymenorraghia, postmenopausal bleeding, or infertility except for Pregnancy, an active infection, and a significant cervical cancer. Endometrial aspiration was performed, and a smear was obtained on a clean glass slide for MGG and PAP staining. Histopathology was taken as the gold standard and few diagnostically challenging cases were confirmed by IHC. Between two groups Chi-square test was used to analyse the results. Results: Endometrial aspiration cytology demonstrated high diagnostic performance with sensitivity 88.3%, specificity 100%, positive predictive value 100%, negative predictive value 86.4%, and overall accuracy 93.3% (P < 0.001). Phase-wise correlation showed 90.9% for proliferative endometrium and 100% for secretory phase. Hyperplasia was diagnosed with 70.3% accuracy (simple 76.7%, complex 33.3%, atypical 100%). Atrophic endometrium showed 66.7% concordance, while adenocarcinoma yielded 100% correlation with histopathology. Conclusions: Endometrial aspiration cytology is a cost-effective, well-tolerated, and accurate outpatient technique for diagnosing both normal and abnormal endometrium. Its high adequacy rate makes it suitable as a screening tool for early detection of endometrial pathology, improving prognostic outcomes.

6. ENDOMETRIAL ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY: ITS EFFICACY IN DIAGNOSING GYNECOLOGICAL DISORDERS AND INTEGRATION WITH THE YOKOHAMA SYSTEM (TYS) OF REPORTING ENDOMETRIAL CYTOLOGY 2016 – A STUDY OF 254 CASES

Loretta Ann Pereira, Hilda Fernandes, Mary Netheya; Department of Oncopathology, Yenepoya Medical College, Mangalore, Karnataka, India

Introduction: Endometrial aspiration cytology (EAC) is a simple, minimally invasive, and cost-effective technique for evaluating endometrial pathology, particularly in women presenting with abnormal uterine bleeding. Lack of uniform reporting has limited its utility. The Yokohama System of Reporting Endometrial Cytology (2016) provides standardized diagnostic categories, potentially improving consistency and clinicopathological correlation. Objectives: 1.To study the cytomorphology of endometrial lesions/neoplasms. 2.To assess the diagnostic efficacy of endometrial aspiration cytology in non malignant vs malignant lesions. 3.To integrate the findings into standardized categories of Yokohama system (TYS) 4.To compare cytological diagnoses with corresponding histopathological findings and determine sensitivity, specificity and diagnostic accuracy of EAC. Materials and Methods: A retrospective study of 254 cases of endometrial aspiration cytology was undertaken over a period of two years. Smears were evaluated for adequacy and cytomorphological features. Wherever available, histopathological correlation was performed. All cases were reclassified initially into three broad categories, as Negative, Positive or Inadequate. They were also categorised as per the standardized Yokohama system into (5 categories) Inadequate, Negative for malignancy, Atypical, Suspicious, and Positive for malignancy. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and diagnostic accuracy were calculated. Results: Of 254 cases, 239 cases (94%) were adequate for evaluation. The predominant benign findings included proliferative endometrium, secretory endometrium, and endometrial hyperplasia. Malignant lesions were identified in 04 cases (1.5%), endometrial carcinoma. On correlation with histopathology, EAC demonstrated sensitivity of 66.7%, specificity of 98.6%, and overall diagnostic accuracy of 97.88%. Application of TYS improved the reproducibility of reporting and a more standardized format based on cytoarchitecture rather than nuclear morphology alone. Conclusion: Endometrial aspiration cytology is a reliable, minimally invasive diagnostic tool for evaluating gynecological disorders. Adoption of TYS enhances standardization and diagnostic clarity, thereby strengthening its role as an effective first-line investigation in routine practice.

7. URINE CYTOLOGY REPORTING SYSTEM: SHOULD WE LOOK FOR A SIMPLIFIED ALTERNATIVE TO THE PARIS SYSTEM?

Santosh Kumar Mondal1, Soumyajit Goswami1, Nirmalya Ghosh2, Vinod Kumar2; 1Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine, AIIMS, Kalyani, West Bengal, India, 2Department of Urosurgery, AIIMS, Kalyani, West Bengal, India

Introduction: The Paris system for reporting urine cytology was adopted in 2016. The system divided the urothelial cells into 6 categories. But many find it complicated and there has been some lapses in the reporting format, especially when it comes to categorization of high grade and low-grade urothelial carcinoma. Aims and Objectives: 1. Proposal of a new alternative to Paris system for reporting urine cytology. 2. Correlation with histological findings in available cases. Materials and Methods: We propose 6 categories. 1. Non-Diagnostic; 2. Negative; 3. Atypical urothelial cells; 4. Suspicious for urothelial carcinoma; 5. Urothelial carcinoma; 6. Others. In Category 2(Negative); negative for epithelial cell abnormality, inflammation, parasites, crystals etc. will be included. In the last category (Category 6), non-urothelial abnormality (squamous cell atypia/carcinoma, sarcoma, lymphoma, prostatic ca, metastatic ca etc.) will be included. Adequacy criteria: Require a high number of diagnostic cells (at least ≥10 cells) for upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC). We propose ≥1/3 N/C ratio for low grade and ≥2/3 ratio for high grade urothelial carcinoma. Results: The primary objective is to set up a multi-tier system comprising 6 categories most importantly Category 4 and Category 5 which will be further classified into low grade and high grade. Conclusion: The hypothesis is to set up a new simplified system of reporting urine cytology with extended categories with set characteristics, namely two separate categories (low grade and high grade) for suspicious of malignancy (Category 4) and urothelial carcinoma (Category 5).

8. IMPLICATION OF CHATGPT IN URINE CYTOLOGY: A COMPARATIVE EVALUATION OF THE PARIS SYSTEM CATEGORIES

Ashi Dubey, Zufian; Vardhaman Medical College, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Urine cytology is a key diagnostic tool for detecting urothelial carcinoma, especially high-grade lesions. Despite structured guidelines under the Paris System (TPS), variability in interpretation persists due to subjectivity. Artificial intelligence, particularly large language models such as ChatGPT, may assist in improving consistency and accuracy in cytological reporting. Objective: To evaluate the role of ChatGPT in urine cytology interpretation and compare its categorisation with consultant-reported Paris System categories. Materials and Methods: A retrospective review of 50 urine cytology cases was performed. Each case was categorised independently into Paris categories by a consultant cytopathologist and by ChatGPT (paid version) using cytological descriptors. Dysplasia grading, suspicion for malignancy, and assignment into TPS II–V were analysed. Consultant categorisation was taken as the reference standard, and concordance with ChatGPT was assessed. Results: Of the 50 cases, the consultant reported 6 as Category II, 10 as Category III, 15 as Category IV, and 19 as Category V. ChatGPT correctly identified all 6 cases in Category II (100%). For Category III, 7 of 10 cases (70%) were concordant, while 3 were upgraded to Category IV. In Category IV, 10 of 15 cases (66.7%) matched, whereas 5 were over-called as Category V. Among Category V cases, 16 of 19 (84.2%) were correctly identified, while 3 were under-called as Category IV. Overall, ChatGPT achieved 78% agreement (39/50), with most discrepancies due to adjacent category shifts between IV and V. Conclusion: ChatGPT shows promise as an adjunct in urine cytology reporting, particularly in high-grade lesions under TPS. While not a substitute for expert judgment, it may enhance consistency, reduce subjectivity, and provide rapid categorization. Larger prospective studies are required before routine clinical adoption.

9. INTEGRATING CYTOLOGY WITH EUS-GUIDED FINE NEEDLE BIOPSY FOR PANCREATIC LESIONS: PAVING THE WAY FOR A COMPREHENSIVE CYTOHISTOPATHOLOGICAL DIAGNOSIS

Sherrin Jacob, Rajni Yadav, Shreya Kaul, Soumya Jagannath, Deepak Gunjan, Prasenjit Das, Deepali Jain, Sandeep Mathur, Venkateswaran K Iyer; AIIMS, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) with fine needle aspiration/biopsy (FNA/B) has emerged as the procedure of choice to reach a definite pathological diagnosis in recent years. While EUS-FNA mainly provides cytological specimens, it does not provide core tissue for histological assessment, in contrast to FNB needles, which were designed to provide a core specimen for architectural assessment. This technique has far superseded the efficacy of traditional percutaneous needle ultrasound guided aspiration, which was performed in earlier times. Our institutional experience and recent studies have suggested that cytological evaluation of the liquid content yielded along with fine needle cores for histopathology further enhances the diagnostic efficacy of EUS-FNB sampling technique. Objective: To assess the diagnostic yield of the cytological smears compared with that of the histological specimens alone obtained via FNB needle in pancreatic lesions. Materials and Methods: We retrospectively evaluated 388 samples from pancreatic lesions in a period of 10 years of which there were 217 USG guided cytology specimens and 171 samples of EUS guided FNB samples. Routine endoscopic ultrasound for evaluation of pancreatic lesions is performed using a linear endoscope (Olympus GF-UCT 180; ME3 processor). Following visualization, the lesion is targeted for sampling through gastric or D1 station. 22G Franseen tip FNB needle (Boston Scientific Acquire) is used for obtaining the fine needle cores. Then, we collect the tissue pieces or core to formalin bottle for further histological assessment, while the liquid (usually bloody) remnant on the smears is spread and fixed in alcohol, air dried or put in eppendorf for cell block preparation for cytological assessment depending on the amount of material available in the same setting. We applied the WHO reporting system for pancreaticobiliary cytology for a combined cytohistopathological assessment for EUS-FNB samples. Results: The total number of cases classified as WHO Category I (Unsatisfactory) was 23 (13.4%) in EUS-guided aspiration versus 55 (25.3%) in abdominal USG-guided aspiration, where the smears showed predominantly haemorrhage and inadequate lesional cells. Amongst the EUS-FNB samples, number of cytohistopathological samples categorized into each category were: Insufficient/Non-Diagnostic (n = 23), Benign/Negative for Malignancy (n = 16), Atypia (n = 17), Pancreaticobiliary Neoplasm (PaN)-Low Risk/Grade (n = 13), PaN-High Risk/Grade (n = 10), Suspicious for Malignancy (n = 21), and Malignant (n = 71). We had an average of 3 alcohol fixed smears and 2 air dried smears along with EUS FNB in every case along with cell blocks in 10 of the cases. Availability of corresponding cytology smears improved the diagnostic efficacy in 17 of 171 (10%) of the cases. 10 of these 17 cases had only blood and fibrin material in the fine needle cores where cytological smears showed enough cellular material for a conclusive diagnosis of SPEN (n = 1) and adenocarcinoma (n = 9). 7 of these 17 cases, on the other hand, had only tiny fragments of tissue with atypical cells and desmoplastic stroma, suspicious or suggestive of malignancy, and the presence of cellularity with definitive atypia in the cytological smears corroborated to a conclusive diagnosis. Conclusion: Pancreatic lesions are difficult to sample due to their anatomical location and limited accessibility. EUS-guided FNB of pancreatic lesions has become the preferred method for obtaining tissue samples. While it has demonstrated greater efficacy compared to traditional US-guided aspiration, our study on utilizing residual liquid material during the procedure for cytological examination adds significant value to the diagnostic process in up to 10% of cases. Therefore, we recommend routinely using cytology alongside fine needle biopsy in pancreatic lesions, adopting a combined cytohistopathological diagnosis and WHO categorization.

10. ASSESSING THE UTILITY OF A CATEGORICAL REPORTING SYSTEM IN PEDIATRIC LIVER CYTOLOGY FOR IMPROVED DIAGNOSTIC STRATIFICATION

P Vijayanirmala1, Rajni Yadav1, Sandeep Agarwala2, Manisha Jana3, Venkateswaran K Iyer1; 1Department of Pathology, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India, 2Department of Pediatric Surgery, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India, 3Department of Radiodiagnosis, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of the liver is an established, minimally invasive technique for evaluating hepatic lesions. In the pediatric population, liver lesions encompass a broad spectrum, ranging from reactive and infective conditions to primary hepatic tumors and metastatic malignancies. Despite its clinical relevance, reporting practices are often variable. Adopting a standardized categorical system can improve diagnostic reproducibility, communication with clinicians, and clinical decision-making. Objectives: To evaluate pediatric liver cytology cases over a 12-year period (2014–2025) with respect to demographics, cytological categorization, and diagnostic spectrum, and to assess the utility of a structured categorical reporting system. Methods: A retrospective analysis was conducted on pediatric liver cytology cases diagnosed in our department between 2014 and 2025. Relevant clinical details were recorded. All cases were categorized cytologically into unsatisfactory, benign, atypical, suspicious for malignancy and malignancy. The malignant cases were classified into specific types and subtypes. Results: Of the 147 cases analyzed, 24.5% cases were negative for malignancy, while a significant proportion of cases (61.2%) were malignant. Within the malignant group, hepatoblastoma (72.2%) was the most common, followed by hepatocellular carcinoma (13.3%) and metastatic tumors (7.8%). We were able to further subtype and assess the grade of differentiation of these malignant tumors. Out of 12 hepatocellular carcinomas, 4 (33.3%) were fibrolamellar, 3 (25.0%) were well-differentiated, 3 (25.0%) were moderately differentiated, and 2 (16.7%) were poorly differentiated. Among hepatoblastomas, mixed epithelial tumors were the most common, followed by predominantly fetal and embryonal types, which were observed in nearly similar proportions. Other subtypes were very rare. Conclusion: This study highlights the role of cytology as a reliable, minimally invasive tool in pediatric liver pathology. By applying a structured categorical reporting framework, our study demonstrates that pediatric liver FNAC can be systematically classified into clinically meaningful groups, aiding communication between pathologists and clinicians and improving patient management.

12. ROLE OF FINE-NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY IN T-CELL LYMPHOMAS: AN EIGHT-YEAR EXPERIENCE FROM NORTH INDIA

Aravind Radhakrishnan1, Rohilla Manish2, Srinivasan Radhika2, Nalini Gupta2, Parikshaa Gupta2, Reetu Kundu2, Amanjit Bal3, Reena Das4; 1Department of Pathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 2Department of Cytology and Gynaecological Pathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 3Department of Histopathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 4Department of Hematology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India

Introduction: T-cell lymphomas are rare, heterogeneous neoplasms with variable clinicopathological features. Accurate diagnosis is challenging in routine practice, particularly on cytology. This study presents an eight-year retrospective analysis of cytology-based diagnoses of T-cell lymphomas at a tertiary care centre in North India, emphasising diagnostic utility and the role of ancillary techniques. Aims and Objectives: To evaluate the cytomorphological spectrum of T-cell lymphomas diagnosed on fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) with ancillary techniques, assess demographics, and correlate with histopathology (HPE) and bone marrow (BM) findings where available. Materials and Methods: All cases diagnosed as T-cell lymphomas between January 2017 and August 2025 were retrieved. Cytological smears and records, with immunocytochemistry and flow cytometry, were reviewed and correlated with available HPE and BM findings. Results: A total of 101 cases were identified: 49 anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL), 37 T-lymphoblastic lymphoma (T-LBL), 11 angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL), three mycosis fungoides (MF), and one NK/T-cell lymphoma. Rare cases included three MF and one NK/T-cell lymphoma, all perfectly correlating with HPE. Conclusion: FNAC, complemented by ancillary techniques, is a rapid, minimally invasive, and reliable tool for diagnosing T-cell lymphomas with substantial accuracy. It enables early therapeutic decisions, particularly in emergencies such as mediastinal masses with superior vena cava syndrome.

13. APPLICATION OF THE WHO REPORTING SYSTEM IN CLASSIFICATION OF LYMPH NODE CYTOLOGY: ASSESSMENT OF INTEROBSERVER VARIABILITY AND COMPARISON WITH THE SYDNEY SYSTEM

S Mekala, Annapoorani Varadarajan, Neha Kawatra Madan, Meetu Agrawal, Charanjeet Ahluwalia, Mukul Singh; Department of Pathology, Vardhman Mahavir Medical College and Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) is a first-line diagnostic tool for lymphadenopathy, but the lack of standardized reporting systems has limited its effectiveness. The Sydney System was introduced in 2019, followed by the WHO reporting system with five categories: non-diagnostic, benign, atypical, suspicious for malignancy, and malignant. The WHO system emphasizes standardized terminology and detailed subcategorization to enhance clinical guidance. Objectives: This study classified lymph node cytology cases using the WHO system, assessed interobserver variability, and compared its diagnostic utility with the Sydney System. Materials and Methods: A retrospective review of 347 lymph node FNAC cases from 1500 total FNA cases was performed. Two observers independently applied the WHO classification. Clinical, radiological, and histopathological follow-up data were collected. Interobserver agreement, diagnostic accuracy, and clinical utility were assessed and compared between both systems. Results: The distribution of cases was similar between the Sydney and WHO systems: non-diagnostic (13.58%), benign (77.2%), atypical (0.62%), suspicious for malignancy (0.62%), and malignant (7.99%). Ambiguities and rigid criteria reduced clinical flexibility. Conclusion: The WHO system shows substantial interobserver agreement and diagnostic accuracy comparable to Sydney, with added specificity from subcategorization. However, overlapping definitions and rigid criteria limit its incremental practical advantage.“

14. FLOWCYTOMETRIC DETERMINATION OF DNA PLOIDY AND S PHASE FRACTION IN NON-HODKIN’S LYMPHOMAS

Rutu S Patel1, Ujjawal Khurana1, Deepti Joshi1, Garima Goel1, Hemlata Panwar1, Shaila Sidam2, Gaurav Dhingra3, Vaishali Walke1; 1Department of Pathology and Lab Medicine, AIIMS, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India, 2Department of Otorhinolaryngology, AIIMS, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India, 3Department of Medical Oncology and Haematology, AIIMS, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Flow cytometric DNA content analysis, also known as DNA ploidy assessment, is used to predict outcomes in various types of cancers. This method is used to examine and quantity DNA within cells and estimate the proportion of cells in different phases of the cell cycle. Objective: To evaluate DNA ploidy and S phase fraction by flowcytometric analysis in Non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas and correlate with Histopathology, Typing and Grade of lymphoma. Materials and Methods: The fine needle aspirates/body fluids samples were subjected to flow cytometric DNA ploidy analysis. The immunophenotyping is carried out with 10 parameter, 8-colour, 3 laser BD FACS Canto-II analyser. Cells are subjected to ethanol treatment, which simultaneously fixes and permeabilizes them, thereby facilitating the entry of DNA-binding fluorochromes into intact cells. No procedural complications or adverse events were reported, indicating the safety of heparin use in this setting. Conclusion: Heparin priming of fine-needle aspiration needles is a simple, safe, and potentially valuable technique to enhance cytological quality in palpation-guided FNA. Although statistical significance was not reached, observed trends suggest improved cellularity and diagnostic performance with heparin use, without compromising safety or procedural ease. These findings support the clinical utility of heparin priming in reducing blood contamination and improving sample quality. Larger, multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these benefits and establish standardized protocols for routine use.

43. A HIGH-SPEED MULTI-CAMERA ARRAY SCANNER WITH WEAKLY SUPERVISED AI FOR RAPID ON-SITE CYTOLOGY

Amey Chaware1, Kanghyun Kim1, Alexandra Balaban2, Joshua Lerner1, Carolyn Glass2, Kamran Mahmood3, Raj Dash2, Roarke Horstmeyera4; 1Department of Biomedical Engineering, Duke University, Durham, NC, USA, 2Department of Pathology, Duke University Medical Center, Durham, NC, USA, 3Division of Pulmonary, Allergy, and Critical Care Medicine, Department of Medicine, Duke University Medical Center, Durham, NC, USA, 4Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Duke University, Durham, NC, USA

Introduction: Digital pathology tools rarely provide timely, actionable feedback for rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE) of fine-needle aspiration specimens due to their large area and volumetric nature. To address this, we developed a Multi-camera Array Scanner (MCAS) that rapidly images entire cytology smears in 3D. We paired this high-throughput hardware with a weakly supervised machine learning pipeline to provide slide-level diagnostic triage with interpretable visual evidence, all within a timeframe compatible with the ROSE workflow. Materials and Methods: Our MCAS utilizes 48 synchronized camera-lens pairs to digitize the entire area of two slides (75 × 50 mm²) in parallel. From an initial capture of 50 z-planes, a real-time pipeline generates a 16-gigapixel focus-fused composite image and saves the 9 most relevant z-planes for detailed review. For the AI pipeline, training was weakly supervised using only slide-level diagnoses. Each slide was tiled into 256 × 256-pixel patches, and features were extracted using a pretrained encoder. An attention-based multiple-instance learning (MIL) model then aggregates these features to predict a slide-level outcome. The system was validated on a dataset of 119 Diff-Quik stained bronchoscopic FNA smears (59 adenocarcinoma, 60 benign) using 5-fold cross-validation. Results: The MCAS digitized entire slides, including full focal stacks, in an average of 2: 21 min per slide. The MIL pipeline successfully distinguished malignant from benign smears with an AUROC of 0.86 ± 0.03 and a slide-level accuracy of 0.84 ± 0.04. Critically, the model’s attention maps consistently highlighted diagnostically relevant regions without requiring direct supervision. Pathologic review confirmed these high-attention regions correlated with malignant cell clusters, providing interpretable evidence for the model’s predictions. Conclusions: The combination of high-speed volumetric scanning and a weakly supervised attention-MIL pipeline enables accurate, transparent diagnostic triage using only slide-level labels. This system demonstrates the feasibility of providing automated, on-procedure guidance compatible with established ROSE timelines.

The complete mitochondrial genome of Sphaeramia nematoptera (Gobiiformes: Apogonidae) and its phylogenetic position
Authors: Siqi Tang, Hanwen Zhang, Chengjun Fang, Chen Wang, Xiao Chen, Junjie Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, the complete mitochondrial genome of Sphaeramia nematoptera (Bleeker, 1856) was sequenced and characterized for the first time. The 16,563 bp circular mitogenome encompasses 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNAs, two rRNAs, and one control region. Its nucleotide composition is 27.0% A, 25.4% T, 30.5% C, and 17.1% G, indicating an AT bias of 52.4%. Phylogenetic analysis confirms that S. nematoptera forms a well-supported monophyletic lineage with maximum (100%) bootstrap support. This study contributes valuable molecular resources for resolving the phylogenetic relationships within the Apogonidae.

Introduction

Sphaeramia nematoptera (Bleeker 1856) is commonly known as the pajama cardinalfish, as a marine teleost belonging to the family Apogonidae within the order Gobiiformes (Fricke et al. 2026). The genus Sphaeramia currently comprises only two valid species, the other being the orbiculate cardinalfish, Sphaeramia orbicularis (Cuvier 1828). Sphaeramia nematoptera widely distributed across the tropical coral reefs of the Indo-Pacific (Froese and Pauly, 2026). It is highly recognizable by its greenish-yellow head, a prominent dark vertical band across its midsection, and a posterior half adorned with distinct red to brown polka dots. Due to its vibrant appearance and peaceful temperament, the species has become an important component of the marine ornamental trade.

Despite its ecological significance and economic value, comprehensive genetic data regarding this species remain limited. Characterizing the complete mitochondrial genome of S. nematoptera is therefore essential. This genomic resource will facilitate accurate molecular identification and provide crucial molecular evidence to resolve the complex phylogenetic relationships and evolutionary history within the family Apogonidae.

Materials and methods

Specimen collection and identification

The specimen was collected from Zhaoshu Island, Hainan Province, China (coordinates: 16°58′48″N, 112°16′16″E), and identified as S. nematoptera based on morphological features by FishBase (https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Sphaeramia-nematoptera.html) (Froese and Pauly, 2026). The voucher specimen was deposited at South China Normal University (contact and email to Dr. Junjie Wang, 20185210@m.scnu.edu.cn), under the voucher number: SCNUfish2025013. A morphological image was taken by Dr. Xiao Chen (Figure 1).

Sequencing, assembly and annotation

Total genomic DNA of S. nematoptera was extracted from the fin tissue by TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (Beijing, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol, and was fragmented using a focused ultrasonicator (Scientz98, Ningbo, China). DNA fragments within the 300-500 bp range were size-selected. Library preparation was executed using the VAHTS™ Universal DNA Library Prep Kit for MGI, which encompassed end-repair, adapter ligation, PCR amplification, and subsequent target enrichment with biotinylated mitogenome probes. The constructed libraries were then sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (150 bp paired-end reads), yielding a robust sequencing depth between 964× and 2269× (Figure S1). For bioinformatics analysis, de novo assembly and initial annotation from the raw high-throughput data were performed using MitoZ v3.6 (Meng et al. 2019). To ensure the accuracy of the gene features, the annotations were further refined and cross-validated against the MitoFish database (Table S1) (Zhu et al. 2023). The circular mitogenome map was visualized using Chloroplot (Figure 2) (Zheng et al. 2020).

Structure of the mitogenome of S. nematoptera. The innermost circle of the images represents GC% per every 5 bp of the mitogenome; the darker lines are, the higher their GC% are.

Phylogenetic analysis

Phylogenetic analysis was performed for the mitogenomes of S. nematoptera and 38 other species of Apogonidae, with Butis koilomatodon (PV972748) and Kurtus gulliveri (NC_022477) as outgroups (Figure 3). All downloaded GenBank sequences, including unpublished accessions, were manually inspected to confirm their completeness. The 12 H-strand protein-coding genes (PCGs) and 2 ribosomal RNA genes (rRNAs) were extracted using PhyloSuite v2 (Xiang et al. 2023), then aligned separately with MAFFT v7 (Katoh and Standley, 2013). The final aligned datasets were concatenated comprising 14 genes. The best-fit model (GTR+F + I + G4) was selected using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). Maximum likelihood phylogenetic tree was inferred using IQ-tree under 5000 ultrafast bootstraps (Nguyen et al. 2015). The resulting phylogenetic tree was visualized by iTOL v6 (Letunic and Bork, 2024).

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of S. nematoptera is a closed-circular DNA molecule spanning exactly 16,563 base pairs (bp) in length (Figure 2). Consistent with the highly conserved vertebrate architectural model, it accommodates a standard set of 37 genes, comparing with 13 PCGs, 22 transfer RNAs (tRNAs), and two rRNAs. It also contains a major non-coding sequence, the putative control region (D-loop), which measures 885 bp and is positioned immediately downstream of trnP and upstream of trnF. Gene orientation exhibits a distinct strand asymmetry. The vast majority of the genetic information is transcribed from the heavy strand (H-strand), which encodes 28 genes. In contrast, the light strand (L-strand) carries only one PCG (nad6) alongside eight tRNA genes (trnQ, trnA, trnN, trnC, trnY, trnS, trnE, and trnP).

Twelve PCGs are initiated by the conventional ATG. The single exception is cox1, which utilizes the alternative GTG start codon. Five genes (nad2, cox1, atp8, nad4L, and nad5) conclude with a complete TAA codon, while nad1 and nad6 utilize TAG and AGA, respectively. Notably, the remaining six PCGs feature incomplete stop codons, appearing either as a single T (cox2, nad3, nad4, and cytb) or a TA dinucleotide (atp6, cox3). The genomic features of S. nematoptera are highly congruent with other members of Apogonidae.

The genome sequence is highly compact, characterized by several contiguous or overlapping reading frames. The most prominent overlaps are identified between atp8 and atp6 (sharing 10 bp), nad4L and nad4 (7 bp), as well as between nad5 and nad6 (5 bp). These overlappings are highly conserved structural features across the family Apogonidae. Regarding the ribosomal components, the 12S (rrnS, 963 bp) and 16S (rrnL, 1686 bp) genes are physically separated by the trnV gene. This specific spatial arrangement strictly follows the traditional clustering pattern observed throughout teleost lineages.

Based on mitogenomes data, this study successfully constructed a phylogenetic tree for the family Apogonidae, encompassing 17 genera. The results demonstrate that the topological structure of the tree is highly stable (Figure 3). The phylogenetic analysis clearly reveals S. nematoptera forms a completely independent monophyletic branch, with 100% bootstrap support. Specifically, it forms a sister-group relationship with a clade comprising the genera Apogonichthys, Nectamia, and Jaydia, with 76% bootstrap support.

Discussion and conclusion

In summary, this study successfully sequenced and characterized the complete mitogenome of S. nematoptera. The circular mitogenome is 16,563 bp in length and strictly adheres to the highly conserved teleostean architectural pattern, comprising a standard set of 37 structural genes and a putative control region. The phylogenetic analysis indicates that the genus Sphaeramia occurred later than that of the genera Nectamia and Apogonichthyoides, but earlier than Apogon and its allied genera. These mitochondrial data-based results not only strongly confirm the validity of S. nematoptera as an independent taxonomic unit but also provide molecular evidence for its critical transitional position in the phylogenetic history of the Apogonidae.

Given the significant commercial value of S. nematoptera in the global marine ornamental trade, these genomic resources will be instrumental for accurate species identification. Since the genus Sphaeramia currently comprises only two valid species, the subsequent sequencing of its congener, Sphaeramia orbicularis, represents a critical next step.

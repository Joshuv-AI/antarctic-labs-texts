Predictive Ability of Breath Holding Time for Expansion of Hematoma in Patients with Intracerebral Haemorrhagic Stroke Undergoing Intensive Blood Pressure Reduction – A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Geetanshu Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionA common and serious complication of Intra-Cerebral Hemorrhage(ICH) within the first 24 hours is hematoma expansion, a major driver of poor outcomes. Although intensive Blood Pressure(BP) reduction can help, it presents important challenges. Bedside assessment of cerebrovasomotor response using the Breath-Holding Index (BHI) via Transcranial Doppler(TCD) offers a promising, noninvasive method to identify patients at highest risk and those most likely to benefit from aggressive therapy. In this study, we employ this novel bedside technique to evaluate BHI and determine its effectiveness as an early predictor of hematoma expansion, aiming to support more tailored and effective clinical management.1–3ObjectivesTo evaluate the potential utility of the BHI by TCD as a non-invasive bedside tool for predicting hematoma expansion in patients with ICH stroke undergoing intensive BP reduction.Materials and methodsThis prospective observational study evaluated cerebrovascular reactivity in 40 patients with primary non-traumatic ICH diagnosed on Non-Contrast Computed Tomograph scan (NCCT) of head, presenting within 24 hours of ictus and admitted to the Intensive care unit (ICU) of Sir Ganga Ram Hospital requiring intensive BP reduction. TCD assessments (at distal M1 segment of the both MCA via the transtemporal windows) were performed by a trained physician, and the BHI was measured twice for reliability. Bilateral MCA flow velocities, BHI, and Pulsatility Index (PI) were recorded at baseline, 6 hours, and 24 hours. Hematoma size was monitored using sequential CT scans, with a ≥33% increase classified as expansion. The predictive value of BHI—specifically the baseline-to-6-hour ratio—for hematoma expansion was analyzed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) methodology.ResultsThe breath-holding index (BHI) demonstrated comparable cerebrovascular reactivity across hemispheres in ICH patients at baseline, 6 hours, and 24 hours. However, the baseline-to-6-hour BHI ratio on the unaffected side was significantly associated with hematoma expansion (p = 0.023), with higher ratios observed in patients who expanded. Patients with hematoma expansion had a higher mean BHI ratio (1.4 ± 0.6) compared to those without expansion (1.0 ± 0.2), highlighting a clear distinction. ROC analysis identified an optimal cut-off of 1.2, yielding 58.3% sensitivity, 89.3% specificity, and an AUC of 0.8. The high specificity and negative predictive value (89.3%) indicate strong utility of the BHI ratio in ruling out hematoma expansion, supporting its role as a useful noninvasive predictor in early clinical decision-making.ConclusionThis study assessed MFV, PI, BHI, and the BHI ratio for predicting hematoma expansion in ICH patients. While MFV and PI showed no hemispheric differences, the BHI ratio was significantly associated with expansion. Higher baseline-to-6-hour BHI ratios on the unaffected side predicted increased risk, with high specificity and negative predictive value, supporting its use as a noninvasive tool to identify patients at low risk and guide early clinical decision-making. This study highlights the value of dynamic cerebrovascular assessment in ICH management.

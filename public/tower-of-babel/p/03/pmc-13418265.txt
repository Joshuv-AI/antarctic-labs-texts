A nurse-led perioperative intervention enhances self-care and supports long-term weight loss after bariatric surgery: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Cláudia Mendes, Manuel Carvalho, Catarina Martins, Maria Marques, Otília Zangão, João Gregório
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBariatric surgery is an effective intervention for severe obesity, but long-term success relies on sustained self-care behaviors, self-efficacy for exercise, and weight management. Nurse-led case management has shown promise in enhancing patient outcomes, yet robust evidence from randomized controlled trials remains limited.ObjectiveThis study evaluated the impact of a nurse-led case management intervention (NURLIFE) on pre- and post-operative weight loss and self-care behaviors in bariatric patients.MethodsA single-centre randomized controlled trial was conducted from January 2024 to August 2025 in a public hospital in southern Portugal. Forty patients were randomized to an intervention group (IG, n=20) receiving NURLIFE (personalized counselling, goal setting, and follow-up) or a control group (CG, n=20) receiving standard care. Outcomes, including weight, Self-Care of Chronic Illness Inventory (SCII), Self-Efficacy for Exercise (SEE), and Social Appearance Anxiety Scale (SAAS), were assessed at baseline, pre-surgery, and 12 months post-surgery. Data were analysed using mixed-model ANOVA and post-hoc tests with Bonferroni correction.ResultsThe IG exhibited significant pre-operative weight loss (7.36 ± 7.09 kg vs. -0.10 ± 7.33 kg in CG, p = 0.001), corresponding to a % total weight loss (TWL) of 5.88% in the intervention group versus -0.62% in the control group. At 12 months, the intervention group achieved a %TWL of 42.29% compared with 34.97% in the control group. Significant improvements were observed pre-operatively in SCII Monitoring (p = 0.001), Management (p= 0.001), and in SEE (p < 0.001) and in SCII Maintenance (p = 0.050) and SEE (p < 0.011) at 12 months. These results suggest a potential additional contribution of the nursing intervention in relation to surgery. In addition, sustained improvements in self-efficacy (SEE) and maintenance of self-care behaviors were observed.ConclusionThe NURLIFE intervention significantly enhanced pre-operative weight loss, which was maintained post-surgery, alongside improvements in self-care and exercise self-efficacy. These findings suggest a potential role of nurse-led case management into perioperative care to optimize bariatric outcomes.

Introduction

Bariatric surgery is the cornerstone treatment for severe obesity, resulting in significant weight loss and amelioration of obesity-related comorbidities, such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia and other cardiovascular diseases (Arterburn et al., 2020). However, the long-term success of bariatric surgery hinges on patients’ ability to adopt and sustain self-care behaviors, including adherence to dietary recommendations, regular physical activity, and psychological self-management (Peluso and Vanek, 2007; Marques et al., 2024).

Despite the benefits of surgery, many patients struggle with these lifestyle changes, leading to weight regain, reduced quality of life, and diminished self-efficacy for health-promoting activities (Pinto et al., 2017). This underscores the need for targeted postoperative interventions to support bariatric patients in navigating the complex behavioral changes required for sustained health outcomes.

Nurse-led case management has emerged as a promising strategy to enhance patient outcomes by providing individualized support, education, and follow-up (Mendes et al., 2023). Owing to their expertise in patient-centered care, nurses are uniquely positioned to address barriers to self-care, foster self-efficacy, and improve quality of life in chronic disease patients, including bariatric patients (Riegel et al., 2021; Marques et al., 2024).

Self-care, defined as the ability to perform activities that maintain health and well-being, is critical for bariatric patients to achieve optimal postoperative outcomes (Riegel et al., 2012). Similarly, self-efficacy for exercise, which reflects confidence in performing physical activity, is a key determinant of long-term weight maintenance and health-related quality of life (Bond et al., 2012). While preliminary studies suggest that nurse-led interventions can improve self-care and quality of life in bariatric populations, rigorous randomized controlled trials evaluating these outcomes are limited (Sen et al., 2025).

The emphasis on self-care within bariatric surgery is consistent with Orem’s Self-Care Deficit Nursing Theory, which conceptualizes nursing as a supportive system aimed at enhancing individuals’ ability to perform activities necessary to maintain health and well-being (Orem & Taylor, 2003). According to this framework, nursing interventions become particularly relevant when individuals experience limitations in their capacity to engage in effective self-care. In the context of bariatric surgery, patients are required to implement complex and sustained behavioral changes related to nutrition, physical activity, symptom monitoring, and psychological adaptation. Nurse-led case management may therefore play a central role in supporting patients’ self-care agency, promoting autonomy, and facilitating long-term adherence to postoperative recommendations.

Specialized perioperative nurses, with their expertise in patient-centered care and deep understanding of the unique physiological and psychological demands of bariatric surgery, are ideally positioned to address these challenges through structured case management (Doménech-Briz et al., 2020). Their role in providing individualized education, counselling, and follow-up is critical to fostering self-care, defined as the ability to perform health-maintaining activities, and enhancing self-efficacy for exercise, a key predictor of persistent weight loss and quality of life improvement (Riegel et al., 2021). Unlike generic postoperative care, interventions led by specialized perioperative nurses leverage their surgical and behavioral expertise to tailor support to the specific needs of bariatric patients, potentially improving adherence and outcomes (Barros et al., 2020).

While randomized controlled trials specifically on nurse-led case management in bariatric surgery remain limited, evidence from related contexts, such as post-interventional cardiac procedures, pre-surgical prehabilitation for major abdominal operations, and post-transplant transitions, provides insights into potential outcomes. These findings suggest that case management can yield benefits in symptom management, quality of life, and resource utilization in post-procedural or surgical settings (Herranz et al., 2023; Kreuzer et al., 2025).

In the context of bariatric surgery, where few randomized controlled trials have evaluated nurse-led case management, a structured and theory-informed approach was used to guide the development of the intervention. Drawing on principles from Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) (Peffers et al., 2018), the NURLIFE intervention was iteratively developed and refined to address the complexity of perioperative care. This approach supported the creation of a tailored nurse-led case-management program designed to improve self-management, self-efficacy for exercise, and weight management in patients undergoing bariatric surgery. The present study reports the evaluation of this intervention through a randomized controlled trial.

Methods

Study design

This single center randomized clinical trial was developed between January 2024 and August 2025, in an Integrated Responsibility Center for Obesity and Metabolic Surgery, at a public hospital in southern Portugal. The full protocol has been previously published (Santos et al., 2023). Patients who met the criteria for bariatric surgery were recruited during outpatient visits and were randomized into either an intervention group (IG) or a control group (CG). All participants provided written informed consent, which was approved by the institutional ethics committees. The NURLIFE intervention protocol began after the first evaluation and ended twelve months after surgery. Outcome assessments were performed at three time points after surgery and two times before surgery, by researchers. Participants

The research team invited consecutive potential participants to join the study during a consultation. Those who agreed to participate completed a free and informed consent form. The participants were then randomly assigned to either a control group or an intervention group. The program began after patients were enrolled for surgery and concluded 12 months after surgery. The intervention group received NURLIFE intervention, whereas the control group received routine care.

The inclusion criterion was enrolment for bariatric surgery at the surgical unit and agreement to participate. The exclusion criteria were enrolment for secondary bariatric procedures, surgical complications, and psychiatric diseases or neurological disorders.

Randomization

A surgeon presented the study information to eligible patients. If a patient verbally agreed to participate, the surgeon provided a detailed explanation of the study, discussed the optimal timing for the intervention, and obtained informed consent from all patients who agreed to participate.

Following completion of the initial assessment and informed consent, each participant was assigned an identification number. Randomization was performed using a simple draw method: participants drew their group allocation (intervention or control) from a box containing group assignments. This procedure ensured random allocation without prior knowledge of group assignment but did not incorporate formal allocation concealment procedures (Fig. 2). All collected data was tagged with a unique ID to safeguard participant confidentiality. To reduce this risk, randomization was overseen by a researcher who was not involved in intervention delivery; however, the possibility of selection bias cannot be entirely excluded and should be considered when interpreting the findings.Fig. 2Flowchart of the participants.Fig 2 dummy alt text

Sample

An a priori sample size calculation was performed using the G*Power software, the number of participants in each group sample was calculated as 17 cases by using two independent samples t tests and taking bilateral α=0.05 and 1-β=0.80. Considering a dropout rate of 15%, the minimum sample size was therefore derived as 40 patients. The sample size was calculated based on a two-sample t-test comparing groups at the primary endpoint, as a conservative approach given the lack of prior data to estimate within-subject correlations required for repeated-measures analyses.

Nursing case management

The CG receives standard care as provided by the Surgical Unit. In contrast, the IG received comprehensive nurse case management and standard care. This NURLIFE intervention began before surgery and concludes one year after surgery.

The nursing interventions started pre-surgery and included telephone contacts, nursing opportunities, and personalized health education. The initial consultation, which lasted approximately one hour, gathered information for developing personalized care plans and setting mutually agreed-upon targets. This session involved targeted health education and an assessment of self-care outcomes. A follow-up telephone contact was conducted before surgery to reassess the patient's healthcare plan; these calls last approximately 10 minutes. All activities focused on promoting self-care and developing healthy habits, with educational topics selected according to each patient's primary needs. All information acquired during the nurse case management process was recorded.

Intervention fidelity was supported through the use of structured consultation protocols, predefined educational content, standardized follow-up schedules, and periodic supervision meetings among the research team to ensure consistency in intervention delivery. Perioperative self-care intervention for bariatric patients – the NURLIFE intervention.

Intervention Group (IG): The intervention program took place during the extended perioperative period, starting with the patient's registration for surgery and ending one year after surgery. The interval between baseline assessment (A1) and surgery (A2) was approximately three months, corresponding to the routine preoperative preparation period at the institution. On the basis of other observational and experimental studies, it was developed with a combination of face-to-face follow-ups and teleconsultations.

A nurse case manager (specialist nurse in perioperative medical-surgical nursing) coordinates the care delivered to bariatric patients based on their health needs and interests. A comprehensive care plan was designed to focus on the patient´s problems, needs, and desires. It develops strategies and measurable goals, including specific outcomes based on the case management process

The disease management and patient empowerment program was based on quality standards in perioperative nursing. It promotes self-care in the sense that the professional practice of the specialist nurse is characterized by an anticipatory attitude and is based on four axes (Fig. 3) and in the principles of acting with professional responsibility and prudence. The monitoring and intervention prioritize recognizing others and their empowerment through the establishment of interpersonal relationships and the recognition of the abilities needed to develop knowledge and self-confidence.Fig. 3Main axis of NURLIFE intervention.Fig 3 dummy alt text

Main axis of NURLIFE intervention.

Control Group (CG): Patients in the control group received standard perioperative care as provided by the institution. This included routine preoperative assessment, standard education regarding the surgical procedure and postoperative recommendations, and scheduled follow-up consultations with the multidisciplinary team (surgeon, nutritionist, psychologist), according to usual clinical practice.

Implementation and data collection

The NURLIFE program for the intervention group had seven face-to-face moments (T0, T2, T3), and three non-face-to-face (T1, T4, T5) moments by teleconsultation for the intervention group.

After the initial consultation with the bariatric surgeon, each patient was referred to a specialist nurse and case manager, who conducted the first face-to-face consultation, followed by subsequent follow-up and clarification of identified needs. Following the flowchart in Fig. 4, previously developed by our team during the design stage (Mendes et al., 2025), the case manager functions as the central pillar of the process and monitors each process individually.Fig. 4Case Management proposal flowchart for the assistance process (Mendes et al., 2025).Fig 4 dummy alt text

Statistical methods

Data analysis was performed using JAMOVI (version 2.3.19). The normality of the data distribution for continuous variables was evaluated with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Parametric data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and nonparametric data are expressed as the median and interquartile range. Repeated-measures ANOVA was the primary analytical approach used to assess longitudinal group-by-time effects across all assessment points. Additional ANCOVA analyses of change scores (delta values) were subsequently performed to explore perioperative changes while adjusting for baseline values. Baseline differences between groups were assessed via independent samples t-tests (parametric) or Mann-Whitney U tests (non-parametric) for continuous variables, and Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests were used for categorical variables.

Repeated-measures ANOVA was used to account for the longitudinal nature of the data and to increase statistical efficiency by incorporating within-subject correlations across time points. In addition to longitudinal mixed-model analyses, change (delta) analyses between pre-operative and post-operative time points were conducted to explore the potential cumulative or additive effect of the intervention beyond the immediate impact of surgery. Analyses were conducted using available-case data. No imputation procedures were performed due to the limited amount of missing data and low attrition throughout follow-up.

Ethical considerations

This study followed all the norms and rules of the principles of good clinical practice according to the principles of the World Medical Association's Declaration of Helsinki and subsequent amendments. Informed consent was obtained from all participants before baseline data collection. Ethical approval was obtained from the ethics committee of the Hospital (CES062/22-HESE1635/21/09/2022).

SEE increased significantly from A1 to A4 and A5 (p < 0.001), and SAAS decreased significantly from A1 to A3, A4, and A5 (p < 0.001). In the CG, SCII Maintenance improved only from A4 to A5 (p = 0.038), and weight decreased significantly from A1 to A4 and A5 (p < 0.001). Between-group comparisons revealed that the IG had significantly higher SCII Maintenance and SEE, and lower SAAS and weight, at A4 and A5 (p < 0.05).

A notable finding was the significant pre-operative weight loss in the IG compared to the CG. From A1 to A2, the IG lost 7.36 ± 7.09 kg, while the CG showed a small inverse change of -0.10 ± 7.33 kg, yielding a highly significant between-group difference (p = 0.001, partial η2 = 0.35), indicative of a large effect size. This pre-operative advantage was maintained throughout the postoperative follow-up period, with the IG achieving a mean weight loss of 50.83 ± 13.55 kg from baseline to 12 months, compared with 42.35 ± 19.81 kg in the CG, corresponding to a mean between-group difference of 8.48 kg (95% CI -19.34 to 2.39; p = 0.033, partial η² = 0.18).

Significant improvements were also observed in SCII Monitoring, SCII Management and SEE. For SCII Monitoring, the IG improved by 6.24 ± 4.98 from A1 to A2, compared to 3.50 ± 7.15 in the CG (p = 0.050, partial η2 = 0.10), reflecting a medium effect size. Post-operatively, changes are different, and we have significant results on SCII Maintenance (p = 0.025, partial η2 = 0.13) and SEE (p <0.001, partial η2 = 0.44). For SEE, the IG increased by 5.48 ± 9.10 from A1 to A2 (p <0.001, partial η2 = 0.27), while the CG decreased by -0.67 ± 10.2, and this advantage grew to 11.09 ± 6.51 versus -7.13 ± 9.21 at A5 (p <0.001, partial η2 = 0.44), indicating a large effect.

While the longitudinal analysis did not show statistically significant between-group differences in weight (Table 6), the change analysis (Table 7) revealed significant differences in weight variation from pre-operative (ΔA1–A2) to post-operative periods (ΔA1–A5). This suggests that the nurse intervention effect may be cumulative and not fully captured in the overall longitudinal model, particularly due to the strong initial impact of surgery. In addition, sustained improvements in SEE and maintenance of self-care behaviors were observed.

While the longitudinal analysis (Table 6) evaluates overall changes across all time points, the delta analysis (Table 7) specifically examines variation between defined perioperative periods, which may explain differences in statistical significance.

Discussion

This randomized controlled trial evaluated the impact of the NURLIFE nurse-led case management intervention on weight, self-care behaviours, self-efficacy for exercise and social appearance anxiety in bariatric surgery patients. Participants receiving the NURLIFE intervention achieved significantly greater pre-operative weight loss, whereas no significant change was observed in the CG. Variation analyses revealed that the intervention group had greater preoperative weight loss and maintained significantly greater weight loss at 12 months, suggesting a potential additional contribution of the nursing intervention to adjuvant treatment in relation to surgery.

While longitudinal analyses did not show statistically significant between-group differences in weight, change (delta) analyses revealed a significant additional effect of the intervention. These findings highlight the importance of complementary analytical approaches when evaluating interventions in contexts where a dominant clinical effect (such as surgery) may mask incremental benefits.

Additionally, constant improvements in of self-care behaviours were observed. This maintenance of early weight loss underscores the efficacy of the NURLIFE intervention, which provided personalized pre-operative counselling, goal setting, and education by specialized perioperative nurses. These findings highlight the critical role of nurse-led case management in fostering early behavioural changes that contribute to continued weight loss post-surgery, aligning with evidence that structured pre-operative interventions enhance long-term bariatric outcomes (Güven and Akyolcu, 2020; Mendes et al., 2023). The persistent benefits observed in the IG reinforce the importance of specialized nursing interventions in optimizing perioperative care for bariatric patients.

The results suggest that the precision nursing intervention was associated with significant weight loss before surgery, which was not compensated for in the postoperative period, maintaining an average difference of approximately 8 kg between groups, although variability remained substantial, particularly in the control group, corresponding to a clinically meaningful difference in %TWL. These findings suggest that the nursing intervention may provide an additional contribution beyond the effect of surgery.

The lack of statistically significant differences in the longitudinal analysis may be explained by the strong and immediate effect of bariatric surgery on weight loss, which occurs in both groups and may mask between-group differences in the short term. In contrast, the delta analysis provides a more sensitive measure of change over time, allowing the detection of incremental effects attributable to the nurse-led intervention.

The preoperative weight loss observed in the intervention group confirms the effectiveness of nursing intervention in promoting behavioural and metabolic changes before surgery. It is important to note that, despite the dominant effect of bariatric surgery on overall weight loss, the difference between the groups remained throughout the follow-up, reinforcing the hypothesis that initial loss does not lead to a compensatory mechanism of lower postoperative loss. On the contrary, the results indicate parallel trajectories of weight loss after surgery, with maintenance of the initial advantage of the intervention group.

Covariance analysis of the deltas clarified this effect, demonstrating that total weight loss at 12 months was significantly higher in the intervention group, even after adjusting for baseline values. This finding is clinically relevant, since apparently modest differences in absolute weight can translate into significant improvements in metabolic control and cardiovascular risk in patients undergoing bariatric surgery. These results highlight the importance of using complementary analytical approaches when evaluating interventions in contexts where a dominant clinical effect, such as surgery, may obscure incremental benefits.

In addition to the anthropometric results, the present study provides important data on the potential behavioural and psychological mechanisms underlying the observed effect. A sustained improvement in SEE was observed in the intervention group, both in the preoperative period and at 12 months, with high effect sizes. This result suggests that nursing intervention may act by reinforcing the perception of control and ability to manage the disease, which is consistent with theoretical models of behavioural change applied to obesity. Additionally, a significant improvement in the maintenance of self-care behaviours was observed at 12 months in the intervention group.

Interestingly, this effect was not evident in the preoperative period, suggesting that the impact of adjuvant treatment on behaviour maintenance may emerge progressively as patients integrate and consolidate self-care strategies in the postoperative context. In contrast, more active components of self-care, such as monitoring and management, showed improvements predominantly in the preoperative period but were not maintained in the long term, possibly due to the standardization of clinical follow-up after surgery in both groups (Akkayaoğlu and Çelik, 2020).

No significant differences were observed between the groups in self-care confidence or weight-related social anxiety (SAAS), suggesting that nurse intervention has a specific effect on behaviours and self-efficacy, with no relevant impact on more stable or socially determined emotional dimensions. This specificity reinforces the plausibility of the proposed mechanisms and reduces the likelihood of spurious effects. A potential floor/ceiling effect may have reduced variability in SAAS scores at later assessments, limiting the ability to detect between-group differences.

The NURLIFE intervention may have supported patients in adopting dietary and lifestyle changes earlier, setting a foundation for continuous weight loss after surgery. This aligns with prior studies suggesting that structured pre-operative interventions enhance adherence to health-promoting behaviours (David et al., 2020; Güven and Akyolcu, 2020; Mendes et al., 2025).

The improvements in self-care and self-efficacy observed in the intervention group highlight the distinct contribution of perioperative nursing expertise. Perioperative nurses are uniquely positioned to bridge surgical and behavioural care, translating complex medical guidelines into actionable self-care behaviours (Ribeiro et al., 2023). Their role extends beyond education to include individualized assessment, reinforcement of self-management strategies, and psychosocial support (Güven and Akyolcu, 2020). By continuously monitoring progress and addressing barriers, the nurse case manager ensured that patients-maintained confidence and engagement in their recovery, which is critical to preventing postoperative complications and weight regain (Ligthart et al., 2015; Mendes et al., 2024).

The NURLIFE intervention aligns with international evidence that emphasizes the effectiveness of nurse-led interventions in chronic managing disease diseases and promoting surgical recovery (Doménech-Briz et al., 2020; Riegel et al., 2021). Unlike conventional postoperative education, this approach embedded the perioperative nurse as a consistent coordinator of care, responsible for promoting health literacy, risk reduction, and adaptation to lifestyle changes. Such individualized, longitudinal support likely explains the sustained improvements in weight, self-care and exercise self-efficacy observed up to 12 months post-surgery.

This randomized controlled trial shows that the NURLIFE nurse-led case management program significantly enhanced preoperative weight loss, self-care behaviours, and exercise self-efficacy score among bariatric surgery patients, when compared to standard care.

The integration of a specialist perioperative nurse as case manager throughout the surgical continuum, from preoperative preparation to postoperative recovery, appears to support behaviour change. Through anticipatory guidance, motivational counselling, and close follow-up, the perioperative nurse established a therapeutic alliance that optimized adherence, empowerment, and recovery.

These findings also reinforce the strategic importance of perioperative nurses in multidisciplinary bariatric teams. Acting as both educators and case managers, they ensure continuity of care across pre, intra, and postoperative settings, helping patients navigate the challenges of surgical preparation, physical recovery, and psychosocial adjustment. By leading structured case management programs such as NURLIFE, perioperative nurses can contribute directly to reducing complications, optimizing long-term weight outcomes, and improving patient satisfaction.

These results demonstrate the importance of ongoing guidance from the perioperative nurse, who provided personalized education, monitoring, and motivation during both preoperative and postoperative phases, showing the effectiveness of structured perioperative nursing care. Overall, the positive evolution of self-care, exercise self-efficacy, and weight outcomes reflects the impact of perioperative nurse-led case management in fostering behavioural adaptation, adherence to lifestyle changes, and early detection of postoperative difficulties.

Although statistically significant differences were observed in change analyses, these findings should be interpreted in the context of the substantial weight loss experienced by both groups following bariatric surgery. The intervention appears to provide a complementary or incremental contribution beyond the dominant physiological effect of surgery, rather than acting as an independent determinant of weight loss.

Implications for practice

The significant pre-operative weight loss in the IG highlights the potential of nurse-led case management to optimize bariatric surgery outcomes by initiating behavioural changes before surgery. Integrating structured nursing support into perioperative care could reduce surgical risks and enhance long-term weight loss and self-care. Perioperative nurses’ expertise in patient-centered care makes them ideal candidates to lead such interventions.

The findings further suggest that investing in perioperative nursing competencies, especially in behavioural coaching, motivational interviewing, and follow-up management, can substantially improve surgical outcomes. The integration of nurse-led case management within standardized bariatric pathways. This would not only enhance patient outcomes but also align with global healthcare priorities emphasizing patient-centered, cost-effective, and multidisciplinary care.

Limitations

The findings of this study should be interpreted in light of several limitations. First, the single-center design and relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the results and reduce the ability to detect more subtle effects in secondary outcomes. In addition, the sample size calculation was based on the primary weight-related outcome and was not specifically powered to detect differences in secondary outcomes, including self-care, self-efficacy for exercise, and social appearance anxiety. Consequently, findings related to these outcomes should be interpreted as exploratory and require confirmation in larger studies.

Furthermore, self-care, self-efficacy, and social appearance anxiety were assessed using self-report instruments. Because participants were aware of their group allocation, responses may have been influenced by social desirability or expectancy effects, potentially introducing response bias.

Although random allocation was implemented, formal allocation concealment procedures were not used. In addition, blinding of participants and intervention providers was not feasible due to the nature of the nurse-led intervention, and outcome assessors were not blinded.

Despite these limitations, the consistency of the findings across weight, BMI, self-care, and self-efficacy outcomes supports the potential value of perioperative nurse-led case management in bariatric care. Future multicenter studies with larger samples, longer follow-up periods, and more robust longitudinal analytical approaches are warranted to confirm and extend these findings.

Future directions

Future research should explore the adaptability and implementation process of the NURLIFE intervention and expand the evaluation to multicentre trials. Also of importance, is to investigate the long-term impact of pre-operative weight loss on surgical and health outcomes and explore patient experiences with nurse-led perioperative programs. Exploring long-term sustainability and the cost-effectiveness of the NURLIFE intervention and its applicability to diverse bariatric populations could support its integration into standard care. The team also wants to develop a self-care scale for bariatric patients, based on self-care theory.

Conclusion

The NURLIFE intervention initiated before bariatric surgery promotes early weight loss that is maintained over time, with no evidence of postoperative compensation, suggesting that the nursing intervention may provide an additional contribution beyond the effect of surgery.

This single-centre randomized controlled trial suggests that the NURLIFE nurse-led case management intervention is feasible within a real-world perioperative setting and may support preoperative weight loss, self-care maintenance, and exercise self-efficacy among patients undergoing bariatric surgery. Participants receiving the intervention achieved greater preoperative weight loss, which was maintained throughout follow-up, with no evidence of postoperative compensation. These findings suggest that perioperative nurse-led case management may provide an additional supportive contribution beyond the effects of surgery alone, potentially facilitating postoperative adaptation and weight management.

The observed benefits may be related, at least in part, to improvements in self-efficacy and the maintenance of self-care behaviours. Overall, the findings support the potential value of integrating adjuvant nurse-led interventions into the perioperative pathway of bariatric surgery. Nevertheless, given the single-centre design, the results should be interpreted with caution. Further multicentre studies with larger samples and longer follow-up periods are needed to confirm the effectiveness, reproducibility, and long-term sustainability of this approach in bariatric care.

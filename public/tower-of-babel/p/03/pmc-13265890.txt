Insights into the therapeutic potential of silymarin-encapsulated CME@ZIF-8 against CCl4-induced toxicity in broiler chicks
Authors: Zhang Jiabin, Mohammed Al-Rasheed, Abdul Rauf, Riaz Hussain, Elsayed S.I. Mohammed, Maha Abdullah Momenah, Mahmoud H.A. Mohamed, Ahmed M.A. Meligy, Mohamed S. Ahmed, Ahmed Alfifi, Ahmed Ezzat Ahmed, Dong Shiqi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) damages several organs by inducing oxidative stress. Silymarin exhibits strong antioxidant properties; however, its therapeutic potential is limited due to its low bioavailability. This study examined the protective efficacy of silymarin-encapsulated Cordia myxa extract-based ZIF-8 nanocomposites (CME@ZIF-8) against CCl4-induced toxicity in the brain, spleen, and intestine of broiler chicks. Following toxicity induction with CCl4, broiler chicks were treated with CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites loaded with silymarin (Sil@CME@ZIF-8). Absolute and relative weights of selected visceral organs, oxidative stress biomarkers (TBARS and ROS), antioxidant enzyme activities (CAT, POD, and SOD), histopathological changes, and genotoxicity, assessed using the comet assay, were evaluated in chickens. Results revealed significantly increased absolute and relative weight of visceral organs, higher values of oxidative stress biomarkers, antioxidant enzyme depletion, and severe microscopic alterations in the brain (neuronal necrosis), spleen (depletion of lymphoid tissue) and in intestine (necrosis of enterocytes and epithelium of villi) in CCl4-treated chickens. The results on genotoxicity determined using the comet assay exhibited extensive fragmentation of DNA in the isolated cells of visceral organs of CCl4-treated chickens. The administration of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (5, 10, and 15 mg/kg body weight) remarkably mitigated toxic/adverse effects in a dose-dependent manner by restoring organ weight, normalizing antioxidant defense system, improving oxidative biomarkers, reducing severity of histopathological abnormalities in visceral organs and DNA damage. Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites displayed substantial therapeutic potential against CCl4-induced multi-organ toxicity in broiler chicks. The nano-formulation enhanced the bioavailability and therapeutic potential of silymarin suggesting that it can be used in veterinary medicine and as a protective agent against chemical-induced toxicity in poultry.

Introduction

The poultry industry, particularly broiler and layer production, is a significant source of protein worldwide and has expanded substantially due to advancements in breeding, nutrition, and efficient production systems (Castro et al., 2023; Korver, 2023; Siddik et al., 2026). Among the poultry industries, broiler chicken has become prominent due to its intensive growth and excellent feed-to-meat ratio to meet the growing demand for eggs and meat (Cao et al., 2024; Bouzari et al., 2025; Hossain et al., 2025). The poultry industry has experienced significant growth across the globe and is known as the most rapidly growing sector in the food industry (Rahman et al., 2025; Imranuzzaman et al., 2025; Kyere et al., 2025). The extensive production of poultry has resulted in a range of stressors, including environmental stressors and chemical toxicants (Imranuzzaman et al., 2025; Hossain et al., 2025; Siddik et al., 2026). Carbon tetrachloride (CCl4), a common industrial product, is one of them and presents a serious risk because of its enduring toxicological effects on the health of livestock (Salman et al., 2022; Apalowo et al., 2024). Reports have indicated that several toxic compounds (natural and synthetic) disrupt normal metabolic/physiological functions through rapid and increased production of lipopolysaccharide and phenylacetic acid, leading to disruption of normal metabolism in birds (Han et al., 2023; Sarsembayeva et al., 2025). Oxidative stress (an imbalance between free radicals and antioxidant enzymes), being an important and key factor routinely induces cell apoptosis and is frequently/persistently monitored in response to exogenous compounds in toxicological studies (Yang et al., 2021).

Oxidative stress due to a variety of synthetic, natural, and industrial compounds may influence the normal physiological functions in exposed organisms via the production of reactive oxygen species (Younis et al., 2023; Gill et al., 2025; Muhyuddin et al., 2025). The induction of oxidative stress in animals interferes with cellular homeostasis, resulting in lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage of various organ systems (Ouyang et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2016; Baradaran et al., 2019). Although CCl4 mainly induces physiological and metabolic disorders in the liver, it may also induce adverse effects on the brain, spleen, and intestine (Habashy et al., 2021). Studies have reported that CCl4 is converted to toxic trichloromethyl radical through metabolic activation of hepatic microsomal cytochrome P450 enzymes, which ultimately induce oxidative stress, destroying hepatocytes and disturbing hepatic functions in chickens (Zheng et al., 2016; Baradaran et al., 2019). CCl4-induced neurotoxicity may impair functions of the brain and brain health via crossing the blood-brain barrier and causing oxidative stress (El-haskoury et al., 2018; Younas et al., 2024). Likewise, the toxicity of the spleen and intestine is characterized by a lowered immune response, decreased growth, and an inflammatory condition that further deteriorates the health issues of target and non-target exposed organisms, including birds (Amer et al., 2022; Iqbal et al., 2024). The adverse toxic effects negatively affect the health and welfare of the animals and reduce their productivity, leading to a loss of economic value

To reduce these toxic effects, natural products, including silymarin, have gained interest with regard to their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects (Akheratdoost et al., 2024; Ike, 2025; Hayat et al., 2025; Masood et al., 2025). Silymarin is a derivative of the seed of Silybum marianum (milk thistle) and is well-known for its antioxidant properties and in its capacity to counteract free radicals (Clichici et al., 2020). In different studies, it has been noted that silymarin enhances cell protection, regulates the inflammatory process, and limits the oxidative stress in most biological systems. Its low bioavailability and low water solubility have limited its overall applications (Mukhtar et al., 2021). The development of new nanotechnology in recent years can help to resolve the challenges by improving the delivery and efficacy of bioactive compounds such as silymarin (Yu et al., 2023; Altaf et al., 2025).

The most interesting new nanocarrier is metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), particularly zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIF-8), due to their high porosity, biocompatibility, and pH-responsive drug delivery (Sasaki et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022; Saif et al., 2025), and it has inherent antioxidant activity (Amur et al., 2025). Various theoretical benefits are associated with the encapsulation of silymarin in ZIF-8 MOFs: the drug is protected against degradation, there is an increased solubility and bioavailability of the drug, the release kinetics of silymarin are prolonged, and silymarin and zinc may display synergistic properties (Tu et al., 2024; Saif et al., 2024a; Saif et al., 2024b). Moreover, the MOF particle size can be reduced to the nano-level, potentially allowing cells to absorb them and enhance tissue permeation relative to traditional formulations (Shahzaib et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024; Prasuna et al., 2025).

Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposite has been shown to have increased solubility, stability, target drug delivery, and controlled drug release, ultimately resulting in prolonged therapeutic effectiveness at low dosages (Yu et al., 2023; Saif et al., 2024a). These positive characteristics make Sil@CME@ZIF-8 a potential nanocarrier system with the promise of reducing CCl4-induced oxidative stress in visceral organs such as the brain, spleen, and intestine of broiler chickens. Hence, the current research can be useful for exploring the possibilities of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites in the reduction of oxidative stress and inflammatory effects caused by CCl4 toxicity in broilers. In assessing their protective effects on neural, immune, and intestinal tissues, this study aims to develop knowledge on the nanotechnology-based interventions to improve the health and productivity of broilers under toxicological stress.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Carbon tetrachloride (CCl4, High-purity of 99.9%) was purchased and prepared according to previous study (Aldawood et al., 2026). All the information regarding the synthesis and characterization of this nano-formulation is available in our previously published studies (Liu et al., 2023; Saif et al., 2024a; Saif et al., 2024b). Other reagents and chemicals used for treatment and analysis were of analytical grade and employed as received without further purification.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Islamia University of Bahawalpur. All the procedures and experimentations were executed ensuring the welfare and ethical treatment of birds.

Bird model

A total of 105-day-old chickens were obtained from a reputable commercial poultry supplier (Punjab Poultry Farms) in the district of Bahawalpur, Punjab province, Pakistan. All the chicks were found to be active and healthy. All the selected chicks had uniform size and body mass. The chicks were carefully transported in order to minimize stress and to ensure optimum health conditions. After transportation, the birds were reared for one week of acclimatization in a house with environmental control at the temperature: 32±2°C, which was lowered to 25±2 °C; humidity: 60±5%; light-dark cycle: 12:12 hours (Frerichs et al., 2022). The chickens were placed in wire cages. Each chicken/poultry cage was designed to accommodate 15 birds. Before the start of the experiment, all the cages, floor, walls, and ceiling were disinfected regularly. The feed provided to experimental chicks met the nutritional standards recommended by the National Research Council.

Group allocation and treatment protocol

On the 7th day of acclimatization, all broilers were individually and randomly weighed. The experimental chickens were assigned to a completely randomized design (CRD) and were divided into seven equal experimental groups (A-F), and each group contained 15 chickens. The chickens reared in different groups were treated with CCL4, silymarin, and Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites (Table 1). The experiment was executed in duplicate, and each experiment was considered as a replicate. Physical parameters

Feed intake was monitored daily, and all the experimental chicks were frequently monitored for any obvious clinical, behavior and physical abnormalities such as diarrhea, abnormal posture, anorexia, and lethargy. Necropsy of chicks and separation of visceral organs

For the removal of visceral organs like the brain, spleen, and intestine, all the chicks were sacrificed. At necropsy, the visceral organs were immediately removed, washed, weighed, and divided into two parts. The one part of the visceral organ was kept at −80°C to estimate oxidative and antioxidant enzymes, while one part of these organs was preserved in 10% formalin for serving histopathological findings (Ali et al., 2023).

Tissue preparation and biochemical analyses

For estimation of oxidative and antioxidant enzyme activity, chickens were sacrificed on different days of the experiment, and visceral organs (brain, spleen, and intestine) were separated from each bird. The homogenates of visceral organs were prepared in 2 mL of 100 mM PBS and were centrifuged for 5 minutes at 6000xg. The supernatant was taken out and kept at 4°C to undergo further examination (Akram et al., 2022; Kanwal et al., 2024). Oxidative stress biomarkers such as TBARS (nmol/TBARS formed/mg protein/min) and ROS (Optical density) in the brain, spleen, and intestine were assessed using a UV-spectrophotometer at 532 nm wavelength (Akram et al., 2022) and 505 nm (Al-Saeed et al., 2023), respectively. The activities of different antioxidative enzymes (CAT; units/min, POD; units/min, and SOD; units/mg protein) were evaluated at 240 nm (Kakkar et al., 1984; Rehman et al., 2021), 470 nm (Kanwal et al., 2024; Alam et al., 2025), and 560 nm (Wu et al., 2021; Akram et al., 2022), respectively, with a UV-vis spectrophotometer (752 UV). Each reading was taken four times every 15 seconds.

Histopathological evaluation

For histopathological analyses, paraffin-embedded sections from the visceral organs of each bird were processed. Approximately 4-5 µm thick sections embedded in paraffin wax were obtained from each of the visceral organs of the bird using a microtome, dehydrated in ascending grades of ethanol, and stained using Hematoxylin and eosin (Paul et al., 2022). A compound microscope (IRMECO IR-850) was used to examine various sections of visceral organs at 40X magnification, and photomicrographs were taken with a DSC-W810 camera. Various histological sections of brain, spleen, and intestine of each experimental chick were examined with the help of light microscope and lesion scores were converted into numerical values (– = 0, Mild: + = 1 (25%), moderate: ++ = 2 (50%), severe: +++ = 3 (75%) and very severe ++++ = 4(100%).

Genotoxicity assessment

The extent of DNA damage in isolated cells of the brain, spleen, and intestine in various treatment groups was assessed using the single-cell gel electrophoresis technique/comet assay according to a previously documented procedure (Ghaffar et al., 2020). At the time of necropsy, the visceral organs (brain, spleen, and intestine) were immediately removed and placed in chilled normal saline. After that, about 200 mg of tissue from each bird of each group was homogenized separately and centrifuged at 4000xg for 10 minutes. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet containing the cells was mixed with 0.8% low-melting-point agarose (LMPA). The isolated cells were mixed in agarose, and a thin layer containing cells was spread on a glass slide pre-coated with 0.1% normal-melting-point agarose (NMPA) and allowed to solidify at 4 °C for 10 minutes. A further coat of LMPA (75 µL) was added. For lysing purposes, all the prepared slides were placed in chilled lysing buffer (10 minutes). Electrophoresis was carried out for 30 minutes in freshly prepared chilled electrophoresis buffer (300 mM NaOH/1 mM EDTA; pH to ensure >13) using 25 V, and then all the prepared slides were stained using ethidium bromide stain according to the earlier protocol (Singh et al., 1988). The slides were observed with a fluorescence microscope at 1000x magnification. The extent of DNA damage was determined by examination of 400-500 cells per slide/bird and was studied based on the appearance of a diffuse cloud or a comet tail of DNA that migrates in the direction of the electric field or fluorescence around the nucleus (Alam et al., 2025).

Statistical analysis

To evaluate the treatment effects of formulated Sil@CME@ZIF-8, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post-hoc test was carried out using the IBM SPSS version 22.0 software. The results from the control and treated groups were presented as means ± standard deviation of all tested parameters, such as absolute and relative organ weight, level of oxidative and antioxidant enzymes, and percentage of DNA damage. The significance level was considered at P ≤ 0.05.

Biochemical indices of the brain, spleen and intestine

The results on assessment of the oxidative stress biomarkers in the brain (Fig. 2), spleen (Fig. 3) and intestine (Fig. 4) tissues of control and the experimental groups at days 10 and 20 treatment showed substantial differences and are presented in Table S2. The results on contents of oxidative stress measured in current study revealed a significantly (P ≤ 0.05) higher production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) in the homogenates of the brain (ROS: 0.72 ± 0.09 and 0.85 ± 0.07; TBARS: 6.90 ± 0.17 and 6.95 ± 0.16), spleen (ROS: 2.01±0.00 and 2.65±0.08; TBARS: 2.73±0.00 and 2.83±0.08) and in intestine of chickens (ROS: 4.30±0.03 and 5.10±0.05; TBARS: 0.74±0.03 and 0.81±0.05) at days 10 and 20 in groups B and C as compared to control group A. Treatment with conventional silymarin at a dose of 100 mg/kg body weight (group C) non-significantly reduced the oxidative stress indices demonstrating its moderate protective effect. The administration of 10 mg/kg body weight of MOF-only (group D) did not show obvious deviation of normal values of biomarkers of oxidative stress indicating the safety and biocompatibility of the nanocarrier system. Conversely, the chickens reared in groups (E, F and G) exposed to Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (5, 10 and 15 mg) respectively demonstrated a gradual reduction of ROS and TBARS contents indicating the dose-dependent protective effects of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (Table S2). The results unveiled that the highest dose of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (15 mg; group G) exhibited more protective effects and restored the oxidative balance almost near to control values in brain (ROS: 0.44 ± 0.03 and 0.31 ± 0.03; TBARS: 4.81 ± 0.15 and 4.62 ± 0.16), spleen (ROS: 1.77±0.00 and 1.78±0.00; TBARS: 2.38±0.00 and 2.37±0.00) and intestine (ROS: 3.76±0.05 and 3.64±0.03; TBARS: 0.48±0.03 and 0.40±0.03). Antioxidant enzyme activity assessment

The results on the status of antioxidant biomarkers measured in homogenates of brain, spleen and intestine of broilers reared in different treated groups are presented in Table S2. The level of antioxidant indicators such as CAT (Brain: 2.30 ± 0.04 and 2.10 ± 0.09; spleen: 2.17±0.00 and 2.77±0.43; intestine: 1.35±0.12 and 1.24±0.13), POD (brain: 1.03 ± 0.04 and 1.02 ± 0.05; Spleen: 2.02±0.00 and 2.37±0.43; Intestine: 0.50±0.06 and 0.40±0.04), and SOD (brain: 1.60 ± 0.04 and 1.51 ± 0.06; spleen: 2.01±0.11 and 1.61±0.06; Intestine: 0.48±0.05 and 0.40±0.04) significantly decreased (P ≤ 0.05) in the CCl4 group (group B) in a time dependent manner showing induction of oxidative stress. Following treatment with Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites in chickens reared in different groups such as group E (5 mg/kg), group F (10 mg/kg) and group G (15 mg/kg) and intoxicated with CCl4, the levels of these antioxidant biomarkers substantially reversed to that of control group A.

The contents of antioxidant biomarker in CCl4-toxicated chickens and co-treated with free silymarin (group C) also exhibited protective effects. However, the results revealed significantly (P ≤ 0.05) mitigation of toxic effects in chickens of group G administered 15 mg/kg Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites. The results indicated that the antioxidant biomarkers in visceral organs of chickens including CAT (Brain: 3.47 ± 0.06 and 4.68 ± 0.07; Spleen: 2.33±0.03 and 3.05±0.08; Intestine: 2.32±0.12 and 2.44±0.16), POD (Brain: 1.32 ± 0.06 and 1.38 ± 0.04; Spleen: 2.33±0.03 and 3.05±0.08; Intestine: 0.81±0.02 and 0.94±0.05) and SOD (Brain: 2.24 ± 0.04 and 2.29 ± 0.07; Spleen: 2.23±0.01 and 1.94±0.03; Intestine: 0.80±0.05 and 1.15±0.08) reversed in group C. Furthermore, the administration of 10 mg/kg body weight dose of MOF-only in chickens of group D did not indicate significant difference to that of control chickens reared in group A.

The present investigation demonstrated that the level of antioxidant biomarkers was restored in the chickens treated with Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites in following order: 5 < 10 < 15 mg/kg body weight as depicted in Figs. 2–4.

Histopathological evaluation

Monitoring of histopathological alterations in visceral organs/tissues are known as an important indicator in toxicological studies for screening of toxic materials/environmental pollutants and development of mitigation strategies. Histopathological analysis of tissue sections in the control group revealed that normal histological structure was maintained in brain and spleen of chickens. At microscopic examination, severe histopathological lesions were observed in visceral organs including brain and spleen of chickens exposed to CCl4 in group B as indicated in Table 2. Various sections of brain of chickens reared in group B exhibited atrophic nuclei of neurons, enlargement cytoplasm of neurons, eccentric nuclei of neurons, degeneration of neurons and necrosis of neurons, edema and disorganization of neurons (Fig. 5). The different sections of brain of chickens placed in group D exposed to MOF alone did not induce histoarchitecture changes suggesting the biocompatibility of the carrier system. Microscopic examination of brain of chickens of groups E, F and G (CCl4 + silymarin@ZIF-8 at 5, 10 and 15 mg doses respectively exhibited dose-dependent neuro-protective effects. The supplementation of silymarin to chickens reared in group C moderately mitigated the toxic effects of CCl4. The severity of histopathological lesions in brain of chickens was substantially reversed in groups (F and G) treated with higher doses of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (10 mg/kg and 15 mg/kg body mass). Histopathological observation of different sections of spleen of chickens of group B showed congestion, degeneration and depletion of red and white pulp, depletion of splenic cells and inflammatory material (Fig. 6). The severity of histopathological ailments in spleen was progressively improved in chickens treated with silymarin@ZIF-8 highlighting enhanced regeneration, restoration of tissue damage and reduced inflammatory responses. The degree of restoration of tissue damage in visceral organs at Sil@CME@ZIF-8 doses was 15 > 10 > 5 mg/kg. It is important to note that the visceral organs of broilers that were given CME@ZIF-8 at 10 mg/kg body weight (group D) retained much of the normal histological features. Intestinal morphometry

The results on intestinal morphometric analyses of chickens exposed to CCl4 indicated severe degradation of the intestinal mucosa with significant decrease in villous height (µm) and crypt depth (µm) of the duodenum, jejunum and ileum compared to the control group (Table S3). These results are reflective of impaired mucosal integrity and poor absorptive capability of intestinal tissues. Silymarin monotherapy significantly reduced the CCl4-induced lesions resulting in a partial recovery of intestinal architecture. The chickens exposed to MOF only in group D did not reveal any prominent alterations in intestinal tissues. Results showed that notable protective effects (dose-dependent) were observed in chickens that were administered Sil@CME@ZIF-8. Increased doses of the silymarin-impregnated MOFs recovered villous height and crypt depth closer to control values than the free silymarin (Fig. 7). Discussion

ROS and TBARS are highly reactive substances that causes injury to DNA, lipids, proteins and other biological components (González-Arostegui et al., 2023). SOD, POD and CAT are considered as useful antioxidative enzymes that protect tissues from damage caused by free radicals (Rao et al., 2025; Caragea et al., 2025). In present study, the absolute and relative weight of brain and spleen was significantly increased in chickens treated with CCL4. The increase in relative weight of brain and spleen in CCl4 treated chickens reflects the induction of inflammatory process. It is tempted to speculate that the estimation of weight of spleen is considered as a reliable and sensitive parameter of CCl4-induced toxicity and the effectiveness of the therapy (Habashy et al., 2021). The increase in absolute and relative weight of visceral organs may be due to the treatment-induced inflammatory response leading to accumulation of inflammatory exudate (Gómez-Apo et al., 2021; Iqbal et al., 2025). The significantly increased weight of brain and spleen of chickens exposed to CCL4 in our study is suggestive of induction of oxidative stress induced by CCL4 (Saif et al., 2024a; Saif et al., 2024b). Conversely, the absolute and relative weight of brain and spleen of the chickens reared in groups (E, F and G) exposed to Sil@CME@ZIF-8 (5, 10, and 15 mg) respectively demonstrated a gradual reduction indicating the dose-dependent protective effects of Sil@CME@ZIF-8. The therapeutic measures exhibited that Sil@CME@ZIF-8 has useful antioxidative potential than silymarin alone revealing that MOF-based delivery enhances therapeutic potential and bioavailability against CCl4-induced oxidative damage in chickens. The ameliorative potential of Sily@CME@ ZIF-8 might also be related to increase antioxidant and immune-boosting potential of cordia myxa and silymarin leading to improved gut health in broilers (Hossain et al., 2025; Siddik et al., 2026). Previously, increased bioavailability and therapeutic potential of CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites suggesting reliable drug delivery system has been reported in albino rats (Saif et al., 2024a; Saif et al., 2024b). The therapeutic potential of collagen particles coated with silymarin that restored the enzyme level to normal values have also been reported in animals (Rathore et al., 2020). Moreover, silymarin-based hepatoprotective potential of PEGylated-Sily@ZnFe2O4 in rats (Waqas et al., 2025) and ameliorative potential of silymarin and vanillic acid loaded silver against neurotoxicity has been recorded (El Rabey et al., 2023). The oxidative stress biomarkers significantly enhanced while the contents of antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD and CAT) reduced in visceral organs (brain and spleen) in chickens of group B (CCL4 intoxicated) while partial ameliorative potential of silymarin on oxidative stress in chickens of group C and a dose dependent ameliorative potential of Sily@CME@ ZIF-8 in chickens of groups (E, F and G) was recorded. Previously, it has also been recorded that the contents of antioxidant enzymes were substantially restored in different tissues of rats exposed to PEGylated-Sily@ZnFe2O4 composites and the contents of ROS and TBARS were remarkably reduced (Waqas et al., 2025). The protective potential of PEG-Sily@CME@ZIF-8 MOFs against CCL4 induced oxidative stress and cardiac toxicity in rats is also suggestive of rapid and reliable drug delivery (Saif et al., 2024a). The mitigation of toxic effects of CCL4 in visceral organs of birds in current study might also be related to proper nutrient absorption, improved activity of digestive enzyme, enhanced immune functions and improved lipid metabolism in chickens due to PEG-Sily@CME@ ZIF-8 treatments (Hossain et al., 2024; Siddik et al., 2026). Moreover, the protective potential of silymarin and its fabricated nanoparticles against aluminum-induced oxidative stress, genotoxicity and hyperlipidemia have also been reported (El-Demerdash et al., 2024). The moderate histopathological alterations in brain, spleen and intestinal tissues of chickens administered different doses of PEG-Sily@CME@ ZIF-8 nanocomposites could be related to enhanced immune functions due to presence of bioactive compounds. Previously, increased aggregation of lymphocytes, increased genesis and dense follicular cortex in Bursa, larger thymic lobules and widened tunica muscularis were observed in response to synbiotic treatment in chickens (Hossain et al., 2025; Siddik et al., 2026).

The histoarchitectural ailments in brain and spleen of chickens might be linked due to oxidative stress and depletion of antioxidant enzymes. Previously, hepatocellular apoptosis linked with depletion of antioxidant indexes and increased expression of cytokines leading to induction of oxidative stress has been reported (Liu et al., 2018). Moreover, studies have highlighted that various toxicants induce injury in tissues via dysfunctions and disorders in dynamics of mitochondria, stress to endoplasmic reticulum and disturbances in cell cycle linked with induction of oxidative stress (Liu et al., 2018; Yin et al., 2023; Fatima et al., 2025; Naseer et al., 2025). The effects of inflammatory reactions were assessed through the measurements of the oxidative stress biomarkers such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) and thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) which are commonly known as reliable and useful indicators of oxidative stress and inflammatory biomarkers (Hermida-Ameijeiras et al., 2023; Ghazizadeh et al., 2024). The noticeably increased DNA damage seen after the intraperitoneal administration of CCl4 is probably attributed to the higher production of free radicals that causes oxidative stress resulting in the subsequent genotoxicity (Alrashdi et al., 2025). The microscopic alterations and increased frequency of DNA damages observed in brain, spleen and intestine in chickens can be linked to rapid and over production of reactive oxygen species and lipid peroxidation product leading to damage to mitochondrial kinetic, activation of process of autophagy and mitochondrial lysosomes as well as release of calcium ions responsible for formation of macrophage extracellular traps (Yin et al., 2023). In previous published literature, it has been recorded that harmful flora and toxic metabolites disrupt vascular barrier in intestinal and induce apoptosis via upregulation of Caspase 8 (Yin et al., 2023). The administration of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nano-drug prominently mitigated the neurotoxicity genotoxicity, splenic and intestinal toxicity in chickens. The meliorative potential of PEGylated-Sily@ZnFe2O4 and Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nano-drug against CCl4 hepatotoxicity, neurotoxicity and genotoxicity in albino rats was elevated (Saif et al., 2024a; Saif et al., 2024b; Waqas et al., 2025). These findings are similar to other literature that has documented CCl4-induced damage to DNA in various body organs (Ahsan et al., 2020). The increased protection potential of silymarin-phospholipid complex in broiler chickens against mycotoxin has also been reported (Tedesco, 2004). Similar to findings of our study regarding the mitigation of toxic effects induced by CCL4, potential of silymarin to mitigate the toxic effects on protein and lipid metabolism (lower serum content of MDA), decreased serum activity of liver enzymes (ALP and AST) and potential to alleviate the oxidative stress in poultry has been reported (Baradaran et al., 2019). The decreased severity/reversal of histopathological alterations (moderate) in brain, spleen and intestine of chickens of group (F and G) treated with higher doses of CME@ZIF-8 could be related to presence of antioxidant properties and pharmacological activities such as flavonoids, phenolic acids, lignans, phenolic glycosides, fatty acids and anthocyanins (El-Nashar et al., 2025). The histopathology results are correlated with previous studies where silymarin-loaded gold nanoparticles were used to reduce the severity of microscopic abnormalities induced by CCl4 (Abdullah et al., 2021). Furthermore, hepatoprotective, wound healing, hypoglycemic, immune-modulator and enzyme inhibitory potential of cordia myxa against butyrylcholinesterase (BChE), cetylcholinesterase (AChE) and tyrosinase has also been reported (El-Nashar et al., 2025).

Conclusion

From the findings of our study, it was estimated that the Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposite has the substantial therapeutic capacity to alleviate the toxic effects induced by CCl4 in broiler chickens. The therapeutic capacity of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 was high compared to the traditional silymarin in terms of its protective potential on brain, spleen and intestine of CCl4-induced toxicity in chickens. The protective potential of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites were confirmed by prominent attenuation of CCl4-induced cellular changes, restoration of activities of different antioxidant enzymes, and substantial decrease in induction of DNA damage in chickens in a dose-dependent manner. The protective potential of Sil@CME@ZIF-8 showed the best therapeutic effects (15 mg/kg) compared to the traditional silymarin, suggesting the increased bioavailability and targeted effect of the drug due to loading in MOF. These results indicate that Sil@CME@ZIF-8 nanocomposites may be an effective agent in safeguarding chickens against the adverse impact of environmental toxicants.

Future studies are required to confirm the results by epidemiological trials on a large scale, with special focus on obtaining data on pharmacokinetics and bioavailability of the loaded drug because it lack of pharmacokinetics and bioavailability data.

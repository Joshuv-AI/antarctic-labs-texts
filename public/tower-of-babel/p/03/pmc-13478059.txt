ZMW-RSVP: a time-frequency prior-guided normalization-free RSVP-BCI decoding model
Authors: XiaoZhong Geng, ZhangHuai Xiong, Ping Yu, TianZe Dai, Shuai Wu, Han Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP)-based brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) are valuable for target detection and rehabilitation because they require no motor involvement and can elicit reliable event-related potentials (ERP). However, single-trial EEG decoding remains challenging due to low signal-to-noise ratio and substantial inter-subject variability. To address the limitations of conventional approaches that rely on handcrafted features and to further explore internal feature transformation in single-trial ERP decoding, this paper proposes an RSVP-BCI decoding model, termed ZMW-RSVP, by extending a time-frequency Transformer framework with oscillatory gated attention and a normalization-free dynamic activation network. The proposed model incorporates neuroscience-inspired priors through a multi-band oscillatory gating mechanism, with an emphasis on theta-related activity associated with RSVP/P300 processing, and further guides attention allocation via a time-window bias. In addition, the model replaces internal LayerNorm modules with Dynamic Tanh, which provides an element-wise internal transformation without explicit mean-variance normalization and is evaluated as a task-related alternative for ERP-related discriminative feature modeling. Experiments on the Tsinghua RSVP dataset and the NeuBCI Target Retrieval RSVP-EEG dataset demonstrate that ZMW-RSVP achieves improved classification performance under the evaluated cross-subject settings, validating the effectiveness of the proposed approach for single-trial RSVP decoding.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-56317-8.Keywords: EEG, RSVP-BCI, Event-related potential, Dynamic Tanh, TransformerSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Neuroscience

Introduction

A brain–computer interface (BCI) converts neural activity into control commands by decoding neurophysiological signals such as electroencephalography (EEG), and it has shown broad potential in assistive communication, rehabilitation, and human–machine interaction1,2. Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP) can elicit reliable event-related potentials (ERP), especially the P300 component, and therefore plays an important role in target detection and information retrieval tasks3,4.

In RSVP paradigms, target stimuli typically appear at a high rate and with a low probability at the same visual location. When a participant detects a target, a characteristic P300 response is evoked, and its temporal waveform and amplitude variations provide key physiological evidence for single-trial discrimination4. However, single-trial EEG decoding for RSVP-BCI still faces two major challenges. First, the signal-to-noise ratio is low, microvolt-level EEG can be easily overwhelmed by environmental noise, EMG and EOG artifacts, and changes in electrode contact, which makes ERP features unstable at the single-trial level5–7. Second, substantial inter-subject variability exists: baseline oscillatory activity, ERP amplitude, and latency can differ markedly across subjects, which limits the stability and transferability of conventional cross-subject models8,9. To cope with these issues, traditional approaches often rely on linear discriminant frameworks with spatial filtering and handcrafted features11. For instance, Hierarchical Discriminant Component Analysis (HDCA), a widely used baseline for RSVP tasks11, extracts discriminant components within multiple time windows and integrates them over time. HDCA can be robust in scenarios with weak ERPs and high noise, but it is limited in modeling complex time–frequency coupling and nonlinear interactions7.

With the adoption of deep learning in BCI research12,13, end-to-end learning has provided stronger representational capacity for EEG spatiotemporal features. For example, EEGNet is a lightweight convolutional network that can learn spatial filtering and temporal dynamics with a small number of parameters, and it has been widely used in RSVP-BCI settings14. Building on this idea, EEG-Conformer combines local modeling by convolution with global dependency modeling by Transformers, enabling richer long-range temporal relationships and cross-channel interactions to be captured15,16. For the characteristics of RSVP-BCI, more structured Transformer variants have also been proposed17–19. TSformer employs a dual-stream time-domain and frequency-domain architecture and integrates features via cross-view interaction and attention-based fusion, achieving strong discriminative performance in multi-subject RSVP decoding. Despite these architectural advances in Transformer-based modeling, two issues remain insufficiently examined in single-trial RSVP-P300 decoding20,23,24. First, most existing attention mechanisms are general-purpose data-driven designs and do not explicitly model latency windows and low-frequency rhythms that are physiologically relevant to RSVP. Second, although normalization is commonly used to stabilize training, the role of internal normalization within Transformer blocks in single-trial RSVP/P300 ERP representation learning remains insufficiently examined25,27.

Based on the above considerations, this paper proposes ZMW-RSVP, an RSVP-BCI decoding model built on a time-frequency Transformer framework and equipped with neurophysiological priors together with a DyT-based replacement of internal LayerNorm modules. First, the proposed attention module explicitly decomposes and gates multi-band features, and it introduces an θ-band enhancement strategy (4–8 Hz), which is closely related to P300 generation28,29. In addition, the model applies a P300 time-window bias (250–500 ms) to guide attention allocation, enabling the model to focus on target-relevant discriminative information in both the time and frequency domains4,30,31. Second, the model replaces internal LayerNorm modules with DyT (Dynamic Tanh), an element-wise parameterized transformation that avoids explicit mean-variance normalization during the forward pass and provides an alternative internal feature transformation for the Transformer encoder32.

To evaluate the proposed method, comparative experiments, normalization-strategy comparisons, ablation studies, sensitivity analyses, cross-dataset testing, and visualization analyses are conducted on the Tsinghua RSVP benchmark dataset and the NeuBCI Target Retrieval RSVP-EEG dataset33,34. These experiments are designed to assess the overall decoding performance, the contribution of each module, the stability of the selected prior weights, and the interpretability of the learned time-frequency representations.

Experiment and results

Comparative methods

In this paper, several classical baseline methods are used for systematic comparison with ZMW-RSVP. These methods are as follows:

HDCA is a commonly used linear baseline method in RSVP-BCI. It first divides the EEG signal into time windows, extracts spatial discriminative components for target and non-target events within each window, and then performs time fusion on the scores from each window to generate the final classification score11. This method is robust in scenarios where the ERP signal is weak and noise is high in single-trial data, and it is widely used as a baseline algorithm.

This method is robust in scenarios where the ERP signal is weak and noise is high in single-trial data, and it is widely used as a baseline algorithm.

EEGNet is a lightweight convolutional neural network designed for end-to-end learning of spatial, temporal, and spectral features of EEG. Its convolutional structure effectively extracts multi-dimensional features with a small number of parameters, demonstrating good generalization and robustness in RSVP and other BCI tasks14.

EEGNet is a lightweight convolutional neural network designed for end-to-end learning of spatial, temporal, and spectral features of EEG. Its convolutional structure effectively extracts multi-dimensional features with a small number of parameters, demonstrating good generalization and robustness in RSVP and other BCI tasks14.

EEG-Conformer is a model that combines convolutional neural networks and Transformer architectures, used to simultaneously model both local spatiotemporal features and global dependencies in EEG. This method extracts local features via convolution and captures long-range relationships using self-attention mechanisms, resulting in stronger feature representation and classification performance in various BCI tasks15.

TCFormer17 is a temporal convolutional Transformer for EEG decoding. It combines a multi-kernel convolutional neural network for spatiotemporal feature extraction, a Transformer encoder with grouped-query attention for contextual modeling, and a temporal convolutional head for decoding. This architecture allows local features and long-range temporal dependencies to be modeled within a unified framework.

TSformer is designed specifically for RSVP-BCI decoding tasks. This method can capture both temporal dynamics and spectral patterns in EEG, demonstrating strong discriminative ability and generalization performance in multi-subject RSVP tasks.

Datasets

This paper uses the Tsinghua RSVP benchmark dataset (THU-RSVP dataset)33, which has been widely adopted in BCI research. The dataset includes EEG recordings from 64 healthy participants (sub1 … sub64). For each participant, the data are chronologically divided into two Group A and Group B. Each group contains two experimental blocks, and each block consists of 40 trials. Each trial corresponds to a sequence of 100 images presented at 10 Hz. After preprocessing and epoch extraction, each single-trial sample is represented as a 64 × 250 tensor, where 64 denotes the number of electrodes and 250 denotes the number of time points. In this study, only Group A data were used for subsequent analysis. For each participant, the numbers of target and non-target samples were approximately 130 and 7870, respectively. The stimuli comprised target images containing a person and non-target images without a person, with an approximate target probability of 1.7%. Participants were instructed to search for target images while ignoring non-target images. EEG signals were acquired using a 64-channel system arranged according to the international 10–20 system at a sampling rate of 250 Hz.

This dataset contains three RSVP-based target image retrieval tasks, namely Task plane, Task car, and Task people. Task people includes 31 subjects. The images are presented at 10 Hz in a randomized order, and the target probability is approximately 4%. EEG signals are recorded using a 64-channel SynAmp2 system at a sampling rate of 1000 Hz. According to the published dataset protocol, the EEG data are down-sampled to 250 Hz and segmented into 1-s trials after stimulus onset. Thus, each sample can be represented as a 64 × 250 tensor. For Task people, each subject contains 560 target samples and 13,440 non-target samples. Both datasets exhibit pronounced class imbalance. The corresponding handling strategy was applied during model training and is described in Sect. 4.1.

Data preprocessing and usage

This paper conducts experiments on both the THU-RSVP dataset and the NeuBCI TR RSVP dataset. The preprocessing procedures for the two datasets follow a unified protocol based on RSVP-ERP–relevant frequency ranges and event alignment.

For the NeuBCI TR RSVP dataset, this paper uses data from the first 12 subjects in Task people. It should be noted that the released EEG data are provided in a preprocessed form. According to the dataset’s preprocessing pipeline implemented with the Python MNE library, 64-channel EEG signals are first read from the original MAT-format recordings at a sampling rate of 1000 Hz. The signals are then band-pass filtered between 0.5 and 15 Hz (3rd-order IIR Butterworth filter with zero-phase forward–backward filtering) to suppress high-frequency noise and low-frequency drift. Representative time-domain waveforms and Welch power spectral density (PSD) plots of the continuous EEG signals before and after band-pass filtering are provided in Supplementary Fig. S1 to illustrate signal quality and the effect of preprocessing. Based on the provided trigger events, ERP epochs are extracted with a time window of 0–0.996 s, and each trial is normalized using z-score standardization.

For the THU-RSVP dataset, this paper selects Group A data from the first 64 subjects for subsequent analysis. This paper applies the same preprocessing procedure as that used for the NeuBCI TR RSVP dataset. Representative time-domain waveforms and PSD plots before and after preprocessing are provided in Supplementary Fig. S1 to illustrate signal quality and the effect of preprocessing.

On the THU-RSVP dataset (Table 1), the proposed ZMW-RSVP model achieves a BN of 84.36%, which is a 3.13% point improvement over the next best TSformer model, a 5.18% point improvement over the TCFormer model, and a 9.90% point improvement over the HDCA model, demonstrating stronger overall discriminative ability. Meanwhile, the TPR increases to 80.13%, indicating more comprehensive detection of target stimuli. The FPR decreases to 11.41%, suggesting effective suppression of false positives. The AUC reaches 0.911, further validating the model’s stability in discrimination across different thresholds.

On the NeuBCI TR RSVP dataset (Table 2), the metrics for all methods are generally higher, but the relative advantage pattern remains consistent. ZMW-RSVP achieves a BN of 87.59%, which is a 3.02% point improvement over TSformer. The TPR reaches 83.16%, a 3.25% point improvement over TSformer, while the FPR is reduced to 7.98%, and the AUC is increased to 93.7 This indicates that the model strikes a more balanced improvement between sensitivity and specificity, which better meets the actual RSVP task requirements of “high detection and low false positives.“It should be emphasized that the overall superior performance in Table 2 compared to Table 1 does not necessarily indicate that the task is easier. It more likely reflects differences in the performance upper limits determined by the scale of the dataset, data quality, and organizational methodology. The NeuBCI TR RSVP dataset has a larger volume of data and provides well-organized preprocessed trial-level data, with more stable event alignment and signal-to-noise ratios, making it easier for methods to achieve higher metrics.

The aforementioned performance improvements can be attributed to the synergistic effect of two core design features in ZMW-RSVP. On one hand, the oscillatory gated attention mechanism enhances the θ rhythm features closely related to P300 and introduces time-window biases to guide attention allocation, strengthening the model’s selective modeling ability for neural oscillations and time-domain response patterns. On the other hand, Dynamic Tanh is adopted in place of LayerNorm within the Transformer encoder as an alternative internal feature transformation strategy, avoiding explicit mean-variance normalization during the forward pass while maintaining stable optimization. Together, these two designs help the model better exploit discriminative time-frequency patterns associated with target-evoked responses under low signal-to-noise ratio and substantial inter-subject variability, leading to consistent improvements across multiple evaluation metrics.

Model complexity and inference efficiency

As shown in Table 4, ZMW-RSVP has a larger parameter scale and slightly higher inference latency than lightweight baselines. This is mainly due to its dual-branch temporal-spectral architecture and the interaction-fusion design introduced to enhance cross-view representation learning. Even so, the measured inference time remains at the millisecond level. It should be noted that the results reported here correspond to the full pretrained model used in the present study. In future subject-adaptive settings, it may be practical to freeze most pretrained backbone parameters and update only a small number of task-specific parameters. Although this would not change the total parameter count of the full model, it may reduce the number of trainable parameters and the computational cost during fine-tuning. In this sense, the current model can also be viewed as a pretrained backbone, while more parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies will be further explored in future work.

Neurophysiological consistency analysis

To further examine whether the proposed priors are consistent with task-related neural patterns, we analyzed the time-frequency responses and ERP morphology under target and non-target conditions using the THU-RSVP dataset. As shown in Fig. 6a and b, the two conditions exhibit markedly different time–frequency power distributions after stimulus onset. Under the non-target condition, the power increase is mainly confined to the low-frequency range, with stronger enhancement around 2–6 Hz within approximately 100–600 ms after stimulus onset. In contrast, the target condition shows a more prominent and continuous increase in low-frequency power, spanning roughly 1–8 Hz and extending over a broader post-stimulus period. These results indicate that target detection is associated with stronger and more sustained low-frequency activity28,29.

Consistent with the above time–frequency power differences, Fig. 6c and d further reveal a clear separation between the two conditions in terms of ERP morphology. Under the non-target condition, the averaged waveform remains close to baseline without a clear dominant event-related peak. In contrast, the target condition exhibits a pronounced positive peak in the mid-latency period, followed by a negative deflection.

Taken together, these signal-level observations provide neurophysiological support for incorporating both a P300 latency-window prior and low-frequency oscillatory guidance into the proposed model.

Discussion

This study developed ZMW-RSVP for single-trial RSVP-BCI decoding by extending an existing time-frequency Transformer framework with task-oriented neurophysiological priors and a DyT-based replacement of internal LayerNorm modules. The model integrates oscillatory gated attention with theta-related low-frequency emphasis and a P300 latency-window prior to improve the modeling of discriminative time-frequency patterns in RSVP decoding. Experimental results on the THU-RSVP dataset and the NeuBCI Target Retrieval RSVP-EEG dataset show that, under the current evaluation settings, ZMW-RSVP achieves consistently better performance than the selected baseline methods. Additional normalization comparison, ablation, sensitivity, cross-dataset, and complexity analyses further support the effectiveness of the proposed design.

Nevertheless, this study is still limited to offline evaluation, and the current model relies on predefined frequency priors and a fixed latency-window prior. Future work will focus on broader online validation, more adaptive prior modeling, and more efficient subject-adaptive deployment for practical RSVP-BCI applications.

Methods

Hyperparameter settings and details

The experiments in this paper are implemented using the PyTorch deep learning framework, and all computational tasks are run on a high-performance workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU.

The oscillatory gated attention mechanism adopts a multi-band collaborative processing strategy within the retained 0.5–15 Hz passband, including δ(0.5–4 Hz), θ(4–8 Hz), α(8–12 Hz) and β4, where β4 denotes the upper retained sub-band within the passband rather than the canonical beta band. Specifically, for the θ band, which is closely associated with the P300 component, a progressive enhancement mechanism is designed. The base θ-enhancement factor is set to 1.8, with a layer-wise increasing strategy, where the enhancement strength increases by 0.2 per layer. This design enables deeper layers to more fully utilize the temporal encoding characteristics of the θ rhythm. The P300 time-window bias mechanism is constructed based on neurophysiological priors3, with the time window set to 250–500 ms after stimulus presentation. The P300 bias weight is optimized and determined to be 0.5, which effectively guides attention allocation while avoiding excessive constraints on the model’s adaptive learning capacity. The bias vector is expanded to all attention heads and batch dimensions using a broadcasting mechanism, ensuring consistent prior guidance for all attention heads within the same time window.

During training, an adaptive learning rate scheduling strategy is employed, with the initial learning rate set to , and dynamic adjustment is implemented using a cosine annealing algorithm35,36. The batch size is set to 256, and the maximum number of training epochs is set to 90. To alleviate class imbalance, random under-sampling was applied to balance target and non-target samples, while the test set retained its original class distribution. The AdamW optimizer is used, with a weight decay coefficient of 0.01, which enhances model generalization while ensuring convergence efficiency. To reduce overfitting, a dropout rate of 0.5 was used in the residual Add-Norm branches and the convolution-based aggregation heads37.

In terms of model-internal normalization settings, Dynamic Tanh (DyT) was used to replace the original LayerNorm modules in the Transformer architecture in a one-to-one manner. For each DyT layer, the learnable input scaling parameter was initialized to 0.5, the affine scaling parameter adopted unit initialization, and the bias term adopted zero initialization, while the remaining implementation details are described in Sect. 4.3. For model selection, we employ a validation-based optimal model selection strategy, saving the model parameters when key validation metrics reach their optimal values to obtain a final model with more stable generalization performance.

Normalization-free transformer architecture with Dynamic Tanh

In commonly used Transformer architectures, LayerNorm stabilizes training by computing the mean and variance over the feature dimension for each token25–27. In single-trial RSVP/P300 decoding, the model needs to extract weak target-evoked ERP patterns from EEG signals with low signal-to-noise ratio, inter-subject variability, and trial-to-trial fluctuations5–9. These target-evoked patterns include ERP waveform characteristics, such as temporal morphology, amplitude, and latency, as well as P300-related low-frequency time-frequency activity. However, repeated mean-variance re-scaling of hidden representations inside the Transformer encoder may affect the transformation of such discriminative hidden features25,26. This issue may be particularly relevant in cross-subject EEG tasks, where distribution shifts are more common and stable optimization is often more challenging. Motivated by this consideration, this paper adopts Dynamic Tanh (DyT), proposed by Zhu et al.32, as a task-related internal replacement for LayerNorm. ZMW-RSVP model

The proposed ZMW-RSVP model is developed on top of TSformer and retains its dual-stream time-frequency architecture as well as the cross-view interaction design18. In this framework, EEG representations are extracted through two parallel branches, namely a time-domain branch and a frequency-domain branch. To better align the feature extraction process with RSVP/P300 characteristics, ZMW-RSVP further refines the attention and encoder components. Specifically, an oscillatory gated attention mechanism is introduced to capture rhythm-related information with additional emphasis on the θ band28,29, and a P300 time-window bias is added to the attention scores to guide the model toward the typical latency range of target-evoked responses4,30,31. In addition, Dynamic Tanh is adopted in place of LayerNorm within the Transformer encoder as an alternative internal transformation strategy32.

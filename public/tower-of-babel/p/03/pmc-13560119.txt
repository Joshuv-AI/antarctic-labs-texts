Multimodal deep learning-based prediction of radiation-induced temporal lobe injury via anatomical imaging and spatial dose: A multicenter study
Authors: Shan-Shan Yang, Lu-Ning Zhang, Yun He, A-lei Feng, Zhe Yang, Pu-Yun OuYang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo develop and validate a multimodal deep learning model for pre-treatment prediction of radiation-induced temporal lobe injury (RTLI), and to evaluate its generalizability across nasopharyngeal carcinoma-endemic and non-endemic regions.MethodsIn this multicenter retrospective study, 6847 patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma from five institutions in southern and northern China were included. A 3D ResNet-based multimodal deep learning model integrating planning CT images, spatial dose distribution maps, dosimetric variables, and clinical factors was developed and validated, and subsequently evaluated in internal and multiple external test cohorts. Model performance was assessed using the concordance index and compared with reduced-modality deep learning models, a dosiomics risk model, and conventional reference models. Model interpretability was explored using gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM).ResultsThe multimodal deep learning model demonstrated robust predictive performance, achieving concordance indices of 0.817, 0.825, 0.767, 0.901, and 0.815 across the internal, three southern external, and northern external test cohorts, respectively, and significantly outperforming all comparator models (all P < 0.001). Incremental performance gains were observed with multimodal integration, and CT imaging improved performance in the deep learning framework but not in the dosiomics risk model. The model enabled reliable identification of patients at high risk of RTLI. Grad-CAM visualization links model outputs to spatial dose-tissue interactions to guide radiotherapy plan optimization.ConclusionsMultimodal deep learning integrating anatomical imaging, spatial dose information, and clinical context enables accurate and generalizable pre-treatment prediction of RTLI across diverse regions. Spatially interpretable outputs further support individualized risk stratification and establish a biologically and dosimetrically informed foundation for risk-adapted radiotherapy planning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05021-5.

Background

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma is primarily treated with radiotherapy, and the adoption of intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT), with or without chemotherapy, has substantially improved long-term survival, with 5-year survival exceeding 80% and 10-year survival approaching 70% [1]. As survival extends, late radiation-related toxicities have become increasingly important. Radiation-induced temporal lobe injury (RTLI) is one of the most common and severe complications, with an incidence of up to 16.1%. RTLI is irreversible and may cause seizures, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and progressive cognitive dysfunction, leading to significant long-term impairment in quality of life [2]. Its insidious onset and the lack of effective treatments make early diagnosis and intervention particularly challenging [3]. Under these circumstances, accurate pre-radiotherapy prediction of RTLI risk, together with individualized dose planning and risk-adapted surveillance, is currently the most practical and feasible strategy to mitigate RTLI.

The traditional normal tissue complication probability (NTCP) model is the most commonly used model for evaluating radiotherapy plans. The QUANTEC guidelines recommend a maximum dose (Dmax) of less than 60 Gy to the temporal lobe [4], and Wen’s model, which integrates D0.5 cc with T stage and age, has shown better predictive ability [5]. However, NTCP models fail to account for the spatial heterogeneity of dose distribution and individual tissue susceptibility to RTLI, resulting in suboptimal predictive performance and limited clinical utility. Our previous study [6] has demonstrated that the dosiomics risk model based on spatial dose distribution maps outperforms the traditional NTCP model in predicting RTLI, underscoring the importance of spatial dose information. However, integrating dosiomics into treatment planning remains challenging, and dosiomics alone is insufficient to fully explain the marked inter-patient variability in RTLI risk. Even after accounting for multiple factors associated with RTLI, inter-patient differences in injury severity remain unexplained, suggesting a potential contribution of genetic susceptibility.

Genome-wide association studies have identified genetic susceptibility gene for RTLI in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [7]. However, as the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of the temporal lobe and the difficulty of obtaining tissue through invasive methods, genomics is limited. Radiogenomic studies have demonstrated a close association between imaging phenotypes and underlying genetic heterogeneity, suggesting that radiological features may serve as noninvasive surrogates of individual biological radiosensitivity [8]. In this context, radiomics and dosiomics have been explored to quantify tissue heterogeneity and spatial dose patterns [9, 10]; however, radiomics relies on handcrafted features constrained by predefined designs and assumptions, which may inadequately capture complex, nonlinear, and multiscale heterogeneity relevant to radiation-induced injury.

Deep learning overcomes these constraints by extracting deep and high-level features from medical images [11, 12]. However, single-modal data cannot fully capture the complexity of radiation induced injury. Recent studies suggest that multimodal deep learning, by integrating complementary data sources, provides a promising framework for advancing precision oncology beyond genomics and conventional molecular approaches [13–15]. In radiotherapy, the joint integration of computed tomography (CT) images and three-dimensional dose maps allows deep learning models to preserve spatial dose heterogeneity while capturing anatomical characteristics of normal tissues. Importantly, CT-based deep features may encode inter-individual anatomical and biological variability, thereby providing indirect insight into genetic susceptibility when direct genomic data are unavailable. Besides, nasopharyngeal carcinoma exhibits pronounced geographic heterogeneity, with substantial differences between endemic regions in southern China and non-endemic regions in the north. Nevertheless, most prior studies of RTLI prediction have been confined to endemic populations or small cohorts, limiting their generalizability.

Accordingly, we developed and validated a multimodal deep learning framework integrating CT imaging, three-dimensional dose maps, dosimetric variables, and clinical data to predict RTLI. Validation across endemic and non-endemic nasopharyngeal carcinoma cohorts enables assessment of model robustness and generalizability. The framework supports individualized risk stratification, with gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) visualization providing spatially resolved insight into dose-tissue interactions to guide radiotherapy plan optimization.

Methods

Patients

A total of 6847 nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients were included in this study, according to the following inclusion criteria: (1) Pathologically confirmed newly diagnosed nasopharyngeal carcinoma; (2) No history of radiotherapy; (3) Regular follow-up with head-and-neck magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) after IMRT; (4) Availability of complete radiotherapy planning data, including planning CT images acquired during the radiotherapy simulation process and dose distribution maps. Among them, 3828 patients treated at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center between January 2014 and December 2015 were retrospectively enrolled and randomly assigned to a training cohort (n = 3253; 85%) and a validation cohort (n = 575; 15%) for model development. Dataset splitting was performed at the patient level with stratification based on RTLI occurrence, ensuring no data leakage and balanced outcome distribution between cohorts. An additional 988 patients from our prospective observational study (NCT03003182; May 2017-December 2018) constituted the internal test cohort. For external test, 607 patients treated at Shandong Provincial Hospital between January 2014 and December 2020 comprised the northern external test cohort from a non-endemic region in North China (EXT1, Shandong test). In contrast, three southern cohorts from nasopharyngeal carcinoma endemic regions in South China were included: 1095 patients from the First People’s Hospital of Foshan (EXT2, Foshan test, January 2014-December 2016), 201 patients from Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital (EXT3, SYMH test, December 2015-October 2018), and 128 patients from the Cancer Center of the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (EXT4, Zhuhai test, November 2015-July 2017) (Fig. 1a). This study follows the transparent reporting of a multivariable prediction model for individual prognosis or diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines [16]. This research was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (B2020-264-01). Informed consent was waived for the retrospective cohort.

Image acquisition and data processing

All planning CT images and dose distribution maps were retrieved from the radiotherapy treatment planning system and used as model inputs, whereas MRI scans were reviewed for diagnostic purposes. The temporal lobes were outlined by a radiation oncologist (SSY, with 9 years of experience) and reviewed again by an expert radiation oncologist, using the ITK-SNAP software. Detailed acquisition parameters and data processing procedures are provided in the Additional file 1.

Diagnosis of temporal lobe injury

Consistent with previous studies [6, 17], RTLI was identified when MRI scans showed distinctive features such as white matter lesions, contrast-enhanced lesions, or the presence of cysts. RTLI was independently assessed on MRI by an experienced radiation oncologist (PYOY, > 15 years of experience in nasopharyngeal carcinoma) and a senior radiologist (YH, > 10 years of experience in head and neck MRI interpretation, reviewing over 500 cases per month). Assessments were cross-checked against original radiology reports and MR spectroscopy findings when available. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus, and the bilateral temporal lobes were evaluated separately.

Multimodal deep learning prediction model

Abbreviations were used to denote combinations of input modalities: C for clinical data; D for dose-related information (including dose–volume histogram [DVH] parameters and/or dose distribution maps); and CT for planning CT images. Accordingly, CD, CDC, CDD, and CDCD models represent different multimodal combinations used in this study (Additional file 1: Supplementary Methods).

The structure of the RTLI prediction model is shown in Fig. 1b-c. It primarily uses 3D ResNet [18] as the backbone network, followed by a non-local module [19], an average pooling layer (Avgpool), and two multilayer perceptron (MLP). Planning CT images, dose distribution maps, and temporal lobe structural images are concatenated into a three-channel image and input into the 3D ResNet to extract image features. A non-local module was then applied to introduce global feature information, enabling richer feature information to subsequent layers. Based on the extracted imaging features, a MLP generated an imaging-derived risk score (Risk 1). In parallel, clinical variables and DVH parameters were input into a separate MLP to produce a clinical-dosimetric risk score (Risk 2). Finally, Risk 1 and Risk 2 were fused to obtain the final RTLI risk prediction. The final loss function combined the Cox loss and concordance index (C-index) loss function, as detailed in the Additional file 1: Supplementary Methods.

Comparison models

Radiomics and dosiomics models were also constructed for comparison following our previously published methods [6], and detailed workflows are provided in the Additional file 1: Supplementary Methods.

Statistics

In line with prior studies [6], the primary endpoint was RTLI-free survival, defined as the interval from the initiation of radiotherapy to the occurrence of RTLI or the final examination. The bilateral temporal lobes were evaluated separately, with each temporal lobe treated as an independent observational unit, resulting in a temporal lobe–level rather than patient-level analysis.

Clinical characteristics were compared using chi-square tests. Model discrimination was assessed by the C-index. Bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) was performed using time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) at 3 and 5 years to assess model robustness. The proposed multimodal deep learning model was compared with the dosiomics risk model, reduced-modality deep learning models, the QUANTEC model, and the Wen model. Calibration curves, decision curve analysis, and net reclassification improvement were used to assess calibration, clinical usefulness, and risk reclassification, respectively. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to determine optimal cutoff values for risk stratification, based on which temporal lobes were classified as high- or low-risk. In addition, the area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) was calculated at fixed time points (3 and 5 years) to assess performance under class imbalance. RTLI-free survival was estimated using Kaplan-Meier analysis, with group differences assessed by the log-rank test. Model interpretability was examined using Grad-CAM, with heatmaps projected onto CT images and dose maps. The model was developed using Python software (https://www.python.org/, version 3.0.1), and statistical analysis was performed using R software (https://cran.r-project.org/). Two-sided tests were used, and a p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparative performance of unimodal deep learning and dosiomics models

The deep learning model based solely on planning CT images showed limited predictive performance, with C-indices of 0.607 (95% CI: 0.533–0.681) and 0.546 (95% CI: 0.493–0.599) in the northern and southern external test cohorts, respectively, exceeding those of the corresponding radiomics model. In parallel, the deep learning model using dose distribution maps alone demonstrated consistently superior performance compared with the dosiomics model, achieving C-indices of 0.714 and 0.766 (shown in Additional file 1: Fig. S1, Table S2).

Incremental performance gains from multimodal integration and CT augmentation

When combining planning CT images and dose distribution maps, the corresponding deep learning model (CD model) demonstrated superior performance compared with all unimodal deep learning models (Additional file 1: Fig. S1, Table S2).

The multimodal deep learning model integrating clinical data, DVH, and dose distribution maps (CDD model) achieved C-indices of 0.820, 0.813, 0.807 and 0.806 in the validation, internal test, northern external test, and southern external test cohorts. In comparison, dosiomics risk model using the same inputs yielded lower C-indices of 0.780, 0.793, 0.753, 0.787. Overall, the CDD model significantly outperformed the unimodal deep learning model, dosiomics risk model, the QUANTEC model, and the Wen model across all cohorts (all p < 0.05, Additional file 1: Fig. S2, Table S3).

With the further addition of planning CT images, the final multimodal model incorporating clinical data, DVH, dose distribution maps, and CT images (CDCD model) demonstrated additional performance gains, with C-indices of 0.825 (95% CI: 0.782–0.869), 0.817 (95% CI: 0.781–0.853), 0.815 (95% CI: 0.767–0.863) and 0.825 (95% CI: 0.793–0.856) across the corresponding cohorts, thereby outperforming all comparator models (all p < 0.05, Fig. 2a, Additional file 1: Table S3). The model performance was also validated in the EXT3 and EXT4 southern external cohorts, with C-indices of 0.767 (95% CI: 0.600–0.935) and 0.901 (95% CI: 0.815–0.988), respectively. Its discriminative ability was further supported by time-dependent ROC analyses (Fig. 3, Additional file 1: Fig. S3). Bootstrap analysis showed stable performance, with mean AUCs of 0.819 at 3 years and 0.866 at 5 years. Consistently, the AUPRC values at 3 and 5 years exceeded the corresponding baseline event rates by approximately threefold (Additional file 1: Table S4).

Clinical utility and risk stratification

The calibration curves shown in Additional file 1: Fig. S4 further indicate good agreement between the predicted and observed 3-year RTLI-free probability across all datasets for the final CDCD multimodal deep learning model. In addition, the model achieved a net reclassification improvement exceeding 20.2% (Additional file 1: Table S6) and provided a higher net benefit across a threshold probability range of 0 to 0.673 in decision curve analysis (Additional file 1: Fig. S5).

Based on the time-dependent ROC analysis, an optimal AUC cutoff value of 0.712 was determined for risk stratification using the CDCD multimodal deep learning model. Temporal lobes with predicted values ≥ 0.712 were classified as high risk, whereas those with values < 0.712 were classified as low risk. Accordingly, the proportions of temporal lobes classified as high risk were 34.3%, 39.7%, 53.8%, 60.4%, 61.6%, 54.5% and 43.8%, and in the training, validation, internal test, EXT1, EXT2, EXT3 and EXT4 external test cohorts, respectively, with the remaining temporal lobes classified as low risk. Across all datasets, the 3-year RTLI-free probability was consistently lower in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group (training set: 85.5% vs. 98.0%; validation set: 88.4% vs. 99.1%; internal test set: 87.6% vs. 98.8%; EXT1: 92.2% vs. 99.3%; EXT2: 93.7% vs. 99.6%; EXT3: 96.5% vs. 98.8%; EXT4: 95.5% vs. 99.1%; all p < 0.05, Fig. 4).

Visualization and interpretability analysis

Figure 5 presents Grad-CAM heatmaps overlaid on planning CT images and dose distribution maps, illustrating the spatial regions contributing most to the multimodal model’s predictions. Warmer colors denote higher relevance. In Fig. 5a-b, CT-based heatmaps show increased attention within the left temporal lobe. After integrating dose distribution information, model attention became more spatially focused, with an elevated predicted risk for the left temporal lobe. Subsequent MRI at 37 months confirmed left-sided RTLI. In Fig. 5c-d, CT-derived attention is more diffusely distributed across both temporal lobes, whereas dose-based heatmaps demonstrate a pronounced focus on the left temporal lobe, consistent with the later development of bilateral injury at 21 months after radiotherapy. Figure 5e-f shows a patient without RTLI, in whom no focal activation was observed in temporal lobe. Overall, these visualizations indicate that planning CT images primarily capture anatomical heterogeneity and tissue susceptibility, whereas dose distribution maps provide spatially localized information related to radiation burden. The combined use of these modalities enables anatomically and dosimetrically informed risk predictions.

Discussion

In this multicenter study including nasopharyngeal carcinoma cohorts from endemic regions in southern China and non-endemic regions in northern China, we developed and externally validated a multimodal deep learning framework to predict RTLI. By integrating clinical features, DVH, planning CT, and dose distribution maps, the proposed model consistently outperformed reduced-modality deep learning models, dosiomics risk model, and conventional approaches across internal and external cohorts. Notably, the stepwise improvement with additional modalities indicates that each input contributes distinct risk information, rather than simple data accumulation. Clinically, this framework enables precise individualized RTLI risk prediction and high-risk stratification, while Grad-CAM visualization links model outputs to spatial dose-tissue interactions to guide radiotherapy plan optimization.

The superior performance of deep learning over radiomics and dosiomics in our study likely reflects differences in feature representation and learning strategies rather than model complexity alone. Traditional feature-based approaches rely on predefined descriptors that summarize imaging or dose information into a limited set of handcrafted features, resulting in loss of spatial context and potential oversimplification of the heterogeneous and spatially localized processes underlying RTLI. In contrast, deep learning learns directly from voxel-level data, preserving spatial context and regional dose variations, and is therefore better suited to capture dose heterogeneity and subtle spatial gradients [20]. In addition, deep neural networks possess a unique capacity to model complex nonlinear and cross-modal interactions. This may explain why CT images, which encode intrinsic tissue heterogeneity [7], contributed meaningful predictive value only within the deep learning framework, whereas no such benefit was observed in dosiomics risk model. What is more, RTLI represents a multifactorial biological process involving spatially heterogeneous dose exposure and individual tissue susceptibility. Such complexity is more effectively captured by high-dimensional pattern learning than by handcrafted features and cannot be adequately represented by any single data modality.

The observed benefit of multimodal modeling in this study further highlights the importance of integrating complementary information across biological and spatial scales. Clinical factors reflect patient-level susceptibility and treatment context, whereas DVH parameters summarize the overall dose burden but obscure spatial heterogeneity. In contrast, dose distributions preserve voxel-level dose gradients and regional exposure patterns, providing essential spatial information for modeling heterogeneous radiation injury [21, 22]. CT images further provide detailed anatomical structure and intrinsic tissue heterogeneity, which can modulate radiosensitivity and influence the biological response to a given dose distribution [7]. The consistent stepwise improvement observed with the integration of clinical, dosimetric, spatial dose, and tissue information suggests that these modalities contribute complementary risk information for RTLI. Ablation analyses suggested that DVH-derived variables provided modest complementary information beyond spatial dose representations. Importantly, deep learning further enables joint representation learning across modalities, allowing spatial dose patterns to be interpreted in the context of underlying tissue characteristics and more closely reflecting the biological and spatial nature of RTLI than feature-based approaches [14, 15].

In addition to improved predictive performance, the multimodal deep learning model enables pre-treatment estimation of RTLI risk and identification of patients at higher risk. Although the absolute AUPRC values were modest because of the low event prevalence, the approximately threefold improvement over baseline across cohorts suggests meaningful enrichment of true positive cases and supports the model’s role in risk stratification. Because RTLI is a localized radiation-induced complication and bilateral temporal lobes exhibit substantial differences in spatial radiation exposure, we selected the temporal lobe as the analytical unit to preserve regional dose heterogeneity, consistent with previous studies [5]. This design supports applications in radiotherapy plan optimization and individualized surveillance. During radiotherapy planning, this prospective risk stratification provides quantitative support for individualized treatment strategies, including adjustment of dose constraints and informed balancing of tumor control and normal tissue protection, consistent with our prior work [6]. Stricter dose constraints or preferential sparing of vulnerable temporal lobe subregions may be considered for high-risk patients, whereas standard planning may suffice for low-risk patients. In patients with bulky T4 disease, particularly with skull base or cavernous sinus involvement, the model may further inform treatment planning and adaptive radiotherapy strategies, including intensified induction therapy prior to radiotherapy and dynamic plan adaptation during treatment, thereby reducing excessive irradiation to the temporal lobes. During post-treatment follow-up, early risk identification may further support personalized follow-up strategies. High-risk patients may benefit from intensified imaging surveillance and neurocognitive assessment, facilitating earlier detection and timely intervention, whereas low-risk patients may follow routine follow-up protocols [23]. This is clinically relevant given that early-stage RTLI may be reversible, while advanced-stage disease is often irreversible. The clinical applicability of these strategies depends on robustness across diverse populations. Unlike prior studies limited to endemic regions, our multicenter cohort includes both endemic and non-endemic regions, capturing substantial heterogeneity in clinical practice. The consistent performance across these cohorts supports the generalizability of the model.

Reflecting the spatially heterogeneous nature of RTLI, Grad-CAM analysis highlights candidate dose-sensitive subregions at the individual level. The resulting heatmaps capture spatial patterns shaped by the interaction between tissue characteristics and local dose exposure, supporting the biological plausibility of the multimodal framework [21]. Clinically, these insights may guide preferential avoidance of high-dose overlap with sensitive regions in high-risk patients, while allowing focus on overall dose optimization in low-risk patients. Automated visualization further enhances interpretability, longitudinal assessment, and clinician–patient communication. Given that all required inputs are routinely available, the model can be readily integrated into clinical workflows. From a translational perspective, inter-individual variability in tissue tolerance highlights the potential to integrate biological information into multimodal frameworks for more precise mapping of radiosensitivity and individualized radiotherapy optimization. This study provides a foundation for such personalized strategies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, despite the inclusion of multicenter cohorts with geographic diversity, this study remains retrospective and prospective validation is needed. Second, the model does not yet capture longitudinal injury evolution or explicitly characterize the biological mechanisms underlying model-attended regions. Future studies integrating radiomics-based habitat analysis and extending the framework to other tumor types and radiation-induced toxicities may further enhance its biological interpretability and clinical generalizability.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this multicenter study demonstrates that multimodal deep learning integrating patient susceptibility, dose magnitude, spatial dose heterogeneity, and tissue characteristics can accurately predict RTLI and identify high-risk patients before radiotherapy. The integration of Grad-CAM further links individualized risk prediction to spatially localized dose-tissue interactions, supporting risk-adapted radiotherapy planning and personalized clinical decision-making.

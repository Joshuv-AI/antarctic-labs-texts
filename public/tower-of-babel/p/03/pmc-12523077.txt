Pathological responses to neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy: implications for survival and recurrence in patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Yixin Li, Yidan Hong, Qishu Tan, Youtao Xu, Jiawei Zhou, Bo Yang, Gang Xiao, Xiaochen Huang, Lingling Gu, Ming Jiang, Yang Zhao, Yu Chen, Zihao Zhu, Xiangzhi Zhu, Yinan Wu, Ning Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study evaluated the prognostic implications of pathological responses of primary tumors (PTs) and lymph nodes (LNs) among locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (LA-ESCC) patients received neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy (nICT).MethodsThis study enrolled patients with LA-ESCC who underwent nICT combined with esophagectomy at Jiangsu Cancer Hospital. Two senior pathologists independently and systematically evaluated the postoperative pathological specimens in a double-blind manner. Tumor regression in PTs was assessed using the Becker criteria, while LNs regression grading was optimized using Harrell’s concordance index and the Akaike Information Criterion. The primary endpoints were overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), with recurrence patterns as the secondary endpoint.ResultsA total of 141 patients with 141 PTs and 2,316 LNs were reviewed. LNs regression classified as LNs complete response (LN-CR); Partial/Poor response (LN-PR); and Truly negative LNs (LNN). The ypT0N0 rate was 21.3%. With median 41-months follow-up (IQR 22–55), the 3-year OS and PFS rates were 61.7% (95% CI 53.3–69.1%) and 48.2% (95% CI 40.2–56.4%), respectively. PTs regression grade was an independent prognostic factor for both OS and PFS (P < 0.05). Multivariable analysis demonstrated that a poorer tumor regression grade (TRG) was significantly associated with increased risks of OS and PFS (P < 0.05). Furthermore, while complete LN-CR correlated with improved PFS compared to LN-PR (P = 0.037), this association did not retain statistical significance in the multivariate analysis. Background

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), the predominant histological subtype of esophageal cancer (EC) in Asia [1], accounts for a substantial global disease burden. Approximately 60% of patients presents with locally advanced disease at diagnosis [2]. For resectable locally advanced ESCC (LA-ESCC), neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) or chemotherapy (nCT) combined with esophagectomy constitutes standard treatment protocols [3–5]. Nevertheless, the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate for EC remained approximately 30% by 2015 [6], emphasizing the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies.

Studies have suggested that immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) demonstrate synergistic effects with chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting for patients with LA-ESCC [7, 8]. Exploratory clinical trials have reported pathological complete response (pCR) rates of 16.7–39.2% following neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (nICT) [7, 9, 10]. Notably, the NICE trial achieved a 2-year OS rate of 78.1% [11], while the ESCORT-NEO study demonstrated that nICT exhibits significantly higher pCR rate compared with nCT [12]. These findings establish nICT as a promising therapeutic approach for LA-ESCC.

Pathological response in primary tumors (PTs) and lymph nodes (LNs) serves as a prognostic indicator [13–15]. However, the relationship between pathological regression patterns in PTs and LNs following nICT and their prognostic significance remains poorly characterized, with standardized assessment criteria currently undefined. While retrospective study has indicated that achieving pathological complete response in PTs (PT-pCR) or major pathological response (MPR) combined with node-negative status (ypN0) may predict favorable disease-free survival (DFS) [16]. Nevertheless, persistent challenges include the absence of validated criteria for evaluating LNs regression following nICT [17–19] and insufficient evidence regarding the prognostic significance of site-specific pathological responses in this therapeutic context.

This study established a refined methodology for assessing LNs regression through prognostic modeling and investigated the association between tumor response and prognosis in both PTs and LNs following nICT. The aim of this study is to provide evidence for formulating effective tertiary prevention and follow-up strategies.

Methods

Study design

This single-center retrospective cohort study included patients with LA-ESCC who received nICT combined with esophagectomy at Jiangsu Cancer Hospital. The inclusion criteria consisted of: (1) Histologically confirmed ESCC staged as cT1-4aN + M0/cT3-4aN0M0 according to the AJCC 8th edition [20]; (2) Aged between 18 and 75 years; (3) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0–1; (4) Availability of complete medical records; (5) Normal hematology, renal, and hepatic function. Patients with a history or concurrent diagnosis of other malignancies, prior neoadjuvant treatments, and those with distant organ metastasis were excluded. The primary endpoints were OS and progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoint was the recurrence patterns. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and has been approved by the Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee of our center. Informed consent was waived due to the anonymity of the data.

Treatment procedure and follow-up

All patients received nICT for up to three cycles prior to surgery every 3 weeks. The chemotherapy regimens for all patients included the TP regimen based on platinum-based drugs and paclitaxel [9], as well as the CF regimen (cisplatin combined with fluorouracil) based on platinum-based drugs [21]. For immunotherapy, PD-1 inhibitors were used in all cases, specifically sintilimab (200 mg, q3w), toripalimab (240 mg, q3w), camrelizumab (200 mg, q3w), pembrolizumab (200 mg, q3w), and tislelizumab (200 mg, q3w).

Esophagectomy was performed within 4–8 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant treatment. As the prevalent surgical method in China, the majority of patients underwent extended two-field lymphadenectomy during esophagectomy, with reconstruction of the esophagus using gastric tissue for cervical or thoracic anastomosis. The right recurrent laryngeal nerve chain was dissected completely, while the left recurrent laryngeal nerve chain was dissected only in select patients with suspected metastatic LNs.

Patients underwent follow-up evaluations every 3 months during the initial 2 years, with the frequency subsequently reduced to every 6 months after 2 years. The follow-up measurement encompassing computed tomography (CT) scans, laboratory indicator monitoring, and clinical evaluations. All recurrences are confirmed based on definitive radiologic findings, with occasional histological evaluation of recurrent lesions conducted.

Pathological sampling

The PTs bed area and LNs were separately marked by surgeons. In case where the tumor bed was visually indistinguishable, preoperative CT scans were utilized to accurately pinpoint its localization. After measuring the size of the tumor bed, the specimen was precisely incised along its maximum diameter. If the measurement fell below 3 cm, the tumor bed and the surrounding 1 cm area were entirely sampled. Conversely, for tumor beds exceeding three centimeters, at least one section was submitted per 0.5 cm, ensuring its maximum dimeson was made and photographed. If no residual tumor or less than 10% viable tumor cells were observed in the initial histologic sampling, the rest of the tumor bed was sampled. LNs were sampled as intact as possible according to their size. Larger LNs underwent dissection along the maximum cross-section, and all samples were excised to dimensions not exceeding 2.0 cm*1.5 cm*0.3 cm. All the specimens were routinely fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin.

Classification of PTs regression

All the slides for each specimen were independently evaluated by two experienced pathologists blinded to clinical data. For the assessment of pathological response in the PTs, the percentage of viable residual tumor, stromal tissue, and necrosis were estimated on each slide. The residual viable tumor percentage (RVT%) was calculated by dividing the area of residual tumor by the total tumor bed and then determining a total RVT% by averaging the results for all slides. This percentage was then classified according to Becker system: TRG 1a, indicating no residual tumor; TRG 1b, indicating < 10% residual tumor; TRG 2, indicating 10–50% residual tumor; and TRG 3, indicating > 50% residual tumor [22].

PCR is defined as no evidence of residual cancer cells in all layers of the esophagus and resected LNs, which means ypT0N0. Notably, PT-pCR are consistent with TRG 1a.

Classification of LNs regression

LNs regression was evaluated according to the presence of significant fibrotic areas, foamy macrophages, and necrotic foci within the LNs parenchyma. LNs regression percentage was calculated as the ratio of residual tumor area to total LNs area. Based on this metric, LNs regression was classified into five grades (LNRG): LNRG 1, complete regression; LNRG 2, < 10% viable tumor; LNRG 3, 10–50% viable tumor; LNRG 4, > 50% viable tumor; LNRG 5, negative LNs exhibiting no viable tumor or histologic evidence of regression. As no standardized LNs regression grading system currently exists following nICT, this study assessed the optimal classification of LNs regression classification by comparing the survival predictive accuracy of different LNRG classifications, based on prior methodology [19], with results detailed in Table 1. This comprehensive evaluation considered both the best and worst LNRG classifications.

Survival

OS is defined as the interval from the date of initial diagnosis to death from any cause or the last documented follow-up date confirming patient status. PFS referring to the interval from the date of initial diagnosis to cancer recurrence, death, or the last outpatient visit. Local–regional recurrence (LRR) is defined as recurrence occurring within the esophagus, anastomotic site, or regional LNs, with it being noteworthy that metastasis to cervical and abdominal LNs are also classified as LRR [11]. Distant metastasis (DMS) refers to recurrence in distant organs or peritoneal carcinomatosis. Meanwhile, mixed recurrence involves both LRR and DMS occurring simultaneously, regardless of time.

Statistical analysis

The predictive accuracy of different LNRG for survival and recurrence was evaluated using Harrell's Concordance Index (C-index) and the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). The C-index quantifies the probability of consistency between predicted and observed outcomes, while the AIC assesses model fit. Classification systems demonstrating higher C-index and lower AIC values were considered to have better discriminatory ability. A C-index of 1.0 indicates perfect prediction, whereas 0.5 indicates no predictive power. Interobserver agreement between two experienced senior pathologists was evaluated using Cohen's kappa coefficient (κ), reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Agreement strength was categorized as follows: κ < 0.00, No agreement; κ = 0.00–0.20, Slight agreement; κ = 0.21–0.40, Fair agreement; κ = 0.41–0.60, Moderate agreement; κ = 0.61–0.80, Substantial agreement; and κ = 0.81–1.00, Near perfect agreement.

OS and PFS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with between-group differences compared using the log-rank test. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was performed to evaluate the association between covariates and OS or PFS. Variables with P < 0.2 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate Cox models. To ensure model robustness and mitigate overfitting, the Events Per Variable (EPV) ratio was calculated for each Cox regression model. An EPV ≥ 10 was defined as exceeding the conservative threshold for reliable parameter estimation. For models with EPV < 10, stability was further validated using bootstrap resampling with 1000 iterations. To address potential immortal time bias associated with nICT, two complementary approaches were employed. Firstly, nICT was modeled as a time-dependent covariate in Cox regression analysis. Second, landmark analyses were conducted at 4-months and 6-months, including patients who remained event-free and at risk at these respective timepoints. All statistical analyses were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was defined statistical significance. Analyses were performed using R version 4.4.0 and SPSS version 27.0.

Results

Patient characteristics

After screening, a total of 141 patients with LA-ESCC who received nICT combined with esophagectomy were included. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 2. The cohort had a median age of 65 years (IQR 59–68), with male predominance (n = 111, 78.7%). Most patients received 2 cycles of nICT (n = 115, 81.6%). The median interval between completion of nICT and surgery was 33 days (IQR 29–39). Regarding surgical approach, the majority underwent extended 2-field lymphadenectomy (n = 137, 97.2%), and only four patients underwent 3-field dissection (2.8%).

Classification of LNs response

Table 1 evaluates the prognostic predictive accuracy of various LNRG systems. The three-tier LNs regression grading system demonstrated significantly superior discriminative capacity compared to alternative classifications, exhibiting higher C-index and lower AIC values, while maintaining both methodological simplicity and reproducibility. This classification system is defined as follows: LNs complete response (LN-CR), indicating complete tumor regression; Partial/Poor response (LN-PR), assigned when residual tumor are present in the LNs; and Truly negative LNs (LNN), showing no evidence of tumor or tumor regression. When multiple LNs from a single patient exhibited heterogeneous pathological responses, classification was based on the most severe pathological findings observed.

Pathological response

Overall, the proportion of patients with ypT0N0 was 21.3% (30/141) (Table 3, Fig. S1), while 5.7% were classified as ypT0N + (8/141), 39.7% as ypT + N0 (56/141), and 33.3% as ypT + N + (47/141). Among these patients, 27.0% (38/141) achieved TRG 1a in PTs, indicating PT-pCR. The proportions of patients classified as TRG 1b, TRG 2, and TRG 3 were 11.3% (16/141), 27.7% (39/141), and 34.0% (48/141), respectively.

The impact of pathological response on prognosis

As of June 2025, the median follow-up duration was 41 months (IQR 22–55). The 3-year OS and PFS rates were 61.7% (95% CI 53.3–69.1%) and 48.2% (95% CI 40.2–56.4%), respectively. Figures 2A and B represent OS and PFS for the entire cohort. The median OS was not reached, while median PFS was 31 months (95% CI 15–47). Higher residual tumor grades in PTs were significantly associated with poorer prognosis (P < 0.05, Fig. 2C, D). Patients with LN-CR demonstrated significantly superior PFS compared to those with LN-PR (P = 0.037, Fig. 2F). Although not statistically significant, similar prognostic trends for different LNs pathological statuses were observed for OS (Fig. 2E). Combined PTs and LNs statuses showed significant prognostic correlations (P < 0.05, Fig. 2G, H), with ypT0N0 patients exhibiting significantly improved outcomes and ypT + N + cases showing significantly poorer prognosis.

In the univariate Cox proportional hazards analyses (Table 5), PTs regression grade showed significant association with both OS (P = 0.003) and PFS (P < 0.001). The pathological response of LNs was significantly associated with PFS (P = 0.024). Different PD-1 inhibitors demonstrated no significant impact on OS or PFS when included as covariates (P > 0.05, Table S1. Variables with P < 0.2 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate analysis (Table 6), confirming PTs regression as an independent prognostic factor for both OS and PFS. Patients with > 10% residual tumor (TRG 2, 3) had significantly worse prognosis compared to those with ≤ 10% residual tumor (TRG 1a, 1b) (P < 0.05). The pathological response of LNs did not have a significant impact on OS and PFS (P > 0.05). EPV values were 11.3 for PFS and 6.5 for OS models (Table S2). Bootstrap resampling validated stability of the OS model (Table S3). Time-dependent Cox regression confirmed multivariate results (Table S4). Furthermore, landmark analyses at 4 months (Fig. S2, Table S5) and 6 months (Fig. S3, Table S6) demonstrated trends consistent with primary findings despite reduced sample size.

The recurrence patterns

Table 7 presents recurrence patterns among patients with varying degrees of PTs and LNs regression. Discussion

Previous studies have established tumor regression following nCT or nCRT as a robust predictor of prognostic in LA-ESCC [23–25]. Multiple studies further suggest independent prognostic significance of PTs and LNs regression [26–28]. However, within immunotherapy contexts, standardized pathological assessment criteria for LNs response remain undefined. Moreover, the impact of tumor regression on prognosis after nICT require clarification. This retrospective study of 141 LA-ESCC patients receiving nICT combined with esophagectomy proposes a clinically practical three-tier LNs regression classification. Results demonstrate that both PTs and LNs regression, along with combined pathological status of the PTs and LNs, serve as prognostic factor for survival. Patients achieving ypT0N0 status exhibit optimal survival outcomes, while immunotherapeutic non-responders with ypT + N + demonstrate inferior OS and PFS. Notably, LRR predominance after nICT combined with esophagectomy, indicating the necessity for enhanced local–regional control strategies.

For PTs regression assessment, this study employed the Becker grading system, which currently lacks universal adoption as a standard tumor regression grading system for EC [29]. While the Mandard system is frequently utilized for esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) [30–32], classifying regression by residual tumor cellularity and fibrosis response [33]. Studies have demonstrated that both categorical and continuous variables possess predictive value in evaluating tumor residue [34]. Notably, estimating the percentage of residual tumor is more reproducible than distinguishing fibrosis resulting from tumor regression or tumor-induced stromal proliferation [35, 36]. Comparative analyses demonstrate that the Becker system outperforms the Mandard system in terms of Kappa scores, clinical accessibility, and reproducibility [37, 38], with established prognostic utility for ESCC risk stratification [14, 39]. Our findings validate the Becker system as a robust PTs prognostic indicator, and subsequently discovered that patients with > 50% residual tumor exhibited significantly increased recurrence risk, while those with > 10% residual tumor showed inferior survival outcomes compared to those with no residual tumors. Based on the CHECKMATE 577 study, ICIs monotherapy is recommended as adjuvant therapy for postoperative residual tumor in LA-ESCC [40]. Whether patients with TRG 1b warrant distinct therapeutic stratification from TRG 2–3, with the latter potentially receiving more intensive adjuvant treatment, requires further clinical research for validation.

LNs regression assessment following nICT was evaluated based on metastatic LNs responses. While studies confirm LNs regression after nCT as a significant prognostic predictor [19, 41], standardized grading remains undefined. A prospective trial classified LNs regression into five grades according to the RVT% and demonstrated that patients with complete regression or residual tumor < 50% after nCT exhibited significantly prolonged OS and PFS [19]. This study established multiple regression models using RVT% thresholds, ultimately adopting a practical three-tier classification prioritizing reproducibility and clinical feasibility, which requires further validation in future prospective studies. This approach addresses challenges in distinguishing treatment-related fibrosis from inflammatory responses that complicate tumor bed quantification. Categorical assessment of tumor response presence/absence minimizes bias and enhances interobserver agreement compared to RVT%-based systems requiring complex measurements. Clinically, the three-tier system demonstrates superior efficiency. Patients achieving LN-CR exhibited significantly improved prognosis than those with LN-PR, which is noteworthy, exhibited better prognosis than LNN. This aligns with evidence indicating LN-CR represents an immune-favorable subgroup with reduced metastasis [42], supported by near-absent DMS in the LN-CR cohort. The comparatively poorer LNN outcomes may reflect surgical methodology limitations, as predominant two-field extended lymphadenectomy in China variable LNN rates dependent on dissection extent. Notably, high LRR rates in LNN patients (29/65, 44.6%) after nICT may contribute to this disparity. These findings suggest LN-CR status correlates with superior therapeutic response in the nICT paradigm. For LNN patients, adjuvant therapy decisions should incorporate factors beyond ypN staging. Further research must identify additional surrogate markers for systemic treatment efficacy within LNN subgroups to guide stratified therapeutic strategies.

Another pivotal finding of this study characterizes recurrence patterns among patients with varying regression status following nICT. Our analysis revealed that LA-ESCC patients treated with nICT predominantly experienced treatment failure through LRR (20.6%) or mixed recurrences combining LRR and DMS (15.6%), with isolated DMS occurring infrequently (6.4%). This recurrence profile, particularly the low rate of isolated DMS, aligns with the established systemic benefit of ICIs in reducing DMS risk, as evidenced by KEYNOTE-590 and CHECKMATE 648 trials [43, 44]. However, the observed elevated LRR rate warrants further investigation. Firstly, a critical divergence exists between our LRR definition and that of the seminal NEOCRTEC5010 study, the NEOCRTEC5010 study reported DMS-predominant patterns at 2 years (LRR: 8.2%) [45]. NEOCRTEC5010 classified supraclavicular LNs involvement as DMS, whereas our study explicitly categorized such events as LRR [45]. Consequently, approximately 12.2% of cases classified as LRR in our cohort would have been designated as DMS under the NEOCRTEC5010 criteria. This differential classification likely contributes significantly to the higher reported LRR rate in our study compared to the DMS-dominant pattern reported in NEOCRTEC5010. Secondly, the extent of surgical resection is a key consideration. The standard radical procedure performed in our cohort was two-field extended lymphadenectomy, widely adopted in China due to its coverage of major metastatic basins and favorable balance between operative morbidity and oncologic yield. However, compared to the more comprehensive three-field lymphadenectomy employed in the JCOG1109 trial [46], the two-field extended approach may be less effective in eliminating occult micrometastatic disease. This limitation in surgical clearance plausibly contributes to the higher LRR rates observed in our study. Notably, 31.2% of patients received adjuvant radiotherapy, suggesting that intensified local therapy may partially mitigate this surgical constraint and confer clinical benefit [47]. Therefore, future prospective studies should validate whether risk-adapted radiotherapy for high-risk ypT0N0 LA-ESCC patients post-nICT with two-field extended lymphadenectomy, can effectively reduce LRR while preserving the systemic benefits of immunotherapy.

Recurrence patterns were further analyzed according to PTs and LNs pathological responses after nICT. Residual tumor in either PTs or LNs predominantly manifested as LRR, with supraclavicular, mediastinum, and abdominal LNs representing the most frequent sites. Consequently, enhanced surveillance and prophylactic radiotherapy targeting these high-risk regions are warranted for patients with residual tumor. Patients achieving PT-pCR or LN-CR demonstrated superior DMS control. Established evidence correlates tumor-infiltrating T-cells with improved survival and reduced distant metastasis [48, 49]. PD-1 inhibitors exert therapeutic efficacy by inducing T-cell expansion and preserving T-cell function through early intervention [50].While ypT0N0 status serves as a validated surrogate endpoint for OS and distant metastasis-free survival in breast cancer and melanoma [51, 52], our findings similarly emphasize the prognostic significance of pCR in either PTs or LNs following nICT. Notably, despite a favorable prognostic trend compared to LN-PR in Fig. 2E, LNN patients exhibited substantial DMS rates. This may reflect inherent treatment insensitivity in an LNN subpopulation, where nICT fails to eradicate micrometastasis disease. Risk-stratified surveillance strategies based on combined PTs and LNs pathological status are therefore clinically imperative for precision management.

While confirming the prognostic relevance of both PTs and LNs status, multivariable analysis revealed that PTs response may constitute a more prominent determinant of both OS and PFS than LNs. This aligns with the CHECKMATE 816 trial, which demonstrated that in NSCLC patients receiving neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy, achieving PT-pCR served as a robust predictor for event-free survival (EFS), whereas lymph node involvement status exhibited relatively limited impact on EFS [53]. The biological basis may involve PT-pCR reflecting systemic immunotherapeutic efficacy, conferring survival advantages that eclipse nodal status impact. Emerging evidence suggests LNs generally exhibit lower tumor mutational burden (TMB) compared to PTs deposits, potentially underlie reduced ICIs efficacy in metastases [54]. Moreover, technical challenges in LN-CR assessment (sampling bias, false-negatives) highlight the need for future prospective validation using standardized pathological protocols in expanded cohorts.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis with a relatively small sample size, although we strictly included only consecutive patients with initially diagnosed ESCC who completed standardized neoadjuvant therapy followed by radical esophagectomy to minimize bias, potential selection bias may still exist. Future multicenter prospective studies are warranted for further validation. Secondly, the median follow-up of 41 months may be insufficient to capture long-term survival patterns, limiting comprehensive prognostic assessment. Thirdly, while subgroup analyses demonstrated no significant differences in OS or PFS among different PD-1 inhibitors, the retrospective nature of this study may introduce inherent biases. Consequently, future head-to-head trials will be required to definitively establish comparative efficacy profiles. Finally, routine monitoring of tumor biomarkers such as PD-L1 status and TMB was not standard practice at our center, precluding their inclusion in the analysis.

Conclusion

PTs response may constitute a more prominent determinant of both OS and PFS than LNs status. Furthermore, the comprehensive pathological assessment of both the PTs and LNs reveals a substantial predictive power in determining prognosis. These discoveries are of immense importance in screening and pinpointing patients who are most likely to reap benefits from nICT.

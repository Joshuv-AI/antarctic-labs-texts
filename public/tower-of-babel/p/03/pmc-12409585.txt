The Influence of Embedded Properties on the Liberation Characteristics of Macerals in Subbituminous Coal
Authors: Chenjie Tang, Wenli Liu, Jia Yu, Yingxin Lin, Menghao Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The utilization pathways of coal macerals exhibit a significant variability. The efficient separation of active and inert components in coal is critical for realizing their high value-added applications. The liberation efficiency of coal macerals directly influences the accuracy of subsequent separation processes. Investigating the influence of embedded properties of inertiniteincluding embedded granularity, embedded morphology, and concatenate particle typeson the liberation characteristics of coal macerals is essential for achieving separation efficiency. This study focuses on the relationship between the embedded properties of inertinite and the liberation characteristics of coal macerals. According to the development situation of coal in China and embedded characteristics of macerals, three sub-bituminous coal samples from different regions of China (Xinjiang Tianchi (TC), Xinjiang Qiuci (QC), and Inner Mongolia (Ordos)), each exhibiting distinct embedded properties, were selected for experimentation. Both ball and rod milling techniques were employed to conduct grinding tests, and the total liberation degree of the coal macerals (the weighted average of the liberation degree of vitrinite and inertinite) was measured at various particle sizes (−0.5 mm) of the ground products. The results demonstrate that coal samples with finer embedded granularity (expressed as equivalent diameter of the area) exhibited a lower total liberation degree at the same ground product size. The observed differences in the embedded morphology (aspect ratio) of coal macerals led to distinct responses to ball milling and rod milling. This research reveals that embedded granularity has the most pronounced influence on the total liberation degree of coal macerals followed by the embedded morphology. The concatenate particle types have the least impact on liberation. These findings provide a theoretical foundation for understanding the differences in liberation characteristics of macerals in subbituminous coals.

Introduction

The vitrinite macerals and inertinite in low-rank coals exhibit different application properties due to their varying chemical compositions and physical properties. The efficient separation of vitrinite and inertinite is critical for the effective fractionation and utilization of coal. For example, vitrinite has high liquefaction performance, while inertinite is more suitable for making activated carbon due to its porous structure. − Complete liberation of vitrinite and inertinite is a prerequisite for their efficient separation. − The liberation characteristics of coal macerals are influenced by two primary factors: the embedded properties of inertinite (endogenous factors) and the parameters of the grinding process (exogenous factors). , Tang et al. have systematically investigated quantitative characterization methods for the embedded properties of coal macerals and proposed appropriate parameters for the characterization of embedded features. Building upon previous research, this study further investigates the influence of the embedded properties of coal macerals (primarily inertinite) on their liberation properties.

Research on the liberation properties of target minerals typically addresses two key aspects. First, it examines how grinding process conditions affect the liberation characteristics of minerals and coal macerals. − Second, it reveals the relationship between the embedded properties of target minerals within the ore and their liberation properties. − In particular, the influence of the embedded granularity of minerals on their liberation properties has been the most widely explored. T. Leißner et al. − pointed out that the different embedded granularity of minerals in ores are associated with differences in crushing and liberation characteristics during grinding. O’Brien et al. pointed out that when the total embedded area of inorganic minerals in coal is the same, the fewer embedded areas (larger individual embedded areas), the easier it is to dissociate. Yuan et al. used a Mineral Liberation Analyzer to characterize the mineral embedded properties in an ore from Guangdong Province, China. They found that the tantalum–niobium mineral phases were disseminated with a grain size of −0.040 mm, mostly disseminated in minerals such as quartz, illite, and mica, and partially intergrown with topaz and zircon, which are difficult to liberate. Liu et al. showed that cassiterite is mainly disseminated in iron minerals and gangue. The embedded granularity of cassiterite is very fine and must be ground to −0.01 mm to achieve a liberation degree of 90%.

Huang et al. measured the embedded granularity of minerals in ludwigite coal using an automatic mineral tester and a polarizing microscope, qualitatively analyzed the embedded morphology of minerals in coal, and explored the relationship between embedded granularity, morphology, and liberation degree. It was found that liberation degree of minerals was influenced not only by embedded granularity but also by embedded morphology. Fu et al. pointed out that different minerals in coal with different embedded granularity and embedded morphology, exhibit distinct liberation properties during impact breakage tests, which significantly affects subsequent separation processes. Wang et al. conducted a qualitative study on the reasons for the difficulty in liberating minerals of coking coal from three aspects: embedded granularity, embedded shape, and embedded pattern, and found that these properties all have an important effect on the liberation of coking coal.

In summary, it can be seen that at present the relationship between the embedded properties of target minerals in ores and their liberation characteristics in the crushing process has received great attention and has been more fully investigated, but most of these studies have focused on the embedded properties of the minerals. However, the relationship between the embedded properties of the coal macerals and the liberation performance has rarely been systematically studied.

In this study, three sub-bituminous coals in China with obvious differences in their embedded properties were selected for investigation. The objective is to explore the intrinsic relationship between the embedded properties of coal macerals and their liberation properties. By conducting a comparative analysis, we studied the influence of embedded properties of macerals in subbituminous coal on their liberation properties was studied. The study aims to provide a theoretical foundation for revealing the differences in liberation characteristics among macerals in subbituminous coals.

Evaluate and Calculate Methods of the Liberation Degree

Different grinding experiments were conducted on the three coal samples using ball and rod milling for varying durations. The grinding conditions were as follows: the feed amounts were set at 100 g for the ball milling and 200 g for the rod milling; the grinding speed was maintained at 110 rpm, with alumina ceramic balls as the grinding media in the ball mill at a filling rate of 40%, 16 steel rods were employed in the rod mill, and the power of the grinder is 120 W. The grinding time was set at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 min. The particle size distribution of the ground products was measured using a laser particle size analyzer (Bettersize 2600, Bettersize, Dandong, China). Screening tests were performed in accordance with the ″Standard Test Method for Performing the Sieve Analysis of Coal and Designating Coal Size” (D4749) to obtain samples of various particle sizes. Following “Standard Practice for Preparing Coal Samples for Microscopical Analysis by Reflected Light” (D2797), the samples of each particle size were prepared as coal petrographic analysis slices, which involved making coal briquettes and polishing. Microscopic images of these coal petrographic analysis slices were captured using a polarizing microscope (DM 4P, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Figure presents typical images from the polarizing microscope, clearly revealing the morphological features of the coal macerals. These images provide a solid foundation for accurately identifying the embedded properties of the coal macerals in subsequent samples.

Similarly, the total liberation degree of coal macerals in QC is higher under the less effective grinding method compared to that of Ordos under the more effective grinding method. In other words, TC exhibits the highest total liberation degree of coal macerals followed by QC, with Ordos showing the lowest. This can primarily be attributed to the fact that the embedded granularity of inertinite has a greater impact on the total liberation degree of the ground product than the embedded morphology.

Tables and demonstrate that for the same grinding method and product particle size after grinding, the total liberation degree of coal macerals is highest in TC followed by the QC coal, with the Ordos coal exhibiting the lowest. As illustrated in Table , the inertinite in the TC coal is characterized by a coarse embedded granularity (15.49 μm) and a low aspect ratio (2.91), characteristics that promote liberation efficiency during comminution. Conversely, the liberation difficulty value (1.81) for the vitrinite-inertinite concatenate particles in the TC coal is high. In contrast, the inertinite in the Ordos coal are distinguished by a fine embedded granularity (9.70 μm) and a high aspect ratio (3.18), not conducive to liberation during crushing. Nonetheless, the liberation difficulty value (1.75) for the vitrinite-inertinite concatenate particles in the Ordos coal remains relatively low. Comprehensive analysis reveals that the embedded granularity and morphology of inertinite exert a stronger influence on liberation characteristics than the liberation difficulty value for the vitrinite-inertinite concatenate particles.

In summary, the embedded granularity of inertinite has the greatest impact on the total liberation degree of coal macerals; the embedded morphology has a secondary influence, while the concatenate particle types have the least influence.

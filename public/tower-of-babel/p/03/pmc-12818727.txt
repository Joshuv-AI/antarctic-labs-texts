Generation of two isogenic induced pluripotent stem cell lines derived from a Hispanic individual with Alzheimer’s Disease and mosaic for loss of the Y chromosome
Authors: Holly N Cukier, Brooke A DeRosa, Lauren E Coombs, Eugene Tang, Denise R Leyva, Ainnara R Lazzaro, Esther Gu, Derek M Dykxhoorn, Jonathan L Haines, Margaret A Pericak-Vance, Yeunjoo E Song, William S Bush, Anthony J Griswold
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMosaic loss of the Y chromosome (LOY) is a somatic phenomenon that occurs in males as they age (Jacobs, 1963; Forsberg, 2017; Danielsson, 2020) and is associated with an elevated risk of developing numerous diseases, including Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) (Dumanski, 2016; Palmer, et al., 2022; Garcia-Gonzalez, 2023), cancer (Holmes, 1985; Loftfield, 2018), diabetes (Loftfield, 2018), age related macular degeneration (Grassmann, 2019), and autoimmune disease (Persani, 2012; Lleo, 2013). The induced pluripotent cell lines (iPSCs) UMi050-A and UMi050-B were derived from a Hispanic male with loss of the Y chromosome in his blood and diagnosed with AD. This isogenic pair of lines with and without a Y chromosome is a unique resource to delve into the mechanism of LOY and its relationship to AD risk.

Resource utility

Somatic loss of the Y chromosome (LOY) is an established aging phenomenon in men associated with an increased risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease (AD). We established two clonal iPSC lines from an AD patient mosaic for LOY, one LOY + and one LOY−, to further investigate LOY mechanisms and consequences.

Resource details

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a complex genetic disorder and the leading cause of dementia in older adults. Recently, we and others reported on an association of loss of the Y chromosome (LOY) with AD risk (Dumanski, 2016; Palmer, et al., 2022; Garcia-Gonzalez, 2023). Therefore, utilizing genome-wide genotyping data from DNA extract from blood, we identified individuals positive for mosaic LOY using the MADloy program (Gonzalez, 2020). We then derived an induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) line from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) donated from LOY positive a 91-year-old Hispanic male with AD. The CytoTune™-iPS 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit was used to generate stem cells from the PBMCs. Individual colonies were isolated and screened by Taqman™ Copy Number Assays (ThermoFisher Scientific) for the presence or absence of the Y chromosome (Fig. 1A). One line with a Y chromosome (UMi050-A) and one line without a Y chromosome (UMi050-B) were selected (denoted by arrows in Fig. 1A) and expanded (Fig. 1C), to generate an isogenic pair of iPSCs from the same individual. Characterization and validation.

Each line was verified to have the expected karyotype by G-banding: 46,XY for UMi050-A (passage 11) and 45,X for UMi050-B (passage 12, Fig. 1B). For each cell line, 20 cells were counted, 8 analyzed and 4 karyogrammed. Immunofluorescence microscopy of immunocytochemically (ICC) stained iPSC colonies for UMi050-A (passage 12) and for UMi050-B (passage 14) showed positive expression of markers of an undifferentiated hPSC state: Nanog, OCT4, and SOX2 (Fig. 1D). The percentage of total cells expressing these markers was quantified by counting marker-positive cells and comparing to DAPI-stained nuclei (Fig. 1E). Moreover, the cell lines were negative for the Sendai virus (SeV) reprogramming vectors and mycoplasma (passage 12, Fig. 1G, Supplemental Fig. 1). Short tandem repeat (STR) analysis of DNA isolated from both UMi050-A (passage 14) and UMi050-B (passage 15), as well as DNA extracted directly from the donor’s blood, demonstrated that the samples matched, with the exception of the Amelogenin marker on the sex chromosomes for LOY positive UMi050-B line (45,X). To determine the pluripotency of the iPSC line, both lines were differentiated into the three germ layers using the STEMDiff™ Trilineage Differentiation Kit (STEMCELL Technologies), at passage 15 for UMi050-A and at passage 16 for UMi050-B. ICC was performed for germ layer markers: ectoderm (PAX6 and SOX1), endoderm (FOX2A and SOX17), and mesoderm (Brachyury and TBX6, Fig. 1F).

Materials and methods

Isolation of Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells

Following informed consent, blood was obtained through venipuncture from a 91-year-old Hispanic male. SepMate-50 tubes with Lymphoprep™ isolated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs, STEMCELL Technologies).

Derivation of iPSCs

Cell cultures were incubated at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 except where oted. PBMCs were reprogrammed with the CytoTune-iPS 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit (ThermoFisher Scientific). Four days before transduction, PBMCs were grown in StemPro™–34 Serum Free Medium (ThermoFisher Scientific), 1X GlutaMAX™-I (ThermoFisher Scientific), and recombinant protein cytokines: 100 ng/mL FLT-3, 20 ng/mL IL-3, 20 ng/mL IL-6, and 100 ng/mL SCF (Peprotech). PBMCs were transduced with nonintegrating SeV reprogramming vectors. After three days, cells were plated onto rhLaminin-521-coated plates (0.5 μg/cm2; ThermoFisher Scientific) and grown without cytokines. On day seven, cells were transitioned to mTeSR Plus medium (STEMCELL Technologies). After day 14, colonies were evaluated to identify those with typical stem cell morphology, selected, and seeded onto vitronectin-coated plates (0.5 μg/cm2; ThermoFisher Scientific). Reprogramming vectors were eliminated by subcloning for 5 passages and incubating at 39 °C for 5 days. iPSCs were expanded by passaging every 4–6 days using 0.5 mM EDTA (Corning) and replated using a 1:6–1:12 split ratio. 10 μM Y27632 (AdipoGen) was added to medium during passaging and thawing.

TaqMan CNV Assay

DNA was isolated with Qiagen’s DNeasy Blood & Tissue kit, and tested with Taqman™ Copy Number Assays (ThermoFisher Scientific): SRY (Hs01026408_cn) on the Y chromosome and the control RNase P. Samples were run on the QuantStudio 12 k Flex Real-Time PCR system and evaluated with CopyCaller™ software.

Immunocytochemistry

Cultures were fixed in 4 % formaldehyde for 15 min, washed with PBS. Samples incubated for 1 h in permeabilization/blocking buffer containing PBS, 10 % normal donkey serum (NDS), 0.3 % Triton X-100, 0.05 % Tween-20, and 0.3 M glycine. Primary antibodies (Table 2) were diluted in antibody dilution buffer (ADB) comprised of PBS, 2 % NDS, 0.1 % Triton X-100, 0.05 % Tween-20, and 0.02 % sodium azide, then applied to cells and incubated overnight at 4 °C. The appropriate secondary antibodies (Table 2) were diluted in ADB and incubated with cells for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. The cells were washed with PBS, incubated for 15 min with NucBlue (DAPI, ThermoFisher Scientific), and visualized on the Keyence BZ-X810 microscope (Fig. 1D,F).

Confirmation of Absence of Sendai Virus

Loss of reprogramming vectors was assessed by RT-PCR targeting the SeV genome (Table 2). The RNeasy Plus Kit (Qiagen) isolated RNA, which was reverse-transcribed to cDNA with iScript Reverse Transcription SuperMix (BioRad). PCR was performed using Platinum SuperFi II PCR Master Mix (ThermoFisher Scientific) and 10 μM primers with the following conditions: 98 °C 30 s, 35 cycles of 98 °C, 10 s, 60 °C, 30 s, 72 °C, 30 s, then 72 °C 5 min, 4 °C hold.

Trilineage Differentiation

iPSCs were differentiated using the STEMDiff™ Trilineage Differentiation Kit (STEMCELL Technologies) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and ICC performed for germ layers markers (Table 2, Fig. 1F).

STR Profiling, Karyotype Analysis, and Mycoplasma Testing

Short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, G-banded karyotype analysis (Fig. 1B), and mycoplasma testing (Supplemental Fig. 1) were performed by WiCell.

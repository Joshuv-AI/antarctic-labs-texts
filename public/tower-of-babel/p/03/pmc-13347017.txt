Microstructural and mechanical behavior analysis of magnesium matrix composites with varying Al2O3 content
Authors: K. Muthusamy, A. Muthiah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Magnesium metal matrix composites demonstrate extremely high potentials of using light materials that are strong in the various industries. As the modern process, the fabrication and synthesis of Magnesium matrix composites which have fortified Al2O3 to different percentages of particles have been achieved. The Mg and Al2O3 composites are analyzed concerning the properties of the Al2O3 particle content which has different weight% of the particles in the X-ray diffraction (XRD), Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), Energy Dispersive X-ray (EDX) and Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and properties of mechanical properties. The results show that the hardness as well as the wearrate of the composites increased respectively and the weight of Al2O3 powders composite decreased respectively. Characterization of mechanical properties indicated that the enhancement of the properties of pure magnesium by the reinforcement of Al2O3 particulates is beneficial. The resulting properties of metal matrix composites are reliant on a broad spectrum of variables and the current knowledge coupled with future research directions are brought up. Cost effectiveness of metal matrix composites in various applications will be the last determinant to their successful commercial production.

Introduction

Powder and conventional materials are poor in the unification of mechanical characteristics like strength, hardness, corrosion as well as wear resistance. In order to eliminate the issue, composite materials have been adopted in place of alloys and conventional materials1. Pistons, cylinder head among others are some of the manufacturing industries that may employ the use of Al2O3 reinforced matrix composite to replace the heavier material in the processes due to its weak nature and low modulus at a higher temperature, corrosion and wear resistive properties are poor2,3. One of the most suitable fabrication techniques of formation of the Mg-Al2O3 composites is the Powder metallurgy. It is inexpensive and the particulates are dispersed in the macromaterials in a uniformly distributed form, which contributes to the increase of the mechanical characteristics of the Mg-Al2O3 composites4. The substitution of harder and stiffer ceramics particulates and alloying elements in the matrix can overcome them. It will however be dependent on the kind, size and volume percentage of particulates to provide the best nature to these composite materials5.

One of the reinforcements that can be used is the nano-sized aluminium oxide ceramic particle (Al2O3)6.The hard Al2O3 particles used usually allow the composite structures to obtain high values of hardness and wear resistance which the metal matrix will not obtain on its own. They also enhance the resistance of the composite material to high temperature as well as inhibition of cracks propagation7. The fabrication technique that will be employed in this study due to its convenience, cost-effective, and adaptable nature is powder metallurgy, which can be conveniently utilized in mass production hence it is highly applicable within the automobile industry8. Hence, a composite material based on magnesium is created in presence of Al2O3 reinforcing particles with a view of exploring the effect and mechanism of nano-Al2O3 reinforcing particle number and shape in enhancing the mechanical properties of a magnesium matrix composites by a Powder metallurgy route9. The influence of Al2O3 reinforcement particle (at various levels of additions) on the hardness and corrosive properties was studied.

Powder metallurgy was convenient to use in making the Mg based materials through the coating process10. The production method (as one of the PM methods) could be the pressing method that has been effectively employed in obtaining the composite specimens with high relative densities, as well as in the processes of achieving the uniform dispersion of the reinforcement particles within the microstructure11. scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and using line-scan profiles through energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) can be employed to observe the production method12. This is whereby it is used in electrical contacts field. By use of various reinforcement materials, i.e. ceramic particles, graphene etc.13. The MM powders and the SPS compacts of Mg and Al2O3 microstructures were observed under the scanning electron microscopy (SEM), as well as the X-ray diffraction (XRD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM)/energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) techniques. Focused ion beam system and ion-beam polish system helped in the preparation of SEM and TEM samples respectively14.

Although numerous studies have investigated magnesium matrix composites reinforced with ceramic particles, much of the existing literature focuses on liquid-state fabrication routes such as stir casting, where issues related to particle clustering, oxidation, and poor interfacial control are prevalent. In contrast, systematic investigations on Mg–Al2O3 composites fabricated exclusively via powder metallurgy particularly with higher reinforcement contents and consistent processing parameters remain limited. Moreover, several reported studies lack direct correlation between processing conditions, microstructural features, and mechanical performance. Aluminium oxide (Al2O3) was selected as the reinforcement in the present study due to its high hardness, excellent thermal stability, good wear resistance, and chemical compatibility with magnesium under solid-state processing conditions. In addition, Al2O3 is widely available, cost-effective, and well suited for powder metallurgy routes, where it can enhance load transfer and restrict plastic deformation without inducing undesirable interfacial reactions. Accordingly, the present work aims to systematically investigate the influence of Al2O3 content on the microstructural, mechanical, and tribological behavior of Mg–Al2O3 composites fabricated via powder metallurgy, while maintaining consistent processing parameters and avoiding liquid-state processing artifacts. The objective of this study is to identify the effect of Al2O3 reinforcement content on the mechanical and wear behavior of magnesium matrix composites produced via a controlled powder metallurgy route.

The observed trend through enhancement of mechanical properties of the Mg- Al2 O3 composites with increment in the amount of Al 2 O3 can be explained by a series of concurrent strengthening mechanisms. One, the hard ceramic Al2 O3 particles enable effective transfer of the loads between the softer magnesium matrix to the reinforcement phase, therefore, enhancing the ability of the composite to resist compressive influence in load. Second, the particles of Al2 O3 have the effect of inhibiting the movement of dislocations, resulting in a higher density of dislocations and a higher plastic deformation resistance. Moreover, the homogeneous distribution of particles of Al2 O3 at lower reinforcement levels leads to refinement of the microstructures and localised deformation decreases, which leads to hardness. The thermal expansion mismatch between magnesium and Al2O3 may also introduce geometrically necessary dislocations during cooling, further contributing to strengthening. At higher Al2O3 contents, however, particle agglomeration and increased porosity reduce the effectiveness of these mechanisms. Agglomerated particles act as stress concentrators and weaken interfacial bonding, which explains the observed reduction in compressive strength and hardness at 40 wt% Al2O3.

Conclusion

In the current paper, composites of Mg- Al 2 O 3 were commonly prepared between powder metallurgy approach wherein powder preyed upon blending, cold compaction, sintering in an argon environment, and post-sintering extrusion.The experimental results indicate that the addition of Al2O3 reinforcement significantly influences the mechanical and tribological behavior of magnesium. Compressive strength and hardness increase with increasing Al2O3 content up to 30 wt%, reaching maximum values of approximately 204 MPa and 66 HV, respectively. A further increase in reinforcement content to 40 wt% results in a reduction in these properties, which is attributed to particle agglomeration, interfacial debonding, and increased porosity. Wear test results reveal that the composite containing 10 wt% Al2O3 exhibits the lowest wear rate under the tested conditions, while higher reinforcement levels lead to increased wear due to third-body abrasion effects. Microstructural analysis ensures the distribution of Al2 O3 particles in the Mg matrix and substantiates the trends of observed qualities.Overall, the study demonstrates that an optimal Al2O3 content exists for balancing strength, hardness, and wear resistance in Mg-based composites fabricated via powder metallurgy. The findings are limited to laboratory-scale conditions, and further investigations are required to evaluate performance under application-specific environments.

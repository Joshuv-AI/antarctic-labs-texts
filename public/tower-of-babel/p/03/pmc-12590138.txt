Nutritional and functional roles of an endo-1,4-β-D-mannanase in young tom turkeys
Authors: AS Aderibigbe, S Dridi, M Baxter, A Sokale, A Fickler, Elke von Heimendahl
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated the effects of increasing doses of β-mannanase (Natupulse® TS) on growth performance, cellular stress, and immune response in turkey toms fed an all-vegetable diet. A total of 1,920 male Hybrid Converter turkeys (40 birds/pen; 12 pens/treatment) were randomly assigned to four dietary treatments (T1 = 0, T2 = 400, T3 = 800, and T4 = 1200 TMU of mannanase per kg feed) in a randomized complete block design. A corn-soybean meal basal diet was fed from 0 to 35 days of age. Feed intake and BW were recorded on day 21 and 35 post hatch for BW gain (BWG) and feed conversion ratio (FCR) calculations. On day 21, ileal samples from five birds per treatment were analyzed for markers of oxidative stress, gut barrier integrity, and inflammation using real-time quantitative PCR. There were no significant differences in all measured performance parameters within the 5-week period, however there was a tendency for longer intestinal length (P = 0.072) in turkeys fed the basal diet compared to enzyme-added treatments. In addition, β-mannanase supplementation reduced (P < 0.05) the mRNA expression of heat shock proteins (HSP60, HSP70, HSP90) in T4 compared to T1. Barrier function genes (CLDN1, JAM3) also showed significant improvements (P < 0.05). The mRNA expression of ileal superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1), catalase (CAT) and glutathione peroxidase 1 (GPX1) was unaffected by treatments. Additionally, inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNFα, IL-4, IL-10) and C-reactive protein (CRP) expression were significantly lower (P < 0.05) in mannanase-fed groups compared to the control group. In conclusion, increasing β-mannanase supplementation (Natupulse® TS) for young turkeys had no significant performance effect but caused an improvement in intestinal physiology, reduction in intestinal inflammation and oxidative stress, with enhanced gut barrier integrity.Keywords: Gene expression, Ileum, Performance, Turkeys, β-mannanase

Introduction

Soybean meal (SBM) is a primary protein source in poultry feeds due to its high protein content and superior amino acid profile. However, SBM also contains several anti-nutritional factors that can adversely impact poultry growth and health metrics. Noteworthy among these factors is β-mannans which is a type of non-starch polysaccharide (NSP) found in the hull and fiber fractions of SBM. β-mannans are composed of mannose units that play essential biological roles in immune response, cell adhesion, and signal transduction (Duncan et al., 2002; van Kooyk and Geijtenbeek, 2003, Kraehenbuhl and Corbett, 2004). Their structural similarity to components of pathogenic microorganisms allows them to act as pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), potentially eliciting localized or systemic immune responses in birds (Arsenault et al., 2017; Kiarie et al., 2021).

In addition, diets high in NSPs are associated with increased intestinal viscosity, which interferes with feed passage and nutrient absorption. This can lead to poor growth performance and higher feed conversion in birds (Choct et al., 1995; Van Nevel et al., 2005). Higher intestinal viscosity along with poor nutrient absorption can also result in gut dysbiosis, facilitating the proliferation of pathogenic bacteria like Clostridium perfringens, enteric diseases such as necrotic enteritis and bacterial translocation and inflammation (Lee et al., 2003; Arsenault et al., 2017). In turkeys, the challenges posed by NSPs are more pronounced due to their higher protein requirements and greater reliance on SBM in their diets. The immunogenic properties of β-mannans may exacerbate these challenges by diverting resources from growth to immune responses, ultimately reducing feed efficiency (Gabler and Spurlock, 2008; Arsenault et al., 2017; Kiarie et al., 2021). Poultry also lacks the endogenous enzymes required to degrade NSPs like β-mannans, making them particularly susceptible to their adverse effects (Knudsen, 2014). This susceptibility is heightened in younger turkeys with underdeveloped intestinal structure and lower enzymatic activity. As such, strategies to mitigate these negative impacts are crucial for enhancing turkey productivity.

One promising approach involves supplementing diets with enzymes such as β-mannanase. β-mannanase hydrolyzes β-mannans within the SBM (plant) cell wall and even intracellularly (Rueckel et al., 2024). This allows the accessibility of endogenous digestive enzymes to the trapped essential nutrients within those mannan structures, thereby increasing nutrient digestibility. In addition, β-mannanase hydrolyzes β-mannans into smaller, less viscous oligosaccharides, thereby increasing the presence of certain metabolites specifically butyrate which is known for its immune modulating effects (Bedford and Gong, 2018; Siddiqui and Cresci, 2021). While studies in turkeys have reported variable results regarding the efficacy of β-mannanase probably due to variability in dietary composition, bird physiology, environmental conditions and immune status, younger birds often show improved feed efficiency and growth performance following enzyme supplementation (Odetallah et al., 2002; Opoku et al., 2015a,b).

The present study investigates the effects of Natupulse TS, a novel β-mannanase enzyme, on the growth performance, cellular stress, and immune response of turkey toms fed an all-vegetable diet. The aim is to optimize enzyme supplementation strategies to support improved health and productivity in turkeys.

Materials and methods

Birds, housing and diets

One thousand, nine hundred and twenty one-day-old hybrid converters tom poults were obtained from Hendrix Genetics commercial hatchery (Grand Island, Nebraska) and transported to the Ozark Avian Research facility (Gravette, Arkansas). Upon arrival, the poults were weighed, tagged individually and assigned by body weight to one of four treatments in a randomized complete block design. The four treatment groups were Control with no mannanase (Natupulse TS), and Control supplemented with 400, 800 or 1,200 TMU/kg Natupulse TS (TMU = Thermostable Mannanase Unit). Each treatment comprises 12 replicate floor pens with 40 birds per pen. All pens were the same size (5 ft W × 10 ft L × 4 ft H) and each had two feeders and two bell drinkers. Poults were placed in pens containing new litter top dressed in equal amount of used litter from a previous flock. Temperature and lighting schedules were based on breeder recommendations and poults were monitored daily for signs of temperature discomfort. All poults were placed on their respective treatments for a 35 day post hatch trial. Diets were formulated to meet or exceed NRC (1994) nutrient requirements of turkey poults using a low protein SBM (44.6%) and contained no animal protein or by-products (Table 1). Poults were maintained on the same diet throughout the experiment and were fed ad libitum. All diets contained a phytase (Natuphos E, BASF SE, Ludwigshafen, Germany) at 1000 FTU/kg feed. Prior to mixing the feed, a representative sample of the SBM was analyzed to determine the β-mannan concentration using gas-liquid chromatography based on the individual sugar constituent as alditol acetates (Oxley et al., 2004). Diets were pelleted at 85 °C (185 °F) with 30 – 40 sec of conditioning time. Diets were analyzed for β-mannanase activity using procedure FEN/0005/01 (BASF SE, Ludwigshafen, Germany). The mannanase unit TMU is defined as the amount of enzyme that produces reducing carbohydrates having a reducing power corresponding to one µmol mannose from locust bean gum (0.3 g/100 ml buffer solution) in one minute under the assay conditions of 50.0 ±0.1 °C and pH 3.5. The β-mannanase (Natupulse® TS) was supplied by BASF SE, Ludwigshafen, Germany.

Sample collection

Individual body weights and pen feed consumption data were determined at 21 and 35 days of age. Daily mortality was recorded throughout the experiment. Body weight gain and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were subsequently calculated and adjusted for mortality. On d 21, 3 birds per pen were randomly selected for tissue sampling. The selected poults were humanely euthanized via carbon dioxide asphyxiation. Ileal tissue samples were collected distal to the Meckel’s diverticulum, washed with PBS – 1X , put in sterile 1.5 mL tube, snap frozen in liquid nitrogen then kept at -80 °C for further molecular analyses. The full length of the intestinal section was measured with a ruler (cm) and calculated as a ratio of bird BW.

Total RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNAs were isolated using the Trizol reagent according to the manufacturer’s recommendation and as described previously (Dridi et al., 2012; Tabler et al., 2020; Dhamad et al., 2021; Greene et al., 2021). The RNA concentrations, quality, and integrities were determined using Synergy HT multimode microplate reader. RNA was reverse transcribed using qScript cDNA SuperMix and amplified using Power SYBRGreen Master Mix and real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR, 7500 Real Time System; Applied Biosystems). The sequences for oligonucleotide primers were specifically designed and synthesized for turkey (Table 2). Relative expressions of target genes were determined using 2-ΔΔCt method (Schmittgen and Livak, 2008), r18S, GAPDH, and ACTB as housekeeping genes, but r18S was the most stable and consistent one. Control (T1) group was used as a calibrator and a control.

Statistical analyses

Where μ.. is the overall mean; the ρi are identically and independently normally distributed random block effects with mean 0 and variance σ2ρ; the τj are fixed factor level effects corresponding to the jth dietary treatment (T1 to T4) such that ∑τj = 0; and the εij are identically and independently normally distributed random errors with mean 0 and a variance σ2. Significant differences between treatments were determined using a Student's t test. Relative gene expressions were analyzed by One-Way ANOVA, and the means were compared by Dunnett’s post-hoc multiple comparison test. Significant effects were declared at P < 0.05 and tendency at P < 0.10.

Results

Analyzed β-mannanase activity in the diet agrees with predicted values (T1 =88; T2=369; T3=830; T4=1233 TMU/kg feed). There was no significant treatment effect but noteworthy numerical trends on growth performance parameters taken within the experimental period day 0 – 21 and d 0 – 35 (Table 3). At day 0 – 21, no difference in BW was observed but feed intake was numerically lower in all treatment groups in comparison to the control (T1) group, only leading to a numerical improvement in FCR. Turkeys in T2 had 3-points improvement in FCR, whereas turkeys in T3 and T4 had respectively 6- and 5-points better FCR compared to the control (T1) group (Table 3). At day 0 – 35, numerically, turkeys in T3 and T4 had 2- and 5-points better FCR, respectively compared to the control (T1) group (Table 3). Additionally, there was a tendency (P = 0.072) for differences in relative intestinal length at d 21 (Fig. 1), with turkeys fed the basal diet showing longer intestinal sections compared to other treatments.

Discussion

To meet the increasing global demand for high quality animal proteins while reducing reliance on soybean protein in poultry feed, it is essential to explore nutritional alternatives such as enzyme supplementation in poultry nutrition. Soybean meal is commonly included in turkey diets at high levels, but its use can negatively impact growth performance due to the presence of β-mannans, which cannot be hydrolyzed efficiently by the birds because they lack the endogenous enzymes (Kiarie et al., 2021). The supplementation of an exogenous β-mannanase to turkey diets may mitigate the negative impacts of β-mannans on growth and immune response. The result of the current study showed an overall numerical effect of β-mannanase supplementation on growth performance parameters in young toms within the 35 day experimental period. The lack of statistically significant effect of β-mannanase supplementation in the current study may be due to variability in the growth of the birds as seen in the Root Mean Square Error value in Table 3. Soybean meal typically contains between 0.7% to 2.1% β-mannans (Kiarie et al., 2021; Hsiao et al., 2006) and in our formulation analyzed β-mannan concentration in the SBM was 2.98 %, indicating ample dietary substrate for enzymic hydrolysis. The β-mannanase, Natupulse® TS exhibits activity at pH range of 2 – 5.5 (unpublished data) allowing for the hydrolysis of β-mannan in the upper gastrointestinal tract. This may mitigate potential viscosity-related and digestibility limitations and dampen unnecessary epithelial innate-immune signaling.

Results from this study agree with earlier reports that demonstrates varying improvements in growth performance following β-mannanase supplementation. For example, Kiarie et al., 2021 demonstrated that β-mannanase effect on growth performance may influenced by the concentration of dietary anti-nutritional factors. Furthermore, Odetallah et al. (2002) observed 2.5% and 4% respective improvements in BW and feed efficiency in toms fed β-mannanase supplemented diets. Also, Jackson et al. (2006, 2008) demonstrated that β-mannanase improved growth and feed efficiency in turkeys fed corn-SBM diets, particularly when corn distillers dried grains with solubles (DDGS) was included. Nevertheless, the variability in performance outcomes reported in the literature compared to the current study highlights the complex interplay between enzyme supplementation, dietary composition, and bird physiology (Adeola and Cowieson, 2011; Bedford et al., 2022). In addition, we observed a tendency for reduced relative intestinal length in β-mannanase-fed birds at 21 days. This may suggest that the mannanase supplementation modulated gut physiology and morphology. Shortened intestinal length has been associated with improved feed efficiency due to reduced maintenance energy costs (Wu et al., 2004; Ravindran and Son, 2011; Ji et al., 2024).

In support of the gut morphology, supplementation of β-mannanase at high dose (T4) downregulated the ileal expression of HSPs, well known conserved chaperones involved in cellular stress responses including innate and adaptive immunity (Albert, 2002). The downregulation of HSPs in this study suggests a lower local stress level in the ileum (Dudeja et al., 2009), better ileal immune status (Routsias et al., 2006; van Eden et al., 2005), and better epithelial integrity and homeostasis (Wettstein et al., 2012). Interestingly, the treatment T4 also reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines ( IL6, and TNFα) and the acute phase protein CRP at day 21, which again indicates a lower inflammatory and better ileal immune status. The CRP is a well-known marker of inflammation produced mainly in the liver but to a minor extent in intestinal tissues (Vermeire et al., 2005), and its downregulation in the present study indicates low or inactive inflammatory processes. Interleukin 6, on the other hand, has been shown to primarily act as a pro-inflammatory cytokine involved in gut inflammation, but also contributing to intestinal epithelial cell repair and regeneration (Shahini and Shahini, 2023). Its decrease here indicates both low inflammatory status and low ileal injury. Depending on the context (physiological status of the animal, stress conditions, age, etc.), TNFα could regulate intestinal homeostasis and barrier integrity by enhancing cell survival or cell death (Ruder et al., 2019). As chicken TNFα has been reported to increase in response to plant antigens (Mi et al., 2025) as well as bacterial antigens (Lu et al., 2022), its downregulation in our experimental conditions supports the low inflammatory status described above. Although IL-10 is anti-inflammatory, its decrease here occurred together with reductions in IL-6, TNFα, CRP, and HSPs, which may reflect lower inflammatory stimulus and thus a reduced need for compensatory IL-10 signaling, rather than a contradiction to inflammation attenuation. Furthermore, the tested mannanase has been shown to have its highest activity in the range of pH 2 – 6.5 (data not published). This means that mannan structures, which are recognized by the Pattern Recognition Receptors (or PPRs) in the enterocytes of the small intestine and would induce the innate immune response might already be degraded prior to the intestine, thus explaining a reduced inflammatory response as indicated by HSPs as well as pro and anti-inflammatory cytokines. However, it is important to mention that because direct redox endpoints (ROS, MDA or antioxidant enzyme activities) were not measured and SOD1, CAT, and GPX1 mRNA were unchanged, we cannot claim a definitive reduction in oxidative stress due to β-mannanase in the current study.

Interestingly, the expression of key cellular stress markers, including SOD1, CAT1, and GPX1, was not significantly affected by increasing dietary doses of β-mannanase (T1 to T4) at day 21, indicating no difference in need to produce those antioxidant enzymes at this point of time. As concentrations of SOD, CAT and GPX in blood were not measured, conclusions on the immediate oxidative status cannot be made. Maintaining unaltered expression of these markers indicates that the antioxidant defense system remained uncompromised at this age in reducing reactive oxygen species and inflammation (Lubos et al., 2010) and maintaining intestinal epithelial cell integrity and homeostasis (Hendren et al., 2021). Additionally, β-mannanase supplementation reduced the expression of CLDN1 and JAM3 at day 21, which suggests an improvement of ileal epithelial cell survival (Eftang et al., 2013) and intestinal barrier integrity. It has been shown that an upregulation of CLDN1 decreases goblet cell number and increases susceptibility to mucosal inflammation (Pope et al., 2014). Furthermore, the downregulation of JAM3, which plays a key role in transmigration and immune cell infiltration (Arcangeli et al., 2013), suggests a low ileal inflammation status in our experimental conditions, most likely because of reduced PAMP but also increased butyrate availability (Segain et al., 2000). Although not measured in the current study, a previous study found that the microbial fermentation of mannan oligosaccharides resulted in increased butyrate concentrations (Kim et al., 2025, data not published) following mannanase supplementation in broiler diets. This may also have contributed to an improved barrier function in the present study, as suggested by Zheng et al (2017).

In summary, supplementation of β-mannanase had not impact on growth performance of toms during 0 – 35 days posthatching. However, ileal expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, HSPs, and tight junction proteins were downregulated by β-mannanase administration, hinting to lower cellular stress, reduction of inflammatory processes, and improved barrier function. Further studies, including the analysis of tight junctions, gap junctions, adherens, desmosomes, and scaffolding and regulatory proteins at both gene and protein levels are warranted to better understand the mode of action of β-mannanase.

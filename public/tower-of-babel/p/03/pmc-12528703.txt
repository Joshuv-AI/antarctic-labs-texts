Model-free adaptive fixed-time prescribed performance control for PEMFC air feeding system based on oxygen excess ratio reconstruction
Authors: Omer Abbaker Ahmed Mohammed, Shusheng Xiong, Liu Qingsheng, Meng Kai, Haoping Wang, Abubakar Unguwanrimi Yakubu, Gomaa Haroun Ali Hamid, Ahmed Mohamed Ishag
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs) systems with unmeasurable variables, output constraints, time-varying disturbances, and uncertain dynamics can hardly be modelled well, such that model-based control strategies become infeasible. Therefore, an ultra-local model (ULM) only utilizing input and output signals of the PEMFC system is innovatively presented. Together with a fixed-time disturbance observer (FxTDO), fixed-time prescribed performance control, and an adaptive technique-based compensator, a novel internal state estimator-based model-free adaptive fixed-time prescribed performance control (MF-AFxTPPC) is naturally developed to estimate and regulate the oxygen excess ratio (OER). Main advantages are presented as follows: (1) The proposed internal state estimator is designed to estimate OER via FxTDO, which is known for its fixed-time convergence and low computational time. (2) The presented ULM algorithm not only reduces the complexity of controller design but also significantly enhances its flexibility and adaptability. (3) The FxTPPC scheme constrains the tracking error and stabilizes the closed-loop system, which is more favorable to practical implementations. (4) The adaptive technique-based compensator can not only compensate for estimation error and mitigate the input chattering but also guarantee the global robustness. Furthermore, the Lyapunov theorem is utilized to analyze the stability of the designed MF-AFxTPPC scheme. Finally, the numerical simulations on a nonlinear PEMFC model with the proposed controller strategy are achieved in a MATLAB/SIMULINK environment, and findings are given to exhibit the robustness and superiority of the presented method.

Introduction

With growing environmental challenges of traditional fuels, fuel cells (FCs) have recently received much attention1,2. Polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs) are an emerging energy technology that has gained widespread application in different fields due to the advantages of zero emissions, low operating temperature, low weight, high efficiency, and high-power density3–5. The PEMFC typically consists of several components, including a hydrogen supply system, an air-feeding system, thermal management system, a humidification system, a power management system, and a stack6,7, where the air-feeding system is responsible for delivering compressed air at the appropriate flow rate and pressure to the stack cathode. The airflow rate has a significant impact on the aging of the PEM and the overall power performance. Additionally, the pressure at the cathode is closely related to the mass transfer and the stress experienced by the PEM. The air feeding system is evaluated based on the OER value. A low OER can lead to oxygen starvation, which reduces the net power generated and can cause harmful degradation of the stack. On the other hand, an excessively high oxygen stoichiometry will result in oxygen deficiency, leading to increased parasitic power draw from the air compressor and significantly decreasing the overall system efficiency. Furthermore, a higher cathode pressure can improve oxygen diffusion through the gas diffusion layer (GDL), thereby accelerating the electrochemical reaction8.

However, OER is considered a key output variable of the air feeding system and significantly impacts the FC power efficiency and the aging of the PEM. Accordingly, accurate air feeding control is essential to enhance the stack’s lifespan and FC power performance9,10. Moreover, uncertainties, external disturbances, output constraints, and the strong nonlinearity of air feeding systems pose significant difficulties for air-feeding control11,12. Furthermore, the total pressure in the PEMFC stack’s cathode, known as cathode pressure (CP), cannot be accurately measured by any advanced sensor. Consequently, CP is used to calculate the OER value, making it a variable that is not directly measurable in practice.

Recently, various observers or estimators, including the algebraic observer, Leuenberger observer, high-gain observer, fuzzy observer, sliding mode observer (SMO), and Kalman filter (KF), have been extensively researched and applied to PEMFCs. Among these, the estimator-based KF, known for its precision, and the observer-based SMO, recognized for its robustness, have become a primary focus in developing improved control strategies for PEMFCs. Liu et al.13 developed an internal state estimator based on extended state observer (ESO) to estimate the oxygen stoichiometry from the measurements of the supply manifold pressure, the compressor flow rate and the load current. The authors performed experimental validations to assess the robustness of the designed ESO against parametric uncertainties and load disturbances. Pilloni et al.14 designed an observer using a high-order sliding mode technique for OER control, demonstrating strong robustness and rapid convergence despite external disturbances and parameter uncertainties. Diab et al.15 developed an observer using an extended KF to approximate the PEMFC parameters. The authors conducted experimental validations to evaluate the dynamic response and effectiveness of the observer using different operating conditions. In addition, Majid et al.16 provided a comparative analysis between the extended KF scheme and super twisting sliding mode (STSM) algorithm, revealing that the extended KF provides better results than the STSM regarding response speed and convergence time. Moreover, several recent algorithms for KFs have been presented; for example, Yuan et al.17 developed a novel observer based on adaptive cubature KF for a 5th-order air-feeding system model, demonstrating exceptional precision and convergence. It is essential to acknowledge that deviations in designed coefficients are inevitable throughout the service lifespan of a PEMFC, resulting from operational conditions and model assumptions. These deviations will significantly impact the observer precision, highlighting the need for further investigation into how the designed gains of the built-in observer influence the performance of estimation.

The issue of regulating OER has been studied and researched in the literature using various approaches. Pukrushpan et al.18 developed a high-precision model for a fuel cell system and proposed an LQR controller based on the linearized model; however, this approach has limited ability to manage significant nonlinearities. PID controllers with extended structures based on fuzzy logic control (FLC) are proposed for PEMFCs6,19–21. It should be realized that the efficiency of an FLC scheme relies largely on how to build its knowledge base, which is usually influenced by practical experience and engineering expertise. A model-based robust control based on the second-order sliding mode algorithm is proposed to regulate the oxygen stoichiometry in the PEMFC system13. This approach aimed to maximize net power output while avoiding oxygen starvation, ensuring optimal performance and system stability. Deng et al.22 introduced a variable structure control (VSC) strategy as a specific form of nonlinear control for regulating airflow. Although the VSC is recognized for its robustness against parameter variations and disturbances, its simple structure and the absence of online system identification in real applications present challenges. One significant issue is the chattering effect during the control process. To address this problem, Silaa et al.23 developed quasi-continuous HOSM techniques for PEMFC to reduce chattering and enhance power quality and output performance. Moreover, nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) is employed for regulating airflow rate due to its ability to manage input constraints and nonlinear dynamics7,8,11. However, the significant computational demand of NMPC presents challenges in achieving real-time performance for the control system. While these presented methods make substantial contributions to flow rate control, it is essential to note that cathode pressure is not considered as a factor in their analysis.

Additionally, managing performance constraints in practical applications is a significant research area. Several recent studies have introduced the funnel function-based backstepping approach and a barrier Lyapunov function controller to ensure prescribed control performance9,24. However, few studies have focused on the performance constraints explicitly associated with controlling the PEMFC air-feeding system. For example, Kim et al.25 developed an adaptive control strategy using a model-based approach to address system nonlinearity, incorporating asymmetric constraints of OER to avoid oxygen deficiency.

Since the complexity of controlled systems continues to grow, developing model-based control schemes has become increasingly challenging, especially when aiming for optimal performance. As a result, model-free control (MFC) techniques have received much attention because they are independent of specific system models such as intelligent PID (iPID)1,26, active disturbance rejection control27, iterative learning adaptive control28, and data-driven model-free adaptive control29,30. Among these advanced MFCs, iPID based on the ULM algorithm has been intensively researched in recent years. Howsoever, its robustness mainly depends on the ability of the proposed estimator/observer to observe unknown and uncertain system dynamics, such as the algebraic observer (AO)31, time delay estimation (TDE)32, extended state observers (ESO)1,33 and disturbance observer (DOB)34. However, both AO and TDE inherently have approximation errors because of time windows and inherent delays. Furthermore, while ESOs and DOBs can achieve asymptotic observation, they can only reduce approximation errors to zero when the time derivative of the accumulated uncertainties approaches a small region near zero. This implies that the approximation error will converge to a small region near zero as time reaches infinity. Consequently, the issues of fixed-time stability and the attainment of zero approximation error are not sufficiently addressed.

Adjusting controller coefficients through trial and error is a commonly employed method to achieve optimal performance in control systems; however, this approach introduces complexities in implementing controllers. Prescribed performance control (PPC) provides a more efficient approach for determining error performance, thereby ensuring the effectiveness of the controller before tuning its parameters35–37. In addition, the PPC algorithm has addressed various practical problems in real systems; for instance, in active suspension systems, PPC has significantly improved passenger comfort by restricting displacement and pitch angle, thereby enhancing both transient and stable suspension responses38. In spacecraft missions, PPC ensures the accuracy and safety of a pursuing spacecraft during tracking and approaching an uncooperative target spacecraft39. In robotic systems, an adaptive control strategy integrating PPC algorithm, neural networks (NN), and sliding mode control was employed for trajectory tracking of a manipulator with uncertain dynamics and input saturation. This approach enhanced both transient response and steady-state performance40. For applications that require rapid and precise response from an inertial reference unit, the PPC scheme-based integral sliding mode control (ISMC) has been proposed37. Furthermore, He et al.41 designed an adaptive sliding mode PPC for a high-fidelity PEMFC system model despite uncertainties and external disturbances.

Although existing SMC schemes provide asymptotic or fast finite-time convergence, they are highly sensitive to initial system states. To address this, fixed-time SMC approaches have gained attention due to their ability to guarantee a convergence time that can be predefined by tuning controller parameters, independent of initial conditions42–44. Enhancing SMC with disturbance observers, such as the extended state observer (ESO)1,33,45,46, has proven effective for reducing chattering and relaxing disturbance bounds. While ESOs are promising for active disturbance rejection, their estimation errors tend to converge slowly and are still influenced by initial conditions. Some recent works have focused on fixed-time ESOs combined with fixed-time sliding mode control, ensuring bounded settling times regardless of initial conditions43,44.

Besides, compared to some existing works of fixed-time SMC techniques43,44,50–52, the proposed control strategy is validated on the complex nonlinear PEMFC system. A comprehensive performance evaluation is conducted, focusing on trajectory tracking using key metrics such as overshoot/undershoot, settling time, and Integral Absolute Error (IAE), Integral Square Error (ISE), Integral Time Square Error (ITSE), and Integral Time Absolute Error (ITAE). Besides, compared to some existing works of fixed-time SMC techniques43,44,50–52, the proposed control strategy is validated on the complex nonlinear PEMFC system. Extensive simulations results are obtained to demonstrate its tracking performance and robustness against uncertainties and external disturbances. In practical applications, the PEMFC is a crucial component in clean energy systems, frequently used in several scenarios such as fuel cell electric vehicles, autonomous drones, hybrid UAV systems, and stationary power generation. The MF-AFxTPPC controller is designed to meet the stringent performance and reliability requirements of these applications by ensuring precise air supply control and minimizing response time to load changes and disturbances. By implementing this controller, industries can achieve more stable and efficient fuel cell operation, ultimately contributing to the development of sustainable energy solutions.

Next, Sect. 4 introduces the details of simulation results and a discussion. Finally, Sect. 5 presents a concluding remark of the paper.

Problem statement

Generally, the automotive PEMFC plant, as shown in Fig. 1, is composed of three major components18: Hydrogen supply system: This system employs a high-pressure tank to store hydrogen and ensure its reliable delivery to the FC stack’s anode.Air-feeding system: This system supplies the cathode with oxygen extracted from the air.Humidification and thermal management system: This system regulates the temperature and humidity levels within the FCs to maintain optimal operating conditions and ensure efficient performance.In the hydrogen supply system, an electronic valve regulates the hydrogen flow to the anode, ensuring a sufficient and well-controlled flow that matches the air pressure at the cathode. Furthermore, because the thermodynamic response is much slower than the aerodynamic response, the humidity and temperature of the PEMFC plant can be controlled independently through local control mechanisms. This system allows the humidity and temperature to be maintained at the required levels, as their values do not fluctuate rapidly. Therefore, the primary focus is on the air supply system. Developing an effective and precise controller for the air supply system is essential to maintaining the required airflow rate.

Control objectives

The primary goal of this work is to construct and implement an effective and highly accurate control strategy for stabilizing the PEMFC plant. This innovative approach is specifically designed to regulate OER and prevent oxygen deficiency, which can lead to inefficiencies and system degradation. Additionally, the strategy is designed to ensure optimal power generation through maximum power point tracking, even under fluctuating load conditions. By achieving these goals, the control scheme significantly enhances the overall operational efficiency and reliability of the PEMFC system through various operating scenarios. Due to practical constraints, the OER control strategy must satisfy the following requirements: Uncertain system dynamics and disturbances: The strategy should exhibit high adaptability to time-varying conditions, such as changes in load current, and demonstrate strong robustness against system uncertainties, including variations in partial model parameters.Indirect measurements: Given the absence of direct measurements for cathode pressure (CP) and OER, a proper observer is essential to estimate these unavailable variables accurately. Remark 5

The observer based-MF-AFxTPPC strategy has been developed to ensure the regulation of the OER within a predefined fixed convergence time. Based on the detailed proof above, the combined FxTDO integrated with the MF-AFxTPPC guarantees that the overall system’s convergence time does not exceed upper boundaries, corresponding to each subcomponent’s fixed-time bound. The total fixed-time convergence for the overall control system in this paper is obtained by summing the individual fixed times of each submodule. Specifically, the overall upper bound of the system’s settling time is given by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{f,max}\le t_{f_{1}}+t_{f_{2}}+t_{f,co}$$\end{document}55.

Remark 6

To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to propose model-free adaptive fixed-time prescribed performance control (AFxTPPC) in PEMFC air feeding system using the fixed-time disturbance observer (FxTDO). The combination of FxTDO and AFxTPPC with fixed time SMC can not only accelerate the convergence time of the overall control system but also enhance the accuracy of the control system. Different from the previously designed adaptive fixed-time prescribed performance control52,55,56 often rely on precise system modelling or asymptotic convergence, our approach employs a model-free adaptive framework that guarantees fixed-time convergence of both the tracking error and disturbance estimation, independent of initial conditions or unknown system parameters. Furthermore, unlike existing FxTDOs51,55,57, our design introduces a more robust and accurate estimator that can achieve fixed-time convergence even under highly nonlinear, uncertain conditions. This enhances real-time disturbance rejection capabilities and improves overall system robustness.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose an innovative observer-based MF-AFxTPPC for the PEMFC system, designed to accurately reconstruct and regulate the OER despite challenges such as unmeasurable variables, output constraints, external disturbances, and uncertain system dynamics. The employed FxTDO technique features a simple structure, effectively estimating unmeasurable variables and unmodeled system dynamics. Furthermore, the robust MF-AFxTPPC ensures optimal tracking performance. Simulation results demonstrate that, compared to PID and FxTDO-iPI controllers, the suggested MF-AFxTPPC approach shows superior performance under various operating conditions.

Although this work addresses several challenges, including output constraints, unmeasurable variables, uncertainties, and time-varying disturbances, the proposed scheme has not yet been tested under fault conditions. Future research will focus on addressing issues such as sensor and actuator faults, as well as amplitude and rate saturation of the actuators. Additionally, the current study’s focus on air supply control overlooks the broader impact on overall fuel cell efficiency; hence, future work will aim to incorporate direct optimization of fuel cell efficiency. Finally, while the validation through MATLAB/Simulink has been promising, real-world implementation and robustness will be further investigated via hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) test benches to ensure practical applicability under realistic operational conditions.

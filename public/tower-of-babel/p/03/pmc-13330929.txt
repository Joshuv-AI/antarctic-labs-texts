Introduction

The incidence of cardiovascular implantable electronic device infection (CIEDI) is increasing and remains associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Transvenous lead extraction (TLE) represents the cornerstone of management, yet outcome prediction after extraction is still suboptimal.1,2

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a well-recognized adverse prognostic factor in this setting, but it is typically treated as a binary variable.1–3 In the IN2DEEP study, we evaluated the association between T2DM phenotype and all-cause mortality after TLE for CIEDI.

Methods

We prospectively enrolled 335 consecutive adult patients undergoing TLE for CIEDI at the Cardiology Unit of IRCCS Azienda Ospedaliero-Universitaria di Bologna (March 2011−October 2024). After excluding two patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus, patients were stratified into three predefined groups: non-diabetic (noT2DM), non–insulin-treated T2DM (ni-T2DM), and insulin-treated T2DM (i-T2DM). CIEDI was diagnosed according to ESC/EHRA recommendations.4–8 Five patients who died during index hospitalization were excluded from time-to-event analyses, yielding 328 patients for survival analysis. The primary endpoint was all-cause mortality over the full available follow-up, obtained through scheduled visits or telephone contact. Kaplan–Meier survival estimates were calculated at 12, 24, and 36 months. The 36-month horizon was pre-specified as the RMST reference time point because it corresponded to the median observed follow-up of the cohort.

Associations with mortality were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models stratified by BMI category (>25 vs. ≤25 kg/m2, due to violation of the proportional hazards assumption by Schoenfeld residuals, P = 0.044). The final multivariable model included insulin treatment status (i-T2DM vs. noT2DM or ni-T2DM), chronic kidney disease (CKD; eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2), LVEF < 50% (LVEF < 50%; HFrEF and HFmrEF showed comparable and significantly higher mortality vs. preserved, P < 0.05 for both), and age per decade.

Results

A total of 127 deaths occurred during a median follow-up 38 (17–68) months. The cohort comprised 228 noT2DM (68.5%), 49 ni-T2DM (14.7%), and 53 i-T2DM (16.0%) patients. Patients with i-T2DM showed significantly higher rates of CKD (59.3% vs. 34.8%, P < 0.001) and reduced LVEF (79.6% vs. 56.6%, P = 0.002) compared to the non-insulin group, while age, sex, BMI, hypertension, and COPD were similarly distributed.

Kaplan–Meier estimates demonstrated consistently higher mortality in i-T2DM patients. At 12 months, mortality was 28.9% in i-T2DM vs. 10.7% in noT2DM and 12.9% in ni-T2DM; at 24 months, 37.0% vs. 17.6% and 18.6%; and at 36 months, 50.6% vs. 26.6% and 21.0%, respectively. Log-rank analysis confirmed significant differences between i-T2DM and noT2DM (P = 0.002) and between i-T2DM and ni-T2DM (P = 0.09 full follow-up; P = 0.004 at 36 months, reflecting the pattern of late curve convergence). NoT2DM and ni-T2DM showed comparable survival curves at all time points (all P > 0.3). A time-split Cox model showed that the prognostic impact of i-T2DM was significant within the first 36 months [hazard ratio (HR) 2.10, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.28–3.44, P = 0.003], with no residual effect thereafter (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.13–2.29, P = 0.409). The RMST at 36 months was 24.5 months (95% CI 20.7–28.3) in i-T2DM vs. 31.1 months (95% CI 29.9–32.3) in the ni-T2DM, corresponding to a loss of 6.6 months of event-free survival (95% CI 2.6–10.6, P = 0.001).

In multivariable analysis, i-T2DM remained independently associated with all-cause mortality (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.07–2.65, P = 0.024), while noT2DM patients and those with ni-T2DM showed comparable and lower risk. CKD (HR 2.05, 95% CI 1.41–2.99, P < 0.001), LVEF < 50% (HR 1.87, 95% CI 1.26–2.78, P = 0.002), and age per decade (HR 1.66, 95% CI 1.36–2.01, P < 0.001) were also independent predictors.

Discussion

In this cohort, the prognostic impact of T2DM in patients undergoing TLE for CIEDI differs according to treatment phenotype. Insulin-treated T2DM was independently associated with increased all-cause mortality across the full available follow-up, whereas non-diabetic patients and those with non–insulin-treated T2DM showed comparable survival curves.

These findings extend those of Zoni et al.8, who reported increased mortality confined to insulin-treated T2DM following CIED implantation or replacement. By showing a similar pattern in the setting of CIED infection and TLE, our results provide extends and builds upon prior registry data in which T2DM was consistently associated with adverse outcomes, albeit with modest effect sizes.9,10

The observation that insulin treatment status rather than diabetes diagnosis per se drives the prognostic signal suggests that insulin requirement functions as a marker of advanced metabolic disease and end-organ vulnerability, rather than reflecting a direct pharmacological effect. This interpretation is supported by the higher prevalence of CKD (59.3% vs. 34.8%, P < 0.001) and reduced LVEF (79.6% vs. 56.6%, P = 0.002) in i-T2DM patients, consistent with a more advanced systemic disease burden, and by the persistence of the i-T2DM association after multivariable adjustment. The RMST difference of 6.6 months at 36 months provides a clinically meaningful quantification of this survival disadvantage beyond the proportional hazards framework.

Together, these observations raise the hypothesis that incorporating insulin treatment status into existing risk stratification models may offer additional prognostic information beyond diabetes status alone. Whether this translates into actionable clinical benefit remains to be established in prospective studies.

Limitations

This was a single-centre study conducted in a high-volume referral setting, which may limit generalizability. Exclusion of in-hospital deaths from survival analyses may introduce selection bias. Detailed data on glycaemic control, diabetes duration, and specific antidiabetic treatments were not available. The observational design precludes causal inference.

Conclusions

Among patients undergoing TLE for CIED infection, insulin-treated T2DM was associated with increased all-cause mortality over long-term follow-up, while non-diabetic patients and those with non–insulin-treated T2DM showed comparable outcomes. The magnitude of this difference, quantified as approximately 6.6 months of restricted mean survival time at 36 months, persisted after adjustment for CKD, LVEF, age, and BMI. These findings suggest that diabetes phenotype may carry prognostic information in this population; whether this should inform clinical decision-making warrants confirmation in larger, multicentre cohorts.

Safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (BMSC) for painful lumbar facet arthropathy: study protocol for a randomised, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, crossover phase II clinical trial
Authors: Dan Yan, Nisha C Durand, Zhen Wang, Michael D Osborne, Jeffrey A Stone, Sahil Gupta, David A Miller, Drew M Witter, Jason S Eldrige, Alfredo Quiñones-Hinojosa, Abba C Zubair, Wenchun Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLumbar facet arthropathy (LFA) is a degenerative, arthritic joint disorder and a common specific cause of axial low back pain and functional impairment. Currently, no disease-modifying therapies are available for symptomatic LFA. Mesenchymal stem/stromal cell (MSC)-based therapy represents an emerging regenerative treatment modality with the promise to target the underlying pathophysiology of LFA. Despite encouraging clinical evidence supporting the safety and efficacy of MSC therapy in other osteoarthritis, its therapeutic utility in LFA remains largely unexplored. We recently completed a prospective, single-arm, open-label phase I clinical trial that demonstrated a favourable safety profile and preliminary clinical benefit following intra-articular administration of bone marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs) in patients with symptomatic LFA. Building on these findings, we present the protocol for CellKine II, a prospective, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, cross-over phase II clinical trial to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of intra-articular delivery of allogeneic, culture-expanded BMSCs in patients with painful LFA.Methods and analysisThis study is a single-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, cross-over phase II trial with a target accrual of 40 patients. Eligible patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive a single intra-articular injection of either Arm A: BMSC or Arm B: Placebo (dimethyl sulfoxide). At the 6-month follow-up, all patients will be unblinded. Patients initially randomised to Arm A will continue follow-up for up to 12 months. Patients initially randomised to Arm B may cross over to receive the BMSC treatment, followed by an additional 12 months of post-BMSC treatment follow-up, or they may continue observation for another 6 months without BMSC treatment. Overall, each patient will be followed up to 12 months after BMSC treatment. The primary endpoints are back pain, functional outcomes and adverse events at 6 months post-randomisation.Ethics and disseminationThis study will be conducted in compliance with U.S. federal regulations and Mayo Clinic institutional research policies and procedures. The protocol has been approved by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board (IRB) and the Data Safety Monitoring Board (IRB No. 21-013278). The results of this trial will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and on ClinicalTrials.gov.Trial registration numberNCT06001853.Keywords: Mesenchymal Stem Cells, Back pain, Pain management, Clinical trials

Introduction

Background and rationale

Lumbar facet arthropathy (LFA), also known as facet joint osteoarthritis (OA), is a prevalent musculoskeletal disorder which is a common cause of axial low back pain and a major contributor to disability and socioeconomic burdens.1 The pathogenesis of LFA is multifactorial, involving mechanical, inflammatory and metabolic processes that collectively drive disease progression.2 The facet joints are susceptible to degenerative and proliferative changes that involve not only the entire joint but also surrounding soft tissues, including the articular cartilage, synovium and subchondral bone. These pathological alterations commonly manifest as localised pain.3

Current treatments for mild to moderate LFA typically begin with non-invasive approaches, such as pharmacological therapies (eg, topical or oral analgesics, opioids) and physical therapy, and may progress to interventional procedures (eg, intra-articular corticosteroid or anaesthetic injections, medial branch rhizotomy).4 5 However, these options are limited to symptomatic management and provide short to medium-term analgesic effects.6 At present, there are no licensed disease-modifying therapies that could address the degenerative and proliferative changes underlying LFA.

Mesenchymal stem/stromal cell (MSC)-based therapy represents a promising regenerative treatment modality, attributable to their multifaceted therapeutic effects, including anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and trophic properties. There is also increasing clinical evidence supporting the safety and efficacy of MSCs in the treatment of knee OA, which shares anatomical and pathophysiological similarities with LFA.7 8 However, its therapeutic utility in LFA remains largely unexplored. With that, we initiated our CellKine I, a single-arm, open-label phase I clinical trial, to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of human allogeneic bone marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs) at Mayo Clinic Florida (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT04410731). The CellKine phase I findings suggested a favourable safety profile for intra-articular BMSC delivery in patients with painful LFA, along with preliminary therapeutic benefits, including back pain relief and improved function, hereby justifying our present CellKine phase II to further investigate its efficacy and safety through a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, cross-over design (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06001853).9 10

Methods/design

Study design, setting and recruitment

This will be a prospective, double-blind, randomised, cross-over, phase II trial evaluating the safety adn efficacy of intra-articular delivery of allogeneic BMSCs for the treatment of painful LFA. A total of 40 patients will be enrolled at the Mayo Clinic in Florida. Eligible patients will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive a single intra-articular injection of either Arm A (BMSC) or Arm B (dimethyl sulfoxide, DMSO). At the 6-month follow-up, all patients will be unblinded. Patients initially randomised to Arm A will continue follow-up up to 12 months. Patients initially randomised to Arm B may cross over to receive the BMSC treatment, followed by an additional 12 months of post-BMSC treatment follow-up, or they may continue observation for another 6 months without BMSC treatment. Overall, each patient will be followed up to 12 months after BMSC treatment. The trial schema is presented in figure 1. With IRB approval, we will inform our referring providers about the initiation of this study.

Eligibility criteria

To be eligible for inclusion in this study, patients must meet all of the following criteria: age ≥18 years; a diagnosis of symptomatic LFA confirmed by clinical and radiographic evidence involving levels L3–S1; and facetogenic pain relief of at least 75% following double medial branch blocks (MBBs). Blinding and randomisation

Personnel preparing the investigational product (IP), the study monitor/clinical research associate and the statistician will remain unblinded throughout the study period. All patients and study personnel involved in patient-related activities, including investigators and research support staff will remain blinded to treatment assignment until completion of the 6-month post-randomisation follow-up visit. Both blinded and unblinded staff can perform screening activities prior to the IP administration (Visit 2). Randomisation will be proceeded after participation eligibility is confirmed after Visit 1 (Screening). The allocation sequence will be generated using Medidata Rave (Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 21 CFR Part 11 compliant Electronic Data Capture solution) built-in algorithms for dynamic allocation or prevalidated methods for permuted blocks, which will not be accessible to staff involved in patient recruitment or related activities. The statistician will perform concealed restricted randomisation in a 1:1 ratio to Arm A (BMSC) or Arm B (placebo/DMSO) using the randomisation tool in Medidata Rave offered by the Mayo Research Service Center. Allocation will be shared only with personnel involved in IP preparation before the procedure on Visit 1 (day 0) to allow sufficient time for preparation of the IP or placebo, including labelling, release verification, documentation and chain-of-custody procedures before injection. The allocation will be concealed from patients, investigators and care providers, with no access to the randomisation list until the time of unblinding.

Early unblinding will occur only if deemed necessary for patient safety by the participating physicians, the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) or a regulatory authority and will be limited to emergency situations. Such unblinding is expected to be rare and does not necessarily require study discontinuation.

IP manufacturing, preparation and administration

Human allogeneic BMSCs will be manufactured and dispensed by the Human Cell Therapy Laboratory at Mayo Clinic, Jacksonville, in compliance with cGMP standards. The MSC stock, derived from bone marrow aspirate, is expanded from the adherent fraction of fresh bone marrow aspirate using the Quantum Cell Expansion System (TerumoBCT) in αMEM supplemented with 5% human platelet lysate to establish a fully tested and characterised Master Cell Bank (MCB).11 The BMSC final product is generated by further expansion of individual MCB samples, then formulated in CryoStor CS5 (5% DMSO) at 10×106 cells/mL in CellSeal vials. The placebo (DMSO, CryoStor CS5) will be prepared in the same manner but without BMSCs. At Visit 1 (day 0), two vials of BMSCs/DMSO are thawed at 37°C using the ThawSTAR Automated Thawing System and aseptically transferred into 3 mL Luer-lock syringes for intra-articular injection.

Investigational agents will be administered via fluoroscopically guided intra-articular injection under sterile conditions. Each injection will contain 10×106 cells in 1 mL per facet joint, with a total of two affected joints treated. Target facet joints between L1 and S1 will be selected based on the screening evaluation. A 22-gauge, 3.5-inch spinal needle will be used, or a 22-gauge, 7-inch needle if additional length is required. Local anaesthesia will be achieved with 2–5 mL of 1% lidocaine to infiltrate peri-articular tissues without entering the disc space or facet joint. Intra-articular placement will be confirmed with fluoroscopy and 0.3 mL of Omnipaque 180 before injecting BMSC/DMSO into the facet joint space. Patients will be monitored for 60 min, with vital signs recorded every 5 min for the first 15 min, then every 15 min thereafter, and discharged once stable. All participants will receive standardised postinjection instructions as follows:

Day 0–1: Bed rest.

Day 3: Initiate gentle ambulation, gradually increasing activity as tolerated.

Days 3–14: Avoid repetitive bending, stooping and lifting >10–15 lbs.

Day 14: Resume normal daily activities.

Day 28: Return to competitive sports.

Study endpoints and outcomes

The primary endpoints are as follows: (1) back pain, measured by Numeric Pain Rating Scale at 6 months post-randomisation; (2) function, measured by Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System Computerized Adaptive Tests (PROMIS CATs) at 6 months post-randomisation and (3) safety evaluated by the nature, incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs), defined as any untoward or undesirable medical occurrence including signs, symptoms, abnormal findings or diseases that emerge or worsen relative to baseline during the study, regardless of causal relationship at 6 months post-randomisation.

The secondary endpoint includes (1) pain and functional outcomes, as defined by the primary endpoints, measured at 12 months post-BMSC treatment; (2) safety, evaluated by AEs at 12 months post-BMSC treatment; (3) narcotic use and work status at 6 months post-randomisation and 12 months post-BMSC treatment and (4) facet joint anatomical structures assessed using Czervionke-Fenton scale on MRI, with a focus on joint effusion, bone marrow lesions, periarticular oedema, osteophytes and the degree of facet synovitis at 6 months post-randomisation and 12 months post-BMSC treatment.

Study procedures

Potential eligible patients from Department of Pain, Mayo Clinic in Florida will be invited. At the screening visit, the study team will obtain written informed consent, confirm patient eligibility and establish baseline clinical characteristics (PROMIS Numeric Rating Scale-Pain Intensity, and VAS, PROMIS CATs, Narcotic Use Questionnaire, Mayo Clinic Work Status Questionnaire). Screening will also include double MBBs, which is the current standard of care for identifying levels of pain with high sensitivity and specificity.12 Eligible patients will then be randomised to either arm A (BMSC) or arm B (DMSO) in a 1:1 ratio. Following the treatment at Visit 2 (day 0), all patients will be followed according to the protocol-defined schedule of events for safety and efficacy assessments at day 3, day 14, month 3 and month 6 post-randomisation. After visit 5 (month 6), all patients will be unblinded. Patients initially randomised to Arm A (BMSC) will continue follow-up at visit 7 (12 months). Patients initially randomised to Arm B (DMSO) may cross over at Visit 7 (BMSC Injection) to receive the BMSC treatment, followed by an additional 12 months of post-BMSC treatment follow-up, or they may continue observation for another 6 months without BMSC treatment at Visit 7 (12 month). Eligibility for BMSC treatment will be re-assessed at the end of visit 6 (month 6) before visit 7 (BMSC Injection). Detailed assessments and schedule of events are outlined in online supplemental table 1 and online supplemental table 2.

Sample size determination

The sample size was calculated based on the numeric pain rating scale. We estimated that we will have 80% power to detect a 4-point difference (SD of 4 points) with a sample size of 34 patients (17 patients in each group) at two-sided α=0.05. With an estimated 15% attrition, 40 patients will be recruited.

Statistical methods/plan

The null hypothesis will be tested that there is no statistically significant difference between Arm A (BMSC)—defined as 50% reduction in low back pain, 4-point increase in PROMIS Physical Function score or 7-point decrease in PROMIS Pain Interference score—compared with the Arm B (DMSO) at 3 and 6 months post-randomisation. The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle. Sensitivity analyses will be performed on the per-protocol analysis to account for protocol deviations that may affect primary outcomes. Primary efficacy and safety analyses will be focused on the comparison in outcomes between the two arms at each visit before cross-over. Exploratory responder analyses will be performed to evaluate changes from baseline to 3, 6 and 12 months following BMSC treatment. For patients initially randomised to Arm B (DMSO) who subsequently cross over to arm A (BMSC), the pretreatment assessment at visit 6 (month 6) will be used as the baseline for post-crossover analyses.

Data collection and management

The study case report form will serve as the primary data collection instrument. Data will be collected by the clinical trial staff. Two web-based database systems will be used: Florence eBinders, a standardised clinical trial eBinder system deployed by Mayo Clinic Research, for regulatory eBinders and the electronic trial master file; and Medidata Rave, an FDA 21 CFR Part 11–compliant (EDC) system provided by the Mayo Research Service Center, for managing and reporting clinical research data. Both database systems incorporate built-in access controls, data integrity safeguards and audit trails. All study staff involved in the conduct of this trial must have documented the completion of training in the protection of human subjects and the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act Privacy Rule. Patient confidentiality and privacy will be protected by the participating investigators, study staff, sponsors and their designated agents, in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines, applicable federal regulations and institutional policies. Electronic study records and files will be accessible only to authorised study personnel. Quality control procedures will be implemented at each stage of data handling to ensure accuracy, completeness and reliability. Original source data will be retained to allow verification of any transformed data against the original records.

Monitoring and oversight

The principal investigator is responsible for overseeing participant safety at the study site. Safety monitoring will include careful assessment and timely reporting of AEs, as described above, as well as the development and implementation of a site-specific data and safety monitoring plan. Medical monitoring will involve regular evaluation of the number and type of serious AEs. In addition, a Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will be established to review the final protocol prior to study initiation and before patient enrolment begins. Following the initial review, and at periodic intervals throughout the study, the DSMB will be responsible for safeguarding participant safety, evaluating factors that may influence study outcomes, and considering relevant external information should it become available. Further information is available in the Mayo Clinic Data and Safety Monitoring Guidelines at: https://www.mayo.edu/research/documents/43-data-and-safety-monitoring-guidelinespdf/doc-10026780.

Discussion

This phase II trial is being conducted to extend our initial phase I findings, which demonstrated the safety and preliminary efficacy profiles of intra-articular delivery of human allogeneic BMSCs in the treatment of painful LFA. The present phase II trial is designed to rigorously evaluate safety adn efficacy in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover setting. In addition to clinical outcomes, biomarker analyses may be incorporated to provide insights on potential predictors for therapeutic response.

This trial will implement an unblinding and crossover procedure at 6 months following the initial treatment allocation. Our preliminary phase I preliminary efficacy data suggests that BMSCs may confer therapeutic benefit; therefore, the crossover design will be implemented to permit patients the option to receive BMSC treatment if desired. The timing of unblinding and crossover was selected jointly by the study investigators and the patient advocate to balance the need for a sufficient blinded observation period to assess safety and efficacy with the ethical obligation to offer the IP within a reasonable timeframe.

We propose using DMSO (CryoStor CS5) as the formulation-matching placebo arm because BMSCs will be manufactured and cryopreserved in CryoStor CS5 under a validated cGMP process and the administration in same vehicle minimises post-thaw manipulation and preserves product integrity. In addition, DMSO has been reported to possess anti-inflammatory properties.13 Therefore, using DMSO/CryoStor CS5 as the fomulation-matched control allows a more rigorous assessment of the therapeutic effects attributable specifically to BMSCs. The placebo arm contains 5% DMSO, a concentration widely used in cryopreservation protocols that has been generally well tolerated in humans.14,16 However, DMSO may not be biologically inert and could affect clinical outcomes. Any beneficial effect may reduce the observed between-group difference, whereas any unfavourable effect may exaggerate the apparent benefit of BMSCs. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as a comparison with a DMSO/CryoStor CS5 control rather than an inert saline placebo or sham procedure.

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration in future clinical trial development. First, the sample size is relatively small with a predefined 4-point difference on numeric pain rating scale between the intervention and the control groups. We may not have sufficient power to detect the significant difference. Second, this trial is being conducted at a single-site quaternary medical centre, which may limit the external validity. The patients recruited in this study may differ from those treated in broader clinical practice. Future studies should consider a multicentre design across diverse clinical settings to enhance the generalisability of the results. Third, although the 6-month randomised stage may be insufficient to fully assess long-term durability, it provides a balanced design for comparative efficacy analysis while limiting prolonged placebo exposure. Lastly, the primary outcome is based on self-reported pain scores, which are inherently subjective. While biochemical markers of inflammation or structural degeneration have been explored in other studies such as knee OA17 18 to assess disease progression and provide mechanistic insight following treatment, local facet joint aspiration for biospecimen collection was not practically feasible in this trial. However, the inclusion of a comparison arm and a prespecified minimal clinically important difference helps strengthen interpretation of the outcomes and reduce bias.

In summary, the study design and methodology presented in this manuscript are expected to offer clinical, operational, and regulatory insights to inform and guide future clinical investigations of MSC-based therapies for degenerative musculoskeletal conditions.

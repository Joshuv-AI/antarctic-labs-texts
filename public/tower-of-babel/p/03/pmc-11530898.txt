Protocol for the dissociation of adult human primary cardiomyocytes using a methylcellulose-supplemented digestion solution
Authors: Xun Shi, Yafei Huang, Bingying Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryHuman primary cardiomyocytes are a valuable in vitro model for studying human heart physiology and disease. Here, we present a protocol for the dissociation of adult human primary cardiomyocytes using a methylcellulose-supplemented digestion solution. We describe steps for sample collection, tissue pre-processing, and cell dissociation. We then detail procedures for calcium reintroduction and viability assessment. This protocol can significantly improve the efficiency of cardiomyocyte isolation and subsequent cell function, making them an even more reliable model for cardiac research.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Shi et al.1Subject areas: cell biology, cell culture, cell isolation, molecular biology

Before you begin

Cardiovascular disease is one of the most common causes of deaths worldwide. Accurate modeling of cardiac homeostasis, dysfunction, and drug response is central to the progression of cardiac research. Adult human primary cardiomyocytes (hPCMs) isolated directly from human heart tissue are a promising cell model, but inconsistent isolation efficiency and quality prevent them from being reliable and flexible in vitro cardiac models. The following protocol describes specific steps for obtaining human primary cardiomyocytes from fresh human myocardial tissue.

Please ensure that all appropriate institutional permissions required to work with human samples are granted prior to beginning the protocol detailed below. In addition, please note that the protocol is optimized for the isolation of human primary cardiomyocytes from adult patients, and also works for younger patients greater than the age of 3.

1.Prepare methylcellulose (MC) solution at least three days in advance.a.Prepare 150 mL of ultrapure water (Milli-Q, IQ 7005, 18.2 MΩ cm) and heat it up to 50°C–60°C (Figure 1A).b.Weigh 7.5 g of MC powder (Sigma, m7027) and pour it into 150 mL ultrapure water (for making 5% MC stock solution) (Figure 1B).c.Keep the temperature at 50°C–60°C for 12 h to let it swell sufficiently2 (Figures 1B and 1C). The powder settles to the bottom of the solution and swells when it is fully dissolved (Figure 1C).d.Stir for 24 h, at 60°C, and 1500 rpm to aid dissolution (Figure 1D).e.Place at 4°C overnight and observe the dissolution state. After complete dissolution, the solution is transparent and light yellow (Figure 1E).

Preparation and sectioning of tissue samples

Timing: 20 min to 1 h

This step is specific to sampling and tissue sectioning. The time of slicing is proportional to the size and the degree of fibrosis or calcification of the tissue. The larger the tissue or the more severe the pathology, the longer the slicing time.

8.Acquisition of tissue: Place the surgically resected cardiac specimen immediately into the pre-cooled UW solution prepared in step preparation 4 from tube #1.

Acquisition of tissue: Place the surgically resected cardiac specimen immediately into the pre-cooled UW solution prepared in step preparation 4 from tube #1.

Note: This protocol works well with cardiac samples from hearts with or without cardioplegia.

It is recommended to switch it to another pre-cooled one for maintaining the low temperature to protect cardiac samples. Using ice is another option.c.Gently transfer the tissue into the petri dish using sterile tweezers.d.Cardiac tissue: gently hold the tissue with a pair of small ophthalmic tweezers cut with a pair of small straight scissors, and remove fatty tissue (yellow) and other impurities (Figure 3B).e.Weigh the tissue.Note: To accurately determine the mass of the tissue, place the tissue on a sterile gauze and gently wipe away any excess liquid. Place the tissue into a small flask containing the appropriate UW liquid (from tube #2). Record the weight increment of the UW, noting the difference in weight before and after placing the tissue. This weight increment is the weight of the tissue.

Expected outcomes

The expected viability of left atrial appendage cells is anticipated to range from 30% to 65%, with an average viability of 50% ± 13%. The left atrial cell yield is expected to range from 2 million to 7 million cells per gram of tissue, with an average yield of 4.68 million ± 2 million cells per gram. For ventricular tissues obtained from heart transplant recipients (end-stage heart failure), the average cell viability is 25.24 ± 6.31%, and the expected yield is 0.1 million to 1 million cells per gram of tissue. This level of viability and cell number is suitable for cell modeling. For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Shi et al.1

Limitations

Batch variability can arise from differences in myocardial tissue obtained from different patients, including variations in cell condition, size, and overall health status. To minimize this variability, it is advisable to use myocardial tissue from patients with the same disease state and to standardize isolation methods. Additionally, it is important to consider and document these variations to determine their impact on experimental results.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Limited Quantity of Isolated Cells.

Reason: The high cell death rate during the isolation process or the inherently limited number of cardiomyocytes in the original tissue.

Potential solution

•Tissue Pre-Processing: During the several steps of tissue processing prior to digestion, including tissue trimming, slicing, and trituration, it is crucial to be as gentle as possible, and avoid pinching or pulling, to minimize mechanical stress-induced damage.

Tissue Pre-Processing: During the several steps of tissue processing prior to digestion, including tissue trimming, slicing, and trituration, it is crucial to be as gentle as possible, and avoid pinching or pulling, to minimize mechanical stress-induced damage.

•Adjustment for Severe Tissue Lesions: In cases of severe tissue damage, consider appropriately reducing the digestion time for Digestion Solution A. The step tissue digestion 2 can be shortened to 20 min to accommodate these conditions.

Adjustment for Severe Tissue Lesions: In cases of severe tissue damage, consider appropriately reducing the digestion time for Digestion Solution A. The step tissue digestion 2 can be shortened to 20 min to accommodate these conditions.

•When small amounts of tissue stick to the nylon cell filter during cell harvesting, they can be gently scraped off with a lab spoon or spatula, and placed back in the digestion solution, try to avoid pulling or damaging the tissue while scraping.

When small amounts of tissue stick to the nylon cell filter during cell harvesting, they can be gently scraped off with a lab spoon or spatula, and placed back in the digestion solution, try to avoid pulling or damaging the tissue while scraping.

Problem 2

Insufficient cell release (tissue does not turn pale).

Reason: The ratio between digestion solution volume and tissue size is critical to efficient digestion.

Potential solution

•Processing Larger Tissue Samples: If the cardiac specimen is too large, it can be divided into smaller tissues blocks, and be processed in groups using the same protocol. Be sure to prepare additional sets of consumables and extra solutions.

Processing Larger Tissue Samples: If the cardiac specimen is too large, it can be divided into smaller tissues blocks, and be processed in groups using the same protocol. Be sure to prepare additional sets of consumables and extra solutions.

•Scaling Up Cell Collection: If a larger of cells are collected, the volume of the collection solution can be increased to 6 mL, taking care that the CaCl2 concentration remains constant.

Scaling Up Cell Collection: If a larger of cells are collected, the volume of the collection solution can be increased to 6 mL, taking care that the CaCl2 concentration remains constant.

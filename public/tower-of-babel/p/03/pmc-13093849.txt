Safety of carbon dioxide extract from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Margarita Aguilera‐Gómez, Francesco Cubadda, Thomas Frenzel, Helle Katrine Knutsen, Henk van Loveren, Harry J McArdle, Peter Moldeus, Monika Neuhäuser‐Berthold, Josef Rudolf Schlatter, Alexandros Siskos, Viviana Trezza, Océane Albert, Elisa Beneventi, Marcello Laganaro, Irene Nuin Garciarena, Annamaria Rossi, Sebastian Berrio Cruz, Francesca Seminati, Maura Magani, Karen Ildico Hirsch‐Ernst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on a carbon dioxide (CO2) extract from industrial hemp varieties of the plant Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food (NF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The NF is proposed to be used in food supplements at maximum use levels ranging from 1.12 mg/day for children above 3 years of age to 3.60 mg/day for adults, excluding pregnant and lactating women. The NF contains cannabidiol (CBD) as well as other cannabinoids. The Panel notes that a substantial proportion of the NF remains uncharacterised. Stability and toxicological tests submitted by the applicant were performed with a material different from the NF under assessment, as it was produced using a different production process (i.e. isopropanol (IPA)‐based extraction instead of CO2 supercritical extraction), potentially resulting in different compositional data. Therefore, the absence of adequate stability and of toxicological studies performed with a representative test item prevents the Panel from drawing conclusions on the safety assessment of the NF. Additionally, no data were provided by the applicant to address the safety of the NF in the general population above 3 years of age. Therefore, the safety of the NF under the proposed conditions of use cannot be established.Keywords: cannabidiol, cannabinoids, Cannabis sativa, CBD, food supplement, hemp extract, novel food

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

On 12 March 2021, the company “Charlotte's Web, Inc.” submitted a request to the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 1 to authorise placing on the market a carbon dioxide extract from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food.

The application requests to authorise the use of the carbon dioxide extract from Cannabis sativa L. in food supplements as defined in Directive 2002/46/EC 2 for the general population excluding infants, young children, and pregnant and lactating women.

The applicant has requested data protection under Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 for data in support of this request.

In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the European Commission asks the European Food Safety Authority to provide a scientific opinion on the safety of carbon dioxide extract from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food.

In this opinion on carbon dioxide extract from Cannabis sativa L., the European Food Safety Authority should also document whether and to what extent the requirements of Article 26(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are fulfilled regarding the data for which the applicant is requesting data protection.

Additional information

Cannabidiol (CBD) is the active substance of Epidyolex®, a medicine approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Epidyolex® contains nearly 100% pure CBD and it is used as an adjuvant to treat patients suffering from Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome or tuberous sclerosis complex in patients from 2 years of age. 3

On 26 April 2022, the NDA Panel adopted a statement on safety of CBD as a novel food (NF), which outlined data gaps and uncertainties in the risk assessment. The Panel concluded that the safety of CBD as a NF cannot be established until gaps in both the experimental animal and human data are addressed (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022). On 16 December 2025, the NDA Panel adopted an update of the statement on safety of cannabidiol as a NF (EFSA NDA Panel, 2026). In this document, despite the remaining data gaps, a provisional safe dose of 0.0275 mg/kg bw per day (approximately 2 mg/day for a 70 kg adult) was derived for food supplement oily formulations with CBD purity ≥ 98%, without nanoparticles, for which the production process is considered safe and genotoxicity is ruled out. This provisional safe dose does not apply to individuals under 25 years of age, pregnant or lactating women, and those on concurrent medications.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The safety assessment of this NF is based on data supplied in the original application and information submitted by the applicant following EFSA's requests for supplementary information. 4

Administrative and scientific requirements for NF applications referred to in Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469. 5

A common and structured format on the presentation of NF applications is described in the EFSA guidance on the preparation and presentation of a NF application (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016). As indicated in this guidance, it is the duty of the applicant to provide all of the available (proprietary, confidential and published) scientific data (including both data in favour and not in favour) that are pertinent to the safety of the NF.

This NF application includes a request for protection of proprietary data in accordance with Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The data requested by the applicant to be protected comprise: Identity, Production process, Compositional data, Specifications, Proposed uses and use levels, and Toxicological information.

Methodologies

The assessment follows the methodology set out in the EFSA guidance on NF applications (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016) and the principles described in the relevant existing guidance documents from the EFSA Scientific Committee. The legal provisions for the assessment are laid down in Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and in Article 7 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469.

This assessment concerns only the risks that might be associated with the consumption of the NF under the proposed conditions of use and is not an assessment of the efficacy of the NF with regard to any claimed benefit.

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The NF which is the subject of the application is a carbon dioxide (CO2) extract from hemp varieties of the plant C. sativa L.

According to Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the NF falls under the category ‘food consisting of, isolated from, or produced from plants and their parts’.

The NF is produced by extraction of the flowers and leaves of C. sativa L. using CO2 and subsequent purification processes such as decarboxylation and separation of cannabinoids yielding approximately 50% weight by weight (w/w) CBD. The NF is proposed to be used as food supplement provided in form of oily liquid solution. 6 The proposed target population is the general population above 3 years of age, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women.

Identity of the NF

The NF is an extract obtained from the flowers and leaves from industrial hemp varieties of the plant C. sativa L. by CO2 supercritical extraction. It is a dark brown or yellow/amber viscous liquid containing approximately 50% w/w CBD. Other minor cannabinoids represent about 5%–6% w/w of the NF and terpenes about 2%–3% w/w.

C. sativa L. is an annual flowering plant belonging to the Cannabaceae family. The applicant specified hemp cultivars used for the production of the NF. The plant is cultivated in the United States (U.S., specifically Colorado, Kentucky and Oregon) and grown using pesticides.

A statement of the non‐use of genetically modified plants has been provided.

The identity of the plant was confirmed by macroscopic examination (i.e. visual inspection). The applicant also performed cannabinoids profiling on plant material using an internal liquid chromatography–diode array detection (LC–DAD) method, for which validation was not provided.

The same in‐house LC–DAD method was also used to characterise the identity of the NF, complementing analyses conducted by an accredited third‐party laboratory based on a modified version of the official Association of Official Analytical Collaboration (AOAC) Method 2018.11 for the quantification of major cannabinoids.

The applicant confirmed that the final formulation of the NF is in an oil matrix. Upon EFSA's request and following the clarification on the use of an octanol–water partition coefficient (Kow)‐based threshold for lipid‐soluble substances as possible appraisal route (Annex B – EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021), the applicant provided scientific rationale based on tabulated data ruling out potential concerns regarding the presence of small and nanoparticles in the oil matrix.

Production process

According to the information provided, the NF is produced in line to Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) principles.

Hemp is grown at dedicated cultivation farms in the U.S. from feminised seeds sourced from a central seed bank that ensure genetic consistency across each farm and each annual harvest. After harvesting, inspection, sampling and release, hemp is transported to a separate production facility for further processing. Operations begin with the receipt of hemp (dried and milled at the farm) and all production materials (externally‐supplied bulk ingredients, extraction solvents, packaging/holding components, and process aids or consumables). Quality inspection takes place before being stored under controlled conditions. Thereafter, an extraction process with supercritical CO2 is applied to the dried and milled hemp. The extract is sampled and labelled for quality control checks and stored until testing confirms it can be released for use in the finished product.

The final formulation described by the applicant entails a liquid form of food supplement where the NF is dissolved in an oil matrix in a concentration of 3.3%. The viscous hemp extract is gently heated to promote dissolution of the NF in the oil matrix.

The Panel considers that the production process is sufficiently described and does not raise safety concerns.

Compositional data

The NF consists of 54.2%–58.0% total cannabinoids and 1.6%–2.7% terpenes. Between 20% and 30% w/w of the NF is uncharacterised.

Total terpenes were present up to 27,000 mg/kg. Upon EFSA's request, the applicant provided data of five additional batches of the NF on the content of phenols and flavonoids. All flavonoids were below their limit of detection (LOD, 0.0017 mg/kg), while for phenols (isoeugenol, trans‐anethole, vanillin and methyl eugenol) the following results were reported. Isoeugenol was detected in all batches at levels ranging from 18.3 to 340.6 mg/kg. Trans‐anethole was detected at levels ranging from 18.3 to 40.9 mg/kg across the batches, while vanillin content ranged from 99 to 339 mg/kg. Methyl eugenol was consistently below its LOD (0.05 mg/kg).

In terms of chemical contaminants, metals and other elements such as cadmium, lead, arsenic and mercury, were analysed via a modified version of the method 2011.19, 993.14 AOAC by an external laboratory and found in compliance with Regulation (EC) 2023/925 when applicable. Aflatoxins (sum of B1, B2, G1 and G2) and ochratoxin A were analysed by ultra high‐performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC–MS/MS) and were found to be below 2 μg/kg and 1 μg/kg, respectively. Deoxynivalenol and HT‐2 toxin were found below 100 μg/kg, T‐2 toxin below 10 μg/kg, fumonisin B1 and B2 below 25 μg/kg, and zearalenone below 30 μg/kg.

Benzo[a]pyrene and the sum of benzo[a]pyrene, benzo[a]anthracene, chrysene and benzo[b]fluoranthene (PAH4) have been analysed by an accredited external laboratory with an internally developed method. The Panel notes that their concentrations were below the maximum levels established for food supplements containing botanicals and their preparations in Regulation (EU) 2023/915.

Pesticides have been analysed using a modified version of AOAC 2007.1 method, and gas chromatography–mass spectroscopy (GC–MS) and/or liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) following acetonitrile extraction/partitioning and clean‐up by dispersive solid phase extraction–quick, easy, cheap, effective, rugged and safe (SPE–QuEChERS) method. All results were found in compliance with the maximum residue limits for pesticides as by Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Upon EFSA's request, the applicant provided experimental data on the sum of dioxins, sum of dioxins and dioxin‐like PCBs and sum of non dioxin‐like PCBs.

Microbiological analyses were performed using the following standard methods: total aerobic plate count was assessed according to AOAC 966.23, total yeasts and moulds according to FDA BAM Chapter 18, E. coli and total coliforms according to AOAC 991.14 and Salmonella spp. according to AOAC‐RI 121501. Total aerobic plate count, total coliforms and E. coli were each below 10 CFU/mL, while total yeasts and moulds were below 20 CFU/mL. Salmonella spp. were absent in 25 mL of the NF. Upon EFSA's request, the applicant also tested the NF for Bacillus cereus (via Bacara Agar), Staphylococcus, Clostridia (both via USP <62> 8 ) and Listeria (via AOAC 2016.07). B. cereus count was below 10 CFU/g, while other microorganisms were not detected in 10 g of the NF.

The Panel notes that incomplete information was provided on the internally modified methods used by the laboratory that conducted the analyses submitted in the application, with the exception of the method used to quantify cannabinoids, which was described in detail.

Overall, the Panel notes that when considering proximate analysis provided of the NF (i.e. moisture: 3.52%–9.29% w/w; ash: 0.1% w/w; proteins: 0.11%–0.18% w/w; fatty acids: 6.80%–7.80% w/w) along with the results presented for the other compounds of the NF (e.g. cannabinoids, terpenes), between 20% and 30% w/w of the NF remains uncharacterised.

Stability

The applicant performed stability tests with a hemp‐derived isopropanol (IPA) CBD extract, both as test material and dissolved in olive oil (i.e. 9% of the IPA extract dissolved in 91% olive oil). Moreover, the applicant submitted an expert opinion on the substantial equivalence of the testing material and the NF, in terms of composition, nutritional value, metabolism, intended use and level of undesirable substances. However, the Panel notes the test material differed from the NF under assessment, as it was produced using a different production process (i.e. IPA‐based extraction instead of CO2 supercritical extraction), potentially resulting in a different composition. Therefore, EFSA requested stability data for the NF, including data on cannabiquinones. In reply, the applicant provided a 36‐month stability study performed with the IPA extract. The presence of cannabiquinones was not examined.

The Panel considers that, overall, the data provided are not sufficient to conclude on the stability of the NF.

Specifications

The applicant proposed specifications for the NF. However, due to the final conclusion of this opinion, the specifications are not reported.

History of use of the NF and/or of its source

History of use of the source

C. sativa L. is a plant species historically cultivated for its fibre as well as food and medicinal uses. Industrial hemp varieties contain low THC levels (< 0.3%). 9 CBD, other cannabinoids and terpenes are produced in the trichomes, which are primarily located on the flowering tops and leaves of the C. sativa L. plant. Traces of CBD can also be found on the surface of the seeds. In hemp varieties grown mainly for fibre production, the concentration of CBD typically ranges between 0.5% and 2.0%.

History of use of the NF

There is no history of use of the NF.

Proposed uses and use levels and anticipated intake

The NF is intended to be used in food supplements.

Target population

The target population proposed by the applicant is the general population above 3 years of age, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

Proposed uses and use levels

The applicant proposed different maximum daily doses of the final formulation containing the NF, ranging from 34 mg/day to 109 mg/day for the various intended population groups. Estimate of exposure to cannabinoids

According to the compositional data provided (Section 3.4), the NF contains, along with CBD, other cannabinoids, for which the applicant did not perform any exposure assessment.

Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME)

No study with the NF was conducted by the applicant; instead a literature search mainly focusing on CBD and THC was provided.

The Panel considers that the uncertainty and data gaps described for CBD by the EFSA NDA Panel (2026) also apply to this NF.

Nutritional information

The nutritional information of the NF was not assessed because of the limitations on the composition of the NF.

Toxicological information

The Panel notes that no toxicological studies conducted with the NF under assessment were provided.

The applicant submitted a bacterial reverse mutation test, an in vivo micronucleus test and a 90‐day study (compliant to OECD Test Guidelines and to Good Laboratory Practices), performed with test items produced via a different production process (IPA extraction) and for which a substantial part was not characterised.

The bacterial reverse mutation test was conducted with hemp‐derived IPA extract whereas the in vivo micronucleus test and the 90‐day study were conducted using a preparation consisting of 9% hemp‐derived IPA extract dissolved in 91% olive oil.

As in the stability study, the applicant justified the use of a different test item by providing an expert opinion on the substantial equivalence of the testing material and the NF which has not been considered sufficient by the Panel.

The Panel considers that the test material used in these studies is not representative of the NF under assessment.

Genotoxicity

The applicant submitted genotoxicity studies conducted using two IPA extracts as described above.

In response to EFSA's request to perform a bacterial reverse mutation test and an in vitro micronucleus test with the NF, the applicant provided published scientific literature (Henderson, Welsh, Rogers, et al., 2023; Henderson, Welsh, Trexler, et al., 2023) to support the toxicological assessment. These studies utilised CBD with a purity of ≥ 99.0%, extracted using ethanol and therefore does not represent the composition of the NF under evaluation.

The Panel notes the absence of genotoxicity studies conducted with representative test material of the NF under assessment and considers that the available evidence is insufficient to assess the genotoxic risk of the NF.

Reproductive and developmental toxicity

The Panel notes that the NF is intended to be used in the general population above 3 years of age, excluding pregnant and lactating women. However, in its previous assessment, EFSA identified a critical data gap concerning the potential of CBD to interfere with the development of the central nervous system (CNS). This concern was based on available evidence indicating that CBD may affect neurodevelopmental processes and therefore could pose a risk to individuals during sensitive windows of development (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022, 2026). The applicant did not perform any study to address this concern in the proposed target population as requested by EFSA.

Human data

The Panel notes that no human intervention studies conducted with the NF under assessment were submitted by the applicant. In its previous scientific statement on CBD (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022, 2026), the Panel identified several critical data gaps relevant for its assessment, including uncertainties regarding liver toxicity and potential effects on the CNS. To address these concerns, the Panel requested the applicant to provide a human intervention study designed to evaluate the potential adverse effects of CBD under conditions representative of the proposed use of the NF.

The applicant did not submit any new human data in response to this request.

Allergenicity

The applicant quantified the protein content in five batches of the NF, which ranged from 0.11% to 0.18%. According to the proposed uses, the protein intake from the NF on a single occasion would range from 0.036 to 0.058 mg.

No studies on allergenicity of the NF were provided by the applicant, who performed a literature search to identify data on the potential allergenicity of CBD. However, the Panel notes that any allergenicity risk associated with the oral consumption of the NF is more likely to arise from the starting material (i.e. the plant proteinaceous components extracted) than from cannabinoids.

Multiple cannabis allergens have been sequenced, four of which are accepted by the WHO/IUIS Allergen Nomenclature Subcommittee 10 . The Panel notes that the production process of the NF uses flowers and leaves of feminised hemp plants. These parts of the plant contain Can s 3, a non‐specific lipid transfer protein, which appears to mediate cross‐reactivity with, and food allergy to, several fruits and vegetables, i.e. the so‐called cannabis‐fruit‐vegetable syndrome (de Larramendi et al., 2008; Drouet et al., 2017). Potentially allergenic proteins have been identified in crude Cannabis sativa extracts from different parts of the plant (i.e. leaf, root, female flower and bud), devoid of seed components, through IgE reactivity in individuals with a positive skin prick test (Nayak et al., 2013). However, it is unclear whether sensitisation and/or allergic reactions to non‐seed hemp allergens may occur via oral consumption.

Overall, the Panel considers that the allergenic potential of the NF is unknown.

DISCUSSION

The NF under assessment is a food supplement provided in form of oily liquid solution. 11 The proposed target population is the general population above 3 years of age, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women.

The NF is an extract obtained from the flowers and leaves from industrial hemp varieties of the plant C. sativa L. using CO2 supercritical extraction. It consists of 54.2%–58.0% w/w total cannabinoids and 1.6%–2.7% w/w terpenes. The Panel notes that among the characterised fractions, cannabinoids other than CBD are present. A substantial fraction (between 20% and 30% w/w) of the NF remains uncharacterised.

The stability and toxicological testing was preformed using test items extracted using IPA. The two different extraction processes (i.e. IPA and supercritical CO2) may result in extracts with different compositional profiles particularly with respect to minor constituents, residual solvents and the relative abundance of cannabinoids and other lipophilic compounds.

Therefore, the Panel does not consider the IPA extracts as representative of the NF since the degree of uncharacterised fraction of the NF prevents a comprehensive understanding of the constituents present in it and their potential toxicological relevance.

Additionally, no data were provided by the applicant to address the safety of the NF in the general population above 3 years of age.

The Panel, therefore, concludes that the submitted data cannot be used to support the stability and toxicological assessment of the NF.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel concludes that the safety of the NF cannot be established.

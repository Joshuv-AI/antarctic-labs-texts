Impact of diabetes mellitus on minimally invasive spine surgery outcome: A systematic review
Authors: Sadegh Bagherzadeh, Faramarz Roohollahi, Cesar Carballo Cuello, Gabriel Flores-Milan, Mark Greenberg, Jay I. Kumar, Alex Haas, Puya Alikhani, Mohsen Rostami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Minimally invasive spine (MIS) surgery has been shown to reduce tissue trauma and perioperative morbidity; however, its safety and effectiveness in diabetic patients remain uncertain. Here, we evaluated the impact of diabetes mellitus on postoperative complications and outcomes following minimally invasive lumbar spine procedures.Methods: This systematic review utilized Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and was registered in The International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO). A comprehensive literature search was performed across PubMed, Scopus, Embase, and Web of Science databases through January 2025. Studies were included if they evaluated adult diabetic patients undergoing MIS lumbar discectomy, interbody fusion, or decompression. Methodological quality was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa scale.Results: A total of 16 studies were included: eleven focused on interbody fusion or discectomy (n = 2041) and five on decompression-only procedures (n = 1256). The majority of studies reported similar rates of surgical complications between diabetic and nondiabetic patients. However, some identified increased risks for medical complications, prolonged hospitalization, or pseudarthrosis in people with diabetes. Although functional outcomes, including pain scores and disability indices, were generally comparable across groups on long-term follow-up, diabetic patients occasionally reported lower postoperative quality of life scores. Due to heterogeneity in study design and reporting, a meta-analysis was not performed.Conclusion: Despite some variability in perioperative risks, MIS appears to offer safe and effective outcomes for diabetic patients. The long-term benefits are largely similar to those observed in nondiabetic populations.

INTRODUCTION

The global diabetes prevalence in 2019 was estimated to be 9.3% (463 million people), rising to 10.9% (700 million) by 2045.[14] The association between diabetes mellitus (DM) and unfavorable outcomes after spine surgery is well-established in the literature, with increased risks including delayed wound healing, urinary tract infections, longer hospital stays, and increased overall hospital costs.[1]

Minimally invasive spine (MIS) surgery has an established lower overall complication rate versus open procedures. Here, we asked whether diabetic patients undergoing MIS spine operations would exhibit comparable and/or better functional outcomes (i.e., correlating with less tissue destruction, lower blood loss, and decreased surgery time).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Protocol, registration, and study selection criteria

A total of 16 studies were included in this systematic review.[2-13,15-18] Given the considerable differences in study methodologies, a formal meta-analysis was not performed. This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. The search included studies from inception to December 2024. Studies were included if they met the following criteria: published in English; designed as a case–control study, cohort study, or case series; evaluated adult patients (age >18) with diabetes undergoing MIS procedures, including lumbar interbody fusion, discectomy, or decompression; and reported relevant clinical outcomes or complication data. Two reviewers independently screened all studies for eligibility and used the Newcastle–Ottawa scale (NOS) for quality assessment; for disagreements, a third reviewer (MR) provided consensus.

DM impact on minimally invasive lumbar discectomy and interbody fusion

Several studies report no significant differences between diabetic and non-diabetic patients. Narain et al.[8] and Senker et al.[15] found similar operative time, blood loss, complication rates, and hospital stay following minimally invasive interbody fusion. Studies of anterior lumbar interbody fusion and oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) procedures reported no association between DM and prolonged hospitalization or postoperative ileus.[6,11] Alternatively, Narain et al.[9] identified higher rates of medical (odds ratio [OR] = 2.5) and surgical complications (OR = 2.7) after MIS-transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion in diabetic patients. Further, Lu et al.[7] reported DM as a risk factor for postoperative spinal subdural hematoma.

DM impact on minimally invasive lumbar canal decompression surgery

Several studies report no significant differences between diabetic and non-diabetic patients undergoing MIS lumbar canal decompression. Regev et al.[13] found similar complication rates, infection risks, frequency of revision surgery, and/or hospital length of stay. Quillo-Olvera et al.[12] observed no differences in postoperative blood glucose changes. Shetty et al.[17] reported no association between DM and incidental durotomy during decompression procedures. Alternatively, in diabetic patients, Guo et al. [2] reported increased hidden blood loss, whereas Sharma et al.[16] reported a higher risk of incidental durotomy for both open and minimally invasive procedures. Regev et al.[13] noted lower postoperative EQ-5D scores in diabetic patients.

CONCLUSION

MIS surgery appears to reduce complication rates and improve clinical outcomes in diabetic patients compared to traditional open procedures.

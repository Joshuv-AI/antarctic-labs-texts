Establishment and characteristic analysis of a novel patient derived cell line of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma
Authors: Shuangshuang Dou, Minghui Gao, Quanwei Li, Mengyin Chai, Buxin Kou, Xiaoni Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) is a primary malignant tumor of the liver, second only to hepatocellular carcinoma. The scarcity of appropriate cell lines has impeded ICC-related research. This study aims to establish a novel patient-derived ICC cell line and systematically evaluate its biological characteristics, thereby providing a valuable cellular tool for ICC research.MethodsA cell line named LPHC-6 of ICC was established using a patient-derived xenograft (PDX) tumor model. We comprehensively characterized this novel cell line through a series of techniques, including cell morphology observation, short tandem repeat (STR) analysis, karyotype analysis, cell proliferation, cell migration assays, western blot analysis, immunofluorescence staining, flow cytometry, gene mutation detection, spheroid formation assays, drug sensitivity testing, and xenograft tumor formation in nude mice.ResultsLPHC-6 cells exhibited typical characteristics of malignant epithelial cells, demonstrating robust proliferation and migration capabilities. These cells possessed a structurally complex triploid karyotype, which was confirmed to be free from cross-contamination with other human cell lines. Tumor biomarker analysis revealed positive expression of AFP and negative expression of GPC3. Additionally, LPHC-6 cells showed high expression levels of stem cell markers CD133 and CD44. The biliary epithelial cell marker CK19 was positive, whereas the hepatocyte marker Hep Par1 was negative. Genomic analysis identified an exon mutation in TP53, copy number loss in PTEN and RAD51, and copy number gain in NTRK1, PDCD1LG2, and VEGFA. Investigations confirmed that LPHC-6 cells exhibited excellent sphere formation ability and tumorigenic potential. Furthermore, LPHC-6 cells demonstrated sensitivity to Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, 5-fluorouracil, Oxaliplatin and Atezolizumab in both 2D and 3D culture system.ConclusionA novel intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line LPHC-6 has been successfully established, which provides a powerful cell tool for the research related to intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma.

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) is an adenocarcinoma originating from the epithelium of the secondary bile duct and its branches, accounting for approximately 10% −15% of primary malignant liver tumors [1]. ICC is the primary malignant tumor of the liver, whose incidence rate is second only to that of hepatocellular carcinoma, and its incidence has been increasing in recent years [2]. Most ICC patients are diagnosed in the advanced stage of disease, and only a few have the opportunity to undergo surgery treatment [1, 3]. New therapies for ICC have long been sought.

Human tumor cell lines have become a powerful tool for tumor research due to their ease of large-scale preparation and genetic engineering modification, low experimental cost, and high similarity to the original tumor [4]. Although there have been some research reports on ICC cell lines, there are no original records of their origin, characteristics and other information, there are also few collections of ICC cell lines in well-known public cell banks. In order to conduct in-depth research on the mechanism and treatment of ICC, it is necessary to establish a suitable ICC cell model system. Due to the limitation of tumor sampling sites and cell anoikis, the success rate of establishing cell lines directly from fresh patient tumor tissue is very low [5, 6]. Recent studies have found that the success rate of establishing new PDX-derived cell lines (PDXC) using human tumor xenografts (PDX) is much higher than that of the former [7]. PDX and PDXC have become important model tools for preclinical cancer research [8, 9].

In this study, we established a novel PDX-derived cell line of ICC using the ICC-PDX from a male ICC patient and named it LPHC-6. Short tandem repeat (STR) identification was conducted for the LPHC-6 cell line, and its biological characteristics and drug response were thoroughly evaluated. This work aims to provide a robust cellular tool for ICC-related scientific research.

Materials and methods

Animals and animal raising

Male NPG mice aged 3–4 weeks were purchased from Beijing Vitalstar Biotechnology Co.,Ltd. Male Balb/c nude mice aged 3–4 weeks were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All mice were raised within a SPF barrier environment at 22 to 26 °C, 40 to 70% humidity and 12 h/12 h light/dark alternating day and night.

Establishment of LPHC-6 cell line

The PDX biopsies of ICC were obtained from samples preserved in our previous research group. The clinical information of the patient corresponding to the PDX was shown in Table 1. PDX biopsies, which had been frozen in Liquid nitrogen were resuscitated in 37 °C water bath and quickly transplanted into the armpit of NPG mice. When the diameter of the tumor in the armpit of NPG mice reached 15 mm, the tumors were aseptically excised, cut into 1–2 mm tissue pieces, and processed as follows: the tissue pieces were incubated with Accutase (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C for 45 min, passed through a 70 μm cell strainer, and centrifuged at 1000 r/min to discard the supernatant, thereby obtaining a single-cell suspension. The cells were resuspended in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and cultured in an incubator (SANYO, Japan). To eliminate fibroblasts, repeated adherence methods were employed, and cells from different passages were cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen for future use.

STR detection

LPHC-6 cells in the growth phase were digested and DNA was extracted. The 20 STR loci and 1 gender loci were amplified by multiplex PCR composite amplification system(CELL STR ID®). PCR amplification products were analyzed with ABI 3130xl DNA Analyzer (Applied Biosystems) ®) and GeneMapper ID-X v1.5 (Applied Biosystems) ®) Software.

Observation of cellular morphological characteristics and growth patterns

The morphological characteristics and growth patterns of different generations of cells cultured in vitro were observed under inverted microscope (Nikon Ti, Japan).

Cell proliferation and migration experiments

The cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded in 96-well plates, with 3000 cells per well. MTT assay was used to measure the absorbance value of the cells at the wavelength of 570 nm with an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (Thermo, USA) for 7 consecutive days. The cell growth curve was drawn, and the cell doubling time was calculated by the formula Td = t × Lg2/lg (N1/N0).

Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were digested and seeded into 6-well plates, at a density of 5 × 105 cells per well. When the cell fusion rate reached 70% (cells tend to fill up overnight), two perpendicular Lines were drawn across the well using a 200 µL pipette tip, The cells were then washed with PBS and cultured in serum-free medium for 48 h. Cell migration was monitored at different time points using an inverted microscope. Cell migration rate (%) = (initial scratch area - scratch area at time T)/initial scratch area.

Western blot analysis

Total cellular protein was extracted routinely and protein concentration was measured by BCA method. The protein was separated on 10% SDS-PAGE gels, transferred to PVDF membranes (BIO-RAD, USA), incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against GAPDH, AFP, GPC3 (Abcam, USA). After incubated at room temperature for 1 h with the secondary antibody labeled with horseradish peroxidase (CST, USA), the PVDF membrane was photographed using a chemiluminescence imager (BIO-RAD, USA).

Immunofluorescence experiment

After cultured the cells overnight in a 24 well plate with pre-placed slides, the cells on the slides were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 1% Triton X-100 at room temperature. Following this, cells was incubated with AFP primary antibody (Abcam, USA) at 4 °C overnight, after washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with a fluorescently labeled secondary antibody (Abcam, USA). Fluorescence microscopy was employed to visualize and document the cells (Leica DM500, Germany).

Immunohistochemical experiments

The cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, centrifuged and precipitates were embedded in paraffin and sliced, stained with CK19 and Hep Par1 immunohistochemistry kits (ZSGB-BIO, China), observed and photographed under a microscope.

Karyotype analysis

After cells in logarithmic growth phase were treated with 0.3 µg/mL colchicine and continued cultivation for 3 h, the cells were digested and collected. The cells were then incubated in a hypotonic solution of 0.07 mm/moL potassium chloride for 30 min, fixed with a 1:3 acetic acid methanol solution for 2 h, then dropped sheet. The metaphase chromosomes were harvested and stained using the standard Giemsa banding method.

Gene mutation detection

Using 808 gene detection kit (AcornMed, China) for testing. Cell DNA was extracted and DNA fragments with length of 180–280 bp were randomly broken by ultrasound fragmentation device. After end repair and A-tail addition, the two ends of the fragments were ligated with connectors to prepare a DNA library. Sequencing was performed on a sequencer (Agilent 2100, USA) and the data were analyzed.

Flow cytometry

The cells were digested and centrifuged, counted and adjusted to 1 × 106, stained with CD133 and CD44 flow cytometry antibodies (BD, USA), and detected using Calibur flow cytometer (BD, USA).

Cell spheroidization experiment

Cells in the logarithmic growth stage were seeded in 96-well low-attachment plates (Thermo Fisher, USA) pre-treated with 20 µL matrigel (Corning, USA) at 3000 cells per well, incubated in an incubator at 37 °C, the cell spheroidization were observed under a microscope, and sphere sizes were measured using Image J software.

Drug sensitivity experiments under 2D and 3D cell culture conditions

Sorafenib (Sora), Lenvatinib (Lenv), Gemcitabine, Paclitaxel, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), Oxaliplatin (Oxa), and Atezolizumab (Atezo) were purchased from MCE company.

Cells in the logarithmic growth stage were digested and inoculated into a 96-well plate, with 3000 cells per well. On the second day, medium containing different concentrations of anti-tumor drug were added. The OD value of cells was measured by MTT or CCK8 assay, and the IC50 value at 24, 48, and 72 h were calculated.

The cells in the logarithmic growth stage were seeded in low adsorption 96 plates that had been pretreated with 20 µL Matrix gel, at 3000 cells per well. Media containing Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, Gemcitabine, Paclitaxel, 5-FU, Oxa, and Atezolizumab were added at 0 h after cell inoculation, respectively. After addition of the drug, the cells were cultured for 24–72 h to observe the cell sphericity under the microscope and cell viability using an ATP detection kit (Promega, USA). Calcein AM-PI cell viability kit (Baiao, China) was used to evaluate the death of the cells at 0 h and 48 h after cell inoculation, and proportions of cell fluorescence area were measured with Image J software.

Tumor formation experiment

In this study, fifteen Balb/c nude mice were randomly allocated into three groups with 5 mice per group and subcutaneously injected with 1 × 10⁵, 5 × 10⁵, and 1 × 10⁶ cells, respectively. When the Maximum diameter of the tumor was 15 mm, the ethical endpoint was reached, and samples were collected.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and graphing were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software.

Results

Morphology, proliferation and migration characteristics and karyotype identification of LPHC-6 cells

The flowchart of LPHC-6 cell construction was shown in Fig. 1. Under the microscope, LPHC-6 cells exhibit a polygonal morphology with abundant cytoplasm and large, circular nuclei. The cells grow in a single layer and are tightly intertwined, displaying typical characteristics of malignant epithelial cell (Fig. 2A). The cell growth curve showed that the cells are in the retardation phase from day 1 to 3, enter the logarithmic growth phase from day 3 to 6, and reach the plateau phase from day 6 to 7, with a doubling time of 36.7 h (Fig. 2B). The scratch experiment showed that migration rates of LPHC-6 cells at 12, 24, 36, 48 h were 8.19 ± 1.95%, 14.94 ± 3.34%, 22.38 ± 4.95%, 32.42 ± 1.15%, respectively (Fig. 2C). The cells are able to stably passaged for more than 20 generations, with passages occurring every 2–3 days. The results of chromosome karyotyping identification showed that LPHC-6 cells exhibited high heterogeneity, which were mutated abnormal karyotypes (Fig. 2D). These results suggested that LPHC-6 cells were malignant epithelial tumor cells.

STR typing of LPHC-6 cells

The STR typing results showed that there was a triallelic phenomenon on chromosome D18S51 in LPHC-6 cells, and there was no polyallelic phenomenon were observed at other loci. No human cell cross-contamination was found in LPHC-6 cells. A search of the Cellosaurus database using the STR typing data for LPHC-6 cells did not identify any cell lines with a Matching degree greater than 80% (8 STR loci matching) (Table 2). These results suggested that LPHC-6 cells were a novel tumor cell line.

Research on biomarkers of LPHC-6 cells

In order to study the biological characteristics of LPHC-6 cells, we first detected the liver cancer markers using western blot and immunofluorescence. The results showed that LPHC-6 cells did not express GPC3 but did express AFP (Fig. 3A and B). To identify the tissue origin of LPHC-6 cells, immunohistochemistry was used to detect hepatocyte markers (Hep Par1) and bile duct cell markers (CK19). The results showed that LPHC-6 cells expressed CK19 but did not express Hep Par1, which was consistent with PDX tumor tissue (Fig. 3C). Further observation of stem cell characteristic marker expression by flow cytometry showed that the percentage of LPHC-6 cells expressing CD44 was 97.9%, the percentage of CD133 cells was 99.8%, and the percentage of CD44 and CD133 double positive cells was 97.5% (Fig. 3D). These results suggested that LPHC-6 cells exhibited characteristics of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma with high stemness.

Analysis of gene mutations in LPHC-6 cells

We utilized the gene detection kit (AcornMed, China) to analyze 808 tumor related hotspot genes in LPHC-6 cells. A total of 29 systemic gene mutations and 16 systemic gene copy number variations were detected in LPHC-6 cells. Among the 8 genes associated with liver cancer, PTEN and RAD51 showed copy number deletions, while NTRK1, PDCD1LG2 and VEGFA showed copy number increase. NTRK2 and NTRK3 were wild-type, and TP53 had a mutation abundance of 100%/X in the exon9 region, indicating that LPHC-6 cells were sensitive to docetaxel, Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, Anlotinib, platinum drugs, bevacizumab, and radiotherapy. Three genes associate with immunotherapy, CD274, JAK2, and MYC, showed copy number increase, which prompted that LPHC-6 cells were sensitive to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (nivolumab, pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, tirelizumab, treprizumab, and srelizumab) (Table 3).

Drug sensitivity experiments on LPHC-6 cells

Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, Gemcitabine, Paclitaxel, 5-FU, Oxa and Atezolizumab are the most commonly used drugs for treating malignant tumors. We evaluated the sensitivity of LPHC-6 cells to these drugs using a 2D drug sensitivity assay. The results showed that the IC50 values of Sorafenib at 24, 48 and 72 h were 13.60, 7.64 and 7.36 µΜ/mL, respectively (Fig. 5A and C); The IC50 values of Lenvatinib at 24, 48 and 72 h were 12.86, 10.95 and 6.31 µΜ/mL, respectively (Fig. 5D and F); The IC50 values of 5-FU at 24, 48 and 72 h were 101.90, 49.25 and 39.00 µΜ/mL, respectively (Fig. 5G and I); The IC50 values of Oxa at 24, 48 and 72 h were 102.70, 43.82 and 24.59 µΜ/mL, respectively (Fig. 5J and L); The IC50 values of Atezolizumab at 24, 48 and 72 h were 69.60, 58.53 and 53.52 µΜ/mL, respectively (Fig. 5M and O). Notably, the IC50 values of Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, Oxa, 5-FU and Atezolizumab gradually decreased in a time-dependent manner as the culture time extended. (Fig. 5V), but LPHC-6 cells showed insensitivity to Paclitaxel and Gemcitabine (Fig. 5P and U). The results of the 3D drug sensitivity experiment showed that after adding medication at 0 h for 24–96 h, compared with the control group, Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, 5-FU, Oxa and Atezolizumab significantly reduced the sphericity of LPHC-6 cells at 48–96 h (Fig. 6). The results of Calcein AM-PI staining showed that, after 24–72 h of treatment, the Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, 5-FU, Oxa and Atezolizumab groups showed a significant decrease in live cells and an increase in dead cells compared to the control group, regardless of whether the medication was added at 0 h (Fig. 7) or 48 h (Fig. 8) after inoculation. The above results suggested that LPHC-6 cells were sensitive to Sorafenib, Lenvatinib, 5-FU, Oxa and Atezolizumab.

Tumor formation experiment of LPHC-6 cells

The tumor formation rate in mice inoculated with different cell densities was 100%, and the time of tumor formation decreased as the number of inoculated cells increased. All five mice inoculated with 1 × 105 cells developed palpable nodules on day14, and the end point of ethical sampling was on day 22 with tumor volumes ranging from 203.27 to 1289.13 mm³ (Fig. 9). For the five mice inoculated with 5 × 105 cells, three of them formed palpable nodules on day 8, and the remaining two mice also formed palpable nodules on day 9, and the end point of ethical sampling was on day 20 with tumor volumes ranging from 154.58 to 714.09 mm³ (Fig. 9). All 5 mice inoculated with 1 × 106 cells developed palpable nodules on day 4, and the end point of ethical sampling was on day 15 with tumor volumes ranging from 407.13 to 859.87 mm³ (Fig. 9). These results indicate that the tumorigenic time of LPHC-6 cells decreases with increasing cell inoculation numbers, suggesting that LPHC-6 cells exhibit robust tumorigenicity.

Discussion

There are currently 20 ICC cell lines available in public cell banks such as NICR (National Institutes for Cancer Research) [10]. For example, HuCC-1 cells are derived from the tumor tissue of a 54-year-old male patient in Thailand [11], HKGZ-CC cell Lines are obtained from the tumor tissue of a 76-year-old male patient in China [12], and SNU-1079 cell lines are originated from the tumor tissue of a Korean patient [13]. However, most of these ICC cell lines are cell clones of various research groups, and are not commercially available through cell banks to ensure quality and standardization [12]. Patient-derived tumor models (PDX, PDXC, and PDXO) are crucial tools for both basic and translational research [14, 15]. In this study, we established a novel ICC cell line, named LPHC-6, from PDX xenograft tumors of patients with ICC. We conducted a comprehensive study on the phenotype, mutation status and drug response of this cell line.

After biological characterization, LPHC-6 cells have been identified as a novel type of ICC cells with a unique CD133 and CD44 stem-like phenotype, exhibiting strong proliferation and migration capabilities, as well as good sphericity and tumorigenicity in vivo. The latest version of the CSCO guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of advanced liver cancer, Sorafenib or Lenvatinib and Oxa-based systemic chemotherapy are recommended as first-line treatment for patients with good liver function (Child Pugh score ≤ 7 points). In terms of second-line treatment, regorafenib and PD-1 monoclonal antibodies (including Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab) are recommended as Grade I. In addition to the CSCO (Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology) guidelines, multiple authoritative guidelines, such as the NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network), ESAL (European Association for the Study of the Liver), and ESMO (European Society for Medical Oncology) guidelines recommend immune checkpoint inhibitors represented by anti-PD-1/anti-PD-L1 as a new frontline treatment for advanced liver cancer [16]. Liver cancer drugs mostly multiple targets, the role of genetic testing in the selection of anti-liver cancer drugs is limited, but still has certain guiding significance [17]. The loss and increase in gene copy numbers may be one of the primary mechanisms leading to abnormal gene expression [18]. The abnormal expression of PTEN, RAD51, NTRK1, PDCD1LG2, and VEGFA in liver cancer is closely related to the malignancy of liver cancer, and these genes are also the important targets of the liver cancer targeted drugs [19–23]. Gene mutation analysis shows that LPHC-6 cells exhibit copy number losses in PTEN and RAD51, while the copy numbers of NTRK1, PDCD1LG2, and VEGFA copy numbers are increase, indicating that LPHC-6 cells are highly malignant and sensitive to targeted drugs, platinum drugs, and radiotherapy. The TP53 gene is the most important tumor suppressor gene and one of the most common mutated genes in liver cancer. Detection of TP53 gene can guide targeted therapy for liver cancer [24]. TP53 mutations can lose their tumor suppressive activity, leading to more aggressive malignant tumors and poorer prognosis [25, 26]. The 100%/X mutation in exon9 of TP53 in LPHC-6 cells further confirmed that LPHC-6 cells were highly malignant and sensitive to TKI drugs. CD274, JAK2, and MYC are important targets for immune checkpoint drugs [27–29]. The increased copy number of CD274, JAK2, and MYC in LPHC-6 cells suggests their sensitivity to immune checkpoint drugs. Subsequent drug sensitivity screening experiments also confirmed that LPHC-6 cells are sensitive to platinum, KTI and immune checkpoint drugs. Tumorigenicity experiment further confirmed that few cells of 1 × 105 can fully form tumors in nude mice, which suggested LPHC-6 cells have high stemness characteristic.

In summary, we have successfully established a novel ICC cell line, LPHC-6, which provides a powerful tool for studying the drug screening, evaluation and mechanisms related to ICC.

We have established and characterized LPHC-6, a novel ICC cell line derived from the PDX of a Chinese patient, which provides a powerful cell tool for the research related to intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma.

Complete Remission of Metastatic Osteosarcoma From a Breast Malignant Phyllodes Tumor: A Case Report
Authors: Haa-Na Song, Min Hye Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Phyllodes tumors (PTs) are biphasic fibroepithelial lesions. Approximately 20% of malignant PTs metastasize hematogenously, most commonly to the lungs and bones. Treatment of metastatic PT is challenging because of its rarity. A 39-year-old woman with a left humeral fracture was admitted to our hospital. She had been diagnosed with breast PT a year prior, and humeral bone tissue pathology showed a metastatic PT similar to her breast PT. The patient received systemic high-dose chemotherapy, including etoposide, ifosfamide, and cisplatin, concurrently combined with radiotherapy to facilitate remission, after which the remnant tumor was removed. After achieving complete remission, the patient received chemotherapy with doxorubicin and cisplatin as adjuvants. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of a metastatic PT in which complete remission was achieved with high-dose chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy, followed by surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy.

INTRODUCTION

Phyllodes tumors (PTs) are rare biphasic fibroepithelial tumors that account for less than 1% of all breast neoplasms [1]. They occur predominantly in middle-aged women and are generally characterized by a rapidly growing, large, palpable, painless breast mass [2]. Most PTs are benign, with malignancy occurring in up to 25% of cases. Moreover, approximately 10%–20% of malignant PTs are associated with distant metastasis, which occurs via the hematogenous route, with the lung being the most common metastatic site, followed by soft tissue and bone. Treatment of malignant PT associated with distant metastasis is limited, and its prognosis remains poor [3].

PTs usually comprise epithelial and stromal tissues. The stromal component carries a high risk of malignant transformation and sarcomatous features, such as chondrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, and liposarcoma [4]. Only a few studies have reported that PT metastasizes to bones, including the iliac bone and femur [5]. However, to our knowledge, there have been no case reports of breast PT metastasis to the upper humeral bone. Little is known about their clinical features and effective treatment modalities owing to the scarcity of cases; therefore, metastatic PTs are challenging to manage despite ongoing efforts to improve their poor prognosis. Here, we report the case of a 39-year-old woman with relapsed malignant PT who presented with humeral bone metastasis and was successfully treated with systemic chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy.

CASE REPORT

A 39-year-old woman presented with a left breast mass measuring 22 × 13 cm, characterized by a hard lump in the lower outer quadrant with no lymph node enlargement. The patient underwent left-sided total mastectomy with axillary lymph node dissection and was diagnosed with malignant PT. She regularly visited the clinic without any evidence of relapse. One year later, she was rehospitalized for a left humeral fracture and underwent surgery. The specimens were sent to the Department of Histopathology, where the patient was diagnosed as having a metastatic PT with osteosarcomatous differentiation (Figure 1). The patient was then transferred to the Medical Oncology Department for treatment. Computed tomography (CT) revealed no evidence of local relapse of the PT; however, a residual mass was observed surrounding the left humerus. The soft tissue around the left proximal humerus (maximum standardized uptake value: 11) showed a heterogeneously increased uptake of 2-deoxy-2-fluoro-D-glucose on positron emission tomography-CT and achieved after six cycles of chemotherapy(Figure 2). The patient subsequently underwent concurrent chemoradiotherapy. Chemotherapy consisted of etoposide, ifosfamide, and cisplatin for six cycles, and radiotherapy was administered to the left humerus, including the surgical bed, at a dose of 60 Gy in 30 fractions (Figure 3).

Overall, chemotherapy was generally well tolerated, with grade 2 anemia and thrombocytopenia being the observed toxicities. The patient was referred to the Department of Orthopedic Surgery for the removal of the remaining tumor. Subsequently, she underwent a wide excision, and pathological examination revealed that some atypical cells remained in the bone, demonstrating a metastatic PT with osteosarcomatous differentiation (Figure 4). Most tumor cells were necrotic, and the resection margins were free of tumor cells. Postoperatively, the patient was treated with a doxorubicin and cisplatin regimen as adjuvant chemotherapy for soft tissue sarcoma. The patient survived without any evidence of tumor relapse for 72 months postoperatively. The patient provided a written informed consent to participate in the study.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we report the case of a patient with metastatic osteosarcoma from a breast PT successfully treated with chemotherapy. Effective treatment regimens are controversial because of the rarity of the disease; therefore, the prognosis is poor.

Some studies have reported that poor prognostic factors associated with metastasis, including positive surgical margins and large tumor size, are possibly significant factors for local recurrence [67]. Other studies have suggested that cellular atypia and heterologous differentiation are indicators of poor prognosis [8]. Furthermore, the presence of mixed mesenchymal components such as osteosarcomas or chondrosarcomas has been linked to worse outcomes [9]. In the present case, the patient was diagnosed with a 22 × 13 cm breast PT exhibiting stromal overgrowth, marked cellularity, and moderate atypia. Based on these features and the previous findings, the risk of distant metastasis was high in this case.

The only curative therapy for PT is complete surgical excision, which includes a margin of the surrounding normal tissues; a surgical margin of at least 1 cm wide is recommended [10]. Adjuvant radiotherapy has been shown to improve local recurrence-free survival in borderline/malignant PT [11]; nonetheless, adjuvant chemotherapy is not routinely recommended because of the limited evidence.

However, no standard therapy exists for metastatic PTs. To our knowledge, the typical approach involves the surgical removal of the primary and metastatic tumors, followed by systemic chemotherapy [12]. Chen et al. [13] suggested that palliative systemic chemotherapy containing ifosfamide and anthracycline for sarcoma treatment was relatively effective, with a median progression-free survival of 2.77 months. Other chemotherapeutic regimens have shown poor treatment responses, with disease progression occurring soon after treatment. In a retrospective study of 37 patients with metastatic PTs, Mituś et al. [14] reported an improved median survival of 9 months using a combination of doxorubicin plus cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, or ifosfamide. The longest mean survival time among the patients with bone metastases was 11.8 months. In the present case, following the diagnosis of left humeral metastatic osteosarcoma from breast PT, the patient underwent concurrent chemoradiotherapy with etoposide, ifosfamide, and cisplatin. After achieving a partial response, she underwent resection of the remnant tumor and received adjuvant chemotherapy with doxorubicin. The patient has remained disease-free for 72 months, which is a considerably longer survival than that in previous reports.

In this study, we report a case of metastatic osteosarcoma arising from a breast PT that was successfully treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. This case has significant clinical implications, demonstrating that curative treatment may be possible in patients with metastatic osteosarcoma arising from malignant breast PT using a multimodal approach comprising chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery. Such individualized treatment approaches may be particularly beneficial for younger patients.

In conclusion, we believe that high-dose chemotherapy may be a suitable curative treatment for patients with metastatic malignant PT presenting with osteosarcoma without compromising their quality of life. Further studies are warranted to establish comprehensive treatment guidelines for patients with metastatic malignant PT.

Mechanical properties optimization and cost analysis of agricultural waste as an alternative in brick production
Authors: G. Nakkeeran, L. Krishnaraj, Pshtiwan Shakor, George Uwadiegwu Alaneme, Obeten Nicholas Otu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In recent years, building materials made from agricultural waste have become popular due to their lower cost and environmental impact. The Bio-Brick is mixed with Cement-Fly Ash and Hydrated Lime and a fine aggregate of groundnut shell in percentages (20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, and 60%). The optimum mix proportions of Bio-Brick and hydrated lime mortar were found from the compressive strength and were further continued to study the dry density, water absorption, and efflorescence. Machine Learning techniques are used to optimize and predict the properties of Bio-Bricks and mortars. Response Surface Methodology (RSM) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) are employed to forecast properties such as compressive strength, dry density, and water absorption with exceptional accuracy. The results from RSM models exhibit high degrees of accuracy, with R-squared values exceeding 0.88 for compressive strength, dry density, and water absorption. ANN models further enhance this predictive power, with R-squared values exceeding 0.99 in predicting these critical properties.

Introduction

The construction industry heavily relies on limited materials and has negative effects on the environment by contributing to the greenhouse effect and the release of harmful gases, particularly CO21–3. In Asia, the combustion of crop residues in the field accounts for 34% of the total biomass burned in a typical year. When it comes to agriculture-based economies such as China and India, burning residues in open fields leads to a considerable amount of greenhouse gas emissions. Significant work and dedication have been devoted to minimizing the environmental consequences of this sector, aligning with the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals, and adhering to the Paris Agreement’s objective of restricting the rise in global average temperature to under 2 °C from pre-industrial levels4,5.

Several ongoing studies are exploring the potential of using biomass from specific crops or agricultural residues as alternatives to aggregates. There is a significant focus on enhancing the sustainability of concrete6,7. These residues have the potential to create new building materials that are both cost-effective and environmentally sustainable8. Agribusiness often produces significant amounts of waste, including corn straw, rice husks, rice straw, groundnut shell, coconut fibers. By incorporating these materials into concrete, you can achieve a dual environmental benefit9–11.

Using agricultural waste in construction materials reduces environmental pollution associated with conventional materials such as cement. For instance, peanut shell ash, rice straw ash, and coconut shell ash have been partially used to replace sand in cement block production while meeting IS and ASTM standards for strength and durability. Agricultural waste-based materials, such as straw bales, have established environmental friendliness and thermal performance in building construction, contributing to sustainability goals, and reducing pollution12,13. Agricultural wastes can be incorporated into sheets, bricks, proof cement, wall panels, cement panels, particle boards, and insulation panels. Amorphous silica, abundant in agricultural waste ash, makes it an available pozzolanic material for concrete production14–17.

Limited studies have been carried on the use of agricultural wastes as fine aggregates in concrete. This allows for a thorough examination of the engineering characteristics of every agricultural-waste-based concrete mix and a comparison with conventional concrete18,19. Agricultural waste ash has been employed as a fine aggregate in concrete in several studies attempts. As a result, other raw agricultural wastes might be utilized in concrete to partially replace the fine aggregate and achieve the desired effects on the characteristics of concrete. Additional studies are needed to establish the strength variations and thermal properties when using these agricultural wastes as a fine aggregate replacement in concrete20,21.

The study focused on the optimum mixture proportions and on the examination of Bio-Brick and mortar samples to assess the effects of field application. In This study investigation focused on examining the fundamental physical and chemical properties by determining the optimal grinding time for the GNS sample. The GNS sample was prepared to investigate the compressive strength of Bio-Brick specimens under various optimized mix combinations. In addition, the optimal blend proportions were formulated for the purpose of conducting performance evaluations, including tests on water absorption, dry density, and efflorescence.

Machine learning techniques

Machine learning techniques are methods and algorithms that enable computers to learn from and make predictions or decisions based on data. These techniques are a subset of artificial intelligence (AI) and have found numerous applications in various fields.

Response surface methodology (RSM)

The utilization of RSM is an optimization statistical and mathematical technique employed in research and experimentation for the purpose of optimizing and analyzing intricate processes23–26. Response Surface Methodology (RSM) entails the development of mathematical models, typically employing polynomial equations, to elucidate the correlation between input variables and the response (output) of a given system or process. Through the analysis of these models, researchers are able to determine the most optimal combination of input variables that results in the desired response. Response Surface Methodology (RSM) is widely utilized across diverse disciplines such as engineering, chemistry, and manufacturing, with the primary objective of optimizing processes, enhancing product quality, and minimizing costs27–31. The methodology encompasses the formulation of experimental designs for the purpose of data collection, followed by the application of statistical techniques to establish the fitting of mathematical models to the acquired data. These models facilitate the ability of researchers to make predictions, conduct sensitivity analyses, and ultimately make well-informed decisions in order to attain optimal outcomes. The practicality and versatility of RSM have resulted in its extensive adoption in various industrial and research.

Artificial neural network (ANN)

The methodology encompasses the utilization of computational models known as Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), which draw inspiration from the structure and functionality of neural networks in the human brain. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are comprised of interconnected nodes, commonly referred to as “neurons,” which are arranged in a hierarchical structure consisting of an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. The strength of the signal passing through each connection between neurons is determined by its associated weight27,32,33.

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) demonstrate exceptional proficiency in the domains of pattern recognition, data analysis, and in the resolution of intricate problem-solving tasks. During the training process, Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) acquire knowledge by iteratively modifying the weights of their connections in response to input data and the desired output34,35. The process encompasses both forward and backward propagation. During forward propagation, the neural network calculates an output by processing the given input. This output is then compared to the desired output, and the resulting error is computed. Subsequently, optimization algorithms such as gradient descent are employed to adjust the weights of the network36–38.

Experimental and machine learning results and discussion

Bio-Brick was formulated using a brick mix consisting of a 1:3 proportion of Cement to Fine aggregate. The mix designs (Cement: Fly Ash: Hydrated Lime) for FH 1 to FH 5 are respectively: 10:20:70, 10:30:60, 10:40:50, 10:50:40, and 10:60:30. The casting process involved molding the Bio-Brick and subjecting it to compression using a testing machine. The cured Bio-Brick underwent 28-day water curing. Various properties were assessed, including compressive strength, dry density, water absorption, and efflorescence. These investigations contribute to understanding the material’s structural and functional characteristics, providing insights for its potential applications in construction.

Compressive strength of Bio-Brick

The investigation involved the optimization of cementitious material combinations for the development of Bio-Bricks. The study encompassed key design parameters such as OPC, Fly Ash (FA), Hydrated Lime (HL), cement content, Fine Aggregate (FA), and Grinded Raw Groundnut Shell (GNS). The research focused on elucidating the impact of these parameters on the physio-mechanical properties of the resulting Bio-Bricks shown in Fig. 7.

Similar to conventional concretes, the interaction of OPC, FA, and HL led to the formation of C-S-H gel, enhancing the compressive strength. This phenomenon was observed in the present study, with significant compressive strength gains being achieved after a curing period of 28 days. Subsequent testing of the cured Bio-Bricks revealed promising outcomes. In accordance with IS 12894:2002 mix design, a compressive strength of 7.5 N/mm2 qualifies as a first-class brick. Adhering to this standard, Bio-Bricks, meeting two mixed ratios, GNS 40% and GNS 30%, were assessed. The GNS 30% mix ratio, characterized by a maximal GNS replacement, demonstrates the requisite properties, achieving a compressive strength indicative of a first-class brick, as per the specified engineering standards. Figure 8 delineates, within the blue boundary, the characteristics of a first-class brick. Notably, the Bio-Brick containing 30% GNS exhibited a commendable compressive strength of 8 N/mm2, aligning with the standards established by IS 12894:200241.

These findings underscore the successful integration of optimized material proportions and curing techniques to enhance the physio-mechanical properties of Bio-Bricks. The utilization of GNS as an additive showcases its potential as a sustainable and effective component in Bio-Brick formulations, presenting opportunities for eco-friendly construction practices.

Efflorescence of Bio-Brick

The assessment of efflorescence in the Bio-Brick adhered to the Indian standard, specifically IS 3495 (Part 3): 1992. This evaluation involved the determination of efflorescence severity, following the classification framework prescribed by IS 1077:199243. The gradations include Nil, Slight, Moderate, Heavy, or Serious, each describing varying degrees of salt deposition on the surface of the brick.

In accordance with the IS 1077:1992 classification, a result of “Slight” efflorescence corresponds to a condition where a thin salt layer covers a maximum of 10% of the exposed brick surface. Conversely, a result of “Moderate” efflorescence indicates a heavier salt deposit, covering up to 50% of the exposed brick surface, yet without accompanying powdering or flaking.

The purpose of this evaluation was to make it easier to compare the costs of the developed bricks with other brick or block options available on the market. The primary objective is to determine the feasibility of real-time production on a commercial scale. Conclusion

The study was conducted to examine the commercial viability and cost-effectiveness of the developed Bio-Brick for enhancing the energy envelope of a building. Based on the results, it was determined that the developed Bio-Brick has brick strength of the First-class brick strength as per IS code. The cost and energy analysis has demonstrated that these Bio-Bricks are less expensive and more energy efficient than conventional wall materials. A machine-learning RSM and ANN were used for the optimization and forecasting of Bio-Brick properties.

According to Indian Standard Code, groundnut shell was ground and prepared as a fine aggregate alternative to Bio-Brick. 95% of Groundnut shells are ground and utilised as substitute materials. The same ratio is used in this Bio-Brick and the cementitious materials comprised of Cement-Fly Ash and Hydrated Lime (10%, 40%, and 50%): Fine aggregate materials are GNS and Sand (27% and 73%, respectively); the compressive strength of Bio-Brick has been increased to 9.78 ( N/mm2 ).Dry Density, Water Absorption, and Efflorescence of Bio-Brick were within the Indian Standard Code. According to Indian Standard code, Bio-Bricks meet the maximum requirements for First-class bricks.Machine Learning tool for optimization of Bio-Brick using Response Surface Methodology model has shown an acceptable degree of accuracy (R2 ≥ 0.8830), (R2 ≥ 0.9977), (R2 ≥ 0.9866) in predicting compressive strength, Dry density and water absorption. Prediction of ANN prediction model has shown as (R2 ≥ 0.99792), (R2 ≥ 0.9999), (R2 ≥ 0.9991) in predicting compressive strength, Dry density and water absorption.

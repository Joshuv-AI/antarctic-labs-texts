BCS II/IV antibiotics blended with SPEEK nanofibrous mat as an alternative for recurrent wound care: An in vitro and in vivo assessment
Authors: Himabindu Padinjarathil, Carmelo Drago, Sandro Dattilo, Libera Vitiello, Kaarthick Raaja Venkatachalam, Thirugnasambandam G Manivasagam, Prasanna Ramani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe repeated dressing changes, especially for infected wounds and those with high exudate, and applying drugs daily, are huge demands in wound management. Sustained and regulated medication release of BCS II/IV antibiotics improves chronic wound therapy. The poor solubility and absorption of BCS-class medicines makes it challenging at target site. This work seeks to build 3D nano scaffolds for administering ciprofloxacin, a BCS II/IV class medication, using biocompatible sulphonated polyether ether ketone mat produced by electrospinning, which is flexible. We demonstrated a reliable method to prevent burst drug release and maintain it for three weeks, with good biocompatibility, minimal cytotoxicity, and excellent tensile strength characterizing sulphonated polyether ether ketone. Nanofibers improve medication interaction and delivery due to their high specific surface area ratio. In vitro and in vivo experiments were used to define and study ciprofloxacin release from 30 % w/v sulphonated polyether ether ketone nanofibers. The SPEEK polymer/mat was analysed using 1H- NMR, FESEM, FTIR, TGA, and EDAX for structural and morphological characteristics. The UV spectroscopy showed that the nanofibrous SPEEK released 90 % ciprofloxacin over 21 days by non-Fickian diffusion (supported by mathematical modelling). Both direct and indirect MTT experiments at 1st, 3rd, and 7th days reveal compatibility with RAW 264.7 cell lines. Live-dead staining and cell adhesion investigations, support adherent cells and growth; scratch assay display migration in electrospun scaffold exhibits wound healing behaviour. In vivo investigations on Balb/C mice demonstrated that SPEEK-CF impregnated healed wounds from 3rd day and preventing infection till 14th day and confirmed by histopathology. This nanofibrous mat is suitable for sustained drug delivery.Keywords: Nanofibrous mat, Controlled drug release, BCS II/IV class drugs, Ciprofloxacin, Drug release kinetics, In vitro and in vivo models

Introduction

The biomedical world urges to fulfil the demand for efficient wound management to meet the market needs. The biodegradable nano fibres are already in the market but are limited by burst drug release by self-degradation. Parallelly the current world is in the race to design multifunctional wound dressing scaffolds. However, fungal and bacterial infections are common during or after wounds. The pharmaceutical industry faces a significant challenge in managing water-soluble drugs (PWSD), which are II and IV by the biopharmaceutics classification system (BCS) (Uhljar et al., 2021). These drugs have poor absorption due to their inability to dissolve or pass through cells easily. Despite their benefits, one-fourth of the drugs on the market have drawbacks such as lower bioavailability and high clearance rates (Roopesh et al., 2016).

Ciprofloxacin, a revolutionary antibiotic used to treat infectious diseases, has inefficient oral bioavailability and inconsistent plasma absorption, leading to treatment failure (Mohsen et al., 2020). Direct administration of these medications in target areas is more beneficial than failure and adverse effects. Topical administration of BCS class II/IV medications for wound healing may provide more effective anti-infectious treatment compared to oral administration (Williams et al., 2013). Ciprofloxacin hydrochloride, a BCS Class-IV drug, faces significant challenges related to bioequivalence/bioavailability, solubility, permeability, therapeutic use, pharmacokinetics, and interactions with other drugs (Lindenberg et al., 2004a). To overcome these issues, a bio-cargo-based approach is needed to address the immediate release of solid oral dosage forms, which can be scaled up in the pharmaceutical industry (Lindenberg et al., 2004b). Some research on controlled ciprofloxacin release from polymer-based systems were discussed below. The carbopol polymers in oral suspensions have release kinetics that depend on pH (Subhashree et al., 2012). Natural rubber latex membranes can release ciprofloxacin for a week with an initial burst release (Dias Murbach et al., 2014). However, natural polymers like alginate and chitosan display a burst release profile, along with biodegradation (Lopes et al., 2017; Hashemikia et al., 2021). Core-shell nanofibers with poly methyl methacrylate shell and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) core demonstrated sustained release over two weeks, avoiding burst release and adhering to a desorption-limited by two-stage model (Zupančič et al., 2016a). Porous PVA scaffolds had released half of the ciprofloxacin drug-loaded in 120 h, after the introduction of polyethylene glycol (PEG) (Zhou et al., 2016). Polyvinyl pyrrolidone-based foils and nanofiber mats showed the highest amount of burst drug release after 6 h and maintained it over 24 h (Rancan et al., 2019). An electrospun degradable polycarbonate urethane (PCNU) nanofiber scaffold released ciprofloxacin for seven days, following first-order kinetics (Wright et al., 2017). Studying these diverse approaches provides a research gap for development of a novel system for sustained and uniform ciprofloxacin delivery.

Certain synthetic polymers cause allergic reactions or other adverse effects; they are expensive and possess limited biodegradability (Srivastava et al., 2015). For these reasons, bio-inert and biocompatible polymer is needed for holding and releasing BCS II and IV-class drugs. Polyether ether ketone (PEEK) exhibits excellent biocompatibility, accompanied by other properties such as strong resistance to chemicals and radiation, low water absorption, and high resistance to impacts and abrasion (Baladhandapania et al., 2018; Padinjarathil et al., 2023a; Smith et al., 2019). Recent studies have explored PEEK and its derivatives for controlled drug release applications. In the past, the most stable drug release profile was from the membrane form of sulfonated polyether ether ketone, which was highly biocompatible (Padinjarathil et al., 2023a). The PEEK surfaces with 3D porous chitosan coatings exhibited smart release of doxorubicin for 10 days by inducing pH-based release, and demonstrate potential for treating bone tumours (Ouyang et al., 2018). Ekambam and co-workers have reported a new combinatorial effect of electrospinning SPEEK and Aloe vera on enhancing faster regeneration in an excisional wound (Ekambaram et al., 2021a). The drug release from SPEEK beads containing amoxicillin showed an initial rapid release of 10–40 % due to quick dissolution from the bead surface. The remaining drug was released when the polymer bead was degraded (Shanmuga Sundar and Sangeetha, 2012), indicating that the polymer influences the release rate and mechanism. In this work, electrospinning technique was employed. This is a promising technology that has gained attention in the pharmaceutical industry because of its potential applications in drug release systems (Sreerekha et al., 2013). The electrospinning process produces drug-loaded fibers with a high surface area and controlled release properties, allowing for precise drug administration and improved therapeutic outcomes (Lopes et al., 2017). Additionally, enhanced drug stability, extended release kinetics, and better bioavailability are advantages of electrospun drug delivery methods. An electrospin fiber by a high voltage drawn into a thin fiber by an electrostatic force that produces a suspended solution or melt of polymer and medicament (Qiu et al., 2017).

The present study aims to enhance and broaden the current approaches towards BCS II/IV drugs by blending the antibiotic ciprofloxacin with SPEEK by fabricating a nanofibrous mat in a one-step electrospinning technique. The SPEEK polymer is anticipated to hold and further control the drug release, which is achieved by a uniform distribution of drugs within the electrospun nanofibrous scaffold. The ultra-violet (UV) spectroscopy and mathematical modelling was analysed to understand the release of the drug and its pattern, respectively. The biocompatible nature of the polymer mat is assessed by in vitro and in vivo studies to understand the release of antibiotics of BCS II/IV drugs for better wound management.

Materials and methods

Materials

PEEK pellets (MW = 20,800 g/mol) were procured from Gharda Chemicals Pvt. Ltd. located in Mumbai, India. Sulfuric acid (98 %) and ciprofloxacin (CF) (CAS No: 85721-33-1) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich (Mumbai, India). Avra Synthesis Pvt. Ltd. (Hyderabad, India) provided the solvents N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF). Bactigras - Chlorhexidine Gauze Dressing 10 cm × 10 cm was purchased from Smith and Nephew healthcare Pvt. Ltd (Mumbai, India). The experiments were conducted using the following instruments: Zeiss Gemini SEM 300, Shimadzu UV-1900 (Japan; 200–600 nm), Bruker Avance 400 MHz NMR spectrophotometer, Bruker (VERTEX70 spectrometer, Mumbai, India), TA Instruments Q100 differential scanning calorimeter (SDT Q600/Q20, USA). Invitrogen Life Technologies (Carlsbad, CA) provided the human primary keratinocyte (HaCaT) cell line, the macrophage cell line (RAW 264.7), Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM), and Fetal bovine serum reagent (FBS).

PEEK sulphonation

The moisture content was eliminated by keeping PEEK (1 g) under vacuum for 24 h. Concentrated H2SO4 (98 %, 20 mL) was taken in a 100 mL single-necked round-bottom flask along with PEEK (1 g) and was continuously stirred at room temperature for 72 h under a nitrogen atmosphere (Padinjarathil et al., 2023a; Zhang et al., 2008). The resulting brown solution was poured into ice-cold deionized water slowly. The solid polymer was filtered and rinsed with deionized water until the pH of the water washings was nearly neutral. The resulting sulphonated PEEK polymer (SPEEK) was dried at 50 °C in a hot air oven (12 h).

Synthesis of nanofibers

The SPEEK was dissolved in DMF with continuous stirring at room temperature for 6 h to prepare of 30 % w/v polymer solution. Consequently, the solution (1 mL) was loaded in a syringe with a blunt-tipped needle, which was placed 15 cm away from the collector (Sadrjahani et al., 2017; Oroujzadeh et al., 2015). The needle with solution and collector is connected to positive and negative electrodes of a high-voltage power supply, respectively (HOLMARC/Model no HO-NFES-040B) to achieve high-speed aligned nanofibers of SPEEK (ESPEEK). Similarly, the ciprofloxacin (5 %) and SPEEK solution (1 mL) were dissolved in DMF to produce drug-loaded ESPEEK nanofibers (ESPEEK-CF). The nanofibers were kept under vacuum (60 °C) for 16 h, and it was used for further studies. In this case, to achieve homogeneity of the drug on each fiber, the SPEEK fibers were dissolved in 1 mL of DMF, and to this homogeneous solution was mixed with ciprofloxacin (5 %) in DMF. The mixture was stirred for a period of 6 h and subsequently sonicated for 30 min.

The FTIR analysis of ESPEEK, ESPEEK-CF, and CF was recorded to identify the presence of functional groups. It also confirms the occurrence of the sulphonation process. To assess the thermal stability of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF, thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed in an inert atmosphere (nitrogen). Water uptake studies

For the water absorption trials, a 1 × 1 cm2 ESPEEK mat was immersed in simulated body fluid (SBF; 10 mL) for 35 days by following our previously reported literature (Padinjarathil et al., 2023b), until a steady hydrated weight was noticed. Likewise, the same procedure was followed at different pH levels by adjusting the pH of SBF. The water uptake capacity of the mat was computed using these parameters.

In vitro drug release

A sample of ESPEEK-CF nanofibrous mat 1 × 1 cm2 was placed in 25 mL of simulated buffer solution (SBF) at 37 °C. It is a 21-day iterative process, where the drug released was traced through the concentration of ciprofloxacin in the SBF. The concentration of ciprofloxacin was measured by a Shimadzu UV-3600 spectrometer (at an optical wavelength of 313 nm). After the drug release experiment, nanofibrous mats were placed in a fume hood to dry. The amount of medication released into the solvent was determined by comparing the absorbance at various time points with the absorbance of reference drug solutions.

Mathematical modelling

Drug release is crucial in a therapeutic system as it is necessary for the absorption of therapeutic agents, as it influences the rate and degree of their availability in the body. The drug release dependency on time is often mathematically correlated (Dash et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2022). The ESPEEK with drug release is encompassed by four different mathematical models, such as zero-first sequence kinetics models (to confirm the drug release sequence from the mat), Hopfenberg model (to ensure diffusion or erosion), and Ritger-Peppas model (for diffusion).

Hemocompatibility studies

The electrospun mat's hemocompatibility was investigated using hemolysis through photometrical analysis. Using the Ficoll-hypaque density gradient centrifugation procedure, the separation of plasma and leukocytes was extracted from the collected blood (Böyum, 1968). The isolated erythrocyte was washed thrice with PBS before further processing. For the experiment, a 2 % erythrocyte solution was made by diluting it in PBS; the ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF were incubated in 0.8 mL of PBS at 37 °C for 30 min. After adding 0.2 mL of diluted erythrocytes to the samples, the mixture was incubated for 2 h. Subsequently, all samples were centrifuged at 1600 rpm (10 min) to determine the amount of free and lysed haemoglobin (As). The absorbance of the supernatants was then measured at 570 nm using a UV spectrophotometer. Water served as the positive control (Ap), and erythrocytes with PBS were used as the negative control (An).

Anti-microbial properties

The antimicrobial potential of electrospun nanofibers was examined against strains of Gram-positive (S. aureus), Gram-negative (E.coli) bacterium, and fungi (C. albicans). The agar well diffusion method is widely used to evaluate the antimicrobial activity of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF. The disk-diffusion method utilizes an agar plate surface that is inoculated by spreading a volume (500 μL) of the microbial inoculum over the entire agar surface. The samples were then carefully placed on the agar plate using forceps. Then, agar plates are incubated under suitable conditions depending on the test microorganism. The antimicrobial agent diffuses in the agar medium, and the zone of inhibition is checked after the incubation period.

In vitro evaluation

Cell viability and proliferation studies

Human macrophages (RAW264.7) were cultured in DMEM with 10 % FBS and 1 % antibiotics. The cultures were then incubated at 37 °C in a humid atmosphere containing 5 % CO2. The cytotoxic effect of both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF nanofibers was studied according to ISO10993-5 standard protocols for measuring direct MTT assay (confirming proliferation activity) for 1st, 3rd, and 7th days and indirect MTT assay (to assess the viability) (Shalumon et al., 2009; Zamani et al., 2013). Both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF mats were sterilized under a UV light, washed thrice with sterile PBS. Subsequently, immersed in culture medium for 30 min, and then placed in a 96-well plate. Cells were detached by trypsin/EDTA, counted by haemocytometer, and seeded into the sample mats at a density of 10,000 cells per well in the case of direct assay and 5000 cells seeded for 24 h in the indirect assay. After 1st, 3rd, and 7th days of incubation, cells were washed with PBS and incubated with pure DMEM containing 5 % MTT reagent for 4 h, and aliquots of every well were pipetted out. Then, MTT lysis buffer was incubated for 2 h, and the absorbance of each well was measured at 590 nm microplate reader and a blank with cells were taken as a control. Cell attachment studies

Cell adhesion was analysed using microscopic examinations in a 24-well culture dish, after seeding the RAW 264.7 cells for 1 h. Both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF mats (1 × 1 cm2) were put on the coverslips to avoid possible floating during the cell culture. Fresh confluent flasks of RAW 264.7 cells were incubated in 1 % trypsin for 5 min and then suspended in full culture medium. Subsequently, the media change was given frequently to maintain the cells for seven days. After the 1st, 3rd, and 7th days of incubation, cell attachment was observed by a phase contrast microscope. The results were obtained from two independent experiments, each performed twice.

Live-dead studies of ESPEEK

For both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF mats, live-dead staining with acridine orange ethidium bromide (AO-EB) dual staining technique were performed to examine adhesion and proliferation. RAW 264.7 cells were cultured on samples with seeding density of 1 x 106 cells/well (in dissolved in media) and at particular time points, stained with AO-EB, rinsed with PBS and visualized under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus 1X51, Singapore) with an excitation wavelength of 490 nm and an emission wavelength of 520 nm (Padinjarathil et al., 2018).

Cell migration studies

The scratch wound healing experiment was conducted on both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF to assess the anti-metastatic effects on RAW 264.7 and HaCaT cells, as outlined in earlier studies (Padinjarathil et al., 2018). The cells were placed in a six-well plate at a concentration of 1 × 106 cells/well and allowed to grow until they covered 80 % of the surface. A sterile 100 μL micropipette tip was used to create a scrape in the confluent monolayer. The plates were cleaned with PBS and then incubated at 37 °C in fresh DMEM containing 10 % FBS with or without the chemicals to be evaluated by samples, which were dissolved in media containing DMSO (10 μg/mL). Three random locations on the underside of each dish were marked to quantify the wound breadth using a 10X objective lens and was shot using a phase contrast microscope at different time intervals until the monolayer had fully healed. The wells were rinsed with PBS to eliminate dead cells, then the plates were incubated at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 environment. The wound closure was calculated as the mean ± SEM of the measures taken at time zero and other time points. The size of the wound was measured using Image J software (version 1.5i). Image J-optimized automated methods effectively quantify the proliferation and migration of adherent cells. This software enables automatic recognition of wound healing sizes, adjusts the average wound width by accounting for inclination, and measures key parameters such as area, wound area fraction, average wound width, and the width deviation in scratch/wound healing assay images (Venter and Niesler, 2019). The experiments were carried out in triplicates to obtain statistically significant results.

In vivo wound healing studies

The in vivo study was carried out under the approval of the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of Regional Cancer Centre in Trivandrum, India Reg no: RCC/IAEC/02/2023. The experiment protocols were investigated and followed the rules by CPCSEA (Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals) guidelines. Animal tests were conducted on a six-to eight-week-old male BALB/c to carry out a skin incision wound model.

Throughout the trial, nine male BALB/c mice, 8 weeks old and weighing 25 ± 3 g, were full access to both water and food. Following, the mice were randomly assigned to three groups each having 3 mice to facilitate adaptability. The first group is named as the control group, and the second group as positive control, where commercially available Bactigras bandages were used, and group 3 consists of ESPEEK-CF. The animals were anesthetized by injecting ketamine and xylazine, intraperitoneally at a dosage of 80 and 10 mg/kg, respectively. Successively, dorsal hair was removed using a commercially available hair remover and cleaned with 70 % ethanol. A biopsy punch was used to obtain full-thickness excisions measuring 5 mm in diameter. Following excisions, scaffolds of uniform dimensions were disinfected using UV light, washed with saline, and then samples were applied directly to the wounds of the respective groups. The experimental wound was covered with gauze, whereas the control wounds were treated with commercially available Bactigras. Following the trial, the animals were housed individually in cages and provided with standard meals and an uninterrupted water supply. The wounds were observed at time points of 3rd, 7th, and 14th days to understand the degree of wound healing and quantified as the percentage of wound area by employing the formula given below

After each time point, mice were euthanized using a chloroform chamber. The injured skin was excised, preserved in 10 % formaldehyde, and then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histological analyses. All photographs were captured digitally and wound swabs were obtained on day 3 and day 7. It was further diluted in sterile PBS, and plated on nutrient agar plates to assess the in vivo antibacterial activity. The colonies were manually counted after 24 h of incubation and recorded as the total viable count.

Statistical analysis

In vitro investigations were conducted three times to obtain an average data expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) were analysed using GraphPad PRISM software version 5.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA). Image J software was used in wound scratch assay studies for image-based calculations. In case of in vivo studies, each group consisted of three mice from which the average wound area was counted. Microbial swabs and histopathological studies were done in replicates to confirm the observations obtained.

Results and DISCUSSION

Characterization of electrospun SPEEK

Sulphonation of PEEK

The nanofibrous mat was prepared from sulphonated polyether ether ketone (ESPEEK). Incorporating sulphonic groups into the polymer increases hydrophilicity and solubility in the solvent. The SPEEK polymer with a high degree of sulphonation shows high solubility in polar aprotic solvents (Wang et al., 2019). In this work, a high degree of sulphonated polymer was achieved by treating the polymer for extended period in sulfuric acid at room temperature, and the rest of the preparation procedure is same as the previous work (Baladhandapania et al., 2018). A high degree of sulphonated polymer was preferred in the current study and was achieved by a long period of sulfuric acid exposure (Padinjarathil et al., 2023a). The degree of sulphonation obtained in the current study, treated in sulfuric acid for 72 h, is 91.3 % as displayed in Fig. S1. Ghasemi and his colleagues have conducted different condition-based sulphonation, according to which the degree of sulphonation varied. The peaks were observed at 7.1–7.9 ppm, but the intensity varied depending on the sulphonation conditions (Ghasemi et al., 2016).

Chemical interaction of ESPEEK and ciprofloxacin

The FTIR analysis was performed on CF, ESPEEK, and ESPEEK-CF in ATR mode (Fig. S2). The peak at 1224 cm−1 in ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF indicates the existence of a C–O–C aromatic linkage, whereas the peak at around 1500 cm−1 in ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF signifies the presence of the C–C aromatic ring. Both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF showed a significant band from 1024 cm−1–1200 cm−1, corresponding to the O–S–O stretching vibration of the sulphonic acid groups. These peaks are identical to membrane-cast ESPEEK from our previous work (Padinjarathil et al., 2023a). It confirms that the electrospinning of the polymer does not disturb the primary cohesive interactions.

The CF spectra showed transmittance at 3088 cm−1 and 2932 cm−1 for aromatic and alkene C-H stretching, 1623 cm−1 for quinolones presence, 1492 cm−1 for C=O stretching, 1342 cm−1 in protonation of the -NH group and 1267 cm−1 for OH in the carboxylic acid. The wavelengths between 1100 cm−1 to 1000 cm−1 indicate the presence of the C-F group, the characteristic peak of ciprofloxacin, which was observed in both CF and ESPEEK-CF (Mohsen et al., 2020). The characteristic functional groups have peaks at 3527 cm−1 for O–H stretching, 1708 cm−1 for C=O stretching, 1025 cm−1 for C–F, and 1624 cm−1 for quinolones in ciprofloxacin (Mohsen et al., 2020; Ghasemi et al., 2016; Thamilselvan et al., 2023). Sahoo and his colleagues also claim these similar peaks (Sahoo et al., 2011). The ESPEEK-CF displayed the peaks corresponding to CF, such as peaks below 1000 cm−1, 1342 cm−1, and 1492 cm−1, which confirms the presence of CF.

Thermal stability of ESPEEK

Thermogravimetric analyses were performed on (6 ± 1 mg) ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF to estimate their thermal stability and degradation using a TGA 8000 apparatus (PerkinElmer). The samples were heated under nitrogen flow from 40 °C up to 800 °C with a heating rate of 10 °C/min and the weight loss was recorded and displayed in Fig. 1A and is the TGA curve. All the samples show a broad, slow initial mass loss below 100 °C related to the evaporation of the absorbed moisture from the fibers. The small amount of weight loss observed before 250 °C can be ascribed to removal of the organic solvent DMF (Jun et al., 2012). The ESPEEK fibers exhibit two distinct breakdown phases at elevated temperatures, regardless of the presence of medication. The first stage of weight loss begins around 310 °C and is related to the breakdown of SO3H groups (Fig. 1A). The second stage of weight loss is related to oxidative pyrolysis of the polymer main chain, initiating around 620 °C. Fig. 1B depicts the DSC (Differential Scanning Calorimetry) of the ESPEEK with and without the drug. The large endothermic peak at the temperatures below 200 °C corresponds to water evaporation. The rest of the other peaks in DSC was used to identify the onset of various stages of weight loss in TGA curves.

Morphological characterization of ESPEEK mat

The SEM image of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF electrospun mat was obtained at room temperature as shown in Fig. 1C–F. A slight non-uniformity among the fibers and bead formation were observed upon drug loading. Coherently, the pristine polymer displayed uniform morphology (Fig. 1C and D). The diameter of the nanofibers mat of the pristine polymer (ESPEEK) is in the range of 192 ± 0.105 nm with a prominent level of consistency (Fig. 1E). In case of drug-embedded mat (ESPEEK-CF), the diameter of nanofibers has decreased to 150 ± 0.117 nm. Wilcoxon test was performed on fibre diameters of both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF and were significant as the p-value was <0.0001. The decrease in diameter might be a drug coat over nanofibers, which affects fiber formation (Fig. 1F and Fig. S3). The EDAX analysis indeed confirmed the presence of ciprofloxacin in the ESPEEK-CF sample, as indicated by the detection of 3 % nitrogen related to the drug addiction (Table S1). Sadrjahan et al. reported that nanofibers produced from high-sulphonated SPEEK, displayed nanoscale dimensions. The electrospun solution will have higher conductivity with increased -SO3H groups. The nanofiber's diameter was decreased as it can be attributed to forces generated by fluid jet stretching when the electrical energy given to the polymer breaks the droplet formation and surface free energy of the polymer solution at the tip of the needle (Sadrjahani et al., 2017). The solution parameters such as conductivity, viscosity, and surface tension significantly influence the morphology of electrospun nanofibers. The addition of an aqueous drug solution to a polymer solution can modify viscosity and surface tension. Dissolving ciprofloxacin in glacial acetic acid prior to the electrospinning process results in an increase in the diameter of the nanofibers. Conversely, the dissolution of ciprofloxacin in water leads to a reduction in nanofiber size owing to decreased viscosity. In the current case, the fibre diameter (430 nm) was reduced with addition of solvents (DMF) which intern increase elevated density and boiling points. Ionized drug molecules augment the solution's conductivity, resulting in an elevated charge density of the jet and a fibrous shape, but the exact value is indeterminate. Ciprofloxacin at reduced concentrations produces homogeneous, non-beaded nanofibers approximately 200 nm in diameter. Moreover, a 5 % concentration exhibited uniformity and steady release over a protracted duration (Abdulhussain et al., 2023; Penton et al., 2022; Uhljar et al., 2022).

The fiber diameter can be changed by using solution of ciprofloxacin with varied viscosity. In the current study, concentration of SPEEK in the solution, used to prepare electrospun fiber, influences the viscosity, thereby impacting the diameter of the nanofibers. The pristine polymer solution is expected to have a higher concentration of SPEEK compared to drug loaded solution (Shanmuga Sundar and Sangeetha, 2012). Numerous factors, such as fiber diameter and alignment, influence diffusion-controlled drug release, and matrix degradation influences drug release characteristics and cell attachment in nanofiber scaffolds for tissue engineering applications (Ekambaram et al., 2021a).

Contact angle of ESPEEK

Nanofibrous materials with a significant moisture content are preferred for skin wound healing. A moist environment within the wound bed promotes fibroblast proliferation and enhances wound contraction while minimizing scarring. These nanofibrous materials demonstrate accelerated healing compared to fibers with lower moisture content, largely due to a reduced inflammatory phase. Furthermore, the water contact angle measurement of ESPEEK polymeric nanofiber, measuring 38.69° ± 3.2° (which is less than 90°), confirms its hydrophilic nature as portrayed in Fig. 2. The hydrophilic characteristics of the polymer were attributed to the incorporation of the SO3H group during functionalization, which enhanced moisture absorption within the polymer backbone in accordance with the degree of sulphonation which increase wettability. The wettability properties of ciprofloxacin-infused ESPEEK nanofibrous scaffolds was observed to be 35.18° ± 6.11°. These findings suggest that the hydrophilic nature of the synthesized biomaterial improves with the increasing drug content which is in support with reported literature (Kwon et al., 2018).

Water uptake capacity of ESPEEK mat

The water uptake results are collected and shown in Fig. 3A and C. The water absorption trials were conducted by submerging the ESPEEK mat in deionized water for approximately 35 days and measuring its weight (Fig. 3A). The ESPEEK mat displayed quick water absorption, doubling its weight within the first 2 h with the swelling. After 7 days of incubation, a maximum absorption of 900 % was observed. The SPEEK polymers exhibit varying degrees of water absorption based on the degree of sulphonation. The SO3H group because of its ionic nature absorbs more water, leading to quick absorption and swelling (Joosten et al., 2023; Zaidi et al., 2000). The degree of sulphonation plays a significant influence on water uptake compared to methods by which the polymer mat or membrane were prepared (Sayed Daud et al., 2020). To prevent wound desiccation and promote the elimination of surplus wound exudate, an optimum skin scaffold for dressing is necessary. They should possess a water absorption capacity ranging from 100 to 800 % of their dry weight to minimize fluid build-up and promote the creation of a new extracellular matrix (Nosrati et al., 2021). PCL scaffolds combined with Syzygium cumini extract showed a swelling percentage exceeding 800 % within 6 h, where the swelling further increased with higher concentrations of the extract (Murugan et al., 2023). Similarly, PVA/P3KBT nanofibers exhibited a remarkable water absorption of 1094 %, although this capacity is expected to decrease with the introduction of cross-linkers (Zakrzewska et al., 2023). In contrast, PTMC (poly(trimethylene carbonate), which is hydrophobic, showed a water uptake of only 3 ± 2 %. In comparison, the hydrophilic GelMA exhibited a significantly higher absorption rate of 445 ± 23 % when formed into mats with PTMC-tMA (Liang et al., 2021).

Water uptake analysis was performed at different pH and the results are as depicted in Fig. 3C. In the case of pH 7, the water uptake reached a maximum at 7th day; at pH 4 and 9, there was no steady increase as compared to pH 7. Acute or chronic wounds typically have an alkaline pH, the pH-dependent swelling characteristic can be effectively used to deliver medications that treat wounds. Non-polar polymer material does not show any significant difference in water absorption upon change in pH. On the other hand, oleic acid (unsaturated fatty acid), being lipophilic, does not cause ionization of ciprofloxacin, leading to pH-dependent solubility (Uhljar et al., 2021; Ju et al., 2024). At low pH (pH 4), the sulfonic acid groups in ESPEEK are protonated, leading to an increase in positive charge and enhanced water uptake due to electrostatic interactions. At neutral pH (pH 7), partial deprotonation diminishes the positive charge, and the increased repulsion among negatively charged groups may result in a decrease in water uptake compared to acidic conditions. At alkaline pH (pH 9), complete deprotonation elevates the concentration of negative charge. The concentration of sulfonic acid groups plays a crucial role in determining the balance of positive and negative charges across different pH levels (Pakalniete et al., 2023; de Castro and Filho, 2023).

In vitro drug release study of ESPEEK-CF

The drug release of the electrospun mat was conducted slightly longer than the conventional procedure. The drug release was monitored over 21 days and was found to be sustained and controlled as displayed in Fig. 3B. The ESPEEK mats resulted in higher swelling at the initial phase of water uptake experiment. Drug release from ESPEEK was calculated using the standard concentration plot of ciprofloxacin. The mat released 3.95 % of the drug in the initial first hour and increased slowly to 8.15 % after 24 h and 53 % in 7 days. These results demonstrated a sustained concentration independent drug release with no sign of drug burst.

The drug release from electrospun polymeric nanofibers may follow a series of mechanisms, which includes diffusion through the polymer matrix, release by polymer degradation, and desorption of the drugs from the nano-porous surface of the nanofibers (Ju et al., 2024). The solute transport from nondegradable polymeric systems is considered diffusion driven (Uhljar et al., 2021). To support the assumption that ESPEEK-CF follows diffusion-controlled release kinetics, the initial drug concentration (5 %) in the release system should be much higher than the drug solubility. Therefore, the drug release kinetics can be considered as diffusion-controlled kinetics obeying non-Fickian mode. Polymers that have a high swelling index demonstrate a diffusion mechanism, and the pace at which they release substances relies on their swelling rate (Uhljar et al., 2021).

Generally, electrospun mat has limited compatibility between drug and organic solvents and is known for instant burst release for an initial few hours due to drug solubility and leaching out from the surface. Ciprofloxacin loaded in PCL-gelatin released 65 % of drug within 15 h (burst release) followed by a sustained release for 4 days (Ajmal et al., 2019). In an another reported literature, the release of ciprofloxacin from the electrospun PLA fibers demonstrated a release within 2 h (Teno et al., 2022). The PMMA polymer exhibited a minimal release of 1.5 % of ciprofloxacin over 40 days. From these reported data, it can be concluded that ciprofloxacin blends directly into electrospun scaffolds imposes challenges due to an inevitable burst release of the drug with a complete release in short duration indicating an erosion-based release mechanism or at very low release rate leading to difficultly in optimizing the dosage for long term applications.

A study by S. Zupancic et al. reported that the combination of PMMA/PVA released 56 % in the first 13 h and additionally, 38 % in the following 19 days, whereas the combinations of PMMA/PEO 69 % released in the first 6 h and 8 % released in the following 17 days. In addition, they have reported another blend of polymers wherein PMMA/Chitosan releases 11 % ciprofloxacin in 20 days (Zupančič et al., 2016b). The initial burst release was observed in all cases, subsequently, the sustained release was noticed at a low drug release rate. SPEEK with NH2–ZrO2 electrospun nanofibers exhibited an initial curcumin release of approximately 20 % within the first 5 h. Subsequently, a significant increase was observed, with the release reaching 70 % by the 18-h mark (Ekambaram et al., 2021b). Besides the integration of bioactives, such as Aloe vera and Morinda citrifolia extract with SPEEK nanofibers facilitates customized and controlled drug release (Ekambaram et al., 2021a; Ekambaram and Dharmalingam, 2020).

Thus, we conclude that our polymer (ESPEEK) without any combination completely releases the drug in a sustained pattern by diffusion mechanism, preventing the initial burst release. The polymer ESPEEK displayed a controlled and sustained release. This could be due to the surface ion interaction between ESPEEK and the drug. This interaction ensured the barrier towards initial burst drug release and uniform release of 82 % of the drug over 21 days. Thus, the ESPEEK demonstrated superior properties of release when compared to literature reports.

Mathematical modelling

Four important mathematical models such as Hopfenberg, Ritger-Peppas, first-order kinetics, and zero-order kinetics models were used to identify the drug release pattern from the experimental procedures (Fig. S4). To get findings that followed the predicted pattern, the experimentally acquired values of the drug release parameters were substituted into the model of interest. The molecular dynamics of zero-order kinetics have a similar tendency to the y = mx plot, in which the slope of the curve represents the response rate. The drug release in this model follows a zero-order release pattern and is almost linear, according to the equation f1 = k0 with R2 of 0.9827.

The Ritger-Peppas model defines drug release by determining the erosion or diffusion mechanism pattern. The sole indication of mathematical discrepancies is the fluctuation in the equation's exponent (n); in the case of the Fickian model (n ≥ 0.5), which governs the diffusion mechanism. On the other hand, the non-Fickian model (0.5<n < 1), which is associated with the zero-order of release, regulates a variety of anomalous circumstances with diffusion mechanism and matches with our results with R2 of 0.9159. The non-Fickian model is observed in the current situations, which was correlated with the Hopfenberg drug release model to verify that medication release was through diffusion rather than erosion kinetics. In our case, the erosion coefficient is in the range of 10−6 m/h. Hence, the erosion of the carrier is not the primary factor causing the drug release with R2 of 0.987. The in vitro drug release pattern, along with water uptake studies in our work directly correlates with the results of mathematical modelling. Previous studies on a core–shell structure combining PHBV and PVP loaded with curcumin have demonstrated a sustained-release pharmacological profile beyond 24 h. The drugs in the fibers were released at a consistent rate, indicated by a high correlation coefficient (R2) in the first-order kinetic model. The Higuchi model characterized the dissolution and diffusion dynamics, while the Rigter–Peppas equation provided insights into drug release kinetics, revealing Fickian diffusion and skeleton erosion processes (Liu et al., 2022). Multiple PVP and ethyl cellulose solutions infused with Ketoprofen were used to build component meshes. As polymer content increased, medication release rates changed from fast to delayed. Drug release by diffusion and swelling (anomalous transport). Case transfer occurs when n > 0.85, including polymer relaxation during matrix dissolution via Higuhi equation and drug kinetics. According to the Ritger-Peppas model, drug release from the polymer happened by Fickian diffusion (n < 0.43). PVP accelerates drug release, whereas ethylcellulose sustains it (Huang et al., 2012).

Anti-microbial properties of ESPEEK

Infection at the wound site is the main issue in the field of wound care. Antibacterial drugs can be incorporated into polymer nanofibers to eradicate the infection and quicken the healing process. The ESPEEK-CF were tested against E. coli, S. aureus, and C. albicans; the results demonstrated that Gram-positive bacteria like S. aureus, showed significant inhibition of 16 mm, whereas Gram-negative bacteria like E.coli and C. albicans showed no notable effects from Table 1 and Fig. S5. The CF present in ESPEEK-CF is the cause for antibacterial inhibition in drug-loaded nanofibers, however, the pristine ESPEEK displayed no significant effect indicating no toxicity toward the microbial niche. In the reported literature, the PVA/alginate-blended electrospun nanofibers with ciprofloxacin showed equal antibacterial efficacy against S. aureus and P. aeruginosa compared to ciprofloxacin by the broth dilution method and isothermal micro-calorimetric assay (Ju et al., 2024). The blend of polymers such as PCL/GEL-loaded ciprofloxacin and quercetin reported an effective zone of inhibition when compared to PCL with ciprofloxacin and quercetin (Ajmal et al., 2019).

Hemocompatibility of ESPEEK

The hemocompatibility is a prerequisite for wound management scaffolds in all biological systems. According to USFDA regulations, it is biologically safe only when the hemolysis rate is observed to be less than 5 %. The biomaterials, when in contact with blood, are expected to induce minimal haemolysis; thereby, the destruction of RBCs will be less in response to shear stress or changes in osmotic pressure. In our investigation, ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF were subjected to hemocompatibility by hemolysis ratio and protein absorption tests. The hemocompatibility of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF is 1.33 % and 0.33 % respectively as displayed in Fig. 4A. According to earlier reports, SPEK membranes demonstrated exceptional hemocompatibility (Padinjarathil et al., 2023b). The biomimetic nanofibrous scaffold of SPEEK reported was found to be highly suitable for bone tissue engineering revealing excellent hemocompatibility below 2 % (Ekambaram and Dharmalingam, 2022). These reports supports our present study on electrospun SPEEK materials that are non-toxic and biocompatible.

Hemocompatibility studies of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF A) haemolysis and B) protein absorption.

The protein absorption studies were carried out by isolating plasma from whole blood and incubating it with the ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF mats. Protein absorption indicates platelet activation and aggregation, which play a key role in the cessation of haemorrhage in wound management. Surface hydrophilicity can affect protein adsorption, which affects cell adherence to the surface (Albayati et al., 2024). In the wound site, where the scaffold is positioned, proteins in the body's fluids come in contact. The proteins adhere to the scaffold's surface, acting as an adhesive surface to activate pathways to clot and angiogenesis. Cell adhesion is the initial stage of angiogenesis, which may directly influence the biocompatibility of scaffolds. Assessing protein absorption can equally serve as a suitable indicator for evaluating the protein absorption capacity of the scaffold or wound dressing (Nosrati et al., 2021). The ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF mats exhibited 20 % absorption of plasma proteins during the first hour, suggesting their efficacy as a protein absorber as displayed in Fig. 4B and the it supports promoting angiogenesis as a barrier towards blood loss at the wound site.

Cytocompatibility of ESPEEK mat

The cytocompatibility of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF scaffolds was assessed by both direct and indirect contact assays against RAW 264.7 cell lines to assess the cell-proliferation-promoting ability. In the indirect contact assay, various concentrations ranging from 10 to 200 μg/mL were investigated and results are shown in Fig. 5A. The lowest concentrations demonstrated cell proliferation, whereas 10 % cytotoxicity was observed till 50 μg/mL of ESPEEK. The proliferation rate of ESPEEK was found to be significant, indicating that ESPEEK is a biocompatible and inert material in biological systems. The indirect contact assay was performed for both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF for a period of 1, 3, and 7 days. In both cases, the results revealed an increase in cell proliferation from the 3rd day. In the case of ESPEEK, viability increased by 126 % on the third day and reached up to 165 % by the 7th day. The ESPEEK-CF exhibited cell viability ranging from 72 % to 138 % from day 1–7, while ESPEEK maintained a cell viability of around 100 % as seen in Fig. 5B.

B) Indirect MTT assay for ESPEEK mat at 1st, 3rd, and 7th days (ANOVA: ∗∗∗ = p < 0.001, ∗∗p < 0.019). In the direct contact assay displayed in Fig. 5C, initial cell adherence to the scaffolds was observed at 24 h. About 130 % and 122 % of cells were found to be attached to ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF, respectively, which indicates that ESPEEK-CF showed more effective adherence than ESPEEK. Furthermore, ESPEEK-CF demonstrated a higher proliferation rate than ESPEEK on day 7, reaching 208 % while the ESPEEK mat did not exhibit any notable change in cell proliferation from the initial day to the seventh day.

The direct MTT assay performed in HaCaT cells for ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF showed an enhancement in the cell viability, which increased over 7 days similar to that of RAW 264.7 cell lines (Fig. S6). Ekambam and co-workers reported the SPEEK with bioactive molecules like Morinda citrifolia revealing biocompatibility in keratinocytes (Ekambaram et al., 2021a). The PCL/gelatin-blended ciprofloxacin showed an extensive cell proliferation rate compared to control, revealing the importance of mechanical support to cells (Ajmal et al., 2019). It can be hypothesized that surface segregation of the ciprofloxacin and SPEEK nanofibrous lead to substantial differences in surface chemistry for each scaffold, affecting protein and cell attachment to the scaffolds. The promotion of cell attachment and spreading are likely be possible in both ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF due to the presence of various functional groups and hetero atoms. The carboxylic acid group present in the drug has been shown to significantly promote the cell attachment of fibroblasts on both 2D surfaces and electrospun nanofibers (Hashemikia et al., 2021; Wright et al., 2017). While multiple other factors of the polymer may also contribute towards increased cell attachment and spreading on scaffolds (Rancan et al., 2019). The mesh-like structure of the ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF facilitates surface segregation and induces growth of cells along with drug release.

Cell adhesion of ESPEEK mat

After achieving significant cytocompatibility for ESPEEK mat, we employed microscopic visualization to exactly analyse the initial interactions between cells and ESPEEK, which revealed promising insights into their adhesion. The ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF were seeded with RAW 264.7 cells at various time intervals to examine the attachment and morphology. In the initial stage, the majority of cells were round-shaped with minimal cytoplasm, but after 24 h, spindle-shaped cells became dominant, indicating the adhesion phenomenon. Over time, there was a rise in the number of cells, which confirms that cell proliferation occurred in the presence of ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF. These scaffolds provide support and stimulation for cells to nourish and proliferate. The contact between scaffolds and the cell monolayer exhibited no indications of rejection. Fig. 6 illustrates the adhesion of cells to ESPEEK-CF and ESPEEK, which mimics an extracellular matrix, facilitating cell adhesion and proliferation. The same was verified from the observation on the 3rd and 7th day after incubating the cells in the scaffolds. Cell adhesion is influenced by wettability and surface hydrophilicity, which causes cells to stick to the surface and use it as a mechanical support for growth and multiplication (Sreerekha et al., 2013). Electrospun scaffolds, which resemble extracellular matrix (ECM), serve as a physical framework and, more importantly, as an insoluble controller of biological activity that impacts cellular functions such as migration, contraction, and division (Wright et al., 2017; Ekambaram and Dharmalingam, 2022).

The varied rates of adhesion are based on varying surface hydrophilicity, which showed significantly greater cell attachment during the initial 6 h (Fig. 6A–D). After 24 h of incubation, cells adhered to the scaffold to multiply using it as external support (Fig. 6B–E). The RAW264.7 cells in ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF scaffold were cultured for 7 days to assess growth, viability, and adherence by examining using immunofluorescence microscopy. The images presented in Fig. 7A and B show that the viable cells are green color, while orange/yellow color indicates dead cells. After 24 h of treatment, fewer cells were dead and floating; some space was visible due to the washed-off dead cells during the imaging process, indicating the limitation of this procedure. The cell morphology after treatment remained unchanged, but the number of dead cells was significantly lower when compared to the control. Cells attached to the scaffolds exhibited higher viability compared to ESPEEK-CF scaffolds. The results of the live-dead staining were consistent with the cytotoxicity assays performed, which are in line with the reported literature (Ekambaram and Dharmalingam, 2022).

Migration studies

To assess the impact of ESPEEK on cell migration, we conducted a wound-healing migration test in RAW 264.7 cell lines. The ESPEEK had a more pronounced reaction, characterized by a substantial migration on the healing of scratch, resulting in the closure of the wound as displayed in Fig. 7C. The results of control cells revealed 80 % healing after 72 h, however, the ESPEEK treatment facilitated the healing in a time-dependent manner (Fig. S7). Migration of cells is a multistage process that involves a many-sided multi-process cascade including cell adhesion, migration, cell-to-cell, and cell-to-extracellular matrix interactions (Ekambaram and Dharmalingam, 2022). In our case, the ESPEEK-CF mat served as external mechanical support to the cells to migrate.

In vivo evaluation of wound healing

Pictorial evaluation and planimetric analysis were conducted for every animal in each group. The wound healing progress was visually documented using an electronic camera from a consistent distance. Planimetric examinations were conducted at regular intervals on the 0th, 3rd, 7th, and 14th day (Fig. 8A); investigations revealed that the experimental group III, treated with ESPEEK-CF, exhibited faster healing when compared to other groups. Animal models used in live organisms are essential for investigating the healing processes of long-lasting wounds (Zindle et al., 2021).

Wound contraction during the healing period was measured by analysing planimetric sheet pictures using Image J software. The third group was treated with ESPEEK-CF, exhibited wound healing from the third day (63 %) to the twelfth day (80 %). The scaffolds demonstrated a significant enhancement in wound closure relative to the standard group and control; besides that, group III exhibited the highest rate of wound contraction among all groups in the initial stage. Group I with untreated wounds exhibited delayed contraction compared to other treated groups, besides exudates were observed beneath the dry scalp (Fig. 8A). The positive control (group II) treated with a commercially available bandage (Bactigras) showed wound closure on the 12th day (51.9 %) with p < 0.001 indicating significant; whereas Group III showed a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001) compared to the other experimental group and control starting from the 12th day (93 % closure). The rate of wound contraction and planimetric evaluation showed a strong correlation with visual evaluation (Fig. 8B). The degradation studies were investigated in ESPEEK-CF, revealing no significant weight loss of the scaffold even after 14 days of treatment in Balb/c mice.

Analysis of microbiological studies

The microbiological analysis of the wound sites involved collecting swab samples at several time intervals, 0th, 3rd, and 14th day, to obtain an insight into the microbial ecosystem present at the wound site. In addition, it is important to determine if ESPEEK-CF scaffolds exhibit effective antimicrobial properties at the site of wound. The microbial colony in the control group rose from the initial day to the 3rd day, whereas in the case of ESPEEK-CF, there was a huge reduction in the microbial colony, resulting in a significant release of anti-microbial drug (Fig. 8C). However, by the 14th day, the microbial population is effectively managed towards wound closure. ESPEEK-CF demonstrated efficient control in the initial stages; there was minimal development observed on the third day, and a subsequent drop by the 14th day, claiming to sustain the release of ciprofloxacin from the ESPEEK-CF mat. This suggests that ESPEEK-CF acts as a reliable barrier against infection at the wound site. Typically, electrospun scaffolds have a degradation rate that allows pathogens to easily attack and cause infection while the wound is healing (Shalumon et al., 2009), in our case, the scaffold demonstrated no significant weight loss or degradation.

Histopathological analysis

The hematoxylin and eosin staining of control, PC, and treatment groups on the 3rd and 14th day of post-wound formation displayed in Fig. 9. The dermis layer of the scaffolds showed a huge surface ulceration in the control group on the 3rd day, and it persisted along with increased inflammation and necrosis by the 14th day (Fig. 9A and D). Thoroughly proliferating fibroblasts and granulation with a huge inflammation were observed in the control group on the 14th day. Promisingly, ESPEEK-CF displayed granulation in the dermis tissue, composed of proliferating fibroblasts and blood vessels along with inflamed cells (Fig. 9C and F). ESPEEK-CF also induces inflammation at the beginning of the animal trial due to the free sulphonic acid groups present in the mat. Generally, at the wound site, inflammatory cells produce angiogenic compounds that promote the formation of new blood vessels. Angiogenesis abnormalities at this point can result in chronic wounds caused by delayed healing (Nosrati et al., 2021). The skin shows an ulcerated surface epidermis, and the dermis shows proliferative fibroblast and myofibroblast cells with a proliferation of a few blood vessels with endothelial cells and infiltrated by inflammatory cells forming granulation tissue observed with ESPEEK-CF on the 14th day. The properties were more effective when ESPEEK-CF was used, showing increased proliferation of granulation tissue and epithelialization. The presence of keratinocytes at the wound edge contributed to faster healing compared to the other group.

The healing efficiency in ESPEEK-CF is enhanced compared to other treatment and control groups due to increased collagen production and fibroblast migration. Collagen, the basic extracellular matrix (ECM) component of skin tissue, plays a crucial role in wound healing by enhancing strength and structure. Fibrous scaffolds serve as skin substitutes because they closely resemble the structure of the extracellular matrix. They provide protection against bacterial infection, promote cellular respiration and development, and offer strong mechanical support. These benefits help in healing chronic wounds by guiding the growth of skin cells, which facilitates rapid skin regeneration (Wiegand and Hipler, 2010). In a wide array of wound healing scenarios, scaffolds play a crucial role in facilitating the regrowth of healthy tissue amidst a challenging microenvironment fraught with inflammation and a heightened risk of recurring infections (Powers et al., 2016). To achieve optimal regeneration of epithelial tissues, it is imperative to ensure uncontaminated tissue healing and regeneration while establishing a barrier for gaseous exchange and preventing the infiltration of foreign substances. Consequently, the potential of combined platform that facilitates both antibiotic release and tissue regeneration presents unparalleled advantages in the realm of wound management. The localized application of ciprofloxacin to promote anti-infection and in the presence of inflammation, in conjunction with the use of SPEEK as a material of support, offers a plethora of advantages. These include heightened bioavailability, potentially reduced doses, minimized side effects, precise drug action, sustained concentration at the site of action, potentially reduced application frequency, and uniform distribution of the drug dose across the healing tissue. Thus, the effectiveness of ESPEEK-CF towards wound healing has been demonstrated by in vivo cutaneous wound excision with histopathological and microbial studies. The release of ciprofloxacin and the non-degradability of ESPEEK-CF were confirmed after examining the post-treated ESPEEK-CF (Fig. S8). Our scaffold has proved to release BCS class II/IV drug in a sustained fashion for 21 days in vitro and 14 days in vivo without having burst release and showed high biocompatibility.

Conclusions

The emergence of the concept of sustained drug release while addressing other features of wound healing has opened new opportunities in wound management and efficient delivery systems for BCS II and IV drugs. Ciprofloxacin, a potent drug for infectious diseases, is a key component in modern medicine. However, the successful clinical translation of these drugs is hindered by insufficient biocompatibility and burst release. Using the electrospun technique, this study developed an ESPEEK nanofibrous mat with ciprofloxacin BCS II/IV drug for topical applications. The mat's enhanced swelling potential, water uptake, and high surface area ratio enable it to release the drug in a zero-order kinetics pattern without erosion. The drug was delivered over 21 days and released 91.7 % of the drug from the ESPEEK-CF nanofibrous mat. ESPEEK and ESPEEK-CF nanofibrous mats demonstrated hemocompatibility and low toxicity, making them suitable for biomedical applications. The scaffold's adhesion and antibacterial activities were evaluated, showing a strong proliferation rate and adhesion time of 6 h in both in vitro and in vivo conditions. This combination was chosen for wound management due to its ability to assist with medication and multi-targeting. The ESPEEK-CF bandages were found to be more favourable than commercially available bandages. Slow-release formulations prevent bacterial recurrence and reduce systemic exposure by focusing on the wound site. This polymer could be further developed for the delivery of various BCS class II and IV APIs, enabling prolonged drug release without degradability and extended usage.

PROQURE-I: a phase I study adding concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 to EBRT and ADT in curative intent treatment of N1 prostate cancer

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeCurrent treatment of N1M0 prostate cancer (PCa) with locoregional external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) and long-term androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) has suboptimal five-year recurrence-free rates. Adding [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 may improve tumour control and reduce reliance on long-term ADT. This phase-1 dose-escalation study explores the safety and tolerability of adding concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 with EBRT and ADT.MethodsIn the multi-centre phase-1 dose-escalation study (PROQURE-I), treatment-naïve cT1-4N1M0 PCa patients received standard locoregional EBRT (25–35 fractions) and 2–3 years ADT with concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 to determine its maximum tolerated dose (MTD). Dose levels included one cycle (3, 6 or 9 GBq in week 2 of EBRT) or two cycles (2 × 7.4 GBq in week 2 and 4). Grade ≥ 3 toxicity (CTCAE v5.0) was dose-limiting. Tumour and organ dosimetry was performed with SPECT/CT and planar imaging at 4, 24 and 168 h post-injection.ResultsFourteen patients were included. No dose-limiting toxicity was observed and the MTD was not reached. Reported grade 1–2 toxicities were related to EBRT. Clinical efficacy should be evaluated in a subsequent phase 2 study.Keywords: Curative, Lutetium-177-PSMA, Node-positive, Phase 1, Prostate cancer radiotherapy

Introduction

Historically, prostate cancer (PCa) patients with nodal metastases were excluded from curative treatment due to the assumption of systemic disease, with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) given for palliative intent. The advent of molecular imaging targeting the prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) and Positron Emission Tomography/Computed Tomography (PET/CT) has improved the detection of small nodal metastases without distant spread (N1M0) [1]. This allowed more reliable patient selection for locoregional treatment, and led to increased confidence that some of these patients can benefit from multimodal treatment with curative intent. Many N1M0 patients now receive high-dose whole pelvis external beam radiation therapy (EBRT) with a boost to all visible tumour lesions in the pelvic area, combined with 2–3 years ADT [2, 3].

Despite treatment advances, patients with N1M0 disease still have a suboptimal prognosis, with improved overall survival rates, but significant toxicity and 5-year biochemical recurrence rates up to 47% [4–7]. Further intensification is challenging since dose escalation of EBRT is limited by toxicity to surrounding organs. Intensification of systemic treatment with next-generation hormonal agents has shown some promise, but may also come with additional toxicities [8]. Therefore, innovative approaches are needed to safely enhance both local and systemic control.

Radionuclide therapy with 177Lu-Lutetium-PSMA-617 ([177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617) presents a novel opportunity to improve both efficacy and tolerability. This radioligand selectively targets PSMA-expressing tumour cells, delivering radiation to both macroscopic and microscopic disease with limited exposure to surrounding tissues. [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 has shown promising results in metastatic castration-resistant PCa, with high response rates and good tolerability [9, 10]. Its known toxicities, mainly involving temporary xerostomia and bone marrow suppression, do not overlap with those of EBRT and ADT. Incorporating [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 in primary treatment of PSMA-positive N1M0 patients could enhance local control, target subclinical disease beyond the EBRT field, and potentially reduce long-term ADT reliance. This phase 1 dose-escalation study aims to explore the safety and tolerability of adding concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 to standard curative-intent EBRT and ADT for curative-intent N1M0 PCa.

Methods

Study design and participants

The PROQURE-I study is a multicentre prospective phase I dose-escalation study conducted at the Netherlands Cancer Institute and the University Medical Centre Utrecht [11].

Eligible patients had histologically confirmed prostate adenocarcinoma, were staged cT1-4 per MRI, showed pelvic lymph node(s) on PSMA-PET/CT, without distant metastases (miN1M0). Positive lymph nodes showed PSMA uptake greater than blood pool activity, with no restriction on the number of involved pelvic lymph nodes, extending anatomically up to the bifurcation of the common iliac arteries. Exclusion criteria included prior EBRT, ADT, or radionuclide therapy, other malignancies, impaired bone marrow (Hb < 6 mmol/L, leukocytes < 2.5 109L or Platelets < 100 109/L), reduced renal function (GFR < 60 mL/min/1,73m2) or subjectively impaired salivary function; or urinary issues requiring interventions, more details can be found in the study protocol [11].

The primary aim was to determine the maximum tolerated dosage (MTD) of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617, using a Bayesian Optimal Interval Design (BOIN) design targeting 30% dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) across three pre-specified, pre-standardised activity levels: 3, 6 and 9 GBq. Further details on the rationale for these dose levels can be found in the published study protocol [12]. DLT was defined as any ≥ grade 3 acute toxicity per CTCAE v5.0, occurring from EBRT start until 3 months post-treatment and attributed to the study treatment. Further details are in the study protocol [11]. After the initial single-dose phase (Cohort A), a protocol amendment introduced Cohort B, with two 7.4 GBq cycles at a 2-week interval. This fractionated dose intensification was intended to maximise anti-tumour effect, supported by prior studies demonstrating the safety and efficacy of two subsequent 177Lu-J591 and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 doses given 2 weeks apart [13, 14], as well as the FDA-approved 7.4 GBq dosing following VISION [15]. In the absence of dose-limiting toxicity in the initial cohorts, the BOIN design does not require to enrol additional patients at lower dose levels. More details on the BOIN design can be found in the study protocol [11].

The study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee and uploaded at the clinical trial registry (NCT05162573). All patients provided written informed consent.

Treatment procedures

EBRT was delivered using intensity modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) in 35 fractions over 7 weeks (5 days/week), with 70 Gy to seminal vesicles, 77 Gy to the prostate, 60–63.35 Gy to macroscopic nodes, and 52.5–56.35 Gy to elective pelvic nodes. Cohort B could receive a mildly hypofractionated schedule of 25 fractions over 6 weeks, delivering 68.75 Gy to the prostate, 57.5 Gy to macroscopic nodes, and 47.5 Gy to elective fields.

ADT included 3-monthly goserelin (10.8 mg) or equivalent, plus bicalutamide 50 mg daily for 4 weeks, starting up to 1 months before EBRT and continuing during and after EBRT for 2–3 years.

[177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto™, Novartis) was administered intravenously in week 2 of EBRT (and week 4 in Cohort B), with patients remaining at the nuclear medicine facility for ≥ 6 h post-injection per national radiation safety guidelines.

Outcome evaluations

The primary outcome of acute toxicity was scored by certified radiation oncologists at baseline, weekly during EBRT, and monthly for 3 months post-EBRT. Late toxicity was evaluated at 6 months. Adverse events were prospectively assessed using CTCAE v5.0, with focus on haematological and renal toxicity, xerostomia, dry eyes, fatigue and gastrointestinal symptoms, in addition to toxicities related to EBRT and ADT.

Image acquisition

Post-[177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 imaging involved total-body planar and pelvic SPECT/CT acquisitions at 4, 24 and 168 h after administration, performed on a Symbia Intevo Bold SPECT/CT scanners (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany). Single bed-position SPECTs were acquired using a Medium Energy Low Penetration (MELP) collimator at 64 projections per detector (continuous mode, 14 s time/projection, photopeak of 208 keV with 20% window, adjacent 16% dual-energy window for scatter correction). Acquisitions were reconstructed using OSEM reconstruction 6 iterations and 8 subsets (matrix 128 × 128) and a 5 mm smoothing Gaussian filter. Additionally, a low-dose CT was acquired (40 mAS, 130 kV) for anatomical correlation and attenuation correction (no PSF-corrections were applied). Total-body planar imaging was obtained at 15 cm/min; triple energy window (TEW) scatter correction was used with a 208 keV photopeak.

Cross-calibration of this workflow included evaluation of a NEMA IEC NM body phantom to acquire a conversion factor (CF, in cps/MBq) for SPECT/CTs. For planar imaging, a standard source was placed near the hip to determine the calibration factor for each patient. All data acquisition and processing was performed in concordance with MIRD Pamphlet No. 26 [16].

Dosimetry

Absorbed doses (mGy) for the prostate tumour, kidneys, parotid glands, and bone marrow were calculated using a combination of voxel-based and planar dosimetric methods. Since no consensus currently exists on combining absorbed dose estimates from radiopharmaceutical and external beam radiotherapy, accumulated absorbed doses were not calculated (Tran‑Gia 2025).

The primary tumour was segmented on the SPECT/CT made 24 h post-treatment, with a 20% threshold, and transferred towards the 168 h SPECT/CT using SPECT/SPECT alignment and visual inspection. Lymph node metastases were manually segmented on diagnostic CT or PSMA-PET/CT if their volume was > 2.5mL and they were PSMA-positive on the PET/CT. These VOIs were registered towards the low-dose CT of SPECT/CTs at 24 and 168 h. The absorbed dose delivered (in Gy or Gy/GBq) was calculated with voxel-based dosimetry in MIM SurePlan™ MRT version 7.3.6 (MIM Software, Beachwood, OH). Time-integrated activity curves (TACs) were determined assuming instantaneous uptake and mono-exponential clearance thereafter based on only two-time-points, 24 and 168 h scans. The 4 h SPECT/CT scan was omitted, since this introduced a severe underestimation of the absorbed dose estimates.

Dosimetry on total-body planar images was only considered for kidneys (left-right) and parotid glands (left-right), since small lymph node metastases and primary tumour(s) could not be accurately quantified. On PSMA-PET/CTs, the organ volume, the anterior-posterior thickness of these organs (T) and body at the height of the organ (x) were measured. On planar images, the kidneys, parotid glands and a standard source (only 24 h-scan) were segmented with a 40% threshold to determine the total counts in a region (RA and RP). The standard was also segmented on the 24 h SPECT/CT to align CFs (k). Background correction was included by tracing the outlines of the ROIs at 0.75 cm distance, for 1 cm wide. TACs were again estimated using a mono-exponential curve fit. Organ-level absorbed doses (AD) were calculated in IDAC-Dose 2.1 software [18], by using corresponding organ- and isotope-specific S-values (SM) and organ weight based on MIRD (mM) and PET volumes (mo) using the formula;

The bone marrow absorbed dose was derived from blood samples collected at 5, 15 and 30 min, 1, 2, 3 and 6 h, and 1, 3 and 7 days post-injection and measured in a gamma counter (WIZARD2, PerkinElmer Singapore Pte Ltd). Dosimetry of these blood-based measurements was performed following the 2022 EANM guideline [17], with a bi-exponential curve fit and S-values adapted from IDAC-Dose 2.1 [18].

Anti-tumour efficacy

Anti-tumour efficacy was evaluated by prostate specific antigen (PSA) response at baseline, 3 months and 6 months post-EBRT. Quality of life (QoL) was assessed using QLQ-C30 and prostate-specific QLQ-PR25 questionnaires at the same time points.

Pharmacokinetics

Pharmacokinetics were evaluated via blood samples taken before and at 5 min until 7 days after [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 administration. Total radioactivity was measured in whole blood and plasma. Pharmacokinetic parameters were derived using non-compartmental analysis (NCA) in R, based on actual sampling times. The linear-up/log-down trapezoidal method was used for AUC and AUMC calculations. Terminal elimination rate constants were estimated via linear regression over the last three time points. Parameters including AUC₀₋ₜ, AUC∞, Cmax, Tmax, t½, MRT, Cl, and Vd were calculated. The regression time ratio (rth) was used to assess the reliability of the terminal phase fit; values ≥ 3 were considered acceptable for robust half-life estimation. More details are available in the study protocol [11].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis followed the BOIN scheme, using descriptive statistics in tabular form due to the small sample size. QoL scores were assessed across the measured domains and compared with established clinical thresholds for the QLQ-C30 [19]. No thresholds were applied for the QLQ-PR25, as these are still under investigation [20]. Data were analysed using R (v4.2.1) and SPSS® (v30), and managed via ALEA.

Genitourinary adverse events were most frequently reported, followed by diarrhoea and hot flushes (Table 3). One patient had a traumatic subdural hematoma shortly after EBRT, which was considered not related to treatment. In the acute setting, all patients experienced toxicity: 3 (21%) had grade 1 only, 3 (21%) grade 2 only, and 8 (57%) both grade 1 and 2. Late toxicity was less frequent: 4 (29%) had none, 6 (43%) grade 1 only, 1 (7%) grade 2 only, 2 (14%) both grade 1 and 2, and 1 (7%) both grade 1 and 3. As a consequence of the limited toxicity, fewer patients were recruited than initially planned, reflecting the favourable safety profile and the efficient progression through the BOIN dose-escalation scheme.

Dosimetry

Absorbed dose calculations were performed for 12 out of 14 patients. The SPECT/CT scans of the two excluded patients had missing calibration factors, which meant that absorbed doses could not be quantified. In the highest dose levels, the median absorbed dose to the primary tumour due to the [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 was 0.71 Gy/GBq (IQR 0.5–2.9) for patients receiving 9 GBq (n = 5); 0.91 Gy/GBq (IQR 0.6–1.0) in the first cycle of 7.4 GBq (n = 5); and 0.47 Gy/GBq (IQR 0.3–0.5) in the second cycle of 7.4 GBq (n = 5). No lymph node metastases were included in dosimetry due to their small sizes (< 2.5 mL). The kidneys and parotid glands received a median absorbed dose of 1.09 Gy/GBq (IQR 1.0–1.1) and 0.48 (IQR 0.4–0.7). Detailed absorbed dose results are found in Tables 4 and 5. Median absorbed dose to the bone marrow was 0.014 Gy/GBq (IQR 0.011–0.016).

Discussion

In this phase-1 trial, the tolerability and efficacy of adding concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 to standard EBRT and ADT was explored in patients treated with curative intent for N1M0 PCa. The highest dose levels of either one cycle 9 GBq or two cycles of 7.4 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 did not give any CTCAE v5.0 grade 3 toxicity or higher and can therefore both be considered safe to administer concurrently with EBRT and ADT. Moreover, we could confirm that this approach increases radiation dose delivered to the tumour.

PSMA-targeted therapy has emerged as a highly effective approach in PCa treatment. [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 monotherapy in advanced castration-resistant PCa is well tolerated up to six cycles (6 × 7.4 GBq), with only mild, temporary toxicity to salivary glands, kidneys and bone marrow [21]. The TheraP and VISION trials further confirmed its clinical benefit, demonstrating improved PSA responses, prolonged survival and fewer severe adverse events compared with standard treatments [9, 10, 22].

Combination radioligand therapy and EBRT have shown favourable safety in other tumour types. Sequential [177Lu]Lu-Octreotate (7.4 ± 0.3 GBq) and EBRT (median 53.0 Gy, 26 fractions) for inoperable meningiomas, and 4-L-[131I]iodo-phenylalanine ((131)IPA) (2–7 GBq) plus EBRT (6 × 5 Gy) for glioblastoma, were both well tolerated [23, 24]. Dietrich et al. reviewed preclinical and phase-1 studies on radiolabeled molecules plus EBRT, noting promising preclinical but limited clinical data [25].

Building on these observations, earlier and concurrent use of [¹⁷⁷Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 with EBRT in PCa has been explored. In six patients with oligometastatic PCa, two cycles 7.5 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 followed by SBRT (27 Gy/3 fx) to all PSMA-avid lesions was feasible [26]. Retrospective data from 92 patients receiving EBRT during or shortly after PSMA-targeted radionuclide therapy (5.9–7.4 GBq) showed no increased toxicity compared with radionuclide therapy alone, although these were palliative treatments to non-prostatic sites [27]. A translational study suggested sequential EBRT and PSMA-RLT may improve tumour control, likely via radiation-induced PSMA upregulation, slowing tumour growth and prolonging survival in mice [28]. In our study, [¹⁷⁷Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 was administered within hours after an EBRT fraction, coinciding with the transient period of PSMA upregulation observed preclinically. This timing aimed to maximise tumour uptake, although absorbed doses remained lower than expected, likely due to concurrent ADT, early EBRT effects and lower baseline PSMA expression in this treatment-naïve cohort. Our current prospective dose-escalation study in treatment-naïve patients cT2-4N1 confirms the safety of concurrent therapy, supporting further evaluation.

Compared with previous studies, we observed a lower than anticipated median absorbed tumour dose (0.47–0.91 Gy/GBq in the highest dose levels). The Lutectomy trial, administering one or two cycles of 5 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 six weeks before radical prostatectomy, reported a much higher tumour dose of 7.1 Gy/GBq to the primary tumour after one cycle [29]. Similarly, Grkovski et al., found higher doses in patients with oligometastatic castration-sensitive disease receiving two cycles of 7.4 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 at 44-day interval (3.7 Gy/GBq for the first cycle), followed by SBRT one week later [26]. Discrepancies between these reported absorbed doses are probably multi-factorial and can add up. Our study applied two time-point imaging to estimate TACs, which can underestimate organ or lesion absorbed doses by up to 10% compared to multi-time point dosimetry [30, 31]. Also, no PSF or recovery correction was applied, so SPECT images will not accurately represent steep dose gradients and heterogenic accumulations, resulting in an underestimations of activity quantification, which is most profound for smaller lesions. Additionally, our patients were at an earlier disease stage with relatively low tumour volume and potentially lower PSMA expression compared to the previously mentioned studies in metastatic populations. Our patients received concurrent ADT which can have a negative effect on PSMA-expression, and could have had early therapeutic effect of EBRT, again resulting in lower tumour absorbed dose. Moreover, early EBRT may alter tumour perfusion potentially influencing radioligand delivery and contribution to a lowered absorbed doses.

Another major difference between these studies and ours, is that they administered [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 prior to subsequent treatments, whereas our patients started 1–2 weeks after EBRT initiation. This timing was based on the rationale that tumour susceptibility for radionuclide therapy would be improved by increasing perfusion, while tumour volume reduction from EBRT remained limited. Nevertheless, our results indicate that this approach led to a lower absorbed tumour dose compared with previous studies, suggesting that earlier [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 administration could further maximise uptake and efficacy.

Furthermore, plasma clearance of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 was lower in all patients in our cohort compared with the 2.5 L/h reported in the EMA Pluvicto assessment [32]. We hypothesise that this is partly attributed to shorter tumour retention caused by the EBRT. In practice, this reduced retention time may reflect early tumour response or disruption of tumour integrity, potentially leading to faster tracer washout. This is further supported by the high AUC and Cmax observed in cycle 2 of cohort B versus cycle 1.

Although combining absorbed doses from EBRT and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 may seem appealing, traditional EBRT dosimetry models (e.g. EQD2) do not directly apply to radiopharmaceutical therapy due to low dose rates, different radiation delivery mechanisms, heterogeneous distributions, and distinct biological response [33]. Metrics such as Biological Effective Dose (BED), which accounts for prolonged dose delivery, and (organ) Equivalent Uniform Dose (EUD), which compensates for dose heterogeneities, have been explored previously. However, their implementation remains challenging due to limited 177Lu-imaging resolution, heterogeneities in pharmacokinetic behaviour, and the absence of standardized dosimetric workflows. Given the exploratory nature of these analyses, the limited sample size, and the lack of standardised approaches for combining doses, we focused in index lesion dosimetry. Dosimetry should therefore be considered a secondary, exploratory outcome, with the primary focus of this phase 1 study on safety and tolerability.

Given the largely distinct toxicity profiles of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617, EBRT and ADT, a marked increased local toxicity from the combined treatment was not necessarily anticipated [25]. In this study, the observed toxicity profile was overall mild and consistent with known effects of EBRT and ADT. However, a potential additive or radiosensitising effect of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 on local tissues cannot be fully excluded. Adverse events, such as the reported grade 3 erectile dysfunction, were considered in line with known toxicities of ADT, although the contribution of combined treatment cannot be definitively disentangled in this setting. The optimal timing of ADT relative to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 and EBRT remains debated. ADT is usually started several weeks before EBRT for early disease control and testosterone suppression, but early biological responses are complex [34]. Initial androgen receptor upregulation may enhance PSMA expression and tracer uptake, while prolonged exposure can reduce uptake due to tumour shrinkage [35, 36]. Starting ADT shortly before EBRT may maximise receptor expression while limiting early tumour response.

QoL assessments showed some variability, with occasional functional impairments, likely reflecting post-treatment fatigue or manageable side effect rather than concerning acute toxicity. Overall, no unexpected or alarming changes in QoL were observed, supporting the continued evaluation of this combination treatment.

While this phase 1 study focused on treatment-naïve N1M0 patients, a well-defined but relatively narrow population, the encouraging safety and dosimetry results may inform future studies in broader patient groups. The Bullseye study for example, has shown that [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 in patients with hormone-sensitive oligometastatic PCa, delays disease progression compared to the standard of care, deferred ADT; with mild side effects and good quality of life [37]. In our planned phase 2 study, we therefore aim to expand eligibility to also include patients with high-risk localised PCa.

This work is subject to several limitations. The small sample size, short 6–months follow-up and exploratory nature of this phase 1 study limit the generalisability and the ability to detect less common toxicities or assess treatment efficacy, especially given concurrent ADT affecting PSA measurements. While severe toxicities (≥ grade 3) and xerostomia were actively monitored, mild adverse events may have been underreported. Additionally, absorbed doses from EBRT and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 were not combined into one calculated absorbed dose, due to differences in radiation type and tumour response. Although (EQD2) can be calculated using the α/β factor, it may not fully reflect the total biological effect due to differences in dose distribution and tumour microenvironment between modalities. Finally, the use of different PSMA PET tracers prior to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 may have introduced heterogeneity, as bladder activity could potentially obscure detection of some pelvic lymph nodes.

This phase 1 study adding concurrent [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 to standard of care EBRT and ADT provides the required safety, dosimetry, pharmacokinetics and tolerability data to adequately design a subsequent trial to investigate the efficacy of added [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 at the applied dose levels. In such a phase 2 study, a wider patient population could be considered including e.g. high risk locally advanced PCa without nodal involvement, with disease-free survival or recurrence rate as the primary outcome measure.

Conclusion

Curative-intent treatment with standard of care EBRT and ADT with the addition of concurrent 1 cycle of 9 GBq or 2 cycles of 7.4 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 is safe and well tolerated in treatment-naïve patients with N1M0 PCa. A subsequent phase 2 study is needed to evaluate efficacy of both regimens and to establish potential long term clinical benefit.

High‐Efficiency Generation of Vectorial Terahertz Beams via Surface‐Wave‐Excited Dielectric Metasurfaces
Authors: Yu He, Yuanxi Liu, Yao Yao, Qingnan Cai, Dumeng Zhang, Xiangyu Jin, Shulin Sun, Zhuo Wang, Lei Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTGenerating pre‐designed vectorial beams via on‐chip devices can yield many fascinating applications, but traditional diffraction‐based devices are bulky and inefficient. While plasmonic metasurfaces exhibit ultra‐compact sizes and deep‐subwavelength resolutions, their working efficiencies are limited by metallic losses. Here, we propose a generic design for highly efficient, ultra‐compact dielectric metasurfaces that generate tailored vectorial beams under surface‐wave (SW) excitations, experimentally validated in the terahertz (THz) regime. We first experimentally characterize the basic dielectric meta‐atom, and then demonstrate a benchmark meta‐device that, under the SW excitation, can generate a circularly polarized focused beam. We next realize two dielectric meta‐devices, each consisting of two subsets of meta‐atoms responsible for generating two pre‐designed circularly polarized beams with opposite helicity, with their interference forming the target vectorial beams. Our experiments show that these two meta‐devices can generate an azimuthally polarized Bessel beam and a vectorial multifocal holographic image, respectively. We experimentally show that the present dielectric meta‐system exhibits significant improvement in the relevant frequency band compared to its metallic counterpart, and numerically demonstrate that it exhibits an absolute working efficiency as high as 92%. Our study paves the way to realize many on‐chip applications with high efficiencies, such as biological sensing, displays, image multiplexing, and beyond.Keywords: dielectric, high‐efficiency, multi‐pixel metasurface, surface waves, terahertz, vector beam

Introduction

On‐chip photonic devices capable of efficiently generating tailored far‐field vectorial beams under SW excitations are highly desirable for a wide range of applications in integrated photonics [1, 2, 3]. Compared to scalar beams exhibiting uniform polarization distributions [4], vectorial beams possess extraordinary advantages in applications such as super‐resolution imaging [5, 6, 7], optical manipulation [8, 9], and sensing [10], thanks to their purposely designed polarization distributions. Traditional approaches to generate vectorial beams require experimental setups involving optical modulators [11, 12] and waveplates [13], which are typically bulky, complex, and under propagating‐wave (PW) excitations, being unfavorable for on‐chip applications. Several on‐chip grating‐based decouplers have been proposed, which can convert SWs into far‐field beams with tailored wavefronts. However, lacking capabilities to manipulate local polarization of light waves [14, 15, 16] in such a Bragg‐scattering mechanism [17], these devices can only generate scalar beams suffering from multimode diffractions. These inherent limitations restrict the applications of such devices in high‐performance on‐chip photonic systems.

Metasurfaces (MSs), ultrathin metamaterials composed of planar subwavelength microstructures with tailored scattering properties, provide an efficient platform to manipulate the wavefronts of light [18, 19, 20, 21]. Many remarkable wave‐manipulation effects have been demonstrated with metasurfaces exhibiting certain phase profiles, including beam deflections [22, 23], PW‐to‐SW conversions [24, 25, 26, 27], meta‐holography [28, 29, 30], and vortex and vector beams [31, 32, 33]. Recently, vectorial optical fields were generated by carefully designed metasurfaces that can manipulate both polarization states and phases of locally scattered light waves. While these meta‐devices are ultra‐compact and immune to higher‐order diffractions, they still rely on PW excitations, which require large working spaces being unfavorable for optical integrations. More recently, SW‐excited metasurfaces that can generate pre‐designed far‐field light beams have attracted significant attention [34, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41]. Employing the Pancharatnam‐Berry (PB) mechanism to generate the desired phase profiles with phases carried by incident SWs considered, various metasurface‐waveguide‐integrated systems have been realized which can generate both scalar [42] and vectorial light beams [43] in pre‐designed manners. However, such systems, where PB meta‐atoms are directly placed on one side of dielectric waveguides without performing optimizations as a whole, inevitably suffer from the low efficiency issue caused by the following two reasons. First, scattering by a single PB meta‐atom is extremely weak since those meta‐atoms are purposely designed to only slightly perturb the waveguide modes; second, waves can also radiate outside of the waveguide from another side without PB meta‐atoms decorated due to multiple scatterings, forming undesired beams with wasted energy [44, 45, 46, 47]. To address this issue, several meta‐devices in a metal‐insulator‐metal (MIM) configuration were recently proposed. With MIM meta‐atoms designed as a whole and with a metal plate added in the system to block the back‐scattering mode, these meta‐devices exhibit better performance through carefully balancing the SW decoupling rates and wavefront flatness. However, working efficiencies of these metal‐based devices are still bounded by intrinsic Ohmic losses [40, 48], which is more serious in high‐frequency domains where metallic losses are more significant.

In this work, we propose a general approach to design dielectric metasurfaces that can generate pre‐designed vectorial beams under SW excitations (see Figure 1), and experimentally verify the concept in the THz regime. After characterizing the scattering property of the building‐block meta‐atom adopted in this work, we experimentally demonstrate two dielectric meta‐devices that can generate pre‐determined circularly polarized beams under surface‐wave excitations at 0.35 THz. We next construct two meta‐devices by merging two subsets of meta‐atoms, which are designed to generate two spin‐polarized beams with opposite helicity, with their interference forming the target vectorial beams. Our experiments show that these two meta‐devices can generate an azimuthally polarized THz Bessel beam and a vectorial holographic image containing eight focal points exhibiting distinct polarizations, respectively, under surface‐wave excitations at 0.35 THz. Finally, our simulations show that the designed dielectric meta‐devices can achieve a working efficiency as high as 92%. Experimental results further confirm their superior performance, with a significant efficiency enhancement over plasmonic counterparts and previous realizations. Our device adopts a unified architecture where the metasurface takes the same configuration with the SW‐eigen system, enabling strong surface‐wave interactions with the metasurface and eliminating the undesired backward scatterings, thus yielding a higher working efficiency than conventional waveguide‐based systems. Compared with plasmonic metasurfaces, our system avoids metal‐induced losses, being highly favorable for on‐chip applications in integrated optics and information processing.

General Principle and Design Method

We begin by discussing how to design an SW‐excited metasurface for generating a pre‐designed circularly polarized beam in the far field. With the phase profile φσMS(r) known, we employ the PB mechanism to design our metasurface. Given an arbitrary meta‐atom exhibiting a specific cross‐polarization conversion ratio (PCR) for incident light, we arrange a set of such meta‐atoms with different orientation angles in a square lattice to form our metasurface. However, since the incident SW beam exhibits a linear polarization that contains two circularly polarized components, we find that waves scattered by such a metasurface include three modes. The first one is the desired circularly polarized beam generated by the phase distribution. φσMS(r), possessing a tangential wavevector kσMS. The second one is also a circularly polarized wave but corresponding to the phase distribution −φσMS(r) and thus carries a tangential wavevector −kσMS, generated by the circularly polarized component with spin − σ inside the injected SW. Finally, as long as the meta‐atom does not exhibit a 100% PCR, a normal mode always exists, with a tangential wavevector of zero. Fortunately, only the target mode possesses a tangential wavevector (k∥target=kMS−kSW) smaller than k0 and thus can be decoupled to the far field. Both the undesired anomalous mode and the normal mode have wavevectors exceeding k0, rendering them evanescent. These two modes remain trapped on the metasurface and eventually dissipate their energies back to the incident surface wave through multiple scattering effects. Such a mechanism ensures that only a single spin‐polarized beam can efficiently radiate out of the metasurface to form the desired far‐field beam (see Section S1).

Now the approach to generate a generic vectorial beam is clear. We need to first design two PB metasurfaces which, under the same SW excitation, can generate an LCP beam and an RCP with field distributions given in Equation (3), respectively. Importantly, in addition to generating the desired field‐amplitude distributions |E ±( r )| on the target plane, these two beams should also exhibit a well‐defined phase correlation: ϕ+(r)−ϕ−(r)=Δϕtar(r), which imposes a constraint as we retrieve two phase distributions φ±tar(r) of two planar sources. Therefore, we can determine the meta‐atom orientation profiles θ±( r ) from the retrieved φ±tar(r) distributions, and then interleave two sets of meta‐atoms, arranged in θ+( r ) and θ−( r ) distributions, respectively, into one meta‐device which can generate the target vectorial beam under the SW excitation.

Designs of Meta‐Atom and Artificial SW Eigen‐System

We now design a suitable meta‐atom to construct our metasurfaces. To completely eliminate the far‐field beam generation from the undesired side of our meta‐devices, we consider a totally reflective meta‐atom exhibiting mirror reflection symmetry, with optical properties described by a reflection Jones matrix: R=[ruu00rvv]. Here u and v denote two principal axes of the meta‐atom, ruu=|ruu|eiΦuuandrvv=|rvv|eiΦvv. Neglecting material dissipations, we get |ruu | = |rvv | = 1 and ruu=eiΦuu and rvv=eiΦvv. It is easy to derive that PCR = | (ruu − rvv )/2|2 = |e iΔΦ − 1| /4, which means we can modulate PCR by controlling the phase difference ΔΦ = Φ uu − Φ vv .

We note that a meta‐atom for an SW‐excited meta‐device should exhibit an optimal PCR to balance performance and efficiency [40]. As being excited by an SW, meta‐atoms can decouple SWs to the far field and thus the field strength of our source SW decays along propagation. The higher the PCR of our meta‐atom, the faster the source SW dissipates along propagation, making the wavefront of the generated beam more non‐flat. Meanwhile, if the PCR is too small, we need a very large metasurface to decouple all energy carried by the source SW outside of the metasurface. Therefore, we choose an optimized PCR = 35% to design our meta‐atom, in order to balance the wavefront flatness and the working efficiency for a meta‐device with a fixed size.

The inset to Figure 2c depicts the geometry of the dielectric meta‐atom that we designed, which consists of a silicon (ε r = 11.9) micropillar with a rectangular cross‐section put on a continuous 60 µm‐thick quartz layer (ε r = 3.9) with a gold (σAu = 5 × 107 S/m) substrate underneath. Based on extensive finite‐difference time‐domain (FDTD) simulations, we fix the optimized structural parameters of our meta‐atom (see caption of Figure 2), and fabricate a sample consisting of an array of designed meta‐atoms using ultraviolet lithography and deep silicon etching with the gold substrate deposited via magnetron sputtering. A scanning electron microscope (SEM) image of the fabricated sample is shown in Figure 2c. Since the gold film completely blocks the transmission, we only need to measure the reflection spectra of the sample using a THz time‐domain spectroscopy. Figure 2a depicts the measured spectra of |ruu |,|rvv | and ΔΦ. Due to instrumental constraints, our measurements are performed at a reflection angle of 23°. We find that |ruu |, |rvv | ≈ 1 within the frequency domain of interest, caused by low intrinsic loss of the constitutional material. Meanwhile, ΔΦ gradually changes as frequency increases from 0.25 to 0.45 THz. From the measured reflection spectra, we retrieve the strengths of the normal mode (Rn = |ruu + rvv |2 /4) and the anomalous mode (Ra = |ruu − rvv |2 /4) generated by the PB meta‐atom [23], and show the results in Figure 2b. Experimental results are in good agreement with numerical simulations.

Finally, we design an artificial system that can support customized SWs as eigenmodes, in order to efficiently inject the SW beam into the metasurface as a source. Different from previous realizations in which meta‐atoms are weakly coupled with the waveguide [36, 44, 45], here in our scheme the eigen‐SW system should also be optimized, which provides an additional possibility to further enhance the overall working efficiency. To facilitate integrating two systems in fabrication, we choose the artificial system to comprise a silicon pillar array placed on the same substrate as that of the metasurface. Also, to minimize scattering losses at the boundary between two systems, we specifically design the upper Si resonator so that the artificial system supports eigen SWs with similar wavevectors as those of a benchmark metasurface consisting of a periodic array of unrotated meta‐atoms. As shown in Figure 2d, the computed dispersion relation of SWs supported by the artificial system matches well with that of the benchmark metasurface within 0.3–0.4 THz, both exhibiting. k SW = 1.25 k 0 at the working frequency 0.35 THz. From the insets to Figure 2d, we find that two resonators exhibit similar field distributions [49] at 0.35 THz, indicating that the two systems exhibit similar impedance for SWs, which can help suppress wave scatterings at the boundary.

The Working Efficiency of Our Dielectric Meta‐Device: A Benchmark Study

To highlight the key advantages of the metasurfaces we developed, we performed a comparative analysis of their performance and efficiency through both experiments and simulations, comparing them with plasmonic counterparts of similar size and functionality. We focused on the simplest type of metasurface, as shown in Figure 6a, which generates a LCP beam traveling along. θr = 0° under excitation by a 0.35 THz SW. To measure the total power carried by the generated beams, we designed independent couplers to couple identical amounts of SW energy, ensuring they were excited by the same source. This allowed us to evaluate the performance of the two metasurfaces under SW excitation. We also performed numerical simulations to calculate the absolute efficiency of both structures, using the incident SW energy as the reference, and derived the corresponding efficiency values. We employ the dielectric meta‐atom with a structure similar to that designed in Figure 2 but with a PCR of 75% to construct our benchmark meta‐device, again integrated with an artificial eigen‐SW plate for injecting the source SW. To make a fair comparison, we also design and fabricate a plasmonic meta‐device implementing the phase profile Equation (6) based on a plasmonic meta‐atom with a similar PCR and an eigen‐system exhibiting a similar SW wavevector at 0.35 THz (see Section S6). Insets of Figure 6b,c show the SEM images of the fabricated dielectric and plasmonic samples, respectively. To ensure that each meta‐device is excited by an SW beam with equal power, we individually optimized the meta‐coupler used to convert the incident PW to an SW in each case.

In principle, determining the relative efficiency requires quantifying the total system losses, which consist of three components: the coupling loss from converting the THz pulse into an SW, the propagation loss as the SW travels, and the scattering loss when the SW radiates to the far field. Nevertheless, directly measuring these three losses is challenging. Therefore, we experimentally evaluated the overall performance of the full system. To ensure both meta‐devices were excited by the same source, we designed independent couplers that couple an identical amount of SW energy into each device. The structural schematic of the complete system is provided in Figure 6a. We now experimentally characterize the performance of two meta‐devices, as they are excited by two SW beams at 0.35 THz generated by two meta‐couplers placed at the right‐hand side of the samples. Using the near‐field scanning technology, we measured the scattered‐field distributions on the x—z planes across the center y = 0 mm of two metasurfaces, with the measured Re(Ex ) patterns shown in Figure 6b,c, respectively. The two measured patterns clearly show that a beam propagating at 0° is generated in both cases, consistent with theoretical predictions. To quantitatively compare the performances of the two meta‐devices, we analyze their scattered‐field distributions across different frequencies when excited by a THz beam at the same input power (see Figure 6a). For each measured Ex field pattern, a Fourier transform is performed to obtain the angular spectrum of the scattered field. This allows us to retrieve the complex amplitudes of the modes propagating along different directions. The intensity of the wave radiated along the given direction (here, the normal direction) is then calculated by peaking up the Fourier component corresponding to that wavevector. For a fair and quantitative comparison, we scaled all experimentally retrieved values, for both the metal and dielectric cases with different frequencies, by the maximum value obtained among these two groups. Figure 6d compares the measured field intensities of scattered waves travelling along the target direction, retrieved from experimentally measured data for metal and dielectric systems, under identical excitation conditions. Overall, our dielectric meta‐system (including the meta‐device and the associated meta‐coupler for converting PW into SW) exhibits significantly higher efficiency than its metallic counterpart, within the working band of 0.35–0.4 THz. We also perform numerical simulations to compare the performances of two meta‐systems, and the obtained results (see Section S5) exhibit similar behaviors with experimental results.

Although the above comparisons have clearly demonstrated the superior performance of our dielectric meta‐system, direct characterization of the absolute efficiency of the meta‐device alone (with the PW‐SW meta‐coupler excluded) is challenging as it involves quantitative characterization of the power carried by the injecting SW beam. Consequently, we turn to employ numerical simulations to obtain these values. Our simulation results show that the dielectric metasurface achieves an absolute efficiency of 92% at the operational frequency of 0.35 THz. The 92% efficiency of our device is numerically evaluated based on the SW power injected into the metasurface without considering the energy losses in the SW excitation process. In our experiments, as the total incident SW power is difficult to measure, we adopt the following indirect scheme to estimate the working performance. Specifically, we set the incident SW power identical in two cases by connecting two meta‐devices with the same SW excitation source, and compare the measured strengths of decoupled signals in two cases. This performance is notably superior to the 68% efficiency of its metallic counterpart (see Section S6). The improvement factor (i.e., 92/68) is slightly lower than that obtained in Figure 6d, which is understandable since the latter also involves PW‐SW coupling and SW propagating processes where additional losses arise in the metallic system. Nevertheless, collecting both experimental and numerical results, we draw the conclusion that the dielectric meta‐device is much more efficient than its metallic counterpart, due to significantly reduced material losses.

Conclusions

In summary, we have proposed a general scheme for designing SW‐excited dielectric metasurfaces that can generate complex vectorial beams in free space with very high efficiencies, and experimentally verify the concept in the THz regime. The proposed metasurface consists of two sets of PB meta‐atoms exhibiting different orientation‐angle distributions, which can separately generate an LCP beam and an RCP beam with pre‐determined wavefronts under SW excitations, with their interference forming the target vectorial field. After characterizing the optical properties of the basic meta‐atom and the eigen‐SW system, we first experimentally demonstrate a benchmark meta‐device to realize a circularly polarized THz beam focused to a point in free space. We next design and fabricate two metasurfaces and experimentally demonstrate that they can generate two vectorial fields under SW excitations, which are an azimuthally polarized Bessel beam and a vectorial holographic pattern containing multiple focal points exhibiting pre‐designed polarizations, respectively. Compared to previous realizations, our scheme blocks the transmission channel, optimizes the meta‐atom and waveguide as a whole, and adopts dielectric resonators to further suppress material losses. Experimental comparison shows that our system exhibits a total efficiency ∼1.6 times higher than its metallic counterpart, with an absolute working efficiency as high as 92% numerically demonstrated. Our study paves the road to realize many high‐performance on‐chip applications, such as biological sensing, optical displays, communications, image multiplexing, and so on.

Methods

Our samples were first fabricated based on theoretical design, using standard photolithography and metallization processes to create an SW excitation coupler on both the upper and lower surfaces of a 60 µm‐thick quartz substrate. Subsequently, the quartz substrate was bonded, in sequence, to a 60 µm‐thick SiO2 layer and a 100 µm high‐resistance Si wafer using ultraviolet (UV) curable adhesive (NOA81). Then, photolithography (Karl Suss MA6 mask aligner) and deep reactive ion etching (Bosch process) were employed on the Si wafer to form the desired patterns.

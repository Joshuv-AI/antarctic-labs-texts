Project Rationale

Readmission rates after heart failure hospitalizations have been intensively investigated for many years1, 2, 3, 4, 5 but have remained a persistent problem. Various approaches, such as scheduling outpatient visits within 3 to 5 days, chronic care management with specialized nurses, and implantable sensors to monitor pulmonary artery pressure, have been implemented, all with some measure of success. However, most efforts are limited because the patient is passive while the health care team retains the responsibility for detecting the “check-engine light.” Moreover, because these interventions depend on extensive staff involvement, they are costly and difficult to scale. Finally, there is a large disconnect, as they rely on synchronous phone communication and the well-known reluctance of patients to initiate a call to the health care team.Take-Home Messages•A pilot program with daily digital outreach combined with a sweepstakes rewards program can effectively engage and activate patients.•All-cause readmissions within 30 days after a heart failure hospitalization were dramatically reduced, likely owing to greater adherence to healthy behaviors and the ability to automatically connect patients to clinical staff without depending on patient-initiated phone calls.•Digital outreach has the additional advantages of being cost-effective and scalable.

We sought to determine if connecting the patient to a proprietary asynchronous digital outreach program with regular texts and emails, combined with a sweepstakes rewards program, could activate patients to become more engaged and involved in their health care. We hypothesized that patients who were active and engaged participants would have enhanced adherence to healthy behaviors, reduced dependency on having to call the clinic, and thereby experience a lower rate of 30-day readmissions.

Project Description

This pilot project represented a collaboration between CareCognitics Inc, a digital health company that developed the proprietary digital outreach program, and Cardiac Solutions, a progressive cardiology practice in Arizona.

Patients who were discharged with an International Classification of Diseases code for heart failure from hospitals staffed by Cardiac Solutions were eligible to enroll. Eligible patients were sent a text or email and could self-enroll simply by scanning a QR (quick response) code or by responding to the email. Patients indicated their preferred channel of communication (text or email) and if they wanted duplicate messages sent to a family member. All messages were designed to be sent by the clinical team at Cardiac Solutions; patients were asked to add the contact information so that they could be assured that this was legitimate messaging.

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)–compliant communications included short educational videos, symptom surveys, and reminders for heart failure monitoring (eg, daily weights) that were sent to patients almost daily over 30 days after discharge. The program did not require downloading an app or accessing a patient portal, which eliminated barriers to patient participation. It tracked the number of communications that were opened and surveys that were completed. The program was completely automated so that no staff involvement was required.

To enhance participation in the program, a sweepstakes rewards program was also implemented. Rather than a nominal reward, patients were given tickets for each video watched and symptom survey completed. The larger the number of tickets, the higher the chances of winning in quarterly sweepstakes drawings. Prize amounts were $100 (10 winners), $50 (20 winners), and $25 (40 winners). All applicable laws and regulations concerning patient compensation were followed. Cardiac Solutions, the health care provider for the patients, funded the sweepstakes prizes.

One question on a symptom survey was especially important: “Are you concerned your current health may cause you to visit an emergency room?” If the patient clicked “yes,” a follow-up question asked if they would like to be connected to clinical staff. If there was a second “yes,” the system automatically connected the patient to clinical staff or to the answering service if after hours. Thus, staff were only required when there was an urgent need to assess a possible important clinical change and determine whether escalation was necessary.

All-cause readmissions were tracked for 30 days after discharge.

Project Outcomes

Enrollment continued for 6 months and included 375 patients with a mean age of 76 years. Of note, nearly 10% of patients were >90 years of age. Most of the patients (62%) were male. Patients had either reduced ejection fraction (61%) or preserved ejection fraction (39%). Comorbidities included atrial fibrillation (46%), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (26%), renal insufficiency (31%), and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (45%). Background medical therapy included beta-blockers (72%), angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitor/angiotensin receptor blocker/angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor (48%), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (11%), and sodium glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (18%).

Of the 375 patients, 58 patients were readmitted, for an overall readmission rate of 15.5%. The second bar shows the overall readmission rate within the study of 15.5%, indicating that 58 of the 375 study patients were readmitted within 30 days, representing a 35% reduction from the historical average. The third bar shows that the “low to moderately engaged” study patients (<50% of texts/emails read) had a readmission rate of 21.6%. In contrast, the fourth bar shows that the readmission rate among the “highly engaged” study patients (>50% of text/emails read) was only 7.7%, representing a 68% decrease compared with the historical average, suggesting that level of engagement in the program was a driver of readmission.

More importantly, among the patients who were considered “highly engaged” because they responded to ≥50% of texts/emails, the readmission rate was only 7.7%, which represents a dramatic 68% decrease from the 24% national historical average (Figure 1). In contrast, among the patients who were “low to moderately engaged” (responded to <50% of texts/emails), the readmission rate was 21.6%, comparable to the national historical average and nearly identical to the 30-day readmission rate of 21.3% that Cardiac Solutions reported in 2023. These data suggest that level of activation was a strong predictor of readmission.

Overall, 56 patients clicked “yes” to both the question regarding the possible need to visit an emergency room and the request to be connected to clinical staff. Of these 56 patients, 16 were readmitted within 30 days, consistent with the severity of heart failure. However, 38 patients avoided readmission, likely owing to the contact and timely interventions from clinical staff.

Patient satisfaction was excellent, with 87.2% reporting that they were extremely or moderately satisfied with the program. Some verbatim responses included “It feels like someone is always watching me,” “It feels like someone cares about me feeling better,” and “I feel like we are being more proactive about health.” Patients especially liked the feature that staff were contacting them, as opposed to having to call the office.

Discussion

This pilot project has demonstrated that an innovative digital outreach program in conjunction with sweepstakes rewards can effectively engage patients with heart failure and reduce the rate of 30-day all-cause readmissions. Patients reported great satisfaction with being monitored and with interventions that kept them out of the hospital. Payers benefit from the reduction in readmissions, each generally costing between $15,000 and $20,000. Providers greatly appreciated that they were only called upon when their expertise was needed.

There are multiple reasons why patients are reluctant to call office staff for early warning signs, ranging from denial to hoping that the symptom may be transient. However, the ability to flag a concern in a digital format, which automatically triggers an outbound call to the patient, may reduce the barriers to timely communications.

There were several limitations to this study. The enrolled patients were a convenience sample who had access to smartphones and/or email, and they were enrolled in a single health system in a single geographic region, and therefore do not represent the totality of patients with heart failure. There was no randomization, so the enrollment process may have selected patients who were destined for favorable outcomes. Nonetheless, the fact that readmission rates in the low to moderately engaged group were similar to the historical average suggests that program participation was key to reducing readmissions.

Although this program only enrolled patients with heart failure, it is important to note that the program could be applied to other medical conditions that are associated with high rates of readmissions and would only require modifications in the educational content. Further, the process is entirely automated, so there are limited obstacles to greater scalability.

Conclusions

This pilot project has suggested that digital outreach combined with a sweepstakes rewards program can effectively engage and activate patients, promote greater adherence to healthy behaviors, and reduce all-cause readmissions within 30 days after a heart failure hospitalization.

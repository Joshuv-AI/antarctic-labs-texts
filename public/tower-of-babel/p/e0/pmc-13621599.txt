Multisequence MRI and clinical data-based deep learning radiomics model for predicting adjacent segment degeneration post-lumbar fusion: a retrospective multicenter study
Authors: Congying Zou, Yue Li, Baodong Wang, Qi Fei, Hongxing Song, Shuo Yuan, Lei Zang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile adjacent segment degeneration (ASDeg) is a major complication following lumbar fusion, objective tools for preoperative risk prediction remain lacking. This study developed a model integrating clinical data, deep learning, and radiomic features from multisequence magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to predict high-risk patients for ASDeg after lumbar fusion.MethodsThis study included 305 patients who underwent lumbar MRI before surgery. The patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 192) and an internal validation cohort (n = 83). An external test cohort (n = 30) was recruited from two additional centers. The Vision Transformer 3D model architecture was used for model development. Deep learning-based and handcrafted radiomic features were extracted from multisequence MR images of patients. A logistic regression model incorporating the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator was established, and both radiomic feature and deep learning radiomics (DLR) feature classification models were developed. ASDeg was defined as degeneration observed during radiological follow-up > 6 months after lumbar surgery and served as the reference standard. The performance of 14 machine learning classification models in predicting high-risk patients for ASDeg was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. A combined model was developed by integrating clinical baseline variables.ResultsAfter feature selection, 17 handcrafted radiomic features, 12 DLR features, and three clinical features were ultimately selected. The most effective machine learning algorithm for the radiomic feature model was the linear support vector machine, and AdaBoost was the best performing algorithm for the DLR feature model. By combining these features with clinical features, a combined model was developed, with the gradient boosting machine being the most effective machine learning algorithm. The AUCs for training, internal validation, and external test cohorts were 0.959, 0.818, and 0.895, respectively. Compared to the combined predictions of two spinal surgeons, the combined model also demonstrated superior performance. Calibration curves showed better calibration in the validation set. Decision curve analysis demonstrated greater net benefit for patients.ConclusionsThe combined model effectively identified patients at high risk of ASDeg following lumbar fusion based on clinical characteristics and MRI, which may help reduce revision surgeries and alleviate the burden on the public healthcare system.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02468-z.

Introduction

Lumbar degenerative disease is a major cause of low back pain and limited mobility. The clinical symptoms of this condition include progressive worsening of lower back pain and neurological impairment in the lower limb, which often significantly affect patients’ daily lives. When conservative treatment fails, lumbar fusion surgery has become a standard treatment option. Adjacent segment degeneration (ASDeg) is defined as radiographic degenerative changes in the segments adjacent to a previous spinal fusion surgery [1]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that lumbar fusion may accelerate the development of ASDeg [2–4]. ASDeg is a progressive disease that is often asymptomatic in its early stages [5]. When ASDeg progresses to the symptomatic stage, patients may require revision surgery or additional intervention. This not only significantly increases the burden on the healthcare system but also greatly impacts patients’ labor force participation and productivity. Therefore, accurate preoperative identification of high-risk patients for ASDeg is crucial for reducing surgical risks, optimizing treatment strategies, and improving prognosis.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a noninvasive, high-resolution, and radiation-free imaging technique that is renowned for its superior soft tissue contrast and multiplanar imaging capabilities. Thus, it is considered the most important examination for the evaluation of lumbar degenerative diseases [6, 7]. However, the identification of high-risk patients for ASDeg based on lumbar MRI largely depends on the subjective judgment of clinicians, leading to variability in the results. Enhancing the predictive ability of MRI for ASDeg is an urgent clinical need.

Radiomics helps indirectly reflect the macro- and micro-structural heterogeneity within the lumbar spine and surrounding soft tissues [8]. Meanwhile, deep learning has been widely used in spinal imaging analysis because of the advantages of three-dimensional convolutional neural networks (3D CNNs) in processing 3D data. By integrating artificial intelligence and radiomics techniques, this study utilized lumbar MR images from a local medical center to develop a prediction model, and the model’s performance in predicting ASDeg after lumbar fusion was compared. Combined models were developed using three types of features to evaluate their use in identifying patients at high risk of ASDeg.

Patients and methods

Patients

This study used medical imaging data from three hospitals in China. To establish internal training and validation cohorts and an external testing cohort, the clinical baseline and imaging data of patients with lumbar degenerative diseases who underwent posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) were collected from January 2016 to June 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who underwent 1–3 segment PLIF at our hospital and two other research centers; (2) patients with at least 6 months of complete medical records and postoperative follow-up data; (3) patients with complete clinical data; and (4) patients with no history of spinal surgery. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with lumbar diseases related to infection or tumors; (2) patients with poor imaging quality or the presence of artifacts; and (3) patients with incomplete follow-up or medical records. Figure 1 presents a detailed flowchart of case selection, in which the training and internal validation cohorts were randomly assigned at a ratio of 7:3. The study design and the Deep Learning Radiomics (DLR) workflow (Figs. 2 and 3) illustrate the processes of case collection and grouping, image preprocessing, feature extraction, feature analysis, and model construction. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Chao-yang Hospital (ID: 2024-KE-346). Because of the retrospective study design, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for informed consent.

Clinical characteristics and MRI acquisition

All patients’ baseline data—including sex, age, number of surgical segments, body mass index (BMI), osteoporosis status, and diagnosis—were extracted from the patients’ clinical medical records. Explanation S1 presents the details of the MRI equipment and imaging parameters.

In our study, ASDeg was defined radiographically based on at least one of the following observed ≥ 6 months postoperatively: (1) Spondylolisthesis > 4 mm, angular motion > 10° on dynamic X-ray, or disc height reduction > 10%; (2) UCLA disc degeneration grade ≥ 2; (3) Pfirrmann grade IV or V in adjacent segment. The assessments were conducted independently by two experienced spine surgeons, each with more than five years of practice. They were blinded to the details of the surgeries, and a senior surgeon confirmed the final diagnoses.

Image analysis and predictions

Two spinal surgeons (C.Z. and B.W.) with 6 and 11 years of experience, respectively, independently predicted whether patients would develop ASDeg after surgery based on the same clinical information used by the algorithm—the patients’ baseline data and MR images. In cases in which the two surgeons’ predictions were inconsistent, an expert with over 20 years of experience in spinal surgery (L.Z.) served as an adjudicator. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient was calculated to assess the predictive agreement between two spinal surgeons. (Table S1).

Image segmentation

Accurate vertebral segmentation is a premise for subsequent image analysis, and it was independently performed manually by two spinal surgeons. First, Surgeon A imported the MR images into the ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0, http://www.itksnap.org). For the sagittal T1 and T2 sequences, the vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs were annotated manually. In addition to the anterior column structures, the spinous processes of the adjacent segments were also annotated to evaluate the integrity of the posterior tension band. To overcome the inherent limitations of MRI in delineating bone boundaries and to minimize partial volume effects from surrounding soft tissues, a targeted annotation protocol was implemented. Specifically, spinous process segmentation was strictly confined to the most central mid-sagittal slice, and these annotations were rigorously cross-referenced with matched computed tomography (CT) images to ensure anatomical precision. For the axial sequences, the borders of the psoas major, quadratus lumborum, and entire paraspinal muscles were manually outlined and filled to ensure the inclusion of information on the spine and surrounding soft tissues. Considering the anisotropic spatial resolution inherent in routine clinical MRI, these aggregated multi-slice masks were defined as a quasi-3D (or 2.5D) representation. The segmentation results were then saved as mask files in the NIfTI format (Fig. 4). One month later, 30 patients were randomly selected from the training cohort, and Surgeons A and B independently performed segmentation again. The intraclass correlation coefficient was used to assess the interobserver consistency of vertebral segmentation.

Radiomics feature extraction

To prevent data leakage, feature extraction was performed on the vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, and paraspinal muscles across all datasets. Considering the anisotropic spatial resolution of the routine clinical MRI scans, radiomic features were extracted using a 2.5D (quasi-3D) approach. This method aggregates spatial information across multiple consecutive slices without introducing interpolation artifacts in the z-axis. However, feature selection was performed only on the training cohort. Before feature extraction, Z-score normalization was applied to all images. This method was specifically chosen over tissue-specific reference normalization (e.g., using cerebrospinal fluid) because obtaining consistent, artifact-free cerebrospinal fluid regions is highly unreliable in patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis. Furthermore, Z-score normalization provides robust standardization of intensity distributions across diverse scanner hardware in our multicenter setting. The feature extraction algorithms were optimized according to the guidelines of the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative. Radiomic features were extracted using the open-source package Pyradiomics (http://pypi.org/project/pyradiomics/) based on Python 3.8. These features included first-order statistics, shape, gray-level co-occurrence matrix, gray-level size zone matrix, gray-level run length matrix, neighboring gray-tone difference matrix, and gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features. A detailed description of the radiomic features extracted in this study can be found in the Pyradiomics documentation (http://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io). After extraction, robust normalization was applied to standardize all features by calculating the median and quartiles for each feature. Robust normalization was achieved by subtracting the median from each feature and then dividing by the interquartile range to reduce data discrepancies between centers. To transparently present the underlying data characteristics and the quantification of the extraction process, the exact number of features retained is detailed in Supplementary Table S3, and the value distributions of the extracted radiomic features across the dataset are provided in Supplementary Figure S1.

Deep transfer learning (DTL) feature extraction

The input images were resized to dimensions of 128 × 128 × 128 using linear pixelation, and the pixel intensities were normalized to a mean of 0 ± 1. In the deep learning library PyTorch based on Python 3.8, a DTL method similar to that used in previous studies was employed [9]. The Vision Transformer 3D (ViT-3D) model was selected as the base model for this study, and the learning rate was carefully adjusted to achieve better transfer performance. The fundamental concept of the ViT is to partition images into small patches and input them into a neural network for processing. Each patch is processed independently, and their outputs are combined, which allows the network to learn the global structure and features of the image. In summary, the ViT decomposes MR images into patches, processes each patch individually, and pools the results through concatenation to generate a representation of the entire image. After processing the raw input data, the outputs of the ViT were used to predict whether ASDeg would occur using a binary classifier. The ViT allows the model to learn the complex features associated with ASDeg, thereby allowing accurate prediction in new images. Because the transfer features were extracted from the penultimate layer of the model (i.e., the average pooling layer that obtains a global image representation by averaging the features of all patches), the model parameters were divided into two parts: the backbone and task-specific parts. The backbone parameters were initialized using the RadImageNet pretrained model [10], and the task-specific parameters were randomly initialized.

Feature selection

Early fusion, also known as feature-level fusion, was performed on radiomic features extracted from the vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, and paraspinal muscles using multisequence MRI. In particular, the radiomic features of these anatomical structures were concatenated into a single radiomic feature vector. To select radiomic features with high repeatability and low redundancy, first, we conducted an independent-sample F-test on the histogram of co-occurrence matrix of regional features, and features with p > 0.05 were removed. Second, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between features; for highly correlated features (correlation coefficient > 0.9), a feature was retained. Importantly, to address the inherent volume-dependency of many radiomic features, this correlation analysis effectively identified and removed redundant features that were highly collinear with the ROI volume, thereby mitigating potential confounding size effects. To maximize the representational capability of the features, a greedy recursive deletion strategy was adopted where, in each step, the feature with the highest correlation to others was removed until the redundancy of the feature set reached an acceptable level. Then, we used the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithm to incorporate stable radiomic features into the analysis by constructing a penalty function λ that shrinks the regression coefficients to zero. Tenfold cross-validation was performed based on the criterion of maximizing the mean cross-validation score to determine the optimal λ value. According to the model corresponding to the optimal λ, radiomic parameters with nonzero coefficients and their weights were selected. Finally, independent and stable radiomic features were identified.

For the DLR features from the vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, and paraspinal muscles, feature-level fusion was also performed by concatenating the features into a single feature vector. Before Principal Component Analysis (PCA) dimensionality reduction, Z-score normalization was applied to the DLR features by subtracting the mean of each feature column and dividing by its standard deviation, converting them to a standard normal distribution. PCA was then performed for dimensionality reduction to extract the principal components, which helped improve the model’s generalization ability and reduced the risk of overfitting. Finally, LASSO regression was used to perform feature selection on the DLR features, and important features with nonzero coefficients were retained.

Model construction and validation

After feature selection, the scikit-learn machine learning library was used to construct classification models, including logistic regression (LR); Naive Bayes; linear support vector machines (linear SVM); polynomial, sigmoid, and radial basis function kernels; decision trees; random forests; Extremely Randomized Trees; eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost); AdaBoost; Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM); multilayer perceptions (MLP); and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM). Grid search algorithms were employed on the training set to tune the hyperparameters of all models, allowing adjustment of commonly used parameters in each model. Tenfold cross-validation was performed on the training set to compare the performance of different classification models and select optimal hyperparameters.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, and the area under the curve (AUC), accuracy (ACC), sensitivity (SEN), F1 score, and specificity (SPE) were calculated to evaluate the performance of the predictive models. Handcrafted radiomic and DLR models were developed separately, and the output probabilities of the optimal classifiers were defined as the Radiomics signature (Rad-sign) and the Deep Learning signature (DL-sign).

Binary LR analysis was performed to assess baseline clinical variables such as surgical segments, sex, age, diagnosis, BMI, and osteoporosis. Statistically significant clinical variables were combined with the Rad-sign and DL-sign to construct a combined model using the aforementioned machine learning algorithms. This was performed to further assess the model’s effectiveness in identifying patients at high risk of ASDeg after lumbar fusion. Modeling was performed using the PixelMed AI platform (https://github.com/410312774/PixelMedAI).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.8.2; https://www.python.org). Continuous variables with normal distribution are expressed as means ± standard deviations, and comparisons between groups were made using independent-sample t-tests. Continuous variables with nonnormal distribution are expressed as medians (interquartile ranges), and comparisons were made using the Mann–Whitney U test. Levene’s test was used to assess the homogeneity of variances before conducting t-tests. Categorical variables are presented as counts (n) and percentages (%), and comparisons between groups were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p-values < 0.05 were used to denote statistical significance. The performance of the classification models was evaluated using ROC curves and AUC. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed by quantifying the net benefit at different threshold probabilities to assess the clinical value of the models. The DeLong test was used to compare differences in AUCs between among models.

Results

Clinical baseline characteristics

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 305 patients, including 162 females and 143 males, participated in this study. The age of the patients ranged from 36 to 90 years. Based on bone mineral density examination results, 143 patients were diagnosed with osteoporosis. Among the included patients, 201 underwent single-segment fusion, 90 underwent two-segment fusion, and 14 underwent three-segment fusion. During the follow-up period, based on the imaging examination results, 50 patients were diagnosed with ASDeg. Table 1 presents the demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients. Binary LR analysis revealed that osteoporosis, diagnosis, and surgical segments were significantly associated with the occurrence of ASDeg (Table S2).

After feature fusion, the LASSO regression model was used for feature selection and dimensionality reduction, and the penalty coefficient λ was 0.012 (Fig. 5A and B). Finally, 17 radiomic features were retained after feature fusion (Fig. 5C).

Based on the fused features and their corresponding regression coefficients, a model was developed using preoperative MRI handcrafted radiomic features to predict the development of ASDeg after lumbar fusion. Among all models, the most effective machine learning algorithm was the linear SVM algorithm. The AUCs for the internal training, internal validation, and external test sets were 0.897, 0.725, and 0.880, respectively (Fig. 6).

Combined model based on baseline clinical data, rad-sign, and DL-sign

A combined model was established using the patients’ baseline clinical data along with the previously extracted Rad-sign and DL-sign. Among all models, the most effective machine learning algorithm was GBM. The AUCs for the internal training, internal validation, and external test sets were 0.959, 0.818, and 0.895, respectively (Fig. 9A).

As shown in the figure, when combining radiomic and deep learning prediction models with the clinical baseline features to construct a combined model, the feature importance bar chart indicates that the Rad-sign, DL-sign, osteoporosis, diagnosis, and surgical segments contributed the most to the prediction results (Fig. 9B). Compared with the previous two models based solely on radiomic or DLR features, the combined model exhibited a significant improvement in predictive performance on the internal validation and external test sets. The calibration curves demonstrated the model’s calibration across different datasets. Although the validation set curve slightly deviated from the ideal calibration line, it was closer than the training set and showed better predictive calibration than the external test set (Fig. 9C). DCA revealed that the combined model provided higher net benefits to patients than the single-feature models (Fig. 9D). Meanwhile, the DeLong test results indicate that the combined model demonstrates a statistically significant advantage over the single-feature model (p < 0.05). The combined model performed better than the combined predictions of the two spinal surgeons, and the comparison between the machine and clinicians exhibited significant differences (Table 2). Therefore, the proposed combined model can more intuitively and effectively identify patients at high risk of ASDeg.

Discussion

In clinical practice, imaging examinations during postoperative follow-up are commonly used to diagnose ASDeg [11]. However, considering that the occurrence of adjacent segment disease (ASDis) can increase the risk of patients returning for additional treatment, identifying high-risk patients during preoperative evaluation is crucial for improving patient prognosis. Recently, several studies have highlighted various risk factors for predicting the risk of ASDeg after spinal fusion. Factors such as age, higher BMI, and degree of preoperative adjacent segment degeneration have been identified as important predictors of ASDeg [12, 13]. Furthermore, paraspinal muscle degeneration has been shown to be associated with ASDeg [12]. However, previous studies predicting postoperative ASDeg based on preoperative imaging data have largely lacked objective quantitative metrics. In this study, datasets from three hospitals were used to construct a combined predictive model that integrated DLRs, traditional radiomics, and baseline clinical data based on multisequence MR images to identify patients at high risk of ASDeg. To further explore this clinical direction, our research group previously developed a dual-modality predictive model integrating both CT and MRI radiomics [14]. Although that complex multi-modal framework demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy, the necessity of supplementary CT scans inherently increases patient exposure to ionizing radiation, escalates medical costs, and demands labor-intensive cross-modality image registration. Driven by the necessity for a safer and more streamlined clinical utility, the present model was intentionally engineered around a minimalist, single-modality philosophy. Strikingly, by utilizing only multi-sequence MRI and baseline clinical data, our current streamlined model achieves a highly competitive predictive performance that is fully comparable to our previous, more complex dual-modality paradigm. This demonstrates a ‘less is more’ clinical efficiency: eliminating redundant CT imaging and minimizing the annotation workload does not compromise the model’s performance. Consequently, this single-modality approach offers a safer, more cost-effective, and easily deployable tool for rapid preoperative risk stratification in routine clinical practice.

MRI typically uses multisequence imaging to observe subtle changes in the spine and surrounding soft tissues. Based on previous studies [15], we believe that sagittal images of the vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, and axial images of muscle tissues provide comprehensive information related to the spine and its microenvironment. Therefore, in this study, we delineated and analyzed the vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs on multi-sequence sagittal MR images (T1 and T2 sequences), and the quadratus lumborum, psoas major, and paraspinal muscles were delineated and analyzed on axial images. In recent years, the medical field has extensively adopted machine learning techniques for clinical image analysis. This advancement offers a novel approach to extracting information from medical images, facilitating the discovery of potential biomarkers that are challenging to detect with traditional imaging methods. These features may enable automated medical imaging analysis and provide intelligent diagnostic support to clinicians, thereby improving diagnostic accuracy and efficiency [16]. Some scholars have used deep learning to process medical images and developed an effective screening method for ASDis based on deep learning and cervical MRI [17]. However, due to the poor interpretability of deep learning models, this method has certain limitations in terms of clinical application. To overcome these limitations, DLR has been introduced and has rapidly advanced owing to the development of network architectures like ResNet [18, 19]. RadImageNet is an open-source dataset tailored specifically for medical imaging applications, and it theoretically outperforms ImageNet in medical tasks [20]. The effectiveness of transfer learning depends on the feature similarity between source and target tasks [21]. Therefore, we implemented deep transfer learning (DTL) using RadImageNet as the pre-trained model for our deep learning endeavors. In this study, we utilized lumbar spine MR images from local medical centers to develop DLR prediction models and assessed their performance in predicting ASDeg after lumbar fusion surgery. This approach not only enhances the interpretability of deep learning-based medical imaging but also innovatively expands traditional radiomics by integrating deep learning methods, thereby laying a solid foundation for future research.

In this study, after final selection, the optimal model with feature fusion retained 17 radiomic, 12 DLR, and three clinical features. First, the Dependence Variance feature in the GLDM after the wavelet transformation (wavelet_HHH_gldm_Dependence Variance) exhibited the highest correlation coefficient with the occurrence of ASDeg; higher values indicate increased heterogeneity of tissue textures [22]. MRI manifestations of lumbar degenerative diseases typically include degenerative changes in the vertebral bodies and surrounding soft tissues, which increase the complexity of texture features. This characteristic may indirectly reflect the profound structural heterogeneity of the tissues surrounding the vertebral bodies and their risk of future degeneration. This finding further supports the high correlation of wavelet_HHH_gldm_DependenceVariance and is consistent with previous MRI radiomics studies evaluating tissue complexity [23]. Overall, feature importance analysis indicated that the texture, morphology, and 3D structural features of the fused regions and their adjacent segments on preoperative MRI played significant roles in the model. Collectively, these characteristics indicate that the intricate mechanical and biological conditions of adjacent segments prior to fusion exert multidimensional effects. Moreover, we found that the DLR features with the highest correlation coefficients all originated from the intervertebral discs. Combined with the findings of previous studies [2, 24–26], we can conclude that pathological changes in the intervertebral discs have a significant impact on postoperative ASDeg. Although the interpretability of current DLR features requires further investigation, this does not prevent us from obtaining preliminary explanations of lesion-specific features by integrating them with traditional radiomic features. Furthermore, our study innovatively incorporated radiomic features from the spinous processes to account for the posterior tension band’s critical role in segmental stability. While extracting bone radiomics from MRI is inherently challenging due to potential boundary un-sharpness, we believe the biomechanical significance of the posterior complex in ASDeg justifies this exploratory inclusion. The potential for reduced precision in bone-boundary definition on MRI should not preclude the investigation of these clinically vital structures. Instead, our targeted mid-sagittal annotation approach serves as a foundational attempt. Future research is encouraged to build upon this framework by developing more refined, AI-automated labeling techniques to further optimize the precision and utility of posterior element radiomics. Finally, our findings indicate that osteoporosis significantly increases the risk of ASDeg, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies [27–29]. The inclusion of patients with three-segment fusion was limited because of the small sample size, resulting in discrepancies in the odds ratio of the surgical segments compared with those reported in previous studies [30]. Notably, the combined model was constructed using the GBM algorithm, where the surgical segment factor had the lowest importance weight. This indicates that its contribution to the model’s predictive outcome was minimal, thereby reducing its potential bias in the study. More patients of this type should be included in the future to reduce the bias of this study.

As deep learning continues to evolve, ViT models have achieved remarkable progress in computer vision applications. Compared to traditional convolutional neural networks, ViT models utilizing self-attention mechanisms excel at capturing global features [31]. This benefit is especially crucial in the analysis of complex medical images, as comprehending the entire image context can result in more precise diagnoses and insights. The development of ViT models, along with their integration with other advanced technologies, has provided new opportunities for advancing medical image analysis and improving diagnostic outcomes. In this study, the combined model exhibited significantly higher AUC values for identifying adjacent segment degeneration than models based solely on conventional radiomic features, in both the internal validation and external test sets. These results indicate that the combined model based on the ViT framework can significantly enhance the predictive performance of the model. Furthermore, the model’s performance in the internal validation set was slightly lower than that in the external test set. We believe that this may be due to the smaller sample size of the external test set, which led to wider confidence intervals and potentially overestimating the model’s performance. Meanwhile, differences in the sources of the external test data (e.g. scanning parameters) could not be eliminated even after standardization, which affected the performance. In contrast, the MRI parameters and patient characteristics of the external test set were similar to those of the training set, which promotes good model performance on this dataset. Finally, we found that the spinal surgeons performed better on the internal validation set than on the external test set, whereas the combined model performed better on the external test set than on the internal validation set. This result indicates that the combined model exhibits superior decision consistency and stability, greater capability for handling complex features, and adaptability.

Some limitations of this study should be acknowledged. Firstly, manual MRI annotation inherently introduces subjectivity and is time-consuming. Future efforts should optimize AI-driven automatic segmentation to achieve efficient and standardized structural assessments. Secondly, despite utilizing multicenter data, our overall sample size remained relatively limited, and inter-scanner variability was not explicitly evaluated. Future research must incorporate larger, diverse cohorts for robust prospective multicenter validation. Thirdly, our model primarily relies on single-timepoint preoperative MRI without stratifying the baseline severity of adjacent segment discs. Distinguishing de novo degeneration from the natural progression of pre-existing degeneration remains a challenge. Inspired by clinical necessity, our future research will focus on longitudinal, time-series radiomics to dynamically track temporal disc changes. Finally, like many radiomics studies, feature interpretability remains challenging. To overcome these limitations and significantly enhance clinical utility, future prospective studies should evolve towards a multi-dimensional, holistic integration. By integrating our deep learning radiomics model with patient-specific biomechanical parameters (e.g., sagittal balance) and potential genetic markers, we can establish a more holistic, robust, and highly individualized preoperative predictive system for ASDeg.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study developed a composite model by integrating radiomic, DLR, and clinical features. Compared to models utilizing features from a single modality, the proposed combined model further improved identification performance for high-risk patients with ASDeg.

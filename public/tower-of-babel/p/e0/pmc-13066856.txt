WENet: a lightweight dermoscopic image segmentation network with wide edge assistance generated by morphological erosion
Authors: Weiye Cao, Kaiyan Zhu, Kaiying Zhu, Shikai Zhang, Yuhang Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDermoscopic image segmentation plays a crucial role in computer-aided diagnostic (CAD) systems as an important tool for diagnosing skin lesions. The precise segmentation of lesion regions in dermatological images facilitates more objective and accurate diagnostic decision-making in clinical practice. However, many existing deep learning models face challenges in accurately segmenting lesion edges and are computationally complex, which limits their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Therefore, this study aimed to design a dermoscopic image segmentation model that simultaneously addresses the challenges of edge segmentation and maintains low computational complexity.MethodsWe developed a wide edge-assisted lightweight dermoscopic image segmentation network (WENet) consisting of a lightweight encoder and a wide-edge-assisted decoder. The encoder is constructed using a squeeze dual-path convolution (SDPC), which adopts a bottleneck design and employs asymmetric convolutions with large and small dilation rates to significantly reduce model complexity while ensuring efficient feature extraction. It also integrates the statistical multi-feature adaptive channel recalibration attention (SACA) module for precise channel feature recalibration. The decoder consists of the wide-boundary generator (WBG), prediction information fusion decoding layer (PFDL), and progressive multi-scale feature fusion segmentation head (PMSSH). The WBG generates wide-edge labels by combining ground-truth annotations with morphological erosion and applies deep supervision to guide the model to learn boundary features, enhancing the model’s edge segmentation performance without increasing the parameter count. The PFDL fuses region and boundary predictions with decoding features and employs a grouped design using the SDPC for feature extraction. It then enhances spatial feature information using group multi-axis Hadamard product attention (GHPA). The PMSSH progressively integrates multi-scale features to bridge the semantic gap across scales, ultimately producing the final segmentation map.ResultsWENet was evaluated on the International Skin Imaging Collaboration (ISIC)2017, ISIC2018, and Pedro Hispano 2 (PH2) datasets, and achieved mean intersection over union (mIoU) scores of 80.37%, 81.34%, and 85.98%, and specificity (Spe) values of 98.37%, 97.39%, and 96.23%, respectively, while maintaining a model size under 15KB. The model has significantly fewer parameters compared to recent state-of-the-art models, while maintaining excellent segmentation performance.ConclusionsThe proposed WENet presents an accurate yet computationally efficient solution for dermoscopic image segmentation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in both model compactness and boundary segmentation precision.Keywords: Dermoscopy, lightweight, wide edge, morphology, progressive fusion

Introduction

Dermoscopic image segmentation plays a crucial role in computer-aided diagnostic (CAD) systems, effectively assisting clinicians to identify and classify skin lesions, including malignant conditions such as melanoma (1-3). Dermoscopic images enhance the visualization of the skin surface through the use of polarized light or immersion fluids, enabling clinicians to observe subtle structures that are often imperceptible to the naked eye, such as pigment networks, blue-white veils, and irregular vascular patterns (4,5). However, the automatic segmentation of skin lesions remains highly challenging, primarily due to the low contrast and similar visual characteristics between lesion boundaries and surrounding healthy skin, as illustrated in Figure 1. For example, the edges of some melanomas may exhibit gradual pigment diffusion, resulting in ambiguous transitions between lesion and normal tissue; conversely, certain benign lesions may also display irregular boundaries, increasing the risk of mis-segmentation (6,7). Moreover, achieving accurate segmentation poses significant challenges due to artifacts such as uneven illumination, hair occlusion, and bubbles, which can adversely affect image interpretability.

In recent years, deep learning has made remarkable progress in the field of medical image segmentation, leading to the emergence of a wide range of innovative segmentation models. UNet (8) pioneered the use of an encoder–decoder architecture with skip connections, which effectively mitigates the loss of fine-grained features during the upsampling process. This seminal work has inspired numerous variants, including DenseUNet (9) with dense connectivity, Attention UNet (10), which incorporates attention mechanisms, and ResUNet++ (11), which integrates residual structures. Additionally, the success of the Transformer architecture in computer vision (12) has led to new breakthroughs in medical image segmentation, leading to hybrid models such as TransUNet (13) and Swin-Unet (14), which integrate the strengths of both convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Transformers. However, task-specific architectures often yield superior performance when applied to particular medical image segmentation tasks. For example, for the precise segmentation of surgical instruments and polyps during endoscopic examinations, Abidin et al. (15) proposed hierarchical feature refinement and fusion network (HFRF-Net), which employs the diverse scope synthesis block (DSSB) to capture multi-scale contextual features and the feature emphasis and aggregation block (FEAB) to refine spatial and semantic representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple publicly available datasets.

Researchers in the field of dermoscopic image segmentation have also introduced a variety of novel network architectures. For example, Xin et al. (16) introduced a hybrid Transformer module that combines multi-scale feature encoding with a position-aware Transformer decoder, significantly improving feature extraction accuracy (Acc). Yang et al. (17) developed a novel attention-based network that leverages multi-level attention mechanisms and feature interaction modules to enhance the recognition of complex lesion regions. Xu et al. (18) proposed an alternative approach based on statistical texture quantification, introducing a kurtosis-guided statistical computation module for the accurate delineation of lesion boundaries. Zhu et al. (19) presented a morphology-aware hybrid network that combines the local feature extraction capability of CNNs with the global modeling strength of vision transformers, enabling the comprehensive capture of lesion morphology.

Existing models demonstrate notable segmentation performance; however, they still suffer from high parameter redundancy and insufficient Acc in lesion boundary regions. Enhancing boundary segmentation precision while maintaining a lightweight design not only enables efficient and accurate dermoscopic image segmentation, but also facilitates subsequent deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Therefore, achieving high-precision boundary segmentation while preserving model lightweightness remains a pressing and important research challenge.

To address the aforementioned issues, we proposed a wide edge-assisted lightweight dermoscopic image segmentation network (WENet), as shown in Figure 2, which achieves accurate dermoscopic image segmentation with low computational complexity. WENet consists of a lightweight encoder and a wide-edge-assisted decoder. The encoder adopts a custom squeeze dual-path convolution (SDPC) block as its core component, incorporating a bottleneck structure and asymmetric dilated convolutions to capture both fine-grained and long-range features while maintaining computational efficiency (20). Channel-wise feature recalibration is achieved via the statistical multi-feature adaptive channel recalibration attention (SACA), which utilizes multiple statistical descriptors and a one-dimensional (1D) convolution to model cross-channel interactions (21). The decoder integrates three modules: the wide-boundary generator (WBG), the prediction information fusion decoding layer (PFDL), and the progressive multi-scale feature fusion segmentation head (PMSSH). The WBG generates wide-edge labels using morphological erosion and applies deep supervision to guide boundary-sensitive learning. The PFDL fuses region and boundary predictions with decoding features, enhancing the representation of lesion interiors and boundaries. The PMSSH progressively fuses multi-scale features from different decoding layers, with SACA applied to improve channel representations, generating the final segmentation output efficiently. Grouped feature extraction and group multi-axis Hadamard product attention (GHPA) further enhance spatial feature representations with linear computational complexity (22). Channel shuffling ensures sufficient interaction during upsampling.

Related work

Lightweight dermoscopic image segmentation

Dermoscopic image segmentation is a key task in CAD systems for skin lesions. Most segmentation models achieve good segmentation Acc but at the cost of model efficiency, often requiring substantial computational resources and making them difficult to deploy on edge devices (23-25). To address this issue, numerous lightweight dermoscopic image segmentation methods have emerged in recent years, aiming to maintain performance while improving computational efficiency. For example, Ruan et al. (26) reduced the number of channels in UNet and designed a set of lightweight attention mechanisms to achieve efficient dermoscopic segmentation. Hu et al. (27) innovatively developed lean networking (LeaNet), which integrates multi-level attention modules into the classic UNet architecture, effectively improving the model’s localization ability for lesion areas while maintaining low computational complexity. Deng et al. (28) proposed a feature enhancement method based on multi-head convolutional attention and dual-path attention mechanisms, significantly improving segmentation Acc while ensuring the model remains lightweight through multi-dimensional feature fusion strategies. Khan et al. (29) developed an encoder structure with residual feature refinement, which achieves a good balance between computational efficiency and segmentation performance through progressive feature optimization mechanisms. Cai et al. (30) used an efficient biological vision-based attention mechanism to generate saliency maps for skin lesions, guiding the network to quickly locate target areas and achieving lightweight and efficient dermoscopic image segmentation. Xu et al. (31) designed a grouped attention module to improve segmentation performance and used a genetic algorithm to generate boundary maps, guiding the model to learn more accurate boundaries. These methods reflect the current research trend in lightweight segmentation models. While these methods focus on lightweight segmentation, none explicitly address the challenges of accurately capturing lesion boundaries, which is critical for precise dermoscopic image segmentation. Methods

Overall architecture of WENet

The overall architecture of WENet is shown in Figure 3A. Based on a U-shaped encoder-decoder structure, WENet comprises a lightweight encoder and a wide-edge-assisted decoder. The lightweight encoder comprises layers with 16, 24, 32, 40, and 48 channels, each implemented with SDPC blocks for extracting detail and semantic features, followed by SACA for channel recalibration. The SDPC and SACA structures are shown in Figure 3B,3C, respectively. The decoder integrates the WBG, PFDL, and PMSSH modules. The PFDL fuses region and boundary predictions with decoding features. Specifically, the region prediction is supervised by the ground-truth labels, while the boundary prediction is supervised by the boundary labels generated by the WBG through the combination of morphological erosion and the ground truth. Grouped SDPC is then employed for efficient feature extraction, GHPA is used to enhance spatial representations, and the upsampling process is used to ensure channel interaction. The PFDL structure is shown in Figure 3D, and the ground-truth labels and the boundary labels generated by the WBG are illustrated in Figure 4. The PMSSH aggregates multi-scale features from all decoding layers, recalibrates channels with SACA, compresses channels, and progressively fuses features to produce the final segmentation output. SDPC module

The primary objective of lightweight segmentation algorithms is to maintain model compactness while ensuring efficient feature extraction. The bottleneck architecture proposed by He et al. (20) compresses channels using a 1×1 convolution, extracts features with a 3×3 convolution, and restores channel dimensions via another 1×1 convolution, effectively reducing model complexity. However, the standard 3×3 convolution has a limited receptive field, mainly capturing local spatial information but providing limited channel enhancement, which restricts the modeling of long-range dependencies and channel-wise discriminative information. To address this issue, asymmetric convolutions with different dilation rates are introduced into the bottleneck structure to enlarge the receptive field, followed by channel feature recalibration using SACA (21).

SACA models cross-channel interactions through multiple statistical descriptors and a 1D convolution, thereby strengthening channel representation. Based on these designs, the SDPC is constructed, achieving efficient multi-scale feature extraction while significantly reducing parameter count. As illustrated in Figure 3B, the SDPC comprises two components: feature extraction and channel recalibration. First, the input is denoted as X∈RB×C1×H×W, where B is the batch size, C is the number of input channels, and H and W are the height and width of the feature map, respectively. To reduce the parameter count and maintain model efficiency, a 1×1 convolution is first applied to squeeze the channel dimension to half of the output channels, which is denoted as X1∈RB×C22×H×W. The feature map is then split into two branches, each performing feature extraction using asymmetric convolutional kernels with dilation rates of 1 and 5, respectively, ensuring that both detailed and semantic features are captured. The features extracted from both branches are summed and passed through another 1×1 convolution to produce the desired number of output channels, denoted again as X′out∈RB×C2×H×W. WBG module

In dermoscopic image segmentation tasks, lesion boundaries often exhibit high visual similarity to surrounding normal skin, making it difficult for models to accurately localize lesion edges. To address this issue, we introduce the WBG module, which generates wide-boundary labels by combining the ground truth with morphological operations. These boundary labels are used together with the original labels for deep supervision, effectively guiding the model to learn both the semantic features of the lesion area and detailed boundary information, thereby improving segmentation Acc. Since ground-truth lesion masks are typically irregular in shape, a morphological erosion operation using an elliptical structuring element is applied to smooth the edges. The wide-boundary label is then obtained by subtracting the eroded mask from the original mask. A comparison between the wide-boundary label and the ground-truth label is shown in Figure 4.

PFDL module

In the U-shaped architecture, the main task of the decoder is feature recovery, further refined by combining encoder features. However, the original decoder structure is overly complex and not suitable for lightweight segmentation models. To achieve efficient feature recovery while maintaining a lightweight design, we developed the PFDL, which integrates depth predictions of both region and boundary information with GHPA (22). GHPA initializes learnable tensors along the height-width, channel-height, and channel-width axes, enhancing spatial feature representations from multiple perspectives without introducing significant computational overhead. As shown in Figure 3D, the PFDL comprises three components: prediction information fusion, grouped feature refinement, and channel shuffling. First, the PFDL significantly reduces the model’s parameter count while efficiently recovering features by combining stage prediction information and grouped feature extraction. PMSSH module

In the encoder-decoder architecture, the use of multi-scale features from the decoder can significantly enhance the model’s segmentation performance. However, most existing models directly upsample features from different scales in the decoder and output the final result without considering the semantic gap between deep and shallow decoding layers, leading to inefficient feature utilization. Therefore, we designed the PMSSH, as shown in Figure 5. We first designed an additional branch to optimize the raw input using the SDPC, with the number of channels remaining at three, denoted as E0∈RB×3×H×W. The PMSSH receives E0 along with features E1∈RB×16×H×W, E2∈RB×16×H×W, E3∈RB×24×H×W, and E4∈RB×32×H×W from different decoding layers. First, E0 is concatenated with E1, and then the multi-scale features are recalibrated using SACA, resulting in E′1∈RB×19×H×W, E′2∈RB×16×H×W, E′3∈RB×24×H×W, and E′4∈RB×32×H×W. Progressive fusion is then used to progressively compress the channel count at each layer to ensure that the module remains lightweight while eliminating the semantic gap. Dataset and pre-processing

This study presents a comprehensive evaluation through comparative and ablation experiments on three public dermoscopic image segmentation datasets: International Skin Imaging Collaboration (ISIC)2017 (33), ISIC2018 (34), and Pedro Hispano 2 (PH2) (35). ISIC2017 and ISIC2018 contain 2,150 and 2,694 dermoscopic images with corresponding segmentation annotations, respectively. Each dataset was split into training and testing sets at a ratio of 7:3: ISIC2017 was divided into 1,500 training images and 650 testing images, while ISIC2018 was divided into 1,886 training images and 808 testing images. Due to the limited size of the PH2 dataset, which contains only 200 annotated images, it was not used for training or hyperparameter tuning. Instead, a model trained on ISIC2017 was directly evaluated on PH2 as an external test dataset to assess its robustness when applied to images from a different data source, rather than to establish a fully independent cross-dataset generalization protocol.

Experimental details

All the experiments in this study were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070Ti SUPER GPU. The experiments were implemented in Python 3.10.16 using PyTorch 2.4.1 and CUDA 12.4. The AdamW optimizer was employed with an initial learning rate of 0.001, and training was performed for 300 epochs with a batch size of eight. Images were preprocessed by standardization and resized to 256×256 pixels. Data augmentation techniques, including random vertical and horizontal flipping, as well as random rotation, were applied during training to enhance model generalization. The hyperparameters, such as the learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs, were empirically selected based on preliminary experiments to ensure stable convergence and optimal segmentation performance.

Evaluation metrics

For performance evaluation, several commonly used segmentation metrics, including mean intersection over union (mIoU), Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), Acc, and specificity (Spe), were used. All metrics were computed at the pixel level based on the overlap between the predicted segmentation mask and the ground-truth annotation rather than at the image level or for clinical benign-versus-malignant diagnosis. Additionally, the term “Params” refers to the total number of trainable parameters of the model, expressed in millions (M), while “FLOPs” indicates the number of floating-point operations required for a single forward pass, measured in gigaflops (G). Effectiveness of the SDPC

We conducted experiments with different dilation rate combinations. To avoid grid artifacts caused by overly sparse receptive fields and to preserve local spatial continuity, one branch was consistently assigned a dilation rate of one, while the dilation rate of the other branch was varied. Moreover, to maintain the lightweight design objective of the proposed network, the dilation rate search space was explicitly constrained to values no greater than six, as excessively large dilation rates substantially increase computational overhead and significantly slow down training. As shown in Table 4, the complexity metrics remain unchanged across different dilation settings. The best performance was observed with the (1,5) dilation combination, where only Spe was not optimal. In this setting, mIoU, DSC, and Acc achieved superior results compared to other combinations, reaching 79.27%, 88.44%, and 94.48%, respectively. Subsequent attention mechanism experiments were conducted on the (1,5) dilation configuration. In terms of the parameter count, replacing ECA with SACA resulted in comparable values, while SACA achieved the lowest FLOPs. Regarding performance metrics, SACA yielded the best results in mIoU, DSC, and Spe—79.76%, 88.74%, and 96.54%, respectively—while its Acc was slightly lower than ECA. These findings demonstrate that the SDPC can substantially reduce the complexity of encoder branches while simultaneously improving segmentation performance.

Effectiveness of the PFDL

As shown in Table 5, replacing different prediction combinations in the PFDL module or adding channel shuffling did not increase the number of parameters or FLOPs. In terms of performance metrics, incorporating region prediction alone improved mIoU, DSC, Acc, and Spe by 0.77, 0.50, 0.35, and 0.04, respectively; incorporating edge prediction alone resulted in improvements of 0.92, 0.59, 0.29, and 0.07, respectively. When both predictions were combined, performance further improved compared using either prediction individually, with mIoU, DSC, Acc, and Spe reaching 78.20%, 87.78%, 94.18%, and 96.58%, respectively. After additionally applying channel shuffling, all performance metrics reached their optimal values: 78.98%, 88.26%, 94.38%, and 96.83%, respectively. These results demonstrate that the PFDL substantially reduces the complexity of decoder branches while effectively leveraging region and edge prediction information from deep supervision to improve segmentation performance.

Effectiveness of the PMSSH

As shown in Table 6, incorporating the PMSSH without SACA led to a slight increase in parameters and FLOPs, but improved mIoU, DSC, Acc, and Spe by 0.88, 0.56, 0.25, and 0.27, reaching 77.36%, 87.23%, 93.91%, and 96.62%, respectively. After adding different attention modules, only ECA had the same parameter count as SACA. However, SACA achieved the lowest FLOPs and the best overall performance. Compared to the configuration without SACA, mIoU, DSC, and Acc improved by 2.61, 1.64, and 0.70, reaching 79.97%, 88.87%, and 94.61%, respectively. These findings indicate that the PMSSH can effectively exploit multi-scale features from different decoding stages, thereby enhancing segmentation performance.

Discussion

In this study, we proposed WENet, which effectively captures challenging boundary features through a novel wide-edge label generation mechanism based on morphological erosion. Further, we designed the SDPC, PFDL, and PMSSH modules, enabling the model to remain highly lightweight, with a parameter count superior to that of existing state-of-the-art methods. However, the computational cost, measured in FLOPs, still leaves considerable room for improvement. We also acknowledge the limitations of the datasets used in this study. The images were primarily composed of melanocytic lesions, including pigmented nevi and melanoma, which may restrict the generalization of WENet to other types of skin lesions. In future work, we plan to further optimize FLOPs and deploy the algorithm on mobile devices to support practical clinical applications. Moreover, we intend to evaluate WENet on a broader range of skin lesion types, including non-melanocytic lesions, and incorporate additional clinical datasets to verify its robustness and generalizability across diverse scenarios. In addition, recent research on lightweight and efficient medical image segmentation has proposed several strategies that can be leveraged. For instance, in the three-dimensional tumor segmentation of automated breast ultrasound, compact models can effectively capture volumetric spatial features and multi-scale contextual information while maintaining high Acc and computational efficiency (42). Similarly, in retinal cup/disc segmentation, efficient feature-preservation strategies, such as dilated residual encoding, recursive decoding, and multi-level feature fusion, can achieve high-performance segmentation with a minimal number of parameters (43). Integrating these design principles into WENet is expected to further improve both FLOPs and segmentation Acc. Overall, these directions offer promising opportunities to extend WENet into a practical, high-performance, and deployable method for medical image segmentation.

Conclusions

In this study, we proposed an ultra-lightweight network, WENet, to address the high computational complexity prevalent in existing dermoscopic image segmentation methods. A novel wide-edge label generation mechanism based on morphological erosion was introduced to guide the model to capture challenging boundary characteristics effectively. Further, we designed three key components: the SDPC, PFDL, and PMSSH. The SDPC replaces the encoder, substantially reducing complexity while enhancing segmentation performance. The PFDL substitutes the decoder and incorporates region and edge prediction masks learned via deep supervision to improve segmentation Acc. The PMSSH progressively fuses multi-scale features from the decoder to efficiently generate the final segmentation results.

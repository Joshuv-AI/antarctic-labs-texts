Social media insights on the introduction of RSV immunoprophylaxis in Italy
Authors: Francesco Gesualdo, Virginia Casigliani, Guglielmo Arzilli, Erica De Vita, Andrea Davide Porretta, Ileana Croci, Andrea Carrozzo, Veronica Bartolucci, Cesare Buquicchio, Caterina Rizzo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe introduction of new immunoprophylaxis strategies against respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), such as maternal vaccination and the administration of monoclonal antibodies to newborns, presents both opportunities and challenges in terms of public understanding and acceptance. Monitoring online discussions can offer timely insights into perceptions of these interventions. We conducted a social media monitoring activity in Italy from September 2024 to January 2025, using the Pulsar platform to analyze posts on X and Facebook, complemented by manual analysis of comments on Instagram and TikTok. We examined content related to RSV prevention, focusing on stance, recurring narratives, and specific questions or doubts. Most content showed a neutral or supportive stance toward RSV immunoprophylaxis, with many users expressing awareness of RSV risk and interest in accessing the monoclonal antibody. However, recurring information needs emerged around eligibility, access, safety, and the distinction between different prevention options. Criticism toward the government was also observed, linked to initial decisions that limited free provision of the antibody to certain regions, later reversed, which left perceptions of inequality. Only a limited proportion of posts contained clear misinformation or disinformation. These results were consistent with recent surveys in other countries showing high parental acceptance of similar interventions. These findings underscore the need to address specific information gaps and ensure clear, consistent communication to support trust in RSV immunoprophylaxis. Proactive efforts will be important to reinforce the generally positive attitudes observed and to mitigate concerns that could affect the acceptance of new preventive measures.KEYWORDS: RSV, monoclonal antibody, immunoprophylaxis, Nirsevimab, social listening, social media monitoring, vaccine confidence, misinformation, public health communication, risk communication

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of acute respiratory infections in young children, with the highest burden of hospitalizations and intensive care unit (ICU) admissions occurring among previously healthy infants under six months of age.1

A recent prospective cohort study conducted across five European countries confirmed the substantial burden of RSV, attributing nearly one-third of acute respiratory tract infections in children under five years (and almost 40% in infants under one year) to RSV. The study also highlighted variations in healthcare resource utilization across countries.2 Notably, it found high medication use in Italy, including antibiotics, bronchodilators, and steroids – practices not aligned with current RSV treatment recommendations.3

In 2022, Nirsevimab, a long-acting monoclonal antibody for RSV prophylaxis, was approved in Europe.4 Since November 2024, it has been available in Italy for all newborns, as well as for infants born after April 2024, administered at birth centers and by family pediatricians. Following Nirsevimab’s approval, RSVpreF, a maternal RSV vaccine designed to provide passive immunization to newborns, was also approved in 2023,5 though it is not yet widely available in Italy. Both immunoprophylaxis strategies have been demonstrated to be safe and effective in preventing severe bronchiolitis in young infants.

As with many new immunization strategies, uptake and adherence are influenced not only by clinical efficacy and safety data but also by how these interventions are perceived and discussed in public conversations, including on social media. In this context, systematically monitoring online conversations can serve as a complementary tool to identify emerging concerns in real time, guide institutional communication, and support evidence-informed public health decisions.

We analyzed the Italian social media conversation on RSV to examine how Nirsevimab is perceived, discussed, and potentially misrepresented online. While we focused on Nirsevimab due to the limited availability of the RSV vaccine in Italy, our keyword filtering approach also allowed us to capture some social media discussions related to the RSV vaccine.

Materials and methods

Study context

We conducted RSV social media monitoring within the broader context of a social listening activity as part of the CRESP project (Comunicare il Rischio nelle Emergenze per la Sanità Pubblica, Communicating Risk in Public Health Emergencies) and with additional support from the BEHAVE_MOD project (Behaviour and Sentiment Monitoring and Modelling for Outbreak Control). The CRESP project has been funded by the Italian Ministry of Health through the CCM Program (National Center for Infectious Disease Prevention and Control), and had the general objectives of providing adequate training on risk communication in health and environmental emergencies, and of developing analysis and measurement tools for traditional and social media content, to provide solid support to infodemic management and epidemic intelligence teams in Italy. More specifically, one of the objectives of the CRESP was to set up a social listening activity focused on public health issues, with the aim of creating and testing a working framework for preparedness, to be adopted in the event of future health emergencies. The listening activity was partially based on the Infodemic Reporting framework, proposed by the World Health Organization (WHO),6 which outlines a structured approach for generating insights during health emergencies. The framework is articulated in six steps, which encompass identification of the key question, selection of information sources, integrated data analysis, development of recommendations, report writing and design and dissemination. According to the framework, in a health emergency, at least three sources of data, which may include surveys, should be considered. However, for the present article, we focus only on the results from monitoring the web and social media.

Data collection

The data collection was conducted prospectively from September 1, 2024, to January 20, 2025.

Social media content was monitored using Pulsar, an audience intelligence and social listening platform that collects, analyzes, and visualizes online conversations across multiple channels, including social media, news sites, and forums (https://www.pulsarplatform.com/.).

Pulsar was primarily used to monitor content from X and Facebook. The platform provides various dashboards that summarize key metrics. For this article, we considered only the outputs related to content volume, topic analysis and sentiment analysis.

We developed and tested the following keyword filter to identify relevant content on the monitoring platform: “RSV” OR “VRS” (the Italian acronym for Virus Respiratorio Sinciziale) OR “virus respiratorio sinciziale” OR “bronchiolite” (the Italian term for bronchiolitis). The keyword filter was developed by a group including pediatricians, public health experts, and parents without a health background. No additional colloquial terms exist in Italian to refer to RSV or bronchiolitis, so the selected keywords were considered sufficient to capture the relevant conversation. Each week, we selected a sample of posts with the highest visibility, a proprietary Pulsar metric that reflects impact based on likes, reposts, and shares. Pulsar does not automatically apply bot filters, and no additional automated tools were applied to detect or exclude bots, as our objective was to capture the conversation in its entirety, including possible amplification through automated activity. For the same reason, duplicate posts were also retained, since they contribute to representing the overall visibility of circulating content. Replies were excluded.

Given the predominantly visual nature of TikTok and Instagram, where keyword-based searches could lack sensitivity, additional strategies were adopted to monitor these platforms. In addition to viewing the content identified by Pulsar, we used the following strategies, partially basing our approach on snowball sampling techniques7: a) we analyzed the videos ranked the highest in the TikTok search bar, identified using RSV-related keywords, b) we identified TikTok and Instagram videos through Google searches, using RSV-related keywords, c) we monitored the accounts of popular med-influencers (i.e. healthcare professionals or students who share health-related content), and d) we explored the networks of identified creators to detect others discussing RSV.

Data analysis

Topic analysis was performed on all available content using Pulsar’s automated natural language processing algorithms, which identify and cluster main themes across posts to detect key narratives and relationships within the conversation. In the resulting network graph, node size reflects the frequency of a term and its number of connections, while link thickness indicates the strength of co-occurrence within individual posts. Nodes are grouped into color-coded clusters, whose labels are derived from the terms with the highest frequency and connectivity. The clustering is based on a PageRank algorithm, and the graph is visualized through a force-directed layout that places more strongly connected nodes closer together. The interpretation of each cluster was refined through a manual review of the posts included in each cluster.

Moreover, the sample selected from X and Facebook (top 15% by visibility) was anonymized and analyzed through qualitative text analysis by a multidisciplinary team of researchers, including clinicians, public health specialists and communication specialists.8 Both deductive and inductive approaches were employed in developing the coding book, and the content was classified into categories identified through these methods. The deductive category that emerged from the published literature was vaccine stance9 (promotional/discouraging/ambivalent/neutral). Inductive categories emerged from the content itself and included whether it was about RSV in general, Nirsevimab, the RSV vaccine or other topics; whether it reported a personal experience; and type of negative narrative (conspiracy theory, false ingredient and components, safety concerns, efficacy concerns, natural immunity superiority, big pharma, distrust in health authorities).

Only posts that actually mentioned RSV or bronchiolitis were included in the analysis; posts that used the RSV acronym (or the Italian VRS) in unrelated contexts were excluded. Included posts were then classified by main topic: RSV in general, Nirsevimab, RSV vaccine, or other. Stance classification was performed only on posts referring to Nirsevimab or the RSV vaccine, using the following categories:

Promotional: highlighted public health benefits or safety, encouraged use, described risks of not immunizing.

Classification was based primarily on the explicit content of posts or headlines, with linked articles reviewed only when necessary.

Comments to 12 high-engagement TikTok and Instagram videos about RSV/bronchiolitis were anonymized and reviewed to identify: key questions or doubts, misconceptions, personal experiences, and expressions of distrust in health authorities.

Weekly or bi-weekly meetings were held among the research team to review classification rules, discuss classifications, discrepancies and unclear cases, and to highlight noteworthy and recurrent content.

Data are presented as counts and percentages for categorical variables, and as median with interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables, as appropriate. For the subgroup of posts on Nirsevimab, associations between incorrect reference to Nirsevimab as a “vaccine” and stance or social platform were assessed using the Chi-square test. All analyses were conducted using STATA version 17.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

Ethical considerations

All data were publicly available and anonymized. No interaction with users occurred, and ethical approval was deemed unnecessary as the study focused on public digital content in line with institutional policies for social media analysis. An analogous interpretation of Article 110 of the Italian Privacy Code was applied, allowing the processing of potentially health-related data without explicit consent, given that the research entailed minimal risk to individuals and was conducted exclusively for scientific purposes.

Topic detection

Topic analysis performed by Pulsar identified five main narratives (see Figure 3).

The first raised awareness about the bronchiolitis season and focused on the risks associated with RSV infection in newborns, often accompanied by references to monoclonal antibodies as a preventive option. The second addressed concerns regarding the initial implementation strategy adopted by the Italian government, particularly the decision to make the antibody freely available only in regions aligned with national healthcare deficit recovery plans. While this criterion was later revised to ensure equitable access across the country, the initial approach was perceived by some as potentially contributing to regional disparities and a sense of institutional neglect. The third and fourth narratives focused on the co-circulation of RSV with other viruses, e.g. influenza and covid, including references to the so-called “Australian flu” and warnings of a potential rise in cases linked to the upcoming Jubilee and the arrival of large numbers of pilgrims. The fifth narrative highlighted the risk posed by RSV and other viruses for the elderly.

Text analysis

A total of 1,472 original posts and reposts were classified by the research team for the text analysis. Given the keyword filter, which focused on RSV and bronchiolitis (and not specifically on the immunoprophylaxis or the vaccine), we identified 199 (13.5%) posts which were about RSV or bronchiolitis in general, and did not mention any prophylaxis or therapeutic approach. Among the remaining 1,273 pieces of content, 1,161 (78.9%) were on the immunoprophylaxis, 102 (6.9%) on the vaccine, and 10 (0.7%) on other preventative or therapeutical approaches (e.g. a nasal spray for capturing and neutralizing viruses in the nasal mucosa, or an anti-viral drug for bronchiolitis treatment). Notably, almost one third (29.8%) of the posts on the immunoprophylaxis incorrectly named the monoclonal antibody as “vaccine.” During our manual review, we did not observe any posts that clearly appeared to be automated or bot-generated.

Monoclonal antibodies

Most of the 1,161 posts on the monoclonal antibodies were neutral (678, 58.4%) and almost one third were promotional (323, 27.3%). 17% of the posts classified as neutral and positive in terms of stance actually expressed distrust in health authorities, mainly showing perplexity or clear critics against the management of the immunoprophylaxis campaign by the government.

A very limited proportion of discouraging posts were detected (157, 13.5%). Notably, most of the discouraging content circulated on X (84.4%).

Among the posts with a discouraging stance, most expressed distrust in health authorities (183, 87,6%), followed by safety concerns (72, 34.3%) and efficacy concerns (31, 14.8%).

We did not observe any statistically significant difference in stance between posts that incorrectly referred to Nirsevimab as a “vaccine” and those that correctly defined it as an antibody. However, the incorrect reference was significantly more frequent on X than on Facebook.

Main themes

A large amount of social media content was news about the beginning of the immunoprophylaxis campaign, often underscoring the burden of the RSV infection in the population and the risk of severe clinical outcomes in young infants. A large proportion of this content also included criticism toward the government.

Based on qualitative text analysis, we identified two broad sets of recurring themes in the monitored content. The first group included general themes, frequently expressed in a neutral or promotional stance:

Promotion of the administration of monoclonal antibodies, primarily through official public health messaging

Criticism related to regional disparities in access to the antibody, initially linked to the government’s decision – later withdrawn – to restrict free provision to certain regions.

The second group consisted of more specific themes associated with skeptical attitudes or discouraging narratives. These included concerns that informed consent was not being requested at the time of monoclonal antibody administration; criticism of the €8 fee given to pediatricians per administered dose, which was framed as a potential conflict of interest; and claims that the Italian National Institute of Health (ISS) discouraged the widespread use of the antibody in the general newborn population. Other narratives referenced statements by a known physician with skeptical views on vaccinations, suggesting that mortality associated with the antibody exceeds RSV-related mortality, citing an article published in a questionable Japanese journal. Additional posts included personal testimony from a parent who reported a Kawasaki syndrome in their child shortly after the administration of both vaccines and Nirsevimab, as well as reports that Moderna had halted a clinical trial of its RSV mRNA vaccine due to serious adverse events in pediatric participants.

RSV vaccine

A total of 102 posts of those classified for the text analysis were on the RSV vaccine. Also in this case, the largest proportion was neutral – although neutral posts were less frequent than among those on monoclonal antibodies (41, 40.2%), followed by promotional content (33, 32.4%). One fourth of the posts were discouraging, and no ambiguous-ambivalent posts were identified. Most of the negative narratives identified in the discouraging posts were safety concerns (23, 82.1%) and a high percentage showed distrust in health authorities (20, 71.4%).

Comments to videos

A total of 2,830 comments were extracted from 12 highly engaging videos about RSV. Of these, 1,649 were included in the text analysis. Among them, 245 comments (14.9%) expressed a doubt or included a question; 872 (53%) described a personal experience, and 208 (12.6%) expressed distrust toward health authorities.

Comments containing questions or doubts were most frequently related to: eligibility by age; local logistics and regional access (i.e., where and how to access the antibody in specific geographic areas); safety and potential adverse events; and general questions about the antibody (e.g., its name, mechanism of action, or composition). A number of comments also referred to special cases and individual health histories, such as prematurity, recent bronchiolitis, or previous vaccinations.

Notably, some users expressed a willingness to pay out of pocket or travel to other regions to access the antibody when it was not available locally. These comments often conveyed a strong interest in the product and a heightened perception of the risk associated with RSV infection.

Several comments revealed confusion between the monoclonal antibody and the maternal vaccination during pregnancy – often reflecting uncertainty about the difference between the the RSV antibody, the RSV vaccine (offered only in few Italian regions) and the pertussis vaccine, the latter being more familiar to many users and more widely available in Italy at the time of monitoring.

Finally, some comments expressed doubt or confusion about the consistency of institutional recommendations, highlighting discrepancies between national guidance and local implementation, or perceiving a lack of official endorsement from the National Health Authority.

Discussion

The introduction of new immunoprophylaxis strategies for RSV, namely maternal vaccination and monoclonal antibodies for newborns, brings both opportunities and challenges for public understanding and acceptance. While surveys remain the primary tool for assessing attitudes toward immunization, social media – where questions, doubts, and institutional messages converge – offers a valuable complementary perspective. In this study, we monitored RSV conversations online, with most of the discussion focusing on Nirsevimab, which was introduced in Italy in November 2024. Despite some uncertainty and misinformation, we found that sentiment toward the monoclonal antibody was generally positive.

On X and Facebook, the majority of posts (85.7%) with a high visibility showed a neutral or promotional stance, and only a limited amount of content opposed or discouraged the intervention. A similar tone was observed in the comment sections of videos on TikTok and Instagram, where many users showed a general awareness of RSV risk in early infancy and, consequently, a strong, proactive intent to access the monoclonal antibody – even in regions where it was not yet available. The favorable perception of RSV immunoprophylaxis was frequently accompanied by recurring requests for clarification, particularly regarding eligibility, access, safety, and the relationship between different RSV prevention options. This highlights the importance of listening to identify doubts and information gaps, and of developing proactive communication to address them and anticipate emerging concerns, which is essential to sustain public trust.

The high level of interest and willingness observed on social media aligns with survey data from several European and North American countries, which show strong parental acceptance of RSV immunoprophylaxis, including both monoclonal antibodies and maternal vaccination. Studies in Spain,10 the UK,11 the Netherlands,12 France,13 the US,14 and Canada15 consistently reported high willingness to vaccinate or administer monoclonal antibodies to infants, typically ranging from around 70% to over 90%. In Italy, a cross-national survey conducted in 2021 found that 63% of respondents considered it very likely – and 35% somewhat likely – that they would accept immunoprophylaxis if recommended by a healthcare professional and included in the national programme.16 More recently, a multicentre survey in the Emilia-Romagna region during the 2024–2025 season reported an 87% acceptance of Nirsevimab.17 Data on the clinical impact of these interventions further support this widespread acceptance. A Spanish case-control study18 found that a universal approach with Nirsevimab reduced RSV hospitalizations, ICU admissions, and mechanical ventilation by about 80%, with slightly lower effectiveness in at-risk groups. Similarly, the HARMONIE trial19 in France, Germany, and the UK reported an 87% reduction in RSV hospitalizations through six months of age. In Italy, real-world data from the Valle d’Aosta region showed that none of the infants immunized with Nirsevimab were hospitalized for RSV bronchiolitis, compared with 8.3% of those who were not immunized.20

Many of the positive or neutral posts recorded in the online conversation were supported by news featuring physicians or representatives of scientific societies who were interviewed about the risks of RSV infection in young infants or the roll-out of immunoprophylaxis interventions in different Italian regions. These consistently factual messages, often tied to the promotion of specific regional or local campaigns, helped shape the conversation’s agenda by providing timely and authoritative information. This structured approach to information dissemination aligns with WHO’s recommendations, which encourage institutions to maintain ongoing, fact-based communication about vaccines even outside emergency contexts, to build public confidence and resilience against misinformation.21 It also reflects a broader consensus, supported by experts and institutions such as the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security, that consistent and proactive engagement on social media is essential to sustain trust and to prevent information voids where misinformation can develop.22,23 Repeated exposure to accurate messages from trusted sources plays a key agenda-setting role,24 helping ensure that evidence-based narratives remain central in public discourse.

Notably, 17% of the promotional or neutral posts expressed distrust toward the government or health institutions. The issue seems connected to the initial management of RSV immunoprophylaxis in Italy. The Italian National Health Service (Servizio Sanitario Nazionale, SSN) is organized regionally, with 20 regions and two autonomous provinces responsible for financing and managing health services under national standards. In this context, on September 18, 2024, the government announced that the antibody would be offered free of charge only in regions that comply with national healthcare deficit recovery plans. These plans are designed to control spending in regions struggling with significant healthcare debt. Several southern regions, namely Lazio, Abruzzo, Molise, Campania, Calabria, Puglia, Sicilia, were initially excluded from access. Although the decision was reversed the following day to ensure nationwide access, it created initial asymmetries in availability and left a perception of inequality and institutional neglect. While trust in the monoclonal antibody itself did not appear to diminish, confidence in institutions may have been affected, which is crucial for the acceptance of preventive interventions.25,26 This situation underscores the importance of transparent and well-coordinated communication. Regular press briefings, proactive consultations with stakeholders, and consistent messaging across all levels of government are essential to ensure that policy decisions are well-explained, thus preventing misunderstandings that could undermine public trust.

A limited proportion of the content emerging from the monitoring addressed doubts, misconceptions, and potential misinformation or disinformation, which would be worth addressing should its circulation increase in future seasons. These content, together with the main questions and doubts identified through the analysis of comments on Instagram and TikTok videos, could be used to inform communication initiatives led by health institutions, scientific societies, and other stakeholders, both at the national and regional/local levels.

Among the misleading narratives circulating on Nirsevimab, one focuses on a supposed increased risk of mortality due to thrombosis. This claim has been amplified by a limited set of social media posts, which argued that the drug provides only a modest reduction in hospitalizations while posing safety concerns and generating high costs for the health system. These narratives often cite a publication from Med Check Japan, a website that presents alternative interpretations of scientific studies through selective or questionable readings of the data, and appears to have characteristics of a low-credibility source. The report comments on three randomized controlled trials4,27,28 comparing Nirsevimab to placebo, focusing in particular on the Melody trial. While the original trials showed that Nirsevimab significantly reduced RSV-related lower respiratory tract infections when administered before the start of the RSV season, the Med Check Japan pointed out a higher mortality in the intervention group and suggested a potential link to thrombotic events. However, the five deaths among infants who received Nirsevimab were unrelated to the antibody and due to other causes, such as congenital heart disease, severe infections, or non-medical events. No thrombosis-related deaths were reported, and the original trial did not identify safety signals related to thrombosis, supporting the use of Nirsevimab as a preventive strategy against RSV in healthy newborns.

In terms of lower-risk disinformation, some users criticized the eight-euro compensation that pediatricians receive for administering the antibody, suggesting it could represent a conflict of interest. It is important to clarify that such compensation is a standard component of programmes funded by Regions to remunerate healthcare professionals for activities that go beyond routine clinical practice. Additionally, there were posts claiming that Nirsevimab might be given without obtaining parental consent, a point that could undermine trust in healthcare providers if left unaddressed. Although these narratives currently appear to have limited reach, they warrant continued monitoring and, where needed, targeted clarification – potentially also through local checks with vaccination providers to reassure families.

This study has several limitations. Data collection focused primarily on X and Facebook via the Pulsar platform, while comments from TikTok and Instagram were retrieved manually from selected popular videos, particularly those posted by med-influencers. In other words, while data collection on X and Facebook followed a systematic approach, the snowball sampling used for TikTok and Instagram may have limited the completeness of data from these platforms. Quantitatively, 9,374 posts from X and Facebook were identified and analyzed automatically by the Pulsar platform, with 1,472 (approximately 15%) included in the qualitative text analysis. On the other hand, 12 TikTok and Instagram videos were selected, and 1,649 comments were reviewed. Most comments came from videos posted by med-influencers, meaning the opinions expressed may reflect the views of followers who already trust these sources. This may have implications for the representativeness of the sample: in particular, perspectives from younger users – more active on TikTok and Instagram – might be underrepresented, along with doubts or misinformation circulating in those communities. Nevertheless, comments on popular videos remain a valuable source of insight, as they often capture users’ spontaneous questions, concerns, and opinions.

Only content in Italian was analyzed, restricting the findings to the national context. Moreover, the analysis covered a defined period (September 2024–January 2025), which may not reflect post-seasonal perceptions. This highlights the need for future longitudinal monitoring, which could also help capture changes in attitudes and the impact of policy decisions over time. No offline data sources were included, highlighting the need for future triangulation with surveys or qualitative research. Independent double coding of posts was not performed, although regular team discussions helped reduce potential classification bias. These meetings were frequent, allowed the review of a substantial number of doubtful posts, and were essential to ensure consistency in the identification of recurring narratives, assess their level of risk, and – most importantly – share interpretations of the results and develop both specific recommendations for the management of each narrative and general guidance for communication strategies. In the future, the potential use of large language models for these interpretative tasks may be considered, but would require careful validation and could not replace the collegial discussion of multidisciplinary teams.

Conclusions

This social media monitoring study provides an overview of how RSV immunoprophylaxis, particularly the introduction of Nirsevimab, was discussed and perceived in Italy during the 2024–2025 season. Overall, the conversation on major platforms was characterized by neutral or supportive tones, with many users showing a clear awareness of RSV risk in infancy and expressing a strong interest in accessing the monoclonal antibody, even in regions where it was not yet widely available. At the same time, recurring questions about eligibility, access, and safety highlight the importance of anticipating information needs through timely and coordinated communication to sustain public trust. Some narratives reflected concerns related to how the intervention was initially rolled out across Italian regions, underscoring the need for clear explanations of policy choices and consistent communication across national and regional levels to avoid perceptions of inequality. While only a limited number of posts conveyed clear misinformation or disinformation, such as unsubstantiated safety concerns, other content raised issues like perceived conflicts of interest, which also warrant monitoring and, where needed, targeted clarification. Taken together, these findings underscore how systematic social media analysis can provide timely insights into public perceptions of new immunoprophylaxis strategies, complementing traditional surveys and supporting efforts to maintain confidence in preventive interventions.

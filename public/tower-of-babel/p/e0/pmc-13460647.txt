RNA Extended Interventional Nucleic Acid (REINA) Longitudinal Study: Decreased Risk of CIN2+/CIN3+ With Low‐Grade Cytology After A Previous Negative HPV Test
Authors: Rosario Granados, Joanny A. Duarte, David R. Luján, Ana M. Gutierrez‐Pecharromán, Isabel Solís, Lourdes Molpeceres, Paloma Bajo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveTo analyse the impact of a previous negative mRNA HPV test in the immediate and 6 year risk of developing CIN2+/CIN3+ lesions in women with low‐grade (LG, including ASC‐US and LSIL) cytology in the second round of cotest screening.MethodsA prospective longitudinal study with LBC (ThinPrep, Hologic) and mRNA Aptima HPV test. Cotest was performed to 5053 asymptomatic women aged 25–65 years within an opportunistic screening programme in Madrid. Women with LG cytology, regardless of the mRNA HPV test result, were followed up (FU) to 10 years. The interventional arm ‘RNA‐based Extended Interventional Nucleic Acid (REINA)’ recruited 2000 women with negative results in the first round for a second cotest 4 years later, and were FU to 6 more years. The risk of a CIN2+/CIN3+ at 6 years after a first LG cytology was calculated.ResultsWith an unknown HPV history, 18.7% of mRNA HPV‐positive women at first cotest had LG cytology. Their immediate CIN2+ and CIN3+ risks were 24.7% and 12.8%, respectively, increasing to 31.8% and 17.0% after a 6‐year FU. The long‐term incidence of CIN2+ was strongly associated with mRNA HPV infection (p = 0.00001). From REINA, 20% of women with a new positive mRNA HPV result at second cotest had LG cytology, carrying much lower immediate and 6‐year cumulative CIN2/CIN3 risks (5.0% and 10.0% respectively).ConclusionsA previous negative Aptima mRNA HPV test confers a protective effect for CIN2+/CIN3+ in mRNA HPV‐positive women with minor cytological changes. The adjusted risks impact patient management, decreasing colposcopy referrals.

Introduction

The scientific evidence supports an essential role of persistent infections with oncogenic or high‐risk human papillomavirus (HR‐HPV) in the carcinogenesis of cervical cancer [1, 2].

HPV vaccination is highly effective in reducing the incidence of cancer in the following decades after implementation. However, it is not able to eradicate the disease unless it is complemented with screening for the early detection of precursor lesions and cancer in non‐previously vaccinated women. Both, HPV infection and an abnormal cytology study, are strong predictors of an elevated risk of precancer or cancer. Therefore, in women with a positive HR‐HPV test, cervical cytology is advised to determine the risk of precancer and the clinical management in most screening programmes [1, 3]. Genotyping, methylation analysis or dual p16/Ki67 staining are also considered as triage tools [4].

While women with a high‐grade cytological diagnosis, such as HSIL, ASC‐H or carcinoma, and those with a positive HPV test for the 16 or 18 genotypes should undergo colposcopy and cervical biopsy, most abnormal cytological results in screening programmes correspond to low‐grade (LG) cytological lesions such as ASC‐US or LSIL. The risk of precancer or cancer (CIN3+) in these women varies depending on the result of the HPV test. For those with a positive HR‐HPV test and an unknown or a positive previous HPV test, the risk ranges from 4% to 24% and the recommendation in the US is an immediate colposcopy [1].

Egemen et al. [5] using data from Kaiser Permanente of Northern California (KPNC) with over 1.5 million women screened with HR‐HPV and cytology cotest showed that the risk of CIN3+ lesions in women with unknown previous screening tests and an LG cytology result surpassed the threshold of 4% when there was a concurrent positive HPV test, recommending colposcopy versus follow‐up. However, the authors show a significant decrease in this risk with a documented previous HPV‐negative test within the past 5 years for all cytological diagnoses, changing clinical management except for women with HSIL+ results.

As primary HPV screening programmes are quite new in high‐income countries, there is scarce literature regarding the impact of a previous negative HPV test in the screening population. There is also a heterogeneity in the consideration of precancer lesions, and most authors consider CIN2+ as part of the endpoint for patient management. The 2019 ASCCP Risk‐based consensus guidelines do not provide enough information regarding women with these findings in screening.

In addition, there are many different HPV assays being used in multiple screening programmes actually in course and limited information in long‐term screening with mRNA‐based HPV tests.

We hereby report the impact of a previous negative mRNA HR‐HPV test in the immediate and in the 6‐year risk of developing a CIN2+ or a CIN3+ lesion in women with the result of ASC‐US/LSIL cytology in the second round of screening with cotest.

Methods

As previously reported [6] this is a prospective longitudinal study performed in the frame of an opportunistic screening programme in Madrid. Health care professional‐taken gynaecological samples were processed from 5053 asymptomatic women aged 25 to 65 years after obtaining their informed consents. Cytology (ThinPrep, Hologic) and mRNA HPV testing (mRNA Aptima HPV Assay, Hologic) were entirely performed in the Pathology Department of the Hospital Universitario de Getafe in Madrid from 2014 to 2016. Study protocols were approved by the hospital's Ethics Committee (A10/16). Cytology reports used the 2014 Bethesda System for reporting cervical cytology [7]. Women with a cytological diagnosis of atypical cells of undetermined significance (ASC‐US) or low‐grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL) in the first cotest, regardless of the mRNA HPV test result, were followed up to 10 years. An interventional arm, named RNA‐based Extended Interventional Nucleic Acid (REINA) study, was designed to analyse the negative predictive value (NPV) of the tests, and 2000 women with negative results in the first round were sequentially recruited for a second cotest 4 years later and then followed up to six more years. Cytology results of ASC‐US or LSIL were denominated ‘low grade’ for the study. Since late 2022, women were followed with a cotest a year later if an mRNA HPV‐positive test coexisted or 3 years later with a negative mRNA HPV test in accordance with the Spanish guidelines for clinical management after screening [8]. Previously, women with a cytological diagnosis of LSIL underwent colposcopy and biopsy or endocervical curettage, and those with ASC‐US were followed up at 1 year with cytology. Double‐negative cases returned to routine screening. The end‐point diseases were CIN2+ and CIN3+ squamous lesions, considered baseline within 12 months from initial cotest.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables are presented as absolute and relative frequency. Cumulative incidence of CIN2+/CIN3+ at 6 years after a first LG cytology result was calculated with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and was plotted stratified by known negative mRNA/unknown previous history of HPV testing. The unknown history included any HR‐HPV test, either mRNA or DNA‐based.

Time to event was calculated as the difference between the diagnosis date and the first cotest with the LG result performed. Time to censor was calculated as the difference between the date of the most recent FU result and the first cotest on women with LG cytology result. A Cox proportional hazards regression model was performed with a categorical variable for the known negative mRNA/unknown previous history of HPV testing, and hazard ratios and 95% CIs were calculated. All statistical tests and 95% CIs were two‐tailed and performed at the 5% significance level, using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Results

The rate of LG cytological lesions in our screened population of 5053 women was 2.9% (n = 149), being 1.9% ASC‐US (n = 97) and 1.0% LSIL (n = 52).

In the first cotest, from 454 women with positive mRNA HPV results, 85 (18.7%) had LG cytology, 46 corresponding to ASC‐US and 39 to LSIL diagnoses (Figure 1). Only 1.4% (64 cases) of mRNA HPV‐negative women (n = 4599) had an LG cytology test, with 51 ASC‐US and 13 LSIL diagnoses. Therefore, 47.4% (46/97) of cases with ASC‐US in cytology were associated with a positive mRNA HPV test, in contrast to 75.0% (39/52) of LSIL cases (p = 0.002 at p < 0.05 significance).

In the REINA interventional arm for a second cotest, 34 patients were excluded for inadequate FU, leaving 1966 women for the study. From those, 100 women had turned mRNA HPV‐positive and 20 (20.0%) of them also had an LG cytology. From the remaining 1866 mRNA HPV‐negative women, only 15 (0.8%) had abnormal LG cytology (Figure 2).

Study flowchart with 6‐year FU information in women with LG cytology after first round with cotest and in REINA interventional arm after second cotest 4 years later (total FU of 10 years in REINA). LG: Low‐grade cytology, meaning ASC‐US or LSIL. Cyt: Cytology.

Along the 10‐year FU of women with LG cytology, 32.9% (28/85) of those with mRNA HPV‐positive tests and 4.7% (3/64) of women with mRNA HPV‐negative cases developed CIN2+ lesions (14 CIN2/14 CIN3 and 2 CIN2/1 CIN3, respectively) (p = 0.001). No carcinomas were observed.

During the first 6 years (data reflected in Figure 2), we recorded precancer lesions in 31.8% of mRNA HPV‐positive women, in contrast to 3.1% in mRNA HPV‐negative cases (p = 0.00001). There were no women with invasive carcinoma.

REINA showed that none of the 1866 participants with a negative mRNA HPV test at the first and second rounds had a CIN2+ lesion after a 6 year FU from the second cotest, including those women with an LG cytology in their second round.

The 6‐year cumulative risk of CIN2+ with LG cytology and a positive Aptima HPV test at first cotest (Table 1) with unknown previous history of HPV testing was 31.8%. However, in the REINA study, this risk dropped to 10.0%, having had a negative Aptima HPV test 4 years before the positive mRNA HPV test in the second round, resulting in a 73% protection. Immediate CIN2+ and CIN3+ risks also dropped from 24.7% and 12.8%, respectively, in women with unknown HPV history to 5.0% when there was a previous negative mRNA HPV result within the REINA study, causing an 83% protective effect.

Discussion

Minor cytological changes are associated with HR‐HPV in a variable percentage and most women are referred to colposcopy. However, it is known that most (up to 90%) of these patients will clear the infection within a few months and low‐grade lesions may regress spontaneously [9].

In our study, 2.9% of the screened population had either ASC‐US or LSIL by cytology, being 1.9% ASC‐US and 1.0% LSIL diagnoses. These percentages vary geographically, are age‐dependent, and are usually higher (7%–8%) in the literature [7, 9].

Of our 149 cases with LG cytology, 57% of them showed a positive Aptima HPV test. We found a significant (p = 0.002) difference (47.4% vs. 75%) in the association of ASC‐US and LSIL cytology results with a positive Aptima HPV test. However, for practical purposes, we joined both diagnoses in the study as LG since the clinical management is the same following the Spanish guideline recommendations [8].

The HR‐HPV prevalence in LG cytology cases reported in the literature is similar to ours, being 52.1% for ASC‐US and 75.2% for LSIL in a systematic review with DNA‐based tests [10].

Aptima mRNA HPV test has shown a good correlation with DNA‐based assays in women with LG cytology [11, 12, 13]. Tewary et al. reported a 53.0% prevalence of the Aptima mRNA HPV test and a 58.2% prevalence with the Cobas 4800 HPV DNA‐based test in a referral population with LG cytology (n = 562) in Ireland. Similar findings, comparing both assays, have been reported from a larger sample (n = 988) in women referred for colposcopy in the US with ASC‐US in cytology [12].

However, the ASCCP established the principle of ‘equal management of equal risks’ determining the immediate risk of HSIL/CIN3+ based on the result of the HPV test, the cytology study and the previous related personal history [5]. A consensus threshold for colposcopy referral varies, relying on different settings and resources among countries. Most women with ASC‐US or LSIL and an HR‐HPV infection are referred to colposcopy since the literature outlines immediate CIN3+ risks ranging between 4% and 24% [1].

Egemen et al. [5] reported an immediate and a 5‐year risk of CIN3+ of 4.4% and 7.3%, respectively, in women with ASC‐US, an HPV‐positive test and an unknown HPV history from the KPNC screening population in the US. Similar values were seen for LSIL.

We found higher risks of CIN3+ in our study (12.8% immediate and 17.0% at 6 years), probably related to the higher specificity and positive predictive values of Aptima mRNA tests compared to DNA‐based HPV tests. The E6 and E7 oncogenic regions targeted by the mRNA test are involved in carcinogenesis of the epithelial cells, being responsible for the higher specificity and prediction of preneoplastic and neoplastic lesions [13].

Johansson et al. [14] found a 29.0% and a 34.3% risk of CIN2+ 4.5 years after a positive DNA and RNA HPV test in a retrospective study of samples with ASC‐US and LSIL cytology diagnoses.

Tewary et al. [11] found equivalent sensitivity of both tests and a superior specificity (p = 0.0004) of Aptima for the detection of a CIN2+ lesion when compared to Cobas 4800 DNA‐based tests. They reported an immediate rate of 37.8% cases of CIN2+ and of 16.3% of CIN3+ lesions in 325 women with LG cytology and positive HPV with colposcopy and biopsy. These values are similar to the ones we found in our study with an unknown previous HPV test.

Persson et al. [15], in their longitudinal study with a 4‐year FU, found that Aptima had higher sensitivity to predict CIN2+ lesions than other triage strategies for women with either ASC‐US or LSIL cytology, including HPV 16/18 DNA testing or repeat cytology. Their rate of CIN2+ lesions for either ASC‐US or LSIL cases without a known previous HPV test was 36%.

On the other hand, Del Pino et al. [16] found only a 7.2%–13.4% progression to CIN2+ after 19.1 months of FU of the referral population with positive HPV (Hybrid Capture‐2) and LG cytology.

The cumulative risk of CIN3+ after a 4‐year FU of women with HPV‐positive tests (HC‐2 from VUSA‐Screen trial and GP5+/6+ from POBASCAM trial) and LG cytology in The Netherlands screening programme is reported to be 19.4%. They also report a two‐fold increase in colposcopy referrals after implementation of primary HPV screening with cytology triage [4].

Egemen et al. also reported the decrease from 4.4% to 2.0% and from 7.3% to 3.8% in immediate and 5‐year CIN3+ risks when women had a history of a negative previous HPV test. These findings are part of the ASCCP risk‐based management consensus guidelines and the 2020 American Cancer Society guidelines [5].

The REINA study reveals that a negative mRNA Aptima test 4 years before confers a 63% protection against harbouring a CIN3 lesion, measured by the immediate risk, compared to women without HPV history (5.0% vs. 12.8%). A 44.4% protection (from 10.0% to 17.0%) is also seen after a 6‐year FU. When we address the CIN2+ end‐point, the protection of a previous Aptima negative test increases to 83% for the immediate (5.0% vs. 24.7%) and to 73% for the 6‐year risk (10.0% vs. 31.8%) in women with an unknown previous HPV test.

The presumption is that a positive HR‐HPV test (either DNA or mRNA‐based clinically validated assay) after a previous negative one indicates either a new infection or a reappearing one after a latent phase. Both situations carry a lower risk of precancer or cancer than a persistent HR‐HPV infection.

The Spanish guidelines for cervical cancer screening [8] recommend colposcopy when the immediate risk of a CIN3+ lesion is > 5%.

Therefore, this study reveals how a previous negative mRNA HPV test modifies patient management from colposcopy referral to a 1‐year FU cotest (Figure 4). In the context of a large‐scale screening programme, this adjustment has an important impact in the workflow, reducing unnecessary invasive tests and overtreatment as well as reducing costs.

We view as one of the strengths of this study the importance of the applicability of the results in the conception of screening programmes as well as in the selection of the appropriate HPV assays for their design.

The study adds relevant data to the scarce existent literature regarding the value of knowing a previous HPV test result in screening populations with minor cytological abnormalities. In particular, there are few reports providing information when mRNA‐based HPV tests are used for screening. Moreover, REINA offers valuable longitudinal data on a large number of mRNA HPV‐negative women, which are usually not the target of most published studies.

A weakness of this study is the limited sample of women with LG cytology and negative mRNA HPV tests, which precludes arriving at sound statistical conclusions in this group of women.

Conclusion

A previous negative Aptima mRNA HPV test provides a high protective long‐term effect, reducing the number of detected preneoplastic and neoplastic cervical lesions in women with minor cytological abnormalities. The decrease in the risk of disease allows a modification in the clinical management, reducing colposcopy referrals, unnecessary treatments and associated costs.

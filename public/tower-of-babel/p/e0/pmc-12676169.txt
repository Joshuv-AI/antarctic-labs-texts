The Role of Coding Accuracy in Enhancing TAVR Risk Adjustment
Authors: Omar Rizvi, Rachel M. Bond, Kerry A. Webb, Angelina R.R. Erwin, Jessica Parsons, Dan Inder S. Sraow
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe low rate of appropriate comorbidity documentation in transcatheter aortic valve replacement patients leads to incorrect Medicare Severity Diagnosis Related Group coding resulting in a skewed risk assessment and revenue loss.Project RationaleImproving capture of a specific comorbidity, heart failure, could result in improved perioperative risk assessment and improve revenue generated.Project SummaryA multidisciplinary team implemented a standardized form, tracking processes using a third-party registry vendor tool (hbCOR and hbRecon), and weekly/monthly reviews. This intervention significantly increased the accurate capture of heart failure and Medicare Severity Diagnosis Related Group 266 coding, resulting in an estimated $414K revenue increase over 6-month time frame and improved preprocedural risk evaluation, demonstrating that simple coding and documentation practices can substantially enhance both financial and clinical outcomes.Take-Home MessageA simple standardized form can improve comorbidity capture, leading to better perioperative risk evaluation and greater revenue generation.

An analysis of Chandler Regional Medical Center's (CRMC) Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) program with the American College of Cardiology (ACC)/Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry data revealed a low rate of heart failure (HF) documentation in the patient records. This deficiency in accurately capturing HF created 2 problems: 1) patients were being assigned to incorrect Medicare Severity Diagnosis Related Groups (MS-DRGs) for billing, leading to a substantial loss of revenue; and 2) inaccurate recording of comorbidities skewing evaluations of patients' preprocedural risk.Take-Home Message•By employing simple and effective coding and documentation practices, both financial and clinical outcomes can be substantially improved through proper reimbursement and risk evaluation.

Case Summary Prompting the Project Launch

Initial efforts were made to reconcile all outside documents to the appropriate folders within the electronic medical record. However, this effort only made a small shift in accounts assigned to the MS-DRG 266. This led to the creation of a multidisciplinary team that began the process of implementing a simple form to capture HF type and acuity for all TAVR patients.

Project Rationale

Recent studies have highlighted the narrow financial margins associated with TAVR procedures. Between 2016 and 2019, the median revenue was approximately $8,300, and the contribution margin (CM) was decreased by approximately $3,800 without significant changes.1

This study proposes a simple yet effective method to enhance coding accuracy, leading to a significant increase in revenue and improved cost-effectiveness for TAVR programs. This is achieved by correctly classifying patients into the appropriate MS-DRG 266, which accounts for acute and acute-on-chronic HF as a significant comorbidity.

Transitioning all of CRMC's TVT data to their third-party clinical registry vendor, Heartbase, they were able to use Heartbase's coding reconciliation and revenue generation tool, hbRecon. CRMC was able to identify the accuracy of risk assessment and MS-DRG 266 coding as an area for significant improvement. Between January 1, 2024, and September 3, 2024, 18.33% of our patients were coded into the 266 MS-DRG (case mix #1). In that same time frame, our HF capture rate in the data registry was 69.23%. Based on these figures, it was understood that there was ample opportunity for documentation improvement.

Project Description

A draft of the form shown in Figure 1 was brought to the Structural Heart Committee for review. Once the Structural Heart Committee approved the form, it was submitted to the Forms Committee for approval to use and become a permanent part of the chart. The Structural Heart Nurse Coordinators were at every TAVR case to ensure that the implanting physician filled out and signed the form. The Structural Heart Nurse Coordinators placed notes referencing relevant laboratory and echocardiogram results, including ejection fraction and any systolic/diastolic dysfunction, on the form. The data analyst tracked each patient from the form's initial use, using manual tracking and hbRecon to ensure comprehensive case capture. After 3 weeks of observing a marked improvement in coding and preprocedural risk, the form was expanded to include all TVT procedures. Ongoing monitoring of the HF capture rate and MS-DRG coding occurs weekly. Any cases necessitating further evaluation are directed back to the coding team. Project Deliverables

The form was implemented to increase the number of patients assigned to the MS-DRG 266 by increasing the number of patients coded with the major complication or comorbidity of acute or acute-on-chronic HF. Project Outcome

Since the implementation of the form on September 10, 2024, through March 2025, CRMC had a 52.1% coding rate to 266 MS-DRG and a 98.57% HF capture rate in the data registry (Figure 3, case mix #2). A chi-square analysis showed a statistical difference between major complication or comorbidity capture and DRG coding before and after form implementation with a P value of <0.0001 (Figure 3). By applying the 266 MS-DRG capture rate from case mix #2 to case mix #1 and using CRMC's Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services Base Rate, we estimated an increased revenue amount of $414K. The rolling 3-year, 30-day mortality observed-to-expected ratio for CRMC from July 2023 to June 2024 was 1.21, with the 50th percentile at 0.98. After 3 weeks of using the form, the observed-to-expected ratio on the report for October 2023 to September 2024 dropped to 1.16, with the 50th percentile still maintaining at 0.98. Then, after using the form for a full quarter, the observed-to-expected ratio dropped to 1.01 on the report for January 2024 to December 2024, with the 50th percentile increased slightly to 0.99.Figure 3Comparison of TAVR Cases Identified With MCC Before and After Implementation of the New FormThis graph represents the difference in the percent of TAVR cases that were identified with the appropriate coding accounting for the patient's MCC. This project shows how simple process changes can improve patient risk evaluation and contribute to hospital revenue generation. The next step for us will be to expand this process to the cardiovascular surgery program where surgeons are already familiar with the TAVR forms. In our system, since 2023, there have been 23 missed opportunities of proper HF documentation highlighting areas of potential revenue loss and improper perioperative risk evaluation.

Discussion

This initiative highlights how active engagement from a multidisciplinary team at each stage of a seemingly simple process can yield positive outcomes. By involving the structural heart coordinators at the outset of the documentation process and streamlining the form, the data manager was able to validate the HF type and acuity in real time. This real-time validation also facilitated the identification of missed opportunities in coding HF acuity and type, both of which are essential for appropriate MS-DRG 266 assignment.

Although prior research has shown that these procedures do not typically generate a high financial margin,1 this initiative demonstrates that when gaps in coding and billing are understood, simple improvements can be implemented. The greatest challenge remains in consistently identifying and monitoring such opportunities. Success requires dedicated personnel who can validate each case, collaborate with the coding team to ensure alignment with coding rules, and regularly use the hbRecon tracker to capture opportunities that might otherwise be overlooked.

The impact of this pilot study is reflected in the improved observed-to-expected ratio of death, which ultimately is a reflection of the risk of the procedure. Enhanced perioperative risk evaluation is vital to any procedure, and in this context, it has and can further help physicians more closely evaluate patients before TAVR. This study highlighted HF as a frequent missed diagnosis in the context of difficulty abstracting appropriate coding by acuity and type, which is not to say that the structural heart team was unaware of the diagnosis. It simply reflects the lack of proper terminology so as to abstract that data for the appropriate MS-DRG coding. Further, it highlights how this intervention might be able to expand and address other inaccuracies in charting to further improve perioperative risk evaluation. Given the positive results of this pilot, expanding implementation to other cardiovascular teams and programs represents a natural next step, with the ultimate goal of improving documentation practices across the entire facility.

Lastly, to address concerns regarding “upcoding,” we want to stress that upcoding implies an unjustified increase in the severity of diagnosis for patient conditions. Our study very specifically discusses the mismatch between our coded diagnoses and what the registry was already capturing. Our intervention was aimed at alleviating this mismatch to improve the accuracy of our coding to match our patient characteristics. This is reflected in the improved ratio of our observed to expected results.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study, given its pilot nature. The observed reduction in the observed-to-expected ratio is multifactorial and may reflect both a decrease in observed mortalities and an increase in expected mortalities. This initiative was not designed or powered to demonstrate causality between the intervention and improvements in the observed-to-expected ratio. A larger, multivariable analysis will be necessary to further validate these findings.

Conclusions

A retrospective look at our MS-DRG and capture of HF led us to collaborate with a multidisciplinary team to create a simple form and weekly/monthly review process that has helped significantly improve the capture of HF, the correct classification of MS-DRG 266, and improve capture of appropriate reimbursement.

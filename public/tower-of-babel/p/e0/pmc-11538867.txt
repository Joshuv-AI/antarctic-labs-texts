Integrative multiomics analysis identifies key genes regulating intramuscular fat deposition during development
Authors: Jinmei Zhu, Richun Cai, Yang Yu, Yongli Wang, Maiqing Zheng, Guiping Zhao, Jie Wen, Shubai Wang, Huanxian Cui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntramuscular fat (IMF) content is an important indicator of livestock and poultry meat quality. Enhancing IMF deposition can significantly improve meat quality. Focusing on the core process of IMF deposition, this study used the Jingxing Yellow (JXY) chickens as a model organism and employed multi-omics approaches, including RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq), Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS), and metabolomics, to identify the key genes influencing IMF deposition in chickens during development. The results indicated that the contents of triglycerides (TG) and phospholipids (PLIP) exhibited an upward trend. The TG content did not differ significantly between day 1 (D1) and day 7 (D7), but increased significantly after 35 days (D35) of age. The WGBS results revealed that CpG methylation was the predominant methylation type in the breast muscle tissue of JXY chickens. Integrative analysis of RNA-seq and WGBS identified 50 genes, including PLA2G4F, PALMD, PLSCR5, ARHGEF26, LUM, DCN, TNRC6B, CACNA1C, ROBO1, and MBTPS2, whose methylation levels were significantly negatively correlated with their expression levels. In addition, the combined Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of differentially-expressed metabolites (DEM) and differentially-expressed genes (DEG) converged on the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway, which was significantly enriched in DEGs such as PLA2G4F, PLA2G15, LPIN1, MBOAT2, DGKH, AGPAT2, and CHKA, as well as DEM like glycerophosphocholine and phosphocholine. Notably, PLA2G4F was identified as a DEG by DNA methylation, suggesting that PLA2G4F could be a key candidate gene influencing IMF deposition during chicken development. These findings are expected to provide a solid theoretical foundation for improving meat quality through targeted genetic and epigenetic interventions.Key words: chicken, intramuscular fat, developmental stage, regulatory gene, DNA methylation

INTRODCUTION

Regulation of lipid metabolism and deposition plays a crucial role not only in human obesity but also in livestock and poultry production (Warzych et al., 2021). Intramuscular fat (IMF) affects meat quality and serves as a crucial indicator for evaluating the quality of livestock and poultry meat (Zhang et al., 2023). Increasing IMF content can enhance meat quality (Yu et al., 2023). Therefore, the regulatory mechanisms of IMF deposition have been a focal point in poultry genetic breeding research aimed at improving IMF levels and meat quality (Zhang et al., 2018). Lipids are primarily stored in the form of triglycerides (TG), and their formation is a complex process involving numerous genes and metabolic pathways (Gai et al., 2023a).

With the continuous advancement of sequencing technologies, various molecular mechanisms regulating IMF deposition have been elucidated (Kang et al., 2021), and several key genes influencing IMF deposition, such as PPARG, FASN, and SLC16A7, have been identified (Wang et al., 2022). RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has been widely applied in poultry genetic breeding (Zhao et al., 2022), for instance, to study fat deposition and development in chicken embryonic adipocytes (Zhuo et al., 2015). A study using transcriptome analysis that examined the differentially expressed genes (DEG) in the breast and thigh muscles of Beijing-you chicken at 42 and 90 days of age found that peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG) and its downstream target genes play a crucial role in regulating IMF deposition (Cui et al., 2018). Recent research on IMF generally focuses on 1 or 2 developmental stages, primarily emphasizing the screening of candidate genes and pathways (Xing et al., 2020). Investigating the formation of IMF and occurrence of deposition throughout the entire developmental process holds significant importance in livestock and poultry production.

The integration of multi-omics technologies, such as transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, is a crucial approach to understand the complex molecular mechanisms of important economic traits in domestic animals (Du et al., 2022). The integrative analysis of whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) and RNA-seq is an important approach to investigate the functional roles of DNA methylation (Lu et al., 2023), For example, a study used a combined transcriptome and DNA methylation to identify 4 genes CACNA1S, IGF2, MUSTN1, and TMEM8C, that influence muscle development and meat quality in yaks (Ma et al., 2019). Also in the context of chicken IMF development, DNA methylation was found to regulate the deposition of IMF by modulating COL6A1 expression (Zhang et al., 2020). Metabolomics has been widely used in animal genetics and breeding research (Liu et al., 2022), mainly focusing on investigating flavor compounds in chicken meat, which provides new insights into the metabolic regulation of flavor formation during chicken growth and development (Kong et al., 2022). This also lays a theoretical foundation for the genetic improvement of meat quality and flavor in broiler chicken (Liu et al., 2023a), a research integrated transcriptomics and metabolomics data to identify critical metabolic pathways involved in flavor formation in the breast muscle of Beijing-You chickens, as well as several key metabolites and regulatory genes affecting meat quality and flavor, including glycine, serine, GATM, GAD2, and PNPLA6 (Gai et al., 2023b). Overall, intramuscular fat deposition is a complex biological process involving various biological pathways and regulatory mechanisms. Integrating data from different omics approaches can provide a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamic changes in this process, enhance the accuracy of gene identification, and reveal the interactions among genes, metabolites, and epigenetic modifications, thereby deepening our understanding of the regulatory mechanisms governing intramuscular fat deposition.

This study used Jingxing yellow (JXY) chickens at 7 different developmental stages. Through integrative analyses including RNA-seq, WGBS, and metabolomics, the primary goal of this study was to identify key genes regulating IMF development from the perspective of age-related development and epigenetics. Through multi-omics integrative analysis, we can thoroughly dissect the key signaling regulatory pathways from multiple levels, thus providing an effective method for large-scale identification of new biological regulatory networks. This approach can ultimately help us fully understand the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms underlying IMF deposition in chickens.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Experimental Material

The JXY chickens of different ages used in this study were obtained from the Institute of Animal Sciences of Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Beijing, China), raised under the same conditions and fed the same diet. Samples were collected at the following developmental stages: embryonic day 15 (E15, n = 8), day 1 (D1, n = 7), day 7 (D7, n = 4), day 35 (D35, n = 5), day 63 (D63, n = 5), day 91 (D91, n = 4), and day 119 (D119, n = 5).

Phenotypic Measurements

The chickens were stunned by electric shock, sacrificed by cervical dislocation at the different developmental stages. Breast muscle samples were collected, and the left breast muscle was dissected, with connective tissue and visible blood vessels being removed. The samples were then homogenized, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80°C for subsequent RNA-seq, WGBS, metabolome, and biochemical phenotype analyses. To determine phenotypic traits associated with IMF, the TG, phospholipid (PLIP), and total cholesterol (TCHO) contents were measured in breast muscle tissues collected at the 6 different developmental stages: D1, D7, D35, D63, D91, and D119. Approximately 2 g of homogenized breast muscle samples from JXY chickens were used to determine the TG, PLIP, and TCHO contents using the corresponding detection kits (Nanjing Jianchen Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China; Beijing leichuang Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China).

RNA Extraction and Sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from the breast muscle tissue samples using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). The quality of the RNA was assessed after separation by electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel, and the RNA concentration was determined by spectrophotometry using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The RNA-seq was performed by Beijing Guoke Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) using the Illumina (PE 150) platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The sequencing workflow included total RNA sample quality assessment, mRNA enrichment, double-stranded cDNA synthesis, end-repair, a-tailing and adapter ligation, fragment size selection and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification, library quality control, and Illumina sequencing. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome using HISAT2, and the mapped reads were then assembled and quantified using String Tie (Zhu et al., 2024).

DNA Extraction and Whole Genome Bisulfite Sequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted from the breast muscle tissue samples using the standard phenol/chloroform extraction method, The quality of the extracted DNA was evaluated by spectrophotometry using a Nanodrop ND-1000 Spectrophotometer to determine the concentration and integrity, as well as by agarose gel electrophoresis to visually assess DNA integrity (Liu et al., 2023b). The WGBS was performed by Beijing Borui Yisheng Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The samples used for methylation sequencing were consistent with those used for the transcriptome analysis. The sequencing workflow consisted of genomic DNA extraction and quality assessment (Burger et al., 2013; Kim et al., 2022), DNA fragmentation and purification, end-repair and A-tailing, adapter ligation, bisulfite conversion, fragment size selection, PCR amplification, library quality control, and Illumina sequencing. The Bismark methylation extractor module was used to call methylation levels in CpG, CHG, and CHH contexts (H = A, C, or T) (Lin et al., 2023). The Bismark2 report function was used to generate an integrated report, including read alignment statistics, methylation extraction, deduplication, nucleotide coverage, and M-bias analysis (Krueger and Andrews, 2011).

Extraction of Metabolites and Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry Analysis

A100-μL aliquot of the sample was transferred into a preprepared 1.5-mL centrifuge tube. Then, after adding 300 μL of methanol and 20 μL of internal standard to the sample-containing tube, the mixture was thoroughly mixed by vortexing, and incubate in an ice-water bath for over 10 min. Subsequently, after sonication, the sample was allowed to stand for 1 h at -20°C, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. After centrifugation, 200 μL of the supernatants was transferred to 2-mL sample vials. A quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining 20 μL from each sample. In addition, another 200 μL of the supernatants was transferred to 2-mL sample vials for instrumental analysis. The liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) system used for the metabolomics analysis consisted of an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) instrument and a high-resolution mass spectrometer, both purchased from Waters Corporation (Milford, MA, USA. The mobile phases used in both positive and negative ionization modes were 0.1% formic acid in water and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (Chong and Xia, 2018). Metabolomics sequencing at different time points was performed by Beijing Baimaike Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), as previously described (Chen et al., 2013).

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes Pathway Enrichment Analysis

The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of the identified DEGs was performed using KOBAS 3.0 (http://bioinfo.org/kobas/kobas3) to identify for significantly enriched pathways.

Time-Series Expression Profile Clustering

The Short Time-series Expression Miner (STEM) software, a Java-based computational tool, can be used to analysis of short-term time series gene expression data. The STEM software supports various functionalities, such as clustering, comparative analysis, and data visualization, which can be applied to gene expression profiles obtained from experiments with a limited number of time points (typically up to 8 time points). The STEM software enables the identification of significant temporal expression patterns and the corresponding DEGs associated with these patterns.

Analysis of Differentially Methylated Genes

After data alignment, the Bismark software was used to detect DNA methylation sites. Specifically, the detection of methylation sites was based on the nucleotides aligned to the reference genome. If the aligned base was a cytosine (C), it was considered a methylated site. Conversely, if the aligned nucleotide was a thymine (T), it was considered an unmethylated site. Based on the identified methylation sites, differentially methylated regions (DMRs) were detected. The differentially methylated sites and regions were then annotated, and differentially methylated genes (DMG) were identified based on the false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.5. These DMGs were found to be distributed across different functional regions of the genes, which can be categorized as either hypermethylated genes or hypomethylated genes according to the methylation levels. The methylation levels in different gene functional regions, such as promoters, gene bodies, and regulatory elements, can have varying regulatory effects on gene expression.

Statistic analysis of data

The statistical significance of the differences in means between groups was evaluated using the IBM SPSS Statistics software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The data are expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

RESULTS

The Developmental Patterns of TG, PLIP, and TCHO Contents in JXY Chicken

With increasing age, the TG and PLIP contents exhibited an upward trend in JXY Chickens. The TG contents at developmental stages D1 and D7 did not show significant differences (P > 0.05). However, the TG content increased significantly (P < 0.05) after D35. Specifically, the TG contents at D63, D91, and D119 were significantly higher than those at D1, D7, and D35. Instead, the TG contents at D63, D91, and D119 did not differ significantly from each other (P < 0.05) (Table 1). The content of PLIP at D35 was significantly higher than that at D1 and D7, and at D119 it was significantly higher than at D63 and D91 (P < 0.05). The content of TCHO peaked at D35, significantly exceeding the levels observed at D1 D7, D63, D91 and D119 (P < 0.05).

Identification of DEG at Different Developmental Stages

In this study, the content of TG significantly increases at D35, so we used the developmental stage D35 as the reference point to screen for DEGs before and after D35 (Figure 1) using the following criteria: fold change (FC) > 2 or FC < 0.5, and P < 0.05. Based on the DEGs, we identified 1,062 and 127 common DEGs expressed before and after D35 (Figure 2). After removing duplicate genes, we obtained 1025 and 90 DEGs that were differentially expressed before and after D35. In addition, we used the criterion of FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads) ≤ 1 to define nonexpressed genes and identify for genes that were differentially expressed before and after D35. We also identified 61 genes that had FPKM ≤ 1 before D35 and FPKM > 1 after D35 (Supplementary Table S1), and 252 genes that had FPKM > 1 before D35 and FPKM ≤ 1 after D35 (Supplementary Table S2), resulting in a total of 313 DEGs. By combining the results of the 2 differential expression analyses and removing any overlapping or redundant genes, we ultimately identified a total of 1,012 genes that were differentially expressed before and after D35.

STEM Analysis of DEG Expression Profiles

To perform differential expression analysis between adjacent time points, we used the STEM software to analyze the expression patterns of all DEGs across the comparisons. Specifically, we first took the union of all DEGs identified from the pairwise comparisons between adjacent time points to compile a comprehensive set of DEGs (Figure 3A). We then conducted trend analysis using the STEM software, which grouped genes with similar expression patterns into the same modules. Genes within the same module have a similar expression profile across the examined time points. Based on the dynamic expression changes observed across the 7 examined time points, the 10,799 identified DEGs were grouped into 20 distinct expression profile clusters using the STEM software. remarkably, the DEGs were significantly enriched in 4 specific expression profiles (Figure 3B). Noteworthy, 1,012 DEGs were primarily expressed in profile 4. The gene expression profile analysis revealed that the genes associated with profile 4 showed a gradual downregulation trend over time, while the genes in profile 14 showed a gradual upregulation trend. To further investigate the biological relevance of these expression patterns, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the genes within profiles 4 and 14. The genes in profile 4 were significantly enriched in signaling pathways related to muscle growth and development, suggesting that this set of genes may play a regulatory role in controlling muscle growth and development (Figure 3C). In contrast, the genes in profile 14 were primarily enriched in signaling pathways associated with lipid metabolism, indicating that this set of genes may be involved in the regulation of fat formation (Figure 3D).

Analysis of the Map of Gene Methylation

The correlation and principal component analysis (PCA) of the DNA methylation levels across the samples revealed high inter-sample correlations. The biological replicates showed excellent reproducibility, and the distinctions between sample groups were clearly evident (Figure 4). A statistical analysis of methylation levels in the 2KB upstream and downstream regions of genes across different age groups revealed that there was a difference in methylation levels between the upstream and downstream regions of genes. For instance, the methylation level was higher at D63 than at D7(Figure 5A and B). A comparison of methylation sites between groups revealed that CpG-type methylation levels were the main method of methylation and its levels were the highest (Figure 5C). Additionally, it was also found that the methylation level in the promoter region was lower than that in other functional regions (Figure 5D).

Identification of DMR and DMG

Based on the analysis of differentially expressed genes, D35 was selected as a pivotal time point, with 2 age groups chosen before and after this point. The D1 were excluded due to their unstable growth and development immediately after hatching. Additionally, since there were no significant differences in TG content after D63, D7 and D63 were selected for comparison.

In this study, the MethylKit software was used to perform the differential methylation analysis (Akalin et al., 2012). DMGs were identified using the criterion of FDR < 0.5. The comparison between the D7 and D63 samples revealed a total of 370,121 differentially methylated sites and 163,286 DMRs. Notably, a significant enrichment of differentially methylated sites was observed on chromosome 16 (Figure 6A), indicating that this genomic region may harbor important epigenetic signatures associated with age-dependent biological processes in chickens. The analysis of the DMRs identified in this study revealed that the 3 kb promoter regions surrounding these DMRs contained annotations for a total of 14,696 genes. Among them, 9,817 genes were found to be upregulated, while 4,879 genes were downregulated (Figure 6B).

Integrative Analysis of WGBS and RNA-Seq Data

A correlation analysis between the overall DNA methylation levels in the D7 and D63 chicken breast muscle promoters and their gene expression levels conducted in this study revealed that the overall DNA methylation levels of the D7 and D63 breast muscle promoter regions were significantly negatively correlated with the gene expression levels (Figure 7A). In addition, according to the transcriptome analysis results for different periods, the combination of DEGs screened before and after D35 with the whole genome DNA methylation sequencing results identified 283 DEGs regulated by methylation in the promoter region (Figures 7B and C). Correlation analysis performed between the DNA methylation levels and expression levels of the 283 DEGs in the promoter regions revealed that the methylation levels in 50 genes, including PLA2G4F, PALMD, PLSCR5, ARHGEF26, LUM, DCN, TNRC6B, CACNA1C, ROBO1, and MBTPS2, were significantly negatively correlated with their respective gene expression levels (Supplementary Table S3).

Identification of Differentially Expressed Metabolites

In the metabolomic data analysis, the selection of differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs) was primarily based on 3 parameters: variable importance in projection (VIP), FC, and P- values. The thresholds were set as follows: VIP > 1, P < 0.05, and FC ≥ 1.5 or ≤ 0.67. By performing differential analysis on the metabolomic profiles of the D7 and D63 chicken breast muscle samples, we identified a total of 336 DEMs (Supplementary Table S4). Among these, 279 DEMs were downregulated, while 57 were upregulated between the 2 developmental stages (Figure 8A).

Identifying Important Signaling Pathway and Key Genes by KEGG Pathway Enrichment Analysis of DEM and DEG

The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the DEGs revealed their significant enrichment in 36 signaling pathways, including ECM-receptor interaction, MAPK signaling, and glycolipid metabolism (Figure 8C). Similarly, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEMs showed their predominant enrichment in pathways related to glycerophospholipid metabolism, β-alanine metabolism, and sphingolipid metabolism (Figure 8D). The KEGG pathway enrichment analyses of both the DEGs and DEMs converged on the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway (Figure 9). The metabolites enriched in this pathway include such as Glycerophosphocholine, Phosphorylcholine and Citicoline. And the genes enriched in this pathway include PLA2G4F, PLA2G15, LPIN1, MBOAT2, DGKH, AGPAT2, and CHKA. Remarkably, PLA2G4F was identified as a gene that is epigenetically regulated by DNA methylation. Based on these findings, we propose that PLA2G4F is a key gene of IMF deposition in chicken breast muscle.

DISCUSSION

The meat of the Yellow-feathered chicken is renowned for its delicious flavor, making it a highly sought-after choice among consumers (Cui et al., 2023). Meat quality is a major factor that strongly influences consumer purchasing decisions (Sao Jose et al., 2023). IMF content is closely associated with meat quality, and increasing IMF levels can effectively improve the overall quality of chicken meat (Weng et al., 2022). TG and PLIP are the major constituents of IMF (Cui et al., 2022), and their synthesis plays a crucial role in the deposition of IMF. TG in particular is the most abundant component and is closely associated with lipid metabolism (Li et al., 2012), making it a representative indicator for assessing IMF content (Liu et al., 2019).Therefore, increasing the TG content is an important strategy for regulating IMF levels. In this study, we analyzed chicken breast muscle samples at 7 different developmental stages of JXY chickens using an integrated multi-omics analysis, including transcriptomics, WGBS, and metabolomics, to identified genes associated with IMF deposition during these developmental phases.

The contents of the major IMF components, TG and PLIP, were influenced by age, with their levels increasing as the chickens grew older. Moreover, we performed differential expression analysis between different growth stages and identified 1,012 DEGs that were differentially expressed around D35. The development of IMF is influenced by various factors, including genetics, environment and age (Albrecht et al., 2017), Furthermore, it is regulated by epigenetic mechanisms, such as DNA methylation, which is an important epigenetic regulatory mechanism (Wang et al., 2017). Although the mechanisms underlying IMF development have been extensively studied at the signaling pathway, transcriptional, and translational levels (Lee et al., 2019), research on the relevant epigenetic regulatory mechanisms remains limited. Linking DNA methylation levels to gene expression patterns can systematically and comprehensively reveal the epigenetic molecular regulation of DEGs (Lim et al., 2019). This study found that the methylation levels in CpG sites were higher than those in CHG and CHH sites at both D7 and D63 in the breast muscle tissue of JXY chickens, which indicates that CpG methylation is the predominant form of DNA methylation in the breast muscle tissue of this chicken breed. Furthermore, a comparative analysis of DNA methylation levels across different genomic functional elements revealed that the methylation levels in the promoter regions were markedly lower than those in other functional regions, both at D7 and D63. This may be attributed to the low methylation levels in the promoter region, which facilitate the maintenance of gene transcriptional competence, allowing cells to rapidly initiate gene expression when needed. DNA methylation in the promoter regions can influence gene transcription and expression. Through integrative analysis of WGBS and RNA-Seq data, we identified 50 DEGs that were transcriptionally regulated by DNA methylation. These candidate IMF regulatory genes, including PALMD, PLSCR5, ARHGEF26, LUM, DCN, TNRC6B, CACNA1C, ROBO1, and MBTPS2, showed a significant negative correlation between promoter methylation levels and expression levels. This is due to the possible suppression of gene expression through DNA methylation in the promoter regions. Therefore, we propose that the aberrant expression of these candidate IMF regulatory genes, due to methylation-mediated regulation, may contribute to the modulation of IMF deposition in the chickens. When a significant number of CpG sites in the gene promoter region are heavily methylated, transcription factors are unable to bind to the promoter, leading to transcriptional repression and subsequent suppression of gene expression. This alteration in gene regulation also affects the associated phenotypes (Kang et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2014).

Also, the KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of both DEGs and DEMs revealed a common enrichment in the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway. This pathway was found to be significantly enriched in many DEGs, including PLA2G4F, PLD4, PLPP3, LCAT, PLA2G2A, PLA2G5, PLA2G2E, ETNK2, PHOSPHO1, PLA2G4A, and AGPAT2, as well as in DEMs, such as glycerophosphocholine and phosphocholine. These findings suggest that the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway is an important mechanism underlying age-related changes in chicken breast muscle metabolism, indicating that methylation may not only affect gene transcriptional activity but also influence the deposition of IMF by regulating lipid metabolism-associated pathways. Notably, PLA2G4F was not only significantly enriched in the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway, but also identified as a DEGs that is regulated by DNA methylation. Therefore, PLA2G4F may be a key gene that influences the deposition of IMF during the developmental process. Relevant studies have shown that the downregulation of PLA2G4F can suppress the degradation of unsaturated fatty acids through multiple mechanisms. In particular, the downregulation of PLA2G4F can inhibit the activity of Acetyl-CoA dehydrogenase, lipoxygenase, and fatty acid dioxygenase, which are key enzymes involved in fatty acid catabolism and activate the PPARG signaling pathway and its downstream target genes (Ge et al., 2019). Also, PLA2G4F plays a crucial role in the development of fatty liver disease in Shitou geese by regulating the synthesis of various lipid metabolites, including TG, phosphatidic acid (PA), 1,2-diacylglycerol (DG), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), and phosphatidylcholine (PC). Specifically, the modulation of these lipid species by PLA2G4F can promote the deposition of TG in the liver, leading to the development of fatty liver disease in Shitou geese (Hong et al., 2024).

CONCLUSION

Our study revealed that PLA2G4F is a key gene that influences IMF deposition during development in chicken, which was found to be regulated by DNA methylation through the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway. We anticipate that the findings of this study will help to fully understand the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms underlying IMF deposition in chickens.

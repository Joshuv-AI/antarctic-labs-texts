Nickel(II)/BINOL-catalyzed enantioselective C–H activation via desymmetrization and kinetic resolution
Authors: Qi-Jun Yao, Fan-Rui Huang, Jia-Hao Chen, Bing-Feng Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The field of nickel catalysis has witnessed remarkable growth in recent years. However, the use of nickel catalysts in enantioselective C–H activation remains a daunting challenge because of their variable oxidation states, intricate coordination chemistry, and unpredictable reactivity patterns. Herein, we report an enantioselective C–H activation reaction catalyzed by commercially available and air-stable nickel(II) catalyst. Readily available and simple (S)-BINOL is used as a chiral ligand. This operationally simple protocol enables the synthesis of planar chiral metallocenes in high yields with excellent enantioselectivity through desymmetrization and kinetic resolution. Air-stable planar chiral nickelacycle intermediates are first synthesized via enantioselective C–H nickelation and shown to be possible intermediates of the reaction. Deuterium-labeling studies, alongside the characterization and transformation of chiral nickel(II) species, suggest that C–H cleavage is the enantio-determining step. Moreover, the large-scale synthesis and diverse synthetic transformations underscore the practicality of this protocol.

Introduction

Transition metal-catalyzed enantioselective C–H functionalization has become an increasingly powerful approach for the rapid access of chiral molecules from abundant and readily available hydrocarbon feedstocks1–8. Precious metal catalysts, in particular palladium catalysis, are still indispensable and dominate this research area, benefiting from the deep understanding of mechanism, the availability of a large libraries of chiral ligands, and the predictable and controllable reactivity and selectivity across a wide range of reactions5–8.

Nickel, lying just above palladium in Group 10 of the periodic table, is more earth-abundant and inexpensive than its noble cousins (Fig. 1a). More importantly, nickel catalysts also exhibit unique properties and reactivity, providing huge opportunities to discover new reactions that are challenging or inaccessible with precious catalysts9–15. The unique reactivity and selectivity have inspired intensified interest in Ni-catalyzed C–H activation16,17. However, the development of asymmetric variants remains underdeveloped. Thus far, the few success examples heavily relied on the specific substrates with activated C–H bonds, and the use of air-sensitive, volatile, and highly toxic Ni(COD)2 as catalyst, precluding the operation without a glovebox and extreme cautions16–28. In this context, the development of enantioselective C–H activation/desymmetrization using commercially available, air-stable, and therefore, operationally simple Ni(II) catalysts is highly attractive, but remains elusive. Several challenges exist that hinder the development of Ni(II)-catalyzed enantioselective functionalization of inactivated C–H bonds (Fig. 1b). First, most reactions require high reaction temperature, which are detrimental to stereo-induction16,17. Second, the oxidation states of Ni species span from 0 to +IV through both single- and two-electron redox processes, rendering the understanding of the mechanistic intricacies is particularly problematic29–32. Moreover, compared to the well-defined coordination pattern of palladium5–7, the coordination numbers of Ni(II) complexes are highly variable, which can be 4, 5, or 6-coordinate, leading to various coordination geometries, including square-planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, square pyramidal, and octahedral8–15,29–32. Here, we report the discovery of an easily accessible and simple chiral ligand, (S)−1,1’-bi-2,2’-naphthol (BINOL), for Ni(II)-catalyzed enantioselective C–H activation. This operationally simple protocol provides a straightforward access to planar chiral metallocenes in good yields with excellent enantioselectivities (Fig. 1c). Mechanistic studies give straightforward information on the reaction.

Results and discussion

Planar chiral ferrocenes are privileged skeletons for chiral ligands and catalysts in asymmetric synthesis and widely applied in pharmaceuticals and material science33–35. Recently, significant progress has been made towards the synthesis of planar chiral ferrocenes through enantioselective C–H activation36–44. We aimed to establish an unprecedented Ni(II)-catalyzed enantioselective C–H functionalization through desymmetrizing prochiral ferrocenes. We commenced our study by evaluating the Ni(II)-catalyzed enantioselective C–H alkynylation of ferrocene carboxamide (1a) with 1-bromo-2-(triisopropylsilyl)acetylene (2a) assisted by bidentate 8-aminoquinoline directing group (DG)45,46, which was found to be an efficient DG to facilitated Ni(II)-catalyzed C–H activation47–54. Taking into account that oxygen-donor ligands, such as dimethoxyethane (DME) and BINOL, have been shown to enhance Ni(II)-catalyzed C–H activation reactions in our previous research51,52, we first investigated a range of O-donor chiral ligands (Supplementary Table 1). To our delight, the combination of Ni(OTf)2 and (S)-BINOL (L1) gave the desired alkynylation product 3aa in 43% yield with 26% ee, and a dialkynylated product 4 was isolated in 7%55. After extensive screening of various parameters, including solvents, bases, additives, reaction temperature, and Ni(II) salts (Supplementary Tables 2–6), the optimized reaction conditions were established (Supplementary Table 6, entry 8): 2 mol% Ni(dppe)Cl2 and 4 mol% (S)-BINOL (L1) with LiOMe and 4 Å MS in CH3CN/1,4-dioxane at 70 oC under nitrogen atmosphere. The desired chiral ferrocenecarboxamide 3aa was obtained in 94% yield with 99% ee, and the dialkynylation product 4 was isolated only in 5%. It is worth noting that the reaction proceeded very efficiently and only 2 mol% Ni(II) catalyst and 4 mol% chiral ligand were used. The absolute configuration of 3aa (CCDC 2205637) was determined by X-ray diffraction analysis as “Rp”. A comprehensive comparison of the changes in chiral O,O-ligands is summarized in Fig. 2. The mono-methyl-protected (S)-BINOL (L2) and di-methyl-protected (S)-BINOL (L3) were inefficient, indicating that BINOL (L1) might act as a dianionic ligand in this reaction. Subsequent evaluation of BINOL-based ligands (L4-L6) with substituents at the 3,3’-positions or H8-BINOL (L7) led to a decrease in both reactivity and enantioselectivity. In addition, the use of alternative C2-symmetric chiral biphenol ligands, such as (S)-SPINOL (L8) and (S)−3,3,3’,3’-tetramethyl-SPINOL (L9), gave satisfactory results. However, considering the cost-efficiency and availability, (S)-BINOL (L1) was selected as the ligand of choice. With the optimized conditions in hand, the scope of the enantioselective C–H alkynylation was examined (Fig. 3). The TIPS group of alkyne bromides replaced by less sterically demanding group, such as TES, TBS, and t-Bu, still gave moderate to good yields and good enantioselectivities (TBS, 3ab, 90%, 99% ee; TES, 3ac, 92%, 99% ee; t-Bu, 3ad, 76%, 97% ee). Propargyl silyl ethers (2e and 2 f) were also compatible with this protocol, giving the desired products in decent yields with good ee values (3ae, R = CEt2OTBS, 71%, 91% ee; 3af, R = CPh2OTBS, 64%, 90% ee). Moreover, (bromoethynyl)benzene 2 g was identified as a viable alkynylation reagent, albeit with a slightly decreased yield (3ag, 45 % yield, 94 % ee). Ferrocenecarboxamides bearing diverse substituents, such as acyl (3ba-3da), alkyl (3ea-3ga), and vinyl (3ha-3ka), on the other Cp (cyclopentadienyl) ring reacted smoothly, giving the planar chiral products in moderate to good yields (56% to 95%) with excellent enantioselectivities (90% to >99% ee). The expected chiral product 3la bearing diphenylhydroxymethyl at the bottom Cp ring was delivered in moderate yield with 95% ee value. Notably, the protocol is also compatible with ruthenocenecarboxamide, furnishing 3ma in 91% yield with >99% ee. In order to shed light on the mechanism of this protocol, a series of mechanistic experiments were performed. First, the diastereoselective C-H alkynylation of ferrocenecarboxamide (Rp)−1v with preinstalled planar chirality57 in the presence of (S)-L1 gave 3va in 51% yield with 98% de, while the use of (R)-L1 led to the formation of the other diastereomer 3va’ in 47% yield with >99% de, indicating the preformed planar chirality does not affect the stereocontrol (Fig. 5a). Therefore, we envisioned that the diastereoselective H/D scrambling experiments of deuterium-labeled (Rp)-1v-d2 would gain straightforward information of whether C–H cleavage was the enantio-determining step (Fig. 5b). Here, “Rp” and “Sp” prochiral positions were respectively defined as “a” and “b”. When (Rp)-1v-d2 was subjected to the H/D exchange experiment in the presence of (S)-BINOL, a significant decrease of deuterated ratio at “a” position was detected (33% Ha) while the deuterated ratio of Hb maintained at 91%. In contrast, when (R)-BINOL was used as ligand, the deuterated ratio of Hb decreased to 59% while the deuterated ratio of Ha maintained. These results suggested that the C(sp2)–H cleavage is the enantio-determining step.Fig. 5Illustrating the enantio-determining step.a Effects of the preinstalled chiral moieties. b H/D scrambling experiments of (Rp)-1v-d2. The synthesis and characterization of possible nickel intermediates was then conducted. A diamagnetic complex Ni(dppe)(BINOLate) was synthesized by ligand exchange between Ni(dppe)Cl2 and deprotonated (S)-L1 (Fig. 6a). Ni(dppe)(BINOLate) was an air stable dark-green solid. The structure was unambiguously determined by X-ray diffraction analysis (CCDC 2257826). The four-coordinate Ni(II) complex adopts a square planar geometry with the Ni atoms bonded to two phosphorus atoms of the dppe moiety together with two oxygen atoms of the bidentate BINOLate ligand. Ni(dppe)(BINOLate) was catalytically active, leading to the formation of 3aa in 61% yield with 98% ee. As shown in Fig. 6b, the preparation of a pair of chiral nickelacycle intermediates stabilized by 4-pyrrolidinopyridine (4-PPy), (–)-C–Ni-1 and (+)-C–Ni-1, was achieved by the stoichiometric reaction of 1a with Ni(dppe)Cl2 in the presence of only catalytic amount of corresponding BINOL ligand (10 mol%). The opposite specific rotation and mirror relationships of circular dichroism (CD) spectra for (–)-C–Ni-1 and (+)-C–Ni-1 further proved that the planar chirality was generated via enantioselective C–H metalation (Fig. 6c). The stoichiometric reaction of (–)-C–Ni-1 suggested that the planar chiral nickelacycle might be involved in the reaction (Fig. 6d). Notably, an air-stable planar chiral nickelacycle (+)-C–Ni-2 was obtained in 47% yield at room temperature when DBU was introduced as a stabilizing neutral ligand (Fig. 6e). The square planar coordination of (+)-C–Ni-2 was confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis (CCDC 2205641), and the configuration of planar ferrocene moiety is well consistent with the alkynylation products. Based on these experimental results and previous reports29–32, a plausible catalytic cycle for the enantioselective C(sp2)–H alkynylation was proposed in Fig. 7. Initially, divalent nickel catalyst coordinates with (S)-BINOLate and gives the chiral Ni(II)(BINOLate)L2 catalyst int-1. Ligand exchange with ferrocenecarboxamide 1a, accompanied by deprotonation by LiOMe, delivers nickel complex int-2. Subsequent enantio-determining C(sp2)–H cleavage occurrs through a concerted metalation-deprotonation (CMD) process facilitated by the oxygen of BINOLate, leading to the formation of chiral Ni(II) pincer complex int-3 and extrusion of L1. Oxidative addition followed by reductive elimination gives the planar chiral product 3aa, regenerating Ni(II) catalyst and completing the catalytic cycle. The isolation of key intermediates, including Ni(dppe)(BINOLate) and chiral nickelacycle complexes provided strong support to the proposed catalytic cycle.Fig. 7Plausible mechanism.Catalytic cycle for Ni(II)/BINOL-catalyzed enantioselective C–H functionalization.

To demonstrate the practicality and synthetic potentials, nickel(II)/BINOL-catalyzed enantioselective C–H alkynylation was conducted on a gram scale and both reactivity and enantioselectivity were well maintained [Fig. 8, (Rp)-3aa, 2.55 g, 95%, 99% ee]. Then, an important platform molecule (Rp)-5 was synthesized efficiently in gram scale (2.84 g, 94%, 99% ee). By activating the amides with tert-butyloxycarbonyl (Boc) group, the removal of directing group (DG) on planar chiral ferrocene 3aa was achieved and could be further transformed into diverse functionalized compounds (6-11). In summary, we report the enantioselective C–H activation protocol enabled by divalent nickel catalyst, providing a streamlined synthesis of planar chiral metallocenes in high yield with excellent enantioselectivity. The key to success lies in the utilization of easily accessible BINOL ligand, which facilitates the enantioselective C–H nickelation even at ambient temperature. More broadly, this study will pave the way for exploring new avenues in nickel-catalyzed asymmetric C − H activation reactions, and boost further development and application of 3d metal-catalyzed asymmetric C–H functionalization strategy.

Methods

General procedure for Ni(II)/BINOL-catalyzed enantioselective C–H alkynylation through desymmetrization

A 50 mL Schlenk tube containing 125 mg 4 Å MS was dried by heat gun under vacuum for 10 min. After cooled to room temperature, substrate 1 (0.50 mmol), Ni(dppe)Cl2 or Ni(OTf)2 (2 to 10 mol%), (S)-L1 (4–20 mol%), LiOMe (1.0 mmol, 2.0 eq) and alkynyl bromide 2 (0.75 mmol, 1.5 eq) were added to the Schlenk tube. Anhydrous MeCN (2.5 mL) and anhydrous 1,4-dioxane (2.5 mL) were then added. The tube was purged with N2 three times, and the mixture was stirred at 70 to 80 °C under N2 for 24 to 36 hours. Subsequently, the reaction mixture was diluted with dichloromethane and filtered through a pad of celite, which was washed with dichloromethane and ethyl acetate. The solvent was removed under vacuum and the resulting residue was purified by preparative TLC to afford 3. The results are provided in Fig. 3. The racemic product was synthesized following the procedure mentioned above, utilizing rac-BINOL (rac-L1) as the ligand.

General procedure for Ni(II)/BINOL-catalyzed enantioselective C–H alkynylation through kinetic resolution

A 50 mL Schlenk tube containing 125 mg 4 Å MS was dried by heat gun under vacuum for 10 min. After cooled to room temperature, substrate rac-1 (0.50 mmol), Ni(dppe)Cl2 (2 mol%), (S)-L1 (4 mol%), LiOMe (1.0 mmol, 2.0 eq) and bromoalkyne 2a (0.375 mmol, 0.75 eq) were added to the Schlenk tube. Anhydrous MeCN (2.5 mL) and anhydrous 1,4-dioxane (2.5 mL) were then added. The tube was purged with N2 three times, and the mixture was stirred at 60 °C under N2 for 36 hours. Subsequently, the reaction mixture was diluted with dichloromethane and filtered through a pad of celite, which was washed with dichloromethane and ethyl acetate. The solvent was removed under vacuum and the resulting residue was purified by preparative TLC to afford 3 and the recovered 1. The results are provided in Fig. 4. The corresponding racemic sample of product was synthesized according to aforementioned procedure by using rac-BINOL (rac-L1) as the ligand.

A switching-based deep learning framework for personalized and adaptive E-commerce recommendations
Authors: Kapil Saini, Ajmer Singh, Manoj Diwakar, Prabhishek Singh, Anchit Bijalwan, Guta Tesema Tufa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In recommendation systems, it is bit challenging to address diverse user profiles, particularly when users demonstrate varied interaction histories and preferences. These chalenges include data sparsity and the cold start problem which requires the innovative solutions to ensure precise and efficient recommendations. Cross-domain recommendations is yet another challenge posed by the diverse user profiles. When users have significantly varied the interests across different domains, recommending the relevant items that cater to their diverse choices becomes very complex. Personalization and diversity trade-offs are also very crucial, which require a trade-off between diverse recommendations and capturing the individuality of certain users. Another serious concern is the Real-time Adaptability because of user’s changing preferences and behaviours. Most of the existing systems can not dynamically adapt to these shifts and are also not capable of handling cross-domain recommendations which requires accurate modelling of the user’s multidimensional preferences to provide up-to-date suggestions. Our innovative system is designed to accommodate diverse user profiles based on their interaction history and engagement levels with the items. Considering the highly dynamic digital landscape, we’ve categorised users into three major groups, namely newbies, light users, and heavy users. This paper proposes an innovative approach within the recommendation systems domain by building switching-based hybrid recommendation systems to cater to the needs of different user profiles. By optimising multiple objectives, including abundant but less informative signals such as product views, the proposed system demonstrates improved performance across different user segments. Experimental results show a reduction in validation loss from 0.3414 to 0.1545 for absolute new users, improvements in HR@10 from 0.30 to 0.60 and NDCG@10 from 0.35 to 0.65 for light users, and strong predictive performance for implicit feedback with an accuracy of 0.91, precision of 0.89, recall of 0.88, and F1 score of 0.89. Experimental results indicate that, relative to the baseline methods, the proposed framework achieves a reduction of approximately 54.7% in validation loss for absolute new users, while HR@10 and NDCG@10 for light users increase by 100% and about 85.7%, respectively.

Introduction

It is impossible to emphasise the significance of the fact that individualised recommendation systems have gathered a large degree of popularity in the extremely dynamic terrain of modern digital platforms. This is something that cannot be overstated1. Typical recommendation systems, which employ collaborative filtering and content-based filtering techniques, are unable to capture the individual user preferences that are inherent in a wide variety of data sources and rapidly changing user behaviours with higher precision. This is because these systems are unable to take into account the fact that user preferences are always evolving. This is because these systems place a strong emphasis on the necessity of cooperating with one another and filtering content based on its characteristics. As a consequence of the rise of digital content, there has been a increase of options that are readily accessible to consumers2. Because of this, it is very difficult for the customers to identify and select the products as per their requirements. This is the reason why things are the way they are in the current situation. One of the most critical obstacles that needs to be addressed is the fact that users have a broad diversity of interaction and preferences3. This situation is one of the most important challenges. In recent years, a number of recommendation frameworks and algorithms have been developed; however, the selection of the most appropriate approach is largely determined by a variety of factors, including the types and nature of the data that is available, the level of engagement attained by the user, the distributional properties of the preferences of the user-item, the modalities that are taken into consideration, and the business use cases. There is no way for the bulk of the algorithms that are now being used to modify themselves in order to accommodate all of these qualities. This is because it is not possible for them to do so. Nevertheless, it seems that it is difficult to generalise performance to higher levels of user engagement, even for scenarios that are pretty basic. This is the case even for these situations. This is because they only use a single sort of interaction, which might be either unconditional or explicit feedback. Among various recommendation systems, the switching-based hybrid recommendation system stands out as a solution that is not only powerful but also flexible and individualised. This system is particularly significant because it emphasises the importance of individualization and flexibility. On the other hand, the hybrid switching-based systems that are now in use are unable to meet the individual requirements of users who have varying degrees of interaction with the products in a manner that is satisfactory. Methodology

Different user profiles, which are determined by the user’s level of involvement and their previous interaction history, are able to be accommodated by the system that have been suggested by us. In order to accommodate these user profiles, they are available. Taking into consideration the very dynamic nature of digital platforms such as websites that support online shopping, we have broadly split the users into three distinct groups, with the key distinction being based on the level of engagement they have with the platform. There are three types of users that are included in these groupings: newcomers, lightweight users, and heavy users. Two categories have been further differentiated into absolute newbie users and newbie users who give some type of implicit input, such as studying things or even purchasing stuff. Both of these categories have been further subdivided even more. Both of these groups are further segmented from the newbie category that was previously mentioned. Every one of these categories has its own one-of-a-kind set of obstacles and opportunities when it comes to the provision of recommendations that are not just pertinent but also customised. With regard to the category in question, each and every one of these qualities is exclusive. These issues are meant to be addressed by the switching hybrid recommendation system that we have proposed. This system is designed to integrate a wide variety of deep learning-based recommendation algorithms in a seamless manner. In addition to this, the system is built to ensure that the method is successful as well as adjustable to different circumstances. Absolute newbie users are those who are entirely new to the platform and have not yet interacted with any items. There is another segment of newbie users who have still not interacted with the items in an explicit way but provide their preferences in the form of implicit feedback. For such users, the recommendation process is highly challenging. Most of the conventional methods that use user-item interactions fail in these cases due to the absence of historical data. To address the cold start problem, we have implemented a solution by employing deep metric learning-based algorithms which leverage multimodalities present in the items.This user segment has engaged with the platform but has only limited interactions in terms of ratings. These lightweight users provide an opportunity to refine the recommendations leveraging their sparse interactions. The switching hybrid recommendation system can detect this and switch to a different algorithm that effectively utilizes the available sparse feedback. There are heavy users as well who have a rich history of interactions with the platform. This level of extensive engagement generates a wealth of data that can be utilised for precise recommendations. This work advances recommendation systems through three principal contributions: first, we introduce a CLIP-based multimodal fusion framework that effectively bridges the cold-start gap by jointly analysing visual and textual product features; second, we develop a dynamic switching mechanism that automatically selects between three specialised recommendation algorithms in real-time based on precise engagement analytics; and third, we propose a novel multi-task learning architecture that synergistically optimises both implicit and explicit user feedback signals. We first performed user categorization to classify users into three major categories based on their engagement levels (Fig. 1) and interactions with the platform. The proposed Switching- Based Hybrid Recommendation System leverages the dynamic switching mechanism to seamlessly transition between the integrated three different recommendation models which adapts to the evolving preferences followed by extensive experiments and evaluations to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed system. Multimodal deep metric learning for newbie users

The CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training) model was leveraged, which is very powerful due to its unique ability to bridge the gap between different modalities, mainly text and images, which help to understand and connect visual and textual information24. CLIP(Algorithm 1) is trained in such a way that it can encode and fuse images and text into a common feature space, enabling direct comparisons between them1. This capability helps the model reason about the textual content and context of images together. The model was fine tuned on two different specific downstream tasks, such as: iVisual recommendations for absolute newbies users: When it comes to users who are completely new and have no previous data, the recommendation system that we have provided is still able to make recommendations that are relevant and useful. This makes it possible for the system to make recommendations that are helpful and relevant. On the basis of the user’s straightforward searches or clicks over the goods, these recommendations are generated. Everytime a new user accesses an E-commerce website, our system does a persistent monitoring of Simple Search or Click Behaviour2. This monitoring is carried out in accordance with the aforementioned statement. To phrase it another way, the system pays a great deal of attention to the actions that the user performs, which are typically either easy searches or clicks on components. When it comes to the process of setting the framework for the development of recommendations, this interaction is the most important thing that takes place. The various metadata related with the products was used, which includes the title, description, brand, category, and price, in addition to the photographs that are attached to the products. Text (product metadata) and pictures (product photos) are the two means of communication that will be utilised in this attempt in order to accomplish the goal of making product suggestions. The implementation of a multimodal deep metric learning model that is capable of learning and fusing embeddings of multiple modalities into a single embedding space was the means by which the goal was acheieved . Beginning with the processing of four distinct forms of text metadata and images that are associated to them, the procedure will get underway. For the purpose of generating text embeddings that are capable of capturing the semantic meaning of the text, the CLIP text model has been utilised to process all of the text metadata. An independent application of the CLIP image model has been utilised in order to process the image of the product. Specifically, this approach was established with the intention of dealing with data that presents itself visually. These embeddings contain the features and characteristics of the product, including its visual qualities and characteristics. By following the processing of all of the textual and visual input, the intermediate embeddings had been pre-trained are then submitted to two separate head models which were produced by the CLIP models that had been . These embeddings are taken as input by these head models, which are are constructed on MLP. On the other hand, they produce embeddings which are more granular and pertinent to the downstream objective of the product suggestion. While the Image Head Model is responsible for processing intermediate image embeddings, the text Head Model is responsible for the processing of text-based embeddings. The output embeddings of the text and image head models are fused together in order to build a joint representation which integrates the information that was gathered from both the embeddings. This is done in order to generate a joint representation. This representation, which is shared and merges all of the objects together, is able to capture the multimodal features of the items. Utilising the MultiNegativesRankLoss loss function is the means by which the training of the model is performed. In situations where only positive examples were there and it is rather challenging to define negative examples in a way that is both obvious and succinct, the selection of this loss function is the most appropriate choice. For individuals who are absolutely unfamiliar with the topic, there is no historical data that can be accessed. It is highly challenging to generate a typical dataset that includes both positive and negative events since there is no negative feedback. This makes the process of constructing such a dataset incredibly tough. In an effort to handle situations such as this one, the uased approached each and every product as if it were a shining example. The practise of employing this approach is commonly known as 1“positive-only feedback.” Through the utilisation of this approach, the model is able to get an understanding of the characteristics or characteristics that people find appealing in other items.

Two tower retrieval model for light users

Our innovative system is designed to accommodate diverse user profiles based on their interaction history and engagement levels with the items. To address the fluidity of user behavior over time, our methodology not only tailors recommendation strategies to each segment but also models transitions between them. For instance, as a user shifts from a newbie to a light user through increased engagement, the system dynamically adapts by switching to a more personalized recommendation model suited to the updated interaction pattern. This transition-aware design ensures that recommendations remain relevant and effective across evolving user states. For light users, a user-product model was developed. This model leverages a two-tower model(Algorithm 3). A user and a product tower were utilized, and (user, product) pairs were used to train them.

This is due to the fact that each tower normally processes a distinct kind of input data, and then the final model aggregates all of these different types of data. When it comes to product recommendation, one tower processes information that is associated with user behaviour, while the other tower processes things that are associated with information (such as the title of the product, its description, and so on). Because the goal was to have a straightforward and lightweight model, the architecture for the product candidate tower consists of a combination of classic layers such as LSTM and dense layers. On the other hand, the design for the user query tower consists of only a lookup and embedding layer. It is clear that a collaborative filtering strategy is being utilised because the model is trained with pairs of (user, product). In order to get a more in-depth comprehension of customer preferences, this training technique guarantees that the model will acquire knowledge via direct interactions between users and products. The model encapsulates both the retrieval loss (based on sampled softmax) and evaluation metric computation. The loss function is optimized to bring the user and positively interacted item embeddings closer in the latent space, while distancing them from randomly sampled negatives. The evaluation metric used is FactorizedTopK, which measures the model’s ability to rank the correct item in the top-k retrieved set from the entire candidate pool.

In a two-tower retrieval model, the loss function is typically based on the sampled softmax approach. Multitask learning model for heavy users

As users engage more regularly with the platform, they naturally progress from the light to heavy user profiles. This transition is identified by the tracking metrics such as frequency of interactions, diversity of consumed content, and the depth of engagement over time. Once a user crosses the predefined threshold of activity, the system switches to a more suitable recommendation model tailored for heavy users. This model leverages the richer behavioral data to provide highly personalized and the context-aware suggestions. By continuously monitoring the user activity and allowing seamless migration across segments, the system ensures that each user receives the most relevant recommendations aligned with their evolving engagement patterns. The methodology of a multi-task learning technique was adopted (Algorithm 4) where an integrated multiple sources of feedback and built a joint model to optimise multiple objectives was used28.

Recommendation systems usually have access to a range of user feedback, including search clicks, product views, cart-added items, purchases, reviews, and returns etc. Abundance and informativeness are different for these feedback signals29. For instance, clicks and views are abundant but might have relatively low informative signals. We leverage integration with all these diverse forms of user feedback to build a powerful and accurate recommendation system. Relying on just one single feedback may lead to suboptimal performance. However, integrating all feedback sources leads to more user-friendly systems and is balanced in their optimization. Being a Multi-Task Model this system has two critical aspects: iSimultaneously optimise for two or more objectives, typically optimising for different user behaviours or preferences.iiSharing Variables: These models share variables among target tasks, which enables transfer learning.

Configuration for Newbie users

The configuration for newbie users focuses on addressing the cold-start problem where users have little or no prior interaction history with the platform. Absolute newbie users are those who are entirely new to the platform and have not interacted with any items, while another segment of newbie users provides implicit feedback in the form of searches, clicks, or views. For such users, traditional recommendation methods based on historical user–item interactions are ineffective due to the absence of sufficient data.To address this challenge, the proposed system employs a multimodal deep metric learning approach that leverages both visual and textual information associated with products. The CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-Training) model is utilized to encode images and multiple forms of textual metadata, including product title, description, brand, category, and price, into a shared embedding space. This enables the system to learn meaningful representations even in the absence of explicit user feedback.A multimodal fusion strategy is applied to combine image and text embeddings, allowing the model to capture cross-modal relationships between visual and semantic features. The system continuously monitors simple user interactions such as searches and clicks, which serve as implicit signals for generating recommendations. By relying on multimodal representations and implicit feedback, the system is able to generate relevant and useful recommendations for users with no or minimal interaction history.

Configuration for light users

The light user configuration is designed for users who have engaged with the platform but possess only limited interaction history. These users provide sparse feedback, such as a small number of views or purchases, which can be leveraged to refine recommendations beyond cold-start strategies.For this user segment, the system employs a two-tower retrieval model consisting of a user query tower and a product candidate tower. The user tower processes user behavior and interaction signals, while the product tower encodes product-related information. Training is performed using (user, product) interaction pairs, enabling the model to learn collaborative representations based on observed interactions.The retrieval model is optimized using a sampled softmax–based objective, which allows efficient training and retrieval in large item catalogs. During evaluation, the FactorizedTopK metric is used to assess the model’s ability to retrieve relevant items within the top-k recommendations. An ablation analysis is conducted on different embedding dimensions to study trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency, highlighting the impact of representation size on retrieval performance.This configuration allows the system to effectively utilize sparse interaction data while maintaining scalability and retrieval efficiency for light users.

Configuration for heavy users

The heavy user configuration targets users with rich and extensive interaction histories, including frequent views, purchases, ratings, and other behavioral signals. This abundance of data enables the use of more expressive models to capture fine-grained user preferences.For this segment, the system adopts a multi-task learning framework that integrates multiple sources of user feedback. The model jointly optimizes rating prediction and retrieval objectives, allowing it to balance explicit and implicit feedback signals. Shared embedding layers are used to capture common representations, while task-specific branches focus on rating prediction and retrieval tasks, respectively.The rating task aims to accurately predict user ratings, while the retrieval task focuses on identifying items that users are likely to interact with or purchase. By sharing representations across tasks, the model facilitates knowledge transfer from data-abundant signals to sparser ones, improving overall recommendation quality. Different weighting strategies are evaluated to analyze the trade-off between rating accuracy and retrieval performance.This configuration enables the system to deliver highly personalized and context-aware recommendations for users with extensive engagement histories (Table 2).

The Results section provides a comprehensive analysis of the model’s performance across different user segments.The experimental evaluation in this study is designed to reflect the switching-based nature of the proposed recommendation framework, where different models are activated based on user engagement levels rather than applying a single algorithm uniformly across all users. Consequently, the evaluation strategy varies across user segments in accordance with data availability and recommendation feasibility.For absolute newbie users, historical user–item interaction data is unavailable, making traditional collaborative filtering baselines inapplicable. Therefore, evaluation for this segment focuses on the effectiveness of the proposed multimodal deep metric learning approach by comparing model performance before and after fine-tuning using identical data splits and candidate sets.For light users, the two-tower retrieval model is evaluated using Top-K categorical accuracy, HR@10, and NDCG@10 metrics, measured under the same training and evaluation settings. The reported results highlight improvements achieved through learned collaborative representations derived from sparse interaction data. For heavy users, multiple model variants—including rating-specialized, retrieval-specialized, and joint multi-task learning models—are evaluated on the same interaction data to analyze the impact of different optimization objectives. These comparisons are intended to distinguish task-specific behavior within the proposed framework rather than serve as external baseline benchmarking.

Overall, the experimental analysis emphasizes within-framework comparisons to demonstrate the effectiveness of dynamic model switching across diverse user engagement scenarios.

Conclusion

This study has demonstrated a switching-based hybrid recommendation system that is specifically built to accommodate a wide range of user profiles operating within highly dynamic E-commerce settings. By classifying users into three unique groups—newcomers, light users, and heavy users, the system is able to respond to the specific requirements associated with each user profile. Our system provides a user-friendly experience by utilizing a wide range of inputs, which also contributes to its overall functionality. Our methodology exhibits both adaptability and effectiveness, which has led to considerable improvements in the performance of recommendation systems across a wide range of user groups. While our switching-based hybrid recommendation system significantly improves the system’s performance across diverse user segments, certain limitations persist. One key limitation lies in the use of our predefined user categories (newbies, light users, and heavy users). While this classification enables targeted recommendation strategies, it may not fully capture the transitions and evolving behaviors of users over time, potentially leading to suboptimal personalization for those whose interaction patterns shift frequently.Furthermore, although the system demonstrates the improved adaptability, it currently lacks advanced temporal modeling mechanisms that can capture and respond to abrupt or non-linear changes in the user preferences in real time. Enhancing the system’s temporal awareness could significantly improve the responsiveness and recommendation accuracy in the dynamic environments where user interests evolve rapidly. These limitations open path for future research which intend to enhance our framework by incorporating functionality that has interpretability requirements. The interpretation of black box models, such as the significance of features and the explanation of individual forecasts, will be rendered possible as a result of this. Systems will become more valuable in corporate contexts as a result of this occurrence. Future work will focus on developing dynamic user profiling methods which captures behavioral transitions over time. Incorporating advanced temporal modeling techniques, such as the recurrent or attention-based architectures, may enhance the real-time adaptability. Additionally, exploring the deeper cross-domain representation learning that could further improve the recommendation accuracy for users with diverse interests.

Pearl 1: Patients with low-risk adenomas (LRAs) are truly at low risk for colorectal cancer (CRC)

Patients with LRA have marginally higher CRC incidence, with similarly low CRC mortality compared to those with no polyps, and have CRC risk substantially lower than the general population (Table).1,2

Pearl 2: After a high-quality colonoscopy with removal of LRAs, up to 10-year interval is safe

About 1 in 450 patients with LRA removal by a colonoscopist with adenoma detection rate ≥ 20% develop CRC,3 with even lower risk expected at the current benchmark of achieving at least 35% adenoma detection rate.

Pearl 3: Improving baseline quality matters more than shortening surveillance intervals

Over half of postpolypectomy cancers occur shortly after the baseline exam, typically attributed to missed or incompletely resected lesions.4 Shortening follow-up intervals does little to mitigate this risk relative to optimizing baseline exam quality.

Pearl 4: Shorter intervals should be reserved for select higher-risk scenarios

Suboptimal bowel preparation or having a first degree relative with CRC justifies earlier surveillance. A history of advanced lesions prior to having LRAs also warrants a 5-year interval.5

Pearl 5: Standardized workflows prevent overuse and improve guideline concordance

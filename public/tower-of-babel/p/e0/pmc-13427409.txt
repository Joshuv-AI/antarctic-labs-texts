Enhancement of solar photovoltaic potentials in central and eastern China due to “Clean Air Actions”
Authors: Zhigao Zhou, Yang Zhong, Zhijiang Zhang, Lijie He, Wenmin Qin, Lunche Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe Chinese central and eastern (CCE) regions are both densely populated and heavily polluted, as well as deeply energy-demanding areas. Meanwhile, improvement of air quality occurred in these regions due to “Clean Air Actions” since 2013, probably resulting in increase in surface solar radiation (SSR). This study identified the major drivers of interannual variations in photovoltaic (PV) potentials during 2014–2019 using the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 product, and we found that the decrease in aerosol radiative effect (ARE) was suggested to be the main influencing factor for the increase in SSR. Compared to 2014, the annual PV potentials per kilowatt-peak (kWp) in the CCE regions increased by 5, 37, 70, 67, and 70 kWh/kWp in 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, and 2019, respectively. Our results highlight that the Clean Air Actions can ‌not only enhance the PV potentials‌ but also alleviate the spatial mismatch between energy supply and demand in the CCE regions.Keywords: surface solar radiation, solar photovoltaic potentials, aerosol radiative effect, eastern and central China

Introduction

Rapid and energy-intensive development in China has led to severe air pollution and negative public health effects over the past several decades, which have become notable environmental and social problems in China.1,2 At the beginning of 2013, news headlines were continually filled with reports of severe PM2.5 (particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter of less than 2.5 μm) pollution across the country. The hourly PM2.5 concentration in the capital of China, Beijing, even reached over 1,000 μg/m3, which is 40 times higher than the World Health Organization (WHO) standard level for good health.3 Meanwhile, PM2.5 exposure in 2015 was estimated to result in approximately 8.9 million deaths globally, among which over a quarter occurred in China.4 According to the Global Burden of Disease 2015 comparative risk assessment,5 outdoor PM2.5 exposure contributes to approximately 1.1 million deaths in China annually.

To address the severe air pollution challenges and safeguard public health, China’s State Council launched the groundbreaking Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan (“Clean Air Actions”) in 2013.6 Between 2013 and 2019, a comprehensive suite of regulatory measures was executed, featuring stricter industrial emission controls, elimination of outdated and highly polluting industrial facilities, modernization of industrial boilers, enhanced vehicular emission standards, and accelerated adoption of renewable energy infrastructure.7 It is expected that the implementation of Clean Air Actions can achieve remarkable improvements in air quality.8,9 A growing body of research has documented substantial improvements in PM2.5 air quality and the concomitant public health benefits in China during the period from 2013 to 2017.10,11,12,13,14 Zheng et al.12 observed a 21.5% reduction in population-weighted annual PM2.5 levels, declining from 60.5 μg/m3 in 2013 to 47.5 μg/m3 in 2015. Correspondingly, PM2.5-associated excess mortality decreased by 9.1%, falling from 1.22 million to 1.10 million cases. Further analysis by Xue et al.13 revealed continued progress, with national PM2.5 levels dropping 32% from 67.4 μg/m3 in 2013 to 45.5 μg/m3 in 2017, accompanied by a 14% reduction in premature deaths attributable to chronic exposure (from 1.2 million to 1.03 million cases). These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of China’s coordinated strategy combining industrial restructuring and emission control upgrades in mitigating environmental health risks.

Solar energy, a pivotal form of renewable energy, has garnered increasing global attention. China’s photovoltaic (PV) installation capacity has experienced rapid expansion over the past two decades, securing its position as the global leader since 2015.15 By the end of December 2024, the PV cumulative installed capacity in China had reached ‌890 million kilowatts (890 GW)‌, marking a ‌45.2% year-on-year growth. ‌‌In contrast, coal share has dropped from 69.2% to 53.2% during 2010–2024. Solar energy substitution for coal toward carbon mitigation can simultaneously reduce considerable aerosol emissions, hence bringing remarkable air quality improvements and associated human health co-benefits in China.16,17,18,19,20

Meanwhile, the variations in aerosol emission also have a significant influence on the solar energy system.21,22,23,24 For example, Li et al.25 found that the PV potentials declined by 25%–35% during 2003–2014 in the North China Plain, equivalent to a loss of 1.5 kWh m−2 days−1 in generation due to air pollution. Sweerts et al.26 estimated the solar energy losses owing to China’s air pollution based on historical surface radiation data for the period 1961–2015 and found that the PV potentials decreased by 11%–15% during this period. Zhou et al.27 estimated the losses in potential concentrated solar thermal power electricity production due to air pollution in China during 1961–2015 using reconstructed dataset. Similarly, Chen et al.28 identified the major drivers of inter-annual variations in PV potential and discussed the possible implications for realizing China’s 2060 carbon neutral target. However, despite the valuable insights from existing literature, it is still unclear how the improvement of air quality affects the PV potentials since 2013 due to stringent control measures. Moreover, clouds and aerosols had been considered as the two major contributors to the variations of solar radiation; however, existing research has failed to adequately account for the simultaneous influence of clouds and aerosols on the PV potentials, which leads to the impacts of anthropogenic air pollution being underestimated or overestimated. Therefore, evaluating how China’s air quality enhancements had affected the PV potentials since 2013 requires differentiating the effects of pollution control outcomes and concurrent cloud trends.

Therefore, a systematic investigation of how decreasing aerosol emissions creates synergistic effects between improved air quality and increased PV potentials—driven by China’s Clean Air Actions—remains notably absent. The main contents of this study are (1) to obtain variation trends of surface solar radiation (SSR) across the Eastern and Central China (ECC) region using derived dataset, (2) to distinguish and determine the main cause of variations in SSR, and (3) to calculate the benefits in PV potentials due to aerosol emissions mitigation.

Results

Validation of satellite retrieval and reanalyses and variation trends of SSR

Although the aforementioned satellite-based SSR products have been extensively validated against global or regional observation networks, including the China Meteorological Administration (CMA) stations, the Global Energy Balance Archive (GEBA), and the Baseline Surface Radiation Network (BSRN) Archive,29,30 their performance during 2014–2019 (after Clean Air Action and before the COVID-19) in the Chinese central and eastern (CCE) regions remains insufficiently validated against ground-based measurements. To address this gap, we employ three distinct statistical metrics—Pearson correlation coefficient (R), root-mean-square error (RMSE), and mean absolute bias error (MAE)—to systematically assess the accuracy of five SSR products in this region.

Figure 1 showed the statistical results of comparisons between the SSR values of CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1, MERRA-2, NCEP-DOE, NCEP-NCAR, and 25 measured stations. Generally, four SSR products significantly overestimated the SSR over the CCE regions. The CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 SSR product was found to have the best accuracy among the four products, with an overall R of 0.95, an RMSE of 20.2 W m−2, and an MAE of 14.8 W m−2 which was in agreement with previous studies.31,32 The MERRA-2 and NCEP-DOE SSR products showed moderate performance with R values of 0.87 and 0.84, RMSEs of 53.6 W m−2 and 54.7 W m−2, and MAEs of 45.8 W m−2 and 47.8 W m−2, respectively. In contrast, the NCEP-NCAR product exhibited comparatively lower accuracy (R = 0.74, RMSE = 86.3 W m−2, and MAE = 78.9 W m−2). The error sources of these SSR products are mainly derived from the uncertainty of the input parameters and the methodological limitations. The high accuracy of the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 SSR product ‌originates from‌ a technical approach ‌predominantly driven by satellite-based direct observations‌, ‌enhanced through‌ multi-satellite synergistic retrievals ‌and‌ advanced instrumental calibration. ‌This methodology effectively circumvents‌ the radiation calculation biases inherent in reanalysis products—which arise from error propagation within data assimilation systems and oversimplified parameterization schemes. ‌Notably, this advantage becomes particularly pronounced‌ in regions sensitive to aerosol-cloud interactions, such as polluted urban clusters or areas with complex terrains.32

Therefore, the CERES-SYN1deg_Ed4.1 product was used to analyze the variation trends of SSR. As depicted in Figure 2, it is obvious that a decreasing trend of SSR was observed in most regions of western China, particularly in the southern Xinjiang, eastern Qinghai, and northern Tibet, where the magnitude of the decreasing trend exceeded −3 W m−2 year−2. Conversely, a significant increasing trend of SSR was observed in the CCE regions, predominately in areas such as Anhui, Jiangsu, Shandong, and Zhejiang provinces, where the magnitude of the increasing trend exceeded 1 W m−2 year−2. To further address the consistency between satellite-derived and ground-based SSR trends, we have conducted an additional analysis comparing the CERES-SYN1deg_Ed4.1 product with surface observational SSR data for the period 2014–2019. A trend comparison plot has been generated (1.14 W m−2 y−2 vs. 1.34 W m−2y−2; see Figure S1). The results indicate that both the CERES-SYN1deg product and the surface observational data exhibit a concurrent positive (increasing) trend in SSR over this period. This agreement strengthens the robustness of the reported brightening signal in the CCE regions.

Globally, numerous studies have documented a steady rise in SSR across the United States and Europe over the past 20−30 years. In Europe, the observed growth rates reached approximately 0.3 W m−2 year−1 during 1985–2012,33 while in the United States, it nearly doubled to 0.6 W m−2 year−1 from 1996 to 2011.34 Against this backdrop, the SSR enhancement in the CCE regions during 2014–2019 stands out as one of the most intense solar brightening episodes recorded globally in recent decades, with rates ‌1.5–3 times higher‌ than those observed in the United States and Europe during their respective study periods. The exceptional brightening in the CCE regions underscores the profound impact of policy-driven air quality improvements on radiative forcing. Meanwhile, the SSR brightening rates reported here are notably higher than the longer-term trends documented for Europe and North America. This likely reflects the uniquely rapid decline in aerosol forcing over our study region and period. The relatively short 6-year record makes the calculated trends more sensitive to interannual variability, potentially amplifying the rate estimate compared to trends derived from decadal-scale observations. This sensitivity, combined with the retrieval uncertainties discussed earlier, should be considered when interpreting the magnitude of these short-term trends.

Drivers for the SSR variation trend in CCE regions

Aerosols and clouds are the two major factors affecting the variation trend of SSR.35,36 Figure 3A illustrated the spatial distribution of the variation trends of annual mean aerosol radiative effects (AREs) derived from the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 satellite product during 2014–2019 in the CCE regions. The data revealed a widespread decline in ARE throughout the CCE regions, with statistically significant reductions of approximately −2 W m−2 year−1 in the northern and central CCE regions, where the most substantial improvements in aerosol optical depth (AOD) were observed.37,38 These marked changes in particulate matter concentrations spatially correlate with areas implementing the strictest environmental regulations. Additionally, the annual mean AREs in the CCE regions exhibited a significant deceasing trend (p < 0.05) of −0.56 W m−2 year−1 over the study period (Figure 3B). Variations in ARE demonstrate strong dependence on atmospheric‌ particulate matter concentrations and AOD. Numerous studies have confirmed that the dramatic decline in PM2.5, PM10, and AOD during this period primarily stems from substantial emission reductions achieved through stringent control policies.39,40 Moreover, previous studies confirmed a distinct inverse relationship: the significant decrease in aerosol loading (shown by declining AOD and PM2.5 trends) coincides with a significant increase in clear-sky SSR over the same period and region (see Figures S2–S4).31,41 Consequently, the reduction in ARE and increasing SSR trends in the CCE regions could be predominantly attributed to the diminished atmospheric particulate levels resulting from the implementation of the Clean Air Actions initiative in 2013.

Figure 4A showed the spatial distribution of the variation trends of annual mean cloud radiative effects (CREs) across the CCE regions, derived from the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 satellite product for the period 2014−2019. The data revealed regional heterogeneous patterns: CRE exhibited insignificant declines (p > 0.05) in some CCE regions, while increasing in the others. However, these trends were statistically insignificant at the regional scale, with only isolated pixel-level exceptions showing meaningful confidence intervals. Although the annual mean CREs in the CCE regions demonstrated a nominal decreasing trend of −0.27 W m−2 year−1, the slope was not statistically significant (p > 0.05) because of large interannual fluctuations (Figure 4B). The spatially inconsistent CRE patterns result in substantially weaker regional impacts compared to ARE, despite localized instances where both effects reach similar magnitudes. This temporal and spatial analysis further reinforces the finding that cloud cover variations cannot adequately explain the increases in SSR across the CCE regions and conversely, that the strongly decreasing ARE played a dominant role in producing the increasing annual SSR trends during this period. Meanwhile, the attribution of clear-sky SSR trends primarily to aerosol changes, while supported by inverse correlations with independent aerosol proxy data, must be considered within the known limitations of satellite-based atmospheric composition retrievals. A significant challenge lies in unequivocally separating the radiative impacts of aerosols, clouds, and trace gases. As highlighted in broader methodological discussions, 3D cloud scattering can introduce substantial biases in trace gas retrievals even for clear-sky pixels,42 and isolating tropospheric signals is inherently difficult due to overlapping contributions and complex radiative transfer.43 Our interpretation of the CERES-derived clear-sky SSR and aerosol proxy trends acknowledges these inherent uncertainties. Moreover, over a 6-year window, interannual variability in large-scale atmospheric circulation, synoptic patterns, and background climatic states (e.g., modes like the El Niño-Southern Oscillation) can correlate with both emission trends and cloudiness, potentially aliasing their respective radiative impacts.

Enhancement of solar PV potentials from Clean Air Actions in CCE regions

Figure 5 illustrated the variations in theoretical solar PV potentials across the CCE regions from 2015 to 2019, with 2014 serving as the baseline year, as derived from the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 SSR product. The analysis revealed a geographically widespread increase in theoretical solar PV potential by 2019 relative to 2014, with statistically significant enhancements observed in nine provinces (Henan, Hebei, Shandong, Hubei, Jiangsu, Anhui, Shanxi, Zhejiang, and Hunan) and two municipalities (Beijing and Tianjin). Quantitative assessments of these increases are systematically presented in Table 1 for 2019, while the corresponding annual variations from 2015 to 2018 are documented in Tables S1–S4 for comprehensive temporal analysis. Compared to 2014, the theoretical PV potentials in the CCE regions increased by 5, 37, 70, and 67 kWh in 2015, 2016, 2017, and 2018, respectively, with an increase in rate of 0, 3, 5, and 5.2.

The magnitude of theoretical solar PV potential augmentation across the 11 provincial administrative units exhibited substantial heterogeneity, ranging from ‌40 to 94 kWh‌ when comparing 2019 to 2014 baseline values, equivalent to relative growth rates of ‌2.64%–7.54%‌. Anhui Province emerged as the regional leader in PV potential improvement, demonstrating a ‌94-kWh increase (7.54% growth rate). In contrast, Shanxi Province recorded the least gains, with only 40 kWh and a growth rate of 2.64%. The mean increase across all evaluated regions amounted to 64 kWh (5% growth rate)‌—a figure underscoring the region-wide positive trend in solar resource availability.

This observed enhancement in theoretical solar PV potential is fundamentally linked to the implementation of China’s Clean Air Actions (2013)‌, which targeted the substantial aerosol emissions from economic activities concentrated in the CCE regions. The policy-driven increase in atmospheric transparency (as evidenced by declining atmospheric radiative effects in prior analyses) has consequently increased SSR by 2.64%–7.54%‌ in affected areas according to the above-mentioned satellite validation studies. The spatial correlation between regions with stringent emission controls (e.g., Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei cluster) and those exhibiting the most pronounced PV potential gains (Anhui, Jiangsu) further reinforces this causal relationship.

Discussion

Over the past few decades, rapid increases in aerosol emissions driven by economic activities in the CCE regions had substantially reduced SSR, thereby weakening the PV potentials. Moreover, the decreasing SSR has significantly suppressed atmospheric convective motion and lowered planetary boundary layer (PBL) height, hindering the dispersion of atmospheric pollutants.44,45 The resulting accumulation of pollutants further attenuates SSR, leading to a negative feedback loop that exacerbates SSR attenuation and air pollution stagnation.16,46 Meanwhile, the radiative forcing induced by light-absorbing particulate matter (e.g., black carbon aerosols) exacerbates atmospheric stability through thermal stratification mechanisms.47 This process elevates surface air temperatures while suppressing PBL development via intensified temperature inversion formation. Concurrently, the restricted vertical mixing dynamics diminishes near-surface turbulent kinetic energy, thereby reducing downward momentum transport and leading to a lower near-surface wind speed, which is vital for wind energy application.

Our analysis reveals that the aerosol emissions not only induce significant atmospheric deterioration but also exacerbate the geospatial mismatch between regional energy supply and demand. This situation stems primarily from aerosol-induced suppression of PV and wind potentials in energy demand centers (aerosol pollution regions). Reversely, such aerosols’ radiative effects present strategic intervention pathways to concurrently address air quality enhancement and optimize renewable energy system efficiency.48

In 2020, China announced a formal commitment to achieve peak carbon emissions by 2030, followed by carbon neutrality before 2060 (officially termed the Carbon Peaking and Carbon Neutrality Goals). It is widely acknowledged that transitioning the nation’s fossil fuel-reliant energy infrastructure to a low-carbon system constitutes the foundational strategy for realizing these climate goals.49 Such structural transformation is projected to generate substantial co-benefits in air quality enhancement and public health improvement, driven by accelerated renewable energy adoption across power generation and industrial sectors. Furthermore, reduced aerosol emissions from decarbonization efforts may amplify renewable electricity production efficiency by mitigating aerosol-induced meteorological interference, thereby establishing a synergistic feedback loop between emission control and renewable energy optimization (Figure 6). That is, carbon neutrality-oriented aerosol mitigation can not only facilitate atmospheric quality enhancement but also optimize prospective PV and wind energy potentials. Notably, the significant renewable resource increases in heavily energy-demanding CCE regions indicate that the Chinese carbon neutrality-induced air quality, human health, and renewable energy synergies primarily occur in areas where such improvements are most needed, therefore helping to alleviate the geospatial mismatch between energy demand and energy supply.

Limitations of the study

Despite the comprehensive validation and quantification of PV potential enhancements, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the temporal scope of this study was intentionally constrained to the 2014–2019 period to isolate the impacts of Clean Air Actions from the unprecedented socio-economic disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. While this ensured a stable baseline for trend analysis, it precludes an assessment of the “new normal” in the post-pandemic era, where industrial recovery and evolving emission standards may introduce new dynamics in the aerosol-radiation-PV nexus. Second, although the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 product demonstrated superior performance among reanalysis and satellite datasets, its 1° spatial resolution remains relatively coarse for capturing sub-grid variations. Localized phenomena, such as urban heat island effects, micro-scale cloud formations, or specific industrial plumes in complex terrains, might be smoothed out, potentially masking extreme local gains or losses in PV potential. Furthermore, while our methodology successfully decoupled the ARE and CRE using the Fu-Liou radiative transfer model, the intricate “indirect effects”—where aerosols alter cloud microphysics and lifetime—remain a challenge to fully disentangle. Consequently, the attributed contributions might still harbor minor uncertainties regarding the synergistic interactions between pollution and meteorology. Finally, the PV potential calculations relied on standardized efficiency parameters and did not account for real-world operational factors such as panel soiling, aging, or the spectral sensitivity of emerging PV technologies, which could influence the actual energy yield. The reliance on a single, albeit validated, satellite product suggests that future ensembles of high-resolution data could further reduce structural uncertainties.

Future research should aim to bridge these gaps by integrating multi-scale observations and advanced modeling frameworks. High-resolution satellite data (e.g., from geostationary platforms like Himawari-9) coupled with regional climate-chemistry models could provide more granular insights into city-level PV planning and the impacts of localized pollution control. Extending the time series into the late 2020s will be crucial to evaluate the long-term sustainability of solar gains as China transitions from the Clean Air Action phase to the more ambitious “dual carbon” goals (Carbon Peak 2030 and Carbon Neutrality 2060). This transition likely involves deeper structural changes in the energy mix, which may alter the composition of aerosol species (e.g., the ratio of scattering sulfates to absorbing black carbon), necessitating a more nuanced analysis of aerosol optical properties. Additionally, future studies should incorporate socio-economic and engineering variables, such as the economic value of air-quality-induced solar gains for the national grid and the performance of next-generation PV modules (e.g., bifacial or perovskite cells) under changing atmospheric conditions. Moreover, the integration of machine learning algorithms to predict PV potential fluctuations based on real-time air quality indices could enhance the resilience of smart grid management. Investigating the “virtuous cycle” between renewable energy deployment and air quality improvement—where solar energy replaces fossil fuels, further reducing aerosols and enhancing solar radiation—could provide a powerful quantitative argument for accelerated energy transitions. Finally, applying this analytical framework to other rapidly industrializing regions in the Global South would offer a global perspective on the co-benefits of environmental regulation and renewable energy security.

Method details

Study area and sites

This study focuses on Chinese central and eastern (CCE) regions (20°N–42°N, 108°E−123°E), an area that encompasses major economic hubs such as the Pearl River Delta, the Yangtze River Delta, the North China Plain, and the Urban Agglomeration along the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River. As the nation’s most developed region, it is characterized by a dense population and significant industrial activity, which historically led to exceptionally high emissions of anthropogenic aerosols. In response to the severe environmental and health problems caused by this pollution, the Chinese government implemented the “Clean Air Actions” in 2013.31 However, policy implementation typically involves a lag phase, during which the full effects of regulatory measures are not yet fully manifested. By starting our study in 2014, we aim to focus on the period when these policies began to yield tangible results, ensuring that our analysis captures the true impact of pollution control efforts rather than the transitional adjustment phase. The year 2020 marked the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, which brought about unprecedented disruptions to human activities, including industrial production, transportation, and social gatherings. These disruptions significantly altered atmospheric emission patterns, making it difficult to isolate the effects of long-term air pollution trends. By ending our study in 2019, we ensure that our data reflects a relatively stable period of atmospheric conditions, free from the confounding effects of the pandemic.

Daily surface solar radiation (SSR) data were acquired from the China Meteorological Administration (CMA). The dataset comprises measurements from 130 stations across China. Before the release, the daily SSR data were subjected to rigorous quality control, which included manual inspection, correction, and spatiotemporal consistency checks. The quality control adhered to the following principles: (i) the measured SSR must be less than the extraterrestrial radiation (SSRa), and (ii) the measured SSR must be greater than a defined lower boundary (0.03 × SSRa).50 After that, 25 stations located in CCE regions (Figure S5) were selected, more detailed information of those stations were clearly shown in Table S5. Data and methods

Satellite retrieval and reanalyses

Multi-source data, including the Clouds and the Earth’s Radiation Energy System deployed on the Earth Observing System’s Terra and Aqua satellite platforms (CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1),51,52 Modern-Era Retrospective analysis for Research and Applications, version 2 (MERRA-2),53,54 National Centers for Environmental Prediction-National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCEP-NCAR)55 and NCEP-DOE,56 was used in this study.

It was widely reported that clouds and aerosols had been considered as the major contributors to the variation trends of SSR around the world.35,57,58 However, it’s ambiguous that which one was the most important factor impacting SSR trends. To investigate the impact of changes in aerosol and clouds on SSR in CCE, aerosol radiative effects (AREs) and cloud radiative effects (CREs) were used to identify relative contributions of cloud and aerosol to the variation trends of SSR. The CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1‌ product uses aerosol optical depth (AOD) data from the ‌MODIS‌ (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) sensor for ARE calculations, employing a combined retrieval strategy of dark target‌ and deep blue‌ algorithms. The resulting aerosol dataset is provided at a ‌1° spatial resolution‌ and ‌hourly temporal resolution‌ on a grid, and is directly input into the ‌Langley Fu-Liou radiative transfer model‌ to compute radiative flux differences under various aerosol scenarios (e.g., aerosol-free vs. all-sky), thereby enabling the quantification of the ARE. Based on the synergistic observation of Terra/Aqua dual satellites and a ‌multi-source calibration system‌ (MODIS/VIIRS + GEO sensor cross-calibration), the CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 SSR product demonstrated ‌long-term stability‌ (interannual fluctuation <2%) during 2003–2022. Instrument degradation errors were constrained below 0.3%/year as validated by the ‌Deep Convective Cloud (DCC) target calibration method.‌59 For the ‌Aerosol Radiative Effect (ARE)‌, the ‌MATCH model assimilating MODIS Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD)‌ scheme was employed. The AOD data underpinning ARE calculations achieved a correlation of R = 0.85 (bias ±0.05) with AERONET ground stations. The ‌Cloud Radiative Effect (CRE)‌ utilized ‌multispectral cloud phase discrimination‌ constrained by the ‌Fu-Liou radiative transfer model‌. Inter-version discrepancies for CRE in ice cloud scenarios decreased by 40% compared to the predecessor edition.60 Moreover, the specific CERES_SYN1deg_Ed4.1 SSR product versions used in the study are consistent throughout, thereby eliminating spurious trends caused by inter-version algorithm changes.

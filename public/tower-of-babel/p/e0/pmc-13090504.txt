Asymmetric Henoch-Schönlein Purpura
Authors: Yujia Wang, Yanfang Xu
Published: 2026

A previously healthy 47-year-old man presented to our nephrology clinic with a 7-day history of joint pain involving his knees and ankles and purplish-red raised rashes on his lower right leg. On physical examination, the patient exhibited diffuse palpable purpura on his lower right leg, with some areas coalescing into patches and forming blisters (Fig 1A). A urinalysis showed microscopic hematuria and proteinuria. Immunofluorescence microscopy of the kidney biopsy showed IgA deposits in the glomerular mesangium (Fig 1B). A diagnosis of IgA vasculitis was made. The skin lesions in IgA vasculitis typically manifest as symmetric palpable purpura mainly in the pressure areas, but in this case, they presented asymmetrically. Treatment with oral glucocorticoids was initiated. Two weeks after presentation, the skin lesions and arthralgia abated.

(A) Asymmetric Henoch-Schönlein purpura on the patient’s lower limbs. (B) A representative immunofluorescence microscopy image showing IgA deposits in the glomerular mesangium.

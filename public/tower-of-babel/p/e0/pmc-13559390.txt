Clinical characteristics and survival of esophageal cancer patients: annual report of the surgical treatment at Shanghai Chest Hospital, 2018
Authors: Jiapeng Ding, Chunji Chen, Chang Yuan, Yuxin Yang, Zhichao Liu, Yifeng Sun, Xufeng Guo, Bin Li, Rong Hua, Yang Yang, Hong Zhang, Yuchen Su, Chunguang Li, Zhigang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEsophageal cancer remains a major health burden in China, and surgical resection is the cornerstone of curative treatment. Continuous annual reports from high-volume centers provide valuable benchmarks for evaluating temporal changes in surgical practice and outcomes. This report summarizes the clinical characteristics, perioperative outcomes and survival of patients who underwent surgical or endoscopic treatment for esophageal cancer at Shanghai Chest Hospital (SCH) in 2018.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed patients treated in 2018 based on data from the prospectively maintained SCH esophageal cancer database. Baseline demographics, treatment modality, pathological findings, postoperative complications, and survival outcomes were collected. The 2016 and 2017 SCH cohorts were included for descriptive temporal comparison.ResultsA total of 714 patients underwent surgical treatment in 2018, including 654 esophagectomies and 60 endoscopic resections. Among the esophagectomy cohort, squamous cell carcinoma predominated (87.0%), and pathological T3 tumors accounted for 42.0%. Pathological nodal involvement was present in 51.7% of patients, with a median of 19 lymph nodes examined. Perioperative multidisciplinary treatment was conducted in 368 esophagectomy patients and four endoscopic submucosal dissection patients. In the esophagectomy cohort, minimally invasive approaches accounted for 80.6% of procedures. Postoperative complications after esophagectomy occurred in 33.9% of patients, with major complications (Clavien-Dindo ≥ III) in 11.5%; pleural effusion (11.0%) and recurrent laryngeal nerve injury (10.9%) were the most frequent specific complications. Thirty-day and 90-day mortality were low (0.7% and 1.8%, respectively). Five-year overall survival and cancer-specific survival were 53.8% and 59.9%, respectively.ConclusionsThe 2018 SCH experience demonstrated a transition toward minimally invasive techniques and centralized care without compromising oncologic adequacy or short-term safety. Compared with 2016–2017, 2018 presented a modest shift toward superficial primary tumor invasion, a stable nodal burden, and a complication profile that may reflect evolving procedure mix and case selection. Continued prospective quality monitoring and data-driven refinement of perioperative management are warranted to further optimize outcomes.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer remains a major health burden in China, where squamous cell carcinoma is the predominant histological subtype (1). Surgical resection plays a central role in the management of both early-stage and locally advanced esophageal cancer. However, esophagectomy is technically demanding and is associated with substantial perioperative morbidity risk. Considerable heterogeneity in surgical volume, operative techniques, and perioperative management across institutions often leads to variability in reported outcomes. Therefore, large, homogeneous, and well-documented surgical cohorts are essential for tracking changes in practice patterns and informing quality improvement in esophageal cancer care.

As described in the previous annual reports, Shanghai Chest Hospital (SCH) has maintained a dedicated esophageal cancer registry that allows year-by-year evaluation of surgical practice, perioperative outcomes, and long-term survival under relatively standardized institutional management. This registry provides a valuable real-world platform to reflect contemporary surgical practice and outcomes in the context of evolving minimally invasive techniques and multidisciplinary treatment strategies. Previous SCH annual reports summarized the clinical characteristics and outcomes of the 2016 and 2017 cohorts and documented progressive adoption of minimally invasive and robot-assisted approaches under standardized perioperative care (2,3).

Given the need for stable definitions and endpoints in annual reports, the present study intentionally retained the core data elements used in previous reports, while further comparing the 2018 cohort with 2016 and 2017 cohorts to assess temporal changes that may reflect refinements in perioperative management and optimization of surgical care. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0841/rc).

Methods

Patients and clinical data

This was a retrospective study based on the prospectively maintained SCH esophageal cancer database. Eligible patients were those who underwent esophagectomy or endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for esophageal cancer at SCH between January 1 and December 31, 2018. Baseline characteristics, tumor-related variables, treatment details, perioperative and survival outcomes were analyzed. For descriptive comparisons of survival outcomes, 2016 and 2017 SCH cohorts were also included (2,3).

Postoperative complications were defined according to Esophagectomy Complications Consensus Group (ECCG) criteria and graded using Clavien-Dindo grading system (4,5). Resections were classified as R0 (negative resection margins), R1 (positive residual tumor at the margin), and R2 (unresectable tumor with invasion of adjacent organs).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Chest Hospital (No. IS26042) (6). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived because of the retrospective study design.

Diagnosis and evaluation

All patients underwent esophagogastroduodenoscopy with biopsy for histopathological confirmation and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) for tumor and nodal staging. Endoscopic ultrasonography was selectively used for suspected superficial lesions. Cervical nodal status was assessed by neck ultrasonography, particularly for cervical and upper thoracic tumors. When CT or clinical findings suggested extra-thoracic disease, targeted magnetic resonance imaging or bone scintigraphy was added. Staging was based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) classification for esophageal cancer (7).

Multidisciplinary treatment

Perioperative systemic treatment was considered mainly for locally advanced disease after multidisciplinary review, which was categorized as preoperative therapy alone, postoperative therapy alone, both preoperative and postoperative therapy, or surgery alone. Preoperative treatment included neoadjuvant chemotherapy and neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. After resection, adjuvant therapy was generally advised for patients with pathologically confirmed lymph node metastases or macroscopic residual disease.

Esophagectomy or endoscopic resection

For operable disease, the standard operation consisted of McKeown or Ivor-Lewis esophagectomy, usually combined with two-field lymphadenectomy and three-field dissection. The choice among thoracoscopic, robot-assisted, and open surgery was individualized according to clinical condition and surgeon assessment. Endoscopic resection was considered for patients staged as cT1N0M0. Circumferential resection was avoided to prevent postoperative stenosis. Standard pathological evaluations were performed after resection. If final pathology showed submucosal invasion or residual disease, additional esophagectomy or definitive chemoradiotherapy was considered.

Follow-up

After surgery, patients were routinely followed up at 3–4-month intervals during the first two years, and every 6–12 months subsequently. Follow-up evaluations were primarily conducted through outpatient visits, including physical examinations, routine laboratory testing, contrast-enhanced chest-abdominal CT imaging, upper endoscopy, and ultrasonography of the neck. Additional examinations were conducted based on the judgments of the attending physician. Follow-up data for the present study were censored in December 2024. The survival endpoints were overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS). OS was measured from the date of surgery to all-cause death or last follow-up, CSS was measured from surgery to esophageal-cancer-related death; deaths from unrelated causes were censored.

Statistical analysis

In this study, categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or median [interquartile range (IQR)] for non-normally distributed data. Survival outcomes were analyzed and compared using the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. Analyses were conducted in SPSS 27.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA), and figures were prepared in GraphPad Prism 10.5 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Statistical significance was defined by a two-sided α of 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The 2018 registry included 714 patients treated surgically or endoscopically at SCH (Figure 1). Most patients were male (82.6%), and 52.5% were aged 60–69 years. The middle thoracic esophagus was the most frequent tumor site, accounting for 49.9% of cases (Table 1, Table S1). Among the 714 patients, esophagectomy and ESD were performed in 654 and 60 patients, respectively (Table 2). Pathological evaluation

Postoperative pathology analysis revealed that squamous cell carcinoma remained the predominant histological type, accounting for 87.0% in esophagectomy cohort and 83.3% in endoscopic resection cohort (Tables 7,8). In the esophagectomy cohort, depth of tumor invasion was dominated by pT3 (42.0%), and lymph node metastasis (N+) was detected in 51.7% of patients, with a median of 19 lymph nodes examined (Tables 9-11). Overall, 244 cases (37.3%) were classified as stage III (Table 12). Notably, tumor differentiation was moderate in 276 cases (42.2%), poor/undifferentiated in 229 cases (35.0%), and well differentiated in 83 cases (12.7%) (Table 13). Lymphovascular invasion (LVI) was detected in 147 patients (22.5%) in the esophagectomy cohort and 4 patients (6.7%) in the ESD cohort (Table 14).

Complications

A total of 222 (33.9%) patients in the esophagectomy cohort experienced postoperative complications, including major complications (Clavien-Dindo ≥ III) in 75 patients (11.5%). The median time of intensive care unit (ICU) stay was 1 (IQR, 0–3) days. The leading specific morbidities were pleural effusion (11.0%) and recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) injury (10.9%) (Tables 11,15, Table S4). No severe complications were recorded in the ESD cohort. Postoperative mortality of the entire cohort was 0.7% (5 cases) at 30 days and 1.8% (13 cases) at 90 days.

Survival analysis

The median follow-up time was 71.83 (IQR, 71.63–71.97) months in the ESD cohort and 71.31 (IQR, 27.23–71.90) months in the esophagectomy cohort. Thirty-four patients lost to follow-up were excluded from the survival analysis. For the remaining 680 patients, 5-year OS and CSS rates were 53.8% and 59.9%, respectively. Patients with ESCC who underwent esophagectomy at SCH in 2016 and 2017 exhibited similar survival trends (Figures 2,3). Discussion

Between 2016 and 2018, the SCH registry documented a continued centralization of esophageal cancer care accompanied by a gradual but consistent shift toward minimally invasive approaches. The 2018 cohort, in particular, represented a further maturation of this trajectory, with higher proportions of minimally invasive esophagectomy (MIE) and ESD compared with the prior two years. This shift was paralleled by an increased representation of earlier-stage lesions managed endoscopically and a broader routine use of thoracoscopic/robotic techniques for resectable cases. Taken together, these findings highlight the value of continuous annual reporting from a high-volume center, which enables identification of incremental but clinically meaningful changes in case selection, procedural preference, and perioperative safety. The observed internal trends are consistent with practice patterns in East Asia, where high-volume centers and national databases report increasing use of MIE and expanding indications for ESD alongside improvements in diagnostic and endoscopic techniques (8-10).

An important issue in interpreting the 2018 cohort is the relatively low proportion of preoperative therapy. This treatment pattern should be understood in its historical context instead of being regarded as representative of current multidisciplinary practice. The CROSS trial had already provided landmark high-level evidence supporting neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery for resectable esophageal or esophagogastric junction cancer in 2012 and had substantially influenced Western practice. However, the adoption of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in China was relatively gradual. One important reason was the difference in disease epidemiology and trial populations. In China, esophageal cancer is predominantly squamous cell carcinoma (1), accounting for 85.79% of all cases, whereas the CROSS trial included a substantial proportion of adenocarcinoma patients (75%) (11). Therefore, although the CROSS trial was recognized as important evidence, many Chinese centers awaited locally generated phase III evidence in Chinese patients with locally advanced ESCC before broadly adopting neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy as a routine strategy.

The publication of the NEOCRTEC5010 trial in 2018 provided China-specific phase III evidence for neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced ESCC and accelerated the subsequent transition toward neoadjuvant multidisciplinary treatment. Therefore, the low use of preoperative therapy in this cohort reflects historical practice rather than current recommendations. In that case, the 2018 SCH cohort represents a transitional period from a surgery/postoperative-treatment dominant strategy toward the contemporary neoadjuvant multidisciplinary era, providing a reference for treatment patterns and survival outcomes from this transition period.

Pathological staging in 2018 again reflected predominance of squamous histology and a persistent burden of nodal disease, despite a modest decline in deep primary invasion (pT3 proportion fell to 42%). This pattern—fewer deeply invasive primary tumors among surgically treated patients but a stable node-positive rate—has been observed in national registries and multicenter series as detection and endoscopic management of superficial lesions expand, while patients proceeding to esophagectomy continue to include a substantial fraction with pathological positive lymph nodes (12). The median number of examined lymph nodes in the present cohort was 19, which exceeds the commonly used minimum threshold of 15 examined lymph nodes for pathological nodal assessment after esophagectomy. However, we acknowledge that it may still appear modest when compared with other high-volume centers at present (13,14). Several factors may have contributed to this finding, including the predominance of two-field lymphadenectomy in our cohort, the transition toward minimally invasive techniques and the evolving use of robotic-assisted esophagectomy during that period. Therefore, although the lymph node yield was acceptable for staging purposes, further standardization of lymphadenectomy templates and pathological lymph node retrieval remains an important target for quality improvement.

Perioperative safety in 2018 remained acceptable: overall complication incidence after esophagectomy was lower than some historical series and major complications (Clavien-Dindo grade ≥ III) stayed near prior local levels, while 30- and 90-day mortality were low. These results align with contemporary Japanese national estimates and Korean high-volume center reports that emphasize low perioperative mortality but substantial overall morbidity and readmission risk (15,16). The concordance between SCH outcomes and East-Asian national/high-volume data suggests that adoption of MIE/robotic techniques within experienced programs can be performed without raising short-term mortality. However, the frequency and pattern of specific complications (pulmonary events, pleural effusion, RLN dysfunction) vary across centers depending on case mix, anastomotic technique and extent of cervical/upper mediastinal dissection (17-19).

RLN injury and respiratory complications were the most common morbidities at SCH. This finding is consistent with evidence from large cohort studies that indicate RLN palsy remains an important and often under-appreciated source of downstream pulmonary complications, which prolonged hospitalization after esophagectomy (20). Recent systematic reviews and multicenter reports suggest heterogeneity in RLN injury rates across MIE, open and robot-assisted approaches, with some evidence that robotic assistance may reduce RLN injury in selected series (21,22). Nevertheless, technique, surgeon experience and the extent of nodal dissection in the upper mediastinum are major determinants of RLN risk (23-25). These observations prompt focused quality initiatives (intraoperative nerve monitoring, standardized nerve-sparing dissection templates, and early vocal cord assessment) to preserve oncologic completeness while reducing nerve-related morbidity.

The temporal changes we observed are plausibly attributable to three interacting factors. First, changing case selection and earlier diagnosis (more ESDs) reduce the relative fraction of deeply invasive primaries among surgically managed patients. Second, increasing procedural centralization and surgeon experience with MIE and robotic platforms likely account for maintained or improved lymph node yields and low perioperative mortality, a pattern echoed in Japanese and Korean high-volume data. Third, evolving multidisciplinary practice (selective neoadjuvant therapy, refined diagnostic/staging examination) changes both the nature of cases arriving to the operating room and the expected postoperative course. Because these factors covary, formal multivariable analyses that adjust for baseline patient characteristics, tumor stage, perioperative therapy and specific technical details are required to quantify independent effects on complications and survival.

From a practical standpoint, comparison with regional experience multicenter studies highlights several priorities for SCH in future development. Firstly, maintaining rigorous and standardized complication capture (ECCG definitions and Clavien-Dindo grading) will enhance comparability with national registries and peer institutions. Secondly, targeted interventions to prevent the most frequent morbidities (reduction of pulmonary complications, optimization of anastomotic technique and reconstruction route) should be prioritized and evaluated prospectively. Finally, participating in national or multinational registries and publishing adjusted outcome models will allow SCH to benchmark performance against East-Asian peers and to contribute to regionally relevant evidence on how best to balance oncologic radicality and functional preservation.

This report remains subject to the inherent limitations of a single-center, retrospective design and cannot provide population-level estimates comparable to nationwide registry studies. Accordingly, inter-year comparisons should be interpreted with caution, as they may be confounded by concurrent changes in staging strategies, case selection and perioperative care.

Conclusions

The SCH experience in 2018 reflected an ongoing transition towards minimally invasive and centralized care without compromising the oncologic outcomes or short-term safety. As part of a continuous annual registry, these findings provide a longitudinal reference for evaluating temporal changes in surgical practice. Further efforts should focus on reducing preventable surgery-related complications through continued refinement of perioperative management.

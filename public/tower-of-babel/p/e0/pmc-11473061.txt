Efficacy of adding ketamine to levobupivacaine in paravertebral block on acute and chronic pain in thoracotomy: a randomized controlled double-blinded trial
Authors: Saad Ahmed Moharam, Amgad Elshikh, Mohamed Abdelbadie, Ahmed Mohamed Ibrahim, Mostafa Mohamed Khedr Shaheen, Mohammed Said ElSharkawy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The addition of ketamine to levobupivacaine for paravertebral block resulted in better analgesia with no significant difference in chronic pain and complications in patients undergoing thoracotomy.

1. Introduction

Pain after thoracotomy is excruciating, and chest motions during respiration exacerbate it. Poor post-thoracotomy pain management results in a splinting effect on the chest, preventing inhaling profoundly and coughing, which can cause or contribute to atelectasis.20

Incision of the epidermis, rib and muscle damage, injury to the intercostal nerve, and pleural irritation all lead to acute postoperative pain development. Furthermore, extreme pain is a factor that contributes to post-thoracotomy pain syndrome (PTPS).6 Insufficient management of pain after thoracotomy in the postoperative phase may cause up to 68% of patients to acquire chronic pain.22

According to the IASP, PTPS is characterized by persistent or recurring pain along the incision site of a thoracotomy, lasting for at least 2 months after the surgical procedure. This pain is typically defined as a burning sensation with localized, distinct tenderness and recurrent dysesthesias at the site of the incision. Symptomatology indicative of neuropathic pain and damage suggests a complicated interaction between central and peripheral injuries.17

Several processes are involved in pain after thoracotomy; thus, treating acute pain after thoracotomy and avoiding the PTPS are serious concerns.5

The administration of appropriate analgesia enhances surgical operation outcomes. However, the ideal method for giving enough analgesia is still debatable, and the quest continues. Intravenous (IV) analgesic drugs, regional blocks, and adjuvant analgesic medications may be used to induce analgesia.13

Regional blocks give a higher level of IV techniques. Thoracic paravertebral block (TPVB) is a substitute for epidural analgesia that generates nerve blocks of many adjacent thoracic dermatomes above and below the injection site.21

Levobupivacaine has been thoroughly investigated for clinical application in regional anaesthesia. The nerve block duration induced by 0.5% levobupivacaine may be prolonged than that of an equal dosage of ropivacaine or bupivacaine.15

Many additives have been applied to increase their efficacy, such as dexamethasone, dexmedetomidine, morphine, ketamine, clonidine, and magnesium sulfate. The justification for using adjuvants to improve the local anesthetics (LA) action is their effect directly on the receptors inside the central nervous system and spinal cord.1

Ketamine blocks N-methyl-d-aspartate (NMDA) receptors within the spine. It also affects voltage-sensitive Ca2+ channels, monoaminergic, and opioid receptors. Therefore, it is deemed overall to affect nociception.26

There is a lack of studies that investigated the impact of ketamine addition to levobupivacaine in TPVB in thoracotomy. So, this trial was designed to assess the impact of adding ketamine to TPVB on chronic and acute pain in thoracotomy.

2. Methods

Sixty patients aged from 18 to 65 years old, both sexes, American Society of Anesthesiology physical status II and III scheduled for open thoracotomy, were enrolled in this randomized controlled double-blinded trial. The research was conducted from August 2023 to January 2024 with the approval of the Tanta University Hospitals Ethical Committee, Egypt (approval code: 36264PR244/6/23) and registration of ClinicalTrials.gov (ID: NCT06011746). All patients gave their informed written consent.

Exclusion criteria were other contraindications for TPVBs or unsuccessful block, body mass index >35 kg/m2, the probability of mechanical ventilation after surgery, depression, or other mental conditions requiring antidepressant medication, coagulopathy, alcohol, or substance abuse and levobupivacaine hypersensitivity.

2.1. Randomization and blindness

Computer‐generated randomization numbers were used for random allocation, and each patient's code was kept in an opaque sealed envelope. Patients were randomly distributed in a 1:1 ratio into 2 groups in a parallel manner: group K: patients received TPVB (19 mL of 0.5% levobupivacaine combined with 1 mL of ketamine [50 mg]).6 Group C: patients received TPVB (19 mL of 0.5% levobupivacaine mixed with 1 mL of normal saline) as a control arm. Both patients and the investigator were blind to the group's intervention. All medications were prepared, and the syringes were covered with aluminium foil by a clinical pharmacist unaware of the study grouping.

Proper history-taking and clinical assessment were done. Routine laboratory investigations were done, such as complete blood count, coagulation profile, and liver and renal function tests.

Each patient was educated on postoperative pain assessment with a numeric rating scale (NRS). Numeric rating scale (0 represents “no pain,” whereas 10 signifies “the utmost suffering conceivable”). At the operative theatre, standard monitoring (pulse oximetry, temperature probe, 5-lead electrocardiogram, noninvasive blood pressure, and capnography) was used. Two 18-gauge peripheral venous cannulas were inserted in all patients. Each subject was premedicated with intravenous IV midazolam, 2 mg. Patients were administered blocks after the induction of general anesthesia (GA).

2.2. Thoracic paravertebral block

On the proposed operation site, patients were placed after general anesthesia before skin incision, in lateral position when the patient's neck and back were bent forward. The patient supported, under strict aseptic technique, the spinous process of T6 was identified. A linear transducer was applied longitudinal at the level of T6 (2.5-3 cm) lateral to the midline with cranially directed orientation. A US (Philips CX50, extreme edition, Amsterdam, the Netherlands) machine with a 5 to 12 MHz linear probe was used. The transducer was moved medially until the transition of ribs to transverse processes was marked by a change in shape (ribs more convex than transverse processes) after identifying the rounded ribs and the parietal pleura underneath. The transducer was then slightly tilted laterally to locate the transverse process superiorly and the lower rib inferiorly on the screen. The desired paravertebral space, the pleura, and the lung tissue were obtained in this position.

Three milliliters of 2% lidocaine was used for skin infiltration. A 22-gauge needle was inserted utilizing the in-plane method in a craniocaudal direction. Negative blood aspiration and air were confirmed once the costotransverse ligament was reached. Nineteen milliliters levobupivacaine 0.5% with either 1 mL ketamine (50 mg) or normal saline (according to the allocation of each patient) was injected into the paravertebral space under ultrasound visualization.

The typical method of GA was utilized in each case. Induction of GA was done by IV propofol 1.5 to 2.5 mg/kg and IV 1 μg/kg fentanyl. The endotracheal tube was inserted after administration of 0.5 mg/kg of IV atracurium. The maintenance of GA was achieved using isoflurane at a concentration of 1% to 1.5% in combination with 50% to 100% oxygen. Incremental doses of IV atracurium 0.1 mg/kg were given. Patients were then mechanically ventilated while maintaining end-tidal CO2 at 30 to 35 mm Hg.

Intravenous fentanyl bolus doses of 1 µg/kg were given when the heart rate (HR) or mean arterial blood pressure (MAP) increased by more than 20% from the baseline (after exclusion of other causes than pain).

Intraoperative HR and MAP were measured at baseline, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 minutes, and at the end of surgical procedures in both groups.

When the anaesthesia was terminated at the end of the operation, the neuromuscular block was reversed with 0.08 mg/kg neostigmine and 0.02 mg/kg atropine; extubation was performed.

Patients were transferred to the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) when they became fully awake. In both groups, NRS was recorded at rest and at deep breathing at PACU, 1, 2, 18, 24, 36, and 48 hours.

All participants in the research were given a 1 g IV infusion of paracetamol every 8 hours. If the NRS pain score was >3, patients were permitted to receive bolus dosages of 3 mg IV morphine as rescue analgesia.

Any intraoperative or postoperative complications, including hematoma, nausea, vomiting, hypotension (described as any decline in the MAP of >20% of the preoperative starting point value or MAP ≤65 mm Hg), and bradycardia (HR <50 beats per minute) were recorded. Hypotension was handled with fluid bolus ± ephedrine 5 to 10 mg IV. Bradycardia was handled by atropine 0.01 mg/kg.

The emergence of PTPS was assessed at 2-month and 3-month follow-up. A diagnostic assessment of neuropathic PTPS was conducted using the Grading System for Neuropathic Pain, with cases scoring ≥2 (probable or definite neuropathic pain) considered positive for a neuropathic component.

The primary outcome was the time to first analgesia. The secondary outcomes were total morphine consumption after 24 hours, pain intensity during rest and deep breathing, incidence of chronic pain, and complications.

2.3. Sample size calculation

The calculation for the number of participants required was conducted with the assistance of G. power 3.1.9.2 software (Universitat Kiel, Kiel, Germany). The parameters set for this computation included an alpha error of 0.05 and a study power of 95%, aimed at revealing a 50% increase in time to first analgesia (the primary outcome) with TPVB with ketamine additive, the mean and SD of time to first analgesia was 5.3 ± 3.1 hours in TPVB without ketamine additive according to a previous study.6 To mitigate the potential impact of participant dropout, 4 additional cases were incorporated into each group, resulting in a total of 30 patients were recruited for each group.

2.4. Statistical analysis

SPSS version 27 (IBM, Armonk, NY) was used for statistical analysis. The Shapiro–Wilks test and histograms were used to determine the normality of the data distribution. The unpaired student t test was used to analyze the quantitative data represented by mean and SD. The Mann–Whitney test was used to analyze the nonparametric quantitative data, represented by the median and interquartile range. We used the χ2 or Fisher exact test to assess qualitative variables presented as frequencies and percentages. Statistical significance was considered when the 2-tailed P was 0.05 or below.

4. Discussion

The study used a dose of 1 mL (50 mg) of ketamine combined with 19 mL of 0.5% levobupivacaine in the paravertebral block. This dose was chosen based on previous studies that have shown the efficacy of ketamine as an adjuvant in regional anesthesia.6,16 The dose selection aimed to balance the analgesic benefits of ketamine with the potential for minimizing side effects associated with higher doses.

The main results of this research revealed that the first-time request for analgesia was significantly delayed in ketamine than in the control group. The total dose of morphine consumption in the first 24 and 48 hours was significantly decreased in the ketamine group than in the control group. Numeric rating scale at rest was significantly lower at 4, 6, 8, and 12 hours, while NRS at deep breathing was significantly lower at 4, 6, 8, 12, 18, and 24 hours in the ketamine group than in control one. Post-thoracotomy pain syndrome incidence at 2 and 3 months was matched between both groups. The hypotension and bradycardia were insignificantly different between both groups.

Research has shown the utility of TPVB and how its early postoperative analgesic effectiveness contributes to the avoidance of acute and chronic pain.19

Ketamine is a widely used, inexpensive, accessible, and safe drug, necessitating no expensive apparatus. Therefore, ketamine has the potential to be used especially in low- and middle-income countries.3,8,10,12

Ketamine decreases the central nervous system's sensitivity to pain by inhibiting NMDA receptors. Stimulation of NMDA receptors inside the spinal cord plays a vital function in acute and chronic pain development. Pain hypersensitivity and altered afferent noxious input processing are maintained after surgically caused tissue damage, in part via NMDA receptor activation.11

Levobupivacaine prevents the transmission of pain signals in the peripheral nerves by blocking sodium channels. They act on both central and peripheral components of the pain pathway when used together, providing enhanced and prolonged pain relief. By acting synergistically, lower doses of both drugs can achieve the desired level of anesthesia and analgesia. This dose reduction can minimize the risk of side effects associated with higher doses of each drug when used alone.16

The analgesic efficacy of ketamine as an adjunct to nerve blocks has inconsistent results in the existing literature. Studies on inguinal4 and brachial plexus14 blocks reported no significant analgesic benefits but a high incidence of adverse effects. In contrast, adding ketamine to femoral-sciatic nerve blockade enhanced the analgesic efficacy.27 Intrathecal administration of ketamine accelerated the onset of sensory and motor block.23,24 The combination of ketamine with pediatric caudal epidurals prolonged postoperative analgesia.7 Given the conflicting evidence, this study investigated the role of the ketamine-levobupivacaine combination in the paravertebral block.

In line with our findings, Chen et al.2 documented that adding ketamine to TPVB resulted in better analgesia, as evidenced by lower total opioid consumption and pain score than the TPVB alone in patients undergoing thoracoscopic surgery.

Also, El Mourad et al.6 illustrated that the time first to rescue analgesia was significantly delayed, and consumption was significantly decreased in the patients who received ketamine with TPVB than in patients who received TPVB alone in patients scheduled for modified radical mastectomy. Also, the incidence of complications was comparable between both groups.

Moreover, Mansour et al.16 found that the first time for analgesia was significantly delayed, and total rescue morphine dosage was significantly decreased in patients who received ketamine added to levobupivacaine than in patients with levobupivacaine only in the transversus abdominis plane block in abdominoplasty. Both groups had comparable adverse effects.

Also, Rashidi et al.18 studied cases undergoing thoracotomy and found that ketamine reduces the intensity of pain when added to LA in the serratus anterior plane block.

In agreement with our investigations, Xiang et al.25 showed that ketamine prolongs the analgesia duration when added to LA in different regional techniques.

The addition of ketamine to the conventional therapy for neuropathic pain resulted in a significant decline in pain intensity 30 days after the termination of ketamine treatment.9

In line with our results, Borys et al.1 demonstrated that ketamine administered before the procedure did not reduce post-thoracotomy pain syndrome severity at 1, 3, and 6 months.

In this study, the incidence of hypotension was 13.33% in group K and 3.33% in group C, and the incidence of bradycardia was 10% in group K and 3.33% in group C. Despite a statistically nonsignificant difference noted, a higher incidence of bradycardia and hypotension in the ketamine group was reported. The small sample size in our study limited to assess the incidence and required further studies to observe whether the incidence is higher in larger groups or not. Also, we reported even negative results with other studies.

The study was limited by a small sample size, making it insufficient to effectively assess secondary outcomes such as chronic pain and adverse events. In addition, the research was conducted at a single institution, limiting the generalizability of the findings. The research did not assess the cognitive functions. Further studies are recommended to compare other doses.

5. Conclusions

The addition of ketamine to levobupivacaine for paravertebral block resulted in better analgesia with no significant difference in chronic pain and complications in patients undergoing thoracotomy.

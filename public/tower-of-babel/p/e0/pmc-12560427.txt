Disease burden of HIV and its behavioral risks among adults aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region from 1990 to 2021: an analysis based on the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021
Authors: Haixia Liu, Qi Jing, Muzi Meng, Chenyu Sun, Qiyu Zhu, Christy Huang, Tianming Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe HIV epidemic remains a significant public health concern among individuals aged 20–54 years. However, comprehensive studies evaluating trends in HIV-related health burdens and associated behavioral risks in China and the Western Pacific Region are limited.MethodsThis study utilizes data from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study 2021 to examine HIV burden indicators—including the age specific rates (ASR) for prevalence (ASPR), incidence (ASIR), mortality (ASMR) and DALYs (ASDR)—among individuals aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region from 1990 to 2021. Long-term trends were analyzed using average annual percentage changes (AAPCs), while short-term trends were assessed through annual percentage changes (APCs). Furthermore, trends in HIV-related DALYs associated with behavioral risks were analyzed.ResultsIn China, ASPR increased by 751.27% (AAPC = 7.19, 95%CI: 7.02–7.35), ASIR by 232.26% (AAPC = 3.8, 95%CI: 2.62–4.99), ASMR by 1535.29% (AAPC = 9.07, 95%CI: 8.51–9.63), and ASDR by 1379.27% (AAPC = 8.72, 95%CI: 8.22–9.23). In the Western Pacific Region, ASPR increased by 1101.91% (AAPC = 8.34, 95%CI: 8.2–8.47), ASIR by 354.24% (AAPC = 4.68, 95%CI: 3.91–5.45), ASMR by 1241.67% (AAPC = 8.56, 95%CI: 8.05–9.08), and ASDR by 1135.86% (AAPC = 8.28, 95%CI: 7.83–8.73). Regarding the HIV-related DALYs rate attributable to behavioral risks, the corresponding rate for unsafe sex, intimate partner violence, and drug use showed a significant increase from 1990 to 2021 in China and the Western Pacific Region (all AAPC > 0, p < 0.05). Notably, the HIV burden was consistently higher in males than females in both regions, with most of gender disparities widening over time.ConclusionDespite significant progress in HIV/AIDS response, the burden remains substantial, with notable gender differences. Strengthened international cooperation, targeted prevention strategies, and expanded access to treatment are crucial to mitigating new HIV infections and improving health outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11812-7.

Introduction

Human immunodeficiency virus and acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (HIV/AIDS) remains a major public health challenge, contributing to significant morbidity and mortality worldwide. The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2021 estimated 1.43 million new HIV infections and 615,000 HIV-related deaths in 2025 [1]. The global age-standardized prevalence, death, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) rates in 2019 increased by 307.26, 4.34, and 221.91 per 100,000 cases, respectively, compared to 1990 [2]. Furthermore, the Asia–Pacific region has witnessed a marked rise in HIV burden, with 72% of its countries experiencing increased age-standardized DALYs, mortality, and incidence from 1990 to 2019 [3]. From 2010 and 2019, in China, both the AIDS incidence and the mortality increased, remaining the leading cause of death among notifiable infectious diseases in 2019 [4]. In addition, the Philippines, the country with the fastest-growing HIV epidemic in the Western Pacific Region, reported a 411% increase in daily incidence between 2012 and 2023 [5]. Notably, the trends in HIV/AIDS incidence and prevalence differ by sex, with females experiencing the highest burden in Central Asia, while males are more affected in Oceania and high-income Asia–Pacific regions [6]. Although globally, young and middle-aged adults bear the highest HIV burden, China exhibits a distinct trend with a significant burden among the elderly [7]. Overall, the disease burden of HIV/AIDS varies considerably by region, sex, and age.

A comprehensive review of existing literature highlights that reducing high-risk behaviors is an effective strategy for mitigating HIV infection, particularly in vulnerable populations [8]. The primary behavioral risk factors contributing to HIV/AIDS DALYs include unsafe sex, drug use, and intimate partner violence (IPV) [2, 9]. In 2019, unsafe sex was the leading cause of HIV-related age-standardized disability-adjusted life years in the 29 countries of the Asia–Pacific region, followed by drug use [3].

Sexual transmission remains the predominant route of HIV infection. It was reported that, 58.6% of people living with HIV (PLWH) in China in 2018 acquired the infection through heterosexual contact, while 30.2% were infected via male-to-male transmission [10]. Globally, the age-standardized mortality rate and DALYs rate attributable to unsafe sex were 11.98 and 570.78 per 100,000 people, respectively. While among unsafe-sex-related diseases, HIV/AIDS had the highest age-standardized mortality rate and DALY rate [11]. Furthermore, unsafe sex accounted for 15,381.16 deaths and 331,140.56 DALYs among adults aged 70 years and older, representing 90.73% of all HIV/AIDS-related deaths and 91.12% of all HIV/AIDS-related DALYs in this age group [6].

Injecting drug use and needle sharing have also been well-documented risk factors for HIV transmission [12]. Early harm reduction programs, such as the needle and syringe programs implemented in Yunnan, China, prevented an estimated 16–20% (5,200–7,500 cases) of expected HIV infections and led to gains of 1,300–1,900 DALYs between 2002 and 2008 [13]. However, 9.0% of Chinese PLWH still acquired the infection through injecting drug use in 2018 [10]. Additionally, high-risk sexual behaviors and drug injecting behaviors are prevalent among drug users [14], and previous studies indicate that the proportion of HIV cases attributable to sexual transmission among drug users will continue to rise from 2005 to 2035 [15]. This trend may be driven by the association between recreational drug use and increased engagement in risky sexual behaviors, further elevating the risk of HIV transmission and the overall disease burden [16].

IPV is another significant behavioral risk factor for HIV/AIDS. According to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2019 study, HIV/AIDS was the leading cause of IPV-related mortality and DALYs [17]. Between 1990 and 2019, the global burden of HIV/AIDS attributable to IPV increased substantially [18], with age-standardized deaths and DALYs due to IPV rising by 12.83% and 4.00% respectively and directly accounted for 0.14% of global all-cause mortality and 0.32% of global DALYs by 2019 [17]. Additionally, among women aged 15 years and older in China, IPV-related HIV/AIDS deaths and DALYs increased by 4.66% and 4.42%, respectively [19].

Implementing interventions targeting high-risk behaviors in vulnerable populations could yield substantial health benefits and be highly cost-effective. However, most existing studies have focused either on the overall disease burden or on isolated risk factors within specific regions. The dynamic interplay between behavioral risk factors and the HIV disease burden remains insufficiently explored, necessitating further research to inform effective public health strategies.

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) dataset serves as a crucial platform for examining the evolving relationship between behavioral risk factors and the HIV disease burden. Utilizing the most recent GBD 2021 data, this study systematically compares the spatial and temporal trends of HIV burden among non-elderly adults aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region from 1990 to 2021. The analysis specifically evaluates the impact of key interventional risk factors, including unsafe sex, drug use, and intimate partner violence, on changes in HIV-related disability-adjusted life years. The findings aim to provide a scientific foundation for the development of regionally tailored prevention and control strategies, targeted behavioral interventions for high-risk populations, and efforts to achieve the United Nations’ goal of ending the AIDS epidemic by 2030.

Methods

Data source and collection

This study utilized data from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2021 [20] to assess the burden of HIV/AIDS and its associated behavioral risk factors among non-elderly adults aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region. The data were obtained from the GBD Results Tool (https://www.healthdata.org/data-tools-practices/interactive-visuals/gbd-results) [21]. The GBD framework provides a comprehensive platform for comparing the magnitude of diseases, injuries, and risk factors across age groups, genders, countries, and regions over time. It generates estimates for all-cause mortality, cause-specific mortality, years of life lost due to premature death (YLLs), years lived with disability (YLDs), and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) [22]. GBD 2021 integrates data from 328,938 sources, enabling insights into health disparities across demographic and socioeconomic groups while underscoring the importance of evidence-based strategies for mitigating risk factors and improving global health outcomes [23].

In this study, HIV/AIDS was categorized under the cause group “HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infections.” The analysis did not differentiate between the two major HIV subtypes (HIV-1 and HIV-2). The case definition was based on the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes: ICD-10: B20–B24, C46–C469, D84.9; ICD-9: 042–044, 112–118 (post-1980), 130 (post-1980), 136.3–136.8 (post-1980), 176.0–176.9 (post-1980), 279 (post-1980); ICD-9 BTL: B184–B185 [24]. The geographic categories analyzed were “China” (excluding data from Taiwan Province) and the “Western Pacific Region” [25]. The study assessed four key indicators of HIV/AIDS disease burden: prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALYs. We did not set specific criteria for data collection but followed the GBD standardized regional division.

Additionally, we extracted HIV/AIDS-related DALY rates linked to behavioral risk factors among non-elderly adults aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region to examine trends in HIV/AIDS burden attributable to behavior-related risks. In GBD 2021, three behavioral risk factors were identified as contributors to HIV/AIDS-related DALYs in this age group: drug use, intimate partner violence, and unsafe sex. A detailed methodological description of each behavioral risk factor is available in the GBD 2021 Methods Appendices (https://www.healthdata.org/gbd/methods-appendices-2021).

Data analysis

Data on the prevalence, incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years attributable to HIV/AIDS among adults aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region had been extracted. The GBD database provides three metrics for each measure: absolute number, percentage, and rate. In this study, we focused on analyzing the absolute number and rate for each index of disease burden. The rates are defined as the corresponding indices per 100,000 population. Detailed definitions of these metrics are provided in GBD data and tools guide Appendix 3 (https://www.healthdata.org/research-analysis/about-gbd/gbd-data-and-tools-guide).

Additionally, the age specific rates (ASR) for prevalence (ASPR), incidence (ASIR), mortality (ASMR) and DALYs (ASDR) were also extracted, categorized into the following age groups: 20–24 years, 25–29 years, 30–34 years, 35–39 years, 40–44 years, 45–49 years, and 50–54 years. To assess temporal trends in disease burden, we employed the Joinpoint regression analysis model, which effectively identifies and quantifies key change points in time-series data [26, 27]. The core function of Joinpoint regression model is to detect turning points in time series data and quantify the rate of trend change at different stages. Considering that the HIV burden in China and the Western Pacific Region may have undergone multiple stages of change between 1990 and 2021, the Joinpoint analysis can use its model advantages to automatically identify the time points when the trend direction has significantly changed. This model calculates the annual percent change (APC) and the average annual percent change (AAPC), along with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs), to illustrate trends over the study period (1990–2021) [27]. The permutation test was conducted to select the final model. A detailed description of the Joinpoint regression analysis model can be found in previous literature [28].

Furthermore, we analyzed trends in HIV/AIDS-related DALY rates associated with behavioral risk factors using the Joinpoint regression analysis model. All statistical analyses were conducted using Joinpoint software (version 5.3.0) [29].

The incidence of HIV among adults aged 20–54 years in China rose by 301.16%, increasing from 5.46 thousand cases in 1990 to 21.9 thousand in 2021. The ASIR grew by 232.26%, from 0.93 per 100,000 in 1990 to 3.09 per 100,000 in 2021. A sustained increase in ASIR was observed during 1990–2021 (AAPC = 3.8, 95% CI: 2.62–4.99), with the most significant surge occurring between 1990 and 1994 (APC = 21.92, 95% CI: 18.9–25.03). However, periods of decline were recorded between 2004 and 2009 (APC = −9.16, 95% CI: −10.64 to −7.66) and 2009 and 2014 (APC = −5.28, 95% CI: −7.25 to −3.28).

HIV-related mortality among adults aged 20–54 years in China surged by 1,870.88%, rising from 1,000 deaths in 1990 to 19.73 thousand in 2021. The ASMR increased by 1,535.29%, from 0.17 per 100,000 in 1990 to 2.78 per 100,000 in 2021. A significant upward trend in ASMR was identified during 1990–2021 (AAPC = 9.07, 95% CI: 8.51–9.63), with the most rapid increase occurring between 1990 and 2001 (APC = 17.35, 95% CI: 16.52–18.2). A decline in ASMR was observed only between 2005 and 2012 (APC = −1.69, 95% CI: −2.59 to −0.78).

The disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to HIV increased by 1,684.47%, from 56.76 thousand in 1990 to 1.01 million in 2021. The ASDR rose by 1,379.27%, from 9.65 per 100,000 in 1990 to 142.75 per 100,000 in 2021. A continuous increase in ASDR was observed from 1990 to 2021 (AAPC = 8.72, 95% CI: 8.22–9.23), with the highest growth rate recorded between 1990 and 2001 (APC = 16.81, 95% CI: 16.03–17.59). A period of decline in ASDR was identified only between 2005 and 2012 (APC = −1.47, 95% CI: −2.29 to −0.65).

Discussion

Significant progress has been made in reducing HIV incidence and mortality, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and several other regions. However, disparities in HIV containment persist [1]. Numerous complex challenges—including the development of preventive vaccines and a cure, as well as issues related to inequities, discrimination, and human rights—continue to impede efforts in HIV control and treatment [30]. In the Philippines, which has experienced the fastest-growing HIV epidemic in the Western Pacific Region over the past decade, the daily incidence of HIV cases increased by 411% from 2012 to 2023. Stigma, inadequate healthcare access for people who inject drugs (PWID), and sociocultural and political barriers remain significant obstacles to providing HIV-related medical services [5, 31].

Furthermore, the impact of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) related policies on the HIV epidemic remains unclear. In China, non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs)—including face masks, social distancing, stay-at-home mandates, lockdown measures, and contact tracing—were implemented as key public health strategies and effectively mitigated the spread of COVID-19 [32, 33]. However, these stringent measures may have inadvertently constrained the allocation of medical resources for non-COVID-19 conditions. Specifically, restrictions on interpersonal contact and gatherings, combined with the uneven distribution of HIV self-testing (HIVST) kits, may have created barriers to HIV testing services. This, in turn, could have delayed diagnoses and led to an underestimation of the true burden of HIV/AIDS [34].

In this study, we analyzed trends in the HIV burden—including prevalence, incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years—among individuals aged 20–54 years in China and the Western Pacific Region. Additionally, we examined the association between HIV-related DALY rates and behavioral risk factors in both China and the broader region. Regrading the reason for choosing China and the Western Pacific Region as research areas, first, though HIV prevalence in the Western Pacific has remained low at 0.1% over the past two decades, the low prevalence in the general population may mask the volume of HIV health burden and high levels of HIV infection among key populations because of the large population base, which gives the analysis on HIV health burden in the Western Pacific Region great importance [35]. Second, as shown in the results, China accounts for a large proportion of the HIV prevalence number (32.8% in 2021), incidence number (28.7% in 2021), number of deaths (64.4% in 2021), and number of DALYs (61.1% in 2021) in the entire Western Pacific Region. Therefore, China's HIV epidemic has a significant impact on the overall HIV prevention and control situation of the region.

In China, the growth on the rate of HIV prevalence gradually slowed from 1990 to 2014 before reversing into a declining trend from 2014 to 2021. HIV incidence surged between 1990 and 2004, followed by a significant decline from 2004 onward, eventually stabilizing around 2014. In contrast, mortality in China exhibited an overall upward trend from 1990 to 2021, with a temporary decline between 2005 and 2012. A similar pattern was observed for HIV-related DALYs, which generally increased over time, except for a slight decline during the same 2005–2012 period. Among behavioral risk factors, the HIV-related DALY rate associated with unsafe sex showed a persistent increase from 1990 to 2021, with a temporary decline between 2005 and 2012. The DALY rate linked to intimate partner violence increased significantly from 1990 to 2005, fluctuated between 2005 and 2016, and remained stable from 2016 to 2021. The DALY rate associated with drug use exhibited a steady rise from 1990 to 2005, plateaued from 2005 to 2013, surged between 2013 and 2016, and fluctuated from 2016 to 2021.

To address the HIV epidemic, China has implemented a series of laws, policies, and guidelines, allocating substantial political and financial resources to HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment. Initially concentrated among injection drug users, the epidemic has since shifted toward sexual transmission in the general population [36]. While China’s HIV prevention and control strategies have effectively stabilized prevalence and incidence among adults aged 20–54 years between 1990 and 2021, the persistent upward trends in mortality and DALYs underscore the significant health burden of HIV in China. This highlights the critical need for strengthened HIV prevention and management strategies, with a particular focus on comprehensive interventions for HIV-infected individuals. These should include psychological support, linkage-to-care programs, treatment and adherence education, and management of HIV-related complications to improve health outcomes and reduce mortality rates [30, 37, 38]. Furthermore, the increasing health burden associated with unsafe sex, along with the sustained impact of intimate partner violence and drug use, underscores the ongoing importance of health education, support services for victims of violence, and condom use advocacy campaigns [39]. The increasing use of synthetic drugs also become a serious issue in HIV prevention in China. Although the use of traditional substances such as heroin still exists in some regions, newer synthetic drugs have gradually replaced heroin and become the drugs of choice in some of entertainment venues in China [40].

The use of synthetic drugs not only increases the frequency of high risk behaviors, but also presents considerable challenges for law enforcement [41]. Additionally, previous studies have shown that stigma can create significant barriers to rehabilitation and recovery in people who use drugs [42, 43]. Therefore, the combined effect of persistent stigma and the rise of synthetic drug use may undermine the effectiveness of harm reduction efforts on drug use in China, which emphasizes the importance of combining health education with law enforcement for high-risk populations. However, our study did not include data beyond 2021, limiting our ability to assess the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the HIV epidemic. Given China's relaxation of its zero-COVID policy at the end of 2022 [44], further research is needed to explore potential rebounds in HIV transmission and changes in sexual behavior patterns among high-risk populations before and after this policy adjustment.

In the Western Pacific Region, distinct patterns of change in the HIV health burden have been observed. The prevalence rate steadily increased from 1990 to 2021, although the growth rate began to slow after 2002 while maintaining an overall upward trend. The incidence rate exhibited a different trajectory, rising significantly from 1990 to 2002, decreasing notably from 2002 to 2012, and then returning to an upward trend from 2012 to 2021. Mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) due to HIV followed a similar pattern: a significant increase from 1990 to 2006, a decline from 2006 to 2013, a subsequent rise from 2013 to 2016, and then stabilization of the mortality rate from 2016 to 2021, while the DALY rate showed a slight decline during this period. HIV-related DALYs associated with behavioral risks displayed varying trends. The DALY rate linked to unsafe sex rose significantly from 1990 to 2006, declined slightly from 2006 to 2013, increased again from 2013 to 2016, and then remained stable from 2016 to 2021. The DALY rate associated with intimate partner violence increased sharply from 1990 to 2005, followed by fluctuations from 2005 to 2021. Similarly, HIV-related DALY rate linked to drug use followed a comparable pattern, increasing from 1990 to 2005 and fluctuating thereafter. Compared to China, the rising prevalence and incidence trends in the broader Western Pacific Region present significant challenges in HIV response efforts. Given that China is a major contributor to the region’s HIV burden—accounting for a substantial proportion of both new and existing infections—the HIV epidemic in other countries within the region, excluding China, may be even more complex. Addressing this ongoing public health challenge requires sustained global leadership, commitment, collaboration, and innovation. HIV prevention and control strategies must be tailored to the specific conditions of each country and region, promoting equitable collaboration, technological advancements, and knowledge sharing. To sustain and enhance the effectiveness of the HIV response, efforts should prioritize the equitable application of existing scientific evidence and the optimal implementation of available tools to maximize public health impact [30].

Our study found that in China and the Western Pacific Region, the HIV disease burden among males aged 20–54 years was consistently higher than that among females between 1990 and 2021, with the gender disparity generally increasing over the past three decades. In China, the gap in prevalence and incidence rates initially widened before stabilizing over time. Additionally, HIV-related disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) associated with behavioral risks, including unsafe sex and drug use, also exhibited gender disparities, with male-associated rates exceeding those of females and the gap continuing to expand. Notably, in China, the gender disparity in HIV-related DALYs attributed to unsafe sex remains significant and continues to grow. The underlying reasons for these differences are multifaceted, involving socio-cultural factors and shifts in transmission patterns. The higher HIV disease burden in males compared to females is likely driven by the evolving HIV/AIDS epidemic in China, where transmission has predominantly shifted to sexual contact [45–47]. The increasing proportion of men who have sex with men (MSM) has further contributed to the rising disease burden among males [44, 48, 49]. Studies from Hong Kong, China, indicate that males exhibit more liberal attitudes toward premarital sex and other high-risk sexual behaviors than females, reporting greater sexual activity and engagement in riskier behaviors [50, 51]. Cultural factors also play a role in these disparities; in East Asia, women are more likely to experience their first sexual encounter within a stable relationship, potentially lowering concerns about sexually transmitted infections. Furthermore, stigma surrounding premarital sex and high-risk behaviors may exert greater psychological pressure on women, discouraging engagement in such activities [51]. Given the growing gender disparities in the HIV disease burden across the Western Pacific Region, targeted HIV interventions tailored to specific populations are essential to address health inequities effectively.

Our study has several limitations that warrant acknowledgment. First, potential deficiencies in data quality and coverage in the Western Pacific Region especially among low-income and middle-income countries (LMICs), as well as the accuracy of the statistical methods employed in GBD 2021, may affect the representativeness of the reported values relative to real-world figures [52–54]. Hence, more comprehensive and higher-quality data sources are needed to be explored in the future [53]. Second, our analysis of the Western Pacific Region provides an overarching assessment but does not account for the heterogeneity of HIV epidemics across different countries and regions. The HIV epidemiology varies across countries in the Western Pacific Region, which could influence national health policies, future research should include cross-national comparisons within the Western Pacific Region and broaden the range of data sources, which could better understand these variations and generate tailored intervention for specific countries. Third, this study focused on individuals aged 20–54 years, examining the HIV-related health burden associated with behavioral factors within this group while excluding other risk factors, such as mother-to-child transmission. As the HIV burden and risk behaviors in this age group do not fully represent the entire population, further studies should assess the broader HIV health burden, including both behavioral and non-behavioral risk factors across all age groups. Finally, according to previous study results, the COVID-19 pandemic has influenced the allocation and acquisition of medical resources related to HIV, which thereby impacted accurate monthly reporting of HIV in China [34]. Although our study spans the period from 1990 to 2021, it does not fully account for the impact of COVID-19 on the HIV epidemic or the potential effects of China's adjustments to its COVID-19 containment policies. Future investigations should explore these influences to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the evolving HIV landscape.

Conclusion

Despite significant advancements in HIV/AIDS response, the disease burden in China and the Western Pacific Region remains substantial, with notable gender disparities. Unsafe sex and drug use persist as critical factors contributing to high-risk behaviors, necessitating targeted intervention strategies. Effective HIV epidemic control requires comprehensive collaboration and communication to facilitate the exchange of knowledge and technologies, develop region-specific prevention and control strategies, and ultimately reduce new infections while enhancing health outcomes for people living with HIV.

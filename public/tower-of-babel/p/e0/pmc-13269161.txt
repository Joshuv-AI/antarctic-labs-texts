Reply to: Blood Pressure‐Lowering Effect of SGLT2 Inhibitors in Patients Without Antihypertensive Treatment: A Real‐World Data Analysis
Authors: Michihiro Satoh, Reina Ito, Hiroki Nobayashi, Yutaro Iwabe, Seiya Izumi, Hideaki Hashimoto, Hirohito Metoki
Published: 2026

We thank Dr. Şahin et al. for their careful reading of our study and for raising important points regarding our recent report [1]. The issues of blood pressure (BP) measurement variability, the clinical significance of modest BP reduction, regression to the mean, potential behavioral confounding, and the limited explanatory capacity of our model are all valid points. We would like to provide additional clarification on each of these points below.

First, we cannot entirely exclude the possibility that measurement heterogeneity contributed to the BP reduction observed in the sodium‐glucose cotransporter‐2 (SGLT2) inhibitor new users. As we have discussed previously, Japanese real‐world databases based on annual health check‐ups consistently involve this issue because health check‐up providers do not always follow recommended measurement protocols [2, 3]. However, because the BP changes in the SGLT2 inhibitor new‐user group and the dipeptidyl peptidase‐4 (DPP‐4) inhibitor new‐user group were evaluated within the same national health check‐up framework, any systematic measurement heterogeneity would be expected to bias the estimates in both groups equally rather than generate a between‐group difference.

Second, the clinical significance of a modest BP reduction in a population with well‐controlled baseline BP warrants debate, as raised by Dr. Şahin et al. We first wish to emphasize that the primary aim of this study was not to establish a large or clinically decisive antihypertensive effect of SGLT2 inhibitors, but rather to characterize the magnitude and determinants of BP change as observed in routine clinical practice in the real world. Furthermore, BP changes at the population level should be distinguished from changes observed within an individual; the former generally have a greater impact on disease prevention from a public health perspective. We should also note that the population‐attributable fraction of BP for cardiovascular diseases has been reported to be relatively high even in individuals not reaching hypertension thresholds [4, 5].

Third, regression to the mean should be considered when interpreting BP changes in observational studies. In the present study, new users of SGLT2 inhibitors were compared with propensity‐matched DPP‐4 inhibitor new users with similar baseline BP levels, which would be expected to balance this statistical phenomenon between groups. Moreover, participants were not selected based on predefined BP thresholds, a situation in which regression to the mean is most pronounced. For instance, as we previously demonstrated using another Japanese real‐world database, restricting analyses to individuals with BP ≥140/≥90 mmHg led to an apparent BP decline among untreated individuals [6]. Taken together, regression to the mean is unlikely to fully explain the differential BP reduction after SGLT2 inhibitor initiation observed in the present study [1]. Meanwhile, individual‐level regression to the mean cannot be excluded and may have contributed to the relatively large standard deviation observed in BP change estimates in the present study [1].

Finally, unmeasured lifestyle changes, such as dietary sodium restriction or increased physical activity, represent an important limitation of the present study. However, if such unmeasured lifestyle changes had critically affected the results, a similar BP change would also have been expected among new users of DPP‐4 inhibitors. Because both groups comprised individuals initiating a new antidiabetic agent, their motivation for lifestyle modification would likely have been similar. The factors raised by Dr. Şahin et al., such as natriuresis, osmotic diuresis, and improved vascular function, were not directly assessed in our analysis and warrant consideration in future studies to better understand the mechanisms underlying SGLT2 inhibitor‐mediated BP reduction. It should be noted, however, that these factors represent intermediate mediators of BP reduction and therefore should not be included in a statistical model designed to estimate the overall amplitude of BP change following SGLT2 inhibitor treatment.

Despite the inherent limitations of real‐world observational data, we believe that our findings contribute to a better understanding of the magnitude and determinants of BP reduction with SGLT2 inhibitor initiation in real‐world settings [1, 3].

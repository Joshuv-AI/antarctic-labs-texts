Histological changes of conjunctiva-associated lymphoid tissue caused by pneumonia in Holstein young cows
Authors: Keigo Kosenda, Yuki Ishiguro, Yuto Sano, Kazuya Matsuda, Tomochika Sugiura, Takashi Murakami, Ayano Sato, Hiromichi Ohtsuka, Shigeo Fukuda, Yasuhiro Kon, Takashi Namba, Osamu Ichii
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The number of conjunctival IgA-positive cells increases in pneumonia cows.•Conjunctival epithelium of pneumonia cows is frequently discontinuous.•Respiratory antigen stimulation by pneumonia alter conjunctival immune responses.

Introduction

Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) is a lymphoid cluster located in the mammalian systemic mucosa (Liebler-Tenorio & Pabst, 2006). In general, MALT cells contain epithelial microfold cells (M cells) specialized in antigen uptake (Owen, 1977) and various organized immune cells (Cesta, 2006). MALT induces antigen-specific immunoglobulin A (IgA) production on mucosal surfaces through these histological features (Cesta, 2006). Unlike other antibody subclasses, IgA can prevent microbial invasion of mucosal surfaces (Kiyono & Fukuyama, 2004). Gut-associated lymphoid tissue (LT) (Parsons et al., 1989), nasopharyngeal-associated LT (NALT) (Meek et al., 2022), bronchus-associated LT (BALT) (Anderson et al., 1986), larynx-associated LT (Casteleyn et al., 2008), and genital organ-associated LT (GOALT) (Chuluunbaatar et al., 2022) have been identified in cows.

In humans and several animals, eye-associated LT (EALT), including conjunctiva-associated LT (CALT) (Chodosh et al., 1998) and tear duct-associated LT (TALT) (Knop & Knop, 2001; Oya et al., 2021) have been identified. CALT induces conjunctival and corneal immune responses (Siebelmann et al., 2013). We previously reported that cows possess CALTs with histological features similar to other MALTs (Kosenda et al., 2023). We discovered that cows have a local mucosal immune system in their conjunctiva, leading to the development of a new preventive method for ocular infectious diseases. We also found that the CALT in calves was less mature than that in adult cows. In mice, ocular antigen stimulation leads to the development of the CALT, which is mainly composed of B cells and CD4-positive (+) and CD8+ T cells (Steven et al., 2008). However, the factors that cause CALT in cows have not yet been investigated in detail.

The immune responses of MALTs localized in different mucosa are related, for example antigen stimulation and B cell activation in one MALT can induce IgA secretion from the other mucosa. This relationship between the immune responses of the different mucosae is known as common mucosal immune system (Kiyono & Fukuyama, 2004). In particular, the conjunctiva is anatomically connected to the nasal and respiratory mucosa via the lacrimal drainage system (Horst and Hans-Georg, 2016). Additionally, it was reported in mice that the EALT and NALT are connected by lymphatic networks (Lohrberg et al., 2018). Furthermore, TALT (Nagatake et al., 2009) and NALT (Fukuyama et al., 2002) develop postnatally, unlike secondary lymphoid tissues such as Peyer's patches with organogenic molecules different from Peyer's patches and other lymphoid tissues. Based on these anatomical continuities and similar developmental characteristics, the EALT and NALT are believed to form a craniofacial mucosal immune system (Okada et al., 2011). These findings suggest a strong mucosal immune relationship between the lacrimal and respiratory mucosa.

Pneumonia is a major infectious disease in cows, resulting in culling, death, and economic losses from reduced production and treatment costs (Ives et al., 2015). Furthermore, pneumonia is the main reason for the increase in antimicrobial use in cows, contributing to antimicrobial resistance (Brault et al., 2019). Therefore, more effective methods are needed to prevent pneumonia. Mucosal vaccination via intranasal inoculation, which induces respiratory mucosal IgA secretion and prevents the onset of pneumonia, has also been used in cows (Osman et al., 2018; Rossi et al., 2021). The link between the lacrimal and respiratory mucosal immune responses has been applied to mucosal vaccines. Eye drop immunization targeting CALT can induce antigen-specific IgA production in the mouse nasal mucosa (Seo et al., 2010). Eye drop vaccination is expected to prevent respiratory infectious diseases such as influenza in mice (Kim et al., 2015) and ferrets (Yoon et al., 2016). Clarifying the immunological relationship between the lacrimal and respiratory mucosa in cows could lead to new preventive methods against infectious respiratory diseases.

While it is established that conjunctival antigen sensitization triggers a respiratory mucosal humoral immune response, the reverse—whether respiratory antigen sensitization induces a conjunctival mucosal humoral immune response—remains unclear. Rabbits with surgically closed nasolacrimal ducts show reduced conjunctival inflammatory responses by interferon-gamma-producing T cells after intranasal immunization (Chentoufi et al., 2010). This suggests that respiratory antigen sensitization triggers a conjunctival cellular immune response through the nasolacrimal duct; however, the humoral immune—specifically involving CALT and IgA-producing cells—remains unclear, particularly in cows. In cases of bovine pneumonia, respiratory mucosal antigen stimulation is intensified by pathogenic microorganisms. This study aimed to clarify whether chronic respiratory antigen sensitization associated with pneumonia may promote CALT development and conjunctival IgA production by performing a histological comparison between pneumonia cows and non-pneumonia controls. This provides evidence linking respiratory inflammation to immune changes at the ocular surface in pneumonia-affected cows.

Materials and methods

Animals and sample preparation

This study included a total of 22 Holstein breed cows autopsied at Rakuno Gakuen University (Ebetsu, Japan) and euthanized for other research purposes at Hokkaido University (Sapporo, Japan). Cows at Rakuno Gakuen University were provided by neighboring dairy farm for poor prognosis or low productivity. The cows were divided into normal and pneumonia groups. Cows without macroscopic pneumonia lesions were classified as the normal, while cows with lung abscesses and caseous necrosis diagnosed as having chronic pneumonia were classified as the pneumonia groups (Fig. 1 A). No mucosal infection or inflammation other than in the lungs was observed at autopsy in any of the cows used. The normal group included 10 cows (2 male, 3 castrated male, and 5 freemartin cows, 29 to 253-day-old, average age: 107.7-day-old), whilst the pneumonia group had 12 cows (3 male and 9 castrated male cows, 32 to 181-day-old, average age: 107.8-day-old). Anamnestic data and histological sample availability of the cows used in this study are shown in Table 1. The cows were euthanized in the presence of an anesthesiologist, in accordance with the regulations of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Rakuno Gakuen University. Cows at Rakuno Gakuen University were sedated with xylazine hydrochloride (0.2 mg/kg body weight) via the intravascular route followed by general anesthesia with intravenous thiamylal sodium (9 mg/kg body weight) injection. Then, cows were administered a suxamethonium chloride hydrate (0.2 mg/kg body weight) via the intravenous route and euthanized. Cows at Hokkaido University were sedated with xylazine hydrochloride (3.0 mg/kg body weight) via intramuscular injection, followed by intravenous pentobarbital sodium (500 mg/kg body weight) for general anesthesia. Then, cows were euthanized by intravenous administration of a saturated potassium chloride solution (at least 0.1 mL/kg body weight). After euthanasia, left and right eyeballs with eyelids were removed and fixed with 10 % neutral buffered formalin for more than 1 week. Following fixation, the upper, lower, and third eyelids were sampled and trimmed as previously described (Kosenda et al., 2023). VH23C7) and the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Hokkaido University (approval no. 19-0097, 24-0069). Validation of the pneumonia model

To confirm pneumonia status, we compared the histological features of representative lung tissues between the two groups. Healthy cows maintained normal alveolar and bronchiolar structures without inflammatory cell infiltration. In contrast, the pneumonia group exhibited disruption of these structures with infiltration of neutrophils and mononuclear cells (Fig. 1 B). These histological findings indicated that the classification of chronic pneumonia in the diseased group was appropriate.

Histological examination

The fixed conjunctival samples were dehydrated using a series of ethanol solutions and embedded in paraffin. Paraffin sections (4 μm thick) were deparaffinized and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H & E) or immunohistochemistry (IHC). IHC for CD3, CD20, IBA1, cytokeratin 18 (CK18), and bovine IgA was performed. For IgA, lung tissues were also examined. After deparaffinization and antigen retrieval, sections were treated with 0.3 % hydrogen peroxide/methanol for 20 min and blocked with 10 % normal goat serum (SABPO kit, Nichirei Bioscience, Tokyo, Japan) for 1 h at room temperature. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibody. After incubation with each secondary antibody for 30 min at room temperature, sections were treated with streptavidin-peroxidase (Nichirei Bioscience) for 30 min and 3,3’-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride-hydrogen peroxide solution for 4 min. Finally, the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. Histoplanimetry

On histological observation, CALT was defined as conjunctival lymphoid clusters of more than 50 mononuclear cells. According to a previous report (Chuluunbaatar et al., 2022), each CALT was classified as diffuse lymphoid tissue (DLT) without a follicular structure or lymphatic follicles (LF). Conjunctival DLTs, LFs, CK18+ epithelial cells, and IgA+ mononuclear cells in the lamina propria were counted by histologically observing the entire length of each section. The appearance rate was calculated by dividing each number by the length of the observed conjunctiva in each section. The length of the conjunctiva, area of the CALT, and epithelium thickness were measured using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA, http://rsbweb.nih.gov/ij/). Epithelium thickness was defined as the distance from the apical side of the epithelial layer to the basement membrane. Measurements were performed on the thinnest epithelial layer covering each DLT and LF as shown in Fig. 4 A. The epithelium thickness was defined as 0 μm when the epithelium was discontinuous, as shown in Fig. 4 B. The percentage of discontinuous epithelial layers covering the DLT and LF was calculated by dividing the number of DLTs and LFs exhibiting epithelial discontinuity by the total number observed. The appearance rate of DLTs and LFs in each cow were calculated as the mean of the upper and lower eyelids on both sides and the mean of the third eyelids on both sides. The area and epithelium thickness of DLTs and LFs in each cow were calculated as the mean of the observed DLT and LF. DLTs and LFs with defects in the epithelium due to artifacts were excluded. Cows without DLT or LF were excluded from the analysis of the area and epithelium thickness. The appearance rate of CK18+ and IgA+ cells were assessed using a representative section from each cow.

Statistical analysis

The results are shown as the mean ± standard error (SE). The histological parameters of the eyelids and third eyelids were compared between the normal and pneumonia groups. The percentages of discontinuous epithelial layers covering the DLT and LF were compared using Fisher's exact test (P < 0.05), and other results were compared using the Mann-Whitney U-test (P < 0.05). Effect sizes for parameters with statistically significant differences were calculated as Cramer's V and r = Z/n for the Fisher's exact test and Mann-Whitney U-test, respectively.

Results

Fig. 2 A shows the histological features of the eyelids and third eyelids of the cows. Lenticular- and oval-shaped DLTs, as well as oval-shaped LFs clearly separated from the surrounding tissue by the connective tissue, were observed on the eyelids, with DLTs being more abundant. Aggregated LFs were also observed in some samples. CALTs in the third eyelid shared the same histological structure as those in the eyelid. Immune cell distribution of CALT in normal and pneumonia cows

CD3+ T cells, CD20+ B cells, and IBA1+ macrophages were diffusely distributed in the DLTs of the eyelids and third eyelids (Fig. 3 A). In the LF of the eyelids and third eyelids (Fig. 3 B), B cells were abundant and localized in the center with peripheral T cells and few macrophages. T cells and B cells were also localized in the epithelial layer of the eyelid and the third eyelid conjunctiva. Most of these intraepithelial lymphocytes were diffusely localized in the epithelium covering the CALT, as well as the whole epithelium. In some LFs, B cells conjugated and infiltrated the epithelial layer, replacing thin or discontinuous epithelium (Fig. 3 C). Histological characteristics and CK18 expression of conjunctival epithelium in normal and pneumonia cows

The epithelial layer covering MALT has a unique histological structure that facilitates easy antigen uptake. We investigated the CALT epithelium in detail and compared it between the normal and pneumonia groups. In general, the epithelium of the eyelid and third eyelid conjunctiva was composed of a stratified squamous epithelium in both groups. Many goblet cells were observed in the conjunctival epithelium, as previously reported (Kosenda et al., 2023), although the epithelium covering the CALT tended to lack them. The thickness of the epithelial layer varied depending on the sample and observation site. Most of the epithelial layer covering the CALT was continuous (Fig. 4 A); however, the epithelial layer covering the large DLTs and LFs was thin and frequently discontinuous (Fig. 4 B). For histoplanimetry, no significant difference was observed in the epithelium thickness of DLTs or LFs in either the eyelid or third eyelid (Fig. 4 C). In the eyelid, there was no significant difference in the percentage of discontinuous epithelial layers covering either DLT (normal, 5/53, 9.4 %; pneumonia, 14/100, 14.0 %) or LF (normal, 3/24, 12.5 %; pneumonia, 6/26, 23.1 %). In the third eyelid, there was no difference in the percentage of discontinuous epithelial layers covering DLT (normal, 4/47, 8.5 %; pneumonia, 6/65, 9.2 %). However, the percentage of discontinuous epithelial layers covering LF was significantly higher in the pneumonia group than in the normal group (P < 0.05, Cramer's V = 0.295; normal, 2/35, 5.7 %; pneumonia, 11/39, 28.2 %) in the third eyelid. As shown in Fig. 5, CK18+ cells, which are an intestinal M cell marker in cows (Hondo et al., 2011), were diffusely observed within the conjunctival epithelium of the eyelid and third eyelid in both groups. Clusters of CK18+ cells were observed in several large CALTs (Fig. 5 A). Distribution of IgA+ cells in conjunctiva of normal and pneumonia cows

IgA+ cells were primarily diffused throughout the lamina propria of the eyelid and third eyelid (Fig. 6 A); however, IgA+ cells in cows with pneumonia tended to form clusters. In CALTs, most IgA+ cells are localized near the epithelial layer. In normal cows, there were few IgA+ cells in the CALTs. However, clusters of IgA+ cells were observed in some CALTs of cows with pneumonia, often infiltrating the epithelial layer, similar to CD20+ B cells. Discussion

In the present study, conjunctival IgA+ cell counts were higher in pneumonia-affected cows than in controls. Additionally, the pneumonia group frequently exhibited disrupted epithelial layers over the LFs of the third eyelid. These histological changes suggest that chronic antigen sensitization associated with pneumonia enhances IgA production and antigen uptake in the CALT, likely via the mucosal immune crosstalk between the conjunctival and respiratory mucosae. Although we previously reported the existence of CALT in cows and found that CALT in calves is immature compared with adult cows (Kosenda et al., 2023), the factors that cause CALT development in cows and their relationship with the respiratory mucosa have not been elucidated.

MALT functions as an immune-inductive or effector site (Cesta, 2006). As IgA production is a major function of the mucosal immune effector site (Kiyono & Fukuyama, 2004), increased IgA+ cells suggest that chronic respiratory sensitization associated with pneumonia enhances the immune effector function of the conjunctiva in cows. Antigen-specific primed T and B cells leave inductive site for the regional lymph nodes, followed by re-entry into the mucosa (Kiyono & Fukuyama, 2004). These lymphocytes can migrate to distant mucosal sites (Xu-Amano et al., 1993) and differentiate into memory or effector cells (Pabst, 1987). Intranasal administration of antigens induces antibody production in the vagina of humans (Johansson et al., 2001), the vagina and saliva of monkey (Russell et al., 1996), and the lacrimal glands of rats (Ridley Lathers et al., 1998). These reports indicate that antigen exposure in the respiratory tract promotes IgA secretion in the distant mucosa, including the mucosa of the lacrimal system. In this study, an increase in pulmonary IgA+ cells was also observed in pneumonia cows. Therefore, we suggest that chronic antigen sensitization from pneumonia promotes antigen presentation and IgA+ B cell activation in the respiratory mucosa, leading to the migration of these cells to the conjunctiva, resulting in an increase in conjunctival IgA+ cells.

In some large DLTs and LFs, the epithelial layer is lost and becomes discontinuous. Additionally, the conjunctival epithelium over third eyelid LFs was more frequently discontinuous in cows with pneumonia compared to normal cows. We believe discontinuous epithelial layer is not simply an artifact because it gradually thins toward the discontinuous part of epithelium and is replaced by mononuclear cells. In addition, a thin epithelial layer of the CALT has also been observed in humans (Knop & Knop, 2000) and pigs (Crespo-Moral et al., 2020). In cows, the GOALT has a discontinuous epithelial layer allowing for direct contact with luminal antigens and easy antigen uptake by LT (Chuluunbaatar et al., 2022). In contrast, we also observed B cells and IgA+ cells replacing the thin conjunctival epithelial layer and no change in CK18+ cells between the two groups in this study. Therefore, the thin epithelial areas in the CALT may serve primarily as sites for IgA production rather than antigen uptake. To determine whether the thin epithelial layer in the CALT signifies activation as an inductive or effector site, ultrastructural analysis of the superficial cells is needed.

Generally, LFs serve as inductive sites (Shikina et al., 2004) while DLTs function as effector sites (Cesta, 2006) . Within the inductive site, macrophages and dendritic cells present antigens to active T cells; subsequently, activated CD4+ T cells trigger the proliferation of antigen-specific B cells (Huang, 2020). Our study demonstrated that pneumonia had not significant impact on the appearance rate, area, or immune cell composition of conjunctival LFs. These findings suggest that pneumonia does not affect the immune-inductive function of the conjunctiva in cows.

Nonspecific chronic inflammation caused by ocular exposure to infectious and noninfectious substances or allergens results in conjunctivitis (Eagle, 2017). In addition, potential antigens like microorganisms and endotoxins are present in the air on dairy farms, with their concentrations varying according to environmental conditions (Dungan et al., 2011). In the present study, we used cows from several neighboring farms. It cannot be denied that histological changes such as frequent discontinuous epithelial layer in third eyelid LF and an increase in IgA+ cells may be due to farm environment and the amount of antigens in the air. However, an increase in IgA+ cells was also confirmed in the lungs. This synchronous increase in IgA+ cells in the conjunctiva and lung strongly supports the idea that the conjunctival changes are not local response, but rather a systemic/common mucosal immune response linked to respiratory antigen sensitization and inflammation.

Microorganisms that cause pneumonia are diverse, including viruses such as bovine respiratory syncytial virus, bovine herpes virus-1, bovine parainfluenza-3 virus, bovine viral diarrhea virus, and bovine corona virus, and bacteria such as Mannheimia haemolytica, Pasteurella multocida, Histophilus somni, and Mycoplasma bovis (Gagea et al., 2006). Some microorganisms can alter the host immune response. For example, Mycoplasma bovis induces immune suppression through the high expression of the immune inhibitory receptor programmed death 1 in peripheral blood mononuclear cells and its ligand in tracheal and lung epithelial cells (Suleman et al., 2018). Bovine respiratory syncytial virus promotes Th2 responses and inhibits the activation of CD8+ T cells (Gershwin, 2012). Although the specific causative agents causing pneumonia were not identified due to the use of field cases, the histopathological confirmation of chronic pneumonia and numerous pulmonary IgA+ cells support our hypothesis: the observed CALT changes represent a host immune response to chronic pulmonary antigen sensitization and inflammation. Future studies involving experimental infections of each pathogen are necessary to elucidate the pathogen-specific variations in the conjunctival immune response. Additionally, there are several limitations related to field cases, including non-random sampling, multi-site animal sourcing, and modest sample sizes. Experimental infections under controlled conditions will allow for identical rearing environments and animal conditions among samples, which provide clearer results, even with small sample sizes.

MALT development is inherently age-dependent; for instance, BALT was observed in 6 to 7-month-old and 2-year-old cows, but not in suckling calves under 2 months of age (Thomasmeyer et al., 2023). We previously reported that CALT in calves is smaller and contains fewer of B cells than in adults (Kosenda et al., 2023), suggesting that both BALT and CALT mature with age in cows. Although the age and sex of the cows in the present study were not perfectly matched between the normal and pneumonia groups, we minimized bias by ensuring a comparable distribution of pre-weaned and weaned cows. Furthermore, we excluded cows under 28-day-old to avoid the confounding effect if maternal antibodies. Because mucosal immune responses can be triggered from neonatal period—as evidenced by effective intranasal vaccinations (Hill et al., 2012)—the slight age variation between two groups is considered acceptable for evaluating the effect of respiratory chronic antigen stimulation on CALT development. Additionally, sex-related hormonal influences were neutralized by using only pre-pubertal or castrated cows. Considering the moderate to high effect sizes observed, our result would hold biological significance, though controlled antigen challenge tests with age-matched cohorts remain necessary to fully elucidate these mechanisms.

Conclusions

Our findings revealed that chronic respiratory antigen sensitization associated with pneumonia upregulates the humoral immune response in the conjunctiva, as evidenced by increased IgA+ cell infiltration and a discontinuous CALT epithelium in pneumonia cows. These results support a broader effect on conjunctival immune-effector and inductive functions. While confounding factors such as the environment, specific pathogens, and age must be considered, the moderate to high effect sizes and the synchronized immune responses observed across both the lungs and conjunctiva support our conclusion: respiratory inflammation drives a broader mucosal immune response extending to the ocular surface. Ocular surface findings may be accessible indicator of broader mucosal immune activation during respiratory inflammation.

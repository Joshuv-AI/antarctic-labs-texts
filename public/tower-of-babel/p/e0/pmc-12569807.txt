A comparative study on the multi-scale, physicochemical properties and enzyme activities of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch with different amylose contents
Authors: Mingming Zhai, Qinglin Wen, Yaning Bu, Wenjing Tian, Xiaorong Wu, Dongwei Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Tetraploid cultivated wheat represents a valuable genetic resource for enhancing starch quality in food and industrial applications. Nevertheless, the relationship between its multi-scale structure and biosynthetic regulation, particularly the role of key enzyme activities, remains insufficiently explored. This study systematically examined the multi-scale structure, enzyme activities, and functional properties of starch from six tetraploid wheat varieties differing in apparent amylose content (AAC). The results indicated that AAC ranged from 17.29 % to 25.29 %, showing a positive correlation with granule size and molecular ordering. Notably, variety TS6 exhibited the highest molecular order (degree of order(DO)₁₀₄₇/₁₀₂₂ = 0.441), gelatinization temperatures (onset temperature(To) = 64.47 °C, peak temperature(Tp) = 68.69 °C), and content of slowly digestible starch (SDS = 38.77 %), along with a comparatively high level of resistant starch (RS =11.48 %), which underscores its superior thermal stability and slow digestibility. All starch granules displayed uniform disc-like morphologies with smooth surfaces and typical A-type crystalline patterns. Furthermore, correlation analysis revealed a significant association between AAC and AGPase activity, suggesting that starch biosynthesis plays a regulatory role in amylose accumulation. Enzyme activity data indicate that synergistic interactions among biosynthetic enzymes across varieties, implying that dynamic enzymatic activities modulate the multi-scale architecture of starch-from molecular to granular levels-thereby influencing its physicochemical properties, including digestibility. These insights elucidate the coordinated regulatory role of starch synthases in determining structure and functionality, offering new perspectives for breeding tetraploid wheat varieties with tailored nutritional and functional traits for health-oriented food applications.

Introduction

Wheat (Triticum aestivum) is one of the world's most widely cultivated food crops, providing over 20 % of total dietary energy intake and constituting 65–70 % of the grain's dry weight (Kumar et al., 2018). The grain is also an important source of energy and raw material for the food industry, and its starch is mainly composed of amylose and amylopectin. Amylose is a linear glucan molecule linked by α-(1,4)-glycosidic bonds, while amylopectin forms a main chain through α-(1,4)-glycosidic bonds and side chains at branching points through α-(1,6)-glycosidic bonds, forming a highly branched cluster structure (Leloup et al., 1992). The proportion, the distribution of molecular chain lengths and spatial configurations of amylose and amylopectin jointly determine the particle morphology and crystallinity of starch, thereby regulating its gelatinization characteristics, digestion rate and other physical and chemical properties, and ultimately affecting the processing quality and nutritional functions of flour products (Huang et al., 2021). For example, high-amylose starch serves as a functional food ingredient by providing SDS and RS, which contribute to reduced postprandial blood glucose levels (Qi et al., 2019), while high amylopectin starch is mainly used in baked goods and other food products (Sarka & Dvoracek, 2017).

As the demand for functional and health-oriented foods continues to grow, the utilization of tetraploid wheat has gained significant importance. Generally, tetraploid cultivated wheat is rich in dietary fiber, phenolic substances, and micronutrients, which contribute to its unique nutritional value and potential for food processing applications (Pino et al., 2022; Suo et al., 2023). It serves as an ideal raw material for functional food development. Therefore, it is widely cultivated in numerous countries, including Canada, Australia, and Italy, with a global production volume of approximately 42.7 million tons recorded during the 2020–2021 period (Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, n.d; Tidiane Sall et al., 2019). Studies have shown that different varieties of tetraploid cultivated wheat have different components, such as amylose content (4.55–35.55 %), particle size (0.67–53.84 μm), and relative crystallinity (32.3–40.39 %) (Karim et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2010). Its diverse genetic resources support the development of various functional starch products. Recently, most studies on tetraploid cultivated wheat starch have focused on the analysis of its characteristics and the relationship between its digestibility and its internal structures, such as particle morphology, crystal structure, and amylose content (Bharath et al., 2021; Chakraborty et al., 2004; Karim et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021). These studies show that the functional properties of starch not only depend on in vitro digestion, hydrolysis and gelatinization, but are also influenced by multi-scale structures. Further, given the complexity of its structure, the multi-scale structure has an uneven degree of order or density, and can reveal its structure-function relationship of starch in different tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties (Karim et al., 2022). The tetraploid cultivated wheat is also applied in the production of pasta and pizza (Chakraborty et al., 2004; Sanfilippo et al., 2025). However, current research lacks a multi-scale analysis of starch structure and fails to address how the dynamic activity of key synthesis enzymes affects structural formation. The absence of data on synchronous changes in enzyme activity and starch architecture across developmental stages further limits the practical application of enzyme-directed strategies for improving starch quality in tetraploid wheat.

The biosynthesis of starch is jointly regulated by multiple key plant enzymes, including glucose pyrophosphorylase (AGPase), granule-bound starch synthase (GBSS), soluble starch synthase (SSS), starch branching enzyme (SBE), and starch debranching enzyme (DBE) through the precise cascade reactions, forming a highly coordinated multi-enzyme synergistic pathway (Blennow, 2018). AGPase and GBSS synthesize amylose, while AGPase, SSS, SBE, and DBE jointly synthesize amylopectin (Lu et al., 2019). Each of these core enzymes has multiple subtypes, such as TaAGPS1, TaAGPS2-a, and TaAGPS2-b, which play a key regulatory role in the biosynthesis of wheat starch. Normally, the starch accumulates simultaneously as the wheat grains develop (Altenbach et al., 2003), In the endosperm of maize, SBEI prefers to use low-branched glucan as the substrate and catalyzes the generation of long-chain branches with a degree of polymerization (DP) ≥ 16, while SBEII acts on highly branched polydextran, forming short-chain branches with DP ≤12 (Chakraborty et al., 2004). In wheat grains, TaSBEIII is associated with the synthesis of starch granules in wheat (Kang et al., 2013), indicating its relationship with the multi-scale structure of starch involved in regulation. The direct impact of starch biosynthesis on the molecular structure of starch in many crops, including rice, barley, corn and potatoes, has been emphasised (Bertoft, 2017; Jeon et al., 2010; Qu et al., 2018; Smith, 2001; Turki et al., 2015; Yu et al., 2019). Therefore, a thorough understanding of the relationship between starch biosynthesis and multi-scale structure is of vital importance to breeders and biotechnology experts to develop predictable engineering and breeding strategies for the bioengineering of starch crops to meet crop yield, performance, and starch function.

In this study, all plant materials were provided by the Wheat Genomics and Molecular Breeding Research Group of Northwest A&F University. We selected six tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties with different amylose contents and determined their molecular, short-range ordered, granular, and helical structures, viscosity and thermal properties, and enzyme activity of their key synthetases to reveal the regulatory role of amylose content on the multi-scale structure of starch. We also clarified the influence of the enzyme activities of key enzymes involved in starch synthesis on the multi-scale structure. The study shows a complex regulatory model of starch synthase activity, multi-scale structure and nutritional function, based on the analysis and comparison, clear targets have been established for the direct utilization or hybrid breeding of specialty health grains. This shift supports the transition from yield-oriented breeding to nutrition-focused breeding, thereby advancing development in the food industry. Furthermore, it offers a theoretical foundation for enhancing the economic value of tetraploid wheat and expanding its application potential across diverse food sectors.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation

Six tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties, including TS1 (D3), TS2 (Langdon), TS3 (82053), TS4 (83673), TS5 (89009), and TS6 (82541), were grown in a greenhouse at 25 ± 2 °C, with a relative humidity of 60 %, and a light cycle of 16 h/8 h. The grains were collected on the 7th, 14th, 21st, 28th, and 35th days (S1-S5) after the flowering period to study the expression characteristics of enzymes related to starch synthesis in different wheat varieties at different stages.

Starch extraction and determination of amylose content

The starch was separated using a modified method from 50 g of wheat grains (Shang et al., 2020). The grains were immersed in a 0.5 % sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution and left to stand overnight, followed by crushing with a juicer, and then sieved through four layers of gauze, and washed in triplicate with ddH2O. The extract was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min at 25 °C, followed by discarding the supernatant. The remaining extract was successively washed with anhydrous ethanol by shaking for 10 min and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 20 min at 25 °C. After discarding the supernatant, the extract was washed in triplicate with ddH2O and mixed with 1.6 g/L of papain, and then evenly stirred and shaken at 37 °C on a shaker for 1 h. The extract was then mixed with 0.25 % sodium hydroxide solution, evenly stirred and suspended on a shaker for 10 min, before sieving through a 200-mesh sieve. The precipitate was air-dried, and then wheat starch was obtained.

The amylose content was determined using the iodine colorimetric method (Juliano, 1971). A 100 mg of starch was mixed with 1 mL of 95 % ethanol and 9 mL of 1 mol/L NaOH and boiled in water for 10 min, then cooled and made up to 100 mL to prepare the stock solution. A 5 mL of the solution was mixed with 50 mL of ddH2O and 1 mL of 1 mol/L acetic acid, shaken, then mixed with 2 mL of iodine, and topped up with water to 100 mL and left to stand for 10 min. The absorbance of the mixture was then measured at 720 nm.

The structure and particle size of the starch

A trace of starch was stuck onto a black double-sided tape and sprayed with gold for 30 s, and its granular structure was observed and imaged at 1000× and 3000× magnification using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (JSM-6360LV, Nippon Electronics, Japan). The particle size of the starch was determined by wet dispersion with distilled water using a laser particle size analyzer (Mastersizer 2000, Malvern, UK), with a refractive index of 1.52, and an analysis range of 0.1–1000 μm.

X-ray diffraction (XRD)

The physical property of the starch was determined using an X-ray diffractometer (D8-Advance, Bruker, Germany), with Cu-Kα radiation (λ = 0.1542 nm, 40 kV, 40 mA), scanning range of 5–40° (2θ), step size of 0.02°, and counting time of 0.8 s/step. The relative crystallinity of the starch was calculated according to a previous formula (Frost et al., 2009).

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR)

To assess the proportion of short-term ordered structures and the interaction level between starch and water, a Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (VERTEX70, Bruker, Germany) was used to collect 4000–400 cm−1 spectra with a resolution of 4 cm−1. The peak intensity ratio of 1047/1022 cm−1 order degree to 995/1022 cm−1 double helix degree (DD) was obtained by OMNIC V9.2 deconvolution analysis.

Gel permeation chromatography (GPC) analysis

For the GPC, starch (5 mg) was dissolved in 0.9 mL ddH2O in a boiling water bath for 15 min. The solution was then mixed with 5 mL of sodium azide (40 mg/mL) and 0.1 mL of acetic acid (0.1 M, pH 3.5) containing 10 μL of isoamylase for enzymatic debranching. Followed by incubation at 37 °C for 3 h. Next, the debranched starch was precipitated using 5 mL absolute ethanol, centrifuged at 4000×g for 10 min, and, finally, redissolved in 1 mL DMSO/LiBr solution for 2 h at 80 °C in a thermomixer with shaking at 350 rpm. The molecular weight distribution of debranched starch was analyzed using a differential refractive index detector (Optilab T-rEX, Wyatt Technology Co., Santa Barbara, CA, USA) at a flow rate of 0.8 mL/min. Standard dextrans of known molecular weights (3.42 × 102–3.76 × 106 Da) were used for column calibration (Cave et al., 2009).

High-performance anion exchange chromatography (HPAEC) analysis

The starch suspension was placed in a 99 °C water bath and heated at a constant temperature for 60 min, then mixed with 10 μL of sodium azide (2 % w/v), 50 μL of acetic acid (0.6 M, pH 4.4), and 10 μL of 0.1 M isoamylase, and eventually gelated and debranched using HPAEC. The HPAEC parameters used included a CarboPac PA-200 anion exchange column measuring 4.0 × 250 mm (Dionex), a pulse amperometric detector (Dionex ICS 5000 system), a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min, and an injection volume of 5 μL. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.2 M NaOH, while mobile phase B contained 0.2 M NaOH +0.2 M sodium acetate. The gradient program at 0–10, 30–50 and 50.1–60 min was 90:10, 40:60 and 90:10 V/V, respectively, as described previously (Zhou et al., 2015).

Pasting properties

Exactly 3 g of starch was mixed with 25 mL of ddH2O, evenly stirred with a paddle, and used to determine gelatinization for 12.5 min using a rapid viscosity analyzer (RVA4500, Perten, Sweden). The heating was maintained at 50 °C for 1 min, then increased at a rate of 12 °C/min to 95 °C and maintained for 2.5 min, and then decreased at a rate of 12 °C/min to 50 °C and maintained for 1.4 min. The peak viscosity (PV), through viscosity (TV), breakdown value (BD), final viscosity (FV), setback value (SB), and pasting temperature (PT) were obtained from the curve graphs.

Differential scanning calourimeter (DSC) analysis

The TA Instruments Differential Scanning Calorimeter Q2000 (TA Instruments, USA) was used to determine the thermal properties of starch. Exactly 5 mg of starch was mixed with 10 μL of ddH₂O in aluminium crucible, equilibrated at room temperature for 24 h, and gelatinized in a DSC device from 40 to 100 °C, at 10 °C/min, with an empty crucible as a reference, and nitrogen as the carrier gas. The onset, peak and conclusion temperature (Tc) of gelatinization were determined from the DSC data, while the gelatinization enthalpy change (ΔH) was calculated based on the peak area.

Digestibility of extracted starch

The digestibility of the extracted starch was determined as described previously (Englyst et al., 1992). A 100 mg of the starch sample was dissolved in 10 mL of 0.1 M NaAc (pH 5.3) and incubated in a shaker at 37 °C for 30 min, then mixed thoroughly with 5 mL of a solution, containing 1160 mg of porcine pancreatic enzyme (Cat. No. P7545, Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA), 692 μL of AMG (Cat. No. A7095) and 6 mL of 0.1 M NaAc (pH = 5.2). After 0, 20, and 120 min of enzymatic hydrolysis, 1 mL of the digestion liquid was collected from each time interval and mixed with 5 mL of anhydrous ethanol to inactivate the enzyme, followed by centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 20 min at 25 °C. Thereafter, 1 mL of the supernatant was mixed with GOPOD (d-glucose, Megazyme, Co. Wicklow, Ireland) and used to measure the absorbance at 520 nm. The digestibility was calculated using the following formulas.

i. RDS/% = (G20 min – G0 min) x 0.9 × 100/TS,

ii. SDS/% = (G120 min – G20 min x 0.9 × 100/TS

iii. RS/% = (1-RDS-SDS) x 100.

where G0min represented the mass of free glucose in the sample in mg, G20min represented the mass of glucose produced by the hydrolysis of starch for 20 min in mg, while G120min was the mass of glucose produced by starch hydrolysis for 120 min in mg. TS was the mass of total starch in the sample in mg.

Enzyme activity

To determine the enzymatic activity of the starch, 0.1 g of wheat grains at different growth stages were mixed with 1 mL of pre-cooled extract, containing 100 mmol/L of Tricine-NaOH (pH 7.5), 8 mmol/L of MgCl2, 2 mmol/L of EDTA, 50 mmol/L of 2-mercaptoethanol, 12.5 % Glycerol and 1 % PPVP 40) and homogenized in ice bath water. The mixture was centrifuged at 4 °C for 10 min at 12,000 rpm to obtain the supernatant using ELISA detection kits for DBE (Borson Bio BES-2375BTK), SSS (Sub-Family Bio ZIKER564), GBSS (Shanghai Chunshi Biology, CS-C8234), AGPase (Shanghai Chunshi Biology, CS-C8234) and SBE (ZIKER564), following the manufacturer's instructions.

Data analysis

All experiments were done in triplicate. One-way analysis of variance was performed using SPSS 22.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), while the differences between groups were tested by Duncan multiple comparison test (P < 0.05). Correlation analysis was based on Pearson coefficients, and the plots were plotted using Origin 2022. The data was expressed as Mean ± standard deviation.

Results and discussion

This difference may stem from genotype specificity due to GBSS allelic variations. Amylose synthesis is predominantly catalyzed by GBSS, and allelic polymorphisms of this enzyme directly influence its activity, thereby regulating amylose accumulation levels (Pfister & Zeeman, 2016). The distribution ratios of all starch granules were similar in most tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties. With the highest proportion of large starch granules (62.41 %; Table 1), TS2 appears to possess a more coherent structure that supports expansion during development (Govindaraju et al., 2022). Meanwhile, our data reveals that the average particle size of TS6 starch is the largest, followed by TS1, TS3 and TS5. Due to the different forms of starch, the average particle size of TS2 starch was the smallest. We also found a positive correlation between AAC and the average particle size of starch (Fig. 7), indicating that as the starch particle size increases, the content of amylose also significantly increases (Chen et al., 2019).

Morphology of starch in tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties

The microstructure of starch granules can affect the physicochemical properties of wheat starch. All tetraploid cultivated wheat starch granules had a smooth surface and a relatively uniform particle shape, with an irregular disc shape. Most wheat varieties mainly consisted of large starch granules, with smaller ones being secondary. The observed starch granule morphology aligns with the characteristic features of tetraploid wheat starch reported by Karim et al. (2022), indicating a conserved morphological trait across tetraploid wheat genotypes, which is governed by genetic factors and exhibits species-specific patterns (Li et al., 2013).However, compared with the other four tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties, the starch particle distribution in TS1 and TS3 was relatively sparse, revealing that the low amylose content can change the proportion of particle size in starch. The differences in particle distribution in TS2, TS4-TS6 tetraploid cultivated wheat starch granules were also relatively small, while the overall density distribution was similar, with no cracks on their surfaces (Fig. 1). This result indicates that the granular shape of starch may be limited by gene regulation and is also influenced by the activity levels of key enzymes involved in amylose synthesis. In tetraploid cultivars with higher amylose content, more amylose molecular chains tend to promote their interaction with amylopectin through entanglement and framework reinforcement, thereby enhancing the stiffness, compactness and volume strength of starch particles, making them less susceptible to external environmental influences (Govindaraju et al., 2022). In addition, at 3000× magnification, the large starch granules showed slight indentations, which may result from the high-density material deposition in the endosperm tissue or the deformation marks of the amyloid body caused by external force compression during the development stage, consistent with the previous findings (Karim et al., 2022).Fig. 1SEM (×1000, ×3000) images of the tetraploid cultivated wheat starch. Crystal structure of wheat starch in tetraploid cultivars

Starch molecules form periodically arranged crystalline unit cells through the directional assembly of the double helix conformation. The unit cells are orderly stacked along the three-dimensional space to form a lattice framework, subsequently creating a crystal structure with long-range orderliness. The XRD patterns of sample particles showed significant diffraction peaks at positions 2θ of 15°, 17°, 18°, and 23°, with the peaks at 17° and 18° having characteristic bimodal structures, confirming that all the sample particles have typical A-type crystal structures (Li et al., 2013). However, a weak peak appeared at 20° (Fig. 2), reflecting the characteristics of the amylose-lipid complex (Zhang et al., 2016). The relative crystallinity of starch among tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties showed considerable variation, with TS6 exhibiting the highest value (Table 1) (Karim et al., 2022). This suggests that elevated amylose content in TS6 promotes more ordered packing of crystalline regions. Although amylopectin does not directly integrate into the crystal lattice, it restricts disordered helix extension through interactions with amylose, thereby reducing amorphous regions and enhancing overall crystallinity (Kumar et al., 2016). Additionally, the relative crystallinity may also be affected by the content and chain length of amylopectin, the content and particle size of the double helix, and the presence of other chemical substances (Li et al., 2013).Fig. 2X-ray diffraction patterns of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch.Fig. 2

Short-range ordered structure of wheat starch in tetraploid cultivars

FT-IR has been used to detect the short-range ordered molecular structure of starch. In this study, the deconvolution FTIR spectra of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch of different varieties ranged from 1200 to 800 cm (Fig. 3). Generally, DO1047/1022 quantitatively characterizes the short-range order of starch, while DO1022/995 reflects the conformational integrity of the double helix structure. TS6 had the highest DO value, while TS1 had the lowest (Table 1), demonstrating that TS6 has a higher degree of starch molecular sequence and a higher proportion of crystalline regions (Warren et al., 2016), which is consistent with the results of XRD (Fig. 2). The DD content showed no significant variation among tetraploid wheat varieties differing in amylose content (Fig. 7), indicating a conserved double-helix architecture across genotypes. The primary structural distinctions arose from differences in molecular packing order rather than helix type. This suggests that modifying short-range order can effectively tune starch functionality without altering the fundamental helical framework, offering a targeted strategy for starch bioengineering.Fig. 3FT-IR spectra of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch.Fig. 3

Distribution of amylose chain lengths in tetraploid cultivated wheat starch

The molecular weight distribution of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch with different amylose contents was determined by GPC (Fig. 4). The results showed three peaks, with peaks 1 and 2 corresponding to the short (A and B) chains and long (long B chain) branches of starch, and peak 3 representing the straight branch (Lin et al., 2016). The area ratio of Peak 1 to Peak 2 indicates amylopectin branching degree, with a higher ratio indicating a higher branching degree (Song & Jane, 2000). In this study, the content of short amylopectin chains ranged from 60.47 % in TS5 to 65.95 % in TS1, while the content of long amylopectin chains ranged from 11.02 % in TS4 to 11.88 % in TS1 (Table 2). The TS2 starch also had a higher degree of amylopectin branching than the other five tetraploid cultivated wheat starches. Further, the differences in molecular weight among the different amylose contents were mainly reflected in Peak 3. The true content of amylose can be quantitatively characterized by the ratio of its characteristic peak area in chromatographic analysis to the total starch peak area. In this study, TS5 had the largest Peak3 area, followed by TS4, which was different from the AAC measured using the iodine colourimetric method. This is because the presence of long branches of amylopectin during AAC determination can interfere with the determination. However, the main reason for the difference in amylose content can be attributed to the influence of starch synthase activity since the correlation analysis revealed a positive correlation between Peak3 and the enzyme activities of different key starch synthesis enzymes (Fig. 7). Distribution of amylopectin chain lengths in tetraploid cultivated wheat starch

Starch chains can be classified into A chains(fa) (DP 6–12), B1 chains(fb1) (DP 13–24), B2 chains(fb2) (DP 25–36), and B3 chains(fb3) (DP > 36) based on the degree of polymerization of amylopectin (Bresciani et al., 2021). The fa chain is located on the outermost layer and is the basis for the formation of the double helix and crystalline region (Kim et al., 2012), while the fb1-fb3 chains belong to the inner layer and contain side chains. In this study, we found an insignificant difference in the average DP of tetraploid cultivated wheat starch with different amylose contents (Fig. 5; Table 2). Compared with DP 13–24 and DP 25–36, the DP6–12 chain ratio was highest in TS5 (27.86 %) and lowest in TS3 (27.284 %) (Fig. 5; Table 2; Fig. S1), indicating that the difference in this ratio led to the main differences in amylopectin short chains among the six tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties. This result provides a crucial basis for the development of low-GI functional foods. Furthermore, our data revealed a positive correlation between different starch chains and the average DP (Fig. 7), which reflects the complexity of the molecular structure. The distribution of chain lengths in different proportions may also affect the gelatinization parameters of starch. Furthermore, TS2 exhibited the highest degree of amylopectin branching, as indicated by the largest peak1/peak 2 area ratio. This structural feature, characterized by a higher proportion of short chains with weaker inter-chain hydrogen bonding, reduces the energy required for gelatinization, consequently resulting in the lowest peak gelatinization temperature among the varieties studied (Table 4).Fig. 5The amylopectin chain length distributions (CLD) of starches from tetraploid cultivated wheat starch.Fig. 5

Thermal properties of starch in tetraploid cultivated wheat cultivars

The thermodynamic behaviour of starch is regulated by its multi-level structure, including particle size distribution, proportion of amylose and crystalline regions, distribution of amylopectin chain length, gelatinization ΔH and short-path orderliness (Shang et al., 2020). Of these, the gelatinization ΔH serves as a key parameter of DSC for characterizing the energy input required to dissociate the starch double helix in the recrystallization region. In this study, the gelatinization enthalpy varied among the cultivars, ranging from 6.382 ± 0.14 J/g in TS3 to 8.007 ± 0.61 J/g in TS1 (Table 3). The higher proportion of fa chains in TS3 contributed to a less compact double-helix structure, which required the lowest energy for disintegration, as reflected by its minimal ΔH – a value lower than those reported in previous studies.(Hsieh et al., 2019; Karim et al., 2022), suggesting that the content of amylose has a significant influence on the enthalpy value. TS1 also had a relatively high proportion of long chains in amylopectin, most likely because short external chains are restricted by steric hindrance and can only form discontinuous short helices, while long external chains form highly crystalline double helices through ordered stacking. Therefore, longer external chains are more inclined to re-associate with intra- or intermolecular chains (Vamadevan & Bertoft, 2018). The gelatinization temperatures (To, Tp, Tc) of TS6 are the highest among the varieties studied, owing to its elevated amylose content and crystalline order. Amylose forms a robust molecular scaffold on the granule surface that impedes water uptake, thereby increasing the energy required for helix unwinding, while high crystallinity necessitates additional thermal input for lattice disruption(Shang et al., 2020). These results are consistent with the results of the ICS determination (Fig. 5). Viscosity characteristics of wheat starch in tetraploid cultivars

The gelatinization characteristics of wheat starch among the six tetraploid cultivated varieties show significant differences. TS2 starch had the highest PT and the lowest BD (Table 4), showing that it has the most complete and hardest particles. TS2 also exhibited excellent anti-swelling properties and structural integrity in water-based environments (Table 4), and can effectively maintain particle morphological stability under thermal mechanical stress and inhibit volume expansion and rupture (Liu et al., 2021). PV usually reflects the strength of starch granules in absorbing water, swelling and breaking. The PV values of TS1 and TS3 starches were relatively high (Table 4), mainly due to the low content of amylose, which can inhibit the thermally induced swelling behaviour of starch granules and maintain the morphological integrity of swollen granules (Abdel-Aal et al., 2002). Digestibility of starch in tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties

The SDS of wheat starch with different amylose contents ranged from 22.46 ± 2.27 % in TS4 to 38.77 ± 0.93 % in TS6, and the RDS ranged from 49.75 ± 1.88 % in TS6 to 67.12 ± 0.96 % in TS2, while RS ranged from 9.17 ± 0.72 % in TS1 to 17.64 ± 0.62 % in TS5, showing extremely significant differences (Table 3). One possible explanation for TS6 having the lowest RDS content is that amylose can enhance the interaction with amylopectin, forming a dense and highly crystalline particle surface structure. This structural characteristic may inhibit the diffusion rate of enzymes at the particle interface and reduce the initial rate of enzymatic hydrolysis reactions, thereby lowering the RDS level (Zhang et al., 2011). Concurrently, its high degree of molecular order contributes to lower enzymatic hydrolysis efficiency. This is similar to the previous research results (Shi et al., 2023). Meanwhile, a higher content of amylose can form a dense structure to prevent the binding ability between starch-dissolving enzymes and starch particles (Wang et al., 2017). In addition, the in vitro digestibility of different starches is also affected by the interaction of multiple factors such as the source of starch, particle size, and crystallinity (Hoover et al., 2010).

Enzyme activity analysis of enzymes related to starch synthesis in tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties

The biosynthetic pathways of starch in endosperm are highly conserved in wheat, and include AGPase, SBE, DBE, SSS, and GBSS enzymes, which are involved in the synthesis of starch in cereal endosperm (Seung & Smith, 2019). AGPase is a rate-limiting enzyme in starch biosynthesis, catalysing the reaction between glucose-1-phosphate and ATP to generate ADP-glucose, which is the core glucose donor for the extension of amylose and amylopectin (Pfister & Zeeman, 2016), and its activity significantly affects the quantity of starch synthesis. In this study, the enzyme activity at different stages had significant changes, especially at the S4 stage, where the enzyme activity reached its maximum value (Fig. 6; Table S1). The decline in AGPase activity from S4 to S5 stages across most varieties (Table S1) likely reflects grain maturation, a phase characterized by a metabolic shift toward protein synthesis and a concomitant reduction in starch accumulation. TS5 reached its maximum enzyme activity at the S5 stage, indicating a significant increase in the total amount of starch, which is consistent with the current GPC data. The positive correlation between AGPase enzyme activity and the mean DP further confirms these results. Moreover, the AGPase in different varieties of tetraploid cultivated wheat also exhibited similar enzymatic activity trends throughout the growth stage and maintained a high level of enzymatic activity, highlighting its significant role in tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties. Amylose in wheat endosperm is catalyzed by GBSSI, which, after phosphorylation modification on the surface of starch granules, forms an active oligomer complex to regulate the biosynthesis of amylose (Pfister & Zeeman, 2016). GBSS maintained consistently high activity across all growth stages in the various tetraploid wheat cultivars, with no significant variation observed among varieties or between the S1 and S5 stages (Fig. 6; Table S2). This sustained activity indicates continuous amylose synthesis during starch granule development, a process consistent with established biosynthetic patterns (Pfister & Zeeman, 2016). Especially in the S3 stage, both TS1 and TS2 reached the maximum enzyme activities, while TS3-TS6 in the S4 stage did not reach the maximum enzyme activities (Table S2). The biosynthesis of amylopectin relies on the synergistic catalytic effect of SSS, SBE, and DBE (Pfister & Zeeman, 2016). Of these, only SBE can specifically hydrolyse the α-1,4-glycosidic bonds and relink the oligosaccharide chains with α-1, 6-glycosidic bonds to form a branched structure (Abt & Zeeman, 2020). The activity of SBE is closely related to the content of amylose and also has a significant impact on digestibility. In this study, all the SBE enzyme activities of the five tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties decreased to varying degrees at the S5 stage, with fluctuating changes at other stages (Fig. 6; Table S3), revealing that the regulation of enzyme activity is a dynamic and complex process. Interestingly, TS1 showed a continuous downward trend throughout the entire growth cycle of wheat, which may have decreased the rate of starch accumulation and significantly affected the chain length distribution of starch (Zhang et al., 2021). Nevertheless, the lowest value of SBE occurred in the S4 stage of TS2, while the highest value occurred in the same stage in TS5 (Table S3), similar to the AGPase results (Table S1), reflecting the synergistic effect among different enzymes.

The DBE can hydrolyze α-1, 6-glycosidic bonds and correct incorrect branching in starch synthesis to ensure the ordered synthesis of amylopectin (Du et al., 2018). In this study, compared with the S1 stage, TS3 and TS6 reached their maximum values in the S5 stage, while the other four remaining wheat varieties reached their maximum values in the S4 stage (Fig. 6; Table S4), which reflects the tissue specificity of DBE at different growth stages of tetraploid wheat. At the same time, in terms of varieties, there was an insignificant difference between TS1 and TS6. However, there was a significant difference between TS1 and TS5 at the S3 and S5 stages (Table S4), indicating that the influence of DBE on amylose content may also be affected by other factors, such as the type of crystal structure and the activity of other key synthetases (Nakamura et al., 2021). SSS is responsible for the extension of the α-1-4 glycosidic bond in amylopectin, and its activity determines the chain length ratio of amylopectin. In this study, the enzyme activity expression levels of SSS in different wheat varieties were consistent, while the variation range was relatively small at different stages (Fig. 6; Table S5). All six tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties reached the maximum value of SSS at stage S4 or earlier, indicating that SSS regulates the chain length ratio of starch by reducing the enzyme activity state in the later stage of the starch synthesis process. Interestingly, TS3 had the highest SSS activity at the S1 stage, indicating the specificity and early maturity of this variety. High SSS activity can also increase the molecular weight of amylopectin (Singh et al., 2017). SSS activity peaked at the S4 stage before declining in later phases. As the enzyme responsible for amylopectin chain elongation, its early-stage high activity facilitates the formation of long amylopectin chains, thereby establishing a structural foundation for subsequent crystalline architecture assembly(Singh et al., 2017). Correlation analysis showed a positive correlation of SSS with Peak1 and Peak2, revealing that its activity may affect the chain length distribution structure of amylopectin (Fig. 7).

Moreover, the different activities of AGPase, GBSS, SSS, SBE, and DBE enzymes at different stages of wheat indicate the complexity and diversity of the joint regulation of key enzymes involved in starch synthesis. The results suggest that the enzymes affect the multi-scale structure of starch from the molecular to the granular level through dynamic changes in their activities, thereby influencing the physicochemical properties of starch such as digestibility.

Correlation analysis of starch in tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties

The Pearson correlation data in Fig. 7 revealed a significant correlation between the multi-level structural parameters of starch-enzyme activity and physicochemical properties. The data indicate that structural differences can drive systematic changes in physicochemical properties through enzyme activity mediation. The gelatinization parameters, including To, Tp, To-Tp, Tc, and enthalpy value were positively correlated to varying degrees with the chain length distribution of amylopectin (fa, fb1, fb2, fb3) and AGPase, SBE, DBE, GBSS, and SSS activities, indicating that high enzyme activity promotes the formation of long chains and high branching structures. For instance, higher GBSS, SSS and SBE activities caused longer molecular chains of starch and more complex molecular structure, leading to a denser crystalline zone within the starch granules, a higher enthalpy value required to destroy their structure, and an increase in physical and chemical properties, such as gelatinization temperature. This can be achieved in tetraploid cultivated wheat breeding by selecting and breeding varieties with high expression of GBSS or SSS to meet the starch requirements for specific industrial uses. Meanwhile, the viscosity parameters, including PV, TV, BD, FV, SB, and PT, were positively correlated with the chain length distribution of amylopectin (fa, fb1, fb2, fb3). Further, PV was positively correlated with the particle size parameters (d(0.1), d(0.5), d(0.9), D[3,4]), but negatively correlated with DO and RC. On the other hand, SB was negatively correlated with the particle size and RC, but significantly positively correlated with DO. These results indicate that the chain length distribution of amylopectin collaboratively regulates the viscosity by influencing the particle size of starch particles, the integrity of the crystal structure, and the short-range ordered structure. The results also reveal the complexity between multi-scale structures and physicochemical properties, and show a significant correlation between the digestion parameters of starch and the chain length distribution of amylopectin. This could be because the loose crystalline regions formed by short chains (DP < 13) are easily recognised and attacked by α-amylase, leading to rapid hydrolysis, while long chains (DP ≥ 25) form a tight double helix structure through hydrogen bonds, hindering enzyme diffusion. There were also positive or negative correlations between multi-scale structures (molecular and particle structures), physicochemical properties (viscosity, gelatinization and digestion parameters) and different enzyme activities. There were also certain correlations among different enzyme activities. This indicates that different enzymes regulate the multi-scale structure of starch through dynamic equilibrium synergy, thereby influencing the physicochemical properties of starch, further revealing the intrinsic connection between the structure and function of biological macromolecules. In conclusion, these results provide a reference for improving the starch characteristics of tetraploid cultivated wheat through genetic engineering and also offer a new perspective for the development and optimization of tetraploid cultivated wheat functional foods.

Conclusion and prospect

This study evaluated the differences and correlations of the multi-scale structure, synthase activity, and physicochemical properties of starch among six tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties (TS1 - TS6), which have significant reference value for the breeding of tetraploid cultivated wheat varieties and the development of functional foods. Our results demonstrate that amylose content (ranging from 17.29 % to 25.29 %) is a key determinant of starch structure and functionality. A significant correlation between AGPase activity and amylose content underscores the enzymatic regulation of starch biosynthesis. Notably, TS6 showed the most ordered molecular structure due to its high amylose and DO values, resulting in the highest thermal parameters (To, Tp, Tc) and SDS/RS content, which indicate strong stability and slow digestibility. These properties, stemming from high amylose content, enhance enzymatic resistance and make TS6 suitable for functional food applications. Structural analysis showed that all starch samples displayed an A-type crystalline pattern and smooth, disc-shaped granules. A positive correlation was observed between amylose content and granule size, suggesting that higher amylose levels promote structural compactness by enhancing amylose–amylopectin interactions. Furthermore, the chain length distribution of amylopectin varied significantly and was associated with the activity of biosynthetic enzymes. The FV and Peak3 of TS5 were the highest, indicating that more molecular chains are leached out, which further accelerates the rearrangement or recrystallization between amylose and amylopectin. The amylopectin (Peak1 and Peak2) chain values, d(0.1), and enthalpy values of TS1 were the largest, revealing that TS1 affects particle volume and double helix ordering through the short chain of amylopectin. These findings offer significant potential for strategic wheat breeding and starch bioengineering. The identified varietal traits can guide the development of specialized wheat varieties for specific food applications, including slow-digestion foods, high-temperature processed products, and functional ingredients with tailored nutritional profiles.

Looking ahead, this study provides critical groundwork for elucidating the genetic determinants and molecular mechanisms governing enzyme–structure–function relationships through integrated multi-omics analyses. For instance, differential gene expression screening could identify transcription factors that regulate enzymatic synergy, thereby pinpointing key genetic variations and regulatory networks controlling starch biosynthesis. Such insights would accelerate marker-assisted and genetic engineering strategies. Collectively, these results establish a valuable foundation for designing starch-based foods with customized digestibility and functionality. Subsequent studies will focus on evaluating TS6 performance under real-world processing conditions to validate its industrial applicability and nutritional benefits.

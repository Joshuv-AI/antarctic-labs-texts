IFPHOR0070 Childhood Anaemia in Marginalised Rural Communities of Quintana Roo, Mexico: Prevalence, Subtypes, and Early Findings from a Community-Based Child Health Program
Authors: Luis Alberto Martinez-Juarez, Juan Pablo Aguilar Mendoza, Karime Arana Cohuo, Mibzar Misael Pech Navarrete, Héctor Gallardo-Rincón
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChildhood anaemia remains undercharacterised in marginalised rural areas of Mexico. We estimated the local burden and haematologic profile of anaemia and assessed early response to an initial 40-day supplementation cycle among children aged 12–59 months in rural Quintana Roo.MethodsThis prospective cohort was embedded in the permanent Child Health Program of Medical Mission Network México across 18 rural communities. Anaemia was defined using 2024 World Health Organization age-specific haemoglobin cutoffs. We also defined an at-risk group as haemoglobin 0.1–1.0 g/dL above the age-specific cutoff. Per program protocol, children received universal vitamin A and deworming; those with anaemia or at risk then started daily liposomal iron, vitamin C, folate, and vitamin B12 for 40 days. Complete blood count was repeated 2–4 weeks after the last dose.ResultsWe enrolled 361 children. At baseline, 47 children (13.02%) had anaemia, 123 (34.07%) were at risk, and 191 (52.91%) had no risk; 76.60% of anaemia cases occurred before 36 months of age. Microcytosis and hypochromia increased across haemoglobin categories, reaching 59.57% and 53.19% among children with anaemia. Among anaemic children with follow-up (n = 29), mean haemoglobin increased by 0.60 g/dL and 51.70% no longer met anaemia criteria after one cycle. Among at-risk children with follow-up (n = 48), 27.10% moved to the no-risk category.ConclusionsCommunity screening within a permanent rural child health program revealed a substantial hidden burden beyond diagnosed anaemia. The large at-risk group supports proactive screening, reassessment, and early community-based supplementation in underserved settings.

The art of positioning in brain surgery: a continual journey of mastery
Authors: P K. Jha, Bipin Chaurasia
Published: 2024

Introduction

In the intricate dance of brain surgery, the positioning of a patient is not merely a preliminary step; it is an art form, a foundational pillar that holds the potential to influence the entire surgical process. Even in the era of advanced Neuronavigation, the significance of physical positioning remains paramount. This article aims to provide insights into this vital aspect of neurosurgery, drawing upon decades of evolving practice and expertise1–3.

The strategic imperative of positioning

Positioning in brain surgery is akin to setting the stage for a critical performance. It is the first strategic decision, the initial maneuver in the elaborate choreography of neurosurgical procedures. It’s more than just a preparatory step; it’s the first digit in a complex combination lock, opening the doors to the intricate world of the human brain.

The benefits of mastery in positioning

Correct positioning can transform the surgical field, offering a clearer view and easier access to the target area. It facilitates gravity retraction, minimizes bleeding, and importantly, allows the surgeon to operate in the most ergonomically favorable position. The comfort of the surgeon is not a luxury but a necessity for precision. For example, many neurosurgeons achieve good neck flexion for posterior fossa surgery. However, when they need to tilt the microscope to an extreme angle and contort their own body and head to visualize tumors under the tentorium roof, they often realize, in hindsight, that the positioning could have been better.

Standardization: the key to efficiency

Over the years, the trend towards standardizing positioning techniques has gained momentum. This approach enhances communication within the surgical team, streamlines the process, and importantly, ensures the consistency of outcomes. Adopting visual aids like photographs, comprehensive step-by-step instructions, and detailed checklists has become an integral part of this standardization3–5.

It is important to recognize that the art of positioning is deeply ingrained in the habits and thoughts of surgeons. This inherent variability and personalization among surgeons make it challenging to adhere to a rigid scientific structure.

The multifaceted factors of positioning

The positioning of a patient is influenced by a confluence of factors: the planned surgical trajectory, the surgeon’s position, the principles of gravity retraction or drainage, and the imperative to avert potential position-related complications. Each of these elements plays a critical role in deciding the patient’s position4,5.

Deciphering the surgical trajectory

The surgical trajectory, the invisible line that connects the point of craniotomy to the target, such as a tumor or lesion, dictates the initial head position. This trajectory is the guiding star, leading the surgeon’s hand through the delicate layers of the brain.

The surgeon’s stance

A surgeon’s preferred position can vary, influencing the chosen trajectory. This personalization reflects the artistry and individuality of each surgeon, honed and refined over years of practice.

Gravity’s role in retraction

In certain surgeries, the principle of gravity retraction becomes pivotal. It guides the positioning, particularly in surgeries involving specific brain areas, demonstrating the harmonious blend of physics and medicine.

Minimizing risks through positioning

Positioning also serves as a proactive measure to minimize risks like increased intracranial pressure, venous congestion, and air embolism. These considerations ensure patient safety and optimize surgical conditions.

The standardization of head positions

Utilizing standardized head positions is more than a practice; it’s a philosophy that enhances orientation and accuracy during surgery. It represents the collective wisdom and experiences of generations of neurosurgeons.

Harmonizing head rotation with body positioning

The ultimate head position is achieved by a symphony of head rotation and body positioning. The range of head rotation, from 0° to 60°, and in some cases up to 90°, is delicately balanced with the desired degree of head flexion and tilting.

Figures and visual representation

The absence of figures of different positioning for different types of surgery is beyond the realm of this type of article and the nature of the topic. Positioning in brain surgery is highly variable and individualized, making it difficult to represent through standardized figures. Each surgical scenario is unique, and the optimal positioning often depends on a confluence of factors that are best understood through narrative description and experiential knowledge rather than static images.

A lifelong journey of refinement

From the early days of a resident’s journey to the seasoned hands of a senior neurosurgeon, the mastery of patient positioning in brain surgery is a continual path of learning and refinement. This article, collating decades of collective wisdom, serves as a testament to the ever-evolving art and science of neurosurgery.

Take-home message

The take-home message is clear: precise patient positioning is critical for the success of neurosurgical procedures. This concept, while general, is universally applicable and essential for minimizing risks and optimizing surgical outcomes. We emphasize that positioning is not just a preliminary step but a central component of the surgical process, deserving of attention and mastery.

Conclusion

This editorial underscores that in the realm of brain surgery, positioning is not just a step but a critical art form, central to the safety, efficacy, and success of every procedure.

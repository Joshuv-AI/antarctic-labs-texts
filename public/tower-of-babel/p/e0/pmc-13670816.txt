Association of hemoglobin trajectories during pregnancy with preterm birth and non-reassuring fetal status: a retrospective cohort study using latent growth mixture modeling
Authors: Xiaohong Yu, Hongli Nie, Wenzhen Yang, Jing Tang, Xuexue Huang, Yongmei Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMost prior studies on anemia in pregnancy, preterm birth (PTB), and non-reassuring fetal status (NRFS) use only single-trimester hemoglobin concentrations and fail to capture gestational hemoglobin changes. This study aimed to assess the associations of pregnancy hemoglobin trajectories with these two adverse perinatal outcomes.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted using pregnancy records from Longquanyi District of Chengdu Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital (from July 2024 to June 2025) , involving 2,954 pregnant women with complete serial hemoglobin measurements. The outcome measures were PTB and NRFS. Latent growth mixture modeling was applied to identify distinct hemoglobin trajectories during pregnancy. Binary logistic regression was constructed to explore the association between hemoglobin trajectories and birth outcomes.ResultsThe incidence of anemia during pregnancy was 29.8% in 2954 pregnant women. Three distinct hemoglobin trajectories were identified: Traj-1 (high-rapid decline), Traj-2 (moderate-gradual decline), and Traj-3 (low-delayed recovery). Compared with the Traj-2 group, both the Traj-1 (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) = 1.780, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.144, 2.769) and Traj-3 (aOR = 2.051, 95% CI 1.084, 3.881) groups were significantly associated with PTB. Moreover, the Traj-3 (aOR = 2.580, 95% CI 1.223, 5.440) group exhibited an increased risk of NRFS.ConclusionThis study suggests that specific longitudinal hemoglobin trajectories, particularly high-rapid decline and low-delayed recovery patterns, exhibited an increased risk of PTB and NRFS, highlighting the clinical value of dynamic monitoring over single-point assessment in the practice of maternal and child health care.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09688-9.

Introduction

Preterm birth (PTB) is defined as delivery before 37 weeks’ gestational age [1], which has short- and long-term negative impacts on the development of newborns [2, 3]. As a frequent adverse pregnancy outcome, PTB is regarded as a major global public health concern [4, 5]. The worldwide PTB rate was estimated at 9.9% (95% CI 9.1, 11.2) in 2020 [6], while over 7.0% of live births were preterm in mainland China in 2022 [7]. Accordingly, identifying PTB risk factors is critical to reducing preterm incidence and improving neonatal outcomes.

Anemia, a major pregnancy complication characterized by subnormal hemoglobin (Hb) or red blood cell levels, is significantly associated with PTB and other adverse birth outcomes [8–10]. In 2023, it is estimated that worldwide, 35.5% of pregnant women aged 15–49 years were anemic [11]. Given this high prevalence, understanding the relationship between maternal Hb and PTB is of clinical importance.

Hb is a key indicator of anemia [12]. Based on Hb concentrations, the World Health Organization (WHO) has established trimester-specific cutoff values for diagnosing anemia [13]. However, most prior studies rely on single-trimester Hb measurements and fail to explore nonlinear relationships between Hb and PTB or other adverse birth outcomes, leading to inconsistent findings across the existing literature. While some studies report that first-trimester maternal anemia raises PTB risk [14, 15], several other investigations find no significant association between PTB and anemia occurring in the second or third trimester [16–18]. However, additional studies have shown that second- or third-trimester anemia is associated with PTB [19, 20]. Furthermore, higher Hb levels during pregnancy do not always confer protection against PTB or other adverse birth outcomes [21]. Notably, recent evidence points to a U-shaped relationship between maternal Hb concentration and adverse birth outcomes, with elevated risks at both low and high Hb levels [22–25]. These findings suggest that a single measurement is insufficient to capture the dynamic physiological changes and associated risks throughout gestation.

To address this gap, it is essential to consider the entire pregnancy as a continuum and investigate dynamic changes in Hb trajectories across gestation in relation to PTB. Notably, studies have shown that non-reassuring fetal status (NRFS) due to intrauterine hypoxia may be influenced by Hb concentration during pregnancy [26, 27]. Given the paucity of studies exploring the association between maternal Hb and NRFS, we incorporated NRFS as a secondary outcome. Therefore, the present study aimed to identify distinct longitudinal trajectories of Hb and analyze their associations with PTB and NRFS.

Methods

Data sources

The data were extracted from the comprehensive Hospital Information System of Longquanyi District of Chengdu Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital. The system comprises two main modules: an outpatient workstation and an inpatient workstation, which systematically archive outpatient visit records and inpatient medical registration information, respectively. Hb and ferritin concentrations during pregnancy were obtained from the outpatient workstation, and the covariate data and birth outcomes were collected from the inpatient workstation. The two datasets were linked via a unique personal identification number.

Potential covariates were selected through literature reviews, encompassing sociodemographic characteristics, menstrual and reproductive history, maternal anthropometric characteristics, and gestational complications [28, 29]. Maternal sociodemographic characteristics included maternal age at delivery, employment status, marital status, and ethnicity. Menstrual and reproductive history included menarcheal age, menstrual duration, cycle length, gravidity, and parity. Maternal anthropometric characteristics included height, pre-pregnancy weight, and prenatal weight. Gestational complications included gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH), and eclampsia gravidarum. Pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI) was calculated as pre-pregnancy weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.

The present study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Longquanyi District of Chengdu Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital (Approval number: LQFY-2026-009). Given the retrospective study design, all data were extracted from the Hospital Information System and subsequently de-identified, resulting in negligible risk to the participants. Informed consent was waived by the institutional ethics committee.

Study population

All enrolled pregnant women underwent their first prenatal visit at 6–13 weeks of gestation. In total, we have identified 3,242 pregnant women who delivered at the hospital between July 2024 and June 2025 from the Hospital Information System. Inclusion criteria for the study were: (1) singleton pregnancy; (2) availability of at least four Hb concentration records from different trimesters during pregnancy, and (3) delivery at or after 32 weeks of gestation. Exclusion criteria included incomplete birth outcome data. Finally, 2,954 participants remained for the final analysis (Fig. 1).

Exposure

Hb levels were assessed via routine blood tests conducted at various stages of pregnancy. To facilitate the description of Hb concentrations and anemia incidence across different gestational periods, the pregnancy period was categorized into six distinct intervals: the first trimester (T1, 6–12 weeks of gestation), the early second trimester (T2, 13–20 weeks), the late second trimester (T3, 21–28 weeks), the early third trimester (T4, 29–32 weeks), the late third trimester (T5, after 33 weeks), and the pre-delivery period (T6, within one week prior to delivery). If a pregnant woman has two or more sets of test data for a certain trimester, the minimum value should be taken to represent the most severe manifestation of maternal anemia [30, 31].

According to WHO, maternal anemia was defined as Hb < 110 g/L in the first and third trimesters, and < 105 g/L in the second trimester [13]. High hemoglobin was defined as Hb higher than 130 g/L [21].

Definition of outcome

Our primary clinical outcome was PTB, defined as delivery occurring before 37 weeks of gestation. The secondary outcome was NRFS, defined as progressive fetal hypoxia or acidemia stemming from insufficient fetal oxygenation, with clinical manifestations including abnormal fetal heart patterns, decreased fetal movement, fetal growth restriction, and meconium-stained amniotic fluid [27]. As described in Sect."Data sources", birth outcomes data were extracted from the Hospital Information System, with all clinical diagnoses adjudicated by attending clinicians according to individual maternal and fetal clinical findings.

Statistical analysis

Normality of continuous variables was tested using the ‌Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were expressed as mean (standard deviation, SD), while non-normal variables were expressed as median (interquartile range, IQR). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages). Intergroup comparisons of population characteristics across distinct Hb trajectory subgroups were performed using one-way ANOVA or the Kruskal-Wallis H test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables.

Latent growth mixture modeling (LGMM) was applied to identify distinct trajectories of Hb concentration during pregnancy [32]. The LGMM divides the total sample into multiple latent classes, each comprising individuals with similar trajectories and characteristics. Hb levels during pregnancy follow different latent trajectories rather than a single homogeneous pattern. Analyzing only the average Hb trend of the overall population may obscure clinically meaningful subgroup differences and potentially mask associations with birth outcomes. Moreover, LGMM appropriately accommodates residual within-class individual variation. LGMM is therefore well-suited to address the limitations of average trend analysis.

We performed LGMM to identify potential Hb trajectories with all available measurements throughout pregnancy for each individual using the “lcmm” package in R [33]. With gestational age specified as the time variable and Hb concentration as the outcome, we constructed quadratic growth models ranging from 1 to 5 latent classes. After selecting the number of groups, the significance of linear, quadratic, and cubic polynomials was assessed to optimize trajectory shapes. The optimal model was selected according to the Akaike information criterion (AIC), the Bayesian information criterion (BIC), average posterior probability (AvePP), and percentage of each class. Lower AIC and BIC values, the percentage of each class > 5%, and AvePP > 0.7 indicate that the model is acceptable. After comprehensive consideration of model fit indices (Table S1) and the clinical interpretability of each subgroup, the 3 classes of trajectories were considered to be the best potential classes. Each participant was assigned to the trajectory group with the highest posterior class-membership probability.

Binary logistic regression models were constructed to evaluate the associations of Hb trajectories with PTB and NRFS, with trajectory 2 specified as the reference group and with adjustment for maternal age at delivery, employment status, menarcheal age, menstrual flow duration, cycle length, gravidity, parity, pre-pregnancy BMI, GDM, PIH, and eclampsia gravidarum.

To verify the robustness of the findings, sensitivity analysis was restricted to pregnant women with more than four Hb records throughout pregnancy. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software (version 22.0) and R software (version 4.0.3), and a two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Hb concentration and anemia prevalence across trimesters

In the first trimester, Hb concentration was the highest, with a prevalence rate of 41.4%. The incidence of anemia during pregnancy was the highest in the early third trimester, at 29.8%. Overall, the Hb concentrations followed a trend of an initial decrease and a subsequent increase, and anemia prevalence changed in the opposite pattern (Table 1).

Longitudinal Hb trajectories

In this study, three dynamic trajectories were identified based on 16,892 Hb records during pregnancy. Trajectory 1 (Traj-1), named high-rapid decline, had the highest initial Hb levels of about 132 g/L, rapidly decreasing to about 110 g/L. Traj-2, named moderate-gradual decline, had the higher initial Hb level of about 128 g/L, slowly decreasing to the lowest point of about 113 g/L and then rising to 121 g/L. Traj-3, named low-delayed recovery, had the lowest initial Hb level of about 119 g/L, slowly decreasing to the lowest point of about 114 g/L and then rising to 129 g/L. All three trajectory groups exhibited a consistent pattern of an initial decrease followed by an upward trend (Fig. 2). The number and proportion in the three trajectories were 827 (28.0%), 1878 (63.6%), and 249 (8.4%), respectively.

Association of Hb trajectories with PTB and NRFS

Compared with the reference Traj-2 group, after adjustment for maternal age at delivery, employment status, menarcheal age, menstrual flow duration, cycle length, gravidity, parity, pre-pregnancy BMI, GDM, PIH, and eclampsia gravidarum, both the Traj-1 group (aOR = 1.780, 95% CI 1.144, 2.769) and Traj-3 group (aOR = 2.051, 95% CI 1.084, 3.881) were significantly associated with PTB. Traj-3 group also showed a significant association with NRFS (aOR = 2.580, 95% CI: 1.223, 5.440) (Table 5).

Sensitivity analysis

In the sensitivity analysis, used 15,913 records of 2,707 women with more than four Hb records in the whole pregnancy, the results were robust and consistent with the main findings. Compared with the reference Traj-2 group, after adjustment for the same confounding factors, both the Traj-1 (aOR = 1.957, 95% CI 1.215, 3.154) and Traj-3 (aOR = 2.299, 95% CI 1.179, 4.484) groups were significantly associated with PTB (Table S4).

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study, we identified three distinct nonlinear longitudinal Hb trajectories during pregnancy and found that Hb trajectories were significantly associated with PTB and NRFS. Compared to the moderate-gradual decline trajectory (Traj-2), the high-rapid decline (Traj-1) and low-delayed recovery (Traj-3) trajectories had higher risk of PTB, and the Traj-3 had a higher risk of NRFS. Sensitivity analysis yielded results consistent with the main analyses in both the direction and magnitude of associations, confirming its robustness. Women with a rapid Hb decline throughout pregnancy and no notable recovery in late gestation had a higher risk of PTB. Notably, pregnant women who had low Hb in early gestation remained at increased risk of PTB and NRFS, even when their Hb gradually rose to relatively high levels later in pregnancy. Our findings highlighted the value of dynamic serial monitoring of Hb throughout pregnancy, in contrast to relying solely on a single measurement.

Accumulating evidence supports the general association between abnormal Hb dynamics and adverse pregnancy outcomes. Deborah A et al. indicated that pregnant women with persistent anemia or a declining Hb trajectory had a higher risk of PTB, at 15.8% and 14.8%, respectively [31]. Similarly, Zhang et al reported that, compared to the stable trajectory, the descending Hb trajectories, but not ascending ones, were associated with significantly increased risks [34]. It should be noted, however, that these prior studies primarily assessed monotonic Hb changes (e.g., persistent anemia or declining trends), whereas our study identified non-linear latent trajectories. Therefore, rather than directly aligning our trajectory groups with previously defined "declining" or "ascending" categories, we interpret our findings as associations between distinct non-linear Hb trajectory patterns and adverse outcomes.

During normal pregnancy, maternal plasma volume expands more than red blood cell mass, leading to a relative decrease in Hb concentration, a phenomenon known as physiological hemodilution. This process reduces blood viscosity and optimizes uteroplacental circulation perfusion [35]. The Traj-1 group with 28.0% participants is characterized by Hb levels rapidly declining from a relatively high baseline to a low level and then remaining stable. This pattern may reflect reduced oxygen-carrying capacity in the third trimester, leading to insufficient oxygen supply to the placenta and uterine myometrium. Hypoxia in the placenta is associated with vascular remodeling, hypertension, metabolic changes, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and endoplasmic reticular stress [36], which directly or indirectly precipitate PTB [37, 38]. Other studies showed that low Hb may cause chronic hypoxia, inducing a stress response with increased placental corticotropin releasing hormone production. If this occurs too early, it may induce PTB [21, 39].

Conversely, the Traj-3 group with 8.4% participants features a rapid Hb rise during the third trimester, reaching 129 g/L before delivery. We interpret this pattern as reflecting an inadequate or delayed physiological response. The rationale is as follows. First, the incidence of first-trimester anemia in Traj-3 was 19.5%, and these women had significantly higher third-trimester serum ferritin levels than the other two groups. Elevated ferritin in late pregnancy may reflect inflammation rather than true iron repletion [40, 41] and has been associated with increased PTB risk [42]. Second, according to Philip J. Steer's research [43], elevated Hb (> 120 g/L) may indicate inadequate plasma volume expansion, which is associated with reduced placental blood flow, fetal growth restriction, PTB, and an increased risk of pre-eclampsia. In this study, the Traj-3 group had significantly higher rates of eclampsia (4.8% vs. 2.5% and 1.7%) compared to the other two groups, supporting this mechanism. Third, a large body of studies has confirmed that early-pregnancy anemia is closely related to PTB [14, 15]. The high prevalence of early anemia in Traj-3 suggests that the increased risk of PTB may remain irreversible even if iron status improves to a relatively high level in late pregnancy.Additionally, the Traj-3 group was significantly associated with NRFS. The mechanism may be that Hb concentration is low in the first trimester, but due to reduced normal plasma expansion and hemoconcentration in the third trimester, the Hb concentration rises rapidly, leading to reduced placental-fetal perfusion and consequently causing fetal distress[26, 44].

Major strengths include the prospective design, repeated measures, use of advanced trajectory modeling, and adjustment for a comprehensive set of potential confounders. By applying the LGMM method, we categorized the dynamic changes in Hb in pregnant women, which may help implement targeted care and early interventions for high-risk groups in clinical practice. Furthermore, the results of multiple subgroup and sensitivity analyses were consistent with the primary analysis, further reinforcing the current study's conclusions. However, our study is not without its limitations. First, our study population was recruited from a single district-level maternal and child health institution in Chengdu, limiting the generalizability of the findings to broader populations and other geographical settings. Second, despite adjustments, residual confounding by unmeasured factors such as detailed dietary intake, underlying hemoglobinopathies, or subclinical infection cannot be ruled out. Thus, future studies should aim to address these limitations to validate our findings and explore their generalizability. Additionally, the biological mechanisms underlying the observed associations remain speculative, and future studies are needed to explore causal pathways.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study shows that specific hemoglobin trajectories during pregnancy are significantly associated with adverse perinatal outcomes. Both a high-rapid decline and a low–delayed recovery pattern are linked to preterm birth, while the latter is also associated with non-reassuring fetal status. Monitoring the dynamic pattern of Hb change, rather than isolated values, offers a more nuanced approach to identifying at-risk pregnancies and paves the way for more personalized and timely antenatal interventions.

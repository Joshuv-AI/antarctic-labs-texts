Urban small-scale hydroponics: A compact, smart home-based hydroponics system
Authors: Akshay Kushawaha, Divya Shah, Deepali Vora, Nilima Zade, Kamatchi Iyer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe increasing population and urbanization have created a massive gap in the demand-supply model of food grains. The world is facing an acute problem with global warming and EI Nino effects, which have affected the equilibrium of the food chain. It is a need of the hour to introduce new reforms in farming to reap increased yields and reduce dependency on natural resources. Hydroponics cultivation is a boon to the agricultural sector, it enhances the cultivation of plants in an organic way by enabling Internet of Things (IoT) technology and combines technology and conventional nutritional mechanisms to enable the co-plant's growth without the strain of nutrient deficiency. This research suggests a system that integrates hardware and software into the traditional hydroponics system that the users can use to have their plantation setup in an urban environment within a small and confined space at home. This system will benefit hobbyist gardeners and small-scale urban farmers seeking an efficient, compact, and smart solution for hydroponic plant cultivation.Keywords: Hydroponics, Microcontroller, Mobile system, Internet of ThingsMethod name: Compact and Smart Home-based Hydroponics System

Background

The increased load of urbanization and industrialization has created a requirement for a mechanism that supports the green revolution with a lesser burden on the environment [1]. There remains a need for a modified growth medium to enhance sustainable production and support the ever-increasing population of our country. Agriculture without soil includes Hydroponics, Aquaponics, Aeroponics, as well as substrate culture [2]. Professor William Gericke coined the word hydroponics in the early 1930s; it describes the growing of plants with their roots suspended in water containing minerals and nutrients [3,4]. Researchers at Purdue University developed the agriculture system in 1940. During the 1960s and 70 s, commercial hydroponics farms were created in Arizona, Abu Dhabi, Belgium, California, Denmark, Germany, Holland, Iran, Italy, Japan, the Russian Federation, and other countries. Due to rapid urbanization and industrialization, the cultivable land is decreasing, and conventional agricultural practices are causing a wide range of adverse environmental impacts [5]. Modifying growth medium is an alternative for sustainable production and conserving fast-depleting land and available water resources [6]. It has also been discussed in an article that hydroponic farming can yield up to twenty times more than traditional farming in some vegetations, like lettuce when calculated on the basis of yield per acre and the fact that plants can be grown throughout the year [7]. In the present scenario, soilless cultivation or Hydroponics techniques are gaining popularity because they efficiently manage resources and food production [7]. We also find that now many people find it amusing to have a hydroponics setup in their personal space instead of the traditional clay-pot setup. This method allows the consumer to have an automatic setup that would minimize the timely interventions needed as per in traditional systems. Various commercial and speciality crops can be grown using hydroponics, including leafy vegetables, tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers, strawberries, and many more [8]. One more benefit of the system is that these setups eliminate the need for herbicides and pesticides [9]. There have been some efforts to develop setups that are automated, as highlighted in one article where the construction and assembly process of the system involved steps like assembling the metal structure, installing lighting, and using a Grow tent storage structure [10]. However, these setups are typically designed for larger spaces, making them unsuitable for smaller urban environments where we need an efficient alternative to traditional clay-pot methods, which will be discussed in this paper. The Kratky hydroponic method is one of the simplest forms of hydroponics setups due to its design and implementation; since it does not require any water pumps or electricity [11], in our experiment, we will be automating the simple setup with low-cost materials to make it viable for the everyday user as an automatic system. It is an effort to test the applicability of the small smart home-based hydroponics system in urban areas. There are IOT based system [12] to monitor the usage of water or forecasting the usage which can be useful and impactful in the systems that is proposed.

a. Micro-controller and Data Acquisition

a. Micro-controller and Data Acquisition

The Microcontroller contains Atmega-16U2 Mega 2560 R3 Improved Version CH340G (Arduino Mega), which acts as the central processing unit for the system. It controls various sensors and the water pump, gathering and processing data from sensors and the NodeMCU, which is used for internet connectivity and remote data monitoring. It enables data transmission and control through Wi-Fi.

b. Water Pump and Power Supply

b. Water Pump and Power Supply

It contains a Submersible Pool Water Pump 1 – 3 L/min, responsible for delivering nutrient-rich water to the hydroponic system. It's controlled by the Arduino Mega and powered by 5 Volts DC through a power supply which will provide the voltage and current to operate the water pump. It also contains MOSFET as a switch to control the water pump, allowing the Arduino to turn it on and off.

c. Sensor Integration

c. Sensor Integration

The water level sensor monitors the water level in the hydroponic system. It provides data to the Arduino Mega to ensure adequate water supply. It is also attached to the Water Flow Sensor G1/2″, used to measure the water flow rate in the system and help regulate the water pump's operation. The soil moisture sensor is employed to assess the moisture level in the growing medium, ensuring that plants receive the appropriate amount of water, and the DHT22/11 Humidity and Temperature Sensor is used to measure temperature and humidity, contributing to environmental control within the hydroponic system. The Light-Dependent Resistor (LDR) monitors ambient light conditions, enabling the system to adjust lighting as needed. The RS485 communication interface connects sensors, such as the soil NPK sensor, and transmits data to the Arduino Mega.

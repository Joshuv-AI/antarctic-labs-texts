​Altered serum biomarkers of oxidative stress and antioxidant capacity in primary open-angle glaucoma
Authors: Erbil Seven, Serek Tekin, Canan Demir, Halit Demir, Muhammed Batur, Muhammet Derda Ozer, Tekin Yasar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the role of oxidative stress in primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) by comparing serum levels of ischemia-modified albumin (IMA), malondialdehyde (MDA), and total thiol (TT) between POAG patients and healthy controls. The study included 48 POAG patients and 54 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Serum concentrations of IMA, MDA, and TT were measured using established assay methods. Statistical analysis revealed significantly higher levels of IMA and MDA and lower levels of TT in POAG patients than controls (p < 0.001). The findings show that patients with POAG tend to have higher oxidative stress levels and a weaker antioxidant defense system. This suggests that POAG patients are exposed to increased oxidative stress and lack sufficient antioxidants to counteract it. The imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants observed in these patients may indicate that oxidative stress plays a potential role in the development of the disease. Further population-based studies are necessary to clarify the causal relationship between these markers and the development or progression of POAG.

Introduction

Glaucoma is one of the leading causes of vision impairment and irreversible blindness worldwide1. Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) constitutes a significant risk factor for both the development and progression of glaucoma2. Elevated intraocular pressure is frequently associated with damage to the optic nerve, which progressively deteriorates and may cause significant damage before any symptoms become apparent3. Open-angle glaucoma is a significant condition characterized by an open angle in the anterior chamber, changes in the optic nerve head, and a gradual loss of peripheral vision that can eventually result in the loss of the central visual field3. Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) represents the most prevalent form of glaucoma, distinguished by an increased resistance to aqueous humor drainage within the trabecular meshwork3. POAG can be diagnosed through various clinical methodologies, with the fundamental triad comprising changes in the optic disc or retinal nerve fiber layer, alterations in visual fields, and elevated IOP4. The treatment strategy for POAG aims to halt the progression of changes in the optic nerve head and to prevent any decline in visual field4.

The pathophysiology of POAG entails a complex process of progressive optic nerve degeneration that can ultimately cause blindness5. Oxidative stress is regarded as a key factor in this process6.

Oxidative stress refers to cellular damage resulting from an excess of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This condition can detrimentally affect cellular components, including the lipid peroxidation of cell membranes, protein damage, and DNA degradation7. Elevated levels of ROS and impairments in antioxidant defense mechanisms have been documented in glaucoma8,9.

There are many biochemical markers used to assess oxidative stress. Biomarkers such as ischemia-modified albumin (IMA), malondialdehyde (MDA), and total thiol (TT) are frequently utilized indicators for assessing the extent of oxidative stress. IMA is formed by post-ischemic modification of the albumin molecule and reflects increased levels of ischemia in the circulation10. While MDA is considered an indicator of oxidative stress as a by-product of lipid peroxidation11, TT represents the cellular antioxidant capacity12.

The present research examined serum concentrations of IMA, MDA, and TT in individuals diagnosed with POAG and healthy participants. This analysis could enhance our understanding of how oxidative stress contributes to glaucoma development and may prove valuable in identifying potential targets for therapy.

Methods

Patient selection

This study included 48 patients with POAG and 54 healthy controls matched for age and sex. The diagnosis of glaucoma was established based on several criteria: an intraocular pressure exceeding 21 mmHg, the presence of glaucomatous cupping observed during fundus examination, an open iridocorneal angle identified through gonioscopy, and consistent visual field defects. These defects were determined using automated perimetry, specifically the 24-2 test on the Humphrey visual field analyzer, with results showing either a pattern standard deviation (PSD) below 5% or glaucoma hemifield test outcomes falling outside the 99% normal limits. Patients diagnosed with primary open-angle glaucoma were included. Detailed medical histories, including age, sex, systemic diseases, prior ocular surgeries, and glaucoma treatments, were recorded. Patients with glaucoma types other than POAG, ocular diseases unrelated to glaucoma, a history of intraocular surgery, systemic diseases affecting antioxidant enzyme levels, smoking habits, systemic steroid use, or antioxidant vitamin supplementation were excluded. The control group comprised individuals who did not have any ocular or systemic conditions.

The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and the local ethics committee (Van Yuzuncu Yil University Clinical Research Ethics Committee, decision date: 17.06.2020, No. 05) approved the methodology. Prior to the study’s initiation, all subjects were provided with study-related information and gave their informed consent to participate.

Blood sample collection

From each patient and control participant, 3 mL of blood was drawn from a vein. The samples were centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min. After completing this step, the specimens were carefully isolated and preserved at − 80 °C for subsequent examination.

IMA assay

Bar-Or et al. described this method13. A mixture was prepared by combining 50 µL of cobalt chloride solution (1 g/L) with 200 µL of serum. This mixture was then allowed to sit for 10 min at ambient temperature. Subsequently, 50 µL of dithiothreitol (DTT) solution at a concentration of 1.5 g/L was introduced and thoroughly combined. The mixture was left to incubate for an additional 2 min at ambient temperature. Following this, 1 mL of NaCl solution with a concentration of 9.0 g/L was incorporated. Control samples were prepared in a similar manner, omitting the DTT addition. The absorbance of the experimental mixtures was measured at a wavelength of 470 nm. The findings were expressed in absorbance units. Corrected-IMA (D-IMA) levels of each patient and healthy control group according to IMA and albumin were calculated with the formula [D-IMA = IMA × (Albumin/Group Albumin Median)] and given as absorbance units (ABSU).

MDA assay

Malondialdehyde (MDA), a byproduct of lipid peroxidation due to free radical interaction with fatty acids, was quantified by measuring the colored complex formed with thiobarbituric acid14. A 200 µL aliquot of serum was extracted from each patient and placed in a tube. The sample was then combined with 800 µL of phosphate buffer, 25 µL of butylated hydroxytoluene solution, and 500 µL of 30% trichloroacetic acid. After thorough mixing, the mixture was left to incubate on ice for 2 h. Post-incubation, the samples underwent centrifugation at 2000 rpm for 15 min. From each centrifuged sample, 1 mL of the supernatant was transferred to a new tube. The collected supernatants were then supplemented with 75 µL of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and 25 µL of thiobarbituric acid. Following a second mixing, the tubes were placed in a hot water bath for a 15-min incubation period. To conclude the process, the samples were allowed to reach room temperature before their absorbance was measured at 532 nm using a UV/Vis spectrophotometer.

TT assay

This technique relies on the interaction between Ellman’s reagent (DTNB) and sulfhydryl groups, resulting in the production of 2-nitro-5-mercaptobenzoic acid through a reduction process15. The nitro-mercaptobenzoic acid anion is light yellow and is based on spectrophotometric measurement of sulfhydryl groups in plasma. Reduced glutathione is used as a standard. To separate plasma and serum, the blood specimens collected in EDTA tubes underwent centrifugation at 3500 rpm for a duration of 5 min. The centrifuge tubes were filled with 0.5 ml of plasma. Subsequently, 1.5 mL of Tris buffer (0.2 M, pH 8.2) was introduced to the tubes. A 0.1 mL quantity of 0.01 M DTNB solution was introduced. The contents of the tubes were thoroughly blended using a vortex mixer. Subsequently, 7.9 mL of methanol was introduced to the mixture. The resulting solution was left undisturbed for 15 min to allow color development. Following this, the mixture underwent centrifugation at 4500 rpm for a duration of 10 min at ambient temperature. It was read at 412 nm against the blank. Disodium EDTA was added instead of plasma, and distilled water was added instead of DTNB.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 23.0 (Armonk, NY), a software package designed for data processing and examination. Statistical analysis was conducted for both patient and control groups, calculating means and standard deviations for age, sex, and serum biomarkers. The Shapiro–Wilk test determined data normality, while a Chi-square test analyzed sex distribution between groups. Comparisons of age, IOP, mean deviation (MD), PSD, IMA, MDA, and TT levels among glaucoma patients and controls were performed using either the Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test. Results were considered statistically significant when p-values were less than 0.05.

Discussion

This investigation compared serum concentrations for the antioxidant indicator TT and the oxidative stress markers IMA and MDA between POAG patients and healthy subjects. The findings suggest that patients with POAG demonstrate elevated levels of oxidative stress, as shown by higher concentrations of serum IMA and MDA when compared to healthy controls. Furthermore, our research showed that POAG patients exhibited reduced levels of the antioxidant marker TT.

Among mammals, albumin stands out as one of the most abundant proteins. Around 40% of the total volume is located in the intravascular compartment, with the rest dispersed throughout the interstitial space16. During ischemic conditions, characterized by oxidative stress, elevated ROS production, and increased acidity, albumin’s properties undergo modifications. Under these conditions, ischemia-modified albumin (IMA) is generated, which exhibits reduced capacity for binding metals, especially transition elements like copper, nickel, and cobalt. In many studies, IMA levels were found to be significantly higher in various diseases17–22. Increased IMA levels have been detected in eye diseases such as diabetic retinopathy, hypertensive retinopathy, allergic conjunctivitis, cataracts, glaucoma23. Gulpamuk et al. found significantly increased IMA levels in POAG patients compared to the ocular hypertension group and healthy control group24. Karakurt et al. observed a significant increase in IMA levels among patients with POAG when compared to healthy controls25. In our study, we observed elevated IMA levels in patients with POAG, consistent with existing literature.

Various cellular and extracellular processes produce ROS, which function as signaling molecules. These ROS play crucial roles in multiple cellular functions, including growth, differentiation, progression, and death26. The composition of ROS includes a diverse array of free radicals, among which are singlet oxygen, superoxide anion, and hydroxyl radical. ROS generated as a result of oxidative stress induce lipid peroxidation. MDA, a product of lipid peroxidation, functions as a robust indicator of systemic oxidative stress and is associated with various pathological conditions27–29. The findings from our study are consistent with earlier research that observed increased levels of MDA in POAG30–33.

Organic compounds known as thiols are defined by the presence of a sulfhydryl (-SH) functional group, also called a thiol group. This group consists of a sulfur atom and a hydrogen atom that are covalently linked to a carbon atom34. Protein thiols in the plasma comprise protein sulfhydryl groups and protein-mixed disulfides with homocysteine, cysteinylglycine, cysteine, and glutathione. The composition of human plasma includes various reduced thiols, namely homocysteine, cysteinylglycine, cysteine, and glutathione. These thiols play crucial roles in maintaining redox homeostasis and protecting cells from oxidative stress. Homocysteine, in particular, has garnered significant attention due to its association with cardiovascular diseases and neurodegenerative disorders. Cysteine, a precursor to glutathione, is essential for protein synthesis and detoxification processes in the body. Many clinical disorders exhibit notable alterations in thiol concentrations34,35–38. In clinical case-control studies conducted in many eye diseases, including glaucoma, total thiol levels were found to be significantly lower in the disease groups24,39–45. In this study, similar to the literature, we found significantly lower total thiol levels in patients with POAG.

This study’s limitations include a small patient sample size and the lack of disease stage classification for individuals with POAG. The absence of a power analysis is also a drawback. Additionally, not collecting aqueous humor samples is another limitation. The cross-sectional design of this study limits its ability to establish causality. Our results are limited to high-pressure primary open-angle glaucoma, and excluding normal-tension glaucoma is another limitation of the study.

In conclusion, these results indicate a potential imbalance between oxidative stress and antioxidant defense mechanisms in POAG patients. The observed alterations in serum biomarkers may contribute to the pathogenesis of POAG by promoting oxidative damage to ocular tissues. Further research, particularly prospective population-based studies with large sample sizes, is needed to elucidate the precise mechanisms by which these oxidative stress markers influence the development and progression of POAG. Such investigations could potentially pave the way for novel treatment strategies that target oxidative stress in the management of glaucoma.

IFPHPOS0055 Lung Cancer Online Communities as a Mirror of Unmet Healthcare Needs: A Content Analysis of Topics, Emotions, and Misinformation
Authors: Fatemeh Homayounifar, Elahe Meftah, Seyed Reza Abdipour Mehrian, Mahdi Salarabedi, Amirreza Jokar, Sadra Valiee, Hossein Molavi Vardanjani, Ali Amanati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCancer patients increasingly turn to online communities for information and peer support, particularly in settings like Iran where financial burden, medication shortages, and unequal access to specialist care are common. These spaces offer emotional support but also risk spreading misinformation. Despite this, research on health misinformation has largely focused on English-language content, leaving a critical gap in understanding Persian-speaking patients’ digital communication patterns.MethodsAll messages from a Persian-language Telegram community for lung cancer patients (January–May 2025) were extracted and structured into conversation threads. A large language model (GPT-4.1-mini) classified each unit by topic, sentiment, emotion, medical urgency, medication mentions, and misinformation. LLM outputs were validated by two independent clinicians reviewing both urgent-flagged messages and a sample of non-urgent messages; discordant cases were adjudicated by a third clinician. Patients with lived experiences contributed to label validation.ResultsWe analysed 3,627 messages across 1,444 conversations from 77 users. Strikingly, 38.8% of messages were sent between 22:00–02:00, reflecting unmet after-hours support needs. Users posed 937 questions but provided only 119 answers. Dominant topics included treatment (14.4%), peer support (12.7%), and symptoms (7.7%). Clinicians confirmed medical urgency in 2.4% of messages. Misinformation appeared in 17 messages, predominantly unproven dietary interventions. Concern (18.4%), hope (17.0%), and sadness (12.1%) were the most frequent emotions.DiscussionThese findings reveal substantial unmet healthcare needs among Persian-speaking lung cancer patients. AI-powered analysis of online communities offers a scalable, reproducible framework for public health surveillance, misinformation detection, and identifying gaps in patient education with particular relevance for low- and middle-income country contexts.

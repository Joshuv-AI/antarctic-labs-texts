Commentary on ‘Immediate mobilization after repair of Achilles tendon rupture may increase the incidence of re-rupture: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials’
Authors: Shilong Meng, Xu Zhang, Baisheng Ye, Xiaolin Shi, Kang Liu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Recently, the article ‘Immediate mobilization after repair of Achilles tendon rupture may increase the incidence of re-rupture: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials,’ published in the International Journal of Surgery, caught our attention1. This study fully followed the PRISMA statement2 and AMSTAR principles3 for literature screening, study inclusion, bias risk assessment, and pooled analysis of outcome measures. The authors mainly studied the effect of different rehabilitation programs on the rate of Achilles tendon re-rupture after Achilles tendon surgery and concluded that: (1) The accelerated rehabilitation intervention has the lowest incidence of re-rupture. (2) Immediate postoperative mobilization may have a higher incidence of re-rupture. (3) It is recommended that ankle joint mobilization be delayed until 1–2 weeks after surgery. This finding provides an evidence-based basis for the early rehabilitation program after Achilles tendon surgery and makes an important contribution to the selection of rehabilitation methods after Achilles tendon surgery. While expressing high appreciation for this study, we also put forward some constructive suggestions.

First, in terms of literature search, this meta-analysis only included studies published in English, which may limit the diversity and representativeness of the included studies, leading to possible publication and language bias in outcome indicators, affecting the accuracy and reliability of the results. We searched relevant studies in Chinese databases and found that some randomized controlled trials (RCTs) could be used for this meta-analysis. Although relevant research is currently dominated by English, the academic value of published research in other languages cannot be ignored. Scientific research is global, and researchers of different languages and cultures contribute to the advancement of science.

Second, the study focused on the impact of different rehabilitation programs on the rate of Achilles tendon re-rupture. The author systematically divided postoperative rehabilitation programs into six categories and conducted a detailed study on the impact of different rehabilitation programs on the rate of Achilles tendon re-rupture. However, other potential confounding factors, such as the influence of different surgical methods and patients’ circumstances on outcome indicators, were not considered. There are certain differences in different surgical methods and patients’ conditions, which will have a great impact on the healing of patients’ Achilles tendons after surgery4, thus affecting the results of the study. At the end of the article, the authors also acknowledge that this meta-analysis did not account for other potential confounding factors, which may lead to some limitations in the results.

Lastly, this meta-analysis included a collection of 43 RCTs, which is currently the largest collection of evidence to date on the incidence of re-rupture after Achilles tendon surgery. On the one hand, the large number of included studies can significantly improve the representativeness and credibility of research results and enhance the communication between researchers in different regions. On the other hand, due to the differences between different countries, regions, and ethnic factors, there may be potential confounding factors for the outcome indicators, and corresponding subgroup analysis is a good choice. We note that the studies included in this meta-analysis can be classified by publication date and region. There may be some differences in surgical methods, rehabilitation programs, and treatment concepts in different periods and regions, which will have a great impact on outcome indicators5.

Achilles tendon rupture is more prevalent in males and individuals over the age of 506. It is suggested that the author conduct a subgroup analysis of different ages, genders, regions, and surgical methods according to the actual situation in the subsequent study to provide personalized rehabilitation programs for patients after Achilles tendon surgery.

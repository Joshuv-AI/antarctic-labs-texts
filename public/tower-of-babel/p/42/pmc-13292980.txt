Combined nonoxynol-9 and antimicrobial peptide LL-37 for barrier contraception
Authors: Murong Xu, Carol Pui Shan Chan, Luping Cong, Jacqueline Pui Wah Chung, Ellis Kin Lam Fok, David Yiu Leung Chan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBirth control and sexually transmitted infections are global concerns. Nonoxynol-9 (N-9) is the most commonly used component in spermicides, with controversial safety and efficacy. LL-37 has been considered the most promising antimicrobial peptide for developing spermicides. Thus, we aimed to refine the regime of N-9 through the adjunction of LL-37 with higher spermicidal efficacy, safety, and antimicrobial activity. Processed sperm from density gradient centrifugation were treated with nonoxynol-9, LL-37, or the combination for the spermicidal activity. Acrosome reaction, DNA fragmentation, and hemizona binding were evaluated. Minimal inhibitory concentrations were measured using E. coli. Mouse experiments were conducted to investigate skin and vagina irritations. The spermicidal activities of nonoxynol-9 and LL-37 were dose-dependent. Premature acrosome reactions were shown in both groups by increasing acrosome-reacted sperm at around 9 and 21% significantly, and interestingly, the combined group showed a synergistic effect by raising over 60%. In the DNA fragmentation, although there were no significant changes in the nonoxynol-9 or LL-37 groups, the combined group showed a 3% increase at a significant level. Both nonoxynol-9 and LL-37 showed reduced binding capacity with the hemizona-binding indexes at 43.2 and 8.0, and the combined group showed the lowest capacity at 5.6. In animal experiments, the addition of LL-37 to the nonoxynol-9 could alleviate the disruptions caused by nonoxynol-9 in reducing skin epidermal hyperplasia and vaginal irritation. However, the presence of nonoxynol-9 would reduce the antibacterial activity of LL-37. This refined regime showed enhanced spermicidal effects and reduced irritations, but only limited antimicrobial activity.Lay summaryBirth control and sexually transmitted infections are global concerns. One of the most common components used in spermicides to kill sperm is nonoxynol-9, but there have been questions about safety and efficacy. LL-37 has been considered a promising component, together with its function in killing microbes. We aimed to design a spermicide using N-9 and LL-37 together, with potentially higher efficacy and safety. This refined regime showed improvements in killing sperm and improved effects on sperm functions. It also reduced the irritation to the skin and vagina, but only showed limited activity in killing the microbes.

Introduction

The world population has reached 8 billion in 2022 and is predicted to exceed 9 billion in 2037, making birth control the utmost important issue to resolve in this century (Pison 2022). Although various contraceptives are available, the contraceptive needs of over 120 million couples were not met yearly (Glasier et al. 2006). As many as 38% of pregnancies are unplanned, and over 50% end in abortion (Singh et al. 2010). Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are also global concerns. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that over 1 million people acquire STIs daily, but better STI prevention methods are yet to be developed (World Health Organization 2018). Thus, the development of improved methods of fertility control will be critical.

Spermicides are a form of chemical barrier to achieve contraception by killing or inactivating sperm. Nonoxynol-9 (N-9) is a surfactant commonly used in spermicides but with controversial efficacy and safety. The estimated in vivo efficacy varies between 50 and 80%, and some users may be allergic, making it far from acceptable in fertile women (Wilkinson et al. 2002). There is clinical evidence showing that N-9 products may increase STI transmission risks through damage to the vaginal epithelium, particularly in individuals engaging in high-risk sexual behaviors (Kreiss et al. 1992, Van Damme et al. 2002, Poynten et al. 2009). High doses and frequent use might also be the causes (Roddy et al. 1993, Raymond et al. 2004). In addition, there are insufficient data to demonstrate any related systemic harm, congenital malformation, or spontaneous abortions related to its use. It is recommended by WHO as a moderately effective contraceptive method but cannot be used for STI protection.

Although various contraceptives already exist on the market, there is no highly efficient spermicide combined with antimicrobial functions (Obiero et al. 2012). LL-37 is an antimicrobial peptide (AMP) that has been confirmed to have the ability to inactivate STI pathogens in vitro (Bergman et al. 2007, Kiattiburut et al. 2018). It is believed to be safe in the female reproductive tract but cannot meet with motile sperm directly. When reacting directly, it demonstrates the ability to disrupt sperm motility through sperm membrane disruption, thus being considered a promising spermicide to be further developed (Srakaew et al. 2014). It can also act to reduce the risk of STI transmission, and its ability to heal wounds may repair vagina damage (Tokumaru et al. 2005).

N-9 has been widely used as the main active ingredient of spermicides over the past half century with an affordable price and easy availability. However, the efficacy and safety of the N-9 spermicides still cause controversy. LL-37 has been considered to be the most promising AMP to be further developed as a contraceptive with spermicidal and antimicrobial activities. LL-37 may have the ability to neutralize the side effects of N-9 and help the products be equipped with dual antimicrobial and spermicidal activity, contributing to safer and more efficient contraception. Thus, we aimed to formulate a new combination of N-9 and LL-37 as a new contraceptive with concurrent antimicrobial activity.

Materials and methods

Ethics statements

This study was approved by the Joint Chinese University of Hong Kong – New Territories East Cluster Clinical Research Ethics Committee (CUHK-CREC No.: 2016.499). Written patient consents were obtained. The animal study was approved by the Animal Experiment Ethics Committee (AEEC: 23-102-MS) of CUHK.

Sperm processing

Semen was collected from participants undergoing semen analysis at the Andrology Laboratory in Prince of Wales Hospital as part of the workup for fertility problems. Leftover samples without any abnormality in sperm concentration and motility (progressive motility (PR); non-progressive motility (NP)) according to the 5th WHO manual and without any known genetic disorders were included (Cooper et al. 2010). Density gradient centrifugation was employed for motile sperm enrichment. Briefly, 90 and 45% gradient solutions (SpermGrad, Vitrolife, Sweden) were loaded into a 15 mL tube orderly and semen samples were overlaid on top (1:1:1). The samples were centrifuged for 20 min at 400 g. The pellets at the bottom of two to three tubes were mixed, washed twice for 10 min at 600 g, and resuspended in Earle’s balanced salt solution (EBSS, Gibco, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA).

Spermicidal activity

Sperm motility was assessed according to the 5th WHO manual (World Health Organisation 2010). The reported minimal concentration of N-9 in inhibiting total sperm motility varies from 20 to 500 μg/mL (White et al. 1995, Centola 1998). The normal physiological concentration of LL-37 ranges from. Naturally produced concentration of ALL-38 (a close analog of LL-37) can be up to 10 μM in the vagina (Srakaew et al. 2014). The spermicidal effect of N-9 (Abcam, UK) was assessed at dilutions of 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125, and 0.00625% (v/v), and that of LL-37 (CPC Scientific, US and InvivoGen, US) was assessed at 50, 25, 12.5, and 6.25 μg/mL. Combined dilutions were prepared to test synergic effects. The solutions were mixed with the processed sperm samples at 5:1 and incubated at 37°C and 6% CO2 for 3, 10, and 30 min (Florez et al. 2011). The total motility (MOT) and PR were categorized with >200 sperm. Based on the motility results, a specific concentration of N-9 and another of LL-37 were chosen, and the four tested groups were as follows: the untreated group, the N-9 group, the LL-37 group, and the combined group.

Acrosome reactions were evaluated using the FluoAcro kit (Microptic, Spain). Briefly, sperm suspensions were incubated with the solutions for 30 min at 37°C and 6% CO2. Sperm spots were stained by peanut agglutinin and a Basic Hoechst type working solution after the ethanol stop. Acrosome-reacted (AR) sperm were in blue or only green at the equatorial segment, while acrosome-intact (AI) sperm were in bright green. Partial bright green in >20% acrosome area was considered partial acrosome-reacted (PAR). A total of 45 processed samples from 101 participants were included, and >200 sperm were counted for each sample.

SpermFunc® DNAf – Sperm DNA Fragmentation Test Kit (BRED, China) based on the sperm chromatin dispersion – was used to evaluate the sperm DNA fragmentation. Briefly, the sperm suspension was embedded in gel and lysed to remove nuclear proteins. The sperm nucleoids were then stained in Wright’s staining solution (Fernández et al. 2018). Sperm with big or medium halos indicated intact DNA, while the others with small (thickness <1/3 of the diameters of sperm heads) or the absence of halo were considered to have fragmented DNA. DNA fragmentation index (DFI), the percentage of sperm with fragmented DNA, was calculated. A total of 44 processed samples from 100 participants were included, and >500 sperm were counted for each sample.

Hemizona-binding assay was used to demonstrate the ability of sperm to bind to the zona pellucida. We included mature and immature oocytes and those with ICSI failure on day 1. Owing to limited availability, 9 zonas from different sources were pooled and used collectively and 15 semen samples were included to test across all treatment conditions. They were bisected into two halves using the RI Saturn 5 Active™ laser (CooperSurgical, USA). Each was incubated with processed or control sperm at approximately 2 × 106/mL for 4 h at 37°C, 6% CO2. After incubation, they were washed 10 times by pipetting to remove untight sperm and stained using ProLong Diamond Antifade Mountant with DAPI (Invitrogen, USA). Hemizona-binding index (HZI) is the ratio of the number of bound sperm in the test droplet to that in the control droplet ×100. HZIs were compared between the untreated group and the N-9, LL-37, or combined group, with three independent experimental replicates, respectively.

Antimicrobial activity

Minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) is developed to determine the lowest concentrations of antimicrobial agents required to inhibit visible bacteria growth (Kowalska-Krochmal & Dudek-Wicher 2021). MICs of N-9 and LL-37 were tested using E. coli, the most common bacteria in reproductive infections. E. coli were diluted to around 105–106/mL first. Then, 180 µL diluted bacteria solution was pipetted into each well containing 20 µL 10× tested solutions to get the final concentrations of N-9 at 3.2, 1.6, 0.8, 0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125, 0.00625% (v/v) and final tested concentrations of LL-37 at 100, 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, 3.125, 1.5625, 0.78125, 0.391, 0.195 μg/mL. LB broth, bacteria working solution, and 199 µL bacteria with 1 µL Penicillin were set as controls. After 18–20 h of incubation (37°C, gentle shaking at 200 rpm), the optical density (OD600) was measured. The synergistic effects were also evaluated.

Skin and vaginal irritations

Skin and vaginal irritations were tested using C57BL/6 mice. The mice were divided into three groups for skin irritations with three mice in each group. Each mouse was shaved to have 5 1 × 1 cm areas in the belly. For individual N-9 and LL-37 groups, overdoses of N-9 (0, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100%) and LL-37 (0, 25, 50, 100, 200 g/mL) were tested on skin for 14 days. For the combined group, besides the first area, the four treated areas were tested using the following combinations: 12.5% N-9 + 25 g/mL LL-37, 25% N-9 + 50 g/mL LL-37, 50% N-9 + 100 g/mL LL-37, and 100% N-9 + 200 g/mL LL-37 for 14 days. Finally, they were sacrificed for histological evaluation. For vaginal irritations, the concentration of N-9 was based on the concentration of one commercial product. Mice were divided into four groups: i) control group, ii) 7% N-9 group, iii) 50 g/mL LL-37 group, and iv) 7% N-9 + 50 g/mL LL-37 group. Mice with no treatment were used as the control. 2 and 8 h were chosen for acute irritations. Briefly, transvaginal injections of 30 µL reagents were applied into the vagina and the mice were sacrificed after the application. In addition, vaginal irritations after multiple applications were tested. Transvaginal injections were given every 2 h for three times, and the mice were sacrificed 8 h after the first application. The vaginal tissues were evaluated based on features of epithelial integrity, epithelial vascular congestion, inflammatory cell infiltration, and edema and scored ranging from 0 to 4. The microscopic irritation score was the sum of the feature scores, and the vaginal irritation indexes were the total scores subtracting the control scores (Lozenski et al. 2012).

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Group differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA to detect intergroup differences and two-way ANOVA to test synergistic effects. P-values<0.05 were considered statistically significant. Graphs were performed using GraphPad Prism.

N-9 at 0.00625% and LL-37 at 50 g/mL increased around 9 and 21% AR sperm significantly (both P values < 0.0001), indicating premature acrosome reactions. After treatment, the combined group could induce acrosome reaction at a higher rate of over 85% (P value < 0.0001), and less than 4% of the sperm had an intact acrosomal structure. In addition, the synergism of N-9 and LL-37 was significant in the percent of AR sperm (P value < 0.0001) (Fig. 2B, C, D). All three treated groups showed rapid responses in inducing acrosome reactions within 30 s. LL-37 mainly worked in the first 10 min, while N-9-induced effects occurred more slowly. To examine the effects of N-9, LL-37, and combined regimen on sperm quality, we tested the levels of DNA fragmentation after treatment. Our results showed that N-9 and LL-37 treatment showed a DFI of 12.37 and 12.68%, respectively. The DFIs in both groups are comparable to those of the control. Of note, the combined regimen resulted in a significant increase in DFI to 15.29% (P value < 0.0001) (Fig. 2H). Although the sole use of N-9 or LL-37 showed no effects on the changes of sperm DNA fragmentation, the combination could fragment sperm DNA significantly.

After incubating with the processed sperm for 4 h, HZIs were 43.2 in the N-9 group, 8.0 in the LL-37 group, and 5.6 in the combined group (Fig. 2I and J). These results suggested that both N-9 and LL-37 reduce the binding capacity of the treated sperm, and the combined treatment could further increase the potency in reducing component of the fertilization potential (Table 2).

Assessment of antimicrobial activity

To assess the antibacterial effects, we tested the MICs of N-9 and LL-37 alone using E. coli. After incubation, LL-37 at 12.5–25 μg/mL showed a minimal inhibitory effect. Although the concentrations of N-9 we used could not show the minimal inhibitory effects on E. coli, even at 10%, the highest concentration we could prepare due to the viscosity of N-9, it showed a decreasing trend in the bacteria growth (Fig. 2K and L). Then, we used the same combined concentrations as those in the sperm motility tests to test the combined antibacterial effects. Although LL-37 at 12.5–25 μg/mL showed the inhibition of visible growth of the bacteria, the addition of N-9 seemed to impact the inhibitory effects of LL-37. When using LL-37 at the concentration of 12.5 g/mL, the addition of N-9 at only 0.00625% made the growth of bacteria visible. With more N-9 added, the concentration of LL-37 required to inhibit visible bacteria growth was higher (Fig. 2M).

Assessment of skin and vaginal irritations

After being treated with N-9, LL-37, or both, no signs such as erythema and edema could be observed during the 14 days on the mouse skin. The sections of the skin showed different extents of pathological changes only in the epidermal thickness (Fig. 3A). The maximum epithelial cell layers and the thickest epidermis (P = 0.0002) (Fig. 3B) was observed with 100% N-9.

Discussion

Birth control and STIs are global concerns. However, the current contraceptive technologies for males are still insufficient and inadequate. When choosing contraceptive methods, efficacy, safety, and availability should all be taken into consideration by the end users. Although various forms of contraceptives for males have already existed on the market, there is no highly effective spermicide combined with antimicrobial functions. N-9 is a surfactant commonly used in spermicides for a long time, but it is controversial due to its low efficacy and safety, which requires improvement. LL-37 is an AMP considered a promising contraceptive with both antimicrobial and spermicidal activities. Thus, we aimed to refine the N-9 regime with the adjunction of LL-37 to formulate a new combination with enhanced spermicidal properties, concurrent antimicrobial properties, and higher safety.

There were three aspects for refining the N-9 receipt through the addition of LL-37.Efficacy: N-9 has been confirmed to have potent spermicidal activities at a concentration as low as 250 g/mL with 20 s (Ping et al. 2005). LL-37 also showed spermicidal activity in both human and mouse sperm (Tanphaichitr et al. 2016). In addition, our results showed that both reagents showed spermicidal activities and the combination of N-9 and LL-37 showed an enhanced spermicidal activity and properties.Safety: multiple uses and high doses of N-9 might disrupt the vaginal tissue, thus facilitating the invasion of pathogens, including those causing STIs (Fichorova et al. 2001). LL-37 could also promote wound healing and anti-inflammation functions on vaginal epithelium during intercourse. Our results showed that the adjunction of LL-37 might help reduce the adverse effect of N-9 and provide an alleviative effect. In addition, LL-37 exerts microbicidal activities against bacteria, viruses, and fungi, including those causing STIs, such as HIV and Neisseria gonorrhoeae, in previous reports (Bergman et al. 2005, Wang et al. 2008). The ability of LL-37 to clear infections also contributes to reducing the risk of STI transmission. In our results, the refined spermicides also showed concurrent moderate antimicrobial effects on E. coli.Availability: although LL-37 is a promising AMP with spermicidal activity, the cost of synthesizing LL-37 and the proper truncated forms are also concerns. It is expensive to synthesize a long peptide with 37 amino acids, which may increase the cost of contraception (Kiattiburut et al. 2018). These limit the stand-alone use of LL-37 as spermicides. Thus, the combination of LL-37 and N-9 may be more cost-effective and affordable.

In the assessments of spermicidal effects, N-9 and LL-37 groups showed premature acrosome reaction. In previous studies, N-9 showed the ability to destroy the acrosome reactions and LL-37 also exhibited spermicidal effects in inducing premature acrosome reactions of sperm with either complete capacitation or partial capacitation status, which were consistent with our results (Wilborn et al. 1983, Centola 1998, Srakaew et al. 2014). In addition, our results showed that the synergy of N-9 and LL-37 could be observed in inducing acrosome reactions in the combined group. LL-37 can bind to the anionic actin, and the intracellular calcium level rises at the same time, thus disrupting the acrosome structure (Bucki et al. 2007, Srakaew et al. 2014). The destabilization of the acrosome structure would be destructed further due to the combination with N-9, thus inducing the acrosome reaction to a greater extent. For DNA fragmentation, there is one study reporting the swollen chromatin structures in some sperm and nuclear decondensation after being treated with 0.05% N-9, whose concentrations were much higher than the concentration we use (Schill & Wolff 1981). The binding of LL-37 to the anionic proteins on the sperm membrane results in membrane disruption and might facilitate the direct contact of N-9 with the nucleus and chromatins. This was associated with an increased DNA fragmentation assessed by the sperm chromatin dispersion method, indicating the increased susceptibility to sperm DNA fragmentation.

In the assessments of antimicrobial effects, although the new combination provided a concurrent moderate antimicrobial activity, the combination of N-9 and LL-37 increased the MICs of LL-37 against E. coli in a dose-dependent manner. It is reported that the antimicrobial activity of LL-37 expressed at the C-terminal of LL-37 can be impacted by factors such as detergents and pH (Johansson et al. 1998, Oren et al. 1999, Zeth & Sancho-Vaello 2021). Thus, N-9, as a detergent, increased the MICs of LL-37 against E. coli. MICs of LL-37 would also increase under the fermentation and anaerobic respiration conditions (Choi et al. 2017). The antibacterial effects in the vagina of N-9 are multifactorial, which should be explored more and cannot only be evaluated in vitro.

In the assessments of skin and vaginal irritations, the irritations of N-9 were moderate to severe, while mild to moderate in the combined group, providing evidence that LL-37 helps reduce the irritations caused by N-9 in the female reproductive tract as well. In addition, in one clinical trial testing the potential symptoms after different frequencies of spermicide use in women, the rates of epithelial disruptions were comparable in both the treated and placebo groups. However, the risks of epithelial disruptions were more than two times higher in the treated groups if the users used the products once or twice daily and even five times higher when using four times daily (Roddy et al. 1993). Based on the results, it is also recommended that although the addition of LL-37 could alleviate the potential disruptions caused to the female reproductive tract by N-9, the new formulation should not be kept in the female reproductive tract for a long time and multiple applications should be avoided in case of potential side effects.

This study has several limitations. First, we used processed sperm in this study. The raw sperm, accompanied by seminal fluid containing key proteins and proteases, should be used in future studies to mimic the physiological conditions. Second, sperm chromatin dispersion assay was used to evaluate the sperm DNA fragmentation indirectly. Although it is a common and simple method, TdT-mediated dUTP nick end labeling and sperm Comet assay are recognized as the only two direct methods in the latest WHO guidelines (World Health Organization 2021). Third, the current antimicrobial activity was based on the effects on E. coli only due to limited conditions, instead of direct evidence of activity against sexually transmitted pathogens. In addition, the sample size was small, which required further validations.

The refined regime of N-9 with the addition of LL-37 should be explored further. First, availability is an important element for couples to choose a contraceptive method. The truncated forms are less expensive than LL-37; however, they retain the spermicidal and microbicidal activities in vitro (Kiattiburut et al. 2018). This offers inspiration for future work to further lower the production cost to make it more economical. Second, the degradation mechanisms of LL-37 should also be taken into consideration, especially when using in vivo. Thus, a steady and continuous method of release should also be developed. Third, designated microbes should be tested, including those causing STIs to evaluate whether any protections for STIs could be provided by the new combination.

In conclusion, as a refined contraceptive formula, the combination of the spermicide N-9 and the antimicrobial component LL-37 possessed enhanced spermicidal properties in spermicidal activity and sperm physiological activities, including acrosome reaction, sperm DNA fragmentation, and hemizona binding. The addition of LL-37 could even alleviate the potential irritations caused by N-9 in both the skin and vagina. However, the new combination possessed only moderate antimicrobial properties in inhibiting E. coli. To summarize, this refined regime with N-9 and LL-37 showed enhanced spermicidal effects and reduced irritations, but only limited antimicrobial activity.

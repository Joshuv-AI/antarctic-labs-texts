IFPHOR0093 Access to Primary Health Care in the Asia-Pacific Region: Determinants, Impacts, and Strategic Interventions for Improved Equity
Authors: Sabi Kaphle, Rasanthika Gunawardhana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrimary health care (PHC) is central to achieving universal health coverage and serves as the first point of contact within health systems. In the Asia-Pacific region (APR), access to PHC is shaped by diverse contextual and structural determinants that influence health outcomes and equity. Understanding these determinants is essential for informing effective policy and system responses.MethodsA scoping review of 68 peer reviewed articles focusing on PHC in the APR was conducted. Evidence was synthesized across four domains: contexts of PHC implementation and access, determinants influencing access, impacts of disparities, and strategies or interventions aimed at improving access. This approach enabled a comprehensive assessment of the challenges and opportunities for strengthening PHC in the region.ResultsMarked disparities in PHC access were identified across the APR, driven by geographic isolation, socioeconomic inequalities, and fragmented health systems. Limited access was associated with poorer health outcomes and the widening of existing inequities. Evidence also highlighted promising interventions, including community led service delivery models, digital health innovations, and targeted policy reforms. These approaches demonstrated potential to enhance accessibility, quality, and sustainability of PHC services.ConclusionsImproving PHC access in the APR requires context specific, equity-oriented strategies that address structural barriers and strengthen health system integration. Sustainable and adaptable interventions, particularly those leveraging community engagement and digital technologies can support more equitable health outcomes. Policymakers should prioritize approaches that respond to regional diversity and advance health equity.

When a suspicious liver lesion is not cancer: primary hepatic myopericytoma in chronic hepatitis B
Authors: Xiao Liu, Xiaokun Chen, Xiaoyan Chang, Shunda Du
Published: 2026

A 47-year-old woman was found to have a 25 mm × 19 mm mass in hepatic segment VI. Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), and protein induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II (PIVKA-II) levels were normal. Magnetic resonance imaging showed slight T1 hypointensity, T2 hyperintensity, arterial patchy enhancement, and progressive portal venous and delayed enhancement. 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) showed increased uptake in the lesion, with a maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of 7.7, and no abnormal uptake elsewhere. She had chronic hepatitis B virus infection, with positive hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), hepatitis B e antibody (HBeAb), and hepatitis B core antibody (HBcAb). Because malignancy was suspected, laparoscopic right posterior sectionectomy, cholecystectomy, and hilar lymph node dissection were performed. Histopathology established the diagnosis of hepatic myopericytoma (MPC). Hematoxylin and eosin staining showed a well-circumscribed tumor composed of bland spindle-to-epithelioid cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, arranged in a characteristic concentric perivascular pattern around thin-walled branching vessels. Immunohistochemistry showed positivity for smooth muscle actin and vimentin, partial desmin expression, vascular CD34 staining, and a Ki-67 labeling index of approximately 2% (Figure 1). The background liver showed mild chronic inflammation and steatosis, without acute hepatitis or cirrhosis.

Imaging and histopathological features of primary hepatic myopericytoma. MRI showed a 25 mm × 19 mm lesion in hepatic segment VI, appearing slightly hypointense on T1-weighted imaging and hyperintense on T2-weighted imaging (A). Dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI demonstrated patchy enhancement in the arterial phase (B) and progressive enhancement in the portal venous and delayed phases (C). 18F-FDG PET/CT showed increased uptake in the segment VI lesion, with an SUVmax of 7.7 (D). Hematoxylin and eosin staining (E) revealed spindle to epithelioid tumor cells arranged concentrically around blood vessels. Immunohistochemistry showed partial desmin expression (F) and a low Ki-67 labeling index of approximately 2% (G). 18F-FDG, 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose; MRI, magnetic resonance imaging; PET/CT, positron emission tomography/computed tomography; SUVmax, maximum standardized uptake value.

Primary hepatic MPC is exceedingly rare, with only six cases reported to date (1). Reported hepatic MPCs may show progressive enhancement and increased 18F-FDG uptake, mimicking intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, perivascular epithelioid cell tumor, or hepatocellular adenoma (2,3). In this case, the concentric perivascular growth pattern and myoid immunophenotype supported the diagnosis. Complete surgical excision is the main treatment, and recurrence appears uncommon (4).

This case is notable for the rarity of primary hepatic MPC and its occurrence in chronic hepatitis B virus infection. In this setting, a small enhancing lesion with bile duct dilatation, hilar lymphadenopathy, and increased 18F-FDG uptake may be misinterpreted as primary liver malignancy. To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of primary hepatic MPC in a patient with chronic hepatitis B virus infection.

SkinDisNet: A comprehensive dataset of clinical images and metadata for skin disease detection and classification
Authors: Marjia Sultana, Rakib Hossain Sajib, Iffat Ara Badhan, Mizu Ahmed, Moumita Yasmin Bushra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSkin diseases are considered among the most important health problems worldwide, affecting millions of people. Early diagnosis can prevent complications and therefore improve the outcomes for patients. However, accurate diagnosis can be challenging due to the visual similarity of different conditions, variability in symptoms, and the shortage of region-specific annotated datasets for developing robust machine learning models. To bridge the gap, this paper presents a new dataset that aims to further the field of skin disease detection through machine learning and computer vision techniques. There are 1710 clinical images of skin diseases, captured using smartphones cameras from hospitals in Bangladesh. The images are organized into six classes of skin diseases—Atopic Dermatitis, Contact Dermatitis, Eczema, Scabies, Seborrheic Dermatitis, and Tinea Corporis. Each image is uniquely identified and stored in categorized folders, with all entries reviewed to ensure accurate classification. Moreover, this paper includes metadata that has been curated meticulously to provide essential clinical and demographic details, enabling precise analysis and reproducible research. Our dataset is intended to facilitate research and advancement in skin disease detection and classification using machine learning and computer vision techniques.Keywords: Skin disease dataset, Dermatology, Medical imaging, Deep learning, Computer Vision, Computer-Aided Diagnosis

Background

Skin diseases are classified as disorders that affect the skin, the largest organ in the body [2]. This is a common disease which can affect people of all ages and ethnicities. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported that in 2020, Bangladesh experienced 1131 fatalities attributed to skin diseases, accounting for 0.16 % of all deaths [3]. Early detection and treatment of skin diseases can prevent complications, reduce discomfort, and enhance an individual's overall quality of life [4]. Skin diseases vary in different regions due to different environmental factors and climate change, pollution and socioeconomic conditions, which influence the prevalence, type, and severity of these conditions [5]. In South Asian developing countries like Bangladesh, India and Nepal, there are a few datasets available for detecting skin disease, which hinders the development of accurate diagnostic tools. This dataset aims to bridge that gap by providing a resource to aid in the early detection and study of skin diseases in these regions.

Data Description

This article presents a clinical image dataset of skin diseases collected between April 2023 and June 2024, from Rangpur Medical College in Rangpur and Shahid Syed Nazrul Islam Medical College in Kishoreganj, two renowned medical colleges in Bangladesh. The dataset is organized into two main folders: Preprocessed and Augmented. Each folder is further categorized by the six skin disease classes: Atopic Dermatitis (AD), Contact Dermatitis (CD), Eczema (EC), Scabies (SC), Seborrheic Dermatitis (SD), and Tinea Corporis (TC). The comprehensive overview of the classes is presented in Table 1. Each folder contains the images labelled with the disease's short name followed by a unique identifier such as AD (1).jpg, CD (5).jpg). Each image is labelled based on expert dermatologists' diagnoses, ensuring the dataset's accuracy and consistency are maintained. The images are accompanied by meticulously curated metadata, encompassing key clinical and demographic details, including patient age, sex, lesion location, and diagnosis. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Data collection

The dataset consists of clinical images of six different categories of skin disease. Images were collected manually by the researchers from the dermatology outdoor department of hospitals during patients’ consultation with dermatology specialists. They were captured using the default cameras of three different smartphones in natural lighting conditions without any prior set up. Table 3 highlights the specifications of the devices employed for data collection. We also obtained patient consents to include their images in this study.

Preprocessing

The collected images exhibited a range of sizes and shapes. Therefore, a series of preprocessing was performed to the dataset for enhancing the quality and consistency of the images. At first, each diagnosed image was carefully labelled according to its corresponding skin disease and organized into a specific folder labelled by the disease name. Afterwards, the images were cropped into a square shape to focus on the disease area by removing unnecessary background elements. Then unwanted objects and backgrounds were removed to enhance the further quality. This involved eliminating the distractions like clothes, shoes, etc. Finally, after cleaning the images, all the images are resized to the uniform size of 512 × 512 pixels for consistency.

Dataset construction

The dataset is organized into two main folders: Preprocessed Images and Augmented Images, each containing six skin disease classes. The preprocessed images folder contains clinically validated and meticulously labelled images and the augmented images folder features enhanced variations generated through augmentation.

Validation of deep learning models

We recommend utilizing a conventional deep learning model to effectively train the dataset, with the goal of achieving cutting-edge outcomes. The validation of a deep learning model involves evaluating its performance on a dataset through processes such as data splitting, model training, and performance analysis on an entirely different testing set. The dataset contains both preprocessed and augmented images which is further split into three subsets: training, validation and testing. After training on the training set, the model’s hyperparameters and intermediate performance are tuned using the validation set, and the final performance of model is assessed using the testing set. Limitations

•The dataset was obtained from only two institutions in Bangladesh. Skin disorders exhibit regional variability influenced by environmental factors, climate change, pollution, and socioeconomic conditions, indicating that this dataset may not cover all types of skin diseases.

The dataset was obtained from only two institutions in Bangladesh. Skin disorders exhibit regional variability influenced by environmental factors, climate change, pollution, and socioeconomic conditions, indicating that this dataset may not cover all types of skin diseases.

•Participation in this study was entirely voluntary, and several individuals may have decided not to provide images because of privacy concerns, personal reasons, or uncertainty about data utilization. If a substantial number of individuals declined to participate, this could result in a smaller dataset and possible selection bias.

Participation in this study was entirely voluntary, and several individuals may have decided not to provide images because of privacy concerns, personal reasons, or uncertainty about data utilization. •Individuals with mild or severe skin conditions may have exhibited varying likelihoods of participation, resulting in an imbalance in data representation. This self-selection bias may affect the dataset's ability to accurately represent all aspects of skin disease severity.

Ethics Statement

The authors ensured adherence to the ethical guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, established by the World Medical Association, as it involved human subjects. It is clarified that patients’ data were anonymized to protect their privacy, and no personal or identifiable information was contained in the dataset. The formal approval for data collection was obtained from the Principal of Syed Nazrul Islam Medical College, Kishoreganj, and the administration of Rangpur Medical College, Rangpur, before the study commenced and ethical approval has also been taken from affiliated department of corresponding author. However, the research's objective was disclosed to all participants, who subsequently granted consent to it. They were also informed of the significance of the research prior to the beginning of data collection. The images were solely used for research purposes and will contribute to the development of the diagnosis and treatment of skin diseases.

Two cases with fenestrations in the left renal veins for the passages of the renal, gonadal, and suprarenal arteries
Authors: Yuine Watanabe, Mio Matsuura, Tetsuhito Kigata, Yasushi Kobayashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFenestrations in the left renal vein (RV) were detected in two Japanese cadavers: a 96-year-old male (Case 1) and an 81-year-old female (Case 2). In Case 1, four left RVs emerged anterior to the renal arteries at the hilum. Fenestration 1 (0.5 cm) was found in the second highest vein. Fenestration 2 (1.0 cm) was found in the third highest vein. Three left renal arteries originated from the abdominal aorta and the inferior branch passed through fenestration 1. The left testicular artery arising 3.5 cm distal to the second-highest renal artery traversed fenestration 2. In Case 2, a fenestration was observed 4.5 cm distal to the left RV drainage into the inferior vena cava. The left middle suprarenal and left ovarian arteries originating from the abdominal aorta at L1/L2 and L2, respectively, passed through the fenestration. These rare variations enhance understanding of the development of the renal, gonadal, and suprarenal arteries.Keywords: Fenestration, Left renal vein, Middle suprarenal artery, Ovarian artery, Testicular artery

Introduction

The renal veins (RVs) are paired vessels that emerge from the renal hilus and drain into the inferior vena cava. Before draining into the inferior vena cava, the left RV collects blood from the ipsilateral suprarenal and gonadal veins and passes anterior to the aorta. By contrast, the right RV exits the renal hilus and drains directly into the inferior vena cava without tributaries.

The left RV shows frequent variations in its ramification patterns, including differences in its course, number, and tributaries [1]. These variations are often associated with variations in the gonadal and suprarenal vessels, resulting in variable positioning of these vessels [2-4]. Preoperative recognition of possible variations in the vascularity of the kidney, genital glands, and suprarenal glands is essential to avoid unexpected complications during retroperitoneal procedures.

Fenestration of the left RV is a rare anatomical variant with a reported incidence of 3.34% [5]. In most previously reported cases, the testicular artery (TA), which arises from the abdominal aorta (AA), passes through the fenestration [5-8]. The left renal artery can also pass through the fenestration [5, 9]. Fenestrations of the left RV represent complex variations involving arteries to the gonads and kidneys and have significant implications in diagnostic imaging and surgical procedures. Knowledge of these rare variants is clinically beneficial and valuable for understanding the morphogenesis of retroperitoneal veins, including the left RV.

Here, we report two cases (male and female cadavers) showing fenestration in the left RV, through which the renal and testicular arteries or the suprarenal and ovarian arteries (OAs) passed, respectively, observed in a routine gross anatomy class for undergraduate students at the National Defense Medical College.

The cadavers used in this study were donated to our institution with written comprehensive consent from the donors and their families who agreed to their use in anatomical education and research. Case Report

Case 1: a 96-year-old Japanese male cadaver

Four veins emerged from the left renal hilus anterior to the renal arteries to form the left RV (Fig. 1). One fenestration (fenestration 1), with a maximum diameter of 0.5 cm, was found at the confluence of the highest and second highest veins. Another fenestration (fenestration 2), with a maximum diameter of 1.0 cm, was located at the terminal confluence, where the lowest vein drained into the others. Three left renal arteries (RA1, 2, and 3) were identified in this case. The top (RA1) and second (RA2) originated from the AA at the L2 vertebral level while RA3 originated from AA at the L3 vertebral level. The branch from RA1 passed through fenestration 1 (Fig. 1), whereas the left TA originating 3.5 cm distal to the origin of RA 2, passed through fenestration 2.

Case 2: an 81-year-old Japanese female cadaver

A fenestration with a maximum diameter of 1.5 cm, was observed in the left RV (Fig. 2). The left middle suprarenal (MSA) and OAs originated from the AA at the L1/L2 intervertebral disc and L2 vertebra, respectively, both passing through the fenestration.

Discussion

Here, the TA and RA or the OA and MSA passed through the fenestration of the left RV. Previous studies have reported that fenestrations in the left RV are usually singular and found near the confluence of the gonadal and suprarenal veins [5-9]. Moreover, only the RA and TA passed through the fenestration of the left RV [5-9]. Thus, the case of the MSA passing through a fenestration in the left RV as well as the incidence of two different arteries passing through the same fenestration, as detected in Case 2, is extremely rare.

Fig. 3 illustrates the developmental process of the left RV and inferior vena cava. These veins are primarily formed through the persistence and regression of paired supracardinal and subcardinal veins and their anastomoses, namely the sub-supracardinal, inter-subcardinal, and inter-supracardinal anastomoses (Fig. 3) [10]. The segment of the RV near the renal hilus is thought to be formed by sub-supracardinal anastomosis, from which RVs emerge (Fig. 3) [7, 10]. The presence of an artery running between the ventral renal vein (VRV) and dorsal renal vein (DRV) is thought to be a factor in the formation of fenestrations, as it impedes complete venous fusion [7]. In the fetus, the RV drains into the inferior vena cava between T12 and L3 [10]. Consequently, arteries such as the gonadal artery or MSA which arise from the aorta at this branching level range, potentially become entrapped in the fusion of the VRV and DRV and sub-supracardinal anastomosis. In the present case, the gonadal artery and MSA arising either from the aorta at a level close to or arising directly from the RA may have been entrapped in the fusion of the VRV and DRV (Fig. 3).

Our cases are unique in the following ways. First, the testicular, renal, suprarenal, and OAs passed through a fenestration in the left RV. Second, in Case 1, the TA arose from the RA and passed through a fenestration in the left RV. Third, in Case 2, two different arteries passed through the same fenestration. The findings in our cases may contribute to a better understanding of the developmental processes of the left RV and its related vessels.

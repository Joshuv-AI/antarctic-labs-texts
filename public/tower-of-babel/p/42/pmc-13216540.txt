Optimizing decision making with Fermatean fuzzy soft Hamachar operators in the analysis of anaphylaxis (a life-threatening allergic reaction)
Authors: Aurang Zeb, Nasir Ali, Muhammad Riaz, Hu Zhao, Muhibullah Mahboob, Muzhou Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe accessibility of affordable medication and emergency response care is essential in preserving the health of children. More youngsters are discovered to suffer from food allergies and are at risk of having life-threatening Anaphylactic responses. Without prompt medical attention, Anaphylaxis can be fatal. The condition is complex as it depends on several factors and exhibits uncertainties due to a range of symptoms. To model multi-faced and uncertain information in these cases, we introduce a fuzzy soft notion, known as the Fermatean fuzzy soft set (FFSS). To fuse this multi-faced data, the Hamacher class of parametric norms are utilized to define novel Fermatean fuzzy soft Hamacher weighted averaging and geometric operators. Data modelling via FFSS, and developed operators are utilized to establish an approach for solving multiple attribute decision making problems. Finally, a decision making (DM) problem related to identifying patients based on symptoms of Anaphylaxis (an allergic reaction that is severe and potentially devastating) is provided for verifying the approach and its practical demonstration. The performance of the operators is examined by aggregating information through different values of the constant . The results revealed that aggregated scores rose significantly for varying , reflecting an enhanced differentiation between membership grades. The insights into the impact of are crucial for selecting the appropriate value in FFSNs, enabling the representation of uncertainty and preferences tailored to various complex DM scenarios. Additionally, it is observed that the triangular norms investigated by classical logic for fuzzy logic are generalizations of the typical two-valued logical conjunction and serve to build the foundation for aggregation operators. The Hamacher norms, being the generalization of algebraic norms, are more flexible. Also, the spatial scope of the Fermatean fuzzy set (FFS) is larger, offering a larger space for uncertainty modelling. The parameterization tool is provided by the addition of soft set theory. These observations are vital for further research, offering a wide range of possibilities to build on in terms of defining operators and novel fuzzy extensions.Keywords: Hamacher aggregation operator, Triangular norms, Decision making, Allergies, AnaphylaxisSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

We can make more deliberate, intelligent decisions by arranging pertinent information and identifying alternatives by following a step-by-step DM process. This technique improves the probability that one will select an alternative that will satisfy them the most. DM is an essential element in all scientific disciplines. When making a decision, experts use their greatest understanding. AOs are the tools used to refer to mathematical operations that combine different pieces of information. These operators have the advantage that they are usually applied to fuse all information and combine it into a single item belonging to the same collection. The fundamental goal of the DM process is to select the most preferred alternative from the given finite set of choices, based on the decision-maker’s assessments. Numerous scientific fields have employed fuzzy sets based on MADM extensively. However, the prior stage involves modelling uncertain information. This stage is challenging as classical or crisp sets cannot handle fuzzy and ambiguous information. It was Zadeh1 who set up the base of fuzzy set (FS) theory to address issues involving fuzziness and uncertainties. Though every FS may represent different pieces of information, but experts deal with the degree of membership (MS) only while performing a decision process. Hence, there may be a degree of indeterminacy while setting the MS degree to some element. The intuitionistic fuzzy set (IFS)2 was introduced by Atanassov, and is backed with the help of MS and non-membership (N-MS) functions. The hesitancy part was not fully elaborated in FS, thus Atanassov explored FS to IFS by recommending the MS () and N-MS grade () to the objects, satisfying the condition that with the hesitancy part .

Uncertainties in real-world issues are linguistically distinct and cannot be modelled with a single extension of FS. Consequently, there are many situations which cannot be elaborated by IFS. For an instant, if an expert gives his opinion about an item in terms of yes as 0.6 and no as 0.5, then These situations cannot be handled with IFS. In3, Yager introduced the concept of Pythagorean fuzzy set (PFS) satisfying the condition that as a generalized set. The spatial scope of the PFS set is greater then IFS and can support problems where IFS cannot be applied. For example, in above situation, but PFS not only provides two-sided information but also gives a sectoral area in a plane for the study of fuzzy information. To allow more freedom in DM process to the experts, authors in4 increased the spatial scope of PFS by taking the 3rd power of the MS and N-MS and introduced FFS which satisfies the limiting condition that . With FFS, the range of admissible orthopairs extends, giving users a greater opportunity to express their opinions regarding MS grades.

Nearly all fuzzy set extensions have certain restrictions, even FFS is not applicable in problems where the limiting condition fails, i.e. . However, the spatial scope or the area of acceptance in the case of FFS is greater than that of IFS and PFS. Another major problem of these extensions in dealing with uncertainties is the absence of a sufficient number of expressive attributes. To solve this problem, the idea of SS theory was put forward by Molodtsov5. SS theory has the fundamental benefit of being limitless and allowing us to deal with fuzziness in a parametric way, as it adds a manageable number of expressive parameters. Molodtsov pointed out that SS philosophy has a vast potential to be used in numerous branches of mathematics. Maji et al.6 provided a detailed theoretical study on SS. Some basic and new type of operations on SS were defined by7. On the other hand, Various -operators, including the min-max, Einstein, Hamacher, Frank, product, Lukasiewicz, Azcel-Alsina, and Dombi operators, are available from an experimental and theoretical point of view. The intersection and union of IFSs can be modelled using a general triangular norm (TN) and triangular conorm (TCN),8. Algebraic sum and product are tools available for modeling the union and intersection of fuzzy numbers. The Hamacher product and sum are two Hamacher operations defined in9, and are preferable to the algebraic product and algebraic sum. Hamacher TN and TCN, being the generalization of algebraic and Einstein norms, are more general and flexible. This work overcomes the issues of handling fuzziness and parametrization in data by modelling it with FFSNs. This information is then fused using newly introduced Hamacher operators based on TNs and TCNs. The work also contributes to the literature by studying basic mathematical properties of these operators and demonstrating their practicality in a real-world DM scenario. Before diving into details, here we present a detailed literature review about fuzzy extensions in the thread of FFSS, and its applications in the DM problem. The practical example is related to the selection of a patient with a severe condition affected by Anaphylaxis which is an allergic reaction that is severe and potentially devastating. The condition is challenging to handle as there are many symptoms and contributing factors to be considered. Challenges in DM are usually due to the ambiguities, imprecision and uncertainty in the environment, especially when there exist more contributing parameters10,11. A person with an allergy experiences an abnormal immunological response to a foreign substance. Allergens are these foreign substances, which can be something like specific meals, pollen, or pet dander12.

Literature review

The IFS-based models are more reasonable to describe the views of experts in situations involving DM. Therefore, it has been applied in many multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) problems of real life13. A novel DM approach in an IFS environment based on hypergraphs is developed by Senapati et al.14, prioritizing sustainable transportation sharing practices. Intuitionistic fuzzy entropy by15 and intuitionistic fuzzy generalized Dice similarity measures were introduced by16. Żmudzińska et al.17 proposed a method that combines the outputs of several deep learning models, applies IFS to represent uncertainty and refines classification results. Their the proposed method facilitates intelligent management of diagnostic workflows in ophthalmology. In medical diagnosis, DM under uncertainty arises. A novel distance measure on IFSs has been introduced by Dutta and Goala18 and used in a DM problem of a medical diagnosis. Distance measures based on a matrix norm between IFSs that are strictly increasing or decreasing binary functions are introduced. These distance measures satisfy the axiomatic definition of distance measure, and overcome the counter-intuitive cases19. Ali et al.20 defined power weighted averaging and geometric AOs for IFSs and utilized them to fuse IFSs in a case study of medical diagnosis, exhibiting efficiency over existing techniques. A new way of representing of the IFSs under complex numbers in polar form is proposed. Set operations, new distance measure, and order relations by polar form of the IFS-complex numbers are defined in21. The combination of IFS with SS has also been employed in several research directions. The Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) under IFSS has been utilized in a DM problem of ovarian cancer in22. Jafar et al.23 developed an approach that uses IFSS-based matrices to a solve multi-criteria DM problem related to the diagnosis of diabetes, opening a new direction for improving the precision.

Work on PFS has also gained much importance. In24,25, the authors have developed new models for multi-attribute group DM under PFS and Pythagorean fuzzy N-soft set and Linguistic Pythagorean fuzzy sets, respectively. The limiting conditions of PFS also suggest that there may occur situations where and so PFS cannot be applied. Kiri?ci26 introduced generalized Pythagorean fuzzy soft set and group-based generalized Pythagorean fuzzy soft set and defined weighted averaging and geometric operators to handle DM problems. Naeem et al.27 performed a mathematical analysis of different types of viral hepatitis with Pythagorean fuzzy soft set and obtained the same optimal solution via techniques like TOPSIS, generalized AOs and VIKOR (Vise Kriterijumska Optimizacija Kompromisno Resenje). Recently, Maheshpraba and Kameswari28 introduced generalized Pythagorean fuzzy soft expert sets and studied basic operations. Similarly, the idea has been extended to Pythagorean fuzzy N-bipolar soft sets and applied to assess risks and sustainability in manufacturing industries29.

As discussed earlier, the limiting condition over membership grades in FFS is relaxed and hence the spatial scope of FFS is larger compared to IFS and PFS. This is the main reason that its application to real-world DM problems has gained much attention. Senapati and Yager4 presented work on applications of Fermatean fuzzy sets in MADM. A hesitant Fermatean fuzzy MCDM technique using Archimedean Bonferroni mean operators was proposed by the authors in30 under an FFS environment. Senapati and Yager31 introduced AOs for Fermatean fuzzy information, and Liu et al.32, established a technique of distance measure for Fermatean fuzzy linguistic term sets. Deveci et al.33 developed a DM model using HAOs under interval-valued FFS for evaluating climate change-resilient transportation alternatives. The idea is extended to cubic FFS, where the authors in34 introduced an extended measurement alternative and ranking based on the compromise solution method. Modified distance measures between Fermatean fuzzy numbers have been defined in35 and integrated to form an MCDM model for uncertainty handling. Biderci, Guneri36 integrated the TOPSIS method with the analytical hierarchy process for weights determination under interval-valued FFSs in a MADM problem of risk assessment.

Although these extensions are useful to handle uncertain information in real-world problems, they cannot support parametrization. In other words, modelling uncertainty becomes challenging when data emerges from several sources simultaneously. To overcome this challenge, the SS theory has been integrated into these extensions of FSs and successfully applied in DM problems. Improved operations for the FFSS are studied and a DM technique based on score matrices of FFSS is developed and implemented in the diagnosis of an infectious disease37. Recently, Ta?köprü and Karaköse38 proposed a novel approach to relations based on SS theory and their applications in DM issues. Similarly, novel variants of SS have also been introduced and utilized in real-world problems. The idea of the N-soft set is developed in39 and applied to DM problems. Other includes Hyper Soft Set, Indeterm Soft Set, Indeterm Hyper Soft Set, and Tree Soft Set40. Several extensions of FS have also been combined to construct powerful extensions of SS and FS that can handle both fuzziness and parametrization. Zeb et al.41,42 combined fuzzy bipolar and Pythagorean fuzzy bipolar sets with SS. They have extended the idea to overcome the constraints over memberships grades by introducing Fermatean fuzzy soft and later the generalized extension with application to different scenarios, addressing DM43,44. Kiri?ci45 introduced a new type of Pythagorean parametrized fuzzy soft set and utilized it to solve DM problem.

Motivation novelty and contributions

It is clear from the literature review that current studies regarding DM using various types of AOs for fuzzy set extensions are not robust, flexible and general. Those techniques that are based on generalized extensions, such as FFS, typically lack parameterization’s fundamental property, which is crucial in complex problems. The application of SS is extremely beneficial in this regard as it generalizes FS theory. In other words, Zadeh’s fuzzy set can be assumed as a subset of SS. The first motivating factor of this work is that, in our recently published article44, we have not discussed some important aspects of SS in combination with the fuzzy sets. It can be deduced that most of the AOs based on algebraic norms defined for different extensions of fuzzy set, IFS, PFS, IFSS, and PFSS comes under the umbrella of the work already introduced under FFSS44. AOs defined for IFS, PFS and even for FFS, can easily be deduced by removing SS from FFSS (like,31,46,47) while AOs defined for IFSS and PFSS can also be obtained by relaxing the limiting condition on FFSS47,48. The second and most important motivating factor is the generalization of the aggregation function. Here, we introduce AOs that are based upon the generalized TN and TCN known as the Hamacher norms. Hamacher TN and TCN are more flexible and general because they generalize algebraic as well as Einstein norms. Due to the greater flexibility of Hamacher TN and TCN, we define some powerful AOs called Fermatean fuzzy soft Hamacher aggregation (FFSHA) operators. The study of AOs based on Hamacher operations and their application to MADM problems has important consequences. As these operators have not been applied for FFSS so far. Therefore, we define Hamacher AOs based on Hamacher TCN and TN in the environment of FFSS. Later on, it can be shown that the plenty of work based on algebraic and Einstein norms becomes the sub-cases of the presented work, which shows the superiority of the novel introduced work. In addition, the sensitivity analysis of H-AOs has always been ignored. We devoted a complete section to analyze the performance of H-AOs for different values of . Considering these factors, this article contributes by covering the above mentioned gaps in the FFSS-based environment. Figure 1 is the flowchart for the DM approach and approach is step-wise given below.

Flowchart for the DM approach.

Step 1: Point out a set of alternatives that are to be evaluated. The set of such alternatives are usually given by the stakeholders of companies or organizations. The task may sometimes be assigned to a team of experts.

Step 2: A set of contributing attributes is constructed. The selection of such factors is important and field experts play a vital role in the construction of the set of attributes.

Step 3: Define weight vectors for the sets of alternatives and attributes. Each component of these vectors will weight the importance of experts and attributes during the aggregation phase.

Step 4: Data related to different alternatives under investigation is collected by the experts. This information needs to be collected corresponding to list of parameters put forward by the experts or by the organization seeking the best choice. In our case, the data is to be collected and arranged in the form of an FFS matrix

Step 5: Data normalization is performed to convert the varied dimensional quantities to the same dimensional form. In this study, the rating values of cost type attribute are changed into benefit type attribute to normalize the information decision matrix,

Step 6: Utilize the developed AOs to aggregate the FFSNs for the set of alternatives into a combined decision matrix. The aggregation process can be performed using any or all of the proposed operators.

Step 7: Using score and accuracy functions, calculate score values of for each

Step 8: Order the options according to their score values obtained in previous step. The best option should be chosen after determining which options are the greatest and worse.

Practical example

Here, we demonstrate how our newly introduced HAOs are used in practice. First, a DM problem has been introduced that is related to the health of children in early stage of their life.

Childhood Allergies: A person with an allergy experiences an abnormal immunological response to a foreign substance. Allergens are these foreign substances, which can be something like specific meals, pollen, or pet dander,12. An child can develop allergies, but those who have a history of allergy issues tend to experience them more frequently. Instead of trying to regulate every aspect of what their child is exposed to or consumes, parents should focus on looking out for warning indicators. Tree pollen, bug bites, animal hair, dust mites, mildew, cigarette smoke, perfume, car exhaust, peanuts, and milk products are some of the allergens that affect children most frequently,50. American children who have been diagnosed with various allergies and allergic responses are examined in a report by the Blue Cross Blue Shield (BCBS) Association and Blue Health Intelligence. Around 1.7 million kids, or 18% of all youngsters, have one or more allergies. In this report, rhinitis (runny, stuffy nose), dermatitis (inflamed skin or a skin rash), and all of these conditions are specifically examined, including a severe allergic reaction known as Anaphylaxis causes a sharp drop in blood pressure and respiratory problems).

Symptoms and Signs of Allergies: The severity of allergy symptoms and their variability from person to person make them unpredictable. When reacting to the same allergen, a person might not always have the same symptoms (see Figure 2). Itchy, watery eyes, itchy skin, sneezing, runny nose, nasal congestion, hives (atopic dermatitis or eczema), problems in breathing (asthma), coughing, and upset stomach are all common symptoms of allergies. Food allergies can also result in nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, mouth tingling, or swollen facial features. Stings from insects can also result in localized pain, redness, and swelling,51.

Anaphylaxis and its Emergency Signs: A severe allergic reaction called Anaphylaxis, which can be fatal, generally involves multiple parts of a body immediately. The most frequent symptoms are to foods, medicines, latex, insect stings, and foods. Within seconds to minutes of exposure, symptoms usually start to manifest. The inflammatory system of cells emits a barrage of poisonous substances during Anaphylaxis, which can be a reason of sending the body of a person into shock. Anaphylaxis can include breathing difficulties, a fast drop in blood pressure (heart palpitations), narrowing of the airways, a rapid, weak pulse, chest tightness, skin rashes, facial swelling, hoarse voice, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea as well as tingling in the hands, feet, or scalp and cyanosis (pale or blue coloring of the skin). Without rapid medical care, such as an epinephrine injection, Anaphylaxis can be fatal (adrenaline) if someone is exhibiting signs of Anaphylaxis, they should go to the emergency department,52. Most severe allergic reactions in children, known as Anaphylaxis, have significantly increased, and they are frequently brought on by foods. Rhinitis and dermatitis, two other frequently identified allergies, did not alter significantly during the course of the study but continued to be a prevalent complaint of allergy sufferers. Boys were more likely than girls to have an allergy diagnosis overall over the study period, which saw a modest increase in the rate of all children being diagnosed with one or more types of allergies.

Diagnosing Allergies: A comprehensive examination of numerous elements is necessary to accurately diagnose an allergy, which is not always a simple process. As a result, allergists receive specific training so they can identify allergies with accuracy and treat them successfully. To assess what a person is allergic to as well as what he or she is not, allergists use the patient’s medical history, physical exam results, and an appropriate allergy test. Blood testing can also be performed to confirm an allergy, even though skin tests are more usually employed. The appropriate identification and diagnosis of pediatric allergies must still be a top priority if we want to keep kids safe. Public health debates continue to center on the accessibility of epinephrine auto-injector medication, as well as the expense of treating children in emergency rooms,53.

Sensitivity analysis

Hamacher TN are the only TN which are rational functions. They are strict if is less than while it is equal to the product TN in case . In our study, we performed a sensitivity analysis of the Hamacher AOs (FFSHWA operator) for FFSNs by varying the parameter from 1 to 1500. The purpose of this analysis was to examine the impact of on the aggregation results and to understand how the choice of influences the outcome. We aggregated FFSNs using the Hamacher operator with different values of and observed the corresponding scores obtained. It was noted that as increased, the scores exhibited minimal variation, indicating that the aggregation results became increasingly stable. Figure 5 shows that the variation decreases as the value of increases for all alternatives. When using values of greater than 1 in the Hamacher AOs, it can lead to interesting observations regarding the behaviour of non-membership degrees and the convergence of score values. As increases, the denominator of the Hamacher operator becomes larger. Since non-membership is represented in the denominator, an increase in results in a stronger emphasis on the degree of membership. Consequently, the non-membership degree tends to approach zero, indicating a higher focus on the membership aspect of the aggregation. In our case, for we got the non-membership value very small like, 0.039008 (for ). As increases, the effect of the denominator in the Hamacher operator becomes more dominant. This leads to a shrinking of the interval in which the resulting score values lie. The aggregation becomes more sensitive to the input values, emphasizing the differences between them. Consequently, the score values tend to converge to a smaller interval, reflecting a higher level of discrimination and differentiation in the aggregated result.

Variation of score values (obtained through FFSHWG operator) for different values of .

It is important to note that these observations are based on the specific formulation and behaviour of the Hamacher operator with higher values of . Different operators or variations may yield different results. Therefore, it is essential to consider the specific context, definition, and properties associated with the chosen AOs when interpreting its behaviour with varying parameter values. Figure 5 is displaying the variation of scores for different values of , and elaborates that the variation of the scores converges to a very small interval. In other words, the variation almost vanishes for higher values of .

At lower values of , such as 1, the operator gave more weight to the degree of non-membership, accentuating the differences between the FFSNs. As a result, the scores showed relatively higher variation, reflecting the influence of the degree of non-membership on the final aggregated result. However, as approached higher values, such as 1500, the operator progressively emphasized the degree of membership over the degree of non-membership. In this scenario, the scores exhibited minimal variation, and the impact of the degree of non-membership on the final result diminished significantly. The diminishing impact of on the scores can be attributed to the nature of the Hamacher AOs. As increases, the denominator of the operator’s expression becomes dominated making the variation in the numerator less influential. Consequently, the operator converges towards a behaviour similar to the maximum operator, which treats the degree of membership and non-membership equally. Our findings indicate that in this specific case, the choice of has a diminishing effect on the aggregation results. Higher values of lead to more stable and consistent outcomes, with minimal variation observed in the scores. Therefore, if the objective is to obtain more robust and less sensitive results, a higher value of , could be favoured. It is important to note that the specific behaviour of the Hamacher AOs and the impact of may vary depending on the characteristics of the FFSNs being aggregated and the specific application domain. Therefore, further investigation and sensitivity analyses should be conducted in different contexts to validate and generalize these findings.

Advantages and limitations of the proposed approach

The proposed DM approach is based on the combined extension of FFS and SS that can handle uncertainty and parametrization simultaneously. Also, the Hamacher class of TNs is a generalized class of parametric norms that can yield others, such as Einstein’s and algebraic norms, by varying the controlling parameter . Hence, the presented approach covers a greater part of the current literature as several classes of AOs operators can be deduced from the newly developed operators. For instance, Fermatean fuzzy soft Einstein’s weighted averaging, geometric and corresponding ordered weighted operators can be deduced by using in Eqs. 22, to 25 (see55). Using in these equations, they are reduced to the algebraic weighted averaging and geometric operators previously introduced44. Similarly, if the number of experts is reduced to only 1, it consequently removes the SS. In other words, there is no need to use SS in this case and hence the developed operators are reduced to Fermatean fuzzy Hamacher, Fermatean fuzzy Einstein (), Fermatean fuzzy weighted averaging and geometric operators (). The latter case is also possible if the number of parameters is reduced to 1 with more than one expert. Therefore, the reduction process can be understood from two perspectives: one by changing the controlling parameter in the Hamacher norms and second, by removing SS (considering the case of only one expert and multiple attributes or one attribute and multiple experts). The developed operators are more flexible because the controlling parameter allows various degrees of interaction between FSs, making them highly suitable for DM problems. The issue of handling extreme values in a fuzzy context is a major problem in the field of FSs-based DM. The Hamacher norms-based operators can handle this problem up to a great extent by varying . These operators are especially helpful in systems that, for example, should severely penalize low satisfaction with one criterion; the parameter can adjust this behaviour. These properties make the Hamacher operators and Fuzzy soft extension tailored-made for real-world DM scenarios. Data fusion is a key process in nowadays machine learning algorithms; hence, the proposed operators can play a vital role in the fusion process when uncertain information exists in the form of FSs.

Although the presented approach is generalized and flexible, still there are various issues that cannot be handled with it. One major reason is the limiting condition over the membership grades in the FFSs. However, it is easy to relax the condition to for . In this case, the fuzzy extension is known as the q-rung orthopair fuzzy set. Thus, novel fuzzy tools, such as q-rung orthopair fuzzy soft sets, complex q-rung orthopair fuzzy soft sets, q-rung picture fuzzy soft sets, and complex q-rung picture fuzzy soft sets, are possible to introduce. Additionally, the FFSS is not capable of handling fuzzy data with multiple poles, such as positive and negative. Such situations can be handled by extending the ideas of Pythagorean fuzzy bi-polar soft sets42 to define Fermatean fuzzy bi-polar soft sets and q-rung fuzzy bi-polar soft sets or even q-rung fuzzy m-polar soft sets. The developed approach needs are utilized in an assumption-based scenario of ranking alternatives. Thus, further analysis needs to be done to validate the performance of the approach.

Conclusion

Complexities in the diagnosis of a problem become more challenging when multiple factors need to be considered. Hence, the proposed work, equipped with Hamacher operators for a flexible extension of the FFSSs, is a generalized approach that can simplify the description of real-world problems using properties such as parameterization and fuzziness. The work also contributes to the field by introducing Hamacher operators for the FFSNs and investigating their fundamental properties. The advantage of the proposed Hamacher norms-based operators is that other classes of AOs, such as Einstein and algebraic, can be deduced from it directly. In this way, the work covers a good part of the existing literature. A case study that involves determining the most serious patient from a group of available patients is used to test the methodology. The whole procedure has been explained with the help of calculating examples and graphical representations. The performance of the Hamacher AOs has been tested for different values of the constant . The sensitivity analysis of the presented AOs indicated that the choice of the parameter has a diminishing effect on the aggregation results. Higher values of lead to more stable and consistent outcomes, with minimal variation observed in the scores. Therefore, if the objective is to obtain more robust and less sensitive results, a higher value of , could be favoured. Moreover, it is important to note that the specific behaviour of the Hamacher AOs and the impact of may vary depending on the characteristics of the FFSNs being aggregated and the specific application domain. Therefore, further investigation and sensitivity analyses should be conducted in different contexts to validate and generalize these findings.

Although, FFSS may not be applicable to many DM scenarios because of the limiting condition over its membership grades, however, the issue can be resolved by extending the idea to define generalized extensions, such as q-rung orthopair fuzzy soft sets, complex q-rung orthopair fuzzy soft sets, q-rung picture fuzzy soft sets, and complex q-rung picture fuzzy soft sets are possible to introduce. Moreover, the extension FFSS lacks the ability to provide support for polarity, like positive, negative or even multiple poles. To overcome the problem, we plan to introduce notions like Pythagorean fuzzy bi-polar soft sets, Fermatean fuzzy bi-polar soft sets and q-rung fuzzy bi-polar soft set or even q-rung fuzzy m-polar soft sets. Finally, the developed approach is only tested in an assumption based DM scenario and needs to be tested on real-data. Thus, analysis on real data need to be done to validate the performance of the approach. We also plan to utilize language models on text data (online reviews) to obtain their polarity (numerical values) and then model them using fuzzy extensions. The aggregated results of these values can give insightful information about the polarity of the reviews. Also, the presented work can easily be extended to develop AOs, such as Dombi prioritized Aczel Alsina, Schweizer-Sklar operators for FFSS and suggested extensions.

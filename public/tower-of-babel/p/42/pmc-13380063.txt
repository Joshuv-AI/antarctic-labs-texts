Multiscale biomimetic design for peripheral nerve repair: Beyond passive bridging
Authors: Mouyuan Sun, Zhixu He, Yaxian Luo, Luying Qin, Binhao Jin, Shuangyang Li, Xuankai Fan, Jingyu Zhang, Xinyi Feng, Lianjie Peng, Tao Qiu, Tian Zhang, Huiming Wang, Yan Tu, Mengfei Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Peripheral nerve injury persists as a formidable clinical challenge, particularly in long-gap and complex lesions where surgical intervention frequently yields suboptimal functional restoration. Current nerve guidance conduits (NGCs) afford limited advantage owing to their passive bridging nature, incapable of recapitulating the native nerve's hierarchical architecture, bioactive microenvironment, or temporally coordinated repair programs. Informed by bibliometric and meta-analytic evidence, this review redefines biomimetic peripheral nerve repair as an orchestrated transition from passive bridging to biologically instructed, adaptive neural interfaces. Departing from a mere enumeration of structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and cross-species biomimicry, each axis is endowed with a specific functional mandate. Structural biomimicry imposes directional and hierarchical guidance, functional biomimicry re-establishes the regenerative niche, spatiotemporal biomimicry synchronizes with stage-dependent repair requirements, and cross-species biomimicry expands the reservoir of transferable design principles. The ways in which these cues converge, compete, or synergize within composite conduits are systematically examined, together with the imperative of prioritizing their configurations for manufacturability, sterilization, regulatory feasibility, and long-gap validation. This synthesis crystallizes a design logic for next-generation NGCs, guiding their evolution from static scaffolds toward adaptive, manufacturable, and clinically translatable regenerative interfaces.

Introduction

Peripheral nerve injury (PNI) afflicts over 4 million individuals globally each year, frequently provoking permanent sensorimotor deficits and chronic pain that erode quality of life and generate substantial socioeconomic burdens [1]. Therapeutic strategies are dictated by defect length. Direct end-to-end neurorrhaphy is feasible for small, tension-free defects (under 5 mm) but proves ineffective for longer gaps owing to tension-induced impairment of vascularization and peripheral nerve regeneration (PNR) [2]. Autologous nerve grafting persists as the clinical gold standard for critical gaps exceeding 2 cm. Nevertheless, clinical outcomes remain suboptimal, with meaningful functional recovery achieved in fewer than half of patients and full recovery rates of merely 10-25% in severe cases [3,4]. Its utility is further compromised by donor site morbidity, limited graft availability, and potential immune rejection. These drawbacks have propelled the development of nerve guidance conduits (NGCs) as a promising platform to support PNR.

Regulatory-cleared NGCs are predominantly fabricated from type I collagen, chitosan, or synthetic polymers including polyglycolic acid (PGA) and poly(L-lactide-co-ε-caprolactone) (PLCL). These constructs typically adopt a simple hollow architecture, achieving efficacy comparable to autografts in short-gap repairs but exhibiting diminished performance in extended nerve gaps [5]. This divergence originates from the inability of simple synthetic conduits to recapitulate the complex pro-regenerative microenvironment of native tissue, instead furnishing only a passive, static enclosure [6]. Autografts remain clinically superior, yet their efficacy hinges on a highly organized architecture and a regenerative niche replete with viable Schwann cells (SCs), extracellular matrix (ECM) cues, and endogenous physicochemical signals. Nevertheless, even autografts fall short of ensuring complete recovery in critical-sized defects owing to ischemia, constrained nutrient diffusion, and SC senescence [7]. Addressing critical-length nerve defects consequently calls not for a progressively elaborate tube, but for a biologically intelligent regenerative interface that orchestrates vectorial guidance, niche reconstitution, and phase-specific regulatory cues, while co-opting transposable design principles from diverse biological contexts to fortify scaffold engineering [8].

Biomimetic strategies converge biology and engineering to translate nature's adaptations into intelligent technologies for PNR, an approach predicated on deciphering the native regenerative cascade. In response to injury, SCs, endothelial cells, and macrophages become acutely activated and orchestrate a coordinated reparative program that encompasses myelin debris clearance, revascularization, Büngner band organization, and neurotrophic factor secretion [9]. In severe transections involving critical gaps, however, complete tissue discontinuity eliminates the topographical and neurotrophic cues essential for PNR. The consequent ischemic, oxidative, and inflammatory microenvironment provokes axonal misguidance, neuroma formation, and SC senescence, thereby culminating in regenerative failure [7,10]. To intervene effectively, bionic neural tissue engineering has become a central focus [8]. It progresses across three dimensions: structural biomimicry to recapitulate hierarchical and anisotropic architecture, functional biomimicry to reconstitute the multifaceted regenerative microenvironment, and process biomimicry to emulate the dynamic temporal continuum via responsive materials [11]. Beyond replicating native tissues, the field also harnesses cross-species evolutionary principles to inform scaffold design. By synergistically integrating these dimensions, next-generation scaffolds can more adeptly orchestrate the complex cellular behaviors necessary for complete morphological and functional restoration.

Notwithstanding advances in neural tissue engineering, biomimetic design remains integral to next-generation NGC development. Guided by bibliometric insights and clinical meta-analyses, this review systematically delineates how structural, functional, process, and cross-species biomimicry constitute four complementary dimensions that inform rational scaffold design for PNR. The generational evolution of these scaffolds from static supports to intelligent 4D systems is discussed, together with their clinical translation landscape. Current translational bottlenecks and emerging opportunities, notably in AI-assisted design and advanced biomanufacturing, are also accentuated. Overall, this review establishes biomimetic design as a foundational route toward next-generation NGCs, with multidimensional biomimicry representing a pivotal future direction.

Clinical bottlenecks of current NGCs and bibliometric landscape of biomimetic strategies for PNR

PNI exacts a considerable clinical and socioeconomic toll worldwide, with pronounced regional disparities in epidemiology and management. In China, roughly 20 million individuals are afflicted, and approximately 2 million new cases emerge each year. In the United States, PNI is documented in 2.6% of upper-extremity and 1.2% of lower-extremity trauma patients, frequently with delayed detection [12]. Although direct neurorrhaphy, autografts, and NGCs are routinely employed, therapeutic efficacy remains heavily contingent on defect length and injury context (Fig. 1A). For defects under 5 mm, direct end-to-end neurorrhaphy accomplishes effective repair owing to a tension-free state [2]. When direct repair proves impracticable, NGCs demonstrate efficacy in short gaps, particularly within 20 mm, where outcomes parallel those of autologous or allogeneic grafts. Collagen, PGA, and autologous vein conduits can likewise afford some sensory recovery in gaps of roughly 10 mm [13,14]. Therapeutic efficacy, however, correlates strongly with gap length, with outcomes predictably deteriorating as distance increases. The clinical failure rate of NGCs (defined as absence of protective sensation) attains 30% for gaps of 15-25 mm, a rate threefold higher than that for short gaps (5-14 mm, 10% failure). Commercially available NGCs are typically indicated only for gaps up to 30 mm. To quantitatively appraise clinical performance of artificial conduits relative to nerve grafts and neurorrhaphy, we performed a systematic meta-analysis. A comprehensive literature search across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library (January 1, 1965 to April 1, 2026) yielded 11 relevant studies (Table 1). Among these, 8 studies compared conduit efficacy with autografts or allografts employing static two-point discrimination (s2PD) as the primary endpoint; 5 studies compared conduits with neurorrhaphy using the Highet classification. Pooled analysis revealed that mean s2PD in the conduit group was marginally higher (+0.30 mm) than in the autograft group, albeit not statistically significant (Fig. 1B). Relative to the neurorrhaphy group, the conduit group exhibited a modestly higher rate of good-to-excellent recovery (S3+, S4), yet the difference did not reach significance (Fig. 1C). These findings merit cautious interpretation, as the restricted number of eligible studies limits statistical power and diminishes the robustness of pooled estimates. The absence of significant differences between artificial conduits and traditional therapies also largely originates from clinical indication biases. NGCs are predominantly confined to short-gap repairs, where tension-free bridging and the body's endogenous regenerative capacity can offset scaffold passivity. Nevertheless, this apparent non-inferiority, observed chiefly in selected short sensory gaps, conceals a critical bottleneck: simple hollow conduits still fail to recapitulate the proactive, multidimensional structural and biological cues inherently furnished by autografts. Thus, they remain inadequate for long-gap repair. Propelled by this conspicuous unmet clinical need, biomimetic neural tissue engineering has increasingly become a focal direction in PNR research. To systematically chart the research landscape, principal hotspots, and evolutionary trajectory of this domain for PNR, we conducted a bibliometric analysis based on the Web of Science Core Collection. The retrieval period extended from 2005 to 2026, employing search terms that included “peripheral nerve regeneration,” “peripheral nerve repair,” “neural tissue engineering,” “nerve conduit,” “biomimicry,” “biomimetics,” “biomimicking,” “bioinspired,” “bioinspiring,” “bioinspiration,” and “bionic.” After deduplication in CiteSpace, 255 publications were retained for subsequent analysis. CiteSpace, Tableau, and Scimago Graphica were utilized to visualize annual publication trends, country/regional distribution, source journals, highly cited works, keyword co-occurrence, thematic clustering, and research evolution.

Overall publication output ascended steadily from 2005 to 2025, featuring pronounced acceleration after 2014 and sustained activity in recent years. This trajectory confirms that biomimetic strategies have become an increasingly pivotal research direction in PNR (Fig. 2A). Country/region distribution identified China and the United States as dominant contributors, occupying central nodes within the global research network. Additional nations including the United Kingdom, Canada, Japan, India, Singapore, Italy, and Switzerland likewise demonstrated continuous engagement (Fig. 2B). Journal distribution further revealed that pertinent studies were predominantly published in Biomaterials, Acta Biomaterialia, Journal of Neural Engineering, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, Advanced Functional Materials, Nature Communications, and Science Translational Medicine. Keyword co-occurrence analysis identified that terms such as “peripheral nerve regeneration,” “electrical stimulation,” “scaffolds,” “tissue engineering,” “Schwann cells,” “biomaterials,” “extracellular matrix,” “conduits,” and “neural interface” form the core knowledge architecture of the field (Fig. 2E). Keyword clustering timeline and timezone views further delineated a discernible evolutionary trajectory. Early investigations centered on stimulation, extracellular matrix, in vitro, and regeneration. Attention subsequently gravitated toward scaffolds, biomaterials, Schwann cells, and surface modification. The frontier has more recently pivoted toward neural interface, targeted muscle reinnervation, 3D printing, hydrogel, and repair (Fig. 2F–G).

Manufacturing technologies in biomimetic neural tissue engineering

Biomimetic neural tissue engineering fundamentally depends on precision manufacturing technologies. These techniques enable systematic recapitulation of the native neural microenvironment via multi-scale precise regulation, ranging from nanoscale ECM topology to macroscale multi-channel structures. Consequently, they transform passive bridging NGCs into actively adaptive regenerative interfaces. Conventional methods including solvent casting, molding, or dip-coating can generate basic tubular architectures yet fail to satisfy requirements for nanoscale anisotropic topography, concentration or stiffness gradients, or spatiotemporal dynamic responses. In recent years, advanced platforms such as electrospinning, 3D/4D bioprinting, freeze-drying or freeze-casting, microfluidic spinning, self-assembly with hydrogel crosslinking, and decellularization have emerged as core modalities. Manufacturing technologies in biomimetic neural tissue engineering.

Electrospinning employs a high-voltage electric field to elongate polymer solutions into continuous nano- to microscale fibers. This approach affords precise control over fiber orientation, surface grooves, and anisotropic ECM topology. It is routinely utilized to generate aligned nanofiber scaffolds for lumen filling or gradient delivery layers, which potently promote directional SC migration and axonal guidance. High porosity facilitates compositing with conductive or bioactive materials, although subsequent winding or assembly into tubular configurations is typically necessary [27]. In a representative study, researchers produced aligned nanofiber conduits bearing longitudinal nano-grooves via electrospinning. In a rat sciatic nerve injury model, the groove topology markedly augmented PNR, directly demonstrating the technology's singular advantage in delivering anisotropic physical guidance [35].

3D/4D bioprinting harnesses layer-by-layer deposition (extrusion, stereolithography (SLA), fused deposition modeling (FDM), or melt electrowriting) to construct multi-channel architectures, gradient microstructures, or shape-memory responsive scaffolds. This approach enables personalized hierarchical designs, precise cell or factor encapsulation, and spatiotemporal dynamic deformation, rendering it particularly suitable for complex biomimetic constructs in long-gap nerve defects [36]. Using digital light processing 3D printing, researchers fabricated gelatin methacryloyl/poly(ethylene glycol) diacrylate (GelMA/PEGDA) hydrogel multi-channel conduits that precisely replicated the hierarchical microstructure of nerve fascicles. In rat sciatic nerve defect models, these conduits markedly enhanced sensory recovery, gait function, and muscle regeneration, directly demonstrating the singular advantage of layer-by-layer deposition for macro-to-micro precise biomimicry [28].

Freeze-drying or freeze-casting precisely governs ice crystal nucleation and propagation to produce anisotropic porous networks. This process yields longitudinally aligned microchannels or interconnected 3D pore architectures while retaining native ECM bioactivity. These techniques are frequently combined with hydrogels or decellularized matrices to augment nutrient diffusion and cell infiltration, affording operational simplicity without loss of bioactive factors. In a representative study, thermoelectric freeze-casting was used to fabricate collagen/chitosan hybrid scaffolds that precisely formed longitudinally aligned microchannels via ice crystal templating. These scaffolds markedly enhanced mechanical performance and SC compatibility under physiological conditions, directly illustrating the singular engineering advantage of directional ice crystal growth in constructing anisotropic porous networks [30].

Microfluidic spinning and molding afford continuous fabrication of concentration-gradient microtubes and multilayer composites. They further permit exact replication of multi-channels or surface micropatterns, thereby realizing chemical or physical gradient biomimicry with inherent high throughput and gradient precision [31]. In a representative study, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT):PSS conductive microfibers produced via microfluidic spinning exhibited mechanical properties approaching those of natural fibroin alongside high conductivity. These fibers were subsequently assembled into NGCs that promoted axonal growth and SC migration, directly illustrating the defining characteristic of microfluidic continuous spinning in achieving micron-scale precise fiber diameter and functional compositing [32].

Self-assembly and hydrogel crosslinking utilize peptides, chitosan, or chiral molecules for spontaneous organization into 3D porous hydrogels. Photo- or enzyme-mediated crosslinking affords dynamic stiffness matching and sustained factor release. These approaches feature mild conditions, high biocompatibility, injectability, and facile hybridization into multi-modal scaffolds [33]. Self-assembling peptide nanofiber hydrogels loaded with laminin (LN)- and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)-mimicking epitopes spontaneously generated long nanofibers via β-sheet structures, synergistically enhancing axonal regeneration and functional recovery. This directly demonstrates the unique biocompatibility advantage of mild self-assembly in emulating ECM nanofibers at the molecular level [37]. Decellularization selectively removes cellular components while preserving native ECM constituents such as laminin and collagen IV, which are subsequently reconstituted into biomimetic conduits via integration with the aforementioned methods. This approach maximizes bioactivity yet necessitates optimization to balance immunogenicity [34]. NGCs fabricated by braiding decellularized porcine small intestinal submucosa achieved excellent biocompatibility while retaining robust mechanical properties, directly highlighting the core attribute of decellularization in completely preserving natural ECM components [38].

These fabrication modalities are not discrete but highly synergistic. Electrospinning establishes the nanoscale topographic foundation. 3D/4D bioprinting confers macro-to-micro hierarchical organization and dynamic responsiveness. Freeze-drying and microfluidics optimize porosity and gradients. Self-assembly furnishes molecular adaptability. Decellularization secures biological authenticity. Collectively, these approaches propel NGCs from static passive constructs toward intelligent 4D or closed-loop regenerative interfaces.

From a translational vantage point, fabrication technologies demand evaluation not merely on biomimetic verisimilitude but also on scalability and sterilization compatibility. Electrospinning adapts with relative ease to scale-up through multi-needle, needleless, or free-surface architectures, yet reproducible clinical manufacturing necessitates exacting batch-to-batch governance over fiber diameter, alignment, residual solvent, and payload uniformity [39]. By contrast, 3D and 4D bioprinting confer superior patient-specific resolution and multimaterial spatial orchestration; their clinical translation, however, remains contingent upon standardized bioink rheology, calibrated instrumentation, in-line quality surveillance, and good manufacturing practice (GMP)-aligned workflows to uphold construct fidelity [40]. Sterilization presents an equivalently formidable bottleneck. Conventional modalities, spanning moist heat, dry heat, ionizing radiation, ethylene oxide, vaporized hydrogen peroxide, and other low-temperature chemical regimens, differentially impact biodegradable polymers, electrospun nanofibers, decellularized matrices, and factor-laden formulations [41,42]. This concern intensifies for multimodal NGCs that amalgamate 3D bioprinting with decellularized ECM hydrogels, growth factors, viable cells, exosomes, or electrically active constituents, as terminal sterilization can jeopardize ECM ultrastructure, mechanical competence, degradation kinetics, conductivity, and the molecular bioactivity of ligands or trophic payloads. Accordingly, translational design should embrace a sterilization-by-design framework: structurally resilient components may be sterilized pre-assembly whenever feasible, while labile biological moieties are best incorporated aseptically at advanced stages, followed by rigorous verification of sterility assurance alongside the retention of architectural integrity, mechanical performance, release profiles, bioactivity, and electrophysiological functionality. For long-gap PNR, such multifaceted considerations will ultimately determine whether sophisticated biomimetic constructs can transcend bespoke preclinical prototypes and emerge as scalable, shelf-stable, and regulatory-ready clinical solutions.

Multi-dimensional biomimetic strategies: structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and cross-species biomimicry

Successful PNR is contingent upon a hierarchical convergence of instructive cues, comprising structural pathways that constrain axonal misrouting, bioactive microenvironments that potentiate SC and immunovascular cascades, time-resolved material behaviors that mirror the evolving repair milieu, and evolutionarily conserved design paradigms translatable from disparate biological systems. Clinically approved hollow NGCs remain largely confined to passive structural shielding and rudimentary spatial compartmentalization, and they consistently fall short of the simultaneous demands for vectorial guidance, niche restoration, and stage-matched regulation imperative for long-gap repair. A multidimensional biomimetic framework therefore mandates a functional division of labor across length scales: structural biomimicry engineers anisotropic tracks that shepherd axonal extension and SC migration; functional biomimicry reconstructs the regenerative niche through provision of ECM constituents, cellular signals, neurotrophic gradients, electroactive interfaces, and mechanical compliance; spatiotemporal biomimicry temporally orchestrates release, degradation, dynamic actuation, and scaffold deformation to match the sequential phases of repair; and cross-species biomimicry broadens the arsenal of manufacturable, reproducible, and minimally traumatic structural or interfacial solutions. The purposeful amalgamation of these biomimetic modalities must be calibrated to achieve defined regenerative endpoints, encompassing attenuation of axonal misdirection, augmentation of vascularization, perpetuation of the repair SC identity, immunomodulatory balance, and superior remyelination.

Structural biomimicry across hierarchical architectures from macro to nanoscale

Aligned with bibliometric trends and hotspots, structural biomimicry in neural tissue engineering centers on recapitulating the hierarchical and anisotropic physical architecture of native nerve tissue. This approach furnishes essential topographical cues that direct cellular behavior, guide axonal outgrowth, and ultimately facilitate robust structural repair alongside comprehensive functional restoration.

Macroscale emulation of connective tissues

At the macroscopic level, native peripheral nerves are hierarchically ensheathed and compartmentalized by three connective tissue layers: the epineurium, perineurium, and endoneurium [43]. The dense outer epineurium affords overall mechanical protection. The perineurium envelops individual fascicles and constitutes a pivotal constituent of the blood-nerve barrier. At a finer scale, the endoneurium aligns single axons and associated SCs into longitudinally oriented regenerative units, providing both structural shielding and topographical cues for axonal outgrowth.

Conventional NGCs fundamentally recapitulate the supportive and protective functions of the epineurium [44]. Simple hollow NGCs afford epineurium-like shielding by preventing adhesions, yet an overly large lumen permits dispersion of regenerating axons, culminating in misdirection [45]. Consequently, achieving precise PNR necessitates microstructural biomimicry that replicates the fascicular and endoneurial hierarchy. Introducing multichannel architectures to mimic the perineurium does not substantially diminish the total count of regenerating axons but effectively curtails axonal dispersion [46]. Pore dimensions must remain within a rational window. Pores exceeding approximately 450-500 μm fail to constrain dispersion, whereas pores smaller than about 85 μm can obstruct cell migration and nutrient diffusion [47,48]. Recent efforts refined perineurial biomimicry by engineering microscale fascicle-like architectures. A 3D-printed multichannel conduit featuring channels in the 200-300 μm range and permeable wall pores improved permeability, reduced deformation, and augmented sensory and motor recovery relative to hollow controls (Fig. 3A-B) [28]. Subsequent advances integrated multichannel compartmentalization with intraluminal anisotropic and gradient cues. A four-channel hydrogel conduit (channel diameter ∼400 μm) recapitulated fascicular partitioning. Directional freeze-casting generated longitudinally aligned honeycomb microstructures to emulate the anisotropic endoneurial matrix. This scaffold additionally incorporated axial conductivity and nerve growth factor (NGF) gradients, reconstituting combined topographical, galvanotactic, and chemotactic cues. Such hierarchical biomimicry promoted neurite alignment, SC adhesion, and migration. In vivo, it translated into enhanced remyelination, angiogenesis, reduced target-muscle atrophy, and functional recovery approaching that of autografts in the reported preclinical animal model (Fig. 3C) [49].Fig. 3Structural biomimicry in peripheral neural tissue engineering. Beyond fascicle-level guidance, refined biomimicry has targeted the endoneurium. Decellularized nerve matrix hydrogel scaffolds featuring longitudinally oriented, size-tunable microchannels were designed to recapitulate endoneurial tubes while retaining native ECM bioactivity. Axially aligned microchannels under 100 μm serve as endoneurium-like guidance units. Channels of 20-50 μm prove more effective at promoting directed neurite outgrowth and SC migration, whereas larger channels encourage axonal fasciculation but compromise orientational precision. In vivo evaluation confirmed that aligned microchannels drive directed axonal extension and myelination, culminating in superior functional recovery relative to microchannel-free controls [54]. Collectively, these findings advocate that biomimetic neural interfaces should progress from epineurium-like external protection to perineurium-like fascicular compartmentalization, and ultimately to endoneurium-like microscale guidance, thereby more faithfully recapitulating the hierarchical regenerative logic of native peripheral nerves [55].

Microscale replication of ECM topography

The most proximate cell-NGC interactions originate from microscale surface topography, which dictates how regenerating cells initially perceive the implanted interface. Biomimicry at this scale targets the native ECM, a hierarchically organized and dynamically remodeled porous 3D network comprising structural proteins, glycoproteins, proteoglycans, and matrix-bound signaling molecules. Rather than serving as a passive substrate, the ECM operates as an instructive niche that integrates topographical, biochemical, and mechanical cues to govern cell adhesion, migration, polarization, proliferation, and differentiation [56].

Replicating ECM topography on scaffold surfaces offers considerable promise for eliciting precise PNR. One straightforward strategy employs decellularized native tissue, already clinically deployed as U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved NGCs [57]. While autologous ECM constitutes the safest biomaterial, donor tissue availability remains severely constrained. Researchers therefore frequently employ ECM secreted by autologous cells expanded in vitro, or reconstitute xenogeneic decellularized nerve ECM into instructive microarchitectures. A representative example is a microfluidically spun microtube featuring a concentration gradient of decellularized porcine sciatic nerve matrix. This construct recapitulated the fibrous mesh-like organization of native nerve ECM while preserving key adhesive basement-membrane cues (laminin, collagen IV, fibronectin) and eliminating inhibitory components (myelin-associated glycoprotein, chondroitin sulfate). The gradient configuration outperformed uniform ECM presentation by directing neurite growth toward the higher-concentration side and facilitating SC infiltration, thereby yielding superior axonal alignment, enhanced remyelination and angiogenesis, and functional recovery comparable to autografts in a rat sciatic nerve defect model (Fig. 3D-E) [50].

Beyond tissue-derived matrices, synthetic polymers can emulate ECM topography via engineering dynamic 3D pore interconnectivity. A chitosan conduit incorporating multifunctional gelatin microcapsules generated an ECM-like interconnected porous network while sustaining insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) presentation. Low- and high-crosslinked microcapsules respectively promoted inter-pore communication and prolonged IGF-1 release. This design augmented SC survival, proliferation, migration, and remyelination, while concurrently facilitating early macrophage recruitment and M2 polarization, thereby enhancing axonal regeneration and motor recovery to near-autograft levels (Fig. 3F) [51]. Nanofibers can likewise emulate the anisotropic architecture of native ECM collagen fibrils (diameters typically 51-100 nm in peripheral nerve connective tissues) [58]. Aligned nanofibers fabricated within this nanoscale regime faithfully replicate native fibrillar ECM and modulate neural cell behavior via contact guidance [59]. Regenerative efficacy of nanofibrous scaffolds hinges on the coupled effects of fiber alignment, diameter, and other parameters (Table 3). Alignment constitutes the most reproducible pro-regenerative cue, whereas diameter influence remains context-dependent. Highly aligned polycaprolactone (PCL) fiber conduits fabricated via one-piece electrospinning attain exceptional alignment. This topography recapitulates the anisotropic ECM architecture, enabling contact-guided uniaxial SC elongation and directional axonal outgrowth, thus mediating functional regeneration comparable to autografts in a rat sciatic nerve defect model [60]. Within such grafts, SCs elongate uniaxially along fibers, forming tracks reminiscent of bands of Büngner [61]. Molecular chiral regulation in neural tissue engineering

Numerous endogenous biomacromolecules within peripheral nerve ECM, including collagen and laminin fibers, possess intrinsic chirality at mesoscopic resolutions. Their stereochemical ordering directs supramolecular assembly and molecular recognition events, thereby orchestrating interactions among proteins, cognate receptors, and cellular effectors [65]. Chiral cues modulate protein folding, receptor engagement, integrin spatial disposition, and cytoskeletal polarization, with downstream consequences for adhesion, motility, phenotypic activation, and differentiation trajectories [52]. Engineering an ECM-mimetic chiral environment thus presents a compelling avenue for establishing a more instructive regenerative niche in PNR, particularly for guiding SC behavioral programming and axonal trajectory determination.

Recent applications of chiral principles to tissue engineering have proceeded via biomimetic design paradigms. Chitosan-gelatin conduits incorporating supramolecular matrices of L- or D-phenylalanine derivatives yielded 3D porous hydrogels defined by counter-rotating helical nanofibers. The right-handed L-Phe variant replicated the chiral fibrillar context of endogenous ECM with heightened authenticity. Chirality thus emerged as an essential design consideration: the hydrogel provided mechanically permissive conditions for cellular proliferation while maintaining sufficient longevity to accommodate SC transit without axonal impedance. Notably, L-Phe manifested greater stereoselective engagement with collagen IV than its D-Phe or achiral R-Phe counterparts. This chirality-governed interaction potentiated collagen IV-integrin α1β1 transduction, preserving repair SC stemness, encouraging bipolar elongation, enhancing neuritic extension, and elevating pro-regenerative gene expression. Systemic outcomes included improved axonal regeneration, remyelination, and nerve conduction restoration showed autograft-comparable functional metrics under the reported preclinical experimental conditions (Fig. 3G) [52]. Separate investigations corroborated that L-chiral molybdenum disulfide constructs surpassed D-chiral iterations in driving SC proliferation and functional recuperation [66].

Within a graphene oxide (GO)-doped phenylalanine hydrogel framework, chiral efficacy hinged upon its conductive coupling with GO, eclipsing the influence of chirality in isolation. Dextro and levo variants were meticulously engineered with matched physicochemical signatures, enabling biological disparities to be confidently traced to stereochemical origin. Judicious GO incorporation attenuated impedance yet preserved cytocompatibility, conferring unequivocal superiority upon the dextro hydrogel relative to the levo counterpart (Fig. 3H). Functionally recapitulating a conductive neural niche, the construct promoted cellular accommodation, skewed macrophages toward an M2 phenotype, expedited SC translocation, and orchestrated vascular realignment alongside enhanced myelination. Consequent functional recovery metrics rivaled those of autografts in the specific rat sciatic nerve defect model [53]. Synthesizing these observations, chirality transcends a static binary preference, operating instead as a tunable vector whose regenerative output remains inextricably tied to nuanced interplay with matrix persistence, protein affinity, and conductive phase distribution.

Although structural biomimicry has generated promising preclinical efficacy, often rivaling autograft performance in short to medium gap scenarios, static topographical instruction falls short when confronting extensive defects. Endogenous PNR depends upon a continuum of physical guidance orchestrated alongside biochemical, cellular, and physicochemical inputs within a perpetually remodeling milieu. Consequently, functional biomimicry has ascended as a pivotal complementary dimension, reorienting the paradigm from inert structural templates toward bioactive constructs that dynamically re-establish the regenerative microenvironment.

The core of structural biomimicry: Efficient reconstruction of regenerative logic

Structural biomimicry derives its value not from mere duplication of native architecture but from the capacity to recapitulate the hierarchical regenerative program intrinsic to peripheral nerves. This program necessitates precise coordination of SC orientation, axonal pathfinding, and efficient long-distance propagation. Contemporary research, however, exhibits a marked predilection for morphological facsimile, exemplified by multichannel constructs or aligned nanofibers, yet seldom interrogates with quantitative rigor whether these configurations translate into substantive biological refinements in SC alignment fidelity, axonal guidance precision, or sustained trophic support across clinically meaningful gaps.

A paradigm shift toward quantitative optimization of hierarchical guidance efficiency is therefore imperative. Critical performance indices encompass axonal misdirection rates, channel occupancy fractions, remyelination homogeneity, and vascular co-alignment fidelity. Retrograde labeling analyses reveal that even sophisticated multichannel conduits inadequately suppress misrouting unless such efficiency parameters are deliberately engineered and empirically validated. Longitudinal microchannels consistently attenuate dispersion relative to random or hollow configurations, yet suboptimal diameter or interconnectivity still permits 20-40% axonal deviation in extended gap models [67]. Concurrently, hierarchical topographies integrating micro- and nanoscale cues potentiate SC bipolar elongation and contact-guided translocation via Yes-associated protein (YAP)-mediated mechanotransduction, thereby augmenting channel utilization and remyelination uniformity independent of supplementary biochemical loading [68].

Collectively, these observations necessitate an evolution from descriptive morphological replication toward predictive, efficiency-centric engineering. Prioritizing low-complexity architectures optimized for quantifiable regenerative nodes will enable next-generation NGCs to secure enhanced long-distance fidelity, seamless integration with spatiotemporal modalities, and a narrowed translational disparity relative to autografts.

Functional biomimicry across multidimensional regenerative microenvironments

The functional regenerative milieu of peripheral nerves constitutes a complex, dynamic system of cellular components, signaling molecules, and physicochemical properties that synergistically govern the neural repair cascade [69]. Within neural tissue engineering, functional biomimetic strategies aim to reconstruct this multidimensional microenvironment through integrating biological and physicochemical cues [70]. This paradigm shift provides a pivotal avenue for modulating cellular behavior and unleashing the inherent regenerative capacity of injured nerves.

Biomimicry of functional components and ECM signaling molecules

The ECM serves as the central functional regulator of the peripheral nerve regenerative milieu. Functioning as an instructive biological interface rather than a passive scaffold, it governs cell-matrix communication during PNR. Through adhesive motifs, spatiotemporal neurotrophic release, and receptor-mediated signaling, the ECM dynamically modulates SC behavior, axonal extension, remyelination, and tissue remodeling [71]. ECM biomimicry resides not in replicating molecular composition alone, but in reconstituting dynamic signaling and regulatory functions [72]. Current strategies for functional ECM biomimicry bifurcate into two directions: incorporating natural ECM-derived components to preserve native bioactivity, or engineering synthetic or modified biomaterials to emulate selected ECM-associated signaling functions [73].

Concerning natural ECM components, structural proteins (laminin, fibronectin, collagen), glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and neurotrophic factors are extensively employed (Fig. 4A–B) [74,75]. ECM proteins furnish adhesion sites and govern SC proliferation and migration [79]. In a 3D comparison, hollow channels within type I collagen hydrogels recapitulated the axonal niche; ECM coating density proved a critical determinant. Fibronectin surpassed laminin and collagen IV, generating the longest axonal extension and most robust SC migration [80]. GAG supplementation elevates myelin protein mRNA and correlates with augmented regenerated axon counts and larger fiber diameters [81]. Decellularized ECM (dECM) preserves native composition and architecture. Human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell (BMSC)-derived dECM, containing fibronectin, laminin, and collagen I, was integrated into a chitin-based scaffold, conferring nutritional support and topographical guidance. This construct attained sensory, motor, and electrophysiological recovery comparable to autografts in a rat sciatic defect model (Fig. 4C–D) [76]. Nevertheless, dECM application confronts a central paradox: balancing maximal preservation of native components against maximal elimination of cellular material [82]. Excessively gentle decellularization invites immunogenicity; aggressive processing may compromise structure or inactivate active components [83]. Biomimicry of cellular components by introducing key actors

The PNR microenvironment consists of multiple components that coordinate repair, with cells serving as the primary functional units. Following PNI, five key cell populations (neurons, SCs, macrophages, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells) assume critical roles. Cellular biomimicry constitutes a pivotal strategy in neural tissue engineering, aimed at offsetting the deficiency of biochemical signals within material-based conduits by introducing cells or their functional derivatives, thereby recapitulating the native regenerative milieu [87]. This strategy primarily encompasses two approaches: direct implantation of seed cells and utilization of cell-derived exosomes to deliver bioactive signals [88]. Importantly, these cellular or exosomal components are not deployed in isolation; they function exclusively as bioactive payloads embedded within a biomimetic scaffold, which serves as the indispensable structural backbone enabling sustained cell retention, controlled signal release, and guided tissue integration.

Implanting supportive cell populations

Within PNR, multiple interdependent cell populations orchestrate a precisely coordinated repair cascade. Neurons extend regenerating axons, whereas SCs function as master regulators. Following PNI, mature SCs dedifferentiate into a repair phenotype, clear myelin debris, secrete neurotrophic factors, recruit macrophages, and align into Büngner bands to furnish guidance and trophic support. Integrating SCs into biomimetic conduits has been extensively explored [89]. A chitosan/collagen conduit encapsulating SCs promoted axonal regeneration and myelination, achieving superior functional recovery in the reported rat model [90]. Modified cells can further augment biomimetic capabilities. A dual-layer poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA)/GelMA conduit encapsulating vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A)-transfected SCs recapitulated early pro-angiogenic support, enhancing angiogenesis and neurite extension, and yielding motor recovery approaching that of autografts under preclinical conditions (Fig. 5A) [91]. Commonly used seed cells in neural tissue engineering.

Co-loading diverse cell types recapitulates the regenerative microenvironment through cell-cell synergy. In a recurrent laryngeal nerve defect model, a laminin-chitosan-PLGA conduit with Matrigel-based co-suspension of SCs and neural stem cells (NSCs) enhanced NSC neuronal differentiation, upregulated BDNF and GDNF expression, and yielded more mature myelin than single-cell controls. These findings underscore rational multicellular composition as a pivotal biomimetic parameter [108].

Two principal strategies fabricate cell-laden scaffolds: direct bioprinting (encapsulating cells within bioinks) and post-printing seeding (introducing cells onto prefabricated scaffolds) [109]. Direct bioprinting affords precise spatial control and homogeneous distribution yet risks damaging sensitive neural cells via shear forces and crosslinking stress [110]. Post-printing seeding circumvents fabrication-induced stresses, permitting precise cell positioning. Perfusion bioreactors facilitate uniform 3D infiltration essential for long-segment repair [104], albeit at the cost of increased manufacturing complexity and time.

Exosomes for harnessing cell-derived nanosystems

Exosomes harbor diverse bioactive molecules and function as key intercellular communication vectors via biochemical signal transfer [111]. Their contributions to PNR encompass myelination promotion, axonal regeneration, angiogenesis, and inflammatory modulation. Relative to seed cell implantation, exosomes exhibit lower immunogenicity and are amenable to engineering for targeted delivery [112]. Exosomes facilitate axonal regeneration and myelination in part through modulating SC proliferation, migration, repair-phenotype activation, and trophic factor secretion [118]. Adipose-derived MSC exosomes (ADSC-Exos) internalized by SCs regulate gene expression, suppress apoptosis, and enhance proliferation [121]. BMSC exosomes incorporated into conductive nerve dressings promote SC attachment and migration (Fig. 5C) [93]. Recognizing the essential role of vascular supply, angiogenesis constitutes a critical determinant of PNR [9]. Exosome engineering enables reconstruction of the early vascular niche. Netrin-1-engineered endothelial cell exosomes (NTN1 EC-EXO) were devised to recreate a pre-regenerative neurovascular niche. NTN1, a pivotal vascular niche regulator upregulated following injury, defines a pro-regenerative endothelial subpopulation. NTN1 engineering augmented endothelial proliferation, migration, tube formation, exosomal neuronal affinity, SC proliferation and migration, and axonal outgrowth. Consequently, NTN1 emerged as a decisive biomimetic parameter that converts endothelial exosomes into niche-constructing nanosystems. Local administration of NTN1-driven exosomes amplified angiogenesis, remyelination, axonal regeneration, functional reinnervation, and mitigated muscle atrophy [122].

Inflammation profoundly influences PNR prognosis [123]. Exosomes modulate immune responses by driving macrophage M1-to-M2 polarization, curbing excessive inflammation, and alleviating pain [124]. OTULIN within SC-derived exosomes governs macrophage phenotypic switching via ERBB2 deubiquitination. Local administration of SC-Exos enhanced M2 polarization, functional restoration, axonal regeneration, and remyelination [125]. Furthermore, umbilical cord MSC exosomes diminish pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), thereby augmenting SC proliferation, myelinated axon counts, and motor recovery [126].

Embedding exosomes within NGCs or hydrogels for local sustained release recapitulates endogenous intercellular communication, circumvents immunogenicity and tumorigenic risks, and provides a safer cell-free platform for PNR [127].

Translational risk–benefit comparison of cell-based and acellular strategies

Although cell-loaded scaffolds and exosome delivery converge on reconstructing the regenerative niche of peripheral nerves, their translational trajectories diverge profoundly. Cell-based strategies, particularly those deploying SCs or Schwann-like derivatives, most faithfully emulate the intrinsic repair cascade by directly furnishing myelination, axonal support, and neurotrophic secretion [89]. Clinical translation remains encumbered, however, by stringent cell sourcing, protracted ex vivo amplification, batch-to-batch variability, cryopreservation, post-graft viability, immunocompatibility, and tumorigenic liability. Autologous SCs pose lower immunologic risk yet invoke invasive donor-nerve harvest and prolonged expansion [97], stem cell-derived Schwann-like cells offer greater scalability but demand exacting governance over differentiation fidelity and residual undifferentiated populations [128].

Exosomes, in contradistinction, function as cell-derived yet acellular nanoscale signaling modules that partially conserve the paracrine secretome of their parent cells while obviating the perils of live-cell implantation, including aberrant proliferation, microvascular occlusion, and tumorigenesis [129]; they also impart superior cryopreservability and standardization potential [117]. Exosome-based therapeutics nonetheless remain subject to persistent challenges surrounding donor-cell variability, isolation and purification benchmarks, cargo heterogeneity, dose definition, potency metrics, and long-term biosafety validation [130].

From a translational vantage point, future strategies need not be circumscribed by a rigid cellular-acellular dichotomy. Rather, lower-risk hybrid formulations, epitomized by acellular structural platforms integrated with controlled exosome or factor delivery, or carefully indicated cell-augmented constructs, may optimally harmonize regenerative potency, biosafety, and scalable manufacturability. Biomimicry of physical properties through engineering the niche's physical language

Complementing biochemical and cellular cues, functional biomimicry also harnesses the physical lexicon of the native regenerative milieu. Through precise tailoring of scaffold electrical and mechanical attributes, this approach recapitulates the sophisticated physicochemical microenvironment of peripheral nerves. Consequently, it furnishes dynamic, context-adaptive support that aligns with the evolving regenerative cascade, from axonal guidance to late-stage remyelination and functional restoration.

Electrophysiological biomimicry for reconstructing the bioelectric microenvironment

Within the native neural milieu, bioelectric activity is organized around stable transmembrane potentials and rapid impulse propagation. Neuronal membranes maintain a resting potential; upon reaching threshold, action potentials depolarize followed by repolarization. In myelinated peripheral axons, impulses regenerate at nodes of Ranvier and propagate via saltatory conduction, markedly enhancing transmission efficiency. Endogenous bioelectric signals transcend mere fundamentals of peripheral nerve function. Substantial evidence confirms that electrical signals govern the metabolism, adhesion, proliferation, migration, and differentiation of excitable cells including neurons [131]. PNI disrupts this ordered electrical continuity, compromising signal transmission and attenuating the electrophysiological support essential for efficient PNR.

Optimal NGCs must display electrical activity replicating that of native nerve tissue. Prevailing sources of biomimetic electrical signals encompass exogenous stimulation and self-powered constructs. Early electrical stimulation typically necessitated open wounds or percutaneous wires, elevating infection risk [111]. Recently, non-invasive modalities have emerged as preferred. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation accelerates healing, fosters axonal myelination, and enhances neuromuscular morphology and electromyographic activity [132].

Self-powered systems constitute an alternative strategy, typically leveraging piezoelectric materials or implanted power sources [133]. Endogenous piezoelectric effects occur within the human body. Mechanosensitive ion channels transduce mechanical forces into potential shifts [134]; these potentials activate voltage-gated channels, serving pivotal roles in excitable cells [135]. Piezoelectric materials generate electrical potential under external mechanical compression [136], enabling non-invasive electrical stimulation for tissue regeneration. Highly aligned fibers can orient the electric field, facilitating precise PNR [137]. A multichannel electroactive NGC integrating an inner cryogel scaffold with in situ polymerized conductive polymer and an outer piezoelectric film recapitulated fascicle-like compartmentalization and the native electrophysiological microenvironment. Here, dual electrical activity (conductivity and piezoelectricity) emerged as the decisive biomimetic parameter. This NGC enhanced SC proliferation, maturation, myelination, and axonal regeneration, achieving nerve recovery approaching autograft performance in a rat sciatic defect model (Fig. 5E) [95].

Toward more precise control, conduits that elicit piezoelectric responses via non-invasive modalities such as ultrasound are under development [138]. An ultrasound-responsive acousto-piezoelectric NGC (APNF-NGC), fabricated from electrospun PLLA/PEG nanofibers, couples electrophysiological biomimicry with topographical guidance. This NGC recapitulates aligned anisotropic topography and fascicular organization via a four-channel configuration. PEG blending tunes piezoelectric properties, with an optimal formulation balancing stiffness and structural integrity. Under ultrasound stimulation, the conduit generates an axially oriented electric field that activates Ca2+-dependent cyclic adenosine monophosphate/protein kinase A (cAMP/PKA) signaling in neurons and SCs, consequently enhancing axonal elongation, SC proliferation, migration, myelination, and neurotrophic factor expression. In a rat sciatic defect model, implantation coupled with ultrasound stimulation yields functional outcomes comparable to autografts (Fig. 5D) [94].

Conventional implanted power sources suffer from biocompatibility and non-degradability limitations. Recent biodegradable materials have rendered battery-integrated conduits feasible, utilizing degradable metals such as magnesium or zinc as electrodes and modifying conduit conductivity to sustain power generation [94,139]. Recapitulating native nerve electrical transmission holds substantial promise for promoting neuronal differentiation and axonal regeneration (Table 7). Injury, however, measurably augments this niche's stiffness, correlating with elevated collagen I deposition and fibrotic remodeling [148]. This stiffness milieu is profoundly instructive for SCs, which exhibit durotaxis and directionally migrate along stiffness gradients [152]. Stiffness exerts phase-dependent effects: compliant matrices favor a repair-like dedifferentiated state, whereas stiffer matrices promote redifferentiation and myelination competence, and intermediate regimes sustain elongation and proliferation [68]. Excessive matrix stiffening activates RhoA and YAP/TAZ signaling while repressing regenerative markers, thus implying that stiffness matched to the appropriate regenerative phase is optimal [153].

A representative illustration of stiffness-directed biomimicry is a 3D-bioprinted NGC precisely tuned to a compliant regime. Stiffness itself emerged as the decisive parameter. The soft matrix recapitulated an early pro-regenerative niche, facilitated MSC transdifferentiation into Schwann-like cells via the PIEZO1/YAP axis, and augmented neurotrophic factor expression. In vivo, this NGC enhanced sensory and motor recovery in a rat sciatic defect model (Fig. 5F) [96]. A subsequent study demonstrated that stiffness biomimicry can indirectly regulate repair through release kinetics. Within a hydrogel loaded with MSC-derived exosomes, a softer matrix accelerated exosome liberation, suppressed macrophage infiltration, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, and improved PNR [154]. Consequently, stiffness matching governs functional regeneration by directly modulating cell phenotype as well as by tuning payload release kinetics and immune remodeling.

Mechanical biomimicry must evolve from generic stimulation toward precise recapitulation of the injury-adaptive stiffness milieu. Matching native nerve compliance, harnessing instructive gradients, and circumventing fibrotic over-stiffening furnish a mechanobiological pathway to govern SC plasticity, axonal extension, and remyelination. Most current scaffolds afford static attributes, whereas the in vivo niche remains continuously dynamic. Future NGCs should integrate programmable stiffness adaptability, transitioning from early repair support to later maturation in synchrony with the healing cascade.

Capturing key regulatory nodes for low-complexity mechanistic reconstruction

The genuine innovation in functional biomimicry resides not in the indiscriminate assembly of multiple factors, cells, exosomes, or conductive components, but in whether the design precisely intercepts the decisive regulatory nodes within the regenerative cascade. Contemporary studies frequently adopt an additive paradigm, simultaneously incorporating numerous bioactive elements into scaffolds. However, escalating component count does not inherently translate into superior biomimicry; instead, it may introduce superfluous complexity, immunogenicity, and regulatory dysregulation. Future functional biomimicry should therefore originate from core cellular state transitions and prioritize precise, node-targeted design. Principal targets encompass sustaining the repair SC phenotype, temporally calibrating macrophage polarization, orchestrating neurovascular synergy, and scheduling remyelination. By discerning the truly decisive minority of variables within the regenerative cascade and emphasizing low-complexity mechanistic reconstitution, biomimetic strategies can reshape the regenerative microenvironment in a more parsimonious, efficacious, and clinically translatable manner while mitigating off-target effects.

Emerging literature corroborates this perspective. Chiral hydrogel-based NGCs achieve precision maintenance of repair SC stemness via stereoselective collagen IV-integrin interactions and c-Jun pathway activation, robustly promoting axonal regeneration and myelination without exogenous cell loading [68]. Nano-immunomodulators afford precise temporal control over the post-injury macrophage M1-to-M2 polarization window while concurrently reinforcing neurovascular synergy, consequently attenuating fibrosis and expediting functional recovery in long-gap defects [155]. These targeted interventions illustrate that concentrating on a select few high-impact nodes provides a more forward-looking and reproducible paradigm for engineering next-generation functional biomimetic NGCs.

Spatiotemporal biomimicry of the PNR process for stage-specific repair

Conventional static NGCs frequently yield inadequate outcomes in long-gap or complex nerve injuries, as they construe regeneration as a monolithic physical event while disregarding its inherently dynamic, time-dependent continuum [156]. Spatiotemporal biomimicry, which integrates temporal programmability into scaffold architecture, represents a pivotal advancement providing a potential strategy for next-generation NGCs to surpass the autograft gold standard. By precisely synchronizing with the distinct pathophysiological phases of adult PNR and reactivating efficient embryonic developmental programs, these adaptive systems orchestrate the complete regenerative cascade, thereby overcoming the limitations of static NGCs that prevent autografts from achieving consistent full functional restoration in complex cases. This advanced paradigm employs temporally programmable materials to recapitulate embryonic programs while synchronizing with adult PNR phases, thus orchestrating the regenerative cascade in its full complexity.

Reactivating embryonic programs as the underlying blueprint of regeneration

Neural tissue expands rapidly and in a highly orchestrated manner during embryogenesis, enabling swift morphological and functional restitution after injury [157]. Neurogenesis wanes in adulthood, and the regenerative capacity of the nervous system becomes severely constrained [158]. Achieving outcomes comparable to autografts remains challenging even with tissue-engineered conduits that accelerate repair. Numerous features of nerve regeneration recapitulate core aspects of neural development. SCs dedifferentiate into repair SCs, reverting to an embryonic-like stemness state [159]. These cells direct regenerating axons and assemble into aligned cords reminiscent of embryonic SC precursors [160]. Other pivotal cell types within the regenerative milieu, including fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and macrophages, likewise share parallels with developmental paradigms [161]. Reconstructing these developmental cues in the injured adult nerve represents a propitious strategy to potentiate pro-regenerative cellular responses.

Building on this developmental-regenerative continuum, process biomimicry seeks to reactivate embryonic programs within the adult injury niche rather than simply offering a passive conduit [162]. A representative tissue-engineered nerve graft (TENG) was fabricated using long, aligned, centimeter-scale living axon tracts generated via stretch-growth and stabilized within a collagenous matrix. This system recapitulates the developmental function of pioneer axons, which first establish optimal growth trajectories and subsequently furnish structural guidance for follower axons. Regenerating host axons extend directly along the engineered tracts through axon-facilitated axon regeneration, while concurrently promoting SC infiltration, migration, and alignment into bands of Büngner-like structures (Fig. 6A). A collagen NGC featuring a development-inspired porous wall microstructure induced peripheral nerve morphogenesis in a rat sciatic transection model. The NGC was progressively supplanted by normal nerve tissue [165].

Beyond structural guidance, reactivation of embryonic programs may be accomplished through recapitulation of developmental bioelectrical and paracrine signaling. Inspired by embryonic neural development, a self-evolving tissue engineering scaffold was devised to replicate the electrical milieu and paracrine signaling characteristic of embryogenesis. MSCs were initially seeded onto a piezoelectric PLLA fiber membrane, followed by integration of an ECM-mimetic hydrogel. Upon ultrasound-induced excitation, the piezoelectric potential generated on the PLLA membrane surface gave rise to an electric field emulating the endogenous bioelectrical environment of embryonic development. This electrical stimulation potentiated MSC differentiation and amplified their paracrine activity, which in turn provoked recruitment and differentiation of endogenous stem cells (Fig. 6B). Ultimately, this process fostered successful axonal growth and facilitated efficient PNR [164].

Phase-specific synchronization with the regenerative continuum

Upon injury, proximal and distal stumps execute distinct yet coordinated transformations. The proximal stump retracts and initiates retrograde neuronal responses, encompassing chromatolysis and perikaryal remodeling. The distal stump undergoes Wallerian degeneration characterized by axonal swelling, cytoskeletal disintegration, and myelin dismantling [7,166]. SCs reprogram into a reparative phenotype, recruiting macrophages for debris clearance [167]. Fibroblasts direct migration, expedite clearance, and secrete neurotrophic factors [168]. Proximal to the lesion, SCs assemble into cellular cords that guide axons; distal SCs form Büngner bands; terminal SCs direct axons toward targets [169]. Hypoxia instigates a pro-repair macrophage phenotype and sustains vascular networks [170]. Neurons elaborate growth cones, extend axons along SC and vascular trajectories, and re-establish target connections [7]. Thereafter, repair SCs redifferentiate into myelinating or non-myelinating states to restore saltatory conduction [160].

Disruption at any stage undermines recovery. Excessive fibroblast activation elicits fibrosis; errant axonal growth induces inappropriate reinnervation [171,172]. Potent biomimetic strategies must align with regenerative phases. Early maneuvers should prioritize SC activation, immune modulation, and debris clearance. Upon axonal extension, the emphasis transitions to recapitulating native guidance cues. Following regeneration, the focus shifts toward pro-myelination signals. These imperatives necessitate NGCs with temporally programmable adaptability, encompassing tunable topography, conductivity, or release kinetics.

Advanced stimuli-responsive scaffolds fulfill this imperative. A 4D oriented dynamic scaffold constructed from shape memory polymer (SMP) furnished spacious microgrooves for early adhesion and proliferation, subsequently transitioning to narrow grooves at a predefined temperature to potentiate maturation and oriented extension. This dynamic NGC accelerated restitution of large segmental defects and attained autograft-comparable efficacy in the reported animal model (Fig. 7A) [173]. A second 4D-printed MXene/poly(L-lactide-co-trimethylene carbonate) (PLATMC) conduit underwent autonomous curling at physiological temperature to enable early adaptive wrapping, while aligned microchannels and conductivity established a dynamic regenerative niche. Beyond dynamic construction, temporally programmed molecular delivery accommodates shifting phase demands. The pro-regenerative potency of NGF and GDNF depended upon dosage, synergistic interplay, and temporal orchestration. Low-dose co-administration recapitulated physiological post-injury kinetics (early elevation followed by a delayed peak) and surpassed burst release. NGF/GDNF-loaded PLGA microparticles embedded within a collagen/hyaluronic acid conduit effected biphasic release, thereby augmenting neurite outgrowth, SC metabolic activity, and functional recovery approximating autograft performance (Fig. 7C) [175]. An electrically conductive, aligned polyurethane conduit biased early macrophage infiltration toward the M2 phenotype and subsequently facilitated SC migration and myelination, culminating in effective remyelination and functional restoration [178]. Additional investigations employed ultrasound or near-infrared light to trigger growth factor release or hydrogen sulfide generation, thereby promoting angiogenesis, attenuating oxidative stress, and enhancing axonal regeneration (Fig. 7D-E) [176,177].

Beyond dynamic structural design and temporally programmed molecular delivery, scaffold degradation kinetics emerge as a pivotal temporal regulatory lever in long-gap nerve repair. Human peripheral axons regenerate at an inherently slow pace (∼1 mm/day), obligating clinically significant long-gap defects to traverse weeks to months of axonal crossing before vascularization, remyelination, and target reinnervation can be accomplished [179]. NGCs must therefore strike a delicate balance: premature resorption risks luminal collapse, fibrotic ingrowth, and abrogation of directional guidance, whereas excessive persistence may incite foreign-body reactions, fibrotic encapsulation, or sustained neural compression. Modern spatiotemporally programmed constructs provide several implementable strategies for calibrating degradation. A prototypical “slow-degrading outer shell plus fast-degrading inner matrix” architecture permits outer wall materials, such as PCL, PLCL, or silk fibroin, to maintain anti-collapse support during early-to-intermediate repair, while inner constituents, including collagen, GelMA, or hyaluronic acid, degrade earlier to generate permissive channels for Schwann cell migration, vascular ingrowth, and axonal extension. Degradation kinetics can be further tuned by adjusting the lactide/glycolide ratio of PLGA, the molecular weight and crystallinity of PCL-based polymers, or the crosslinking density of collagen/GelMA hydrogels [[180], [181], [182], [183]]. Spatiotemporal biomimicry thus elevates PNR to a temporally orchestrated regenerative cascade, reinforcing the imperative for adaptive conduits that faithfully synchronize with the evolving biological mandates of nerve repair.

Cross-species biomimicry for leveraging evolutionary blueprints in neural regeneration

Nature's evolutionarily refined architectures furnish rich design repertoires for neural engineering. Cross-species biomimicry transcends mammalian tissue constraints, affording access to superior structural and functional diversity [184]. This domain has advanced from rudimentary structural replication toward rigorously engineerable, highly modular design paradigms. These paradigms encompass micro- and nanoscale topographical contact guidance via naturally occurring microgrooves, microchannels, or concavities that drive aligned SC and axonal organization, thereby forestalling dispersion and misdirection. They additionally encompass anisotropic hierarchical architectures inspired by longitudinal microchannel networks within plant or animal stems and tubular tissues, augmented with mechanical strength, permeability, and collapse resistance to enable multi-scale compartmentalization and nutrient diffusion. Another paradigm entails biofunctional property integration via piezoelectricity, conductivity, or electroactivity, which synchronously delivers galvanotactic or mechano-electric signals to potentiate SC migration, axonal extension, and remyelination. A fourth paradigm involves dynamic adaptive biointerfaces endowed with responsive self-curling, bioadhesive, or microneedle anchoring mechanisms that facilitate intraoperative conformal attachment, mitigate secondary injury, and prolong growth factor retention [55,[185], [186], [187]].

These design rules directly tackle key PNR clinical challenges: long gaps (>3 cm) with few autograft donors and donor site problems, plus regular tubes that often cause axon spreading, slow healing, poor recovery, and muscle wasting. By fixing wrong turns, boosting repair speed, adding natural electric signals, and improving surgical fit with immune balance, they turn biomimetic NGCs from passive wraps into active repair environments that match or beat autografts in animal tests, while giving scalable, repeatable paths toward clinical use.

The faithful reproduction of oriented topographies, such as grooves and channels, now represents a mainstream strategy in peripheral nerve repair. Morpho menelaus (M.m.) wings are endowed with intrinsic micro- and nanogrooves, whereas Papilio ulysses telegonus (P.u.t.) wings display micro- and nanoconcavities. SCs seeded on P.u.t. substrates adopted a disorganized arrangement, while those on M.m. surfaces aligned strictly with the groove direction, unambiguously demonstrating contact guidance. Integration of M.m. wings into silicone tubes robustly augmented sciatic nerve regeneration in rats (Fig. 8A-B) [185]. Emulating the redwood trunk's hierarchical architecture, a composite NGC was constructed, uniting a thermoresponsive shape-memory outer sheath with an internally aligned multichannel fiber scaffold that was further nanotextured. The rugged, anisotropic xylem-like microchannel network emerged as the dominant biomimetic motif, markedly potentiating SC infiltration, elongation, migration, and neuronal differentiation, and thereby accelerating sciatic defect repair and motor recovery [55]. In a parallel cross-species design, the anisotropic vasculature of fern stems inspired a biomimetic electroactive cellulose hydrogel conduit that retained innate longitudinal microchannels while acquiring electrical conductivity, surface hydrophilicity, and structural collapse resistance. These integrated properties directed neurite alignment, boosted axonal regeneration and remyelination, and ultimately yielded autograft-equivalent functional restoration in a sciatic defect model [189]. Critically, peripheral nerves inhabit a mechanically compliant niche; juxtaposing rigid xylem-mimetic frameworks against soft nerve stumps can provoke localized stress concentration, frictional injury, foreign-body reactivity, and fibrotic encapsulation. To defuse this biomechanical mismatch, researchers routinely use the longitudinal microchannel patterns of redwood xylem or fern stems as conceptual blueprints rather than implanting native rigid plant tissues, and then faithfully recreate their guidance topology with compliant materials such as PLATMC, PCL/polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), cellulose hydrogels, or collagen hydrogels. Further interfacial protection is afforded by equipping plant-inspired scaffolds with elastic outer casings, soft hydrogel linings, or lubricious surface layers, ensuring that nerve-contacting territories remain mechanically gentle; meanwhile, hydrogel concentration, crosslinking density, porosity, and wall thickness can be systematically tuned to strike a balance between low-modulus conformability and anti-collapse mechanical support [190]. Investigators have additionally integrated structural and functional motifs from evolutionarily disparate organisms. Inspired by bamboo, a composite conduit featuring a directional hollow-tube bilayer architecture and cellulose-mimetic piezoelectricity augmented SC neurotrophic activity, axonal elongation, myelination, and angiogenesis, achieving outcomes approximating autografts (Fig. 8D) [188]. Drawing from sea cucumbers, a biomimetic microneedle NGC (MNGC) was engineered with exterior microneedles for electrical stimulation and muscle anchoring, alongside interior longitudinal microchannels to facilitate SC migration. The conductive and piezoelectric matrix attenuated muscle atrophy and fostered myelin regeneration, axonal growth, and functional restoration (Fig. 8C) [186]. Inspired by electric eels, a self-powered ionic hydrogel battery was fabricated from chitosan, chondroitin sulfate, and hydroxyethyl cellulose via layer-by-layer assembly that recapitulates the multilayered electrocyte architecture. This fully biodegradable conduit generates endogenous electrical stimulation through ionic gradients, synergistically optimizing mechanical properties and conductivity to promote axonal regeneration, myelination, and functional recovery in PNI models [191].

Cross-species biomimicry need not directly replicate biological architectures; it may instead extract adaptive physical principles. Inspired by Mimosa pudica curling, a self-curling bioadhesive tape hydrogel was fabricated using a chitosan-based interpenetrating network featuring a glutaraldehyde crosslinking gradient. Upon contact with wet surfaces, water redistribution drives conformal wrapping; subsequent physical adhesion followed by covalent bonding stabilizes the interface and prolongs IGF-1 retention. This dynamic biointerface mitigated secondary injury and inflammation while concurrently promoting axonal regeneration, remyelination, and functional restoration (Fig. 8E-F) [187]. In parallel, inspired by pinecone scale deformation triggered by water absorption, a water-responsive self-curling adhesive conduit was engineered through the creation of hydrophilic and hydrophobic component gradients across film layers. The resultant conduit adaptively envelops nerve stumps upon exposure to physiological fluids, enabling suture-free adhesive fixation while minimizing collateral damage and supporting axonal regeneration and functional recovery in PNR [192].

Cross-species biomimicry enriches neural tissue engineering paradigms by fusing biological principles with engineering ingenuity. These evolutionarily refined strategies afford unique approaches to tackle complex PNR challenges, highlighting the formidable potential of biomimetics in neural restoration.

Multidimensional biomimetic strategies must be appraised against the length scale of long-gap repair. The term “maximum effective repair length” refers to the longest defect distance over which a given strategy has produced definitive morphological, electrophysiological, or functional improvement in reported animal or clinical studies, and does not constitute a formal clinical indication threshold. Clinically, commercial hollow or filled NGCs are overwhelmingly deployed for small-diameter, short-gap nerve defects and dependably exceeding the ∼3 cm barrier remains an unmet challenge; beyond this demarcation, autologous nerve grafts or processed nerve allografts represent more established options [193]. Viewed through this lens, the evidentiary landscape of biomimetic strategies is markedly uneven. Structural and cross-species topological biomimicry have been predominantly validated in 10-15 mm rat sciatic nerve models, whereas several multichannel or longitudinally oriented scaffolds have reached 30 mm defects in canine models, suggesting they approach but do not yet consistently transcend the clinical 3 cm threshold [194]. Conversely, functional biomimicry that integrates native ECM, decellularized matrices, and sustained neurotrophic factor delivery currently constitutes the most authoritative long-gap evidence, as demonstrated by GDNF-loaded decellularized nerve matrix hydrogel-modified scaffolds bridging 50 mm beagle sciatic nerve defects and sustained GDNF-releasing PCL conduits spanning 50 mm nonhuman primate median nerve defects [86,195]. Cell- or exosome-loaded conduits, conductive hydrogels, piezoelectric conduits, and 4D dynamic scaffolds remain largely circumscribed to 10-15 mm rodent models, with only a handful of cell-loaded scaffolds attaining 30 mm rat defects [173,196]. Future reports on biomimetic NGCs should therefore explicitly delineate both the mechanistic advantage and the length boundary of each design, and should prioritize validation in ≥30 mm large-animal defects, 50 mm-scale nonhuman primate models, or standardized clinical cohorts to determine whether the long-gap bottleneck has truly been surmounted.

Multidimensional biomimetic synergy and antagonism

Once structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and cross-species strategies are examined in isolation, the pivotal question shifts from the sheer multiplicity of biomimetic elements within an NGC to their directional coherence, amplitude matching, and temporal coordination inside a unified regenerative construct. Multidimensional biomimetic cues do not merely summate; they can generate synergy, hierarchical dominance, or antagonism [68]. Productive synergy generally arises when separate cues align along a common regenerative axis and bestow complementary biological functions. Longitudinal grooves, aligned nanofibers, or multichannel architectures can impart contact guidance that constrains SC migration and axonal elongation, whereas graded distributions of NGF, BDNF, GDNF, or netrin-1 concurrently furnish supplementary chemotactic and maturation-promoting stimuli. NGCs that amalgamate multichannel architecture with NGF gradients have demonstrated superior nerve recovery compared with single-cue designs [49]. Likewise, GelMA/silk-graphene conductive hydrogels that embed a netrin-1 gradient, a compliant hydrogel matrix, and an electroconductive network have proven more efficacious than uniform factor delivery in diabetic PNI models [197].

Conversely, multidimensional biomimicry can also unleash antagonistic effects. Where topographical and molecular gradients are spatially discordant, cells and growth cones tend to obey the prepotent contact-guidance cue; fundamental neuroengineering studies have shown that contact guidance can prevail over diffusible signals such as NGF or laminin in directing axonal polarization [198]. Accordingly, if groove orientation runs perpendicular to, or is locally misaligned with, a growth factor gradient, structural biomimicry may attenuate or even extinguish the chemotactic contribution of functional biomimicry. Moreover, excessive or temporally uncalibrated neurotrophic factor release can provoke aberrant sprouting, axonal misdirection, or non-target reinnervation [199], while excessively compact multichannel structures, supra-physiological crosslinking, or high conductive-filler loadings may compromise pore interconnectivity, nutrient diffusion, and mechanical compliance, thereby negating the benefits of bioactive signaling. Multidimensional biomimetic design should therefore be governed by directional fidelity, cue-intensity matching, temporal prioritization, and deliberate simplification. Structural cues should establish low-misdirection physical trajectories; functional cues should potentiate cell migration and axonal maturation along the same axis; electrical and mechanical cues should reside within physiological tolerance windows; and spatiotemporal release and degradation kinetics should avert early over-stimulation and late chronic interference. Future investigations should deploy orthogonal designs, targeted cue-ablation experiments, and physics-informed computational models to disentangle and quantify the contribution of each cue, thus steering the field from brute-force multi-factor stacking toward mechanism-guided synergistic refinement.

From biomimetic design to clinical translation and future directions

Peripheral nerve tissue engineering has evolved from passive tubular bridging toward biologically intelligent interfaces that integrate guidance, regulatory, and feedback competencies. Rather than reiterating the definitions of structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and cross-species biomimicry, this section interrogates their translational maturity, the imperative for their synergistic combination, and the risks inherent in their practical deployment.

The evolutionary trajectory of biomimetic neural tissue engineering

Biomimetic neural tissue engineering advances via cumulative accretion of biological fidelity, not linear succession of earlier technologies. Each phase builds upon preceding achievements, incorporating attributes that more faithfully recapitulate the native peripheral nerve microenvironment. The field traverses four interconnected, partially overlapping stages: structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and the emergent paradigm of intelligent or interactive biomimicry.

Structural biomimicry originated in the 1990s, redirecting the objective from mere gap bridging toward recapitulation of the hierarchical, anisotropic architecture of native nerve tissue [200]. Conduits progressed from epineurium-mimetic shells to multichannel fascicle-like architectures that attenuate axonal dispersion, and to aligned intraluminal microchannels furnishing endoneurium-like guidance [28,49]. This paradigm underwent refinement via decellularized matrices, porous ECM-mimetic scaffolds, gradient microstructures, and aligned nanofibers, thereby facilitating SC alignment, neurite elongation, and functional restoration [61,201]. More recently, molecular-scale chirality has been integrated to modulate protein adsorption and integrin signaling [52]. Consequently, structural biomimicry rapidly advanced from elementary external shielding to hierarchical guidance spanning connective tissue, ECM, and molecular strata, establishing itself as the universal physical underpinning for subsequent strategies.

Functional biomimicry gradually emerged during 2000-2010 and subsequently attained broad adoption. Whereas structural biomimicry addressed geometric verisimilitude, functional biomimicry concerned active modulation of cellular behavior analogous to the native milieu. Although preliminary efforts date to 1997 [202], sustained inquiry commenced in the 2000s with the integration of ECM molecules, SCs, neurotrophic factors, and controlled release into conduits. SC transplantation, laminin/collagen modification, sustained NGF delivery, and biodegradable multichannel conduits featuring cell-adhesive interfaces propelled the transition from passive bridges to bioactive platforms [203]. Functional biomimicry continued advancing beyond 2010, encompassing decellularized matrices, dECM coatings, cell-derived matrices, and hybrid conductive-biochemical interfaces, thereby engendering composite strategies that harmonize structure, matrix, cellular components, and electrophysiological cues.

Spatiotemporal biomimicry emerged and attained widespread adoption during 2010-2020. The central revelation was that PNR constitutes not a static event but a temporally orchestrated program encompassing inflammation, SC dedifferentiation, axonal extension, angiogenesis, remyelination, and target reconstruction [166]. Merely reproducing structure or delivering fixed factors at a singular time point proved inadequate. Process biomimicry engendered spatiotemporally programmable strategies. Shape-memory polymers, 4D-printed constructs, and self-curling conduits enable scaffolds to reconfigure in response to stimuli [173]. Spatiotemporal biomimicry additionally encompasses sustained and gradient delivery of neurotrophic factors, on-demand release triggered by ultrasound, dynamic immune modulation, and regenerative paradigms coordinated with vascularization. Conduits featuring longitudinal factor gradients, ultrasound-responsive architectures, and ECM-mimetic porous networks with sustained release collectively embody adaptation to stage-specific progression [51,175,176]. The defining attribute is the metamorphosis of NGCs from static platforms into temporally adaptive interfaces that co-evolve with the repair process.

The fourth stage, presently crystallizing, may be termed intelligent or interactive biomimicry. It endeavors to construct a regenerative interface endowed with sensing, decision-making, and response capabilities. This trajectory remains transitional from responsive architectures toward closed-loop systems. Early investigations attempted real-time PNR monitoring via implantable microelectrode arrays [204]. More recently, battery-free, wirelessly operated multimodal sensing platforms have been engineered to continuously monitor nerve temperature and strain [205]. Biodegradable, recoverable peripheral nerve interfaces have enabled wireless real-time tracking and recovery assessment, complemented by machine-learning-assisted analysis of recovery status and neuroma risk [206,207]. Therapeutic modalities are likewise advancing rapidly. Biodegradable flexible optoelectronic neural interfaces have demonstrated transcutaneous optoelectronic stimulation for nerve modulation; bioresorbable wireless dual-site stimulators have propelled long-term, on-demand electrical stimulation closer to closed-loop therapy [208,209]. Although a fully closed-loop platform that integrates monitoring, analysis, and adaptive intervention within a single scaffold remains an objective being approached rather than widely realized, the practical foundation of intelligent biomimicry has already commenced to emerge.

The evolutionary trajectory delineates a discernible progression from structural mimicry to functional reconstitution, process orchestration, and toward intelligent interactivity. Yet this progression should not be construed as mutually exclusive generations. Structural biomimicry remains foundational; functional biomimicry continues to mature; process biomimicry has become an indispensable nexus. The enduring challenge is not merely to render NGCs more biomimetic in a descriptive sense, but to convert them into context-aware therapeutic interfaces capable of dynamically aligning with the biological logic of PNR (Fig. 9).Fig. 9The four-generation evolutionary trajectory of biomimetic neural tissue engineering. Current landscape of clinical translation in biomimetic neural tissue engineering

Presently, clinical translation of biomimetic neural tissue engineering remains predominantly centered on NGCs. These should be conceived as practical carrier platforms for the progressive integration of distinct biomimetic strata, rather than as isolated devices. From a regulatory approval perspective, structural biomimicry constitutes the most mature translational tier, as clinically available conduits already recapitulate selected physical attributes of native nerve tissue. Early constructs were simple biodegradable hollow tubes [210]; subsequent iterations introduced refined structural analogies to connective tissue. An NMPA-approved artificial nerve sheath featuring a spongy bovine collagen matrix exhibits superior mechanical properties, biocompatibility, and controlled degradability. Its microporous wall emulates neural connective tissue, thereby facilitating oxygen and nutrient delivery. Similarly, the NeuraGen® Nerve Guide (Integra, USA) comprises a semi-transparent type I collagen matrix with a microporous architecture that permits efficient exchange of biological signals and metabolic substances [[211], [212], [213]]. Furthermore, a composite NGC fabricated from chitosan, chitin, and gelatin, reinforced with PLGA fibers, furnishes intrinsic guidance cues.

Concurrently, second-generation functional biomimetic designs have progressed into clinical practice. The Avance® Nerve Graft, a processed acellular nerve allograft, retains native architecture and bioactivity, attaining an 82% clinical success rate for PNR [5,214]. The AxoGuard Nerve Connector® (porcine SIS ECM) diminishes tension and ischemia, prevents axonal escape, and promotes vascularization [215,216]. The AxoGuard Nerve Protector® (also SIS ECM) precludes soft tissue adhesion and mitigates re-compression risk [217]. Alternative functional strategies incorporate active ECM constituents or cellular therapies. The NeuraGen® 3D Nerve Guide Matrix (Integra) comprises a type I collagen conduit with an integrated porous matrix containing collagen and chondroitin, thereby guiding axonal regeneration and attenuating scar formation. A study encompassing 126 patients reported an 87.3% reconstruction success rate [218,219]. Beyond ECM replication, a collagen tube loaded with autologous skin-derived stem cells (authorized by the Italian Medicines Agency) has effectively repaired nerve defects, yielding substantial functional restoration of median and ulnar nerves within three years [220].

The crux of translating biomimetic neural tissue engineering from bench to bedside resides in the seamless integration of precision manufacturing with structural, functional, spatiotemporal, and cross-species biomimetic paradigms. This convergence propels the evolution from passive bridging toward actively adaptive regenerative interfaces, furnishing scalable, personalized, and clinically efficacious solutions for long-gap peripheral nerve defects. Although autologous nerve grafting persists as the gold standard, its utility is constrained by donor scarcity and donor-site morbidity. Commercially available simple collagen- or chitosan-based conduits cleared by the FDA are primarily indicated for defects below 3 cm and exhibit limited efficacy in extended gap injuries, thereby failing to address real-world clinical imperatives.

By faithfully recapitulating the native neural microenvironment, multi-dimensional biomimetic NGCs hold substantial promise for enhancing regeneration quality and functional recovery rates, and have already advanced into clinical trial phases. Key translational pathways include technical scalability and reproducible fabrication, synergistic augmentation via multi-dimensional biomimetic paradigms, and regulatory trajectories buttressed by accumulating clinical evidence. State-of-the-art manufacturing modalities, including 3D/4D bioprinting and electrospinning, enable patient-specific, expedited customized production, markedly reducing costs and bolstering reproducibility. Recent investigations have confirmed that bio-3D-printed NGCs have been successfully deployed in human clinical nerve reconstructions, with no adverse events reported and progressive functional restoration observed within 48 weeks post-implantation, thereby validating their feasibility regarding surgical implantability, degradation kinetics, and immunocompatibility [221]. Structural biomimicry furnishes foundational physical guidance; functional biomimicry supplies bioactive cues; spatiotemporal biomimicry enables dynamic adaptability; cross-species biomimicry expands material repertoires. Collectively, these strategies surmount fibrosis, immune rejection, and long-gap regenerative barriers. Biomimetic ECM-NGCs that integrate these paradigms achieve electrophysiological outcomes, mitigation of muscle atrophy, and motor recovery comparable to autografts in long-gap animal models, further expediting clinical translation via immune microenvironment modulation [222]. Nevertheless, the translational maturity of these strategies is markedly uneven. Most multifunctional biomimetic designs have yet to transcend preclinical investigation, and their perceived parity with autografts largely stems from surrogate animal endpoints, be they axon counts, myelin thickness, electrophysiological recovery, muscle weight preservation, or gait analysis. Valuable as these measures are, they cannot supplant human clinical outcomes, which span sensory discrimination, motor recovery, pain, quality of life, complication rates, and long-term safety. Several biomimetic NGCs have progressed to FDA Investigational Device Exemption submissions or early-stage feasibility studies. Notably, biologics-free 3D-printed NGCs are advancing through human pilot trials [29]. Concurrently, extant clinical data indicate that micro- and nano-composite multi-channel conduits significantly enhance sensory and motor recovery in human short-gap repairs, establishing a foundation for extension to long-gap indications. Nevertheless, challenges remain: larger animal studies, especially primates, and randomized human trials are requisite to confirm long-term safety and efficacy; degradation kinetics must be meticulously tailored to align with regenerative timelines; cost and standardization hurdles in regulatory approval must be addressed [223].

Notwithstanding encouraging trends, comprehensive translation of multifunctional biomimetic NGCs confronts formidable bottlenecks. A paramount issue is that NGCs serving as carrier platforms must preserve intraoperative handling characteristics and mechanical integrity compatible with microsurgical practice. Numerous hydrogel-rich systems remain susceptible to tearing, deformation, or inadequate suture retention under tensile load [224]. Furthermore, incorporation of functional, process-oriented, or intelligent modules escalates manufacturing complexity and complicates sterilization protocols, particularly when delicate bioactive constituents, viable cells, or electronic elements are entailed [225]. Another substantial impediment is the excessive reliance of preclinical evaluation on rodent models. Future integration of multiscale architectures and sensing capabilities will necessitate standardized large-animal validation to reliably predict clinical performance in human long-gap injuries [226].

The prevailing clinical landscape suggests that biomimetic neural tissue engineering is progressing via incremental enrichment of NGC-based platforms, rather than through entirely novel product categories. Existing regulatory approvals and early-stage applications confirm that select biomimetic principles (architecture, ECM mimicry, bioactive support) possess tangible translational merit. Nevertheless, current systems capture only a fraction of native nerve complexity, particularly in long-gap and complex injuries. Consequently, while NGCs furnish a practical foundation, the ensuing challenge resides in surmounting scientific, engineering, and translational impediments to engineer more integrated, adaptive, and clinically robust biomimetic neural scaffolds.

Implementation-oriented evaluation criteria and translational prioritization of biomimetic strategies

A candid complexity-translatability trade-off must be embedded in the design calculus of biomimetic NGCs. We therefore advance the concept of a “minimum effective biomimetic combination,” defined as the most parsimonious constellation of structural, biochemical, electrophysiological, cellular, or intelligent cues that elicits a quantifiable, clinically significant regenerative benefit while maintaining congruence with regulatory classification, GMP-compliant manufacturing, sterilization, shelf-life stability, and batch release. Regulatory pathways and evidentiary requirements are dictated primarily by intended use, patient risk, the existence of an appropriate predicate, and the interactions among the product's constituent elements, rather than by biological sophistication alone [227]. Each additional biomimetic layer must consequently be regarded as both a potential biological dividend and a regulatory liability. The introduction of growth factors, cells, exosomes, living tissues, drug-release reservoirs, conductive or piezoelectric modules, wireless electronics, or AI-empowered decision functions can reclassify an NGC from a familiar acellular implant into a combination product, an AI/machine learning (ML)-enabled device, a biologics-adjacent construct, or an entity requiring supplementary clinical investigation [228]. FDA guidance on combination products underscores the necessity of clarity, predictability, efficiency, and inter-center coordination for products that straddle device, drug, and biologic domains [229].

Operationally, this philosophy reorients biomimetic design from indiscriminate feature aggregation toward endpoint-driven parsimony. A candidate module should be retained only when it satisfies three imperatives: it targets a decisive biological node in PNR, such as repair-SC maintenance, axonal guidance fidelity, early neurovascularization, immune remodeling, or late remyelination; it confers incremental benefit over a simpler structural or ECM-mimetic conduit in clinically relevant models and endpoints; and its critical quality attributes can be prospectively defined, reliably measured, released, and reproduced during scale-up. Conversely, incorporating cells, exosomes, conductive fillers, piezoelectric layers, growth factors, wireless electronics, or AI decision modules without unequivocal, module-specific necessity is likely to amplify regulatory category ambiguity, expand sterility and safety testing, inflate release specifications, elevate cost of goods, and magnify batch-to-batch variability without proportional clinical return [230].

Implementation obliges that each strategy be interrogated against a suite of clinically oriented metrics. Scalable manufacturing must safeguard dimensional fidelity, channel reproducibility, porosity control, mechanical integrity, degradation consistency, and batch-to-batch uniformity through a delineated manufacturing workflow, a robust scale-up/scale-out strategy, and a quality-control architecture encompassing product yield, purity, potency, viability, and scaffold quality attributes [231,232]. Sterilization and storage constitute equally decisive translational filters. Terminal sterilization remains preferable for acellular conduits, yet ethylene oxide, radiation, or moist heat sterilization demands rigorous validation of their impact on scaffold morphology, mechanics, degradation kinetics, bioactivity, and electrical performance [227,233]. Sterile barrier integrity, accelerated aging, and real-time shelf-life verification must also be addressed, especially when room-temperature storage is claimed [234,235]. Immunological and biological safety should be evaluated through a risk-stratified framework that includes cytotoxicity, sensitization, irritation, systemic toxicity, genotoxicity when applicable, implantation response, hemocompatibility when relevant, and local neuroinflammatory sequelae [236]. For animal-derived ECM or dECM materials, source provenance, tissue collection, viral and TSE risk mitigation, decellularization completeness, residual DNA burden, and inter-lot compositional consistency are indispensable [235,237]. Moreover, regulatory pathway selection should be contemplated from the earliest design stage. Acellular conduits fabricated from clinically familiar materials and demonstrating substantial equivalence to predicate devices may be aligned with a more straightforward 510(k) pathway, whereas highly innovative devices may necessitate De Novo or PMA-oriented evidence packages. Products incorporating growth factors, cells, exosomes, living tissues, or electronically active therapeutic modules may traverse biologic-device, drug-device, or combination-product pathways, requiring expanded chemistry, manufacturing and controls, potency assays, biodistribution or persistence studies, and phased clinical validation [232,238].

For next-generation biomimetic NGCs, clinical translation must be adjudicated by the convergence of regenerative efficacy and implementation feasibility. Based on current regulatory and manufacturing realities, biomimetic strategies may be stratified into several translational echelons. The highest near-term readiness resides in acellular, biologics-free structural conduits manufactured from clinically established polymers or collagen-based biomaterials. These designs, which encompass aligned fibers, multichannel architectures, optimized porosity, and mechanically reinforced hollow conduits, superimpose instructive guidance cues upon relatively uncomplicated product definitions. Their cardinal advantages include compatibility with terminal sterilization, room-temperature storage viability, clearer device-oriented regulatory pathways, and simplified release testing [227].

A second echelon comprises acellular ECM/dECM-mimetic conduits and electroactive or piezoelectric conduits. ECM/dECM strategies retain biologically instructive matrix cues, yet their clinical readiness depends upon stringent source control, decellularization reproducibility, residual DNA reduction, pathogen risk mitigation, and immunological safety [235,237]. Electroactive and piezoelectric conduits may similarly progress toward validation, given that they can remain acellular and biologics-free; however, they require supplementary verification of electrical output, polarization stability, mechanical endurance, degradation behavior, and post-sterilization functional integrity [233,239].

A later echelon encompasses temporally programmed release systems, cell-loaded scaffolds, exosome-loaded conduits, and intelligent closed-loop interfaces. These platforms offer exceptional promise for long-gap and complex injuries because they can modulate the inflammatory, trophic, vascular, electrical, and remodeling phases of PNR. Nonetheless, their clinical progression is likely to be protracted, as they introduce additional uncertainties in manufacturing reproducibility, potency assay development, release kinetics, storage and cold-chain logistics, immunogenicity, biodistribution, and regulatory classification [238]. For these strategies, large-animal investigations should be designed not merely to confirm regeneration but to scrutinize surgical handling, suture retention, degradation-matched mechanical support, immune response, sterility assurance, and functional durability under clinically representative defect dimensions [227].

Recent regulatory experience further illustrates that acellular tissue-derived peripheral nerve scaffolds can advance through biologics-oriented approval routes when their classification extends beyond a conventional device framework [240]. Consequently, the translational trajectory for biomimetic NGCs should be prospectively selected based on product composition, mechanism of action, the presence or absence of living or bioactive components, sterilization modality, shelf-life requirements, and intended clinical indication. The most clinically actionable path is not inherently the most biologically intricate design, but the design that achieves an optimal equilibrium among regenerative potency, manufacturability, safety, storage stability, and regulatory predictability [232]. For short sensory gaps, low-complexity acellular structural guidance may prove sufficient and more readily translatable than multi-component living constructs. For longer or more hostile defects, a stepwise escalation may be warranted, beginning with anisotropic architecture and ECM-mimetic cues, subsequently appending electroactive or immunomodulatory modules only when they resolve a defined bottleneck, and reserving cells, exosomes, drug reservoirs, or closed-loop AI-enabled functions for indications where simpler platforms demonstrably fail. The “minimum effective biomimetic combination” principle thus does not stifle innovation; instead, it channels biomimicry into a clinically rigorous design discipline that reconciles regenerative efficacy, patient safety, regulatory predictability, and scalable GMP production.

Bottlenecks and future directions for next-generation biomimetic neural interfaces

Notwithstanding notable strides in biomimetic neural tissue engineering, formidable challenges endure in translating complex designs into mechanistically grounded, manufacturable, and clinically dependable regenerative interfaces. Simultaneous advances in additive manufacturing, artificial intelligence, and tissueoid-based engineering are unlocking novel avenues for next-generation biomimetic PNR. This section consequently delineates prevailing bottlenecks and prospective trajectories of the field.

Current challenges

Notwithstanding advances in biomimetic neural tissue engineering, a formidable challenge endures: limited mechanistic insight into the integration of multiple biomimetic cues within a single regenerative system. PNR relies on orchestrated interactions among SCs, macrophages, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and neurons, yet the temporal logic and signaling hierarchy governing this crosstalk remain incompletely elucidated [241]. Consequently, prevailing multimodal designs often amalgamate structural, biochemical, electrical, or immunomodulatory elements empirically rather than adhering to a defined biological blueprint. This knowledge gap also constrains development-informed biomimicry, as the roles and phenotypic transitions of regenerative cell subtypes, alongside the mechanisms of cues including topography, conductivity, piezoelectricity, and staged factor delivery, remain unresolved. Simultaneously, escalating multimodal complexity engenders manufacturing and translational obstacles, encompassing difficulties in multi-material integration, microstructural control, reproducibility, and long-term reliability. Hence, the central challenge is not merely appending more features, but integrating them in a biologically coordinated, manufacturable, and clinically robust fashion.

Another formidable challenge resides in translating burgeoning biological data into actionable intelligence for AI-enabled biomimetic neural tissue engineering. Although transcriptomics, single-cell sequencing, spatial analysis, electrophysiological monitoring, and imaging have amassed rich PNR datasets, these data remain fragmented across disparate platforms, models, and temporal milestones [242]. Presently, no unified framework correlates multimodal data with distinct regenerative stages, dominant cellular phenotypes, and corresponding intervention paradigms. The scarcity of high-quality, standardized, and longitudinal datasets further impedes model training and validation. Given the highly dynamic and heterogeneous nature of PNR, AI models trained on inconsistent datasets risk capturing statistical correlations devoid of genuine biological causality. These constraints become particularly critical in closed-loop regenerative systems, where AI must not only interpret biological status but also orchestrate adaptive modulation of scaffold attributes, release kinetics, or stimulation regimens. Consequently, the clinical reliability, interpretability, and regulatory endorsement of AI-guided biomimetic systems constitute major translational impediments.

An additional formidable challenge is the nascent maturity of tissueoid-based strategies for PNR. Relative to the accelerated evolution of central nervous system organoids, peripheral nerve tissueoids reside at an incipient stage, and their biological complexity has yet to align with the exigencies of clinically consequential regeneration. Existing models can replicate select features, including aligned axonal outgrowth, glial support, or localized trophic augmentation, but they remain incapable of recapitulating the full multicellular, vascularized, and dynamically remodeling milieu of injured peripheral nerves [243]. Fabrication additionally poses substantial hurdles, necessitating not only cellular viability but also precise architectural organization, appropriate ECM composition, and tightly regulated signaling. Variability across stem cell sources, induction protocols, scaffold composition, and maturation states constrains reproducibility and therapeutic uniformity. Safety and translational feasibility further encumber this domain, notably regarding tumorigenicity, immune compatibility, and long-term in vivo performance. Consequently, before tissueoids can evolve into a pragmatic platform for biomimetic neural tissue engineering, further strides are imperative in biological fidelity, manufacturability, reproducibility, and safety.

Outlook

Multi-modal biomimicry and application of advanced manufacturing technologies

Contemporary research is progressively transitioning from unidimensional imitation toward multimodal biomimicry, integrating structural, functional, and process-level cues within a single nerve guidance system. A bilayer NGC endowed with dual functionality (neurotrophic factor loading and piezoelectric responsiveness) furnished sustained trophic support while preserving bioelectrical activity, thereby potentiating PNR [244]. Nevertheless, the prospective value of multimodal biomimicry resides not in the mere amalgamation of materials or factors, but in choreographing the phased behavior of principal cellular constituents (macrophages, endothelial cells, SCs, fibroblasts, and neurons) throughout PNR. Within an idealized construct, early signals would modulate inflammation via macrophage recruitment and polarization; subsequent cues would foster angiogenesis and fibroblast-mediated matrix remodeling; intermediate signals would orchestrate SC dedifferentiation, migration, and reprogramming; later-stage regulation would facilitate axonal extension, remyelination, and target reinnervation [9]. Consequently, a paramount future trajectory involves optimizing both synergistic effects within each biomimetic category and temporal coordination across disparate cell populations during distinct regenerative phases.

Next-generation biomimetic strategies should emphasize development-informed biomimicry. Currently, many neurodevelopment-inspired designs recapitulate broad developmental phenomena without faithfully reproducing the underlying regulatory logic. Nevertheless, multi-omics modalities (bulk transcriptomics, single-cell RNA sequencing, spatially resolved atlases, intercellular communication analyses) are progressively elucidating mechanisms governing SC plasticity, stromal remodeling, neurovascular coupling, and remyelination [242,245]. These investigations delineate how transient cellular states and intercellular signaling networks evolve temporally, revealing not only which developmental-like programs warrant mimicry, but also when, where, and for which cellular subpopulations they should be reconstituted. Consequently, future biomimicry must transcend phenomenological imitation and advance toward mechanism-informed reconstruction, enabling engineered conduits to emulate developmental gradients, transient repair states, and evolving microenvironmental signals, rather than merely replicating static architectures or delivering invariant factors.

Realizing these sophisticated concepts as practical constructs necessitates advanced manufacturing technologies. Traditional fabrication modalities inadequately address the requirements of multi-material integration, regional heterogeneity, and spatiotemporal programmability in multimodal biomimetic neural tissue engineering [246]. In situ bioprinting deposits bioinks directly onto tissue surfaces, generating complex geometries with suitable stability [247], concurrently reducing scanning and design expenditures and augmenting wound-scaffold integration (Fig. 10A) [248]. Multi-material bioprinting entails programmed sequential or simultaneous deposition of two or more bioinks to achieve region-specific attributes (Fig. 10B) [249]. This approach is particularly suited for constructing bilayer or multilayer scaffolds that recapitulate heterogeneous, biomimetic architectures with intricate compositions and stimulus responsiveness [253]. Moreover, 4D bioprinting endows 3D-printed constructs with pre-programmed spatiotemporal transformations in shape or functionality upon exposure to stimuli including heat, light, or pH. AI in biomimetic neural tissue engineering

AI is increasingly deployed in regenerative medicine for disease detection, prediction, and targeted drug screening [255]. Its paramount value in biomimetic neural tissue engineering may reside in real-time recognition of the regeneration stage and identification of stage-specific cellular requirements of the injured nerve microenvironment. Recent transcriptomic and single-cell atlases reveal that PNR constitutes a temporally phased, heterogeneous process involving coordinated transitions among macrophages, SCs, fibroblasts, vascular-associated cells, and neurons, rather than a linear regrowth event [242,256,257]. Consequently, future AI-assisted biomimetic systems should aim not merely to ascertain whether regeneration is proceeding, but to deduce which biological phase is active, which cell populations predominate, and which intervention is warranted at that instant.

Viewed from this vantage, AI constitutes the pivotal bridge connecting in vivo monitoring with adaptive biomimetic execution. Gross metrics such as regrowth length alone remain insufficient to ascertain whether the local microenvironment predominantly demands inflammatory resolution, angiogenic support, SC maintenance, matrix remodeling, remyelination, or target reinnervation. A biodegradable restorative peripheral neural interface has enabled wireless real-time tracking of long-gap nerve recovery and leveraged machine learning to decode recovery status and detect early neuroma formation. Furthermore, biodegradable optoelectronic neural interfaces and durable bioresorbable wireless stimulators exemplify how future NGCs could integrate sensing with stage-aware intervention modules (Fig. 10B) [206,209]. Accordingly, AI is optimally conceived as a decision-support stratum that fuses electrophysiological, biomechanical, imaging, and omics-derived information to estimate regeneration stage and align it with the evolving requirements of distinct cell populations.

An implementation-oriented closed-loop paradigm, recombining established modules from neural engineering and smart biomaterials, can be envisioned to operationalize AI-enabled biomimetic design. In the epilepsy field, hydrogel bioelectronics have already converged electrical recording with controlled chemical release under sophisticated spatiotemporal control, and on-demand seizure-suppression paradigms have validated real-time detection-triggered neuromodulation [258]. Translating this framework to PNR, a regenerative conduit could be instrumented with flexible microelectrode arrays or high-density, multi-contact nerve cuffs positioned at the proximal stump, along the conduit, and at the distal stump [259,260]. This sensing architecture would continuously capture compound action potentials (CAPs), compound nerve action potentials (CNAPs), conduction velocity, signal amplitude, recruitment curves, and signal-to-noise ratio, functioning as longitudinal biomarkers. AI models, trained on these electrophysiological signatures together with functional recovery data, could classify the repair trajectory into early inflammatory/debris-clearance, axonal-extension, reinnervation, and myelination-dominant phases. Upon detecting diminished distal CNAP recovery, delayed conduction velocity, or inadequate recruitment, the interface could trigger low-intensity electrical stimulation or localized release of neurotrophic or immunomodulatory factors; conversely, once conduction velocity and recruitment curves reached predefined maturation benchmarks, stimulation intensity could be titrated downward to prevent overstimulation. Electrophysiological monitoring would thus transition from a passive assessment tool to a stage-adaptive intervention strategy [258,260].

Upon inference of stage-specific demands, AI can further facilitate the inverse design and optimization of biomimetic materials [261]. Rather than relying exclusively on empirical trial and error, AI models may inform how scaffold architecture, stiffness, degradation kinetics, conductivity, porosity, bioactive factor cocktails, release profiles, and stimulation regimens should be calibrated to distinct repair phases. Early-stage materials might prioritize immunomodulation and inflammatory resolution; intermediate-stage designs could emphasize angiogenic support, stromal remodeling, and preservation of repair SC phenotypes; later-stage systems may be optimized for axonal elongation, remyelination, and neuromuscular reinnervation. Existing investigations already demonstrate that machine learning can accelerate hydrogel and drug delivery design. Moreover, high-throughput optimization platforms have identified injectable hydrogel formulations possessing targeted release characteristics, thereby substantiating the feasibility of data-driven biomaterial engineering for future intelligent NGCs [262,263].

To operationalize the decisive-minority concept proposed in Section 4.2.4, AI-enabled NGC design should be orchestrated as a staged training-selection-validation cascade. The training tier amalgamates high-throughput scaffold libraries, multi-omics and spatial datasets, imaging features, electrophysiological traces, and functional recovery endpoints, with each scaffold sample encoded by design descriptors and stage-resolved biological outputs [264]. The selection tier harnesses sparse models or embedded feature-selection algorithms to collapse the high-dimensional design space into a compact set of candidate variables; nonlinear learners employed in parallel are rigorously vetted through SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), permutation importance, and counterfactual perturbation to fortify interpretability [265,266]. The validation tier deploys active learning to iteratively nominate minimal scaffold configurations that maximize regenerative endpoints while penalizing component cardinality, manufacturing overhead, and regulatory risk [267,268].

Overall, AI's future role in biomimetic neural tissue engineering may convert NGCs from passive or preprogrammed constructs into stage-aware, cell-informed, adaptively optimized regenerative interfaces. By synergizing real-time sensing, multi-omics-informed inference, and data-driven design, AI could enable biomimetic systems to dynamically tune their outputs in accordance with the evolving cellular logic of PNR.

Application of organoids and tissueoids in biomimetic neural tissue engineering

Peripheral nerve organoids and tissueoids embody two emerging yet conceptually orthogonal strategies for building nerve-mimetic architectures in vitro and for generating regenerative grafts. Organoids arise as self-organizing three-dimensional neural systems sourced from stem cells or tissue-resident progenitors; their hallmark is the partial reconstitution of neural developmental programs, cellular complexity, spatial compartmentalization, and functional attributes. The term “organoid” thereby accentuates intrinsic biological self-assembly and developmental mimicry. Tissueoids, in contrast, are engineered tissue-like regenerative units purpose-built to recapitulate the fundamental repair logic of peripheral nerves. They are not predicated on spontaneous self-organization or on generating a miniature organ; instead, they are constructed by amalgamating cells, ECM, biomaterial scaffolds, neurotrophic factors, and, when indicated, vascular or electrophysiological modules. This integrative assembly enables tissueoids to restore cardinal regenerative functions, including longitudinal axonal guidance, SC maintenance, matrix adhesion, neurotrophic retention, remyelination, and host incorporation. Thus, organoids principally model neural development and provide biologically organized neural building blocks, whereas tissueoids operate as engineered regenerative microenvironments tailored for PNR [243,269].

Peripheral nerve organoid research remains nascent relative to the rapidly maturing field of central nervous system and spinal cord organoids. Microfluidic culture platforms have recently enabled the derivation of hiPSC-based sensory nerve organoids by channelling unidirectional axonal outgrowth from neurospheres through microgrooves, thereby generating extended sensory axon fascicles and supporting protracted in vitro culture [270]. Microfluidic devices, in this context, function as enabling conduits that accelerate organoid maturation and axonal patterning, rather than constituting a separate conceptual entity. The artificial neurite bundles shown in Fig. 10E likewise reside within the organoid-related spectrum. Those acellular neurite bundles were harvested from human iPSC-derived motor and sensory nerve organoids through a microfluidic workflow and subsequently packaged within nerve conduits for transplantation [251]. Although the elimination of neuronal somata disqualifies these bundles as complete organoids, their biological origin, axonal ultrastructure, and regenerative logic remain intrinsically organoid-derived. They are therefore more accurately classified as organoid-derived acellular neurite-bundle constructs, not as tissueoids. Notably, these constructs augmented nerve regeneration, underwent gradual resorption post-transplantation, and displayed no tumorigenic potential or persistent cellular expansion, positioning them as a potentially safer paradigm than conventional live-cell-laden grafts [251].

Within the tissueoid realm, 3D-printed conduits, aligned microchannel scaffolds, electrospun matrices, and decellularized matrices serve as structural or bioactive platforms that underpin tissueoid assembly; they do not, per se, qualify as tissueoids. A tissueoid strategy crystallizes only when these scaffolds are further integrated with living cells, ECM-associated cues, neurotrophic factors, vascularizing modules, or other functional constituents to forge a tissue-like regenerative microenvironment [271]. An exemplary embodiment is the peripheral nerve tissueoid anchored on a decellularized optic nerve scaffold (Fig. 10F). In this construct, the inherent longitudinal microchannels of the decellularized optic nerve impart anisotropic contact guidance, while NT-3-overexpressing SCs establish a regenerative niche saturated with laminin and NT-3. Through this synergistic design, the construct recapitulates several hallmark features of the PNR niche, potentiating chemotaxis, axonal adhesion, vectorial axonal extension, remyelination, and functional restoration following sciatic nerve injury [252]. The quintessential feature of a tissueoid, therefore, is not the scaffold itself, but the coordinated amalgamation of structural guidance, living cellular support, ECM-associated signals, and regenerative bioactivity.

Methodological lessons may also be extracted from spinal cord organoid and spinal cord-like tissue engineering, disciplines in which fabrication strategies are presently more refined. Thoracic segment-specific engineered spinal cord organoids have been generated from iPSCs within bioactive matrices, enabling superior transplantation-site matching, synaptic integration, and neuroelectric conduction after spinal cord injury [272]. These paradigms hold direct relevance for PNR but should be adapted through the two-category lens elaborated above. For peripheral nerve organoids, developmental patterning and microfluidic compartmentalization may facilitate the generation of organized axonal tracts and glial-associated neural surrogates. For peripheral nerve tissueoids, analogous design principles can be translated into implantable regenerative units by merging aligned microchannel carriers, controlled morphogen or neurotrophic-factor delivery, tissue-specific ECM, vascularizing support modules, and, where advantageous, organoid-derived axon bundles as subordinate regenerative elements. Through this strategic convergence, the field can progress from rudimentary cell-laden NGCs toward living, programmable, and structurally hierarchical peripheral nerve tissueoids.

These living and programmable tissueoids cannot subsist on vascular ingrowth from the nerve stumps alone, because delayed oxygen and nutrient diffusion may imperil transplanted cells, SC migration, and axonal outgrowth. Future designs must therefore integrate vascularization strategies at two complementary levels. At one level, tissueoids can be pre-endowed with endothelial cells, pericyte-like support cells, fibroblasts, or microvessel fragments to generate immature but perfusion-competent vascular beds that rapidly inosculate with host microvasculature after implantation [273]. At a second level, temporally programmed release of angiogenic and vessel-stabilizing factors, such as VEGF-A or endothelial-cell-derived exosomes, can accelerate early angiogenesis while promoting vascular maturation and suppressing leaky or chaotic neovessels [274]. For clinically relevant long defects, an additional option is in vivo prevascularization using vascular bundle or arteriovenous loop strategies, wherein the tissueoid is first vascularized in a sheltered compartment and then transferred with an axial vascular pedicle to the nerve defect [275]. Accordingly, programmable peripheral nerve tissueoids should be envisioned not as purely neural constructs, but as neurovascular tissueoids engineered for rapid host inosculation, sustained perfusion, and synchronized neurovascular remodeling post-implantation.

Conclusion

The overarching contribution of this review resides not in appending additional biomimetic categories, but in consolidating disparate biomimetic cues into a design framework predicated on explicit functional allotment, hierarchical dominance, and translational constraints. Failure of PNR is not attributable to a single material deficit; instead, it arises from insufficient coordination among long-distance axonal channeling, repair-SC phenotype perpetuation, immune-vascular synchronization, remyelination fidelity, and target reinnervation. Accordingly, next-generation NGCs should pivot from indiscriminate mimicry of native features toward the economical reconstitution of the decisive regenerative logic. Structural cues must enforce low-misdirection, long-range conduits; functional cues should reinstate indispensable cellular and ECM-associated signaling; spatiotemporal cues must temporally couple payload release, scaffold degradation, and stimulation to distinct repair epochs; and cross-species cues ought to supply engineerable design motifs rather than superficial morphological surrogates. Future endeavors need to disentangle the synergy and antagonism among multimodal cues, institute predictive large-animal and clinical assessment frameworks, and elevate advanced manufacturing, AI, and tissueoid engineering into clinically tractable products that satisfy requirements for scalable fabrication, terminal sterilization, regulatory compliance, and economic viability. Through this transition from taxonomic enumeration to mechanistic integration and translational prioritization, multidimensional biomimicry can illuminate a precise design corridor for next-generation neural interfaces tailored to long-gap and complex PNI.

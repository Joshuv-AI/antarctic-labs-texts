ENCORE: an energy-efficient cluster-based multi-hop routing framework for WNSNs in IoNT
Authors: M Yuvaraj, S Sivaprakash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe internet of nano-things (IoNT) uses wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs) with stringent network limitations for its many applications, such as biological monitoring and environmental sensing. Network lifetime and communication reliability are fundamentally limited by the ultra-restricted onboard energy of nano-sensor nodes and the absence of post-deployment energy replenishment. This study proposes Energy-efficient Network Clustering and Optimized Routing based on Environments (ENCORE), an energy-aware clustering and multi-hop routing framework for dense three-dimensional WNSNs. ENCORE employs topology-adaptive cluster-head selection based on residual energy and sink proximity, together with a structured, sink-oriented, loop-free multi-hop backbone to balance communication load and mitigate premature node depletion. Network behaviour is evaluated using a pulse-oriented nano-communication energy model capturing continuous energy degradation. Simulation results across multiple randomized deployments show that ENCORE improves network lifetime, residual energy retention, and packet delivery compared with Flooding (FLD), LGN, and DEEPNT, achieving up to a 45% lifetime improvement over DEEPNT and nearly a threefold gain over LGN.Keywords: Cluster head selection, Energy efficiency, Internet of Nano-Things (IoNT), Multi-hop routing, Network lifetime, Wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs).Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Recent advances in nanotechnology have enabled the development of nano-sensors capable of sensing, computation, and communication at molecular and sub-molecular scales1,2. These devices constitute the fundamental building blocks of wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs), where densely deployed nodes cooperatively monitor physical, chemical, and biological phenomena3. Due to extreme miniaturization, nano-sensors operate with ultra-limited energy reserves, constrained processing capability, and very short communication ranges, severely restricting network lifetime and scalability4,5.

The integration of WNSNs with existing communication infrastructure has led to the Internet of Nano-Things (IoNT), enabling nano-sensors to communicate with micro- and macro-scale devices via nano–micro gateway3,4. IoNT communication is mainly operational in the terahertz (THz) frequency range, which supports ultra-high data transfer rates but suffers from high molecular absorption losses and distance attenuation5,6. These characteristics, combined with limited energy resources in nano-nodes, cause the nodes to fail rapidly, fragment the network, and make data transfer unreliable in a large-scale network7.

The traditional routing protocols are, therefore, not appropriate for wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs)8. Flooding-based routing methods are prone to high overhead in terms of redundant transmissions and quick energy drainages, while gradient-based and clustering-based routing methods are often susceptible to imbalanced energy consumption, increased control overhead, and hotspots in dense networks7.

To overcome these issues, this paper proposes Energy-efficient Network Clustering and Optimized Routing based on Environments (ENCORE), a novel, clustering-based routing solution for dense Internet of Nano-Things (IoNT) networks. ENCORE integrates an energy- and location-aware cluster-head selection algorithm and builds a sink-progressive, loop-free multi-hop backbone among cluster-heads to reduce long-range terahertz (THz) communications.

Related works

Wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs) under the Internet of Nano-Things (IoNT) concept face inherent challenges due to highly constrained node energy, nanoscale hardware, and short-range terahertz (THz) communication3,5,6. These limitations have led to a vast body of research on broadcast control9, clustering algorithms10, and energy-efficient routing protocols11–16, which are specifically adapted for nano-scale communication systems.

The first communication models were mainly based on broadcast-forwarding techniques. Ni et al. presented the traditional flooding (FLD) scheme, where nodes blindly retransmitted received packets to guarantee connectivity. Although flooding does not require routing information and guarantees wide area coverage, it causes broadcast storm problems8, high energy consumption17, and scalability issues in dense WNSNs.

At the MAC layer, Rikhtegar et al. presented the energy-efficient wireless nano-sensor network MAC protocol (EEWNSN-MAC)18, which is a clustering algorithm-based TDMA protocol for THz nano-communication networks using time-slot-on-the-pulse (TS-OOK) modulation. EEWNSN-MAC significantly alleviates packet collisions19 and idle listening in nano-sensor networks. However, its fixed slot assignment and lack of dynamic MAC-layer routing coordination make it less responsive to dynamic network traffic.

Clustering and routing protocols that consider energy awareness have been widely investigated to maximize network lifetime. Xu et al. introduced the Energy Balance Clustering Routing (EBCR) protocol20, which combines adaptive cluster-head (CH) selection and THz-aware routing to balance energy consumption. Although EBCR improves energy resource utilization and packet reliability, its high control overhead and lack of scalability make it less applicable for large-scale IoNT networks.

To overcome the limitations of deterministic routing protocols, metaheuristic-based routing protocols have been introduced. Gulec presented nanoFFBAT21, a hybrid Firefly-BAT optimization algorithm for routing, which builds energy-efficient multi-hop routes. Although nanoFFBAT maximizes network lifetime, the high computational complexity and routing latency caused by hybrid optimization make nanoFFBAT less applicable for lightweight nano-IoNT networks. Similarly, Gulec and Sahin introduced nanoRDA22, which uses the Red Deer Algorithm for adaptive CH selection and builds a dominating-set-based backbone. Although nanoRDA minimizes the number of CHs and maximizes coverage efficiency, it relies on static clustering and has higher computational complexity.

Decision-driven routing schemes have also been investigated. Al-Turjman introduced the E3A algorithm23, which uses Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)-based multi-metric decision-making to improve routing efficiency and network lifetime. Nevertheless, E3A uses static decision logic and causes additional routing delay in dynamic networks.

Broadcast optimization has been explored independently of routing. Oukhatar et al. introduced the Adaptive Distributed Broadcast Protocol (ADBP)24, which uses a game-theoretic approach to minimize redundant rebroadcasts and maximize packet delivery ratio. Although efficient for broadcast management, ADBP.

fails to incorporate routing and energy-efficient clustering schemes. Relay-based routing has also been investigated. Rathore et al. introduced the Shortest Path Selection Relay Node (SPSRN) protocol25, in which cluster heads (CHs) relay data through shortest paths using relay nodes. Although SPSRN balances energy consumption, it is highly path-dependent and based on static base station location.

Lightweight greedy routing algorithms have been proposed to this end. Al-Turjman and Kilic proposed the LaGOON (LGN) protocol26, which uses backward learning with time-to-live (TTL) messages to guide the routing process. Although LGN significantly cuts overhead compared to flooding, the lack of clustering and local routing decisions prevents effective global energy equalization. To overcome these drawbacks, Gulec presented DEEPNT7, a distributed clustering and CH routing algorithm that significantly prolongs network lifetime and is scalable for dense wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs). However, DEEPNT uses static CH election and lacks learning adaptation, and joint optimization of routing and clustering is not performed.

Findings from underwater wireless sensor networks (UWSNs) have also impacted clustering design. Mohan et al. proposed IMCMR-UWSN27, a hybrid approach that integrates CH election using the chaotic krill herd algorithm (CKHA) with self-adaptive glow worm swarm optimization (SA-GSO) routing. Although IMCMR-UWSN significantly prolongs network lifetime, its complexity and UWSN-specific design prevent direct application to IoNT. Likewise, Murali and Shankar proposed Hybrid PSO-EULC28, which combines particle swarm optimization with unequal layered clustering to enhance energy equalization, but at the expense of increased algorithmic complexity.

Recent studies have focused on adaptive clustering algorithms. Karim et al. proposed the Enhanced Centroid-based Energy Efficient Clustering (ECEEC) algorithm29, which balances the load through centroid-based cluster-head (CH) selection and gateway-assisted routing, although it requires additional infrastructure and static configuration. Guo et al. proposed an intelligent mobile sink routing strategy30, which uses adaptive clustering and ring-based sink monitoring to decrease delay and energy consumption; however, its applicability is highly dependent on accurate sink location. Fan and Xin proposed the FC-CRA31, a fast changes-clustering and routing algorithm that dynamically changes the cluster radius based on path energy functions to overcome energy-hole problems. Although FC-CRA improves network lifetime, repeated re-clustering causes additional overhead.

Additional studies have explored electromagnetic nano-network architectures32, security-aware IoNT communication frameworks33, cross-layer MAC/routing integration34, dynamic THz routing design35, AI-driven metaheuristic routing strategies10, IoT-based intelligent routing integration12, dominating-set lifetime maximization approaches13, recursive geographic forwarding14, and deep reinforcement learning-based adaptive routing15.

Based on the above-mentioned studies summarized in Table 1 and the recent thorough survey work3, it is clear that the existing solutions are mainly focused on addressing the issues of clustering efficiency, routing optimization, broadcast management, and infrastructure support independently. The existing solutions have one or more drawbacks, such as rigid assumptions, high computational complexity, low adaptability to dynamic energy environments, generation of hotspots around cluster heads (CHs) or gateways, and unsuitability in dense IoNT networks. The above-mentioned open issues clearly indicate the need for an integrated, lightweight, and adaptive clustering and routing solution that can efficiently address the trade-offs between scalability, simplicity, and energy efficiency under the tight constraints of nano-sensor technologies. Motivated by these observations, the next section introduces ENCORE, designed to address these gaps and support large-scale IoNT applications.

Summary of related works.

Recent advancements in edge computing and adaptive resource optimization have introduced two-timescale decision-making frameworks, such as Dynamic Human Digital Twin deployment and service migration strategies in Mobile Edge Computing (MEC). These approaches improve task execution accuracy and resource allocation by separating long-term strategic decisions from short-term operational adjustments. Similarly, semi-distributed relay selection algorithms in wireless networks enhance communication reliability through cooperative forwarding mechanisms.

However, these approaches are primarily designed for macro-scale networks and do not consider the extreme constraints of wireless nano-sensor networks (WNSNs), including ultra-limited energy availability, THz communication losses, and dense three-dimensional deployments. As a result, their direct application to IoNT-enabled WNSNs is not feasible.

In the context of WNSNs, existing routing and clustering approaches exhibit several limitations. Flooding-based methods suffer from excessive energy consumption and poor scalability due to redundant transmissions. Gradient-based and greedy routing protocols reduce communication overhead but fail to achieve global energy balancing. Clustering-based approaches, such as DEEPNT, improve efficiency but rely on static cluster-head selection and do not jointly optimize clustering and routing decisions. Metaheuristic-based methods, including nanoFFBAT and nanoRDA, enhance performance but introduce high computational complexity, making them less suitable for lightweight nano-scale networks.

Furthermore, most existing solutions treat clustering and routing as independent problems, without considering the dynamic interaction between residual energy distribution, node density, and spatial topology. This results in energy imbalance, hotspot formation near the sink, and reduced network lifetime.

To overcome these limitations, the proposed ENCORE framework introduces a unified, environment-aware clustering and multi-hop routing mechanism that jointly considers residual energy, node proximity to the sink, and local topology conditions. This integrated design enables adaptive, energy-balanced, and scalable routing decisions tailored for large-scale IoNT-enabled WNSNs.

Therefore, ENCORE bridges the gap between macro-scale optimization frameworks and nano-scale communication constraints by providing a lightweight, energy-efficient, and topology-adaptive routing solution.

Proposed framework

In large-scale WNSNs, energy efficiency remains the primary challenge because of the very limited power budgets and limited energy collecting capabilities of nano-devices. Inefficient routing decisions can cause unnecessary packet forwarding actions, rapid battery drain, and premature disconnections in networks. Since every data transfer, reception, and forwarding operation consumes a quantifiable amount of residual energy in a node. To mitigate such difficulties, this study proposes ENCORE, a novel routing infrastructure designed specifically for IoNT, namely for efficient network clustering and environment-aware route optimization regarding energy efficiency. To mitigate unnecessary data packet retransmissions and balance energy consumption in the network, ENCORE incorporates both adaptive cluster-routing infrastructure along with environment-aware scoring and orthogonal two-hop route optimizations. In highly dense nano-routing networks, ENCORE enhances network lifetime and ensures reliable data communication through efficient clustering and routing design.

These environmental factors collectively determine the suitability of nodes for cluster-head selection and routing decisions, enabling ENCORE to adapt its behavior according to network conditions.

Energy-aware clustering, effective multi-hop routing toward the sink, and neighbor identification are made possible by each nano-device in the suggested model taking part in a structured multi-phase workflow. The overall operational sequence of the ENCORE framework is illustrated in Fig. 1. Every nano-node is first placed at random in a three-dimensional sensing environment with the same beginning energy levels and no past knowledge about nearby nodes. Nodes use spatial proximity queries to locate nearby devices within their broadcast range during each operating cycle. Each node uses this local neighbourhood information to decide if it is possible to communicate directly with the sink or if it is necessary to choose an intermediate relay from neighbours who offer forward progress toward the sink.

ENCORE workflow.

Each node calculates its transmission energy depending on communication distance, packet size, and pulse characteristics in order to precisely simulate true nano-communication behaviour. After each operation, it updates its residual energy accordingly. After evaluating topology, ENCORE starts an energy-aware clustering phase where each node calculates a composite fitness value that favours high-energy and well-positioned nodes by normalizing their distance to the sink and residual energy. In an effort to ensure balanced and energy-optimized clusters for the whole network, a selection of the nodes with the highest fitness values is made for the cluster-head selection. The rest will join the nearest cluster head.

ENCORE establishes a representative multiple-hop routing backbone with the help of loop discovery and next-hop energy adaptation employing the routing links established during the data transmission process. Performance metrics will then be examined, such as the network’s throughput, energy consumption rate, successful reception of packets to the sink node, and the number of nodes in the network in the operational state. The operational life of dense networks in a WNSN environment has been extended by the integrated procedure practiced by ENCORE for accurate energy modelling, identification of the optimal cluster-head, and optimal routing paths.

Cluster head selection

For dense configurations of WNSNs, ENCORE employs an energy-aware and topology-adaptive Cluster Head (CH) selection mechanism to ensure reliable communication structures and balanced energy consumption across the network. In nano-scale networks, where node energy resources are extremely limited and post-deployment energy replenishment is infeasible, randomly or improperly selected CHs can result in rapid energy depletion, early node failures, and network partitioning.

To mitigate these effects, ENCORE enables each nano-sensor node to independently evaluate its suitability for assuming CH responsibilities based on two primary criteria: geometric proximity to the sink and locally available residual energy. Together, these factors capture both the communication efficiency and the expected operational longevity of a node.

Results and discussion

This section presents a thorough assessment of the proposed ENCORE framework through simulation-based experiments. The assessment focuses on network structure, routing performance, energy conservation, lifetime properties, packet delivery performance, and scalability, with comparisons to FLD, LGN, and DEEPNT.

ENCORE uses an energy-aware clustering approach to improve network structure in dense wireless nano-sensor networks. The selection of Cluster Head (CH) is based on both residual energy and distance to the sink node, allowing nodes with better energy and geographical properties to take up aggregation and forwarding roles. Regular nodes are mainly involved in local intra-cluster communication, which greatly reduces unnecessary long-distance communication overheads. The cluster formation behavior of the proposed ENCORE framework is illustrated in Fig. 5.

Clusters formation for 350 nodes.

As shown in Fig. 5, the cluster-head (CH) selection for a typical network deployment of 350 nano-sensor nodes is illustrated. Compact clusters are established around the CHs, leading to shorter intra-cluster communication ranges and better geographical structure. While CHs suffer from extra transmission and reception overheads because of data aggregation and forwarding tasks, the energy-aware CH selection approach can avoid the selection of nodes with high loads. As a result, energy consumption is more evenly distributed across the network, and important nodes are less likely to be exhausted prematurely.

Overall, the clustering phase provides a stable hierarchical communication framework, which is the basis for scalable and energy-efficient multi-hop routing.

After the completion of the clustering phase, ENCORE develops a multi-hop routing backbone that functions solely at the CH level. This hierarchical routing topology limits long-range communication to a short list of energy-qualified nodes and overcomes the inefficiencies introduced by flat routing topologies. A multi-hop routing backbone constructed among cluster heads is illustrated in Fig. 6.

As shown in Fig. 6, the final three-dimensional routing backbone for a sample topology is illustrated. The routing backbone is sink-oriented and loop-free, where each routing decision ensures a unique Euclidean distance reduction to the sink, ensuring deterministic packet transport. Energy-aware optimization is used to avoid an overload of geometrically central CHs with forwarding traffic, thus promoting balanced energy consumption in the network. The organized routing backbone significantly limits communication overhead per round and ensures stable routing dynamics in dense nano-sensor networks.

Analysis of average network lifetime improvement

The observed improvement in the average network lifetime achieved by the proposed ENCORE algorithm is primarily attributed to its integrated cluster-based optimization and adaptive routing strategy. Unlike conventional approaches such as FLD and LGN, which rely on flooding or gradient-based forwarding, ENCORE introduces a balanced combination of energy-aware clustering and cost-efficient routing mechanisms.

First, the cluster head (CH) selection in ENCORE is governed by a fitness function that jointly considers normalized residual energy and relative distance to the sink. This ensures that nodes with higher energy reserves and favorable spatial positions are selected as cluster heads, thereby preventing premature energy depletion of weaker nodes. As a result, the energy consumption is distributed more uniformly across the network.

Second, the routing mechanism is optimized using a multi-factor cost function (Eq. 12), which incorporates geographic progress toward the sink, residual energy of candidate nodes, and forwarding load. This enables ENCORE to dynamically select next-hop nodes that not only minimize transmission distance but also avoid overburdening specific nodes. Consequently, energy hotspots and routing congestion are significantly reduced.

Third, the formation of a structured multi-hop backbone among cluster heads restricts communication to energy-efficient paths, thereby eliminating redundant transmissions and reducing overall communication overhead. Unlike flat routing approaches, where excessive packet forwarding leads to rapid energy depletion, ENCORE ensures controlled and optimized data dissemination.

Furthermore, the inclusion of a load-aware routing component penalizes heavily utilized nodes during next-hop selection, which enhances load balancing and prevents early exhaustion of critical nodes. This contributes to sustained network operation and improved stability during the mid and later stages of the network lifetime.

Due to these combined effects, ENCORE significantly delays the occurrence of First Node Death (FND), maintains prolonged stability during Half Node Death (HND), and extends the Last Node Death (LND) phase. Since the average network lifetime is defined as the mean of these three metrics, the proposed approach achieves a substantial improvement in overall network longevity compared to FLD, LGN, and DEEPNT.

This improvement is further validated by the simulation results presented in Figs. 9 and 11, where ENCORE consistently demonstrates higher LND values and a more balanced distribution of lifetime phases across all evaluated network sizes.

Analysis of packet reception behavior in low-density networks

Although the proposed ENCORE framework demonstrates superior packet reception performance for large-scale networks, it exhibits relatively lower packet delivery compared to DEEPNT in small-scale or low-density configurations. This behavior can be attributed to the structural and operational characteristics of the ENCORE framework.

In ENCORE, a cluster-based hierarchical routing mechanism is employed, where cluster heads (CHs) are responsible for data aggregation and forwarding through a multi-hop backbone. In low-density networks, the number of available nodes within the transmission range is limited, which reduces the diversity of candidate relay nodes. As a result, the multi-hop backbone may become less optimal, and some cluster heads may experience longer or indirect routing paths toward the sink.

Furthermore, ENCORE introduces an energy-aware and load-balanced routing strategy (Eq. 12), which prioritizes nodes with sufficient residual energy and avoids over-utilization of specific nodes. While this improves long-term network stability, it may lead to the selection of suboptimal paths in sparse networks, where geographically closer nodes are sometimes bypassed due to lower residual energy or higher load conditions. Consequently, this may slightly reduce the number of packets successfully delivered to the sink in the early or low-density scenarios.

In contrast, DEEPNT relies on a more direct and less restrictive forwarding mechanism, which allows packets to reach the sink through shorter or more aggressive routing paths in smaller networks. Since node density is low, the risk of congestion and energy imbalance is minimal, enabling DEEPNT to achieve higher packet reception in such conditions.

However, this advantage is limited to low-density scenarios. As network size increases, DEEPNT suffers from uneven energy consumption and routing congestion, leading to rapid performance degradation. In contrast, ENCORE leverages its structured clustering and backbone-based routing to maintain stable communication, balanced energy usage, and significantly higher packet delivery in medium- and large-scale networks, as evidenced in Fig. 10.

Therefore, the comparatively lower packet reception of ENCORE in low-density networks is a trade-off resulting from its energy-aware and load-balanced design, which prioritizes long-term network sustainability over short-term transmission efficiency.

Key performance highlights of ENCORE

The proposed ENCORE framework achieves several notable performance improvements over existing routing protocols, as demonstrated through extensive simulations.

First, ENCORE significantly improves network lifetime, achieving an average lifetime of 47.47 s for a 350-node network, compared to 35.92 s in DEEPNT, representing an improvement of approximately 32%. In large-scale scenarios, ENCORE consistently maintains an average lifetime above 46 s, while DEEPNT remains around 31–32 s, confirming its scalability advantage.

Second, ENCORE achieves the highest packet delivery ratio (PDR) among all compared methods, reaching approximately 0.85, which is about 10–15% higher than recent approaches such as nanoRDA, nanoFFBAT, and E3A. This improvement reflects enhanced routing reliability and reduced packet loss.

Third, ENCORE provides higher throughput values across all network sizes, achieving up to 650 packets in large-scale networks, demonstrating its capability to sustain efficient data transmission under dense deployment conditions.

Fourth, ENCORE exhibits superior energy balancing, as indicated by significantly higher HND and LND values. For instance, the HND value of ENCORE (66.18 s) is nearly double that of DEEPNT (35.93 s), indicating prolonged network stability.

Finally, ENCORE maintains consistent performance across 100 random topologies with low standard deviation, confirming its robustness under varying network conditions.

These results collectively demonstrate that ENCORE achieves the best overall performance in terms of network lifetime, packet delivery, throughput, and stability, making it a highly effective solution for large-scale IoNT-enabled WNSNs.

Conclusion

This paper introduces ENCORE, an energy-efficient cluster-based optimization framework for dense three-dimensional Wireless Nano Sensor Networks (WNSNs) under IoNT constraints. By combining energy-efficient cluster-head selection with structured multi-hop backbone routing and a realistic nano-communication energy model, ENCORE enables balanced energy consumption and prolonged network lifetime. Extensive simulation experiments on randomized topologies demonstrate that ENCORE significantly improves network lifetime, residual energy distribution, and packet reception compared to Flooding, LaGOON, and DEEPNT by up to 45% relative to DEEPNT. These results show that ENCORE is a scalable and reliable solution for large-scale IoNT-enabled nano-sensor networks. Future research will focus on learning-based extensions and large-scale validation using advanced nano-network simulation tools.

Reduced Fetuin‐A Levels Are Associated With Exercise Intolerance and Predict the Risk of Adverse Outcomes in Patients With Heart Failure: The Role of Cardiac–Hepatic–Peripheral Interaction
Authors: Yusuke Tomita, Tomofumi Misaka, Yukiko Sugawara, Yasuhiro Ichijo, Fumiya Anzai, Yu Sato, Yusuke Kimishima, Tetsuro Yokokawa, Takamasa Sato, Masayoshi Oikawa, Atsushi Kobayashi, Akiomi Yoshihisa, Yasuchika Takeishi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExercise intolerance in heart failure arises from multifactorial pathophysiological mechanisms. Hepatokines, liver‐synthesized molecules, regulate systemic metabolisms in peripheral tissues. We previously identified the hepatokine fetuin‐A as being linked to liver hypoperfusion in heart failure. Here, we investigated the role of fetuin‐A in connecting cardiac–hepatic–peripheral interaction.Methods and ResultsWe conducted a prospective study involving 202 consecutive hospitalized patients (mean age, 56.8 years; 76.2% men) with heart failure who underwent cardiopulmonary exercise testing. We measured the serum concentration of fetuin‐A by ELISA. Correlation analysis revealed a negative association between fetuin‐A levels and the ratio of minimum minute ventilation to carbon dioxide production, its slope, and a tendency toward a positive correlation with peak oxygen uptake. Patients with impaired exercise tolerance exhibited lower fetuin‐A levels. During a median follow‐up of 1045 days, 18.3% experienced cardiac events, including 4 cardiac deaths and 33 cases of worsening heart failure. Classification and regression tree analysis identified a high‐risk subgroup with lower fetuin‐A (<24.3 mg/L) and impaired exercise tolerance (peak oxygen uptake<14.2 mL/kg per min). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that this subgroup had the highest risk of cardiac events. In a multivariable Cox proportional hazard model, the combination of lower fetuin‐A and exercise intolerance was independently associated with increased risks of cardiac events.ConclusionsReduced circulating fetuin‐A levels were associated with exercise intolerance in heart failure patients. Fetuin‐A could emerge as a target implicated in exercise capacity connecting cardiac–hepatic–peripheral interaction and as a valuable biomarker for predicting prognosis when combined with peak oxygen uptake.

Impaired exercise tolerance (ET) is a principal feature of heart failure and is associated with a poor quality of life and adverse outcomes in patients with heart failure. 1 The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying exercise intolerance in heart failure are diverse and multifactorial. It is considered that impairments in cardiac and pulmonary reserve, coupled with compromised perfusion and function in peripheral and respiratory skeletal muscles, contribute to exercise intolerance, 1 while the precise molecular mechanisms remain thoroughly investigated. Regular aerobic exercise training has been shown to enhance the quality of life in patients with heart failure. 2 , 3 However, despite the associated benefits and safety aspects, there were modest reductions observed in all‐cause death and hospitalization rates, 2 and adherence to such exercise regimens remains an issue. 4 Unveiling the specific pathways responsible for reduced functional exercise capacity emerges as a crucial clinical priority, holding significant therapeutic and prognostic implications.

Interorgan communications play a pivotal role in heart failure, 5 and liver–heart cross‐talk is emerging as a significant contributor. 6 , 7 Hepatokines, synthesized and secreted by the liver, regulate systemic metabolisms of peripheral tissues 8 and are associated with disease pathogenies. 9 Fetuin‐A, a liver‐secreted heterodimeric glycoprotein encoded by the ASHG gene, plays a role in different tissues with cellular mechanisms, serving as one of the essential hepatokines. 9 We have recently reported that decreases in circulating fetuin‐A levels are associated with hepatic hypoperfusion in patients with heart failure. 10 However, the impact of fetuin‐A levels on ET in patients with heart failure, as well as the combined effects on prognostic outcomes, remains unclear.

The present study aims to investigate the relationship between exercise capacity and circulating fetuin‐A and its prognostic role in patients with heart failure.

METHODS

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Population

This study was a prospective observational study involving 202 consecutively hospitalized patients with symptomatic stage C/D heart failure, who had undergone cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) at Fukushima Medical University Hospital between February 2017 and August 2022. The diagnosis of heart failure was made according to the Framingham criteria by independent board‐certified cardiologists. 11 We excluded patients undergoing hemodialysis and a history of chronic liver diseases. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fukushima Medical University (approval number 823) and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent for study participation.

Measurement of Serum Concentration of Fetuin‐A

Blood samples were collected from the participants and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 minutes at room temperature. 10 The resulting serum was then preserved at −80 °C until analysis. The concentration of serum fetuin‐A was determined using a sandwich ELISA (KE00097; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL) following the manufacturer's instructions. The serum samples were diluted 20 000 times and corresponding standards were applied to each well. Subsequently, reagents were introduced, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (SpectraMax i3; Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA) with a correction wavelength set at 630 nm. The serum fetuin‐A concentrations were calculated based on the standard curve.

Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing

Symptom‐limited CPET was conducted using an upright cycle ergometer (Strength Ergo 8; Fukudadenshi Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) with electrocardiographic monitoring according to the established guideline 12 and our laboratory protocol. 13 Each participant had baseline metabolic data recorded before the examination. Then, the exercise protocol commenced with a 3‐minute warmup at 10 to 20 watts, followed by a progressively intensifying exercise test using a ramp protocol (10–20 watts/min). During exercise, oxygen uptake (Vo 2), carbon dioxide generation (Vco 2), and minute ventilation to carbon dioxide production ratio (VE/Vco 2) and its slope were monitored on a breath‐by‐breath basis using an Aeromonitor AE‐300S (Minato Medical Science Co., Ltd., Osaka, Japan). The average peak Vo 2 was calculated on the basis of the final 30 seconds of activity. The anaerobic threshold was identified using the V‐slope method. This approach plots Vco 2 against Vo 2 and determines anaerobic threshold when the rate of increase in Vco 2 relative to Vo 2 steepens. The carbon dioxide production rates (peak R) were calculated by dividing Vco 2 by Vo 2 at peak exercise. The calculation of the oxygen uptake efficiency slope was derived from the linear correlation between Vo 2 and the logarithmic value of VE observed during the graded exercise. The Vo 2 to work rate ratio reflects the change in oxygen uptake relative to the change in work rate during exercise.

Follow‐Up and Clinical Outcomes

All patients with heart failure were monitored and underwent monitoring through a comprehensive review of their medical records, conducted either at the same hospital or referral hospitals. 10 This follow‐up was aimed at tracking composite cardiac events, including either cardiac death or unplanned rehospitalization due to worsening heart failure. Cardiac death was defined as death due to ventricular fibrillation and heart failure, and worsening heart failure was characterized by symptoms and signs requiring urgent treatment and leading to hospitalization. In cases in which patients encountered ≥2 events, only the initial event was considered for inclusion in the subsequent analysis.

Classification and Regression Tree Analysis

Classification and regression tree analysis enhanced the precision of cardiac event prediction, focusing on evaluating fetuin‐A and peak Vo 2. Classification and regression tree generates a tree‐like model comprising nodes and branches. Nodes signify decision points derived from input variables, while branches depict the resultant paths. 14 The Gini index, a metric employed to generate binary divisions in data sets, is defined by the formula: Gini index=1−Σ (p_i)2. 15 In this equation, “Σ” represents the summation across all relevant terms, and “p_i” signifies the probability of an object being categorized into a particular class. This index is crucial for determining attribute‐based splits in data analysis. The Gini index was used to gauge accuracy at each node, with lower values signifying a more precise distinction between event occurrence and nonoccurrence. The optimal cutoff in the decision tree was determined by identifying the node showing the most significant reduction in the Gini index relative to its predecessor. 16

Statistical Analysis

Continuous data are presented as mean±standard deviation, and skewed data are expressed as median and interquartile range. Categorical variables are presented using numbers and percentages. The comparison of categorical variables used either the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test. Spearman's correlation analysis was employed to assess correlations. Comparisons of values were analyzed by the Mann–Whitney U test, 1‐way ANOVA followed by the Tukey post hoc test, or the Kruskal–Wallis H test followed by the Dunn post hoc test with multiplicity adjustment. Power calculation was conducted to estimate the sample size. Composites of cardiac death or unplanned rehospitalization due to worsening heart failure were determined through Kaplan–Meier analysis, and the log‐rank test was applied to assess their significance. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazard analyses were used to find the predictors for cardiac events. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed based on the scaled Schoenfeld residuals. We conducted statistical analyses using Prism version 8.1.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA), Statistical Package for Social Sciences version 29.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY), and Python version 3.10.12 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE) involved in specific libraries and packages, including pandas, numpy, scipy, and lifelines. 17

RESULTS

The clinical characteristics of all patients with heart failure are summarized in Table 1. The mean age was 56.8±14.5 years, and 76.2% of them were men. The median value for left ventricular ejection fraction was 38.9%±16.4%, and the B‐type natriuretic peptide level measured 135.9 (57.6–364.2) pg/mL. The median value of serum fetuin‐A levels was 23.8 (20.8–27.8) mg/L. Results from CPET indicated a median anaerobic threshold of 10.7±2.7 mL/kg per min, suggesting a moderate level of exercise intolerance. 18

Correlation analysis investigated the relationship between fetuin‐A levels and CPET parameters, revealing a negative correlation between fetuin‐A levels and the minimum VE/Vco 2 and its slope (Table 2). There was a tendency toward a positive relationship between fetuin‐A levels and peak Vo 2. In contrast, fetuin‐A levels did not correlate with peak R or Vo 2 to work rate ratio. The data imply that fetuin‐A is specifically linked to certain aspects of ET, such as pulmonary reserve, as indicated by the minimum VE/Vco 2 and its slope.

We next followed up with the patients for a median follow‐up of 1045 days, during which we observed 37 composite cardiac events (18.3%) encompassing 4 cardiac deaths and 33 cases of worsening heart failure. There were no cases of sudden cardiac death. Following this prognostic finding, a classification and regression tree analysis was conducted to establish the cutoff values of fetuin‐A levels and peak Vo 2 for their prognostic impacts on cardiac events (Figure 1). This analysis adopted a threshold for fetuin‐A levels at 24.3 mg/L and peak Vo 2 at 14.2 mL/kg per min, a value closely aligned with the criterion for listing candidates for cardiac transplantation of 14.0 mL/kg per min. 19 We found that patients with impaired ET (peak VO2<14.2 mL/kg per min) exhibited significantly lower levels of fetuin‐A compared with those with preserved ET (peak Vo 2≥14.2 mL/kg per min; Figure 2).

Subsequently, patients were categorized into 4 groups on the basis of the defined cutoff values for fetuin‐A levels and peak Vo 2, and the clinical data comparisons are summarized in Table 3. The group characterized by low fetuin‐A levels and impaired ET demonstrated elevated B‐type natriuretic peptide levels, lower estimated glomerular filtration rates, and reduced anaerobic threshold values compared with other groups (Table 3). Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that patients with low fetuin‐A levels and impaired ET had the lowest incidence of cardiac event‐free rates (Figure 3). The univariable Cox proportional hazard analysis revealed that the presence of low fetuin‐A levels and impaired ET was correlated with increased cardiac event rates (Table 4). In addition, the multivariable Cox proportional hazard analysis showed that low fetuin‐A levels and impaired ET were independently predictive of an increased risk of cardiac events, even after adjusting for demographic data, commodities, and severity of heart failure (Table 4).

DISCUSSION

Our current study was the first to identify that downregulation of circulating fetuin‐A levels is associated with exercise intolerance in patients with heart failure. The presence of both low fetuin‐A levels and impaired ET was independently predictive of an increased risk of cardiac events in patients with heart failure. Fetuin‐A may serve as a novel target involved in the exercise capacity connecting cardiac–hepatic–peripheral interaction in heart failure, as well as a valuable biomarker for predicting prognosis.

It is increasingly recognized that liver‐derived molecules, hepatokines, have functional impacts on peripheral tissues, including skeletal muscles and therefore are considered potential therapeutic targets in types of metabolic diseases. 8 Among the hepatokines, the present study indicates that fetuin‐A benefits exercise capacity in patients with heart failure. It has been reported that fetuin‐A mitigated the adverse effects of calcium phosphate crystal deposits on vascular smooth muscle cells. 20 It serves as a local calcium mineral scavenger during hypoxic stress, 21 thereby improving vascular reserve and potentially affecting exercise capacity. Fetuin‐A may impact in maintaining preserving ET by protecting vascular smooth muscle cells. In contrast, another hepatokine, selenoprotein P, has been shown to affect exercise capacity by impairing exercise‐induced molecular adaptations in the skeletal muscle in healthy subjects 22 and in patients with heart failure. 23 The unique molecular characteristics of each hepatokine may contribute to differences in their impact on ET, and the specific functional distinctions of fetuin‐A compared with other hepatokines in relation to ET require further investigation. In addition, future studies are needed to demonstrate the association of fetuin‐A and ET with arrhythmic events, sudden cardiac death, or noncardiac death.

Given that peak Vo 2 is recognized as the most influential prognosticator among CPET parameters in heart failure, we chose peak Vo 2 and established the threshold value of 14.2 mL/kg per min through classification and regression tree analysis. This threshold value aligns with the previous evidence that indicates that a peak Vo 2 of 14.0 mL/kg per min served as an independent predictor for the 1‐year mortality rate in patients with heart failure and therefore has been employed as a cutoff value for assessing eligibility for cardiac transplantation. 24 Adjustments to the threshold may be warranted as medical therapy advances and improves patient prognosis, as the threshold for peak Vo 2 could be reevaluated and a lower cutoff may be more appropriate when a β blocker is used. 25 By combining the cutoff value of fetuin‐A with that of peak Vo 2, we could identify the patients at the highest risk for future cardiac events, independent of known risk factors for adverse outcomes. Assessing fetuin‐A, in addition to conducting CPET at hospital discharge, may be useful for risk stratification and enable personalized management strategies. The VE/Vco 2 slope, shown to correlate with fetuin‐A, is crucial for evaluating arterial CO2 tension and physiological dead space during exercise. 26 An elevated VE/Vco 2 slope indicates severe conditions like chronic heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, and interstitial lung disease, predicting death in affected patients. 27 The positive correlation between fetuin‐A levels and the VE/Vco 2 slope could be pivotal in uncovering the underlying mechanisms driving disease progression in these conditions.

Aerobic exercise training for 6 months has been shown to effectively increase plasma fetuin‐A levels in older overweight men, 28 while acute exercise did not affect fetuin‐A levels in normal‐weight and overweight men. 29 Although future research is needed to clarify whether cardiac rehabilitation in patients with heart failure could upregulate circulating fetuin‐A levels, cardiac rehabilitation may be more impactful in preventing adverse outcomes, 12 particularly when targeting the patient group with low fetuin‐A and impaired ET. Furthermore, supplementation with fetuin‐A reduced fibrosis and inflammation after ischemia–reperfusion injury in rodent kidney models. 21 The contemporary landscape of heart failure guideline‐directed medical therapy including angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor and sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor 19 could potentially influence the cutoff values for fetuin‐A and peak Vo 2. Targeting the combination of fetuin‐A and exercise capacity may hold therapeutic potential in heart failure and related complications, yet further study is warranted to fully understand its implications.

This study has several limitations, including conducting at a single center with a relatively small number of patients during a short follow‐up period. Due to its observational nature, future research is required to establish the causal relationship between fetuin‐A and impaired ET.

CONCLUSIONS

Reduced circulating fetuin‐A levels were associated with exercise intolerance in patients with heart failure. Fetuin‐A may serve as a novel target implicated in the exercise capacity connecting cardiac–hepatic–peripheral interaction and as a valuable biomarker for predicting prognosis.

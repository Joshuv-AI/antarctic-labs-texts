A bibliometric analysis of research on PD-1/PD-L1 in urinary tract tumors
Authors: Yongming Chen, Xiaojin Lu, Genyuan Peng, Shengjie Liu, Miao Wang, Huimin Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTProgrammed cell death 1 (PD-1) and programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) are key components in immune checkpoint studies across various tumors, including those in the urinary tract. The utilization of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in urinary tract tumors is on the rise. This study provides a comprehensive overview of PD-1/PD-L1 research in urinary tract tumors through bibliometric analysis. A search was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database for academic papers on PD-1/PD-L1 in urinary tract tumors published between January 1, 1999, and September 3, 2022. Tools such as VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and an online bibliometric platform, were used for an in-depth analysis covering countries, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keywords. A total of 1,711 articles on PD-1/PD-L1 in urinary tract tumors were analyzed. The United States led in article contributions, followed by China and Japan. Harvard University was the top institution in this research area. With notable conctributions from Choueiri TK, who authored 48 related articles. The Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer was the top publisher, and Topalian SL’s 2012 publication in The New England Journal of Medicine was the most cited article. Key author keywords included “immunotherapy,” “PD-L1,” “renal cell carcinoma,” “bladder cancer,” and “immune checkpoint inhibitors.” Notably, research on the role of PD-1/PD-L1 in kidney and bladder cancer has garnered significant attention.

Introduction

PD-1/PD-L1 have emerged as crucial players in cancer immunotherapy, demonstrating significant efficacy across various tumor types. PD-1, also known as differentiation cluster 279, is encoded by the PDCD1 gene located on chromosome 2q37.1 And is widely expressed in immune cells, including T cells and dendritic cells. Conversely, PD-L1, encoded by a gene situated on human chromosome 9p24.1, exhibits widespread expression in numerous tumor cells.2–4 The PD-1/PD-L1 pathway typically protects tissues from autoimmune attacks.5

However, when PD-1 binds to PD-L1, it can induce immune suppression and tolerance in cancer cells, facilitating immune evasion. Immune evasion is a critical strategy for tumor survival and progression, making the PD-1/PD-L1 axis a pivotal target in recent immunotherapy research. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitors for clinical use, particularly in treating urothelial cancer6 and renal cell carcinoma.7

This study aims to investigate the utilization of PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitors in urinary tract tumors through bibliometric analysis. Bibliometric analysis visualizes information and provides insights into the knowledge structure of a specific field. It identifies and summarizes the research frontiers or hotspots by using mathematical and statistical techniques to analyze literature and its characteristics. This approach quantifies research output, allowing comparisons of research status among different countries, institutions, authors, or journals, and helps evaluate cutting-edge research and understand scientific publication trends.8–11

Methods

Data collection

The data was retrieved from the WoSCC, a widely used database comprising nearly 9,000 high-impact journals, on September 3rd, 2022. The search query was formulated as follows: Topic = (urological neoplasm OR urinary tract neoplasm OR urologic neoplasm OR cancer of urinary tract OR urological cancer OR urinary tract cancer OR urologic cancer or urinary bladder neoplasm OR bladder tumor OR bladder neoplasm OR urinary bladder cancer OR malignant tumor of urinary bladder OR bladder cancer OR cancer of bladder OR prostate neoplasm OR prostatic neoplasm OR prostate cancer OR cancer of the prostate OR prostatic cancer OR nephroid carcinoma OR adenocarcinoma of kidney OR renal cell carcinoma OR renal cell cancer OR renal adenocarcinoma OR renal carcinoma) AND (PD-1 OR PD-L1) AND Publication date = (January 1, 1999–September 3, 2022). The search results were downloaded in plain text and tab-separated formats (“Full Records and Cited References”). Only original articles written in English were included. Initially, a total of 2,838 publications were identified, but 1,086 publications were excluded as they were not original articles, and 41 were excluded due to language restrictions (Figure 1). Data analysis

Bibliometric analysis examines relationships among authors, institutions, and countries using indicators such as co-authorship, co-occurrence, and co-citation. VOSviewer and CiteSpace, widely used software tools for bibliometric analysis, were employed to visualize the data. VOSviewer creates network maps based on bibliographic data and represents temporal proximity using different colors, enabling the prediction of future research hotspots. CiteSpace, developed by Chaomei Chen, analyzes co-authorship, co-citation, and identifies research hotspots through citation bursts. An online platform for bibliometric analysis was also utilized to analyze publications of countries/regions.

Results

Analysis of trends in data collection and publication

We collected and analyzed 2,838 publications from the WoSCC database from January 1, 1999 to September 3, 2022 (Figure 1). After excluding 1,086 papers (including reviews, meeting abstracts, editorial materials, online publications, letters, conferences, book sections, and other types) and 41 non-English publications, a total of 1,711 articles were included for analysis. The number of published articles on the research of PD-1/PD-L1 in urinary tract tumors has significantly increased from 2003 to 2021, with the most rapid increase noted from 2014 to 2021 (Figure 2). Discussion

Immunotherapy plays a vital role in treating tumors, with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors being commonly used drugs in this area. This research offers a comprehensive review of the PD-1/PD-L1 research in urologic tumors. Our analysis included 1,711 articles from 2,644 organizations across 68 countries. Strengthening academic exchanges is of great significance for scientific research, as the country’s article output is related to academic exchanges. Academic exchanges are relatively limited to developed countries in Europe and the United States. Harvard University and Choueiri TK are the most productive institution and author, respectively, leading the United States in the number of articles published in this field. Researchers in the United States have conducted in-depth research, with eight of the top 10 researchers coming from the United States. The Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer publishes the most articles, and Topalian SL 2012 (The New England Journal of Medicine) is the most cited reference, playing a significant role in the field’s development. Keyword analysis shows that researchers are primarily concerned with the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of prostate cancer, as well as the mechanism and treatment of bone metastasis.

In recent years, there have been significant advancements in studying the immune system, focusing on the dysregulation of immune checkpoints as a key mechanism in cancer initiation and progression. Various immune checkpoint molecules, including CTLA-4, PD-1, PD-L1, LAG-3, TIM-3, and TIGIT, have been extensively researched.12 Among these, the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is considered the most promising target for immunotherapy.13 PD-1 is found on the surface of immune cells, including activated T cells, B cells, and NK cells, suggesting its involvement in regulating a broad spectrum of immune responses.14 The PD-1/PD-L1 axis also plays a role in complex interactions between immune cells and the local microenvironment, both in pathological processes and physiological immune responses.15–17

The use of α-PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies in immunotherapy has shown promise in restoring the immune response against tumor cells. However, their low response rates and the suboptimal outcomes of single use have led to the exploration of combination therapies. Combinations such as α-PD-1/PD-L1 with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, angiogenesis inhibitors, targeted therapy, endocrine therapy, and other treatments have demonstrated increased response rates and enhanced anti-tumor effects. This approach holds potential for improving the effectiveness of immunotherapy in treating different cancer types.18

A study by Brahmer JR et al. administered varying doses of anti-PD-1 intravenously to 39 patients with advanced metastatic melanoma, colorectal cancer, castration-resistant prostate cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, or renal cell carcinoma. The study showed that intermittent dosing of PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitors exhibited favorable tolerability and displayed promising signs of anti-tumor activity.19 This article triggered the first citation burst in the field and clinical studies with larger sample sizes have emerged since then. Topalian, SL and colleagues increased the sample size to 296 individuals and observed that around 20% to 25% of patients with non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, or renal cell carcinoma exhibited an objective response to PD-1 antibodies. This finding provided initial evidence of a potential link between PD-L1 expression in tumor cells and objective responses.20 It played a key role in the field’s development and received the highest number of citations. Adjuvant nivolumab has shown effectiveness in patients with high-risk muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma and PD-L1 expression levels of 1% or more who have undergone radical surgery.21 Additionally, four highly influential journal articles have maintained a strong citation burst status since 2020. These include Rini BI’s 2019 publication in The New England Journal of Medicine, Motzer RJ’s 2019 publication in The New England Journal of Medicine, Motzer RJ’s 2018 publication in The New England Journal of Medicine, and Mariathasan S’s 2018publication in the prestigious journal Nature. A clinical trial led by Rini BI et al. involving 861 patients with untreated advanced clear cell renal cell carcinoma revealed that pembrolizumab combined with axitinib led to a significant extension in overall survival and progression-free survival compared to sunitinib.22 Motzer RJ et al. demonstrated that avelumab in combination with axitinib and nivolumab in combination with ipilimumab produced superior effects compared to sunitinib in patients with untreated renal cancer, resulting in higher overall survival and objective response rates.23,24 These studies play an important role in the development of immunotherapy for kidney cancer, leading to changes in the clinical immunotherapy regimens. New studies have emerged since then. Masayuki H et al. suggest that PARP1 can be used as a biomarker to predict the response to immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment in patients with PBRM1-mutated RCC.25 Stéphane T et al. demonstrated that AXL, a receptor tyrosine kinase frequently linked to drug resistance and unfavorable prognosis across various cancer types, could potentially be linked to the effectiveness of anti-PD-1 blockade in clear-cell renal cell carcinoma.26 These findings highlight the critical role of combination therapies in immunotherapy for cancer treatment. Mariathasan S et al. found that blocking TGF-β combined with anti-PD-L1 therapy inhibited TGF-β signaling, allowing T cells to penetrate the tumor center and elicit strong anti-tumor immunity, enhancing the therapeutic effect.27 These articles have consistently received a significant citations, indicating their profound impact and continued relevance.

PD-1/PD-L1 blockade immunotherapies have limited efficacy in treating bladder cancer. However, strategies exist to enhance the effectiveness of PD-1/PD-L1 blockers in this context. Bérengère Salomé et al. showed that bladder tumors with a high abundance of CD8+ T cells exhibited improved survival and increased responsiveness to PD-L1 blockade immunotherapy when associated with NKG2A.28 Recent studies have revealed that NEDD4, an important E3 ubiquitin ligase, plays a crucial role in degrading PD-L1 in FGFR3-activated bladder cancer. This discovery offers mechanistic insight and preclinical evidence for the potential of combining immune-targeted therapies in FGFR3-activated bladder cancer.29

Previous studies have suggested that immunotherapy treatments have largely failed in patients with advanced prostate cancer due to its characteristic of being immunologically “cold” tumor.30–33 However, Xiangnan Guan et al. stated that directly blocking the androgen receptor (AR) enhances CD8+ T cell function and sensitizes the tumor-bearing host to effective checkpoint blockade. Inhibiting AR activity in CD8+ T cells prevents T cell exhaustion and improves responsiveness to PD-1 targeted therapy by increasing IFNγ expression.34 This indicates that combination therapies can enhance the role of PD-1 inhibitors to a greater extent,35–37 which has been a hot topic of research in recent years.

However, there are still limitations to our research. Firstly, we cannot include all the latest articles. Secondly, we cannot guarantee that all articles will be included in our search results.

Conclusion

PD-1/PD-L1 are crucial immune checkpoints that have gained significant attention in the treatment of urinary tumors in recent years. Since 2014, there has been a growing number of published articles in this field, with the United States leading the way and Chinese scholars making noteworthy contributions. PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitors play a pivotal role in tumor immunotherapy, and combination therapies involving these drugs have shown the most promising results in clinical research. Specifically, the investigation of PD-1/PD-L1 in renal cell carcinoma remains the most prominent and extensively studied area of research, attracting considerable attention.

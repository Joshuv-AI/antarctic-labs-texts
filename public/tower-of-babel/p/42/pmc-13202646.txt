The mediating effect role of methylmalonic acid on the association between oxidative balance score and diabetic kidney disease: a cross-sectional study from NHANES 2011–2014
Authors: Hongli Yang, Lulu Ma, Lianglan Shen, Wubin Yao, Huaxing Huang, Yan Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a prevalent complication of diabetes. Methylmalonic acid (MMA) reflects mitochondrial dysfunction. Oxidative balance score (OBS) assesses the pro-oxidant/anti-oxidant propensity of lifestyle. This study explored the associations of OBS and MMA with the risk of DKD. In this cross-sectional study, diabetes patients aged ≥ 20 years were identified from National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2011–2014. Dialysis patients, those without serum and urine creatinine/albumin/education/serum vitamin B12/MMA/OBS data were excluded. The weighted logistic analysis evaluated the associations of OBS and MMA with the risk of DKD. Odds ratio (OR) and confidence interval were estimated. In total, 1618 patients were included. The percentage of participants with OBS <20 in the DKD group was higher than the non-DKD group (53.35% vs 44.98%). The percentage of patients with MMA ≥ 151 in the DKD group was higher than the non-DKD group (67.88% vs 42.36%). The risk of DKD was elevated in those with OBS <20 (OR = 1.42, 95% CI: 1.06–1.91). Elevated risk of DKD was observed in patients with MMA ≥ 151 (OR = 2.13, 95% CI: 1.45–3.14). The total effect of OBS on DKD was 1.42 (95% CI: 1.06–1.91). The direct effect of OBS on DKD was 1.38 (95% CI: 1.03–1.85). The percentage of excess risk mediated of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD was 9.52%. In conclusion, MMA potentially played a mediating role in the association between OBS and DKD. The findings may offer an insight for future screening and prevention of DKD.

Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a prevalent chronic microvascular complication of diabetes, which affects approximately 40% of patients with diabetes [1]. DKD is the primary etiology of end-stage kidney disease, accounting for over 50% of all cases [2]. DKD is associated with an elevated risk of cardiovascular events, progression to kidney failure, and mortality [3]. DKD seriously threatens the quality of life and prognosis of patients, and has caused a huge disease burden all over the world [4]. Early detection and management of DKD is of great importance.

Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction were reported to play key roles in the occurrence and development of DKD [5]. However, there is no reliable biomarker reflecting mitochondrial dysfunction at present [6]. Recently, methylmalonic acid (MMA) has been shown to reflect mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress [7]. Higher MMA levels have been identified to be associated with diabetic polyneuropathy [8], and the risk of mortality in patients with diabetes and chronic kidney disease [9]. However, to date, no study has investigated the relationship between MMA level and the risk of DKD. Various studies have confirmed the significance of healthy lifestyle such as antioxidant nutrients, exercise, and weight control in reducing the risk of DKD [10,11]. However, a single factor cannot reflect the overall impact of multiple lifestyles on the oxidative balance of the body. Oxidative balance score (OBS) is a composite score based on multiple dimensions of nutrient intake, exercise, tobacco exposure, alcohol consumption and BMI to assess the pro-oxidant/anti-oxidant propensity of overall lifestyle [12]. A recent study in Korean population found that lower OBS was associated with a higher risk of chronic kidney disease [13]. In addition, OBS was observed to influence the risk of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease by affecting oxidative stress markers such as bilirubin and gamma-glutamyltransferase [14]. Thus, the hypothesis of our study was that OBS might be associated with the risk of DKD through regulating MMA levels.

This study aims to explore the associations of OBS and MMA level with the risk of DKD, and further analyze whether MMA plays a mediating role on the association between OBS and DKD using the data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2011–2014. Subgroup analysis was performed according to age, and complicated with hypertension or not.

Methods

Patients

In this cross-sectional study, diabetes patients aged ≥ 20 years were identified in the NHANES database between 2011 and 2014 as MMA data were available during these cycles. NHANES is designed to assess the demographic, health, and nutritional information in the United States, which is nationally representative of the noninstitutionalized civilian population in the United States [15]. The survey received ethical clearance from the relevant ethics committee, and informed consent was given by all participants. Detailed documentation of NHANES methodology and data is publicly available at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) website. Thus, the use of these data was allowed by the Nantong Clinical Medical College of Kangda College of Nanjing Medical University. In our study, diabetes was diagnosed as any of the following: HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, serum glucose at 2 h following a 75 g glucose load (OGTT) ≥200 mg/dL, fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL, any self-reported diagnosis of diabetes, or any self-reported use of insulin or other diabetes medication [16]. Dialysis patients, those without measurement of serum creatinine, urine creatinine, albumin, serum MMA, and data on information for calculating OBS were not included. Also, patients without data on education level, and serum vitamin B12 were excluded. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Potential covariates

This investigation included potential covariates that were previously reported to be related to DKD, which may impact the relationship between OBS and DKD, or MMA and DKD [17–19]. Age (years), sex (female or male), race (Non-Hispanic White, Mexican American, Non-Hispanic Black, other Hispanic, or other race-including multi-racial), educational level (less than high school, high school grade or equivalent or some college or above), poverty-to-income ratio (PIR) (<1.3, 1.3–3.5, >3.5, or unknown), diabetic retinopathy (yes, no, or unknown), hypertension (yes or no), dyslipidemia (yes or no), cardiovascular disease (CVD) (yes or no), nephrotoxic drugs (yes or no), anti-diabetes drug (insulin, biguanide, other, or no), anti-hypertension drug (yes or no), anti-dyslipidemia drug (yes or no), and serum vitamin B12 (pg/mL) were potential covariates.

Main variables

OBS and MMA were main variables. OBS was calculated based on a scoring method [20]. OBS comprises 16 dietary and 4 lifestyle components. Among these, 15 are classified as antioxidants and 5 as pro-oxidants. The dietary data was derived from NHANES’ 24-h dietary recall. Lifestyle factors included physical activity, alcohol consumption, body mass index, and cotinine levels. Physical activity was quantified as a metabolic equivalent (MET) score. Alcohol consumption was categorized into three groups: nondrinkers (0 gm), non-heavy drinkers (male: 0–30 gm, and female 0–15 gm) and heavy drinkers (male: ≥30 gm, and female: ≥15 gm). Serum cotinine, a metabolite of nicotine, was used as an indicator of exposure to tobacco smoke, encompassing both active and passive smoking. All factors were grouped based on tertiles. Antioxidant factors were scored as 0, 1, and 2 from the lowest to highest tertiles, while pro-oxidant factors were scored inversely, from 2 to 0, across the same tertiles. The total OBS score, which represents the cumulative sum of all factor scores, signifies an enhanced oxidative balance when it is higher. MMA was identified based on the variable LBXMMASI in the NHANES database. The patients were divided into two groups according to the median (<151 nmol/L or ≥151 nmol/L). Sensitivity analysis based on the quartiles of OBS (≥27, 21–27, 15–21, and <15) and MMA (<118 nmol/L, 118–151 nmol/L, 151–209 nmol/L, and ≥209 nmol/L).

Outcome variable

DKD was the outcome, which was defined based on eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 or urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) ≥ 30 mg/g [21]. The eGFRCKD-EPI (mL/min/1.73m2) =141 × min (Scr/κ, 1)α × max(Scr/κ, 1)-1.029 × 0.993age × 1.108 (if female), κ is 0.7 for females and 0.9 for males, α is −0.329 for females and −0.411 for males, min indicates the minimum of Scr/κ or 1, and max indicates the maximum of Scr/κ or 1.

Statistical analysis

The continuous variables were described as mean and standard error (SE), and the differences between two groups were compare using the weighted t test. The categorical data were described as number and percentage [n (%)], and chi-squared test was used for comparison between groups. All the data were subjected to a weighted manner using SDMVSTRA (the masked variance unit pseudo-stratum, SDMVPSU (the masked variance unit pseudo-primary sampling units), and WTDRD1 (dietary day one 2-year sample weight). The weighted univariate logistic association analysis was used to screen out the variables associated with the risk of DKD. Variables with p < 0.05 were adjusted as covariates. The weighted univariate and weighted multivariate logistic association analyses were applied to evaluate the associations of OBS and MMA with the risk of DKD. The potential mediating role of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD was analyzed via establishing M-X, and Y-X + M models (Supplementary Figure 1), and the percentage of excess risk mediated was calculated using the ‘Rmediation’ package. Subgroup analysis was stratified according to age, and complicated with hypertension or not. Odds ratio (OR) and confidence interval were estimated. R version 4.2.3 was employed for data analysis.

Results

Comparisons of characteristics of participants with or without DKD

In total, 2059 patients diagnosed as diabetes aged ≥20 years old from the NHANES database between 2011 and 2014. We excluded dialysis patients (n = 27), patients without measurement of serum creatinine, urine creatinine, and albumin (n = 57), those without data on serum MMA (n = 113), and data on information for calculating OBS (n = 239). As educational level and serum vitamin B12 were previously reported to be associated lifestyles and the risk of DKD [22–24], and MMA is a biomarker of the vitamin B12 metabolism pathway, so patients without data on education level (n = 2), and serum vitamin B12 (n = 3) were excluded. Finally, 1618 participants were included, and the screening process was exhibited in Figure 1.

Among all the patients, 542 participants had DKD, and 1076 participants did not have DKD. The percentage of participants with OBS <20 in the DKD group was higher than those in the non-DKD group (53.35% vs 44.98%). The percentage of patients with MMA ≥ 151 in the DKD group was higher than those in the non-DKD group (67.88% vs 42.36%). The percentage of patients with diabetic retinopathy in the DKD group was higher than those in the non-DKD (15.9% vs 8.31%). More data of participants were shown in Table 1.

The effect of MMA on DKD was 2.10 (95%CI: 1.44–3.08). The indirect effect of OBS on the risk of DKD was 1.23 (95%CI: 1.04–1.54). The percentage of excess risk mediated of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD was 9.52% (Figure 2, Table 5).

Discussion

The present study analyzed the associations of OBS and MMA level with the risk of DKD, and the mediating role of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD was also investigated. We found that OBS <20 or MMA ≥ 151 was related to increased risk of DKD. MMA played a mediating role on the association between OBS and DKD. The findings might provide a reference for the screening and prevention of DKD in the future.

Previously, OBS has been extensively documented in numerous studies, and a higher level of OBS has consistently shown an inverse association with the prevalence and incidence of chronic kidney disease (CKD). For example, there was evidence suggested that higher OBS was associated with lower prevalence of CKD [25]. A higher OBS was associated with a lower risk of incident CKD after adjustments for confounding factors [13]. Wen et al. also revealed that OBS was inversely correlated to the risk of CKD risk in the U.S. population [26]. Additionally, higher levels of OBS and dietary OBS were identified to be associated with a lower risk of DKD [27]. In our study, OBS <20 was identified to be related to increased risk of DKD. OBS is a comprehensive indicator that takes into account various dietary components and lifestyle factors to evaluate an individual’s exposure to pro-oxidants and antioxidants [28]. Higher OBS suggests a stronger antioxidant capacity, and oxidative stress plays a significant role in the pathogenesis of DKD [29]. In addition, a number of studies have explored the effects of lifeway-related factors on oxidative stress and mitochondrial function [30,31]. Effectively managing and improving oxidative balance becomes a crucial strategy for both preventing and treating DKD. The consumption of an adequate amount of antioxidants in the diet, along with the maintenance of a healthy diet and lifestyle that enhances OBS, may contribute to the prevention of DKD.

In addition, a cross-sectional study conducted during NHANES 2013–2014 revealed a positive correlation between serum MMA accumulation and the risk of CKD [32]. Elevated serum MMA levels was found to be associated with more severe renal impairment and an increased risk of both the prevalence of CKD and mortality in individuals with CKD [33]. Circulating MMA levels was also reported to be correlated with all-cause and cause-specific mortality in patients with diabetes [34]. In this study, MMA ≥ 151 was related to increased risk of DKD. The potential mechanisms underlying the association between MMA and DKD might be that MMA is a biomarker of the vitamin B12 metabolism pathway, along with folate and homocysteine [35], which is involved in the adverse effects of CKD progression [36]. MMA has also been considered a biomarker for mitochondrial dysfunction, partially due to its role in disrupting the mitochondrial respiratory chain and promoting the release of reactive oxygen species [37]. Mitochondrial dysfunction is recognized as a contributing factor to the progression of diabetes and its associated complications, such as diabetic nephropathy [38]. Therefore, MMA might regulate the mitochondrial dysfunction to mediate the association between OBS and DKD.

Sex differences-related attitudes and behaviors significantly affect the dietary habits and physical activity, which result in different patterns of healthy or unhealthy lifestyles among women and men [39]. This might be the reason why the mediating effect of MMA on the relationship between OBS and DKD was significant in females. Also, an unhealthy lifestyle was positively associated with the prevalence of hypertension [40], and those without hypertension might have a healthier lifestyle, and the mediating effect of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD might significant in those without hypertension.

This study presents the initial investigation documenting the mediating role of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD, which may provide evidence for the management of DKD in the future. Also, the analyzed data were obtained from the NAHNES database through multi-stage complex sampling, which was well representative of US population. The limitation was that the current study is a cross-sectional study, and we could not establish a causal relationship between OBS, MMA, with DKD, only indicating an association, and thus cannot establish a strict causal temporal sequence. The mediating effect in this study was tested using the product distribution method, which is a type of statistical path analysis. This method is employed to examine the association paths and indirect effects between variables, rather than to confirm strict causal association or mediation. The need for future prospective cohort studies to validate the causal relationship is necessary. Some information collected by the questionnaire may be affected by recall bias, for example, OBS relied on 24-h dietary recall, which may introduce recall bias. Although possible confounding factors that may affect the results were adjusted, due to the limitation of the database, some potential factors such as the duration of diabetes and glycemic control (e.g., HbA1c) and the detailed medication information were not included, which might affect the results. Prospective studies are needed for further confirmation of the findings in our study.

Conclusions

The associations of OBS and MMA level with the risk of DKD, as well as the potential mediating role of MMA on the association between OBS and DKD were evaluated in this study. The results indicated that OBS <20 or MMA ≥ 151 was correlated with elevated risk of DKD. MMA potentially played a mediating role on the association between OBS and DKD. The findings may offer an insight for future screening and prevention of DKD.

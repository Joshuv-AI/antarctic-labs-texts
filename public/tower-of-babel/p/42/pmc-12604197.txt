Incidence and risk factors for unplanned intensive care unit transfer from the postanesthesia care unit: a case-control study
Authors: Na Zhang, Zhen Xue, Bing Xu, Susu Zhou, Jiaqi Chen, Xi Chen, Xin Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the incidence and risk factors for unplanned intensive care unit (ICU) admission from the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) in adults following general anesthesia.MethodsIn this case-control study, we retrospectively collected data from 98,738 adult patients who underwent general anesthesia and entered into PACU between January 2021 and January 2024. Patients with unplanned ICU admission from the PACU (UIAP) were classified as the UIA group. Control group (who were routinely discharged from the PACU to the ward) were matched 1:3 with UIA group based on operative date (± 1 month), sex, and age (± 3 years). Clinical data were collected through medical record. Univariate and multivariate analysis were performed to identify risk factors for UIAP and to develop a predictive model.ResultsDuring the study period, a total of 98,539 eligible patients were transferred to the PACU after surgery, of whom 113 (0.115%) required a UIAP. The occurrence of UIAP significantly increased the risk of ICU readmission [odds ratio (OR) = 21.0, 95% confidence interval (CI): 2.612-168.843, P < 0.001] and 30-day readmission (OR = 6.5, 95% CI: 2.530–16.700, P < 0.001) and did not significantly affect In-hospital mortality. Hypoxemia (34.51%) and delayed awakening (29.20%) were the most common reasons for UIAP. Multivariate analysis identified emergency surgery (OR = 17.494, 95% CI: 4.653–65.776, P < 0.001), ASA classification (OR = 5.662, 95% CI: 1.888–16.981, P = 0.002), ≥ 2 comorbidities (OR = 4.337, 95% CI: 1.867–10.078, P = 0.001), increased intraoperative blood loss (OR = 1.003, 95% CI: 1.000-1.005, P = 0.029) and longer operative time (OR = 1.007, 95% CI: 1.003–1.012, P = 0.001) as risk factors for UIAP. Moreover, increased preoperative hemoglobin level was identified as protective factor for UIAP (OR = 0.975, 95% CI: 0.957–0.993, P = 0.007). Analysis indicates that when preoperative hemoglobin level fall below 120 g/L, the risk of developing UIAP increases by 3.69 times (95% CI: 2.097–6.490, P < 0.001). The nomogram model in this study demonstrated strong discriminatory power (AUC = 0.888, 95% CI: 0.844–0.932) and good calibration upon internal validation.ConclusionIn adult patients who underwent general anesthesia surgery, the incidence of UIAP was 0.115%. UIAP was significantly associated with an increased risk of ICU readmission. We identified emergency surgery, a higher ASA classification, ≥ 2 comorbidities, increased intraoperative blood loss and longer operative time as potential risk factors for unplanned transfer from the PACU to the ICU after general anesthesia. Additionally, increased preoperative hemoglobin level was a protective factor for UIAP patients.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No.ChiCTR2500104205), 12 June, 2025. Introduction

Unplanned intensive care unit (ICU) admission (UIA) after surgery has been recognized as a measurement of quality management and control of patient safety in the perioperative period [1, 2]. UIA can prolong mechanical ventilation support, lengthen ICU and hospital stays and increase hospitalization expenses, and UIA is significantly correlated with multiple organ complications and mortality of surgical patients [3–5]. Postoperative UIA is usually determined in the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) or in the general surgical ward. Previous studies have only analyzed risk factors for UIA in a single surgical type or specific population. Cao et al. reported that in neurosurgical patients, low mean oxygen saturation, hypoalbuminemia, bradycardia, hyperglycemia, blood transfusion, emergency surgery, and pre-existing lung disease were independent risk factors for UIA [6]. Another study reported that in children, UIA was associated with several risk factors: age < 1 year, ASA classification III or IV, longer surgery duration, and general anesthesia [7]. But few reports focused on unplanned ICU admission from the PACU in general hospital and surgical population. Consequently, the incidence and potential risk factors remains unclear. Therefore, identifying potential risk factors for unplanned ICU transfer from the PACU is crucial to improving the safety of postoperative patients under general anesthesia.

This retrospective study analyzed data from adult patients admitted to the PACU following general anesthesia between January 2021 and January 2024, including relevant perioperative clinical information concerning different types of surgery. To provide guidance and information for clinicians and PACU nurses to improve their clinical practice. The primary objective of this study was to investigate the incidence and risk factors of UIA in the PACU.

Methods

This study was conducted at the main surgical center of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science & Technology of China (Anhui Provincial Hospital), which is a provincial-level tertiary general hospital, with orthopedic, general and abdominal, and thoracic.

operations being the featured surgical departments, accounting for 30.22%, 12.95%, and 9.08% of the annual surgical volume respectively. The number of brain and cardiac surgeries is relatively low compared to that of other types of surgeries.

Design and patients

This was a single center, case-control study. Data were collected between January 2021 and January 2024. The inclusion criteria were adult patients (aged ≥ 18 years) who admitted to the PACU after surgery with general anesthesia. Exclusion criteria included: (1) missing crucial PACU records, (2) planned transfer to ICU. The protocol of this research was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China (number: 2024-RE-453). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee. The trial was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No. ChiCTR2500104205, http://www.chictr.org.cn). The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Controls

The clinical data of patients in the UIA group were retrospectively collected. For each case, three patients in control group were selected and matched (1:3) from the electronic medical record system based on operative date (± 1 month), sex, and age (± 3 years). Researchers reviewed the data from patients in the control group to ensure the completeness of the clinical data of the included patients.

Data collection

Patient characteristics and clinical data were collected from the electronic medical record system. The following data were recorded for analysis: General characteristics, including the patient’s age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and previous comorbidities. Preoperative information, American society of anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, the most recent laboratory tests prior to surgery: preoperative prealbumin, albumin, hemoglobin, hematocrit, platelet count, and serum potassium. Intraoperative data, operative date, emergency/elective surgery, type of surgery, surgical classification (I-IV) [8], blood loss, blood transfusion or not, and operative time. The tympanic temperature upon admission to the PACU, the time of PACU admission, postoperative analgesia, the recovery time, the reason of unplanned transfers to the ICU. Postoperative information, postoperative length of stay, length of hospital stay, ICU length of stay, ICU readmission, 30-day readmission, in-hospital mortality.

Definition of variables

In this study, UIAP was defined as not anticipated admission from the PACU to the ICU preoperatively. The adverse event in the PACU including hypoxemia, defined as SpO2 < 90% [9, 10]; and delayed awakening, defined as the inability to regain consciousness at more than 90 min after the end of general anesthesia, as well as failure to respond correctly to external stimuli and verbal commands [11]. Admission to PACU hypothermia was defined as a patient core temperature below 36.0 °C when entering the PACU [12, 13]. Pulmonary diseases included chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma. Cerebrovascular diseases included cerebral infarction and Parkinson’s disease. Other surgical procedures included those from obstetrics and burn and plastic surgery. In-hospital mortality was defined as death from any cause during the hospital stay. 30-day readmission was defined as an unplanned readmission within 30 days of discharge, excluding scheduled readmissions such as those for chemotherapy or routine follow-up examinations.

Statistical methods

Multiple imputation was performed to handle the minimal missing data (< 1% across all variables in this study), ensuring a complete case analysis while preserving statistical power [14]. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation if normally distributed, or as median with interquartile range (IQR) otherwise. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. For comparisons between two groups, independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed continuous variables with homogeneity of variance; otherwise, the Mann-Whitney U test was employed. The Mann-Whitney U test was also used for ordinal variables. Comparisons of nominal categorical variables were performed using the Chi-square test, with continuity correction applied if the minimum expected frequency was less than 5. Binary logistic regression with backward Wald method was used for variable selection to identify factors associated with the outcome. To enhance clinical interpretability, the optimal cutoff value (120 g/L) for preoperative hemoglobin level was determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and this variable was subsequently transformed into a dichotomous variable (< 120 g/L vs. ≥120 g/L) for further analysis. The discriminative ability of the model was evaluated by calculating the area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. Calibration was assessed using calibration curves. The test level was 0.05. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1) and SPSS (version 24.0).

Results

Between January 2021 and January 2024, a total of 98,738 adult patients who underwent general anesthesia were admitted to the PACU. 174 patients were excluded due to missing PACU records and 25 patients with a planned transfer to ICU. Among the remaining 98,539 patients, 113 experienced UIAP, the incidence of UIAP was 0.115% (Fig. 1). The most common reasons for UIAP were respiratory and anesthetic events, followed by cardiovascular events. In the respiratory category, hypoxemia (34.51%) was most prevalent. Delayed awakening (29.20%) was the primary anesthetic cause, while hypotension (12.39%) was the main cardiovascular event. Association of UIAP with short‑term outcomes

Patients in the UIA group had a significantly longer PACU recovery time (119 min vs. 60 min, P < 0.001), postoperative length of stay (10 days vs. 4 days, P < 0.001), and the length of hospital stay (15 days vs. 7 days, P < 0.001) than controls. The median ICU length of stay was 2 days in the UIA group. UIA group patients also showed higher rates of ICU readmission (6.19% vs. 0.29%, OR = 21.0, 95% CI: 2.612–168.843.612.843, P < 0.001) and 30-day readmission (11.50% vs. 1.77%, OR = 6.5, 95% CI: 2.530–16.700, P < 0.001). Two patients (1.77%) in the UIA group died during their postoperative hospital stay. In detail, one patient died of multiorgan failure due to infectious shock, and one patient died of respiratory failure. Risk factors associated with UIAP

After matching, 339 cases in control group who transferred from PACU to ward were selected and entered into analysis. Univariate analysis revealed several factors significantly associated with UIAP, including patient’s comorbidities (coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, ≥ 2 comorbidities), poorer nutritional status (lower preoperative albumin and prealbumin level), and decreased preoperative hemoglobin and hematocrit. Other factors included emergency surgery, ASA classification, whether intraoperative transfusion, blood loss, prolonged operative time and the time of PACU admission (Table 3). Multivariable analysis identified potential risk factors significantly associated with UIAP: emergency surgery, higher ASA classification, ≥ 2 comorbidities, increased intraoperative blood loss, and prolonged operative time, while an increased preoperative hemoglobin level was a protective factor for UIAP (Table 4). Through ROC curve analysis of preoperative hemoglobin levels yielded an AUC of 0.69 (95% CI: 0.62–0.76), the optimal preoperative hemoglobin cutoff value was determined to be 120 g/L (Supplementary Fig. 1). Analysis indicates that when preoperative hemoglobin level fall below 120 g/L, patients exhibit a 3.69 times increased risk of developing UIAP (Table 4).

Nomogram model: construction, predictive performance, and validation

Based on the six selected predictors, a nomogram was developed to visualize and predict an individual patient’s risk of UIAP (Fig. 2). The nomogram’s discriminatory ability was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was 0.888 (95% CI: 0.844–0.932), indicating strong discriminatory power (Fig. 3). Internal validation via bootstrap resampling demonstrated good calibration between the predicted probabilities and the actual outcomes (Fig. 4).

Discussion

This case-control study investigated the incidence and risk factors for UIAP after general anesthesia in adult patients. The key findings of our study are as follows: the incidence of UIAP was 0.115%; the most common reasons for UIAP were hypoxemia and delayed awakening; emergency surgery, ASA classification, ≥ 2 comorbidities, intraoperative blood loss, and prolonged operative time have been identified as potential risk factors for UIAP, while an increased preoperative hemoglobin level was a protective factor.

The incidence of UIAP in our study was 0.115%. A previous study analyzing 4,658 patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery reported 105 (2.3%) cases of UIA [15]. Another study by Katori et al. which investigated 43,553 patients admitted to the PACU for observation, found that 73 (0.17%) required unplanned ICU admission [4]. This result is closer to our findings. Our findings demonstrated a relatively lower incidence of UIAP, which may be attributed to the different types of surgeries and their proportions in our institution, as well as the advancements in surgical techniques in the past few years. Additionally, these discrepancies could have been attributed to differences in sample sizes or to the clinical characteristics of the patient populations in each study.

Compared to the control group, the UIA group demonstrated a significantly longer PACU recovery time, postoperative length of stay, and length of hospital stay. The median ICU stay of 2 days in the UIA group was consistent with previous reports [4]. The ICU readmission rate of 6.19% also aligns with existing literature (6%) [16]. Although in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly between groups, UIA was associated with a substantially increased risks of ICU readmission (OR = 21.0) and 30-day readmission (OR = 6.5).

In this study, hypoxemia and delayed awakening were the two most common reasons for UIAP; both are multifactorial, involving patient, surgical, and anesthetic factors. Advanced age is associated with reduced cardiopulmonary reserve, increased comorbidities, and enhanced sensitivity to anesthetics. Prolonged surgical duration leads to accumulation of anesthetic drugs and promotes atelectasis, while significant bleeding impairs oxygen delivery. All these factors can contribute to the development of hypoxemia and delayed awakening [17–21]. Existing research has confirmed that both hypoxemia and delayed awakening are common reasons for UIAP [4]. Although hypoxemia is a common postoperative condition [22], delayed awakening is relatively uncommon [23].

Our study found that patients undergoing emergency surgery had a higher risk of UIA compared to those undergoing elective surgery. Emergency surgery reflects the patient’s acute condition, insufficient time for thorough preoperative evaluation and optimization, and underlying unstable pathophysiological states, all of which contribute to poorer tolerance of anesthesia and surgery, a more complicated postoperative recovery, and a higher risk of complications, which is consistent with previous findings [6, 24, 25].

This study revealed that ASA classification and the presence of two or more comorbidities were potential risk factors for UIAP. In our study, the most common comorbidities were hypertension and cerebrovascular disease. These patients usually recognized as higher ASA classes (III–IV), indicating a synergistic interaction between ASA classification and comorbidity burden in determining postoperative risk. Previous studies have shown that patients with higher ASA classification and greater burden of comorbidities are more likely to experience UIA [15, 26–28]. For example, a study reported that the risk of UIA was 19.4 times higher in ASA II patients and 16.2 times higher in ASA III patients compared to ASA I patients [27].

Prolonged operative time and intraoperative blood loss were interrelated risk factors that reflect greater surgical trauma and complexity. Extended surgery prolongs exposure to mechanical ventilation, increasing the risk of atelectasis, and contributes to anesthetic accumulation and hypothermia. Major blood loss directly reduces effective circulating volume, impairs tissue perfusion and oxygen delivery, and exacerbates hypothermia and acidosis. All these factors contribute to the occurrence of hypoxemia and delayed recovery. Previous reports indicated that procedures exceeding 4 h are associated with higher ICU admission rates [29]. Other studies have shown that patients with an estimated blood loss exceeding 750 mL were 12.8 times more likely to have an unplanned ICU admission [27]. Together, prolonged surgery and substantial hemorrhage amplify intraoperative instability and predispose patients to a higher risk of adverse postoperative outcomes.

In this study, increased hemoglobin level was identified as a protective factor for UIAP. This finding provides a complementary perspective to previous studies that identified anemia as a risk factor, suggesting that patients with higher levels of hemoglobin may be less likely to need UIAP. However, this result has little clinical practical significance, because in clinical practice, we rarely initiatively increase the hemoglobin level in patients who already have normal value. Therefore, our analysis further quantifies its threshold. Patients with preoperative hemoglobin < 120 g/L had 3.69 times the risk of UIAP compared to those with ≥ 120 g/L. This finding is consistent with previous studies, which also reported a significant increase in risk when hemoglobin level fell below 120 g/L, although the magnitude of the risk (35.1 times) may be attributable to differences in the study populations [27]. Another study also demonstrated that preoperative anemia was associated with adverse outcomes including in-hospital mortality and prolonged hospitalization in patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery. Furthermore, patients with anemia are more likely to be admitted to the ICU postoperatively [30]. Our findings further reinforce the critical role of preoperative hemoglobin levels in predicting the risk of UIAP.

Based on these factors, we developed a predictive nomogram that demonstrated good discriminatory performance in our cohort. This model may serve as a tool to help identify high-risk patients preoperatively. Future studies are needed to externally validate this nomogram and to determine whether targeted interventions for high-risk patients can effectively reduce the incidence of UIAP.

As a retrospective study, our study has several limitations. Firstly, as a single-center study, the generalizability of our findings may be limited by unique institutional practices and patient population characteristics. Second, the selection of analyzed variables was constrained by the data available in our records, potentially missed other unmeasured confounders (e.g., detailed surgical complexity or specific medication use), which could lead to incomplete conclusions. Finally, the risk factors we identified, while significant, should be interpreted as associations rather than causative. Multicenter, large-sample prospective studies are needed to further validate and refine our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this single-center study found that unplanned ICU admission from PACU (UIAP) after general anesthesia was an uncommon (0.115%) but clinically significant event. UIAP was significantly associated with an increased risk of ICU readmission. Respiratory, anesthetic and cardiovascular complications were main reasons for UIAP. We identified several potential risk factors for UIAP, including emergency surgery, higher ASA classification, ≥ 2 comorbidities, increased intraoperative blood loss, and prolonged operative time; an increased preoperative hemoglobin level was a protective factor against UIAP.

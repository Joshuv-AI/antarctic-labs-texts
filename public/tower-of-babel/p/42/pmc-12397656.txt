Development of a prediction model for HER2 low breast cancer using quantitative intra- and peri-tumoral heterogeneity and MRI features on high-spatial resolution ultrafast DCE-MRI
Authors: Hongbing Luo, Shixuan Zhao, Zhe Chen, Juan Ji, Jing Ren, Yongjie Li, Peng Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAccurate preoperative human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status assessment is crucial for guiding treatment selection, particularly with the emergence of anti-HER2 antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) for HER2-low breast cancer. However, current immunohistochemistry (IHC)-based classification is limited by spatial heterogeneity and sampling bias. Quantitative analysis of intra- and peri-tumoral heterogeneity (ITH) on imaging may offer a non-invasive, objective, and reproducible approach to distinguish HER2-low breast cancer from other subtypes. This study aimed to investigate quantitative ITH from high-spatial resolution ultrafast dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (UF DCE-MRI) based kinetic curves in distinguishing HER2 low from HER2 zero or positive breast cancer.MethodsConsecutive breast cancer patients who underwent preoperative high-spatial-resolution UF DCE-MRI were retrospectively enrolled. They were stratified into HER2 zero, HER2 low, or HER2 positive groups based on IHC and in situ hybridization results. Traditional MRI findings and clinicopathological characteristics were evaluated, and personalized ITH scores were constructed using semi-quantitative parameters derived from kinetic curves. Models incorporating ITH, MRI, and clinicopathological distinctions were developed for dichotomized HER2 statuses prediction using multivariable logistic regression. The added value of ITH in the Final Combined Model was evaluated.ResultsThis study enrolled 368 patients, with 45.9% (169/368) having HER2-low breast cancer. The ITH score was higher in HER2 low than that in HER2 zero (P<0.001), but lower than that in HER2 positive (P<0.001). The ITH score was higher in HER2 positive compared to HER2 zero (P<0.001). The Final Combined Model integrating ITH, MRI, and clinicopathological variables achieved good predictive performance, achieving area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.80 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.75–0.86] for HER2 low vs. zero, 0.85 (95% CI: 0.80–0.89) for HER2 low vs. positive, and 0.83 (95% CI: 0.77–0.88) for HER2 zero vs. positive. The corresponding sensitivity/specificity values were 77%/72%, 77%/81%, and 94%/58%, respectively. The ITH score significantly enhanced HER2 status prediction, supported by AUC improvement (DeLong test, P<0.05), along with statistical significance in net reclassification improvement (NRI) (P<0.001) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) (P<0.001) across all tasks.ConclusionsIntegrating ITH from high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI-based kinetic curves improved the non-invasive differentiation of HER2-low breast cancer. This approach may guide targeted biopsy strategies and aid in selecting candidates for anti-HER2 ADC therapy, optimizing HER2-targeted precision medicine.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) demonstrates inherent heterogeneity and is categorized into distinct molecular subtypes based on hormone receptor (HR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, providing a vital framework for personalized treatment (1). HER2 status, in particular, is a pivotal biomarker for clinical decision-making, previously classified as positive or negative via immunohistochemistry (IHC) and in situ hybridization (ISH) assays (2). HER2-positive BC (15–20% of all BCs) (3) is characterized by HER2 overexpression, resulting in increased aggressiveness and poorer prognosis. However, HER2 targeted agents have significantly improved its clinical outcomes (4). Conversely, HER2-targeted therapy was not recommended for HER2 negative BC (IHC 0 and 1+, or IHC 2+ with negative ISH assay). Recently, the DESTINY-Breast 04 trial revealed significant advancements in antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) therapy, emphasizing the noteworthy clinical benefits of trastuzumab deruxtecan in treating HER2-low BC (defined as IHC score of 1+ or IHC 2+ with negative ISH assay) (5). This challenges the binary paradigm of HER2 expression, expanding it beyond negative or positive to include intermediate expression levels.

HER2-low BC, accounting for approximately half of all BC (6), has recently emerged as a targetable subset of BC, based on the evidence from clinical trials of novel anti-HER2 ADC (5,7,8). Simultaneously, these findings have sparked increased interest in redefining the biology, diagnosis, and treatment of HER2-low BC (9). Despite ongoing controversies in current research findings regarding whether HER2-low BC constitutes a distinct entity, previous studies consistently indicate that HER2-low BC exhibits specific genomic (10) and clinicopathological characteristics (11,12). Further, it also shows variations in therapy response and prognosis compared to HER2 zero or HER2-positive BC (11,13,14).

Current HER2 status determination relies solely on semi-quantitative assessment of HER2 protein expression via IHC and ERBB2 gene amplification through ISH. However, these methods face limitations in ADC therapy due to spatial, temporal, and intra- and peri-tumoral heterogeneity (ITH) (15), as well as technical constraints from formalin fixation and sampling bias (16). Spatial heterogeneity of HER2 status is demonstrated by the declining concordance between core needle biopsy (CNB) and surgical specimens as the classification system becomes more complex (17). The highest discordance occurs in CNB-HER2 zero cases, with 42.8% reclassified as HER2 low upon excision. Temporal heterogeneity is also evident, as HER2 zero and HER2 low tumors exhibit greater expression variability after neoadjuvant therapy (18) and reduced stability between primary and metastatic sites compared to HER2-positive tumors (19). Furthermore, ITH complicates diagnosis, with approximately 10% of tumors showing variable HER2 expression within the same lesion (20). These dynamic HER2 status changes impact therapeutic response and resistance to HER2-targeted therapies, underscoring the need for advanced imaging techniques that provide non-invasive, real-time assessment of tumor heterogeneity to aid precise biopsy targeting and identify HER2 low candidates for ADC therapy.

However, the imaging distinctions among HER2 low, HER2 zero, and HER2-positive BC are still elusive. Three pioneering studies delved into multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) radiomics to differentiate HER2 low from HER2 zero or HER2-positive BC. Their multiparametric MRI-based radiomic models achieved area under the curve (AUC) values ranging from 0.77 to 0.87 in discriminating HER2 low from HER2 positive in various studies (21,22) and AUC values from 0.71 to 0.82 in discriminating HER2 low from HER2 zero in different datasets within a single study (23). However, these radiomic studies did not effectively address the nuanced challenge of comprehensively differentiating HER2-low BC, particularly in the domain of temporal ITH, which is conceptually more understandable but challenging to characterize. Hence, it is necessary to develop innovative imaging techniques to enhance the characterization of HER2-low BC.

Ultrafast dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (UF DCE-MRI) is an emerging four-dimensional imaging technique that enables noninvasive visualization of tumors with high temporal resolution alongside acceptable spatial resolution (24). Moreover, quantitative analysis of the time-signal intensity (kinetic) curve derived from UF DCE-MRI enables in vivo assessment of tumor vascularity and perfusion, thereby providing a foundation for characterizing ITH. Prior studies have demonstrated its clinical utility in various aspects of BC management, including screening (25), diagnostic assessment (26,27), molecular subtype differentiation (28,29), and monitoring treatment response (30,31). Moreover, some studies revealed that specific quantitative features based on UF DCE-MRI were associated with BC aggressiveness (32-34). Shin et al. showed that HER2-positive BC had a shorter time-to-enhancement (TTE) compared to HER2-negative cases (34). However, the distinction in ITH, characterized by quantitative parameters derived from UF DCE-MRI-based kinetic curves, between HER2 low and HER2 zero or positive BC remains unknown.

The evidence (35,36) indicates well-documented HER2 heterogeneity in BC. The complexity of HER2-low BC necessitates an updated, and more comprehensive imaging approach. Integrating ITH with MRI and clinicopathological variables is essential for improving the prediction of HER2 status. Thus, our hypothesis was that ITH, characterized through quantitative parameters derived from clustering voxels with similar enhancement patterns of kinetic curves on high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI, could serve as promising imaging biomarkers for discerning HER2 status, particularly in characterizing HER2-low BC. We aimed to compare the quantitative and qualitative image differences among HER2 zero, HER2 low, and HER2-positive BC, investigating whether high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI-based features could help differentiate them. Additionally, we analyzed disparities in clinicopathological characteristics among the three newly identified HER2 subtypes. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-24-976/rc) (37).

Methods

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sichuan Cancer Hospital (No. SCCHEC2015029) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived.

Patients

Between September 2017 and December 2019, this study retrospectively enrolled 654 consecutive female patients. The inclusion criteria comprised patients with a breast mass who underwent surgical treatment and received high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI within 1 week prior to surgery. A total of 183 cases overlapped with a prior investigation that utilized radiomics to explore a different clinical question (38). Exclusion criteria were: benign lesions (n=82), pure ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) (n=78), prior breast surgery (n=64), history of neoadjuvant or induction chemotherapy (n=48), occult BC (n=2), lymphoma (n=3), and inadequate imaging quality (n=9) (Figure 1). Assessed clinicopathological variables included age, menopausal status, gestational history, family history of BC, and personal history of other tumors.

Flowchart showing criteria for patient inclusion and exclusion. HER2 categorization follows American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines (2). DCE-MRI, dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging; DCIS, ductal carcinoma in situ; HER2, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2.

HER2 status and pathologic analysis

IHC scores of HER2 expression were centrally determined by an expert pathologist (J.J. with 10 years of experience in BC pathology interpretation) based on surgical pathology, following the American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines (2). Tumors with equivocal scores of 2+ underwent further ISH testing. In this study, all retained patients were categorized into three groups according to their true HER2 status: HER2 zero (IHC score of 0), HER2 low (IHC score of 1+ or 2+ without ISH amplification), or HER2 positive (IHC score of 3+ or 2+ with ISH amplification).

The presence and nuclear grade of intraductal components (IC) within invasive cancers were evaluated. Estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) positivity were defined as ≥1% of tumor cell nuclei showing positive staining in at 10 high-power fields.

MRI protocol

All eligible patients underwent MRI on the 3.0 T scanner (Skyra, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) with a 16-channel breast coil. Axial T2WI images were acquired using Turbo-Inversion Recovery-Magnitude sequences (TIRM). Axial T1-weighted UF DCE-MRI images were acquired using the CAIPIRINHA-Dixon-TWIST-VIBE sequence (CDT-VIBE) (Siemens Healthcare) (38), comprising 26 phases and lasting for at least 5 minutes to generate kinetic curves of the entire acquisition process. Gadodiamide (0.1 mmol/kg; Omniscan, GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA) was injected at 2.5 mL/s, followed by a 20 mL saline flush at the same rate.

MRI features interpretation

Two radiologists, H.L. (15 years of breast imaging experience) and Z.C. (5 years of breast imaging experience), independently evaluated DCE-MRI and T2WI features based on the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) MRI lexicon, second edition, for inter-observer agreement. For intra-observer agreement, Radiologist 1 re-analyzed images from 60 randomly selected patients after a one-month interval. The early-phase DCE-MRI images were used to assist in accurately localizing the index tumor on T2WI. Both radiologists were blinded to pathological information. H.L.’s assessments were used for subsequent statistical analyses. Detailed MRI features based on DCE-MRI and T2WI in this study were provided in Table S1. Peritumoral edema (PE) was categorized into five types according to its distribution within the ipsilateral breast, as defined in prior studies (39,40): Type 0 (no edema), Type 1 (localized PE), Type 2 (pre-pectoral edema), Type 3 (subcutaneous edema), and Type 4 (diffuse edema).

Intra and peri-tumor heterogeneity (ITH) characterization

The ITH characterization, including segmentation, semi-quantitative parameter analysis, ITH analysis and personalized ITH score construction, was performed by S.Z. using MATLAB (R2018b, https://www.mathworks.com) (Figure 2).

Segmentation

The intra-tumoral volume region of interest (VOI), encompassing the entire MRI-visible tumor, was manually delineated slice by slice in axial plane using ITK-SNAP (version 3.8.0, http://www.itksnap.org) by Z.C. The largest tumor was selected as the index tumor when there were multiple lesions. The peritumoral VOI was generated by equidistant 3D dilation of the intra-tumoral region by 4 mm. To evaluate reproducibility, 60 randomly selected cases were independently re-segmented by a second radiologist (H.L.) for intraclass correlation coefficient analysis.

Semi-quantitative parameters analysis

Semi-quantitative analysis was performed by expressing signal intensity changes as percentage ratios relative to the pre-contrast baseline across the entire post-contrast series. Seven parameters were then computed from the mean voxel values within each VOI:

The quantitative signature (QS) was computed through univariate and multivariate analyses of these semi-quantitative parameters across dichotomized HER2 statuses (HER2 zero vs. low, zero vs. positive, low vs. positive BC, respectively).

Clustering strategy for ITH analysis

To characterize ITH, voxels within each VOI were grouped based on their time-to-peak (TTP) values, which reflect variation in contrast enhancement kinetics (41). Voxels reaching peak enhancement earliest and latest were defined as top-K (fastest enhancing) and bottom-K (slowest enhancing), respectively. For both the tumor and peri-tumor regions, ten voxel subsets were constructed (top-10 to top-50 and bottom-10 to bottom-50). Within each subset, seven semi-quantitative parameters (TTE, MS, SImax, TTP, WIS, AUC, and WR) were recalculated to capture kinetic diversity, yielding a comprehensive set of quantitative descriptors for ITH.

Personalized ITH score construction

ITH parameters were excluded from further analysis if their intraclass correlation coefficient was <0.75 or Pearson correlation coefficient exceeded 0.80. The residual ITH parameters were standardized using z-score and normalized via the min-max scaling method. The most predictive ITH parameters associated with true dichotomized HER2 status were selected using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method with 10-fold cross-validation. Subsequently, logistic regression was employed to construct a model based on these selected ITH parameters. Finally, ITH score, was calculated for each patient based on the coefficients of the constructed model.

Model development for HER2 status prediction

Three classification models were constructed to differentiate dichotomized HER2 status: an MRI-based model, an Imaging Combined Model, and a Final Combined Model. The MRI model was built using MRI features through univariable and multivariable analysis. The Imaging Combined Model incorporated significant MRI features from univariable analysis together with the QS and ITH score. The Final Combined Model integrated significant clinical-pathological characteristics and MRI features identified in the univariable analysis, QS, and ITH score. These models were developed using multivariable logistic regression with backward stepwise selection, applying the likelihood ratio test and Akaike’s information criterion as the stopping criterion.

Model evaluation

The model’s discriminatory capacity was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, reporting the AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV), with thresholds determined by the Youden index. Box plots were used to visualize the predictive performance of the ITH scores and the Final Combined Models for dichotomized HER2 status prediction. The added value of ITH in the Final Combined Model was assessed by comparing the AUC using the DeLong test, supplemented with net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) analyses. The calibration and clinical utility were evaluated using calibration plots with Hosmer-Lemeshow test and decision curve analysis (DCA). Nomograms were constructed for personalized dichotomized HER2 status prediction.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.3, http://www.r-project.org). The specific R packages used are detailed in Appendix 1. Missing data were excluded from further analysis. Differences in baseline clinicopathological variables between dichotomized groups were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Chi-squared test, as appropriate. Cohen’s κ coefficient was calculated to assess inter- and intra-observer agreement for MRI features. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The sample size was determined based on patient availability within the defined retrospective study period. Our sample size meets the minimum requirement calculated using the “WebPower” package in R, with a significance level (α) of 0.05, and a power of 0.8.

HER2-low BC displayed notable distinctions in clinicopathological features, including a higher incidence of IC presence (P=0.03), distinct molecular types (P<0.001), a higher rate of ER and PR positivity (P<0.001, P=0.004), and lower Ki-67 levels (P=0.004) compared to HER2 zero. In comparison to HER2-positive BC, HER2-low BC exhibited a lower ratio of no special type BC (P=0.02), lower cell grade (P=0.02), higher prevalence of non-high nuclear grade IC (P<0.001), higher rate of ER and PR positivity (P<0.001), and lower Ki-67 levels (P<0.001).

MRI features differences between dichotomized HER2 status

Univariate analysis revealed that HER2-low BC had a smaller short diameter (P=0.046) compared to HER2 zero. In comparison to HER2-positive BC, HER2-low BC exhibited no or slight PE (P=0.04), more dense fibro-glandular tissue (P=0.04), and smaller long and short diameters (P<0.001). Besides, HER2-positive BC demonstrated more non-mass enhancement (NME), a larger long diameter, and a higher incidence of peri-tumoral early NME compared to HER2 zero BC (P<0.05) (Table S2). The inter- and intra-observer agreement of MRI features were provided in Table S3.

Model development and evaluation

For each dichotomized task, MRI model, QS, ITH score, Imaging Combined Model, and Final Combined Model, five models were developed. The performance of the Final Combined Models in distinguishing dichotomized HER2 status was presented in Table 2, with visualizations in Figures 5,6. The Final Combined Models achieved good predictive performance with AUC values of 0.80 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.75–0.86] for HER2 low vs. zero, 0.85 (95% CI: 0.80–0.89) for HER2 low vs. positive, and 0.83 (95% CI: 0.77–0.88) for HER2 zero vs. positive. The performances of other models were provided in Table S5 and visualized in Figure S1.

Calibration and clinical use

For each dichotomized task, a nomogram was created using predictors from the Final Combined Model (Figure S2). The calibration curves of the nomograms yielded non-significant results for HER2 low vs. zero prediction (χ2=9.31, P=0.32), HER2 low vs. positive prediction (χ2=3.96, P=0.86), and HER2 zero vs. positive prediction (χ2=4.36, P=0.82) (Figure S3). DCA demonstrated that the models with ITH score provided greater net benefit in predicting dichotomized HER2 status compared to those without ITH score (Figure S4).

Discussion

Accurate preoperative prediction of HER2 status is crucial for guiding individualized therapy, particularly for the majority of HER2 low. Our study quantified personalized ITH across different HER2 status using high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI-based kinetic curves analysis. We found that HER2-low BC demonstrated a unique ITH profile, characterized by a higher ITH score (0.71±0.17) than HER2 zero (0.53±0.19; P<0.001) and a lower score than HER2-positive BC (0.32±0.18 vs. 0.50±0.21; P<0.001). Disparities in clinicopathological and MRI features among HER2 statuses are also evident. Leveraging these insights, our Final Combined Models achieved good predictive performance with AUC values of 0.80 for HER2 low vs. zero, 0.85 for HER2 low vs. positive, and 0.83 for HER2 zero vs. positive. Importantly, the inclusion of the ITH score significantly improved the prediction of dichotomized HER2 status, as evidenced by statistical significance in the Delong test (P<0.05), categorical NRI (P<0.001), and IDI (P<0.001) across all tasks.

In parallel, our findings resonate with three recent groundbreaking radiomic studies in this domain. One noteworthy study focused on utilizing multiparametric MRI radiomics to distinguish HER2 zero from HER2 low and positive BC. By integrating pathology and MRI features with radiomic signatures, they also attempted to develop a model discriminating HER2 low from HER2-positive BC, achieving an AUC of 0.77 (21). Another investigation employed radiomic signatures to differentiate between HER2-positive and -negative BC, and subsequently, to distinguish HER2 low from HER2 zero BC, albeit with somewhat unstable accuracies (AUC declining from 0.82 in training to 0.71 in external validation) (23). A recent study developed a nomogram that integrated multiparametric MRI radiomic signature with key pathologic features (histologic grade III, high Ki-67, positive PR) to predict HER2 low from HER2-positive BC. Consistent with our findings, their nomogram demonstrated high accuracies, with AUC values of 0.87, 0.83, and 0.80 in the training, internal validation, and external validation sets, respectively (22). Notably, our results highlight significant variations in personalized ITH scores among different HER2 statuses especially for HER2 low, unraveling the intricate landscape of HER2 heterogeneity as evidenced in recent studies (35,36). The personalized ITH score, derived from selected kinetic parameters, provides a non-invasive measure of tumor heterogeneity, capturing differences in angiogenesis, perfusion dynamics, and extracellular matrix composition (24). These findings offer valuable biological insights and enhance HER2 status prediction. Consistent with prior research (42), our study reinforces the value of non-invasive, reproducible, in vivo imaging for characterizing HER2 heterogeneity, highlighting its potential clinical utility in the era of ADC therapy.

The established method for determining HER2 status involves semi-quantitatively detecting HER2 protein overexpression on IHC and ERBB2 gene amplification through ISH in a single biopsy. However, this gold standard method has limitations, particularly in the evolving era of ADC. Firstly, the subjective categorization of HER2 status based on a limited sample in vitro is not comprehensive, given the spatial and temporal heterogeneity and dynamic HER2 expression (6,36). A recent large-scale cohort study has demonstrated intra-patient primary-to-metastatic and inter-metastatic HER2 heterogeneity (19). Besides, methodological limitations of the IHC platform could impact results. Formalin fixation may artificially reduce the detection of protein expression, and a semiquantitative assay like IHC might lack the sensitivity to accurately detect low levels of HER2 expression. A study examining local and centralized HER2 inconsistency revealed that up to 76% of patients initially classified as IHC 0 were later identified as 1+ (16). Our method addresses these challenges by enabling real-time tumor heterogeneity assessment and precise biopsy guidance. Additionally, our models could assist clinicians in identifying patients with a high likelihood of HER2-low BC, aiding in the selection of candidates for ADC treatment.

There are several limitations in this study. First, its retrospective design may introduce inherent variability and selection bias. Future prospective studies are needed to validate the findings. Second, the subjective interpretation of MRI features may lead to observer variability; however, inter- and intra-observer agreement analyses demonstrated satisfactory consistency in our study. Third, the models were developed using high-spatial resolution UF DCE-MRI acquired on a single scanner at a single center. As such, the influence of inter-scanner variability was not assessed. External validation using multicenter datasets and different MRI systems is necessary to improve the generalizability of our results.

Conclusions

Our personalized ITH-based models offer a non-invasive prediction of HER2 status, particularly for HER2 low, providing guidance for precise biopsies and assisting in the selection of candidates for ADC treatment.

1. Background

In the 21st century, tuberculosis (TB) continues to pose a significant global health challenge despite medical advancements. According to the WHO’s 2020 Global TB Report, Southeast Asia accounts for over 44% of TB cases, while Africa reports 25%. TB has surpassed AIDS as the leading cause of infectious mortality before the COVID-19 pandemic, with 14.1 million people receiving treatment in 2018-2019, projected to reach 40 million by 2022. Preventive measures aim to target 30 million people by the same year, and timely diagnosis and treatment from 2000 to 2019 saved an estimated 60 million lives ( 1 , 2 ). The Global TB Report 2022 noted a decrease in cases from 7.1 million in 2019 to 5.8 million in 2020, with a partial increase to 6.4 million in 2021. In Iran, there were 34,744 new TB cases reported from 2011 to 2018, with 27,752 recoveries in 2021 ( 3 ). The emergence of drug-resistant strains remains a serious concern, contributing to rising global TB prevalence. Despite efforts to improve vaccination practices, the report of one million annual deaths highlights the ongoing severity of tuberculosis risk ( 4 , 5 ).

Early detection is crucial for controlling tuberculosis (TB), especially in developed countries, where tests like the skin test and interferon gamma release assay (IGRA) are vital for preventive policies. However, in developing countries, these tests are underutilized due to high costs and the risk of reinfection ( 6 ). Interferon-gamma release assays (IGRAs) are blood-based immunological tests that detect latent or active Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection by measuring the release of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) from sensitized T-cells in response to specific M. tuberculosis antigens, such as ESAT-6 and CFP-10. Unlike the tuberculin skin test (TST), IGRAs are not influenced by prior Bacillus Calmette–Guerin (BCG) vaccination or most environmental non-tuberculous mycobacteria, making them more specific for M. tuberculosis. The two most commonly used commercial IGRAs are QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus (QFT-Plus) and T-SPOT.TB, both of which are widely implemented in clinical practice and epidemiological studies.

Recent guidelines from the WHO and CDC have endorsed IGRAs as suitable alternatives to TST (Tuberculous Skin Test) in many clinical scenarios, particularly in high-income and low TB incidence settings. However, IGRAs still face limitations, including reduced sensitivity in immunocompromised individuals, inability to distinguish between latent and active TB, and higher cost compared to TST. Despite these limitations, IGRAs remain a cornerstone in TB control programs due to their rapid turnaround, single patient visit, and improved specificity, prompting continuous development of novel antigenic targets to enhance their diagnostic performance ( 7 - 9 ).

ESAT-6, a 6-kilodalton protein expressed in mycobacteria, is notable for its strong antigenic properties. It is uniquely present in pathogenic species such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Mycobacterium bovis, and Mycobacterium africanum, but absent in non-pathogenic species and the BCG vaccine ( 10 ).

ESAT-6, encoded by the RV3875 gene in the RD1 operon, becomes detectable after 7 days of bacterial growth. Studies show that the immune system recognizes ESAT-6 early in infection, triggering robust cellular immune responses. Upon re-exposure, memory lymphocytes produce cytokines like IFN-γ, IL-10, and IL-4. The IGRA diagnostic test utilizes the immunological response to tuberculosis and is known for its high accuracy ( 11 ). In the IGRA test, minimal activity of antigen-presenting cells is noted, relying mainly on T cells previously exposed to the antigen. Improving the availability of T cell-specific epitopes by separating them from their native structures could enhance the stimulation of effector T cells compared to using complete antigens ( 12 , 13 ).

2. Objectives

In the present study, we designed and engineered recombinant ESAT-6 protein to identify T cell-specific antigenic regions with the aim of evaluating its immunogenic potential in inducing interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) secretion from lymphocytes of individuals infected with tuberculosis.

Materials and Methods

3.1. Protein Engineering

To engineer the ESAT-6 protein of M. tuberculosis, its cDNA and protein sequences were obtained from Gen Bank with DQ451152.1 number.

(https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/DQ4511 52.1?report=fasta). These sequences were used to predict linear T cell epitopes using a T cell epitope prediction tool (http://tools.iedb.org/main/tcell/). The process involved in putting the protein sequence into the software, selecting the host type for T cell prediction, choosing prevalent Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) alleles, and configuring other recommended settings.

A total of 135 amino acid epitopes were predicted and sorted according to their rank after removing similar and allele sequences. The molecular weight of the engineered protein was calculated using the ExPASy tool (https://web.expasy.org/compute_pi/), confirming it to be approximately 14 kilodaltons. The 3D structure of the engineered protein was predicted using the I-TASSER tool (https://zhanggroup.org/I-TASSER/). Finally, the gene with the engineered sequence was constructed by Pishgam Biotech Company in Iran.

3.2. Cloning of Target Gene ESAT-6

To perform restriction cloning of the constructed gene, compatibility for efficient cloning was ensured by using the NEBcutter tool to identify restriction sites within the DNA sequences, confirming that the template did not contain sites for certain endonucleases. NdeI and XhoI restriction enzymes were selected for digesting the PCR product. The engineered sequence was used as a template for PCR amplification of the ESAT-6 gene, with designed cloning primers: forward primer 5’ GAGCTCCGCATAGTCCCGCAG 3’ and reverse primer 5’ CACTGGTCGTAAGTAGTATAC 3’, which included artificial restriction enzyme recognition sites at their ends.

The PCR conditions were as follows: 95 ºC for 5 min, followed by 30 cycles of 94 ºC for 1 min, 60 ºC for 30 s, and 72 ºC for 30 s, with a final step at 72 ºC for 5 min. The 50 µL reaction mixture included 1X PCR buffer, 1.5 mM MgSO4, 200 µM dNTPs, 0.5 µM of each primer, and 1 U Taq DNA polymerase in DNase-free water. The products were analyzed on a 1% agarose gel.

The PCR products and plasmid were subjected to double digestion with NdeI and XhoI restriction enzymes (1.5 µL of each enzyme in 1X reaction buffer at 37 °C for 16 h), and the digested products were analyzed on a 1% agarose gel. The digested product was ligated into a pET21a expression vector using T4 DNA ligase. The ligation reaction mixture contained 2 µL of 10X T4 DNA ligase buffer, vector DNA (0.020 pmol), insert DNA (0.060 pmol), and 1 µL of T4 DNA ligase, in a total volume of 20 µL adjusted with nuclease-free water. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 10 min, heat inactivated at 65 °C for 10 min, and chilled on ice. Next, 5 µL of the ligation mixture was transformed into 50 µL of BL21 competent E. coli. The recombinant plasmid pET21a-ESAT-6 from positive PCR colonies was purified from cultured cells and subjected to plasmid DNA sequencing. Transfected colonies were identified by growth on antibiotic- containing LB agar plates (70 µg.mL-1 ampicillin) under standard culture conditions ( 14 ).

3.3. Expression of Protein ESAT-6

Transformed E. coli BL21 cells carrying the recombinant pET-21a (+) plasmid were used to express the ESAT-6 protein. The bacteria were cultured under 37 °C, 5% CO2 in Luria-Bertani (LB) broth for 48 hours. The transformed bacteria were cultured in LB broth with 70 µg/ml ampicillin under 37 °C, 5% CO2. When the optical density (OD) of the culture reached 0.6-0.7 at 600 nm, protein expression was induced by adding 1 mmol/ml IPTG. The culture was stirred at 220 rpm and 25 °C for 4 hours. After freezing overnight at -20 °C, the cells were lysed by sonication (6×30 sec at 40% amplitude) using a Branson Digital Sonifier, followed by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 5 minutes. The expressed protein was visualized in the lysed cells by SDS-PAGE using tricine gels with 12% polyacrylamide, followed by Coomassie blue staining ( 15 ).

3.4. Protein Extraction

Protein was extracted from the lysates of E. coli BL21 cells expressing ESAT-6 protein. After freezing overnight at -20 °C, the cells were lysed by sonication (6×30 sec at 40% amplitude) using a Branson Digital Sonifier, followed by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 5 minutes. The expressed protein was visualized in the lysed cells by SDS-PAGE using tricine gels with 12% polyacrylamide, followed by Coomassie blue staining ( 15 ).

We collected cells by centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 20 minutes and re-suspended in lysing buffer. The cells were lysed via sonication (6×30 sec at 40% amplitude) and centrifuged at 1300 rpm for 20 minutes at 4 °C. The lysis process was repeated, and then proteins were extracted using urea at increasing concentrations (2 mM, 4 mM, and 8 mM). After each step, the purified protein was collected by centrifugation and analyzed using SDS-PAGE. The protein was transferred to a PVDF membrane for western blot detection. Western blotting was performed using the following reagents: Primary antibody: Mouse anti-His tag monoclonal antibody (1:2000; Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# H1029). Secondary antibody: HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (1:5000; Abcam, Cat# ab97023). Substrate: Enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate (Thermo Scientific, Cat# 32106) and the final product was stored at -70 °C ( 16 ).

3.5. Investigation of the Effect of Recombinant Protein ESAT-6 On IFN-Γ Secretion from Lymphocytes of Tuberculosis Patients

To assess IFN-γ secretion, peripheral blood samples were obtained from 30 patients with active tuberculosis. mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from the blood samples and were cultured utilizing the classical Ficoll-Hypaque method ( 17 ). Cells were divided into 5 groups of 6 including a control group, three treatment groups which received 5, 10, and 15 μg ESAT-6, and a positive control group which was treated with phytohemagglutinin. After 24 hours of incubation, the amount of IFN-γ in the cell culture supernatant was quantified using the commercial PicoKineTM ELISA kit (EK0373 CAT: Tested). All experiments were repeated 3 times and the average results were obtained and were compared using the statistical regression test.

3.6. Statistical Analysis

The statistical analysis subsection was updated to include specific software and tests used. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 and SPSS version 26. Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Since the data followed a normal distribution, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons post-hoc test was applied. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant

4.2. Gene Synthesis and PCR Amplification

The gene fragment encoding the engineered ESAT-6 protein, consisting of 405 nucleotides, was synthesized with restriction enzyme recognition sites and a poly-Histidine (poly-His) tag. PCR amplification of the gene produced a 450 bp fragment, as confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Further validation was achieved by double digestion of the recombinant plasmid with NdeI and XhoI restriction enzymes, resulting in bands corresponding to the linearized plasmid and the ESAT-6 gene. 5. Discussion

Tuberculosis (TB) continues to pose a significant public health challenge worldwide, with high prevalence rates and associated mortality, particularly in developing countries and during the COVID-19 pandemic. Accurate diagnosis and effective prevention strategies remain essential to combat TB. Advances in immunology have facilitated the identification of immunogenic components of Mycobacterium tuberculosis that hold promise for vaccine and diagnostic test development. This study contributes to these efforts by engineering a recombinant ESAT-6 protein and evaluating its ability to stimulate immune responses in tuberculosis patients ( 18 ).

DNA vaccines have emerged as a transformative approach to infectious disease prevention, offering enhanced safety profiles and robust immune activation. Unlike traditional vaccines, DNA vaccines pose no risk of infection while efficiently eliciting both humoral and cellular immune responses. Several key antigens, including the antigen 85 complex (Ag85A, Ag85B, Ag85C), TB10.4, and ESAT-6, have been extensively studied for their immunogenic properties ( 19 ). Among these, ESAT-6 has gained prominence due to its specificity to pathogenic Mycobacterium species and its strong T-cell-stimulating potential. Studies, such as those by Scarpellini et al. (2004) and Chiappini et al. (2016), have demonstrated the utility of ESAT-6 in diagnostic assays like the QuantiFERON-TB Gold test, emphasizing its ability to induce IFN-γ secretion in response to TB antigens ( 20 - 22 ).

The recombinant ESAT-6 protein engineered in this study demonstrated a significant capacity to stimulate IFN-γ secretion from PBMCs of tuberculosis patients in a dose-dependent manner. This aligns with previous findings, such as those by Kassa et al. (2012) and Kirby et al. (2013), who reported heightened immune responses to ESAT-6 and related antigens ( 23 , 24 ). By focusing on linear epitopes and optimizing the antigenic structure, this study enhances the potential utility of ESAT-6 in diagnostic assays and as a vaccine candidate.

Several studies have highlighted the diagnostic and therapeutic potential of recombinant ESAT-6. For instance, Shin et al. (2008) showed that combining ESAT-6 with other antigens improved diagnostic sensitivity ( 25 ), while Souza et al. (2012) demonstrated the efficacy of recombinant ESAT-6 in diagnosing bovine tuberculosis ( 26 ). More recently, Kwon et al. (2024) engineered a quadrivalent vaccine incorporating ESAT-6, which elicited protective immune responses characterized by CD4+IFN-γ+IL-17A+ cells ( 27 ). The results of this study further support these findings, particularly regarding the ability of ESAT-6 to stimulate robust IFN-γ production.

While the increase in IFN-γ secretion observed in this study highlights the immunogenic potential of the engineered ESAT-6 protein, IFN-γ alone is not an optimal indicator of protective immunity. Future research should evaluate the production of additional cytokines, such as IL-17 and IL-10, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the immune response. Moreover, expanding the sample size and including diverse patient populations could enhance the generalizability of the findings ( 27 ).

Another promising avenue is the integration of ESAT-6 with other antigens in multi-antigenic vaccine formulations. As demonstrated by Leung-Theung-Long et al. (2018) and Dijkman et al. (2023), such approaches have the potential to induce more robust and long-lasting immune responses compared to single-antigen formulations ( 28 , 29 ). The inclusion of ESAT-6 in DNA vaccines could also be explored, leveraging its strong T-cell activation properties to enhance vaccine efficacy. The findings of this study underscore the potential of recombinant ESAT-6 as a diagnostic and therapeutic tool. By optimizing its antigenic structure, this protein could improve the sensitivity and specificity of TB diagnostic assays, particularly in resource-limited settings where traditional methods face challenges. Furthermore, its ability to stimulate strong immune responses makes it a promising candidate for inclusion in next-generation TB vaccines.

6. Conclusion

This study demonstrates the successful engineering of a recombinant ESAT-6 protein capable of inducing significant IFN-γ secretion from PBMCs of tuberculosis patients. These findings highlight its potential utility in diagnostic and therapeutic applications and provide a foundation for future research on multi-antigenic vaccine formulations and immune response optimization. Expanding our understanding of the immunogenic properties of ESAT-6 could significantly contribute to global efforts in TB prevention and control.

Cancer-metastasis-on-a-chip reveals efficacy of bevacizumab and siRNA in overcoming carboplatin resistance in SKOV3 ovarian cancer within a fibrotic metastatic microenvironment
Authors: Sun-Young Hwang, Danbi Lee, Yu-Gyeong Lee, Jungho Ahn, Youn-Jung Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMicrofluidic platforms have emerged as powerful tools for investigating complex interactions between cells and their microenvironment. Conventional in vitro cancer models often fail to accurately replicate the complexities of the in vivo tumor microenvironment. In contrast, cancer-metastasis-on-a-chip models integrate the benefits of three-dimensional cell cultures with microfluidic technology, providing more physiologically relevant platforms for studying cancer biology and improving precision of drug screening. These platforms enable the compartmentalization of the metastatic cascade, allowing for more detailed understanding of its fundamental mechanisms. In this study, we employed an advanced microfluidic cancer-on-a-chip model to examine the invasion dynamics of SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells under fibrotic conditions. Specifically, we assessed the therapeutic efficacy of bevacizumab in combination with siRNA targeting ROBO1, LCP1, and GATA3—genes previously identified as upregulated and implicated in enhanced invasion during the progression from non-resistant (Non-R) SKOV3 to carboplatin-resistant (Carbo-hR) variants. Our results demonstrated a modest increase in invasive behavior in Non-R SKOV3 cells within the fibrotic microenvironment. In contrast, Carbo-hR cells exhibited dramatically increased invasion and a strong metastatic affinity for fibrotic lung tissue. Monotherapy with bevacizumab showed limited efficacy in Carbo-hR cells compared to non-R cells. However, the combined treatment with bevacizumab and siRNA targeting ROBO1, LCP1, and GATA3 substantially suppressed the invasive capacity of Carbo-hR cells, effectively restoring their phenotype to that of non-R cells. These findings highlight the promise of combining anti-angiogenic agents with targeted gene silencing strategies to overcome drug resistance and inhibit metastasis, particularly within fibrotic tumor microenvironments.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains one of the most significant challenges among gynecological malignancies, marked by its high morbidity and mortality rates. According to the American Cancer Society, approximately 19,710 new cases are anticipated to be diagnosed in the United States in 2024, with an estimated 13,270 deaths resulting from the disease [1]. A major clinical challenge in the treatment of ovarian cancer is the development of chemoresistance, particularly in cases involving distant metastases [2]. The most common distant metastatic site of ovarian cancer has been reported the liver (37.49 %), followed by distant lymph nodes (29.36 %) and lung (28.42 %) [3]. Notably, patients with lung metastasis exhibit the poorest survival outcomes [4]. Recent studies have revealed that fibrotic lung tissue provides a favorable microenvironment for metastatic colonization of ovarian cancer, facilitated by alterations in the extracellular matrix (ECM) and lung microenvironment that enhance tumor cell adhesion, survival, and proliferation, ultimately accelerating disease progression [[5], [6], [7]].

Traditional two-dimensional (2D) models have long been used to study the behavior in metastasis research [8,9]. However, these models exhibit significant limitations in accurately simulating the complex dynamics of cancer cell interactions and their ability to invade surrounding tissues, including stromal cells [10,11]. The primary drawback of 2D models lies in their inability to mimic the three-dimensional (3D) architecture and biomechanical properties of the tumor microenvironment, which are critical determinants of tumor progression and metastasis [[12], [13], [14]]. To address these limitations, microfluidic-based 3D “cancer-on-a-chip” platforms have emerged as an advanced and more physiologically relevant alternative. These systems are designed to faithfully replicate the tumor microenvironment, incorporated key factors, such as ECM composition, stromal-cancer interactions, and vascularization, all of which are essential for understanding metastatic processes [[15], [16], [17], [18]]. Recent studies of microfluidic platform mimicking tumor-stromal interactions have provided valuable insight into how the microenvironment influences cancer progression [19,20]. Biomimetic tumor microenvironments reconstructed within microfluidic platforms have proven effective in capturing the dynamics of tumor invasion and metastasis [21]. These advanced platforms offer the capability to model tumor angiogenesis, a fundamental process in metastasis. Tumor vasculature has been successfully engineered on injection-molded plastic array 3D culture platforms, replicating neovascularization, which supports metastatic growth [22,23]. Furthermore, 3D vascularized organotypic microfluidic assays have been employed to study the extravasation of breast cancer cells, providing a more accurate representation of how circulating tumor cells exit the vasculature to invade distant tissues [21,24,25]. A key advancement in cancer-on-a-chip systems is their mass producibility, which allows for high-throughput drug screening and consistent modeling of the metastatic process [22,23]. This includes the study of circulating tumor cells, which play a critical role in metastasis, particularly in lung metastasis [26,27]. These innovations offer great potential for advancing predictive models of metastasis, including the specific context of ovarian cancer circulating tumor cells metastasizing to fibrotic lung tissue, paving the way for more effective therapeutic strategies. Recent advances in cancer therapy have highlighted the potential of combining small interfering RNA (siRNA) with established treatments, such as bevacizumab, an anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) monoclonal antibody, to address chemoresistant ovarian cancer [28,29]. siRNA offers a gene-specific therapeutic approach to silence key drivers of drug resistance and metastatic progression, thus enhancing the efficacy of standard therapies [[30], [31], [32]]. This advanced approach directly targets the molecular pathways underlying resistance and dissemination, aiming to overcome the limitations of current treatment modalities [33,34]. Conventional therapies often fail due to the adaptive mechanisms of cancer cells, which lead to resistance and recurrence [33,35]. Furthermore, integrating precision medicine techniques, such as biomarker-driven patient stratification and personalized treatment regimens, may further improve therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects [36,37]. Ultimately, these approaches have the potential to not only suppress tumor growth but also prevent the emergence of aggressive, treatment-resistant phenotypes—addressing critical gaps in existing cancer therapies [38,39].

In this study, we introduce a novel ovarian cancer metastasis (OCM) chip platform designed to evaluate the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions targeting ovarian cancer with lung metastasis. Our advanced platform is optimized to assess the impact of siRNA targeting genes that are strongly related to drug-resistance and to evaluate the therapeutic potential of bevacizumab in mitigating metastatic progression. By leveraging this advanced model, we demonstrate its ability to simulate key aspects of ovarian cancer lung metastasis, providing a valuable tool for preclinical evaluation of combination therapies. Our exploration on metastasis-on-a-chip technology suggests its potential to accelerate drug discovery and advance personalized treatment for patients with ovarian cancer.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Human ovarian adenocarcinoma cell line, SKOV3, was obtained from the Korea Cell Line Bank, and maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium supplemented with HEPES, containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (all from GIBCO, USA). Carboplatin-resistant SKOV3 cell line was generated by exposing cells to 2.5 μM of carboplatin (Selleckchem, Germany) over 10 passages to reach the intermediate-resistance (Carbo-iR) and 20 passages to high levels of resistance (Carbo-hR) following an established protocol [40]. Parental cells were referred to as non-resistant (non-R). Cell culture media were replaced every 2–3 days, and passaged using 0.25 % Trypsin-EDTA (GenDEPOT, USA) when reaching ∼80 % confluence (∼2 × 106 cells). Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) and lung fibroblasts (LFs), obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC), were cultured under standard conditions. HUVECs were maintained in Endothelial Cell Growth Medium (EBM™, Lonza, Switzerland) supplemented with Endothelial Growth Medium BulletKit™ (EGM™-2, Lonza, Switzerland), and LFs were cultured in Fibroblast Growth Medium (FBM™, Lonza, Switzerland) supplemented with Fibroblast Growth Factor BulletKit™ (FGM™-2, Lonza, Switzerland). The culture media were refreshed every 2–3 days, and cells were passaged at 70–80 % confluence using Trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, USA). Only early passage cells (passages 3–6) were used for experiments.

Metastasis-on-a-chip cell seeding and drug treatment

Microfluidic chips were fabricated as described previously [41], with the physical chip architecture remaining unchanged. The biological setup within the chip, whereas was specifically modified for this study to reconstruct the fibrotic metastatic niche in a stepwise manner. First, LF cells (2 × 106 cells/mL) embedded in fibrin gel (2.5 mg/mL fibrinogen with 0.15 U/mL aprotinin) were loaded into the chip on Day 1. Fibrotic conditions were induced by treating LF cells with TGF-β (20 ng/mL) for 24 h. HUVECs (1 × 106 cells/mL) were seeded on Day 2 after a 10 % Matrigel coating. Finally, On Day 3, siRNA-transfected SKOV3 cells were seeded into the top reservoir (1 × 106 cells/mL). Bevacizumab (10 mg/mL) was added to specific media reservoirs. After 5 days, the chip was fixed with 4 % Paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 15 min and imaged using a Zeiss LSM880 confocal microscope. ZEN BLACK software was used for image analysis. All invasion assays were performed under static conditions without continuous perfusion. Following initial cell seeding, the platform was maintained for up to 120 h with manual media changes every 24 h. This static setup allowed for the specific observation of invasion driven by cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions, independent of hemodynamic shear stress.

Wound healing assay

Cells were seeded in a 6-well plate and grown to confluency. To induce additional DNA damage, cells were treated with a 10-fold increase in carboplatin concentrations for 0, 24, and 48 h. Cell viability was determined by direct cell counting. For the wound healing assay, linear scratches were made after 24 h of serum starvation. Wound closure distances were measured at different time points and quantified using ImageJ software (NIH).

Fluorescein isothiocyanate(FITC)-Dextran permeability assay in a microfluidic platform

Permeability of tumor cell layer was assessed using a FITC-dextran diffusion assay in a microfluidic platform as described previously [41]. SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells were seeded into the designated tumor compartment of the microfluidic chip and cultured until reaching confluence. Permeability was evaluated by introducing 70 kDa FITC-dextran (1 mg/mL in PBS, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) into the upper vascular channel. Time-lapse imaging was performed every 10 s using an inverted Zeiss LSM880 confocal microscope (Zeiss, Germany) to monitor diffusion across the tumor cell layer.

Sprouting assay in a microfluidic platform

On Day 1, HUVECs (1 × 106 cells/mL) were seeded into the media-exposed channel, allowing them to adhere and form a confluent layer along the interface with the tumor-seeding channel. The chips were incubated at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 to facilitate endothelial attachment and stabilization. On Day 2, SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells (1 × 106 cells/mL) were embedded in fibrin gel (2.5 mg/mL fibrinogen with 0.15 U/mL aprotinin, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and introduced into the upper tumor-seeding channel. Two groups were prepared: Non-R and Carbo-hR cells. The embedded cancer cells were allowed to interact with the endothelial monolayer through paracrine signaling. After 3 days, the chips were fixed with 4 % PFA for 20 min at room temperature and washed three times with PBS. Samples were imaged using a Zeiss LSM880 confocal microscope, and image analysis was performed using ZEN BLACK software (Zeiss, Germany).

Immunofluorescence and microscopy

Immunofluorescence was performed as described previously [41]. In brief, cells were fixed in 4 % PFA and labeled as follows: SKOV3 cells with CellTracker 647 (Thermo Fisher, USA), LF cells with phalloidin (Thermo Fisher, USA), and HUVECs with 488-conjugated CD31 (R&D Biosystems, USA). Fibrosis induction in LF cells was performed by treating cells with 20 ng/mL TGF-β1 (PeproTech, USA) 2 days. After treatment, cells were fixed in 4 % PFA and stained with anti-α-SMA primary antibody (1:200, Abcam, UK) followed by Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated secondary antibody (Thermo Fisher, USA). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Thermo Fisher, USA). Stained samples were imaged using a Zeiss LSM880 confocal microscope and images were analyzed using ZEN BLACK software.

RNA-seq analysis

To investigate the candidate genes, our previous RNA-seq data (GEO173579) were analyzed [40]. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined based on a fold-change ≥2.0, p-value <0.05, and FDR <0.05. Gene classification for Gene Ontology (GO). Candidate genes were initially filtered based on differential expression patterns among the three groups (Non-R, Carbo-iR, and Carbo-hR). GO analysis was performed to categorize DEGs related to cell migration and cell invasion, identifying genes with potential roles in metastatic progression. To focus on genes exhibiting a progressive increase in expression associated with the status of resistance to carboplatin, gradually upregulated genes from Non-R to Carbo-iR and further to Carbo-hR were selected. A heatmap, generated using R Studio 4.1 with the heatmap package [42], clearly sorted genes with a gradual increase in expression along the resistance spectrum.

Quantitative RT-PCR-based analysis of mRNA expression

Fibrotic LF cells were lysed using Labozol reagent (CMRZ001, Cosmo, South Korea), and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara, Japan) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Quantitative RT-PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted using SYBR Green (Enzynomics, South Korea) with 18S rRNA as a housekeeping gene. Gene expression was analyzed using two-way ANOVA with Dunnett's multiple comparisons.

RNA silencing and transfection

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting human ROBO1 (Forward: GACCAAUUGAGUAAGAAC; Reverse: CAGGGGAUGUUCUUACUCA), GATA3 (Forward: CUUUUUAACAUCGACGGU; Reverse: UGCCUUGACCGUCGAUCGU), and LCP1 (Forward: AACUUGGCUUUUAUUGCC; Reverse: AGAGGUUGGCAAUAAAA) were obtained from IDT (USA). Cells were transfected with 10 nM siRNAs using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. In brief, Carbo-hR SKOV3 cells were seeded at a density of 3 × 105 cells per well in 6-well plates and cultured until they reached approximately 80 % confluency. siRNA-lipid complexes were prepared in Opti-MEM (Gibco, USA) and added to the cells. After 6 h of incubation, the medium was replaced with fresh RPMI 1640 containing 10 % FBS and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection, and total RNA was extracted to confirm the efficiency of siRNA transfection by qRT-PCR using SYBR Green Master Mix (Enzynomics, South Korea) on a Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, USA). Primer sequence pairs used in the experiments are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Comparisons between groups were performed using unpaired t-tests for parametric distributions, one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's multiple comparisons, or two-way ANOVA with Sidak's multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 and is represented as follows: ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.005, and ∗∗∗∗p < 0.0001.

Results

Biomimetic in vitro metastasis-on-a-chip model of drug-induced resistant ovarian cancer

To systematically investigate the metastatic cascade and drug resistance mechanisms of carboplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells (Carbo-hR), we developed a microfluidic ovarian cancer metastasis (OCM) chip model that incorporates key elements of the tumor microenvironment. The OCM chip, composed of a vascular channel lined with HUVECs and a lung-like metastatic niche composed of lung fibroblasts (LF), enables real-time analysis of multiple cancer cell behaviors beyond migration and metastasis, including drug permeability resistance and angiogenesis-associated adaptations (Fig. 1b). This platform is comprised of two key compartments: (1) a vascular channel, simulating the tumor-associated vasculature through which circulating tumor cells (CTCs) traverse an endothelial barrier, and (2) a lung channel, designed to replicate the fibrotic stromal environment characteristics of metastatic colonization (Fig. 1a). The vascular channel is lined with red fluorescent protein(RFP)-labeled HUVECs to model tumor circulation dynamics, while the lung channel is seeded with LFs to recreate the metastatic niche.

To establish a clinically relevant model of chemoresistance, SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells were subjected to prolonged carboplatin exposure, resulting in the development of a Carbo-hR cell line (Fig. 1b). Phase-contrast imaging revealed distinct morphological changes associated with chemoresistance, displaying more elongated and spindle-shape cells with significantly higher growth rates (3.4-fold) compared to non-resistant (Non-R) SKOV3 (Fig. 1c and d), as described previously [43]. A key feature of the OCM chip is its ability to dynamically simulate tumor-endothelial interactions under controlled perfusion conditions, enabling the assessment of tumor cell extravasation and stromal invasion (Fig. 1e). Confocal microscopy confirmed the formation of a continuous endothelial barrier along the vascular channel, effectively separating it from the lung fibroblast compartment while allowing selective permeability. The 2D schematic illustrates trans-endothelial migration of tumor cells, while the 3D cross-sectional view depicts the direct invasion of Carbo-hR into the lung fibroblast compartment. Beyond modeling metastatic progression, the OCM platform facilitates the functional screening of genes associated with drug resistance to identify potential therapeutic targets. Transcriptomic profiling of Carbo-hR revealed upregulation of genes implicated in chemoresistance and metastasis, including LCP1, GATA3, and ROBO1 (Fig. 1f). Based on these findings, a dual therapeutic strategy was employed, combining bevacizumab, an anti-angiogenic agent, with targeted inhibition of metastasis-associated genes, demonstrating a synergistic effect in suppressing tumor migration and metastatic potential. Our OCM chip represents a robust and physiologically relevant platform for investigating ovarian cancer metastasis, elucidating drug resistance mechanisms, and evaluating therapeutic efficacy in a preclinical setting.

Acquired carboplatin resistance in SKOV3 enhances migratory capacity and reduces endothelial permeability

The invasive and migratory features of Carbo-hR SKOV3 were assessed compared to Non-R parental cells within the OCM chip (Fig. 2a–e). Quantitative analysis of migration distances over an 8-day period in the OCM chip revealed a significantly higher capacity of migratory (5.1-fold) and wound closure (2.6-fold) in Carbo-hR compared to Non-R SKOV3 (Fig. 2a–d). This was consistently observed in the condition of additional carboplatin treatment (Fig. 2c–e). Our findings demonstrate acquired drug resistance renders ovarian cancer cells more invasiveness and metastatic capacity. Furthermore, FITC-dextran vascular permeability across the endothelial barrier was measured to examine the impact of acquired resistance to carboplatin (Fig. 2f). Carbo-hR induced a significantly higher permeability coefficient compared to Non-R cells, implying less endothelial integrity mediated with carboplatin resistance (Fig. 2f–h). The paracrine effect of cancer cells on endothelial migration was assessed, allowing the evaluation of paracrine signaling without direct cell contact. HUVECs were seeded on the opposite side of the channel from either Carbo-hR or Non-R cells in the OCM chip system (Fig. 2i). Quantification of HUVEC migration demonstrated that Carbo-hR induced significantly higher endothelial migration (1.3-fold) compared to Non-R cells (Fig. 2j and k). This might imply that resistant cells secrete factors that promote endothelial migration and angiogenesis, thereby facilitating vascular remodeling and potentially enhancing metastatic potential.

Carboplatin-resistant SKOV3 cells exhibit enhanced migratory capacity in a TGF-β–induced fibrotic lung microenvironment

To investigate the impact of a fibrotic lung microenvironment on the metastatic behavior of drug-resistant ovarian cancer cells, LFs were pre-treated with transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) for 48 h prior to seeding SKOV3 into the OCM chip to mimic the fibrotic lung microenvironment (Fig. 3a). Successful fibrotic induction was validated by a significant increase in α-SMA expression at both protein and mRNA levels, with a 1.3-fold increase in α-SMA protein intensity and a 2.4-fold upregulation in ACTA2 mRNA along with significant elevation of COL1A1 (3.5-fold) and COL4A1 (4.9-fold) expression in TGF-β-treated LFs, compared with untreated controls (Fig. S1a–f). Using the OCM chip, we examined the migration and invasion of SKOV3 under both fibrotic and non-fibrotic lung conditions (Fig. 3a and b). Enhanced invasion was observed in both Non-R and Carbo-hR SKOV3 cells towards the TGF-β-treated LF channel, following penetration of the endothelial cell barrier, compared to non-treated conditions (Fig. 3c). In the absence of TGF-β treatment, both Non-R and Carbo-hR cells exhibited limited migration into the LF channel, with minimal disruption to the surrounding endothelial interface. However, under TGF-β-treated conditions, Carbo-hR cells exhibited significantly higher invasion rates than Non-R. Consistently, quantitative analysis revealed that Carbo-hR cells migrated 2.5-fold farther than Non-R cells under normal conditions and 3.2-fold farther than Non-R cells under fibrotic conditions. Moreover, TGF-β treatment increased the migration distance and number of Non-R cells by 6.2- and 3.2-fold, respectively (Fig. 3d and e). Under the same fibrotic conditions, Carbo-hR cells exhibited a 3.0-fold increase in migration distance and a 1.8-fold increase in cell number compared to Non-R cells (Fig. 3d and e). To further confirm that this enhanced invasion was directly mediated by fibrosis, we treated the OCM system with pirfenidone, a clinical anti-fibrotic agent (Fig. S1g). We first validated that pirfenidone treatment effectively reversed the fibrotic activation in LFs by significant suppression of ACTA2 in the TGF-β-mediated induced fibrotic cells (Fig. S1h). While TGF-β treatment led to substantial increase in the invasive capacity of both Non-R and Carbo-hR cells, this pro-invasive effect was significantly mitigated by incorporation of pirfenidone (Fig. S1i–j). Specifically, in Non-R cells, pirfenidone reduced invasion by 1.33-fold reduction compared to that in TGF-β sole treatment condition (the fibrosis-only group). In Carbo-hR cells, the effect was even more pronounced with pirfenidone treatment decreasing the number of migrated cells by 1.6-fold in invasion. These results indicate that TGF-β-induced fibrosis significantly enhances ovarian cancer cell invasion, with drug-resistant cells exhibiting heightened sensitivity to fibrotic cues, underscoring the critical role of fibrosis in facilitating metastatic progression.

Transcriptomic profiling of carboplatin-resistant SKOV3 cells reveals key regulators involved in the acquisition of carboplatin resistance

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying acquired carboplatin resistance and associated metastatic features in SKOV3 cells, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis was conducted. The experimental design involved the stepwise exposure of SKOV3 cells to carboplatin, resulting in three groups representing distinct resistance stages: Non-R (non-resistant), Carbo-iR (intermediate-resistant, treated for 10 passages), and Carbo-hR (highly resistant, treated for over 20 passages). This model enabled the identification of differentially expressed genes associated with the progressive acquisition of resistance and metastatic potential (Fig. 4a). Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed distinct clustering of Non-R, Carbo-iR, and Carbo-hR, with a clear transcriptional separation between the non-resistant and resistant groups (Fig. 4b). Notably, the Carbo-iR and -hR profiles were more closely aligned, indicating shared molecular mechanisms contributing to the progression of carboplatin resistance. Differential expression analysis identified 186 genes commonly altered (173; upregulated, 13; downregulated) in both Carbo-iR and -hR groups compared to Non-R SKOV3 (Fig. 4c), suggesting these altered genes are involved in core pathways driving carboplatin resistance. Furthermore, 280 genes were uniquely altered (249; upregulated, 31; downregulated) in Carbo-iR group, and 292 genes were specifically altered (215; upregulated, 77; downregulated) in Carbo-hR group, highlighting distinct molecular signatures at different resistant stages. A heatmap of differentially expressed genes revealed a progressive upregulation of key genes particularly involved in molecular pathways of extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, migration, and invasion, including GATA3, LCP1, and ROBO1 (Fig. 4e). The expression levels of these genes exhibited a gradual increase from Non-R to Carbo-hR, a finding that was further validated using RNA-seq normalized log2 values (Fig. 4f). This suggests that these genes are involved in carboplatin resistance and that they have the potential to enhance metastatic potential. Functional enrichment analyses of 173 DEGs differentially expressed genes including biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway of gene ontology (GO) were conducted by using the Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) online tools [44] (Fig. 4d). Enriched GO terms in each category including gene counts, p-value, and FDR are displayed in dot plot (Fig. 4d). Enriched GO terms in BP, including ‘positive regulation of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)’ and ‘cell migration’ were included, and CC analysis highlighted ‘cell adhesion molecules’ and ‘extracellular matrix’, underscoring the critical role of ECM interactions in driving drug resistance. In addition, KEGG pathway identified ‘Hippo signaling’ and ‘signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells’, which may contribute to the plasticity and aggressive behavior of carboplatin-resistant cells. These findings reveal that carboplatin resistance is accompanied by progressive transcriptional changes, including the upregulation of genes implicated in EMT, migration, and ECM remodeling. The identification of GATA3, LCP1, and ROBO1 as key regulators highlights their potential as therapeutic targets for mitigating resistance and metastatic progression in carboplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells. To further validate the clinical relevance of these gene signatures, an analysis was conducted using publicly available three independent high-grade serous ovarian cancer patient datasets (GSE51373 [45], GSE156699 [46], GSE63885 [47]. Across the examined cohorts, ROBO1, GATA3, and LCP1 expression levels were significantly elevated in patients who were resistant to platinum drugs compared to those who were sensitive to platinum drugs (Fig. S2). These findings support the translational relevance of the OCM chip model and confirm that the identified gene signatures reflect clinical patterns of chemoresistance.

Inhibitory effect of combined bevacizumab treatment and gene silencing on SKOV3 cell invasion toward fibrotic lung tissue

The OCM chip was utilized to examine the metastatic behavior of carboplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells in the fibrotic lung microenvironment and assess the inhibitory effects in various therapeutic conditions. The experimental design began with the sequential seeding of LFs on day 0, HUVECs on day 1 to establish endothelial barrier to the lung, and SKOV3 on day 2. Fibrotic lung condition was induced by TGF-β treatment on day 1. To explore the therapeutic potential of anti-angiogenic and gene-silencing strategies, SKOV3 cells were pre-treated with siRNAs targeting ROBO1, GATA3, or LCP1, identified key regulators of carboplatin resistance, 1 day prior to seeding, and bevacizumab treatment was initiated simultaneously with SKOV3 loading. Observations of migratory features were made on day 7 using immunofluorescence microscopy (Fig. 5a). Efficiency of knockdown of the ROBO1, GATA3, or LCP1 gene was confirmed via qPCR, displaying significant reductions in mRNA levels for all three genes relative to siNC(Non-targeting)-treated and non-treated controls (Fig. S3a–d). Moreover, siRNA and bevacizumab sole or combinatorial treatment-mediated cytotoxicity was confirmed by viability assay using Calcein-AM and Hoechst staining, constantly displaying above 98 % of viability across all treatment conditions (Fig. S3e–j). Immunofluorescence analysis revealed distinct invasion dynamics between Carbo-hR and Non-R SKOV3 under the various conditions (Fig. 5b). Non-R cells exhibited limited invasive behavior across all conditions, whereas Carbo-hR SKOV3 cells showed markedly enhanced invasion towards the TGF-β-induced fibrotic LF channel. Treatment with bevacizumab barely reduced invasion of Non-R cells, however its inhibitory effect was substantially diminished in Carbo-hR, particularly in the context of TGF-β-induced fibrosis. These findings underscore reduced efficacy of anti-angiogenic therapy in metastatic Carbo-hR within a fibrotic lung microenvironment. This was further supported by quantitative analyses (Fig. 5d–k). In Non-R cells, migration remained consistently low across all experimental groups, with no significant differences observed between non-treated and bevacizumab-treated, regardless of LF fibrosis status (Fig. 5c). In contrast, Carbo-hR cells demonstrated a 2.0-fold increase in migration in the fibrotic condition, and bevacizumab treatment failed to significantly reduce migration in non-fibrotic conditions. Notably, in the presence of fibrosis, Carbo-hR migration distance remained elevated—2.4-fold higher— even following bevacizumab treatment, indicating that fibrosis-induced migration is resistant to anti-angiogenic intervention (Fig. 5c). These findings highlight that fibrotic microenvironment evidently enhances ovarian cancer cell invasion and that the efficacy of bevacizumab is substantially diminished in the context of acquired carboplatin resistance.

To further investigate the molecular driver of fibrosis-associated migration, siRNA-mediated knockdown of ROBO1, GATA3, and LCP1 was performed in Carbo-hR cells (Fig. 5d–f). In the siNC-transfected Carbo-hR control group, migration distance was increased by 2.6-fold with TGF-β-induced fibrosis, and an additional bevacizumab treatment resulted in a 1.6-fold reduction, indicating that fibrosis-driven migration in Carbo-hR cells is not effectively inhibited by bevacizumab sole treatment (Fig. 5d). Notably, ROBO1 knockdown significantly attenuated migration by 38.6-fold in non-treated condition. Interestingly, the addition of bevacizumab with ROBO1 knockdown further enhanced the inhibitory effect under fibrotic condition, reducing migration by an additional 6.8-fold (Fig. 5d). Similarly, GATA3 knockdown resulted in a 13.1-fold decrease in migration under fibrotic condition, which was further reduced by 9.1-fold with bevacizumab treatment (Fig. 5e). Moreover, LCP1 knockdown exhibited the significant effect, decreasing migratory behavior by 29.4-fold in the fibrotic condition, and bevacizumab provided a further 1.7-fold decrease (Fig. 5f).

Quantification of migrated cell numbers across different treatment conditions revealed clear distinctions (Fig. 5g–j). In Non-R cells, TGF-β-induced fibrosis elevated the number of migrated cells by 2.6-fold, while bevacizumab effectively suppressed migration, resulting in a 3.4-fold decrease (Fig. 5g). In contrast, Carbo-hR cells exhibited increased migration by 1.7-fold with TGF-β-induced fibrosis, and an additional bevacizumab treatment only resulted in a 1.6-fold reduction, suggesting that limited efficacy of anti-angiogenic therapy in counteracting fibrosis-induced migration in drug-resistant ovarian cancer cells (Fig. 5h). Further analysis of the siRNA-transfected Carbo-hR cells confirmed the inhibitory effect of ROBO1, GATA3, and LCP1 knockdowns in attenuating fibrosis-induced migration (Fig. 5i and j). ROBO1 knockdown led to a 6.4-fold reduction in the number of migrating cells under fibrotic conditions, with an additional 1.3-fold decreased upon bevacizumab treatment (Fig. 5h). GATA3 knockdown suppressed migration by 7.4-fold, and bevacizumab co-treatment provided a further 1.5-fold decrease (Fig. 5i). Moreover, LCP1 knockdown demonstrated the most pronounced effect, decreasing fibrosis-induced migration by 14.7-fold, with an additional 1.3-fold reduction following bevacizumab administration (Fig. 5j). These findings were further corroborated by supplemental analyses of migration distance (Fig. S4a) and migrated cell counts (Fig. S4b), both of which reinforced our findings that fibrosis significantly amplifies the migratory potential of Carbo-hR cells. Importantly, bevacizumab alone was insufficient to fully counteract this enhancement. Among all conditions tested, the combination of bevacizumab with GATA3 knockdown produced the most substantial suppression of migration, highlighting GATA3 as a critical regulator of fibrosis-driven extravasation. Furthermore, analysis of the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) markers associated with fibrosis-driven migration revealed partial reversal of EMT following siRNA treatment (Fig. S4c–f). VIM expression was reduced by 3.1-fold following siROBO1 treatment, 2.9-fold after siLCP1, and 3.5-fold after siGATA3 compared to Carbo-hR control. In addition, CDH2 exhibited a decrease in expression ranging from 2.5- to 3.3-fold, while CDH1 expression exhibited an increase ranging from 2.9- to 4.5-fold in the groups treated with siRNAs. Together, these results underscore the role of the fibrotic microenvironment in promoting aggressive metastatic behavior in carboplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells and suggest that combinatorial targeting strategies are essential to overcome resistance to anti-angiogenic therapies.

Discussion

We developed an organ-specific 3D microfluidic model termed the OCM chip to study the metastatic cascade and drug resistance mechanisms of carboplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells within a biomimetic lung microenvironment. This triculture model integrates endothelial cells and a fibrotic lung microenvironment with metastatic ovarian cancer cells to mimic advanced-stage malignancy, where tumors have spread from the ovaries to distant organs, particularly the lung, liver, and intestines [48,49]. Approximately 70 % of ovarian cancer patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, often presenting with distant metastases [50]. Despite standard therapies, most patients experience recurrence and develop chemoresistance, contributing to a global 5-year survival rate of only ∼30 %. As such, the development of physiologically relevant platforms that accurately recapitulate the metastatic ovarian tumor microenvironment is a critical need.

By integrating microfluidic technology with real-time analysis, our OCM chip allowed us to identify key mechanisms driving the enhanced metastatic potential of Carbo-hR cells and facilitated assessment of limitations of anti-angiogenic monotherapy in fibrosis-driven metastasis. The OCM chip faithfully models complex metastatic processes, including intravasation, migration, and extravasation, within a dynamic, controlled setting. While many previously reported metastasis-on-a chip systems have effectively mimicked key steps of the metastatic cascade, such as interactions with the vascular endothelium and extravasation, they often lack critical components of the clinical tumor microenvironment [18,21,24]. Notably, stromal fibrosis and acquired drug resistance, which are main contributors to treatment failure in patients, have been frequently overlooked. Our OCM platform distinguishes itself by integrating these critical components. First, we incorporated a TGF-β-induced fibrotic microenvironment to emulate the metastatic lung niche commonly observed in advanced ovarian cancer. Second, we utilized Carbo-hR cells to study metastasis under conditions of clinically relevant chemoresistance. Furthermore, the platform was designed for combinatorial therapeutic screening, enabling concurrent evaluation of cancer cell invasion and endothelial barrier function in response to treatments including siRNA and/or bevacizumab. To our knowledge, this degree of integration-combining fibrosis, drug resistance, and dual-modality therapeutic testing in a single dynamic system represents a significant advancement, offering a more physiologically relevant model for investigating advanced metastatic disease.

A key finding of our study is that carboplatin resistance significantly increases metastatic potential, enhancing cell migration, invasion, and extravasation into fibrotic microenvironments. Resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy not only renders cancer cells unresponsive to standard treatment but also promotes aggressive phenotypes that exploit pro-fibrotic cues to facilitate metastasis [4,51]. Using the OCM chip, we observed that Carbo-hR cells exhibit a markedly greater ability to traverse endothelial barriers and invade fibrotic lung tissues compared to Non-R cells. TGF-β-induced fibrosis further amplified the invasive behavior of both cell types; however, only Non-R cells remained responsive to anti-angiogenic therapy. Carbo-hR cells maintained a significant metastatic advantage even under VEGF inhibition. These results align with clinical observations indicating that fibrotic remodeling of metastatic niches fosters a tumor-promoting microenvironment [52,53]. Fibrosis provides mechanical and biochemical signals that enhance tumor cell migration. The upregulation of α-SMA expression in LFs following TGF-β stimulation suggests active interactions between Carbo-hR cells and fibrotic stroma, which serves as a scaffold for invasion. This finding supports prior studies linking tumor-associated fibrosis with increased extracellular matrix stiffness and activation of mechanotransduction pathways, thereby enhancing metastasis [[54], [55], [56]]. While the stiffness of hydrogel formulated in our study was not directly measured, the concentration of fibrinogen employed has been previously shown to produce matrices with stiffness comparable to fibrotic tissue, thereby providing a physiologically relevant mechanical environment for our invasion assays [11,13,57]. Our results emphasize that the fibrotic microenvironment is a key driver of metastatic progression in drug-resistant ovarian cancer. Importantly, the therapeutic efficacy of bevacizumab was limited under fibrotic conditions. While bevacizumab significantly reduced migration in Non-R cells, Carbo-hR cells remained largely resistant particularly under TGF-β-induced fibrosis, where migration persisted despite VEGF inhibition. These findings underscore that fibrosis-driven signaling enables Carbo-hR cells to bypass VEGF-targeted therapies [58].

To address this limitation, we employed siRNA-mediated knockdown of ROBO1, GATA3, and LCP1 —genes implicated in metastasis and drug resistance. We acknowledge that future study should incorporate more advanced validation approaches, such as using multiple siRNA sequences or alternative knockdown methods like CRISPR interference. Knockdown of each gene significantly reduced fibrosis-induced migration. When combined with bevacizumab, siRNA treatment synergistically suppressed Carbo-hR cell migration. Notably, combination therapy further suppressed Carbo-hR cell invasion, suggesting that targeting both VEGF and cytoskeletal/EMT regulators produces a synergistic anti-metastatic effect. Among the tested genes, LCP1 knockdown exhibited the most pronounced reduction in migration, with additional suppression upon VEGF inhibition. Similarly, GATA3 silencing reduced migratory behavior and enhanced the efficacy of bevacizumab, highlighting the role of these genes in epithelial–mesenchymal transition(EMT)-related pathways in fibrosis-driven invasion. Consistently, our qPCR analysis demonstrated a decrease in the expression of key mesenchymal markers, such as VIM and CDH2, along with a matrix-degrading enzyme MMP3, upon treatment with siRNAs. Concurrently, an epithelial marker, such as CDH1 showed an increase in expression, indicating a mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET) (Fig. S4c–f). These molecular changes suggest that suppression of ROBO1, LCP1, and GATA3 attenuates migratory and invasive phenotypes by reducing EMT and extracellular matrix remodeling capacities. This finding aligns with prior reports indicating that the suppression of EMT enhances the sensitivity of cancer cells to chemotherapy and reduces their metastatic potential in various cancer models [[59], [60], [61]]. Beyond their immediate functional roles in cell migration, these genes are increasingly recognized as clinically relevant biomarkers. ROBO1 is frequently overexpressed in metastatic ovarian and breast tumors, exhibiting significant correlation with poor prognosis due to its role in directional invasion via SLIT-ROBO signaling [30,62]. GATA3, while initially characterized as a suppressor of metastasis in breast cancer, has emerged as a context-dependent EMT driver in ovarian cancer [35,63]. Moreover, LCP1 has been shown to enhance chemoresistance by stabilizing actin filaments and promoting cell motility in aggressive tumor types [30,33].

Collectively, our findings support a growing body of evidence indicating that combination strategies targeting both angiogenic and genetic drivers are more effective than monotherapy in managing drug-resistant cancers. Recent advancements in siRNA-based therapeutics highlight their promise in precision medicine, enabling targeted disruption of oncogenic pathways [58,64]. However, siRNA monotherapy alone may be insufficient to fully suppress metastatic progression, necessitating combination strategies to maximize therapeutic efficacy [65,66]. Our study demonstrates that combining siRNA knockdown with VEGF inhibition effectively suppresses metastatic activity in Carbo-hR cells.

Although immune components were not included in our triculture model, we recognize their critical role in shaping the tumor microenvironment. Incorporating tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) would enable exploration of how M2-polarized TAMs enhance ECM remodeling and angiogenesis through the secretion of factors like VEGF-A and MMP-9. Likewise, the inclusion of lymphocytes would allow the study of immune evasion mechanisms. For example, regulatory T cells (Tregs) cooperate with cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) to suppress CD8+ T cell infiltration and function via checkpoint signaling and immunosuppressive cytokines, thereby facilitating tumor progression and vascular remodeling [53]. Recent advances in perfused 3D co-culture systems have already demonstrated that including immune cells enhances the physiological relevance of cancer-on-chip models by mimicking the immune-stromal-tumor axis. Incorporating these elements will enable a more comprehensive analysis of invasion dynamics, angiogenic signaling, and the complex interplay between immunity, fibrosis, and chemoresistance in future studies.

To further enhance the utility of the OCM platform for dynamic drug screening, future iterations will incorporate real-time imaging and label-free biosensing technologies. The continuous monitoring of key cellular behavior such as barrier integrity, migration, and invasion can be achieved using integrated Transepithelial/Transendothelial Electrical Resistance (TEER) or impedance sensors, alongside live cell imaging systems. Recent advancements have demonstrated the feasibility of such approaches in both 3D tumor models and organ-on-a chip systems [67,68]. The integration of these technologies will facilitate dynamic assessment of therapeutic responses, thereby expanding the platforms applications in mechanistic studies and precision oncology. To enable more widespread utilization in the fields of drug discovery and precision oncology, the scalability of the OCM platform must be taken into consideration. The current design is compatible with multi-well formats and automated imaging, enabling medium-throughput screening of drug combinations in a controlled micro-physiological environment [36]. However, for wider adoption to occur, technical challenges must be addressed, including the standardization of chip fabrication, batch-to-batch reproducibility, and assay validation across different research centers. From a clinical perspective, robust translation will require not only model validation against patient outcomes but also careful consideration of patient stratification strategies to account for tumor heterogeneity in ovarian cancer [36]. To enhance clinical translation, future iteration of the OCM chip will incorporate patient-derived tumor organoids or circulating tumor cells, enabling personalized modeling of metastatic behavior and drug responsiveness. We acknowledge the limitation of using a single ovarian cancer cell line (SKOV3), which while widely utilized, may not capture the extensive molecular heterogeneity of high-grade serous ovarian cancer, and a fibrosis model based solely on TGF-β stimulation. Further studies will incorporate multiple patient-derived cell lines and multifactorial fibrosis-inducing cues (e.g., hypoxia or matrix stiffness) to better reflect the clinical heterogeneity of ovarian cancer metastasis [6,52]. Furthermore, although our study focused on the cellular responses within a pre-established fibrotic state rather than on the dynamic process of ECM remodeling, future investigations incorporating assays for matrix degradation and collagen fibril organization would provide deeper insight into the reciprocal interactions between invading cells and the stromal matrix. Such integration would help bridge the gap between preclinical findings and individualized cancer therapy [27,36]. Moreover, to strengthen the translational relevance of our findings, future studies will incorporate orthotopic xenograft models—established by implanting carboplatin-resistant SKOV3 into the mouse ovary to closely mimic tumor growth and lung metastasis—as well as patient-derived organoid-based metastasis models. This will allow us to validate whether the pro-metastatic role of fibrosis and the therapeutic efficacy of combined siRNA and bevacizumab treatments observed in the OCM chip are recapitulated under more physiologically relevant conditions. Ultimately, the OCM platform holds promise as a predictive tool for tailoring personalized therapeutic regimens in metastatic ovarian cancer. By acknowledging both the opportunities and the translational challenges, our study provides a balanced foundation for future development of this platform as a predictive tool for therapeutic decision-making.

Conclusions

Collectively, our study establishes the OCM chip as a robust and versatile in vitro model for dissecting the complex interplay between chemoresistant tumor cells and the metastatic microenvironment. By faithfully recapitulating key steps of the in vivo metastatic cascade, including intravasation, migration, and extravasation, the platform enables high-resolution analysis of metastatic behavior, barrier integrity, angiogenesis, and therapeutic response. This makes it well-suited for the preclinical screening of drug combinations. Importantly, while our findings rely on a single HGSC cell line, SKOV3, which limits the generalizability of the results, the identification of ROBO1, GATA3, and LCP1 as actionable targets provides a valuable foundation for future validation in additional models. To further strengthen translational relevance, in vivo validation will be necessary to confirm the roles of these targets and therapeutic potential. These targets offer new opportunities for combination strategies aimed at overcoming the limitations of current anti-angiogenic therapies, particularly in the context of stromal remodeling and chemoresistance. Longitudinal studies to assess the durability of gene-silencing and combined therapies will be essential for the clinical translation of these findings. Ultimately, the OCM platform holds promise as a predictive tool for tailoring personalized therapeutic regimens in metastatic ovarian cancer.

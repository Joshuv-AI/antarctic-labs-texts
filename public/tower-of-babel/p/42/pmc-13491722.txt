18F-FDG PET/CT radiomics combined with double-expressor status enhances prognostication and high-risk stratification in DLBCL
Authors: Qi Jiang, Silu Cui, Panpan Luan, Faquan Ji, Yuxiao Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the R-CHOP era, the IPI score has reduced efficacy in identifying high-risk diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) patients. Precise prognostic biomarkers and scoring systems are urgently needed to achieve refined stratification of this high-risk population. This study aimed to clarify the combined prognostic significance of 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) radiomics and double-expressor status in DLBCL patients (DEL) receiving first-line R-CHOP therapy, and to evaluate their incremental value over the the International Prognostic Index (IPI) score in identifying high-risk DLBCL patients and predicting their prognosis.MethodsThis study included 210 real-world patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL treated with R-CHOP. Radiomic parameters including maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV), total lesion glycolysis (TLG), and maximum lesion diameter (Dmax) were extracted from 18F-FDG PET/CT, with lesion segmentation performed at a 41% SUV threshold. With PFS as the primary endpoint and OS as the secondary endpoint, optimal cutoffs were identified by the max-stat method. Survival analysis and Cox regression were applied to construct a prognostic risk score model. Model validation was performed using five-fold cross-validation and 1000 bootstrap resamplings. Predictive performance was evaluated by AIC, c-index and AUC, with AUC differences compared via multiple statistical tests.ResultsMultivariate analysis identified MTV, Dmax and DEL status as independent predictors of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS), leading to the development of the MDE prognostic model. Five-fold cross-validation confirmed significant PFS/OS risk stratification across all training and validation cohorts (all P < 0.05). MDE was a significant independent PFS predictor (P < 0.0001), outperforming the IPI (P = 0.205). The MDE model showed superior predictive performance over IPI: for PFS, mean AIC (536.564 vs. 575.546) and C-index (0.778 vs. 0.677); for OS, mean AIC (424.227 vs. 445.86) and C-index (0.775 vs. 0.724). Its AUC (0.797) was significantly higher than IPI (0.700, all P < 0.05) per DeLong’s test, bootstrap method and Venkatraman’s permutation test. Compared with IPI high-risk patients (5-year PFS: 34.6%, OS: 32.6%), MDE identified a higher-risk subgroup (5-year PFS: 22.4%, OS: 29.7%) and improved discriminatory capacity across IPI subgroups (all P < 0.05).ConclusionsThe MDE scoring system integrates baseline 18F-FDG-PET/CT radiomic features and DEL status. It identifies higher-risk DLBCL patients more effectively than the IPI, exhibits superior prognostic predictive value for treatment-naive DLBCL patients, and demonstrates excellent reproducibility.Trial registrationThe research protocol of this study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Jiangsu Cancer Hospital (Approval KY-2025-095, approval date: 16 July 2025).Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02552-4.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) accounts for 30–35% of all non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) cases and is a heterogeneous malignancy characterized by distinct biological features, clinical behaviors and treatment responses [1]. Although first-line chemoimmunotherapy with R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine and prednisone) achieves long-term disease control in approximately 60% of patients [2, 3], 25–30% develop relapsed or refractory (R/R) disease post-treatment [4]. Such patients carry an extremely poor prognosis, with a 2-year overall survival rate of less than 20% [5]. Early identification of high-risk patients is therefore an urgent clinical need. The International Prognostic Index (IPI) is the primary baseline risk stratification tool for DLBCL patients and has been widely adopted in clinical practice [6]. However, its predictive value has been questioned in the R-CHOP era [7, 8]. Comprising only five clinical parameters (age, Ann Arbor stage, extranodal involvement [EN], serum lactate dehydrogenase [LDH] level, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status [ECOG PS]), this clinical parameter-based prognostic scoring system is considered insufficient for accurately identifying high-risk patients with poor outcomes [9–11]. Thus, there is an active search for prognostic biomarkers capable of predicting patients at high risk of treatment failure.

18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) directly reflects the glucose metabolic status of tumor cells [12]. Guidelines [6, 13, 14] recommend the routine use of 18F-FDG PET/CT for the diagnosis, staging, restaging, and post-treatment response assessment of lymphoma. The parameters derived from 18F-FDG PET/CT mainly include metabolic and distance-related indices. Most studies have validated the prognostic significance of metabolic parameters in DLBCL, such as maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) [14], total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV) [15], and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) [16]. The dissemination distance parameter, primarily the maximum distance between two farthest lesions (Dmax), has been identified as a strong prognostic predictor for DLBCL patients [17]. In the past five years, these baseline metabolic and dissemination features have been incorporated into various prognostic scoring models to optimize prognostic prediction for DLBCL patients. Several such models have been shown to identify DLBCL patients with poor prognosis [14, 18–20] and, in some cases, outperform the International Prognostic Index (IPI) risk score [14, 18, 20].

Double-expressor lymphoma (DEL) refers primarily to the subtype of DLBCL with concurrent high expression of MYC and BCL-2 proteins. It is a risk stratification index based on protein levels rather than genetic alterations [21, 22]. Reports have indicated that 18–44% of DLBCL cases coexpress MYC and BCL2 [23]. Multiple studies have identified double-expressor status as an adverse prognostic factor for the efficacy of R-CHOP therapy in DLBCL [21, 24–26]. DLBCL with double-expressor status confers poor prognosis and suboptimal treatment response following standard R-CHOP induction therapy, regardless of MYC/BCL2 rearrangement status [21, 27]. Thus, the prognostic role of double-expressor status in DLBCL warrants attention.

18F-FDG PET/CT reflects the metabolic activity and dissemination of tumors, while pathological parameters compensate for its limitations in evaluating molecular expression. Compared with the IPI score, which fails to incorporate these key indicators, the combination of 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and DEL status is expected to develop a prognostic scoring system that enhances the predictive accuracy of DLBCL. However, in the rituximab era, the correlation between baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and DEL status, as well as their combined clinical value, remains insufficiently evaluated. The objectives of this study were: (1) To elucidate the correlation between radiomic features derived from baseline 18F‑FDG PET/CT and DEL status, as well as their independent prognostic value in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL; (2) To develop and validate a novel prognostic scoring model integrating 18F‑FDG PET/CT radiomic features and DEL status, and to compare its predictive efficacy with that of the classic IPI model.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study retrospectively analyzed 210 patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL who underwent baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT scanning at Jiangsu Cancer Hospital between February 2017 and February 2024. The inclusion and exclusion criteria are presented in Fig. 1. Double-expressor lymphoma was defined according to the cutoff values recommended by the 2016 revised World Health Organization (WHO) classification [28], with a cutoff of 40% for MYC and 50% for BCL2 protein expression.

Baseline clinical characteristics were collected from medical records, including age, LDH level, ECOG PS, Ann Arbor stage, and EN. These variables were used to calculate the IPI score with the following criteria: age < 60 years (0 points) vs. ≥ 60 years (1 point); serum LDH > upper limit of normal (ULN) (1 point) vs. ≤ ULN (0 points); Ann Arbor stage III/IV or ECOG PS ≥ 2 (1 point for each); and EN involvement of major organs (1 point). The total IPI score ranged from 0 to 5 points. Patients were then stratified into three risk groups according to NCCN Guidelines [29]: low risk (0–1 points), intermediate risk (2–3 points), and high risk (4–5 points).

18F-FDG PET/CT acquisition

All patients underwent 18F-FDG PET/CT (Discovery 710, GE Medical Systems) following ≥ 6 h fasting. 18F-FDG (0.1–0.2 mCi/kg) was intravenously administered after confirming blood glucose < 11 mmol/L. Patients rested quietly for radiotracer distribution (oral hydration: 1,000 ml water) and emptied their bladder pre-scan. Imaging (vertex to mid-thigh) started 50–70 min post-injection: CT (140 kV, Auto mA [noise index 28.5], 0.8s rotation, 3.75 mm slice thickness); PET (2 min/bed position, 6–7 beds) with CT-based attenuation correction, iterative reconstruction (VUE Point FX, 192 × 192 matrix, 24 subsets/2 iterations, 6.4 post-filter). Images were analyzed on AW 4.6 workstation (GE Healthcare).

18F-FDG PET/CT image analysis

Two experienced nuclear medicine physicians, who were blinded to patient outcomes, analyzed the 18F-FDG PET/CT images. Discrepancies regarding abnormal lesions were resolved through consensus discussion. PET image data in DICOM format were exported from the GE Medical Systems post-processing workstation and uploaded to LIFEx software (version 5.10; http://www.lifexsoft.org) [30]. According to the recommendations of the European Association of Nuclear Medicine (EANM) guidelines [31], a 41% SUVmax threshold was used to delineate the volume of interest (VOI) for calculating TMTV, TLG, and SUVmax. The highest SUV among all lesions was defined as the final SUVmax. TMTV was defined as the sum of metabolic volumes of all measured lesions, and TLG as TMTV multiplied by the mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean).

For each PET dataset, physiological uptakes (e.g., bladder, kidneys, brain, myocardium) and false-positive lesions (e.g., inflammation, infection) were excluded, with missed tumor regions added. Additionally, adjacent non-tumor areas were manually subtracted incrementally using the eraser tool if necessary. Bone marrow (BM) and liver were measured only in the presence of focal uptake, while the spleen was considered involved if there was focal uptake or diffuse uptake exceeding 150% of the liver background [32]. Visual assessment was performed to confirm the inclusion of only pathological lesions.

Follow-Up and endpoints

All patients were followed up via retrospective review of medical records after completion of standard R-CHOP therapy, until April 2025 or death. Survival status was confirmed by reviewing death records, or via direct communication with patients or their immediate family members. Progression and relapse were defined per updated lymphoma response criteria [33, 34]. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the primary endpoint of the study, and overall survival (OS) as the secondary prognostic endpoint for patients with DLBCL. Disease recurrence was defined as clinical or radiological evidence of recurrence confirmed during follow-up. PFS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of disease progression (new lesions or enlargement of pre-existing lesions), recurrence, or death. OS was estimated from the date of diagnosis to the date of death from any cause or the last follow-up date.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.1.1, R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (normal distribution) or median (interquartile range, IQR, Q1–Q3; skewed distribution). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Between-group differences were compared using the chi-square test (categorical variables), Student’s t-test (normal distribution), or Mann-Whitney U test (skewed distribution). Correlations were evaluated by Pearson correlation analysis. The primary endpoint was PFS, and the secondary endpoint was OS. The optimal cutoff values for each PET-derived parameter for predicting PFS/OS were determined by the max-stat method. Univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted using the Cox proportional hazards model, with hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) calculated. A prognostic risk score model (higher scores indicated poorer prognosis) was constructed based on multivariate Cox regression, and its stability was validated using five-fold cross-validation. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan-Meier method with the Log-rank test. Predictive performance was assessed by the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), concordance index (C-index), and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). AIC selects the optimal model, with smaller values indicating better fit and fewer redundant variables. Higher C-index reflects stronger prognostic discrimination for survival models. Internal validation was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamplings. Differences in AUC were compared using DeLong’s test, the bootstrap method, and Venkatraman’s permutation test. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 210 patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL were enrolled in this study. Their clinical characteristics, DEL status, and 18F-FDG PET/CT quantitative parameters are summarized in Table 1. Among them, 107 were female (51%) and 103 were male (49%), with a median age of 62 years (IQR, Q1–Q3: 53–71 years). Disease progression/recurrence occurred in 59 patients (28.1%), with a median time to progression of 11 months (range: 0–52 months); 47 patients (22.4%) died, with a median overall survival time of 12 months (range: 0–59 months). The 5-year PFS rate and OS rate of the entire cohort were 67.0% and 72.3%, respectively.

Correlation between 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic, dissemination parameters and DEL status

Pearson correlation analysis revealed a strong positive correlation between TMTV and TLG for PFS (ρ = 0.681, P < 0.001). Based on this finding, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was further calculated to assess collinearity between TMTV and TLG, with results indicating no multicollinearity (VIF = 1.864). For overall survival (OS), moderate correlations were observed between TMTV and TLG (ρ = 0.495, P < 0.001), maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) and TLG (ρ = 0.529, P < 0.001), and TMTV and Dmax (ρ = 0.408, P < 0.001). Otherwise, only weak or very weak correlations were detected among TMTV, Dmax, and SUVmax for both PFS and OS (Supplementary Table 1). Metabolic parameters measured by 18F-FDG PET/CT exhibited excellent interobserver consistency (intraclass correlation coefficient [ICC] = 0.995, P < 0.001).

Survival analysis of 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters and DEL status

Among the 210 patients with DLBCL enrolled in this study, the median values of key 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters including SUVmax, TLG, TMTV, and Dmax were 18 g/mL, 255 g/mL, 43 cm3, and 19 cm, respectively (Table 1). To clarify their prognostic stratification value, the optimal cutoff values for predicting PFS and OS were determined using the maximum selected rank statistics method: for PFS, the cutoffs were 5 g/mL (SUVmax), 89 cm3 (TMTV), 32 cm (Dmax), and 556 g (TLG); for OS, the corresponding cutoffs were 5 g/mL (SUVmax), 93 cm³ (TMTV), 30 cm (Dmax), and 158 g (TLG). To verify the reliability and stability of these cutoffs, kernel density distribution curves of each parameter were plotted based on 1000 bootstrap resamplings, and internal validation demonstrated that all optimal cutoffs were concentrated in repeated sampling without significant deviation, indicating favorable reproducibility and stability (Supplementary Fig. 1). K-M survival analysis combined with the log-rank test revealed that the optimal cutoffs of all PET/CT metabolic parameters effectively distinguished differences in PFS and OS among patients (all P < 0.05): specifically, patients in the high TMTV group (≥ 89 cm3 for PFS, n = 62; ≥ 93 cm3 for OS, n = 60) had 5-year PFS and OS rates of 35.7% and 43.6%, respectively, compared with 79.8% and 83.2% in the low TMTV group (< 89 cm3 for PFS, n = 148; < 93 cm3 for OS, n = 150) (both P < 0.001, Fig. 2A and D); the 5-year PFS and OS rates were 42.7% and 47.5% in the high Dmax group (≥ 32 cm for PFS, n = 79; ≥ 30 cm for OS, n = 81), versus 81.4% and 87.8% in the low Dmax group (< 32 cm for PFS, n = 131; < 30 cm for OS, n = 129) (both P < 0.001, Fig. 2B and E); regarding DEL status, the 5-year PFS and OS rates were 20.2% and 33.9% in DEL-positive patients (n = 30 for both PFS and OS), compared with 74.3% and 78.4% in DEL-negative patients (n = 180 for both PFS and OS), with DEL-positive patients exhibiting significantly shorter PFS and OS (both log-rank P < 0.0001, Fig. 2C and F); patients with high SUVmax (≥ 5 g/mL for both PFS and OS, n = 177) had 5-year PFS and OS rates of 63.5% and 69.7%, respectively, whereas those with low SUVmax (< 5 g/mL for both PFS and OS, n = 33) had rates of 86.6% and 86.6% (P = 0.009 and P = 0.045, respectively, Supplementary Figs. 2 A and 2 C); and the 5-year PFS and OS rates were 48.9% and 55.6% in the high TLG group (≥ 556 g for PFS, n = 74; ≥ 158 g for OS, n = 126), compared with 76.8% and 81.3% in the low TLG group (< 556 g for PFS, n = 136; < 159 g for OS, n = 84) (both P < 0.001, Supplementary Figs. 2B and 2D).

Cox regression analysis of18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters

In univariate Cox regression analysis (Table 2), TLG (P < 0.0001 for PFS, P = 0.0002 for OS), TMTV (both P < 0.0001 for PFS and OS), Dmax (both P < 0.0001 for PFS and OS), and DEL status (both P < 0.0001 for PFS and OS) were significantly associated with PFS and OS. SUVmax was significantly correlated with PFS (P = 0.016) but not with OS (P = 0.059). Variables with significant prognostic effects in the univariate analysis were further incorporated into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The results indicated that only TMTV (hazard ratio [HR] = 3.128, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.537–6.367, P = 0.002 for PFS; HR = 2.441, 95% CI: 1.139–5.233, P = 0.022 for OS), Dmax (HR = 2.488, 95% CI: 1.397–4.433, P = 0.002 for PFS; HR = 3.297, 95% CI: 1.655–6.568, P = 0.0007 for OS), and DEL status (HR = 2.920, 95% CI: 1.666–5.117, P = 0.0002 for PFS; HR = 3.917, 95% CI: 2.120–7.236, P < 0.0001 for OS) were independent prognostic factors for PFS and OS. TLG lost its statistical significance for predicting both PFS and OS, and SUVmax no longer exhibited a significant association with PFS in the multivariate model (Fig. 5, Model 1).

Integration of 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters with double expression

Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified three independent prognostic factors: TMTV, Dmax, and DEL status, with the β-coefficients of these three variables being 1.207, 0.901, and 1.183 in the PFS model (Table 3) and 0.964, 1.193, and 1.423 in the OS model (Table 4), respectively; an integrated model incorporating 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and DEL status was subsequently developed based on these three parameters, and given the small differences in β-coefficients among variables within the two models, separate prognostic scoring systems were constructed for PFS and OS to facilitate rapid clinical application, in which each positive risk factor was uniformly assigned a simplified score of 1 point and the total risk score was calculated as the sum of individual factor scores ranging from 0 to 3 points, and a unified three-tier stratification criterion was adopted in reference to the IPI(both log-rank P < 0.001; Fig. 3A and D): a score of 0 indicated the low-risk group, 1 indicated the intermediate-risk group, and 2–3 indicated the high-risk group, with the results showing statistically significant differences in PFS and OS outcomes among the three risk strata classified by the scoring system (both log-rank P < 0.001; Fig. 3B and E). Furthermore, 5-fold cross-validation stratified by PFS and OS events was performed to ensure consistent event distribution between each fold and the entire cohort and evaluate the stability of the MDE model in predicting PFS and OS, and in each round of cross-validation, the study cohort was cyclically split into a training set (80%) and a validation set (20%); significant intergroup outcome differences were detected in all cross-validation subsets, and after 5-fold cross-validation, all 10 training subsets and 10 validation subsets exhibited significant differences in PFS and OS risk stratification across the 5 rounds of training and validation (all P < 0.05; Supplementary Figs. 3 and 4), suggesting that the MDE prognostic model established in this study and the stratification strategy with a 1-point assignment for risk factors can effectively distinguish differences in PFS and OS among the low-, intermediate- and high-risk groups and possess stable and reliable stratification efficacy.

Comparison of prognostic predictive performance between the MDE and IPI

The prognostic performance of the integrated model combining 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and DEL status (MDE model) was compared with that of the IPI model. Patients with DLBCL in the low-risk group of the MDE model (PFS risk score: 0 points, n = 99, events = 8; OS risk score: 0 points, n = 98, events = 5) had higher survival rates than those in the low-risk group of the IPI scoring system (score: 0–1 points, n = 97; PFS events = 12; OS events = 7), with 5-year PFS rates of 88.2% vs. 84.5% and 5-year OS rates of 92.5% vs. 89.1%, respectively. Patients in the intermediate-risk group of the MDE model (PFS risk score: 1.183–1.207 points, n = 60, events = 17; OS risk score: 0.9641–1.4232 points, n = 63, events = 14) showed higher survival rates than those in the intermediate-risk group of the IPI system (score: 2–3 points, n = 95; PFS events = 36; OS events = 29), with 5-year PFS rates of 67.0% vs. 54.9% and 5-year OS rates of 72.2% vs. 62.6%, respectively. In contrast, patients in the high-risk group of the MDE model (PFS risk score: 2.084–3.291 points, n = 50, events = 34; OS risk score: 2.1566–3.5798 points, n = 49, events = 28) had lower survival rates than those in the high-risk group of the IPI system (score: 4–5 points, n = 18; PFS events = 18; OS events = 11), with 5-year PFS rates of 22.4% vs. 34.6% and 5-year OS rates of 29.7% vs. 32.6%, respectively. Notably, compared with the IPI system, the high-risk group identified by the MDE risk scoring model not only had lower survival rates but also captured more PFS events (MDE: 34 vs. IPI: 18) and OS events (MDE: 28 vs. IPI: 11).

Examples of risk reclassification of the IPI score using the MDE model, which integrates 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters and DEL status, are shown in Fig. 3C and F. By ranking individual MDE scores, nearly half of the patients were reclassified into different MDE risk categories for PFS and OS compared with their original IPI classifications (92 patients [43.8%] for PFS reclassification; 89 patients [42.4%] for OS reclassification; Fig. 3C and F). For PFS, 36 patients (39.1%) were reclassified as high-risk, 31 patients (33.7%) were adjusted to the low-risk group, and 25 patients (27.2%) were reallocated to the intermediate-risk group. For OS, 35 patients (39.3%) were moved to the high-risk group, 29 patients (32.6%) to the low-risk group, and 25 patients (28.1%) to the intermediate-risk group. Survival analysis of the reclassified patients revealed that the MDE score significantly improved the discriminative ability for both PFS and OS across the original IPI low-risk, intermediate-risk, and high-risk groups (all P < 0.05, Fig. 4).

Finally, the IPI and MDE risk scoring models were incorporated into multivariate Cox regression analysis (Table 5). For PFS, IPI lost its prognostic significance (HR = 1.326, 95% CI: 0.857–2.053, P = 0.205), whereas MDE remained independently associated with shorter PFS (HR = 3.418, 95% CI: 2.285–5.113, P < 0.0001). For OS, both IPI (HR = 1.750, 95% CI: 1.080–2.837, P = 0.023) and MDE (HR = 3.187, 95% CI: 2.005–5.065, P < 0.0001) were independently correlated with inferior OS (Table 5). In the entire cohort of patients with DLBCL, internal 1000-fold bootstrap validation revealed that the novel model exhibited superior predictive performance for both PFS and OS: for PFS, the mean AIC value of the new model was lower than that of IPI (536.564, 95% CI: 415.783–660.080 vs. 575.546, 95% CI: 451.237–707.339), and the mean C-index was higher (0.778, 95% CI: 0.725–0.827 vs. 0.677, 95% CI: 0.615–0.741); for OS, the mean AIC value of the new model was also lower than that of IPI (424.227, 95% CI: 316.617–537.873 vs. 445.860, 95% CI: 339.716–565.139), with a higher mean C-index (0.775, 95% CI: 0.709–0.836 vs. 0.724, 95% CI: 0.660–0.785). ROC curves were constructed with PFS and OS as the outcome endpoints, respectively (Fig. 5). For PFS, the AUC of the MDE model (0.797) was larger than that of the IPI model (0.700); the DeLong test showed a statistically significant difference in AUC between the two models (P = 0.0146), bootstrap validation confirmed the robustness of the result (P = 0.0135), and the Venkatraman permutation test further verified a significant difference in the overall distribution of the two ROC curves (P = 0.0135), indicating that the predictive efficacy of the MDE model for PFS was superior to that of the IPI model. For OS, although the AUC of the MDE model (0.8) was higher than that of the IPI model (0.735), the DeLong test (P = 0.1222), bootstrap method (P = 0.1268), and Venkatraman permutation test (P = 0.053) all demonstrated no statistically significant difference in predictive performance between the two models.

Discussion

In the present study, we developed a novel prognostic scoring model (MDE) by integrating 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters (TMTV and Dmax) with DEL status. This model demonstrated superior precision in stratifying high-risk patients with DLBCL for adverse outcomes relative to clinical prognostic biomarkers typified by the IPI. Specifically, two pivotal findings were derived from this research: (1) TMTV, Dmax, and DEL status were validated as independent prognostic factors for PFS and OS in patients with DLBCL prior to treatment with the R-CHOP regimen; (2) In comparison with the IPI score, the novel integrated MDE model was capable of identifying a subset of DLBCL patients at an even higher risk, and exhibited better predictive performance for the overall prognosis of DLBCL.

Various 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic features have been proven to predict the prognosis of DLBCL. Compared with SUVmax, a simple two-dimensional parameter, three-dimensional parameters including TMTV, TLG, and Dmax have demonstrated significant prognostic value for DLBCL in more studies. This superior performance may be attributed to their ability to capture spatial heterogeneity and disease dissemination [16, 35, 36]. Although TLG (Total Lesion Glycolysis) represents total lesion glycolysis and has shown prognostic value in certain specific studies, TMTV has been validated as a more robust and reliable independent prognostic factor in the vast majority of large-cohort and multicenter studies [37, 38]. This is consistent with the findings of the present study: multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that TLG was not a significant prognostic factor for either PFS or OS (PFS: P = 0.874; OS: P = 0.64), whereas TMTV remained significantly associated with both outcomes (all P < 0.05). This discrepancy may be explained by the fact that TLG is the product of tumor volume and metabolic activity—errors in either dimension during calculation can be amplified due to the combined effect of volume and activity. In contrast, TMTV directly quantifies tumor burden, resulting in relatively smaller measurement errors. Furthermore, several studies [6, 39–41] have developed a novel prognostic index named the International Metabolic Prognostic Index (IMPI) that incorporates TMTV, age, and stage. This index has been shown to outperform the IPI and enable individualized estimation of patient outcomes [6]. This superior performance may be attributed to the fact that TMTV directly quantifies the viable tumor volume. Such a feature more accurately reflects tumor burden compared with clinical factors that indirectly indicate tumor burden, primarily the Ann Arbor stage and LDH level. In addition to metabolic parameters represented by TMTV, Dmax, a radiomic dissemination parameter of 18F-FDG PET/CT, was first identified as an independent prognostic factor for DLBCL patients in the LNH073B trial cohort by the French team led by Cottereau A.S. et al. in 2020 [17]. Subsequently, Dmax has been shown to have significant prognostic predictive value for lymphoma patients in multiple studies [14, 18, 42]. Previous studies have demonstrated that Dmax and TMTV are mutually independent, and their combination even outperforms the IPI model [14, 17]. This superior performance may be explained by the fact that Dmax supplements the limitation that TMTV can only represent lymphoma tumor burden but cannot reflect tumor distribution by providing information on disease dissemination. The combination of the two further enhances the prognostic predictive value for DLBCL patients. This is consistent with the findings of the present study, in which TMTV and Dmax were identified as mutually independent prognostic factors for DLBCL patients.

In DLBCL, the concurrent high expression of MYC and BCL2 proteins detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC), defined as double expression, has been established as a novel molecular subtype associated with poor prognosis [22]. Given the low clinical detection rate of double-hit lymphoma, which accounts for approximately 2% of all B-cell lymphomas [43], this disease is associated with extremely poor prognosis and patients generally receive treatments other than the standard R-CHOP regimen [44], making clinical research focused on this population challenging. In contrast to double-hit lymphoma, which harbors concurrent chromosomal translocations of MYC and BCL2 or BCL6, DE can be detected solely by routine IHC staining without complex genetic assays such as fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), thus enabling its routine clinical implementation. Studies have demonstrated that DEL confers an inferior prognosis following standard R-CHOP induction therapy, regardless of MYC/BCL2 rearrangement status [27]. This may be attributed to the fact that the co-expression of MYC and BCL2 typically reflects enhanced cellular proliferation driven by MYC and anti-apoptotic capacity mediated by BCL2, representing a highly aggressive disease phenotype [45, 46]. Absence of MYC/BCL2 co-expression has been associated with a significantly increased likelihood of complete remission [21], and several studies have even identified DEL status as an adverse prognostic factor independent of the IPI [47]. These findings are consistent with those of the present study, which demonstrated that double expression status is indeed an adverse prognostic factor for DLBCL patients receiving R-CHOP therapy, and is significantly associated with inferior clinical outcomes.

As previously described, although baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and BCL2/BCL6 double expression status have been recognized as valuable prognostic predictors for DLBCL patients in existing studies, investigations combining these two indicators remain relatively limited. This may be attributed to the unavailability of MYC and BCL2 protein expression data for all patients, which hinders the assessment of their combined prognostic value and further compromises the generalizability and accuracy of the conclusions [48]. Given that DEL status directly reflects the molecular expression profile of tumors and may compensate for the limitation of 18F-FDG PET/CT in only capturing phenotypic features without directly reflecting molecular heterogeneity, we conducted this study involving a cohort of 210 patients with DLBCL at baseline. Multivariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated that TMTV, Dmax, and DEL status were all independent prognostic factors for PFS and OS in DLBCL patients. Based on these findings, we innovatively constructed a three-factor prognostic model (MDE). This model exhibited superior prognostic value in predicting DLBCL outcomes compared with the IPI: validated by 1000-fold bootstrap resampling, the MDE model yielded a lower mean AIC and a higher mean C-index. For PFS, the area under the receiver AUC of the MDE model (0.797) was significantly larger than that of the IPI model (0.700), with statistical significance confirmed by three different tests (DeLong test: P = 0.0146; bootstrap validation: P = 0.0135; Venkatraman permutation test: P = 0.0135). In the multivariate analysis incorporating both the IPI score and the MDE model, the MDE model emerged as the only significant prognostic factor for the primary endpoint of PFS (IPI did not reach statistical significance), indicating that the MDE model is distinct from the IPI model and offers superior prognostic predictive value. Subsequent survival analysis of the MDE model revealed that pairwise comparisons between the low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups all showed statistically significant differences in outcomes (all P < 0.05). For OS, both MDE and IPI serve as independent prognostic predictors, which may stem from differing sets of influencing factors between OS and PFS [49].

Most importantly, the MDE model identified a high-risk subgroup with extremely poor prognosis, whose 5-year PFS and OS rates (22.4% and 29.7%, respectively) were significantly lower than those of high-risk patients defined by the IPI (34.6% and 32.6%), indicating that this MDE-identified high-risk population exhibits notably elevated relapse rates and inferior overall survival outcomes that demand close clinical attention in the early stage; to further explore the value of the MDE model in risk reclassification relative to the IPI, we performed survival analyses on patients re-stratified by MDE scores within their original IPI risk groups, and notably, nearly two-fifths of these reclassified patients who were initially categorized as low- or intermediate-risk by the IPI were reassigned to the MDE high-risk group, with survival analysis results revealing significant differences in both PFS and OS among patients re-stratified by the MDE model within all original IPI risk groups (low, intermediate, and high) (all P < 0.0001), demonstrating the MDE scoring system’s enhanced discriminative ability compared with the IPI. These superior performances may be attributed to the MDE model’s integration of 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters (reflecting macroscopic tumor metabolic burden and distribution) with DEL status (directly embodying tumor molecular aggressiveness), as the latter likely compensates for 18F-FDG PET/CT’s limitation of only capturing phenotypic features without directly reflecting molecular heterogeneity, enabling “phenotypic-molecular” dual-dimensional prognostic assessment that allows the MDE model to more fully reflect tumor burden, extent of dissemination, and heterogeneity rather than being confined to simple clinical and biological indicators. In summary, our findings indicate that compared with clinical prognostic biomarkers represented by the IPI, integrating tumor burden, tumor dissemination distance, and DEL status enables more accurate identification of DLBCL patients at high risk of relapse or refractoriness following first-line R-CHOP therapy, and early identification of such patients facilitates the implementation of more intensive follow-up and timely intensified treatment, ultimately improving their survival outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective single-center study, the prognostic value of the novel MDE model integrating tumor burden, tumor dissemination distance, and DEL status requires further validation through large-sample, multi-center prospective studies. Second, since 18F-FDG PET/CT is not a mandatory requirement for baseline assessment, differences in patients’ economic status and physicians’ preferences have inevitably introduced selection bias. Third, no unified cutoff value standards have been established for 18F-FDG PET/CT variables to date [6, 17, 50]. Although maximum selected rank statistics were used in this study to determine the optimal cutoff values for each 18F-FDG PET/CT variable in predicting PFS to ensure consistency in the analysis of all available data, the cutoffs identified in this study need further validation and standardization in other DLBCL patient cohorts to confirm their prognostic utility.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that the MDE risk score, integrating baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomic features and DEL status, exhibits superior prognostic performance to the IPI in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients receiving first-line R-CHOP therapy. It significantly improves PFS and OS stratification across all IPI risk subgroups and enables more precise identification of high-risk patients, providing a basis for personalized treatment strategies to ultimately optimize clinical outcomes.

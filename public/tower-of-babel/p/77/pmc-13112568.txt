Surgical Diagram and Bifurcated Stent Graft System Stent Diagrams

Endovascular aortic repair has emerged as a cornerstone in the management of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms (TAAAs),1 offering minimally invasive intervention with a favorable safety profile characterized by low complication rates. However, TAAAs frequently pose unique challenges due to their involvement of multiple abdominal visceral arteries. The standard approach for treating such complex aortic pathologies typically relies on custom-made stents—a treatment modality unavailable in numerous countries, including China. Existing alternative techniques, such as extracorporeal modification or intraoperative customization (eg, fenestration of covered stents), may compromise the structural integrity of the stent.2 Similarly, parallel stent techniques (eg, the octopus technique) are associated with a heightened risk of severe endoleaks.3

To address these complexities in endovascular TAAA treatment, there is an urgent need for an off-the-shelf device that eliminates reliance on custom-made stents, avoids in vitro modifications that could weaken stent structure, maintains a low endoleak rate, and simplifies the surgical procedure. This paper introduces a novel bifurcated stent graft system (BSGS) (LifeTech Scientific) designed specifically for TAAA treatment.4

Case Summary

A 75-year-old man presented with a type III TAAA, with a maximum diameter of 70 mm (Figures 1 and 2A, Video 1). To reduce the risk of spinal cord ischemia and paraplegia, a lumbar puncture catheter was inserted preoperatively.

Intraoperative Procedure Steps

(A) Preoperative imaging. (B) Placement of a bifurcated abdominal aortic coated stent (XJZDZ34120). (C) Placement of the first bifurcated stent graft system (BSGS) (IB1614-080-130) bridging the long leg of the main stent (the direction of the red arrows is the side branch of the stent). (D) Reconstruction of the celiac trunk artery through the side branch of the BSGS (PS-C-10060-XL). (E) Placement of the second BSGS (IB1614-080-130) bridging the short leg of the main stent. (F) Reconstruction of the superior mesenteric artery (PS-C-10080-XL) via the BSGS side branch. (G) Placement of a third BSGS (IB1614-50-120) bridging the first BSGS. (H) Reconstruction of the right renal artery (PS-C-06060-XL) via the BSGS side branch. (I) Placement of the fourth BSGS (IB1614-50-130) bridging the second BSGS. (J) Reconstruction of the left renal artery (PS-C-06060-XL) through the BSGS side branch. (K) Bilateral leg branches continued to bridge the overlying stents (IB1414-120-120, IB1416-120-120) for reconstruction to the bilateral common iliac arteries. (L) Postoperative imaging.

Under general anesthesia, vascular access was established via bilateral femoral arteries and the left brachial artery. A bifurcated abdominal aortic overlay stent (XJZDZ34120; LifeTech Scientific) was introduced into the left femoral artery approach (Figure 2B, Video 2), and the first BSGS (IB1614-080-130; LifeTech Scientific) was inserted to bridge the long leg of the main stent graft (Figure 2C, Video 3). The left arm arterial approach was introduced with a 9-F adjustable curved sheath (LifeTech Scientific), and the guidewire with a catheter was selectively advanced into the celiac trunk artery and reconstructed (PS-C-10060-XL, LifeTech Scientific) via the lateral branch of the BSGS (Figure 2D, Video 4). The right femoral artery approach introduces a second BSGS (IB1614-080-130; LifeTech Scientific) bridging the short leg of the main stent and reconstructing the superior mesenteric artery (PS-C-10080-XL, LifeTech Scientific) in a similar way (Figures 2E and 2F, Videos 5 and 6). The third BSGS (IB1614-50-120, LifeTech Scientific) bridges the first BSGS and reconstructs the right renal artery (PS-C-06060-XL, LifeTech Scientific) (Figures 2G and 2H, Videos 7 and 8). The short leg of the main stent was continued to receive the fourth BSGS (IB1614-50-130; LifeTech Scientific), and the left renal artery was reconstructed (PS-C-06060-XL, LifeTech Scientific) (Figures 2I and 2J, Videos 9 and 10). Bilateral leg branches were continued with bridging overlay stents (IB1414-120-120, IB1416-120-120, LifeTech Scientific) for reconstruction to the bilateral common iliac arteries (Figure 2K, Video 11). The final angiogram revealed 4 visceral arteries with excellent visualization and no significant internal leakage (Figure 2L, Videos 12 and 13). Postoperative cerebrospinal fluid drainage was performed. Follow-up computed tomography angiography of the entire aorta at 6, 12, and 24 months postoperatively confirmed patency of all visceral branches with no evidence of endoleak (Figure 3). At 27 months postoperatively, the patient maintained normal gastrointestinal and renal function, with no abnormalities in limb movement.

Novelty of the Submission

Current therapeutic options for TAAAs include open surgery, hybrid surgery, and total endovascular repair. Total endovascular repair remains challenging, primarily due to the complexity of reconstructing multiple abdominal visceral arteries. The Cook T-branch graft is an off-the-shelf device specifically designed for endovascular repair of TAAAs.5 However, it has not yet been launched in China. Domestically, the parallel stent technique or on-table customized approaches are typically used, though both present certain drawbacks. We have successfully treated TAAAs for the first time using a novel off-the-shelf device, achieving favorable therapeutic outcomes.

Future Directions

The BSGS presents a safer, simpler, and more effective strategy for the endovascular management of TAAAs.

Conclusions

Addressing the clinical challenges of endovascular treatment for TAAAs, the BSGS delivers a standardized, one-stop intervention solution. Its core advantage lies in eliminating the treatment delays caused by the production cycle of customized devices, while simultaneously reducing the risk of endoleaks through optimized therapeutic strategies. This technological innovation injects a breakthrough impetus into the advancement of aortic interventional therapy.

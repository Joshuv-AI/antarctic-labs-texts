Addendum: Site occupancy of interstitial deuterium atoms in face-centred cubic iron
Authors: Akihiko Machida, Hiroyuki Saitoh, Hidehiko Sugimoto, Takanori Hattori, Asami Sano-Furukawa, Naruki Endo, Yoshinori Katayama, Riko Iizuka, Toyoto Sato, Motoaki Matsuo, Shin-ichi Orimo, Katsutoshi Aoki
Published: 2024

Addendum to: Nature Communications 10.1038/ncomms6063, published online 26 September 2014

In the previous structural study of fcc-FeDx, we determined the site occupancies of D atoms in an fcc Fe metal lattice via Rietveld refinement of neutron powder diffraction patterns collected at 988 K and 6.3 GPa1. The fcc metal lattice has interstitial octahedral (O) and tetrahedral (T) sites for accommodating D atoms. Two occupation models were examined: only octahedral occupation (model-1) and both octahedral and tetrahedral occupations (model-2). The D-atom occupancies optimized with model-2, 0.532(9) and 0.056(5) for the octahedral and tetrahedral sites, respectively, reproduced the experimental diffraction pattern better than that optimized with model-1. Deuterium atoms occupied mainly the octahedral sites and also slightly the tetrahedral sites. Subsequent density-functional-theory (DFT) calculations by Antonov2 revealed that the T-site occupation energy was significantly higher than the O-site occupation energy; the T-site occupation could be excluded even at temperatures as high as 988 K.

Inspired by the DFT calculations, we reexamined the D-atom occupation models with the powder diffraction patterns used in the previous refinement. In the Rietveld refinement, we included extinction correction3; the deuteride synthesized at 988 K likely consisted of relatively large-size crystallites and constituting Fe and D atoms have long scattering lengths for neutron. The specific characteristics of the hydride suggested the need for extinction correction using crystallite size, Cs, as a variable parameter. We also added a constraint on the atomic displacement parameter (ADP) for D atom4. The ADPs of O-site and T-site D atoms, BDO and BDT, respectively, were optimized under a constraint of BDO = 2.2BDT instead of BDO = BDT in the previous refinement. The factor of 2.2 was calculated using the formula derived for estimating the ratio of BDO/BDT at a given temperature4 and the vibrational energies of the O-site and T-site D atoms3. The experimental diffraction patterns were well reproduced with the structure parameters refined with the two occupation models and accordingly, the goodness-of-fit χ2 and weighted profile residual Rwp were improved. In refinement with model-2, the gDO and gDT, starting with the previous values, converged to 0.62(2) and 0.006(5), respectively. The former value agreed, within the fitting errors, with 0.60(1) refined with the O-site occupation model-1, and the latter value, comparable to the fitting error, can be considered zero. The refined site occupancies excluded the T-site occupation and concluded only the O-site occupation. We adopted gDO = 0.60(1) with a small fitting error as the revised octahedral occupancy. The revised gDO, or D-content x, increased the hydrogen-induced volume expansion coefficient from the previous value of 2.21 to 2.36 Å3.

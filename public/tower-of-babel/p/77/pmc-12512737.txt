Successful treatment of osimertinib-resistant EGFR-mutant NSCLC with acquired BRAF V600E mutation using triple combination therapy: a case report with pathological confirmation
Authors: MingHui Lin, XuYu Chen, Fan Lin, YanBo Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNon-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with EGFR mutations often develops resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), with acquired BRAF V600E mutation being a rare but clinically challenging mechanism. The efficacy of combined EGFR and BRAF/MEK inhibition in this setting is not sufficiently characterized.Case presentationA 56-year-old never-smoker with stage IVA EGFR exon 19del-mutant lung adenocarcinoma developed progressive disease on osimertinib, with a new BRAF V600E mutation detected via next-generation sequencing (NGS). She was treated with osimertinib (80 mg daily), dabrafenib (150 mg twice daily), and trametinib (2 mg daily), achieving: Radiological response: Regression of metastatic lesions (left lower lobe residual nodule) and stable disease in the primary lesion. Pathological confirmation: Post-surgical resection revealed only 1% residual tumor viability, indicating a major pathological response. Tolerability: Only grade 1 rash (CTCAE v5.0) was observed. Durable control: Progression-free survival (PFS) of 11 months (last follow-up: May 2025).ConclusionTriple therapy with osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib represents a viable and well-tolerated approach for EGFR-mutant NSCLC with acquired BRAFV600E resistance. Molecular profiling upon progression is essential to guide targeted therapy.

Introduction

Lung cancer persists as the leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) constituting the predominant histological subtype [1]. Over the past three decades, substantial advancements in NSCLC treatment have been achieved through the identification of oncogenic driver mutations and the subsequent development of molecularly targeted therapies. Among these, activating mutations in the tyrosine kinase domain of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene represent the most extensively characterized oncogenic drivers, with exon 19 deletions (19del) and the L858R substitution in exon 21 accounting for the majority of cases [2]. Herein, we present a novel case of EGFR-mutant NSCLC in which the patient developed an acquired BRAF V600E mutation following osimertinib treatment. The patient subsequently received combination therapy with osimertinib, dabrafenib (a BRAF inhibitor), and trametinib (a MEK inhibitor). No grade ≥ 3 adverse events were observed during the triple therapy. The patient experienced only rash (grade 1), which did not require dose modification. The patient was closely monitored for toxicity, with regular clinical and laboratory assessments. Treatment was well-tolerated throughout the 11-month PFS period. We present the following case in accordance with the CARE reporting checklist.

Discussion

Osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR-TKI, has become the standard first-line treatment for metastatic EGFR-mutant NSCLC [3]. However, the inevitable emergence of acquired resistance represents a major clinical challenge, and optimal treatment strategies following disease progression remain to be established. Unlike BRAF mutations as a primary oncogenic driver in NSCLC, BRAF mutations as an acquired resistance mechanism to osimertinib can occur concomitantly with EGFR mutations. BRAF V600 mutations as an acquired resistance mechanism, specifically, occur in approximately 3% of EGFR-mutant NSCLCs exposed to osimertinib in the first- or second-line setting [4]. Here we present a case of advanced lung adenocarcinoma harboring an EGFR exon 19del that developed acquired resistance following osimertinib therapy. Through comprehensive genomic analysis using NGS on the progressive tumor biopsy specimen from the dorsal segment of the left lower lobe, the co-occurrence of the original EGFR 19del mutation and the acquired BRAF V600E mutation was identified.

Dabrafenib, a BRAF inhibitor, and trametinib, a MEK inhibitor, received U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval in June 2017 for the treatment of NSCLC harboring the BRAF V600E mutation [5]. This regulatory approval was granted based on findings from a phase II multicenter clinical trial (BRF113928; NCT01336634) that evaluated the efficacy of dabrafenib and trametinib in both treatment-naïve and pre-treated patients with metastatic NSCLC positive for the BRAF V600E mutation. Notably, the trial excluded patients with previously treated EGFR or ALK alterations and those with active brain metastases [6]. The combination therapy demonstrated significant clinical benefits, achieving an objective response rate (ORR) of 63%, a median progression-free survival (mPFS) of 10.2 months, and a median overall survival (mOS) of 18.2 months.

While the combination of dabrafenib and trametinib has shown remarkable efficacy in treatment-naïve patients with BRAF V600E-mutated NSCLC, its therapeutic potential in patients developing acquired BRAF V600E mutation following EGFR-TKI resistance remains poorly characterized. The lack of robust clinical trial data in this specific patient population presents a critical challenge in therapeutic decision-making. In this context, we present a case of a patient who developed an acquired BRAF V600E mutation after osimertinib resistance and was successfully treated with the combination of osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib. The therapeutic efficacy of this regimen was histologically confirmed through pathological analysis of surgical specimens obtained following combination therapy. For patients with stage IV driver gene-positive lung cancer, identifying the optimal time window for local treatment after achieving a good response to systemic therapy, but before the development of resistance, is ideal [7].

Notably, the pre-treatment biopsy (left upper lobe) and post-resistance biopsy were derived from anatomically distinct sites. The initial testing (10-gene lung cancer panel, covering BRAF variants including V600E/K/R/D) revealed an EGFR 19del mutation but no BRAF alterations (the testing included V600E, V600K, V600E2, V600R, V600D1, and V600D2). Unfortunately, since the patient’s initial diagnosis and treatment were not conducted at our hospital, we are unable to obtain additional genetic testing information for the left upper lobe biopsy for comparison. Consequently, the observed BRAF V600E discordance could not be conclusively attributed to either tumor heterogeneity or bona fide resistance mechanisms. However, the stability of the EGFR 19del mutation supports the interpretation that BRAF V600E likely represents an acquired resistance alteration induced by osimertinib.

The emergence of acquired BRAF V600E mutations as a resistance mechanism to third-generation EGFR-TKIs presents a substantial therapeutic challenge in advanced NSCLC management. The clinical development of effective treatment strategies is hindered by the infrequent occurrence of secondary BRAF driver mutations following EGFR-TKI progression and the substantial molecular heterogeneity of resistance mechanisms. Currently, the absence of prospective clinical trial data necessitates reliance on case reports and retrospective case series for therapeutic decision-making [8, 9].In a notable case series by Giustini et al. [10]. involving eight patients, NGS analysis revealed the persistence of original EGFR mutations upon disease progression, a finding consistent with our clinical observations. This molecular evidence supports the rationale for implementing combination therapy with osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib in this clinical context.

Therefore, for NSCLC with acquired BRAF V600E and epidermal growth factor receptor mutations, the combination therapy of osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib is both effective and tolerable. However, it is important to consider the potential adverse reactions associated with this three-drug regimen. Given the limited clinical data on this combination, we initiated standard dosing based on the patients’ preferences and the needs of tumor treatment. However, we acknowledge that a dose-escalation approach (e.g., starting with reduced doses) could be considered in future cases to further minimize potential toxicity. The standard treatment approach after progression on EGFR-TKIs is to conduct comprehensive molecular testing following progression on osimertinib to identify any actionable mutations that may be present. Future studies should explore optimal dosing and toxicity management of this triple regimen.

Conclusion

This case demonstrates that triple therapy with osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib can achieve a significant pathological and radiological response in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with acquired BRAF V600E-mediated resistance. The 1% residual tumor viability (MPR) and 11-month PFS underscore the potential of this combinatorial strategy, particularly when supported by rigorous molecular profiling and histopathological confirmation.

While BRAF V600E remains a rare resistance mechanism, its actionable nature highlights the critical role of re-biopsy and comprehensive genomic testing at disease progression. Our findings align with emerging evidence on bypass signaling inhibition but also reveal gaps in current guidelines, which lack standardized approaches for EGFR/BRAF co-mutations. Future studies should explore optimal dosing and toxicity management of this triple regimen.

This case adds to the growing body of evidence supporting molecularly tailored therapy in advanced NSCLC, advocating for personalized strategies beyond conventional algorithms.

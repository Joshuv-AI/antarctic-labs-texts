Comparison of Split-Type Postman and C-MAC Video Laryngoscopes for Ease of Intubation in Simulated Restricted Neck Mobility: A Randomised Clinical Trial

Amina K, Thalangara Mukhthar

Yenepoya Medical College, Mangalore, Karnataka

Background and Aims: This study compared the Split-Type Postman and C-MAC video laryngoscopes for ease of endotracheal intubation in simulated restricted neck mobility. The primary objective was to compare intubation time. Secondary objectives included first-attempt success rate, haemodynamic responses, and need for ancillary airway devices.

Methods: This is a prospective, randomised study conducted on 50 American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) I and II patients aged 18–60 years with BMI <30 kg/m² undergoing elective surgery under general anaesthesia. Prior to the study, Institutional Ethics Committee approval, Clinical Trials Registry-India registration, and written informed consent from patients were obtained. Restricted neck mobility was simulated by applying hard cervical collar after induction. Patients were randomly assigned to undergo intubation with either the Split-Type Postman or the C-MAC video laryngoscope using computer-generated randomisation and the sealed-envelope technique. The intubation time, first-attempt success, haemodynamic changes, and use of ancillary devices were recorded.

Results: The intubation time was significantly shorter in the Postman group (28.95 ± 2.41 seconds) compared to the C-MAC group (36.77 ± 2.31 seconds) (P < 0.001). First-attempt success rates were comparable between groups. Although the C-MAC group required slightly fewer attempts overall, the difference was not statistically significant. Use of ancillary devices and haemodynamic parameters showed no significant difference.

Conclusion: The Split-Type Postman video laryngoscope resulted in significantly shorter intubation time with comparable haemodynamic stability to the C-MAC video laryngoscope. It may therefore be a more effective option for airway management in patients with restricted neck mobility.

Keywords: C-MAC video laryngoscope, split-type postman

Effect of Phenylephrine on Body Temperature During Elective Delivery Under Spinal Anaesthesia: A randomised Clinical Trial

Neha Mary Noble, Kunal Singh, Nishant Sahay, Abhyuday Kumar, Mukta Agarwal

AIIMS, Patna, Bihar

Background and Aims: Phenylephrine, a selective alpha-1 adrenergic receptor agonist, helps mitigate the drop in core body temperature during spinal anaesthesia by reducing the redistribution of heat from the body’s core to the periphery. Thus, we hypothesised that phenylephrine infusion attenuates the decrease in core body temperature in parturients undergoing caesarean delivery under spinal anaesthesia, compared to standard care without phenylephrine.

Methods: This double-blinded randomised controlled trial comprised 100 parturients, aged 18 to 45 years scheduled to undergo elective caesarean delivery under spinal anaesthesia. Participants were divided into two groups randomly containing 50 in each group. Patients in the phenylephrine group received an infusion of phenylephrine at 0.5 mcg/kg/min, and the control group received normal saline at a similar rate. The primary outcome was to evaluate the change in core temperature. Secondary outcomes included shivering, incidence of hypotension, bradycardia, and APGAR scores.

Results: The demographic characteristics of both the groups were similar. The change in core temperature (0.58 °C ± 0.13 vs 0.88 °C ± 0.12 with a P-value of <0.001) was significantly lower in the phenylephrine group compared to the control group. The incidence of shivering in the phenylephrine and control group was 8% and 50%, respectively, with severity being higher in the control group (p-value <0.001). The incidence of hypotension was significantly higher in the control group (p-value <0.001), but the incidence of bradycardia was insignificant.

Conclusions: Phenylephrine infusion attenuates the change in core temperature and decreases hypothermia in patients undergoing caesarean delivery under spinal anaesthesia.

Keywords: Hypothermia, phenylephrine hydrochloride, spinal anaesthesia

Comparison of Ultrasound-Guided External Oblique Intercostal Plane Block Versus Subcostal Transversus Abdominis Plane Block for Postoperative Analgesia in Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy Surgeries: A Randomised Controlled Clinical Study

Gopinath Reddy, Mamta Sinha, Mayank Kumar, Chethan Anand

AIIMS, Raipur, Chhattisgarh

Background and Aims: Laparoscopic cholecystectomy is a common surgical procedure often associated with significant postoperative pain. Ultrasound-guided fascial plane blocks, such as subcostal transversus abdominis plane (STAP) block and the newer external oblique intercostal plane (EOIP) block, have been increasingly utilised for analgesia, but comparative evidence is limited.

Methods: In this prospective, randomised controlled study, 64 adult patients undergoing elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy under general anaesthesia were randomly allocated into two groups: EOIP block (Group E) and STAP block (Group T). Both groups received bilateral blocks with 20 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine plus 4 mg dexamethasone per side. The primary outcome was time to first rescue analgesia. The secondary outcomes were visual analogue scale (VAS) scores (at 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h)24 h tramadol and diclofenac consumption, and block-related complications.

Results: Demographic and intraoperative parameters were comparable between groups. An equal number of patients in both groups did not require analgesics postoperatively (37.5%). The mean [SD] of time to rescue analgesia [minutes] was 294.57 [118.23] and 267.78 [114.45] with p > 0.05 in Groups E and T, respectively. Pain scores gradually increased over 24 hours in both groups. Although Group E showed slightly lower pain scores at early time points, no statistically significant difference was observed at any interval (all p > 0.05). At 12 and 24 hours, a similar proportion of patients in both groups required rescue analgesia. No significant differences were observed in type or amount of analgesic used (p > 0.05).

Conclusion: EOIP block and STAP block offer comparable postoperative analgesia after laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Given its ease of administration and favourable anatomical landmarks, EOIP block can be considered a safe and effective alternative to the STAP block in multimodal analgesic regimens.

Keywords: External oblique intercostal plane block, laparoscopic cholecystectomy, subcostal transversus abdominis plane block

Use of Chatbot to Assist Pre-Anaesthetic Clinic: A Feasibility Study

Nikita Saji, Zubair Mohamed, Kamal Bijlani, Marva Abdul Khader, Rajesh Kesavan

Amrita Institute of Medical Sciences and Research Center, Kochi, Kerala

Background and Aims: Integration of a chatbot in a pre-anaesthetic clinic (PAC) could improve efficiency, convenience, and data collection. We evaluated the feasibility and acceptability of chatbot-assisted PAC.

Methods: Ethics approval (ECASM-AIMS-2024-281) and Clinical Trials Registry-India registration (CTRI/2024/11/076403) were obtained. The entire PAC questions were algorithmised, integrated to chatbot, and made accessible via QR code. Consenting patients 18–60 years scheduled for elective abdominal surgery, who had access to smartphones, and who were comfortable in English were included. Others were excluded. Patients were randomised into two groups: Group C (chatbot PAC followed by anaesthestist PAC) and Group H (anaesthestist PAC followed by chatbot PAC). Anaesthetists and patients were blinded to allocation sequence, and the time taken for PAC was recorded. Anonymised chatbot transcripts were summarised to 250 words by prompt engineering using five Large Language Models (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Llama, Grok, DeepSeek). The summaries were evaluated for lexical overlap (ROUGE Score) and semantic similarity (BERT Score) using Python and also manually using a validated questionnaire.

Spider plot displaying the performance of various LLMs

Results: A total of 212 patients were recruited (sample size determined via pilot study to show 20% reduction in PAC time with 80% power and 95% confidence). Chatbot PAC saved 20.4% time (15.15+3.73 v/s 19.04+3.77minutes, p < 0.0001). Over 98% anaesthetists reported satisfaction, citing efficiency and documentation ease. 5% patients preferred anaesthestist PAC. Llama produced the best summaries.

Conclusion: Chatbot-assisted Llama-summarised PAC reduced consultation time, improved workflow efficiency, and was highly acceptable to patients and clinicians. Future work should expand chatbot functions to airway assessment, assist differently abled, and provide multilingual options.

Keywords: Anaesthesiology, artificial intelligence, natural language processing, patient satisfaction, preoperative care

Correlation of Preoperative Phase Angle with Postoperative Quality Of Recovery in Patients Undergoing Elective Major Non-Cardiac Surgeries: A Prospective, Observational Single-Centre Study

B. Athira, Satyajeet Misra

AIIMS, Bhubaneswar, Orissa

Background and Aims: Accurate prediction of postoperative recovery is essential for optimising perioperative care, especially in patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgeries. Quality of recovery-15 (QoR-15) is a measure of the global recovery after surgery and predicts 30-day adverse outcomes after non-cardiac surgery. However, there are no tools to predict which patients may have a poor postoperative recovery since the QoR can be applied only postoperatively. Phase angle (PhA), derived from bioelectrical impedance analysis, reflects cellular integrity, hydration status, and nutritional reserve. Its association with postoperative QoR has not been adequately investigated. The aim of the study was to investigate the correlation of PhA with QoR-15.

Methods: This prospective observational study enroled adult patients undergoing elective major non-cardiac surgeries. Preoperative PhA was measured using a Bodystat Quadscan 1500 device. QoR was assessed using the QoR-15 questionnaire at 24 and 48 hours after surgery. Demographic data were collected, including Body mass index (BMI) and surgical variables, and followed up for complications and mortality.

Results: A total of 234 patients completed the study. PhA demonstrated strong positive correlation with QoR15 scores at 24 hours (r = 0.75, p < 0.001) and 48 hours (r = 0.70, p < 0.001). ROC analysis revealed a PhA ≤4.9 and predicted poor 24-hour QoR with 92.1% sensitivity and 80.1% specificity and a PhA cut-off of ≤4.9 in males and ≤4.8 in females as predictive of poor QoR at 24 hours. Similar trends were observed for complications and mortality: Patients with lower PhA had significantly higher risk of adverse outcomes at both 30 days and 6 months.

Conclusion: This study establishes PhA as a robust, non-invasive, and independent predictor of early postoperative recovery. It correlates strongly with postoperative QoR as well as complications and mortality. Integrating PhA into routine preoperative assessments may enhance personalised risk stratification, guide nutritional and prehabilitation strategies, and improve surgical outcomes.

Keywords: Angle, phase, quality, recovery

Evaluation of Predictive Ability of Sonography-Based Airway Assessment Parameters and Clinical Parameters as Predictors of Difficult Laryngoscopy And Intubation: An Observational Study

Vishakha Sinsinwal, Kavita Lalchandani

Government Medical College & SSG Hospital, Baroda, Gujarat

Background and Aims: Clinical parameters used routinely in airway assessment lack sensitivity and specificity for detecting difficult airway. Ultrasound is non-invasive and aids in rapid visualisation of structures. This study aims to evaluate predictive ability of ultrasound-based and clinical airway assessment methods for difficult laryngoscopy and intubation.

Methods: Fifty-four patients scheduled for elective surgical procedure requiring intubation were assessed pre-operatively for various clinical and ultrasonography- guided parameters. Assessed clinical parameters were: Mouth opening (MO), Modified Mallampati grade (MMP), Upper lip bite test (ULBT), Thyromental distance (TMD), mylohyoid distance (MHD), and Neck circumference (NC) and USG-guided parametes were: Hyomental distance neutral neck (HMDN), Hyomental distance extended neck (HMDE), Tongue width (TW), Tongue thickness (TT), Skin-to-epiglottis distance (SED), Skin to vocal cords distance (SVC), Anterior neck soft tissue distance at level of thyrohyoid membrane (ANS TM), and Anterior neck soft tissue distance at level of hyoid bone (ANS-Hyoid bone). The distribution of POGO Score (Percentage of glottic opening) and Intubation Difficulty scale (IDS) was correlated with clinical and ultrasound parameters.

Results: The MMP Grading, upper lip bite test, neck circumference, HMDN/HMDE ratio, skin-to-epiglottis distance, SED/SVC ratio, ANS-TM, and ANS-Hyoid parameters have p-values <0.05 and therefore are significant in determining difficult airway with ANS-TM having the highest specificity.

Conclusion: The ability to combine sonographic and clinical parameters increases the sensitivity and specificity of detecting difficult airway.

Twarita Singh, Rudrashish Haldar, Ashish Kumar Kannaujia, Devendra Gupta, Ruchi Verma, Nidhi Singh

Sanjay Gandhi Postgraduate Institute of Medical Sciences, Lucknow, UP

Background and Aims: Intracranial neurosurgery carries significant risk of damage to neural structures. Transcranial motor evoked potentials (TcMEPs) effectively monitor the integrity of motor pathways, which is critically dependent on anaesthetic techniques. Propofol infusion is routinely used during neuromonitoring. Use of ketofol infusion (1:4) has shown to increase amplitude during neuromonitoring in spinal surgeries. We hypothesised that similar observations will be recorded during conduct of cerebellopontine angle (CPA) tumour surgeries. The primary outcome studied was the comparison of TcMEPs between the two regimes. Secondary outcomes were comparison of haemodynamic parameters and complications.

Methods: Seventy patients belonging to ASA I and II, aged 18–65 years undergoing CPA tumour surgeries in a tertiary care hospital were enrolled over 18 months and randomised into Propofol (maintained on propofol infusion) and Ketofol (maintained on propofol and ketamine fixed dose infusion) groups. The TcMEPs were recorded from orbicularis oculi, frontalis, masseter, soft palate, and tongue along with haemodynamic parameters and complications.

Continuous variables were presented as mean and standard deviation or median (interquartile range) and compared using independent samples T test/Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented in number (%) and compared by the Chi-square test/Fisher exact test.

Results: Demographic parameters were comparable. No difference in amplitude and latency were detected over time in the Propofol group (p > 0.05). The Ketofol group, exhibited an amplitude increase in masseter, orbicularis oculi, and tongue (p < 0.05) after 2 hours with no change in latency. Haemodynamics and complications were comparable.

Conclusion: Ketofol facilitates TcMEPs amplitude without affecting latency in CPA tumour surgeries with stable haemodynamics.

Jyoti

Pt. B. D. Sharma Post Graduate Institute of Medical Sciences, Rohtak, Haryana

Background and Aims: Noise in operating theatres can impair communication, concentration, and staff performance. Neurosurgical procedures require utmost precision, yet limited data exist on noise exposure in these settings. The aim of the study was to measure noise levels in neurosurgical operation theatres and assess their effects on anaesthesia residents.

Methods: A prospective observational study was conducted in the neurosurgical OTs of Pt. B.D. Sharma PGIMS, Rohtak. Anaesthesia residents completed a structured questionnaire regarding perceived noise impact.

Intraoperative Hypotension Management with Adrenaline Infusion Versus Noradrenaline Infusion and Complications After Vascular Surgery – A Pilot, Pragmatic, Randomised Controlled Trial

Deepika NV, Ajay Kumar Jha, Satyen Parida, Sandeep K Mishra, Hemachandran M

JIPMER, Pondicherry

Background and Aims: The appropriate inotrope or vasopressor to prevent and manage intraoperative hypotension during vascular surgery has not been identified. Therefore, the study aimed to compare the effects of noradrenaline and adrenaline on the composite of postoperative complications among vascular surgery patients.

Methods: This was a pilot, pragmatic, randomised-controlled trial conducted in JIPMER, Puducherry. The study got approval from the institute's ethics committee and was registered at Clinical Trials Registry-India (CTRI/2023/07/055132). The patients provided written informed consent. Participants were randomised to receive either adrenaline or noradrenaline infusion as first-line treatment once the mean blood pressure decreased below 80 mm Hg despite maintaining normal stroke volume variation. The infusion was titrated to maintain the intraoperative mean blood pressure between 80 and 100 mmHg. Outcome measures were analysed by an independent t- test and Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analysed using the Chi-Square test or Fisher’s exact test, or the Wilcoxon test, as appropriate.

Mean arterial blood pressure trends-T1 baseline MAP, followed by timepoints every 15 minutes

Results: One hundred patients were randomised to receive noradrenaline or adrenaline infusion. The mean arterial pressure, heart rate, intraoperative fluid infusion, and duration of surgery were comparable. The composite of complications on the 7th postoperative day was significantly higher in the adrenaline group (46% vs 16%; RR; 2.87 (1.47-5.8); P = 0.003). However, the organ-specific complication remained comparable. The MoCA score (26.52 ± 4.03 vs 24.31 ± 6.66; p = 0.04) was higher in the noradrenaline arm, while quality of life remained similar at 3 months follow-up.

Conclusion: Proactively maintaining mean arterial blood pressure using noradrenaline showed a significant reduction in the composite of postoperative complications during vascular surgery.

Keywords: Adrenaline, hypotension, noradrenaline, vascular surgery

Comparison of Thoracic Epidural Analgesia and Ultrasound-Guided Erector Spinae Plane Block for Postoperative Pain Control Following Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy: A Randomised Clinical Trial

Prem kumar P, L. Pradipkumar Singh

Regional Institute of Medical Sciences, Imphal, Manipur, India

Background and Aims: Thoracic epidural analgesia (TEA) is considered the gold standard for postoperative pain relief but is associated with complications such as hypotension, urinary retention, and motor blockade. The erector spinae plane block (ESPB) is a newer, ultrasound-guided, technically simpler alternative. This study aimed to compare the analgesic efficacy and safety of TEA and ESPB using Ropivacaine with dexmedetomidine as an adjuvant in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

Methods: Researchers conducted this prospective, randomised, single-blinded clinical trial after obtaining Institutional Ethical Committee approval (A/206/REB/RIMS/973/04/2023) and trial registration (CTRI/2025/02/081249). Researchers randomised 80 adult American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA I-III) patients undergoing elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy into two groups: Group A received TEA (15 ml of 0.375% ropivacaine with 7.5 μg dexmedetomidine), and Group B received bilateral ESPB (15 ml per side of 0.187% ropivacaine with 7.5 μg dexmedetomidine). Data were analysed using SPSS v26.0. The primary outcome was duration of analgesia. Secondary outcomes included VAS scores, analgesic requirement, haemodynamics parameters, and complications.

Results: The mean duration of analgesia was significantly longer in Group A (894 ± 115.931 min) compared to Group B (828 ± 81.750 min); P = 0.004, 95% CI: 23.6–110.4.

Genomic characterization of growth differentiation factor 9 (GDF9): mutational and functional impact on reproductive traits of Cholistani Buchi sheep

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•GDF9 is a candidate gene associated with reproductive traits in sheep.•It is critical for folliculogenesis and oogenesis, since it plays a vital role in female fertility in sheep.•Various molecular techniques are widely used to identify genes associated with economically important traits in domestic animals.•Specific GDF9 gene mutations can improve prolificacy, while others may impair fertility.•GDF9 serves as a valuable molecular marker for genetic improvement and selection in sheep breeding programs.Keywords: Buchi sheep, GDF9, Mutations, PCR, Reproductive traits

Introduction

A sheep (Ovis aries) provides valuable agricultural products to people, making it an important economic animal. Increasing ewe productivity and reproductive performance generally enhances the economic and biological efficiency of the sheep industry. Many factors affect reproductive performance, including litter size, season, photoperiod, age, nutrition, management, male behavior, and genetics (Ajafar et al., 2023; Ali et al., 2022). In order to accurately quantify genetic variation for economically significant traits and animal breeding values and to implement successful genetic improvement strategies, it is necessary to correctly estimate these factors (Akhtar et al., 2012). Domesticated approximately 11,000 years ago, sheep have spread to different continents and adapted to diverse agricultural ecological conditions accompanied human migration, including extreme weather such as highlands and deserts (Mas-Coma et al., 2022). Thus, sheep represent an ideal system for studies on convergent evolution between closely related domestic species because of similar selective pressures on production traits (e.g., meat, milk, and wool/hair) during their domestication and genetic improvement (Abd-Allah et al., 2019; Alberto et al., 2018).

Comprehending the genetic makeup of female fertility in sheep is crucial for the profitable production of meat. Increase in litter size ultimately influenced by ovulation rate, increases the economic value of genetic advancement under selection. For a long time, increasing the litter size of sheep has been a pressing scientific concern and researchers have found main genes linked to prolificacy of sheep (Ajafar et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2018). Growth differentiation factor 9 (GDF9), bone morphogenetic protein 15 (BMP15), bone morphogenetic protein 7 (BMP7), and bone morphogenetic protein receptor 1B (BMPR1B) are among the genes with known target mutations that have been studied in connection with litter size (Çelikeloğlu et al., 2021; Paz et al., 2015; Saleh et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2019). Additionally, several other genes such as SLC5A1 (Solute Carrier Family 5 Member 1), CCNA1 (Cyclin A1), and ABCC1 (ATP Binding Cassette Subfamily C Member 1) have been reported to influence reproductive outcomes and litter size in sheep (Ajafar et al., 2022; La et al., 2019). Consistent with other BMP family genes, BMP4 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein 4) plays a crucial role in ovarian follicular development, which directly impacts litter size (Ajafar et al., 2022).

GDF9, also known as FecG, is found on sheep fifth autosome and plays an important role in folliculogenesis from the early stages of follicle growth to ovulation. GDF9 is critical for ovulation (folliculogenesis and oogenesis), since it plays a vital role in female fertility in sheep (de Castro et al., 2015; Kidder & Vanderhyden, 2010). GDF9 has an impact on both in vitro oocyte maturation and embryo viability by its active participation in crucial follicle-oocyte interactions (maturation, fertilization, and embryonic development) necessary for successful fertilization (Fountas et al., 2024). Various point mutations (G1-G8) have been reported in the Cambridge productive and Belclare sheep breeds (Hanrahan et al., 2004). The high ovulation rate is mostly determined by a mutation caused in one site (heterozygous) of the GDF9 gene; however, if this mutation occurs in both sites of the gene, follicle growth is disrupted, resulting in homozygous infertility (Nicol et al., 2009). Genetic variations of the GDF9 gene were verified as the key gene markers for the improvement of prolificacy in sheep (Aboelhassan et al., 2021).

To find causative variations and define specific genomic regions, approaches based on single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were reported in many household animals (Rupp et al., 2016; Xu & Li, 2017; Zhao et al., 2026). Other methods for comprehending the genetic background and clarifying the relationships between genes and phenotypic features in mammals include sequencing technology and bioinformatics (Gebreselassie et al., 2019). Scientists have discovered genetic regions and polymorphisms that affect economically important animal traits such as productivity, reproduction, and welfare (Adebayo et al., 2024; Kubota et al., 2025). Thus, various molecular techniques have been used to identify genes linked to economically significant features and their functions in domestic animals.

Owing to limited genomic studies on Buchi sheep breed, there is a need to characterize and analyze genetic mutations in Buchi sheep for better understanding of common and unique polymorphisms that give the species its desirable traits. To date, no study has been conducted so far to explore the genetic mutations across GDF9 gene in Cholistani Buchi sheep breed of Southern Punjab, Pakistan. Thereby, we initiated this study to document genetic mutations and their possible impact on fertile and infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep using comprehensive molecular and In Silico analyses. The outcome of this study will enhance the landscape of GDF9 gene mutations role in the growth and litter size of Cholistani Buchi sheep breed in the studied characteristics.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

The current study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB-08-ORIC) and Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBSC), Office of Research Innovation and Commercialization (ORIC) of The Government Sadiq College Women University, Bahawalpur. Prior consent was obtained from livestock owners before collecting blood samples from the animals. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, and the study is reported in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

Sample collection

In this study, Cholistani Buchi Sheep breed was selected for blood sampling. 108 blood samples of Cholistani Buchi sheep (54 infertile samples and 54 fertile samples) were collected. The animals were selected from different livestock farms and smallholder herds in the Cholistan region of Southern Punjab, Pakistan. Fertility status was determined based on reproductive history, kidding records, and information provided by farm owners and veterinarians (Supplementary Table 1). Fertility and infertility in 108 Cholistani Buchi sheep samples was further confirmed by Progesterone and Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) tests. Infertile sheep indicated significantly lower serum progesterone (0.587 ± 0.172 ng/mL) and lower AMH levels (1.039 ± 0.128 ng/mL) compared with fertile sheep, indicating higher serum progesterone (4.440 ± 0.489 ng/mL) and higher AMH levels (3.174 ± 0.275 ng/mL) (Supplementary Table 2). Approximately 5ml of blood was collected from the jugular vein of each sheep using sterile syringes and transferred into the EDTA-coated tubes, and then stored at -20°C till future use. Prior permission was obtained from livestock owners, and all procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the ethical use and care of animals in research.

DNA extraction

The genomic DNA of Buchi sheep was extracted using Qiagen AMP DNA mini extraction kit. The concentration and quality of genomic DNA was determined using 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. To check the concentration and purity, the DNA was quantified using ultramicro spectrophotometer (NanoDrop One, Thermo Scientific, USA). The samples were then stored at -20°C till further use.

PCR amplification and DNA sequencing

The amplification of GDF9 gene in sheep (Gene ID: 100217402) was performed using a pair of Forward: CCATGACTTTAGACTTAGC and Reverse: TGGTTTTACTTGACAGGAG primers. These primers were previously synthesized by Shanghai Invitrogen Biotechnology Limited Corporation (Shanghai, P.R. China), as reported by Chu et al. (2011). The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed in a total 25 μL volume including the following: 3 μL of DNA, 12.5 μL 2 × Taq PCR Master Mix, 1 μL of both forward and reverse primers (10 mol/L), and 7.5 μL PCR water. The PCR reaction procedure involved initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 s, and annealing at the optimal temperature 51°C for 40 s, then extension at 72°C for 30 s, and final extension at 72°C for 5 min. The PCR products were analyzed via 1.2% agarose gel electrophoresis. The amplified and purified PCR amplicon were sequenced by Sanger sequencing from APICAL SCIENTIFIC SDN. BHD, formerly known as First BASE Laboratories Sdn Bhd, a Biotechnology company in Seri Kembangan, Malaysia to confirm the presence of mutation.

Prediction of functional and structural impact of observed GDF9 mutations

The impact of observed GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep was predicted using DynaMut2, available at https://biosig.lab.uq.edu.au/dynamut2/submit_prediction. DynaMut2 is a web used to analyze and visualize protein dynamics by sampling conformations, as well as to evaluate the impact of mutations on protein dynamics and stability caused by the vibrational entropy changes. Physiochemical properties of GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep was also analyzed. Using ProtParam tool, available at https://web.expasy.org/protparam/, the molecular weight (MW), theoretical isoelectric point (pI), estimated half-life, instability index (II), aliphatic index (AI), and grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) of both wild type and mutated GDF9 gene in Cholistani Buchi sheep were calculated. ProtParam calculates numerous physicochemical parameters that can be derived from a protein sequence.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) and gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis

The Protein-Protein interaction (PPI) networks of GDF9 gene in Cholistani Buchi sheep was predicted using String (https://string-db.org/), a database to predict both the physical and functional linkages. Besides, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed using ShinyGO 0.81 (https://bioinformatics.sdstate.edu/go81/) and their GO biological process, GO cellular components and GO molecular functions were evaluated.

Allele frequency calculation

Allele frequencies of GDF9 mutations were calculated to determine the prevalence of mutant alleles within the studied populations. Since sheep are diploid, each individual contributes two alleles per locus, giving a total of 2 × N alleles, where N is the number of individuals in the group. For each mutation, the number of mutant alleles was counted across all samples, assuming heterozygosity unless zygosity was explicitly determined from sequencing data. Results

Identification of GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep

In Cholistani Buchi sheep, the GDF9 mutations were identified by Sanger sequencing analysis and EMBOSS pair wise sequence alignment tool was used further for pair wise sequence alignment of the amplicon sequences. Different mutations were identified at gene level which were then converted into mRNA, and then further translated into proteins. Out of 54 infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep samples, 47 samples were found positive for GDF9 gene mutations. Of these 47 samples, 33 mutations were identified in infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep (Fig. 1). These GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep include C2285T, A2360G, G2307A, T2370A, C2470A, T2402A, G2266T, C2431T, A2436T, C2253A, G2406C, A2456G, C2390G, G2465A, A2311T, G2324A, T2291C, T2378C, A2331C, G2321C, A2255G, G2260T, T2472C, A2338G, A2469C, C2475A, G2412C, T2442A, G2346T, C2281A, T2401G, C2481G, and C2274A. All the mutations are the novel one except G2307A (c. 1189 A>G). GDF9 mutations in infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep at gene level, mRNA level, and protein level are represented in Supplementary Table 3 and their chromatograms are represented in Supplementary Figure 1 to Figure 33. In fertile Buchi sheep, out of 54 samples only 46 samples were positive for GDF9 mutations but in this case just 15 mutations were identified in 46 samples. These GDF9 mutations in fertile Buchi sheep include C2285T, C2292T, A2366G, G2406C, A2419C, A2360G, A2382G, T2370A, G2307A, A2454C, G2355C, A2311T, G2324A, G2321C, and T2401G (Fig. 2). Detailed information of these observed mutations are represented in Supplementary Table 4 and their chromatograms are represented in Supplementary Figure 34 to Figure 48.

Functional impact of observed GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep

To determine the functional impact of GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep, DynaMut2 was used and the predicted stability change in GDF9 mutations observed in infertile and fertile Cholistani Buchi sheep was compared. In infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep, V397I mutation in sample 3, 5, 8, 11, 14, 24, 27, and 30, T451N mutation in sample 10, G383V mutation in sample 14, 28, and 40, A438V mutation in sample 15, and 44, V428G mutation in sample 19, 50, and 53, I446M mutation in sample 21, 35, S424R mutation in sample 22, I405L mutation in sample 32, C381F and C452R mutation in sample 38, E407G mutation in sample 39, T451P mutation in sample 43, R453S mutation in sample 45, E432Q mutation in sample 46, Y442N mutation in sample 47, D410Y mutation in sample 48, A388E mutation in sample 49, P386T mutation in sample 54 are the identified destabilizing mutations that may alter the protein structure and functions. The negative sign in ΔΔG indicates that the mutant protein is slightly less stable than the wild-type. While C418S mutation in sample 7, 33, and 41, A430P mutation in sample 17, H398L mutation in sample 26 are the observed stabilizing mutations that stabilize the protein structure and functions.

In fertile Cholistani Buchi sheep, mutations such as R392W mutation in sample 1, and 26, A430P mutation in sample 2, 6, 14, 21, 35, 36, 44, and 53, K422E mutation in sample 3, 9, 16, 28, 32, 34, 43, and 45, C418S mutation in sample 4, 7, 12, 18, 19, 25, 37, 47, 51, and 54, H398L mutation in sample 23, 33, 40, 46, 50, and 52 are the stabilizing mutations that have no effect on protein structure and functions while V397I mutation identified in sample 5, 11, 30, and 42, I446L mutation in sample 10, and 20, D434A mutation in sample 15 and 27, V413L mutation in sample 22, V428G mutation in sample 38, destabilize the protein structure and functions and are the destabilizing mutations. Thus, in fertile Cholistani Buchi sheep, mutations in majority of samples did not alter the protein structure and functions. The positive sign in ΔΔG indicates that the mutant protein is more stable than the wild-type. Impact of GDF9 mutations on the physicochemical parameters of the encoded proteins in Cholistani Buchi sheep

The ProtParam tool was used to investigate the effect of GDF9 mutations on the physicochemical properties of the encoded proteins in Cholistani Buchi sheep. Various properties of all the mutated samples were determined and compared to the wild-type, including Molecular Weight (MW), Theoretical Isoelectric Point (pI), Estimated Half Life, Instability Index (II), Aliphatic Index (AI), and Grand Average of Hydropathicity. In infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep, it was observed that sample 38 with molecular weight 51873.45 have higher molecular weight compared with the wild-type having 51776.36 Da, thus demonstrating notable changes in its biological processes. In addition, significant changes in molecular weight were also observed in other samples, which could bring changes in the structure and functions of proteins. While in fertile samples, highest molecular weight was 51806.38 Da for sample 1 and 26 that brings slight variations in the protein structure and functions compared with the wild-type 51776.36 Da. The calculated theoretical isoelectric point (pI) in the wild type is 8.96 while all other infertile samples also have pI values ranges from 8.96–9.09, indicating alkaline nature of proteins. In fertile samples, pI values from 8.83–9.02, also showed basic nature of proteins. These changes further indicated that mutations may have functional impact by altering the protein charge state, possible interactions, its stability, or even cellular location. The calculated instability index (II) for wild-type was 54.85, indicating the unstable nature of protein. For infertile samples, the instability index ranges from 53.88–55.78, and in fertile samples it varies from 54.70–55.44, indicating unstable protein nature, affecting the protein structures and functions. A higher aliphatic index is typically linked to greater thermostability. The calculated aliphatic index for wild-type was 77.31 and highest aliphatic index in infertile sample was 78.66 while in fertile one it was 78.17, thus indicating more thermostability than others. Grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) indicated negative GRAVY scores in the current study, indicating protein are primarily made up of hydrophilic amino acids and have high affinity for water, thus improves the solubility of protein. The calculated values of physicochemical parameters for both infertile and fertile mutated samples and wild-type are mentioned in Supplementary Table 7 and Table 8.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis and gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis

To identify the particular molecular network of GDF9-associated proteins, the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using STRING database (Fig. 5). The predicted functional partners of GDF9 gene in sheep are BMPR2 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein Receptor Type 2), BMPR1B (Bone Morphogenetic Protein Receptor Type 1B), ACVR1B (Activin A Receptor Type 1B), ZP2 (Zona Pellucida Glycoprotein 2), FSHR (Follicle Stimulating Hormone Receptor), AMH (Anti-Mullerian Hormone), BMP15 (Bone Morphogenetic Protein 15), LHCGR (Luteinizing Hormone/Choriogonadotropin Receptor), TGFBR1 (Transforming Growth Factor Beta Receptor 1), SMAD2 (Mothers Against Decapentaplegic Homolog 2). Furthermore, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of GDF9 and its associated genes revealed their roles in diverse biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF) (Fig. 6). Their biological functions include negative regulation of chondrocyte proliferation, ovarian follicle development, regulation of the reproductive process, development of reproductive structure and system, transmembrane receptor protein serine/threonine kinase signalling pathway and enzyme linked receptor protein signalling pathway. Their cellular components are linked with the activin receptor complexes, egg coat, external encapsulating structure, protein serine/threonine kinase signalling pathway, and transferase complexes. Furthermore, their molecular functions indicated strong enrichment with transforming growth factor beta-activated receptor activity, activin binding receptor activity, protein-hormone receptor activity, SMAD-mediated signaling, growth factor and phosphotransferase activity.

Discussion

In sheep, the GDF9 gene, which belongs to the transforming growth factor superfamily (TGF-β), has been thoroughly investigated. Studies have demonstrated that GDF9 gene is essential for bovine folliculogenesis, suggesting its crucial role in controlling the early development of follicles and oocytes. Extensive research has demonstrated significant genetic variations in ovulation rates among sheep, revealing notable differences between breeds and, in several instances, remarkable diversity within specific breeds (Gootwine, 2020; Xu et al., 2018). Genomic research on Buchi Sheep breed is limited, so we initiated this study to improve our knowledge of the common and distinctive polymorphisms that contribute to breeds desirable features by defining and studying their genomes. Since the genetic mutations of GDF9 in Cholistani Buchi Sheep breed has not been studied in Southern Punjab, Pakistan, so the current study was designed to evaluate the genetic mutations and their possible impacts on Cholistani Buchi sheep using molecular and In Silico analyses.

In the current study, 33 GDF9 mutations were identified in infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep breed These include C2285T, A2360G, G2307A, T2370A, C2470A, T2402A, G2266T, C2431T, A2436T, C2253A, G2406C, A2456G, C2390G, G2465A, A2311T, G2324A, T2291C, T2378C, A2331C, G2321C, A2255G, G2260T, T2472C, A2338G, A2469C, C2475A, G2412C, T2442A, G2346T, C2281A, T2401G, C2481G, and C2274A. A large number of mutations in sheep breeds were previously reported in different studies. A study indicated that c.2039G > A (G1), g.3451T > C (G2), g.3457 A > G (G3), g.3701 A > G (G4), g.3958 A > G (G5), g.3974 G > A (G6), c.1111G > A (G7), and c.1184C > T (G8) are the major reported mutations in different sheep breeds (Aymaz et al., 2024; Khodabakhshzadeh et al., 2016; Kirikci, 2023). Of all these eight mutations, c.2039G > A (G1) is linked with increase ovulation rate and is considered as a candidate gene for the litter size in sheep (Dimitrova et al., 2024). But no such mutation is identified in the current study and all the observed mutations identified in the current study are the novel mutations that might be responsible for infertility in Cholistani Buchi sheep. V397I mutation is the most reported mutation in goats (Chairunissa et al., 2022; Song et al., 2023) but in the current study, V397I mutation might also be linked with reproductive traits in Cholistani Buchi sheep. These results offer crucial information for creating sheep molecular breeding plans and enhancing sheep fertility and productivity. In another study, S427R/c.1279 A > C is responsible for increased ovulation rate sheep (Chen et al., 2023) but in the current study, S424R/c. 1272 G > C is a destabilizing mutation that might be responsible for infertility.

The predicted functional impact of GDF9 mutations in infertile Cholistani Buchi sheep indicated that V397I, T451N, G383V, A438V, V428G, I446M, S424R, I405L, C381F, C452R, E407G, T451P, R453S, E432Q, Y442N, D410Y, A388E, P386T are the destabilizing mutations that are responsible for altering the protein structure and functions. DynaMut2 analyzes protein dynamics and predicts how the missense mutations affects their structural stability. It is helpful for protein function and stability research since it combines normal mode analysis with graph-based signatures to provide precise insights into both stabilizing and destabilizing mutations (Rodrigues et al., 2018). The physicochemical properties determined by using ProtParam indicated that the molecular weight of wild-type GDF9 in sheep is 51776.36d Da, theoretical isoelectric point is 8.96, instability index is 54.85, aliphatic index is 77.31, GRAVY value is -0.403, and the estimated half-life is 30 h. When wild-type was compared with other samples, the maximum molecular weight observed was 51873.45 Da. This slight change in molecular weight might be responsible for the valuable changes in their biological processes. Furthermore, slight changes in molecular weight were also observed in other samples, which could bring changes in the structure and functions of proteins. The maximum theoretical pI is 9.09, indicating basic nature of protein. All other samples also have alkaline nature as pI value is above 8.90. The optimal instability index calculated was 55.78. As the critical instability index is 40, so in the current study greater than 40 value indicated unstable nature of protein. Highest aliphatic index in infertile sample was 78.66, hence linked with greater thermostability compared with other samples. The physicochemical properties of GDF9 mutations in Cholistani Buchi sheep further indicated that the half-life (30 h) remains constant in all sample while other properties such as molecular weight (MW), theoretical pI, instability index (II), aliphatic index (AI), and GRAVY brings slight changes across all the noted GDF9 mutations, thus affecting their structure and functions. The biological information analysis helped to elucidate the structure and functions of protein. In the current study structural variations were brought about by novel mutations that might have affected key functioning areas similar to those reported in previous studies (Bhattacharya et al., 2017; Qiao et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). Thus, the identified mutations affects the proper folding of GDF9 gene in sheep.

The resulting PPI network analysis demonstrated that GDF9 in sheep interacts with a cluster of proteins related to ovarian follicle growth and reproductive signaling. The PPI analysis indicated that GDF9 gene functional partners are BMPR2, BMPR1B, ACVR1B, ZP2, FSHR, AMH, BMP15, LHCGR, TGFBR1 and SMAD2. The strongest and most biologically relevant interaction was observed between GDF9 and BMP15 (0.894) which is crucial for granulosa cell proliferation and normal follicle development. BMP15 and GDF9 are secreted from oocytes and then bind to BMPR1B receptor on granulosa cells. Thus, a synergistic impact of BMP15, GDF9, and BMPR1B on litter size in sheep may exist (Di et al., 2021). GDF9 and BMP15 can also promote the expression of anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), which is essential for ovarian function (Roy et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021). There is growing evidence that the SMAD family of genes is fundamentally involved in determining litter size, and plays a crucial role in reproductive traits in livestock species. GDF9 mutations that change the structure or processing of proteins, changing the dynamics of SMAD2/3 signaling, affecting follicular maturation and cumulus expansion. These mutations subtly change the signaling dynamics across the whole regulatory network rather than just eliminating their functions (Tang et al., 2024). As a result, the entire system operates as an integrated network in which oocytes connect with granulosa cells via BMP15 or GDF9, granulosa cells use inhibin to signal the pituitary, the pituitary uses FSH to control follicular development, and SMAD proteins integrate these signals intracellularly. Different but related phenotypes are produced by mutations at different locations, and some combinations have additive or even multiplicative effects on ovulation rate (Han et al., 2025). Additionally, ovarian steroidogenesis and organism-specific biosystem pathways in sheep are also influenced by the luteinizing hormone/choriogonadotropin receptor (LHCGR) (Abdoli et al., 2018).

The gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of GDF9 and its associated genes in sheep were found to be significantly involved in all major GO categories. In the biological process, these genes were enriched in functions related to ovarian follicular development, gonad development, transmembrane receptor protein serine/threonine kinase signalling pathway, reproductive structure development, and activin receptor signalling pathway. Their cellular component indicated strong association with the activin receptor complexes while their molecular functions indicated their enrichment in transmembrane receptor protein serine/threonine kinase activity, transforming growth factor beta receptor activity, transforming growth factor beta receptor binding, SMAD binding and activin receptor activity. The biological and physiological functions of GDF9 and BMP15, have been found to play crucial roles in female fertility in a number of mammalian species. Besides improving oocyte developmental competence, these factors directly regulate granulosa cell proliferation, differentiation, steroidogenesis, apoptosis, and cumulus expansion through TGF-β mediated activation of serine/threonine kinase receptors and SMAD2/3 signaling (Sanfins et al., 2018). Both GDF9 and BMP15 bind to serine/threonine kinase receptors on granulosa cells, activating intracellular signaling that leads to SMAD2/3 phosphorylation and form complex with SMAD4. These complexes translocate to the nucleus, regulating genes essential for granulosa cell proliferation, differentiation, and follicular development, while their synergistic action enhances follicle and oocyte maturation (Fountas et al., 2024). Since GDF9 and BMP15 function synergistically in oocyte growth, ovulation regulation, and folliculogenesis, codon changes in these genes disrupt their interactions, and ultimately results in deleterious effects on the ovarian response to ovulation induction (Mehdizadeh et al., 2024). Thus, mutations at specific sites variably affect sheep ovulation rate and litter size, demonstrating how genetic variations in signaling pathways influence economically important reproductive traits in livestock.

The allele frequency analysis of GDF9 mutations revealed notable differences between infertile and fertile Cholistani Buchi sheep, highlighting the potential association of specific variants with reproductive performance. Several mutations, including V397I, A2360G, and C2285T, were observed at higher frequencies in infertile individuals compared with fertile counterparts, suggesting their possible role in disrupting protein structure or function. Conversely, some mutations were more prevalent in fertile sheep, consistent with stabilizing effects predicted by DynaMut2 and minimal changes in protein physicochemical properties. The combined population frequencies further emphasize the genetic diversity of the GDF9 gene in this breed, providing a comprehensive view of allele distribution.

Conclusion

This study reveals the genetic variation landscape of GDF9 gene in Cholistani Buchi sheep, highlighting its crucial role in regulating folliculogenesis and ovulation. The current study reported eighteen novel mutations including V397I, T451N, G383V, A438V, V428G, I446M, S424R, I405L, C381F, C452R, E407G, T451P, R453S, E432Q, Y442N, D410Y, A388E, and P386T. These mutations are the destabilizing mutations that disrupt protein structure and function and may contribute to infertility in Cholistani Buchi sheep. Thus, understanding these mutations offers a valuable insights for future breeding programs and may serve as promising molecular genetic markers to support selection for improved growth and litter size traits in sheep breeds.

Limitations

This study has a few limitations. As the data were obtained from a single center, there is also a possibility of population-specific bias. Moreover, in vitro or in vivo models were not prepared so its application might be limited. Their molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways can be explored further. Future research including diverse populations, along with functional and pathway-based analyses, would help to confirm and expand these findings.

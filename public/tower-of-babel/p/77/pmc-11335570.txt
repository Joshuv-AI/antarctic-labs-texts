The use of negative pressure therapy for the treatment of gunshot wounds in a limited resource setting in eastern part of the Democratic Republic of Congo: Case series
Authors: Paul Budema Munguakonkwa, Jean de Dieu Namegabe Tumsifu, Gauthier Bahizire Murhula, Georges Toha Kuhigwa, Fabrice Gulimwentuga Cikomola, Théophile Barwamire Kabesha, Zacharie Tsongo Kibendelwa, Olivier Mukuku, Stanis Wembonyama Okitotsho
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key Clinical MessageThe use of negative pressure therapy in the management of gunshot wound have a favorable outcome, NPT is easy to build, accelerates wound budding, reduces patient's stay in hospital and its low cost. It showed reassuring results in the management of wound with soft tissue lost, comparatively to other dressing.AbstractWounds due to gunshot injuries are common in east of Republic Democratic of Congo and its management should be well known by surgeons and other health practitioners. Reconstruction is possible with simple surgeries using skin grafts and negative pressure therapy (NPT). In this study, we present our results of wounds treatment after gunshot injury with a simple non‐surgical procedure using NPT. We present four patients with soft tissue losses caused by gunshot injury. These patients underwent an average of four to eight sessions of vacuum dressing, the length of hospital stay was decrease and soft tissue losses were reconstructed using at the end skin grafts.

INTRODUCTION

Globally more than 60 ongoing conflicts are described, lower‐ and middle‐income countries (LMICs) are most concern and fourth of them are based in the Eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (D.R.C). 1

The incidence of gunshot injuries is increasing in East of D.R.C; Consequence of that unfortunate trend is that surgeons in trauma centers are more frequently confronted with this type of lesions.

Infection of gunshot wounds is a relevant clinical problem and an unpleasant burden for patients at eastern part of D.R.C. 2

Gunshot injuries represent a challenge for surgeon, these wounds often require debridement in the operating room and usually are associated with wide soft tissue losses and infections. 3 Wound complications can result in prolonged treatment times and increased risk of morbidity and mortality.

However, the management of gunshot wounds requires initial treatment upon receipt of the patient: debridement. If tissue loss, hence the use of vacuum dressing as an alternative to other much more complex techniques.

Negative pressure wound therapy is a non‐invasive therapeutic method that supports the healing of acute and chronic non‐healing wounds: even ulcers, open abdomen, abdominal compartment syndrome, diabetes‐foot syndrome, burns, sternotomy coverage as a preventive measure. 4 , 5

Despite the large body of literature dealing with various indications and aspects of the NPT, there are few series reporting its use in gunshot wounds, even less in the context of limited resources. The present study aims to report a part of our experience, in a low‐income country.

METHOD

This study was done at and authorized by the institutional review board of the Provincial General Reference Hospital of Bukavu. The Provincial General Reference Hospital of Bukavu is a tertiary referral hospital and level 1 trauma center in Bukavu, South Kivu. It offers advanced trauma care thanks to its multidisciplinary team (i.e., at least nine specialty surgeons including two orthopedic trauma surgeons, two urologic surgeons, two general surgeons, one pediatric surgeon, one maxillo facial surgeon, and one neurosurgeon), an equipped and staffed surgical intensive care unit, the implementation of trauma evaluation and management protocols including massive transfusion, on‐call trauma surgeon schedule, and Advanced Trauma Life Support. Of note, our institution is supported by the international committee of the Red Cross (ICRC). 1

PROCEDURE

To achieve the results we proceed as follows:

The vacuum therapy changes were performed every 48 h, using Vacuum Assisted Closure device adapted to our limited resources setting.

The components of the system are: a negative pressure device, a suction drain tube, gauze pads, and a PVC plastic film. The system unit is programmed to deliver controlled negative pressure ranging from 70 to 120 mmHg.

The technique involves seven steps as follows: The wound was meticulously debrided, and necrotic tissue removed.The wound was filled by a gauze compress in fluffy form. The gauze dressing was placed into direct contact with the deepest surface of the wound. Major vessels should not be exposed in order to avoid massive bleeding. It must be emphasized the VAC technique was developed as an adjunct to wound care. It is not meant to, and will never, replace surgical procedures. In most cases, the technique presented is used to prepare the wound bed so that a lesser surgical procedure can be performed with a greater chance for successful wound closure, minimizing time to complete wound closure and thus minimizing cost and hospitalization.

A 41‐years‐old male after an open fracture of fibula with gunshot associated to a major soft tissue losses. (Figure 6).

Case 2

A 20 years‐old male, full abdominal gunshot with ileal lesion at 130 cm from the angle of Treitz, with soft tissues lost in the right iliac fossa and fracture of the right femoral neck.

He underwent damage control laparotomy with ileostomy and hemostasis. External fixation was also done for the fracture. The wounds were managed by serial debridement and five sessions of NPT and skin graft was performed after 10 days without any complication (Figures 8, 9).

DISCUSSION

Gunshot wounds always been a high‐energy trauma, its association with major soft tissue losses is frequent. Soft tissue defects are caused by the effects of trauma and posttraumatic debridement. 3

Surgical method which will be used in closing the defect is decided according to the size of the wound, Some surgical techniques can be used in closing defects, which cannot be closed primarily after gunshot injury: Skin grafts, local myocutaneous and fasciocutaneous flaps, distant flaps, and free flaps. 6

As well as the size and depth of the wound increases, the need for more extensive surgeries arises; and as a result of these major surgeries, donor site morbidity, frequency of complications, and length of hospital stay increases. 3 , 6

NPT systems are a non‐surgical method in wound treatment for the last 20 years. It has been developed for closing large, chronic, and infected wounds, has also started to be used in traumatic wounds. 7

Different positive effect are describe by using NPT method, which is administered after appropriate surgical debridement of gunshot injuries, increases granulation tissue formation and thus, makes it possible to close the wound with less aggressive surgical procedures. 8 , 9 It is also an alternative treatment option in patients not suitable for surgical procedures and those with small defects. 8

Blume et al., 9 in one of the largest randomized controlled trial, found that NPT is as safe as and more efficacious than advanced moist wound therapy in the treatment of Diabetic foot and no significant difference was observed in ulcer‐related complications such as infection, cellulitis, and osteomyelitis.

In 2003, Also, Eginton et al., 10 had also observed that the wound volume and depth decreased significantly in VAC dressings as compared to moist gauze dressings in the treatment of Diabetic foot wound.

The positive effect of NPT on wound healing is due to multiple factors. With NPT, excess wound exudates, which contains factors that inhibit fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and keratinocytes are removed from the site. 6 , 8

Also, the edema surrounding the wound decreases with NPT and with this decrease, the blood vessels around the wound dilate and the blood flow to the wound increases, which helps the granulation tissues.

However; NPT prevent colonization of anaerobic bacteria and decrease bacterial load of the wound. It has been shown that with NPT, the bacterial load reduced from 107 to 103 in 4–5 days. 6 , 7 , 11

In ours hot study, NPT treatment was administered in patients who had defects due to gunshot injury. The defects were reconstructed with skin grafts.

NPT is generally accepted as a reliable method of treatment of gunshot injuries with loss of soft tissues. The most important complication of the treatment is the pain endured while changing the sponges. 12 The pain is intermittent and is particularly due to the negative pressure itself, and the pain can mostly be eliminated by lowering the pressure. The small bleeding due to Excessive granulation tissue formation and pains were most complications in our study.

NPT is not more expensive than wet dressing and it also has advantages other than its positive effect on wound healing such as allowing ambulation of patients during the treatment and requiring only a few dressing changes.

CONCLUSION

NPWT has an effect on accelerating tissue granulation, decreasing wound size, and decreasing bacterial load within wounds. It thus finds its place in the care of gunshot wounds, where cavities and infection are not rare. In a context with limited resources, it ensures rapid healing of wounds and, consequently, a reduction in the costs of care. The following research will be devoted to a randomized comparative study between vacuum dressing and other types of dressing for gunshot wounds in the same setting.

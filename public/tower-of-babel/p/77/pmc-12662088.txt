Characterizations of a novel triple-reassortant H3N3 avian influenza A virus isolated from chickens in China
Authors: Xinran Chu, Xuefeng Yin, Ye Tian, Wenjie Jiang, Zhehong Zhao, Jixiang Wang, Xudong Cao, Jianjun Sang, Quan Xie, Tuofan Li, Hongxia Shao, Aijian Qin, Jianqiang Ye, Zhimin Wan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe emergence of novel H3N8 and H10N3 avian influenza A viruses (IAVs) circulating in chicken flocks in China has raised significant concerns to their spillovers in humans and associated economic losses. In this study, we isolated a novel H3N3 strain from chickens exhibiting respiratory symptoms, designated as A/chicken/Shandong/118/2023(H3N3) (SD118). Genetic analyses revealed that SD118 was a triple-reassortment virus, possessing hemagglutinin (HA) gene originating from H3N8, neuraminidase (NA) gene originating from H10N3, and other six internal genes were from H9N2, each of which is currently circulating in chicken flocks in China. In vitro studies confirmed that SD118 could effectively replicate in mammalian cells (MDCK and A549 cells), and exhibited dual receptor-binding affinity for both avian-like (α-2, 3 sialic acid) and human-like (α-2, 6 sialic acid) receptors. Moreover, SD118 could replicated effectively not only in the lungs and nasal turbinates of the infected mouse with more than 10 % bodyweight loss, but also in the upper respiratory tract and intestines of the infected chickens with efficient transmission ability. Our findings highlight the ongoing evolution of H3 avian IAVs through reassortment with other subtypes in poultry and the enhanced surveillance is critical to monitor the genetic diversity and zoonotic potential of H3 viruses in chickens.Keywords: H3N3, Triple-reassortment, Receptor binding, Replication, Pathogenicity

Introduction

Influenza A virus (IAV), a member of the Orthomyxoviridae family, is a negative-sense, single-stranded RNA virus (Vasin et al., 2014). Based on the antigenic properties of the two surface glycoproteins, hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), IAVs are classified into 18 HA (H1-H18) and 11 NA (N1-N11) subtypes. Among IAVs, H1-H16 and N1-N9 subtypes are naturally hosted by wild aquatic birds (Webster, et al., 1992), while H17N10 and H18N11 were identified in bats (Tong, et al., 2013). Recently, a novel candidate HA subtype (H19) has been identified in wild birds (Fereidouni, et al., 2023; Karakus, et al., 2024). Among all IAV subtypes, H3 subtype IAV exhibits the broadest host range, including wild birds, poultry, swine, canines, equines, felids, seals and humans (Bean, et al., 1992; Karlsson, et al., 2014). Notably, H3N8 IAVs have established stable transmission lineages in wild birds, canines and equines (Daly, et al., 2011; Lewis, et al., 2011), while H3N2 remains a major seasonal influenza virus circulating in humans. These data underscore the significant public health threat posed by H3 subtype IAVs.

Since 2020, a novel avian-origin H3N8 IAV has emerged in chicken flocks in China, causing respiratory symptoms resembling those caused by H9N2 infection (Li, et al., 2021). Phylogenetic analysis revealed that this emerging H3N8 is a triple-reassortant virus, carrying the H3 gene of Eurasian lineage, then N8 gene of North American lineage, and internal genes derived from the G57 genotype of H9N2 (Wan, et al., 2022). Notably, this novel H3N8 virus has caused three cases of human infection in 2022 (n = 2) and 2023 (n = 1), including one fatality (Cheng, et al., 2022; Yang, et al., 2022; Zhuang, et al., 2024), raising public health concerns.

Similarly, H10N3 avian IAV has been increasingly detected in poultry flocks in China in recent years, with three human cases also documented (Liu, et al., 2022; Qi, et al., 2022). Genetic characterization indicated that the H10N3 possesses an HA gene of Eurasian lineage, originating from duck-derived H10N3, while its NA gene, also of Eurasian lineage, is closely related to duck-origin H7N3 (Zhang, et al., 2023). Strikingly, its internal genes were entirely derived from G57 genotype of H9N2 (Liu, et al., 2022), indicating extensive reassortment.

IAVs are known for their frequent co-circulation and reassortment among subtypes, leading to increased genetic diversity and the potential for antigenic shift (Dong, et al., 2011; Su, et al., 2021). Furthermore, multiple reassortment events may lead to the generation the novel IAVs with increased capacity to replicate and transmit in mammals, including humans. In this study, we report the identification and characterization of a novel H3N3 avian IAV, which originated through a triple-reassortment of H3N8, H9N2, and H10N3. This strain of H3N3 virus demonstrated efficient replication in mammalian cells, and dual receptor-binding affinity and pathogenic phenotypes in mice and chickens, underscoring its zoonotic potential and the necessity for enhanced surveillance.

Materials and methods

Viral isolation and identification

Throat swabs were collected from chickens exhibiting respiratory symptoms in Shandong Province, China in 2023. Total RNAs were extracted from these throat swabs, and complementary DNAs (cDNAs) were synthesized by reverse transcription (RT) with Unit 12 primers. The matrix (M) genes were amplified by PCR using the specific primers as described previously (Shao, et al., 2015). PCR-positive samples were inoculated into 10-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) chicken embryos. After 72 hours (h) of incubation, the allantoic fluid was harvested and tested for hemagglutination activity using standard hemagglutination assays.

Genome sequencing

Viral RNAs of HA-positive allantoic fluid samples were extracted, and cDNAs were synthesized as described above. All eight segments were amplified by PCR with the gene segment-specific primers (Shao, et al., 2015), and all PCR products were sequenced by Sanger sequencing.

Phylogenetic analysis

The genomic sequences of additional IAVs were downloaded from the Global Initiative on Sharing Avian Influenza Data (GISAID). Multiple sequence alignments were performed using the MAFFT version 7.530, and FASTA module embedded in BioEdit software. Phylogenetic trees were constructed using the neighbor-joining method with bootstrap analysis (1, 000 replicates) in MEGA 11 software. All phylogenetic trees were visualized with iTOL software.

Titration of 50 % tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50)

90 % confluent Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) and human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells were infected with serial 10-fold dilutions of the H3N3 virus and incubated at 37°C. After 2 h, the virus was removed, and the cells were washed two times with PBS and then maintained inopti-MEM media containing 1 µg/ml TPCK-trypsin, and incubated at 37°Cfor 72 h. The supernatant was tested by hemagglutination assay. The TCID50 value was calculated using the method of Reed and Muench.

Viral growth kinetics

MDCK and A549 cells were infected with H3N3 isolate at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01 and maintained in Opti-MEM media containing 1 μg/mL TPCK-trypsin and incubated at 37°C. Supernatants were collected at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h post-infection (hpi). Then, viral titers were determined by TCID50 in MDCK and A549 cells, respectively. The experiments were performed in triplicate.

Receptor binding preference analysis

The receptor binding specificity of the H3N3 isolate was analyzed as previously described (Wan, et al., 2023), using two different glycopolymers: Neu5Acα2-3Galb1-4GlcNAcb-PAA-biotin and Neu5Acα2-6Galb1-4GlcNAcb-PAA-biotin (Glycitech, USA). Briefly, the two glycopolymers were serially two-fold diluted and coated onto the Perce streptavidin high binding capacity coated 96-well plates. After blocking with 5 % skim milk in PBST, 64 hemagglutination units (HAUs) of the H3N3 isolate were added to the each well. Following three washes with PBST, the plates were incubated with H3-specific monoclonal antibody (MAb) 4C2, which was generated in our lab. After washed three times with PBST, the plates were incubated with a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-mouse IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch, USA) antibody for 2 h at 4°C, and then washed three times with PBST again. 100 μL TMB solution was added to each well and incubated 10 min. The reaction was terminated by adding 2 M H2SO4, and the OD450 values were measured. The experiments were performed in triplicate.

Mouse challenge study

Five-week-old BALB/c were randomly distributed into two groups (11 mice/group). Mice in the experimental group were anesthetized with isoflurane and intranasally inoculated with 10⁵ TCID₅₀ of the H3N3 virus in 40 μL volume. Control group mice received an equal volume of PBS using the same method. At 3- and 5-days post-infection (dpi), 3 mice of each group were sacrificed, and nasal turbinate and lung samples were collected to determine viral titers by qRT-PCR. The remaining four mice in each group were monitored daily for bodyweight loss and clinical signs. The mice with body weight loss of more than 25 % will be anesthetized and euthanized.

Chicken challenge study

Two-week-old SPF chickens were randomly distributed into two groups. Eleven chickens of infected group were infected with SD118 at 105 TCID50 in a volume of 0.1 mL by intranasal inoculation, while the other group were inoculated with same volume of PBS. Five additional uninfected chickens were co-housed with infected group to asses direct-contact group. At 3 and 5 dpi, three infected chickens were sacrificed and lungs were collected to determine viral titers by qRT-PCR. At 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 dpi, oropharyngeal and cloaca swabs (four samples each groups) were collected to determine viral shedding by qRT-PCR.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA of mouse and chicken samples was extracted and reverse transcribed into cDNA. The cDNA was used to amplify using specific qPCR primers using the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix instructions from Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd.

Ethics statement and biosafety

The animal study was performed in accordance with the institutional animal guidelines approved by the Animal Care Committee at Yangzhou University, China. The ethical permission codes (no. 202402035 and 202402137) were provided by the Animal Committee of Yangzhou University.

Statistical analysis

All the results are presented as means ± standard deviations. The statistical analysis in this study was performed with a Student's t-test using GraphPad 10 software. A P value of below 0.05 was considered significant. *, **, and *** indicate P values of less than 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, respectively.

Results

Isolation and identification of a novel reassortant H3N3

The throat swabs from diseased chickens were identified of IAV by PCR. Then the positive samples were subsequently inoculated into 10-day-old SPF embryonated chicken eggs. After incubation, one H3N3 isolate was obtained, and whole genome was sequenced, designated as A/chicken/Shandong/118/2023(H3N3), hereafter referred to as SD118. All full-genome sequences were submitted to the GISAID (accession number is EPI_ISL_20205503).

To investigate the evolutionary origins of SD118, we performed phylogenetic analysis of its HA and NA genes. As shown in Fig. 1A, the HA gene of SD118 clustered within the Eurasian H3 lineage, and was confirmed to have originated from H3N8 avian IAVs circulating among chickens in China since 2020 (Fig. 1A). The NA gene of SD118 clustered with the Eurasian lineage of N3 subtype avian IAVs, and was closely related to H10N3 viruses currently circulating in chicken flocks in China (Liu, et al., 2022). Phylogenetic analyses of the remaining six internal genes of SD118 were also performed. Overall, the six internal genes of SD118 were classified into the linage of H9N2 viruses as described in Fig. 2. Among these six internal genes, two genes (PB2 and M) belonged to the h9.4 (G1-like) lineage of H9N2 viruses, predominantly in poultry across Middle East, Egypt, India Bangladesh. The remaining four genes (PB1, PA, NP and NS) were assigned to the h9.3 (BJ/94-like) lineage of H9N2 viruses, which is primarily prevalent in poultry in China and Vietnam (Fig. 2). These results indicate that SD118 isolate is a novel reassortant virus with HA from H3N8, NA from H10N3, and internal genes from H9N2 viruses, all of which are recently endemic in poultry in China.

Molecular characterization of the novel H3N3 isolate SD118

To assess the potential risk of the novel H3N3 isolate spilling over to mammals, the key amino acids of SD118 related with pathogenicity and transmission were analyzed. The amino acid sequence at the cleavage site of SD118 was PEKQTR/GIF, indicating SD118 belonged to low pathogenic avian influenza strain. The Q226 and G228 in receptor binding site (RBS) of SD118 were identified (Table 1), revealing that SD118 binds to the avian-like receptor (de Vries, et al., 2017; Stevens, et al., 2006). However, the T160S mutation (Table 1) was also found in SD118, which may alter the receptor specificity (Gao, et al., 2009).

Notably, although E627K and D701N in PB2 for pathogenesis in mammals (Steel, et al., 2009; Sun, et al., 2023) and H276Y in NA for reducing susceptibility to oseltamivir were not found, SD118 isolate harbored some other mutations linked to enhanced pathogenesis in mammals or reduced susceptibility to anti-influenza drug, such as I117T in NA, I292V, A588V and T598I in PB2, D622G and P598L in PB1, A37S, V63I, P190S, K356R, N383D in PA, M105V, A184K in NP, N30D, I43M and T215A in M1, S31N in M2, P42S, C138F in NS1 (Suttie, et al., 2019) as described in Table 1. SD118 could effectively replicate in MDCK and A549 cells

To evaluate the replication ability of SD118 in mammalian cells, we examined its growth kinetics in MDCK and A549 cells. The results showed that SD118 could replicate effectively on both cells, with viral titers peaking at 48 h post-inoculation (hpi) at approximately 106 TCID50/mL. Notably, SD118 achieved higher titers in A549 cells compared to MDCK cells from 12 to 48 hpi, especially at12 hpi, the viral titer in A549 cells was significantly higher than that in MDCK cells (P < 0.01) (Fig. 3A), suggesting potential adaptation to human epithelial cells.

SD118 exhibited dual receptor binding specificity

To investigate the receptor binding specificity of SD118, we examined the receptor-binding preference with Neu5Acα−2, 3Galb1-4GlcNAcb and Neu5Acα2-6Galb1-4GlcNAcb. As shown in Fig. 3B, SD118 isolate could bind to both receptor types, with a higher affinity for α−2, 3 sialic acids (SA). These findings confirm the dual receptor-binding capability of SD118, indicating a potential for cross-species transmission.

Pathogenicity of SD118 in mice

To assess the pathogenicity of SD118 in mammals, 5-week-old BALB/c mice were challenged with SD118. The infected mice showed minor bodyweight loss at 2 dpi and further decreased by more than 10 % at 3 dpi, then the mice recovered from day 4 onward, and all mice survived during the observation period (Fig. 4A). The viral titers of nasal turbinate and lung samples at 3 and 6 dpi were titrated by qRT-PCR. The results showed that SD118 could be detected on 3 and 6 dpi. The viral titers in nasal turbinates were similar with those in lungs, about 102 copies/mL on 3 dpi and 101 TCID50/mL on 6 dpi (Fig. 4B and C), respectively. The histopathology analysis of lung tissues revealed significant interstitial pneumonia at 3 dpi, and the worsened by 6 dpi (Fig. 4D and E). These findings indicate that SD118 can replicate in mice without prior adaptation and induce moderate pulmonary pathology.

Pathogenicity of SD118 in chickens

To evaluate the pathogenicity of SD118 in poultry, 14-day old SPF chickens were intranasally infected with SD118. Uninfected chickens were introduced as the direct-contact group. No clinical signs or mortality were observed in the infected chickens during 14-day observation period. No virus was detected in the lungs from infected chickens at either 3 or 5 dpi (data not shown). The oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs were collected from both infected and direct-contact chickens at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 dpi and viral titers were determined by qRT-PCR. In the infected group, the virus was detected in oropharyngeal swabs from 2 to 10 dpi, with peak shedding levels occurring at 2 and 4 dpi (exceeding 103 copies/mL) (Fig. 5B). Low viral titers were observed in cloacal swabs from 2 to 8 dpi. The virus in direct-contact group also was detected in oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs, but the viral titers was significantly lower than those in the infected group (Fig. 5). These findings suggest that the isolate SD118 confined to the upper respiratory tract of chickens, and is low pathogenicity but transmissible in chickens and could spread via direct contact transmission.

Discussion

Wild aquatic birds are widely recognized as the primary nature reservoir of IAVs, which frequently spill over into domestic poultry and mammals, including humans (Webster, et al., 1992). Among poultry, chickens are considered key hosts for multiple subtypes of IAV, such as H3N8, H5Ny, H7N9, H9N2 and H10Ny. All these IAVs have been associated with human infections, with particularly high mortality being observed for the H5Ny and H7N9 (Chen, et al., 2014; Gao, et al., 2013; Guan, et al., 1999; Qi, et al., 2022). These observations highlight the pivotal role of chickens in the interspecies transmission of avian IAVs and underscore the urgent need for sustained surveillance of IAVs in poultry populations.

In the present study, we identified and characterized a novel triple-reassortant H3N3 isolate (SD118) from chickens exhibiting respiratory symptom. Genetic analysis revealed that HA and NA genes of SD118 were derived from H3N8 and H10N3 viruses, respectively, both of which are known to circulate in chicken flocks and have caused human infections in China (Liuet al., 2022; Qi et al., 2022; Wan, et al., 2022; Yang, et al., 2022). Intriguingly, all internal genes of SD118 were derived from the G57 genotype of H9N2 viruses, which are known donors of internal genes to other zoonotic avian IAVs, such as H3N8, H5Ny, H7N9 and H10Ny (Gao, et al., 2013; Qi, et al., 2022; Yang, et al., 2022). Several recent studies have reported the emergence of genetically similar H3N3 reassortants in chickens (He, et al., 2024; Li, et al., 2024; Mao, et al., 2024; Zhang, et al., 2025), suggesting that H3N3 viruses may already be widespread in chicken flocks.

The pathogenicity of influenza viruses is influenced in part by the amino acid sequence at HA cleavage site. The cleavage site motif of HA in SD118 does not contain consecutive basic amino acids, indicating that it is a low pathogenic IAV, which is supported by chicken challenge study. Chickens inoculated with 10⁵ TCID₅₀ of SD118 exhibited no clinical signs or mortality over a 14-day observation period, and virus replication was not detected in lungs. However, virus shedding via the oropharyngeal and cloacal routes was observed in both directly infected and contact-exposed birds, indicating efficient transmission among chickens despite low pathogenicity of SD118. This combination of low pathogenicity and high transmissibility enables silent circulation among poultry industry, and amplifies the risk of reassortment with co-circulating avian or mammalian IAV strains, potentially generating novel IAVs that post a threat to the poultry industry and even public health.

Host receptor binding specificity is a major determinant of host range and interspecies transmission. Modification of receptor-binding property is often a critical step in cross-species viral transmission. Avian influenza viruses preferentially bind to α−2, 3 SA whereas human-adapted IAVs tend to favor α−2, 6 SA (Thompson and Paulson, 2021). Sequence analysis showed that HA of SD118 retained Q226 and G228, indicative of a preference for avian-type receptors. However, T160S substitution known to influence receptor binding was also present. Consistent with this, receptor binding assay demonstrated that SD118 is capable of binding both α−2, 3 SA and α−2, 6 SA receptors which is consistent with the dual receptors binding ability of H3N8 (Wan, et al., 2022; Yan, et al., 2025; Yang, et al., 2022). This dual binding affinity may facilitate cross-species transmission and raises public health concerns.

Furthermore, the isolate SD118 isolate replicated effectively in mammal cells (Fig. 3), and caused approximately more than 10 % weightloss in mice without prior adaptation, indicating partial mammalian adaptation (Fig. 4). Polymerase activity in mammals is one of key factors for the adaptation of IAVs to mammals (Stevaert and Naesens, 2016).The mutation E627K in PB2, a molecular marker that has been proven to significantly increase the activities of avian IAVs in mammalian cells and lead to pathogenicity in mammals (Herfst, et al., 2012; Sediri, et al., 2016), was not found in SD118. However, some other mutations associated with increased the polymerase activity of avian IAV in mammals and the virulence in mice were detected. These included I229V, A588V and T598T in PB2, D622G and P598L in PB1, A37S, V63I, P190S, K356R, N383D in PA (Suttie, et al., 2019). The exact contribution of these mutations to SD118’s pathogenicity and replication efficiency in mammals warrants further investigation.

In conclusion, we report the isolation and characterization of a novel H3N3 reassortant virus SD118 from chickens, composed of gene segments from H3N8, H10N3, and G57 genotype H9N2 viruses. This H3N3 strain SD118 exhibits dual receptor-binding capability, replicates effectively in mammalian cells, and induces bodyweight loss in mice, highlighting its zoonotic potential. From One-Health perspective, we need to conduct low-pathogenic avian IAV monitoring on healthy poultry to preempt potential threats to public health.

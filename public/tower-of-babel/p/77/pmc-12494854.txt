Toward blockchain based electronic health record management with fine grained attribute based encryption and decentralized storage mechanisms
Authors: Asad Ullah, Zia Ullah, Sanam Shahla Rizvi, Lubna Gul, Se Jin Kwon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCurrent Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems exhibit several critical drawbacks, including significant vulnerabilities in security, privacy, and scalability. Traditional centralized databases are highly susceptible to cyber attacks, data breaches, and unauthorized access, posing severe risks to the confidentiality and integrity of sensitive patient information. In addition, these systems often face interoperability issues, which hinder seamless information sharing and access among healthcare providers. The absence of a unified and secure framework exacerbates these problems, leading to operational inefficiencies and increased administrative costs in the management of health records. This study introduces an innovative framework designed to overcome these challenges by integrating blockchain technology with the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS). The proposed EHRChain solution leverages attribute-based encryption to provide fine-grained access control, ensuring that only authorized users can retrieve and decrypt medical records. The IPFS framework eliminates single points of failure by storing encrypted data in a decentralized fashion, thus increasing the resilience of the system to cyber attacks. Incorporating blockchain technology ensures the immutability and traceability of medical data, significantly reducing the risks of data tampering. Performance evaluations using real data set simulation demonstrate the efficiency and feasibility of our framework, highlighting its potential to revolutionize the handling of electronic medical records. This study not only underscores the advantages of integrating blockchain with EHR systems but also presents a scalable and secure solution tailored to meet the demands of modern healthcare infrastructures. Introduction

With the emergence of Internet of Medical Things (IoMT)1, the volume of medical data generated daily has increased dramatically. As the healthcare sector transitions from traditional paper-based charts to Electronic Health Record (EHR), various solutions have been implemented to manage these records efficiently, aiming to enhance patient safety by preventing errors and increasing information accessibility. EHR2, not only serve as legal evidence and tools for predicting and preventing infectious diseases but also enable physicians to reflect on and share treatment experiences with patients in real-time. By 20253, it is projected that electronic storage will encompass at least 15% of patient medical records, marking a 110% increase from 2018 levels.

Typically, medical institutions rely on third-party servers, like cloud servers, to manage encrypted data, which helps reduce computational costs and save space while facilitating efficient data retrieval4. However, the sharing of electronic medical data among medical facilities, though beneficial for providing immediate feedback on patient responses to treatment, raises serious privacy concerns. Once shared, this data is susceptible to illegal misuse, which could compromise patient privacy. Ensuring proper control over access to patient medical records is of paramount importance, with attribute-based encryption (ABE) emerging as a key technique for enforcing access control5. According to reports from the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)6 and the Journal of Healthcare Security7, approximately 81% of healthcare records of Americans have been compromised between 2009 and 2023. In 2023 alone, data breach costs for healthcare organizations reached a record $7, 13 million, a 10% increase over the previous year. These security and privacy breaches present substantial barriers to the effective management and sharing of health records. Thus, there is an urgent need for innovative solutions that ensure the secure and controlled sharing of sensitive medical information among different participants without risking data disclosure or manipulation. An efficient data access mechanism is essential to ensure data security and restrict access to authorized users only. Patients should have the autonomy to decide who can access their medical information8.

In recent years, a variety of healthcare applications have been developed that leverage blockchain technology. Azaria et al. introduced MedRec, the first functional shared Electronic Health Record (EHR) system that utilizes blockchain, although it raises concerns related to confidentiality and privacy9. FHIRChain, a system similar to MedRec, uses cryptographic techniques to encrypt and securely share data among clinicians10. Another framework proposed by Dubovitskaya et al. shares similarities with MedRec, allowing patients to share their health records but lacking a mechanism for consent revocation management11. The Centralized Electronic Health Record systems exhibit significant architectural limitations, as demonstrated in Fig. 1. The dependence on opaque third-party storage solutions creates inherent vulnerabilities in data governance, with users lacking visibility into data location and access patterns. With the advent of blockchain technology15 presents a transformative approach to tackling challenges in healthcare by enabling the secure, tamper-resistant, and decentralized storage of medical records. To mitigate these limitations, we developed a permissionless blockchain-based decentralized system that demonstrates superior performance in terms of response time, processing capacity, and success rate compared to traditional solutions16. Additionally, adopting a blockchain-based model can lead to reduced expenditures related to data administration and inter-institutional data exchange. This blockchain framework is particularly advantageous for enterprise-level applications, including healthcare, as it aligns with the current practices of hospitals and governmental regulatory authorities responsible for generating, overseeing, and regulating healthcare-related data. In contrast to conventional systems that centralize data, this design improves the system’s resilience against security breaches, including Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks and data integrity threats.

Motivation

The purpose of this article is to emphasize the significant gaps and weaknesses present in modern EHR systems18–20. Despite the ongoing adoption of EHR with the goal of optimizing healthcare delivery, existing centralized architectures expose healthcare data to substantial security and privacy risks. According to a recent research21, over 70%of healthcare organizations have suffered a data breach at one point in time, resulting in the exposure of millions of patient data. Additionally, HIPAA violations have also seen a rise, and data breaches in the healthcare sector accounts for 45% of all breaches in 2024 to date. Sadly, these alarming statistics illustrate the failings of centralized EHR systems that leave sensitive patient information exposed. Moreover, the rise of cybercriminal attacks in healthcare environments highlights the necessity for more secure and resilient solutions. Traditional security solutions are becoming insufficient to address the sophisticated threats that now target the healthcare industry22.

This paper demonstrates how a hybrid solution has been incorporated into medical record management that is securing medical records while also giving patients the opportunity to manage their own data. With the use of blockchain, The integration of Distributed File System (IPFS) and advanced data restriction mechanisms aims to address the inherent weaknesses of current systems, ensuring the privacy and integrity of sensitive medical data, thereby achieving the state-of-the-art in healthcare information management.

Blockchain technology overview

Blockchain technology17 emerges as a cutting-edge solution facilitating decentralized applications without reliance on a central authority, all while upholding crucial security attributes like data integrity, authenticity, non-repudiation, and accountability.

At its core, the blockchain data structure consists of interconnected blocks, each linked to its predecessor via cryptographic hash functions. The content of each block contributes to the generation of a hash value, which is then included as a reference in the subsequent block. The Bitcoin white paper was published by Satoshi Nakamoto in 200815, which marked a major technical advancement. Nakamoto envisioned a secure and safe money system using distributed ledger technology called the blockchain. Blockchain technology revolutionized the financial industry.

Role of bilinear mapping in attribute-based encryption

In the proposed scheme, bilinear mapping is crucial for implementing attribute-based encryption (ABE), chosen for its unique properties that enhance secure and efficient access control for electronic medical records (EMRs). Bilinear mappings facilitate the construction of ABE schemes, allowing for fine-grained access control. This means access to encrypted data can be regulated based on the user’s attributes rather than their identity, enabling a more nuanced and secure access control mechanism. Additionally, bilinear mappings support the encryption and decryption processes for complex access policies efficiently, which is critical for medical records where different users (e.g., doctors, nurses, administrators) require varying levels of access based on their roles.

Our proposed EHRChain model

In our proposed EHRChain model, four primary entities collaborate to ensure secure and efficient electronic health record management: the blockchain, the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS), the medical system (comprising doctors and smart healthcare devices), and the data user system. These components collectively represent the main entities and operational processes that underpin our framework (see Fig. 3).

Our proposed system model securely manages Electronic Health Record (EHR) using Ethereum blockchain and IPFS. Medical records are encrypted and stored on IPFS, with the hash address recorded on the blockchain. Authorized users can retrieve and decrypt the records, ensuring data integrity and confidentiality through cryptographic techniques.

Our proposed EHRChain framework ensures secure data handling and scalable storage by integrating two core technologies: Attribute-Based Encryption (ABE) and the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS). ABE provides a fine-grained cryptographic access control mechanism that allows data owners to define access policies based on descriptive user attributes (e.g., role, department, or clearance level). Only users whose attributes satisfy the specified access policy can decrypt the associated data. This enables decentralized and flexible access control, aligning with the complex authorization requirements inherent in healthcare data sharing. For off-chain storage, EHRChain leverages IPFS, a peer-to-peer distributed file system that retrieves content using unique cryptographic hashes instead of traditional location-based addresses. Encrypted patient records are stored on IPFS, while their corresponding hash references are anchored on the blockchain. This design preserves data immutability and integrity, reduces on-chain storage overhead, and ensures efficient data retrieval. Together, ABE and IPFS form the foundational pillars of the EHRChain framework, facilitating privacy-preserving access control and scalable, decentralized storage for secure electronic health record management.

Medical system

The medical system comprises physicians and smart devices of the system. Doctors are responsible for encrypting and saving medical records. They utilize encryption and indexing algorithms to secure medical records and generate indexes for keywords. The encrypted records are then stored in IPFS, and their hash values, along with IPFS hash addresses, are recorded on the blockchain. System smart devices handle the registration of hospital personnel, assigning secret private keys associated with their attributes.

Data user (DU)

Data users (DU) can be doctors, nurses, researchers, patients, etc. Each DU is assigned a key pair associated with their attributes by the system. To access medical records, a DU must request access from the hospital. After confirming their attributes meet system access policies, the DU receives a token to search for medical records.

Ethereum blockchain network

In our scheme, medical data is stored and searched using the Ethereum blockchain platform. Since blockchains are immutable, they are a reliable source for verifying data authenticity and integrity, due to their inability to be arbitrarily modified.

InterPlanetary file system (IPFS)

IPFS enables permanent storage and sharing of various types of files. A unique hash value is assigned to each file based on its content. Furthermore, IPFS uses deduplication to effectively avoid redundant data storage. IPFS is used for medical records storage in our scheme.

Proposed EHRChain system model: a use case scenario

In our proposed framework (see Fig. 3), a key feature of our proposed EHRChain Model is the incorporation of a fine-grained access control scheme, which ensures that only authorized users can access specific medical records. This access control mechanism is tightly integrated with advanced data encryption techniques, safeguarding sensitive patient information from unauthorized access. We utilize robust key management protocols to securely distribute and manage encryption keys, adding an additional layer of security. Furthermore, EHRChain Model employs a hybrid storage solution where actual medical data is stored in a blockchain-friendly off-chain tool like IPFS (InterPlanetary File System). The hash generated from storing data on IPFS is then placed on the blockchain. This approach combines the immutable and transparent properties of blockchain with the scalable and efficient storage capabilities of IPFS, ensuring both security and efficiency in managing large volumes of medical data.

The EHRChain framework is architected to support interoperability with widely recognized Electronic Health Record (EHR) standards, specifically Health Level Seven (HL7) and Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR). This interoperability is achieved through standardized API interfaces and a data translation layer capable of mapping blockchain-anchored metadata and IPFS-stored encrypted patient records into FHIR-compliant JSON resources message formats.

During the retrieval process, the framework queries the blockchain to obtain the corresponding IPFS content identifier, fetches the encrypted data, and converts them into a FHIR Patient or Observation resource for seamless integration with existing hospital information systems. In contrast, clinical data originating in HL7 format are processed by the ingestion module, which parses HL7 segments and maps them to the standardized schema of EHRChain prior to encryption and offline storage. By incorporating these mechanisms, EHRChain enables healthcare organizations to integrate the proposed blockchain-based system without disrupting existing EHR infrastructures, while maintaining data portability, regulatory compliance, and alignment with established interoperability frameworks.

The hybrid consensus protocol, combined with off-chain IPFS storage, delivers a balanced performance profile that addresses both the scalability limitations of fully on-chain systems and the latency constraints of cloud-based hybrids. Handling emergency access and revocation in attribute-based encryption

In healthcare systems, access control mechanisms must strike a balance between preserving patient privacy and ensuring rapid data availability, particularly during mission-critical situations such as medical emergencies, natural disasters, or rapid administrative changes in user roles. Traditional Attribute-Based Encryption (ABE) offers a robust fine-grained access control paradigm, yet it exhibits inherent limitations in handling exceptional access requirements and dynamically revoking privileges. The proposed EHRChain framework addresses these challenges by incorporating advanced extensions to the baseline ABE model, specifically to support:

Emergency access provisioning – granting controlled, short-term access to patient data under verified urgent conditions.

Emergency access provisioning

Under conventional ABE, decryption is rigidly policy-bound: if a user attribute set does not match the embedded access policy, access is unequivocally denied. While this ensures strict adherence to predefined policies, it can hinder timely interventions in acute medical emergencies, where treating physicians or authorized responders may require immediate access to life-critical patient data.

Our proposed EHRChain introduces a context-aware override mechanism facilitated through a dedicated EmergencyAccess attribute. This attribute is issued by a designated authority, such as a hospital administrator, security officer, or a smart contract-governed trust entity, following the verification of emergency conditions. The mechanism is designed with the following safeguards:

Time-bound validity: Emergency access credentials are embedded with cryptographically enforced expiration timestamps, automatically revoking privileges after the predefined duration (e.g., 30 minutes to 2 hours), thereby preventing prolonged or unmonitored access.

This design ensures that emergency access is operationally efficient while preserving the overarching privacy and compliance guarantees of the system.

Revocation of access rights

Access revocation presents a well-documented challenge in traditional ABE systems, as the attribute–ciphertext relationship is static and re-encryption typically requires system-wide re-keying. In healthcare settings, where staff assignments, operational roles, and trust statuses can change frequently, this limitation can result in security risks if not addressed effectively.

To overcome this, EHRChain incorporates a dual-layered revocation strategy:

Expiration-based key validity: All attribute keys are issued with cryptographically embedded expiration timestamps. Users must periodically request renewed keys, which are only issued if their current role and authorization remain valid, thereby automatically removing access for inactive or reassigned personnel.

Expiration-based key validity: All attribute keys are issued with cryptographically embedded expiration timestamps. Users must periodically request renewed keys, which are only issued if their current role and authorization remain valid, thereby automatically removing access for inactive or reassigned personnel.

Proxy-assisted re-encryption: Upon the occurrence of a revocation event, such as role reassignment, employment termination, or detected key compromise, a trusted proxy entity (e.g., a hospital blockchain node) re-encrypts affected ciphertexts using updated access policies. This ensures that previously valid credentials cannot be used to decrypt data post-revocation, without necessitating full re-keying of the entire user base.

Deployment feasibility and compliance considerations

The practical realization of the proposed EHRChain framework is grounded in healthcare-specific regulatory, operational, and compliance imperatives, ensuring its suitability for deployment in real-world clinical environments. The framework adopts a permissioned blockchain architecture in conjunction with a consortium-governed IPFS network, enabling authenticated participation within a secure and controlled trust domain. This design not only mitigates the risk of unauthorized node inclusion but also inherently satisfies the stringent privacy, security, and accountability requirements associated with the management of sensitive electronic health information. From a regulatory perspective, EHRChain is architected to align with internationally recognized compliance regimes, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) in the United States, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union, and established interoperability specifications such as HL7 and Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR). Conformance with these standards is achieved through the integration of Attribute-Based Encryption (ABE) for fine-grained, policy-driven access control; immutable blockchain-anchored transaction logs for verifiable auditability; and the off-chain storage of encrypted EHR data within IPFS to prevent unauthorized data disclosure. Collectively, these mechanisms safeguard the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of medical records while supporting comprehensive compliance auditing.

Operational viability is reinforced through the modular and standards-compliant architecture of the framework, which facilitates seamless integration with existing Hospital Information Systems (HIS) and Regional Health Information Exchanges (HIE) without necessitating disruptive infrastructure replacement. This integration model reduces deployment complexity and cost while preserving continuity of care and existing clinical workflows. Furthermore, as demonstrated in “Scalability and latency analysis of the EHRChain framework”, empirical performance evaluations confirm that EHRChain sustains high throughput and low latency transaction processing under varying concurrent user loads, validating its scalability and robustness for large-scale healthcare ecosystems.

In addition, governance policies embedded within the permissioned network enforce role-based participation, transaction validation rules, and jurisdiction-specific compliance constraints. These measures ensure that deployment across multiple healthcare organizations can be tailored to local regulatory requirements while maintaining interoperability across the broader ecosystem. As such, EHRChain not only offers a technically robust solution but also demonstrates regulatory and operational readiness for secure, scalable, and privacy-preserving electronic health record management in diverse healthcare settings.

Conclusion and future work

This research presents a comprehensive evaluation of a novel framework aimed at enhancing the security and efficiency of blockchain-based medical record management systems. Utilizing Ethereum smart contracts and detailed transaction payload analysis, the study explores various aspects of performance, including execution time, latency, and throughput. Our findings indicate that the proposed framework offers substantial improvements over existing methods. Through rigorous testing using tools like Remix, Metamask, and the Rinkeby test network, we have shown that the framework can handle complex transactions with improved speed and reduced delays. The detailed analysis of transaction payloads and their encoding demonstrates the robustness and scalability of our approach, ensuring that the system can support a high volume of transactions while maintaining security and integrity. Furthermore, the study provides insights into the costs associated with Ethereum transactions, offering a clear understanding of the economic implications of deploying such a framework in real-world scenarios. The research introduces a robust framework to enhance the security and efficiency of blockchain-based medical record management systems, showcasing significant performance improvements in execution time, latency, and throughput. However, the conclusion lacks explicit mention of the study’s limitations. Future work will address this by focusing on implementing attribute revocation functions for better access control, integrating smart contracts to enhance data functionality, exploring advanced cryptographic techniques for improved security, investigating interoperability with other blockchain platforms, optimizing gas consumption for cost-effectiveness, developing dynamic access control algorithms, and conducting real-world implementation and testing in healthcare environments to validate the practical utility of the framework and identify potential challenges.

Future studies could also delve into optimizing the gas consumption and transaction fees associated with Ethereum, making the framework more cost-effective for large-scale deployments. Another potential direction is the development of more sophisticated algorithms for dynamic access control, ensuring that only authorized users can access specific medical records based on evolving policies and user roles. Finally, real-world implementation and testing in healthcare environments will be crucial to validate the framework’s practical utility and identify any additional challenges that may arise in a production setting. These future efforts will help in refining the framework, making it a viable solution for secure and efficient medical record management on the blockchain.

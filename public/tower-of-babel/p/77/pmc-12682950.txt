Reply: Turn down for WHO? Understanding adequate patient selection for thoracic duct decompression in Fontan-associated protein-losing enteropathy (PLE)
Authors: Sebastian Quinones-Carrasquillo, Deepti Bhat, Mohamad Alaeddine
Published: 2025

Reply to the Editor:

We appreciate Shahid and colleagues' comments1 on our recent publication. The editorial correctly notes that our series is small; however, several points merit clarification. Fontan-associated protein-losing enteropathy (PLE) is rare (3%-18% incidence, mean onset ∼5 years).2,3 Previous reports of thoracic duct decompression (TDD) are mostly isolated cases; our 5-patient series is among the largest since TDD's description in 2013.4

Careful patient selection is the hallmark of success for TDD. We agree and do not claim that the results of the study are generalizable to all patients with chronic PLE and failing Fontan circulation. However, we do not agree with the statement of “selection bias.” All specialized procedures require a specific selection criterion. Our manuscript provides encouraging results in patients who met these criteria and therefore these results can be used to decide TDD approach in these patients.

The pathophysiology of PLE is multifactorial, and treatment cannot be generalized across this heterogeneous group. In our observation, some patients with preserved function and acceptable ventricular compliance still developed PLE. Alsaied and colleagues2 showed that PLE can occur even in patients with normal central venous “Fontan” pressures. Committing these patients to transplantation with lifelong immunosuppression, limited organ availability, and graft dysfunction motivated us to offer the TDD procedure.

We do not believe TDD should be offered to all patients with Fontan-associated PLE. We offer TDD only when systemic ventricular end-diastolic pressure is ≤13 mm Hg, a threshold based on our lymphatic intervention group's consensus, multidisciplinary discussions, and previous studies.5 In contrast, patients with PLE and severe systolic dysfunction are referred for transplant evaluation.6

Our manuscript details the meticulous preoperative planning using a multidisciplinary approach, multimodality imaging, and clinical decision-making that went into these patient's management. Shahid and colleagues are highly encouraged to look at our presurgical planning section as well as the tables. All patients in our series had a confirmed diagnosis of PLE, underwent preprocedural dynamic magnetic resonance lymphangiography, and a received a full diagnostic cardiac catheterization.

Currently, transcatheter TDD is limited to the hands of a few interventional cardiologists with expertise.6 However, when reviewing transcatheter TDD at our center, we identified important limitations and reservations, including risks of bleeding, fistula formation, and Fontan circuit occlusion. Patients requiring subsequent transplantation may also face a greater risk in the presence of extracardiac covered stents. For these reasons, we favored the surgical approach, which we found to be more controlled, reproducible, and associated with fewer complications. All the technical aspects are detailed and have been previously described. Any congenital-trained cardiac surgeon should be able to reproduce them. Only one novel technique was implemented in patient number 5. It was described in the surgical detail section and was illustrated.7

In our initial outcomes, 5 patients were transplant-free and off PLE medications at 6 to 12 months. Ongoing follow-up will clarify durability; to date, 1 patient required successful transplantation for worsening heart failure without PLE recurrence.

The letter recommends inclusion of quality-of-life assessments. We agree validated instruments would be valuable, but none have been developed for the specific concerns of Fontan-associated PLE. In a population in which PLE leads to cachexia, edema, and recurrent hospitalizations, our patients reported resolution of edema, freedom from infusions, and return to school. These subjective gains are central to patient well-being.

Our series should be viewed as an initial step, demonstrating the feasibility of surgical TDD for selected patients with Fontan circulation who have PLE. More experience and long-term follow-up are needed to define the role of TDD and refine the selection criteria. We encourage collaboration with other centers to identify potential candidates to expand knowledge on this promising procedure.

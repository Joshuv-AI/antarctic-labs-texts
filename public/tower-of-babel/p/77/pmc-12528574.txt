Evolution of Research Published in Sports and Exercise Medicine Journals: A Quantitative Analysis of Global Trends
Authors: Clara C Zwack, Milad Haghani, Amanda C Benson
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSports and exercise medicine is important in the prevention, diagnosis, and management of a variety of musculoskeletal presentations and injuries as well as aiding athletes’ recovery and enhancing public health by promoting physical activity for all. It has evolved from a subspecialty into a distinct interdisciplinary domain. Using scientometric analysis, this study examines sports and exercise medicine’s scope, history, current landscape and future directions. Utilising data from the Web of Science, covering 150,380 publications up to November 2022, trends from the early 1970s to 2022 are analysed.Main FindingsThere was gradual growth in the field between 1980 and 2000 and a significant surge post-2000. The study reveals global distribution, with the United States contributing over half of the field’s output. However, questions arise about international collaboration and resource allocation. Citespace analysis visualises 26 research streams in sports and exercise medicine, with many emerging around 2000, showcasing adaptability to trends. Document co-citation and citation burst analyses highlight pivotal moments in sports medicine’s intellectual history and foundational knowledge. Semantic analysis categorises sports medicine into four primary divisions: Exercise, Lower limb injuries, Physical activity, and Biomechanics in sport, emphasising its multidisciplinary nature.ConclusionsThis scientometric analysis highlights sports medicine’s evolution into a diverse, interdisciplinary field, with significant growth since 2000. By identifying and mapping trends, global contributions, and key research themes (Exercise variables, Lower limb injuries, Sports dynamics, and Biomechanics) this study provides valuable insights for guiding collaboration, research, and resource allocation to advance athletes’ well-being and health at a population level.Key Points Sports and exercise medicine research has seen steady growth since the 1970s, with peaks in publication numbers, notably in 2020, followed by a decline attributed to changes in publication indexing practices.Analysis reveals a diverse array of 26 distinct research streams, evolving from a singular focus on anaerobic capacity to encompass modern topics like concussion management and sports psychology. Background

Sport is deeply ingrained in the national identity of many countries. When a prominent sportsperson sustains an injury, their recovery is often seen as a matter of national interest. However, sports-related problems extend beyond elite athletes to include recreational athletes, middle-aged individuals striving for fitness, and older adults seeking to maintain or enhance their physical activity levels [1]. The field of sports and exercise medicine, which has evolved into a standalone interdisciplinary domain rather than a subspecialty [2], bridges the gap between science and practice in promoting exercise, treating injuries, and enhancing health for all members of the community [3, 4].

Despite its growth and recognition, sports and exercise medicine lacks a universally accepted definition, reflecting its complex and multidisciplinary nature. Broadly, the field encompasses four key elements: (i) managing sports injuries and medical conditions in active individuals; (ii) understanding the pathophysiology and biomechanics of human performance; (iii) leveraging exercise for disease management, treatment, and prevention; and (iv) Promoting health at the population level [5]. The absence of a universal definition stresses the need for a comprehensive analysis to better define and understand this field’s scope and structure.

Existing bibliometric studies have primarily focused on specific aspects, such as top-cited documents [6], individual sports [7], injuries [8], or publication sources [9]. However, these fragmented approaches provide limited insight into the broader evolution of sports medicine as a discipline. This study addresses this gap by employing scientometric analysis to estimate the field’s size and explore its history, current trends, and future directions. Through analysis of research published in sports and exercise medicine journals, this paper aims to map the structure of sports and exercise medicine, identify prominent research streams and influential works, and provide actionable insights for practitioners, researchers, and policymakers to guide research priorities and funding strategies.

Main Text

Methods

Scientometrics was used to quantitatively measure the overall trends of the field of sports and exercise medicine, with no limit on the size of the scholarly literature output. Scientometrics, as a methodology, evaluates the structure, growth, and impact of research fields by analyzing patterns in scholarly literature [10]. It complements traditional systematic reviews by providing quantitative insights into research dynamics and emerging trends, making it particularly valuable for mapping interdisciplinary domains like sports medicine. Whilst systematic reviews are the gold standard within the medical field, scientometrics adds an extra dimension to the review process and provides measurements of research metrics and trending topics. Although this study is not a systematic review, it incorporates key principles of rigor and transparency from frameworks like PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) [11] and CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) [12]. These frameworks emphasize comprehensive searches, structured methodologies, and replicability, which align with the approach taken in this scientometric review.

Dataset

To retrieve the data for this study, the Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection was accessed and searched in November 2022. WoS was selected for its comprehensive indexing of high impact sports and exercise medicine-specific journals, ensuring a focused and high-quality dataset tailored to this field. In contrast, databases like Medline and PubMed index a broader range of biomedical literature, which may dilute specificity for a domain-specific analysis like this one. A search query was formulated using the names of the sources categorised under “Sport sciences” in WoS. An expert in the field selected 36 sources to be included in the analysis as they were exclusive to the Sport Sciences category (and not indexed under other categories such as Physiology, Rehabilitation, Psychology and Nutrition & Dietetics, for example). The selected sources almost exclusively publish literature relevant to sport medicine, in the English language only. Title, abstract and author keyword research in sports was not used in the WoS query due to the extensive number of terms that are utilised within sports medicine, and the fact that these terms can be used across other fields such as medicine, nursing and allied health.

Analyses

General findings: The size of the literature was estimated using the metadata directly extracted from WoS. Highly cited documents, countries and institutions with the highest scholarly output in the field were also identified.

Temporal analysis: Using Citespace 5.7.R1 [13], the research streams (clusters of references) within sports medicine are visualised in a static map to identify networks, nodes and connections. From WoS output, it was determined that documents indexed in the field of sports medicine started around 1970; hence, the time period for this analysis was from 1970 to 2021, set at one-year intervals, and limited to reference lists published no more than 50 years ago. A dynamic visualisation of the emergence of each research stream since 1990 was acquired. Parts of the network that are most active during each year are highlighted in the dynamic map, representing instances of co-citation. In the map, the nodes are connected by links which indicate the occurrence of co-citation to create a network of the major research streams within the field of sports medicine. The size of the node is proportional to the references’ total number of local citations. Citespace analysis also provides a timeline view of the evolution of the research streams within sports medicine (based on the year of publications from 1950 to 2021).

Influential article analysis: Using Citespace 5.7.R1, document co-citation and citation burst analyses were also completed. Document co-citation is used to find the most influential documents within sports medicine as well as the clusters of thematically similar references. Document co-citation methodology, developed by Chen [14], discovers cohorts of references that are frequently co-cited in the reference list of sports medicine papers. This is on the principle that these references are similar in subject and represent foundational knowledge of a certain subject within the field of sports medicine. The benefit of this analysis is that a new set of documents is identified that are instrumental and relevant to sports medicine but were not originally captured by the original WoS search.

Semantic analysis: VOSviewer 1.6.15 [15] was utilised to conduct title and abstract, author keyword3 analyses. Clusters of terms are formed from the frequency in which they occur in the title and abstract (set to a minimum of 50) and as author keywords (set to a minimum of 20) within documents. They provide an objective overview of the structure and divisions within this research field and the relationships between the divisions.

Results

General findings

As of November 2022, there were 150,380 publications indexed in WoS in the field of sports and exercise medicine. Figure 1 shows the number of publications by year since the early 1970s. A slow increase is seen between 1980 and 2000. After 2000, it starts to increase at a faster rate; however, a number of troughs are observed over the years (e.g., 2003, 2007, 2009). The peak year is 2020, with 9,055 sports medicine documents published, before it drops in 2021 and 2022. Most of the documents are original research papers (48%), meeting abstracts (36%), and review papers (6.5%), with the remainder consisting of editorials, letters, news items, or similar.

Number of sports medicine publications by year

When excluding meeting abstracts, the year 2021 had approximately 200 more publications than 2020. However, the number of documents in 2022 was still significantly lower than in 2021, with approximately 2000 documents, excluding abstracts.

All the documents were indexed under Sport Sciences in WoS Categories. Other categories they are also included in are Orthopaedics (21%), Hospitality Leisure and Tourism (10%), Psychology Applied (10%), Psychology (9.5%), and Surgery (5%). Discussion

This scientometric review of more than 150,000 sports and exercise medicine research documents provides a comprehensive overview of published work in the field up to 2022. Emerging research changes our understanding and management of common injuries, and thus the importance of analysing this field is emphasised. Academic rigour is now being applied to clinical problems which were previously being managed through empirical evidence only [28]. The results highlight the significant growth of sports medicine research over the past five decades, with a sharp increase in publications after 2000. The peak in 2020 reflects heightened research activity, possibly influenced by global interest in exercise and health during the COVID-19 pandemic. The subsequent decline in 2021 and 2022 is partially explained by the WoS shift toward indexing comprehensive, peer-reviewed articles rather than meeting abstracts.

The majority of sports and exercise medicine literature has been produced by the USA, UK, Canada, and Australia, with the USA contributing 55% of all publications. This dominance reflects the USA’s strong infrastructure, investment in sports medicine, and its integration of sport into national identity and economy [29]. While other English-speaking countries like the UK, Australia, and Canada play significant roles, few non-OECD countries feature among the top contributors. For instance, despite its prominence in other scientific fields, China is not a major contributor to sports medicine research [30]. This concentration of output highlights the disparity in research capabilities between high-income and low-income countries. High-income countries often have the resources to fund cutting-edge research and maintain infrastructure that facilitates high productivity in this field. In contrast, low-income and emerging nations may lack access to similar resources, limiting their contributions. To address this imbalance, international funding programs, partnerships, and capacity-building initiatives should be encouraged to enhance inclusivity and broaden the global impact of sports medicine research.

The diversity of research streams in sports and exercise medicine, as shown by the document co-citation analysis, demonstrates the field’s multidimensional nature. Unlike other disciplines with highly interconnected clusters [31, 32], sports and exercise medicine exhibits standalone clusters with distinct knowledge foundations. This fragmented yet specialized body of knowledge poses challenges for cross-disciplinary integration. The field’s evolution has been marked by a shift from a singular focus on Anaerobic capacity to a diverse array of research streams that emerged around the year 2000. For instance, new topics such as Hamstring injury, Concussion, and Knee injuries gained prominence, reflecting advancements in technology, diagnostics, and treatment methods. Conversely, older topics like Anaerobic capacity and Heat Stress have faded, highlighting the dynamic nature of research priorities.

Strong citation bursts, as indicated in Figs. 4 and 5, reveal pivotal moments in sports and exercise medicine’s history, where breakthrough discoveries or influential documents garnered widespread attention. For example, the adoption of concussion management guidelines [33] marked a major turning point in athlete safety and reflects the responsiveness of sports and exercise medicine to emerging health issues. Similarly, the increased focus on topics such as resistance training aligns with changes in physical activity guidelines, emphasizing the importance of muscular strength for overall health [34, 35]. These shifts underline the field’s ability to adapt to societal needs and scientific advancements. Semantic analysis identifies divisions such as Exercise variables, Biomechanics, and Sports dynamics, which represent core areas of focus. The identification of Sports injuries as a separate cluster aligns with its practical and clinical relevance, further emphasizing the multidisciplinary scope of the field.

Advances in technology since the early 2000s have enabled researchers to explore areas such as wearable sensors, GPS tracking, and biometric monitoring more comprehensively [36–38]. These innovations have often been integrated within existing streams, such as Measuring physical activity. However, no standalone research streams dedicated to technological advancements were identified in this review. It is possible that within existing streams, documents address technology-specific aspects relevant to the research area. A repeat analysis of the field in the next decade may reveal a distinct digital technology stream as the influx of research into this area continues to grow.

This study also highlights the rise and fall of various research streams, illustrating the fluid nature of sports medicine interests. Topics like Interval training, Hamstring injury, and Sport psychology have maintained their relevance over the past two decades. However, subjects such as Anaerobic capacity and Metabolism have declined in prominence, reflecting shifts in both scientific interest and societal relevance. For instance, increasing concerns over the long-term consequences of sports-related head injuries [39, 40] have driven significant growth in concussion-related studies, leading to the widespread adoption of concussion management guidelines [41, 42].

The transition within Sport psychology research from performance psychology to behavioral psychology reflects broader recognition of the interconnectedness between physical and mental health. Seminal works like “Do anxious swimmers swim slower? Re-examining the elusive anxiety-performance relationship” [43] and “Social cognitive theory and physical activity: Mechanisms of behavior change, critique, and legacy” [44], published decades apart, exemplify this shift. This change highlights the growing importance of behavioral change research, reflecting a holistic approach to athlete well-being.

Technological advancements have also enabled greater exploration of exercise physiology, biomechanics, and performance optimization, leading to innovative tools like imaging technologies, telemedicine, sensors and virtual reality simulations [45, 46]. These advances are transforming athlete monitoring and injury prevention, although their specific contributions to sports and exercise medicine research are yet to be fully quantified. As technology becomes more integrated into healthcare and sports science, the role of artificial intelligence and machine learning in optimizing athlete performance and injury prevention will likely continue to expand, further diversifying the field [47, 48].

Several landmark documents have shaped sports and exercise medicine research, including the first American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) position statement published in 1978 [49], which aligns with peaks in Anaerobic capacity and Metabolism research streams. The 1996 Surgeon General’s Report on Physical Activity and Health [50] catalyzed interest in Physical activity research, influencing public health policies and awareness campaigns. Conversely, the World Health Organization’s 2010 guidelines on physical activity [51] did not correspond to significant shifts in research activity, perhaps reflecting the USA’s outsized influence on the field.

The most cited document, the “International physical activity questionnaire: 12-country reliability and validity” (IPAQ) [52], plays a pivotal role in cross-national monitoring of physical activity and sedentary behavior. Similarly, “Progressive Statistics for Studies in Sports Medicine and Exercise Science” [53] is the most influential work based on local citation count, significantly shaping research methodologies and statistical approaches. These foundational documents continue to inform both theoretical and practical advancements in the field.

Limitations and Future Directions

This review’s reliance on top-tier journals introduces potential biases by excluding emerging or innovative trends published elsewhere. Furthermore, a time lag between research completion and publication in high-impact journals may prevent timely reflection of cutting-edge developments. Expanding future analyses to include a broader range of sources could somewhat address these limitations.

Looking ahead, the future of sports and exercise medicine research is poised for remarkable advancements, driven by interdisciplinary collaboration, personalized athlete care, and continued technological innovation [54, 55]. These trends promise to reshape the field, enhancing athlete performance, recovery, and overall well-being.

Conclusion

This comprehensive scientometric review of research in the sports and exercise medicine field has shed light on its historical evolution, current state, and potential future directions. Sports medicine, once a subspecialty, has now matured into a distinct and complex field of study. The findings provide valuable insights for practitioners, researchers, and policymakers in the sports medicine field. As sports and exercise medicine continues to evolve, a deeper understanding of its historical roots and current landscape will help inform future research priorities and foster advancements in athlete care, performance enhancement, and public health.

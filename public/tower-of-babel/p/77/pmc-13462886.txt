Development and frontier trends of pancreaticojejunostomy over the past 26 years: bibliometric and visual analysis
Authors: Xiao Cui, Piao Wang, Xiao-Zhong Jiang, Wen-Hao Zhou, Chang-Song Wang, Yu Yu, Song Su, Bin Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite continuous advancements in pancreaticojejunostomy (PJ) techniques, there remains a lack of unified clinical guidelines for anastomosis selection. To date, bibliometric analysis has not yet been applied to systematically characterize the global development of PJ research. Accordingly, this study aimed to perform comprehensive bibliometric and visual analysis to clarify the developmental trajectory, research hotspots and emerging frontiers.MethodsA systematic literature search was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for English-language original research and reviews on PJ published from January 1, 2000, to May 1, 2026. Bibliometric and visual analyses were performed using CiteSpace 6.4 R1, VOSviewer 1.6.20, Bibliometrix 4.1, and Scimago Graphica to characterize publication trends, research collaboration networks (country, institution, author), journal distribution, keyword co-occurrence, clustering, and citation burst characteristics.ResultsA total of 3,329 eligible studies were included. PJ-related publications showed a continuous upward trend with a marked surge over the past 26 years. Japan, the United States and China were the most prolific countries. The United States, Italy, and Germany exhibited stronger international collaboration and higher citation impact. Zhejiang University, Sichuan University, and the University of Verona were the leading contributing institutions, with the University of Pennsylvania, the University of Pittsburgh, and Mayo Clinic emerging as core nodes in the global collaboration network. The largest author collaboration cluster was led by Claudio Bassi, Roberto Salvia, and Giovanni Marchegiani (University of Verona). Journal of Gastrointestinal Surgery, Hepato-Gastroenterology, and HPB were the primary journals for knowledge dissemination, and interdisciplinary integration with molecular biology, genetics, and computational science was increasingly prominent. Pancreatic fistula was the most frequent and core keyword, and citation burst and trend analysis revealed that current and future research hotspots focus on international standardized definitions, risk stratification tools, and clinical application of robot-assisted technology.ConclusionsPJ research has experienced progressive development from technical exploration to standardized norms and further to precision innovation over the past 26 years, with postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) prevention and management as the enduring core theme. International cooperation, technological innovation, and interdisciplinary integration are the key driving forces for PJ development. Future PJ research should focus on strengthening international multicenter collaboration, developing personalized risk assessment strategies, advancing robot-assisted anastomotic techniques, and promoting the popularization of advanced PJ technologies in resource-limited regions.

Introduction

Pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD), commonly referred to as Whipple procedure, is the standard surgical approach for treating ampullary tumors and periampullary malignancy (1,2). Nevertheless, postoperative complications, particularly postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF), remain significant factors influencing surgical outcomes and patient prognosis (3,4). The incidence of POPF reported in the literature ranges from approximately 21% to 32%, making it a leading cause of postoperative mortality (3,5). Pancreaticojejunostomy (PJ) is a crucial step in PD and plays a vital role in determining the occurrence of POPF (6,7). In recent years, with advancements in surgical techniques, PJ has undergone numerous evolutions, and new anastomosis methods have been proposed and implemented in clinical practice (8-10). However, significant variations exist among different anastomosis methods, which indirectly result in varying POPF incidence rates (11). Furthermore, there are no unified standards or guidelines for selecting and implementing PJ.

The literature review method offers valuable insights, but its perspective is limited. For instance, while qualitative analysis is insightful, it may not fully capture the growth in numbers and theme evolution in this field (5). Additionally, meta-analysis can provide robust evidence but may be constrained by the scope of research and may overlook emerging trends in the current research landscape (11).

Bibliometrics, as a discipline that examines literature and related phenomena, has gained widespread use in medical research in recent years (12,13). Through quantitative analysis of medical literature, researchers can identify research hotspots, trends, and academic influence, thereby providing guidance for future research directions and clinical decision-making. After visualization, the analysis results can not only intuitively display research trends, hot fields, and academic collaboration networks but also reveal the relationships between different research topics, enabling researchers to discover potential cross-disciplinary fields and collaboration opportunities.

However, there is currently no bibliometric study on PJ, and it is essential to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric evaluation of PJ-related research to elucidate its evolution, identify current research hotspots and trends, and provide guidance for future research in this field. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-0195/rc).

Methods

Data sources and search strategies

We performed an extensive literature search using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database to identify studies on PJ published from January 1, 2000, to May 1, 2026. The search query was ((((((TS= (pancreaticojejunostomy)) OR TS= (pancreatic anastomosis)) OR TS= (duct-to-mucosa anastomosis)) OR TS= (Blumgart anastomosis)) OR TS= (invagination anastomosis)) OR TS= (pancreaticoenterostomy)). Given that English is the predominant language of international scientific communication, we restricted our analysis to research published in English. To ensure data integrity and avoid complications from future database updates, the dataset was locked on the retrieval date. We included only original research and review articles, excluding proceeding paper, meeting abstract, editorial material, letter, early access, book chapters, correction, retracted publication, expression of concern. To ensure objectivity, two independent reviewers (X.C., P.W.) work together with a third reviewer to screen the title and abstract and negotiate to rule on any differences. Differences in the screening process are resolved through discussion and consensus. If consensus cannot be reached, consult with senior authors. Since this study involves the analysis of public literature and does not include human subjects or patient data, ethical approval is not required.

Statistical analysis

CiteSpace 6.4 R1, VOSviewer 1.6.20, Bibliometrix 4.1, and Scimago Graphica were employed for the bibliometric and visual analysis of PJ literature. CiteSpace facilitates the identification of influential publications through visual analysis, tracking of citation and keyword bursts, generation of co-occurrence and clustering maps, and detection of emerging research frontiers. VOSviewer is utilized to establish collaborative networks and create shared citation graphs among co-authors, institutions, and countries. Bibliometrix 4.1, an open-source R suite, generates additional visualization results, including publication trends of institutions and authors and trend theme analysis. Scimago Graphica, in combination with VOSviewer, is used to generate map visualization results. The bibliometric indicators analyzed in this study include author, country, institution, journal, citation distribution, keyword distribution, and clustering.

Results

The initial search yielded a total of 3,633 relevant studies, and the final dataset included 3,329 independent studies (Figure 1A). This study conducted a comprehensive analysis of contributions from 100 different countries, 3,067 institutions, and 15,901 researchers, published in 520 different journals. Figure 1B shows the trend of annual publications and the overall annual publication volume related to PJ showed an increasing trend, reflecting the growth of academic interest and research in this area. Overall, the literature output in this field has shown a sustained growth trend, which can be roughly divided into three stages: the initial exploration period from 2000 to 2008, during which the number of publications slowly increased from 45 to 105; the steady development period from 2009 to 2018, during which the annual number of publications remained between 96 and 170, with research interest gradually increasing; and the rapid growth period since 2019, during which the number of publications climbed from 188 to a peak of 234 in 2025, reflecting the sustained attention to research related to PJ in recent years. In addition, the number of publications in 2026 (63 publications) is only partial data, and it is expected that the number of publications this year will continue to remain at a high level.

Research strength and collaborative network analysis

National cooperation network

Figure 2A illustrates the primary nations involved in the research and depicts a map of international collaboration. Japan (623 publications), the United States (560 publications), and China (539 publications) rank among the top three globally in terms of publication output and are the primary contributors to research in this field. In terms of collaboration intensity, the United States had the highest total link strength [279], followed by European countries such as Italy [244], the United Kingdom [176], Germany [165], and France [154], which also demonstrated high levels of cross-national collaboration activity. The overall network exhibited characteristics of multi-centrality and high connectivity, with dense collaboration links among core countries, reflecting the trend of globalized collaborative research in this field.

Institutional cooperation network

The institutional collaboration network depicted in Figure 2B highlights the primary institutions spearheading the research. Sichuan University [54], Zhejiang University [52], the University of Verona [41], the University of Amsterdam [39], and the Mayo Clinic [38] are the top five contributors. While the Mayo Clinic [162], the University of Pittsburgh [145], the University of Pennsylvania [144], the University of Amsterdam [138], and the University of Verona [122] have the highest total link strengths, acting as key collaboration hubs. The network is multi-centered and clustered. While European and American institutions demonstrate close transnational collaboration, Chinese and Japan institutions are characterized by dense regional networks, reflecting both global and regional collaborative trends.

Author collaboration network

The author collaboration network (Figure 2C) revealed multiple distinct research clusters, with several influential institutional teams forming the core of the collaborative landscape. The largest and most interconnected cluster is centered on Claudio Bassi, Roberto Salvia, and Giovanni Marchegiani from the University of Verona, Italy. This high-output team has published a series of influential studies in the field, driving key academic consensus. Parallel to this, several prominent regional clusters exist, including a North American hub led by researchers from the Mayo Clinic (e.g., Markus W. Buechler) and the University of Pittsburgh (e.g., Shailesh V. Shrikhande), whose high-impact publications and collaborative links underscore their role in advancing clinical and translational research. An Asian cluster, meanwhile, involves Yoshiaki Murakami and Kenichiro Uemura from Hiroshima University, contributing significantly to regional research output. Additionally, teams from Chinese institutions including Sichuan University and Zhejiang University are represented in the network, with core authors like Xin Wang publishing steadily but maintaining limited cross-regional connections.

Journal citation and co-citation network

The journal citation and co-citation networks (Figure 2D,2E) illustrate the publication distribution and intellectual structure of journals in this field. In terms of publication output, the Journal of Gastrointestinal Surgery (152 documents), Hepato-Gastroenterology (123 documents), HPB (91 documents) are the most productive journals, reflecting their role as primary publication venues for research in this field. Meanwhile, citation metrics reveal that Annals of Surgery, Surgery, and the Journal of Gastrointestinal Surgery are the top influential journals. These high-impact journals form the core of the co-citation network, with strong mutual citation relationships, indicating their central role in shaping the field’s knowledge structure and research trends.

Analysis of knowledge flow in journals

The journal dual-map overlay (Figure 2F) illustrates the disciplinary distribution and knowledge flow of the field. Citing journals are concentrated in Medicine, Medical, Clinical and Molecular, Biology, Immunology, while cited literature centers on Health, Nursing, Medicine and clinical medicine， which demonstrates that the field’s knowledge structure is anchored in clinical medicine, with significant integration of basic biological and health sciences.

Keywords, research hotspots, and evolution

In the co-occurrence network of keywords (Figure 3A), the top 5 keywords with the highest frequency and centrality were “pancreaticoduodenectomy” “pancreaticojejunostomy” “pancreatic fistula” “international study group” and “complications”. Cluster analysis (Figure 3B) generated eight moderately reliable and structurally meaningful clusters (module value Q=0.4548, average contour value S=0.5777). Pancreatic fistula was the largest and most core red module in the graph, indicating that pancreatic fistula is the most concerning serious complication of PJ and the primary focus of research in this field.

Discussion

This study systematically mapped the global research landscape of PJ over the past 26 years using bibliometric methods, uncovering key patterns in publication trends, research collaboration, and knowledge dissemination. It also highlighted the current intellectual frontiers and future research directions. The steady increase in studies related to PJ suggests that PJ research has transitioned from an emerging field to a highly active discipline, closely linked to the enhanced technical accessibility of PD and the ongoing exploration of complications such as POPF by surgeons.

The national, institutional, and author collaboration networks collectively reflect the distribution of academic strength and collaborative patterns in this field. The United States and its institutions, as well as authors, have become the core hub of the global collaborative network due to their high strength of collaborative links, and are a key force in promoting cross regional academic exchanges. At the same time, American and European countries, institutions, and core scholars have formed closely connected regional cooperation clusters and leading the research direction and clinical consensus in the field of PJ. In contrast, China ranks among the top three in terms of publication volume, but the overall intensity of international cooperation is relatively weak. Such as, Sichuan University and Zhejiang University in China have published a large number of papers, their cooperation scope is mostly limited to domestic institutions and there is insufficient cooperation with institutions in Europe and America. As for Japan, involves Yoshiaki Murakami and Kenichiro Uemura from Hiroshima University, not only has it built a high-density and high-level cooperation system internally, but it has also maintained close cooperation with institutions in European and American countries, becoming an important bridge connecting Eastern and Western research. Overall, the cooperation network presents the characteristics of globalization and regionalization coexisting. In the future, it is necessary to further strengthen international cooperation and promote innovation and development in PJ field.

Journal citation and co-citation network reveal the journal distribution and evaluate journal influence. Journal of Gastrointestinal Surgery, Hepato-Gastroenterology and HPB rank the top three in publication output, acting as the most important publishing platforms for relevant clinical and basic research. In terms of overall academic influence reflected by total link strength, top journals such as Annals of Surgery and Surgery take a leading position. These high-impact journals maintain close co-citation connections with other core periodicals and serve as the intellectual cornerstone of knowledge dissemination within the discipline. Dual map overlay analysis of journals revealed a significant shift toward interdisciplinary research. While the field of PJ is rooted in surgery, its integration with molecular biology, genetics, and computational science is becoming increasingly extensive. This interdisciplinary integration is crucial for addressing the complex biological and technical challenges of PJ and is expected to drive the next wave of innovation in the field. For instance, the development of more accurate artificial intelligence-based POPF risk prediction models and the research and development of new anastomotic materials to reduce fistula leakage risk are promising directions.

As the most frequently occurring keyword, pancreatic fistula underscores its role as the primary research driver in the pancreatic surgery field, and its prevention, risk stratification, and management are central to improving patient outcomes. The closely related cluster fistula risk score further reflects the field’s emphasis on developing objectives, predictive tools to stratify risk and guide perioperative management, indicating a shift toward evidence-based, individualized care. In terms of disease indications, the keyword clusters reveal the broad scope of pancreatic surgery applications. Chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer form the largest disease-related clusters, representing the two most common conditions requiring pancreatic resection. Meanwhile, central pancreatectomy focuses on organ-preserving surgical techniques, representing the current pursuit of function-sparing and minimally invasive surgery by clinicians. Complication-related clusters, including delayed gastric emptying and postoperative complications, highlight the field’s extensive attention to perioperative morbidity beyond pancreatic fistula, such as gastrointestinal dysfunction and postoperative bleeding. The endoscopic ultrasound cluster indicates the critical role of minimally invasive diagnostic and interventional techniques in the workup of pancreatic diseases. Finally, the pancreas transplantation cluster represents a distinct, albeit smaller, area of research focused on the treatment of end-stage pancreatic disease, marking the field’s expansion beyond oncologic and benign resection to include transplantation as a definitive therapy.

The trend topics and keyword burst analyses together trace the evolving research priorities in PJ over the past 26 years, revealing a clear shift from foundational surgical techniques to modern, standardized, and minimally invasive approaches. At the beginning of the period, research was primarily focused on establishing core surgical practices. Terms such as Whipple procedure and duodenum-preserving resection dominate the early timeline, reflecting efforts to standardize pancreatic resection and reconstruction techniques. The citation bursts of mortality and prevention further highlight the field’s emphasis on evaluating surgical safety and efficacy. From 2010 to 2020, the research landscape expanded to include broader perioperative management and evidence synthesis. The citation burst of prospective randomized trial and meta-analysis during this period signals a shift toward high-level evidence, as researchers sought to define best practices through large-scale studies and systematic reviews. Such as, multiple high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have provided crucial evidence for optimizing PJ techniques. Hirono et al. (8) compared modified Blumgart mattress suturing with conventional interrupted duct-to-mucosa anastomosis and confirmed the safety and feasibility of the modified Blumgart technique. Similarly, the PANasta trial was a multicenter RCT designed to directly compare the efficacy of Blumgart anastomosis with standard Cattell-Warren duct-to-mucosa anastomosis after PD and indicating that Blumgart anastomosis did not significantly reduce complications (9). Since 2020, the PJ field has entered an era of standardization, innovation, and minimally invasive surgery. The keyword bursts of definition, classification, and international study group indicate a critical push toward globally standardized criteria, aiming to improve the universality of technology and definitions (14,15). Meanwhile, the trend topic chart shows a sharp rise in robotic PD, marking the shift toward minimally invasive and robot-assisted techniques as the new frontier of surgical innovation (16).

The analysis of keyword evolution and trend themes further highlights the unique value of bibliometric analysis in identifying unmet clinical needs that cannot be fully captured by traditional narrative reviews. Based on the present bibliometric findings, future investigations of PJ should concentrate on the following priorities. Firstly, driven by the strong and sustained burst of keywords including risk score, classification, and definition, there is an urgent demand to establish globally unified, standardized, and externally validated risk stratification systems for POPF. Such systems would integrate preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative factors to enable personalized surgical decision-making and reduce heterogeneity in clinical practice. Secondly, in response to the rapidly rising keyword burst of robotic PJ, future studies should focus on formulating standardized operative protocols and technical specifications for robotic PJ. High-quality real-world evidence and RCTs are warranted to compare efficiency, learning curves, postoperative outcomes, and cost-effectiveness relative to laparoscopic and open approaches. Thirdly, guided by the emerging focus on international study groups, global academic consortia should be established to promote methodological standardization and cross-regional collaboration. This includes unifying indications for different anastomotic techniques, standardizing outcome measures, and reducing outcome reporting bias. Finally, our geographic mapping reveals a substantial imbalance between high PD volume but low research output in several regions including Southeast Asia, South America, and parts of Africa. Future efforts should strengthen international multicenter cooperation to improve the generalizability and accessibility of PJ techniques in resource-limited settings, thereby developing more universally applicable and cost-effective reconstructive strategies.

As a bibliometric analysis, this study inevitably has limitations. First, this study relied solely on the Web of Science database, which may have overlooked relevant studies published in non-indexed or regional journals. Second, the indicators adopted in this study, such as publication volume, citation count, and centrality, mainly reflect the quantitative characteristics and dissemination breadth of research, and cannot directly evaluate the clinical value of research. For example, some highly cited studies may have questionable conclusions due to flawed methodological design, while some low-cited innovative technical studies may have important clinical guiding significance. Third, this study only included English publications and excluded relevant studies published in other languages. This language bias may limit the generalizability of the research results and overlook contributions from non-English-speaking regions. Fourth，the identified research hotspots and influential publications should guide future research priorities, but should not be interpreted as direct evidence for clinical decision-making. Future steps should include correlation with high-quality clinical evidence, such as randomized trials, multicenter prospective studies, and systematic reviews focused on patient-level outcomes. Finally, for the application of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and radiomics in PJ, due to the short publication time of relevant studies, their citation burst characteristics have not yet fully emerged, which may affect the accuracy of predicting future research hotspots.

Conclusions

In conclusion, PJ research in recent 26 years has achieved progressive development from technical exploration to standardized norms and then to precision innovation. The prevention and treatment of POPF have always been the core theme, while international cooperation, technological innovation, and interdisciplinary integration are the core driving forces for the development of the field. Although there are still problems such as unbalanced cooperation and insufficient evidence levels in current research, with the joint efforts of the global surgical community, PJ technology will evolve toward a more precise, safer, and more personalized direction, ultimately achieving continuous improvement in patient outcomes.
